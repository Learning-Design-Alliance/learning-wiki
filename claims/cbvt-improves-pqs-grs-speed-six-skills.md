# Novice medical students' product quality, technique (GRS), and speed rose significantly from pre-test to post-test across all six assessed knot-tying and suturing skills after a self-directed sequential CBVT course, in one uncontrolled pre-post study

## Evidence

### Kumins Norman H. 2021

Kumins Norman H., Qin Vivian L., Driscoll Erin C., Morrow Katherine L., Kashyap Vikram S., Ning Anne Y., Tucker Nicholas J., King Alexander H., Quereshy Humzah A., Dash Siddhartha, Grobaty Lauren, Zhou George. (2021). Computer-based video training is effective in teaching basic surgical skills to novices without faculty involvement using a self-directed, sequential and incremental program. American Journal of Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjsurg.2020.08.011

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre/post practical skills exam for 221 students in the Self-Directed Surgical Skills elective, with videotaped exams graded blind by a single reviewer. "The increase from pre-to post-course is significant for all 6 skills assessed by each of the three measures, P < 0.01"; pre-course PQS and GRS were <0.4, rising to ≥3.25 except tension-tying GRS of 2.51.

> "Every student who completed the course demonstrated a significant increase in their Product Quality Score (PQS), technique as measured by the Global Rating Scale (GRS), and speed for each of 6 skills assessed."
