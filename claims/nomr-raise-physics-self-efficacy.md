## Evidence

### Jared P. Canright and Suzanne White Brahmia 2024

Jared P. Canright and Suzanne White Brahmia (2024). Modeling novel physics in virtual reality labs: An affective analysis of student learning. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010146](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010146)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on paired pre-post PhIS self-efficacy Likert responses in both populations. The article reports "a positive shift for every self-efficacy item at a 99.7% confidence level or better (p <0.003) in both populations"; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "The effect sizes vary, but there is a positive shift for every self-efficacy item at a 99.7% confidence level or better (p <0.003) in both populations."

### Jared P. Canright and Suzanne White Brahmia 2024 (2)

Jared P. Canright and Suzanne White Brahmia (2024). Modeling novel physics in virtual reality labs: An affective analysis of student learning. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010146](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010146)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of interpolated-median shifts between the two populations across all five self-efficacy items. The shift in the 200-level students' responses is "consistently greater than that of the 100-level students, by roughly 50% on average."

> "Comparing the size of the populations' arrows for each item, we note that the shift in the 200-level students' responses is consistently greater than that of the 100-level students, by roughly 50% on average."
