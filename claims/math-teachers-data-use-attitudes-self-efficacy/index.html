Evidence¶

Rebecka Rundquist 2024¶

Rebecka Rundquist, Kristina Holmberg, John Rack, Zeynab Mohseni and Italo Masiello. (2024). Use of Learning Analytics in K–12 Mathematics Education: Systematic Scoping Review of the Impact on Teaching and Learning. Journal of Learning Analytics 11(3). https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2024.8299

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports Hawn's (2019b) survey-based comparison of mathematics teachers with other teachers on data-use attitudes, usage, actions, and self-efficacy.

"mathematics teachers when compared to other teachers, showed a positive attitude towards data use, had higher levels of usage, reported more actions based on data, higher usefulness for data ac tivities or formali zed testing, higher self -efficacy for DBDM but lower self-efficacy for teaching in general."

Rebecka Rundquist 2024 (2)¶

Rebecka Rundquist, Kristina Holmberg, John Rack, Zeynab Mohseni and Italo Masiello. (2024). Use of Learning Analytics in K–12 Mathematics Education: Systematic Scoping Review of the Impact on Teaching and Learning. Journal of Learning Analytics 11(3). https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2024.8299

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports Hawn's (2019a) finding that the high-variety/high-rate user group (8%) consisted almost exclusively of mathematics teachers, whose use centred on test creation and management.

"However, they mainly used the DLS to create and manage tests, as opposed to viewing and analyzing testing results. Mathematics teachers were also the only ones seen engaging in Student-Centred Analysis, a specialized use where they show a high rate of use based on multiple types of data for individual students."
