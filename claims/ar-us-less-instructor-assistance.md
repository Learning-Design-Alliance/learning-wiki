## Evidence

### Liao Shu-Chen 2024

Liao Shu-Chen, Shao Shih-Chieh, Gao Shi-Ying, Lai Edward Chia-Cheng. (2024). Augmented reality visualization for ultrasound-guided interventions: a pilot randomized crossover trial to assess trainee performance and cognitive load. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05998-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Scoring-system analysis of video-recorded performance, rating the level of instructor assistance across six CVC skill components on a three-level scale. The article reports the AR-US group "exhibited a higher level of independence in successfully completing ultrasound operations".

> "For each step, approximately 10% of the AR-US trainees needed assistance, but not hands-on help from the instructor. By contrast, a higher proportion of the S-US group, around 15%, needed assistance, including hands-on instructor guidance in every step."

### Liao Shu-Chen 2024 (2)

Liao Shu-Chen, Shao Shih-Chieh, Gao Shi-Ying, Lai Edward Chia-Cheng. (2024). Augmented reality visualization for ultrasound-guided interventions: a pilot randomized crossover trial to assess trainee performance and cognitive load. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05998-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same scoring-system analysis; the assistance gap was largest in needle tip visualization (4.26% vs. 10.64%), which the article says demands significant hand-eye coordination.

> "This difference was particularly evident in the needle tip visualization step (AR-US vs. S-US: 4.26% vs. 10.64%), a task that demands significant hand–eye coordination."
