## Subclaims
`q4 i3` Across 503 independent effect sizes, individuals made more internal, stable, global attributions for positive/success events than for negative/failure events, with a random-effects weighted mean d = 0.96 (95% CI 0.89–1.02). [→ Mezulis and Hyde 2004](#mezulis-and-hyde-2004)

## Evidence

### Mezulis and Hyde 2004

Amy H. Mezulis, Lyn Y. Abramson, Janet S. Hyde, and Benjamin L. Hankin. (2004). Is There a Universal Positivity Bias in Attributions? A Meta-Analytic Review of Individual, Developmental, and Cultural Differences in the Self-Serving Attributional Bias. Psychological Bulletin. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.130.5.711

`q4 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analysis of 266 studies identified through database and ancestry searches, yielding 503 independent effect sizes from 41,538 participants; 20 outlier effect sizes were removed. The random-effects weighted mean was d = 0.96, "representing a very large effect by Cohen's (1969) criteria."

> "Averaged over the 503 independent effect sizes, the random- effects estimate of the weighted mean effect size for the self- serving attributional bias was 0.96, representing a very large effect by Cohen's (1969) criteria. The 95% confidence interval for d was 0.89 to 1.02."
