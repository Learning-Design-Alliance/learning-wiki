## Evidence

### Gradl-Dietsch G 2016

Gradl-Dietsch G, Lübke C, Horst K, Simon M, Modabber A, Sönmez TT, Münker R, Nebelung S, Knobe M. (2016). Peyton's four-step approach for teaching complex spinal manipulation techniques – a prospective randomized trial. BMC Medical Education 16:284. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0804-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures analysis of the 24 participants available for the identical second OSPE showed significant decline in both groups (e.g., OSPE total 42.8 vs 57 in Peyton group; 34.6 vs 54.7 in control women). The Peyton group remained numerically higher but the difference did not reach significance.

> "Participants were asked to take a second OSPE, identical to the first, at least 6 months after completion of the course and results showed a significant decline in knowledge and competency to perform techniques."
