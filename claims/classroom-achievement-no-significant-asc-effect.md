# Classroom-average mathematics achievement shows no statistically significant association with ASC development when clique achievement is modeled simultaneously

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The classroom-level BFLPE was not statistically significant, and average classroom academic achievement did not predict ASC development. [→ Tanja Auer 2025](#tanja-auer-2025)

## Evidence

### Tanja Auer 2025

Tanja Auer, Marion Reindl, Burkhard Gniewosz. (2025). Is the clique a pond? The big-fish-little-pond effect and the relative meaning of clique and classroom. Learning and Instruction 95 (2025) 101997. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.101997

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same three-level longitudinal model as the clique finding; this entry reports the classroom-level BFLPE estimate from the Results section. The printed confidence interval [ 1.615, 0.356] includes zero, so no effect size is claimed; equivalence was not tested.

> "At the classroom level, the BFLPE was not statistically significant: b = 0.68, posterior SD = 0.48, 95% CI [ 1.615, 0.356]. Average classroom academic achievement did not predict ASC development."
