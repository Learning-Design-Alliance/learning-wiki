# Scaffolded play in Quantum Odyssey enables learners to intuitively work with established QIS topics without prior mathematics or physics exposure

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` With appropriate scaffolding, learners developed a visual rather than mathematical intuition enabling them to correctly answer entry-level quantum information science questions posed in focus groups. [→ Nita 2021](#nita-2021)

## Evidence

### Nita 2021

Nita, L., Chancellor, N., Mazzoli Smith, L., Cramman, H. & Dost, G. (2021). Inclusive learning for quantum computing: supporting the aims of quantum literacy using the puzzle game Quantum Odyssey. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2106.07077](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2106.07077)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Focus-group evidence from the pilot shows participants "were able to correctly understand the question and provide working answers" about reaching quantum states without the visual map, which the authors interpret as evidence of visual intuition; no effect sizes are reported.

> "During the focus groups participants were asked technical questions about how to reach certain quantum states without having the in-game Computation Map in front of them and as a group were able to correctly understand the question and provide working answers, by recalling what quantum gates were doing and how they combined to build quantum algorithms."
