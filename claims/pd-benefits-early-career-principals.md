# Professional development is associated with larger mathematics gains for students of early-career principals than for students of mid-career and veteran principals

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The benefits of professional development for student mathematics gains are significantly larger for principals with 3 or fewer years of experience, with significant differentials for managing change, leading instruction, and developing people. [→ Campoli 2022](#campoli-2022)
`q3 i?` These differentials by principal experience level do not appear when the outcome is student gains in English language arts. [→ Campoli 2022](#campoli-2022)

## Evidence

### Campoli 2022

Campoli, A. K., & Darling-Hammond, L. (with Podolsky, A., & Levin, S.). (2022). Principal learning opportunities and school outcomes: Evidence from California. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/438.376

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Interaction analysis (Table 5) of professional development components by principal experience group for mathematics gains. The main effect for principals with 3 or fewer years is 2.186* overall, with managing change at 2.685*** and developing people at 1.8**; the 4–9 years and 10+ years groups show negative relative coefficients (e.g., managing change -2.048* and -1.79*), meaning their professional development experiences are associated with smaller gains than those of inexperienced principals. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "The greater benefits for early-career principals are statistically significant for professional development associated with managing change, leading instruction, and developing people."
