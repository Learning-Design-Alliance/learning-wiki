# Post-hoc group differences in FFA activation were mostly non-significant, except a significant laypeople-versus-residents difference in the right FFA on short runs for correctly diagnosed trials (d = 0.71)

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` When only correctly diagnosed trials were analyzed, post-hoc t-tests showed a significant right FFA difference between groups on short runs, with Cohen's d = 0.71; other post-hoc tests were non-significant. [→ Kok Ellen M. 2021](#kok-ellen-m-2021)

## Evidence

### Kok Ellen M. 2021

Kok Ellen M., Sorger Bettina, van Geel Koos, Gegenfurtner Andreas, van Merriënboer Jeroen J. G., Robben Simon G. F., de Bruin Anique B. H. (2021). Holistic processing only? The role of the right fusiform face area in radiological expertise. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0256849

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Post-hoc t-tests following the correctly-diagnosed-trials-only ANOVA in the fMRI diagnostic-reasoning study. The article reports "t(26) = 1.80, p = .04 (one-sided t-test), Cohen's d = 0.71" for the right FFA short runs, with all other post-hoc t's below 1.0.

> "Post-hoc t-tests show a significant effect for the right FFA short runs, t(26) = 1.80, p = .04 (one-sided t-test), Cohen's d = 0.71 (medium-to-large effect); for the other t-tests all t's <1.0."
