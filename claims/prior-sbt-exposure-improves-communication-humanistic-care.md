## Evidence

### Liu Zhenhua 2022

Liu Zhenhua, Chen Qiong, Wu Jing, Li Xinhua, He Yuchen, Yu Qiao. (2022). Simulation-based training in asthma exacerbation for medical students: effect of prior exposure to simulation training on performance. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-022-03300-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Global rating scale ratings by two blinded assessors during the asthma simulation in the same 203-student observational study. The article reports both "communication skills" and "medical humanistic care" scores were higher in the pre-exposure group with printed means and p values.

> "scores in communication skills (2.64 ± 0.64 in the unexposed group vs. 3.24 ± 0.60 in the pre-exposure group, p = 0.002) and medical humanistic care (2.60 ± 0.71 in the unexposed group vs. 3.16 ± 0.62 in the pre-exposure group, p = 0.005) were higher in the pre-exposure group compared with the unexposed group."

### Liu Zhenhua 2022 (2)

Liu Zhenhua, Chen Qiong, Wu Jing, Li Xinhua, He Yuchen, Yu Qiao. (2022). Simulation-based training in asthma exacerbation for medical students: effect of prior exposure to simulation training on performance. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-022-03300-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same GRS assessment; the humanistic care contrast between the two named groups is reported with printed means and p = 0.005. Communication was evaluated using the DPC questionnaire and ComOn check rating scale; humanistic care used the ICARE Scale.

> "scores in communication skills (2.64 ± 0.64 in the unexposed group vs. 3.24 ± 0.60 in the pre-exposure group, p = 0.002) and medical humanistic care (2.60 ± 0.71 in the unexposed group vs. 3.16 ± 0.62 in the pre-exposure group, p = 0.005) were higher in the pre-exposure group compared with the unexposed group."
