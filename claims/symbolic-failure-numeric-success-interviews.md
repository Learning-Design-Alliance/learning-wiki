# In the interview study, it was common for students unable to solve a symbolic problem to find their earlier mistakes and easily reach the correct answer when given the numeric version immediately afterward

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Eugene T. Torigoe 2015

Eugene T. Torigoe. (2015). Unpacking Symbolic Equations in Introductory Physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1508.00535](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1508.00535)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Speak-aloud problem-solving interviews with thirteen introductory physics students, using modified versions of questions from an earlier final exam study; students first attempted the symbolic version and, if they struggled, the numeric version of the same question. The author reports it was "common to observe" symbolic failure followed by easy numeric success.

> "During these interviews it was common to observe a students who were unable to correctly solve the symbolic problem, but when given the numeric problem immediately afterward, could ﬁnd their earlier mistakes and could easily ﬁnd the correct answer."
