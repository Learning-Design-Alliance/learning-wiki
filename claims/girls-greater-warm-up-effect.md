## Evidence

### Srdjan Verbić 2010

Srdjan Verbić. (2010). Does it matter if you answer slowly?. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1005.0714](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1005.0714)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Period-level comparison using the four rolling-section test variants of the PD09 test. The article reports girls spending 41 seconds more in the first period, "t(893)=-4.4, p<2e-5", with non-significant differences in the following three periods.

> "In the first period, girls, on average, spend 41 second more to answer questions than boys do. This difference is statistically very significant: t(893)=-4.4, p<2e-5. In the following three periods, girls are also slower than boys, but that difference is not significant."

### Srdjan Verbić 2010 (2)

Srdjan Verbić. (2010). Does it matter if you answer slowly?. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1005.0714](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1005.0714)

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Position-level analysis of mean normalized item response times by gender (Figure 3). The article reports "Correlation between difference in τi for boys and girls and the item position i is r=-0.62, p<0.0002", with the difference decreasing gradually with item position.

> "Correlation between difference in τi for boys and girls and the item position i is r=-0.62, p<0.0002."
