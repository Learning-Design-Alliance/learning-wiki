# UDL treatment students were significantly more actively engaged while control students were significantly more passively engaged and spent more time not engaged

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Treatment students spent roughly twice as much time actively engaged as control students (partial η = .582 for active engagement), while control students were significantly more passively engaged (η = .391) and spent significantly more time not engaged (η = .336). [→ Katz 2013](#katz-2013)

## Evidence

### Katz 2013

Jennifer Katz. (2013). The Three Block Model of Universal Design for Learning (UDL): Engaging students in inclusive education. Canadian Journal of Education, 36(1). https://journals.sfu.ca/cje/index.php/cje

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Observational data from 94 randomly selected students using one-minute time sampling during core academic instruction; MANCOVA results in Table 6 show partial η of .582 (active), .391 (passive), and .336 (non-engagement), all p < .001. Table 3 means show treatment active engagement rising from 20.75 to 43.80 per 60 minutes while control fell from 34.39 to 19.36.

> "In particular, students in treatment classes were significantly more actively engaged, while student in control classes were significantly more passively engaged, and spent significantly more time not engaged. In fact, means were almost doubled in both directions"
