## Evidence

### Rudling 2026

Rudling, M., Rumetshofer, T., Nirme, J. & Mårtensson, J. (2026). Embodied Word Learning in Schools and Sustained Attention in Virtual Reality. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00395-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Correlational analyses of headset-derived behaviour in the crossover study. Assembly time (r = -0.01, p = 0.954) and rotation (r = -0.01, p = 0.914) also did not correlate significantly with VR recall.

> "We first correlated Word gaze with the VR recall scores, but found no significant correlation (r = -0.06, p = 0.609, BCaCI 95% CI = -0.27; 0.14, n = 71)."

### Rudling 2026 (2)

Rudling, M., Rumetshofer, T., Nirme, J. & Mårtensson, J. (2026). Embodied Word Learning in Schools and Sustained Attention in Virtual Reality. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00395-2

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Correlational and t-test analyses of the eye-tracking Word Gaze measure. Older children looked longer at the written word (r = 0.34); girls looked longer than boys (Cohen's d = 0.79). Rotation correlated with age (r = -0.37) and gender (r = -0.38).

> "Word gaze was however significantly correlated with age (r = 0.34, p = 0.003, 95% BCaCI = 0.13; 0.53, n = 72) as older children seemed to have looked longer at the written word. Additionally, there was a significant difference between genders (t(70) = 3.35, p = 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.79) where girls (M = 0.23 % of the time the word was displayed, SD = 0.10) looked longer than boys (M = 0.16 %, SD = 0.06)."
