## Evidence

### Gross D 2015

Gross D, Pietri ES, Anderson G, Moyano-Camihort K, Graham MJ. (2015). Increased Preclass Preparation Underlies Student Outcome Improvement in the Flipped Classroom. CBE—Life Sciences Education 14(4). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-02-0040

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Mixed-model ANCOVA of accuracy on online questions across weeks relative to exams, controlling for GPA. The printed effect size is partial η² = 0.023 (partial η2 = 0.023), a small effect, with F(1, 249) = 5.84, p < 0.02, for flipped students who "more accurately answered homework problems."

> "students in the flipped class more accurately answered homework problems on average than students in the standard class (F(1, 249) = 5.84, p < 0.02, partial η2 = 0.023)"

### Gross D 2015 (2)

Gross D, Pietri ES, Anderson G, Moyano-Camihort K, Graham MJ. (2015). Increased Preclass Preparation Underlies Student Outcome Improvement in the Flipped Classroom. CBE—Life Sciences Education 14(4). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-02-0040

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Mediation analysis using a Sobel test (Sobel's z = 3.24, p < 0.01) and Hayes' PROCESS macro with 5000 bootstrap samples. The article reports "a significant indirect effect" with indirect effect = 1.00, 95% CI = 0.49–1.68; the class-type effect remained significant after controlling for accuracy.

> "There was a significant indirect effect (i.e., the 95% confidence interval did not cross 0; indirect effect = 1.00, 95% CI = 0.49–1.68)."
