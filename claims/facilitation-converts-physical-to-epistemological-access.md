# Physical access to AI-provided material does not automatically produce epistemological access; facilitation converts one into the other

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Without facilitation that presses students beyond surface engagement with AI-provided material, expanded access produces the illusion of deeper inquiry rather than the reality of it. [→ Michael Rowe 2026](#michael-rowe-2026)

## Evidence

### Michael Rowe 2026

Michael Rowe. (2026). Problem-based learning and the structural conditions for productive AI integration. https://doi.org/10.35542/osf.io/haet3_v1

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Conceptual argument (§4.2), drawing the physical/epistemological access distinction from Rowe (2025a), building on Morrow (2009) and Warschauer (2004). The facilitator's role of pressing on reasoning is what converts physical access into epistemological access.

> "Physical access, the ability to reach a resource, retrieve information, or interact with a tool, does not automatically produce the epistemological access necessary for learning: the knowledge, practices, and dispositions required to make that resource intellectually productive."
