Evidence¶

Jake Renzella 2026¶

Jake Renzella, Christian Bergh, Natasha Banks, and Alexandra Vassar. (2026). Gender Differences in AI Literacy Workshop Outcomes and Deepfake Engagement. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14718 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2606.14718

q3 · i3 · causal · r1

Baseline OLS regression on pre-workshop survey data from Npre = 199 Australian secondary students, controlling for year level and school, with gender dummy-coded. Males scored higher on all three career-interest items; the article reports "d=−.81" for engineering as the largest difference.

"The largest gender differences emerged in STEM career aspirations, with male students reporting significantly higher interest in engineering (MM = 2.86vs.M F = 1.94, d=−.81,p=<.001), computer science (M M = 2.31vs.M F = 1.61,d=−.68, p=<.001), and AI (M M = 2.06vs.M F = 1.55,d=−.58,p=<.001)."
