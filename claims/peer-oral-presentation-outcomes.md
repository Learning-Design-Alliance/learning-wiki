# Peer assessment of oral presentations improves self-concept relative to control and improves presentation performance, but outcome studies remain few

## Evidence

### Keith Topping 1998

Keith Topping. (1998). Peer Assessment Between Students in Colleges and Universities. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543068003249

`q2 · i?`

Quasi-experimental study by Heun (1969) comparing effects of peer and staff assessment of four public speeches on student self-concept in a basic speech course; peer influence "reached a significant level for the final speech" while instructor influence was nonsignificant.

> "Relative to a control group, peer influence on the self-concept of students reached a significant level for the final speech, while instructor influence was nonsignificant across all four speeches."

### Keith Topping 1998 (2)

Keith Topping. (1998). Peer Assessment Between Students in Colleges and Universities. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543068003249

`q2 · i?`

Outcome study reported by the review: peer review of oral presentation skills "led to significantly improved performance". The review cautions there are insufficient presentation studies to draw conclusions about the most productive kinds or quality of feedback.

> "Mitchell and Bakewell (1995) found that peer review of oral presentation skills led to significantly improved performance."
