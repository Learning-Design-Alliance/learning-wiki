# The number of slow oscillations during sleep is positively correlated with attractiveness ratings of faces associated with cued names but not uncued names

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Slow oscillation count correlated positively with cued-name facial attractiveness ratings (r(74) = 0.40, a printed correlation effect size) but not with uncued-name ratings (r(74) = −0.04). [→ Zhao 2025](#zhao-2025)

## Evidence

### Zhao 2025

Zhao, H., Lin, X., Yuan, K., Hu, X., Wang, X., Su, W., Wang, Q., & Lu, L. (2025). Updating social evaluation during sleep. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00356-9

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Pearson correlational analysis of slow oscillation counts against attractiveness ratings. The printed effect size is r(74) = 0.40 for cued names (p_adjusted < 0.001); a "significant positive correlation" contrasted with no significant correlation for uncued names (r(74) = −0.04, p_adjusted = 0.742).

> "revealed a significant positive correlation between the number of SOs and the attractiveness ratings of the faces associated with the cued names (r(74) = 0.40, padjusted < 0.001). In contrast, the correlation between the number of SOs and attractiveness ratings for the faces associated with the uncued names was not significant (r(74) = −0.04, padjusted = 0.742)"

### Zhao 2025 (2)

Zhao, H., Lin, X., Yuan, K., Hu, X., Wang, X., Su, W., Wang, Q., & Lu, L. (2025). Updating social evaluation during sleep. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00356-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary control analysis ruled out N1 sleep differences between groups as a confound: N1 duration and percentage showed no significant correlation with cued-name attractiveness ratings (r(104) = −0.047 and −.096), since cues were delivered primarily during SWS.

> "This was conﬁrmed by the lack of signiﬁcant correlations between N1 duration/percentage and attractiveness ratings of the mental representation of the cued name (N1 duration:r(104) =−0.047, p = 0.632; N1 percentage: r(104) = -.096,p = 0.330)"
