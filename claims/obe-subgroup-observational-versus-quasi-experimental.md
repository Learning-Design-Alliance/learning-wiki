## Evidence

### Mizori R 2025

Mizori R, Sadiq M, Ahmad MT, Siu A, Ahmad RR, Yang Z, et al. (2025). STEM exam performance: Open‐ versus closed‐book methods in the large language model era. The Clinical Teacher. https://doi.org/10.1111/tct.13839

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r1`

Sub-group meta-analysis by study design (Figure 2b) across the eight included studies. Observational designs showed "a strong effect favouring OBEs" (MD 6.89, I² = 98%); quasi-experimental designs a more modest but significant benefit (MD 3.79, I² = 0%).

> "the mean difference for observational studies was 6.89 (95% CI: 2.45 to 11.32; I 2 = 98%), indicating a strong effect favouring OBEs. Quasi‐experimental studies presented a mean difference of 3.79 (95% CI: 1.96 to 5.62; I 2 = 0%)"

### Mizori R 2025 (2)

Mizori R, Sadiq M, Ahmad MT, Siu A, Ahmad RR, Yang Z, et al. (2025). STEM exam performance: Open‐ versus closed‐book methods in the large language model era. The Clinical Teacher. https://doi.org/10.1111/tct.13839

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r1`

Test of the sub-group interaction in the same meta-analysis. The review reports no statistically significant difference between the two design sub-groups (Chi2 p = 0.21); equivalence was not tested.

> "a Chi‐square test for sub‐group differences showed no statistically significant variance in effect sizes between observational and quasi‐experimental studies (Chi2 p = 0.21)."
