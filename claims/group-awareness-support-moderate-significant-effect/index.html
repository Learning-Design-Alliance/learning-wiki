Evidence¶

Michael J. Baker 2024¶

Michael J. Baker, Peter Reimann. (2024). CSCL has come of age. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn (2024) 19:1–10. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-024-09419-2

q1 · i? · theoretical · r2

The editorial summarizes the meta-analysis by Chen et al. in this issue. It reports the corpus (45 articles, 58 studies, 96 effect sizes) and the overall moderate significant effect of GA support on learning performance; no effect size value is printed in this editorial.

"The study included 45 articles reporting on 58 independent studies with 96 effect sizes. The results showed a moderate significant effect of GA support on students' learning performance, with a greater influence on cognitive development."

Michael J. Baker 2024 (2)¶

Michael J. Baker, Peter Reimann. (2024). CSCL has come of age. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn (2024) 19:1–10. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-024-09419-2

q1 · i? · theoretical · r2

The editorial reports, from the meta-analysis summary, the significant moderators: "GA support type" and group size, with behavioural support favouring behavioural participation, cognitive support favouring cognitive development, and larger groups benefiting more.

"Significant moderating factors identified were GA support type (behavioural, cognitive, social awareness) and group size. Behavioural GA support had the largest effect on behavioural participation, while cognitive GA support showed a higher impact on cognitive development. Larger groups benefited more from GA support compared to smaller groups."

Michael J. Baker 2024 (3)¶

Michael J. Baker, Peter Reimann. (2024). CSCL has come of age. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn (2024) 19:1–10. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-024-09419-2

q1 · i? · theoretical · r3

The editorial reports that the meta-analysis found no significant differences in effect sizes across disciplinary domains, intervention durations, and educational levels; a reported null result from the summary, not an equivalence test description.

"The study also found no significant differences in effect sizes across disciplinary domains, intervention durations, and educational levels."
