# During member checks, multilingual 8th-grade students contested both LLM classifications of their talk and the coding scheme itself, especially the Off-task and Claim codes

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Student member checks revealed misalignments between how students understood their own talk and how the LLM and coding scheme classified it, with students contesting Off-task and Claim labels by foregrounding their intentions. [→ Liliana Santos-Deonizio 2027](#liliana-santos-deonizio-2027)

## Evidence

### Liliana Santos-Deonizio 2027

Liliana Santos-Deonizio, James Malamut, Ramón Antonio Martínez, and Dorottya Demszky. (2027). When Youth Enter The Chat: An Epistemic Shift in the Validation of LLM-Based Measures of Student Talk. In Proceedings of TBD (CSCW '27). ACM, New York, NY, USA. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23780

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Case-study member checks with four focal students in one 8th-grade math classroom, tabulating disagreements with LLM classifications (Table 4). Students were "especially vocal about the Claim and Off-task codes" and pushed back by foregrounding intentions behind their utterances.

> "While students generally agreed with the LLM classifications, the moments of contestation—and the reasons they gave for them—highlighted misalignments with the LLMs’ classification of a given utterance, and with the coding scheme itself."
