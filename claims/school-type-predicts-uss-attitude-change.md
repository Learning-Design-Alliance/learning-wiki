# School type significantly predicted change in environmental-protection attitudes, with academic-track students changing more than combined-school students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` School type was a significant univariate predictor only for USS attitude change; academic-track students had significantly higher change than the combined group (p = 0.016). [→ Mulders 2025](#mulders-2025)

## Evidence

### Mulders 2025

Mulders, M., Träg, K. H., & Kirner, L. (2025). Go green: evaluating an XR application on biodiversity in German secondary school classrooms. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09697-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Univariate follow-up to the MANOVA on the 10-item Environmental Protection Scale (USS); the only significant school-type effect was on USS attitude change, with academic-track students increasing more than combined-school students. No effect size is printed for this contrast.

> "Univariate comparisons revealed that school type was a significant predictor only for differences in attitude measured by the USS (F(2, 157) = 5.29; p = 0.006). Post-hoc Tukey-tests revealed that the academic track students specifically had a significantly higher change USS attitude (p = 0.016) than students in the combined group."
