# Unedited voice prompts have lower odds of first-attempt success than text prompts on two of three Prompt Problems

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who initially used unedited voice prompting had lower odds of immediate success than text prompters on P1 (OR = 0.50) and P2 (OR = 0.43). [→ rq1-logistic](#rq1-logistic)

## Evidence

### rq1-logistic

Kaitlin Riegel, Yan Cathy Hua, Paul Denny, Victor-Alexandru Pădurean, Juho Leinonen, James Prather, and Adish Singla. 2026. Say What? Examining Text and Voice Input Modalities for Prompt-Based Programming in Computing Education. Proceedings of the 31st ACM Conference on Innovation and Technology in Computer Science Education V. 1 (ITiCSE 2026). https://doi.org/10.1145/3803400.3809397

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Binary logistic regressions across problems P1 and P2 in an introductory C programming course lab examined initial prompt method's effect on immediate success. Unedited voice prompting showed "lower odds of immediate success" versus text, with OR𝑃1 = 0.50 (p= 0.02) and OR𝑃2 = 0.43 (p= 0.01).

> "Compared totextprompting, students who initially used uneditedvoice prompting had lower odds of immediate success (OR𝑃1 = 0.50, 95% CI [0.27, 0.89],p= 0.02; OR 𝑃 2= 0.43, 95% CI [0.23, 0.83],p= 0.01)"
