# First-language heterogeneity in CSCL groups reduces students' precourse–postcourse gains in online interaction self-efficacy, independent of students' own language status

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Students in first-language heterogeneous CSCL groups exhibited smaller pre-to-post gains in online interaction self-efficacy than students in homogeneous groups, with a significant heterogeneity × time interaction (η2 = .004) alongside a large time main effect (η2 = .11). [→ Natalia Reich-Stiebert 2023](#natalia-reich-stiebert-2023)

## Evidence

### Natalia Reich-Stiebert 2023

Natalia Reich-Stiebert, Jan-Bennet Voltmer, Jennifer Raimann, Stefan Stürmer. (2023). The role of first-language heterogeneity in the acquisition of online interaction self-efficacy in CSCL. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn 18:513–530. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-023-09411-2

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r3`

Repeated-measures ANOVA in a nine-week CSCL course with 1525 students in 343 groups, comparing heterogeneous (n = 967) versus homogeneous groups on self-efficacy gains; heterogeneous groups rose from Mpre = 3.74 to only Mpost = 3.91 while homogeneous groups rose to Mpost = 4.03, with a "significant interaction effect between group heterogeneity and time point of measurement".

> "The analysis indicated a significant main effect of the time point of measurement, F(1, 1521) = 195.06, p < .001, η2 = .11, a significant main effect of the group heterogeneity factor, F(1, 1521) = 6.93, p = .009, η2 = .005, and a significant interaction effect between group heterogeneity and time point of measurement, F(1, 1521) = 6.11, p = .01, η2 = .004."
