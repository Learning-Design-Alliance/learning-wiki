## Evidence

### Shabajee 2004

Shabajee, P. and Postlethwaite, K. (2004). What happened to modern physics? School Science Review, 81(297), p51-56. Originally published 2000. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0401016](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0401016)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The authors' documentary analysis of the UK National Curriculum for Science 5-16, presented in the paper's problem statement. It reports the curriculum 'contains no reference to the physical theories' of relativity, quantum mechanics, statistical processes and chaos theory; no new empirical data are reported.

> "the current UK National Curriculum in Science 5-16 contains no reference to the physical theories which do offer at least some appropriate descriptions: relativity, quantum mechanics, statistical processes and chaos theory."

### Shabajee 2004 (2)

Shabajee, P. and Postlethwaite, K. (2004). What happened to modern physics? School Science Review, 81(297), p51-56. Originally published 2000. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0401016](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0401016)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The authors' analysis of post-16 provision, citing QCA (1999) subject criteria and AQA A2 options such as Turning Points in Physics. They report that 'even at A-level, 20th Century physics gets only limited attention' and chaos theory is still largely overlooked.

> "even at A-level, 20th Century physics gets only limited attention and, for example, chaos theory is still largely overlooked."
