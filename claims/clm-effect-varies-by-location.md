# The effect of constructivist learning models on student outcomes varies significantly by location, with Turkey (ES=.87) and Indonesia (ES=1.07) showing high-level effects and China (ES=.58) and mixed-country samples (ES=.67) medium-level effects

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Location moderates the effect of constructivist learning models (Qb(3)=15.55, p<.01), with meta-analyses of Turkey and Indonesia samples producing high-level effects and China and mixed-country samples medium-level effects. [→ Demir 2022](#demir-2022)
`q3 i?` The authors suggest the location differences may arise because constructivist models are more flexible and student-centered than the traditional methods dominant in some school systems, and they caution that location bias is a problem in meta-analysis research. [→ Demir 2022 (2)](#demir-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Demir 2022

Demir, M., & Kaya, M. (2022). Analysis of constructivist learning model's effects on student outcomes: A second order meta-analysis. Journal of Theoretical Educational Science, 15(4), 938-957. http://doi.org/10.30831/akukeg.1122136

`q3 · i?`

Q-between moderator analysis by location of the meta-analyses (mixed k=5, Turkey k=10, China k=3, Indonesia k=3) under a random effect model. The article reports "Qb(3)=15.55 p<.01" with per-location effect sizes ES=.67, ES=.58, ES=.87 and ES=1.07.

> "Similarly, CLM's impact on student outcomes according to the location included in the meta -analysis research statistically varies (Qb(3)=15.55 p<.01). A mixed-type meta-analysis, including research from different countries, had a lower or medium-level effect (ES=.67). Similarly, meta-analysis research, including China sampling , produced medium -level impact size (ES=.58). On the other hand, meta-analysis research, including Turkey and Indonesia sampling, produced high-level impact (ES=.87; ES=1.07, respectively)."

### Demir 2022 (2)

Demir, M., & Kaya, M. (2022). Analysis of constructivist learning model's effects on student outcomes: A second order meta-analysis. Journal of Theoretical Educational Science, 15(4), 938-957. http://doi.org/10.30831/akukeg.1122136

`q1 · i?`

Authors' interpretation (type e) offered in the Discussion to explain the location moderator, citing prior work that Turkey's education system is dominated by traditional methods; it is a hypothesized explanation, not a tested result.

> "School systems that produce high impact size might be the countries in which t raditional education methods are dominant. Turkey's education system is dominated by traditional  methods (Kayabaşı, 2012; Terzi, 2011). Constructivist learning models are more flexible and put students at the center when compared to traditional learning models. This flexibility might be the reason some countries produced higher impact sizes."
