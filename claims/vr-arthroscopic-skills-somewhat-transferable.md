## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The article reasons that experienced surgeons' markedly better VR performance indicates arthroscopic skills such as hand-eye coordination and triangulation are somewhat transferable between the operating theater and the VR unit. [→ Andersen C 2011](#andersen-c-2011)

## Evidence

### Andersen C 2011

Andersen C, Winding TN, Vesterby MS. (2011). Development of simulated arthroscopic skills: A randomized trial of virtual-reality training of 21 orthopedic surgeons. Acta Orthopaedica 82(1). https://doi.org/10.3109/17453674.2011.552776

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

This is the authors' interpretation, a theoretical argument rather than a tested result in this trial: they cite prior studies (e.g. Pedowitz et al. 2002, Srivastava et al. 2004) showing experienced surgeons perform markedly better on VR units, and argue this "indicates that the skills are somewhat transferable".

> "On the other hand, the mere fact that experienced surgeons generally performed markedly better than inexperienced doctors on a VR unit indicates that the skills are somewhat transferable;"
