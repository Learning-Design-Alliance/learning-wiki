## Evidence

### Klerman Elizabeth B. 2016

Klerman Elizabeth B., Beckett Scott A., Landrigan Christopher P. (2016). Applying mathematical models to predict resident physician performance and alertness on traditional and novel work schedules. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0751-9

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Simulation results across all five group schedules show night work is worse than day work because circadian phase and extended wake both lower predicted values; the article notes "less attending and senior resident oversight" at night.

> "for all schedules, the performance and alertness is worse during the night - when both the circadian system and length of time awake factors both predict lower performance and alertness and when there is less attending and senior resident oversight - than during the day"

### Klerman Elizabeth B. 2016 (2)

Klerman Elizabeth B., Beckett Scott A., Landrigan Christopher P. (2016). Applying mathematical models to predict resident physician performance and alertness on traditional and novel work schedules. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0751-9

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

> "During the day work hours, there is almost no difference among group schedules."
