# During peer-feedback provision, students deal with errors in one of two patterns: quick discovery (identification simultaneous with decoding, often without evaluation) or elaborate discovery (identification resulting from an interpreting/evaluating phase)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Error identification during feedback provision occurred either simultaneously with decoding and often lacked evaluation-related thoughts, or resulted from an interpreting/evaluating phase. [→ Jochem E. J. Aben 2024](#jochem-e-j-aben-2024)
`q2 i?` Quick discoveries often concerned lower-order concerns, while elaborate discoveries often concerned higher-order concerns and followed a thorough, iterative interpretation process. [→ Jochem E. J. Aben 2024](#jochem-e-j-aben-2024)

## Evidence

### Jochem E. J. Aben 2024

Jochem E. J. Aben, Mayra Mascareño Lara, Anneke C. Timmermans, Filitsa Dingyloudi, Jan-Willem Strijbos. (2024). The impact of interpersonal perceptions on the process of dealing with errors while providing and processing peer-feedback on writing. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09660-0

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative thematic analysis of think-aloud recordings from six dyads of Dutch 11th-grade students providing peer-feedback on argumentative texts. The analysis identified "'quick discovery of errors' and 'elaborate discovery of errors'" as the two consistent patterns; quick discovery often occurred for lower-order concerns, elaborate discovery for higher-order concerns.

> "During the provision of feedback, students dealt with errors consistently in line with one of two patterns that could be described as 'quick discovery of errors' and 'elaborate discovery of errors'."
