Evidence¶

Roderick 2000¶

Roderick, M., Nagaoka, J., Bacon, J., & Easton, J. Q. (2000). Update: Ending Social Promotion: Passing, Retention, and Achievement Trends Among Promoted and Retained Students 1995-1999. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://www.consortium-chicago.org

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Cohort-comparison trend analysis of CPS students facing the promotional test cutoffs in 1997, 1998, and 1999, compared to a 1995 pre-policy reference group. The brief reports "an 8 percentage point increase for third graders from 1997 and a 6 percentage point increase for sixth graders."

"By August 1999, 76 percent of third grad- ers, 85 percent of sixth graders and 86 percent of eighth graders met the cutoffs in both reading and math for promotion."

Related Claims¶

  • Summer Bridge produced short-term test-score gains and allowed more students to meet promotion cutoffs, particularly among sixth and eighth graders — reports the opposite
  • Summer Bridge's second chance substantially raised promotion rates: by August 1997, 68% of included third graders, 79% of sixth graders, and 83% of eighth graders had met the cutoff — related
  • Fewer than 60 percent of retained third and sixth graders in 1998 and 1999 raised their test scores to the promotional cutoff even with an extra January chance to pass — related
  • Students who failed both reading and math made up the majority of retained students, and fewer than 20% of them passed both subjects in Summer Bridge — related
  • Despite higher passing rates, retention rates did not fall because fewer students who failed the cutoff were promoted with waivers — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Passing rates improved from 1997 to 1998 in third and sixth grades, but retention rates did not decline because fewer students received waivers — related
  • Sixth graders promoted in 1997 without meeting the test cutoff fared poorly at the eighth-grade gate, while Summer Bridge passers did relatively well — related
