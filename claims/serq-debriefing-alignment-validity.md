## Evidence

### Grewal 2026

Grewal, K., Azher, S., Moreno, M., Pekrun, R., Wiseman, J., Flake, J., Lajoie, S., Sun, N.‐Z., Fried, G. M., Khalil, E., & Harley, J. M. (2026). Medical trainees' emotions and their effects on perceptions of performance and team mood in team‐based simulations. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 96, 306–336. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.70017

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Chi-square tests comparing SERQ responses with coded debriefing responses from 13 debriefings (40 observations, 32 participants) found "the SERQ and debriefing responses being significantly aligned" for team performance with effect size Cramer's V = .45 and for team mood with effect size Cramer's V = .88.

> "Chi‐square tests (Table 8) found statistically significant associations between medical trainees' responses in the SERQ and their debriefing responses for participants' perceptions of team performance (χ2(1) = 6.82, p = .009, V = .45, power = .97) and team mood (χ2(1) = 12.25, p < .001, V = .88, power > .99), specifically with the SERQ and debriefing responses being significantly aligned."

### Grewal 2026 (2)

Grewal, K., Azher, S., Moreno, M., Pekrun, R., Wiseman, J., Flake, J., Lajoie, S., Sun, N.‐Z., Fried, G. M., Khalil, E., & Harley, J. M. (2026). Medical trainees' emotions and their effects on perceptions of performance and team mood in team‐based simulations. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 96, 306–336. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.70017

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Qualitative content analysis of debriefing transcripts found discrete emotion alignment was weak: "4 were in alignment with their SERQ responses while 8 were not in alignment"; the authors suggest the SERQ may capture general emotional states rather than specific emotions.

> "From the observations of discrete affective states during the debriefing, 4 were in alignment with their SERQ responses while 8 were not in alignment (Table 6)."
