# Ethnic achievement gaps in Sao Paulo are statistically significant between White and Black, and White and Pardo students across income levels, with gaps of about 2-3.4% at low income

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In Sao Paulo, White students scored significantly higher than Black and Pardo students at almost all household income levels in both mathematics and writing, with low-income gaps of about 2% in mathematics and 3.4% in writing. [→ Luis E C Rocha 2018](#luis-e-c-rocha-2018)

## Evidence

### Luis E C Rocha 2018

Luis E C Rocha, Luana de F Nascimento. (2018). Assessing student's achievement gap between ethnic groups in Brazil. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1801.10544](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1801.10544)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of ENEM 2014 scores for senior high-school students meeting eleven inclusion criteria, stratified by state and six household income levels, tested with Welch's ANOVA and pairwise Welch's t-tests at α = 0.99. Low-income gaps were "∆ = 3 .4%" for writing versus about 2% for mathematics.

> "The p-values indicate statistically significant gaps (∆) in the mean scores of White (higher mean score) and Black (lower), and White (higher) and Pardo (lower) students for almost all income levels in both disciplines in the state of Sao Paulo. For low income, this gap is slightly higher for writing (∆ = 3 .4%) in comparison to mathematics (∆∼ 2%)."
