Evidence¶

Greene Nolan 2023¶

Greene Nolan, H. L., & Vang, M. C. (2023). Automated essay scoring in middle school writing: Understanding key predictors of students' growth and comparing artificial intelligence- and teacher-generated scores and feedback. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/187

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Domain-level score distributions (Figures 2–5) from the 160-essay convening scoring show tail agreement and middle-score divergence in Support & Evidence and Claim & Focus (not in Organization or Language & Style); teachers described erring toward indicating a student is still on their way.

"Just like in the domain of Support & Evidence, teachers were more likely to give a 2 as a score, whereas the AES appeared more likely to give a 3 as a score"

Greene Nolan 2023 (2)¶

Greene Nolan, H. L., & Vang, M. C. (2023). Automated essay scoring in middle school writing: Understanding key predictors of students' growth and comparing artificial intelligence- and teacher-generated scores and feedback. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/187

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Teachers' interpretation of the Organization distribution (Figure 4): AES reads and provides feedback line by line, so sentence-level edits can produce incoherent essays that teachers penalize but the tool rewards.

"Teachers' tendency to consider essays as 1's or 2's in Organization is likely due to the ability of a human scorer to see and grasp essays on a much more holistic level than AES systems can currently do."
