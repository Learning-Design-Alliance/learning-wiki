# Progress is greatest when vocabulary questions match the student's proficiency level while grammar and reading comprehension questions are set one level lower

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The proposed group scored significantly better on the posttest than the motivated group (questions one level lower), while not significantly better than the challenged group (questions one level higher). [→ Yi-Ting Huang 2018](#yi-ting-huang-2018)

## Evidence

### Yi-Ting Huang 2018

Yi-Ting Huang, Meng Chang Chen, and Yeali S. Sun. (2018). Development and Evaluation of a Personalized Computer-aided Question Generation for English Learners to Improve Proficiency and Correct Mistakes. IEEE Transactions on Emerging Topics in Computing. Originally published 2014. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1808.09732](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1808.09732)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Follow-up comparison experiment (August-September 2012) with second-year Taiwanese high school students in three groups: proposed (N=28), motivated (questions one level easier, N=40), and challenged (questions one level harder, N=30). The proposed strategy significantly outperformed the motivated condition on the posttest; the challenged condition showed no significant difference.

> "An independent t-test of overall scores indicated a significant difference (p=0.35 in the pretest and p=0.04 in the post-test) between the proposed group and the motivated group, while there was no significant effect (p=0.38 in the pretest and p=0.56 in the post-test) between the proposed group and the challenged group."

### Yi-Ting Huang 2018 (2)

Yi-Ting Huang, Meng Chang Chen, and Yeali S. Sun. (2018). Development and Evaluation of a Personalized Computer-aided Question Generation for English Learners to Improve Proficiency and Correct Mistakes. IEEE Transactions on Emerging Topics in Computing. Originally published 2014. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1808.09732](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1808.09732)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Per-question-type posttest comparisons from the same three-group experiment (Table 5 reports descriptive statistics). The authors conclude that grammar and reading comprehension questions one level easier than the student's proficiency level motivated students and facilitated significant progress, consistent with Klinkenberg et al.

> "In addition, the scores for the grammar questions (p=0.07) and the reading comprehension questions (p=0.01) on the post-test revealed especially significant results between the proposed group and the motivated group."
