## Evidence

### Pratim Sengupta 2012

Pratim Sengupta, Amy Voss Farris, Mason Wright. (2012). From Agents to Continuous Change via Aesthetics: Learning Mechanics with Visual Agent-based Computational Modeling. Technology, Knowledge & Learning (accepted for publication). [doi:10.1007/s10758-012-9190-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10758-012-9190-9)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Qualitative observations from two studies: Study 1 with fourteen 3rd-4th grade children in a six-session weekend course, and Study 2 with seven ninth graders over eight one-hour sessions, none with prior programming experience. The authors report the shapes supported "agent-based thinking, and debugging."

> "Results from both the studies show that these constant speed shapes play an important role in the development of students' understanding of two key aspects of computational thinking: learning to think like a turtle (or agent-based thinking), and debugging."
