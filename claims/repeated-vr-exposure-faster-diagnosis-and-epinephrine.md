## Evidence

### Giese Julian 2026

Giese Julian, Ehlers Jan P., Weichert Olaf, Nitsche Julia. (2026). Effect of repeated exposure to an identical multi-user virtual reality anaphylaxis scenario on paramedic trainee performance: a randomized controlled trial. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-69133-x

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Between-group comparison of time to correct working diagnosis in the common VR2 assessment scenario, analyzed with Welch's t-test; the article reports "1.45 vs. 2.90 min; mean difference, −1.45 min; 95% CI, −1.86 to −1.03; t(85.53) = −6.9, p < 0.001, d = 1.15".

> "The mean time to correct working diagnosis was 1.45 min shorter in VR-I than in VR-C (1.45 vs. 2.90 min; mean difference, −1.45 min; 95% CI, −1.86 to −1.03; t(85.53) = −6.9, p < 0.001, d = 1.15; Welch's t-test)."

### Giese Julian 2026 (2)

Giese Julian, Ehlers Jan P., Weichert Olaf, Nitsche Julia. (2026). Effect of repeated exposure to an identical multi-user virtual reality anaphylaxis scenario on paramedic trainee performance: a randomized controlled trial. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-69133-x

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Welch's t-test comparison of time to first IM epinephrine in VR2; the article reports "4.58 vs. 7.43 min; mean difference, −2.85 min; 95% CI, −3.41 to −2.24; t(115) = −9.58, p < 0.001, d = 1.59".

> "The mean time to first IM epinephrine administration was 2.85 min shorter in VR-I than in VR-C (4.58 vs. 7.43 min; mean difference, −2.85 min; 95% CI, −3.41 to −2.24; t(115) = −9.58, p < 0.001, d = 1.59; Welch's t-test)."
