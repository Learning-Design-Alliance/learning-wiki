# First-year teachers with mentors declined in self and task concerns and increased impact concerns over the school year

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among 14 first-year teachers with mentors, self concerns declined from 50% to 36%, task concerns from 43% to 29%, and impact concerns rose from 7% to 29% across three interviews, consistent with Fuller's theory. [→ Olsen 1990](#olsen-1990)

## Evidence

### Olsen 1990

Olsen, Dwayne G.; Heyse, Kathy L. (1990). Development and Concerns of First-year and Reentry Teachers with and without Mentors. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED323188

`q2 · i?`

Longitudinal panel study of 14 first-year teachers with mentors interviewed in October 1986, February and May 1987; concerns categorized from semistructured interviews using frequencies. The panel data show the decline in self and task concerns and rise in impact concerns, which the authors call "consistent with the Fuller Theory."

> "first-year teachers with mentors declined in both "self" (from 50% in interview one to 36% in interview three) and "task" concerns (from 43% In interview one to 29% in interview three) and developed more concern for impact during the course of the year (from 7% in interview one to 29% in interview three)"
