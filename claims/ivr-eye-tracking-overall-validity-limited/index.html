Evidence¶

Lee 2026¶

Lee, J. Y., Moon, J., Paas, F., Cho, M., Darejeh, A., Holmer, S., & Ayres, P. (2026). Validity of Eye-Tracking Measures in Immersive Virtual Reality: Using Cognitive Theories of Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10186-9

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Systematic review of 38 empirical IVR eye-tracking studies (2014–2024) scoring validity of measures on a 0–4 scheme. The review reports "The average score 1.93 out of 4 (SD = 0.77)" with only 2 measures (2%) reaching the maximum score of 4.

"The average score 1.93 out of 4 (SD = 0.77), ranging from 0.5 to 4. In terms of distribution, 12 measures (13%) received as a score of 0 or 0.5; 15 (17%) scored 1 or 1.5; 36 (39%) scored 2 or 2.5; 26 (29%) scored 3 or 3.5 point; and 2 (2%) achieved the maximum score of 4."

Lee 2026 (2)¶

Lee, J. Y., Moon, J., Paas, F., Cho, M., Darejeh, A., Holmer, S., & Ayres, P. (2026). Validity of Eye-Tracking Measures in Immersive Virtual Reality: Using Cognitive Theories of Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10186-9

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Component-level analysis in the review's Results (Table 4) shows data quality averaged 0.32, the lowest of the four components. The review states "Scores for relations and data quality were below 0.5 on average," attributing low data quality to underreported methodological details.

"Scores for relations and data quality were below 0.5 on average, while content and consequences scored above 0.5, indicating variation in the strength of evidence across validity components."
