# Concept-test scores rose significantly from pre-test to post-test on time dilation and the relativity of simultaneity after the RTR lab, in one single-group pre-post study with no comparison group

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Students' scores on concept tests administered before and after the RTR lab rose significantly on questions about time dilation and the relativity of simultaneity, in one group with no comparison group. [→ C. Savage 2009](#c-savage-2009)

`q2 i?` Pre- to post-laboratory concept test performance improved across a broad range of special relativity topics, with significant gains on time dilation and simultaneity questions (p < 0.05). [→ Dominic McGrath 2009](#dominic-mcgrath-2009)

## Evidence

### C. Savage 2009

C. Savage, D. McGrath, T. McIntyre, M. Wegener, M. Williamson. (2009). Teaching Physics Using Virtual Reality. https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.5776 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0910.5776](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0910.5776)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre/post concept-test design around the RTR lab, part of the authors' extensive evaluations including testing of hundreds of students. The article reports "statistically significant improvements" on the two named concept areas but prints no test statistic or effect size.

> "Concept tests were given to students before and after the RTR lab. The responses showed statistically significant improvements in questions relating to time dilation and to the relativity of simultaneity, which are conceptually challenging aspects of relativity."

### Dominic McGrath 2009

Dominic McGrath, Margaret Wegener, Timothy J. McIntyre, Craig Savage, Michael Williamson. (2009). Student experiences of virtual reality - a case study in learning special relativity. arXiv:0911.0226. https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.0226 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0910.5776](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0910.5776)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Pre/post concept-based multi-choice tests with randomly assigned question sets given to RTR laboratory students (UQ Semester I, 2009, n=28, Figure 3). The article reports "signiﬁcant increased understand- ing (p <0.05)" on time dilation and simultaneity questions; no effect size is printed.

> "Performance on these concept questions generally improved acro ss a broad range of topics, showing an overall trend towards a deeper understanding of spec ial relativity. In particular, questions concerning time dilation and simultaneity showed signiﬁcant increased understand- ing (p <0.05)."
