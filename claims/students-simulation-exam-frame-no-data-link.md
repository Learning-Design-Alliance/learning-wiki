## Evidence

### M. Stella 2021

M. Stella, A. Kapuza, C. Cramer and S. Uzzo. (2021). Mapping computational thinking mindsets between educational levels with cognitive network science. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1093/comnet/cnab020](https://doi.org/10.1093/comnet/cnab020)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Results-section comparison of students' and researchers' mindsets around "simulation": students' associations pointed only to "exams, grades and related anxiety-eliciting concepts", revealing a deep lack of awareness about computer simulations prominently featured in experts' mindsets.

> "For students, "simulation" reminds them only of exams, grades and related anxiety-eliciting concepts (e.g. a simulation of a quiz)."

### M. Stella 2021 (2)

M. Stella, A. Kapuza, C. Cramer and S. Uzzo. (2021). Mapping computational thinking mindsets between educational levels with cognitive network science. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1093/comnet/cnab020](https://doi.org/10.1093/comnet/cnab020)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Network comparison in the Results section: in students' reconstructed mindset "simulation" was not associated with "data", while researchers' networked mindset showed a "positive and strong link" between the two concepts.

> "Notice how in students' reconstructed mindset, "simulation" was not associated with "data", whereas this was a positive and strong link in researchers' networked mindset."
