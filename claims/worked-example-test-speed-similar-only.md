# Worked examples speed subsequent solution of structurally similar test problems but not dissimilar ones

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Worked example groups solved similar test problems significantly faster than conventional groups in Experiment 3 and in the similar-dissimilar order of Experiment 4. [→ Sweller and & Cooper 1985](#sweller-and-cooper-1985)
`q3 i?` No significant difference between groups appeared on dissimilar test problems, so the speed benefit was specific to problems identical in structure to the initial ones. [→ Sweller and & Cooper 1985 (2)](#sweller-and-cooper-1985-2)

## Evidence

### Sweller and & Cooper 1985

John Sweller and Graham A. Cooper. (1985). The Use of Worked Examples as a Substitute for Problem Solving in Learning Algebra. Cognition and Instruction, 2(1), 59-89. https://www.jstor.org/stable/3233555

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Experiment 3 test phase with 22 Year 9 students: despite far less acquisition time, the worked example group solved test problems faster, "U (11,11) = 27.5". Test problems were identical in structure to acquisition problems.

> "The difference between groups in total time to solve the test problems is significant, U (11,11) = 27.5."

### Sweller and & Cooper 1985 (2)

John Sweller and Graham A. Cooper. (1985). The Use of Worked Examples as a Substitute for Problem Solving in Learning Algebra. Cognition and Instruction, 2(1), 59-89. https://www.jstor.org/stable/3233555

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Experiment 4 (40 Year 8 students, four groups) replicated the similar-problem speed advantage, "U (10,10) = 27", but found no group difference on dissimilar transfer problems, "U (10,10) = 41"; equivalence was not tested.

> "The worked example group required significantly less time on the similar test problems than did the conventional problem group, U (10,10) = 27. No difference between these two groups was obtained on the dissimilar test problems which followed, U (10,10) = 41."
