## Evidence

### McManus I C 2013

McManus I C, Elder Andrew T, Dacre Jane. (2013). Investigating possible ethnicity and sex bias in clinical examiners: an analysis of data from the MRCP(UK) PACES and nPACES examinations. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-103

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Analysis of 1790 PACES and 1498 nPACES examiners comparing each against co-examiner baskets. The article reports a slight uncorrected excess (PACES 8.5%; nPACES 7.9%) but "none of the individual examiners reach significance on the Bonferroni corrected criterion".

> "For sex bias none of the individual examiners reach significance on the Bonferroni corrected criterion."

### McManus I C 2013 (2)

McManus I C, Elder Andrew T, Dacre Jane. (2013). Investigating possible ethnicity and sex bias in clinical examiners: an analysis of data from the MRCP(UK) PACES and nPACES examinations. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-103

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Ethnic bias analysis across both examination formats identified examiner v as significant at Bonferroni-corrected p < .05 in both PACES and nPACES, favouring non-White candidates; two other PACES examiners did not replicate in nPACES.

> "Only one examiner (v) reached the Bonferroni-corrected p < .05 significance level in both PACES and nPACES. This examiner was non-White and appeared to be systematically awarding relatively higher marks to non-White candidates."
