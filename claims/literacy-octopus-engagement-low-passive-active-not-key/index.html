Evidence¶

Lord et al. 2017¶

Pippa Lord, Adam Rabiasz, Palak Roy, Jennie Harland, Ben Styles, Katherine Fowler. (2017). Evidence-based literacy support: the 'Literacy Octopus' trial — Evaluation report and executive summary. Education Endowment Foundation. http://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN84508118

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Monitoring-data analysis of engagement across the 540 intervention schools, categorised against providers' expected levels of engagement. Six out of ten schools did not engage to the level expected, although a small proportion engaged more than expected, for example by hosting CPD sessions.

"Monitoring data showed that schools' levels of engagement with the interventions were relatively low: just over four in ten of the 540 interventio n schools did not engage , and only four in ten engaged as much as, or more than, was expected."

Lord et al. 2017 (2)¶

Pippa Lord, Adam Rabiasz, Palak Roy, Jennie Harland, Ben Styles, Katherine Fowler. (2017). Evidence-based literacy support: the 'Literacy Octopus' trial — Evaluation report and executive summary. Education Endowment Foundation. http://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN84508118

q2 · i? · qualitative · r?

Process-evaluation finding from monitoring data, observations, interviews and case studies on why schools engaged. Stated reasons for not engaging included lack of time, timing and location of events, and a preference for face-to-face support rather than online or remote formats only.

"Teachers engaged where they felt the materials or support were communicated in an accessible way (such as posters that were easy to display, or easy event location) and that the content of materials was relevant (for example, linked to an existing need)."
