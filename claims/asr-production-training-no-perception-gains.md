# No group improved in perception of French /y/, so production-focused ASR training did not transfer to perception

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Despite slightly greater gains for the ASR group, none of the three groups improved significantly in /y/ perception (ASR: p > 0.05; Non-ASR: p > 0.38; Control: p > 0.37). [→ Liakin et al. 2015](#liakin-et-al-2015)

## Evidence

### Liakin et al. 2015

Denis Liakin, Walcir Cardoso and Natallia Liakina. (2015). Learning L2 pronunciation with a mobile speech recognizer: French /y/. CALICO Journal 32.1. https://doi.org/10.1558/cj.v32i1.25962

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Dependent samples t-tests on the four-item multiple-choice pseudoword perception task (45 pseudowords containing /y/, /u/, /i/) showed no significant pre/post gains in any group; only p-values are printed, so no effect size is reported.

> "the three groups behaved in a similar way (pre/posttest differences: ASR: p > 0.05; Non-ASR: p > 0.38; Control: p > 0.37). This indicates that the group that received ASR-based treatment was not able to extend the newly acquired knowledge detected in production to perception"
