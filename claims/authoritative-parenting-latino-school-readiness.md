# Authoritative parenting is associated with better social-emotional and academic school readiness in Mexican and Dominican American students

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` More authoritative parenting was associated with better adaptive behaviors and higher academic readiness among MA students, and with better adaptive behaviors among DA students. [→ Kim Yeonwoo 2018](#kim-yeonwoo-2018)

## Evidence

### Kim Yeonwoo 2018

Kim Yeonwoo, Calzada Esther J., Barajas-Gonzalez R. Gabriela, Huang Keng-Yen, Brotman Laurie M., Castro Ashley, Pichardo Catherine. (2018). The Role of Authoritative and Authoritarian Parenting in the Early Academic Achievement of Latino Students. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000192

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multi-group SEM on longitudinal data from 750 Mexican and Dominican American mother-child pairs, baseline predictors and end-of-first-grade outcomes. The MA model showed "more authoritative parenting was associated with better adaptive behaviors" and higher academic readiness; the DA model showed authoritative parenting associated with better adaptive behaviors (standardized coefficient = .14).

> "more authoritative parenting was associated with better adaptive behaviors (standardized coefficient = .13) and higher academic readiness (standardized coefficient = .14) in students."
