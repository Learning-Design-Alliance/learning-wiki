## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Combining the four primarily conceptual lab-related items on the physics 2 exam showed no significant difference between groups (t(311.22)=-0.09, p=.928); with Bonferroni correction (α=.003) only one item (item 16) was significant (z=3.51, p=.0005), which the authors deem not practically significant. ## Evidence

### Carl Wieman and N.G. Holmes 2015

Carl Wieman and N.G. Holmes. (2015). Measuring the impact of an instructional laboratory on the learning of introductory physics. The American Journal of Physics. [doi:10.1119/1.4931717](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4931717)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Question-by-question analysis of the 20 multiple-choice physics 2 final exam items, coded as primarily conceptual or calculational and lab-related or not (Table III). The combined conceptual-item ratio comparison reports t(311.22)=-0.09, p=.928; item 16 alone shows z=3.51, p=.0005. No effect size printed.

> "If we combine the four primarily conceptual items and set up a ratio similar to that used in the previous analysis (ratio of conceptual lab-items/other items), again, there is no significant difference between the two groups: t(311.22)=-0.09, p=.928."
