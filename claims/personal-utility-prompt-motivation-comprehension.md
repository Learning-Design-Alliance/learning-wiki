## Evidence

### Nückles 2020

Nückles, M., Roelle, J., Glogger-Frey, I., Waldeyer, J., & Renkl, A. (2020). The Self-Regulation-View in Writing-to-Learn: Using Journal Writing to Optimize Cognitive Load in Self-Regulated Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09541-1

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review synthesizing 16 experimental and 4 correlational studies of journal writing. It reports Schmidt et al. (2012), an experiment with 40 high school students (ages 13–14, immunology content; Table 2), in which a personal utility prompt added to cognitive and metacognitive prompts increased acceptance of journal writing, content-related motivation and comprehension. It also reports that Wäschle et al. (2015, Exp. 2) replicated the motivation effect with tenth graders but found no comprehension advantage (the prompt improved a critical-reflection argumentation task instead). The review prints no effect sizes for these studies.

> "Writing about the personal utility of learning contents in a learning journal improves learning motivation and comprehension"
