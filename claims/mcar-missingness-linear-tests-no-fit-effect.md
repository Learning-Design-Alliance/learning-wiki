# MCAR item and response missingness in linear tests shows no differential effect on model fit across Designs 1 to 4

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Item and response missing data characterized as MCAR for linear tests have no differential effects on model fit across Designs 1 to 4, so linear-test constructs recover reasonably well at the item level. [→ Shudong Wang 2012](#shudong-wang-2012)

## Evidence

### Shudong Wang 2012

Shudong Wang, Hong Jiao. (2012). Examine Construct Validity of Computerized Adaptive Test in K-12 Assessments. Paper presented at the annual meeting of the National Council on Measurement in Education. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/examine-construct-validity-of-computerized-adaptive-test-in-k-12-assessments/

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Simulation results (Tables 7 and 8) comparing model fit across Designs 1-4, which vary MCAR item and response missingness; the article reports "Both unidimensional models (Rasch and 2PL) fit data well" while the bifactor model recovered data poorly.

> "First, at the item level, results show that both item and response missing data characterized as MCAR for linear tests have no differential effects on models fit across Designs 1 to 4. Both unidimensional models (Rasch and 2PL) fit data well but the multidimensional model (bifactor model) recoveres data poorly."
