## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In think-aloud interviews, physics faculty reasoned deliberately, used intuition but double-checked it via approximations and calculations, and knew where relevant knowledge was in their knowledge structure so they could refine solutions. [→ Alexandru Maries 2025](#alexandru-maries-2025)

## Evidence

### Alexandru Maries 2025

Alexandru Maries, Ryan Sayer, and Chandralekha Singh. (2025). Investigation of student and faculty problem solving: An example from quantum mechanics. Physics Education Research Conference. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2504.17487](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2504.17487)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Semi-structured think-aloud interviews with five faculty members (plus four additional QM students interviewed on Q3 only). Faculty drew on equations such as K = p2/2m, λ = h/p, and dsinθ = nλ and performed rough calculations to check intuition about proton and sand-grain interference.

> "the responses of interviewed faculty members  indicate that they reasoned quite deliberately , and even though they made use of intuition, they also double checked it by making approximations and simplifications and using them to carry out calculations (or estimations)."
