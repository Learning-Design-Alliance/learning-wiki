Evidence¶

Tatlow-Golden M 2016¶

Tatlow-Golden M, Prihodova L, Gavin B, Cullen W, McNicholas F. (2016). What do general practitioners know about ADHD? Attitudes and knowledge among first-contact gatekeepers: systematic narrative review. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-016-0516-x

q2 · i? · review · r2

Thematic narrative synthesis of GP diagnosis findings across the review's included studies. The review reports only 1–5% of GPs in the UK, Iran and Canada stated diagnosis was a suitable role for them. No effect size is printed.

"Of concern, however, was that almost all had low confidence in their recognition/diagnostic ability, citing their lack of training, and complexity and uncertainty regarding ADHD."

Tatlow-Golden M 2016 (2)¶

Tatlow-Golden M, Prihodova L, Gavin B, Cullen W, McNicholas F. (2016). What do general practitioners know about ADHD? Attitudes and knowledge among first-contact gatekeepers: systematic narrative review. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-016-0516-x

q2 · i? · review · r3

Symptom-recognition assessment findings from two studies (Australia 2002, UK 2005) as synthesised in the review. The review reports this raises the possibility that children presenting with ADHD symptoms alone may not be recognised as having ADHD. No effect size is printed.

"GPs’ identification of the three key ADHD symptoms of inattention, hyperactivity and impulsivity was very high, but many GPs mislabelled conduct and oppositional defiant symptoms as ADHD."
