## Evidence

### Sunny Yu 2026

Sunny Yu, Myra Cheng, Ahmad Jabbar, Ilia Sucholutsky, Katherine M. Collins, Dan Jurafsky, Robert D. Hawkins. (2026). The efficiency-gain illusion: People underestimate the rate of AI use and overestimate its benefits on simple tasks. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22687

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Pre-registered Study 2 (prediction sample N=600, completion sample N=1001 on Prolific) compared predicted versus actual times for independent and AI-assisted completion. The speedup illusion held "across all four task categories and both difficulty levels" per the article.

> "Specifically, participants expected AI to save more time (55.7 seconds) than it actually did (7.5 seconds) (Equation (1);β=−48.33,p<0.001). This difference primarily arises from miscalibration about AI-assisted performance, i.e., ˆtA(τ) versust A(τ): the actual completion time with AI (86.2 seconds) is nearly double the predicted time with AI (43.3 seconds) (β= 42.9,p<0.001)."
