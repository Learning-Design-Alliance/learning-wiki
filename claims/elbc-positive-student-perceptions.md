## Evidence

### Aboueisha Hadeel 2025

Aboueisha Hadeel, Abouzeid Enjy, Sallam Moataz A., Talaat Wagdy. (2025). A digital recipe for enhancing clinical reasoning: the role of e-learning by concordance (E-LbC): a quasi-experimental study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08005-w

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Anonymous 24-item questionnaire administered via Google Forms to all 60 students (100% response rate, no missing data), rated on a 5-point Likert scale; reliability coefficient 0.823. The article prints the 90% and 62% figures.

> "Most students (90%) strongly agreed or agreed that the e-LbC approach aids in improving clinical reasoning ability for the future and (62%) of students reported that they were overall satisfied with the instructional session"

### Aboueisha Hadeel 2025 (2)

Aboueisha Hadeel, Abouzeid Enjy, Sallam Moataz A., Talaat Wagdy. (2025). A digital recipe for enhancing clinical reasoning: the role of e-learning by concordance (E-LbC): a quasi-experimental study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08005-w

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same validated 24-item perception questionnaire completed by all 60 study-group students after the post-SCT. The article prints agreement percentages of 69% and 75% for SCT's instructional and assessment value.

> "most students (69%) strongly agreed or agreed that SCT can be used as a useful instructional method for the future and (75%) strongly agreed or agreed that SCT is an effective assessment tool"
