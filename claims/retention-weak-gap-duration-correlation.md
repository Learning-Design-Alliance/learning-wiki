## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox 2020

Bethany R. Wilcox, Steven J. Pollock, and Daniel R. Bolton. (2020). Retention of conceptual learning after an interactive introductory physics course. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010140](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010140)

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis relating each matched student's FMCE score shift to their gap duration (1, 3, 8, 13, or 15 months). The article reports a Spearman coefficient of "r = −0.1" that was "statistically signiﬁcant ( p < 0.001)" but quite small.

> "We found a Spearman correlation coeﬃcient of r = −0.1 that, while quite small, was statistically signiﬁcant ( p < 0.001)."

### Bethany R. Wilcox 2020 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox, Steven J. Pollock, and Daniel R. Bolton. (2020). Retention of conceptual learning after an interactive introductory physics course. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010140](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010140)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Box-and-whisker plots of FMCE raw score shift for each of the five gap durations (Fig. 2) show medians that stay nearly flat, while the spread of shifts narrows "after the ﬁrst 1-3 semesters."

> "Figure 2 shows that there is very little variation in the median shifts as the gap size increases, though the overall range in the scores tends to decrease after the ﬁrst 1-3 semesters."
