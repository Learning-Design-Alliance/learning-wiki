## Evidence

### Huynh 2020

Huynh, T., Jambuge, A. P., Khong, H., Laverty, J. T., & Sayre, E. C. (2020). Positioning in groups: The roles of expertise and being in charge. Department of Physics, Kansas State University. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2003.01529](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2003.01529)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative analysis of the catching up storyline from video data of the second lab meeting. Abbey initiated activities such as requesting information but "received brief, dictating and expository responses", and peers often intervened with other topics or withdrew before giving full details.

> "Although Abbey initiated many of the activities (requesting information, offering lab materials, etc.), she usually waited for her peers' availability to do so and received brief, dictating and expository responses."

### Huynh 2020 (2)

Huynh, T., Jambuge, A. P., Khong, H., Laverty, J. T., & Sayre, E. C. (2020). Positioning in groups: The roles of expertise and being in charge. Department of Physics, Kansas State University. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2003.01529](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2003.01529)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative analysis of the moving forward storyline, in which Charlie and Will set topics about intellectual aspects of tasks, asked and answered questions sequentially, and made decisions without explicitly addressing Abbey, who occasionally took bids such as operating the X-ray.

> "Throughout the "moving forward" storyline, her peers had more authority to move the lab forward, whereas Abbey played a minimum and passive contributive role."
