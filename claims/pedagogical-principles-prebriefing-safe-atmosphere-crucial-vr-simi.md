## Evidence

### Birkheim SL 2024

Birkheim SL, Calogiuri G, Hvalstad M, Martinsen R. (2024). Exploring the experiences of resident doctors in child and adolescent psychiatry with virtual reality-based simulation training: a qualitative study. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-024-11941-w

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Focus-group data on the theme 'The importance of pedagogical principles': prebriefing activities (lectures, advance reading) were considered useful, and facilitators' experience, structure and non-judgmental style were perceived as strengths; one participant called the session 'one of the best classes'.

> "The prebrief strengthens the learning process, as long as it is relevant to the learning objectives."

### Birkheim SL 2024 (2)

Birkheim SL, Calogiuri G, Hvalstad M, Martinsen R. (2024). Exploring the experiences of resident doctors in child and adolescent psychiatry with virtual reality-based simulation training: a qualitative study. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-024-11941-w

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Participants stated they would prefer smaller groups for more active engagement, but judged group safety, not size, as decisive for participation in reflection.

> "Nevertheless, the most important factor was to have a pleasant, safe atmosphere within the group, to encourage all participants to share their thoughts and experiences."
