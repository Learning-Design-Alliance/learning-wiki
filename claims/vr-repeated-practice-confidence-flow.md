## Evidence

### Sapci AH 2026

Sapci AH, Ugbor A, Ayub R, Lauritzen C, Reedtz C, Kristensen KB, Mathisen V, Næss ET, Furu R, Nermo H, Jentoft R. (2026). Development of Therapeutic Competencies in Health Care Students: Qualitative Focus Group Study Using 360-Degree Video and Virtual Reality Technology. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/75776

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

From the discussion of the focus group findings (n=12): students in module 3 practiced conversations with the family in a simulated therapy room without dialogue, and described getting into the task gradually with repeated practice, initially feeling stressed by the family staring at them.

> "Students reported that they experienced increased confidence and flow in communication after repeated exercises in the VR environment. This underscores the importance of practice for achieving mastery and confidence in therapeutic practice."

### Sapci AH 2026 (2)

Sapci AH, Ugbor A, Ayub R, Lauritzen C, Reedtz C, Kristensen KB, Mathisen V, Næss ET, Furu R, Nermo H, Jentoft R. (2026). JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/75776

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Focus group interviews showed students valued open questions that stimulated reflection on several possibilities rather than giving answers immediately, and that group discussion of differing perceptions of the same VR scenario triggered longer thought processes.

> "Participants appreciated the opportunity to discuss and reflect on their actions and decisions in the VR scenarios, as shown in the dialogue below"
