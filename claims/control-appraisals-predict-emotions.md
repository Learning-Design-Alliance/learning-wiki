# Control appraisals strongly predict student teachers' emotions, whereas value appraisals show no significant relation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Higher within-lesson control appraisals predicted more enjoyment (β = .25, p < .01), less anger (β = −.56, p < .01) and less anxiety (β = .75, p < .01); value appraisals were not significantly related to any emotion. [→ Becker 2025](#becker-2025)

## Evidence

### Becker 2025

Becker, E. S., Hascher, T., Goetz, T., & Staub, F. C. (2025). Exploring antecedents of student teachers' emotions during instructional experiences: A situation‐specific analysis. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 95(Suppl. 1), S133–S152. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12738

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Full MSEM models with classroom conditions and appraisals as latent predictors at the within level. The article reports "more enjoyment (β = .25, p < .01)" and the anger and anxiety coefficients above; explained within-level variance rose to 47% for enjoyment, 28% for anger and 31% for anxiety when appraisals were included.

> "Teachers who reported a higher sense of control over their instructional practice within a lesson also reported more enjoyment (β = .25, p < .01) and less anger (β = −.56, p < .01) and anxiety (β = .75, p < .01)."
