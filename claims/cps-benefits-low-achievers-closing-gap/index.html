Evidence¶

Jiun-Wei Guo · Hsiao-Ching She · Meng-Jun Chen · Pei-Yi Tsai 2023¶

Jiun-Wei Guo · Hsiao-Ching She · Meng-Jun Chen · Pei-Yi Tsai. (2023). Can CPS better prepare 8th graders for problem-solving in electromagnetism and bridging the gap between high- and low-achievers than IPS? Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-023-09407-y

q3 · i? · causal · r2

A one-factor ANCOVA comparing conditions within achievement levels found "the low-achievers in the CPS group significantly outperformed the low-achievers in the IPS group" on the EPST, with printed posttest means of 9.04 (CPS) versus 6.80 (IPS).

"the results showed that the low-achievers in the CPS group significantly outperformed the low-achievers in the IPS group (F = 4.13, p < 0.05, partial η2 = 0.51, effect size = medium) on the EPST"

Jiun-Wei Guo · Hsiao-Ching She · Meng-Jun Chen · Pei-Yi Tsai 2023 (2)¶

Jiun-Wei Guo · Hsiao-Ching She · Meng-Jun Chen · Pei-Yi Tsai. (2023). Can CPS better prepare 8th graders for problem-solving in electromagnetism and bridging the gap between high- and low-achievers than IPS? Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-023-09407-y

q3 · i? · causal · r2

The same one-factor ANCOVA found high-achievers in CPS and IPS "performed the same" on the EPST (F = 1.48, p = 0.229), with printed posttest means of 9.88 (CPS) and 9.36 (IPS); equivalence was not formally tested.

"while high-achievers in the two groups performed the same (F = 1.48, p = 0.229)"

Jiun-Wei Guo · Hsiao-Ching She · Meng-Jun Chen · Pei-Yi Tsai 2023 (3)¶

Jiun-Wei Guo · Hsiao-Ching She · Meng-Jun Chen · Pei-Yi Tsai. (2023). Can CPS better prepare 8th graders for problem-solving in electromagnetism and bridging the gap between high- and low-achievers than IPS? Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-023-09407-y

q2 · i? · causal · r2

A scatter plot with marginal histograms (Fig. 4) comparing high- and low-achievers in both groups showed "CPS was more effective than IPS in reducing the discrepancy in EPST scores between high- and low-achievers"; CPS low vs. high posttest means were 9.04 vs. 9.88, while IPS remained 6.80 vs. 9.36.
