# Employee-oriented organizational culture negatively moderates the relationship between teachers' contingent punishment and residents' self-efficacy

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The interaction of contingent punishment with EOC on self-efficacy is significant and negative (β = −0.24, p < 0.001), such that the promotion of self-efficacy by punishment increases in the context of low EOC level. [→ Deng Guangwei 2019](#deng-guangwei-2019)

## Evidence

### Deng Guangwei 2019

Deng Guangwei, Zhao Di, Lio Jonathan, Chen Xinyu, Ma Xiaopeng, Liang Liang, Feng Chenpeng. (2019). Strategic elements of residency training in China: transactional leadership, self-efficacy, and employee-orientation culture. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1792-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical moderated regression on the 247-resident sample with centralized variables. The article reports the punishment-by-EOC interaction on self-efficacy as β = −0.24, p < 0.001, and interprets that punishment promotes self-efficacy more where employee-oriented culture is low.

> "As shown in Model 9 of Table 4, EOC plays a significant role in the punishment behavior of clinical teachers with TLS and self-efficacy of residents (β = − 0.24, p < 0.001). It indicates that, in the context of low EOC level, the promotion of the punishment behavior of clinical teachers with TLS to self-efficacy of residents increase, that is, hypothesis H3 is established."
