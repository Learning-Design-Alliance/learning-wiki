# Definitions of free and bound charges differ across textbooks in ways where the literal meaning of the adjective does not adequately describe the object's properties

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Tamm's definition of free charges includes charges unable to move macroscopic distances, which fits the notion of freedom poorly; Irodov's external charges include a conductor's own charges arising by electrostatic induction. [→ Yu.A. Koksharov 2020](#yua-koksharov-2020)
`q1 i?` The term bound charges is also not fully specific, since free-by-Tamm charges can be localized on a body's surface, e.g. by contact electrification. [→ Yu.A. Koksharov 2020](#yua-koksharov-2020)

### Yu.A. Koksharov 2020

Yu.A. Koksharov. (2020). Sources of E, D, B, H in electro- and magnetostatics: critical comments. Faculty of Physics, M.V. Lomonosov Moscow State University. Originally published 1944. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2002.03748](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2002.03748)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Comparative analysis of definitions in sections 4, quoting Tamm, Sivukhin, Irodov, Feynman and Purcell. The author concludes the literal meaning of the adjectives "free" and "bound" "не всегда адекватно описывает свойства объекта" and notes Purcell's proposed alternative term structural charges.

> "Сравнение определений " свободных " и " связанных " зарядов в различных учебниках показывает , что буквальный смысл используемого прилагательного не всегда адекватно описывает свойства объекта ."
