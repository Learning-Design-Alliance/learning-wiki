# In a number comparison task, adults outperform 6- to 7-year-old children in accuracy, Arabic numerals are easier than dot arrays, and easy 0.5 ratios are easier than difficult 0.8 ratios

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Adults were more accurate than children, Arabic numerals more accurate than dot arrays, and 0.5 ratio trials more accurate than 0.8 ratio trials. [→ Cantlon Jessica F. 2009](#cantlon-jessica-f-2009)
`q3 i?` The numerical ratio effect on accuracy was larger for dot arrays (15%) than for Arabic numerals (3%). [→ Cantlon Jessica F. 2009 (2)](#cantlon-jessica-f-2009-2)

## Evidence

### Cantlon Jessica F. 2009

Cantlon Jessica F., Libertus Melissa E., Pinel Philippe, Dehaene Stanislas, Brannon Elizabeth M., Pelphrey Kevin A. (2009). The Neural Development of an Abstract Concept of Number. Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn.2008.21159

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Behavioral accuracy analysis of 14 children and 14 adults performing symbolic and nonsymbolic number comparisons in the scanner. The article reports that "adults performed more accurately than children (93% vs. 80%)" and that the easy 0.5 ratio beat the difficult 0.8 ratio (91% vs. 82%).

> "An ANOVA with factors of Group (children, adults) × Notation (Arabic, dots) × Ratio (0.5, 0.8) with a repeated measure of accuracy revealed main effects of group [F(1, 52) = 35.3, p < .001], notation [F(1, 52) = 9.28, p < .005], and ratio [F(1, 52) = 92.24, p < .001]."

### Cantlon Jessica F. 2009 (2)

Cantlon Jessica F., Libertus Melissa E., Pinel Philippe, Dehaene Stanislas, Brannon Elizabeth M., Pelphrey Kevin A. (2009). The Neural Development of an Abstract Concept of Number. Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn.2008.21159

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same accuracy ANOVA in the behavioral results section: the notation × ratio interaction showed a "greater effect of ratio in the dot arrays condition than in the Arabic numerals condition" (15% vs. 3%).

> "The only significant interaction was between notation and ratio, owing to a greater effect of ratio in the dot arrays condition than in the Arabic numerals condition (dot arrays ratio effect = 15%; Arabic numerals ratio effect = 3%)."
