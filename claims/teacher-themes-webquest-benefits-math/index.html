Evidence¶

Yenmez 2017¶

Yenmez, A. A., Özpinar, İ., & Gökçe, S. (2017). Use of WebQuests in Mathematics Instruction: Academic Achievement, Teacher and Student Opinions. Universal Journal of Educational Research 5(9). https://doi.org/10.13189/ujer.2017.050913

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Qualitative content analysis of semi-structured interviews with the executive teacher and four observer teachers, coded by three experts with a fit index of 85.24%. Teachers also reported students could improve critical thinking, creative thinking, and relationship-establishing skills.

"the following themes were identified out of student opinions on their learning and the benefits of using WebQuests in mathematics instruction (executive teacher was encoded as ET while other teachers were encoded as T1, T2, T3, and T4): permanent learning (ET, T1, T2, T3, and T4), appeal (ET, T1, T2, T3, and T4), active participation (ET, T1, T2, T3, and T4), rehearsal (even if students are not at school) (ET, T1 and T4), and association with daily life (ET, T1, T2, T3, and T4)."
