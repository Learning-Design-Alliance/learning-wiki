# Narrative comprehension increases from low to high education, independent of narrative codality

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Narrative comprehension increased from low to high education in a representative adult sample. [→ Markus Huff 2025](#markus-huff-2025)
`q3 i1` Bayesian main-effects model shows higher education predicts higher comprehension (OR 1.41 middle, 1.62 high). [→ Markus Huff 2025 (2)](#markus-huff-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Markus Huff 2025

Markus Huff, Natalia Gagarina, Ekaterina Varkentin, Irina R. Brich. (2025). Education, Not Age, Linked to Narrative Comprehension. https://aspredicted.org/WT9_TK6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Preregistered online experiment with a representative German quota sample (N = 1487, ages 18–87); bridging inference task on MAIN-based stories analyzed with logistic mixed-effects models. The frequentist test confirmed the education effect: "narrative comprehension increased from low to high education, χ2(2) = 57.89, p < .001." No effect size was printed for this test.

> "Confirming H1, narrative comprehension increased from low to high education, χ2(2) = 57.89, p < .001."

### Markus Huff 2025 (2)

Markus Huff, Natalia Gagarina, Ekaterina Varkentin, Irina R. Brich. (2025). Education, Not Age, Linked to Narrative Comprehension. https://aspredicted.org/WT9_TK6

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r3`

Bayesian main-effects model (brms) with education and codality as fixed effects; Table 1 prints odds ratios of 1.41 for middle and 1.62 for high education versus low. The article states "Performance increased with education (conforming H1)".

> "Performance increased with education (conforming H1) and is lower in the text than the picture condition (non-conforming H3)."
