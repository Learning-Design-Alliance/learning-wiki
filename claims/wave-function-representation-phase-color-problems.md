# In interviews, students understood real-and-imaginary wave-function representations but the phase-color representation caused significant problems for most students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Apart from two students whose confusion stemmed from expectations, students did not express confusion over the real and imaginary representations of the wave function, and several learned concepts such as the 90-degree phase offset from them. [→ S. B. McKagan 2008](#s-b-mckagan-2008)
`q2 i?` Of students who explored the phase-color view in interviews, none could explain it on their own, and none spent much time in phase mode when given a choice. [→ S. B. McKagan 2008 (2)](#s-b-mckagan-2008-2)

## Evidence

### S. B. McKagan 2008

S. B. McKagan, K. K. Perkins, M. Dubson, C. Malley, S. Reid, R. LeMaster, and C. E. Wieman. (2008). Developing and Researching PhET simulations for Teaching Quantum Mechanics. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.4503 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0709.4503](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0709.4503)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Think-aloud interview observations on Quantum Tunneling and Wave Packets and Quantum Wave Interference, reported in Section V. The quote reports the printed absence of confusion about real/imaginary representations; the article also prints that students learned the components are 90 degrees out of phase.

> "Aside these two students, whose confusion stemmed more from their expectations than from the simulation, students did not express any confusion over the real and imaginary representations of the wave function in interviews on Quantum Tunneling and Wave Packets and Quantum Wave Interference ."

### S. B. McKagan 2008 (2)

S. B. McKagan, K. K. Perkins, M. Dubson, C. Malley, S. Reid, R. LeMaster, and C. E. Wieman. (2008). Developing and Researching PhET simulations for Teaching Quantum Mechanics. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.4503 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0709.4503](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0709.4503)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Think-aloud interviews on Quantum Wave Interference and Quantum Tunneling and Wave Packets: three of five interviewed students explored the phase-color view and none could explain it unprompted; additional students interviewed on the tunneling simulation also could not explain it. No effect size is printed.

> "the “phase color” representation caused signiﬁcant problems for most students. In interviews on Quantum Wave Interference , three out of ﬁve students interviewed explored this view. None of the three made any comments on it on their own"
