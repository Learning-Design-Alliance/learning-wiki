# CMC integration yields an overall small-to-medium positive effect (d = .554) on L2 learning outcomes compared with face-to-face communication

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Across 56 primary studies, the overall mean effect size favoring CMC over face-to-face or non-computer-mediated communication was d = .554, with a 95% confidence interval of .482 to .626, described as a small to medium effect. [→ Lin 2012](#lin-2012)

## Evidence

### Lin 2012

Lin, H. (2012). The Effectiveness of Computer-Mediated Communication on SLA: A Meta-Analysis and Research Synthesis. In L. Bradley & S. Thouësny (Eds.), CALL: Using, Learning, Knowing, EUROCALL Conference, Gothenburg, Sweden, Proceedings. Research-publishing.net. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED574920

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analysis of 56 eligible empirical studies (2000–2011) using experimental or quasi-experimental designs; effect sizes were Cohen's d, weighted and corrected for small sample sizes. The meta-analysis reports "The overall effect size is .554, with 95% confidence interval between .482 and .626."
