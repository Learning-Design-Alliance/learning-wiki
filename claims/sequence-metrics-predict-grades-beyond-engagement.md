# Theory-grounded reading sequence metrics explain 20.1% of grade variance without engagement, adding 8.2% beyond it; Quickness negatively predicts grades while Stickiness benefits from jumping

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Selected sequence metrics (Stickiness, its interaction with N_Jumps, and Quickness) explained 20.1% of final-grade variance; adding them to an engagement-only model significantly improved fit (ΔR² = 0.082, F(3,95) = 3.92, p = 0.01). [→ Erwin Lopez and Atsushi Shimada 2026](#erwin-lopez-and-atsushi-shimada-2026)
`q3 i?` Quickness (tendency to read pages quickly) was negatively related to final grades (β coefficient -2.79, p = 0.02), while Stickiness was detrimental without jumps but beneficial with frequent jumps (Stickiness:N_Jumps, p < 0.01). [→ Erwin Lopez and Atsushi Shimada 2026](#erwin-lopez-and-atsushi-shimada-2026)
