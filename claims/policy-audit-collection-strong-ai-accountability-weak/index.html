Meghna Manoj Nair 2026¶

Meghna Manoj Nair, Rachel Greenstadt. (2026). "We'll Fix It Later": Education, AI, and the Deferral of Student Privacy in EdTech. New York University. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28137

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Audit finding from the Results section: two coders scored 48 platforms on a five-dimension rubric (mean Cohen's kappa 0.782), with AI disclosure (C4) among the lowest-scoring dimensions (mean 0.90 of 2).

"Thirty-three percent of platforms (16/48) made no meaningful AI disclosure despite visible AI features."

Meghna Manoj Nair 2026 (2)¶

Meghna Manoj Nair, Rachel Greenstadt. (2026). "We'll Fix It Later": Education, AI, and the Deferral of Student Privacy in EdTech. New York University. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28137

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Audit finding from the Results section. Accountability (C5) mean was 1.07 of 2, with 8% scoring 0, 73% scoring 1, and 19% scoring 2, indicating mostly generic rather than operational accountability disclosure.

"Accountability was similarly shallow: 73% received the middle score, typically a generic email address, vague breach language, or claims of 'industry -standard' security without operational detail."

Meghna Manoj Nair 2026 (3)¶

Meghna Manoj Nair, Rachel Greenstadt. (2026). "We'll Fix It Later": Education, AI, and the Deferral of Student Privacy in EdTech. New York University. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28137

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Audit finding from the Results section: C1 scored 0% at zero, 21% at one, and 79% at two, mean 1.81 of 2, so platforms describe collection better than subsequent governance of that data.

"Data collection disclosure (C1) was strongest, while downstream governance was weaker."
