# A 4-week social media training workshop improved inhibitory control within the intervention group of older adults, with a large effect over the full study period

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Intervention participants improved in inhibitory control both during the workshop (d = .555) and over the entire 4-month study period (d = 2.176). [→ Kelly Quinn 2018](#kelly-quinn-2018)

## Evidence

### Kelly Quinn 2018

Kelly Quinn. (2018). Cognitive Effects of Social Media Use: A Case of Older Adults. Social Media + Society. https://doi.org/10.1177/2056305118787203

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Within-group dependent t-tests in a randomized wait-list controlled study of 34 novice older adult social media users (17 intervention, 17 control) measured with the California Older Adult Stroop Test. The article reports "d = 2.176" over the full period, while noting "some of this change might be attributed to practice effects."

> "The improvement in inhibitory function scores for the intervention group occurred both over the period in which the workshop was conducted (t16 = 2.289, p = .036, d = .555) as well as over the entire study period (t16 = 8.97, p < .001, d = 2.176)."
