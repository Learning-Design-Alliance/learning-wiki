# After a year of repetitive practice, the boot camp cohort showed significantly higher confidence than the routine-training cohort on six surgical tasks, including acute abdomen management, trauma management, and laparoscopic camera navigation.

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The experimental group had significantly higher confidence than controls on six specific surgical-task items after repetitive practice (p < 0.05). [→ Wang Wei 2020](#wang-wei-2020)

## Evidence

### Wang Wei 2020

Wang Wei, Ma Hucheng, Ren Haozhen, Wang Zhongxia, Mao Liang, He Ningning. (2020). The Impact of Surgical Boot Camp and Subsequent Repetitive Practice on the Surgical Skills and Confidence of Residents. World Journal of Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00268-020-05669-x

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Cross-cohort comparison of the experimental group's annual confidence survey against the control group's annual survey. Six surgical tasks favored the experimental group with "significantly higher confidence" (p < 0.05); no effect sizes are printed.

> "after a year of repetitive practice, the residents in the experimental group had significantly higher confidence in their skills related to "differential diagnosis and basic management of an acute abdomen problem (question 6)," "management of trauma patients (question 7)," "deciding on the incision to be used in an abdominal surgery (question 12)," "determining the margins when excising a lesion (question 14)," "doing a vessel ligation at depth (question 18)," and "navigating a camera during laparoscopy (question 21)" (p < 0.05)."
