## Evidence

### dos Santos Kawata KH 2021

dos Santos Kawata KH, Ueno Y, Hashimoto R, Yoshino S, Ohta K, Nishida A, Ando S, Nakatani H, Kasai K and Koike S (2021) Development of Metacognition in Adolescence: The Congruency-Based Metacognition Scale. Front. Psychol. 11:565231. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.565231

`q2 · i2`

Correlational analysis across all 633 child-parent pairs examined age effects on CMS scores. Child age correlated negatively with CMS metacognition (r = -0.27, p < 0.01), meaning lower scores (higher ability) at older ages, but not with self-judgment accuracy (r = -0.03, p = 0.40).

> "A negative correlation was observed between child age and the CMS metacognition score ( r = −0.27, p < 0.01), but not on self-judgment accuracy score ( r=− 0.03, p= 0.40)."

### dos Santos Kawata KH 2021 (2)

dos Santos Kawata KH, Ueno Y, Hashimoto R, Yoshino S, Ohta K, Nishida A, Ando S, Nakatani H, Kasai K and Koike S (2021) Development of Metacognition in Adolescence: The Congruency-Based Metacognition Scale. Front. Psychol. 11:565231. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.565231

`q2 · i?`

Regression analysis with age as the independent variable on all participant samples tested linear and logarithmic models for CMS metacognition. Both models were significant, and the logarithmic model had a lower standard error of the regression (SE = 2.396 vs. 2.406), suggesting it fit better.

> "the regression analysis showed that both the linear model [ F(1, 631)= 49.6, p < 0.01; B=− 0.22, B SE= 0.03, β=− 0.27, p < 0.01] and non-linear model (logarithmic) [ F(1, 631) = 55.7, p < 0.01; B=− 3.76, B SE= 0.50, β=− 0.29, p < 0.01] were signiﬁcant for CMS metacognition."
