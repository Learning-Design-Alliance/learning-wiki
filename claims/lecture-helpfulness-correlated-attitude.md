# Perceived helpfulness of in-class lectures is positively correlated with science attitudes, while the instructor-attitude correlation disappears in that subset

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For the 712 students who attended in-class lectures, Lecture Helpfulness Index is weakly but significantly positively correlated with Attitude Index (coefficient 0.071, p = 1.7E-04). [→ Adam S. Trotter 2019](#adam-s-trotter-2019)
`q2 i?` The Instructor Attitude Index, significant in the baseline analysis, is no longer significantly correlated in this subset (p = 0.2) when Lecture Helpfulness Index is included. [→ Adam S. Trotter 2019 (2)](#adam-s-trotter-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Adam S. Trotter 2019

Adam S. Trotter, Daniel E. Reichart, Aaron P. LaCluyzé & Rachel Freed. (2019). Factors Contributing to Attitudinal Gains in Introductory Astronomy Courses. Robotic Telescopes, Student Research and Education (RTSRE) Proceedings, Vol. 1, No. 1. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.32374/rtsre.2019.014](https://doi.org/10.32374/rtsre.2019.014)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Subset regression on 712 of the 749 respondents who attended in-class lectures, performed on Attitude Index vs the baseline independent variables plus Lecture Helpfulness Index. Table 5 prints Lecture Helpfulness Index coefficient 0.071 with p-value 1.7E-04 (0.077, p = 2.1E-05 without Instructor Index).

> "We ﬁnd a weak but statis- tically signiﬁcant positive correlation between the Lecture Helpfulness Index and the Attitude Index: the more helpful a student ﬁnds attending class to be, the more positive their attitude towards science at the end of the course."

### Adam S. Trotter 2019 (2)

Adam S. Trotter, Daniel E. Reichart, Aaron P. LaCluyzé & Rachel Freed. (2019). Factors Contributing to Attitudinal Gains in Introductory Astronomy Courses. Robotic Telescopes, Student Research and Education (RTSRE) Proceedings, Vol. 1, No. 1. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.32374/rtsre.2019.014](https://doi.org/10.32374/rtsre.2019.014)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Subset regression (N = 712) including both Lecture Helpfulness Index and Instructor Attitude Index. Table 5 prints Instructor Attitude Index coefficient 0.036 with p-value 2.0E-01, indicating students' attitudes toward instructors and lecture effectiveness are largely measures of the same thing.

> "However, note that the Instructor Attitude Index, which exhibited weak but signiﬁcant positive correlation in the earlier base- line analysis (Table 4), is no longer signiﬁcantly correlated in this subset ( p = 0.2), when Lecture Helpfulness Index is included."
