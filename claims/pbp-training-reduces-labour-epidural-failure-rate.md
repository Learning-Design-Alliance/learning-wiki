## Evidence

### Kallidaikurichi Srinivasan Karthikeyan 2018

Kallidaikurichi Srinivasan Karthikeyan, Gallagher Anthony, O'Brien Niall, Sudir Vinod, Barrett Nick, O'Connor Raymund, Holt Francesca, Lee Peter, O'Donnell Brian, Shorten George. (2018). Proficiency-based progression training: an 'end to end' model for decreasing error applied to achievement of effective epidural analgesia during labour: a randomised control study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2017-020099

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Prospective, randomised, single-blind controlled trial at Cork University Maternity Hospital: 14 novice trainees (8 simulation-only, 6 PBP) performed their next 10 labour epidurals each (140 procedures analysed). The quote shows the primary outcome contrast: "greater in group S (23/80, 28.7%) than in group P (8/60, 13.3%)".

> "The principal outcome— proportion of epidural failures— was greater in group S (23/80, 28.7%) than in group P (8/60, 13.3%) (p=0.04, Chi square test). The absolute risk reduction was 15.4% (CI 2% to 28.8%)."
