# No training condition affects letter script stroke direction

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r3` · `q4`

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` None of the three training conditions showed significant effects on stroke direction acquisitions. [→ Fernando Núñez-Regueiro 2024](#fernando-nunez-regueiro-2024)

## Evidence

### Fernando Núñez-Regueiro 2024

Fernando Núñez-Regueiro, Natacha Boissicat, Fanny Gimbert, Céline Pobel-Burtin, Marie-Caroline Croset, Marie-Line Bosse, and Cécile Nurra. (2024). Involving the Body to Improve Letter Knowledge and Script: An Experimental Study in French Kindergarten. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09923-9

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Ordinal regression on stroke direction (6-point rated copying task) in the RCT; the likelihood ratio test for the model was not significant [chi2(2) = 0.207, p = .902]. No effect size indicates a detectable effect; equivalence was not tested.

> "None of the experimental groups showed significant effects on skill acquisitions in letter stroke direction, and effects were very similar for pencil-and-paper training (b = -0.035, OR = 0.966, d = -.019, p = .872)"
