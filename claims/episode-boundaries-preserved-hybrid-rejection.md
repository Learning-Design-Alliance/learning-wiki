# Children preserve boundaries of separate learning episodes: hybrid statements combining content from two passages are rejected below chance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` A main effect of statement type on endorsement (η² = .65) showed children endorsed old statements above chance and rejected both new and hybrid statements below chance, indicating episode boundaries were preserved rather than blended. [→ Bauer 2020](#bauer-2020)

## Evidence

### Bauer 2020

Bauer, P. J., Esposito, A. G., & Daly, J. J. (2020). Self-derivation through memory integration: A model for accumulation of semantic knowledge. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2019.101271

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Recognition test one week after learning in Experiment 2 (103 children). A 2 × 3 mixed ANOVA found "a main effect of statement type, F(2, 100) = 92.39, p < .001, η2 = .65." Old statements were endorsed above chance (mean difference .20); new and hybrid statements were endorsed below chance (mean differences −.17 and −.10).

> "there was a main effect of statement type, F(2, 100) = 92.39, p < .001, η2 = .65. Follow-up pairwise comparisons with Bonferroni corrections revealed significant differences in children's rates of endorsement of all three statement types."
