# Yes/no feedback that participants were correct nearly quenched curiosity for correct answers, whereas without feedback curiosity peaked at moderate-to-high confidence

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` When participants were told they were correct, curiosity fell to close to zero, while in the no-feedback condition curiosity peaked at moderate certainty and declined as certainty increased. [→ Metcalfe 2023](#metcalfe-2023)

## Evidence

### Metcalfe 2023

Metcalfe, J., Vuorre, M., Towner, E., & Eich, T. S. (2023). Curiosity: The effects of feedback and confidence on the desire to know. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, vol. 152, no. 2, pp. 464-482. https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0001284

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2, a within-subjects study of 80 Columbia/Barnard students with yes/no feedback on a random half of trials and no feedback on the other half, modeled curiosity with multilevel regression. In the feedback condition "curiosity fell to close to zero" (bIntercept = 8.97), versus an intercept of 52.98 without feedback, where curiosity peaked at moderate certainty.

> "Once they were told that they were correct, however, curiosity fell to close to zero, just as was expected ( bIntercept = 8.97, SE = 2.50,"
