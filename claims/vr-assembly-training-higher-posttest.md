# Immersive VR assembly training produced significantly higher delayed post-test scores than desktop virtual and physical assembly training among novices

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In a randomized three-group experiment with pre-test as covariate, learning method had a significant main effect on post-test scores, with the VR group highest (η²=0.210). [→ Weichao Lin 2023](#weichao-lin-2023)

## Evidence

### Weichao Lin 2023

Weichao Lin, Liang Chen, Wei Xiong, Kang Ran, Anlan Fan. (2023). Measuring the Sense of Presence and Learning Efficacy in Immersive Virtual Assembly Training. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2312.10387](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2312.10387)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANCOVA of post-test scores with pre-test as covariate, comparing 24 VR, 22 desktop, and 7 physical assembly students. "significant main effect of learning method on posttest scores" with "F = 6.512, p = 0.003, η2=0.210"; estimated marginal means were highest for VR (69.453), desktop second, physical lowest.

> "The results showed a significant main effect of learning method on posttest scores (Table II, F = 6.512, p = 0.003, η2=0.210), indicating that the group category accounted for about 21% of total variance."
