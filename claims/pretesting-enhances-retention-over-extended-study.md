# Pretesting before study enhances posttest recall of tested content compared to extended study time, even when pretest answers are unsuccessful

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Participants who attempted pretest questions before reading recalled tested items better on a posttest (M = 75%) than those given extended study time (M = 56%), despite answering only 5% of pretest questions correctly. [→ Richland 2009](#richland-2009)

## Evidence

### Richland 2009

Richland, L. E., Kornell, N., & Kao, L. S. (2009). The Pretesting Effect: Do Unsuccessful Retrieval Attempts Enhance Learning? Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied, 15(3), 243–257. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016496

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1: 63 undergraduates randomly assigned to a test-and-study condition or an extended-study condition, reading a passage about cerebral achromatopsia. Items answered correctly on the pretest were removed from analysis. The study found "testing resulted in better posttest performance" with d = 1.1.

> "testing resulted in better posttest performance (M ⫽ 75%, SE ⫽ 3.2) than did the provision of extra time to study the same material (M ⫽ 56%, SE ⫽ 2.7), t(61) ⫽ 4.26, p ⬍ .0001, d ⫽ 1.1."
