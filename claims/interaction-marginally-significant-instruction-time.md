# The instruction-by-time interaction favoring the simulation group was only marginally significant (one-tailed p = 0.085, g2 = 0.05), and the main effect of instruction was not significant

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a two-way mixed-design ANOVA, the instruction-by-time interaction was marginally significant with a one-tailed test at alpha = 0.10 (p = 0.085, g2 = 0.05), while the between-subject effect of instruction method was not significant. [→ Luo 2016](#luo-2016)

## Evidence

### Luo 2016

Luo, W., Pelletier, J., Duffin, K., Ormand, C., Hung, W., Shernoff, D., Zhai, X., Iverson, E., Whalley, K., Gallaher, C., and Furness, W. (2016). Advantages of Computer Simulation in Enhancing Students' Learning About Landform Evolution: A Case Study Using the Grand Canyon. Journal of Geoscience Education 64, 60-73. https://doi.org/10.5408/15-080.1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Two-way mixed-design ANOVA on pretest-posttest scores of 43 students (20 simulation, 23 paper), with alpha set at 0.10 due to small sample. The article reports the interaction "marginally signiﬁcant" with p = 0.085 and g2 = 0.05 under a one-tailed test.

> "With a one-tailed test, the interaction is marginally signiﬁcant (at a = 0.10), with p = 0.085 and a moderate effect size ( g2 = 0.05) (Cohen, 1988). Relative to the paper group, the simulation group showed moderately greater growth from pretest to posttest"

### Luo 2016 (2)

Luo, W., Pelletier, J., Duffin, K., Ormand, C., Hung, W., Shernoff, D., Zhai, X., Iverson, E., Whalley, K., Gallaher, C., and Furness, W. (2016). Advantages of Computer Simulation in Enhancing Students' Learning About Landform Evolution: A Case Study Using the Grand Canyon. Journal of Geoscience Education 64, 60-73. https://doi.org/10.5408/15-080.1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same ANOVA: the between-subjects instruction effect was not significant, F(1, 41) = 0.04, p = 0.95, so combined mean scores did not differ significantly between the simulation and paper groups.

> "The between-subject effect of intervention method was not signiﬁcant [ F(1, 41) = 0.04, p = 0.95]; mean test scores (combined across pretest and posttest) were not signiﬁcantly different between the two groups."
