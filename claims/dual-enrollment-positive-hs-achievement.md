# Dual enrollment programs have positive effects on general academic achievement (high school), with an average improvement index of +7 percentile points

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Two RCTs found statistically significant positive mean effects across multiple high school achievement measures, though not every individual measure was statistically significant. [→ What Works Clearinghouse 2017](#what-works-clearinghouse-2017)

## Evidence

### What Works Clearinghouse 2017

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2017). Dual Enrollment Programs. WWC Intervention Report, Transition to College. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/671

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r1`

Systematic review synthesis of two RCTs including 2,817 students. Table 1 reports an average improvement index of +7 percentile points (range +3 to +13); no standardized effect size is printed. Berger et al. found significant effects on English language arts scores; "The other two outcomes in this domain were not statistically significant."

> "Thus, for the general academic achievement (high school) domain, two studies showed statistically significant positive effects. Dual enrollment programs have a positive effect with a medium to large extent of evidence on general academic achievement (high school)."
