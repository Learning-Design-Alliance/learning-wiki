# Instructor, AI and student code examples differ significantly in their use of C keywords, with AI mirroring exemplars and students showing greater syntactic variety

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The distribution of reserved C keyword occurrences differs significantly across instructor, AI and student groups. [→ Paul Denny 2023](#paul-denny-2023)
`q3 i?` Specific keyword behaviours differ: AI rarely uses 'double' and uses 'while' and 'return' more than humans, while students alone used many additional keywords such as 'float', 'do' and 'case'. [→ Paul Denny 2023 (2)](#paul-denny-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Paul Denny 2023

Paul Denny, Hassan Khosravi, Arto Hellas, Juho Leinonen, Sami Sarsa. (2023). Can We Trust AI-Generated Educational Content? Comparative Analysis of Human and AI-Generated Learning Resources. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2306.10509](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2306.10509)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Chi-squared test of association on raw keyword occurrences across the three groups (instructor exemplars, AI-generated, student-generated resources) was statistically significant at p < .001.

> "The difference in the distributions between all three groups (based on the raw number of keyword occurrences) is statistically significant using a Chi-squared test (p < .001)."

### Paul Denny 2023 (2)

Paul Denny, Hassan Khosravi, Arto Hellas, Juho Leinonen, Sami Sarsa. (2023). Can We Trust AI-Generated Educational Content? Comparative Analysis of Human and AI-Generated Learning Resources. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2306.10509](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2306.10509)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Per-keyword comparison of occurrences per 1000 characters showed distinct group behaviours; the article also notes only students used keywords including 'float', 'do', 'case', 'continue' and others, indicating greater syntactic variety in student resources.

> "code examples produced by the AI very rarely use the keyword 'double' compared to examples produced by instructors and students, with students using 'double' the most. The AI uses 'while' frequently compared to humans: four times more than students, and slightly under twice as often as instructors."
