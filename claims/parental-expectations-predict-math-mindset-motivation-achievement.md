## Subclaims
`q3 i1` At the within-class (student) level, parental expectations negatively predicted students' fixed mindset and test anxiety and positively predicted students' self-efficacy and math achievement. [→ Lee 2025](#lee-2025)

## Evidence

### Lee 2025

Lee, H. J., Kim, S., & Bong, M. (2025). Social antecedents of children's mindsets, motivation and achievement in math: Investigating parental beliefs and perceived teacher beliefs. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 95, 849–870. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12764

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel path analysis of baseline survey and achievement data from 507 third- and fourth-graders in 28 Korean classrooms. At the student level, "parental expectations negatively predicted students' fixed mindset" and test anxiety, and positively predicted self-efficacy and achievement, with standardized βs from −.11 to .28.

> "parental expectations negatively predicted students' fixed mindset (β = −.11, p = .018) and test anxiety (β = −.10, p = .025) and positively predicted students' self‐efficacy (β = .15, p < .001) and achievement in math (β = .28, p < .001)."
