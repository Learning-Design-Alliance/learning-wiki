## Evidence

### L. D. Carr and S. B. McKagan 2008

L. D. Carr and S. B. McKagan. (2008). Graduate quantum mechanics reform. https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.2628 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0806.2628](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0806.2628)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Pre/post administration of the 12 common QMCS questions across the three-year longitudinal study at CSM. Average score was "82% ± 3% both pre and post"; post-test scores for years 1–3 were 78% ± 6%, 91% ± 2%, and 76% ± 6%, none statistically significant.

> "The average gain was not statistically diﬀerent from zero for any of the years. As can be seen in Fig. 1, nega- tive, positive, and zero gains were about equally common among individual students, and most students (70%) had one point or less diﬀerence between the pre-test and the post-test."
