# Most states set meaningful exit criteria for Priority and Focus schools, but some use signals schools cannot target, and the Department of Education required no course of action for schools that keep missing exit criteria

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Some states, including Arkansas, Connecticut, and Washington, use their achievement goals as exit criteria, giving schools clear signals of expected improvement. [→ Daria Hall 2013](#daria-hall-2013)
`q2 i?` The U.S. Department of Education did not require states to articulate a course of action for schools that miss exit criteria year after year, and in several states missing criteria brings only more improvement planning. [→ Daria Hall 2013 (2)](#daria-hall-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Daria Hall 2013

Daria Hall. (2013). A Step Forward or a Step Back? State Accountability in the Waiver Era. The Education Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED543222

`q2 · i?` · `review · r1`

The report's review of state exit criteria finds these states require schools to "meet all of their goals for multiple years in order to get out of Priority status," creating coherence between goals and exit signals.

> "Some states, including Arkansas, Connecticut, and Washington, have built coherence into their systems — and are providing Priority and Focus schools with very clear signals of what the expectations are — by using their reading and math achievement goals as exit criteria."

### Daria Hall 2013 (2)

Daria Hall. (2013). A Step Forward or a Step Back? State Accountability in the Waiver Era. The Education Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED543222

`q2 · i?` · `review · r1`

The report finds the federal waiver guidelines set no requirement for consequences after repeated failure to meet exit criteria; a number of states respond only with more improvement planning, though Michigan, Tennessee, and Colorado set stronger consequences.

> "Despite this well-documented tendency, the U.S. Department of Education didn't require states receiving waivers to articulate a course of action for schools that miss exit criteria year after year."
