## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a 4th-grade study, dyads designing machines and user guides for real users showed greatly improved quality of mathematical explanations at User Testing 2 compared to User Testing 1. [→ Sengupta 2018](#sengupta-2018)

## Evidence

### Sengupta 2018

Sengupta, P., Dickes, A., & Farris, A.V. (2018). Toward a Phenomenology of Computational Thinking in STEM Education. In Computational Thinking in STEM Discipline: Foundations and Research Highlights (Khine, M.S., Ed.). Springer. [doi:10.1007/978-3-319-93566-9_4](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-93566-9_4)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Design study (Sengupta et al., 2015b) in a 90% free-lunch charter school: dyads built ViMAP programs plus Lego control interfaces, tested twice by graduate students serving as users. Explanations at UT2 "made explicit the mathematical relationships between algorithmic elements" and Turtle actions. No statistical test printed.

> "compared to UT1, attending to what the user needs to know resulted in improving greatly the quality of students’ mathematical explanations during UT2"
