# In the nested solenoids episode, students cycled among conceptual physics, algorithmic physics, and algorithmic math, with shorter conceptual-physics visits (64 s average) than algorithmic ones, while the instructor stayed entirely in conceptual physics

## Evidence

### Deepa N. Chari 2019

Deepa N. Chari, Hai D. Nguyen, Dean A. Zollman, and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2019). Student and instructor framing in upper-division physics. [doi:10.1119/1.5120392](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.5120392)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative timeline analysis (Figure 5) of one group's (Matt, Abbey, Jessy, and Chen) problem solving on the nested solenoids induced-EMF problem, coded segment by segment. The episode shows frame shifting triggered both by student questions (Jessy quoting the problem statement) and by the instructor's conceptual explanation about flux created by the outer solenoid.

> "Though they return to conceptual physics four times (marks 3, 5, 7, 9), they only spend an average of 64 seconds each time there. In contrast, they enter the algorithmic physics (AP) frame once, for 111 seconds, and the algorithmic math (AM) frame twice, for an average of 107 seconds each time."
