# External empathy ratings do not differ significantly between students who trained with an incognito simulated patient and those who trained with a real patient

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` The IP-group and RP-group did not differ significantly on the CARE scale (p = 0.45; Cohen's d: 0.25), indicating that believing the patient to be real produced ratings similar to encountering a real patient. [→ Britz Vanessa 2024](#britz-vanessa-2024)

## Evidence

### Britz Vanessa 2024

Britz Vanessa, Sterz Jasmina, Koch Yannik, Schreckenbach Teresa, Stefanescu Maria-Christina, Zinßer Uwe, Verboket Rene Danilo, Sommer Katharina, Ruesseler Miriam. (2024). Impact of simulated patient-based communication training vs. real patient-based communication training on empathetic behaviour in undergraduate students – a prospective evaluation study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05801-8

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r1`

External CARE assessment comparison between the incognito-patient group and the real-patient group in the prospective three-group evaluation (Table 2: IP mean 2.33, RP mean 2.14). The article reports "no significant difference" with "Cohen's d: 0.25"; equivalence was not tested.

> "There was no significant difference between IP-group and RP-group (p = 0.45; Cohen's d: 0.25). Accordingly, students who trained with real patients (or who believed the patients to be real) were rated as less emphathetic (i.e. they had a significantly higher score on the CARE scale)."
