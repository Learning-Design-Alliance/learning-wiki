Evidence¶

Darling-Hammond 2018¶

Darling-Hammond, L., & Cook-Harvey, C. M. (2018). Educating the whole child: Improving school climate to support student success. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/educating-whole-child

q2 · i? · review · r1

The report's synthesis of adversity science states that "chronic stress can have a negative effect on the chemical and physical structures of a child’s brain," citing impacts on attention, concentration, memory, and creativity, and links ACEs to absenteeism and changed school performance.

"Further, “chronic stress can have a negative effect on the chemical and physical structures of a child’s brain, causing trouble with attention, concentration, memory, and creativity.”"

Darling-Hammond 2018 (2)¶

Darling-Hammond, L., & Cook-Harvey, C. M. (2018). Educating the whole child: Improving school climate to support student success. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/educating-whole-child

q2 · i? · review · r1

The report presents the Center on the Developing Child at Harvard University's identified set of actions schools, families, and communities can take to make positive outcomes more likely in the face of significant adversity, including "facilitating supportive adult-child relationships that extend over time."

"These include facilitating supportive adult-child relationships that extend over time; building a sense of self-efficacy and control by teaching and reinforcing social and emotional skills that help children handle adversity, such as the ability to calm emotions and manage responses; and creating strong, dependable, supportive routines for both managing classrooms and checking in on student needs."
