# Across basic and extended RI-CLPMs and CLPMs of five-wave secondary-school data, math self-concept and achievement show consistent reciprocal (bidirectional) directional ordering

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 associational `r2`–`r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Support for the reciprocal effects model was consistent across all basic and extended RI-CLPMs and CLPMs relating math self-concept, school grades, and test scores over five secondary-school years. [→ Herbert W. Marsh 2022](#herbert-w-marsh-2022)

`q2 i?` Across a taxonomy of models with varying lag0, lag1, lag2, random-intercept and covariate features, reciprocal effects of academic self-concept and achievement on each other were supported, described as medium or large. [→ Marsh 2024](#marsh-2024)

## Evidence

### Herbert W. Marsh 2022

Herbert W. Marsh, Reinhard Pekrun, & Oliver Lüdtke. (2022). Directional Ordering of Self-Concept, School Grades, and Standardized Tests Over Five Years: New Tripartite Models Juxtaposing Within- and Between-Person Perspectives. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-022-09662-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Secondary analysis of the PALMA longitudinal study (N = 3,425; Grades 5–9) juxtaposing CLPMs and RI-CLPMs in tripartite models of math self-concept, grades, and test scores. The article reports "consistent support for the REM bidirectional-ordering hypothesis" across all models.

> "Across all basic and extended RI-CLPMs and CLPMs, there was consistent support for the REM bidirectional-ordering hypothesis that self-concept and achievement are each a cause and an effect of the other."

### Marsh 2024

Marsh, H. W., Guo, J., Pekrun, R., Lüdtke, O., & Núñez-Regueiro, F. (2024). Cracking Chicken-Egg Conundrums: Juxtaposing Contemporaneous and Lagged Reciprocal Effects Models of Academic Self-Concept and Achievement's Directional Ordering. Educational Psychology Review, 36. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09887-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Reanalysis of the PALMA longitudinal study (five annual waves of mathematics data, Years 5-9, Bavarian secondary schools; N = 3370 in the Method section) using new SEMs in Mplus. The article reports that its model taxonomy "consistently supported a priori reciprocal effect model (REM) predictions" with medium or large reciprocal effects; no numeric effect size is printed in the available text.

> "consistently supported a priori reciprocal effect model (REM) predictions—medium or large reciprocal effects of ASC and achievement on each other."
