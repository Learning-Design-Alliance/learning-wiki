## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Sutiarso 2018

Sutiarso, S., Coesamin, M., & Nurhanurawati. (2018). The effect of various media scaffolding on increasing understanding of students' geometry concepts. Journal on Mathematics Education, 9(1), 95-102. https://ejournal.unsri.ac.id/index.php/jme

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental pretest-posttest control group study with 20 students per class. The scaffolding class scored g = 0.65 ("Effective Enough") versus g = 0.14 ("Less Effective") on Hake's Normalized Gain; pretest means were 35.90 and 34.29.

> "Based on Table 3, it appears that increasing understanding of the concept of geometry students in the class with media scaffolding is higher than the class without scaffolding media."

### Sutiarso 2018 (2)

Sutiarso, S., Coesamin, M., & Nurhanurawati. (2018). The effect of various media scaffolding on increasing understanding of students' geometry concepts. Journal on Mathematics Education, 9(1), 95-102. https://ejournal.unsri.ac.id/index.php/jme

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

After a Kolmogorov-Smirnov normality check on gain values, an independent t-test on the gain values gave t = -8.620, df = 38, sig. .000, so the authors conclude a significant difference between the scaffolding and non-scaffolding classes.

> "Based on t test in Table 5 above, because asymp. sig. 0.000 < 0.05 then it can be concluded that there is a significant difference on increasing Understanding of Geometry Concepts Students between classes with scaffolding media than without scaffolding."
