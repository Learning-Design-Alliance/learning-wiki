## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Ulsperger 2026

Ulsperger, A., Pfannenschwarz, L., Schetla, C., Poletilo, K., Strecker, L., Egner, M., & Kimmerle, J. (2026). Authors' academic but not personal expertise or gender affect message credibility in science communication. Scientific Reports, 16, 18000. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-55148-x

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Preregistered online experiment (Study 3, n = 206) with a 2 × 2 design independently varying academic and personal expertise. The linear model (F(3, 202) = 3.65, p = 0.013, R² = 0.05) showed academic expertise as "a significant positive predictor" (β = 0.52) while personal expertise was not.

> "Academic expertise was a significant positive predictor of perceived message credibility (β = 0.52, p = 0.002), whereas personal expertise (β = -0.12, p = 0.459) and the interaction (β = 0.21, p = 0.524) were not significant. This supports H3, while H4 could not be supported and ERQ4 was negated."
