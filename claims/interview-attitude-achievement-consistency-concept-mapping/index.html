Evidence¶

Luchembe et al. 2014¶

Dennis Luchembe, Kaumba Chinyama, Jack Jumbe. (2014). The Effect of Using Concept Mapping on Student's Attitude and Achievement When Learning the Physics Topic of Circular and Rotational Motion. European J of Physics Education, Volume 5 Issue 4. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1068159

q1 · i? · causal · r2

Semi-structured interviews with six purposively selected experimental-group students (two higher, two medium, two lower achievers), analysed under three themes: interest, understanding, and usefulness of concept mapping. The study found that "students whose post test scores improved or remained the same had showed interest in the use of concept maps" while uninterested students performed poorly.

"The interview revealed that students whose post test scores improved or remained the same had showed interest in the use of concept maps."

Luchembe et al. 2014 (2)¶

Dennis Luchembe, Kaumba Chinyama, Jack Jumbe. (2014). The Effect of Using Concept Mapping on Student's Attitude and Achievement When Learning the Physics Topic of Circular and Rotational Motion. European J of Physics Education, Volume 5 Issue 4. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1068159

q1 · i? · causal · r2

Within the same interview analysis, the authors note that "Despite all the interviewee stating that they find concept mapping useful, this did not translate into higher achievement in the test", citing interviewee 4 whose score fell from 60% to 58%. They cite Harris and Brown (2010) that questionnaire-interview consistency is generally weak.
