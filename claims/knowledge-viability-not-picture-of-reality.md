# Knowledge is not a picture of the real world; its adequacy is tested for logical consistency and experiential validity

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### von Glasersfeld 1995

von Glasersfeld, E. (1995). Radical Constructivism: A Way of Knowing and Learning. Falmer Press. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED381352

`q1 · i1`

Theoretical argument in Chapter 3, where the author interprets Piaget's statement that knowledge is not a picture of the real world. He argues the "meaning or value of knowledge lies in its function" and adequacy is tested for consistency and experiential validity. No empirical data are offered; this is a philosophical interpretation.

> "he is in fact saying that the meaning or value of knowledge lies in its function; and when he says that the adequacy of knowledge must be evaluated by logicians or scientists, he is simply explaining that it must be tested for logical consistency (non-contradictoriness) and for experiential validity (e.g., in experiments)."
