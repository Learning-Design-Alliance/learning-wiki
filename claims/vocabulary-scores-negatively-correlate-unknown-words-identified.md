## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Nelson-Denny vocabulary scores correlated negatively with the number of unknown words identified (r = -.53, p < .001): students with larger vocabularies identified fewer unknown words. [→ Craigo L. 2017](#craigo-l-2017)

## Evidence

### Craigo L. 2017

Craigo L., Ehri, L. C., & Hart, M. (2017). Teaching community college students strategies for learning unknown words as they read expository text. Higher Learning Research Communications, 7(1). https://doi.org/10.18870/hlrc.v7i1.350

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA on unknown words identified across the four passages (26 maximum), with Nelson-Denny vocabulary as covariate, in the randomized experiment; the printed correlation was "r = -.53, p < .001", and the covariate was significantly related to scores (p = .003).

> "The correlation between Nelson -Denny vocabulary s cores and the number of unknown words identified was r = -.53, p < .001, indicating that students with larger vocabularies identified fewer unknown words."

### Craigo L. 2017 (2)

Craigo L., Ehri, L. C., & Hart, M. (2017). Teaching community college students strategies for learning unknown words as they read expository text. Higher Learning Research Communications, 7(1). https://doi.org/10.18870/hlrc.v7i1.350

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive analysis of words students identified as unknown after first readings; no effect size is printed for this proportion, and the article suggests students may need training to become aware of words whose meanings are unfamiliar.

> "The fact that students identified as unfamiliar no more than 40% of the words on average was an unexpected finding."
