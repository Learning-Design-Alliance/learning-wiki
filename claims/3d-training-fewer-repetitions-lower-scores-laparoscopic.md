# Novices training in 3D take fewer repetitions and achieve lower time-plus-error performance scores than novices training in 2D during proficiency-based laparoscopic training

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` 3D-trained groups took significantly fewer repetitions to reach proficiency than 2D-trained groups (η2 = .28). [→ Beattie 2021](#beattie-2021)
`q3 i3` 3D-trained groups achieved significantly lower total performance scores (time + error penalties) during training than 2D-trained groups (η2 = .27). [→ Beattie 2021 (2)](#beattie-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Beattie 2021

Beattie, K. L., Hill, A., Horswill, M. S., Grove, P. M., & Stevenson, A. R. L. (2021). Laparoscopic skills training: the effects of viewing mode (2D vs. 3D) on skill acquisition and transfer. Surgical Endoscopy, 35(8), 4332–4344. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-020-07923-8

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Training-phase analysis of a counterbalanced four-group experiment in which sixty novices trained on six 3-Dmed tasks to a proficiency criterion. The main effect of group on total repetitions was significant (F = 7.27, p < .001, η2 = .28), a large effect, with "2D > 3D" on all post hoc comparisons.

> "Further post hoc tests revealed that the 3D-trained groups (the 3D → 3D and 3D → 2D groups) took significantly fewer repetitions to reach proficiency than the 2D-trained groups (the 2D → 2D and 2D → 3D groups)"

### Beattie 2021 (2)

Beattie, K. L., Hill, A., Horswill, M. S., Grove, P. M., & Stevenson, A. R. L. (2021). Laparoscopic skills training: the effects of viewing mode (2D vs. 3D) on skill acquisition and transfer. Surgical Endoscopy, 35(8), 4332–4344. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-020-07923-8

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Same training-phase analysis, on total performance score (time + error penalties in seconds). Main effect of group significant (F = 6.99, p < .001, η2 = .27), a large effect; all 2D vs. 3D post hoc comparisons showed "2D > 3D" (adjusted p ≤ .016).

> "Further post hoc tests revealed that the 3D-trained groups (the 3D → 3D and 3D → 2D groups) performed significantly better during training (i.e. lower total performance scores) than the 2D-trained groups (the 2D → 2D and 2D → 3D groups)"
