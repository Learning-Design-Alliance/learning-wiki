# Everyday Mathematics® showed positive effects on TAAS math subtests (Concepts, Operations, Problem Solving) with effect sizes of 0.25 to 0.31 that were not statistically significant after clustering correction

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Supplemental TAAS subtest analyses showed effect sizes of 0.25 (Concepts), 0.26 (Operations), and 0.31 (Problem Solving), all favoring the intervention group, with WWC-computed p-values of .34, .32, and .25 after clustering correction. [→ waite-subtests](#waite-subtests)

## Evidence

### waite-subtests

U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, What Works Clearinghouse. (2015, November). Primary Mathematics intervention report: Everyday Mathematics®. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/166

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Supplemental findings from the same Waite (2000) quasi-experiment (18 schools, 3,436 students): unadjusted means favored the intervention on all three TAAS math subtests, with effect sizes 0.25, 0.26, and 0.31 and improvement indices +10, +10, and +12. WWC-computed p-values after clustering correction (.34, .32, .25) were not statistically significant; a multiple-comparisons correction did not change significance.

> "Waite (2000) also reported results on subtests of the TAAS (Concepts, Operations, and Problem Solving), which are presented in the supplemental findings. These subtest analyses do not factor into the intervention's rating of effectiveness."
