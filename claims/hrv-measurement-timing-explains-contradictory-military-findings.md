## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors propose that HRV measured just before an extreme stressor may reflect sympathetically driven (state) reactive HRV, whereas HRV measured long before a stressor may reflect parasympathetically driven resting (trait) HRV, explaining why some studies found low HRV predictive and others high HRV predictive. ## Evidence

### Kula Yosef 2025

Kula Yosef, Horosov Roy, Gidron Yori, Ekshtein Aya, Gordon Barak, Iversen Zev, Tehori Omer, Ben-Ari Oded. (2025). Pre-training heart rate variability as a predictor of Air Force Academy completion. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0327406

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion, offered to reconcile contradictory prior military findings: in three prospective studies low HRV just before intensive training predicted better performance, while in another study high HRV taken two months before predicted better performance. This is an explanatory account, not a tested result of the present study.

> "The measuring of HRV just before an extreme stressor may reflect a sympathetically driven (state) reactive HRV. In contrast, HRV measured long before a stressor may reflect parasympathetically driven resting (trait) HRV"
