## Evidence

### Klimas J. 2017

Klimas J., Small W., Ahamad K., Cullen W., Mead A., Rieb L., Wood E., McNeil R. (2017). Barriers and facilitators to implementing addiction medicine fellowships: a qualitative study with fellows, medical students, residents and preceptors. Addiction Science & Clinical Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-017-0086-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In the mentorship domain of the 26-participant interview study, the article reports "considerable overlap between mentors for the clinical rotations, research projects and fellowships", allowing participants to continue professional growth and move between educational programmes.

> "There was considerable overlap between mentors for the clinical rotations, research projects and fellowships that fostered development of working relationships between faculty and learners."

### Klimas J. 2017 (2)

Klimas J., Small W., Ahamad K., Cullen W., Mead A., Rieb L., Wood E., McNeil R. (2017). Barriers and facilitators to implementing addiction medicine fellowships: a qualitative study with fellows, medical students, residents and preceptors. Addiction Science & Clinical Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-017-0086-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

The analysis found learners felt better understood by physicians who "went through the medical school" and saw clinical mentors as role models; a faculty participant stated "I'm a better doctor because I'm a teacher at the fellowship". Some learners suggested mandatory monthly half-hour mentor meetings.

> "If the teacher was from the same medical discipline, learners perceived it as being especially helpful. Furthermore, non-physician mentors sometimes induced stress in learners by requesting too many updates."
