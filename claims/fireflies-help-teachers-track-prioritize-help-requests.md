# FireFlies helped teachers see which students still needed help and reach students who would otherwise lower their hands

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2`–`r3` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2020 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Request help kept students' help requests visible, so teachers could approach students whose raised hands would normally have tired and been lowered. [→ Nine Sellier and Pengcheng An 2020](#nine-sellier-and-pengcheng-an-2020)
`q2 i?` Students sometimes waited more than ten minutes for help and gave up waiting, which the lamps made visible to the teacher. [→ Nine Sellier and Pengcheng An 2020 (2)](#nine-sellier-and-pengcheng-an-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Nine Sellier and Pengcheng An 2020

Nine Sellier and Pengcheng An. (2020). How Peripheral Interactive Systems Can Support Teachers with Differentiated Instruction: Using FireFlies as a Probe. To appear in DIS '20, July 6–10, 2020, Eindhoven, Netherlands. https://doi.org/10.1145/3357236.3395497

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Classroom observation during individual exercise work: with request help, teachers could see which students still had a question after being busy for a while; P4 said "Normally, when the students put their hand up they will get tired after a while and lower it again".

> "I can see more [when using the FireFlies]. Normally, when the students put their hand up they will get tired after a while and lower it again."

### Nine Sellier and Pengcheng An 2020 (2)

Nine Sellier and Pengcheng An. (2020). How Peripheral Interactive Systems Can Support Teachers with Differentiated Instruction: Using FireFlies as a Probe. To appear in DIS '20, July 6–10, 2020, Eindhoven, Netherlands. https://doi.org/10.1145/3357236.3395497

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Observation of exercise sessions in which teachers walked around providing support: multiple students needed help at once and "would have to wait for more than ten minutes before they were able to receive help", sometimes chatting or switching assignments while waiting.

> "Sometimes, the students would have to wait for more than ten minutes before they were able to receive help."
