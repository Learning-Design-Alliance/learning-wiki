## Evidence

### Sebastian Maier 2026

Sebastian Maier, Kai Schwabe, Manuel Schneider, and Stefan Feuerriegel. (2026). Designing Against Deskilling: Metacognitive Feedback Reduces Cognitive Offloading to LLM Assistants. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20143

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Exploratory mixed-effects logistic regression on pairs of consecutive learning items, predicting offloading on item t+1 from offloading on item t, feedback, and their interaction. The article concludes feedback "disrupted the tendency to repeat answer offloading across consecutive items."

> "After offloading an item, participants offloaded the subsequent item in69.7%of the cases without metacognitive feedback and55.9%with metacognitive feedback. The interaction was significant ( OR= 0.54, 95% CI[ 0.30, 0.97],𝑧=− 2.05,𝑝=. 040)"

### Sebastian Maier 2026 (2)

Sebastian Maier, Kai Schwabe, Manuel Schneider, and Stefan Feuerriegel. (2026). Designing Against Deskilling: Metacognitive Feedback Reduces Cognitive Offloading to LLM Assistants. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20143

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Descriptive comparison of the metacognitive feedback and AI-only conditions in the same experiment, reported in Section 5.3: feedback did not change whether participants engaged with the assistant at all, only how often they offloaded once engaged.

> "Participants in the two conditions were similarly likely to use the assistant at least once (66.7% vs. 68.6%) and to request at least one complete answer (38.2% vs. 39.4%; Table 2)"
