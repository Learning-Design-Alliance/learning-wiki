# Low-VSA trainees reach performance levels similar to high-VSA trainees after structured VR training

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Training-group participants with pre-existing low VSA scores achieved performance levels similar to those with high VSA scores on the two VR simulator tasks after training. [→ Sommer GM 2021](#sommer-gm-2021)
`q3 i?` Low-VSA subgroup members significantly increased their VSA scores during training but did not reach the VSA scores of the high-VSA group. [→ Sommer GM 2021 (2)](#sommer-gm-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Sommer GM 2021

Sommer GM, Broschewitz J, Huppert S, Sommer CG, Jahn N, Jansen-Winkeln B, Gockel I, Hau HM. (2021). The role of virtual reality simulation in surgical training in the light of COVID-19 pandemic: Visual spatial ability as a predictor for improved surgical performance: a randomized trial. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000027844

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Median-split subgroup analysis of the training group (Group A high VSA ≥12 points, Group B low VSA ≤12 points) on the SOR and cholecystectomy dissection tasks. The low-VSA group reached similar day-9 scores on the basic task and on 2 of 4 complex-task parameters; no effect size is printed.

> "Interestingly, participants of Group B reached similar scores on day 9 after training in the basic task “SOR”, as well as in 2 of the 4 analyzed parameters of the complex CD task"

### Sommer GM 2021 (2)

Sommer GM, Broschewitz J, Huppert S, Sommer CG, Jahn N, Jansen-Winkeln B, Gockel I, Hau HM. (2021). The role of virtual reality simulation in surgical training in the light of COVID-19 pandemic: Visual spatial ability as a predictor for improved surgical performance: a randomized trial. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000027844

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

VSA learning-curve analysis of the median-split subgroups: the high-VSA group did not improve significantly, while the low-VSA group significantly increased VSA scores (P = .001) yet remained below Group A (P = .05). No effect size is printed.

> "The group A did not improve their abilities significantly during the 9-day VR curriculum training whereas group B members significantly increased their abilities in VSA scores after simulator training (P = .001), but did not reach the VSA scores of Group A (P = .05)"
