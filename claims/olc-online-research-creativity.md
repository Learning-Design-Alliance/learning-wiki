## Subclaims
`q2 i?` OLC students showed higher levels of creativity, defined as novelty and elaboration, than face-to-face students. [→ Lovin 2014](#lovin-2014)

## Evidence

### Lovin 2014

Lovin, E., & Lambeth, D. T. (2014). Effects of information and communication technology on engagement and art production for eighth-grade students. li-manager's Journal of School Educational Technology, 10(3). https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Effects+of+Information+and+Communication+Technology+on+Engagement+and+Art+Production+for+Eighth-Grade+Students

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Field-note and project observation from the two 15-student classes; the article reports OLC students "produced work that was more novel and expressed a more distinct artistic voice," and only 4 of 15 face-to-face students consistently sought ideas from artists not presented in class.

> "Students in the OLC class were exposed to a wider variety of artists as they researched a theme, and as a result, students in the OLC class produced work that was more novel and expressed a more distinct artistic voice than students in the face-to-face class."
