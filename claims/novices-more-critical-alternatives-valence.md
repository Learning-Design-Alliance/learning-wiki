# Novice teachers overwhelmingly frame alternative teaching strategies as improvements to their own teaching, whereas expert teachers weigh alternatives as improvements and inferior options about equally often

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` 81.4% of novices' mentioned alternatives were framed as improvements to their teaching, versus 42.7% for experts, who framed 41.8% of alternatives as inferior to their own instruction. [→ Keller 2022](#keller-2022)

## Evidence

### Keller 2022

Keller, L., Cortina, K. S., Müller, K., & Miller, K. F. (2022). Noticing and weighing alternatives in the reflection of regular classroom teaching: Evidence of expertise using mobile eye-tracking. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-021-09570-5

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Loglinear analysis of 207 coded alternative-strategy clauses showed a significant Alternatives × Expertise association (χ2 = 39.1, df = 2, p < .001 in the crosstabulation). "Novice teachers mentioned more alternative teaching strategies with which they would improve their own teaching (81.4%)"; experts split roughly evenly between improvement and inferior framings. No standardized effect size was printed.

> "Novice teachers mentioned more alternative teaching strategies with which they would improve their own teaching (81.4%), whereas expert teachers mentioned alternative teaching strategies with which they would improve their own teaching (42.7%) and which they consider inferior to their own instruction (41.8%) with about the same frequency"
