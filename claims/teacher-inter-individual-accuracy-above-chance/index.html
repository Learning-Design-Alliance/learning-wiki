Evidence¶

Jan A. A. Engelen 2018¶

Jan A. A. Engelen, Gino Camp, Janneke van de Pol, Anique B. H. de Bruin. (2018). Teachers' monitoring of students' text comprehension: can students' keywords and summaries improve teachers' judgment accuracy? Metacognition and Learning 13:287–307. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-018-9187-4

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Experiment 1 supplementary analysis (footnote): Pearson correlations between each teacher's average T-JOL for a student and the student's average test performance (condition means .52, .64, .43). The grand mean was ".528, 95% CI [.377 .679]", above chance, with no cue-prompt effect, F(2, 24) = 1.288, p = .294.

"The grand mean of the model was .528, 95% CI [.377 .679], suggesting that overall, teachers' inter-individual judgments were more accurate than chance. The effect of cue-prompt was not significant,F(2, 24) = 1.288, p = .294, suggesting that inter-individual judgment accuracy did not differ between cue-prompt conditions."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • The quality of sixth-graders' keywords is not diagnostic of intra-individual variation in test performance (Experiment 1).
  • Teachers' intra-individual monitoring accuracy of students' text-by-text learning is not better than chance and is not improved by students' keywords or summaries (Experiment 1).
  • Sixth-graders' own monitoring accuracy is low and does not differ between no-cue, keyword, and summary cue-prompt conditions (Experiment 1). — related
  • Teachers show stronger regulation than students: both select lower-JOL texts for restudy, but the JOL–restudy correlation is significantly stronger for teachers (Experiment 1). — related
