# Mastery goals predict recognition of a disagreeing partner's competence after a real sociocognitive conflict

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Among high-school students in a real computer-mediated sociocognitive conflict, mastery goals significantly predicted reported partner competence (η² = .06), while performance goals did not. [→ Darnon 2006](#darnon-2006)

## Evidence

### Darnon 2006

Darnon, C., Muller, D., Schrager, S. M., Pannuzzo, N., & Butera, F. (2006). Mastery and Performance Goals Predict Epistemic and Relational Conflict Regulation. Journal of Educational Psychology, 98(4), 766–776. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.98.4.766

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Study 2 regression (63 tenth-graders in a computer-mediated cooperative task with prerecorded disagreeing answers) showed "mastery goals sig- nificantly predicted reports of the other's competence" (B = 0.38, η² = .06); performance goals and the interaction were non-significant.

> "As expected, mastery goals sig- nificantly predicted reports of the other's competence ( B /H110050.38), t(55) /H110052.26, p /H11021.03 (/H92572 /H11005.06). The more participants endorsed mastery goals, the more they thought their partner was competent."
