### Rachele Toniolo 2026

Rachele Toniolo, Anita Zanella, Andrea Cottinelli, Giovanni Liuzzi, Sara Ricciardi, Massimo Grassi, Stefano Delle Monache. (2026). AMACA: Astronomy education with a Multi-sensory, Accessible, and Circular Approach. International Journal of Science Education, Part B: Communication and Public Engagement. https://doi.org/10.1080/21548455.2025.2594048

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Qualitative comparison of first and second AMACA Design Canvas versions across project editions, concerning content, message and target audience. The authors relate the change to micro-learning methodology of presenting one single topic at a time.

> "In the first design, the statements were vague and encompassed vast astronomical topics, while more specific topics were reported after the trial with primary-school students."

### Rachele Toniolo 2026 (2)

Rachele Toniolo, Anita Zanella, Andrea Cottinelli, Giovanni Liuzzi, Sara Ricciardi, Massimo Grassi, Stefano Delle Monache. (2026). AMACA: Astronomy education with a Multi-sensory, Accessible, and Circular Approach. International Journal of Science Education, Part B: Communication and Public Engagement. https://doi.org/10.1080/21548455.2025.2594048

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Feedback Wheel debrief data from child testers of the activity "The many looks of a galaxy"; the target audience moved from primary to high-school students to school students older than 8 years old. Message changes included the words scientists and collaboration appearing more often.

> "Indeed, in the Feedback Wheel, two children reported some issues about "drawing and cutting out" the materials, leading to the introduction of a lower limit on the age of participants in the second AMACA Design Canvas."
