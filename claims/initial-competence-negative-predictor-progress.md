# Initial language competence negatively predicted language progress on both the OPT and the self-assessment scale

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

> **Currency** · newest study 2010 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Higher initial OPT competence had a statistically significant negative effect on OPT-measured progress, predicting eighteen per cent of the difference in OPT scores. [→ Jurkovič 2010](#jurkovic-2010)
`q2 i?` Initial self-assessed competence also had a statistically significant negative effect on self-assessed progress. [→ Jurkovič 2010 (2)](#jurkovic-2010-2)

## Evidence

### Jurkovič 2010

Jurkovič, V. (2010). Effect of Explicit Language Learning Strategy Instruction on Language-test and Self-assessment Scores. English Language Teaching, 3(1). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v3n1p16

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Regression analysis (Table 2, N=77) found initial OPT score a significant negative predictor (beta -0.42, p < 0.01; R2 = 0.18, F = 7.89, p < 0.01). The article calls the negative direction surprising.

> "initial language competence as measured by the OPT had a statistically significant negative effect on language progress as measured by the OPT. In fact, eighteen per cent of the difference in OPT scores could be predicted through initial language competence."

### Jurkovič 2010 (2)

Jurkovič, V. (2010). Effect of Explicit Language Learning Strategy Instruction on Language-test and Self-assessment Scores. English Language Teaching, 3(1). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v3n1p16

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Second regression model (Table 4, N=77) found initial self-assessment score a significant negative predictor of self-assessed progress (beta -0.29; R2 = 0.09, F = 3.44, p < 0.01).

> "initial language competence as measured by the self-assessment global rating scale had a statistically significant negative effect on language progress."
