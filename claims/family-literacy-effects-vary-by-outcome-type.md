# In group design studies, effects of family-implemented literacy interventions vary by outcome type: meaning-focused outcomes (g = 0.41) show larger effects than code-focused outcomes (g = 0.28)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` For group design studies, the mean effect for meaning-focused outcomes (vocabulary, listening comprehension, reading comprehension) was g = 0.41, larger than the mean effect for code-focused outcomes (PA, decoding/word reading, spelling, text reading) of g = 0.28, both significant. [→ Dahl-Leonard 2025](#dahl-leonard-2025)

## Evidence

### Dahl-Leonard 2025

Dahl-Leonard, K., Hall, C., Cho, E., Capin, P., Roberts, G. J., Kehoe, K. F., Haring, C., Peacott, D., & Demchak, A. (2025). Examining the Effects of Family-Implemented Literacy Interventions for School-Aged Children: A Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-09985-3

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Follow-up moderator-style analysis by outcome type within the group design meta-analysis (61 effect sizes). The article reports "g = 0.28" for code-focused and "g = 0.41" for meaning-focused outcomes, both p < 0.01. No formal moderator significance test was conducted; the authors note they did not consider moderator analyses given the small corpus.

> "the mean effect for code-focused outcomes (i.e., PA, decoding/word reading, spelling, text reading) was g = 0.28 (p < 0.01; k = 37; m = 9) and the mean effect for meaning-focused outcomes (i.e., vocabulary, listening comprehension, reading comprehension) was g = 0.41 (p < 0.01; k = 24; m = 11)."
