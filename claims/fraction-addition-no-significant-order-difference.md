## Subclaims
`q2 i?` SimStudent's step scores in fraction addition did not differ significantly between interleaved- and blocked-ordering curricula (p = 0.50). [→ Nan Li 2013](#nan-li-2013)

## Evidence

### Nan Li 2013

Nan Li, William W. Cohen, Kenneth R. Koedinger. (2013). Problem Order Implications for Learning. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-013-0005-5

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Simulation results in the fraction addition domain: interleaved curricula yielded a step score of 0.995 (SD = 0.008) versus 0.993 (SD = 0.007) for blocked curricula; the article reports "no significant difference ( p = 0.50) between the two conditions".

> "In fraction addition, SimStudent got a step score of 0 .995,S D = 0.008 when trained with interleaved-ordering curricula, which is slightly higher than the step score SimStudent received (0 .993,S D = 0.007) when trained with blocked- ordering curricula."
