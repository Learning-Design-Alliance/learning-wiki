## Evidence

### Stevens Niall T. 2019

Stevens Niall T., Holmes Killian, Grainger Rachel J., Connolly Roisín, Prior Anna-Rose, Fitzpatrick Fidelma, O'Neill Eoghan, Boland Fiona, Pawlikowska Teresa, Humphreys Hilary. (2019). Can e-learning improve the performance of undergraduate medical students in Clinical Microbiology examinations? BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1843-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Observational engagement analysis of the 2017/2018 cohort across the 12-week semester, with engagement categorised in five bands. "a high of 227/309 students (73.5%) having ≥80% engagement with the online content".

> "the level of engagement varied from a low of 23/309 students (7.4%) having < 20% engagement to a high of 227/309 students (73.5%) having ≥80% engagement with the online content"

### Stevens Niall T. 2019 (2)

Stevens Niall T., Holmes Killian, Grainger Rachel J., Connolly Roisín, Prior Anna-Rose, Fitzpatrick Fidelma, O'Neill Eoghan, Boland Fiona, Pawlikowska Teresa, Humphreys Hilary. (2019). Can e-learning improve the performance of undergraduate medical students in Clinical Microbiology examinations? BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1843-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Access-count analysis (Table 2). "Students accessed online content related to healthcare-associated infections (HCAIs) most"; the pathogen index was used by 329/334 (98.5%) of registered students and the glossary least.

> "Students accessed online content related to healthcare-associated infections (HCAIs) most with the activity that covers core concepts related to this topic being accessed 71,737 times and the podcast on the same topic being accessed 12,340 times."
