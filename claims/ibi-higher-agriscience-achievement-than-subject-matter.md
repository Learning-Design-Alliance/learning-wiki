# Inquiry-based instruction produced higher content knowledge achievement than the subject matter approach in secondary agriscience classes on six of seven unit posttests

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After adjusting for pretest scores, students taught through inquiry-based instruction scored significantly higher on content knowledge posttests than students taught through the subject matter approach on six of seven instructional units. [→ Thoron 2011](#thoron-2011)
`q2 i?` The largest adjusted difference appeared on the sixth instructional unit, where the inquiry group's posttest advantage was statistically significant. [→ Thoron 2011 (2)](#thoron-2011-2)

## Evidence

### Thoron 2011

Thoron, A. C., & Myers, B. E. (2011). Effects of Inquiry–based Agriscience Instruction on Student Achievement. Journal of Agricultural Education, 52(4). https://doi.org/10.5032/jae.2011.04175

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental ANCOVA with student pretest as covariate, comparing intact inquiry-based (n = 170) and subject matter (n = 135) classes across seven units; the article reports this second-unit contrast as "statistically significant" with F(19,550) = 17.30, p ≤ .001, r2 = .20.

> "This difference in posttest scores was found to be statistically significant , F(19,550) = 17.30, p ≤ .001, r2 = .20."

### Thoron 2011 (2)

Thoron, A. C., & Myers, B. E. (2011). Effects of Inquiry–based Agriscience Instruction on Student Achievement. Journal of Agricultural Education, 52(4). https://doi.org/10.5032/jae.2011.04175

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same ANCOVA analysis for the sixth instructional unit: the inquiry-based group mean was 81.64 (SD = 10.32) versus the subject matter group mean of 60.00 (SD = 16.35), reported as "statistically significant" with F(41,219) = 230.72, p ≤ .001, r2 = .62.

> "This difference in posttest scores was found to be statistically significant, F(41,219) = 230.72, p ≤ .001, r2 = .62."
