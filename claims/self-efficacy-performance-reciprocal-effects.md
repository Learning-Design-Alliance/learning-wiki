## Evidence

### Talsma 2018

Talsma, K., Schüz, B., Schwarzer, R., & Norris, K. (2018). I believe, therefore I achieve (and vice versa): A meta-analytic cross-lagged panel analysis of self-efficacy and academic performance. Learning and Individual Differences, 61, 136-150. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2017.11.015

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analytic cross-lagged panel analysis of pooled correlations from k=11 two-wave longitudinal studies (N=2,688), estimated as a fully saturated model. Both paths were positive and significant, p<.001, with "the performance → self-efficacy effect was β=.205" and the reverse path β=.071.

> "Focusing on the cross-lagged paths, the performance → self-efficacy effect was β=.205, whereas the self-efficacy → performance effect was β=.071."

### Talsma 2018 (2)

Talsma, K., Schüz, B., Schwarzer, R., & Norris, K. (2018). I believe, therefore I achieve (and vice versa): A meta-analytic cross-lagged panel analysis of self-efficacy and academic performance. Learning and Individual Differences, 61, 136-150. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2017.11.015

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Chi-squared difference test comparing the constrained equal-paths model to the saturated model on the same pooled correlation matrix. The constrained model "provided a significantly poorer fit to the data, χ2 = 37.47, p<.0001", so the two directional effects differ in strength.

> "the constrained model provided a significantly poorer fit to the data, χ2 = 37.47, p<.0001; thus, the cross-lagged paths are significantly different from each other."
