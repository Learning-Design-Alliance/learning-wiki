# Sleep restriction lowers tonic arousal (raises EEG theta power) in a simulated classroom, with a medium effect

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Sleep-deprived adolescents tended to have higher theta power, indicating lower tonic arousal, than when healthy-slept; the difference was marginal (p=.08). [→ Beebe 2010](#beebe-2010)

## Evidence

### Beebe 2010

Beebe, D. W., Rose, D., & Amin, R. (2010). Attention, Learning, and Arousal of Experimentally Sleep-Restricted Adolescents in a Simulated Classroom. The Journal of Adolescent Health. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jadohealth.2010.03.005

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

EEG-monitored subset (n=8) of the counterbalanced sleep manipulation; tonic arousal was operationalized as C3/A2 theta (4–7 Hz) spectral power, log-transformed, with higher theta power reflecting lower arousal. Participants "tended to have higher theta power, t(7)=1.5, p=.08, d=.53".
