Evidence¶

Thakore 2024¶

Thakore, K., Das, T., Jahan, S., & Sweller, N. (2024). From Hands to Mind: How Gesture, Emotional Valence, and Individual Differences Impact Narrative Recall. Educational Psychology Review, 36, 111. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09948-0

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Linear mixed model on Study 1 free recall (N = 100) with VM as continuous predictor. The article reports "a significant interaction between gesture and VM, χ2(1) = 23.38, p < 0.001", with no gesture effect at −1 SD VM but spontaneous better at mean and +1 SD VM.

"However, there was a significant interaction between gesture and VM, χ2(1) = 23.38, p < 0.001 (see Fig. 1). For VM one standard deviation below the mean, gesture condition did not impact recall, χ2(1) = 1.41, p = 0.234."

Thakore 2024 (2)¶

Thakore, K., Das, T., Jahan, S., & Sweller, N. (2024). From Hands to Mind: How Gesture, Emotional Valence, and Individual Differences Impact Narrative Recall. Educational Psychology Review, 36, 111. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09948-0

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Study 1 specific recall model showed a significant gesture × VM interaction, χ2(1) = 4.23, p = 0.040; simple effects showed no condition difference at low or mean VM, and "participants in the spontaneous gesture condition had significantly better recall" at +1 SD VM.

"For those with VM one standard deviation above the mean, however, participants in the spontaneous gesture condition had significantly better recall than those in the instructed gesture condition, χ 2(1) = 6.39, p = 0.012."
