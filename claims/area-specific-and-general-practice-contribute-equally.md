# Area-of-law-specific and general exam practice contribute about equally and independently to performance development

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The marginal effects of practicing law exams in general and practicing exams in the specific area of law are about equally sized (test for differences between coefficients: p = 0.67). [→ Glöckner 2013](#glockner-2013)

## Evidence

### Glöckner 2013

Glöckner, A., Towfigh, E., & Traxler, C. (2013). Development of legal expertise. Instructional Science, 41, 989–1007. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-013-9266-5

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Regression analysis with fixed effects on 71,405 exams showing students "proﬁt about equally from area- of-law speciﬁc practice and from general practice". The authors note that "exams in different areas of law have only little (if any) overlap in legal content since they concern completely different legal codes," making it "quite remarkable that practicing exams outside the specific area of law has such a huge effect."

> "law students proﬁt about equally from area- of-law speciﬁc practice and from general practice of exams (test for differences between coefﬁcients: p = 0.67)."
