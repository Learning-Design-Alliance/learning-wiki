# Conventional Gibbs vector calculus hinders students' intuitive understanding of physics because vectors do not integrate with basic algebraic operations

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Conventional vectors lack a division operation, do not support the cross product in two dimensions, and cannot be freely added to scalars, reducing students' intuitive understanding of physics concepts. [→ James M. Chappell 2010](#james-m-chappell-2010)

## Evidence

### James M. Chappell 2010

James M. Chappell, Azhar Iqbal, Derek Abbott. (2010). A simplified approach to electromagnetism using geometric algebra. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1010.4947](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1010.4947)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The authors' theoretical argument in the introduction: conventional vectors form "a monolithic structure unto itself" because there is no division operation and vectors cannot be freely added to scalars. No empirical data on student understanding is presented.

> "Hence the intuitive understanding of physics concepts, as well as general geometric understanding, which depends on the understanding of vectors, is signiﬁcantly reduced."
