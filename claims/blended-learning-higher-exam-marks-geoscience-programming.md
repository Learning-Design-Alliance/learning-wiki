# Adopting a flipped/blended learning approach coincided with substantially higher mean exam marks than traditional passive lecturing in an introductory programming course for geoscience undergraduates

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Mean exam marks rose from 50.5% in 2010 under passive lecturing to 74.5% in 2013 under a flipped classroom approach, with the exam format and difficulty kept consistent across years. [→ Christian T. Jacobs 2016](#christian-t-jacobs-2016)

## Evidence

### Christian T. Jacobs 2016

Christian T. Jacobs, Gerard J. Gorman, Huw E. Rees, Lorraine Craig. (2016). Experiences with efficient methodologies for teaching computer programming to geoscientists. arXiv. [doi:10.5408/15-101.1](https://doi.org/10.5408/15-101.1)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Five-year observational comparison of anonymised exam marks across course iterations; the 2010 passive-lecture run had a "mean exam mark of 50.5%". In 2013, after the flipped change, "the mean course mark of 74.5% was considerably higher than previous years".

> "Indeed, this proved to be the case, with the 2010 mean exam mark of 50.5% reﬂecting this (see Figure 1)."
