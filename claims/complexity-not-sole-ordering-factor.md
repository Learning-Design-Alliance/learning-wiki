## Evidence

### Ungerer 2026

Ungerer, T., Rastle, K., & Armstrong, B. C. (2026). Bringing the Reading Sciences Into the Classroom: Insights for Phonics Instruction. Perspectives on Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916261438004

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports Vadasy and Sanders' (2021, Study 2) experiment with kindergartners who had little prior phonics knowledge: single-letter and two-letter GPCs were learned "equally well across a range of reading and spelling tasks."

> "Vadasy and Sanders (2021, Study 2) found that kindergartners (who had little prior phonics knowledge) were able to learn single-letter and two-letter GPCs equally well across a range of reading and spelling tasks."

### Ungerer 2026 (2)

Ungerer, T., Rastle, K., & Armstrong, B. C. (2026). Bringing the Reading Sciences Into the Classroom: Insights for Phonics Instruction. Perspectives on Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916261438004

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Roembke et al.'s (2020) finding that first-grade students learned GPCs sharing letters with previously taught GPCs better than GPCs containing only distinct letters, suggesting similarity to existing knowledge aids acquisition.

> "Roembke et al. found that first-grade students acquired such "overlapping" GPCs (i.e., that share letters) better than "nonoverlapping" GPCs (i.e., that contain only distinct letters)."
