## Evidence

### Garvin Brod and Jasmin Breitwieser 2019

Garvin Brod and Jasmin Breitwieser. (2019). Lighting the wick in the candle of learning: generating a prediction stimulates curiosity. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0056-y

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Pupillometry contrast in the 29-participant experiment, measured 1.5–4 s after anticipation onset; the article reports "d = 0.417" for the prediction-versus-example difference during anticipation.

> "the average pupillary response during the anticipation phase was greater in the prediction condition than in the example condition, t(28) = 2.24, p = 0.016, and d = 0.417"

### Garvin Brod and Jasmin Breitwieser 2019 (2)

Garvin Brod and Jasmin Breitwieser. (2019). Lighting the wick in the candle of learning: generating a prediction stimulates curiosity. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0056-y

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory ANOVA of the pupillary response to seeing the correct answer (0.5–2 s window, baseline at results onset) in the same 29-participant experiment; the main effect of condition had "ηp = 0.463". There was no main effect of curiosity.

> "A within-subjects ANOVA revealed a main effect of condition, F(1, 28) = 24.18, p < 0.001, and ηp = 0.463; pupil dilation was greater in the prediction condition than in the example condition, indicating that generating a prediction promotes surprise"
