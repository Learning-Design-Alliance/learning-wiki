## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Segmentation handles concepts too complex for parallel processing by breaking tasks into serial steps that do not exceed capacity. [→ Graeme S. Halford 1998](#graeme-s-halford-1998)

## Evidence

### Graeme S. Halford 1998

Graeme S. Halford, William H. Wilson, Steven Phillips. (1998). Processing capacity defined by relational complexity: Implications for comparative, developmental, and cognitive psychology. Behavioral and Brain Sciences 21, 803-865. https://www.psy.uq.edu.au/people/Department/gsh/index.html

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Definitional statement of the second capacity-reduction mechanism: tasks such as arithmetic algorithms, counting, and ordering are executed as serial steps that "do not exceed processing capacity and that are processed serially."

> ""Segmentation" is breaking tasks into steps that do not exceed processing capacity and that are processed serially."

### Graeme S. Halford 1998 (2)

Graeme S. Halford, William H. Wilson, Steven Phillips. (1998). Processing capacity defined by relational complexity: Implications for comparative, developmental, and cognitive psychology. Behavioral and Brain Sciences 21, 803-865. https://www.psy.uq.edu.au/people/Department/gsh/index.html

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument linking segmentation to strategy development: autonomous development of serial strategies requires planning based on representing relations; otherwise "a strategy cannot be developed without didactic help".

> "If processing limitations prevent the structure of the task being represented, then a strategy cannot be developed without didactic help, which can be available for only a small subset of the cognitive tasks we perform."
