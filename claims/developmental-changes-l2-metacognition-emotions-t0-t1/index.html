Evidence¶

Palladino 2025¶

Palladino, P., Trotta, E., Bonvino, A., Carlucci, L., & Cottini, M. (2025). How do you feel during English class? Emotions and metacognition in primary school children learning English as a second language. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09414-4

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Paired-samples t tests on 305 children assessed in November 2021 and May 2022. Table 2 reports vocabulary rising from M = 7.76 to M = 9.97, t(268) = ‒9.87, p < 0.001, Cohen's d = ‒0.60, alongside gains in relative monitoring accuracy (d = ‒0.44) and metacognitive control (composite d = ‒0.36).

"As assumed, children's English vocabulary scores were significantly greater at T1 compared to T0."

Palladino 2025 (2)¶

Palladino, P., Trotta, E., Bonvino, A., Carlucci, L., & Cottini, M. (2025). How do you feel during English class? Emotions and metacognition in primary school children learning English as a second language. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09414-4

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Paired t tests on the AEQ-ES emotion scales showed boredom rising from M = 1.51 to M = 2.14 (d = ‒0.41), while enjoyment (M = 3.81 to 3.70, p =.167) and anxiety (M = 2.12 to 2.11, p =.952) showed no significant change. This did not confirm the hypothesis that all achievement emotions would change.

"Finally, with respect to achievement emotions, only boredom significantly changed between the two administration points, increasing from T0 to T1, whereas enjoyment and anxiety did not reach the significance threshold."
