## Evidence

### McKagan 2010

McKagan, S. B., Perkins, K. K., & Wieman, C. E. (2010). The Design and Validation of the Quantum Mechanics Conceptual Survey. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.6.020121](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.6.020121)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Item statistics computed on posttest data from modern physics students, N=370, combining the five courses in which all Version 2.0 questions were given. Difficulty is percent correct per item (Table V).

> "The QMCS contains questions with wide range of diﬃculties, from 0.27 for the most diﬃcult question to 0.91 for the easiest question, with most questions falling in a range between 0.5 and 0.8."

### McKagan 2010 (2)

McKagan, S. B., Perkins, K. K., & Wieman, C. E. (2010). The Design and Validation of the Quantum Mechanics Conceptual Survey. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.6.020121](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.6.020121)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Test-level reliability analysis of the QMCS. The article reports "a Cronbach Alpha of 0.44", below the common 0.7 cutoff, and invokes Adams and Wieman's argument that multi-concept instruments may legitimately have low alpha.

> "However, Adams and Wieman argue that instruments designed to measure multiple concepts may have low Cronbach Alpha because these concepts may be independent.27 This is the case for the QMCS, which has a Cronbach Alpha of 0.44."

### McKagan 2010 (3)

McKagan, S. B., Perkins, K. K., & Wieman, C. E. (2010). The Design and Validation of the Quantum Mechanics Conceptual Survey. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.6.020121](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.6.020121)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Whole-test discrimination statistic for the QMCS: "Ferguson’s delta for the QMCS is 0.93", exceeding the commonly stated 0.9 threshold. Most items have discrimination above 0.4; questions 8, 10, and 11 are exceptions.

> "It is commonly stated that for a test to have good discrimination, Ferguson’s delta should be ≥ 0.9.52 Ferguson’s delta for the QMCS is 0.93."
