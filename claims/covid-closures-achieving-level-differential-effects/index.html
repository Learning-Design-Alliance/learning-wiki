Evidence¶

Hammerstein Svenja 2021¶

Hammerstein Svenja, König Christoph, Dreisörner Thomas, Frey Andreas. (2021). Effects of COVID-19-Related School Closures on Student Achievement-A Systematic Review. Frontiers in Psychology. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.746289

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Review synthesis of differential effects, reporting the subject-by-achievement-level contrast from the German change-score study (Schult et al., ~80,000 secondary students). Table 1 notes larger effects on reading in high-performing students and on mathematics in low-performing students; no effect size is printed for this contrast.

"Third, low-performing students were more affected by COVID-19-related school closures in mathematics, while high-performing students were more affected by COVID-19-related school closures in reading (Schult et al., 2021)."

Hammerstein Svenja 2021 (2)¶

Hammerstein Svenja, König Christoph, Dreisörner Thomas, Frey Andreas. (2021). Effects of COVID-19-Related School Closures on Student Achievement-A Systematic Review. Frontiers in Psychology. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.746289

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Review synthesis citing the Chinese DiD study (Clark et al.) and the German online mathematics software study (Spitzer and Musslick, >2,500 students, −2.43% error rate). The review explains this by low-performing students being less distracted at home and online programs adapting assignments individually; no effect size is printed for this differential.

"Finally, low-performing students benefited more from systematic online-learning methods (Clark et al., 2020; Spitzer and Musslick, 2021)."
