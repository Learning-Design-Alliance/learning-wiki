## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The College Algebra item alge026 (simplifying a quotient of products of powers) was mostly basic: in Fall 2010, 88% of incoming students completed it, passers passed 75% of the time versus a 71% ambient rate, and 57% of the 12% lacking it failed, with odds-ratio z-score 3.87 and similar 2009 statistics. `q2 i?` The piecewise-function evaluation item fun030 was advanced for Business Calculus: in 2008, the 24% of students holding it passed 94% of the time versus 80% overall, and it had large z-scores in several calculus courses and years. [→ Marc Harper 2013 (2)](#marc-harper-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Marc Harper 2013

Marc Harper, Alison Ahlgren Reddy. (2013). Detecting Concepts Crucial for Success in Mathematics Courses from Knowledge State-based Placement Data. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1312.1127](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1312.1127)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Worked item example from College Algebra placement data, Fall 2010, with the odds-ratio z-score of 3.87 reported and similar statistics in 2009; the authors classify it as a mostly basic item detecting very unprepared students.

> "In the Fall of 2010, 88% of the incoming students were able to complete this problem and passed 75% of the time (the ambient pass rate was 71%). Of the 12% of stud ents unable to complete this item, 57% failed the course."

### Marc Harper 2013 (2)

Marc Harper, Alison Ahlgren Reddy. (2013). Detecting Concepts Crucial for Success in Mathematics Courses from Knowledge State-based Placement Data. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1312.1127](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1312.1127)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Worked item example from Business Calculus, 2008: computing outputs of a piecewise-defined polynomial function. The authors classify it as an advanced item indicating well-prepared students, with similar statistics in 2009 and large z-scores in several calculus courses.

> "In Business Calculus in 2008, 24% of the students had this item in their knowledge s tate and passed the course 94% of the time versus 80% overall."
