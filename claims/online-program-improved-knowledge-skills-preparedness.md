# An 8-hour online educational program significantly improved emergency nurses' knowledge, skills, and personal preparedness, though scores remained moderate

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a quasi-experimental one-group pretest–posttest study of 50 registered emergency nurses, knowledge, skills, personal preparedness, and total DPET scores rose significantly from pretest to posttest after an 8-hour online program, but post-intervention scores reached only a moderate level. [→ Delbene Lara 2026](#delbene-lara-2026)

## Evidence

### Delbene Lara 2026

Delbene Lara, Mezini Sara, Cacciabue Camilla, Watson Roger, Aleo Giuseppe, Di Nitto Marco, Zanini Milko, Catania Gianluca, Sasso Loredana, Bagnasco Annamaria. (2026). Effective Educational Programs for Disaster Preparedness and Management: A Rapid Evidence Assessment. International Nursing Review. https://doi.org/10.1111/inr.70193

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental one-group pretest–posttest study (Al‐Qbelat et al. 2022, Jordan) of an 8-hour online educational program delivered over one week via Zoom, covering disaster preparedness concepts, START triage, decontamination and PPE, BLS/CPR, and advanced trauma support, in 50 registered emergency nurses. The review reports "t = 4.79" through "t = 9.56"; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Significant improvements pre–post intervention: Knowledge (t = 4.79, p ≤ 0.001), skills (t = 6.66, p ≤ 0.001), personal preparedness (t = 9.56, p ≤ 0.001), total DPET (t = 7.20, p ≤ 0.001). Scores increased but remained at moderate level post‐intervention."
