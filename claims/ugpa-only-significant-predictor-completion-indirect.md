# UGPA is the only tested admissions metric that significantly predicts PhD completion, and its effect is entirely indirect through graduate GPA (total effect 6.0% for a 25th-to-75th percentile change)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The counterfactual mediation analysis estimates UGPA's total effect on completion probability at 6.0% (p < 0.01), entirely attributable to the indirect effect through graduate GPA; the direct effect is not significant. [→ Mike Verostek 2021](#mike-verostek-2021)

## Evidence

### Mike Verostek 2021

Mike Verostek, Casey Miller and Benjamin Zwickl. (2021). Analyzing admissions metrics as predictors of graduate GPA and whether graduate GPA mediates PhD completion. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020115](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020115)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Counterfactual mediation analysis (potential outcomes framework) for a change from 3.46 to 3.90 UGPA (25th to 75th percentile), conducted with the mediation package in R on US students. Indirect effect estimated at 6.0%, significant; direct effect -0.1%, not significant.

> "We observe that the predicted total eﬀect of this change is to increase the student's overall probability of PhD com­pletion by 6.0% ( p < 0.01). That eﬀect is entirely at­tributable to the indirect eﬀect of UGPA on PhD com­pletion through graduate GPA, since the direct eﬀect is estimated to be -0.1% and is not statistically signiﬁcant (p = 0.97)"
