## Evidence

### Boyce Matthew R. 2018

Boyce Matthew R., Menya Diana, Turner Elizabeth L., Laktabai Jeremiah, Prudhomme-O'Meara Wendy. (2018). Evaluation of malaria rapid diagnostic test (RDT) use by community health workers: a longitudinal study in western Kenya. Malaria Journal. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12936-018-2358-6

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Interpretation assessment at 12-month follow-up in which each of 103 CHWs interpreted ten previously conducted RDT cassettes (positive, negative, invalid) against the research team's gold standard. The article reports sensitivity 92.0% and specificity 97.3%, with faint-positive cassettes most frequently misinterpreted.

> "The overall sensitivity at follow-up observations was 92.0% and the overall specificity was 97.3% (Table 5). The interpretation test also included one invalid test cassette, which all CHWs (n = 103) correctly interpreted. The cassettes most frequently misinterpreted were 2 with faint-positive results."
