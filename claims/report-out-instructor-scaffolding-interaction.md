# Report Out performance showed a significant instructor-by-scaffolding-level interaction, with Mrs. Lewis's students significantly outperforming Mr. Green's at Medium and Low material support but not at High

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A repeated-measures ANCOVA revealed a significant interaction between instructor and material scaffolding level on Report Out scores. [→ Martin 2019](#martin-2019)
`q2 i?` Mrs. Lewis's students performed significantly better than Mr. Green's students on Report Out activities at Medium and Low material support, but not at High support. [→ Martin 2019 (2)](#martin-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Martin 2019

Martin, Nicole D., Tissenbaum, Catherine Dornfeld, Gnesdilow, Dana, Puntambekar, Sadhana. (2019). Fading distributed scaffolds: the importance of complementarity between teacher and material scaffolds. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-018-9474-0

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Repeated-measures ANCOVA of coded Report Out scores (0–3 rubric, Cohen's kappa = .82) with teacher as between-subjects factor, scaffolding level as within-subjects factor, and both pre-tests as covariates, for n = 28 and n = 44 students across nine experiments.

> "Our analyses revealed a significant interaction between the instructor and the material scaffolding level (High, Medium, or Low Material Support) (F(2,208) = 8.923, p < .001)."

### Martin 2019 (2)

Martin, Nicole D., Tissenbaum, Catherine Dornfeld, Gnesdilow, Dana, Puntambekar, Sadhana. (2019). Fading distributed scaffolds: the importance of complementarity between teacher and material scaffolds. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-018-9474-0

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pairwise t-tests with Holm–Bonferroni correction following the significant interaction. Scores were not significantly different across teachers at High Material Support (t(66.84) = 1.92, p = .059); the teacher difference emerged only as material scaffolds faded.

> "Mrs. Lewis’s students performed significantly better than Mr. Green’s students on the Report Out activities in the experiments with Medium Material Support (t(54.37) = 2.81, p = .013) and Low Material Support (t(55.27) = 6.23, p < .001)."
