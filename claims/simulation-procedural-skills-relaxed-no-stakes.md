## Evidence

### Niyongombwa Irenee 2025

Niyongombwa Irenee, Sibomana Isaie, Wallace Lorraine S., Stein David. (2025). Exploring medical students’ simulation learning experience in Rwanda: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07981-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview finding from Theme 2 in the qualitative study of Rwandan medical students: the safe learning environment "allowed repeated practice", and students reported gaining knowledge, skills, and confidence from practicing procedures such as molar pregnancy and abortion management.

> "“The good thing with simulation is that you get the opportunity to practice…without the fear to harm anyone.” (Student 3)."

### Niyongombwa Irenee 2025 (2)

Niyongombwa Irenee, Sibomana Isaie, Wallace Lorraine S., Stein David. (2025). Exploring medical students’ simulation learning experience in Rwanda: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07981-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview finding from Theme 2, sub-theme 3: students reported simulation "enabled preparation for rarely encountered conditions" like shoulder dystocia, which they rarely see in referral hospitals where they train.

> "“The benefit is that we practice rare procedures…if I meet shoulder dystocia… I could do something about its management because we had a demonstration about that.” (Student 14)."
