# Clinically interviewed students recognize the Taylor series as a relevant physics tool but lack criteria for when to use it and how many terms to keep

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` All five clinically interviewed students used approximation and estimation language and could list areas of physics where Taylor series are useful, but none could state general conditions for when a Taylor series is appropriate or how to terminate one, except when a derivative is constant. ## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith 2011

Trevor I. Smith, John R. Thompson, Donald B. Mountcastle. (2011). Student Understanding of Taylor Series Expansions in Statistical Mechanics. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1112.4231](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1112.4231)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Clinical interviews with five students, several weeks after the Year 2 tutorial implementation, probing familiarity with Taylor series. All students spontaneously used terms like "approximation" and "estimation" and all spontaneously referred to the kinematic equation as an example of a Taylor series.

> "Students also did not have rigorous criteria for determining how many terms should be kept (except when one of the derivatives is a constant, resulting in all higher derivatives being identically zero)."
