# Divergent thinking shows an overall upward developmental trend across Grades 1–12, peaking in Grade 9, with significant drops in Grades 7, 10, and 11

## Evidence

### Said-Metwaly 2019

Said-Metwaly, S., Fernández-Castilla, B., Kyndt, E., Van den Noortgate, W., & Barbot, B. (2019). Does the Fourth-Grade Slump in Creativity Actually Exist? A Meta-Analysis of the Development of Divergent Thinking in School-Age Children and Adolescents. https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Does+the+Fourth-Grade+Slump+in+Creativity+Actually+Exist

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Three-level meta-analysis of overall DT across 41 studies (2,139 SMs, 40,918 subjects), reported in Results. The analysis found "DT was significantly higher at each successive grade level" with a peak in Grade 9 and significant drops in Grades 7, 10, and 11.

> "In essence, DT was significantly higher at each successive grade level, and reached its peak in Grade 9. No overall drop in Grade 4 was observed; rather, there was a plateau (i.e., non-significant difference between Grade 3 and Grade 4). Further, significant drops were obtained for Grades 7, 10, and 11."
