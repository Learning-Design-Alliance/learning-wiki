# Increased physical activity does not negatively affect academic performance, but academic performance is not caused solely by increased physical activity

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The analysis shows physical activity itself does not negatively affect academic performance, and changes in academic performance are not caused solely by increases in the frequency and/or duration of physical activity. [→ Sember Vedrana 2020](#sember-vedrana-2020)

## Evidence

### Sember Vedrana 2020

Sember Vedrana, Jurak Gregor, Kovač Marjeta, Morrison Shawnda A., Starc Gregor. (2020). Children's Physical Activity, Academic Performance, and Cognitive Functioning: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Frontiers in Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2020.00307

`q3 · i?`

Conclusion drawn from the random-effects meta-analysis of 44 interventions. The article states "PA itself does not negatively affect AP" and that intensity of PA must also be considered, since most studies did not report intensity.

> "The present analysis shows that PA itself does not negatively affect AP; moreover, there are positive, (although relatively small) relationships between the two, and that changes in AP itself is not caused solely by an increase in frequency and/or duration of PA"
