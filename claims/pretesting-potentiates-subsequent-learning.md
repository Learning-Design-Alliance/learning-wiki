## Evidence

### Brame CJ 2015

Brame CJ, Biel R. (2015). Test-Enhanced Learning: The Potential for Testing to Promote Greater Learning in Undergraduate Science Courses. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-11-0208

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Little and Bjork (2011), a laboratory experiment in which undergraduates read two texts, one preceded by a 10-question multiple-choice pretest, then took a cued-recall test after 5 min. The review suggests pretesting may cue students to focus on key ideas and common distracters.

> "A multiple-choice pretest improved performance on a final test, both for information that was included on the pretest and related information."

### Brame CJ 2015 (2)

Brame CJ, Biel R. (2015). Test-Enhanced Learning: The Potential for Testing to Promote Greater Learning in Undergraduate Science Courses. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-11-0208

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

The review reports, citing Wissman et al. (2011), a laboratory experiment in which undergraduates read three text sections with or without free-recall interim tests. The effect held whether sections covered different or related topics.

> "Interestingly, the group that had completed interim tests on sections 1 and 2 recalled about twice as many “idea units” from section 3 as the students who did not take interim tests."
