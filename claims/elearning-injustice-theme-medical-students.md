## Evidence

### Reid Helen J. 2016

Reid Helen J., Thomson Clare, McGlade Kieran J. (2016). Content and discontent: a qualitative exploration of obstacles to elearning engagement in medical students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0710-5

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of three focus groups (31 fourth-year medical students at Queen's University Belfast) generated the injustice theme; participants 'considered the digital elearning elements to be of intrinsically lower value than face-to-face teaching', with resentment linked to tuition fees and perceived lack of assessment alignment.

> "They considered the digital elearning elements to be of intrinsically lower value than face-to-face teaching. Indeed, there was a cultural perception, reinforced by senior clinical staff, that elearning was not ‘real’ teaching at all."
