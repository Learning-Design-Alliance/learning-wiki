## Subclaims
`q2 i1` On Q14 (electrical shielding), the hardest item, the group score was only 40% (individual 26%, d = 0.30), and of 10 groups with co-construction opportunity only one converged on the correct answer. [→ Ghimire 2024](#ghimire-2024)

## Evidence

### Ghimire 2024

Ghimire, A. and Singh, C. (2024). How often does unguided peer interaction lead to correct response consensus? An example from Conceptual Survey of Electricity and Magnetism. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ad336c](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ad336c)

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r1`

Item-level analysis of Q14 in the graduate study: individual score 26%, group score 40%, effect size 0.30, construction rate 70%, co-construction rate 10%. "Out of the 10 groups who had an opportunity for co -construction, only one group converged to the correct option D after group discussion"; 7 groups converged on incorrect option A.

> "Out of the 10 groups who had an opportunity for co -construction, only one group converged to the correct option D after group discussion."

### Ghimire 2024 (2)

Ghimire, A. and Singh, C. (2024). How often does unguided peer interaction lead to correct response consensus? An example from Conceptual Survey of Electricity and Magnetism. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ad336c](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ad336c)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

On Q30 (Faraday's law, d = 0.10), option E (none of the above) increased from 5% to 10% after peer interaction. The authors' interpretation: "group interaction can also have a negative impact if students are not comfortable with the concepts" and the problem is beyond the group's ZPF.

> "This shift from the individual to group choice suggests that group interaction can also have a negative impact if students are not comfortable with the concepts and the difficulty level of the problem is beyond a particular group’s ZPF [5, 7]."
