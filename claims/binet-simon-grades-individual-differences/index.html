Evidence¶

Carl C. Brigham 1914¶

Carl C. Brigham. (1914). An Experimental Critique of the Binet-Simon Scale. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0071183

q2 · i? · associational · r1

194 cases under twelve, judged by teachers into five groups (very bright to very dull) and checked against the principal's ratings; Table IV prints average age differences from +.91 (very bright, 17 cases) to −1.8 (very dull, 9 cases), supporting that the scale "measured and accurately graded fairly minute individual differences."

"From these results the conclusion is safe that in the long run the Binet-Simon scale measured and accurately graded fairly minute individual differences in mental ability."

Carl C. Brigham 1914 (2)¶

Carl C. Brigham. (1914). An Experimental Critique of the Binet-Simon Scale. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0071183

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Check on judgment reliability: 215 cases under twelve were judged by both teachers and the principal; judgments "coincided in 41 per cent, of the cases" and varied by one group in 50 per cent, with cases differing by two groups thrown out, leaving 194 cases.

"The judgments concerning the child's mental capacity coincided in 41 per cent, of the cases. In 50 per cent, of the cases the judgments varied a magnitude of one group."
