Evidence¶

Sunny Yu 2026¶

Sunny Yu, Myra Cheng, Ahmad Jabbar, Ilia Sucholutsky, Katherine M. Collins, Dan Jurafsky, Robert D. Hawkins. (2026). The efficiency-gain illusion: People underestimate the rate of AI use and overestimate its benefits on simple tasks. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22687

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Pre-registered Study 1 (N=498, Prolific US adult sample): participants completed four tasks with the option to prompt a GPT-4o chatbot; a separate sample stated preferences. The study found "the population-level rate of AI use is 47%" versus 33% predicted, with the gap larger on easy variants.

"On average, participants reported that they would use AI in 33% of tasks, but the population-level rate of AI use is 47% (β= 1.07,p <0.001). This gap is larger for easy task variants (β= 0.69,p<0.001): participants predicted a 20% AI use but the actual rate of AI use was 38% (β= 1.42,p <0.001)"
