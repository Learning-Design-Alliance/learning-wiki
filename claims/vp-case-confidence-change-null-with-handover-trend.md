## Evidence

### Samira J 2018

Samira J, Wang JY, Richardson L. (2018). Evaluation of virtual patient cases for teaching diagnostic and management skills in internal medicine: a mixed methods study. BMC Research Notes. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-018-3463-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the randomized comparison (VP case n = 23, PowerPoint n = 29), confidence was rated on a 5-point scale before and after the module; the article reports "no significant differences in participants’ perceived confidence" overall. Equivalence was not tested.

> "Overall, there were no significant differences in participants’ perceived confidence (Additional file 2: Tables S1 and Additional file 3: Table S2)."

### Samira J 2018 (2)

Samira J, Wang JY, Richardson L. (2018). Evaluation of virtual patient cases for teaching diagnostic and management skills in internal medicine: a mixed methods study. BMC Research Notes. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-018-3463-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary confidence measure from the same randomized comparison: the article reports handover confidence "trended towards being higher in the VP-case arm" with Mann–Whitney P = 0.051, a marginal, non-significant result.

> "Change in level of confidence for clinical hand-over trended towards being higher in the VP-case arm (Mann–Whitney P = 0.051, Table 2)."
