# Confidence on the WMPT predicts numeracy but not reading or spelling

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Confidence on the WMPT was a significant predictor only of WIAT-II Numerical Operations, in a model accounting for 16% of the variance; it did not significantly predict Word Reading, Spelling or Composite Score. [→ Freeman 2017](#freeman-2017)

## Evidence

### Freeman 2017

Freeman, Emily E.; Karayanidis, Frini; Chalmers, Kerry A. (2017). Metacognitive monitoring of working memory performance and its relationship to academic achievement in Grade 4 children. Learning and Individual Differences, Vol. 57. http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2017.06.003

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression models using WMPT confidence as predictor with gender and age covariates; only the Numerical Operations model was significant (16% of variance). The authors suggest confidence may relate more to numerical problem solving than to reading and spelling.

> "For confidence, the only significant model was for Numerical Operations, accounting for 16% of the variance. Both confidence and age were significant predictors, with higher confidence and age associated with higher Numerical Operations scores."
