## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Quizzing before reading yielded significantly better final performance on trained questions than reading twice (pre-testing effect, d = 0.35). [→ Latimier 2019](#latimier-2019)

## Evidence

### Latimier 2019

Latimier, A., Riegert, A., Peyre, H., Ly, S. T., Casati, R. & Ramus, F. (2019). Does pre-testing promote better retention than post-testing? npj Science of Learning 4:15. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0053-1

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

In the same 285-participant three-condition online experiment, the quiz-reading group outperformed the reading-reading group on trained questions; Table 4 reports "d = 0.35 [0.07, 0.64]", p = 0.013.

> "Regarding the pre-testing effect, we found a smaller but signiﬁcant effect on trained questions ( d = 0.35) but not on untrained questions ( d = −0.01)."
