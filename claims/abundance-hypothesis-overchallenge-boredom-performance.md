## Evidence

### Goetz 2023

Goetz, T., Bieleke, M., Yanagida, T., Krannich, M., Roos, A.-L., Frenzel, A. C., Lipnevich, A. A., & Pekrun, R. (2023). Test boredom: Exploring a neglected emotion. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://osf.io/ftr4g/?view_only=beac4ca87987492294aeac4a1bb96c86

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Study 1 part-specific correlations in 208 8th graders working an easy and a difficult test block designed to induce under- and overchallenge; manipulation checks confirmed the easy part felt less challenging. The difficult-part correlation was "significant negative correlation between state boredom and the test score" with r = -.22.

> "In line with the abundance hypothesis (H4), we found a significant negative correlation between state boredom and the test score for the difficult part of the test (r = -.22)."

### Goetz 2023 (2)

Goetz, T., Bieleke, M., Yanagida, T., Krannich, M., Roos, A.-L., Frenzel, A. C., Lipnevich, A. A., & Pekrun, R. (2023). Test boredom: Exploring a neglected emotion. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://osf.io/ftr4g/?view_only=beac4ca87987492294aeac4a1bb96c86

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r?`

Study 1 easy-part correlation between state boredom and the easy-part test score in the same 208 8th graders. The article reports the correlation was "not significant (r = .09)", a negligible printed effect size; the two correlations had non-overlapping confidence intervals.

> "In contrast, the correlation for the easy part of the test was not significant (r = .09)."
