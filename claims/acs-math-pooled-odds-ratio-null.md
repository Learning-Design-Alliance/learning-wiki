## Evidence

### Phansikar 2019

Phansikar, M.; Ashrafi, S.A.; Khan, N.A.; Massey, W.V.; Mullen, S.P. (2019). Active Commute in Relation to Cognition and Academic Achievement in Children and Adolescents: A Systematic Review and Future Recommendations. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph16245103

`q3 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Quantitative analysis (random effects model, Stata 14.2) pooling two of six math-performance studies due to heterogeneity (I2 > 85.00%). The review reports "pooled odds ratio = 1.18; CI = 0.40, 3.48; p = 0.77", a non-significant positive association.

> "Compared to those engaging in passive commuting, engaging in ACS was not associated with math performance (pooled odds ratio = 1.18; CI = 0.40, 3.48; p = 0.77)."
