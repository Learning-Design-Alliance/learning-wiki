# Countries whose languages are gendered show larger average gender gaps in mathematics achievement than genderless or gender-neutral language countries

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The 2015 PISA average maths gender gap is 6.15 in gendered-language countries versus 1.90 in genderless or gender-neutral language countries (t-test, p = 0.039); similar patterns hold in 2018 data. [→ Tamar Kricheli-Katz and Tali Regev 2021](#tamar-kricheli-katz-and-tali-regev-2021)

## Evidence

### Tamar Kricheli-Katz and Tali Regev 2021

Tamar Kricheli-Katz and Tali Regev. (2021). The effect of language on performance: do gendered languages fail women in maths? npj Science of Learning 6:9. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00087-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A cross-country observational comparison merging 2015 PISA gender-gap data (59 countries merged with the Prewitt-Freilino et al. language typology) with language gendering; Supplementary Fig. 1 shows similar patterns for 2018 PISA data (p < 0.01). Correlational only.

> "The average gender gap in mathematics achievements in countries whose languages are gendered is 6.15, whereas the average gender gap in mathematics achievements in countries whose languages are genderless or gender neutral is only 1.90 ( t-test, p = 0.039)."
