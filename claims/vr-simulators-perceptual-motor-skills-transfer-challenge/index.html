Evidence¶

Zia Ud Din 2024¶

Zia Ud Din, Payam Mohammad, Rachael Sherman. (2024). A systematic review and analysis of the viability of virtual reality (VR) in construction work and education. https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5059219

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

The review reports, citing Dunston et al., that VR training platforms with equipment-control mock-ups and 3D-modeled scenarios develop "essential perceptual -motor skills", while true transfer to real-world tasks is hard to assess. No effect size is printed.

"Their findings i ndicate that while VR simulators effectively develop essential perceptual -motor skills required for operating complex machinery, assessing the true transfer of these skills to real-world tasks remains a significant challenge."

Related Claims¶

  • No longitudinal studies or RCTs demonstrate that AI-acquired skills transfer to the operating room — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Evidence on transfer to clinical behavior and patient outcomes is scarce: only 2 studies reached Kirkpatrick level 3 and 1 reached level 4 — a broader claim this one bears on
  • In 82% of studies on transferability, simulation performance was correlated with better operating room performance — reports the opposite
  • Three of four studies demonstrated transfer of surgical skills from robotic VR simulators to the operating room measured by time and technical surgical performance — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Only one included study assessed predictive validity, showing a curriculum including body donor lab sessions improved real-patient laparoscopic performance over conventional training — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
