Fiber crafting can engage proportional reasoning through nested, personally-defined units¶

Claim · All claims
Evidence · 1 study · qualitative r? · q2 · n=3 focal youth (ages 9-11) selected from a 17-participant, 3-day public-library summer camp
Currency · newest study 2025 · checked against its sources 2026-10-20 (automated, 1 of 1 entry) · not reviewed by a person

Three youth engaging in knitting, crochet, and fabric pleating each engaged with multiplicative proportional relationships specific to their craft's material structure — gauge in knitting, sector growth in crochet, and hidden-layer ratios in pleating — with material properties (tension, needle size, fabric spring-back) actively shaping what unit each child defined and discovered.

A 9-year-old crocheter distributing stitches across a magic-circle foundation to keep the shape flat (target ratio 1:6, 2:12, 3:18 stitches per row) produced an uneven distribution in one row (five double-stitches instead of six), then corrected the resulting shape by deliberately loosening the tension on one stitch — compensating for a proportional error using material tension rather than recounting stitches. → Peppler et al. 2025

An 11-year-old pleater produced six fabric segments forming two pleats, a structure the researchers describe as a 3:1 unfolded-to-folded ratio (equivalently 6:2 segments) and later expressed as the formula L=l+2np (the authors state this notation was not part of the camp experience); she made the folds hold only after turning to ironing, which a facilitator introduced, when pinning alone failed to hold them. → Peppler et al. 2025

Evidence¶

Peppler et al. 2025¶

Peppler, K., Keune, A., Bender, S., & Yankova, N. (2025). Materialized Action: Reformulating the "Doing of" Math Through Fiber Crafting. Cognition and Instruction, 43(3), 175-200. https://doi.org/10.1080/07370008.2025.2485070

q2 · qualitative case study with rich multimodal data (40 hours of video across 3-4 cameras, 231 photographs, artifact reverse-engineering) and a deliberate design phase involving 5 adult crafters to align craft structure with the target mathematical concept; no control group, no pre/post outcome measure, small purposive sample i? · the abstract prints no effect size; the full text may n=3 focal youth (ages 9-11) selected from a 17-participant, 3-day public-library summer camp · qualitative · r?

Seventeen youth (ages 9-12) attended a 3-day, 4-hours-per-day summer camp in which each day was devoted to one fiber craft — knitting, crochet, and pleating — each designed by the research team (with input from adult crafters and mathematicians) to instantiate proportional reasoning through a different material structure. Three focal youth were analyzed in depth via video, photographs, and artifact analysis, using the Materialized Action framework's three nested levels (stitch/fold unit, pattern unit, project unit) to trace how each child's proportional reasoning emerged from their specific interactions with their craft's materials. This directly grounds Fiber Crafting for Proportional Reasoning.

Discussion¶

This is a small, purposive case study (3 focal youth from a 17-participant camp) with no pre/post assessment and no control condition; the authors explicitly frame it as demonstrating feasibility and mechanism ("possibilities for engaging deeply... in preparation for future work") rather than a tested intervention. All three examples come from a single camp implementation designed collaboratively with adult crafters and mathematicians specifically to elicit these mathematical relationships, so the craft activities themselves were engineered for this outcome — not naturally-occurring fiber-craft instruction. Generalization to typical craft instruction, larger cohorts, or different demographic contexts (the camp population and setting are not detailed in demographic terms) is untested.
