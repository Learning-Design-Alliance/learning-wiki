# Parental enjoyment is negatively associated with English GCSE grades at age 16, independent of stimulation and teaching

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Parental enjoyment is negatively associated with English grades (β = −0.244, p < 0.001), and enjoyment did not interact with stimulation. [→ Culpin I 2020](#culpin-i-2020)

## Evidence

### Culpin I 2020

Culpin I, Bornstein MH, Putnick DL, Sallis H, Lee R, Cordero M, Rajyaguru P, Kordas K, Cadman T, Pearson RM. (2020). Specific domains of early parenting, their heritability and differential association with adolescent behavioural and emotional disorders and academic achievement. European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry 29(10), 1401-1409. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00787-019-01449-8

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

In the mutually adjusted ALSPAC SEM, the enjoyment factor (higher scores = less enjoyment) was negatively associated with English GCSE achievement, 'independent of stimulation and teaching', and there was no evidence for an enjoyment × stimulation interaction.

> "There was evidence for a negative association between parental enjoyment and English grades, independent of stimulation and teaching (β = − 0.244, p < 0.001, 95% CI − 0. 295 to − 0.193)."
