# Older adults with mild dementia showed worse baseline cognitive performance than healthy older adults on most measures, though two d2 attention indices were significantly better in the dementia group

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants with mild dementia demonstrated lower baseline cognitive performance than healthy older adults on the majority of cognitive measures, with large between-group effect sizes on several memory measures (e.g., Hedges's g = 1.24 on AVLT trial 5, g = 1.28 on Famous Faces). ## Evidence

### Zając-Lamparska Ludmiła 2019

Zając-Lamparska Ludmiła, Wiłkość-Dębczyńska Monika, Wojciechowski Adam, Podhorecka Marta, Polak-Szabela Anna, Warchoł Łukasz, Kędziora-Kornatowska Kornelia, Araszkiewicz Aleksander, Izdebski Paweł. (2019). Effects of virtual reality-based cognitive training in older adults living without and with mild dementia: a pretest–posttest design pilot study. BMC Research Notes. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-019-4810-2

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Baseline comparison of 72 healthy older adults and 27 older adults with mild dementia using t tests and Mann–Whitney U tests across the cognitive battery (Table 1). The article reports "Hotelling T2 = 130.868; p < 0.001" overall, with Hedges's g values such as 1.24 for AVLT trial 5.

> "Better baseline cognitive performance was observed in the group of healthy older adults in general (Hotelling T2 = 130.868; p < 0.001) and in the majority of cognitive measures (Table 1)."

### Zając-Lamparska Ludmiła 2019 (2)

Zając-Lamparska Ludmiła, Wiłkość-Dębczyńska Monika, Wojciechowski Adam, Podhorecka Marta, Polak-Szabela Anna, Warchoł Łukasz, Kędziora-Kornatowska Kornelia, Araszkiewicz Aleksander, Izdebski Paweł. (2019). Effects of virtual reality-based cognitive training in older adults living without and with mild dementia: a pretest–posttest design pilot study. BMC Research Notes. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-019-4810-2

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Baseline group comparison (Results, Table 1 context). The article notes exceptions to the overall pattern: "no significant differences between groups were observed" on some measures and the two d2 indices favoured the dementia group.

> "In a few of the cognitive measures, no significant differences between groups were observed. Two performance indices in the sustained attention task (d2) were significantly better in the group of older adults with dementia."
