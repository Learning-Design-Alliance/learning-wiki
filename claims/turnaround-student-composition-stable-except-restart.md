## Evidence

### de la Torre 2013

de la Torre, M., Allensworth, E., Jagesic, S., Sebastian, J., & Salmonowicz, M.; with Meyers, C., & Gerdeman, R. D. (2013). Turning Around Low-Performing Schools in Chicago. University of Chicago Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/turning-around-low-performing-schools-chicago-full-report

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of September enrollment the year before versus the first year of intervention using CPS administrative records, including home-address neighborhood indicators. Reenrollment under Reconstitution, STSP, AUSL, and OSI models ranged from 55 to 89 percent, similar to prior rates.

> "With the exception of schools in the Closure and Restart model, schools reenrolled between 55 and 89 percent of students eligible to reenroll in the year after intervention—rates that were similar to their year-to-year reenrollment rates prior to intervention."

### de la Torre 2013 (2)

de la Torre, M., Allensworth, E., Jagesic, S., Sebastian, J., & Salmonowicz, M.; with Meyers, C., & Gerdeman, R. D. (2013). Turning Around Low-Performing Schools in Chicago. University of Chicago Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/turning-around-low-performing-schools-chicago-full-report

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of student composition before and after Closure and Restart reforms, which closed schools for a year and reopened them with lottery-based application admission. Composition shifted toward more advantaged, higher-achieving students and fewer special education students.

> "Schools under the Closure and Restart model experienced substantial changes to their student body composition, serving more economically advantaged students, students of higher prior achievement, and fewer special education students."
