# Final-year medical students' self-reported confidence in diagnosing and managing medical emergencies and in using the ALS algorithm rose from pre-test to post-test after a pre-foundation high-fidelity simulation course, in one uncontrolled pre-post study

Moore Amber Jennifer Sara, Dhas Kallpana, Karunaratne Dilroshini, Dasigan Krishna, Brennan-Davies Ailie, Sahni Ankita, Patrick James, Rasool Lisa Saleh, Hridi Syeda, Carter Sef, Patel Shiras, Fakes Lianne, Kelada Mary, Shohayeb Ammar, Baptista Ana, Bartholomew Brent. `q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Pre-post questionnaire analysis of 252 paired responses (98.4% response rate) using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test; Cronbach's alpha was 0.855. Table 1 reports r = 0.702 for diagnosing and managing medical emergencies and r = 0.794 for the ALS algorithm, both p < 0.001.

> "Students reported improved confidence in diagnosing and managing medical emergencies, and carrying out the ALS algorithm with large effect sizes and improvement by 1 point on a 5-point Likert scale as shown in Table 1."
