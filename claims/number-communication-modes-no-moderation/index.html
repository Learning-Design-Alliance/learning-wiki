q3 · i?

Model A Tau results across studies categorized as one mode (k = 68), two modes (k = 29), or three or more modes (k = 22). One-mode studies showed the largest point estimate (Tau = 0.754) but "the differences were not systematically different from zero"; patterns held across models B and C.

"the differences were not systematically different from zero, F (2,22.9) = 1.1, p = 0.345. These patterns were consistent across all three models."

LRR analysis of the same moderator: studies with three or more modes had the largest point estimate (LRR = 1.887), but "Average LRR effect sizes across modes were not statistically distinct"; non-significance held across all three models.

Related Claims¶

  • AAC interventions yield moderately large average effects on communication outcomes for school-aged participants with ASD and/or ID, with substantial between-study heterogeneity — related
  • Communicative function taught (behavioral regulation, joint attention, social interaction, or multiple) does not statistically moderate AAC effects — related
  • Type of communication mode shows only a marginal, non-robust moderation of AAC effects, with aided AAC descriptively outperforming manual signs/gestures — related
  • Aided versus unaided AAC does not statistically moderate AAC effects, though exclusive aided AAC descriptively yields the largest estimates — related
  • Under-reported participant characteristics and implementation factors, plus effect-size metric limitations, explain the scarcity of significant moderator differences — related
