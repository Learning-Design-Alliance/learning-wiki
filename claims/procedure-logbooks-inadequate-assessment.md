# Residents judged procedure logbooks inadequate as assessment because they do not reflect performance, ensure direct observation or provide feedback

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants stated that logging procedures gave no insight into whether procedures were properly completed, supervised, or followed by feedback, and should not count as assessment. [→ Touchie Claire 2013](#touchie-claire-2013)

## Evidence

### Touchie Claire 2013

Touchie Claire, Humphrey-Murto Susan, Varpio Lara. (2013). Teaching and assessing procedural skills: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-69

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Focus group theme on method of assessment; residents concluded logs "should not be considered a form of assessment". The discussion adds that logbooks can still track training, monitor progress and identify deficiencies, so they should not be discounted altogether.

> "Participants stated that the method of logging procedures did not provide them with insights into whether or not the procedure was properly completed, nor if the procedure was supervised, nor if feedback was given."
