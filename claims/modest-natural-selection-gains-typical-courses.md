## Evidence

### Andrews T. M. 2011

Andrews T. M., Leonard M. J., Colgrove C. A., Kalinowski S. T. (2011). Active Learning Not Associated with Student Learning in a Random Sample of College Biology Courses. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.11-07-0061

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive statistics across the 33 fully participating courses, pre/post testing with the 10-question CINS-abbr. The article reports "the mean effect size was 0.49 (SD = 0.31)" and that 39% of courses fell below the one-question-equivalent threshold of 0.42.

> "Effect sizes (Cohen's d) on the CINS-abbr ranged from −0.11–1.26 and the mean effect size was 0.49 (SD = 0.31). Thirty-nine percent (n = 13) of courses had an effect size lower than 0.42"

### Andrews T. M. 2011 (2)

Andrews T. M., Leonard M. J., Colgrove C. A., Kalinowski S. T. (2011). Active Learning Not Associated with Student Learning in a Random Sample of College Biology Courses. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.11-07-0061

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Pre/post scores on the open-ended cheetah question, scored with a rubric weighted toward variation, heritability, and differential reproductive success, from a subsample of about 50 students per course (mean subsample 42, SD = 12). The article reports "the mean effect size was 0.15 (SD = 0.19)."

> "On the cheetah question, learning gains were even lower. Effect sizes ranged from −0.16–0.58. The mean effect size was 0.15 (SD = 0.19) and the mean normalized gain for the cheetah question was 0.06 (SD = 0.08)."
