## Evidence

### Alfstad E 2025

Alfstad E, Lockertsen V, Johannessen A-K, Werner A. (2025). The applicability of a structured learning programme focusing on improving observational competencies to strengthen patient safety: a qualitative study with professionals in primary healthcare services. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-025-12692-y

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview finding; an RN reports assistant nurses acting more independently and giving concrete reports instead of vague calls like "'He is unwell!'". RNs reported delegating more confidently after observing colleagues' competencies.

> "It has been helpful for us RNs that the assistant nurses do a bit more independently when we have duty shifts or are on call for the whole house. They don't just call and say, 'He is unwell!' Instead, you get something more concrete."

### Alfstad E 2025 (2)

Alfstad E, Lockertsen V, Johannessen A-K, Werner A. (2025). The applicability of a structured learning programme focusing on improving observational competencies to strengthen patient safety: a qualitative study with professionals in primary healthcare services. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-025-12692-y

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview finding on the negative side of redistribution; an RN reports that assistant nurses' "readiness for action may have decreased", which RNs attributed to heightened awareness of risks and complexity.

> "The readiness for action may have decreased because nurse assistants now think calling the emergency room is a bit scary. We have been a bit puzzled because we [RNs] aren't always available, so they must manage."
