# Mild time pressure increases learning efficiency: recall per mean second of study time is higher in the timed than the untimed condition for all age groups

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Efficiency (correctly recalled items divided by mean study time) was higher in the timed condition than the untimed condition, supporting the enhancement hypothesis. [→ Gönül 2021](#gonul-2021)

## Evidence

### Gönül 2021

Gönül, G., Tsalas, N., Paulus, M. (2021). The effect of time pressure on metacognitive control: developmental changes in self-regulation and efficiency during learning. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-021-09262-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

GLMM on the efficiency rate (correct recalls per mean second) from the same 183-participant experiment. The article reports higher return rates under time pressure; no effect size was printed for this GLMM test.

> "There were also significant main effects of condition, F(1, 722) = 84.759, p < 0.001, with higher return rates in timed condition compared to untimed condition."
