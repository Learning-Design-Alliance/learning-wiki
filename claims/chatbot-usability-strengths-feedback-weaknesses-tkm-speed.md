## Evidence

### Lee Hye-Yoon 2025

Lee Hye-Yoon, Kim Jundong, Choi Hyojae, Bae Hyojin, Jeong Aram, Choi Sungyoul, Kim Ji-Hwan, Kim Chang-Eop. (2025). Comparing AI chatbot simulation and peer role-play for OSCE preparation: a pilot randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08308-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Within-group Chatbot Usability and Improvement Survey (chatbot group only, n = 9) tested against a benchmark of 4 on a 5-point Likert scale using single-group Hedges' g and bootstrap CIs. Scenario accuracy reached adjusted significance at p = 0.02.

> "‘Q1. The chatbot accurately reflected the patient information given in the scenario’ (Mean=4.78, Hedges’ g=1.61, adjusted p = 0.02), ‘Q18. I could apply the content of the feedback’ (Mean=4.67, Hedges’ g=1.22, adjusted p = 0.06)"

### Lee Hye-Yoon 2025 (2)

Lee Hye-Yoon, Kim Jundong, Choi Hyojae, Bae Hyojin, Jeong Aram, Choi Sungyoul, Kim Ji-Hwan, Kim Chang-Eop. (2025). Comparing AI chatbot simulation and peer role-play for OSCE preparation: a pilot randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08308-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same within-group usability survey: scoring speed and TKM terminology understanding fell below the benchmark value of 4, with moderate negative effect sizes relative to the benchmark (adjusted p = 0.22).

> "items identified as needing improvement were ‘Q25. Scoring speed: The chatbot scored at an appropriate speed’ (Mean = 3.11, Hedges’ g=−0.77) and ‘Q6. The chatbot appropriately understood traditional Korean medicine (TKM) terminology’ (Mean = 3.00, Hedges’ g=−0.82)"
