# A split sleep schedule raises subjective alertness and focus in the afternoon during learning, and post-nap psychomotor vigilance, in sleep-restricted adolescents

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Subjective alertness was significantly higher in the afternoon for the split sleep group, and focus showed a significant group × time interaction with higher afternoon focus in the split group. [→ Cousins 2019](#cousins-2019)

## Evidence

### Cousins 2019

Cousins, J. N., van Rijn, E., Ong, J. L., Wong, K. F., & Chee, M. W. L. (2019). Does splitting sleep improve long-term memory in chronically sleep deprived adolescents? npj Science of Learning 4:8. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0047-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Karolinska Sleepiness Scale and focus/ability/motivation ratings collected during learning blocks across three days; alertness showed a significant group × time interaction, F(1,56) = 25.62, p < 0.001. "Alertness was signiﬁcantly higher in the afternoon for the split sleep group"; focus was also higher in the afternoon, t(56) = 2.647, p = 0.011. No effect sizes printed.

> "Alertness was signiﬁcantly higher in the afternoon for the split sleep group, t(56) = 3.34, p = 0.001, but groups did not differ in the morning, t(56) = 0.291, p = 0.772."

### Cousins 2019 (2)

Cousins, J. N., van Rijn, E., Ong, J. L., Wong, K. F., & Chee, M. W. L. (2019). Does splitting sleep improve long-term memory in chronically sleep deprived adolescents? npj Science of Learning 4:8. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0047-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Ten-minute psychomotor vigilance task at 10:15 and 16:30, collapsed across the three learning days and analyzed with a mixed ANOVA showing a significant group × time interaction, F(1,56) = 25.3, p < 0.001. "the split sleep group had signiﬁcantly fewer lapses than the continuous sleep group in the afternoon"; no effect size printed.

> "Groups did not differ in the morning, t(56) = 0.851, p = 0.398, although the split sleep group had signiﬁcantly fewer lapses than the continuous sleep group in the afternoon t(56) = 4.987, p < 0.001."
