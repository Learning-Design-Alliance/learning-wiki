## Evidence

### Tiffin Paul A. 2016

Tiffin Paul A., Mwandigha Lazaro M., Paton Lewis W., Hesselgreaves H., McLachlan John C., Finn Gabrielle M., Kasim Adetayo S. (2016). Predictive validity of the UKCAT for medical school undergraduate performance: a national prospective cohort study. BMC Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-016-0682-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Univariable multilevel regression of theory-based exam scores on UKCAT scales in a national cohort of 6812 medical entrants (2007–8 entry), with scores standardised within institution and cohort. The article reports that coefficients were "generally relatively small, at approximately 0.1 to 0.2"; no effect size beyond the coefficients is printed.

> "All the scales and total scores from the UKCAT significantly predicted performance at theory exams throughout all 5 years of medical school (Fig. 2). The coefficients were generally relatively small, at approximately 0.1 to 0.2."
