# Full HPSF schools (healthy lunch plus structured physical activity) showed significant, growing mathematics gains versus control schools over four years

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` After one year, full HPSF children outperformed control children in mathematics (ES = 0.30, p = 0.011), and the effect grew to ES = 0.66 (p < 0.001) after four years. [→ van Engelen Bo H. W. 2026](#van-engelen-bo-h-w-2026)

## Evidence

### van Engelen Bo H. W. 2026

van Engelen Bo H. W., Hahnraths Marla T. H., Winkens Bjorn, Schils Trudie, van Schayck Onno C. P. (2026). Food for thought? The effects of the Healthy Primary School of the Future on children's educational outcomes. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0334638

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Mixed model for repeated measures analysis of national standardised CITO mathematics test scores from eight Dutch primary schools over four years, corrected for baseline, age, sex and test version. Year 1 showed "a small-to-medium ES of 0.30"; the article reports the effect remained significant with ES ranging from 0.37 to 0.66 in later years.

> "The full HPSF group showed a significant improvement in mean mathematics score compared to the control group in the first year (corrected mean difference = 6.2, 95%CI 1.4 to 10.9, p = 0.011), corresponding to a small-to-medium ES of 0.30."
