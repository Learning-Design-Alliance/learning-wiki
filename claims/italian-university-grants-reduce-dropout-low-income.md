# University grants reduce dropout among students from families with economic measure around 15000 euros, with a 13.9% reduction for grant recipients

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Receiving a grant reduces dropout rate by an estimated 13.9% (95% CI: 3.4%–24.7%) for students within 1000 euros of the threshold. [→ Fan Li 2015](#fan-li-2015)

## Evidence

### Fan Li 2015

Fan Li, Alessandra Mattei and Fabrizia Mealli. (2015). Evaluating the Causal Effect of University Grants on Student Dropout: Evidence from a Regression Discontinuity Design Using Principal Stratification. Submitted to the Annals of Applied Statistics. [doi:10.1214/15-aoas881](https://doi.org/10.1214/15-aoas881)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Bayesian principal stratification analysis of fuzzy RD data from first-year students (2004–2006) at Universities of Pisa and Florence; the finite-sample estimate within h = 1000 euros shows a "13 .9% (95% CI: (3 .4%; 24.7%)) reduction in dropout rate" for grant recipients.

> "The estimated τs0 suggests a 13 .9% (95% CI: (3 .4%; 24.7%)) reduction in dropout rate for the students who receive the grants."
