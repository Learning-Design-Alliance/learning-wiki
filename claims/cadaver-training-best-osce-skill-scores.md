# Training on cadavers produced the best OSCE skill scores for infiltrations, followed by training on anatomical models, for proficiency, systematics and completeness in the glenohumeral joint, subacromial space and lateral epicondyle

## Evidence

### Michels Nele R. 2019

Michels Nele R., Vanhomwegen Els. (2019). An educational study to investigate the efficacy of three training methods for infiltration techniques on self-efficacy and skills of trainees in general practice. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-019-1023-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Between-group comparison of OSCE results in the randomized three-group study (lecture n = 18, models n = 19, cadavers n = 11), analyzed with Kruskal-Wallis tests (p ≤ 0.05). The cadaver group scored "significantly higher scores on the OSCE for proficiency, systematics and completeness" for the three named regions. No effect size is printed.

> "Compared with participants in the theoretical lecture or training on anatomical models, participants in the training on cadavers had significantly higher scores on the OSCE for proficiency, systematics and completeness when infiltrating the glenohumeral joint, the subacromial space and the lateral epicondyle."
