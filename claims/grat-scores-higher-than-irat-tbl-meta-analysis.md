## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Across 11 studies (1575 participants), pooled GRAT scores statistically exceeded IRAT scores with SMD = 2.05 (95% CI 1.64–2.46) under a random-effects model. [→ Ngoc Phan Nguyen 2020](#ngoc-phan-nguyen-2020)

## Evidence

### Ngoc Phan Nguyen 2020

Ngoc Phan Nguyen, Cheng Chao-Ling, Lin Yen-Kuang, Wu Ming-Shun, Chu Jan-Show, Tang Kung-Pei. (2020). A meta-analysis of students’ readiness assurance test performance with team-based learning. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02139-9

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analysis pooling GRAT–IRAT differences from 11 studies of healthcare-professions students (1575 participants) in a random-effects model, reporting "SMD = 2.05" with very high heterogeneity (I2 = 95%).

> "the GRAT scores were statistically greater than IRAT scores when TBL was applied in a random-effects model (standardized mean difference (SMD) = 2.05, 95% confidence interval (CI) = 1.64 ~ 2.46, pheterogeneity < 0.001 I2 = 95%)"
