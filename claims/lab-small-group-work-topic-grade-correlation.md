# In lab courses, small-group work is the only interaction topic that significantly correlates with final course grade, corresponding to about 0.2 GPA points

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### L. N. Simpfendoerfer 2024

L. N. Simpfendoerfer, Meagan Sundstrom, Matthew Dew, and N. G. Holmes. (2024). What topics of peer interactions correlate with student performance in physics courses?. arXiv. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ad358b](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ad358b)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear mixed model on lab-course survey data with final GPA as outcome and binary topic predictors, with a random effect for lecture-course offering. The article reports the 0.2 GPA-point increase for small-group work; this unstandardized difference is not a standardized effect size.

> "Interacting with at least one person about small-group work corresponds to an increase in final course grade by 0.2 GPA points as compared with students who did not interact with any peers about small-group work."

### L. N. Simpfendoerfer 2024 (2)

L. N. Simpfendoerfer, Meagan Sundstrom, Matthew Dew, and N. G. Holmes. (2024). What topics of peer interactions correlate with student performance in physics courses?. arXiv. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ad358b](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ad358b)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same lab linear mixed model. The article reports the lab-lecture topic coefficient as about 0.1 GPA points but not statistically distinguishable from zero; equivalence was not tested.

> "Interacting with at least one peer about the lab lecture also positively correlates with students' final grades (corresponding to an increase in final course grade by about 0.1 GPA points), though the effect is not statistically distinguishable from zero."
