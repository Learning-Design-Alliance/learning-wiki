## Evidence

### Melissa Dancy 2022

Melissa Dancy, Charles Henderson, Nanah Apkarian, Estrella Johnson, Marilyne Stains, Jeffrey R. Raker, Alexandra Lau. (2022). Physics instructors' knowledge and use of active learning has increased over the last decade but most still lecture too much. Proceedings of the Physics Education Research Conference. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2211.13082](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2211.13082)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Classification of respondents by knowledge and use level in both surveys (Figures 3 and 4), where discontinuation means having tried one or more RBISs and now reporting use of none. Discontinuation fell from 32% of triers in 2008 to 5% in 2019.

> "In 2008, about ⅓ (23%/72% = 32%) of those who tried ended up discontinuing useof all RBISs.  In 2019, discontinuation is a very low 5% of those who tried a RBIS."

### Melissa Dancy 2022 (2)

Melissa Dancy, Charles Henderson, Nanah Apkarian, Estrella Johnson, Marilyne Stains, Jeffrey R. Raker, Alexandra Lau. (2022). Physics instructors' knowledge and use of active learning has increased over the last decade but most still lecture too much. Proceedings of the Physics Education Research Conference. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2211.13082](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2211.13082)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same classification of respondents by knowledge and use across the two surveys: no-knowledge respondents fell from 12% to 2%, non-triers from 16% to 8%, and triers rose from 72% to 90%.

> "the percentage of instructors with no knowledge of any RBIS fell from12% in 2008 to 2% in 2019. The percentage of instructors who have not tried a RBIS fell from 16% in2008 to 8% in 2019. The percentage of instructors who had tried 1 or more RBISs increased from 72% in2008 to 90% in 2019."
