# After the promotion gate, general education eighth graders were retained at far higher rates while retention of students with disabilities fell, raising the disabled share of ninth graders

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Before 1995–96, eighth graders with disabilities were retained at higher rates than general education students; after the gate this reversed — general education retention rose from 2.1 to a high of 14.4 percent while retention of students with disabilities fell to 4.7 percent. [→ Shazia Rafiullah Miller and Robert M. Gladden 2002](#shazia-rafiullah-miller-and-robert-m-gladden-2002)

## Evidence

### Shazia Rafiullah Miller and Robert M. Gladden 2002

Shazia Rafiullah Miller and Robert M. Gladden. (2002). Changing Special Education Enrollments: Causes and Distribution Among Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/changing-special-education-enrollments-causes-and-distribution-among-schools

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Retention trend analysis of eighth graders with and without disabilities, 1993–94 to 1999–00 (Figure 6). Because students with disabilities promote via IEP requirements, more passed into high school while general education retention rose, reducing the number of general education ninth graders and raising the disabled proportion.

> "the rate of retention for students with disabilities rose from 7.3 to 9.2 percent, but then it fell to 4.7 percent by 1999–00. At the same time, general education students were substantially more likely to be retained; their rate of retention rose from 2.1 percent to a high of 14.4 percent"
