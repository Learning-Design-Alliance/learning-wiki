# Guidance during inquiry learning produces a medium-to-large improvement in learners' use of inquiry skills during the inquiry

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Lazonder 2016

Lazonder, A. W., & Harmsen, R. (2016). Meta-analysis of inquiry-based learning: Effects of guidance. Review of Educational Research, 86(3), 681-718. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315627366

`q4 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 20 of the 72 included studies assessing learning activities (N = 2,374). The summary effect was d = 0.66, and the authors report that "the true effect size is likely to fall in the range of 0.44 to 0.88, which according to Cohen (1988) can be considered a medium to large effect."

> "there was a significant overall effect of guidance in that guided inquiry learning led to a more proficient use of inquiry skills than did unguided inquiry learning, z = 5.85, p < .001"

### Lazonder 2016 (2)

Lazonder, A. W., & Harmsen, R. (2016). Meta-analysis of inquiry-based learning: Effects of guidance. Review of Educational Research, 86(3), 681-718. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315627366

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Q-test moderator analyses in the same meta-analysis examined whether between-study variation in learning-activities effect sizes was attributable to basic study features. "Again no significant moderation effect was found" for type of guidance, so the overall effect did not differ by guidance type.

> "Results showed that this was not the case, as neither outcome focus nor publication type, study design, and duration moderated the findings."
