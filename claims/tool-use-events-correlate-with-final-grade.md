## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among 79 students with covs ≥ 0.40, six of the analyzed event factors show a positive and significant correlation with the final grade (p < 0.05), with WorkTime the strongest (rs = 0.43, r² = 0.18). [→ Romero-Zaldivar 2012](#romero-zaldivar-2012)

## Evidence

### Romero-Zaldivar 2012

Romero-Zaldivar, V.-A., Pardo, A., Burgos, D., & Delgado Kloos, C. (2012). Monitoring student progress using virtual appliances: A case study. Computers & Education, 58(4), 1058–1067. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2011.12.003

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Bivariate correlation analysis (Table 3, Nstudents = 79) of event counts versus final course grade. The six significant factors include WorkTime (rs = 0.43), Compile (0.40), Editor (0.37), Profiler (0.37), Command (0.27) and IDE (0.26); the article reports the six factors "show a positive and signi ﬁcant correlation with the student grade ( p < 0.05)". No standardized effect size beyond the printed rs values is reported.

> "Six of the analyzed factors show a positive and signi ﬁcant correlation with the student grade ( p < 0.05)."

### Romero-Zaldivar 2012 (2)

Romero-Zaldivar, V.-A., Pardo, A., Burgos, D., & Delgado Kloos, C. (2012). Monitoring student progress using virtual appliances: A case study. Computers & Education, 58(4), 1058–1067. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2011.12.003

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Interpretation of the bivariate results (Table 3): Debugger correlates weakly and non-significantly (rs = 0.13, p = 0.24), and Visit URL is non-significant (rs = 0.17, p = 0.13) because browser use may be unrelated to the course. No effect size threshold applies; correlations are as printed.

> "The last factor, “Debugger”, r e ﬂects an interesting situation. Although students are encouraged to use this tool for convenience, the low event count suggests that the advice is being systematically ignored."
