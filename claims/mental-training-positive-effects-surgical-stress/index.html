Evidence¶

Tjønnås Maria Suong 2022¶

Tjønnås Maria Suong, Guzmán-García Carmen, Sánchez-González Patricia, Gómez Enrique Javier, Oropesa Ignacio, Våpenstad Cecilie. (2022). Stress in surgical educational environments: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-022-03841-6

q2 · i? · review · r3

Review synthesis of 13 articles investigating mental training methods for stress management in surgical environments. The review reports the methods "demonstrated to have positive effects on participants’ stress experience" and improved technical performance.

"The mental training methods demonstrated to have positive effects on participants’ stress experience and to reduce their cognitive stress [69, 73, 89, 92], as well as improve their technical performance [69–71, 90]."

Tjønnås Maria Suong 2022 (2)¶

Tjønnås Maria Suong, Guzmán-García Carmen, Sánchez-González Patricia, Gómez Enrique Javier, Oropesa Ignacio, Våpenstad Cecilie. (2022). Stress in surgical educational environments: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-022-03841-6

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Review synthesis noting a dissociation between subjective and physiological outcomes: "the effect of mental training was not always reflected in physiological stress measurements in participants". Most methods were initiated weeks ahead of the intervention.

"However, the effect of mental training was not always reflected in physiological stress measurements in participants [71, 92]."
