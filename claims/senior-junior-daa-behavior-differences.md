## Evidence

### Rochlen LR 2017

Rochlen LR, Housey M, Gannon I, Mitchell S, Rooney DM, Tait AR, Engoren M. (2017). Assessing anesthesiology residents’ out-of-the-operating-room (OOOR) emergent airway management. BMC Anesthesiology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12871-017-0387-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Chi-square/Fisher's exact comparison of 45 junior (CA1–2) and 18 senior (CA3) residents' survey and simulation behaviors. Only p-values printed; no effect sizes, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "Based on pre-survey responses, senior residents were more familiar with the content of the DAA compared to junior residents (95% vs. 67%, respectively, p = 0.026). As seen in Table 1, there were no significant differences between senior and junior residents’ ability to successfully intubate (p = 0.316). In the unsuccessful intubation group, senior residents were more likely to attempt a second laryngoscopy with a different blade compared to junior residents (p-value = 0.005)."
