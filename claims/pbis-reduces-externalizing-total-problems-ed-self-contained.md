# PBIS implementation is associated with significant pre-to-post reductions in externalizing and total problem behaviors of students with ED in self-contained settings

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2` · `i1` small

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Students with ED (N = 37) showed statistically significant reductions on TRF Externalizing Problems (p = .028) and Total Problems (p = .004) after a year of teacher PBIS training. [→ Benner 2008](#benner-2008)

`q2 i?` Statistically significant pre-to-post reductions were found on Thought Problems (p = .034), Attention Problems (p = .003), and Aggressive Behavior (p = .014) syndrome scores. [→ Benner 2008](#benner-2008)

`q2 i?` Significant differences were found in percentages of students with clinically significant internalizing (54% to 46%), externalizing (76% to 68%), and total (76% to 62%) behavior problems. [→ Benner 2008](#benner-2008)

## Evidence

### Benner 2008

Benner, G. J., Beaudoin, K. M., Chen, P.-Y., Davis, C., & Ralston, N. (2008). The Impact of Intensive Positive Behavioral Supports on the Behavioral Functioning of Students with Emotional Disturbance: How Much Does Fidelity Matter? JBAIC, Volume 1, No. 1. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Impact+of+Intensive+Positive+Behavioral+Supports

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r1`

Pre-post naturalistic study of 37 students with ED in self-contained classrooms; Wilcoxon Signed Ranks Tests on TRF scores showed "statistically significant reductions" on Externalizing (p = .028) and Total Problems (p = .004). No effect size printed.

> "Inspection of Table 1 reveals that statistically significant reductions on the TRF Externalizing Problems (p = .028) and Total Problems (p = .004) broadband scores were found from pre- to post-test."
