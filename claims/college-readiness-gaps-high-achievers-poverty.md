# Most high-achieving middle schoolers were on track for college readiness, but students in wealthy schools were more often on track than those in poor schools

## Evidence

### Michael Dahlin and Beth Tarasawa 2013

Michael Dahlin and Beth Tarasawa. (2013). A Level Playing Field? How College Readiness Standards Change the Accountability Game. Northwest Evaluation Association. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/a-level-playing-field-college-readiness-standards-whitepaper/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal observational analysis of NWEA MAP scores for a middle school cohort followed from sixth through eighth grade, with college readiness estimated by linking MAP scores to ACT benchmarks. The report finds that "over 95%" of low-poverty high achievers were on track, compared with "over 85%" in reading and "just over 80%" in mathematics in high-poverty schools, stable across the study period.

> "over 95% of the middle school high achievers in low-poverty schools started and remained on track to meet the college readiness benchmarks in reading and mathematics. Among the high-poverty schools over 85% of the middle school cohort was on track to achieve the college readiness benchmarks in reading at the end of grade eight and just over 80% were on track in mathematics."
