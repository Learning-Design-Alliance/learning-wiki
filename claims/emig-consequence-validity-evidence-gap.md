Authors' statement in the discussion identifying an open validity question for the EMIG assessment. The article reports that consequence evidence is rarely reported as part of health professions education validity studies (5–20%) and is lacking for FLS use with obstetrics and gynecology trainees, citing prior reviews.

> "The long-term significance and consequence evidence of the Essentials in Minimally Invasive Gynecology simulation-based assessment was neither evaluated nor defined at the outset of this study. Consequence validity evidence examines the intended and unintended implications of deciding to use an assessment on downstream outcomes (eg, health systems, surgical practice variation, surgical outcomes, and what is not taught in order to learn how to pass the assessment)."
