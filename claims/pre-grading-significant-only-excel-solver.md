# Pre-grading/resubmission was associated with a statistically significant learning difference only for the Excel Solver assignment, not for Access or Excel IF assignments

> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For the Excel Solver assignment, the difference in learning between students who used pre-grading and those who did not was significant at the 1.2% level. [→ Janicki et al. 2011](#janicki-et-al-2011)
`q2 i?` For Access Hands-On (p = .114) and Excel IF's (p = .524), the difference was not significant at the 5% level. [→ Janicki et al. 2011](#janicki-et-al-2011)

## Evidence

### Janicki et al. 2011

Thomas N. Janicki, Judith Gebauer, Ulku Yaylacicegi. (2011). Impact of Pre-Grading / Resubmission of Projects on Test Grades in an Introductory Computer Literacy Course. Information Systems Education Journal 9(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1145436

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison within one section of 87 students taught by one instructor; students were grouped by whether they used pre-grading before each of four tests, and two-tailed t-tests (modified for unequal variances in two cases) compared relative score differences. The article reports p-values of .114, .524 and .012 for the three assignments.

> "the results of the t-test analysis suggest that for two out of three assignments, the difference in learning as a result of pre-grading is NOT significant at the 5%-level. For the third assignment (Excel Solver), however, a difference is found that is highly significant at 1.2%."
