# Delegating the terminal evaluation to AI is self-defeating: each hand-off returns one more output someone must accept or not

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Vigilance is not strictly impossible to delegate but self-defeating to delegate: accepting a model's check unexamined is the absence of vigilance, and the unevaluated claim has only moved one step along. [→ Marcus Kubsch 2026](#marcus-kubsch-2026)

## Evidence

### Marcus Kubsch 2026

Marcus Kubsch. (2026). AI as a Partner in Learning about, Doing, and Engaging with Science: Vigilance as the Key to Productive Augmentation. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16822

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument answering the objection that deep checking can itself be handed to the AI: a learner can ask a model to check a step or argue the other side, and chain judgments across models, but the regress stops only where a human accepts or rejects the returned output. Conceptual, no data.

> "each hand-off returns one more output that someone must accept or not, and to accept it unexamined is not a thinner version of vigilance but its absence; the unevaluated claim has only moved one step along."
