# Comma errors were significantly lower in the post-LSA group (p < 0.01)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The average number of comma errors per message dropped from 3.34 (pre-LSA, N=87) to 1.47 (post-LSA, N=85), a statistically significant reduction (p < 0.01). [→ Austin 2017](#austin-2017)

## Evidence

### Austin 2017

Austin, T. L., Sigmar, L. S., Mehta, G. B., & Shirk, J. L. (2017). The impact of web-assisted instruction on student writing outcomes in business communication. Journal of Instructional Pedagogies, 20. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1178728

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Independent-samples t-test on comma errors: pre-LSA mean 3.34 (SD 2.415, N=87) vs. post-LSA mean 1.47 (SD 1.350, N=85), t = 6.300, p = 0.000.
