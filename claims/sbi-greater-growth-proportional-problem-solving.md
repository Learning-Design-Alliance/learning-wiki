## Evidence

### Jitendra 2015

Jitendra, A. K., Harwell, M. R., Dupuis, D. N., Karl, S. R., Lein, A. E., Simonson, G., & Slater, S. C. (2015). Effects of a research-based intervention to improve seventh-grade students’ proportional problem solving: A cluster randomized trial. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000039

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

An ancillary analysis of PPS longitudinal data explored "student change over time and whether such change was impacted by the treatment", finding the SBI group "showed significantly more growth in proportional problem solving" than the control group.

> "also showed significantly more growth in proportional problem solving"

### Jitendra 2017

Jitendra, A. K., Harwell, M. R., Karl, S. R., Im, S-H., & Slater, S. C. (2017). Investigating a Tier 1 intervention focused on proportional reasoning: A follow-up study. Journal of Learning Disabilities, 83(4), 340-358. https://doi.org/10.1177/0014402917691017

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory two-level repeated-measures model of PPS data across pretest, posttest, and delayed posttest, with treatment as the only predictor beyond time. The average linear slope was also significant (B = .63, t = 3.41, p < .001). No effect size is printed for the growth difference.

> "The results also indicated that the treatment variable was a statistically significant predictor of student linear growth (B = .73, t = 3.02, p = .003), meaning that SBI students improved at a faster rate than control students."
