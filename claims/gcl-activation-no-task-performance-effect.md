# GCL-raising activations did not improve learning outcomes: neither prompted imagination nor disfluency produced a significant task-performance advantage

## Evidence

### Melina Klepsch 2020

Melina Klepsch, Tina Seufert. (2020). Understanding instructional design effects by differentiated measurement of intrinsic, extraneous, and germane cognitive load. Instructional Science (2020) 48:45–77. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-020-09502-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-test performance in the clutch study (N = 31), measured with 14 questions worth at most 39 points, analyzed with prior knowledge and FAM subscales as covariates. No significant main effect of the imagination instruction was found.

> "For task performance, we found no main effect (F  < 1, n.s.): Both groups reached similar performance levels."

### Melina Klepsch 2020 (2)

Melina Klepsch, Tina Seufert. (2020). Understanding instructional design effects by differentiated measurement of intrinsic, extraneous, and germane cognitive load. Instructional Science (2020) 48:45–77. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-020-09502-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-test performance in the time-zones study (N = 36), a 17-question test worth at most 34 points, with prior knowledge as covariate. No significant difference between fluent and disfluent conditions; equivalence was not tested.

> "For task performance, we found no main effect (F (1,33) = 1.65, p = 0.21, η2 = 0.05): Both groups reached similar performance levels."
