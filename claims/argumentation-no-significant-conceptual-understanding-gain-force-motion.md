# Argumentation-based learning activities did not produce a statistically significant difference in 7th graders' conceptual understanding of Force and Motion compared with regular instruction

## Evidence

### Buber 2017

Buber, A., & Coban Unal, G. (2017). The effects of learning activities based on argumentation on conceptual understanding of 7th graders about “force and motion” unit and establishing thinking friendly classroom environment. European Journal of Educational Research, 6(3), 367-384. https://doi.org/10.12973/eu-jer.6.3.367

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental pretest-posttest control group study with 39 7th graders (FMCT analyzed for N=16 experimental, N=15 control). Independent samples t-test on posttest FMCT found no significant difference; descriptively the experimental group's mean misconception score fell more (8.56 to 8.00) than the control group's (9.33 to 9.00).

> "According to posttest results of FMCT, there isn’t significant difference statistically although both groups (progress of the experiment group is better than that of the control group according to decreases of means) advanced and decreased misconceptions."
