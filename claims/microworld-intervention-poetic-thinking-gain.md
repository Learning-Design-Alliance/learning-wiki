# A microworld-based intervention produced a modestly greater gain in poetic thinking than comparison teaching, with a modest effect size (d = 0.46) that was not statistically significant

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Learners following the microworld-based teaching scheme improved poetic thinking performance by 5.61% more than the comparison group. [→ Jenkins 2015](#jenkins-2015)
`q2 i2` The poetic thinking gain difference had a modest effect size (d = 0.46) with p = 0.242, not statistically significant. [→ Jenkins 2015](#jenkins-2015)

## Evidence

### Jenkins 2015

Craig Jenkins. (2015). Poem Generator: A comparative quantitative evaluation of a microworlds-based learning approach for teaching English. International Journal of Education and Development using Information and Communication Technology, 11(2), 153-167. http://ijedict.dec.uwi.edu/viewarticle.php?id=1972

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Independent samples t-test on English (poetic thinking) gain scores from the same quasi-experimental study (n = 27 across both groups). The English gain difference was p = 0.242 with Cohen's d = 0.46, which the author calls "a modest effect size (d = 0.46)".

> "a modest effect size (d = 0.46) in poetic thinking. Potentially, then, the data reveals a positive effect of the microworld intervention in terms of both computational thinking and poetic thinking in English."
