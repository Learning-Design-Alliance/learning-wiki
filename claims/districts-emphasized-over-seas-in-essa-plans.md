# Most state ESSA plans emphasized the district's role in school improvement over the state agency's role, a shift from No Child Left Behind.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the reviewed state plans, LEAs were positioned as the lead actors in school turnaround, marking a notable change from the state-led approach under NCLB. [→ Dunn 2019](#dunn-2019)

## Evidence

### Dunn 2019

Dunn, L., & Ambroso, E. (2019). Balancing act: State and district roles in school improvement under ESSA. San Francisco, CA: WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED602966

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Analysis of the 23 state ESSA plans found school improvement and turnaround "typically framed as a systemic issue with a large role for LEAs," with most plans emphasizing districts over SEAs, unlike under NCLB.

> "In fact, most state plans emphasized the role of districts in school improvement over the role of the SEA. This is a notable change from school improvement under No Child Left Behind"
