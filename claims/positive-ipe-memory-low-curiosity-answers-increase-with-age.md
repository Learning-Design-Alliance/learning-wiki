# Positive information prediction errors enhance memory for low-curiosity answers increasingly with age

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Trial-level mixed models showed IPE and curiosity each enhanced recall; for low-curiosity questions, the IPE effect on recall grew more pronounced with older age, while for high-curiosity questions no strong IPE or IPE-by-age effect emerged. [→ Fandakova Yana 2021](#fandakova-yana-2021)

## Evidence

### Fandakova Yana 2021

Fandakova Yana, Gruber Matthias J. (2021). States of curiosity and interest enhance memory differently in adolescents and in children. Developmental Science, 24(1), e13005. https://doi.org/10.1111/desc.13005

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Trial-level Bayesian mixed-effects logistic regression on single-trial recall accuracy (brms), predicting accuracy from curiosity ratings, IPE scores, and age. For low-curiosity answers the model showed "an effect of IPE on answer memory accuracy (95%CrI = [0.07,0.28], Post.Prob. = 1)" and an IPE-by-age interaction favoring older participants; for high-curiosity answers no strong IPE effect was found.

> "for answers to low-curiosity questions, we observed an effect of IPE on answer memory accuracy (95%CrI = [0.07,0.28], Post.Prob. = 1), along with an IPE-by-age interaction (95%CrI = [0.00,0.14], Post.Prob. = .98) such that older age was associated with a more pronounced effect of IPE on recall of low-curiosity answers"
