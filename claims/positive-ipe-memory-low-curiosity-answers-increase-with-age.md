## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Trial-level mixed models showed higher curiosity and higher IPE scores were each associated with better recall; for low-curiosity questions, the IPE–recall association was stronger at older ages, while for high-curiosity questions no strong IPE or IPE-by-age effect emerged. Curiosity and interest were rated, not manipulated, so these are associations, not shown causes. [→ Fandakova Yana 2021](#fandakova-yana-2021)

## Evidence

### Fandakova Yana 2021

Fandakova Yana, Gruber Matthias J. (2021). States of curiosity and interest enhance memory differently in adolescents and in children. Developmental Science, 24(1), e13005. https://doi.org/10.1111/desc.13005

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Trial-level Bayesian mixed-effects logistic regression on single-trial recall accuracy (brms), predicting accuracy from curiosity ratings, IPE scores, and age. For low-curiosity answers the model showed "an effect of IPE on answer memory accuracy (95%CrI = [0.07,0.28], Post.Prob. = 1)" and an IPE-by-age interaction favoring older participants; for high-curiosity answers no strong IPE effect was found. IPE was computed from participants' own curiosity and interest ratings rather than manipulated, so the model shows an association between IPE and recall, not that IPEs caused better memory.

> "for answers to low-curiosity questions, we observed an effect of IPE on answer memory accuracy (95%CrI = [0.07,0.28], Post.Prob. = 1), along with an IPE-by-age interaction (95%CrI = [0.00,0.14], Post.Prob. = .98) such that older age was associated with a more pronounced effect of IPE on recall of low-curiosity answers"
