Evidence¶

Richard E. West 2017¶

Richard E. West, Gregory S. Williams. (2017). 'I don't think that word means what you think it means': A proposed framework for defining learning communities. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-017-9535-0

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

The review's methodological critique notes scholars have used social network analysis, case studies, and survey research, yet "rarely are these methods used together in a mixed-methods approach". The authors recommend solid research verifying or adapting their framework to enable comparative research and measurement.

"Each of these methods contributes advantages and disadvantages to the study of learning communities, yet rarely are these methods used together in a mixed-methods approach."

Richard E. West 2017 (2)¶

Richard E. West, Gregory S. Williams. (2017). 'I don't think that word means what you think it means': A proposed framework for defining learning communities. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-017-9535-0

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review identifies "the rampant positive bias" in learning-community research: dysfunctional groups are relabeled as mere collections of persons, so the field cannot learn from non-examples. The authors quote Renshaw (2003) on communities as potentially difficult and dangerous places.

"Another weakness in current research about learning communities is the rampant positive bias."
