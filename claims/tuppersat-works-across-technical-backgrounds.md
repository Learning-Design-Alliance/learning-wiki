# The module works for students across a wide range of prior technical backgrounds, as shown by the differing CrabSat and DiscoSat approaches

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Two 2018 teams with different electronics backgrounds each built working radiation detectors suited to their skill level, supporting the authors' conclusion that the module does not depend on extensive prior technical knowledge. [→ David Murphy 2020](#david-murphy-2020)

## Evidence

### David Murphy 2020

David Murphy, Robert Jeffrey, Deirdre Coffey, Morgan Fraser, Sheila McBreen, Lorraine Hanlon. (2020). TupperSats: Thinking Inside the Box for Space Systems Engineering. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2010.03332](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2010.03332)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Authors' interpretation of the CrabSat (kit-based Geiger counter, students mostly from physics or mechanical engineering) and DiscoSat (custom scintillator detector, students with electronics experience) case studies; this is an interpretive conclusion from two team cases, not a tested comparison.

> "Both teams found solutions to the same problem that suited their own skill level, and so were able to engage with, learn from, and enjoy the project and module. In other words, the module is not dependent on students having a large amount of prior technical knowledge for it to work or for students to enjoy it."
