## Evidence

### Chorianopoulos 2018

Chorianopoulos, K. (2018). A taxonomy of asynchronous instructional video styles. The International Review of Research in Open and Distributed Learning. [doi:10.19173/irrodl.v19i1.2920](https://doi.org/10.19173/irrodl.v19i1.2920)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r?`

The review attributes to Li et al. (2016) a comparison of alternatives replacing the instructor's talking head, including animated human, animated robot, and real robot; preference favored humans but recall was mixed.

> "They found that learners preferred the real or animated hu- man condition to the real or animated robot, but the recall rates were mixed across conditions and genders."

### Chorianopoulos 2018 (2)

Chorianopoulos, K. (2018). A taxonomy of asynchronous instructional video styles. The International Review of Research in Open and Distributed Learning. [doi:10.19173/irrodl.v19i1.2920](https://doi.org/10.19173/irrodl.v19i1.2920)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r?`

The review attributes to Kizilcec et al. (2015) a comparison of constant versus moderate talking-head presence in slide-presentation videos; no significant learning-performance differences were found, with preference effects noted.

> "They found that there were no signiﬁcant differences in terms of learning performance, but it seems that some students just prefer the presence of the instructor’s face."
