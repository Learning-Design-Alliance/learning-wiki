## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Learning orientation does not differentiate FCI posttest scores, and course grades show only borderline differences among orientations (Framework vs. Performance p = 0.63; Performance vs. Vague p = 0.05). [→ Mason 2018](#mason-2018)
`q2 i?` Framework-oriented students' normalized FCI gains are statistically larger than Vague-oriented gains (p < .05), but this does not carry to posttest score differences. [→ Mason 2018](#mason-2018)

## Evidence

### Mason 2018

Mason, A. J., & Bertram, C. A. (2018). Consideration of Learning Orientations as an Application of Achievement Goals in Evaluating Life Science Majors in Introductory Physics. University of Central Arkansas, Department of Physics and Astronomy. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010125](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010125)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

FCI pre/post analysis by learning orientation (Table III, n = 218). Vague-oriented students' slight pretest advantage disappears on the posttest ("p > 0.30, all comparisons"); only the Framework vs. Vague gain contrast is significant (p < .05). No equivalence testing reported.

> "However, this advantage does not exist on the posttest scores (p > 0.30,  all comparisons), and framework-oriented students' gains are statistically larger than vague -oriented students' gains (p < .05). In other words, learning orientation does not appear to show any differences overall on the FCI posttest."
