## Evidence

### Michael B. Bennett 2024

Michael B. Bennett, Joan Étude Arrow, Sasha Novack, and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2024). Investigating Student Participation in Quantum Workforce Initiatives. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2407.14698](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2407.14698)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Cross-program interview analysis. The article prints "12 of 13 students interviewed" although it elsewhere reports 12 interviews total; the printed count is reported here as stated. Students tied partial career awareness to program interactions such as lectures and networking events.

> "12 of 13 students interviewed admitted that their understanding of QIS careers and jobs – that is, what QIS professionals do on a day-to-day basis, job opportunities for entry into the field, long-term career prospects, etc. – was appreciably underdeveloped, even after program participation."

### Michael B. Bennett 2024 (2)

Michael B. Bennett, Joan Étude Arrow, Sasha Novack, and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2024). Investigating Student Participation in Quantum Workforce Initiatives. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2407.14698](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2407.14698)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Discussion-section synthesis of the interview findings, noting the result supports a finding the authors attribute to Rosenberg et al. that students largely do not understand the quantum field or how they may fit into it.

> "students generally expressed strong interest in quantum careers despite their lack of knowledge; in particular, as described in Section IV, QRX students went from being largely unaware of QIS entirely to considering it as a possible career path."
