## Evidence

### Alexander Borg 2026

Alexander Borg, Jonathan Schiött, William Ivegren, Cidem Gentline, Viking Huss, Anna Margareta Hugelius, Benjamin Jobs, Mini Ruiz, Samuel Edelbring, Carina Georg, Gabriel Skantze, Ioannis Parodis. (2026). AI-generated Feedback Following Social Robotic Virtual Patient Interactions and Medical Student Performance: Nonrandomized Quasi-Experimental Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/90368

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Secondary outcome analysis using Mann-Whitney U tests across 5 OSCE domains with Bonferroni-adjusted threshold of P<.01. The feedback group scored "mean 2.46, SD 0.65 vs mean 2.03, SD 0.79 points" in generic medical history, r=0.27.

> "Generic medical history was the only domain that demonstrated a statistically significant difference in favor of the integration of AI-generated feedback; this difference remained significant after applying Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons (mean 2.46, SD 0.65 vs mean 2.03, SD 0.79 points; r=0.27; P=.004)."

### Alexander Borg 2026 (2)

Alexander Borg, Jonathan Schiött, William Ivegren, Cidem Gentline, Viking Huss, Anna Margareta Hugelius, Benjamin Jobs, Mini Ruiz, Samuel Edelbring, Carina Georg, Gabriel Skantze, Ioannis Parodis. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/90368

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mann-Whitney U tests on the remaining four domains found no significant differences after Bonferroni correction; the article reports "numerical advantages in most assessment domains" and suggests possible underpowering or a ceiling effect.

> "No statistically significant differences were observed for communication at consultation start, targeted medical history taking, diagnostics and management reasoning, or communication at consultation end after correction for multiple testing."
