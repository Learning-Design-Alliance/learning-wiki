## Evidence

### Andria C. Schwortz 2016

Andria C. Schwortz, Andrea C. Burrows, Sarah Katie Guffey. (2016). Mentoring partnerships in science education. Educational Action Research. https://doi.org/10.1080/09650792.2016.1221838

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Observational tally of student/instructor interactions on eight class dates, with assistants counting non-studio sections for comparison; interaction rate scaled per student per session. The article reports discussion-section values as "similar between the two forms of physics."

> "In lecture sessions, studio physics students interacted 2. 7 times more with their TA than non-studio physics students did with either of their instructors."

### Andria C. Schwortz 2016 (2)

Andria C. Schwortz, Andrea C. Burrows, Sarah Katie Guffey. (2016). Mentoring partnerships in science education. Educational Action Research. https://doi.org/10.1080/09650792.2016.1221838

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same observational tally analysis. The authors offer three possible explanations, including that studio lab numbers counted only TA interactions and might otherwise reach 0.667 versus the non-studio value of 0.538.

> "The authors found it surprising that the interaction rates f or lab sections were greater in non-studio physics than in studio physics."
