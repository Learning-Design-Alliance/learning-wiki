Evidence¶

Mokhtari Saba 2026¶

Mokhtari Saba, Mokhtari Asieh, Shariat Seyed Vahid, Nazeri Astaneh Ali, Shalbafan Mohammadreza. (2026). Innovative technologies in psychiatry education: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09573-1

q3 · i3 · review · r3

RCT with 19 psychiatry residents randomized to HFS (n = 10) or traditional training (n = 9) for ECT, with blinded observers using the ECT-OSATS checklist. The review reports "p < 0.001, Cohen d = 2.8" for procedural skill but no significant knowledge or confidence differences.

"The residents in the simulation group scored significantly higher on the ECT-OSATS global rating, with all 10 trainees passing the assessment. In contrast, only one out of nine in the control group passed (p < 0.001, Cohen d = 2.8). However, no significant differences were detected between the groups in knowledge scores (p = 0.36) or self-reported confidence (p = 0.21)."

Mokhtari Saba 2026 (2)¶

Mokhtari Saba, Mokhtari Asieh, Shariat Seyed Vahid, Nazeri Astaneh Ali, Shalbafan Mohammadreza. (2026). Innovative technologies in psychiatry education: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09573-1

q3 · i? · review · r3

Block-RCT with 35 psychiatry residents assigned to SIM-ECT (n = 18) or traditional didactic training (n = 17). The review reports knowledge rising from 51% to 69% (p = 0.02) and procedural skills from 22% to 51% (p < 0.001). Rated low risk of bias (RoB 2).

"Knowledge scores increased from 51% to 69% (p = 0.02), and procedural skills improved from 22% to 51% (p < 0.001). Confidence and comfort levels also increased in the simulation group, highlighting the benefits of using HFS in psychiatry education."
