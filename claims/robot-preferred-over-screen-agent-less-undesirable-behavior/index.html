Evidence¶

Michael J Timms 2016¶

Michael J Timms. (2016). Letting Artificial Intelligence in Education Out of the Box: Educational Cobots and Smart Classrooms. Int J Artif Intell Educ 26:701–712. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-016-0095-y

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

The paper reports, citing Kiesler et al. (2008), a study comparing interaction with a humanoid robot versus an on-screen agent in social assistive situations. Participants also 'were more inhibited with the robot than with the agent and showed less socially undesirable behaviour'.

"found that people preferred interaction with a robot than a comparable on-screen agent. They attributed the robot with stronger, more positive personality traits and felt it was more lifelike."

Related Claims¶

  • A conceptual framework paper reports, citing a meta-analysis (Roesler et al., 2021), that anthropomorphism benefits human-robot interaction with effects varying across task contexts and design implementations — related
  • Different humanlike design dimensions cue distinct classes of anthropomorphism inferences — related
  • Human embodiment in video: perceived social presence benefits learning ratings, but instructor-face inclusion shows no significant learning-performance difference, and learners prefer human over robot presenters with mixed recall — related
  • Hypotheses that specific robot appearances would be trusted more in matching tasks (humanoids for teaching, task-oriented robots for instructions, soft robots for personal disclosure) were not confirmed — related
  • Manipulating anthropomorphism shows no clear causal effect on trust in controlled post-generative-AI studies, though long-term use and cue type condition its effects — related
