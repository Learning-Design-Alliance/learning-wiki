## Evidence

### Bernard Aaron W. 2017

Bernard Aaron W., Ceccolini Gabbriel, Feinn Richard, Rockfeld Jennifer, Rosenberg Ilene, Thomas Listy, Cassese Todd. (2017). Medical students review of formative OSCE scores, checklists, and videos improves with student-faculty debriefing meetings. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2017.1324718

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear mixed-model analysis of 92 first-year students' timely data review before subsequent F-OSCEs; checklist regression coefficient 1.07 (SE 0.51, t = 2.09, p = .038); score and video coefficients non-significant. The authors question whether 1.07 points is clinically meaningful.

> "For year-1 students, the checklist review was associated with improved performance on the subsequent FOSCE (p = 0.038) by 1.07 percentage points on a scale of 0–100, but score and video review were not associated with improvement."

### Bernard Aaron W. 2017 (2)

Bernard Aaron W., Ceccolini Gabbriel, Feinn Richard, Rockfeld Jennifer, Rosenberg Ilene, Thomas Listy, Cassese Todd. (2017). Medical students review of formative OSCE scores, checklists, and videos improves with student-faculty debriefing meetings. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2017.1324718

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear mixed-model analysis of 86 second-year students found no significant association for any modality (checklist p = .184, score p = .979, video p = .490). No effect sizes were printed, so the null indicates non-detection, not measured equivalence.

> "For year-2 students, no review modality was significantly associated with improved performance on a subsequent F-OSCE"
