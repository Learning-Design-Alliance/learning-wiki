# In one South African hospital's obstetrics and paediatrics internship, knower-code attributes (teamwork, organisation, efficiency, communication) outweighed specialised knowledge as the basis of legitimacy

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among 15 first-year interns in the obstetrics and gynaecology and paediatrics departments of one regional public hospital in Johannesburg, knower-code attributes (ER–, SR+), that is personal and social attributes rather than specialised knowledge (ER+), were the greatest determinants of legitimacy within the clinical team; the authors note the single site may limit generalisability. ## Evidence

### Pattinson 2025

Pattinson, S. R., Savelberg, H., & Atherley, A. (2025). Readiness for the Realities of Internship: An Ethnographic Study Using Legitimation Code Theory. Perspectives on Medical Education, 14(1), 628–640. https://doi.org/10.5334/pme.1802

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Focused ethnography (participant observation, shadowing, informal conversations over seven weeks; semi-structured interviews with 13 of 15 interns) analysed via reflexive thematic analysis with LCT specialisation coding. The authors report that knower-code attributes were "the greatest determinants of legitimacy within the internship setting".

> "While the interns who seemed to be succeeding generally demonstrated elite code competencies (ER+, SR+), it was specifically the knower code (ER–, SR+) attributes which were the greatest determinants of legitimacy within the internship setting."
