## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Most students supported the false Newtonian statement that gas molecules' positions and velocities allow exact prediction of future behavior, and the statement that nobody can know an electron's position accurately because it is small and fast. [→ Bayram Akarsu 2010](#bayram-akarsu-2010)

## Evidence

### Bayram Akarsu 2010

Bayram Akarsu. (2010). An extensive Study of Teaching / Learning Quantum Mechanics in College. Originally published 2007. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1002.4975](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1002.4975)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive analysis of the 86-student questionnaire responses. The author states both statements are false under quantum theory because the Heisenberg uncertainty principle makes exact electron position unidentifiable; no percentage or test statistic is printed for this result.

> "most of the students supported the statement 'if a container has a few gas molecules in it and we know their instantaneous positions and velocities then we can use the Newtonian mechanics to predict exactly how they behave as time goes by' and 'nobody knows the position accurately of an electron in orbit around the nucleus because it is very small and moves very fast'."
