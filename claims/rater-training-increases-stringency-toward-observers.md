## Evidence

### Weitz G 2014

Weitz G, Vinzentius C, Twesten C, Lehnert H, Bonnemeier H, König IR. (2014). Effects of a rater training on rating accuracy in a physical examination skills assessment. GMS Zeitschrift für Medizinische Ausbildung. [doi:10.3205/zma000933](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma000933)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Reported medians with quartiles from the randomised trial: trained raters' median grade was 2 [1-;2-] versus untrained raters 2+ [1;2], the trained group's grading lying nearer the observers' median of 2 [1-;2-].

> "The median overall grading [1st;3rd quartile] of the trained raters was 2 [1-;2-] and of the untrained raters 2+ [1;2], respectively."

### Weitz G 2014 (2)

Weitz G, Vinzentius C, Twesten C, Lehnert H, Bonnemeier H, König IR. (2014). Effects of a rater training on rating accuracy in a physical examination skills assessment. GMS Zeitschrift für Medizinische Ausbildung. [doi:10.3205/zma000933](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma000933)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

GEE model on real-life assessments shows trained raters were significantly more stringent (β=-0.94 ±0.36, P=0.01); the article notes this stringency was "significantly closer to the observers' gradings" and interprets it as a partial standardisation of the frame of reference.

> "In the generalised estimating equations model, the trained raters were more stringent than those without the training (β=-0.94 ±0.36, P=0.01)."
