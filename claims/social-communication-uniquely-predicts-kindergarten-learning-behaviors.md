# Social communication skills uniquely predict teacher-rated kindergarten learning behaviors after accounting for vocabulary

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Initial levels (β = .52, p < .001) and rate of growth (β = .42, p < .001) in social communication skills predicted learning behaviors, while vocabulary level (β = −.05, p = .57) and growth (β = .02, p = .76) did not. [→ Ramsook K. Ashana 2020](#ramsook-k-ashana-2020)

## Evidence

### Ramsook K. Ashana 2020

Ramsook K. Ashana, Welsh Janet A., Bierman Karen L. (2020). What you say, and how you say it: Preschoolers' growth in vocabulary and communication skills differentially predict kindergarten academic achievement and self‐regulation. Social Development, 29(3), 783–800. https://doi.org/10.1111/sode.12425

`q3 · i?`

Multilevel models predicting Wave 3 teacher-rated learning behaviors (School Readiness Questionnaire and reverse-scored ADHD Inattention composite) in 164 Head Start children. "Initial levels of social communication skills were significantly associated with learning behaviors at Wave 3" while vocabulary was not.

> "Initial levels of social communication skills were significantly associated with learning behaviors at Wave 3 (β = .52, SE = .18, p < .001), but initial levels of vocabulary were not (β = −.05, SE = .01, p = .57)."
