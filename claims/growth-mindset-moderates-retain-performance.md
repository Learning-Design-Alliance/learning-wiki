# Self-reported growth mindset moderated HCPs' task performance in the RETAIN video game

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among 50 HCPs playing the RETAIN video game, participants who endorsed a higher level of growth mindset made fewer mistakes, and growth mindset moderated the relationship between task performance and time since their last refresher NRP course. [→ Ghoman SK 2020](#ghoman-sk-2020)

## Evidence

### Ghoman SK 2020

Ghoman SK, Patel SD, Cutumisu M, von Hauff P, Jeffery T, Brown MRG, Schmölzer GM. (2020). Serious games, a game changer in teaching neonatal resuscitation? A review. Archives of Disease in Childhood. Fetal and Neonatal Edition. https://doi.org/10.1136/archdischild-2019-317011

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Study of 50 HCPs from the Royal Alexandra Hospital who completed a tutorial, three rounds of the RETAIN game and a postgame growth-mindset questionnaire out of 10; the review reports the printed mean and the moderation finding. No effect size is printed.

> "Participants reported high levels of growth mindset (mean=9.17). Participants made fewer mistakes in the game if they endorsed a higher level of growth mindset."
