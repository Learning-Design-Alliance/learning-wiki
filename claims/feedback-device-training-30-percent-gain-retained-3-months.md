# CPR overall and compression scores rose by roughly 30% from pre-test to follow-up after training with a feedback device, in one uncontrolled pre-post study, with skills largely retained at 3 months

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Overall score rose significantly from pre-test to post-test (mean difference 32.4%, 95% CI 25.3–39.5, p < 0.001) and pre-test to follow-up (mean difference 29.6%, 95% CI 22.6–36.7, p < 0.001), with no significant post-to-follow-up difference. ## Evidence

### Akizuki Kazunori 2019

Akizuki Kazunori, Yamamoto Ryohei, Yamaguchi Kazuto, Yabuki Jun, Ohashi Yukari. (2019). The effects of feedback timing and frequency on the acquisition of cardiopulmonary resuscitation skills of health sciences undergraduate students: A 2 x 2 factorial quasi randomized study. PLoS ONE 14(7): e0220004. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0220004

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Tukey post hoc comparisons following the significant measurement-sequence main effect on test-session overall scores. Gains of about 30% were retained at 3 months (post-to-follow-up difference 2.8%, p = 1.00). Mean differences are descriptive; no standardized effect size printed.

> "significant differences were observed between the pre-test and post-test (mean difference: 32.4%, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 25.3–39.5, p < 0.001), and between the pre-test and follow-up test (mean difference: 29.6%, 95% CI: 22.6–36.7, p < 0.001)"
