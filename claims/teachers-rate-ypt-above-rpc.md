# Teachers rate YPT-related activities as more useful than RPC across all hard-skill types, with a self-selection caveat

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2022 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teachers rate YPT-related activities more useful than RPC for designing experiments, data analysis, theoretical modelling, simulations, literature research, and critical assessment (all p < .001), with no significant difference for cookbook experiments and high school physics, and RPC rated better for mathematics and solving close-ended problems. [→ Boyka Aneva 2022](#boyka-aneva-2022)

## Evidence

### Boyka Aneva 2022

Boyka Aneva, Sergej Faletič, Mihály Hömöstrei, Péter Jenei, Isza Éva, František Kundracik, Assen Kyuldjiev, Thomas Lindner, Hynek Němec, Harald Puhr, and Martin Plesch. (2022). Development of Scientific Skills via IYPT: How does YPT participation lead to hard-skill development? ELTE University Budapest. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2212.09076](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2212.09076)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Teacher survey (33 responses) with paired t-tests/Wilcoxon tests comparing RPC and YPT usefulness; e.g. developing own theoretical model t = -9.332, df = 32, p < .001, while cookbook experiments showed no significant difference (t = 0.291, p = 0.773).

> "Across all types of hard skills, we observe greater perceived usefulness for YPT-related activities than for regular physics classes."
