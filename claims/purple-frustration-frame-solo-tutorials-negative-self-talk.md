### Josephine C. Meyer 2026

Josephine C. Meyer, Steven J. Pollock, Bethany R. Wilcox, and Gina Passante. (2026). Computer-generated QIS tutorial feedback is valued by students, but does not replicate in-class collaboration. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2607.15444](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.15444)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Observational coding of solo AcePhysics think-aloud interviews using color frames. Examples include Diana remaining focused on a minor decimal error and critiquing her equivalent answer's aesthetics, and Carlos's inward critique after misremembering an operator matrix.

> "However, students completing the AcePhysics solo tutorial tended to double down on negative self-talk to the exclusion of productive reflection."

### Josephine C. Meyer 2026 (2)

Josephine C. Meyer, Steven J. Pollock, Bethany R. Wilcox, and Gina Passante. (2026). Computer-generated QIS tutorial feedback is valued by students, but does not replicate in-class collaboration. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2607.15444](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.15444)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The article contrasts the two conditions: the newly described "frustration" frame, often activated by incorrect-answer feedback or boredom, appeared in solo tutorials but not in the group pencil-and-paper tutorial, where Green and Yellow frames predominated after mistakes.

> "In contrast, the Purple frame was not seen to a meaningful extent in the group discussion."
