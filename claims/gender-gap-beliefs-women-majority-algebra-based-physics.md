## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Female students had statistically significantly lower mean scores than male students on pre/post self-efficacy, interest, perceived recognition, peer interaction, belonging, and physics identity (all p < 0.001). [→ Sonja Cwik 2022](#sonja-cwik-2022)

## Evidence

### Sonja Cwik 2022

Sonja Cwik, Chandralekha Singh. (2022). Role of inclusiveness of learning environment in predicting students' outcomes in courses in which women are not underrepresented. [doi:10.33423/jhetp.v22i17.5666](https://doi.org/10.33423/jhetp.v22i17.5666)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Pre/post survey of 501 students (35% male, 65% female) in physics 1 for bioscience majors; t-tests with Cohen's d. Table 3 prints d values: pre self-efficacy 0.58, belonging 0.60, post self-efficacy 0.66, post interest 0.69, identity 0.59, recognition 0.43, all p < 0.001. The authors note this pattern is "qualitatively similar" to calculus-based courses.

> "the mean values of all constructs in our model were statistically significantly different disadvantaging female students (TABLE 3)"
