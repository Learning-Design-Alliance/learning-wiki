# Lack of instructor knowledge is a new systemic force for computation integration, manifesting as lack of coding experience or as lack of activity-design expertise for novice programmers

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Instructors who want to integrate computation but lack coding experience feel uncomfortable teaching fundamentals they do not know themselves. [→ Ashleigh Leary 2019](#ashleigh-leary-2019)
`q2 i?` Even instructors who can code may not know how to design activities for students just learning computation or how to tie computational lessons to scheduled physics lessons, given heterogeneous student skill levels. [→ Ashleigh Leary 2019 (2)](#ashleigh-leary-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Ashleigh Leary 2019

Ashleigh Leary, Paul W. Irving, and Marcos D. Caballero. (2019). The difficulties associated with integrating computation into undergraduate physics. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1119/perc.2018.pr.leary](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2018.pr.leary)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview evidence of the first manifestation: an instructor who was unprepared for GlowScript himself "just quickly backed off and went back to vPython," illustrating discomfort teaching tools one is not expert in.

> "The only snag that I hit was I decided to experiment with GlowScript. And, and you know, my students are... getting comfortable with vPython, then I said, you know, you could use GlowScript...but I wasn't too prepared for GlowScript myself. And so I just quickly backed off and went back to vPython."

### Ashleigh Leary 2019 (2)

Ashleigh Leary, Paul W. Irving, and Marcos D. Caballero. (2019). The difficulties associated with integrating computation into undergraduate physics. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1119/perc.2018.pr.leary](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2018.pr.leary)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview evidence of the second manifestation: a coding-capable instructor needed help with activity design decisions such as breaking material into exercises and timing, not technical language; the article notes design skill develops through trial and error.

> "I didn't need help so much with the technical language stuff, but like, you know, How should I... You know, Should I break this up into multiple exercises? How long would this take? How much time should I dedicate to this?"
