# Preschoolers with autism given a multi-component communication intervention used more social communicative utterances with their caregivers than comparison children 4 months after it ended, a small difference that was not significant immediately after intervention

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` At the 4-month follow-up, intervention-group children used significantly more social communicative utterances with their caregivers (SCU-CCX) than comparison children (β = 0.84, p = 0.049; d = 0.259, small), primarily in spoken language; immediately after intervention the same difference was not significant (p = 0.076), and SCU in the examiner language sample did not differ at either point. ## Evidence

### Hampton et al. 2020

Lauren H Hampton, Ann P Kaiser and Elizabeth A Fuller. (2020). Multi-component communication intervention for children with autism: A randomized controlled trial. Autism, 24(8), 2104–2116. https://doi.org/10.1177/1362361320934558

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial of a 36-session multi-component intervention (caregiver training, Discrete Trial Teaching, JASP + EMT + SGD) for 3- to 5-year-olds with autism against a community comparison group. 73 children were randomized (37 intervention, 36 comparison); 5 dropped out before baseline assessment, so 68 were analysed intent-to-treat with multiple imputation, in regressions controlling for autism severity and pretest. SCU-CCX did not differ significantly at posttest (β = 0.63, p = 0.076) and did at follow-up (β = 0.84, p = 0.049). The discussion reports 'Although this difference was small (d = 0.259), the improvement was observed primarily in spoken language'.

> "There was a significant difference between groups in SCU-CCX (p = 0.049) but no significant between-group differences on SCU-NLS (p = 0.460)"
