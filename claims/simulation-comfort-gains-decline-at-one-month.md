## Evidence

### Kerr Brendan 2013

Kerr Brendan, Hawkins Trisha Lee-Ann, Herman Robert, Barnes Sue, Kaufmann Stephanie, Fraser Kristin, Ma Irene W. Y. (2013). Feasibility of scenario-based simulation training versus traditional workshops in continuing medical education: a randomized controlled trial. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.3402/meo.v18i0.21312

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

One-month retention data were available for only eight participants (four per group); the article reports comfort measures "decreased at 1-month for the simulation group" while the traditional group's rose steadily across the three time points.

> "While these measures increased post-workshop, they decreased at 1-month for the simulation group (Fig. 2b–d, respectively)."

### Kerr Brendan 2013 (2)

Kerr Brendan, Hawkins Trisha Lee-Ann, Herman Robert, Barnes Sue, Kaufmann Stephanie, Fraser Kristin, Ma Irene W. Y. (2013). Feasibility of scenario-based simulation training versus traditional workshops in continuing medical education: a randomized controlled trial. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.3402/meo.v18i0.21312

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Mixed-factor repeated-measures ANOVA on the N=8 retention subset found a significant main effect of scores but "no effect of group" and no significant scores-by-group interaction; post-hoc comparisons showed no differences (p>0.05 in all instances).

> "For all retention scores, results from mixed ANOVA indicated a significant main effect of scores but no effect of group. No significant interaction between scores and group was found."
