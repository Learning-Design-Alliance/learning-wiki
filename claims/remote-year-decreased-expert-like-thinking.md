## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Teemu Hynninen 2023

Teemu Hynninen, Henna Pesonen, Olli Lintu, and Petriina Paturi. (2023). Ongoing eﬀects of pandemic-imposed learning disruption on student attitudes. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010101](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010101)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pre/post CLASS comparison of the 2020–2021 cohort (N = 30) taught fully remotely; the article reports that "the portion of favorable answers are seen to decrease while unfavorable answers become more common" and that not a single category shows any positive gains.

> "The results are very diﬀerent when no contact teaching was possible, as the portion of favorable answers are seen to decrease while unfavorable answers become more common."

### Teemu Hynninen 2023 (2)

Teemu Hynninen, Henna Pesonen, Olli Lintu, and Petriina Paturi. (2023). Ongoing eﬀects of pandemic-imposed learning disruption on student attitudes. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010101](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010101)

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Cohen's d effect sizes from pre- to post-survey for the no-contact cohort; "The strongest increase in unfavorable answers is seen for conceptual connections where the eﬀect size is 1.0" (d = 1.0), driven by agreement with the memorization statement rising from 10 % to 40 %.

> "The strongest increase in unfavorable answers is seen for conceptual connections where the eﬀect size is 1.0."

### Teemu Hynninen 2023 (3)

Teemu Hynninen, Henna Pesonen, Olli Lintu, and Petriina Paturi. (2023). Ongoing eﬀects of pandemic-imposed learning disruption on student attitudes. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010101](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010101)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Distribution analysis of overall favorable CLASS scores (Fig. 3) for the no-contact cohort: the post-survey distribution broadened as "the number of students scoring between 60 % and 90 % has decreased", and the change distribution showed more negative than positive changes.

> "That is, the number of students scoring between 60 % and 90 % has decreased while those scoring between 50 % and 60 % has increased."
