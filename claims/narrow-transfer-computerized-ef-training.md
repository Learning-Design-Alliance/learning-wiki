# Transfer from computer-based working-memory and reasoning training in children is narrow: gains stay within the trained domain

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Training on working memory improves untrained working-memory tasks but not inhibition, and probably not reasoning; reasoning training does not improve working memory or processing speed. [→ Diamond Adele 2012](#diamond-adele-2012)
`q2 i?` Nonverbal-reasoning training transfers to the same type of nonverbal reasoning but not to a different type, and nonverbal working-memory training did not transfer to verbal working memory. [→ Diamond Adele 2012](#diamond-adele-2012)

## Evidence

### Diamond Adele 2012

Diamond Adele. (2012). Activities and Programs That Improve Children's Executive Functions. Current Directions in Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721412453722

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review synthesis of computer-based training studies; the review reports that working-memory training "does not improve inhibition" and only probably not reasoning, citing Klingberg et al. (2005) as a possible exception.

> "In children, training on working memory improves performance on untrained working memory tasks, but it does not improve inhibition (Thorell et al., 2009) and probably does not improve reasoning or problem solving (Bergman Nutley et al., 2011; Thorell et al., 2009; but see Klingberg et al., 2005)."
