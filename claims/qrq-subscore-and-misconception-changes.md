## Evidence

### Masel J. 2015

Masel J., Humphrey P. T., Blackburn B., Levine J. A. (2015). Evidence-Based Medicine as a Tool for Undergraduate Probability and Statistics Education. CBE—Life Sciences Education 14(4). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-04-0079

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVA of individual QRQ subscores in the Spring 2014 course; Table 4 shows the correlation/causation subscore rose from 3.57 to 4.03 (p = 0.018) and sampling variability from 2.56 to 3.33 (p < 0.001). The article reports "QRQ subscores that improved included distinguishing correlation and causation"; no effect size is printed, so impact is uncoded.

> "QRQ subscores that improved included distinguishing correlation and causation, a task for which statistically significant deteriorations have previously been observed (Delmas et al., 2007)."

### Masel J. 2015 (2)

Masel J., Humphrey P. T., Blackburn B., Levine J. A. (2015). Evidence-Based Medicine as a Tool for Undergraduate Probability and Statistics Education. CBE—Life Sciences Education 14(4). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-04-0079

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Cross-semester comparison of QRQ misconception subscores; the equiprobability bias subscore did not improve significantly in either year (Table 4: p = 0.562). The article calls it "a consistent underperformer across both semesters"; no effect size is printed, so impact is uncoded.

> "A consistent underperformer across both semesters was equiprobability bias, which we intend to target more actively next time."
