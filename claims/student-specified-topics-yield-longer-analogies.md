# Analogies generated with a student-specified topic are longer on average (235.5 words) than analogies where the LLM selects the topic (179.2 words)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Specifying a topic in the prompt was associated with a 31.4% increase in average analogy word count (from 179.2 to 235.5 words). [→ Seth Bernstein 2024](#seth-bernstein-2024)

## Evidence

### Seth Bernstein 2024

Seth Bernstein, Paul Denny, Juho Leinonen, Lauren Kan, Arto Hellas, Matt Littlefield, Sami Sarsa, and Stephen MacNeil. (2024). “Like a Nesting Doll”: Analyzing Recursion Analogies Generated by CS Students using Large Language Models. [doi:10.1145/3649217.3653533](https://doi.org/10.1145/3649217.3653533)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive comparison of word counts among the 385 coded analogies from the recursion lab. The authors report GPT-Selected analogies averaged 179.2 words, and "the average w ord count increased to 235.5 (a 31.4% increase)" when students specified the topic.

> "The average word count for analogies where the topic was not speciﬁed ( GPT-Selected) was 179.2 words. When students speciﬁed the topic, the average w ord count increased to 235.5 (a 31.4% increase)."
