## Evidence

### Sotunsa JO 2016

Sotunsa JO, Vidler M, Akeju DO, Osiberu MO, Orenuga EO, Oladapo OT, Qureshi R, Sawchuck D, Adetoro OO, von Dadelszen P, Dada OA, the CLIP Nigeria Feasibility Working Group. (2016). Community health workers’ knowledge and practice in relation to pre-eclampsia in Ogun State, Nigeria: an essential bridge to maternal survival. Reproductive Health 13(Suppl 2). https://doi.org/10.1186/s12978-016-0218-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative finding from a CHEW focus group in Imeko-Afon: CHEWs “claimed to be comfortable managing pre-eclampsia and referring when necessary,” illustrated by a participant account of women accepting treatment or referral.

> "Overall, CHEWs claimed to be comfortable managing pre-eclampsia and referring when necessary. This is demonstrated by the statement below: If they have high blood pressure, if it’s something that we can still manage, they will accept the treatment, otherwise anywhere you refer them to is where they will go."

### Sotunsa JO 2016 (2)

Sotunsa JO, Vidler M, Akeju DO, Osiberu MO, Orenuga EO, Oladapo OT, Qureshi R, Sawchuck D, Adetoro OO, von Dadelszen P, Dada OA, the CLIP Nigeria Feasibility Working Group. (2016). Community health workers’ knowledge and practice in relation to pre-eclampsia in Ogun State, Nigeria: an essential bridge to maternal survival. Reproductive Health 13(Suppl 2). https://doi.org/10.1186/s12978-016-0218-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative finding from interviews with Head CHEWs: supervisors stated “additional training is required for CHEWs to effectively identify and manage women suffering from pre-eclampsia,” covering warning-signal identification and medication administration.

> "CHEW supervisors stated that additional training is required for CHEWs to effectively identify and manage women suffering from pre-eclampsia. This training should include identification of warning signals and medication administration."

### Sotunsa JO 2016 (3)

Sotunsa JO, Vidler M, Akeju DO, Osiberu MO, Orenuga EO, Oladapo OT, Qureshi R, Sawchuck D, Adetoro OO, von Dadelszen P, Dada OA, the CLIP Nigeria Feasibility Working Group. (2016). Community health workers’ knowledge and practice in relation to pre-eclampsia in Ogun State, Nigeria: an essential bridge to maternal survival. Reproductive Health 13(Suppl 2). https://doi.org/10.1186/s12978-016-0218-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative finding from a focus group with SOGON representatives: they argued CHEW training “should be comprehensive and include identification, triage and treatment,” focused predominantly on clinical skills, with monitoring and retraining.

> "Representatives of the Society of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists of Nigeria (SOGON) argued that the training of CHEWs should be comprehensive and include identification, triage and treatment for women with pre-eclampsia and eclampsia. The suggested training should focus predominantly on clinical skills."
