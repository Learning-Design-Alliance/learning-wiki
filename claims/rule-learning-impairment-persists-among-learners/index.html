Evidence¶

Kit S. Double 2025¶

Kit S. Double, Micah B. Goldwater, Damian P. Birney. (2025). Reactivity to confidence ratings: evidence of impaired rule-learning. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09413-5

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Analysis restricted to the 557 (78%) participants who met the learning criterion adopted from Little and McDaniel (2015). "Pairwise comparisons indicated that the only significant differences were for the ambiguous transfer items, where, again, the confidence rating group performed significantly worse than both the JOL group and the no-rating group." A post-hoc power analysis indicated > 99% power for the interaction effect.

"There was also a main effect of condition, F(2,554) = 7.97, p <.001, ηp 2 = 0.028 and a sig - nificant interaction between condition and item type, F(4,1108) = 3.45, p =.008, ηp 2 = 0.012, see Table 3 for group mean and pairwise comparisons."

Related Claims¶

  • Confidence ratings do not impede category learning per se: no condition differences in training accuracy or on unambiguous and trained transfer items
  • Reactivity differs by rating scope: item-focused confidence ratings impaired rule learning while task-focused judgments of learning did not
