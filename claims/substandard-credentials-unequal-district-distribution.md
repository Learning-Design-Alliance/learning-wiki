# Teachers on substandard credentials are unequally distributed, concentrated in districts serving more low-income students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` About one in three new-hire teachers statewide was on a substandard credential in 2017–18; districts where more than half of new hires had substandard credentials served more low-income students (72% vs. 52%) than districts hiring none. [→ Carver-Thomas 2020](#carver-thomas-2020)

## Evidence

### Carver-Thomas 2020

Carver-Thomas, D., Kini, T., & Burns, D. (2020). Sharpening the divide: How California's teacher shortages expand inequality. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/sharp-divide-california-teacher-shortages

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

LPI analysis of CDE staff demographic and credential records (Figure 2) shows district-level distribution of new hires on substandard credentials; roughly 15% of districts hired none, while a roughly equal number hired mostly underprepared teachers, and these served "many more students from low-income families (72% vs. 52%)".

> "In a roughly equal number of districts, representing many more students from low-income families (72% vs. 52%), more than half of new hires had substandard credentials."
