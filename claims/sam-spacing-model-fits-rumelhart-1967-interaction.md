## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The SAM spacing model provides a good fit to the Rumelhart (1967) data (χ² = 38.01, df = 34) and reproduces the interaction between spacing interval and retention interval. [→ Raaijmakers 2003](#raaijmakers-2003)

## Evidence

### Raaijmakers 2003

Raaijmakers, J.G.W. (2003). Spacing and repetition effects in human memory: application of the SAM model. Cognitive Science 27 (2003) 431–452. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0364-0213(03)00007-7

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Numerical model fit to Rumelhart's (1967) continuous paired-associate experiment (66 CVC-digit items, six presentations, 50 subjects). The article reports "the χ 2 -value of 38.01 (df = 34)" for the SAM spacing model's predictions.

> "The fit of the model is obviously quite good as indicated by the χ 2 -value of 38.01 (df = 34; if the data from the first guessing trial are also taken into account as in Rumelhart (1967): χ 2 = 47.76, df = 42)."

### Raaijmakers 2003 (2)

Raaijmakers, J.G.W. (2003). Spacing and repetition effects in human memory: application of the SAM model. Cognitive Science 27 (2003) 431–452. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0364-0213(03)00007-7

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

In the same fitted dataset, at a retention interval of 10, Trial 4 performance for massed-start items was 0.777 versus 0.847 for spaced-start items, which the model predicted (0.788 vs. 0.841). The article calls this "clear evidence for an interaction effect".

> "performance on those items that began with a spacing of 1-1 (massed presentation) was clearly worse than the performance on the items that started with spaced presentation (10-10): 0.777 versus 0.847"
