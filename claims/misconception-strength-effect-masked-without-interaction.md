## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In M1 the fixed effect of misconception strength on posttest correctness was not significant (coefficient 0.083, SE 0.050, OR 1.087), but in M2 with interactions the MS effect became significant and negative (OR 0.407). [→ Chen 2020](#chen-2020)

## Evidence

### Chen 2020

Chen, C., Sonnert, G., Sadler, P. M., Sunbury, S. (2020). The Impact of High School Life Science Teachers’ Subject Matter Knowledge and Knowledge of Student Misconceptions on Students’ Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-08-0164

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Comparison of the item-level models M1 (no interactions) and M2 (with SMK-only × MS and SMK&KOSM × MS cross-level interactions) in Table 3, after false discovery rate adjustment. The article notes only the interaction model revealed the opposing slopes by teacher knowledge group.

> "In the main effects model, misconception strength appeared to be unrelated to the correct rate, but once we broke down our sample by the teachers’ KOSM (and SMK), we found a bifurcated relationship between misconception and correct rate."
