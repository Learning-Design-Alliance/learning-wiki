## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among diagnosed youth, two or more comorbid disorders elevated risk for slow PS (OR = 2.20, p < .001) while one comorbid disorder did not (OR = 1.19, p = .42). [→ Braaten EB 2020](#braaten-eb-2020)

## Evidence

### Braaten EB 2020

Braaten EB, Ward AK, Forchelli G, Vuijk PJ, Cook NE, McGuinness P, Lee BA, Samkavitz A, Lind H, O'Keefe SM, Doyle AE. (2020). Characteristics of child psychiatric outpatients with slow processing speed and potential mechanisms of academic impact. European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00787-019-01455-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Logistic regression in 636 diagnosed youth compared comorbidity levels, controlling for age, sex, and medication. Similar patterns held within ADHD (two or more additional diagnoses: OR = 1.88, p = .02) and anxiety (OR = 2.72, p = .04), but not ASD.

> "the presence of any second diagnosis (i.e., two conditions) over any one condition did not significantly increase the risk for slow PS (OR = 1.19, 95% CI 0.77 – 1.85, p = .42); however, having any 3 or more disorders did increase risk for slow PS (OR = 2.20, 95% CI 1.39 – 3.48, p < .001)."
