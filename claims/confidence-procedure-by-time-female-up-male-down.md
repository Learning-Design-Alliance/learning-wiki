# Confidence in female catheterization increased over time in both groups while confidence in male catheterization decreased, showing a significant procedure-by-time interaction (P < .001)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There was a statistically significant interaction between procedure (female vs male catheterization) and time for confidence levels (F2,56.5 = 19.13, P < .001): female-catheterization confidence rose for both groups while male-catheterization confidence fell. [→ Hansen M 2011](#hansen-m-2011)
`q3 i?` There was no statistically significant group-by-procedure-by-time or group-by-time interaction for confidence levels. [→ Hansen M 2011 (2)](#hansen-m-2011-2)

## Evidence

### Hansen M 2011

Hansen M, Oosthuizen G, Windsor J, Doherty I, Greig S, McHardy K, McCann L. (2011). Enhancement of Medical Interns' Levels of Clinical Skills Competence and Self-Confidence Levels via Video iPods: Pilot Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.1596

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Repeated-measures ANOVA of confidence levels (N = 21) across pre-, post-, and 3-month follow-up, reported in Table 5: procedure-by-time F2,56.5 = 19.13, P < .001. "the confidence scores in performing the female catheterization procedure increased for both groups."

> "Over time the confidence scores in performing the female catheterization procedure increased for both groups, while the confidence scores in performing the male catheterization procedure decreased."

### Hansen M 2011 (2)

Hansen M, Oosthuizen G, Windsor J, Doherty I, Greig S, McHardy K, McCann L. (2011). Enhancement of Medical Interns' Levels of Clinical Skills Competence and Self-Confidence Levels via Video iPods: Pilot Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.1596

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Null ANOVA interaction results for confidence levels (N = 21), Table 5: group-by-procedure-by-time F2,53.7 = 0.49, P = .62 and group-by-time F2,55.4 = 0.33, P = .72. Equivalence was not tested.

> "there was no statistically significant interaction between group (control vs intervention), procedure (female vs male catheterization), and time (post- vs 3-month follow-up course). Neither was there a statistically significant interaction between group (control vs intervention) and time"
