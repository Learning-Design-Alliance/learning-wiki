# The repetition effect is specific to individual stimuli: repeated items speed up relative to new-item controls

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In lexical decision experiments, reaction times to repeated items decreased substantially relative to new-item controls, supporting item-based rather than process-based learning. [→ Logan 1988](#logan-1988)

## Evidence

### Logan 1988

Logan, G. D. (1988). Toward an instance theory of automatization. Psychological Review, 95(4), 492-527. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.95.4.492

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Experiment 1, lexical decision on the same 10 words and 10 nonwords presented 16 times each, with new words and nonwords in control blocks. Effects appeared for nonwords as well as words.

> "the specific practice effect was much stronger: Reaction times to repeated items decreased substantially over blocks, both absolutely and relative to the new-item controls"
