## Evidence

### Fritúz Gábor 2026

Fritúz Gábor, Kovács Gergely, Kállai András, Nagy Tamás, Maár Csaba, Szvath Petra, Berczi Máté, Fábry Szabolcs, Skultéti Dalma, Iványi Zsolt, Gál János, Kovács Enikő. (2026). The efficacy of BLS training among fifth-year medical students—a randomized, assessor-blinded, parallel group trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08606-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

First skill retention assessment two weeks after the intervention (n=20). Table 2 reports 100% (9/9) of the additional practice group versus 36% (4/11) of the practical examination group placed a surgical mask on the patient's face (p = 0.005).

> "Significantly more students placed a surgical mask on patients’ faces at the beginning of the BLS process in the additional practice group than in practical examination group."

### Fritúz Gábor 2026 (2)

Fritúz Gábor, Kovács Gergely, Kállai András, Nagy Tamás, Maár Csaba, Szvath Petra, Berczi Máté, Fábry Szabolcs, Skultéti Dalma, Iványi Zsolt, Gál János, Kovács Enikő. (2026). The efficacy of BLS training among fifth-year medical students—a randomized, assessor-blinded, parallel group trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08606-z

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Comparison of all three skill retention assessments (Table 5): the between-group mask difference at two weeks was not present at two months (p = 0.282) or one year (p > 0.999), and correct mask placement declined over time.

> "The placement of a surgical mask on the patient’s face was absent in approximately 30% of the patients during the first and second skill retention assessments and in 65% of the patients during the third skill retention assessment."
