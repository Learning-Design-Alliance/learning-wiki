# Anisomycin infusion into the GC does not affect taste recognition in naive rats tested 14 days later

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Naive rats infused with anisomycin showed no significant difference from saline controls in aversion to saccharin or to the bitter taste quinine at the 14-day test. [→ Levitan 2016](#levitan-2016)

## Evidence

### Levitan 2016

Levitan, D., Gal-Ben-Ari, S., Heise, C., Rosenberg, T., Elkobi, A., Inberg, S., Sala, C. & Rosenblum, K. (2016). The differential role of cortical protein synthesis in taste memory formation and persistence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/npjscilearn.2016.1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Control experiment in naive animals: anisomycin (n = 15) or vehicle (n = 18) was infused into the GC and taste aversion tested 14 days later, showing "no signi ﬁcant difference between the groups" (Z = − 0.353; P = 0.8); the quinine test likewise showed no difference (P = 0.616). No effect size is printed.

> "We infused anisomycin or saline to the GC (Figure 4d), and tested the naive animals for aversion to saccharin as in our previous memory tests. There was no signi ﬁcant difference between the groups ( n =1 5–18, Mann –Whitney: Z = − 0.353; P = 0.8)."
