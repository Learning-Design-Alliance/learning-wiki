# A pronounced AI adaptation gap exists across university groups: students report higher current AI-use intensity and perceived usefulness than faculty and administrative staff, whereas staff report stronger academic integrity concerns and greater endorsement of responsible-use norms

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q3` · `i2`–`i3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Students had significantly higher current AI-use intensity than faculty (g = 0.79) and administrative staff (g = 0.98), and higher perceived usefulness than both groups. [→ Braun 2026](#braun-2026)
`q3 i3` Faculty and administrative staff reported stronger academic integrity concerns than students (g = 0.81 and g = 0.75 respectively), with no significant difference between the two staff groups. [→ Braun 2026 (2)](#braun-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Braun 2026

Braun, Y. S., & Khafizov, S. M. (2026). The AI Adaptation Gap in Higher Education: Students, Faculty, and Administrative Staff: AI Use, Trust, and Institutional Policy at a University Specializing in Teacher Education. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25063

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Welch one-way ANOVA and Bonferroni-adjusted Welch t-tests across the three role groups of the cross-sectional survey found a significant group effect on current AI-use intensity (eta² = 0.083); "students had higher current AI-use intensity than faculty ( g = 0.79, adjusted p < 0.001)".

> "In pairwise comparisons, students had higher current AI-use intensity than faculty ( g = 0.79, adjusted p < 0.001) and administrative staff ( g = 0.98, p < 0.001)."

### Braun 2026 (2)

Braun, Y. S., & Khafizov, S. M. (2026). The AI Adaptation Gap in Higher Education: Students, Faculty, and Administrative Staff: AI Use, Trust, and Institutional Policy at a University Specializing in Teacher Education. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25063

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Pairwise Welch comparisons on the academic integrity concerns index showed "g = 0.81 for faculty versus students and g = 0.75 for administrative staff versus students", both significant; faculty and administrative staff did not differ (p = 1.000).

> "Faculty and administrative staff reported stronger academic integrity concerns than students: g = 0.81 for faculty versus students and g = 0.75 for administrative staff versus students, with p < 0.001 in both cases."
