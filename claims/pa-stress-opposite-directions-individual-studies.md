## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Two of three studies reporting PA–stress results found significant associations, but in opposite directions: a negative relation in a low-PA sample (Kayani et al.) and a positive relation in which highly active students perceived more academic stress (Decamps et al.). [→ Wunsch K. 2021](#wunsch-k-2021)

## Evidence

### Wunsch K. 2021

Wunsch K., Fiedler J., Bachert P., Woll A. (2021). The Tridirectional Relationship among Physical Activity, Stress, and Academic Performance in University Students: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18020739

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Narrative synthesis of individual-study results in the discussion. Kayani et al. (cross-sectional, low-PA sample) found a negative PA–stress relation while Decamps et al. (cross-sectional, 1071 first-year students) found "highly active students perceived more academic stress", which the authors say contradicts the stress-buffering hypothesis.

> "Whereas Kayani and colleagues [43] found a negative relationship between PA and stress in participants with an overall low PA, Decamps and colleagues [44] found a positive one, meaning that highly active students perceived more academic stress."
