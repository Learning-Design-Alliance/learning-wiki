Evidence¶

Unal Cakiroglu 2017¶

Unal Cakiroglu, Huseyin Yilmaz. (2017). Using Videos and 3D Animations for Conceptual Learning in Basic Computer Units. Contemporary Educational Technology, 8(4), 390-405. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1158165

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Quasi-experimental pretest-posttest nonequivalent control group study with 34 tenth-grade vocational students (CG n = 15, HG n = 19). Independent t-test on post-test CUT scores found "no statistically significant difference between the mean post -test scores of the groups (t(32) =1.66, p= 0.10)". No effect size was printed.

"Also, independent t -test results indicated that there was no statistically significant difference between the mean post -test scores of the groups (t(32) =1.66, p= 0.10)"

Related Claims¶

  • The quantity of videos and 3D animations was related to success in remedying misconceptions: where materials were quite limited, incorrect or partly true answers remained high — related
  • Vocational high school students held various misconceptions about basic computer system units, including processor, main board, ethernet card, video card, sound card, memory, hard disk, optical disks, and monitor — related
  • Students reported that videos and 3D animations facilitated conceptual understanding via concretization, pausing, slowing down, replaying, and enlarging features — related
  • Using videos and 3D animations both in the classroom and in homework activities provided significant impact in remedying misconceptions about basic computer units — related
  • 3D virtual-reality lecture was statistically non-inferior to 2D lecture on knowledge and rated better on spatial understanding and enjoyment (Kockro et al.) — related
