# Contrast effects appear between math-like and verbal-like domains in both self-concept and intrinsic value

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Significant negative cross-domain achievement–self-concept relations emerged between math-like and verbal-like domains (e.g., German achievement with math self-concept; math achievement with German and English self-concepts). [→ Arens 2025](#arens-2025)
`q2 i?` Significant negative cross-domain achievement–intrinsic value relations also emerged between math-like and verbal-like domains (e.g., math achievement with German intrinsic value). [→ Arens 2025 (2)](#arens-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Arens 2025

Arens, A. Katrin; Fiedler, Daniel; Hasselhorn, Johannes; Möller, Jens. (2025). The formation of self‐concept and intrinsic value in arts‐related domains: Extending the generalized internal/external frame of reference model to music and visual arts. British Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.70038

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

GI/E SEM results (Model 2) showed multiple significant negative cross-domain paths; the article reports "significant negative relations between German achievement and math self‐concept" among others, indicating contrast effects.

> "Across domains, the findings indicated contrast effects between math‐like and verbal‐like domains given significant negative relations between German achievement and math self‐concept, German achievement and physics self‐concept, math achievement and German self‐concept, math achievement and English self‐concept, English achievement and math self‐concept, and physics achievement and second foreign language self‐concept."

### Arens 2025 (2)

Arens, A. Katrin; Fiedler, Daniel; Hasselhorn, Johannes; Möller, Jens. (2025). The formation of self‐concept and intrinsic value in arts‐related domains: Extending the generalized internal/external frame of reference model to music and visual arts. British Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.70038

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

For intrinsic value outcomes in the same GI/E model, the article reports "few negative relations between math‐like and verbal‐like domains", including math achievement with German intrinsic value, indicating contrast effects.

> "Across domains, there were few negative relations between math‐like and verbal‐like domains, such as between math achievement and German intrinsic value, German achievement and physics intrinsic value, physics achievement and second language intrinsic value, and physics achievement and English intrinsic value, indicating contrast effects."
