# Expert surgeons outperformed novices on all four quantitative measures of the resection task: smaller margin errors, lower carbonization, faster resection, fewer grasps

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Sakamoto Kayo 2026

Sakamoto Kayo, Mitani Sohei, Nishio Naoki, Kitani Takashi, Sato Eriko, Tanaka Keiko, Ugumori Toru, Wakisaka Hiroyuki, Hato Naohito. (2026). Development of an electrosurgery-compatible simulation task for quantitatively assessing oral cancer resection skills: initial validity evidence. BMC Medical Education, 26, 408. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08743-5

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Exploratory performance study comparing five experts (n=5) and 12 novices (n=12), each performing the task three consecutive times; Mann–Whitney U tests. Table 2 prints margin error 0.79±0.46 vs 2.45±0.76 mm, ΔE 2.33±0.96 vs 8.70±4.71, time 295±63 vs 466±183 s, grasps 13±9 vs 45±23, with P < 0.0001 for margin error, carbonization and grasps and P = 0.0005 for time. No effect sizes are printed.

> "Overall performance differed between groups, as experts exhibited smaller margin errors, lesser carbonization, shorter resection times, and fewer repeated grasps compared to novices (Table 2)."
