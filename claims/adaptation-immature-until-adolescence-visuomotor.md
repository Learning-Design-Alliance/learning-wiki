# Initial-angle adaptation in a visuomotor rotation task is not adult-like until roughly 13–14 years of age, with younger children adapting less

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the Push ball task, initial angle (adaptation) at the end of learning was significantly lower than adults for age groups 3–4 through 11–12 years, but not for 13–14 or 15–17 year olds, indicating adaptation matures around adolescence. [→ Malone 2025](#malone-2025)

## Evidence

### Malone 2025

Malone, L. A., Hill, N. M., Tripp, H., Zipunnikov, V., Wolpert, D. M. & Bastian, A. J. (2025). The control of movement gradually transitions from feedback control to feedforward adaptation throughout childhood. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00304-7

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Repeated measures ANOVA on 494 Push ball participants aged 3–17 and adults, epoch × age-group design. For initial angle there was a significant effect for epoch and age-group and interaction (F29.6,2056 = 4.54, p < 0.001); post hoc comparisons to adults showed younger groups differed while the 13–14yo (p = 0.76) and 15–17yo (p = 0.47) "groups were not".

> "At the end of learning, post hoc analysis revealed that when compared to the adult group, the 3 –4yo ( p < 0.001), 5–6yo ( p <0 . 0 0 1 ) , 7–8yo (p < 0.001), 9–10yo (p = 0.004), 11–12yo (p = 0.01) all were statisti- cally different, whereas the 13 –14yo ( p = 0.76) and 15 –17yo ( p =0 . 4 7 ) groups were not."
