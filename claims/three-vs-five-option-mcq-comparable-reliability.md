## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A three-option MCQ test achieved reliability (Cronbach's alpha 0.89) statistically comparable to a five-option version (0.81) on the same items, p = 0.19. [→ Al-lawama Manar 2023](#al-lawama-manar-2023)

## Evidence

### Al-lawama Manar 2023

Al-lawama Manar, Kumwenda Ben. (2023). Decreasing the options’ number in multiple choice questions in the assessment of senior medical students and its effect on exam psychometrics and distractors’ function. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04206-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized comparison in which 23 students sat the three-option test and 30 sat the five-option test; the difference in Cronbach's alpha was not statistically significant ("p-value = 0.19"). No effect size for the alpha difference is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "The three-option test was more reliable than the five-option one, with reliability coefficients of 0.89 and 0.81, respectively, p-value = 0.19."

### Al-lawama Manar 2023 (2)

Al-lawama Manar, Kumwenda Ben. (2023). Decreasing the options’ number in multiple choice questions in the assessment of senior medical students and its effect on exam psychometrics and distractors’ function. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04206-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive SEM comparison from the same randomized test comparison; the article prints the SEM values 2.6 and 2.8 but no significance test for this difference.

> "The SEM was lower for the three-option test (2.6) than for the five-option one (2.8)."
