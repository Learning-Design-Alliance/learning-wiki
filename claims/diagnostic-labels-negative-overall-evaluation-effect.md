# Diagnostic labels cause a moderately negative overall effect on how adults evaluate children (g = −0.42)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r?` · `q4` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q4 i2` Across 284 experimental effects, labeled children were evaluated significantly worse than unlabeled children, with an overall effect of g = −0.42. [→ Franz 2023](#franz-2023)

## Evidence

### Franz 2023

Franz, D. J., Richter, T., Lenhard, W., Marx, P., Stein, R., & Ratz, C. (2023). The Influence of Diagnostic Labels on the Evaluation of Students: a Multilevel Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09716-6

`q4 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Multilevel meta-analysis of 284 effect sizes nested in 60 experiments from 57 publications, based on 8,295 participants, synthesizing experimental studies manipulating label presence. The pooled random-effects estimate was "g = -0.42" with a significant t test and 95% CI [−0.56, −0.28].

> "We found an overall negative label effect size of g = -0.42 (k = 284, t(283) = -5.76, p < .001, 95% CI [−0.56, −0.28], Fig. 2)."
