## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Covariates reduce between-class variances more than within-class variances (33% Reading, 20% Math, 26% Science vs about 15% within), yet residual ICCs remain 16.3% (Reading), 27.6% (Math) and 26.9% (Science). [→ Grilli 2015](#grilli-2015)

## Evidence

### Grilli 2015

Grilli, L., Pennoni, F., Rampichini, C., and Romeo, I. (2015). Exploiting TIMSS and PIRLS combined data: multivariate multilevel modelling of student achievement. [doi:10.1214/16-aoas988](https://doi.org/10.1214/16-aoas988)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Comparison of final-model (M3) and null-model variance estimates from the multivariate multilevel analysis of the Italian sample. The article reports within-class variances reduce by about 15% for the three outcomes, with the greater between-class reduction for Reading attributed to compositional effects.

> "the residual ICC's derived from the estimated variances of Table 5 are quite high, speciﬁcally 16 .3% for Reading, 27 .6% for Math and 26 .9% for Science. These values point out the existence of unobserved relevant class level factors."
