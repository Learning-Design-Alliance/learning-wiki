## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Doing numerical calculations makes students regard a ket as a column vector, a bra as a row vector, and an operator as a matrix, because that is how they are concretely represented in the computer. [→ R. Garcia 2007](#r-garcia-2007)
`q1 i?` The authors' year of classroom experimentation at WPI identified intuitive acceptance of functions-as-vectors and operators-as-matrices as the most important benefit of incorporating numerical simulation. [→ R. Garcia 2007 (2)](#r-garcia-2007-2)

## Evidence

### R. Garcia 2007

R. Garcia, A. Zozulya, and J. Stickney. (2007). MATLAB codes for teaching quantum physics: Part 1. https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.1622 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0704.1622](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0704.1622)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

The authors' motivational argument, not a measured outcome: computer representation concretely instantiates the Dirac formalism. The claim is an interpretation offered to justify the exercise design, with no assessment data reported.

> "Anyone who has done numerical calculations can’t help but regard a ket |Ψ⟩ as a column vector, a bra ⟨Ψ| as a row vector and an operator H as a matrix because that is how they concretely represented in the computer"

### R. Garcia 2007 (2)

R. Garcia, A. Zozulya, and J. Stickney. (2007). MATLAB codes for teaching quantum physics: Part 1. https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.1622 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0704.1622](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0704.1622)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

The authors' interpretation drawn from one year of classroom use at WPI; no measures, sample sizes, or comparisons are reported, so the finding is qualitative and unquantified.

> "our experimentation incorporating these simulations in quantum physics at WPI during the past year has shown us that the most important beneﬁt is a type of side-eﬀect to doing numerical simulation: the acceptance on an intuitive level by the student that functions are like vectors and diﬀerential operators are like matrices"
