# Instruction-only students perceived learning control comparably high to example generators, while provided-example students perceived lower control

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Self-generation yielded higher perceived learning control than provided examples (d = 0.57). [→ Krause-Wichmann 2025](#krause-wichmann-2025)
`q3 i2` Instruction-only students reported higher perceived control than provided-example students (d = 0.70). [→ Krause-Wichmann 2025](#krause-wichmann-2025)
`q3 i?` The difference in perceived control between self-generation and instruction only was not significant. [→ Krause-Wichmann 2025](#krause-wichmann-2025)

## Evidence

### Krause-Wichmann 2025

Krause-Wichmann, T., Klopp, E., Stark, L., & Stark, R. (2025). Promoting future teachers' pedagogical knowledge: The role of self-generated vs. provided illustrative examples after instruction. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09694-4

`q3 · i2`

Planned contrasts on retrospectively assessed perceived learning control (Cronbach's α = 0.76) in the randomized experiment; Table 3 prints d = 0.57 for I-GE/I-PE and d = 0.70 for I-PE/I, both significant; I-GE/I was not (d = 0.15).

> "Students who generated examples perceived their control to be higher than students who studied examples. Students who learned with instruction only (I) reported a higher control perception than students who were additionally provided with examples (I-PE). The result was significant with medium effect size. The difference between instruction followed by self-generated examples (I-GE) and instruction only (I) was not significant."
