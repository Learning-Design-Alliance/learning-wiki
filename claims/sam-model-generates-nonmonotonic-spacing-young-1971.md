# The SAM spacing model can generate non-monotonic spacing effects, as shown by its fit to Young's 1971 data

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For longer retention intervals, recall does not increase monotonically with spacing lag: Young (1971) reported a clear non-monotonic spacing effect, and the fitted SAM model reproduces it (χ² = 8.47, df = 12). [→ Raaijmakers 2003](#raaijmakers-2003)

## Evidence

### Raaijmakers 2003

Raaijmakers, J.G.W. (2003). Spacing and repetition effects in human memory: application of the SAM model. Cognitive Science 27 (2003) 431–452. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0364-0213(03)00007-7

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Numerical fit to Young's (1971) P1–P2–T data with retention interval fixed at a lag of 10 items and spacing varied from 0 to 17 items. The article reports "the χ 2 -value of 8.47 (df = 12)" and says the main point is showing the model can generate non-monotonic spacing effects.

> "However, it is not the case that (for longer retention intervals) the probability of retrieval increases monotonically with increasing lag between successive presentations. Young (1971) reports a clear non-monotonic spacing effect."
