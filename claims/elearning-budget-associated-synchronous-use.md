## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Programs with an e-learning budget had higher odds of regular synchronous e-learning use (OR 3.00, 95% CI 1.04–8.68; P = .04); the asynchronous association was not significant (OR 1.95, 95% CI 0.84–4.51). [→ Wittich CM 2017](#wittich-cm-2017)

## Evidence

### Wittich CM 2017

Wittich CM, Agrawal A, Cook DA, Halvorsen AJ, Mandrekar J, Chaudhry S, Dupras DM, Oxentenko AS, Beckman TJ. (2017). E-learning in graduate medical education: survey of residency program directors. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0953-9

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple logistic regression from the national PD survey (Table 4). Programs with a budget had "OR, 3.00 [95% CI, 1.04–8.68]" for regular synchronous e-learning use; the budget association with asynchronous use was OR 1.95 (0.84–4.51), not significant at the P < .05 threshold. No standardised effect size was printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "The odds of synchronous e-learning use was higher in programs with a budget for implementation (OR, 3.00 [95% CI, 1.04–8.68]; P = .04) and in programs with more international medical graduates (OR, 1.018 [95% CI, 1.001–1.035]; P = .04)."
