Evidence¶

Hou 2025¶

Hou, C., Kuhlmann, S. L., Greene, J. A., Bernacki, M. L., & Hogan, K. A. (2025). Process mining measures students' help-seeking transitions when completing assignments in an online learning and assessment platform. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09442-0

q3 · i2 · associational · r1

Kruskal-Wallis tests across the four exam-performance quartiles of 482 biology students showed solution access differed across groups, η² = 0.04, a "small effect size (Richardson, 2011)". Dunn's tests showed the lowest group accessed solutions significantly more than each other group.

"a Kruskal-Wallis test between the groups indicated a statistically significant difference in the frequency at which they accessed solutions, (c2(3) = 24.31, p <.001, η2 = 0.04), with a small effect size (Richardson, 2011)"

Hou 2025 (2)¶

Hou, C., Kuhlmann, S. L., Greene, J. A., Bernacki, M. L., & Hogan, K. A. (2025). Process mining measures students' help-seeking transitions when completing assignments in an online learning and assessment platform. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09442-0

q3 · i? · associational · r2

The same Kruskal-Wallis frequency analysis of 482 students' trace data found "no statistically significant differences between the groups in hint access" (χ²(3) = 2.88, p = .41); no effect size was printed, so the null is not evidence of equivalence.

"a Krusal-Wallis test indicated no statistically significant differences between the groups in hint access (c2(3) = 2.88, p =.41."
