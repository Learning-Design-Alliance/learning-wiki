## Evidence

### Goff Eric E. 2017

Goff Eric E., Reindl Katie M., Johnson Christina, McClean Phillip, Offerdahl Erika G., Schroeder Noah L., White Alan R. (2017). Efficacy of a Meiosis Learning Module Developed for the Virtual Cell Animation Collection. CBE—Life Sciences Education 16(1):ar9. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-03-0141

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experiment in four sections of introductory biology; sections randomly assigned to the online-learning module group (n = 131) or traditional lecture group (n = 403). The module group "showed significantly higher normalized gain scores than students in the traditional lecture group" (t(317.03) = 4.42, p < 0.001, d = 0.40).

> "Students who interacted with the learning module showed significantly higher normalized gain scores than students in the traditional lecture group (t(317.03) = 4.42, p < 0.001, d = 0.40; Figure 4; Supplemental Material 2)."

### Goff Eric E. 2017 (2)

Goff Eric E., Reindl Katie M., Johnson Christina, McClean Phillip, Offerdahl Erika G., Schroeder Noah L., White Alan R. (2017). Efficacy of a Meiosis Learning Module Developed for the Virtual Cell Animation Collection. CBE—Life Sciences Education 16(1):ar9. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-03-0141

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Item-level analysis of the 10-question meiosis assessment found the module group had "a higher percentage of students who answered correctly than the traditional group on all questions, except for one."

> "Descriptive statistics relating to individual posttest items show that the learning module group had a higher percentage of students who answered correctly than the traditional group on all questions, except for one (Supplemental Material 3)."
