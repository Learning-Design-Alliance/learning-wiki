## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Gateway students are significantly more likely to be from an ethnic minority, state-educated, first-generation, and from deprived areas. [→ Curtis Sally 2020](#curtis-sally-2020)

## Evidence

### Curtis Sally 2020

Curtis Sally, Smith Daniel. (2020). A comparison of undergraduate outcomes for students from gateway courses and standard entry medicine courses. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1918-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Chi-square comparisons of demographic variables in Table 1 (N = 4340) with Bonferroni correction; e.g. BME ethnicity 65.2% gateway vs 42.2% SEMED (Χ2 = 105.1). The article reports significance but no effect size.

> "Table 1 shows that students on gateway courses are significantly more likely (p < 0.001) than students on SEMED courses to be from an ethnic minority group; have studied at a state-funded school; have parents who have not gained higher education qualifications; live in an area with lower participation rates in higher education; be from a lower socio-economic group; have applied for a UCAT bursary, and live in a more deprived area."
