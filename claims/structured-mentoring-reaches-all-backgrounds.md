Theoretical argument in the guide's opening section on why mentoring programs can support more diverse, equitable, and inclusive STEM departments. It offers no new data; the claim rests on the authors' practitioner reasoning that "structured mentoring communities can intentionally provide information and foster relationships across all student backgrounds."

> "while informal mentoring tends to be readily accessible to students coming from more well-resourced backgrounds (for example, in the form of "hidden curriculum" or social networks), structured mentoring communities can intentionally provide information and foster relationships across all student backgrounds."
