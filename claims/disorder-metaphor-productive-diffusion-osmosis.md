## Evidence

### Benjamin D. Geller 2013

Benjamin D. Geller, Benjamin W. Dreyfus, Julia Gouvea, Vashti Sawtelle, Chandra Turpen, and Edward F. Redish. (2013). Entropy and spontaneity in an introductory physics course for life science students. Department of Physics, University of Maryland, College Park. [doi:10.1119/1.4870389](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4870389)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Authors' interpretation of the case study interviews: Otto described osmosis as water becoming "no longer contained" and Gavin described particles that "want to increase [their] disorder." The authors conclude the challenge is refining the metaphor's definition, not replacing it.

> "To Gavin and Otto, processes like diffusion and osmosis make good sense in the context of metaphors like "disorder" or "chaos." The metaphors are doing productive work for them in making sense of entropic phenomena."
