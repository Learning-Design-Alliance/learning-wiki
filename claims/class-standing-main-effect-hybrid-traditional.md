# Only sophomores showed a significant between-section performance difference; a significant main effect of class standing reflected freshmen performing less well in both sections

## Evidence

### Adams A. E. M. 2015

Adams A. E. M., Randall S., Traustadóttir T. (2015). A Tale of Two Sections: An Experiment to Compare the Effectiveness of a Hybrid versus a Traditional Lecture Format in Introductory Microbiology. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-08-0118

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Univariate general linear model analysis of final scores (excluding quizzes) in the two-section randomized comparison; the article reports a significant main effect of class standing within each section (p < 0.01), with post hoc testing showing "freshmen had lower scores than sophomores, juniors, and seniors (all: p < 0.001)".

> "Similarly, there was a significant effect of class standing within each section (p < 0.01)."

### Adams A. E. M. 2015 (2)

Adams A. E. M., Randall S., Traustadóttir T. (2015). A Tale of Two Sections: An Experiment to Compare the Effectiveness of a Hybrid versus a Traditional Lecture Format in Introductory Microbiology. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-08-0118

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post hoc between-section comparisons by class year in the same study. The article reports untested descriptive gaps of 3.4% for freshmen, 7.5% for sophomores, 2.9% for juniors, and 4% for seniors, with only the sophomore difference significant.

> "When students of the same standing were compared separately across sections, sophomores in the traditional section did significantly better than those in the hybrid section (p < 0.001), but the scores of freshmen, juniors, and seniors were not significantly different between sections (Table 3)."
