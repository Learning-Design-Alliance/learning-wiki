## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Item format (recall vs. recognition) did not significantly affect the amount of performance decline over the 50-year retention interval, contrasting with much of the experimental literature. [→ Bahrick 1991](#bahrick-1991)

## Evidence

### Bahrick 1991

Bahrick, H. P., & Hall, L. K. (1991). Lifetime maintenance of high school mathematics content. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 120(1), 20-33. https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.120.1.20

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Item-level hierarchical regression of Algebra 1 items; adding item format and content variables did not significantly increase variance accounted for beyond difficulty and control-group performance. The authors attribute the contrast with laboratory findings to better control of degree of overlearning relative to the performance criterion.

> "The results indicate no statistically significant difference in the amount of performance decline for questions presented in recognition versus recall format, and this finding contrasts with much of the experimental literature."
