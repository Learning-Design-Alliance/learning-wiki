# Students rate virtual patient cases as a rewarding learning experience that prepares them for real patients, but only partially experience authentic clinical decision-making

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After completing the four VP cases, students felt better prepared to diagnose (median = 4; average = 4.3) and treat (median = 4; average = 4.0) real patients and judged the cases a worthwhile learning experience (median = 5; average = 4.6). [→ Seifert Lukas B. 2019](#seifert-lukas-b-2019)

## Evidence

### Seifert Lukas B. 2019

Seifert Lukas B., Socolan Octavian, Sader Robert, Rüsseler Miriam, Sterz Jasmina. (2019). Virtual patients versus small-group teaching in the training of oral and maxillofacial surgery: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1887-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Form 1 of the Huwendiek and de Leng Virtual patient design evaluation toolkit, completed by the VP group after case completion on a 5-point Likert scale. Students "felt better prepared to diagnose (median = 4; average = 4.3) and treat (median = 4; average = 4.0) real patients".

> "Overall, students felt better prepared to diagnose (median = 4; average = 4.3) and treat (median = 4; average = 4.0) real patients after completing the four VP cases (See Table 2)."

### Seifert Lukas B. 2019 (2)

Seifert Lukas B., Socolan Octavian, Sader Robert, Rüsseler Miriam, Sterz Jasmina. (2019). Virtual patients versus small-group teaching in the training of oral and maxillofacial surgery: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1887-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same toolkit evaluation and open comments. Students "only partially found that working on the VP cases felt like making real life clinical decisions (median = 3; average = 3.1)" and reported missing lecturer interaction and insufficient time.

> "However, students only partially found that working on the VP cases felt like making real life clinical decisions (median = 3; average = 3.1). Also, they missed direct interaction with a lecturer to clarify open-ended questions and found the given time for case completion (240 min) to be too short."
