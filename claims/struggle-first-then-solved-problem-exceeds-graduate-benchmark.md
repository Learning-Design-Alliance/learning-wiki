# Calculus-based students' quiz-problem scores rose from first to second attempt after seeing an isomorphic solved problem, averaging 9.1 out of 10 on the second attempt versus an 8.4 graduate-student benchmark

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Calculus-based intervention 2 students averaged 9.1 out of 10 on their second attempt at the quiz problem, above the 8.4 graduate-student benchmark; their scores rose significantly from the first to the second attempt in this group. [→ Lin 2011](#lin-2011)

## Evidence

### Lin 2011

Lin, S.-Y. and Singh, C. (2011). Using Isomorphic Problems to Learn Introductory Physics. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.7.020104](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.7.020104)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-group pre/post comparison of calculus-based intervention 2 students (n=34) who attempted the quiz problem, then learned from the solved isomorphic problem, then solved it again; the article states "a significant improvement in the scores was found". Means are descriptive; no effect size printed.

> "the calculus-based students achieved an average score of 9.1 (out of 10) the second time they solved the quiz problem, which was a higher score than the benchmark (8.4) set by the graduate students"
