## Evidence

### Thorndike 1922

Thorndike, E. L., & Upton, C. B. (1922). An Experiment in Learning an Abstract Subject. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 13(6). https://doi.org/10.1037/h0065229

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The authors' qualitative inspection of twenty answers to the U-magnet question, given after ten hours of study, found "impulsive, uncritical, and confused thinking to a notable degree." This is the writers' interpretation of the written work, not a tested comparison.

> "Many of them show an impulsive, uncritical, and confused thinking to a notable degree—to the person who is really master of the topic."

### Thorndike 1922 (2)

Thorndike, E. L., & Upton, C. B. (1922). An Experiment in Learning an Abstract Subject. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 13(6). https://doi.org/10.1037/h0065229

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The authors' interpretive conclusion from inspecting the adults' work: the adult-child contrast in rationality is "in part, a consequence of specialized habits." No effect size or test is reported; this is the writers' opinion based on the collected work.

> "our inspection of the work of this score of superior trained adult thinkers strongly suggests that the paradox has a large element of truth, that the difference in thinking is not all-pervasive and constitutional, but is, in part, a consequence of specialized habits."
