# In a 12-week RCT, the treatment group's rate of decline in real-time Moments of Stress was significantly steeper than the control group's

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2025 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The treatment group showed a significantly steeper decline in Moments of Stress over time than the control group (significant treatment-by-time interaction). [→ Ta 2025](#ta-2025)

## Evidence

### Ta 2025

Ta, A.; Salgin, N.; Demir, M.; Reindel, K. P.; Mehta, R. K.; McDonald, A.; McCord, C.; Sasangohar, F. (2025). Real-Time Stress Monitoring, Detection, and Management in College Students: A Wearable Technology and Machine-Learning Approach. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2505.15974](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.15974)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

GLMM analysis (Gamma distribution, log link) of daily Moments of Stress from the 12-week randomized trial (86 treatment, 31 control completers). The treatment-by-time interaction was significant, p < .001, with the "rate of decline in the moment of stress is significantly steeper for the treatment group".

> "the simple slope analysis indicates that the rate of decline in the moment of stress is significantly steeper for the treatment group [βStd = −0.127 (0.013), t(81) = -9.74, p < .001] than the control [βStd = −0.026 (0.008), t(81) = -3.29, p < .001]"
