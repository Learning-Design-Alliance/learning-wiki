## Evidence

### Kasia Muldner · Winslow Burleson · Brett Van de Sande · Kurt VanLehn 2010

Kasia Muldner · Winslow Burleson · Brett Van de Sande · Kurt VanLehn (2010). An analysis of students’ gaming behaviors in an intelligent tutoring system: predictors and impacts. User Model User-Adap Inter. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11257-010-9086-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Basic hint-utility analysis of Andes logs on short-term performance, excluding entry correctness. The article reports low gamers responded to a high-level hint with an entry about 36% of the time, contrasting with high gamers' roughly 16%.

> "high gamers tried to generate an entry only about 16% of the time after receiving a high-level hint, asking for another hint the other 84% of the time"

### Kasia Muldner · Winslow Burleson · Brett Van de Sande · Kurt VanLehn 2010 (2)

Kasia Muldner · Winslow Burleson · Brett Van de Sande · Kurt VanLehn (2010). An analysis of students’ gaming behaviors in an intelligent tutoring system: predictors and impacts. User Model User-Adap Inter. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11257-010-9086-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same short-term performance analysis of Andes logs. The article attributes the near-universal response to the fact that asking for a hint after the bottom-out hint merely repeats the hint, so students only do so by accident.

> "This is in contrast to bottom-out hints, when both low and high gamers responded to the hint with an entry about 97% of the time"
