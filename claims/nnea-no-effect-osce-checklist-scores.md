# OSCE checklist item scores do not differ significantly between performances with and without a non-native English accent

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Checklist sums given to OSCE videos with a non-native English accent did not differ significantly from those given to videos without the accent (p = 0.787 median test; p = 0.982 t-test). [→ Kozato An 2020](#kozato-an-2020)

## Evidence

### Kozato An 2020

Kozato An, Patel Nimesh, Shikino Kiyoshi. (2020). A randomised controlled pilot trial of the influence of non-native English accents on examiners’ scores in OSCEs. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02198-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Randomised single-blind crossover trial: 32 UK OSCE examiners each scored two videos of one actor performing a history-taking station, one with and one without an Indian accent. Checklist sums were identical in means (41.6) and the t-test showed "no statistically significant difference in the mean scores (p = 0.982)".

> "The mean value of the checklist sums for videos with and without the NNEA were 41.6 ± Standard Deviation (SD) points v 41.6 ± SD, respectively (Table 2). Independent samples t-test for equality of means showed there was no statistically significant difference in the mean scores (p = 0.982)."

### Kozato An 2020 (2)

Kozato An, Patel Nimesh, Shikino Kiyoshi. (2020). A randomised controlled pilot trial of the influence of non-native English accents on examiners’ scores in OSCEs. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02198-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The non-parametric median test on the same 32-examiner dataset found "no statistically significant effect of the accent variable on checklist sums (p = 0.787)". An additional analysis of individual checklist items also showed no significant difference for any item.

> "Non-parametric independent samples median test was performed to analyse the relationship between the accent and checklist sums. The analysis showed there was no statistically significant effect of the accent variable on checklist sums (p = 0.787)."
