## Evidence

### Jin et al. 2026

Yimeng Jin, Chunyu Ma, Danyang Liu, Junpeng You, Johan Lauwereyns. (2026). Effort enhances understanding, while appreciation drives engagement in Cross-sentence semantic processing. Acta Psychologica. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2026.107927

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Bayesian ordinal mixed-effects model of Pairing-up Test accuracy (38 of 43 participants returned after a mean 9.08-day delay), showing "Chosen-effort Trials had credibly higher odds of a more accurate response category" than Blocked-effort trials.

> "compared to Blocked-effort Trials, Chosen-effort Trials had credibly higher odds of a more accurate response category (β = 1.13, 89% CI \[0.71, 1.50\]), whereas Low-effort Trials had credibly lower odds (β = − 0.45, 89% CI \[− 0.79, − 0.11\])"

### Jin et al. 2026 (2)

Yimeng Jin, Chunyu Ma, Danyang Liu, Junpeng You, Johan Lauwereyns. (2026). Effort enhances understanding, while appreciation drives engagement in Cross-sentence semantic processing. Acta Psychologica. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2026.107927

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Bayesian multinomial mixed-effects model of the Recalling Test (same 38 returning participants); Blocked-effort trials were frequently misremembered as Low-effort (34.5%) or Chosen-effort (20.1%), showing "Active Engagement boosted recognition of the sentence pairs themselves" while blocked elaboration left a weaker context trace.

> "The Bayesian multinomial mixed-effects model indicated that correct recall rates were 85.3% for Chosen-effort, 45.4% for Blocked-effort, and 69.4% for Low-effort Trials."
