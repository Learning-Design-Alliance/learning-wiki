## Evidence

### Orzeszko Z 2025

Orzeszko Z, Gach T, Necka S, Ochwat K, Major P, Szura M. (2025). The implementation of computer-aided detection in an initial endoscopy training improves the quality measures of trainees' future colonoscopies: a retrospective cohort study. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-025-11890-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Null finding from the retrospective cohort's intraprocedural quality-indicator comparison: cecal intubation rates were 96.3% (AI-trained) versus 94.8% (conventionally trained), with high data quality (missing data rate 0%). Equivalence was not explicitly tested.

> "Groups had similar CIR (mean difference 1.5%; 95% CI: − 0.3% to 3.3%)."

### Orzeszko Z 2025 (2)

Orzeszko Z, Gach T, Necka S, Ochwat K, Major P, Szura M. (2025). The implementation of computer-aided detection in an initial endoscopy training improves the quality measures of trainees' future colonoscopies: a retrospective cohort study. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-025-11890-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression on the same cohort found AI-enhanced training had no significant impact on high-quality cecal intubation performance (OR 1.20, 95% CI: 0.95–1.52, p = 0.132, high data quality).
