Tang 2021¶

Tang, H. (2021). Implementing open educational resources in digital education. Education Tech Research Dev 69, 389–392. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-020-09879-x

The editorial reports, citing Hilton's (2016) review, that "OER can help decrease college students' textbook spending without undermining student learning effectiveness." The review's own design is not described here; this is a second-hand synthesis reported by the editorial.

"Hilton's review article (2016) provides synthesized evidence stating that OER can help decrease college students' textbook spending without undermining student learning effectiveness."

Related Claims¶

  • The commentary offers a practice-based perspective on Hilton's (2016) OER efficacy review in the context of the COVID-19 pivot to remote instruction — related
  • In direct comparisons of OER and commercial textbooks, a strong majority of participants report OER are as good or better. — related
  • Across 2002-2018, OER efficacy research consistently shows that OER does not harm student learning, with a small positive impact. — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Open textbooks appear to offer financial benefits to students without decreasing learning outcomes in higher education — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • The number of published OER efficacy and perceptions studies rose rapidly between 2015 and 2018, with more studies published in those 3 years than in the previous 15. — related
  • In studies comparing ratings from separate groups of OER users and CT users, results were mixed: two studies favored CT, two favored OER, and one showed similar ratings. — related
  • Synthesized studies of OER in college courses found academic outcomes comparable to traditional textbooks and positive perceptions among students and instructors — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Substituting OER for traditional textbooks does not appear to decrease student learning outcomes in higher education. — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Students using OER save significant amounts of money compared to purchasing commercial textbooks. — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
