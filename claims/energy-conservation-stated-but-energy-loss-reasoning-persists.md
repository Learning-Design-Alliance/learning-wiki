## Evidence

### S. B. McKagan 2008

S. B. McKagan, K. K. Perkins, and C. E. Wieman. (2008). A Deeper Look at Student Learning of Quantum Mechanics: the Case of Tunneling. https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.3194 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0802.3194](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0802.3194)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Direct exam and homework questions in the transformed courses: on the exam, "between 70% and 93% an- swered correctly that it is equal"; on homework, "between 95% and 96% answered correctly that it does not", with clear explanations invoking conservation of energy and lack of dissipation.

> "between 70% and 93% an- swered correctly that it is equal. In homework, when students were asked “Does an electron lose energy when it tunnels?” between 95% and 96% answered correctly that it does not"

### S. B. McKagan 2008 (2)

S. B. McKagan, K. K. Perkins, and C. E. Wieman. (2008). A Deeper Look at Student Learning of Quantum Mechanics: the Case of Tunneling. https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.3194 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0802.3194](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0802.3194)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r3`

Observations and interviews in the transformed courses showed students who had answered correctly on homework still asked "Why is the total energy the same after tunneling?" and gave answers implying energy loss on questions not directly about energy. The article reports no effect size for this interview finding.

> "However, energy loss in tunneling continued to be an issue."
