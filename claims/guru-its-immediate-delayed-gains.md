# Scores in the Guru ITS group were significantly higher on immediate and delayed tests than at pre-test in a 9-week biology tutoring study

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3` · `i2` medium
> **Currency** · newest study 2025 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Students in the ITS condition were significantly more likely to answer post-test items correctly than pre-test items (OR = 3.13, d = .71). [→ Olney 2025](#olney-2025)
`q3 i1` Students in the ITS condition were significantly more likely to answer delayed-test items correctly than pre-test items (OR = 1.77, d = .36). [→ Olney 2025](#olney-2025)

## Evidence

### Olney 2025

Olney, A.M., D'Mello, S.K., Person, N., Cade, W., Hays, P., Dempsey, C.W., Lehman, B., Williams, B., Graesser, A. (2025). Efficacy of a Computer Tutor that Models Expert Human Tutors. [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-98462-4_38](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-98462-4_38)

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Logistic mixed-effects contrasts within the ITS condition of a 9-week repeated-measures study with 34 tenth graders, from the Results contrasts section. The article reports "OR = 3.13, d= .71" pre-to-post and "OR = 1.77, d= .36" pre-to-delayed, both significant at p < .001.

> "such that the ITS condition was more likely to correctly answer questions on the post-test than the pre-test,OR = 3.13, d= .71, and more likely to correctly answer questions on the delayed test than the pre-test, OR = 1.77, d= .36"
