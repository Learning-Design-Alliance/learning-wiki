# Novices and experts differ in problem representation: novices segment the problem and use surface analogies, while experts apply general underlying principles

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Novices tended to produce separate explanations for each phenomenon and searched for analogies based on surface characteristics, whereas experts postulated a not-directly-observable process and recognized the problem as an instance of a general principle. [→ Schmidt 1989](#schmidt-1989)

`q2 i?` Transcript analysis showed subjects actively constructed explanatory models fitting the specific problem, combining known objects, processes and attributes; novices used surface-feature analogies and segmented the problem, whereas experts postulated underlying physical processes. [→ Schmidt 1989](#schmidt-1989-2)

## Evidence

### Schmidt 1989

Schmidt, H. G., De Volder, M. L., De Grave, W. S., Moust, J. H. C., & Patel, V. L. (1989). Explanatory Models in the Processing of Science Text: The Role of Prior Knowledge Activation Through Small-Group Discussion. Journal of Educational Psychology, 81(4), 610-619. https://doi.org/10.1037//0022-0663.81.4.610

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Transcription analysis of Experiment 2 discussions found only one novice group eventually related the different phenomena, while experts explained them "by one or two underlying principles"; the authors note this accords with Chi, Feltovich, and Glaser (1981) on cue dependence.

> "novices dealt with the blood cell problem mainly by segmentation; that is, they showed a tendency to come up with separate explanations for each of the phenomena described."

### Schmidt 1989 (2)

Schmidt, H. G., De Volder, M. L., De Grave, W. S., Moust, J. H. C., & Patel, V. L. (1989). Explanatory Models in the Processing of Science Text: The Role of Prior Knowledge Activation Through Small-Group Discussion. Journal of Educational Psychology, 81(4), 610-619. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.81.4.610

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative analysis of verbatim transcriptions of the novices' blood cell discussions in Experiment 2 (Appendix B lists 12 explanatory models; none occurred more than twice across groups). Novices dealt with the problem mainly by segmentation and surface analogies such as sponges; experts explained the phenomena by one or two underlying principles.

> "while they discussed the problem, subjects indeed constructed explanatory models that fit the situation described in the problem and that were based on whatever prior knowledge and experience they could bring to that situation."
