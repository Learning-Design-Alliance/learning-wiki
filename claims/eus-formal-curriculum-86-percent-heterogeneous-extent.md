## Evidence

### Kim Daniel J. MD 2014

Kim Daniel J. MD, Theoret Jonathan MDCM, Liao Michael M. MD, Kendall John L. MD. (2014). Experience with Emergency Ultrasound Training by Canadian Emergency Medicine Residents. West J Emerg Med. https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2013.9.18025

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Web-based survey of all 39 Canadian Royal College PGY-5 EM residents (37 responses, 95% response rate), reporting descriptive statistics as number and proportion. The survey found "EUS was part of the formal residency curriculum for 86% (32/37) of respondents."

> "EUS was part of the formal residency curriculum for 86% (32/37) of respondents."

### Kim Daniel J. MD 2014 (2)

Kim Daniel J. MD, Theoret Jonathan MDCM, Liao Michael M. MD, Kendall John L. MD. (2014). Experience with Emergency Ultrasound Training by Canadian Emergency Medicine Residents. West J Emerg Med. https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2013.9.18025

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same descriptive survey of PGY-5 residents; residents self-rated their EUS training extent, with "minimal for 22% (8/37) of residents, moderate for 51% (19/37), and extensive for 27% (10/37)". No effect size or inferential test was reported.

> "EUS training was described as minimal for 22% (8/37) of residents, moderate for 51% (19/37), and extensive for 27% (10/37)."
