# Taking only a single semester of the ICB curriculum did not harm student performance relative to peers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2017 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` First-semester-only ICB students performed equivalently to peers in a traditional second-semester course (83.5% vs. 83.2%), and students who joined ICB late were not significantly lower than full-year ICB peers (73.85% vs. 77.48%, p = 0.4361). [→ Luckie Douglas B. 2017](#luckie-douglas-b-2017)

## Evidence

### Luckie Douglas B. 2017

Luckie Douglas B., Hoskinson Anne-Marie, Griffin Caleigh E., Hess Andrea L., Price Katrina J., Tawa Alex, Thacker Samantha M. (2017). Integrating Concepts in Biology Textbook Increases Learning: Assessment Triangulation Using Concept Inventory, Card Sorting, and MCAT Instruments, Followed by Longitudinal Tracking. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-06-0204

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Longitudinal tracking of a small group of 10 students who entered ICB in the second semester after a traditional first semester, compared with full-year ICB peers (n = 68); the difference was not statistically significant.

> "The average final grade of the cohort of 10 students was lower, but not significantly so (73.85% vs. 77.48%, p = 0.4361), than peers from a full yearlong ICB experience (Figure 4C)."
