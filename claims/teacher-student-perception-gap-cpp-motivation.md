# Teachers rate contextualized physics problems as far more motivating and interesting for students than students themselves do, with moderate-to-large perception gaps on motivation/interest items

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Teacher and student responses differ significantly on all motivation-and-interest items (Q1–Q4, Q7–Q9), with Cohen's d from 0.72 to 0.89. [→ Wei 2025](#wei-2025)

## Evidence

### Wei 2025

Wei, Y., Peng, X., Zhong, Y., Pi, F., Zhai, Y., & Bao, L. (2025). Can Contextualized Physics Problems Enhance Student Motivation? Uncovering a Student-Teacher Perception Gap through a Large-Scale Survey. [doi:10.1103/2g1b-hmhq](https://doi.org/10.1103/2g1b-hmhq)

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Mann-Whitney U-tests comparing 154 teachers and 868 students on nine parallel survey items, treating responses as a continuous 1–4 Likert scale. Motivation/interest items showed significant differences with "Cohen’s d ranging from 0.72 to 0.89" (p < 0.001), i.e. printed effect sizes of d = 0.72 to d = 0.89.

> "Significant differences with moderate-to-strong practical significance were found for questions Q1–Q4 and Q7–Q9 (p < 0.001, Cohen’s d ranging from 0.72 to 0.89)."

### Wei 2025 (2)

Wei, Y., Peng, X., Zhong, Y., Pi, F., Zhai, Y., & Bao, L. (2025). Can Contextualized Physics Problems Enhance Student Motivation? Uncovering a Student-Teacher Perception Gap through a Large-Scale Survey. [doi:10.1103/2g1b-hmhq](https://doi.org/10.1103/2g1b-hmhq)

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Same U-test comparison of teacher and student responses on the two difficulty-perception items. The items were non-significant (p > 0.05) with "negligible effect sizes (Cohen’s d = 0.03~0.12)", i.e. printed effect sizes of d = 0.03 to d = 0.12.

> "Questions Q5 and Q6 showed no significant differences ( p > 0.05) and negligible effect sizes (Cohen’s d = 0.03~0.12)."
