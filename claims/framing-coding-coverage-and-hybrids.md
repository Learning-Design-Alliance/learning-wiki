## Evidence

### Bing 2007

Bing, T. J., & Redish, E. F. (2007). Analyzing Problem Solving Using Math in Physics: Epistemological Framing via Warrants. https://www.compadre.org/PER/

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Coverage analysis of the study's coded video data reported in Section IV.F: only a minority of the data forms minute-or-longer pure-state framings. The figure is the article's own estimate; no effect size is printed.

> "Of all the data analyzed for this study, perhaps less than 50% can be cleanly coded in minute-or-longer chunks under one of these general framings."

### Bing 2007 (2)

Bing, T. J., & Redish, E. F. (2007). Analyzing Problem Solving Using Math in Physics: Epistemological Framing via Warrants. https://www.compadre.org/PER/

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The same section reports that the four framings do a reasonable job of spanning the space of students' mathematical arguments when smaller chunks within hybrids are counted, with hybrids common among upper-division majors.

> "We observed that about 90% of a random episode or more can be seen as made up of behavior indicative of those four landmark framings."
