# Participation in HFS is independently associated with roughly fourfold odds of scoring at or above 70% on the ALS post-test

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2015 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a stepwise logistic regression, group (HFS vs control) independently predicted a post-test score ≥ 70% (OR 4.45, CI 1.74–11.3), as did pre-test score (OR 1.04, CI 1.02–1.07); last year of medical school did not. [→ Cortegiani A 2015](#cortegiani-a-2015)

## Evidence

### Cortegiani A 2015

Cortegiani A, Russotto V, Montalto F, Iozzo P, Palmeri C, Raineri SM, Giarratano A. (2015). Effect of High-Fidelity Simulation on Medical Students' Knowledge about Advanced Life Support: A Randomized Study. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0125685

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc multiple logistic regression on the randomized data using a 70% correct-answer threshold selected by consensus; the quote prints "OR 4.45" for group and OR 1.04 for pre-test score. A significantly higher 71.7% vs 37.5% of group S reached the threshold (p = 0.001).

> "Both group and pre-test score resulted as independently associated with the selected outcome (group, OR 4.45, C.I. 1,74–11,3; pre-test score, OR 1.04, C.I. 1,02 to 1,07), with a Hosmer and Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test for the model of p = 0.72."
