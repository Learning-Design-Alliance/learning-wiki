# Rubber-band and steel-ruler vibration demonstrations tend to cause primary students to confuse amplitude with frequency when learning the frequency concept

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Teachers report that rubber-band and steel-ruler demonstration experiments tend to cause students to confuse amplitude and frequency, and that the naked eye cannot easily distinguish vibration speed. [→ Zhong Wang 2023](#zhong-wang-2023)

## Evidence

### Zhong Wang 2023

Zhong Wang. (2023). Soliciting opinions and solutions on the "Q Zhang's Problem". [doi:10.31235/osf.io/a3tw5](https://doi.org/10.31235/osf.io/a3tw5)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

A practitioner-reported teaching problem from a Beijing primary science teacher (2022), not a controlled study. The teacher found the demonstrations' effect "was not obvious" and that students tugging harder change amplitude rather than frequency; teachers using other textbook versions reported the same tendency, suggesting universality.

> "experiments on the vibration of objects such as rubber bands and steel rulers were used to assist students in learning, but the effect was not obvious, because it was difficult for the naked eye to distinguish the speed of vibration"
