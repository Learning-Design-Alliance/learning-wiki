# CTT item selection dropped five low-discrimination items, retaining 20 items of moderate difficulty

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Five items with discrimination indices below 0.3 were excluded from the GLAT. [→ Yueqiao Jin 2024](#yueqiao-jin-2024)
`q2 i?` Retained items showed discrimination indices from 0.33 to 0.55 and difficulties from 0.25 to 0.90. [→ Yueqiao Jin 2024 (2)](#yueqiao-jin-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Yueqiao Jin 2024

Yueqiao Jin, Roberto Martinez-Maldonado, Dragan Gašević and Lixiang Yan. (2024). GLAT: The Generative AI Literacy Assessment Test. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00283 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2411.00283](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2411.00283)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Classical test theory item analysis with 200 higher education students computed item difficulty and point-biserial discrimination; items 6, 9, 14, 15, and 20 fell below the 0.3 discrimination threshold and were excluded.

> "atotaloffiveitems(Items6,9,14,15,and20)haddiscriminationindicesbelowthethresholdof0.3."

### Yueqiao Jin 2024 (2)

Yueqiao Jin, Roberto Martinez-Maldonado, Dragan Gašević and Lixiang Yan. (2024). GLAT: The Generative AI Literacy Assessment Test. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00283

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

The article reports the retained items' difficulty spread and that discrimination indices ranged from 0.33 to 0.55 (M = 0.41, SD = 0.06), indicating items differentiate high and low performers across proficiency levels.

> "Of the 20 items retained, the item difficulties ranged between 0.25 and 0.90 (M = 0.67, SD = 0.14)"
