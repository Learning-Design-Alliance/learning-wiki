# Programs offering only literacy-related activities yield larger effects than programs also targeting other cognitive or socioemotional skills

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Literacy-only programs yielded larger overall and comprehension-related effects (d = 0.66 and 0.68) than programs also targeting other skills. [→ Suzanne Fikrat-Wevers 2021](#suzanne-fikrat-wevers-2021)

## Evidence

### Suzanne Fikrat-Wevers 2021

Suzanne Fikrat-Wevers, Roel van Steensel, Lidia Arends. (2021). Effects of Family Literacy Programs on the Emergent Literacy Skills of Children From Low-SES Families: A Meta-Analysis. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654321998075

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Moderator analysis of immediate posttests (k overall = 48) found literacy-only programs averaged d = 0.66 (SE = 0.10) overall versus d = 0.13–0.24 for programs adding cognitive, socioemotional, or both skill types; Q(3) = 11.13* overall.

> "Programs only offering literacy-related activities yielded both larger overall effects and larger effects on comprehension-related skills than interventions also targeting other skills."
