## Evidence

### Melby-Lervåg 2016

Melby-Lervåg, M., Redick, T. S., & Hulme, C. (2016). Working Memory Training Does Not Improve Performance on Measures of Intelligence or Other Measures of “Far Transfer”: Evidence From a Meta-Analytic Review. Perspectives on Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616635612

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analysis of 87 publications with 145 experimental comparisons using pretest-posttest gain effect sizes (Hedges's g) under a random-effects model. Table 1 reports treated-control effects of g = 0.05 for nonverbal abilities (67 studies), 0.05 verbal abilities, 0.08 word decoding, and 0.06 arithmetic, all with confidence intervals including zero.

> "In studies with treated controls, the effects of training on nonverbal ability, verbal ability, word decoding, and arithmetic are close to zero and not significant (see Table 1)."
