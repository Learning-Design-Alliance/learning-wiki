# FTE thinking times correlate with term relatedness: expert-judged unrelated terms take longer to recall

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In FTE response data, longer thinking times occur for terms judged unrelated to the previous few terms (jumps), and shorter times for related terms (non-jumps), though the distributions overlap. [→ Beatty 2002](#beatty-2002)

## Evidence

### Beatty 2002

Beatty, I. D., Gerace, W. J., & Dufresne, R. J. (2002). Measuring and Modeling Physics Students' Conceptual Knowledge Structures Through Term Association Times. UMass Physics Education Research Group. [doi:10.1119/1.1482067](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1482067)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Analysis of the Physics I study, in which a domain expert classified each term of each of 16 subjects' response lists as a jump or non-jump relative to the three preceding terms. For all but one subject, "the mean and median of the jump distribution are clearly larger" than for non-jumps; the exception had atypically few points.

> "for any given subject, the thinking times associated with jumps are generally larger than the thinking times associated with non-jumps, though the two distributions overlap significantly."
