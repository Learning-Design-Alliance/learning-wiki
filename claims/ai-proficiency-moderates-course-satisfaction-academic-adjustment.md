## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Zhang J and Yin Y 2026

Zhang J and Yin Y (2026). A study on AI-enabled course development, AI proficiency, and first-year students' academic and psychological adjustment. Front. Psychol. 17:1909803. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1909803

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

A simple moderation model (PROCESS Model 1) estimated in the 551-student cross-sectional survey tested H5. The article reports the interaction "was positive and statistically significant (B = 0.12, SE = 0.04, t = 3.01, p = 0.003)"; main effects of course development satisfaction (B = 0.34) and AI proficiency (B = 0.27) were also significant.

> "The interaction term between course development satisfaction and AI proficiency was positive and statistically significant (B = 0.12, SE = 0.04, t = 3.01, p = 0.003), indicating that the positive association between course development satisfaction and academic adjustment was significantly stronger among students with higher AI proficiency"
