The review reports, citing Dunleavy et al. (2009) and Klopfer and Squire (2008), that real-world formats uniquely afford embodiment and social immersion; comparative studies indicate real-world formats "elicit more collaborative talk and excitement, whereas virtual formats facilitate more systematic trials and resets."

> "Unique affordances to these real-world, experiential simulations that differed from virtual only included a heightened presence and relevance through embodiment, place, and face-to-face social immersion (Dunleavy et al., 2009; Klopfer & Squire, 2008)."
