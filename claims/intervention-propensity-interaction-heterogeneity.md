# Treatment effects varied by propensity of treatment assignment: schools with better pre-treatment conditions (lower propensity) gained more, with a significant negative intervention-by-propensity interaction in Grade 8 math

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Negative intervention-by-propensity interactions (significant only in Grade 8 math, β = -1.850, p < .01) indicate treatment schools with better pre-treatment conditions produced more PI gains. [→ Lee 2013](#lee-2013)

## Evidence

### Lee 2013

Lee, J., Shin, H. & Amo, L. C. (2013). Evaluating the impact of NCLB school interventions in New York state: Does one size fit all? Education Policy Analysis Archives, 21 (67). http://epaa.asu.edu/ojs/article/view/1122

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Interaction terms from the IPTW regression models (Table 3), illustrated for Grade 8 ELA in Figure 3: the treatment group showed higher PI gain than the control group at lower propensity ranges but lower gain at higher propensity ranges. The article offers two possible explanations: schools closer to AYP targets, or more favorable educational conditions and capacity.

> "there were also some tendency of negative interactions between intervention and propensity (i.e., chance of treatment group assignment): Grade 4 ELA (β = -.058, p > .05), Grade 8 ELA (β = -.133, p > .05), Grade 4 math (β = -.359, p > .05) and Grade 8 math (β = -1.850, p < .01)."
