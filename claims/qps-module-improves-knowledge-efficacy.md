## Evidence

### Qureshi Muhammad Fazal Hussain; Asif Fozia; Khan Shahmeer Raza; Akhtar Munazza; Shah Muzna; Altaf Ahmed; Latif Asad 2026

Qureshi Muhammad Fazal Hussain; Asif Fozia; Khan Shahmeer Raza; Akhtar Munazza; Shah Muzna; Altaf Ahmed; Latif Asad. (2026). Strengthening patient safety competencies among physician interns through a structured quality and safety course. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09875-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-then-pre self-efficacy survey (18 items, nine domains, 5-point Likert) administered after course completion among 166 interns; the overall mean rose from 3.54 ± 0.27 to 4.21 ± 0.17 with "mean difference = 0.67; p < 0.001". The error reporting item gained 1.34 points (2.73 to 4.07, p < 0.001).

> "Participants demonstrated a substantial and statistically significant improvement in knowledge efficacy following the intervention, with the overall mean score increasing from 3.54 ± 0.27 to 4.21 ± 0.17 (mean difference = 0.67; p < 0.001)"

### Qureshi Muhammad Fazal Hussain; Asif Fozia; Khan Shahmeer Raza; Akhtar Munazza; Shah Muzna; Altaf Ahmed; Latif Asad 2026 (2)

Qureshi Muhammad Fazal Hussain; Asif Fozia; Khan Shahmeer Raza; Akhtar Munazza; Shah Muzna; Altaf Ahmed; Latif Asad. (2026). Strengthening patient safety competencies among physician interns through a structured quality and safety course. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09875-4

`q2 · i0` · `causal · r1`

In the same knowledge efficacy analysis, the PPE item (item 16) moved from 3.83 ± 0.95 to 3.93 ± 1.02 (mean difference 0.10, p = 0.21); the article reports "PPE knowledge showing no statistically significant improvement". The authors attribute this to the topic not being discussed in the course due to time constraints.

> "Changes related to isolation precautions and personal protective equipment use were comparatively modest, with PPE knowledge showing no statistically significant improvement, suggesting a higher baseline familiarity in these areas"
