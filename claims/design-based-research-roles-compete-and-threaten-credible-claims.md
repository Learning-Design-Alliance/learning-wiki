# Design-based research asks one person to be researcher, designer, project manager and theorist, and those roles compete.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 source · `q2` narrative review · no effect size

The claim is about the method's structure: the risks follow from combining the roles, whatever the skill of the person holding them.

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` The literature names four roles a design-based researcher holds at once (researcher, project manager, theorist, designer), and excess focus on any one can jeopardise the others. [→ Christensen & West 2018](#christensen-west-2018)

`q2 i?` A researcher intimately involved in conceiving, building, implementing and studying an intervention is exposed to bias in analysis, and the design as built may not stay faithful to the theory it is meant to test. [→ Christensen & West 2018](#christensen-west-2018)

`q2 · narrative review of the design-based research literature` · `i? · no effect size` · `n=N/A`

A review of how design-based research developed from 1960s design science, and of the problems facing it. On roles, it draws on Barab and Squire (2004): "if a researcher is intimately involved in the conceptualization, design, development, implementation, and researching of a pedagogical approach, then ensuring that researchers can make credible and trustworthy assertions is a challenge". It also draws on Hoadley (2004), that "the treatment's fidelity to theory [is] initially, and sometimes continually, suspect", and on Anderson and Shattuck (2012) on bias in analysis. It adds that design decisions alter the research, and research decisions constrain the design.

## Discussion

**What would reduce the risk.** The authors' remedies are structural rather than personal. Each study should state whether it is research *into*, *for* or *through* design, so one study does not try to do all three. Its papers should report the design process and each iteration explicitly, with local and transferable theory separated. See [Design-Based Research](../processes/design-based-research.md).

**For a reader of DBR findings.** A claim resting on a DBR study should be read with its roles in view: who designed the intervention, and who analysed the data. Where it is the same person, the finding is closer to a well-documented design case than to an independent test.
