## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students with more Global/Monitoring metacognitive skills were more likely to correctly answer comprehension questions about hypertexts read at home. [→ Burin 2020](#burin-2020)

## Evidence

### Burin 2020

Burin, Debora Ines; Gonzalez, Federico Martin; Barreyro, Juan Pablo; Injoque Ricle, Irene. (2020). Metacognitive regulation contributes to digital text comprehension in E-learning. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-020-09226-8

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Study of 219 college students reading two low previous knowledge hypertexts at home in an e-learning environment, with a metacognitive inventory referred to the tasks just completed. The article reports that "more Global/Monitor metacognitive skills" increased the likelihood of correctly answering comprehension questions; no effect size is printed.

> "Students with more verbal ability, more Global/Monitor metacognitive skills, and more Internet experience were more likely to correctly answer comprehension questions."
