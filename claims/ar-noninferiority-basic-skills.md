## Evidence

### El Ashry M 2026

El Ashry M, El Ashry A, Khalique H, Abdalle Y, Yeung T. (2026). Augmented Reality in Surgical Training: Systematic Review of Its Impact on Technical Performance in Surgical Trainees. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/71572

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Parallel RCT (n=38 suturing-novice medical students) comparing HoloLens 2 self-training with 2D instructional video for subcuticular suturing; the review reports the null contrasts (P=.54, P=.91); equivalence was not tested.

> "Nagayo et al [9] found no significant difference between AR and video-based self-training in suturing skill improvement (global rating: P=.54; task specific: P=.91)"

### El Ashry M 2026 (2)

El Ashry M, El Ashry A, Khalique H, Abdalle Y, Yeung T. (2026). Augmented Reality in Surgical Training: Systematic Review of Its Impact on Technical Performance in Surgical Trainees. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/71572

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Evaluator-blinded parallel RCT (n=20 medical students) comparing Vuzix M300XL smart glasses with remote telestration against traditional on-site teaching for five suture types; the review reports null mOSATS difference with a time advantage (P=.04).

> "Lopes et al [5] similarly found no significant difference in mOSATS scores, although the telestration group performed tasks significantly faster when working independently (1393 s vs 1679 s; P=.04)."
