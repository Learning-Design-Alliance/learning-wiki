# Baseline self-efficacy and knowledge differed by university type and GPA, and gain patterns differed: public students started higher while private students and low-GPA students gained more

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Pre-training self-efficacy and overall self-efficacy/self-esteem were significantly higher in public university students and students with higher GPAs (p < 0.05). [→ Moustafa HAM 2024](#moustafa-ham-2024)
`q3 i?` Private university students showed a significantly greater pre-to-post knowledge gain than public university students (p < 0.05), and students with the lowest GPAs had the largest self-efficacy increase. [→ Moustafa HAM 2024 (2)](#moustafa-ham-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Moustafa HAM 2024

Moustafa HAM, Hamid AE, Hassoub G, Kassem AB. (2024). Assessing the impact of critical care training on pharmacy students in Egypt: a pre-post study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06427-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Table 5 of the pre-post study (n = 72): public vs private self-efficacy pre U = 295.000 (p = 0.002), overall pre U = 331.000 (p = 0.007); GPA Kruskal-Wallis self-efficacy pre H = 14.447 (p = 0.002).

> "Both self-efficacy and overall self-efficacy and self-esteem were significantly higher in public university students pre-training compared to private university students and in students with higher GPAs compared with those with lower GPAs (p < 0.05)"

### Moustafa HAM 2024 (2)

Moustafa HAM, Hamid AE, Hassoub G, Kassem AB. (2024). Assessing the impact of critical care training on pharmacy students in Egypt: a pre-post study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06427-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Table 6: overall difference public 0.06 ± 0.45 vs private 0.36 ± 0.82, U = 363.500 (p = 0.023); GPA < 2 self-efficacy difference 1.75 ± 1.30, H = 12.007 (p = 0.007). Table 7: knowledge gain private 0.40 ± 0.15 vs public 0.30 ± 0.18, U = 332.00 (p = 0.008).

> "Students from public universities had a greater difference in overall self-efficacy and self-esteem scores between pre- and post-training. Also, those with the lowest GPAs had the most significant increase in their self-efficacy scores post-training versus pre-training (p < 0.05)"
