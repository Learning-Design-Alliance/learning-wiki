Evidence¶

Maier 2017¶

Maier, A., Daniel, J., Oakes, J., & Lam, L. (2017). Community schools as an effective school improvement strategy: A review of the evidence. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/community-schools-effective-school-improvement-report

q2 · i? · review · r3

The report's synthesis finding for Pillar 4, based on 35 studies including 13 research syntheses of collaborative leadership and practice. The review frames this as creating conditions for improvement rather than a direct tested effect; no effect size is printed.

"The collaborative leadership, practice, and relationships found in community schools can create the conditions necessary to improve student learning and well-being, as well as improve relationships within and beyond the school walls."

Maier 2017 (2)¶

Maier, A., Daniel, J., Oakes, J., & Lam, L. (2017). Community schools as an effective school improvement strategy: A review of the evidence. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/community-schools-effective-school-improvement-report

q2 · i? · review · r3

A mechanism statement from the same synthesis finding: the review reports, with hedging (appear to be), that social capital and teacher-peer learning explain the collaboration-achievement link. No effect size is printed.

"The development of social capital and teacher-peer learning appear to be the factors that explain the link between collaboration and better student achievement."
