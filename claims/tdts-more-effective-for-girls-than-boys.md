## Evidence

### Pam Hanley 2015

Pam Hanley, Robert Slavin, Louise Elliott. (2015). Thinking, Doing, Talking Science: Evaluation report and Executive summary. Education Endowment Foundation. http://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN54969918

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Subgroup analysis of the clustered RCT comparing boys (n=656 in model) and girls (n=601 in model). Girls' effect size was +0.32 (95% CI 0.16–0.48) versus +0.12 for boys (95% CI -0.03–0.27); the gender contrast was significant at "p=.023".

> "The diffe rence between the effect on the girls and boys was sufficiently large to provide evidence that TDTS was more effective for girls than boys (p=.023)."
