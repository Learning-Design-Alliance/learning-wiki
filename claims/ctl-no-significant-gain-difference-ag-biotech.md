# A contextual teaching and learning curriculum produced no statistically significant difference in pre/posttest gain versus a scientific-basis curriculum in a postsecondary agricultural biotechnology course

## Evidence

### Curry 2012

Curry, Wilson, Flowers, & Farin. (2012). Scientific Basis vs. Contextualized Teaching and Learning: The Effect on the Achievement of Postsecondary Students. Journal of Agricultural Education. https://doi.org/10.5032/jae.2012.01057

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental non-equivalent control-group study with two treatment groups (n=16 experimental, n=22 comparison) taught the same seven environmental biotechnology units via distance education in different semesters, assessed with an identical 25-item pre/posttest. The t-test on gains was "not a statistically significant difference (p=.48)". No effect size was printed.

> "A t–test showed that even though the experimental treatment had a greater raw gain score, it was not a statistically significant difference (p=.48). Thus, the null hypothesis was not rejected."
