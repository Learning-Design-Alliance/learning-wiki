Evidence¶

Sirmandi and Sardareh 2016¶

Effat Heidari Sirmandi, Sedigheh Abbasnasab Sardareh. (2016). The Effect of BBC World Clips With and Without Subtitles on Intermediate EFL Learners' Vocabulary Development. Malaysian Online Journal of Educational Sciences. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1116325

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Three-group experiment with 60 intermediate Iranian EFL learners (20 per group) watching ten BBC World clips over ten sessions; posttest means were 41.2 (subtitle), 34.9 (no subtitle), and 30.0 (control), and "the group with bimodal subtitles significantly outperformed the other groups".

"As shown in Table 2, the group with bimodal subtitles significantly outperformed the other groups and had the highest mean score. The experimental without subtitle group performed better in the posttest than the control group."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • One-way ANOVA on posttest scores showed a statistically significant difference among the subtitle, no-subtitle, and control groups (F = 84.161, Sig. = .000) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Watching BBC World clips without English subtitles also improved intermediate EFL learners' vocabulary development relative to no treatment — related
  • Watching cartoons with English subtitles produces no statistically significant vocabulary-gain advantage over watching without subtitles among upper-intermediate ELT students — reports the opposite
  • The three groups did not differ significantly in vocabulary knowledge at pretest, supporting group homogeneity — related
  • Vocabulary improvement occurred incidentally regardless of subtitles, attributed to target words being contextualized in the cartoons — related
