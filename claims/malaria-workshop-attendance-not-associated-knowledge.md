## Evidence

### Rusk A 2012

Rusk A, Smith N, Menya D, Obala A, Simiyu C, Khwa-Otsyula B, O'Meara W. (2012). Does anti-malarial drug knowledge predict anti-malarial dispensing practice in drug outlets? A survey of medicine retailers in western Kenya. Malaria Journal. https://doi.org/10.1186/1475-2875-11-263

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey analysis in Table 4a: workshop attendees were 65% correct versus 67% for non-attendees, OR = 0.94 (0.42 - 2.07; 0.873), non-significant; the article reports attendees "were not more likely to know the correct first-line anti-malarial". No effect size printed.

> "However, those who attended a workshop on malaria were not more likely to know the correct first-line anti-malarial, even if that workshop was after the change to AL as the first-line therapy in 2006 (Table 4a)."

### Rusk A 2012 (2)

Rusk A, Smith N, Menya D, Obala A, Simiyu C, Khwa-Otsyula B, O'Meara W. (2012). Does anti-malarial drug knowledge predict anti-malarial dispensing practice in drug outlets? A survey of medicine retailers in western Kenya. Malaria Journal. https://doi.org/10.1186/1475-2875-11-263

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey analysis of dispensing practices: recommending AL "was not associated with having attended a workshop on malaria or anti-malarial drugs", nor with receiving MOH drug information; no test statistics or effect sizes are printed for this contrast.

> "Recommending the current first-line anti-malarial to either adult or pediatric clients was not associated with having attended a workshop on malaria or anti-malarial drugs (Table 4), regardless of whether the workshop was held before or after the change to AL in 2006."
