# Positive attitude toward communication skills correlates positively with communication performance in assistants, interns, and externs, but not professors

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` In the assistant, intern, and extern groups, positive attitude showed significant positive Pearson correlations with communication performance (r = 0.229, 0.349, 0.321 respectively); no significant correlation appeared in professors. [→ Moezzi Masoumeh 2024](#moezzi-masoumeh-2024)

## Evidence

### Moezzi Masoumeh 2024

Moezzi Masoumeh, Rasekh Sara, Zare Elahe, Karimi Masoud. (2024). Evaluating clinical communication skills of medical students, assistants, and professors. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-05015-4

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Pearson correlation analysis within the cross-sectional survey; Table 3 prints r = 0.229 (assistants, p < 0.05), r = 0.349 (interns, p < 0.001), r = 0.321 (externs, p < 0.001). The article states "the more positive the attitude toward doctor-patient communication skills, the better the performance."

> "in the other three groups significant positive relationships were observed between positive attitude and communication performance, that is, the more positive the attitude toward doctor-patient communication skills, the better the performance."
