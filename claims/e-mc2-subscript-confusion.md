# Writing the famous formula as E = mc2 without the subscript 0 generates the false idea that mass depends on velocity

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Dropping the subscript 0 in E = mc2, though seemingly innocuous, produces unacceptable confusion, including the false idea that the mass of a body is equivalent to its total energy and depends on its velocity. [→ L. B. Okun 2008](#l-b-okun-2008)

## Evidence

L. B. Okun. (2008). The theory of relativity and the Pythagorean theorem. https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.2379 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0809.2379](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0809.2379)

The author's argument in section 5.4: the popular form "E = mc 2" without the subscript 0 "results in unacceptable confusion in u nderstanding the foundations of physics", generating the false idea that mass depends on velocity. This is an interpretive claim, not a tested result.

> "This simpliﬁcation, to which Einstein himself sometimes re sorted, might seem innocuous at ﬁrst glance, but it results in unacceptable confusion in u nderstanding the foundations of physics."
