## Evidence

### Kulp MT 2026

Kulp MT, Ciner EB, Candy TR, Ying G-s, Hall C, Quinn G, Moore B. (2026). Impact of uncorrected hyperopia on visual function and academic performance in preschool and school-age children. Optometry and Vision Science. https://doi.org/10.1002/ovs2.70018

`q3 · i1` · `review · r3`

The review reports the VIP-HIP study, a comparison of 244 hyperopic and 248 age-typical children determined by cycloplegic refraction, finding "statistically significant difficulties in early literacy" with "a moderate effect size of 0.41" deemed by education experts to warrant intervention; deficits were greatest in print knowledge.

> "The VIP-HIP study of 492 children (244 hyperopic and 248 with age-typical refractive error, determined by cycloplegic refraction) in preschool and kindergarten reported statistically significant difficulties in early literacy for children with moderate uncorrected hyperopia and reduced near visual function compared to children with age-typical refractive error."

### Kulp MT 2026 (2)

Kulp MT, Ciner EB, Candy TR, Ying G-s, Hall C, Quinn G, Moore B. (2026). Impact of uncorrected hyperopia on visual function and academic performance in preschool and school-age children. Optometry and Vision Science. https://doi.org/10.1002/ovs2.70018

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The review attributes to Shankar et al. a small pilot comparison of 13 hyperopic versus 19 age-typical children showing "reduced performance on tests of emergent literacy" with no phonological awareness differences. No effect size is printed.

> "Shankar et al. found reduced performance on tests of emergent literacy (letter and word recognition, emergent orthography) in 13 children with uncorrected hyperopia (>=2 D) as compared to 19 children with age-typical refractive error (<=1.5 D), with no differences in phonological awareness or family variables."
