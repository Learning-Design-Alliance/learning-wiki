## Evidence

### Jacobsen Mads Emil 2026

Jacobsen Mads Emil, Ghidinelli Monica, Subramaniam Chitra, Hare Kristoffer Borbjerg, Nayahangan Leizl Joy, Konge Lars, Gustafsson Amandus. (2026). Assessment of surgical skills on a novel expert-informed immersive virtual reality simulator for distal radius fracture fixation: a cross-sectional validation study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08983-5

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Exploratory group comparisons of 32 automated metrics (Fisher's exact test for binary; bootstrapped t tests for continuous metrics; unadjusted p values). Effect sizes ranged from 0.09 to 2.7, e.g. facet image d = 2.7.

> "Experienced surgeons significantly outperformed novices on nine of 32 metrics. Conversely, novices outperformed experienced surgeons on one metric: metric 24 (distance from screw to fracture lines)."

### Jacobsen Mads Emil 2026 (2)

Jacobsen Mads Emil, Ghidinelli Monica, Subramaniam Chitra, Hare Kristoffer Borbjerg, Nayahangan Leizl Joy, Konge Lars, Gustafsson Amandus. (2026). Assessment of surgical skills on a novel expert-informed immersive virtual reality simulator for distal radius fracture fixation: a cross-sectional validation study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08983-5

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Authors' interpretation from review of performance data and captured videos: novices often placed the plate more distally, and the simulator does not allow tactile identification of fracture lines after reduction.

> "This combination of fracture anatomy and lack of haptic feedback likely explains why experienced surgeons – who would likely normally detect such interference tactilely – appeared to perform worse on this metric, despite superior overall technical ability."
