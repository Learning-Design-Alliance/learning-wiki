# Instruction must be challenging for learning gains, but raising challenge without classroom control and student support harms grades and engagement

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Classroom instruction needs to be challenging for students to show learning gains. [→ Stevens 2014](#stevens-2014)
`q2 i?` Increasing challenge does not lead to more learning unless teachers maintain high classroom control, and grades decline with higher challenge unless teachers increase support. [→ Stevens 2014 (2)](#stevens-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Stevens 2014

Stevens, W. D., Allensworth, E. M., Pareja, A. S., de la Torre, M., Gwynne, J. A., Rosenkranz, T., & Sebastian, J. (2014). Free to Fail or On-Track to College: An Introduction to the UChicago CCSR Research Series. UChicago CCSR Research Brief. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/free-fail-or-track-college

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The Instruction Study used district-wide student and teacher survey reports of specific classes with statistical models adjusted for background and prior achievement to relate instructional elements to grades and EPAS test gains. It found instruction "needs to be challenging for students to show learning gains."

> "The report shows that classroom instruction needs to be challenging for students to show learning gains."

### Stevens 2014 (2)

Stevens, W. D., Allensworth, E. M., Pareja, A. S., de la Torre, M., Gwynne, J. A., Rosenkranz, T., & Sebastian, J. (2014). Free to Fail or On-Track to College: An Introduction to the UChicago CCSR Research Series. UChicago CCSR Research Brief. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/free-fail-or-track-college

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same statistical modeling of classroom instruction showed challenge alone is insufficient: "Increasing challenge does not lead to more learning unless teachers can maintain high levels of classroom control," and grades decline with higher challenge unless support increases. The brief warns teachers adopting harder standards risk higher failure and disengagement without monitoring and support.

> "Increasing challenge does not lead  to more learning unless teachers can maintain high levels of classroom control. Furthermore, grades decline with higher challenge unless  teachers increase the level of support they give  students."
