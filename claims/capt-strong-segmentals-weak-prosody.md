## Evidence

### Ali 2016

Ali, S. (2016). Towards the development of a comprehensive pedagogical framework for pronunciation training based on adapted automatic speech recognition systems. In S. Papadima-Sophocleous, L. Bradley & S. Thouësny (Eds), CALL communities and culture – short papers from EUROCALL 2016 (pp. 7-13). Research-publishing.net. https://doi.org/10.14705/rpnet.2016.eurocall2016.530

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

This is the article's own narrative-review synthesis of existing CAPT research and tools, stating that "most CAPT softwares show promising results" for segmental sounds while prosody and fluency "still seem to require further research and development." No effect sizes are printed.

> "On the whole and to our knowledge, most CAPT softwares show promising results and very positive impacts on the pronunciation of segmental sounds among various types of learners. Prosodic features and fluency generally speaking are areas of pronunciation training that still seem to require further research and development."

### Ngo et al. 2024

Ngo, T. T.-N., Chen, H. H.-J., & Lai, K. K.-W. (2024). The effectiveness of automatic speech recognition in ESL/EFL pronunciation: A meta-analysis. *ReCALL, 36*(1), 4–21. [doi:10.1017/S0958344023000113](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0958344023000113)

`q3 · meta-analysis (three-level, random effects)` · `i2 · medium effect, g=0.69` · `n=15 studies (38 effect sizes)` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

A meta-analysis of 15 studies (2008–2021, 38 effect sizes) comparing ESL/EFL learners who practised pronunciation with automatic speech recognition (ASR) against a non-ASR condition. The pooled effect was g = 0.69 (95% CI 0.31 to 1.08), with substantial heterogeneity (Q = 227.70; I² 56.56% between studies). The authors did not test for publication bias, and name the small number of primary studies as the main limitation. Read from the publisher's open-access full text; the article was published online in 2023. By outcome, the effect on segmental pronunciation was g = 0.82 (10 studies, 95% CI 0.24 to 1.41) and on suprasegmental pronunciation g = 0.37 (5 studies, 95% CI −0.22 to 0.97); studies measuring both gave g = 0.70. The synthesis covers ASR only, one kind of CAPT.

> "The pooled effect size was medium (g = 0.69), and the confidence interval was not across zero (95% CI = [0.31, 1.08])."

## Discussion

Ngo et al. (2024) support this claim for ASR-based training, with a large pooled effect on segmentals and a small, uncertain one on suprasegmentals. They also note that an earlier meta-analysis of CAPT that included multimedia tools rather than ASR found the reverse pattern, so the segmental-over-prosody pattern may be a property of ASR rather than of CAPT in general.
