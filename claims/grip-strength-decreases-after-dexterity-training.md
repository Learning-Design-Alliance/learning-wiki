## Evidence

### Pérez-Chicharro María 2025

Pérez-Chicharro María, González-Olmo María José, Nieto-Moraleda Carolina, Obeso-Benítez Paula, Martínez-Piédrola Rosa M, Sánchez-Herrera-Baeza Patricia, Pérez-de-Heredia-Torres Marta, Romero-Maroto Martín, Huertas-Hoyas Elisabet. (2025). Improving dental students’ dexterity and strength: an intervention study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08015-8

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Wilcoxon pre-post comparison in the 37-student cohort: dominant-hand grip fell from 30.01 to 27.68 kg and non-dominant from 28.36 to 25.51 kg, with the article reporting "a medium effect size (r >.35, p <.01)". The authors call this an unexpected finding and attribute it plausibly to training that emphasised fine motor control rather than muscle strengthening, or to transient fatigue.

> "Similarly, strength has significantly decreased with a medium effect size (r >.35, p <.01), as we obtain positive results in all dynamometer tests."
