# The ToM–achievement association does not differ significantly across reading comprehension, math, and general academic performance domains

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · quant-synthesis `r2`–`r3` · `q4` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` Academic achievement domain did not significantly moderate the ToM–achievement association, F(2,86) = 2.44, p = .09. [→ Lecce 2026](#lecce-2026)
`q4 i2` The association was significant within each domain: reading r = .318, math r = .380, academic performance r = .254. [→ Lecce 2026 (2)](#lecce-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Lecce 2026

Lecce, S., Stagnitto, S. M., Mascheretti, S., Lampis, V., & Devine, R. T. (2026). Children's Theory of Mind and Academic Achievement: a Meta-analysis on Reading Comprehension and Math Skills. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10185-w

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-regression over all 89 cross-sectional effect sizes tested whether domain (reading, math, academic performance) moderated the association. The moderator test was not significant, "F(2,86) = 2.44, p = .09"; no effect size is printed for this moderator test.

> "The academic achievement domain was not significant, F(2,86) = 2.44, p = .09, indicating that the strength of the association between ToM and academic achievement did not differ significantly across the academic achievement domains."

### Lecce 2026 (2)

Lecce, S., Stagnitto, S. M., Mascheretti, S., Lampis, V., & Devine, R. T. (2026). Children's Theory of Mind and Academic Achievement: a Meta-analysis on Reading Comprehension and Math Skills. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10185-w

`q4 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Post-hoc tests within the cross-sectional meta-analysis showed significant pooled correlations in each domain, each a medium printed effect size: reading comprehension r = .318, math r = .380, and general academic performance r = .254, all p < .001.

> "The association between ToM and academic achievement was significant for all the three AA domains, rToM-ReadingComprehension = .318, 95% CI [.26 .37], p < .001 (Fig. 2B); rToM-Math = .380, 95% CI [.29 .43], p < .001 (see Fig. 2C), rToM-AcademicPerformance = .254, 95% CI [.16 .34], p < .001 (Fig. 2D)."
