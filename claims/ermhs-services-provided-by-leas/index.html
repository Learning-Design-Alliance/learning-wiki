Evidence¶

Doutre 2021¶

Doutre, S. M., Willis, J., Barrett, T., Ripma, T., Caparas, R., & Krausen, K. (2021). California State Special Education Funding System Study, Part 2: Findings, Implications, and Considerations for Improving Special Education Funding in California. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED616106

q2 · i? · associational · r2

The study's distribution analysis found "Eighty-four percent of mental health services for students with disabilities were provided by students' LEAs" despite ERMHS funds being allocated to SELPAs, suggesting possible coordination and cost-saving opportunities at the LEA level.

"Eighty-four percent of mental health services for students with disabilities were provided by students’ LEAs, 7 percent by NPS providers, and 9 percent by other LEAs or by SELPAs."

Related Claims¶

  • California serves more than 725,000 K-12 students with disabilities and invests roughly $12 billion annually in special education, with LEA unrestricted funds covering the majority of spending — related
  • More than 90 percent of California LEAs belong to multi-LEA SELPAs whose governing boards set their own funding formulas, giving the state little influence over amounts reaching member LEAs — related
  • LEAs most frequently used short-term behavioral health funds to expand internal staffing and direct services rather than partnerships and structural investments — related
  • School-based mental health supports benefit students, but staffing ratios and service availability fall far short of recommendations — related
  • California's statewide high-cost pools are among the lowest-funded in the nation, and SELPA directors report paperwork burdens outweigh their benefit
