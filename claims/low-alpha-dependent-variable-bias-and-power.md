# A low alpha for the dependent variable leaves the unstandardized regression coefficient unbiased but attenuates the standardized coefficient and reduces statistical power

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` When the dependent variable has a low alpha, the unstandardized coefficient is unbiased while the standardized coefficient is attenuated, particularly when alpha is low. [→ Steffen Zitzmann 2025](#steffen-zitzmann-2025)
`q1 i?` A low alpha of the dependent variable inflates the standard error of the regression coefficient, raising the risk of failing to detect an existing effect. [→ Steffen Zitzmann 2025 (2)](#steffen-zitzmann-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Steffen Zitzmann 2025

Steffen Zitzmann, Gabe A. Orona. (2025). Why We Might Still be Concerned About Low Cronbach's Alphas in Domain-specific Knowledge Tests. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10015-5

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical derivation in simple regression with an error-prone dependent variable: the unstandardized coefficient equals the true-score coefficient and is unbiased, but the derivation shows "the standardized coefficient will be attenuated, particularly when alpha is low."

> "showing that unlike the unstandardized coefficient, the standardized coefficient will be attenuated, particularly when alpha is low."

### Steffen Zitzmann 2025 (2)

Steffen Zitzmann, Gabe A. Orona. (2025). Why We Might Still be Concerned About Low Cronbach's Alphas in Domain-specific Knowledge Tests. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10015-5

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical derivation of the standard error of the unstandardized coefficient: with a low alpha of the dependent variable, 1∕αY becomes large and B∗2 small, so "the square root of the difference between these two terms will be large", implying a large standard error and high risk of failing to detect an existing effect.

> "Note that if /u1D6FCY is low, 1∕/u1D6FCY will be large, while B∗2 will be small, together imply- ing that the square root of the difference between these two terms will be large."
