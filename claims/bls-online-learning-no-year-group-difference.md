# Theoretical learning gains did not differ significantly between 1st–2nd year and 3rd–4th year nursing students

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` ANOVA showed no significant difference (p=0.475) in post-test learning between 1st–2nd year students (9.20 ± 1.60) and 3rd–4th year students (9.67 ± 0.61), while the increase from pre- to post-test was significant (p<0.001) regardless of course year. [→ Tobase L 2017](#tobase-l-2017)

`q3 i?` Post-course learning did not differ significantly between first/second-year and third/fourth-year students (p = 0.475), though pre-test scores differed by year. [→ Tobase Lucia 2017](#tobase-lucia-2017)

## Evidence

### Tobase L 2017

Tobase L, Peres HHC, Gianotto-Oliveira R, Smith N, Polastri TF, Timerman S. (2017). The effects of an online basic life support course on undergraduate nursing students' learning. Int J Med Educ 8:309-313. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.5985.cbce

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

ANOVA on the written test compared 56 students in the first two years (post-test 9.20 ± 1.60) with 6 students in the last two years (9.67 ± 0.61); "no difference (p= 0.475) in learning" was found between the groups.

> "The analysis indicated no difference (p= 0.475) in learning between 1st–2nd year students (9.20 ± 1.60) and 3rd–4th year students (9.67 ± 0.61). However, a significant increase in the average scores after taking the online course was observed (p<0.001), regardless of the course year of the students"

### Tobase Lucia 2017

Tobase Lucia, Peres Heloisa Helena Ciqueto, Tomazini Edenir Aparecida Sartorelli, Teodoro Simone Valentim, Ramos Meire Bruna, Polastri Thatiane Facholi. (2017). Basic life support: evaluation of learning using simulation and immediate feedback devices. Revista Latino-Americana de Enfermagem. https://doi.org/10.1590/1518-8345.1957.2942

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

ANOVA comparing pre- and post-test score gains by course-year group (56 students in years 1-2, six in years 3-4). The course-year interaction was non-significant with "no significant difference (p = 0.475)"; no effect size is printed, so equivalence was not tested.

> "At the end of the online course, no significant difference (p = 0.475) was identified in learning among first and second year students, compared to third and fourth year students."
