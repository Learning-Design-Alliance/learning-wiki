## Evidence

### Ryu Jong-Sik 2021

Ryu Jong-Sik, Chung Hae Ryong, Meador Benjamin M., Seo Yongsuk, Kim Kyung-O. (2021). The Associations between Physical Fitness, Complex vs Simple Movement, and Academic Achievement in a Cohort of Fourth Graders. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18052293

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Pearson correlation analysis in the Results section of a cross-sectional study of 110 fourth graders (50 girls). Faster soda pop foot times went with better grades; e.g., "total grades (r = −0.409, p = 0.03)".

> "Soda pop foot times correlated with social studies (r = −0.404, p = 0.004), science (r = −0.320, p = 0.023), English (r = −0.341, p = 0.015), and total grades (r = −0.409, p = 0.03)."

### Ryu Jong-Sik 2021 (2)

Ryu Jong-Sik, Chung Hae Ryong, Meador Benjamin M., Seo Yongsuk, Kim Kyung-O. (2021). The Associations between Physical Fitness, Complex vs Simple Movement, and Academic Achievement in a Cohort of Fourth Graders. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18052293

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Same Pearson correlation analysis in girls. Faster Illinois Agility times were associated with better academic scores, e.g., "science (r = −0.373, p = 0.008)"; correlations are small-to-moderate.

> "Illinois agility times correlated with Korean literature (r = −0.287, p = 0.043), social studies (r = −0.352, p = 0.012), science (r = −0.373, p = 0.008), and total grades (r = −0.313, p = 0.027)."
