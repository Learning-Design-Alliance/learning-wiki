# The mediation effect of professional recognition is statistically significant in all four disciplines, with no significant pairwise differences in indirect effects

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The indirect effect (PQ→PR→EC) is significant in every discipline, e.g. Humanities β = 0.179, Social Sciences β = 0.159, Natural Sciences β = 0.195, Engineering β = 0.174. [→ Tian Qi 2026](#tian-qi-2026)
`q2 i?` Pairwise contrasts of indirect effects show no significant differences between any two disciplines (all 95% CIs of differences include zero). [→ Tian Qi 2026 (2)](#tian-qi-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Tian Qi 2026

Tian Qi, Huang Yinpin, Xiong Jun. (2026). Psychological quality and entrepreneurial competence: the mediating role of professional recognition and moderating effect of academic discipline among Chinese university students. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-03954-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Discipline-specific bootstrap mediation tests in the multi-group SEM (n = 741): "the indirect effect (PQ→PR→EC) was statistically significant in all four disciplines" with the printed betas and confidence intervals as listed in Tables 13–14.

> "Bias-corrected bootstrapping (5,000 resamples) confirmed that the indirect effect (PQ→PR→EC) was statistically significant in all four disciplines (Humanities: β = 0.179, p < 0.01, 95% CI = [0.054, 0.366]; Social Sciences: β = 0.159, p < 0.01, 95% CI = [0.026, 0.334]; Natural Sciences: β = 0.195, p < 0.01, 95% CI = [0.067, 0.372]; and Engineering Sciences: β = 0.174, p < 0.01, 95% CI = [0.039, 0.412])."

### Tian Qi 2026 (2)

Tian Qi, Huang Yinpin, Xiong Jun. (2026). Psychological quality and entrepreneurial competence: the mediating role of professional recognition and moderating effect of academic discipline among Chinese university students. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-03954-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Pairwise contrast tests of indirect effects across disciplines (n = 741): "the pairwise contrasts presented in Table 15 revealed no significant differences between any two disciplines"; for example, the Social Sciences–Humanities difference was 0.019 (95% CI [−0.242, 0.293]).

> "the pairwise contrasts presented in Table 15 revealed no significant differences between any two disciplines (all 95% confidence intervals for the differences included zero)."
