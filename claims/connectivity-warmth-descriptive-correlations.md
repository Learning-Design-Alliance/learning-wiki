## Subclaims
`q2 i?` At the bivariate level, FPN-striatum connectivity was positively associated with grades and performance concurrently and 2 years later, warmth was positively associated with achievement, and connectivity was not associated with warmth. [→ Yang 2025](#yang-2025)

## Evidence

### Yang 2025

Yang, B., Zhou, Z., Chen, Y.-Y., Devakonda, V., Cai, T., Lee, T.-H., & Qu, Y. (2025). Parental warmth buffers the negative impact of weaker fronto‐striatal connectivity on early adolescents' academic achievement. Journal of Research on Adolescence, 35, e12949. https://doi.org/10.1111/jora.12949

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Descriptive statistics and bivariate correlations reported in Table 1 of the ABCD sample. The table prints small correlations (e.g., T1 connectivity with T2 grades r = .06***); connectivity and warmth were uncorrelated (r = .01).

> "FPN‐striatum connectivity was positively associated with school grades and parent‐perceived school performance both concurrently and 2 years later. Parental warmth was also positively associated with school grades and parent‐perceived school performance at both timepoints. However, FPN‐striatum connectivity was not associated with parental warmth."
