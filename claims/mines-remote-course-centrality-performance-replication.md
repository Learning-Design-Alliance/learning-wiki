# In a fully remote pandemic-era math methods course at Mines, student in-strength, out-strength, closeness, and harmonic centrality were strongly and significantly correlated with performance, matching pre-pandemic patterns

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The fall 2020 Mines math methods course showed strong, statistically significant positive correlations between performance and in-strength, out-strength, closeness centrality, and harmonic centrality, closely matching the pre-pandemic study's patterns. ## Evidence

### Nathan Crossette 2023

Nathan Crossette, Lincoln D. Carr, and Bethany R. Wilcox. (2023). Correlations between student connectivity and academic performance: a pandemic follow-up. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010106](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010106)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Observational analysis of a fully remote fall 2020 Mines math methods course network built from 23 consenting students' homework collaboration reports. The results section reports "strong correlations between performance" for these four centrality measures; the non-significant net-strength and betweenness correlations also matched the pre-pandemic findings.

> "The fall 2020 course at Mines had strong correlations between performance and the in-strength, out-strength, closeness centrality, and harmonic centrality."

### Nathan Crossette 2023 (2)

Null result from the same observational analysis of the pandemic-era Mines course network: the article reports that "the in-disparity and out-disparity were not correlated sta tisti- cally signiﬁcantly with performance". This differed from the prior study; no effect size is printed for the null.
