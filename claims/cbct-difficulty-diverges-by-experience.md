# Perceived decision-making difficulty rose from pre-test to post-test for dental students and fell for endodontic specialists in one uncontrolled pre-post study of CBCT use

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students' perceived difficulty for both diagnosis and treatment planning rose from pre-test to post-test in one uncontrolled pre-post study of CBCT use (p = 0.000 and p = 0.0011). [→ Fonseca 2025](#fonseca-2025)
`q2 i?` Endodontic specialists' perceived difficulty fell from pre-test to post-test in one uncontrolled pre-post study of CBCT use (p = 0.019 diagnosis, p = 0.0075 treatment plan). [→ Fonseca 2025 (2)](#fonseca-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Fonseca 2025

Fonseca, Braz, Vieira, de Souza Ferreira, Damian. (2025). Do CBCT and Clinical Experience Impact Decision‐Making in Endodontic Diagnosis and Treatment Planning? A Before‐After Study. Australian Endodontic Journal. https://doi.org/10.1111/aej.12966

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Wilcoxon signed-rank tests compared self-reported difficulty on a 5-point scale between PR and CBCT stages for five students. The article reports "61.43% of students reported high difficulty" with CBCT diagnosis (p = 0.000) and 55.00% for treatment planning (p = 0.0011); no effect size is printed.

> "Specifically, 61.43% of students reported high difficulty when establishing a diagnosis with CBCT (p = 0.000), and 55.00% did so when formulating a treatment plan (p = 0.0011)."

### Fonseca 2025 (2)

Fonseca, Braz, Vieira, de Souza Ferreira, Damian. (2025). Do CBCT and Clinical Experience Impact Decision‐Making in Endodontic Diagnosis and Treatment Planning? A Before‐After Study. Australian Endodontic Journal. https://doi.org/10.1111/aej.12966

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same Wilcoxon signed-rank analyses for the five professionals; Table 3 shows high-difficulty ratings falling from 35.77% (PR) to 20.59% (CBCT) for diagnosis (p = 0.019) and from 33.09% to 18.38% for treatment planning (p = 0.0075); no effect size is printed.

> "For both self‐reported difficulty in diagnostic decision‐making and treatment planning outcomes, there was a statistically significant increase in perceived difficulty among undergraduate students when using CBCT. In contrast, professionals reported a significant reduction in perceived difficulty with the use of CBCT"
