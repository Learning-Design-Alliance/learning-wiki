## Evidence

### Zeng Zhaolan 2026

Zeng Zhaolan, Shi Zeyao, Hou Shulin, Yan Jing, Yang Ru, Li Xiaowen. (2026). Comprehensive effects and dose–response relationship of screen exposure on university students' physical and mental health and academic performance in the digital age: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-026-27025-9

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r1`

Random-effects meta-analysis of four studies measuring academic performance via grade point average, comparing high versus low categorical screen exposure; heterogeneity was low (I2 = 31.4%).

> "Four studies (involving 886 university students) explored the association between categorical exposure to screen time (high vs. low) and academic performance. Meta-analysis showed that university students with longer daily screen time had a higher risk of academic performance decline (OR = 1.79; 95%CI: 1.29–2.48; I2 = 31.4%)"
