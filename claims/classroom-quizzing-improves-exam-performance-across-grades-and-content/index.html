Related Claims¶

  • Classroom testing (quizzing) significantly boosts student academic achievement to a medium extent (g = 0.499). — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it

  • Retrieval practice improves long-term retention — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it

  • Most middle and high school students surveyed after classroom retrieval practice programs viewed them positively and said frequent retrieval practice helped them feel less nervous about exams — related
  • Peer Discussion Improves Conceptual Understanding — related
  • Active learning conditions more often include quizzes, homework, and learning/exam preparation than lecture conditions — related
  • Retrieval practice benefits have been observed in children, healthy older adults, and memory-impaired patient groups, not only college students — related
  • Benefits of retrieval practice have persisted for 6 months in medical students and 9 months in middle-school students — related
  • Students rate clicker questions and lecture as more helpful than the textbook — related
  • HOCS clicker questions during learning improve performance on LOCS exam questions more than LOCS clicker questions do — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • After the TBL module, students were better prepared to design and implement independent final research projects — related
  • Quiz-level first-attempt performance analysis across cohorts identifies persistently challenging topics in online biomedical science courses — related
  • Classroom pretesting improves high-stakes exam performance on pretested content by 8–9% over a multi-week course — related
  • Test-enhanced learning generalizes across education levels, from elementary school (g = 0.328) through university/college (g = 0.486). — related
  • A significant but negligible benefit of peer assessment (d = .122) persisted to the final exam in one college statistics course — related
  • Participating in peer assessment causes a small but significant improvement in short-term achievement on unit quizzes in a college statistics course — related
  • More frequent exams are associated with better learning outcomes, including better grades and lower dropout, in classroom comparisons — related
  • Clicker participation and homework completion each significantly predict performance on isomorphic exam questions, with no synergistic interaction — related
  • Any clicker participation outperforms absence on exam questions, but the lowest quartile sees no significant benefit — related
  • Weekly spaced recall quizzes on fundamental concepts significantly improve 6-month retention of those concepts in a precalculus course — related
  • Completing the follow-up homework assignment modestly boosted exam question performance — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Students created and took peer assessments in Forms, learning through writing quiz questions and answer options — related
  • The testing effect from frequent retrieval is revealed only on later comprehensive tests, not in performance in other courses — related
  • In the author's cited longitudinal classroom experiments, lessons with retrieval practice raised course grades from a C to an A level, and three spaced retrieval opportunities were optimal even nine months later — related
