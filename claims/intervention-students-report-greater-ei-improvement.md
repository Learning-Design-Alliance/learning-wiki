## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students who received either type of EI intervention were more likely to report improvements in monitoring their own emotions, monitoring others' emotions, empathizing, and working collaboratively than control students. [→ Cotler 2017](#cotler-2017)

## Evidence

### Cotler 2017

Cotler, J. L., DiTursi, D., Goldstein, I., Yates, J., & DelBelso, D. (2017). A Mindful Approach to Teaching Emotional Intelligence to Undergraduate Students Online and in Person. Information Systems Education Journal, 15(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1135358

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Interviews with 21 students across the six experimental groups at semester end. Reported improvement in empathizing ranged from 25% (control) to 100% (online); reported improvement in collaborative teamwork ranged from 63% (control) to 100% (face-to-face). Self-reported percentages only; no effect size printed.

> "students who received either type of intervention were more likely to report changes in specific EI skills than those who did not recei ve any intervention. Notably, we saw little differentiation between the face -to-face and online groups"
