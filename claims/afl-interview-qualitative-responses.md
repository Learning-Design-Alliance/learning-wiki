## Evidence

### Al-Tayib Umar 2018

Abdul Majeed Al-Tayib Umar. (2018). The Impact of Assessment for Learning on Students' Achievement in English for Specific Purposes: A Case Study of Pre-Medical Students at Khartoum University: Sudan. English Language Teaching, 11(2). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v11n2p15

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Qualitative analysis of interviews with experimental-group students; one student said the approach allows more freedom and self-paced progress, and others reported it helped them see how far they had progressed and correct errors.

> "This type of assessment allows us more freedom. We are left to progress at our own pace"

### Al-Tayib Umar 2018 (2)

Abdul Majeed Al-Tayib Umar. (2018). The Impact of Assessment for Learning on Students' Achievement in English for Specific Purposes: A Case Study of Pre-Medical Students at Khartoum University: Sudan. English Language Teaching, 11(2). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v11n2p15

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The interview analysis also recorded negative views from a minority: grade-free marking felt vague to one student, and others were frustrated by the many required assessment procedures.

> "One student complains that without grades, the whole process seems vague and it doesn't tell exactly where one stands. Some other students have expressed their frustration with the too many requirements of assessment for learning procedures."
