# Peer support for academic work declines as Chicago students move from sixth through tenth grade

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The share of students saying all or most of their friends try hard to get good grades falls from 61 percent in sixth grade to 53 percent in eighth grade to 45 percent in tenth grade. [→ Sebring 1996](#sebring-1996)

## Evidence

### Sebring 1996

Sebring, P. B., Bryk, A. S., Roderick, M., Camburn, E., Luppescu, S., Thum, Y. M., Smith, B., & Kahne, J. (1996). Charting Reform in Chicago: The Students Speak. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/charting-reform-chicago-students-speak

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-grade comparison from the spring 1994 student survey on five peer-effort questions. The printed percentages show a "declining level of commitment across grades," which the authors link to the documented "cooling out" phenomenon as students move through high school.

> "among sixth graders, 61 percent answered that all or most of their friends try hard to get good grades; 53 percent of the eighth graders say this is true of their friends, but only 45 percent of the tenth graders claim this is so."
