# Published knowledge test Alphas show distributional bias with underrepresentation of low Alphas

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · quant-synthesis `r2`–`r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The distribution of transformed Alphas is right-skewed (skewness = 0.97, p < .001), indicating underrepresentation of low Alphas in published studies. [→ Peter A. Edelsbrunner 2025](#peter-a-edelsbrunner-2025)
`q3 i?` There are fewer short tests with low Alphas than the Spearman–Brown formula predicts. [→ Peter A. Edelsbrunner 2025 (2)](#peter-a-edelsbrunner-2025-2)
`q3 i?` There are increased frequencies of Alphas just above the cut-offs of 0.70, 0.80, and 0.90. [→ Peter A. Edelsbrunner 2025 (3)](#peter-a-edelsbrunner-2025-3)

## Evidence

### Peter A. Edelsbrunner 2025

Peter A. Edelsbrunner, Bianca A. Simonsmeier, Michael Schneider. (2025). The Cronbach’s Alpha of Domain‑Specific Knowledge Tests Before and After Learning: A Meta‑Analysis of Published Studies. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09982-y

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

After Bonett transformation, the distribution of Alphas showed "moderate right-skewness" with skewness index 0.97 (p < .001). After removing nine exceptionally high Alphas from two studies, skewness remained moderate at 0.44 (p = .003).

> "the distribution indicates moderate right-skewness, as indicated by a skewness index of 0.97 (SE = 0.14; bootstrapped p < 0.001)"

### Peter A. Edelsbrunner 2025 (2)

Peter A. Edelsbrunner, Bianca A. Simonsmeier, Michael Schneider. (2025). The Cronbach’s Alpha of Domain‑Specific Knowledge Tests Before and After Learning: A Meta‑Analysis of Published Studies. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09982-y

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Comparison of the empirical Alpha-by-test-length curve with the Spearman–Brown predicted curve showed the empirical curve deviates for tests below 20 items, indicating "an underrepresentation of short tests with low Alphas."

> "this finding indicates an underrepresentation of short tests with low Alphas compared to what can be expected for an unbiased distribution"

### Peter A. Edelsbrunner 2025 (3)

Peter A. Edelsbrunner, Bianca A. Simonsmeier, Michael Schneider. (2025). The Cronbach’s Alpha of Domain‑Specific Knowledge Tests Before and After Learning: A Meta‑Analysis of Published Studies. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09982-y

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Frequency distributions showed "moderate yet salient peaks in the numbers of reported Alphas just above" the cut-offs of 0.70, 0.80, and 0.90, consistent with researchers modifying tests to exceed cut-offs.

> "Figs. 2 and 4 show moderate yet salient peaks in the numbers of reported Alphas just above these three cut-offs"
