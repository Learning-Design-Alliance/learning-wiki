Children's cognitive biases come to parallel media biases: viewers of a televised story place it in a circumscribed spatial-temporal framework and draw inferences from provided information, while story-book readers attribute a more expansive framework and draw on their own experience. → Salomon 1983

Salomon 1983¶

Salomon, Gavriel, Gardner, Howard. (1983). The Computer as Educator: Lessons from Television Research. Harvard Project Zero. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED253201

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review attributes this information-pickup-bias finding to Meringoff (1980): with 'the same story' presented in different media, each medium encourages a characteristic pattern of pick-up by children. The review narrates the finding; its underlying design is not described here.

"Thus it has been observed (e.g. Meringoff, 1980) that, even in the case of 'the same story,' television tends to highlight the action properties of a narrative, picture books call attention to the figurative language, and radio foregrounds the sound effects."
