## Evidence

### Dankbaar Mary E. W. 2017

Dankbaar Mary E. W., Richters Olivier, Kalkman Cor J., Prins Gerrie, ten Cate Olle T. J., van Merrienboer Jeroen J. G., Schuit Stephanie C. E. (2017). Comparative effectiveness of a serious game and an e-module to support patient safety knowledge and awareness. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0836-5

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis of log-data from 18 game-group students (log-data available only for 18 because the game lacked auto-save). Students who successfully treated virtual patients and played longer reported higher self-efficacy and less stress during clinical practice.

> "Game-time and patient-care points correlated with self-efficacy (r = 0.51/ 0.50, p = 0.04) and negatively with reported stress during rotation (game-time – reported stress: r = − 0.65, p = 0.02; patient care points – reported stress: r = − 0.78, p = 0.002)."
