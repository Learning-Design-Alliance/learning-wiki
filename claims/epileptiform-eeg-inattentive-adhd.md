# Epileptiform EEG activity is more prevalent in the inattentive ADHD subtype

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among 40 ADHD children, 15 (37.5%) had abnormal non-epileptiform EEG activity and 5 (15%) had epileptiform activity, with the inattentive subtype showing the highest proportion of epileptiform activity (4 of 9, 44.4%). [→ Mohamed A. Ahmed 2014](#mohamed-a-ahmed-2014)

## Evidence

### Mohamed A. Ahmed 2014

Mohamed A. Ahmed, Khaled A. El-Beh, Talal A. Mohammed, Dina F. Mansour, Mohammed Y. Ezz-Eldine. (2014). Learning Disabilities in Different Types of Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorders and its Relation to Cortical and Brainstem Function. J Neurol Res. 2014;4(1):22-30. https://doi.org/10.14740/jnr268w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive EEG classification (normal, abnormal non-epileptiform, epileptiform) by a professor of neurology in two sessions. By subtype, epileptiform activity occurred in 44.4% of inattentive, 3.6% of combined and 33.3% of hyperactive-impulsive children (Fig. 2).

> "Nineteen (47.5%) of the studied children had normal EEG, 15 (37.5%) children with abnormal non-epileptiform activity and five (15%) children with epileptiform activity."
