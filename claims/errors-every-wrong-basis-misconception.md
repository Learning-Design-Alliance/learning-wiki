## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Explanations of how errors come about were correct for only 41.1% of introductory and 71.4% of advanced students; the most common incorrect idea was that errors occur every time Eve chooses the wrong basis, when only 50% of those cases lead to errors. `q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Analysis of coded activity responses from the 2014 trials (N=73 introductory, N=14 advanced); the article prints "41.1% correct at the introductory level and 71.4% at the advanced level" and identifies the wrong-basis-every-time idea as the most common error.

> "The explanations for how errors come about were not always correct, with 41.1% correct at the introductory level and 71.4% at the advanced level. The most common incorrect idea seen at both levels was that errors occur every time Eve chooses the wrong basis."
