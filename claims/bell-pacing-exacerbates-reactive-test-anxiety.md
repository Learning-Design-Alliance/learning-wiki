## Evidence

### obj2-posthoc-reactive

Bentley DC, Sotto A, Lombardi P. Comparison of examination performance and test anxiety during bell‐paced and self‐paced gross anatomy laboratory examinations. Anat Sci Educ. 2026;19:732–742. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.70215

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc comparisons using estimated marginal means from the randomized crossover study's second mixed-effects model. Findings showed a statistically significant increase in anxiety pre- to post-exam for BP writers, with one verbatim fragment of source_quote: M = 5.67 ± 1.73, p < 0.01. These are within-condition changes: the model's test of whether the pre-post change differed between conditions (the timepoint × pacing interaction) was marginally non-significant (β = 4.59, p = 0.064), and the study reports that "the overall pacing effect on test anxiety was not significant", so a significant between-condition difference in reactive anxiety is not established.

> "Post-hoc comparisons supported this finding; while completing the BP condition, students experienced a statistically significant increase in anxiety pre- to post-exam (M = 5.67 ± 1.73, p < 0.01), whereas while completing the SP condition, students experienced no significant change (M = 1.08 ± 1.73, p = 0.54), suggesting that the bell-paced condition exacerbated reactive test anxiety (Figure 2)."

### obj2-posthoc-consequent

Bentley DC, Sotto A, Lombardi P. Comparison of examination performance and test anxiety during bell‐paced and self‐paced gross anatomy laboratory examinations. Anat Sci Educ. 2026;19:732–742. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.70215

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same pairwise comparisons from the randomized crossover study's second mixed-effects model. Findings showed higher consequent test anxiety for BP writers, with one verbatim fragment of source_quote: 45.7 ± 1.6 compared to the SP condition (40.7 ± 1.6). This post-exam (consequent) difference is significant; the difference in pre-to-post change (reactive anxiety) between conditions, tested by the interaction, was marginally non-significant (p = 0.064).

> "The same pairwise comparisons revealed that higher consequent test anxiety was experienced while completing the BP condition (45.7 ± 1.6) compared to the SP condition (40.7 ± 1.6; M = 5.01 ± 1.73, p < 0.01) (Figure 2)."

### obj2-interaction

Bentley DC, Sotto A, Lombardi P. Comparison of examination performance and test anxiety during bell‐paced and self‐paced gross anatomy laboratory examinations. Anat Sci Educ. 2026;19:732–742. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.70215

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The second mixed-effects model from the randomized crossover study examined the effects of assigned pacing condition and timepoint on STAI score. Findings showed a marginally non-significant interaction effect, with one verbatim fragment of source_quote: β = 4.59, 95% CI [−0.19, 9.37], p = 0.064.

> "However, our model revealed a marginally non-significant interaction effect between timepoint and pacing condition (β = 4.59, 95% CI [−0.19, 9.37], p = 0.064), suggesting a trend towards greater increases in test anxiety while completing the BP condition."
