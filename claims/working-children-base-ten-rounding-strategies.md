## Evidence

### Banerjee 2025

Banerjee, A. V., Bhattacharjee, S., Chattopadhyay, R., Duflo, E., Ganimian, A. J., Rajah, K. & Spelke, E. S. (2025). Children's arithmetic skills do not transfer between applied and academic mathematics. Nature. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08502-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Study 3 process analysis of how working children solved hypothetical market problems involving goods from their store. The article reports children simplified calculations "using rounding and decomposition techniques that leverage the base-ten structure of the number system" (44% at market prices, 36% at new prices; 26% and 16% used approximations).

> "using rounding and decomposition techniques that leverage the base-ten structure of the number system (44% when problems involved standard market prices and 36% when they involved new prices)"

### Banerjee 2025 (2)

Banerjee, A. V., Bhattacharjee, S., Chattopadhyay, R., Duflo, E., Ganimian, A. J., Rajah, K. & Spelke, E. S. (2025). Children's arithmetic skills do not transfer between applied and academic mathematics. Nature. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08502-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Study 3 comparison of performance on problems with operands ending in 1 or 9 versus similar-sized non-roundable numbers. The article reports "Working children performed slightly better on roundable problems (67% correct)" than non-roundable (64%, P = 0.083); non-working children showed no difference (75% vs 74%, P = 0.868).

> "Working children performed slightly better on roundable problems (67% correct) than on non-roundable problems, although the difference was not significant at the 5% level (64% correct, β = 0.03, s.e.m. = 0.02, 95% CI = 0.00–0.07, P = 0.083"
