# Paying students for performance on standardized tests or for course grades does not increase achievement test scores in urban public schools

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Incentives for test scores in NYC (grades 4 and 7) and for course grades in Chicago (grade 9) yielded statistically zero and substantively small effects on math and reading achievement. [→ Roland G. Fryer 2010](#roland-g-fryer-2010)

## Evidence

### Roland G. Fryer 2010

Roland G. Fryer, Jr. (2010). Financial Incentives and Student Achievement: Evidence from Randomized Trials. NBER Working Paper No. 15898. http://www.nber.org/papers/w15898

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

School-level randomized trials in NYC (fourth and seventh graders paid for interim assessment performance) and Chicago (ninth graders paid for grades in five core courses). The article reports the impact is "statistically zero and substantively small in all specifications."

> "The impact of oﬀering incentives to students for test scores or grades is statistically zero and substantively small in all speciﬁcations."
