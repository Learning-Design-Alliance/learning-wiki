## Evidence

### Connell 2016

Connell, G. L., Donovan, D. A., & Chambers, T. G. (2016). Increasing the Use of Student-Centered Pedagogies from Moderate to High Improves Student Learning and Attitudes about Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-03-0062

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre/post administration of the VASS—Biology (n = 128 Extensive, 115 Moderate completing both surveys); initial profiles were statistically similar (t = 1.89, p = 0.06). Expert shifts were scored as movement across profile groups toward the expert view and compared with a Student's t test.

> "Students in the Extensive section had a significantly greater mean expert shift (0.40 ± 1.10) compared with students in the Moderate section (−0.02 ± 0.88; t = 3.23, p = 0.001)."

### Connell 2016 (2)

Connell, G. L., Donovan, D. A., & Chambers, T. G. (2016). Increasing the Use of Student-Centered Pedagogies from Moderate to High Improves Student Learning and Attitudes about Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-03-0062

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive profile distributions from the same pre/post VASS administration: at posttest "20% of the students in the Extensive section were classified as experts" while low transitional membership fell from 43 to 28%. The Moderate section's profile showed very little change over the quarter.

> "By the end of the class, however, fully 20% of the students in the Extensive section were classified as experts, and the number of students classified as low transitional dropped from 43 to 28%."
