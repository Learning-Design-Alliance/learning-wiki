## Evidence

### Sala 2016

Giovanni Sala, Fernand Gobet. (2016). Do the benefits of chess instruction transfer to academic and cognitive skills? A meta-analysis. Educational Research Review. http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2016.02.002

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analytic model comparison across outcome categories in the discussion of Hypothesis 2 (Thorndike and Woodworth's common-elements prediction): the pooled effect was "larger with mathematics than with reading (g ¼ 0.382 vs. g ¼ 0.248)"; the moderator test was not significant. Separate models gave g = 0.382 (maths, K=17), g = 0.330 (cognitive, K=16), g = 0.248 (reading, K=7).

> "the overall effect size was larger with mathematics than with reading (*g* ¼ 0.382 vs.
*g* ¼ 0.248). Although outcome was not a significant moderator, reading seemed to benefit less from chess instruction than mathematics"
