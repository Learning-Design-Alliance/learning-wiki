# In young patients, the presence of symptoms significantly shifts dental students' caries-removal planning toward non-selective (complete) removal (P < .001)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In case scenarios 1 and 2 (young patients), the presence or absence of symptoms significantly altered treatment planning decisions for both undergraduates and postgraduates, with more preferring non-selective removal when symptoms were present. ## Evidence

### Nagendrababu Venkateshbabu et al 2025

Nagendrababu Venkateshbabu et al. (2025). A Multi-National Questionnaire-Based Analysis of Dental Students’ Knowledge of the Management of Deep Caries and the Exposed Pulp. International Dental Journal. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.identj.2025.100844

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey of ten dental schools using McNemar tests on paired scenario responses from 401 undergraduates and 127 postgraduates. The article reports the scenario contrast was "significantly altered" with "P < .001"; no effect size is printed.

> "The presence or absence of symptoms significantly altered treatment planning decisions in young patients (case scenarios 1 and 2; P < .001) for both undergraduate and postgraduate students."
