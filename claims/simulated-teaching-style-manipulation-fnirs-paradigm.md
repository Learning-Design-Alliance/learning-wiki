# Teacher autonomy support and control styles can be experimentally manipulated in simulated one-to-one teaching with strong perceived-style checks, but style differences in lPFC INS do not extend to the rTPJ

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students perceived the assigned styles as intended: the support group reported higher perceived autonomy support and the control group higher perceived control, confirming the manipulation. [→ Wang Guoxia 2025](#wang-guoxia-2025)
`q3 i?` No teacher–student INS differences were found in the rTPJ, despite it being a pre-registered region of interest. [→ Wang Guoxia 2025 (2)](#wang-guoxia-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Wang Guoxia 2025

Wang Guoxia, Zhang Qin, Wang Lumeng, Liu Yi. (2025). The impact of teachers’ autonomy support on students’ academic performance from the perspective of hyperscanning: the mediating role of autonomy need satisfaction. Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsaf115

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Manipulation check on the 42 valid student datasets using the Learning Climate Questionnaire and Controlling Teacher Scale after instruction priming training of four teachers. The article reports significant perceived-style differences "t(19) = 16.902, P < .001" and "t(21) = 9.673, P < .001", confirming the manipulation; no effect sizes are printed.

> "Students in the teacher autonomy support group perceived significantly higher teacher autonomy support, t(19) = 16.902, P < .001, while the teacher control group perceived significantly higher teacher control, t(21) = 9.673, P < .001. These findings confirm the manipulation."

### Wang Guoxia 2025 (2)

Wang Guoxia, Zhang Qin, Wang Lumeng, Liu Yi. (2025). The impact of teachers’ autonomy support on students’ academic performance from the perspective of hyperscanning: the mediating role of autonomy need satisfaction. Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsaf115

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Wavelet-coherence INS analysis in the rTPJ ROI (six channels) across the 42 dyads found no group differences or task-related INS, despite the mentalizing rationale for including the rTPJ. The article states "teacher–student INS was absent in the rTPJ" and treats this as consistent with prior teaching hyperscanning findings.

> "However, teacher–student INS was absent in the rTPJ, consistent with previous teaching hyperscanning studies (Jiang et al. 2015, Pan et al. 2018, Liu et al. 2019), which found INS in frontal regions like the lPFC and subfrontal cortex."
