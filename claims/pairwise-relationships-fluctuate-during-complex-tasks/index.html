Evidence¶

Katrin Schuessler 2026¶

Katrin Schuessler, Jenna Koenen, Elke Sumfleth, Marvin Rost. (2026). Variations in Repeated Measures of Cognitive Load and Interest During Complex Learning Tasks. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10105-4

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Pairwise correlations of raw data at each of the 21 measurement points (Fig. 10) in the N = 360 sample. The article adds that "many correlations are stronger in the sections where new content knowledge is introduced, compared to introductory sections"; no standardized effect size summarizes the fluctuation.

"This approach shows that some pairs of variables exhibit predominantly stable relationships (e.g. mental effort invested and perceived task difficulty, or self-assessed understanding and perceived task difficulty), while others appear to have no (stable) correlation (e.g. mental effort invested and self-assessed understanding)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Perceived task difficulty correlates negatively with self-assessed understanding and with interest at essentially all measurement points, while interest and self-assessed understanding correlate positively throughout — related
  • Invested mental effort and perceived task difficulty are significantly positively correlated at all measurement points except MP2.5, with correlations increasing as material complexity rises — related
  • Invested mental effort and interest are only weakly and inconsistently positively correlated across measurement points, with some correlations non-significant — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • The relationship between invested mental effort and self-assessed understanding is weak and inconsistent, with most correlations non-significant and mixed directions — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Cognitive load ratings rise and interest and self-assessed understanding fall in sections introducing new content knowledge compared with introductory sections of worked examples — related
  • Repeated measures of invested mental effort, perceived task difficulty, interest, and self-assessed understanding differ significantly across measurement points within complex learning tasks and are not adequately represented by their mean — related
  • Model-based posterior-prediction correlations between the four variables show distinct dynamic patterns across the 21 time points and four intervention groups — related
  • Greater time and effort invested in the task correlate with better integration and smaller calibration error — related
  • Invested mental effort and perceived task difficulty are strongly correlated yet relate differently to relative task difficulty, indicating the two ratings measure different aspects of overall cognitive load — related
  • Mental effort is more suitably modeled as a non-linear than linear predictor of learning outcomes in most re-analyzed studies — related
  • The model's assumed negative correlation between cognitive load and learner resources was not supported; the overall correlation was positive — related
