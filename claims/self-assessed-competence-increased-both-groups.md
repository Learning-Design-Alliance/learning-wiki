## Evidence

### Jurkovič 2010

Jurkovič, V. (2010). Effect of Explicit Language Learning Strategy Instruction on Language-test and Self-assessment Scores. English Language Teaching, 3(1). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v3n1p16

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive comparison of CEFR global self-assessment rating scale scores at the beginning and end of the course for both groups; dispersion was almost equal between groups.

> "Data presented in Table 3 show that the average values indicating self-assessed language competence increased in both groups (by 0.30 in the treatment group and 0.20 in the contrast group)."

### Jurkovič 2010 (2)

Jurkovič, V. (2010). Effect of Explicit Language Learning Strategy Instruction on Language-test and Self-assessment Scores. English Language Teaching, 3(1). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v3n1p16

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Distribution analysis of self-assessed CEFR levels (Figure 5): the share of students at level A1 increased by nine percent while the share at A2 decreased by the same value; the author offers this as an interpretive pattern, not a tested result.

> "It seems that in the contrast group the perceived language competence among low-level students decreased while increasing among high-level students."
