## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In multivariable mixed-effects analysis, males scored significantly lower than females on one-year skills scores (p = 0.001); only sex was independently associated with scores. [→ Kalabamu FS 2026](#kalabamu-fs-2026)

## Evidence

### Kalabamu FS 2026

Kalabamu FS, Daudi V, Moshiro R, Kamala B, Mdoe P, Bishanga D, Kvaløy JT, Ersdal H, Mpembeni R. (2026). Neonatal resuscitation skills retention among healthcare providers one year after implementation of in situ low-dose high-frequency simulations using innovative tools across two regions in Tanzania. Global Health Action. https://doi.org/10.1080/16549716.2026.2639774

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Random intercept linear mixed effects model (adjusting for within-facility dependency) of one-year skills scores among 226 providers; the article reports males scored significantly lower than females (p = 0.001) with no effect size printed.

> "However, on multivariable analysis with all explanatory variables included in the model, only sex was independently associated with skill scores one year after implementation of in situ simulations. Males scored significantly lower than females (p = 0.001), as shown in Supplemental Table S3."
