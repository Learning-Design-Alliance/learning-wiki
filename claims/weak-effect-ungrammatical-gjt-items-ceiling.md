# Feedback effects on ungrammatical GJT items were weak because learners already possessed the relevant explicit knowledge

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There were no group differences on ungrammatical grammaticality judgment items at either posttest, which the authors attribute to learners' high pretest explicit knowledge of the structure. [→ Rod Ellis 2006](#rod-ellis-2006)

## Evidence

### Rod Ellis 2006

Rod Ellis, Shawn Loewen and Rosemary Erlam. (2006). Implicit and Explicit Corrective Feedback and the Acquisition of L2 Grammar. Studies in Second Language Acquisition, Volume 28, Issue 2. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263106060141

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

ANCOVA on ungrammatical GJT items at delayed posttest, n = 34; "no differences on the delayed posttest for the ungrammatical items, F (2, 34) = 0.900, p = .417". The authors attribute this to near-ceiling pretest explicit knowledge.
