# Technology access problems during remote learning fell more heavily on minoritized and lower-income students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students from minoritized race/ethnicity groups were more likely than non-minoritized students to experience internet connectivity problems often or very often (22% vs. 12%), and lower-income students (under $50,000) were almost twice as likely as households earning $100,000 or more (22% vs. 12%). [→ Means 2021](#means-2021)

## Evidence

### Means 2021

Means, B., Peters, V., Neisler, J., Wiley, K., & Grifths, R. (2021). Lessons from remote learning during COVID-19. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/116

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same national student survey (n = 1,008, weighted sample 23% Hispanic, 15% Black, 37% under $50,000 income). The survey found minoritized students more likely to experience connectivity problems "often or very often (22%)" versus 12% of non-minoritized students; hardware/software problems showed the same pattern (12% vs. 5%).
