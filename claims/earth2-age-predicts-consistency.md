## Evidence

### Straatemeier 2008

Straatemeier, M., van der Maas, H. L. J., & Jansen, B. R. J. (2008). Children's knowledge of the earth: A new methodological and statistical approach. Journal of Experimental Child Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2008.03.004

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

ANCOVA on consistency scores in the main experiment, again verified with nonparametric tests. The article reports F(1, 369) = 138.12, p < .001, g2 = .27, and no significant differences between schools in consistency.

> "The effect of age on the consistency of children's answers was signiﬁcant, F(1, 369) = 138.12, p < .001, g2 = .27. Fig. 2D shows that the consistency was higher for older children."

### Straatemeier 2008 (2)

Straatemeier, M., van der Maas, H. L. J., & Jansen, B. R. J. (2008). Children's knowledge of the earth: A new methodological and statistical approach. Journal of Experimental Child Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2008.03.004

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis within the main experiment reporting rs = .67, p < .001; the article also reports a higher pilot-experiment correlation, "rs = .86, p < .001", between scientifically correct knowledge and response consistency.

> "As in the pilot experiment, there was a positive association between the mean score on the EARTH-2 and the consistency score, rs = .67, p < .001. The more knowledge children had, the more consistent were their answers."
