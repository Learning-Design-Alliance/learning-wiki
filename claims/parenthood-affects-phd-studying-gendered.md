## Evidence

### Meytal Eran-Jona and Yosef Nir 2020

Meytal Eran-Jona and Yosef Nir. (2020). Ph.D. in physics as a hurdle race, and the “glass hurdles” for women. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2007.02251](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2007.02251)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey of Israeli physics Ph.D. students, of whom 40% were already parents. The printed percentages are descriptive; no significance test is reported for these contrasts.

> "95% of mothers and 86% of fathers stated that becoming parents aﬀected their way of studying. A large majority of them (73%) reduced the time spent on studies and research, and for a large fraction (34%) this led to a reduced rate of progress in their research."

### Meytal Eran-Jona and Yosef Nir 2020 (2)

Meytal Eran-Jona and Yosef Nir. (2020). Ph.D. in physics as a hurdle race, and the “glass hurdles” for women. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2007.02251](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2007.02251)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same survey's gender comparison of adaptation strategies after parenthood; the article reports the percentages without a statistical test.

> "Women mentioned more frequently than men that they learned to make their schedule more ﬂexible (60% vs. 48%) and to be more eﬃcient and productive (40% vs. 27%)."
