# Groups differing in frequency of map visits differed on orientation and access ratios but not significantly on task-test scores

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 1997 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Kruskal–Wallis tests showed significant differences among three map-visit-frequency groups on orientation ratio (H(2)= 10.0, P<0.05) and access ratio (H(2)= 11.8, P<0.05). [→ Stanton 1997](#stanton-1997)
`q2 i?` Differences among the frequency groups on task-test scores were not statistically significant (H(2)= 5.8, P=0.06); students visiting the map 6–10 times scored highest, and heavy visitors (more than 11 times) were attributed high cognitive overhead. [→ Stanton 1997 (2)](#stanton-1997-2)

## Evidence

### Stanton 1997

Stanton, N., Correia, A. P. and Dias, P. (1997). Efficacy of a map on search, orientation and access behaviour in a hypermedia system. https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Efficacy+of+a+map+on+search+orientation+and+access+behaviour+in+a+hypermedia+system

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Causal-comparative Kruskal–Wallis analysis by ranks at the 0.05 level compared the three groups defined by map-visit frequency; only orientation and access ratios reached significance, with "H(2)= 11.8, P<0.05" for access. No effect size is printed.

> "The results showed significant differences between the frequency of visit to the map relatively to the orientation ratio (H(2)= 10.0, P<0.05) and to the access ratio (H(2)= 11.8, P<0.05)."

### Stanton 1997 (2)

Stanton, N., Correia, A. P. and Dias, P. (1997). Efficacy of a map on search, orientation and access behaviour in a hypermedia system. https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Efficacy+of+a+map+on+search+orientation+and+access+behaviour+in+a+hypermedia+system

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The same Kruskal–Wallis comparison found a marginal, non-significant difference on task-test scores (H(2)= 5.8, P=0.06); the 6–10-visit group "had the highest mean rank score".

> "We also found a value of H(2)= 5.8 (P=0.06) for the variable task-test. Although not significant, the present value showed some differences among the three groups of frequency of visits to the map relatively to the score obtained in the task-test."
