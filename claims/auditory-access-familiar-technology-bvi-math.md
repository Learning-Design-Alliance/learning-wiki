# Auditory math access works best when the technology is familiar: Math Speak aided expression identification, and BVI students performed similarly to or slightly better than traditional methods with familiar apps

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Listeners hearing math expressions voiced with Math Speak guidelines performed significantly better than those hearing other auditory forms of the same expressions. [→ Steinbach 2022](#steinbach-2022)
`q2 i?` BVI students felt motivated using an iPad pre-algebra app and performed slightly better than with traditional methods, while a MathPlayer-voiced condition performed similarly to traditional methods. [→ Steinbach 2022 (2)](#steinbach-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Steinbach 2022

Steinbach, S. (2022). Looking beyond vision: Supports for students who are blind or visually impaired in mathematics. NWEA. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/looking-beyond-vision-supports-for-students-who-are-blind-or-visually-impaired-in-mathematics/

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Isaacson et al. (2010), that participants hearing Math Speak-voiced expressions "performed significantly better than those who heard other auditory forms of the same expression." No effect size printed.

> "The authors found that those who heard expressions using Math Speak performed significantly better than those who heard other auditory forms of the same expression."

### Steinbach 2022 (2)

Steinbach, S. (2022). Looking beyond vision: Supports for students who are blind or visually impaired in mathematics. NWEA. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/looking-beyond-vision-supports-for-students-who-are-blind-or-visually-impaired-in-mathematics/

`q2 · i?` · `review · r?`

The review reports, citing Beal & Rosenblum (2018), that BVI students felt motivated using an iPad app for pre-algebra word problems and "performed slightly better with the app than with their traditional methods." No effect size printed.

> "In contrast, students felt motivated to use an iPad app to solve pre-algebra word problems (Beal & Rosenblum, 2018), and even performed slightly better with the app than with their traditional methods."
