# Perceived mental effort is negatively associated with monitoring judgments (β = -0.19) in a meta-analytic structural equation model

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r?` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Across 83 independent samples, higher perceived mental effort is associated with lower monitoring judgments of learning. [→ Louise David 2024](#louise-david-2024)

## Evidence

### Louise David 2024

Louise David, Felicitas Biwer, Martine Baars, Lisette Wijnia, Fred Paas, Anique de Bruin. (2024). The Relation Between Perceived Mental Effort, Monitoring Judgments, and Learning Outcomes: A Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09903-z

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

MASEM synthesis of 35 papers, 83 independent samples, and 236 effect sizes (N = 3973) using a three-level WPL approach. The full mediation model showed "a negative parameter estimate between mental effort and monitoring judgments was found β = -0.19" with a 95% LBCI excluding zero.

> "In support of Hypothesis 1, a negative parameter estimate between mental effort and monitoring judgments was found β = -0.19, 95% LBCI [-0.27; -0.12], indicating that with each unit increase in perceived mental effort, monitoring judgments decrease by 0.19 units on average."
