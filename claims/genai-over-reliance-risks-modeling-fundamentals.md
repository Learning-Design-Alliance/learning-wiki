## Evidence

### Karl Henrik Fredly 2026

Karl Henrik Fredly, Tor Ole B. Odden and Benjamin M. Zwickl. (2026). How undergraduate physics students use generative AI for computational modeling. [doi:10.1103/vg1v-dkrh](https://doi.org/10.1103/vg1v-dkrh)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview thematic analysis of planning practices found "students who relied too heavily on genAI at this stage had worse results", including one student who reached a point where he could not explain the code he had created.

> "However, students who relied too heavily on genAI at this stage had worse results. GenAI often made incorrect assumptions, and without careful consideration along the way students risked wasting time or ending up with results they did not understand"

### Karl Henrik Fredly 2026 (2)

Karl Henrik Fredly, Tor Ole B. Odden and Benjamin M. Zwickl. (2026). How undergraduate physics students use generative AI for computational modeling. [doi:10.1103/vg1v-dkrh](https://doi.org/10.1103/vg1v-dkrh)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

The authors' analysis of implementation practices states that a student who does not understand genAI-generated code is "unlikely to find its mistakes" and may learn the wrong things, illustrated by a student who fought with an equation too late.

> "If the student doesn’t understand the code generated by the genAI, they are unlikely to find its mistakes, might learn the wrong things, and will be unable to produce a good assignment."
