## Evidence

### Tweed M.J. 2017

Tweed M.J., Stein S., Wilkinson T.J., Purdie G., Smith J. (2017). Certainty and safe consequence responses provide additional information from multiple choice question assessments. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0942-z

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis combining moderately and highly unsafe responses among incorrect responses found "no significant difference in the chance of a moderately or highly unsafe response between levels of certainty (F = 2.3 df = 2452 p = 0.098)".

> "When students gave an incorrect response, there was no significant difference in the chance of a moderately or highly unsafe response between levels of certainty (F = 2.3 df = 2452 p = 0.098)."
