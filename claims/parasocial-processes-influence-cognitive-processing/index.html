Evidence¶

Sascha Schneider 2021¶

Sascha Schneider, Maik Beege, Steve Nebel, Lenka Schnaubert, Günter Daniel Rey. (2021). The Cognitive-Affective-Social Theory of Learning in digital Environments (CASTLE). Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09626-5

q1 · i? · theoretical · r3

Theoretical argument derived from the two-level model of parasocial interaction (Hartmann et al. 2004), whose cognitive facet includes attention, evaluation, activation of prior knowledge, and understanding processes. The article derives the hypothesis; no empirical effect size is reported for it directly.

"According to this theory, elicited social and parasocial processes thus influence the cognitive processing of new information leading to the following hypothesis: Cognitive Influence Hypothesis: (Para-)Social processes influence how information is selected, organized, integrated, and retrieved."

Related Claims¶

  • CASTLE's interaction hypothesis states that social processes influence and are influenced by motivational, emotional, and metacognitive processes — related
  • CASTLE's activation hypothesis states that social cues activate social schemata and trigger social processes — related
  • Strength and speed of social schema activation depend on the number and strength of social cues, schema development, and material modality, dynamics, and interactivity — related
  • Items requiring integration have higher relative item difficulty than items requiring selecting or organizing, and delayed perceived task difficulty ratings appear closer to integrating-item difficulty, though that correlation comparison is not significant — related
