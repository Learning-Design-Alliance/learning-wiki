# Email reminder messages increase average homework performance by about 4 percentage points

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who received reminder messages consistently performed better on homework than control students, an aggregate increase of about 4 percentage points. [→ Angela Zavaleta-Bernuy 2022](#angela-zavaleta-bernuy-2022)

## Evidence

### Angela Zavaleta-Bernuy 2022

Angela Zavaleta-Bernuy, Ziwen Han, Hammad Shaikh, Qi Yin Zheng, Lisa-Angelique Lim, Anna Rafferty, Andrew Petersen, and Joseph Jay Williams. 2022. How can Email Interventions Increase Students’ Completion of Online Homework? A Case Study Using A/B Comparisons. In LAK22: 12th International Learning Analytics and Knowledge Conference (LAK22), March 21–25, 2022, Online, USA. ACM, New York, NY, USA. https://doi.org/10.1145/3506860.3506874

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Panel data regression aggregating weekly effects across weeks 5–8 of the randomized deployment. The article reports the reminder "increased the homework average by 4 percentage points" with coefficient = 0.0388; z = 2.80; p < .005. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "we observe that the reminder messages increased the homework average by 4 percentage points (coefficient = 0.0388; z = 2.80; p < .005; panel regression with time effects and participant effects)."
