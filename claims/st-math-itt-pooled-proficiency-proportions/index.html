Evidence¶

Staci Wendt 2014¶

Staci Wendt, John Rice, Jonathan Nakamoto. (2014). Evaluation of the MIND Research Institute's Spatial-Temporal Math (ST Math) Program in California. WestEd. https://nces.ed.gov/ccd/elsi/

q2 · i0 · causal · r2

Pooled HLM analyses (Exhibit 3) of adjusted proportions across 926 grades in 544 schools: 35.01% versus 32.54% advanced with "The effect size for this difference was 0.17" and 64.88% versus 62.58% proficient or advanced with effect size 0.16, both significant after BH correction; both printed effect sizes are below 0.2.

"grades that were provided with ST Math had a significantly larger proportion of students who scored at the advanced level in mathematics on the CST (35.01 percent) compared to grades that were not provided with the program (32.54 percent). The effect size for this difference was 0.17. Also, grades that were provided with ST Math had a significantly larger proportion of students that scored at either the proficient or advanced level of mathematics on the CST (64.88 percent) compared to grades that were not provided with the program (62.58 percent). The effect size for this difference was 0.16."
