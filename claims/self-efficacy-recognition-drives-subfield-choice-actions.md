## Evidence

### Dina Zohrabi Alaee and Benjamin M. Zwickl 2025

Dina Zohrabi Alaee and Benjamin M. Zwickl. (2025). Exploring Subfield Interest Development in Undergraduate Physics Students through Social Cognitive Career Theory. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1103/cgcb-4k6z](https://doi.org/10.1103/cgcb-4k6z)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis within the SCCT self-efficacy construct from the 27 interviews found two emergent sources of self-efficacy development: "self-recognition" and "other-recognition" from peers and mentors, which boosted motivation to learn and pursue the subfield.

> "This transformation was influenced by both self-recognition, where students saw their own growth and potential, and other-recognition, where they were acknowledged and praised by peers or mentors."

### Dina Zohrabi Alaee and Benjamin M. Zwickl 2025 (2)

Dina Zohrabi Alaee and Benjamin M. Zwickl. (2025). Exploring Subfield Interest Development in Undergraduate Physics Students through Social Cognitive Career Theory. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1103/cgcb-4k6z](https://doi.org/10.1103/cgcb-4k6z)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Within the benefits-of-high-self-efficacy theme, the article reports that "Having high self-efficacy often leads to seek- ing new opportunities and taking action"; students with high self-efficacy expressed willingness to explore research positions and positive feelings about future careers.

> "Having high self-efficacy often leads to seek- ing new opportunities and taking action [18]. Students who strongly believe in their capabilities are more moti- vated to actively seek opportunities to learn more about them."
