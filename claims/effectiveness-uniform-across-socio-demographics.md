# The session's effectiveness in knowledge and attitude does not vary by socio-demographic characteristics, though baseline knowledge differs by education, occupation, income and household-head status

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` No significant time-by-group interaction effects were found for any socio-demographic variable, indicating equal effectiveness across groups. [→ Kadomoto Noriko 2011](#kadomoto-noriko-2011)
`q2 i?` Significant main effects appeared for education, occupation, monthly income and household-head status on knowledge, and for education on attitude. [→ Kadomoto Noriko 2011 (2)](#kadomoto-noriko-2011-2)

## Evidence

### Kadomoto Noriko 2011

Kadomoto Noriko, Iwasa Hajime, Takahashi Miyako, Dulnuan Marcelyn M, Kai Ichiro. (2011). Ifugao males, learning and teaching for the improvement of maternal and child health status in the Philippines: an evaluation of a program. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2458-11-280

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Repeated measures ANOVA interaction tests in Group 1 across socio-demographic variables listed in Table 2 (age, education, occupation, income, household headship, etc.). The article reports no significant interactions; no effect sizes are printed.

> "There was no significant interaction effect in any of the variables, demonstrating that effectiveness did not vary regardless of these socio-demographic characteristics."

### Kadomoto Noriko 2011 (2)

Kadomoto Noriko, Iwasa Hajime, Takahashi Miyako, Dulnuan Marcelyn M, Kai Ichiro. (2011). Ifugao males, learning and teaching for the improvement of maternal and child health status in the Philippines: an evaluation of a program. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2458-11-280

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Main-effect tests from the same repeated measures ANOVA models in Group 1. Public workers scored higher than farmers, and higher education and income were associated with higher knowledge scores; no effect sizes are printed.

> "The variables that had significant main effects were education (F = 38.99, p < 0.001), occupation (F = 8.64, p < 0.001), monthly income (F = 9.84, p = 0.002), and household-head (F = 7.43, p = 0.007) for knowledge, and education (F = 13.763, p < 0.001) for attitude (Table 4)."
