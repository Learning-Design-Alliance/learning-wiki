# Knowledge test scores did not differ significantly overall between DMIS and control groups, but the DMIS group scored significantly higher on Lecture 2

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Overall knowledge test scores showed no statistically significant difference between groups (P=.075), despite a higher DMIS mean. [→ Xu J 2025](#xu-j-2025)
`q3 i?` The DMIS group scored significantly higher than controls on the Lecture 2 knowledge test, which covered microscopy-based enumeration of viable cells (P<.05). [→ Xu J 2025 (2)](#xu-j-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Xu J 2025

Xu J, Sha J, Jia S, Li J, Xu L, Shao Z. (2025). Effectiveness of a 5G Local Area Network–Based Digital Microscopy Interactive System: Quasi-Experimental Design. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/70256

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental knowledge test of 18 true-false questions across three laboratory lectures, completed anonymously by all 39 students via the Sojump platform; the overall difference was not statistically significant (P=.075).

> "In the knowledge test, the DMIS group had a higher mean score of 77.15 (SD 11.10) compared to 69.58 (SD 14.56) for the control group; however, this difference was not statistically significant (P=.075; see Figure 3A)."

### Xu J 2025 (2)

Xu J, Sha J, Jia S, Li J, Xu L, Shao Z. (2025). Effectiveness of a 5G Local Area Network–Based Digital Microscopy Interactive System: Quasi-Experimental Design. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/70256

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Per-lecture analysis of the same knowledge test found the DMIS group significantly higher in Lecture 2 (P<.05), the session on counting unstained viable cells, which the authors say demanded stronger identification and operational skill.

> "The DMIS group demonstrated significantly higher mean scores than controls in Lecture 2 (P<.05; see Figure 3B), which focused on microscopy-based enumeration of viable cells in prepared samples."
