## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When the test is invalid but used in top-10% compensatory selection, a strong negative correlation between test scores and degree completion emerges among accepted students. [→ Huber David E. 2022](#huber-david-e-2022)

## Evidence

### Huber David E. 2022

Huber David E., Cohen Andrew L., Staub Adrian. (2022). Compensatory selection with standardized tests. PLoS ONE 17(5): e0265459. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0265459

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Simulation ground truth #1 with Test scores drawn at random (non-diagnostic); under top-10% compensatory selection "this results in a strong negative correlation between Test scores and degree completion", unlike the accept-everyone case.

> "Paradoxically, this results in a strong negative correlation between Test scores and degree completion."

### Huber David E. 2022 (2)

Huber David E., Cohen Andrew L., Staub Adrian. (2022). Compensatory selection with standardized tests. PLoS ONE 17(5): e0265459. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0265459

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Sweep of acceptance percentage across levels of selectivity for each ground truth; for an invalid test used in admissions, "any level of selectivity results in a negative slope, which becomes progressively more negative with increasing selectivity".

> "if the standardized test is invalid, yet used in the admissions process, any level of selectivity results in a negative slope, which becomes progressively more negative with increasing selectivity"

### Huber David E. 2022 (3)

Huber David E., Cohen Andrew L., Staub Adrian. (2022). Compensatory selection with standardized tests. PLoS ONE 17(5): e0265459. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0265459

`q2 · i2` · `theoretical · r3`

In the same invalid-test simulation, compensatory selection "introduced a negative correlation of r = -.248 among the accepted applicants between Test scores and Grades, Letters, and the personal Statement", the mechanism producing the negative correlation with completion.

> "the selective admissions process introduced a negative correlation of r = -.248 among the accepted applicants between Test scores and Grades, Letters, and the personal Statement."
