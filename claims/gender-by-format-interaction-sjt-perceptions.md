# The gender advantage for rating over pick-one formats on applicant differentiation and study relatedness is stronger for men than for women

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Gender interacted with response format on applicant differentiation (d = 0.46 vs 0.15) and study relatedness (d = 0.61 vs 0.08), with the rating-format preference stronger for men. [→ De Leng Wendy E. 2018](#de-leng-wendy-e-2018)

## Evidence

### De Leng Wendy E. 2018

De Leng Wendy E., Stegers-Jager Karen M., Born Marise Ph., Themmen Axel P. N. (2018). Influence of response instructions and response format on applicant perceptions of a situational judgement test for medical school selection. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1390-0

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Interaction ANOVAs in the N = 372 survey: gender × response format on applicant differentiation (F(1, 362) = 4.80, p = .029, η2 = .01) and study relatedness (F(1, 362) = 7.64, p = .006, η2 = .02).

> "The more positive judgement of the rating format than the pick-one format was stronger for men than for women on the applicant differentiation item (d = 0.46 vs. d = 0.15) and on the study-relatedness item (d = 0.61 vs. d = 0.08)."
