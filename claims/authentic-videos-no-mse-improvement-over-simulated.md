# Adding an authentic patient video e-library to a simulated-video e-module did not significantly improve medical students' MSE accuracy compared to the simulated-video module alone

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Fifth-year medical students with add-on access to authentic patient videos scored no better on a digital MSE test one week after rotation than students with simulated videos only (10.1 vs 9.9). [→ Mikkelsen N 2025](#mikkelsen-n-2025)

## Evidence

### Mikkelsen N 2025

Mikkelsen N, Larsen EB, Arnfred SM. (2025). Authentic Versus Simulated Patient Videos: Effect on Mental Status Examination—An Educational Randomized Controlled Trial. Academic Psychiatry 49(3):222–230. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40596-024-02111-5

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Parallel-group cluster-RCT (intention-to-treat) with 190 students completing at least one MSE test (98 Full, 92 Limited) after a 3-week psychiatric rotation; MSE accuracy tested on unseen video cases via forced-choice MCQ. The trial reports "This difference was not significant" (F1,188 = 0.152; p = 0.7). No effect size printed, so no magnitude is reported.

> "our primary outcome—the mean MSE test score—for the Full group was 10.1 (SD 2.6), while the Limited group scored 9.9 (SD 2.9). This difference was not significant (F1,188, 0.152; p = 0.7)"
