## Evidence

### Kyle M. Whitcomb and Chandralekha Singh 2020

Kyle M. Whitcomb and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). In a physics curriculum only introductory physics course grades show gender differences but do not predict future course performance for physics majors. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ab9f1d](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ab9f1d)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Structural equation modeling on the full 451-student sample with FIML for missing data; final model fit CFI = 0.947, TLI = 0.933, RMSEA = 0.053, with non-significant regression paths trimmed. The model shows introductory physics has no predictive path to advanced physics.

> "One notable feature of Fig. 1 is that introductory mathemat- ics strongly predicts advanced mathematics, as expected, which covaries strongly with advanced physics. However, introductory physics does not predict advanced physics at all while introductory mathematics does, indicating that the primary predictor of success in advanced physics courses is success in mathematics courses."

### Kyle M. Whitcomb and Chandralekha Singh 2020 (2)

Kyle M. Whitcomb and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). In a physics curriculum only introductory physics course grades show gender differences but do not predict future course performance for physics majors. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ab9f1d](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ab9f1d)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The authors tested alternate SEM specifications (Fig. 2 with advanced mathematics predicting advanced physics, CFI = 0.946; Fig. 3 splitting advanced physics by year, CFI = 0.950) and report the null introductory-physics path holds across all tested models.

> "No models tested show introductory physics pre- dicting advanced physics when controlling for introduc- tory and/or advanced mathematics, including those not shown here such as a model in which introductory math- ematics is allowed to predict introductory physics, rather than covary with it."
