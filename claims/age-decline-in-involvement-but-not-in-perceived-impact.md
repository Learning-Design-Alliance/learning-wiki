# Bullying and cyberbullying involvement declines with age, but neither cyberbullies' perceived impact nor cybervictims' reported impact correlates with age.

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Involvement declined across age (55.3% of those involved were 12-14, 41% were 15-17, 3.7% were 18-20), while age was not significantly correlated with cyberbullies' perceived impact (r = -.07, p = .67) or cybervictims' reported impact (r = -.06, p = .600). [→ Ana M. Giménez Gualdo 2014](#ana-m-gimenez-gualdo-2014)

## Evidence

### Ana M. Giménez Gualdo 2014

Ana M. Giménez Gualdo, Simon C. Hunter, Kevin Durkin, Pilar Arnaiz, and Javier J. Maquilón. (2014). The emotional impact of cyberbullying: Differences in perceptions and experiences as a function of role. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2014.11.013

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analyses within the survey sample; age was also not correlated with cybervictims' reported impact, r (N = 84) = -.06, p = .600, and gender had no effect, t (82) = -1.50, p = .14. Involvement frequencies declined with age in both bullying forms.

> "The correlation between age and cyberbullies’ perceived impact was not significant, r (N = 36) = -.07, p = .67, and there was no impact of gender on those perceptions, t (34) = 1.13, p = .27 (two-tailed)."
