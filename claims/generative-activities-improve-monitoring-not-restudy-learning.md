# Generative activities improve students' monitoring accuracy but not their actual learning after restudy

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Although monitoring accuracy benefits from generative activities, students' actual learning after restudy does not, partly because comprehension-based regulation accuracy is low. [→ Janneke van de Pol 2020](#janneke-van-de-pol-2020)

## Evidence

### Janneke van de Pol 2020

Janneke van de Pol, et al. (2020). Mapping and Drawing to Improve Students’ and Teachers’ Monitoring and Regulation of Students’ Learning from Text: Current Findings and Future Directions. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09560-y

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's synthesis across studies: regulation decisions may follow monitoring judgments but do not match students' actual comprehension, so students may restudy texts they already understand or skip texts they do not. Two studies without restudy (Thiede et al., 2010; Wiley, 2019) did find higher test scores in generative conditions.

> "Although monitoring accuracy seems to benefit from generative activities, students’ actual learning after restudy does not."
