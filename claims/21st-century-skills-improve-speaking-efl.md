## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In two intact classes of female second-grade high school EFL students in Birjand, Iran (n = 55, convenience sample), the class taught speaking via 21st century skills scored significantly higher on a national speaking post-test than the class taught traditionally (p = 0.006). ## Evidence

### Ashraf 2017

Ashraf, H., Ahmadi, F., & Hosseinnia, M. (2017). Integrating 21st Century Skills into Teaching English: Investigating its Effect on Listening and Speaking Skills. i-manager's Journal on English Language Teaching, 7(4). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1173769

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Independent samples t-test on speaking post-test scores of 55 female second-grade high school students in two intact classes in Birjand, Iran (convenience sampling; the article gives the split as 25/30, with the methods and abstract assigning the 25 to opposite groups), after 12 sessions of treatment by the same teacher; the article reports "p-value = 0.006 < α = 0.05". Descriptives: control mean 9.45 (SD 3.30), experimental mean 12.30 (SD 2.40). One study in one school and population; it does not establish the effect for EFL instruction generally.

> "results of the t-test indicated that there was a significant difference between the mean scores of the control and experimental groups in post-test because: p-value = 0.006 < α = 0.05."
