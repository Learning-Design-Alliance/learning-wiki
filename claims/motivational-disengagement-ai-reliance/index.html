Frequency counts from the grounded theory coding of student accounts. Representative statements include "It makes me feel lazy to do my schoolwork" (n = 74) and "I lost confidence because I rely on it too much" (n = 41). No effect sizes are printed.

Related Claims¶

  • Students who used AI reported feeling less accomplished, with heavier reliance associated with greater loss of satisfaction — related
  • Grounded theory analysis of 1,054 university students identifies five dimensions of AI fatigue, each with two indicators — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Learners' task-specific self-efficacy beliefs predict whether they initiate, persist through, and recover from difficulty on academic tasks. — related
  • Attentional Drift, reported by 125 participants, reflects pace impatience and restless switching after AI-supported work — related
  • Moral Unease, reported by 190 participants, reflects authorship guilt and a gap between AI output quality and actual understanding — related
  • Students with a better understanding of how AI systems work show higher academic self-efficacy — related
  • AI use threatens students' computing identities as competent, hardworking, and ethical, with students identifying as cheaters, lazy, or incompetent even where AI is permitted — related
  • Students attributed initial passive reliance on GenAI to prompting paralysis and an authority bias compounded by low academic self-efficacy — related
  • Physical Strain, reported by 120 participants, comprises visual fatigue and session exhaustion from reading and verifying AI outputs — related
  • Students in both conditions named awareness of their reliance on AI as the most valuable course outcome, and unrestricted-AI students described shallow engagement they could not stop — related
