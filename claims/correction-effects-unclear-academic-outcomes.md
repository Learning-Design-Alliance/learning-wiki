## Evidence

### Kulp MT 2026

Kulp MT, Ciner EB, Candy TR, Ying G-s, Hall C, Quinn G, Moore B. (2026). Impact of uncorrected hyperopia on visual function and academic performance in preschool and school-age children. Optometry and Vision Science. https://doi.org/10.1002/ovs2.70018

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports the Pediatric Eye Disease Investigator Group Hyperopia Treatment Study, a randomized assignment of 3- to 5-year-olds with uncorrected moderate hyperopia (3-6 D spherical equivalent) to glasses or observation, finding "low rates of decreased visual acuity after 3-years of follow-up that were similar between groups."

> "The study found low rates of decreased visual acuity after 3-years of follow-up that were similar between groups, concluding that correction may not be necessary to prevent development of amblyopia or strabismus in moderately hyperopic 3- to 5-year-olds with age-normal distance visual acuity and eye alignment."

### Kulp MT 2026 (2)

Kulp MT, Ciner EB, Candy TR, Ying G-s, Hall C, Quinn G, Moore B. (2026). Impact of uncorrected hyperopia on visual function and academic performance in preschool and school-age children. Optometry and Vision Science. https://doi.org/10.1002/ovs2.70018

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports a Cochrane review of four trials on spectacle correction versus no spectacles for prevention of strabismus in hyperopic children, which concluded the effect was unclear with "low confidence in the results of this review of four trials due to high risk of bias, inconsistency, and imprecision."

> "the authors had 'low confidence in the results of this review of four trials due to high risk of bias, inconsistency, and imprecision'."

### Kulp MT 2026 (3)

Kulp MT, Ciner EB, Candy TR, Ying G-s, Hall C, Quinn G, Moore B. (2026). Impact of uncorrected hyperopia on visual function and academic performance in preschool and school-age children. Optometry and Vision Science. https://doi.org/10.1002/ovs2.70018

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review characterizes existing correction studies as "preliminary evidence" that correction may significantly improve near visual function, visual attention, visual acuity, and visual-motor deficits. No effect sizes are printed for these correction effects.

> "there is preliminary evidence that hyperopic correction may significantly improve near visual function (binocular 52 and accommodative function 52 , 53 ), visual attention, 54 visual acuity, 44 and visual-motor deficits 55 in children."
