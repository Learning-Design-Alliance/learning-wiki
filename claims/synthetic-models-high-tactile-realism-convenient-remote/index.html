Evidence¶

Chawla Shreya 2022¶

Chawla Shreya, Devi Sharmila, Calvachi Paola, Gormley William B., Rueda-Esteban Roberto. (2022). Evaluation of simulation models in neurosurgical training according to face, content, and construct validity: a systematic review. Acta Neurochirurgica. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00701-021-05003-x

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

The review's synthesis across model types (seven synthetic-model studies) reports "a high median tactile realism (4 out of 5) compared to other models"; the value is a median Likert rating, not an effect size.

"The synthetic models ranked a high median tactile realism (4 out of 5) compared to other models."

Chawla Shreya 2022 (2)¶

Chawla Shreya, Devi Sharmila, Calvachi Paola, Gormley William B., Rueda-Esteban Roberto. (2022). Evaluation of simulation models in neurosurgical training according to face, content, and construct validity: a systematic review. Acta Neurochirurgica. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00701-021-05003-x

q2 · i? · review · r3

The authors' interpretation in the COVID-19 discussion section: "synthetic models, due to their visual and haptic realism, are the most convenient forms of simulation models to be used safely in remote settings"; an author judgement, not a tested comparison.

"Based on our review and current literature findings on residents' concerns of surgical training in the pandemic, synthetic models, due to their visual and haptic realism, are the most convenient forms of simulation models to be used safely in remote settings."
