## Evidence

### Apthorp 2016

Apthorp, H., Klute, M., Petrites, T., Harlacher, J., & Reale, M. (2016). Valuing a more rigorous review of formative assessment's effectiveness. SREE Spring 2016 Conference Proposal. https://www.relcentral.org/news-and-events/the-essence-of-formative-assessment-creating-a-common-understanding-of-the-formative-assessment-process/

`q2 · i?` · `review · r?`

The review's background section attributes these differing estimates to named prior reviews and a meta-analysis, reporting that estimates range "from 0.40 and 0.70 standard deviations in one review ... to 0.20 in a meta-analysis". The authors suggest definitional differences may explain the discrepancy.

> "Prior reviews of evidence for the impact of formative assessment on student achievement suggest widely different estimates of formative assessment's effectiveness, ranging from 0.40 and 0.70 standard deviations in one review (Black & Wiliam, 1998a; 1998b) to 0.20 in a meta-analysis (Kingston & Nash, 2011; 2015)."

### Offerdahl E. G. 2018

Offerdahl E. G., McConnell M., Boyer J. (2018). Can I Have Your Recipe? Using a Fidelity of Implementation (FOI) Framework to Identify the Key Ingredients of Formative Assessment for Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.18-02-0029

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Narrative review of the formative assessment literature in the article's introduction. The authors note that historically reported effect sizes ran "between 0.4 and 0.7" and that a more recent meta-analysis (Kingston and Nash, 2011) calculates a much smaller effect size, on the order of 0.2, with calculation methods possibly biasing the estimate (Briggs et al., 2012).

> "Effect sizes between 0.4 and 0.7 have historically been reported based on work by Black and Wiliam (1998a,b). However, sparse description of the methodology involved in calculating these statistics has led to recent scrutiny of the reported effect"
