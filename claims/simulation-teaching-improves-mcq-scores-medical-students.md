## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Simulation teaching yields higher multiple-choice question scores than traditional teaching among medical students (SMD = 0.33). [→ Zhang Z 2024](#zhang-z-2024)

## Evidence

### Zhang Z 2024

Zhang Z, Zhang Z, Yu Y, Jiang M, Deng L, Guo L. (2024). Impact of different teaching modes on medical students' performance under the scoring criteria for multiple-choice questions: A meta-analysis. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000041008

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

A meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials and crossover trials comparing simulation teaching with traditional teaching. The pooled analysis found "the multiple-choice test scores in simulation teaching were higher than those in traditional teaching, with a SMD of 0.33".

> "The analysis results indicated that the multiple-choice test scores in simulation teaching were higher than those in traditional teaching, with a SMD of 0.33, 95% CI (0.18, 0.48)"
