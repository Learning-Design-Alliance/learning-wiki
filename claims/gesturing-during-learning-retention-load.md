## Evidence

### Björn B. de Koning & Huib K. Tabbers 2011

Björn B. de Koning & Huib K. Tabbers. (2011). Facilitating Understanding of Movements in Dynamic Visualizations: an Embodied Perspective. Educ Psychol Rev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-011-9173-8

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports a study by Cook et al. in which children encouraged to gesture while learning an arithmetical strategy retained it, while children asked to speak without gesturing did not. The review attributes the benefit to gesturing providing an alternative, action-embodied representation; no effect size is printed.

> "Cook et al. (2008) found that encouraging children to make gestures while learning a new arithmetical strategy are more likely to retain the knowledge they gained during instruction. In contrast, asking children to speak but not gesture during learning did not result in retention of the strategy."

### Björn B. de Koning & Huib K. Tabbers 2011 (2)

Björn B. de Koning & Huib K. Tabbers. (2011). Facilitating Understanding of Movements in Dynamic Visualizations: an Embodied Perspective. Educ Psychol Rev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-011-9173-8

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Goldin-Meadow et al.'s study in which children and adults explained a mathematical solution while remembering letters or words; both age groups remembered more when gesturing. The review concludes gesturing freed cognitive resources; no effect size is printed.

> "Both age groups remembered more items when they gestured during their explanations than when they did not gesture. It was concluded that gesturing freed up cognitive resources needed for the explanation task"
