# Undergraduate course satisfaction dropped sharply after courses moved fully online during COVID-19, with very satisfied students falling from 51 percent to 19 percent

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The proportion of students very satisfied with their course fell from 51 percent before the move online to 19 percent after, though 59 percent remained at least somewhat satisfied. [→ Means 2020](#means-2020)

## Evidence

### Means 2020

Means, B., and Neisler, J., with Langer Research Associates. (2020). Suddenly Online: A National Survey of Undergraduates During the COVID-19 Pandemic. San Mateo, CA: Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/32aee7c4-5363-4d50-bc9c-a338e7dfac7e

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

National random-sample survey of 1,008 undergraduates whose in-person courses moved fully online in spring 2020, fielded May 13–June 1, 2020 via the Ipsos KnowledgePanel. Satisfaction "dramatically lower" after the shift, with 59 percent at least somewhat satisfied afterward.

> "About half of the students in the survey (51 percent) said they were very satisfied with their course before it went fully online. Students’ ratings of their satisfaction with their courses after moving fully online were dramatically lower, with only 19 percent of students being very satisfied"
