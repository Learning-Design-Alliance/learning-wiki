# Practicing cover letter writing with an AI tool improved unassisted writing skill more than practicing without AI or not practicing, with benefits persisting one day later

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r3` · `q4` · `i1`–`i2`

## Subclaims
`q4 i1` Participants who practiced with the AI tool wrote better cover letters on a no-AI test than those who practiced without AI (d=.38) or did not practice (d=.47). [→ Benjamin Lira 2025](#benjamin-lira-2025)
`q4 i2` The benefit persisted at a one-day follow-up test (d=.41 vs. no-AI practice; d=.46 vs. no practice). [→ Benjamin Lira 2025 (2)](#benjamin-lira-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Benjamin Lira 2025

Benjamin Lira, Todd Rogers, Daniel G. Goldstein, Lyle Ungar, and Angela L. Duckworth. (2025). Coach not crutch: Evidence that AI can improve writing skill despite reducing effort. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02880

`q4 · i1` · `causal · r3`

Pre-registered randomized experiment (N=2,238, Prolific). After a lesson on five writing principles, participants were assigned to practice with AI, practice without AI, or no practice, then took a no-AI writing test. "wrote better cover letters" with d=.38 and d=.47.

> "participants who had practiced with the AI tool wrote better cover letters than participants who either had practiced without the AI tool (d= .38,p<.001) or who had not practiced at all (d= .47,p<.001)"

### Benjamin Lira 2025 (2)

Benjamin Lira, Todd Rogers, Daniel G. Goldstein, Lyle Ungar, and Angela L. Duckworth. (2025). Coach not crutch: Evidence that AI can improve writing skill despite reducing effort. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02880

`q4 · i2` · `causal · r3`

One-day follow-up with 87% retention; attrition did not differ by condition (χ²=.68, p=.710). Participants "continued to outperform" those who practiced without AI (d=.41) and those who did not practice (d=.46). Condition × time interactions were non-significant (ps>.69).

> "participants who had practiced with the AI tool the previous day continued to outperform those who had practiced without the tool (d= .41,p<.001) as well as those who had not practiced at all (d= .46,p< .001)"
