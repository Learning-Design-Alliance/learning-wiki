## Evidence

### Shu-Wei Liu 2025

Shu-Wei Liu, Hung-Yu Chan, & Huei-Tse Hou. (2025). Role-playing monument exploration: an online educational game with a role-playing mechanism and multi-dimensional scaffolding for monument tours. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10480-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Wilcoxon signed rank tests of pretest–posttest learning effectiveness (23-point test) in a quasi-experiment with 12 experimental and 9 control participants. The article reports significant gains in both groups, indicating "positive learning effectiveness for both groups of activities".

> "They showed that there were significant differences in both the experimental group (Z = − 2.728, p = 0.006) and the control group (Z = − 2.684, p = 0.007), which indicated positive learning effectiveness for both groups of activities."

### Shu-Wei Liu 2025 (2)

Shu-Wei Liu, Hung-Yu Chan, & Huei-Tse Hou. (2025). Role-playing monument exploration: an online educational game with a role-playing mechanism and multi-dimensional scaffolding for monument tours. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10480-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Mann–Whitney U comparison of pretest and posttest learning effectiveness between the game-based and traditional guide groups (n = 12 vs. 9). No significant difference was found; equivalence was not tested. The authors note more game play time might produce higher learning effectiveness.

> "The results showed that there were no significant differences between the two groups, indicating that there was no significant difference in the learning effectiveness of the two groups."
