# The motivation manipulation improved attention performance in children with ADHD but did not change TD children's performance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` The motivation factor significantly differentiated attention indicators in children with ADHD, while TD children's indicators were similar across conditions. [→ Sebastian Skalski 2021](#sebastian-skalski-2021)

## Evidence

### Sebastian Skalski 2021

Sebastian Skalski, Grzegorz Pochwatko, Robert Balas. (2021). Impact of Motivation on Selected Aspects of Attention in Children with ADHD. Child Psychiatry & Human Development. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10578-020-01042-0

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Simple main effects of the motivation factor in the quasi-experiment; for children with ADHD all indicators except commission errors improved significantly (also RT slope r = 0.38, single-tasking r = 0.51, multi-tasking r = 0.49), whereas for TD children all indicators were similar across conditions.

> "The motivation factor significantly differentiated the indicators of selected attention aspects in children with ADHD: omission errors (t = 4.12, p < 0.001, r = 0.48), RT in visual search of 5–20 elements (t = 3.95, p < 0.001, r = 0.46)"
