# Conservers' certainty over conservation is relative: they seldom express feelings of logical necessity, and mere exposure to an opposing answer often suffices for yielding

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Miller 1974

Miller, Scott A.; Brownell, Celia A. (1974). Peers, Persuasion, and Piaget: Dyadic Interaction Between Conservers and Nonconservers. Developmental Program Report No. 44, University of Michigan. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED105975

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Interpretive analysis of the taped discussions: the authors report that statements reflecting feelings of necessity were rare, so the two scoring categories for explicit certainty and inferred necessity were abandoned, consistent with extinction research.

> "Occurrences of such responses were so infrequent, however, that both categories had .o be abandoned."

### Miller 1974 (2)

Miller, Scott A.; Brownell, Celia A. (1974). Peers, Persuasion, and Piaget: Dyadic Interaction Between Conservers and Nonconservers. Developmental Program Report No. 44, University of Michigan. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED105975

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive analysis of yielding in the taped interactions: the article reports that on "25 trials the loser never offered an explanation, and on 20 of these trials he never even asserted his original answer", suggesting mere exposure to an opposing answer sufficed for many children.

> "Thus, on 25 trials the loser never offered an explanation, and on 20 of these trials he never even asserted his original answer."
