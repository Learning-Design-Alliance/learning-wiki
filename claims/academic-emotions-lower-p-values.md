## Evidence

### Eliot 2019

Eliot, J. A. R., & Hirumi, A. (2019). Emotion theory in education research practice: an interdisciplinary critical literature review. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-018-09642-3

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

This is the review's own qualitative synthesis of the pattern across educational studies, presented around its Fig. 4 taxonomy of emotion taxonomies. The review states the result "tends to be markedly lower or insignificant p-values" for academic-emotion constructs. No effect size is printed; the claim is the review's interpretive conclusion, not a pooled estimate.

> "when constructs recognized as emotions only by multidimensional schemas are applied to research questions, the result tends to be markedly lower or insignificant p-values compared to the basic emotions used in the same study"

### Eliot 2019 (2)

Eliot, J. A. R., & Hirumi, A. (2019). Emotion theory in education research practice: an interdisciplinary critical literature review. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-018-09642-3

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes this to D'Mello's meta-analysis of affective states during learning with technology, reading its Table 3 (p. 1090). The review reports D'Mello "finds insignificant and puzzling data" more often for academic emotions. No effect size is printed for this contrast.

> "D'Mello (2013) finds insignificant and puzzling data for emotions much more frequently in studies of academic rather than basic emotions (p. 1090, Table 3)"
