# Parental funding of about 10 currency units per month per child can plausibly sustain advanced studies by two teachers per school per year, yielding roughly 18 thousand units per teacher per year.

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` An order-of-magnitude estimate based on a 300-student Polish high school suggests one parent per child paying 10 currency units per month produces a 36-thousand-unit annual budget for two teachers' advanced studies, about 18 thousand units per teacher. ## Evidence

### Stanis/suppress law D. G/suppress lazek 2012

Stanis/suppress law D. G/suppress lazek. (2012). Estimate of resources required for a meaningful reform of education. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1211.5514](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1211.5514)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r1`

This is the author's illustrative cost calculation (Section IV C) using an assumed 300-student high school, the average Polish size cited from 2011 government statistics, and assumed contributions of one parent per child. The author states the numbers "This amount is comparable to tuitions in respectable public colleges." No empirical test is reported; this is a theoretical order-of-magnitude argument.

> "If they paid 10 units per month, they would pay 120 units per year. When the latter is multiplied by 300 (the number of students in the school), the budget for advanced studies by two teachers per year turns out to be 36 thousand, which means 18 thousand per teacher per year."
