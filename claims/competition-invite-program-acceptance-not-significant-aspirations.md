# Being invited by adults to STEM competitions and being accepted into application-based STEM programs were not statistically significant predictors of STEM career aspirations

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Two recognition experiences — adult invitation to STEM competitions and acceptance into application-requiring STEM programs — did not significantly predict STEM career aspirations. [→ Awsaf 2026](#awsaf-2026)

## Evidence

### Awsaf 2026

Awsaf, A.A., Dou, R., Sonnert, G., & Sadler, P. (2025, March). Exploring associations between event-based (mis)recognition by STEM authorities with STEM identity and career aspirations [Presentation]. 2025 NARST Annual International Conference, National Harbor, MD. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2607.06043](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.06043)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression analysis of the same 1,134-respondent MSI survey found these two recognition items non-significant for career aspirations; no effect size is printed, so the null is reported without magnitude.

> "Interestingly, having the experience of being invited by adults to participate in a STEM competition and getting accepted into a STEM program or school that required an application was not statistically significant in predicting STEM career aspirations."
