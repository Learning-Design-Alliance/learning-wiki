## Evidence

### Horiuchi Sayaka 2025

Horiuchi Sayaka, Smith Rachel, Phrasithideth Bouasengnignom, Kounnavongsa Outhevanh, Thilakoune Chitsana, Xayavong Hongkham, Sakamaki Kentaro, Rattana Sommana, Vogel Joshua P (2025). An innovative, digital approach to training district health care providers on essential newborn care skills: findings from a pilot cluster-randomised trial in Lao People’s Democratic Republic. Journal of Global Health. https://doi.org/10.7189/jogh.15.04163

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Thematic analysis of six endline focus group discussions with providers and facilitators found positive attitudes towards the intervention, with most feeling provider skills needed to improve and the intervention well-designed to achieve this.

> "Both district health care providers and facilitators had positive attitudes towards the intervention (Table S1 in the Online Supplementary Document)."

### Horiuchi Sayaka 2025 (2)

Horiuchi Sayaka, Smith Rachel, Phrasithideth Bouasengnignom, Kounnavongsa Outhevanh, Thilakoune Chitsana, Xayavong Hongkham, Sakamaki Kentaro, Rattana Sommana, Vogel Joshua P (2025). An innovative, digital approach to training district health care providers on essential newborn care skills: findings from a pilot cluster-randomised trial in Lao People’s Democratic Republic. Journal of Global Health. https://doi.org/10.7189/jogh.15.04163

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same focus group discussions, facilitators appreciated the resource-saving aspect of remote supportive supervision, compared to on-site supervisory visits, and felt it feasible to send feedback by WhatsApp instead of travelling to each district hospital.

> "They particularly appreciated the resource-saving aspect of remote supportive supervision, compared to on-site supervisory visits."
