# Students highly absent (5-9.9% of days) in the prior year, below the chronic threshold, start the next year .14 to .22 SDs lower in mathematics than no-absence peers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Highly absent students who did not meet the chronic definition still showed significant, persistent starting-achievement deficits in math across all grade levels. [→ King 2020](#king-2020)

## Evidence

### King 2020

King, G., & Jensen, N. (2020). Looking Back: How Prior-Year Attendance Impacts Starting Achievement. (The Center for School and Student Progress at NWEA Working Paper). https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/looking-back-how-prior-year-attendance-impacts-starting-achievement/

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Same fixed-effects models as the chronic-absence analysis, with HA defined as 5-9.9% of days absent (9-17 days of a 180-day year). Cohen's d effect sizes were ".14 and .22 SDs lower" in math and significant across grade levels; reading effects were smaller.

> "HA students started the subsequent school year at an achievement level between .14 and .22 SDs lower than NA students in math. These effects are all negative and significant across grade levels."
