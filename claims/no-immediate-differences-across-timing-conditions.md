## Evidence

### Congdon Eliza L. 2017

Congdon Eliza L., Novack Miriam A., Brooks Neon, Hemani-Lopez Naureen, O'Keefe Lucy, Goldin-Meadow Susan. (2017). Better together: Simultaneous presentation of speech and gesture in math instruction supports generalization and retention. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2017.03.005

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Immediate posttest of the randomized experiment (72 children, 24 per condition) on six basic problems after instruction with experimenter feedback; no significant condition effect. Equivalence between conditions was not tested.

> "A mixed-effects binomial regression with condition as a fixed effect and participant as a random effect showed no significant differences in performance at immediate test among any of the three conditions (Main effect of condition: χ2 (2) = 0.95, p = .62;"

### Congdon Eliza L. 2017 (2)

Congdon Eliza L., Novack Miriam A., Brooks Neon, Hemani-Lopez Naureen, O'Keefe Lucy, Goldin-Meadow Susan. (2017). Better together: Simultaneous presentation of speech and gesture in math instruction supports generalization and retention. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2017.03.005

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Immediate generalization test (five novel-format problems) in the same experiment; no significant condition effect. The authors interpret this as showing all groups initially comprehended the lesson.

> "A regression model predicting the probability of a correct answer by condition with participant as a random effect showed no significant differences performance on novel problem types at immediate test among the three conditions (Main effect of condition: χ2 (2) = 2.03, p = .36;"
