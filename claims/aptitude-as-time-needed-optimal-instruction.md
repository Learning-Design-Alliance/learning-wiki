## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Aptitude for a task is measured as the amount of time needed to learn it under optimal teaching conditions, with shorter needed time meaning higher aptitude, and is specific to the task. [→ Carroll 1962](#carroll-1962)

## Evidence

### Carroll 1962

John B. Carroll. (1962). A Model Of School Learning. Harvard University. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22A+Model+Of+School+Learning%22+Carroll

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Definitional/theoretical statement in the Time Needed in Learning section. Aptitude is measured in the opposite direction from usual practice, is task-specific, and may depend on prior learning and on basic aptitude traits; some learners may need an indefinitely large amount of time.

> "the amount of time the pupil will need to learn the task under these conditions as the primary measure of a variable which we shall call his aptitude for learning this task"
