## Evidence

### Yuqin Yang · Gaoxia Zhu · Carol K. K. Chan 2022

Yuqin Yang · Gaoxia Zhu · Carol K. K. Chan. (2022). Evolution of the academic emotions of academically low-achieving students in knowledge building. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-022-09380-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Lag sequential analysis per class with a z-score greater than 1.96 indicating a significant transition between two codes. Class A data suggest "a tendency for emotions to transition to the same or similar emotions," reflecting the spread of similar emotions in students' inquiry threads.

> "As shown in Fig.  3(a), there were ten significant transitions of emotions in Class A: boredom → frustration (z-score = 5.1), frustration → frustration (3.74), joy → joy (3.6), confusion → none (2.88)"

### Yuqin Yang · Gaoxia Zhu · Carol K. K. Chan 2022 (2)

Yuqin Yang · Gaoxia Zhu · Carol K. K. Chan. (2022). Evolution of the academic emotions of academically low-achieving students in knowledge building. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-022-09380-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Class B lag sequential analysis identified four significant emotion sequences, including joy to confusion (2.46) and frustration to boredom (2.25); the article interprets these as indicating negative emotion spread absent intervention.

> "the significant transitions suggest a greater possibility for transitions from anxiety to anxiety, surprise to frustration, and frustration to boredom, suggesting the spread of negative emotions if teachers or students take no specific actions to intervene in students’ emotions."

### Yuqin Yang · Gaoxia Zhu · Carol K. K. Chan 2022 (3)

Yuqin Yang · Gaoxia Zhu · Carol K. K. Chan. (2022). Evolution of the academic emotions of academically low-achieving students in knowledge building. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-022-09380-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Class C also showed a curiosity–frustration–surprise–anxiety path and confusion leading to boredom; the article offers an interpretation, not a tested explanation, for the surprise transition.

> "Class C featured the unique transition of frustration → surprise (5.73). These results might be due to the ability of the older low-achieving students (those in Grade 11) to use cognitive and metacognitive learning strategies to resolve the contradictions they faced."
