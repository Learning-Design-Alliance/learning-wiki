# Hedges' g thresholds classify ak parameter comparisons: g < 0.5 means responses are equally correct, g ≥ 1.3 means they represent different levels of understanding, and values between are inconclusive

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The authors use Hedges' g effect sizes between sampling distributions of ak parameters with thresholds g < 0.5 (effectively equal), g ≥ 1.3 (significantly different), and 0.5 ≤ g < 1.3 (no conclusive determination). [→ Trevor I. Smith 2020](#trevor-i-smith-2020)

## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith 2020

Trevor I. Smith, Kyle J. Louis, Bartholomew J. Ricci, IV, and Nasrine Bendjilali. (2020). Quantitatively ranking incorrect responses to multiple-choice questions using item response theory. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010107](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010107)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Methodological result from the bootstrap sampling analysis: ak distributions were generated by repeatedly sampling 7,300 response sets over 100,000 repetitions, and Hedges' g was computed for each nearest-neighbor pair using the effsize package. The authors report the value of g for all comparisons so readers can apply other thresholds.

> "if g is small (g <0.5) we conclude that the parameter values are eﬀectively equal and the responses are equally correct, if g is very large ( g≥ 1.3) we conclude that the parameters are signiﬁcantly diﬀerent and that the responses represent diﬀerent levels of understanding"
