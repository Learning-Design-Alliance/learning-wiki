## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The frequency of simulation-based training in and assessment of seven generic NTSs was statistically significantly greater for HEMS than for GEMS during 2015, with all differences significant on two-sided Fisher's exact tests. [→ Langdalen H 2018](#langdalen-h-2018)

## Evidence

### Langdalen H 2018

Langdalen H, Abrahamsen EB, Sollid SJM, Sørskår LIK, Abrahamsen HB. (2018). A comparative study on the frequency of simulation-based training and assessment of non-technical skills in the Norwegian ground ambulance services and helicopter emergency medical services. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-018-3325-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional questionnaire study of Norwegian prehospital EMS employees (1107 analysed: GEMS n = 998, HEMS n = 109) on training and assessment during 2015. The article reports that "These apparent differences between the two EMSs are all statistically significant, supported by two-sided Fisher’s exact tests (Table 1)"; Table 1 prints p-values from < 0.001 to 0.015 for training and < 0.001 to 0.002 for assessment.

> "Visual inspection of the frequency of simulation-based training and assessment in the HEMS and GEMS (Fig. 2) indicates that the HEMS personnel are generally exposed to training and assessment of NTSs more frequently than the GEMS personnel. These apparent differences between the two EMSs are all statistically significant, supported by two-sided Fisher’s exact tests (Table 1)."
