# Later cohorts fell off schedule for graduation at earlier ages, but the percentage off schedule by age 16 changed very little

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Cohorts subject to promotion gates were off schedule earlier (e.g., the 1998 cohort was almost one-quarter off schedule by age 13), yet by age 16 the off-schedule percentage stayed around 27-29 percent across cohorts. [→ Elaine M. Allensworth & Shazia Rafiullah Miller 2002](#elaine-m-allensworth-shazia-rafiullah-miller-2002)

## Evidence

### Elaine M. Allensworth & Shazia Rafiullah Miller 2002

Elaine M. Allensworth & Shazia Rafiullah Miller. (2002). Declining High School Enrollment: An Exploration of Causes. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/declining-high-school-enrollment-exploration-causes

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cohort progression analysis tracking 13-year-old cohorts from 1991 through 1998 (Section III, Figure 6). The report concludes "there was very little change in the percentage of students off schedule for graduation at age 16," though earlier cohorts fell behind at younger ages.

> "by age 16, 27 percent of the 1991 cohort was off schedule for gradua- tion. This compares to 29 percent of the 1992, 1993 and 1994 cohorts, 28 percent of the 1995 cohort, and 29 percent of the 1996, 1997, and 1998 cohorts."
