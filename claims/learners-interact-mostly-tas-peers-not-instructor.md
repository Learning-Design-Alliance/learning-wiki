# Learners in both teaching models interacted mostly with TAs and peers rather than with the instructor

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Interview comparison showed both groups interacted mostly with TAs and peers instead of the instructor, which the authors propose explains the lack of significant difference in interaction scores. [→ Sun and Wu 2015](#sun-and-wu-2015)

## Evidence

### Sun and Wu 2015

Sun and Wu. (2015). Analysis of Learning Achievement and Teacher–Student Interactions in Flipped and Conventional Classrooms. http://www.oeconsortium.org/about-oec/

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Authors' interpretation of interview data from both groups: "the objects of the learners' interactions in the collaborative learning are possibly TAs or peers, while the self-study learners also chose to ask TAs or peers." The article notes learners positioned instructor and TAs in different roles, an effect not evaluated.

> "These aspects thus contribute to the lack of significant difference in the scores for teacher-student interactions."
