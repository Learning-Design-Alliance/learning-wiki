## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The advantage of blocked and increased conditions over interleaved was significant only for low prior knowledge students at immediate post-test for representational knowledge, with significant interaction between condition and prior knowledge group. [→ Rau 2010](#rau-2010)

## Evidence

### Rau 2010

Rau, M. A., Aleven, V., & Rummel, N. (2010). Blocked versus Interleaved Practice with Multiple Representations in an Intelligent Tutoring System for Fractions. In Proceedings of the 10th International Conference on Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITS 2010). https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-13388-6_45

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

HLM analysis of the in vivo experiment split by median pre-test performance found significant condition × prior knowledge interactions across both knowledge types at both post-test times, indicating the sequencing effect differs by prior knowledge level.

> "An interaction between condition and the prior knowledge groups was significant for representational knowledge at both the immediate post-test, F(4, 201) = 17.56, p < .01, and the delayed post-test, F(4, 202) = 6.08, p < .01, as well as for operational knowledge at the immediate post-test, F(4, 199) = 21.74, p < .01, and the delayed post-test, F(4, 198) = 17.90, p < .01."
