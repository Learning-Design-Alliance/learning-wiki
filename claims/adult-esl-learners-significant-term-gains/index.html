Evidence¶

Condelli 2010¶

Condelli, L., Cronen, S., Bos, J., Tseng, F., and Altuna, J. (2010). The Impact of a Reading Intervention for Low-Literate Adult ESL Learners (NCEE 2011-4003). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee/

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Pre/post assessment of study students on seven reading and English language measures at term start and after about 12 weeks of instruction. "statistically significant gains over the course of the term (effect sizes of 0.23 to 0.40)"; the article cautions months-of-growth conversions rely on norming populations that differ from low-literate adult ESL learners.

"On average, students participating in the study made statistically significant gains over the course of the term (effect sizes of 0.23 to 0.40). These gains are equivalent to 1 to 2 months of growth on the reading assessments, and 5 to 6 months of growth on the English language assessments."
