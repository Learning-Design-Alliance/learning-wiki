# Specialisation is strongly associated with sorting into higher-wage occupations but only weakly related to adaptive outcomes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Workers with more specialised skill portfolios are more likely to sort into higher-wage occupations, while specialisation is only weakly related to adaptive outcomes such as skill acquisition and mobility. [→ Carpanelli 2026](#carpanelli-2026)

## Evidence

### Carpanelli 2026

Carpanelli, M., Duszynski, J. & Stephany, F. (2026). Navigating the skill diversity frontier: How skill complexity explains worker resilience. Working paper, Oxford Internet Institute. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02102 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2608.02102](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2608.02102)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression analysis of a longitudinal LinkedIn panel of approximately 2.4 million US workers, with explanatory variables measured 2020–2022 and outcomes 2023–2025. The study reports that "Specialisation is strongly associated with sorting into higher wage occupations" while adaptive outcomes relate only weakly to it. No effect sizes are printed.

> "Specialisation is strongly associated with sorting into higher wage occupations but only weakly related to adaptive outcomes, conﬁrming that productive depth alone is insuﬃcient for resilience."
