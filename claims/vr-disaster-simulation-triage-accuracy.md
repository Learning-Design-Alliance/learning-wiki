## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Nursing students who received VR disaster simulation education showed significantly greater gains in disaster triage accuracy than those receiving written case simulation. [→ Kim Kyeng-Jin 2025](#kim-kyeng-jin-2025)

## Evidence

### Kim Kyeng-Jin 2025

Kim Kyeng-Jin, Choi Moon-Ji, Kim MinJi. (2025). Effects of virtual reality-based disaster simulation education on nursing students. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0329563

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental non-equivalent control group pretest-posttest study of 67 nursing students (n=33 VR, n=34 written case simulation). Triage accuracy (0–5 scale) gain was "1.70 ± 0.81 in the experimental group and 0.68 ± 0.54 in the control group", t = 6.11, p < .001; no effect size printed.

> "Disaster triage accuracy improved between the pretest and posttest from 2.33 ± 0.60 to 4.03 ± 0.47 in the experimental group and from 2.26 ± 0.62 to 2.94 ± 0.34 in the control group. The post-pre difference was 1.70 ± 0.81 in the experimental group and 0.68 ± 0.54 in the control group, with a statistically significant difference between groups (t = 6.11, p < .001)."
