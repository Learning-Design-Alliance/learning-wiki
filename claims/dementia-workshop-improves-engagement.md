# Self-rated GP Engagement scores rose from pre-test to post-test within both registrar and supervisor groups following the workshop, with supervisors scoring higher than registrars at both time points

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Both registrars and supervisors recorded significantly higher Engagement scores postworkshop, with a moderate effect size for each group (d = 0.610 and d = 0.530), and supervisors scored higher at both time points. [→ Mason Ron 2020](#mason-ron-2020)

## Evidence

### Mason Ron 2020

Mason Ron, Doherty Kathleen, Eccleston Claire, Winbolt Margaret, Long Marita, Robinson Andrew. (2020). Effect of a dementia education intervention on the confidence and attitudes of general practitioners in Australia: a pretest post-test study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2019-033218

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Paired and independent t-tests on the Engagement subscale, which measures perceptions towards treating dementia including fear of communicating a diagnosis and frustration. Registrars rose 2.98 to 3.42 (d = 0.610); supervisors 3.44 to 3.84 (d = 0.530).

> "Both supervisors and GPRs recorded a significantly higher score for Engagement postworkshop, while supervisors reported greater Engagement than GPRs at baseline (t(439)=5.877; p<0.001) and after the workshop (t(422)=5.091; p<0.001). A moderate effect size was observed for the score change shown for each group."
