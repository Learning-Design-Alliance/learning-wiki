## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Separate meta-analyses per attitude subscale found significant positive effects for general interest (d = 0.22, 20 studies) and career interest (d = 0.40, 4 studies), but not for relevance, self-efficacy, or normality of scientist. [→ Savelsbergh 2016](#savelsbergh-2016)

## Evidence

### Savelsbergh 2016

Savelsbergh, E. R., Prins, G. T., Rietbergen, C., Fechner, S., Vaessen, B. E., Draijer, J. M., & Bakker, A. (2016). Effects of innovative science and mathematics teaching on student attitudes and achievement: A meta-analytic study. Educational Research Review, 19, 158-172. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2016.07.003

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Separate random-effects meta-analyses per subscale (Table 4), with Prokop et al. removed as outlier. The authors report "The effects for General Interest and Career Interest are signi ﬁcant and positive" (printed effect sizes d = 0.22 and d = 0.40), while general relevance (d = 0.15), self-efficacy (d = 0.12), and normality of scientist (d = 0.16) were nonsignificant.

> "The effects for General Interest and Career Interest are signi ﬁcant and positive. However, there are no signiﬁcant differences between any of the subscales, so there is no ground to declare one of the effects larger than the others."
