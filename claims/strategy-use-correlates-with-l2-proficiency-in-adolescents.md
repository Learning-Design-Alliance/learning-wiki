# Adolescents who use more language learning strategies are the more proficient ones, and strategy adoption varies with cultural background.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment · n=157

Correlational, so the direction is open: strategies may build proficiency, proficiency may afford
strategies, or both may follow from something else.

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Across 157 newly arrived migrant students aged 11–19 in nine north-east Italian secondary schools, overall strategy use was medium (M = 3.09) with a preference for indirect over direct strategies. [→ Mantovani 2026](#mantovani-2026)

`q2 i?` Low strategy use correlated with weaker Italian, and adoption was influenced by cross-cultural differences among the adolescents rather than being uniform. [→ Mantovani 2026](#mantovani-2026)

## Evidence

### Mantovani 2026

Mantovani, A. (2026). Investigating language learning strategies through the strategy inventory: A study among immigrant adolescents in Italian secondary schools. *Glottodidactica, 53*(1), 221–239. [doi:10.14746/gl.2026.53.1.10](https://doi.org/10.14746/gl.2026.53.1.10)

`q2 · cross-sectional correlational self-report study` · `i? · correlation reported as present, magnitude not given` · `n=157`

157 newly arrived migrant students across nine secondary schools in north-east Italy completed an adapted Strategy Inventory for Language Learning. The paper reports mean strategy use, the indirect-over-direct preference, the correlation with proficiency, and variation by cultural background, and recommends explicitly promoting cognitive, social and compensatory strategies through goal-oriented and collaborative tasks.

## Discussion

**Strategy use is a real axis of learner variation, not a proxy for effort or motivation.** It is
also the actionable one in this set: unlike first-language background, it is something instruction
can change — which is what [Strategy instruction is more effective when contextualized in authentic
tasks](learning-strategy-instruction-contextualized-more-effective.md) [+M] says about how.

**The learners using fewest strategies are the ones who need most support, and are least likely to
go looking for it.** Help placed behind an optional control therefore reaches the learners who least
need it. This argues for putting pattern notes, worked reasoning and review into the flow rather
than behind a hint button — the same asymmetry that makes
[Incidental Vocabulary Exposure Limited](incidental-vocabulary-exposure-limited.md) [-M] a caution
against relying on learners to pick things up unaided.

**Population.** Migrant adolescents learning the majority language in-country: a high-stakes
immersion context, not foreign-language study abroad. Self-report, cross-sectional, correlational.
