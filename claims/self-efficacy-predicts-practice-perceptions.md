# High-academic-self-efficacy students rate some individual practices as causing less anxiety and rate some practices as contributing more to learning

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Academic self-efficacy significantly predicted lower anxiety ratings for lecturing, personal response system alone, and in-class quiz alone (ß = −0.20 to −0.27), practices worked on independently. [→ Hood 2021](#hood-2021)
`q3 i?` Academic self-efficacy positively predicted ratings of personal response system alone, in-class quiz alone, in-class quiz in a group, and cold calling for contribution to learning, with modest effect sizes. [→ Hood 2021 (2)](#hood-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Hood 2021

Hood, S., Barrickman, N., Djerdjian, N., Farr, M., Magner, S., Roychowdhury, H., Gerrits, R., Lawford, H., Ott, B., Ross, K., Paige, O., Stowe, S., Jensen, M., & Hull, K. (2021). “I Like and Prefer to Work Alone”: Social Anxiety, Academic Self-Efficacy, and Students’ Perceptions of Active Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-12-0271

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear regressions from the same beginning-of-term survey data with GPA covariate. Table 4 reports ß = −0.20 (lecturing), −0.27 (PRS alone), −0.23 (in-class quiz alone); the authors note three of these four practices involve working independently.

> "Within the same regression models, we found that academic self-efficacy significantly predicted anxiety ratings of some instructional practices, such that high-efficacy students rated these practices as causing less anxiety. However, these effect sizes were modest."

### Hood 2021 (2)

Hood, S., Barrickman, N., Djerdjian, N., Farr, M., Magner, S., Roychowdhury, H., Gerrits, R., Lawford, H., Ott, B., Ross, K., Paige, O., Stowe, S., Jensen, M., & Hull, K. (2021). “I Like and Prefer to Work Alone”: Social Anxiety, Academic Self-Efficacy, and Students’ Perceptions of Active Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-12-0271

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regressions predicting beginning-of-term contribution-to-learning ratings with GPA covariate (Table 5). The authors note three of these four practices involved working independently on an activity that would receive feedback in class.

> "In contrast, academic self-efficacy positively predicted ratings of some instructional practices for their contribution to learning, specifically personal response system alone, in-class quiz alone, in-class quiz in a group, and cold calling. Effect sizes were modest, however."
