# A co-tapping partner reduces synchronization variability significantly more at 150 bpm metronome (near children's SMT) than in music or slow bimanual tasks

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i0` negligible

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` The reduction in synchronization variability from solo to joint tapping was significantly more pronounced in the 150 bpm metronome condition than in the music and 50 bpm bimanual conditions. [→ Yunhee Kim 2026](#yunhee-kim-2026)

## Evidence

### Yunhee Kim 2026

Yunhee Kim, Doyeon Chae, Jaehwan Jang, Kyeong Taek Oh, Byeong-Sun Park & Min-gu Kim. (2026). Does tapping in sync reflect cognitive control? Links between sensorimotor synchronization, social context, and executive function in early childhood. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00443-5

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Linear mixed-effects model (REML) on log-transformed DTW scores of 140 preschoolers tapping solo versus with an experimenter. The interaction was significant ("F (1, 1111) = 6.15, p < .05, ηp2 = .006"), with the social benefit "more pronounced in the Metronome condition".

> "A significant Task Type × Social Context interaction effect was found, F (1, 1111) = 6.15, p < .05, ηp2 = .006. Specifically, the reduction in DTW when moving from the Solo to the Joint-Tapping condition was more pronounced in the Metronome condition"
