# Students reported lecture recordings more valuable for conceptual material than for sections that are just maths and facts

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students in post-exam group discussions reported that recordings were notably more useful for some material than others, with printed notes preferred for fact-heavy sections and recordings most valuable for conceptual material. [→ Norman Gray 2013](#norman-gray-2013)

## Evidence

### Norman Gray 2013

Norman Gray, Nicolas Labrosse, Sarah Honeychurch, Steve Draper, Niall Barr (2013). Tagging and Linking Lecture Audio Recordings: Goals and Practice. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1311.1725](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1311.1725)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Small-group discussions with students shortly after the corresponding exams: students 'stressed that the recordings were in practice notably more useful for some material than for others'; printed notes or slides were preferred for fact-heavy sections, recordings for conceptual material.

> "A course, or a section, which was "just maths and facts" (to quote one student) might be more effectively revised using printed notes or slides, rather than a recorded oral explanation"
