## Evidence

### Xining Wang 2023

Xining Wang, Gareth W. Young, Adéla Plechatá, Conor Mc Guckin, Guido Makransky. (2023). Utilizing virtual reality to assist social competence education and social support for children from under-represented backgrounds. Computers & Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2023.104815

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Mixed ANOVA (Study 2) of 50 middle school children randomized to VR (n = 10), Lego (n = 10), or traditional classroom (n = 30); the MSCS total interaction had ηp² = .240, described as a "significant interaction effect over time by group (VR, Lego, and TC)".

> "Moreover, there was also a significant interaction effect over time by group (VR, Lego, and TC) on general social competence (p = .002) and social knowledge (p = .004), verbal conversation skills (p = .004), and nonverbal sending skills (p = .003) (Table 4)."

### Xining Wang 2023 (2)

Xining Wang, Gareth W. Young, Adéla Plechatá, Conor Mc Guckin, Guido Makransky. (2023). Utilizing virtual reality to assist social competence education and social support for children from under-represented backgrounds. Computers & Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2023.104815

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post hoc Bonferroni-adjusted Tukey comparisons in Study 2 found "the VR group improved significantly more than the TC group in general social competence (p = .005)"; the VR group also exceeded the TC group in nonverbal sending skills (p = .048).

> "The Post hoc Tukey test results showed that the VR group improved significantly more than the TC group in general social competence (p = .005)."
