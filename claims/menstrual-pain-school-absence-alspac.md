## Evidence

### Sawyer 2025

Sawyer, G., Fraser, A., Lawlor, D. A., Sharp, G. C. & Howe, L. D. (2025). Associations of adolescent menstrual symptoms with school absences and educational attainment: analysis of a prospective cohort study. npj Science of Learning 10:54. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00338-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Confounder-adjusted linear regression of percentage absence on menstrual pain in ALSPAC (N = 2698), using linked National Pupil Database absence data. No standardised effect size is printed.

> "Participants reporting severe menstrual pain had, on average, 12.8% (95% CI: 6.1, 20.0) more time absent from school (1.2 days more)."

### Sawyer 2025 (2)

Sawyer, G., Fraser, A., Lawlor, D. A., Sharp, G. C. & Howe, L. D. (2025). Associations of adolescent menstrual symptoms with school absences and educational attainment: analysis of a prospective cohort study. npj Science of Learning 10:54. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00338-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression for persistent absence (10% or more) in the same cohort, shown in Fig. 1. Unadjusted analyses supported the same conclusions.

> "menstrual pain (OR 1.42; 95% CI: 1.39, 1.46: absolute risk difference 7.2%) were associated with an increased likelihood of being persistently absent"
