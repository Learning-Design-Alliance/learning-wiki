# Personalized video feedback produces higher BCLS skills scores one week and one month after training than in-person feedback alone

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` One week and one month after training, the mean BCLS skills scores of nursing students receiving personalized video feedback were significantly higher than those of the control group (p < 0.0001 at both intervals). [→ Mohsenzadeh Habibeh 2025](#mohsenzadeh-habibeh-2025)

## Evidence

### Mohsenzadeh Habibeh 2025

Mohsenzadeh Habibeh, Farsi Zahra, Afaghi Effat. (2025). The effects of personalized video feedback of basic cardiac life support on knowledge and skills levels in undergraduate nursing students: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06935-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial of 40 incoming nursing students randomized to experimental or control groups after identical lecture and simulation training; the only difference was access to a recorded practice video with personalized feedback. Follow-up t-tests showed the experimental group higher at one week (t = -4.466) and one month (t = -4.982), with "the mean scores in the experimental group were higher than those in the control group."

> "The BCLS skills mean scores before (p = 0.715) and immediately after training (p = 0.303) in the experimental and control groups further demonstrated no significant difference, while one week (p < 0.0001) and one month (p < 0.0001) after training, the mean scores in the experimental group were higher than those in the control group."
