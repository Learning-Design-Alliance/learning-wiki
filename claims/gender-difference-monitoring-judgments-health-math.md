## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Males reported higher monitoring judgments than females on the pretest problem and on two of three posttest problems when other predictors were in the model. [→ Scheibe 2022](#scheibe-2022)

## Evidence

### Scheibe 2022

Scheibe, D. A., Fitzsimmons, C. J., Mielicki, M. K., Taber, J. M., Sidney, P. G., Coifman, K., & Thompson, C. A. (2022). Confidence in COVID problem solving: What factors predict adults’ item-level metacognitive judgments on health-related math problems before and after an educational intervention? Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09300-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Regression on pretest monitoring judgments in the 1,177-participant sample: accuracy predicted judgments (β = 0.08, p = 0.009) but not once gender was added, while "males reported higher monitoring judgments than females, β = 0.19, t = 6.43, p < 0.001".
