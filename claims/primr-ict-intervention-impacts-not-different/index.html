Between-treatment comparison from the Kisumu County randomized controlled trial reported in the Results section. The article states the "similarity in pupil outcomes across the three treatment groups" prevented determining a best intervention; no effect size or test statistic for the between-treatment contrasts is printed in this paper.

"definitive answers about which ICT intervention was better for Kenya was impossible to determine from this experimental study given the similarity in pupil outcomes across the three treatment groups"

Related Claims¶

  • ICT investments did not improve learning outcomes significantly more than the basic non-ICT PRIMR education program — related
  • All three PRIMR ICT interventions (TAC tutor tablets, teacher tablets, and pupil e-readers) produced statistically significant literacy gains over the control group in grade 2 — related
  • The TAC tutor tablet intervention was by far the most cost-effective of the three PRIMR ICT interventions — related
  • Despite greater expense and improved access to reading materials, pupil e-readers produced smaller oral reading fluency gains than either tablet intervention — related
  • Frequency of TAC tutors' classroom visits was positively associated with learning outcomes, though the relationship is noncausal — related
  • The PRIMR literacy intervention had a positive impact on Kenyan pupils' literacy outcomes, including letter sound identification fluency, oral reading fluency, reading comprehension, and the percentage of pupils reading at the Ministry of Education benchmark, though not on all combinations of language, grade, and school type. — related
