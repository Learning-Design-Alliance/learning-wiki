## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across 11 participants, mean compression depth (54.49 mm) and release (4.74 mm) matched CPR guidelines while mean rate was 121.59 compressions/minute, slightly above the guideline range. [→ Di Mitri Daniele 2019](#di-mitri-daniele-2019)

## Evidence

### Di Mitri Daniele 2019

Di Mitri Daniele, Schneider Jan, Specht Marcus, Drachsler Hendrik. (2019). Detecting Mistakes in CPR Training with Multimodal Data and Neural Networks. Sensors (Basel, Switzerland). https://doi.org/10.3390/s19143099

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Descriptive annotation data from the SimPad SkillsReporter across the 11 participants (Table 5); the rate interpretation is the authors' own reading of the means rather than a tested comparison, and rate is a descriptive statistic, not an effect size.

> "While the mean value for compression depth 54.49 mm and for compression release 4.74 mm matches with the CPR guidelines in Table 1, the mean compression rate was 121.59 compression/minute, which is slightly above the guideline’s range. For this reason, the training mistake which participants seemed to make most often was to compress the chest too fast."
