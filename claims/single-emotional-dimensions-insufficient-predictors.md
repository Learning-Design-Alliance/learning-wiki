# Neither negative nor positive emotional functioning alone was statistically powerful enough to explain achievement scores independently of the other

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Alone, negative emotional functioning explained 13.2% and positive emotional functioning 18.2% of achievement-score variance, but neither single factor was statistically powerful enough independently; both were needed to predict best AA scores. [→ Bahmani Dena Sadeghi 2018](#bahmani-dena-sadeghi-2018)

## Evidence

### Bahmani Dena Sadeghi 2018

Bahmani Dena Sadeghi, Faraji Parviz, Faraji Robab, Lang Undine E., Holsboer-Trachsler Edith, Brand Serge. (2018). Is emotional functioning related to academic achievement among university students? Results from a cross-sectional Iranian sample. Brazilian Journal of Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1590/1516-4446-2017-2434

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression results reported in Results and Table 2: negative emotional functioning alone R2 = 0.132, positive emotional functioning alone R2 = 0.182, versus R2 = 0.314 for the combined model, indicating both dimensions were needed to predict best AA scores.

> "the two predictors (negative and positive emotional functioning), together, explained 31.4% of the variance in AA scores, while the single factors (negative emotional functioning: 13.2%; positive emotional function: 18.2%) were not statistically powerful enough to explain AA scores independently from each other."
