# Logistic regression yields propensity scores separating 256 low effect size studies (d̄=1.345) from 144 high effect size studies (d̄=7.284) with overlapping distributions suitable for matching

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Propensity score analysis classified 400 studies into a low effect size group of 256 studies (d̄=1.345, SD=0.960) and a high effect size group of 144 studies (d̄=7.284, SD=0.277). [→ Itsarangkul Na Ayutthaya 2022](#itsarangkul-na-ayutthaya-2022)
`q2 i?` Propensity score distributions of both groups spanned the same 0.00–1.00 range across quartiles Q1–Q3, enabling matching. [→ Itsarangkul Na Ayutthaya 2022 (2)](#itsarangkul-na-ayutthaya-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Itsarangkul Na Ayutthaya 2022

Itsarangkul Na Ayutthaya, T., & Damrongpanit, S. (2022). A Meta-Analysis of Instructional Management Model for Students’ Creative Thinking Development: An Application of Propensity Score Matching. European Journal of Educational Research, 11(4), 2429-2444. https://doi.org/10.12973/eu-jer.11.4.2429

`q2 · i?`

Logistic regression propensity score analysis of 400 studies, grouped by the mean effect size d̄=3.483 as criterion. Table 2 prints the group means and standard deviations as quoted.

> "the propensity scores for the low effect size group consisted of 256 subjects (d̅=1.345, SD=0.960), and the propensity scores for the high effect group had 144 subjects (d̅=7.284, SD=0.277)."

### Itsarangkul Na Ayutthaya 2022 (2)

Itsarangkul Na Ayutthaya, T., & Damrongpanit, S. (2022). A Meta-Analysis of Instructional Management Model for Students’ Creative Thinking Development: An Application of Propensity Score Matching. European Journal of Educational Research, 11(4), 2429-2444. https://doi.org/10.12973/eu-jer.11.4.2429

`q2 · i?`

Comparison of propensity score distributions across quartile ranges Q1 (0.00–0.33), Q2 (0.33001–0.66), Q3 (0.66001–1.00), reported in the Conclusion; Figure 2 shows right-skewed (low group) and left-skewed (high group) distributions.

> "When comparing the dis tributions of propensity scores for both groups, there were the same distributions of 0.00 to 1.00 scores for both groups. Thus, the propensity score matching method can be applied;"
