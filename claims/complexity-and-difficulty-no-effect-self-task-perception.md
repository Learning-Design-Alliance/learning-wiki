# Task complexity and desirable difficulties do not significantly affect intrinsic interest or expectancies for success

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Neither factor nor their interaction significantly affected intrinsic interest or expectancies for success, contrary to hypotheses 3.1–3.6. [→ Ines Zeitlhofer 2026](#ines-zeitlhofer-2026)

## Evidence

### Ines Zeitlhofer 2026

Ines Zeitlhofer, Joerg Zumbach. (2026). Germane processes moderate the effectiveness of complex desirable difficulty tasks. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09807-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

The RQ3 MANOVA on post-intervention intrinsic interest and expectancies for success found "no significant multivariate main effect of complexity" or desirable difficulty, and no interaction; univariate tests were likewise non-significant. Equivalence was not tested.

> "The analysis revealed no significant multivariate main effect of complexity ( F (2, 126) = 1.15, p = .32, η² = 0.02), or of desirable difficulty ( F (2,126) = 0.22, p = .80, η² = 0.003). The interaction between complexity and desirable difficulty was also not significant at the multivariate level (F (2,126) = 1.26, p = .29, η² = 0.02)."
