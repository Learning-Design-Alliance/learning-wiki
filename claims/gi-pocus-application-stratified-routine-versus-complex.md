Factors Influencing ICU Nurses’ Proficiency in Point‐of‐Care Gastrointestinal Ultrasound: A National Cross‐Sectional Study. Journal of Nursing Management. https://doi.org/10.1155/jonm/3178958

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive survey results on clinical application scenarios reported for Figure 2 in the 2009-nurse study. The authors suggest nurses "predominantly use GI‐POCUS for routine enteral nutrition management rather than advanced diagnostic assessments"; percentages are descriptive with no effect size.

> "the most common application of GI‐POCUS was assessing gastric emptying or residual volume (61.3%), followed by guiding feeding tube placement (53.2%). Less frequent applications included screening for intestinal obstruction (22.1%) and evaluating intestinal mucosal perfusion and injury (14.2%)"

### Wang Gang 2026 (2)

Wang Gang, Liu Yingying, Wang Shengyun, Gao Silong, Lin Hui, Wu Qian, Huang Debin, Pan Yueshuai, Zhao Yuxiao, Wei Huimin, Wei Lili. (2026). Factors Influencing ICU Nurses’ Proficiency in Point‐of‐Care Gastrointestinal Ultrasound: A National Cross‐Sectional Study. Journal of Nursing Management. https://doi.org/10.1155/jonm/3178958

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive image-interpretation results reported for Figure 3 in the same survey. Basic anatomical recognition (51.4%) far exceeded pathological recognition, with blood-flow signals lowest at "intestinal wall blood flow signals (18.4%)"; no effect size is printed.

> "more than half of the nurses could accurately identify gastric transverse and longitudinal sections (51.4%). However, recognition rates for pathological findings were substantially lower: gas‐fluid levels (26.9%), intestinal wall thickening (23.2%), and intestinal wall blood flow signals (18.4%)."
