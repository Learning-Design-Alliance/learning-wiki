## Evidence

### Robinson SJA 2024

Robinson SJA, Ritchie AMA, Pacilli M, Nestel D, McLeod E, Nataraja RM. (2024). Simulation-Based Education of Health Workers in Low- and Middle-Income Countries: A Systematic Review. Glob Health Sci Pract. https://doi.org/10.9745/GHSP-D-24-00187

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Walker et al., a cluster-randomized trial in Kenya and Uganda where facilities were pair-matched and assigned intervention or control; the package added data-strengthening, quality improvement collaboratives, and a childbirth checklist to PRONTO simulation. The review prints "15.3% vs. 23.3%, P<.0001"; no effect size is given.

> "The findings demonstrated reduced neonatal mortality and stillbirth among the intervention facilities (15.3% vs. 23.3%, P<.0001)."

### Yousef Nadya 2022

Yousef Nadya, Moreau Romain, Soghier Lamia. (2022). Simulation in neonatal care: towards a change in traditional training? European Journal of Pediatrics 181:1429–1436. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00431-022-04373-3

`q4 · i2` · `review · r2`

The review reports a cluster-randomised facility-based trial (Walker et al.) of simulation training every 5–8 weeks covering gestational age assessment, kangaroo care, breastfeeding, and neonatal resuscitation, with the printed effect size "OR 0.66 (CI 0.54–0.81)" comparing intervention 15·3% against control 23.3%.

> "Use of simulation training for obstetric and neonatal management of preterm births (and infants > 1000 g birthweight) in a cluster randomized trial (PRONTO training) has been shown to decrease neonatal mortality and stillbirth rates in Uganda and Kenya (control (23.3%) vs intervention (15·3%) OR 0.66 (CI 0.54–0.81)"
