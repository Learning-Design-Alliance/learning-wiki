# Feedback quantity and timeliness varied across three clinical schools, with each student receiving between 4 – 5.4 feedback episodes for eight long cases

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across three clinical schools, each student received between 4 – 5.4 feedback episodes for his or her eight required long cases. [→ Bleasel J 2016](#bleasel-j-2016)
`q2 i?` Timeliness of feedback varied widely: 100 % of Clinical School A students received timely feedback, compared with 68 % at school B and 46 % at school C. [→ Bleasel J 2016 (2)](#bleasel-j-2016-2)
`q2 i?` Students at clinical school C perceived they received less feedback that was delayed, reducing its value, while schools A and B did not differ in perceived adequacy. [→ Bleasel J 2016 (3)](#bleasel-j-2016-3)

## Evidence

### Bleasel J 2016

Bleasel J, Burgess A, Weeks R, Haq I. (2016). Feedback using an ePortfolio for medicine long cases: quality not quantity. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0801-3

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Descriptive analysis of ePortfolio upload data from three Sydney clinical schools (40–48 students each) found the average total number of FE was 191 per clinical school, and "Each student received between 4 – 5.4 FE for his or her eight cases."

> "There were between 40–48 students at each clinical school and the average total number of FE was 191 per clinical school. Each student received between 4 – 5.4 FE for his or her eight cases."

### Bleasel J 2016 (2)

Bleasel J, Burgess A, Weeks R, Haq I. (2016). Feedback using an ePortfolio for medicine long cases: quality not quantity. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0801-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Feedback-episode records assessed whether students received at least one episode per four-week medicine term at term completion: "In Clinical school A, 100 % of students had achieved this feedback whereas at clinical school B and C, the percentage of students who had received timely feedback reduced to 68 and 46 % respectively."

> "In Clinical school A, 100 % of students had achieved this feedback whereas at clinical school B and C, the percentage of students who had received timely feedback reduced to 68 and 46 % respectively."

### Bleasel J 2016 (3)

Bleasel J, Burgess A, Weeks R, Haq I. (2016). Feedback using an ePortfolio for medicine long cases: quality not quantity. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0801-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of student focus groups found "students at clinical school C perceived that they received less feedback, and that it was delayed, therefore reducing its value", while no difference in perceived adequacy emerged between schools A and B.

> "Of note, there was no difference in the perception of adequacy of feedback between two of clinical schools, A and B. However, students at clinical school C perceived that they received less feedback, and that it was delayed, therefore reducing its value."
