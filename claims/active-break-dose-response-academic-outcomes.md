# Dose-response evidence suggests 10-minute active breaks, moderate intensity, and twice-daily frequency favour academic-related outcomes

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` On-task behaviour significantly improved after the 10-min active break condition, and math scores were highest after the 10-min (ES = 0.24) and 20-min (ES = 0.27) conditions. [→ Watson Amanda 2017](#watson-amanda-2017)
`q2 i?` Selective attention improved most after a moderate-intensity active break, and twice-daily frequency produced significantly better selective attention scores than once-daily. [→ Watson Amanda 2017 (2)](#watson-amanda-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Watson Amanda 2017

Watson Amanda, Timperio Anna, Brown Helen, Best Keren, Hesketh Kylie D. (2017). Effect of classroom-based physical activity interventions on academic and physical activity outcomes: a systematic review and meta-analysis. The International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-017-0569-9

`q2 · i1`

Within-subject dose-comparison studies by Howie and colleagues comparing 5-, 10- and 20-min active breaks with a 10-min no-break condition. The review reports on-task behaviour "significantly improved after the 10-min active break condition" and math ES of 0.24 and 0.27 for the 10- and 20-min conditions.

> "Results showed on-task classroom behaviour significantly improved after the 10-min active break condition [39] and math scores were highest after the 10-min (ES = 0.24) and 20-min (ES = 0.27) active break conditions [40]."

### Watson Amanda 2017 (2)

Watson Amanda, Timperio Anna, Brown Helen, Best Keren, Hesketh Kylie D. (2017). Effect of classroom-based physical activity interventions on academic and physical activity outcomes: a systematic review and meta-analysis. The International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-017-0569-9

`q2 · i?`

Narrative synthesis of two dose studies: Janssen et al. compared no break, passive, moderate- and vigorous-intensity 15-min breaks, and Altenburg et al. compared once- versus twice-daily 20-min moderate breaks, with significantly better selective attention for the twice-daily frequency; no effect sizes printed for these contrasts.

> "Results showed that selective attention scores improved most after the moderate-intensity active break [41]."
