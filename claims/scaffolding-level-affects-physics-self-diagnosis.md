## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students given an instructor-outlined correct solution and a self-diagnosis rubric achieved higher physics self-diagnosis grades (mean 0.73) than the worked-example group (0.57) and the minimal-guidance group (0.24), with all groups differing significantly. [→ E. Cohen 2008](#e-cohen-2008)

## Evidence

### E. Cohen 2008

E. Cohen, A. Mason, C. Singh and E. Yerushalmi. (2008). Identifying Differences in Diagnostic Skills between Physics Students: Students' Self-Diagnostic Performance Given Alternative Scaffolding. Proceedings of the Phys. Ed. Res. Conference. [doi:10.1063/1.3021284](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3021284)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of three recitation groups (B: 31, C: 28, D: 25 students) in an introductory algebra-based course, using ANCOVA with quiz physics grade as covariate. Means were "between 0.24 - 0.73 on a 0-1 scale"; all pairwise differences significant.

> "The ANCOVA analysis shows that all groups differ from each other (p value < 0.05); B does the best, while D does the worst."
