## Evidence

### Curtis Northcutt 2026

Curtis Northcutt, Inaara Hasmani, Kevin Feng, Trevor Khangi, Andreas Plesner, Jonas Mueller. (2026). StudentBench: AI and human tutoring yield equivalent GRE learning gains. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28470 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.28470](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2609.28470)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA-adjusted comparison against the no-tutoring control in the same randomized study; the difference "was 6.15 percentage points ([4.08,8.21])", about 1.5–2 more correct answers out of 27. Percentages only, no standardized effect size printed.

> "After adjustment for pre-test score, the AI− control difference in learning gain was 6.86 percentage points in Quantitative (95% CI[4.02, 9.69]) and 5.47 in Verbal ([2.46, 8.47]). Gains with human tutoring were also higher than those in the control condition in both sections. Across both sections, the AI− control difference was 6.15 percentage points ([4.08,8.21])."
