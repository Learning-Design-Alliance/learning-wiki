## Evidence

### Alexia Katrimpouza 2017

Alexia Katrimpouza, Nikolaos Tselios and Maria-Christina Kasimati. (2017). Twitter adoption, students' perceptions, Big Five personality traits and learning outcome: Lessons learned from 3 case studies. Originally published 2015. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1703.04047](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1703.04047)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

One-group pre-posttest case studies in three course offerings compared participants' grades to non-participants' grades. In the first study participants' laboratory grade (mean=9.05/10) exceeded non-participants' (mean=8.12/10); the article reports the same pattern for final grades in all three studies. This comparison is descriptive; no effect size or test is printed.

> "The 19 students who participated in the first study  had a hi gher laboratory grade (mean=9.05/10, SD= .39 ) than the  17 students who did not participated (mean=8.12/10, SD=1.58)."

### Alexia Katrimpouza 2017 (2)

Alexia Katrimpouza, Nikolaos Tselios and Maria-Christina Kasimati. (2017). Twitter adoption, students' perceptions, Big Five personality traits and learning outcome: Lessons learned from 3 case studies. Originally published 2015. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1703.04047](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1703.04047)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis of Twitter activity against laboratory and final grades in the second study (N=80) and third study (N=46) found no significant relationship; equivalence was not tested. Table 3 reports r values between .043 and .229 with non-significant p values for these two studies.

> "However the statistical analysis did not show any significant relationship between Twitter activity and students' academic performance in the context of the course."
