## Evidence

### Yurdal Mustafa Onur & Kıncal Remzi Y 2025

Yurdal Mustafa Onur & Kıncal Remzi Y. (2025). Development of a digital peer-feedback tool for clinical skills training: a pilot study with second-year medical students. Scientific Reports 15, 40807. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-24650-z

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Semistructured individual interviews (n = 24, stopped at thematic saturation) and one focus group (n = 5), coded by two researchers using open, axial, and selective coding into 29 codes, 17 subthemes, and 6 themes; "Educational Benefit is recognized as the central concept to which other factors are related".

> "The analysis of individual interviews and focus group discussions revealed six main themes: (1) Educational benefits, (2) peer interaction, (3) moderation and trust, (4) new usage scenarios, (5) awareness and motivation, and (6) technical infrastructure"

### Yurdal Mustafa Onur & Kıncal Remzi Y 2025 (2)

Yurdal Mustafa Onur & Kıncal Remzi Y. (2025). Development of a digital peer-feedback tool for clinical skills training: a pilot study with second-year medical students. Scientific Reports 15, 40807. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-24650-z

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Participant quotation from the moderation-and-trust theme illustrating the perceived misinformation risk in peer ratings: "If a poorly executed procedure receives high ratings, students might assume it is correct". Positive-value quotes in the same themes included self-review to detect and correct errors and watching upper-year students' videos.
