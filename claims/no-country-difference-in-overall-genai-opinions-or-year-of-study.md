# Canadian and Korean students did not differ significantly in overall opinions about GenAI's impact on academic integrity and learning quality, and academic progress did not affect scenario judgments

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Mann–Whitney U tests showed no statistically significant difference between Canadian and Korean students on overall-opinion prompts about GenAI's negative impact on academic integrity or learning quality. [→ Brian Harrington 2025](#brian-harrington-2025)
`q2 i?` A two-way ANOVA found year of study was not a significant factor for either judgment outcome, with very little country-by-year interaction (p > 0.99 in both categories). [→ Brian Harrington 2025 (2)](#brian-harrington-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Brian Harrington 2025

Brian Harrington, Irina Zlotnikova, Gayathri Nadarajan, and Samuel Ekundayo. (2025). Did Alice Do Wrong? Cross-Cultural Differences in Student Perceptions of Generative AI Use in University Computing Education. ACM Trans. Comput. Educ. 26, 1, Article 15. https://doi.org/10.1145/3776558

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Bias-assessment items administered after the scenario prompts showed very similar distributions between groups; no statistically significant country difference on either overall-opinion prompt (effect sizes 0.0085 and 0.0301, p = 0.88 and 0.59612). Equivalence was not tested.

> "As shown in Table4, Mann–Whitney U tests showed no statistically significant difference in responses between the groups for either prompt."

### Brian Harrington 2025 (2)

Brian Harrington, Irina Zlotnikova, Gayathri Nadarajan, and Samuel Ekundayo. (2025). Did Alice Do Wrong? Cross-Cultural Differences in Student Perceptions of Generative AI Use in University Computing Education. ACM Trans. Comput. Educ. 26, 1, Article 15. https://doi.org/10.1145/3776558

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Two-way ANOVA with factors Country and Year of Study across the full dataset ruled out academic progress as an explanation for the cross-country differences; Korean students were mostly in third and fourth year while Canadian students were mostly in their first two years.

> "AsshowninTable3,theacademicprogresswasnotasignificantfactorforeitherjudgmentoutcome (“Acted unethically” p = 0.4891 or “Against the rules” p = 0.4406), with very little interaction effect (p > 0.99 in both categories)."
