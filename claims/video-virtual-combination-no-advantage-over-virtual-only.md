# Combining a video experiment with a virtual experiment does not foster conceptual understanding more than a virtual experiment alone during remote schooling

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Hypothesis H2 was not supported: combinations of virtual and video experiments did not have a stronger effect on conceptual understanding than learning with a virtual experiment only. [→ Flegr 2023](#flegr-2023)

## Evidence

### Flegr 2023

Flegr, S., Kuhn, J., & Scheiter, K. (2023). How to foster STEM learning during Covid-19 remote schooling: Combining virtual and video experiments. Learning and Instruction, 86, 101778. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2023.101778

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Direct test of H2 in the randomized three-condition experiment (N = 154): an ANCOVA on post-test scores controlling for pre-test scores found "no effect of condition in the post-test when controlling for the students' pre-test scores". The mixed ANOVA interaction was likewise non-significant.

> "The ANCOVA revealed no effect of condition in the post-test when controlling for the students' pre-test scores, F(2, 150) = 1.45; MSE = 483.43; p = .24; partial η2 = 0.02 (small effect)."
