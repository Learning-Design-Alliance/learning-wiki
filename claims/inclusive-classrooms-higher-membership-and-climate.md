## Evidence

### Strain 2017

Phillip S. Strain. (2017). Four-Year Follow-Up of Children in the LEAP Randomized Trial: Some Planned and Accidental Findings. Topics in Early Childhood Special Education, 37(2), 121–126. https://doi.org/10.1177/0271121417711531

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Classroom quality observations by observers trained to 80%+ agreement with a gold standard found fully inclusive classes averaged a membership score of 4.7 versus 1.9 in less inclusive classes, "significant (t[90] = 2.66, p < .01)". The author notes inclusive academic performance occurred largely without isolated instruction.

> "In fully inclusive classes, the mean “membership” score was 4.7, with a range of 3 to 5. By contrast, the mean “membership” score in less than fully inclusive classes was 1.9 with a range of 1 to 4. Using a two-tailed t test, these mean differences are significant (t[90] = 2.66, p < .01)."

### Strain 2017 (2)

Phillip S. Strain. (2017). Four-Year Follow-Up of Children in the LEAP Randomized Trial: Some Planned and Accidental Findings. Topics in Early Childhood Special Education, 37(2), 121–126. https://doi.org/10.1177/0271121417711531

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same observational quality measure's 5-point climate rating showed fully inclusive settings averaging 4.3 versus 3.1 for autism classes, "t(90) = 2.01, p < .05". The author notes this contrasts with the expectation that children with autism need more structured, intensive segregated settings.

> "Their mean classroom climate rating was 4.3 with a range of 3 to 5. For “autism” classes, the mean was 3.1 and the range 2 to 4, t(90) = 2.01, p < .05."
