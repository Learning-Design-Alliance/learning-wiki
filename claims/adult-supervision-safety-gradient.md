# Students feel safest in classrooms and least safe in areas just outside the school, with safety tracking the level of adult supervision

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` CPS students report feeling safest in classrooms where a teacher is present, less safe in hallways and bathrooms with some supervision, and least safe in the area just outside the school with little adult supervision. [→ Matthew P. Steinberg 2011](#matthew-p-steinberg-2011)

## Evidence

### Matthew P. Steinberg 2011

Matthew P. Steinberg, Elaine Allensworth, David W. Johnson. (2011). Student and Teacher Safety in Chicago Public Schools: The Roles of Community Context and School Social Organization. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago Urban Education Institute. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/student-and-teacher-safety-chicago-public-schools-roles-community-context-and-school

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Survey of CPS students in grades 6-12 (spring 2009) across 310 elementary and 62 high schools asked about safety in four locations. The report finds a gradient matching adult supervision levels: "students feel least safe in areas outside of the school" and most safe in classrooms.

> "student safety appears related to the level of adult supervision, as students feel least safe in areas outside of the school, more safe in common areas such as hallways and bathrooms where there may be some supervision, and most safe in their classrooms where there is a teacher present"
