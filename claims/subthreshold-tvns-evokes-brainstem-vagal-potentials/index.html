Evidence¶

Llanos 2020¶

Llanos, F., McHaney, J. R., Schuerman, W. L., Yi, H. G., Leonard, M. K. and Chandrasekaran, B. (2020). Non-invasive peripheral nerve stimulation selectively enhances speech category learning in adults. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-020-0070-0

q3 · i? · causal · r2

EEG recorded during training from all stimulation participants, with pulse artifacts removed and responses averaged in a 15 ms window time-locked to pulse offset. Latencies of 2–15 ms were consistent with prior above-threshold tVNS work.

"we found three tVNS pulse-evoked brainstem components with peak magnitudes signi ﬁcantly different from the pre-pulse baseline magnitude: N1 ( M = −1.07 µV, t40 = −2.96, p = 0.0051), P1 ( M = 0.27 µV, t40 = 4.75, p < 0.001), and N2 ( M = −0.15 µV, t34 = −5.77, p < 0.01)."

Llanos 2020 (2)¶

Llanos, F., McHaney, J. R., Schuerman, W. L., Yi, H. G., Leonard, M. K. and Chandrasekaran, B. (2020). Non-invasive peripheral nerve stimulation selectively enhances speech category learning in adults. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-020-0070-0

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Correlational analysis of individual sensory thresholds against peak magnitudes found no significant linear correlations (e.g., N1: r = 0.051, p = 0.82); the intensity relationship instead followed a non-linear inverted-U pattern shown in Fig. 3a bottom.

"However, we found stronger N1 and P1 peaks for intermediate tVNS intensities (1 mA < intensity ≤ 2 mA), as compared to low (0.2 mA < intensity ≤ 1 mA) and high (2 mA < intensity ≤ 3 mA) intensities."
