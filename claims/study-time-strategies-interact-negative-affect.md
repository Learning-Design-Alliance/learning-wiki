## Evidence

### Theobald 2025

Theobald, M. (2025). Study longer or study effectively? Better study strategies can compensate for less study time and predict goal achievement and lower negative affect. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 95, 405–420. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12725

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical models with negative affect as outcome (Table 3, Model 3) on the same 231-student, k = 3411-observation diary dataset; only concentration and procrastination moderated the study time–affect relation, and planning, monitoring interactions were non-significant.

> "When concentration was high (1 SD above the personal mean), students' perceived study time was not related to negative affect (β = −.001, 95% CI [−.19, .18], p = .979). However, when concentration was low (1 SD below the personal mean), negative affect increased as a function of perceived study time (β = −.03, 95% CI [−.05, −.01], p = .039)."
