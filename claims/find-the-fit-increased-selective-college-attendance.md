# Find the Fit led about 3 percentage points more students to attend colleges that were at least very competitive immediately after high school

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students in Find the Fit projects were 3 percentage points more likely than those in regular UB advising projects to attend colleges that were at least very competitive in the first fall after graduation (about 16 vs. 13.1 percent), a statistically significant difference. [→ Linkow 2021](#linkow-2021)

## Evidence

### Linkow 2021

Linkow, T., Parsad, A., Martinez, A., & Miller, H. (2021). Study of Enhanced College Advising in Upward Bound: Impacts on Where and How Long Students Attend College (NCEE 2021-2022-002r). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

RCT selectivity outcome (Exhibit 8; 2,336 Find the Fit vs. 2,107 regular advising students) using FSA, NSC, and NCES-Barron's data. The report states the difference "meets the study's bar for statistical significance at the .05 level"; no standardized effect size is printed, so no magnitude is coded.

> "students in Find the Fit projects were 3 percentage points more likely than those in regular UB advising projects  to attend colleges that were at least very competitive in the first fall after their high school graduation ( about 16 percent vs. about 13.1 percent, respectively)"
