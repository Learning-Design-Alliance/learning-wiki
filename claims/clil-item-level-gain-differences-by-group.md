# Item-level gains differed by group: the experimental group improved significantly on items E and F while the control group did not, and vice versa for items A and C

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On paired item-level t-tests, the experimental group showed significant pre-post gains on items E and F where the control group's posttest was similar to its pretest, while the control group gained significantly on items A and C where the experimental group did not. ## Evidence

### Carolina Salamanca & Sara I Montoya 2018

Carolina Salamanca & Sara I Montoya. (2018). Using CLIL Approach to Improve English Language in a Colombian Higher Educational Institution. English Language Teaching, Vol. 11, No. 11. https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v11n11p19

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Paired-samples statistics by item (Table 7): experimental item E t -3.024 sig .005 and item F t -2.953 sig .006, versus control item E sig .580 and item F sig .769. The article adds that for items A and C the control group gained significantly while the experimental group was similar to its pretest.

> "For the experimental group, the mean of the posttest is significantly higher than the mean of the pretest in the items E and F, while for the control group, the average of the posttest is similar to the pretest."
