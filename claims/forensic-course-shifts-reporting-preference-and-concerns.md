# Residents’ preference for final forensic reports rose from pre-test to post-test after forensic medicine training (large effect size), while concerns about legal authorities and malpractice pressure fell from pre-test to post-test (small effect sizes), in one uncontrolled pre-post study

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Residents’ preference for preparing forensic reports as final rather than provisional reports significantly increased after training (p < 0.001, r = 0.563). [→ Özkök Alper 2025](#ozkok-alper-2025)
`q2 i1` Concerns about mistakes in front of legal authorities and malpractice pressure significantly decreased after training (p = 0.032 and p = 0.014), with small effect sizes (r < 0.3). [→ Özkök Alper 2025 (2)](#ozkok-alper-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Özkök Alper 2025

Özkök Alper, Saçal Seda, Şimşek Ümit, Terece Cem, Helvacı Özant, Akar Taner, Demirel Birol. (2025). Residents’ perspectives on forensic issues and the impact of postgraduate forensic medicine education on perceived competence and attitudes: a multidisciplinary pre- and post-test study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08259-4

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Wilcoxon signed-rank comparison of paired pre- and post-training responses (Table 3). Preference for final reports "significantly increased following the training (p < 0.001), with a large effect size (r = 0.563)". r = 0.563 is a large printed rank-biserial effect.

> "Residents’ preference for preparing forensic reports as final rather than provisional reports significantly increased following the training (p < 0.001), with a large effect size (r = 0.563)."

### Özkök Alper 2025 (2)

Özkök Alper, Saçal Seda, Şimşek Ümit, Terece Cem, Helvacı Özant, Akar Taner, Demirel Birol. (2025). Residents’ perspectives on forensic issues and the impact of postgraduate forensic medicine education on perceived competence and attitudes: a multidisciplinary pre- and post-test study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08259-4

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r1`

Wilcoxon signed-rank comparison of pre- and post-training concern items (Table 3). Concern and malpractice-pressure scores "significantly decreased after the training (p = 0.032 and p = 0.014, respectively)" with "small effect sizes (r < 0.3)".

> "the scores related to concerns about making mistakes in front of legal authorities and the feeling of pressure caused by the concept of malpractice significantly decreased after the training (p = 0.032 and p = 0.014, respectively); however, both changes showed small effect sizes (r < 0.3)."
