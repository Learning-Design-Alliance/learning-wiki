# In Study 2, the performance-task section showed significant pre/post improvement in total quantitative-reasoning scores while the No PT section did not

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Kruse 2013

Kruse, G. & Drews, D. (2013). Using Performance Tasks to Improve Quantitative Reasoning in an Introductory Mathematics Course. International Journal for the Scholarship of Teaching and Learning, 7(2), Article 19. https://doi.org/10.20429/ijsotl.2013.070219

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Study 2 quasi-experiment with 28 graded pre/post pairs in the PT section and 25 in the No PT section. Planned comparisons on total scores (range 0-21) showed "pre/post improvement in the PT section" with d = .796; the No PT section did not improve significantly.

> "Planned comparisons showed pre/post improvement in the PT section, F(1,51) = 15.568, p = .000, d = .796, but not in the No PT section, F(1,51) = 1.455, p = .233."

### Kruse 2013 (2)

Kruse, G. & Drews, D. (2013). Using Performance Tasks to Improve Quantitative Reasoning in an Introductory Mathematics Course. International Journal for the Scholarship of Teaching and Learning, 7(2), Article 19. https://doi.org/10.20429/ijsotl.2013.070219

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The primary Study 2 analysis used PT/No PT x pre/post two-way ANOVAs with repeated measures. The article reports the total-score interaction "approached significance" at p = .073, so the between-section difference in gains was marginal.

> "The interaction for "total scores" approached significance, F(1,51) = 3.360, p = .073."
