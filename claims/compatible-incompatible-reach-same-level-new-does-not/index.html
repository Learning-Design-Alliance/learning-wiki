Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Compatible and Incompatible groups performed comparably across learning, indicating that the basis of compatibility in implicit MSL is shared movement transitions rather than ordinal positions. — related
  • After three prior training sessions, implicit learning of sequences compatible in movement transitions (both Compatible and Incompatible groups) is faster than learning a completely new sequence in experiment 1. — related
  • In experiment 1, novel movement transitions were performed better in the Compatible and Incompatible groups than in the New group, while initially learned transitions were retained better in the Same group. — related
  • The facilitative learning effect in implicit MSL depends on the amount of previous training: after only one prior session, the Compatible group did not outperform the New group and no novel-transition integration advantage emerged. — related
  • In implicit motor sequence learning, sequence-specific performance shows a significant timepoint-by-group interaction in both a 4-day and a 2-day training design. — related
  • Switching from a trained implicit motor sequence to a new, compatible, or incompatible sequence significantly reduces sequence-specific performance compared with continuing the same sequence, in both experiments. — related
