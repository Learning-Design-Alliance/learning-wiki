# Domain-specific interference, attributed by the embedded-processes model to similarity-based interference, underpins multimedia learning's modality benefits

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Two visuo-spatial or two verbal operations interfere more strongly than a visuo-spatial and a verbal one, and the embedded-processes model attributes this to similarity-based interference. [→ Judith Schweppe & Ralf Rummer 2015](#judith-schweppe-ralf-rummer-2015)

### Judith Schweppe & Ralf Rummer 2015

Judith Schweppe & Ralf Rummer. (2015). Attention and Long-Term Memory in System and Process Theories of Working Memory: A Reply to Soemer's (2015) Comment on Schweppe and Rummer (2014). Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-015-9336-0

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument citing research inspired by the multicomponent model on domain-specific interference, as Soemer (2015) pointed out. The article attributes the similarity-based interpretation to Cowan (1999) and notes the modality distinction is not equivalent to Baddeley and Hitch's domain-specific distinction.

> "This finding, which the embedded-processes model attributes to similarity-based interference (Cowan 1999), can even be regarded as the basis of multimedia learning because it emphasizes the benefit due to combining pictorial and verbal information"
