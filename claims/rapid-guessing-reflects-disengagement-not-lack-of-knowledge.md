# Rapid guesses on MAP Growth reflect a lack of engagement rather than a lack of knowledge

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Score gains associated with lower retest rapid guessing support the assumption that rapid guesses reflect a lack of engagement rather than a lack of knowledge, since re-engagement should not improve performance on unknown content. [→ Wise 2019](#wise-2019)

## Evidence

### Wise 2019

Wise, S. (2019). The Relationship Between Test-Taking Disengagement and Performance on MAP Growth Retests. (The Collaborative for Student Growth at NWEA Working Paper). https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/the-relationship-between-test-taking-disengagement-and-performance-on-map-growth-retests/

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The article's interpretive takeaway from its same-day retest analysis: if rapid guessing indicated unknown answers, spending more time on items should not yield the magnitude of score improvements observed. The evidence is the counterfactual contrast between the two competing explanations, not a direct test.

> "The first is that the score gains associated with lower amounts of retest rapid guessing support our basic assumption that rapid guesses reflect a lack of engagement rather than a lack of knowledge. If they had been due to lack of knowledge, exhibiting less rapid guessing on the retest should not have resulted in improved test performance."
