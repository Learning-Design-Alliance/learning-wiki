# Stepped teaching models enhance acquisition of simple procedural skills but may have limited utility for complex skills

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Stepped approaches (e.g., George and Doto's five-step model, Peyton's four-step model) enhance acquisition of simple skills such as dental skills and suturing. [→ Burgess A 2020](#burgess-a-2020)
`q2 i?` For complex tasks such as simulated manual defibrillation, laryngeal mask airway insertion, and simulated gastric tube insertion, some studies report no difference in learning outcomes across two-, four-, or five-step approaches. [→ Burgess A 2020 (2)](#burgess-a-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Burgess A 2020

Burgess A, van Diggele C, Roberts C, Mellis C. (2020). Tips for teaching procedural skills. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02284-1

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review reporting second-hand studies: a five-step model taught a simple dental skill in one attempt, and Peyton's four-step model "enhanced medical students' aquisition of simple skills when learning suturing". Review attributes these to cited primary studies.

> "It has been reported that when using George and Doto’s (2001) five-step model to teach a simple dental skill, novices were able to perform the task after one attempt [10]. Similarly it has been reported that use of Peyton's [7] four-step model enhanced medical students' aquisition of simple skills when learning suturing [11]."

### Burgess A 2020 (2)

Burgess A, van Diggele C, Roberts C, Mellis C. (2020). Tips for teaching procedural skills. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02284-1

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review reports that for complex skills including defibrillation, laryngeal mask insertion and gastric tube insertion, "no difference in learning outcomes" was found across two-, four-, and five-step approaches in cited studies.

> "For example, some studies have reported no difference in learning outcomes when using a two step, four step or five step approach to teaching complex skills, such as simulated manual defibrillation [12], laryngeal mask airway insertion [13], and simulated gastric tub insertion [14]."
