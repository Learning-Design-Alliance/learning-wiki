### Mainwaring Elizabeth M. A. 2026

Mainwaring Elizabeth M. A., Guidozzi Nadia, James Jonnie P., Pantling Jai, Menon Nainika, Salem Riadh, Markar Sheraz R. (2026). Objective technical skills assessment in laparoscopic cholecystectomy: a systematic review of manual, kinematic, and AI-based tools. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-026-13309-z

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's narrative synthesis comparing the three assessment modalities reports that manual tools "are the most well-studied instruments, with the largest body of evidence across multiple independent cohorts"; kinematic and AI systems were predominantly single-centre developmental studies.

> "Manual-assessment tools are the most well-studied instruments, with the largest body of evidence across multiple independent cohorts."

### Mainwaring Elizabeth M. A. 2026 (2)

Mainwaring Elizabeth M. A., Guidozzi Nadia, James Jonnie P., Pantling Jai, Menon Nainika, Salem Riadh, Markar Sheraz R. (2026). Objective technical skills assessment in laparoscopic cholecystectomy: a systematic review of manual, kinematic, and AI-based tools. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-026-13309-z

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

In the manual-tools synthesis section, the review reports that relations-to-other-variables evidence rested mainly on correlations with trainee level, and "No study evaluated associations between assessment scores and patient outcomes". The 41 manual studies spanned global rating scales, procedural-based tools, error-based scales, and one hybrid instrument.

> "No study evaluated associations between assessment scores and patient outcomes, and consequential validity evidence was largely limited to speculation of potential positive effect on trainee progression."
