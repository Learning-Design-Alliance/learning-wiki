Evidence¶

Michael McGuire & Jenifer Larson-Hall 2017¶

Michael McGuire & Jenifer Larson-Hall. (2017). Teaching Formulaic Sequences in the Classroom: Effects on Spoken Fluency. TESL Canada Journal/Revue TESL du Canada, 34(3), 1–25. http://dx.doi.org/10.18806/tesl.v34i3.1271

q1 · i? · causal · r1

Narrative review of Boers et al. (2006) and its replication Stengers et al. (2010), attributed as the article reports them: noticing training raised attention to FS, but without immediate written models learners did not generate FS in speech. The review reports no effect sizes for this contrast.

"In sum, the two studies reviewed here seem to show that training language learners to notice FS in writing will help them pay attention to FS more than those who do not receive such training. However, such noticing will not result in actual increased use of FS in spontaneous conversation."
