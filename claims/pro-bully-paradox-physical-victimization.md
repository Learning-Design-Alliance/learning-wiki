## Evidence

### Herbert W. Marsh 2023

Herbert W. Marsh, Jiesi Guo, Philip D. Parker, Reinhard Pekrun, Geetanjali Basarkod, Theresa Dicke, et al. (2023). Peer Victimization: an Integrative Review and Cross-National Test of a Tripartite Model. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09765-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

The review reports the OECD's PISA-based finding, from a global victimization measure, that "students who were not frequently bullied were more likely to report stronger anti-bullying attitudes" — victims had weaker, not stronger, anti-bullying attitudes. No effect size is printed.

> "the OECD reported that “students who were not frequently bullied were more likely to report stronger anti-bullying attitudes” (OECD, 2019a, p. 46)."

### Herbert W. Marsh 2023 (2)

Herbert W. Marsh, Jiesi Guo, Philip D. Parker, Reinhard Pekrun, Geetanjali Basarkod, Theresa Dicke, et al. (2023). Peer Victimization: an Integrative Review and Cross-National Test of a Tripartite Model. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09765-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The review attributes to Marsh et al. (2011) that "physical victimization positively correlated with pro-bully attitudes and negatively with pro-victim attitudes", with the pattern "only evident for the physical component"; pro-bully attitudes were nearly uncorrelated with relational and verbal victimization.

> "physical victimization positively correlated with pro-bully attitudes and negatively with pro-victim attitudes. However, this pattern of relations was only evident for the physical component of victimization."
