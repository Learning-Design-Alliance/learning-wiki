# Dual enrollment programs have positive effects on completing high school, with an average improvement index of +7 percentile points

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Two RCTs of early college high schools found statistically significant positive effects on high school graduation rates, including 5-year graduation rates. [→ What Works Clearinghouse 2017](#what-works-clearinghouse-2017)

## Evidence

### What Works Clearinghouse 2017

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2017). Dual Enrollment Programs. WWC Intervention Report, Transition to College. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/671

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Systematic review synthesis of two randomized controlled trials without reservations (Berger et al. 2014; Edmunds et al. 2015) including 4,052 students. Table 1 reports an average improvement index of +7 percentile points (range +5 to +9) on high school graduation rates; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Thus, for the completing high school domain, two studies showed statistically positive effects. Dual enrollment programs have a positive effect on completing high school with a medium to large extent of evidence."
