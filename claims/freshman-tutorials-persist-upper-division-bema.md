# Upper-division students who had freshman Tutorials score about 15 points higher on the BEMA than comparable upper-division students without Tutorials

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Juniors who completed a Tutorial-based freshman E&M course scored significantly higher on the post-upper-division BEMA (71±2%) than bypass students in the same courses (p=.02), with an effect size of 0.4, and higher than the earlier control group (p<<.01, effect size exceeding 0.7). ## Evidence

### S. J. Pollock 2009

S. J. Pollock. (2009). A Longitudinal Study of Student Conceptual Understanding in Electricity and Magnetism. Physics Education Research Conference proceedings. https://www.compadre.org/PER/ [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0907.5597](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0907.5597)

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of upper-division BEMA post-scores across seven semesters of Phys 301/302, comparing Tutorial (N=67), bypass (N=29), and control (N=71) groups. Tutorial students averaged "71±2%" versus ~56±2% for non-Tutorial students; the printed effect size is 0.4 versus bypass and exceeds 0.7 versus control.

> "These students (bar on the right of Fig 1a) have an average BEMA score of 71±2%, statistically significantly higher than their non-Tutorial compatriots in the same courses, the "bypass" population (p=.02 for a 2-tailed t-test) and with the control group of all upper-division students from earlier terms (p<<.01)"
