## Evidence

### Schlett Christopher L 2010

Schlett Christopher L, Doll Hinnerk, Dahmen Janosch, Polacsek Ole, Federkeil Gero, Fischer Martin R, Bamberg Fabian, Butzlaff Martin. (2010). Job requirements compared to medical school education: differences between graduates from problem-based learning and conventional curricula. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-10-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Paired two-tailed t-tests of mean differences (Δ) between required and taught ratings for PBL graduates, illustrated in Figure 1. Positive mean differences indicate a surplus of teaching over job requirements.

> "significant surplus of medical school education (positive values) as compared to requirements at the day-to-day work were identified for the PBL-based curriculum in "Interdisciplinary thinking" (Δ +0.88, p < 0.001), "Independent learning/working" (Δ +0.57, p < 0.001), "Psycho social competence" (Δ +0.56, p = 0.003), "Teamwork" (Δ +0.39, p = 0.03), and "Problem-solving skills" (Δ +0.36, p = 0.02)"

### Schlett Christopher L 2010 (2)

Schlett Christopher L, Doll Hinnerk, Dahmen Janosch, Polacsek Ole, Federkeil Gero, Fischer Martin R, Bamberg Fabian, Butzlaff Martin. (2010). Job requirements compared to medical school education: differences between graduates from problem-based learning and conventional curricula. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-10-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same paired t-test analysis of deficit/surplus for PBL graduates; negative mean differences indicate a deficit in teaching compared to job requirements.

> "The ratings given by graduates of the PBL-based curriculum indicated a significant deficit (negative values) in the teaching of "Business competence" (Δ - 1.44, p < 0.001) and "Research competence" (Δ -1.23, p < 0.001)"
