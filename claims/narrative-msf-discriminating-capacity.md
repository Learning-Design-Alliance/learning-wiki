# Experienced supervisors can discriminate between trainees based on narrative MSF reports, with reports of concerning trainees containing significantly more negative and constructive comments

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Four experienced supervisors independently classified MSF reports as no concern or some concern with kappa values of 0.7–1, and classification was significantly associated with the number of negative comments (p<0.001). [→ Holm Ellen Astrid 2021](#holm-ellen-astrid-2021)
`q2 i?` Trainees judged as 'some concern' (n=8) received significantly more negative (11.75 vs 3.18) and constructive (7.88 vs 3.05) comments than those judged 'no concern' (n=40), with no significant difference in positive, specific or behavioural comments. [→ Holm Ellen Astrid 2021 (2)](#holm-ellen-astrid-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Holm Ellen Astrid 2021

Holm Ellen Astrid, Al-Bayati Shaymaa Jaafar Lafta, Barfod Toke Seierøe, Lembeck Maurice A, Pedersen Hanne, Ramberg Emilie, Klemmensen Åse Kathrine, Sorensen Jette Led. (2021). Feasibility, quality and validity of narrative multisource feedback in postgraduate training: a mixed-method study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2020-047019

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Classification study of all 48 MSF reports by four experienced supervisors; Table 3 prints pairwise kappa coefficients of 0.695–1.0 (all p<0.001) and Table 4 prints negative comments mean 3.18 (no concern) vs 11.75 (some concern), p<0.001.

> "There was a significant association between the judgements of the four authors and the number of negative comments found in the text analysis of the MSF reports (see table 4)."

### Holm Ellen Astrid 2021 (2)

Holm Ellen Astrid, Al-Bayati Shaymaa Jaafar Lafta, Barfod Toke Seierøe, Lembeck Maurice A, Pedersen Hanne, Ramberg Emilie, Klemmensen Åse Kathrine, Sorensen Jette Led. (2021). Feasibility, quality and validity of narrative multisource feedback in postgraduate training: a mixed-method study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2020-047019

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Table 4 comparison of the two judgement groups: negative comments p<0.001 and constructive comments p<0.001; positive comments p=0.06, specific p=0.10, behavioural p=0.08, number of respondents p=0.09.

> "Comparing the group of trainees judged by one of the authors to arise some concern to those judged to arise no concern showed that those awakening concern received significantly more negative and constructive comments whereas there was no significant differences in the number of positive comments, specific comments or comments targeting behaviour."
