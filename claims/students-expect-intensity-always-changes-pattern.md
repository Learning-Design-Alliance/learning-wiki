# Students expected that changing lamp intensity would always affect the interference pattern regardless of photon wavelength, reflecting a phenomenological primitive that changing input must change output

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Many students predicted that reducing lamp intensity from 100% to 50% would change the screen pattern even when photon wavelength was much larger than slit separation (where full interference should be observed regardless of intensity). [→ Ryan Sayer 2017](#ryan-sayer-2017)

## Evidence

### Ryan Sayer 2017

Ryan Sayer, Alexandru Maries, and Chandralekha Singh. (2017). Quantum interactive learning tutorial on the double-slit experiment to improve student understanding of quantum mechanics. Physical Review Physics Education Research 13, 010123. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.13.010123

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

From individual interviews where students predicted patterns for photon wavelength much larger than slit separation with intensity reduced from 100% to 50%. Students predicted different patterns in the two cases despite wavelength making intensity irrelevant.

> "many students expect that changing the intensity will affect the interference pattern regardless of the wavelength of the photons emitted by the lamp"
