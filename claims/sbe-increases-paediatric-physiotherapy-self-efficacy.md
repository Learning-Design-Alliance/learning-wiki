# In one uncontrolled pre-post study, undergraduate physiotherapy students' self-rated self-efficacy in paediatric assessment and management rose significantly from pre-test to post-test across all surveyed skills after simulation-based education

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2019 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Mean student self-efficacy increased significantly for all questions after each of three SBE scenarios (p < 0.001), with mean improvements of 0.73–0.97 on a 0–4 scale. [→ Hough J 2019](#hough-j-2019)
`q3 i?` Positive ranks outnumbered negative ranks for every question, with positive ranks ranging from 62.8–80.8% and negative ranks from 0 to 2.6% of respondents. [→ Hough J 2019 (2)](#hough-j-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Hough J 2019

Hough J, Levan D, Steele M, Kelly K, Dalton M. (2019). Simulation-based education improves student self-efficacy in physiotherapy assessment and management of paediatric patients. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1894-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Prospective observational pre-post study of 164 students in three SBE scenarios (musculoskeletal, cardiorespiratory, neurological), analysed with Wilcoxon Signed Ranks Tests. "Significant increases in mean student self-efficacy were recorded for all questions" with no effect size reported.

> "Significant increases in mean student self-efficacy were recorded for all questions in each scenario (p < 0.001) with improvements in mean self-efficacy scores ranging from 0.73–0.97 for all questions across all scenarios"

### Hough J 2019 (2)

Hough J, Levan D, Steele M, Kelly K, Dalton M. (2019). Simulation-based education improves student self-efficacy in physiotherapy assessment and management of paediatric patients. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1894-2

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Rank analysis of paired pre-post responses across all scenarios and questions; "positive ranks outnumbered participants who reported a decrease (negative ranks) or no change (ties)" in every question.

> "In every question, participants who reported an increase in self-efficacy post SBE (positive ranks) outnumbered participants who reported a decrease (negative ranks) or no change (ties)."
