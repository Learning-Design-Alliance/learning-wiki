# Adults' confidence judgments track answer accuracy: confidence is higher on correct than incorrect general-knowledge trials (metacognitive monitoring)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Participants rated their confidence higher on trials where they selected the correct answer than on trials where they selected an incorrect answer. [→ Sunae Kim 2025](#sunae-kim-2025)

## Evidence

### Sunae Kim 2025

Sunae Kim, Paul L Harris, Benjamin Néher. (2025). Curiosity can influence metacognitive processes. Metacognition and Learning (2025) 20:39. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09444-y

`q3 · i3`

Experiment 1 (65 Hungarian adults, 40 general-knowledge trials with two answer options, Qualtrics). Mixed-effects regression with participants as random effect showed higher confidence on correct trials, "F(1, 2591) = 273, p <.001" with β = 0.69; means were 73.7 (correct) vs 51.5 (incorrect).

> "Participants' confidence level was higher for correct than for incorrect answers F(1, 2591) = 273, p <.001 (b = 20.22, SE = 1.22, β = 0.69)."
