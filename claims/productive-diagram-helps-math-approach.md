# Among students using a primarily mathematical approach, those who also drew a productive diagram scored better than those who did not, in both quiz and midterm

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 causal `r1` · `q2`–`q3` · `i3` large
> **Currency** · newest study 2023 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` Students who primarily used the mathematical approach but also drew a productive diagram performed better than those who used math without a productive diagram (p = 0.002 for the quiz and p = 0.006 for the midterm exam). [→ Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2013](#alexandru-maries-and-chandralekha-singh-2013)

`q2 i3` Among students primarily using the mathematical approach, those who drew a productive diagram scored significantly better than those who did not, in both the quiz (p = 0.002, Cohen's d = 0.900) and the midterm (p = 0.006, Cohen's d = 0.697). [→ Chandralekha Singh and Alexandru Maries 2023](#chandralekha-singh-and-alexandru-maries-2023)

## Evidence

### Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2013

Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2013). A good diagram is valuable despite the choice of a mathematical approach to problem solving. 2013 PERC Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2013.inv.006

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of students using a primarily mathematical approach with and without a productive diagram: quiz averages 8.1 (N=45) vs 6.6 (N=24); midterm averages 8.8 (N=68) vs 7.3 (N=24). The article reports "p = 0.002 for the quiz and p = 0.006 for the midterm exam".

> "Both in the midterm exam and quiz, the students who primarily used mathematical approach to solve the problems but also drew a productive diagram, performed better than those who primarily used mathematical approach but did not draw a productive diagram (p = 0.002 for the quiz and p = 0.006 for the midterm exam)."

### Chandralekha Singh and Alexandru Maries 2023

Chandralekha Singh and Alexandru Maries. (2023). Helping students become proficient problem solvers Part II: An example from waves. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2304.06188](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2304.06188)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of quiz and midterm scores for mathematical-approach students with and without productive diagrams. The article states the values "suggest that the difference between the performance of these two groups of students is quite large in both the quiz and the midterm" (d = 0.900 quiz; d = 0.697 midterm).

> "students who primarily employed the mathematical approach but also drew a productive diagram performed better than students who chose the mathematical approach without drawing a productive diagram: p values/Cohen's d for comparing these two groups of students are, 0.002/0.900 and 0.006/0.697 in the quiz and the midterm, respectively"
