# Among students using a primarily mathematical approach, those who also drew a productive diagram scored better than those who did not, in both quiz and midterm

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who primarily used the mathematical approach but also drew a productive diagram performed better than those who used math without a productive diagram (p = 0.002 for the quiz and p = 0.006 for the midterm exam). [→ Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2013](#alexandru-maries-and-chandralekha-singh-2013)

## Evidence

### Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2013

Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2013). A good diagram is valuable despite the choice of a mathematical approach to problem solving. 2013 PERC Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2013.inv.006

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of students using a primarily mathematical approach with and without a productive diagram: quiz averages 8.1 (N=45) vs 6.6 (N=24); midterm averages 8.8 (N=68) vs 7.3 (N=24). The article reports "p = 0.002 for the quiz and p = 0.006 for the midterm exam".

> "Both in the midterm exam and quiz, the students who primarily used mathematical approach to solve the problems but also drew a productive diagram, performed better than those who primarily used mathematical approach but did not draw a productive diagram (p = 0.002 for the quiz and p = 0.006 for the midterm exam)."
