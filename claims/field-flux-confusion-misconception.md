## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Student understanding of symmetry and Gauss’s law of electricity. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07376](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07376)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Distractor analysis of introductory students' responses on Problem 12 of the multiple-choice test; interviews suggest students were confident because they believed Φ = |E|A is always true instead of the flux integral.

> "The most common diﬃculty with Problem 12 was assuming that both electric ﬂux and electric ﬁeld can be determined using Gauss’s law; 30% of the students chose option (c)."

### Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Student understanding of symmetry and Gauss’s law of electricity. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07376](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07376)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative analysis of written responses and interviews shows students interpreted zero net electric flux through a closed surface to imply zero electric field at all points of the surface, e.g., on Problems 13, 15, 19, 21, and 23.

> "our investigation shows that students often believed that because there was no charge enclosed inside the Gaussian surfaces that are exclusively contained in the hollow region, the electric ﬁeld must be zero everywhere inside the hollow region"
