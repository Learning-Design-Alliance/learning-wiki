## Evidence

### Ansari 2018

Ansari, A. (2018). The persistence of preschool effects from early childhood through adolescence. Journal of Educational Psychology, 110(7). https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000255

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Re-estimated propensity-score-matched path models compared full-day (20+ hours/week) and part-day (5–20 hours/week) preschool attendees with informal-care children. Both dosage groups outperformed informal care academically (8–22% of a SD) with no full-versus-part difference, while negative psychosocial associations were only true among children in full-day programs (8–33% of a SD vs informal care; 12–22% vs part-day).

> "the negative associations between preschool enrollment and children’s psychosocial functioning were only true among children in full-day programs, with effect sizes ranging from 8–33% of a SD when compared with children in informal care and 12–22% of a SD when compared with children in part-day programs."

### Ansari 2018 (2)

Ansari, A. (2018). The persistence of preschool effects from early childhood through adolescence. Journal of Educational Psychology, 110(7). https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000255

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

After propensity score matching children in public prekindergarten programs (n = 2,576) and center-based care (n = 6,631) against informal care, the re-estimated path models showed no evidence of differences between the two program types; no effect sizes were printed for this null contrast.

> "I found no evidence of differences across the two mutually exclusive groups of public prekindergarten programs and center-based care"
