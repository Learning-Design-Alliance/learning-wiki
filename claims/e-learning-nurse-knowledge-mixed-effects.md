## Evidence

### Rouleau G 2019

Rouleau G, Gagnon MP, Côté J, Payne-Gagnon J, Hudson E, Dubois CA, Bouix-Picasso J. (2019). Effects of E-Learning in a Continuing Education Context on Nursing Care: Systematic Review of Systematic Qualitative, Quantitative, and Mixed-Studies Reviews. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/15118

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Narrative synthesis of level 2 knowledge outcomes from 13 of 22 included SRs. The review reports that "nurses improved their knowledge with the help of e-learning interventions on many topics" including neurological function assessment and medication administration.

> "In 13 SRs out of 22 (59%) [1,3,10,37,53,40-45,49,52], nurses improved their knowledge with the help of e-learning interventions on many topics, including assessment of ability of neurological function [42], medication administration and calculation [45], physiology and chronic health evaluation [10]"

### Rouleau G 2019 (2)

Rouleau G, Gagnon MP, Côté J, Payne-Gagnon J, Hudson E, Dubois CA, Bouix-Picasso J. (2019). Effects of E-Learning in a Continuing Education Context on Nursing Care: Systematic Review of Systematic Qualitative, Quantitative, and Mixed-Studies Reviews. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/15118

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Same knowledge synthesis section. The review reports no effects in "7 SRs out of 22 (32%)", with nonsignificant differences in drug dose calculation knowledge and between face-to-face and videoconference formats; equivalence was not tested.

> "In 7 SRs out of 22 (32%) [10,36,53,40,46,49,52], no effects were reported on nurse knowledge. There were nonsignificant differences in knowledge scores on drug dose calculation between groups [46] and in learning effectiveness outcomes between the face-to-face versus videoconference formats [53,52]."
