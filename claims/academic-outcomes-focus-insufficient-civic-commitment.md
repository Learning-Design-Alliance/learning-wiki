# General academic supports and quality instruction are insufficient to foster civic commitment; supportive teachers and quality instruction were not statistically related to it

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Practices associated with better academic outcomes, including peer academic support, supportive teachers, and quality instruction, do not by themselves foster students' commitment to civic participation. [→ Susan E. Sporte and Joseph E. Kahne 2007](#susan-e-sporte-and-joseph-e-kahne-2007)

## Evidence

### Susan E. Sporte and Joseph E. Kahne 2007

Susan E. Sporte and Joseph E. Kahne. (2007). Educating for Democracy: Lessons from Chicago. Consortium on Chicago School Research Research Brief. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/educating-democracy-lessons-chicago

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the longitudinal Chicago survey analysis, measures of general school experience (belonging, instruction quality, academic supports) were compared with civic commitment. The study found "our measures of supportive teachers and quality instruction were not statistically re- lated to students’ civic commitments," and peer academic support had far less effect than service learning.

> "Being part of a community of peers who support academic achievement has nowhere near the effect of participating in service learning or being exposed to in-class civic learning opportunities; our measures of supportive teachers and quality instruction were not statistically re- lated to students’ civic commitments."
