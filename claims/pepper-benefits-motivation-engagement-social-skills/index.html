Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Technical difficulties were the most frequently reported implementation limitation, mentioned in ten of the thirteen studies — related
  • Pepper's classroom operation required frequent teacher supervision in over half of the studies, mainly due to voice recognition and technical failures — related
  • Pepper has been deployed in three predominant pedagogical roles: tutor/teaching assistant, learning partner or tutored agent, and social mediator in inclusive settings — related
  • Empirical research on the Pepper robot in formal education is recent, concentrated in primary education, and dominated by qualitative designs with small samples — related
  • In second-language teaching for pupils with special educational needs, Pepper was associated with higher participation than digital tablets — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • RALL studies report learning gains when social robots are used to teach vocabulary, reading skills, speaking skills, grammar, and sign language — related
  • Contrary to similar research, an asocial robot produced higher learning gains than a more social robot in a tutoring study — related
  • Children's vocabulary acquisition benefited from the robot tutor's instruction and knowledge demonstration — related
