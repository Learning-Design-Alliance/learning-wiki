# Invention activities moderate the effect of mastery-approach orientation on transfer: orientation strongly predicts transfer for tell-and-practice students (odds ×4.8 per unit) but barely for invention students (odds ×1.09)

## Evidence

### Belenky 2012

Daniel M. Belenky and Timothy J. Nokes-Malach. (2012). Motivation and Transfer: The Role of Mastery-Approach Goals in Preparation for Future Learning. The Journal of the Learning Sciences. https://doi.org/10.1080/10508406.2011.651232

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Binary logistic regression on the 52 worked-example participants with mastery-approach orientation, learning activity, and their interaction predicting transfer; overall model significant, χ2(3, N = 52) = 19.72, p < .05, 71% accuracy.

> "Of critical note is that the coef- ficient for the interaction term was also significant, B = −1.50, Exp (B) = 4.88, Wald's χ2(1, N = 52) = 4.45, p \< .05, indicating that the effect of mastery- approach orientation was different for the invention and tell-and-practice groups."

### Belenky 2012 (2)

Daniel M. Belenky and Timothy J. Nokes-Malach. (2012). Motivation and Transfer: The Role of Mastery-Approach Goals in Preparation for Future Learning. The Journal of the Learning Sciences. https://doi.org/10.1080/10508406.2011.651232

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Simple-slope contrast from the same regression: tell-and-practice students' odds were multiplied by almost 5 per unit of mastery-approach orientation, while invention students' odds rose only 9% per unit. Invention students transferred with about the same likelihood regardless of orientation.

> "For participants in the invention condition, each unit change in mastery-approach orientation resulted in the odds changing by a factor of only 1.09 (see Figure 10a). For those who invented, it is clear that the relationship with mastery-approach goals was less important, as every unit increase in mastery-approach goals only increased the odds of successfully transferring by 9%."
