Evidence¶

Hu 2024¶

Hu, Y., Elliot, A. J., Wouters, P., van der Schaaf, M., Kester, L., & Pekrun, R. (2024). Effects of peers' emotions on students' emotions, achievement goals, mental effort, and performance. Journal of Educational Psychology, 116(7), 1283–1299. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000895

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Main effect results of a preregistered randomized online experiment (N = 210) comparing enjoyment, neutral, and frustration peer-model videos, analyzed with RI-CLPMs and GORICA in both Analysis A and B; order-restricted hypothesis "ae > 0 > af" was supported for enjoyment and "ae < 0 < af" for frustration. No effect sizes were printed.

"students exposed to peers’ enjoyment reported higher enjoyment (ae > 0 > af) and lower frustration (ae < 0 < af) than those exposed to peers’ neutral state, followed by those exposed to peers’ frustration"

Hu 2024 (2)¶

Hu, Y., Elliot, A. J., Wouters, P., van der Schaaf, M., Kester, L., & Pekrun, R. (2024). Effects of peers' emotions on students' emotions, achievement goals, mental effort, and performance. Journal of Educational Psychology, 116(7), 1283–1299. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000895

q3 · i? · causal · r2

GORICA evaluation in the same randomized experiment found the order-restricted pattern "ae = 0 > af" for relaxation and "ae = 0 < af" for anger; the enjoyment and neutral groups did not differ. No effect sizes were printed.

"students exposed to peers’ enjoyment and those exposed to peers’ neutral state reported higher relaxation (ae = 0 > af) and lower anger (ae = 0 < af) than those exposed to peers’ frustration"
