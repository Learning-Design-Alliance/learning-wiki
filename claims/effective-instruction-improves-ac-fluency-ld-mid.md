## Evidence

### Agaliotis and Teli 2016

Agaliotis, I., & Teli, A. (2016). Teaching Arithmetic Combinations of Multiplication and Division to Students with Learning Disabilities or Mild Intellectual Disability: The Impact of Alternative Fact Grouping and the Role of Cognitive and Learning Factors. Journal of Education and Learning, 5(4). https://doi.org/10.5539/jel.v5n4p90

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Wilcoxon Signed Rank intra-group comparison of the control group (30 students with LD or MID) in the 12-lesson effective-instruction intervention, reporting significant pre-post fluency gains with "z = -4.713, p = .000" for multiplication and z = -4.541 for division; no effect size printed for this test.

> "Intra-group comparisons revealed that the control group presented a significant difference between initial and final fluency in the use of multiplication ACs (z = -4.713, p = .000) and division ACs (z = -4.541, p = .000)."

### Agaliotis and Teli 2016 (2)

Agaliotis, I., & Teli, A. (2016). Teaching Arithmetic Combinations of Multiplication and Division to Students with Learning Disabilities or Mild Intellectual Disability: The Impact of Alternative Fact Grouping and the Role of Cognitive and Learning Factors. Journal of Education and Learning, 5(4). https://doi.org/10.5539/jel.v5n4p90

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Wilcoxon Signed Rank intra-group comparison of the experimental group (23 students) receiving effective instruction plus alternative AC grouping; significant pre-post gains for multiplication (z = -3.989, p = .000) and division (z = -4.205, p = .000), with no effect size printed.

> "In the experimental group the differences between initial and final fluency were also statistically significant for multiplication ACs (z = -3.989, p = .000) and division ACs (z = -4.205, p = .000)"
