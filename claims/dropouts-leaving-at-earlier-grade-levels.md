# Dropouts in more recent CPS cohorts left school at earlier grade levels, with a growing share not going beyond ninth grade

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among students dropping out by age 16, the share that did not go beyond ninth grade rose from 54 percent in the 1991 cohort to 81 percent in the 1997 cohort. [→ Elaine Allensworth and John Q. Easton 2001](#elaine-allensworth-and-john-q-easton-2001)

## Evidence

### Elaine Allensworth and John Q. Easton 2001

Elaine Allensworth and John Q. Easton. (2001). Calculating a Cohort Dropout Rate for the Chicago Public Schools: A Technical Research Report. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/calculating-cohort-dropout-rate-chicago-public-schools

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of highest grade attained among dropouts, shown in Figure 2 for dropouts by age 16 and by age 19 across cohorts. The printed percentages are descriptive; no test statistic is reported.

> "Fifty-four percent of the students that dropped out by age 16 in the 1991 cohort did not go beyond ninth grade. This increased with subsequent cohorts of students so that 81 percent of the 16-year-old dropouts in the 1997 cohort had not gone beyond ninth grade."

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive comparison of last grade attained among 19-year-old dropouts in the 1991 versus 1994 cohorts, from the Figure 2 analysis. Percentages are descriptive with no inferential test.

> "Thirty percent of the dropouts from the 1991 cohort had not yet finished ninth grade, while 40 percent of the dropouts from the 1994 cohort never finished ninth grade."
