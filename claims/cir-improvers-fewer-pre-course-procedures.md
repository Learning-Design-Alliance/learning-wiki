# Trainees whose caecal intubation rate improved after the course had performed significantly fewer pre-course procedures (median 98 vs 122, P = 0.006) and had a lower pre-course CIR (median 36% vs 56%, P < 0.001)

`q3 i?` Improvement in post-course CIR was associated with fewer pre-course procedures and lower pre-course CIR; improvement did not differ significantly by trainee specialty (χ2: P = 0.564). [→ Siau K 2020](#siau-k-2020)

## Evidence

### Siau K 2020

Siau K, Hodson J, Anderson JT, Valori R, Smith G, Hagan P, Iacucci M, Dunckley P. (2020). Impact of a national basic skills in colonoscopy course on trainee performance: An interrupted time series analysis. World Journal of Gastroenterology. https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v26.i23.3283

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary analysis comparing trainees with versus without CIR improvement between the 25 procedures before and after the course, using Mann-Whitney U tests. Mean CIR increase was 9.3 pp (SD 18.0), with improvement in 64% of trainees (n = 236). No effect size printed.

> "Trainees with an improvement in post-course CIR had performed significantly fewer procedures prior to the course (median: 98 vs 122, P = 0.006) and, as such, had a significantly lower pre-course CIR (median: 36% vs 56%, P < 0.001)."
