# In the Rashotte, MacPhee, and Torgesen (2001) RCT, SpellRead™ produced statistically significant positive effects on alphabetics and reading fluency for fifth- and sixth-grade struggling readers after eight weeks

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2`–`i3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In a randomized trial with 33 fifth- and sixth-grade students, the authors reported statistically significant positive effects on seven of eight alphabetics outcomes and on both reading fluency outcomes, with domain-average effect sizes of 1.03 (alphabetics) and 0.65 (fluency). [→ rashotte-findings](#rashotte-findings)
`q3 i2` In the same trial, the average comprehension effect size across two outcomes was 0.54, which the WWC characterized as a substantively important positive effect although it could not confirm statistical significance. [→ rashotte-comprehension](#rashotte-comprehension)

## Evidence

### rashotte-findings

U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, What Works Clearinghouse. (2013, January). Adolescent Literacy intervention report: SpellRead™. http://whatworks.ed.gov

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Randomized trial in which 33 fifth- and sixth-grade students in Newfoundland were matched in pairs and randomly assigned to SpellRead™ or the regular literacy program, receiving 31–35 hours over eight weeks. The alphabetics domain average effect size was 1.03 and the fluency average was 0.65, both statistically significant per the WWC tables.

> "The authors reported statistically significant positive effects on fifth and sixth graders’ scores on seven of eight measured outcomes, the exception being the TOWRE Sight Word Efficiency subtest."

### rashotte-comprehension

U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, What Works Clearinghouse. (2013, January). Adolescent Literacy intervention report: SpellRead™. http://whatworks.ed.gov

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Same randomized trial, comprehension domain: GORT-3 Comprehension (effect size 0.64) and WDRB Passage Comprehension (0.43), with a domain average of 0.54 and improvement index +20. The WWC characterized this as a substantively important positive effect.

> "Although the WWC could not confirm the statistical significance of the findings, the average effect size across the two outcomes was large enough to be considered substantively important."
