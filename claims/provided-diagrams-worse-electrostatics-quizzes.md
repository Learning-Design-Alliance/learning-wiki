# Students provided with diagrams performed significantly worse than prompted and no-support students on two electrostatics quiz problems, with large effect sizes

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` On problem 1, the diagram-provided (DO) group scored significantly worse than the prompted (PO) group (p = 0.024, effect size 0.634) and the no-support (NS) group (p < 0.001, effect size 0.922). [→ Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2018](#alexandru-maries-and-chandralekha-singh-2018)
`q3 i3` On problem 2, the DO group scored significantly worse than both the PO and NS groups (p < 0.001 for both), with effect sizes of 1.073 and 0.949. [→ Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2018 (2)](#alexandru-maries-and-chandralekha-singh-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2018

Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Case of two electrostatics problems: Can providing a diagram adversely impact introductory physics students' problem solving performance? Physical Review Physics Education Research 14, 010114. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.14.010114

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Study 1 quiz comparison across three recitation interventions (PO, DO, NS) in a class of 111 students, scored with rubrics; Table III reports Scheffe p values and Cohen's d, including problem 1 DO-NS p < 0.001, effect size 0.922.

> "Table III shows that students who were provided diagrams (DO group) performed significantly worse than students in the other two groups."

### Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2018 (2)

Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Case of two electrostatics problems: Can providing a diagram adversely impact introductory physics students' problem solving performance? Physical Review Physics Education Research 14, 010114. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.14.010114

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Same study 1 quiz data: on problem 2 the DO group averaged 6.4 out of 10 versus 9.0 (PO) and 8.6 (NS), with DO-PO effect size 1.073 and DO-NS 0.949, described as "quite large".

> "The effect sizes for comparing the performance of students in the DO group with the other groups are quite large, especially for problem 2 where the performance of students in the DO group was on average one standard deviation lower than the performance of students in the other groups."
