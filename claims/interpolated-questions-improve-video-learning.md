# Questions interpolated between video lecture segments improve subsequent test performance and reduce mind wandering

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students who answered questions interpolated between about 5-minute video lectures performed significantly better on later tests and reported less mind wandering than students who did arithmetic problems between videos. [→ Brame Cynthia J 2016](#brame-cynthia-j-2016)

## Evidence

### Brame Cynthia J 2016

Brame Cynthia J. (2016). Effective Educational Videos: Principles and Guidelines for Maximizing Student Learning from Video Content. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-03-0125

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The essay reports, citing Szpunar et al. (2013), a comparison of interpolated-question and arithmetic-control groups between video lectures: "the students in the interpolated question group performed significantly better". The interpolated group also took more notes and reported less anxiety.

> "finding that the students in the interpolated question group performed significantly better on subsequent tests of the material and reported less mind wandering (Szpunar et al., 2013)."
