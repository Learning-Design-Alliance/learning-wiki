Related Claims¶

  • Retrieval Practice Improves Transfer
  • Whether prompting students to construct explanations during retrieval adds benefit beyond initial free recall is mixed across studies — related
  • Retrieval practice effects on mediator-cued final tests have been positive, but Coppens et al. (2016) concluded the true effect may be only about 0.10 to 0.20 — related
  • The benefits of retrieval practice do not depend on an exact match between initial retrieval practice conditions and the final test format — related
  • Retrieval practice benefits extend to nonverbal visual materials, spatial map learning, and educational texts with different structures — related
  • The argument that retrieval practice effects do not occur with materials high in element interactivity is contested, and the chapter judges its research base not convincing — related
  • Transfer of test-enhanced learning is greatest across test formats, to application and inference questions, to medical diagnosis problems, and to mediator and related word cues — related
  • On the final cumulative test, JOLs with word stems and retrieval practice improve List-5 recall over restudy, while restudy shows a List-1 advantage — related
  • Retrieval practice improved literal premise recall but did not improve deductive inference transfer compared to restudy — reports the opposite
  • The testing effect has been demonstrated across a variety of mostly low-element-interactivity materials, but problem-solving tasks had received very little testing-effect research attention — related
  • No intervention benefits on source references in students' written conclusions (far transfer question) — related
  • Practice testing outperforms no-activity controls and restudy, with format and feedback moderators — related
  • Addressing did not enhance transfer performance in study 1 — related
  • Comprehension gains appeared on main-idea and inference items but not on word-meaning-guessing items — related
