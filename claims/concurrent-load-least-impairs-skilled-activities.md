`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review reports, citing Allport, Antonis & Reynolds (1972) and Shaffer (1975), that "concurrent memory tasks appear to cause the least impairment in highly skilled activities" such as piano playing, typing, and reading. It interprets this as consistent with its hypothesis that working memory in a specific skilled activity increases as one aspect of acquired skill.

> "Of particular interest to the LT-WM we propose is the observation that concurrent memory tasks appear to cause the least impairment in highly skilled activities such as piano playing (Allport, Antonis & Reynolds, 1972), typing (Shaffer, 1975), and reading"
