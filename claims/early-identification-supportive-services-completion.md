## Evidence

### Chou Calvin L. 2019

Chou Calvin L., Kalet Adina, Costa Manuel Joao, Cleland Jennifer, Winston Kalman. (2019). Guidelines: The dos, don’ts and don’t knows of remediation in medical education. Perspectives on Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40037-019-00544-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The guidelines report a UK medical school program of early identification at 4, 7, and 12 months: "more students participated in supportive services, compared with historical controls", and students engaging one-on-one remediation services were more likely to complete pre-clerkship studies successfully.

> "when early identification of struggling pre-clerkship students occurred at 4, 7, and 12 months after starting, more students participated in supportive services, compared with historical controls, and those engaging in one-on-one remediation services were more likely to successfully complete pre-clerkship studies"

### Bennion Layne D. 2018

Bennion Layne D., Durning Steven J., LaRochelle Jeffrey, Yoon Michelle, Schreiber-Gregory Deanna, Reamy Brian V., Torre Dario. (2018). Untying the Gordian knot: remediation problems in medical schools that need remediation. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1219-x

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The paper reports, citing Stegers-Jager and colleagues, a Dutch model with warnings at 4 and 7 months and academic probation at 12 months; more students used supportive services, and one-on-one remediation participants were "significantly more likely to satisfactorily complete their preclerkship studies". No effect size is printed.

> "They did find more students participating in supportive services under this model (than the previous process). They found those students participating in one-on-one remediation services were significantly more likely to satisfactorily complete their preclerkship studies."
