# Excessive study behaviour partially mediates the effect of academic self-esteem contingency on reduced self-efficacy, but the effect on cynicism is exclusively direct

## Subclaims
`q2 i0` The total effect of academic self-esteem contingency on reduced self-efficacy (β = 0.47) proceeds directly and via a small mediated path through excessive study behaviour (β = 0.09). [→ Brueckmann M 2025](#brueckmann-m-2025)
`q2 i1` The effect of academic self-esteem contingency on cynicism is exclusively direct (β = 0.16) and not mediated by excessive study behaviour. [→ Brueckmann M 2025 (2)](#brueckmann-m-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Brueckmann M 2025

Brueckmann M, Wild E, Wolff F. (2025). Planting the seed: how parental negative conditional regard boosts vulnerability to excessive study behaviour and burnout. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-025-03354-z

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Same serial mediation SEM of 624 students. The reduced self-efficacy pathway was "mediated by excessive study behaviour (β = 0.09, 95% CI [0.01, 0.18])" alongside a direct effect of β = 0.37.

> "For reduced self-efficacy, there was also a significant total effect based on academic self-esteem contingency (β = 0.47, 95% CI [0.37, 0.56]), which, on the one hand, proceeded directly (β = 0.37, 95% CI [0.23, 0.51]) and, on the other hand, was mediated by excessive study behaviour (β = 0.09, 95% CI [0.01, 0.18])."

### Brueckmann M 2025 (2)

Brueckmann M, Wild E, Wolff F. (2025). Planting the seed: how parental negative conditional regard boosts vulnerability to excessive study behaviour and burnout. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-025-03354-z

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

In the same SEM, the cynicism pathway showed no mediation through excessive study behaviour; Hypothesis 1 was only partially confirmed. The authors suggest cynicism may be a passive form of self-esteem protection.

> "The effect of academic self-esteem contingency on cynicism was exclusively direct (β = 0.16, 95% CI [0.04, 0.28]), that is, it was not mediated by excessive study behaviour."
