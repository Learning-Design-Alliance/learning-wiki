# Students overgeneralize time-independence of energy eigenfunctions to all observables and believe the wave function gets stuck in an eigenstate after measurement

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Many students believe that after measurement of any observable the system remains in the eigenstate forever unless an external perturbation is applied, overgeneralizing a property valid only for observables whose operators commute with the Hamiltonian. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016)

## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Helping Students Learn Quantum Mechanics for Quantum Computing. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06666](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06666)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Written tests and think-aloud interviews with advanced undergraduates and beginning graduate students found students believe the system "gets “stuck” in the eigen- state" after measurement of any observable, overgeneralizing from observables commuting with the Hamiltonian.

> "Many students believe that after the measurement of any observable, the system gets “stuck” in the eigen- state of the corresponding operator forever unless an ex- ternal perturbation is applied."

### Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Helping Students Learn Quantum Mechanics for Quantum Computing. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06666](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06666)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Written surveys and interviews found students conflate eigenfunctions of different observables; one student said energy, momentum and position eigenfunctions "are all eigenfunctions after all" and should be proportional. The authors attribute this to energy eigenfunctions being simply called “eigenfunctions” in courses.

> "Written tests and interviews suggest that many students do not understand the meaning of “an eigenfunction of an operator corresponding to a physical observable” and believe that the eigenfunctions of all observables are the same as the energy eigenfunctions."
