## Evidence

### Neel Haldolaarachchige and Kalani Hettiarachchilage 2022

Neel Haldolaarachchige and Kalani Hettiarachchilage. (2022). Comparison of student performance between virtual and in-person modalities of introductory calculus-based physics I. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2201.01809](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2201.01809)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cross-semester comparison of mid-exam pass rates in calculus-based physics I at Bergen Community College, same instructor and teaching style across semesters: the percentage of students above 70% was "about the same in F A20 (virtual) and F A19 (in-person)".

> "The percentage of students with above 70% of mid-exams (the bright blue color) is about the same in F A20 (virtual) and F A19 (in-person)."

### Neel Haldolaarachchige and Kalani Hettiarachchilage 2022 (2)

Neel Haldolaarachchige and Kalani Hettiarachchilage. (2022). Comparison of student performance between virtual and in-person modalities of introductory calculus-based physics I. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2201.01809](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2201.01809)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Final-exam analysis across the three semesters (FA19 in-person, FA20 and SP21 virtual-synchronous) using the same 3.00-hour closed-book final format: the percentage of students above 70% was "very similar in all three semesters".

> "It can be observed that the percentage of students above 70% score is very similar in all three semesters. This is a very good indication that the class modality has no effect on student learning."
