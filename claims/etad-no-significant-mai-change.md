# Eye-tracking augmented debriefing produced no statistically significant difference in resident metacognitive awareness change compared with traditional PEARLS debriefing

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a multisite RCT, the change in Metacognition Awareness Inventory score from baseline to after the second debrief did not differ significantly between the ETAD and control arms (adjusted mean difference 9.8 points higher in control, P = 0.14). [→ Braund H 2024](#braund-h-2024)

## Evidence

### Braund H 2024

Braund H, Hall AK, Caners K, Walker M, Dagnone D, Sherbino J, Sibbald M, Wang B, Howes D, Day AG, Wu W, Szulewski A. (2024). Evaluating the Value of Eye-Tracking Augmented Debriefing in Medical Simulation—A Pilot Randomized Controlled Trial. Simulation in Healthcare. https://doi.org/10.1097/SIH.0000000000000825

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial of 54 emergency medicine residents randomized with concealed allocation to ETAD or control; MAI change analyzed by linear regression controlling for site and baseline MAI. The adjusted difference of "9.8 (95% confidence interval, −3.2 to 22.8, P = 0.14)" favored control but was not statistically significant.

> "The adjusted mean difference in this improvement was 9.8 (95% confidence interval, −3.2 to 22.8, P = 0.14) higher in the control arm than the eye-tracking arm."
