## Evidence

### Glodowski 2025

Glodowski, K. R., & Hayashi, Y. (2025). Toward cross‐disciplinary translation of the testing effect: A systematic replication. Journal of Applied Behavior Analysis, 58(3), 653–663. https://doi.org/10.1002/jaba.70012

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Post hoc comparisons of self-reported studying behavior in the within-subject study; practicing quiz questions showed an omnibus effect of F = 7.58, p < .001, ηp2 = .3.68 (as printed), and "students practiced quiz questions significantly more frequently in the 5Q conditions" (Lec vs. 5Q p = .038, d = 0.87).

> "students practiced quiz questions significantly more frequently in the 5Q conditions than in the lecture and NQ conditions"

### Glodowski 2025 (2)

Glodowski, K. R., & Hayashi, Y. (2025). Toward cross‐disciplinary translation of the testing effect: A systematic replication. Journal of Applied Behavior Analysis, 58(3), 653–663. https://doi.org/10.1002/jaba.70012

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Participation, defined as asking at least one question during a class period, showed an omnibus effect (F = 3.62, p = .021, ηp2 = .206); post hoc Lec vs. 10Q p = .039, d = 0.82, showing "students participated more frequently in the 10Q condition than in the lecture condition".

> "students participated more frequently in the 10Q condition than in the lecture condition"

### Glodowski 2025 (3)

Glodowski, K. R., & Hayashi, Y. (2025). Toward cross‐disciplinary translation of the testing effect: A systematic replication. Journal of Applied Behavior Analysis, 58(3), 653–663. https://doi.org/10.1002/jaba.70012

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same within-subject study, attendance showed no omnibus effect (F = 2.47, p = .108) and post hoc comparisons of reading 10 min or more were non-significant; "No statistically significant differences in student attendance or reports of reading across conditions were observed". Equivalence was not tested.

> "No statistically significant differences in student attendance or reports of reading across conditions were observed"
