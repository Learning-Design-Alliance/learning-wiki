## Evidence

### Kyle M. Whitcomb 2023

Kyle M. Whitcomb, Alexandru Maries, and Chandralekha Singh. (2023). Progression in self-efficacy, interest, identity, sense of belonging, perceived recognition and effectiveness of peer interaction of physics majors and comparison with non-majors and Ph.D. students. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2304.06192](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2304.06192)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of matched pre/post survey responses from majors and non-majors in the same calculus-based first-year physics courses. The article reports "the difference is 1.03 in pre and 0.85 in post" for identity and 0.67/0.78 for interest, versus only 0.34/0.37 for self-efficacy.

> "In particular, for identity (Fig. 1 c), the difference is 1.03 in pre and 0.85 in post and for interest (Fig. 1 b), the difference is 0.67 in pre and 0.78 in post. However, majors and non-majors respond much more similarly in self -efficacy measures, with majors only 0.34 and 0.37 points higher on average in pre and post, respectively."
