## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students reported that generating problems encouraged in-depth discussion within their own teams (interrogation logic), whereas solving algebra-based problems prompted seeking specific answers from other groups. [→ Javier Pulgar 2021](#javier-pulgar-2021)

## Evidence

### Javier Pulgar 2021

Javier Pulgar, Alexis Spina, Carlos Ríos. (2021). Investigating how students collaborate to generate physics problems through structured tasks. arXiv. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010120](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010120)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview data from students S1, S3 and S4 on the nature of collaboration. The article attributes the within-group pattern to interrogation logic and reports that for algebra-based problems interactions took the form of asking "what equation did you use?" or "what did you get?".

> "According to students S1,S3 and S4, collaboration for generating a physics problem occurred mostly within the group, where they recognized that their solutions came up after collective decision-making, but without the need to reach out to members of other groups."
