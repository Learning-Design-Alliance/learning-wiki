## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Qualitative analysis of students' reflection logs showed that instructors gave clues rather than answers, students interacted more with peers and instructors, and students felt more enthusiastic about scenario simulations. [→ Huang Cheng-Yi 2020](#huang-cheng-yi-2020)

## Evidence

### Huang Cheng-Yi 2020

Huang Cheng-Yi, Wang Ya-huei. (2020). Toward an Integrative Nursing Curriculum: Combining Team-Based and Problem-Based Learning with Emergency-Care Scenario Simulation. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17124612

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Qualitative summarization of the students' reflection logs, used "to triangulate the scale results" in the same 18-week intervention. Students also reported being "excited about encountering the different scenarios" and having more opportunity to practice applying professional knowledge and skills in clinical cases.

> "the students stated that the instructors in the integrative course, instead of directly providing all the answers about the problem cases, led a discussion that promoted self-directed inquiry by giving clues and key points to help students engage in team-based discussions about the scenario cases."
