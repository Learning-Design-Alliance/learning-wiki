## Evidence

### Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr 2017

Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr. (2017). Eliciting explanations: Constraints on when self-explanation aids learning. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 24, 1501–1510. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1079-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Berthold et al. (2011), where college students studied a tax-law e-learning module with why-explanation prompts or unguided note taking. Why-prompts yielded more elaborations on domain principles but "decreased the number of calculations performed during learning."

> "However, the prompts also decreased the number of calculations performed during learning (Berthold et al.,
2011)."

### Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr 2017 (2)

Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr. (2017). Eliciting explanations: Constraints on when self-explanation aids learning. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 24, 1501–1510. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1079-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Große & Renkl (2006): prompts focused on solution procedures "reduced comprehension relative to studying the examples without generating explanations," illustrating the hidden cost of misdirected attention.

> "In contrast, prompts that focused attention on solution
procedures (e.g., BWhere do you see commonalities and
differences between the two solution methods? ^) reduced
comprehension relative to studying the examples without
generating explanations (Große & Renkl,2006)."

### Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr 2017 (3)

Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr. (2017). Eliciting explanations: Constraints on when self-explanation aids learning. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 24, 1501–1510. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1079-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Nokes et al. (2011), high-school students explaining worked physics examples with how- or why-prompts. How-prompts produced greater procedural transfer; the authors argued these prompts better matched the target content. Conceptual knowledge was not assessed.

> "The how-explanation prompts led to greater procedural
transfer than the why-explanation prompts (conceptual
knowledge was not assessed)."
