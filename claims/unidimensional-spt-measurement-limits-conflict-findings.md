## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Self-reported SPT propensity correlates moderately with mutually focused conflict resolution styles, but without measuring ability these correlations are difficult to interpret. [→ Gehlbach 2004](#gehlbach-2004)

## Evidence

### Gehlbach 2004

Gehlbach, Hunter. (2004). A New Perspective on Perspective Taking: A Multidimensional Approach to Conceptualizing an Aptitude. Educational Psychology Review 16 (3). https://doi.org/10.1023/B:EDPR.0000034021.12899.11

`q2 · i2` · `review · r2`

The review reports a study correlating self-reported propensity, measured with the Davis (1983) seven-item subscale (α > .70), with conflict resolution styles, finding "the compromising, r = .29, and integration styles, r = .39". The review notes self-report biases and the unmeasured ability dimension make such correlations hard to interpret.

> "Corcoran and Mallinckrodt (2000) found moderate relationships between self-reported perspective taking propensity (using the Davis, 1983 scale) and the two mutually focused styles of conflict resolution (i.e., with the compromising, r = .29,  and integration styles, r = .39, of conflict resolution)."
