## Evidence

### Yurdal Mustafa Onur & Kıncal Remzi Y 2025

Yurdal Mustafa Onur & Kıncal Remzi Y. (2025). Development of a digital peer-feedback tool for clinical skills training: a pilot study with second-year medical students. Scientific Reports 15, 40807. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-24650-z

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Online-form forced ranking of 10 attributes by the 31 pilot users, analyzed with the Friedman test; the article reports "Kendall’s W = 0.309, indicating moderate agreement in priority rankings across participants". Only a test statistic and W are printed; the article reports no standardized effect size in d/g/r/η² form.

> "The Friedman test showed a clear overall difference among the ten attributes, χ2(9) = 86.162, p = 9.56 × 10− 15, with Kendall’s W = 0.309, indicating moderate agreement in priority rankings across participants"

### Yurdal Mustafa Onur & Kıncal Remzi Y 2025 (2)

Yurdal Mustafa Onur & Kıncal Remzi Y. (2025). Development of a digital peer-feedback tool for clinical skills training: a pilot study with second-year medical students. Scientific Reports 15, 40807. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-24650-z

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Descriptive rank ordering from the same Friedman analysis; "Educationally beneficial" had the lowest mean rank (≈ 2.13; median 1), ahead of "Offering a different experience" (≈ 4.35) and "Digitally user-friendly" (≈ 4.84). Post-hoc Wilcoxon (Holm) tests showed it significantly more highly prioritized than each of the other nine attributes, with 18 of 45 pairwise comparisons significant; only p-values are printed.

> "“Educationally beneficial” had the lowest mean rank (≈ 2.13; median 1) and thus the highest priority, followed by “Offering a different experience” (≈ 4.35), “Digitally user-friendly” (≈ 4.84)"
