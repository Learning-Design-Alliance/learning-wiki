## Evidence

### Colin S. Wallace 2017

Colin S. Wallace, Timothy G. Chambers, Edward E. Prather. (2017). An item response theory evaluation of the Light and Spectroscopy Concept Inventory national data set. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1709.05255](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1709.05255)

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Class-level analysis plotting average Δθ versus ⟨g⟩ for the classes with at least 25 students in the national data set (Fig. 10, Table IV). The article reports "r = 0.99" and says this "supports the robustness" of the prior CTT results.

> "There is a large correlation between these two measures (r = 0.99), which supports the robustness of the results reported in Prather et al. [3], Rudolph et al. [4], and Schlingman et al. [5]."

### Colin S. Wallace 2017 (2)

Colin S. Wallace, Timothy G. Chambers, Edward E. Prather. (2017). An item response theory evaluation of the Light and Spectroscopy Concept Inventory national data set. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1709.05255](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1709.05255)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Plot of average Δθ versus IAS for classes with at least 25 students (Fig. 11). The article states the 25% threshold is "a necessary, though not suﬃcient, condition" and that simply making a class more interactive is not enough to maximize learning.

> "Fig. 11 also suggests that an IAS of 25% is a necessary, though not suﬃcient, condition to achieve average ∆θ> 1."
