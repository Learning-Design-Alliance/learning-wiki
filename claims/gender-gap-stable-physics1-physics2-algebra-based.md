# The gender gap in self-efficacy and interest remains stable from physics 1 to physics 2 in algebra-based courses, unlike calculus-based courses where it grows

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Post self-efficacy and post interest show no statistically significant differences in scores from physics 1 to physics 2, so the gap neither worsens nor improves across the year. [→ Sonja Cwik 2020](#sonja-cwik-2020)

## Evidence

### Sonja Cwik 2020

Sonja Cwik, Kyle Whitcomb, and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). How the learning environment predicts male and female students' motivational beliefs in algebra-based introductory physics courses. Physics Education Research Conference Proceedings. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.cwik](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.cwik)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Matched longitudinal survey data from the same students at the end of physics 1 and physics 2; t-tests comparing male and female scores across the two courses. The article reports "no statistically significant differences in the scores from physics 1 to physics 2"; equivalence was not tested.

> "we find no statistically significant differences in the scores from physics 1 to physics 2 (i.e., these post scores are stable from physics 1 to physics 2)."
