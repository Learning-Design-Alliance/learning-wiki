# Type of reflection-in-action (verbal vs. written) did not significantly affect preservice teachers' content knowledge scores

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There was no statistically significant difference in content knowledge between students who engaged in verbal reflection-in-action and those who engaged in written reflection-in-action. [→ Blackburn 2015](#blackburn-2015)

## Evidence

### Blackburn 2015

Blackburn, J. J., Robinson, J. S., & Kacal, A. (2015). Determining the effects of reflection type and cognitive style on students' content knowledge. Journal of Agricultural Education, 56(3), 195-209. https://doi.org/10.5032/jae.2015.03195

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A two-way independent ANOVA in a completely randomized factorial 2x2 design with 48 preservice agriculture teachers tested the main effect of reflection type on a 25-item criterion-referenced biofuels test. The article reports the main effect of type of reflection was determined to be non-significant (F(1,44) = 1.12, p = 0.30).

> "Regarding type of reflection-in-action, the ANOVA yielded F(1,44) = 1.12, p = 0.30. Therefore, the main effect of type of reflection was determined to be non-significant; thus, the researchers failed to reject the second null hypothesis."
