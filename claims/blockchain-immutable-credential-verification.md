`q2 · i?` · `review · r1`

Narrative review citing prior research (attributed to Agarwal et al., 2021). It reports that blockchain lets providers "issue, validate, and share certificates" to reduce certificate fraud; multiple studies indicating candidates forged academic credentials are cited as motivation.

> "Education providers may also use blockchain technology, a decentralized data exchange, to issue, validate, and share certificates, which will assist to reduce the prevalence of certificate fraud"

### Islam 2022 (2)

Islam, M. A. (2022). Blockchain Technology: A tool to solve the challenges of education sector in developing countries. Oxford Brookes University. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2211.11562](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2211.11562)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r1`

Narrative review of literature on student data and blockchain. It reports that records are immutable — "no user may change it" — and corrections require a new record with both remaining visible, supporting student ownership of academic records.

> "Once a record is recorded on a blockchain, no user may change it. In the unlikely event that a record contains an error, a new record should be created to corr ect it"
