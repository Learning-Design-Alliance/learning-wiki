# Retention effects are developmentally invariant across students retained in Years 5, 6, 7, and 8, and mostly positive overall

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A developmental invariance model constraining all lagged effects equal across the four retention groups fit remarkably well, indicating retention effects were consistent across the early-to-middle adolescent period. [→ Marsh 2017](#marsh-2017)
`q3 i?` Across the 40 Lag 1 effects (four retention groups x 10 outcomes), only one was significantly negative and 23 were significantly positive; the grand mean Lag 1 effect was .384. [→ Marsh 2017 (2)](#marsh-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Marsh 2017

Marsh, H. W., Pekrun, R., Parker, P. D., Murayama, K., Guo, J., Dicke, T. and Lichtenfeld, S. (2017). Long-term positive effects of repeating a year in school: six-year longitudinal study of self-beliefs, anxiety, social relations, school grades, and test scores. Journal of Educational Psychology, 109 (3). pp. 425-438. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000144

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The developmental invariance model imposed a total of 60 invariance constraints on lagged effects across the four retention groups in the longitudinal SEM; the article reports its fit was remarkably good, supporting invariance of retention effects. No effect size is printed for model fit.

> "Particularly given the large number of constraints, the fit of this model was remarkably good, providing strong support for the developmental invariance of retention effects across the four retention groups."

### Marsh 2017 (2)

Marsh, H. W., Pekrun, R., Parker, P. D., Murayama, K., Guo, J., Dicke, T. and Lichtenfeld, S. (2017). Long-term positive effects of repeating a year in school: six-year longitudinal study of self-beliefs, anxiety, social relations, school grades, and test scores. Journal of Educational Psychology, 109 (3). pp. 425-438. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000144

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Summary of the invariant and group-specific results: of the 40 Lag 1 effects, only one was significantly negative and 23 were significantly positive; the mean of Lag 1 effects averaged across the 10 outcomes was statistically significant (ES = .384). No standardised effect sizes are printed for individual group-level effects here.

> "For the critical 40 Lag 1 effects (i.e., four retention groups x 10 outcomes) based on the first year following the intervention, only one of the 40 effects was significantly negative (.05 < p < .01)."
