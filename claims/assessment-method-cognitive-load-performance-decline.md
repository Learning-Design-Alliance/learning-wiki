## Evidence

### Howard 2017

Howard, S. J., Burianová, H., Calleia, A., Fynes-Clinton, S., Kervin, L. and Bokosmaty, S. (2017). The method of educational assessment affects children's neural processing and performance: behavioural and fMRI Evidence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-017-0010-9

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Repeated-measures ANOVA on accuracy scores across the three conditions in the fMRI study of 14 recruited Grade 2 children (one removed for motion). The article reports "a large main effect of Condition, F(2, 26) = 47.11, p < .001, η2 = .78".

> "Despite the constrained sample, which limits the ability to detect potentially genuine differences across conditions, results indicated a large main effect of Condition, F(2, 26) = 47.11, p < .001, η2 = .78."

### Howard 2017 (2)

Howard, S. J., Burianová, H., Calleia, A., Fynes-Clinton, S., Kervin, L. and Bokosmaty, S. (2017). The method of educational assessment affects children's neural processing and performance: behavioural and fMRI Evidence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-017-0010-9

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Post-hoc pairwise ordering of the same repeated-measures experiment, in the Results behavioural section. Descriptive means support the hypothesised decline "with decreasing WM demands"; the η2 = .78 omnibus effect anchors impact.

> "post- hoc analyses indicated that accuracy was greatest in the multiple- choice condition ( M = 0.74, SD = 0.20), followed by the cloze dictation condition ( M = 0.57, SD = 0.28), and then the proof- reading condition ( M = 0.46, SD = 0.29)."
