## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Egger's test among published studies was significant, but asymmetry ran contrary to small-study effects: smaller standard errors were associated with larger effect sizes (r = −0.14) and longer training durations (r = −0.19). [→ Donath 2025](#donath-2025)

## Evidence

### Donath 2025

Donath, J. L., Lüke, T., Tran, U. S., Botes, E., & Goetz, T. (2025). The Transfer of Teacher Training to Inclusive Classroom Practice: A Meta-Analytic SEM Approach. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10081-9

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Publication-bias analyses using multilevel Egger's regression and power-enhanced funnel plots on published studies. The article reports the asymmetry direction was "contrary to small-study effects" and attributes it to higher-power studies investigating more extended training programs; median study power was low (22.3%).

> "smaller standard errors of effect sizes were associated with both larger effect sizes (r = −0.14 [−0.22; −0.05], p = 0.002) and longer training durations in the studies (r = −0.19 [−0.27; −0.10], p < 0.001)."
