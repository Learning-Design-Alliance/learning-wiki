Evidence¶

James-Brabham 2024¶

James-Brabham, E., Jay, T., & Sella, F. (2024). No evidence that playing a linear number board game improves numerical skills beyond teaching as usual: a randomized controlled trial in four- to five-year-old primary school children. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000857

q4 · i? · causal · r3

Pre-test game-preference question (pointing to number or letter pictures) analyzed with a chi-square test in the RCT; printed proportions were 61%, 54.9%, and 61.2% preferring the number game across the three conditions.

"Children preferred playing a number game and such preference did not significantly vary among conditions (Χ2(2)=0.87, p=0.65; Number game – Forward: 61%; Number Game – Bidirectional: 54.9%; Alphabet Lotto: 61.2%)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • In a pre-registered RCT, neither the forward nor bidirectional number board game (nor the alphabet control) produced a statistically significant condition effect on numerical or letter-sound outcomes in four- to five-year-olds — related
  • Children improved their numerical and letter-sound knowledge between pre-test and post-test regardless of condition — related
  • The game sessions showed high attendance, engagement, and enjoyment, with no differences among conditions — related
  • The game was equally engaging across children's age and sex, with no significant relationship between number of sessions played and age or sex — related
