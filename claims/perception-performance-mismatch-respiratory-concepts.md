## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students rated their understanding of the volume-pressure relationship highest (88.3% favorable) but performed worst on it, and rated disease understanding lowest (69.1% favorable) while performing best on it. [→ Wolf et al. 2015](#wolf-et-al-2015)

## Evidence

### Wolf et al. 2015

Alex M. Wolf, Carlos Liachovitzky & Abass S. Abdullahi. (2015). Active Learning Improves Student Performance in a Respiratory Physiology Lab. Journal of Curriculum and Teaching, 4(1). http://dx.doi.org/10.5430/jct.v4n1p19

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Comparison of survey favorability ratings against midterm performance per concept in the test sections. "88.3% favorable response" for volume-pressure understanding contrasted with the lowest performance, while "69.1% favorable response" for disease understanding accompanied the highest performance.

> "On one hand, students thought they understood the relation between volume and pressure very well (88.3% favorable response), but had the lowest performance on this concept. On the other hand, they weren't as confident with their perceptions of pulmonary disease understanding (69.1% favorable response) but had the highest performance on this concept."
