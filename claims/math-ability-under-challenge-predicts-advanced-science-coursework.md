# Tenth grade perceived mathematics ability under challenge is the most influential predictor of advanced high school science course completion, and girls have 24.0% lower odds than boys of completing the most advanced science courses

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In ordered logistic regressions of the highest high school science course taken, 10th grade perceived mathematics ability under challenge was the most influential predictor, and girls' odds of completing the most advanced science courses were 24.0% lower than boys', all else equal. [→ Perez-Felkner L 2017](#perez-felkner-l-2017)

## Evidence

### Perez-Felkner L 2017

Perez-Felkner L, Nix S and Thomas K (2017) Gendered Pathways: How Mathematics Ability Beliefs Shape Secondary and Postsecondary Course and Degree Field Choices. Front. Psychol. 8:386. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00386

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Ordered logistic regression models from the 2015 study (Nix et al., 2015) predicted the highest science course taken in high school using ELS panel data, controlling for background characteristics and objective ability measures. The article reports the direct gender effect and the belief effect as printed.

> "10th grade perceived mathematics ability under challenge was the most inﬂuential predictor of advance d science course completion. Moreover, holding perceived math ability under challenge, objective math ability and other factors constant, girls have 24.0% lower odds than boys of completing the most advanced science courses, all else being equal."
