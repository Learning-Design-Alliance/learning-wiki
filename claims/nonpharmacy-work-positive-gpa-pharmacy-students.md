# Nonpharmacy-related work is significantly and positively associated with pharmacy students' GPA in both fall and spring semesters

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Nonpharmacy-related work, compared to no employment, was significantly and positively associated with GPA across all four regression models in both semesters. [→ Okogbaa John 2020](#okogbaa-john-2020)

## Evidence

### Okogbaa John 2020

Okogbaa John, Allen Rondall E., Sarpong Daniel F. (2020). Time Spent at Work and Its Impact on the Academic Performance of Pharmacy Students. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17020496

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Stratified multiple linear regression analysis of survey and academic-record data from 384 P1–P3 pharmacy students, with four staged models adjusting for classification, board hours, demographics, and organization involvement. The fully adjusted Model IV fall coefficient was 0.295 ± 0.118 (p = 0.0131) and spring 0.274 ± 0.117 (p = 0.0202), showing "nonpharmacy-related work was significantly and positively associated with GPA".

> "However, nonpharmacy-related work was significantly and positively associated with GPA, from the unadjusted model (Model I) to the fully adjusted model (Model IV)."
