# In introductory biology peer discussions, women are less likely than matched male peers to prefer the leader/explainer role and more likely to prefer collaborator, listener, or recorder roles

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Women were four times more likely than matched male peers to prefer collaborator over leader/explainer, and 3.6 and 11.2 times more likely to prefer listener and recorder roles. [→ Eddy SL 2015](#eddy-sl-2015)

## Evidence

### Eddy SL 2015

Eddy SL, Brownell SE, Thummaphan P, Lan M-C, Wenderoth MP. (2015). Caution, Student Experience May Vary: Social Identities Impact a Student's Experience in Peer Discussions. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-05-0108

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Study I multinomial regression with model-averaged coefficients on self-reported preferred roles from 297 students in two terms of an active-learning biology course, controlling for relative biology competency, friend in group, and term. The article reports "Women were four times more likely to prefer to be collaborators relative to leaders/explainers" with the printed p values.

> "Women were four times more likely to prefer to be collaborators relative to leaders/explainers compared with their matched male peers (p = 0.0001). Although fewer students preferred to be listeners or recorders, women were 3.6 times and 11.2 times more likely to be in these categories than their matched male peers (p = 0.005 and 0.0025, respectively)."
