# Printing training increases letter response in right anterior fusiform but produces no change in right posterior fusiform

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Right anterior fusiform showed a significant three-way interaction, with greater sensori-motor-group response to letters only after training (t(10) = 5.48, p < .005). [→ James 2010](#james-2010)
`q3 i?` In right posterior fusiform, no interactions were significant; only stimulus type differed, with shapes producing greater signal change than letters. [→ James 2010 (2)](#james-2010-2)

## Evidence

### James 2010

James, K. H. (2010). Sensori-motor experience leads to changes in visual processing in the developing brain. Developmental Science, 13(2), 279-288. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-7687.2009.00883.x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

ROI ANOVA in right anterior fusiform of the twelve children: significant three-way interaction (F(2,20) = 3.9, p < .05), with the sensori-motor group responding more to letters after training, "t(10) = 5.48, p < .005".

> "As depicted in Figure 7, this interaction is a result of the greater response of the sensori-motor training group to letter stimuli only after the training session (t(10) = 5.48, p < .005 letters pre-training versus post-training)."

### James 2010 (2)

James, K. H. (2010). Sensori-motor experience leads to changes in visual processing in the developing brain. Developmental Science, 13(2), 279-288. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-7687.2009.00883.x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Right posterior fusiform ROI ANOVA in the same sample: significant stimulus-type effect (F(2,20) = 7.0, p < .005), shapes > letters, letters > pseudo-letters; "No interactions were significant in this region", so training changed nothing here.

> "In this region, the 2 × 2 × 3 ANOVA resulted in a significant difference among the test stimuli (F(2,20) = 7.0, p < .005, MSe = .02), with a greater percentage signal change to shapes than to letters (t(10) = 6.23, p < .0001) and a greater activation to letters than to pseudo-letters (t(10) = 6.01, p < .004). No interactions were significant in this region."
