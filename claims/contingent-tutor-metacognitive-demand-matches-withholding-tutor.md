# A contingent AI tutor places metacognitive demand equal to a question-only tutor and higher than an answer-on-request assistant

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Under contingent support, learners reported more effort on the task and on the approach than under the answer-on-request tutor, and did not differ from the question-only tutor on approach effort. [→ Hou 2026](#hou-2026)
`q3 i?` Learners most often named the contingent tutor as the one they thought hardest with and did the most thinking themselves. [→ Hou 2026 (2)](#hou-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Hou 2026

Hou, X., Weng, Y., Yeh, C. H., Lee, D. L., Zheng, L., Li, F., Wang, W., & Liu, Y. (2026). Adaptive Scaffolding Needs Contingency: An AI Tutor That Escalates and Fades on What the Learner Does. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22993

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-subjects comparison (N=131, 393 sessions) of three tutors on three Python tasks, analysed with linear mixed models and Holm correction. The contingent tutor exceeded the answer-on-request tutor on "effort spent on the approach rather than the typing"; against the question-only tutor approach effort was equivalent within 0.25 scale points (pTOST = .004).

> "Tutor C exceeded Tutor A on effort (+0.46,𝑆𝐸 0.09,𝑝Holm <. 001) and on effort spent on the approach rather than the typing (+0.33, 𝑆𝐸 0.09,𝑝Holm <. 001)."

### Hou 2026 (2)

Hou, X., Weng, Y., Yeh, C. H., Lee, D. L., Zheng, L., Li, F., Wang, W., & Liu, Y. (2026). Adaptive Scaffolding Needs Contingency: An AI Tutor That Escalates and Fades on What the Learner Does. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22993

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Forced-choice comparative attributions collected once after all three tasks from the same 131 participants, tested against equal attribution with Holm correction. Tutor C was named most often for thinking hardest (52 of 131) and doing the most thinking (49), with Tutor B second and Tutor A third.

> "Learners also named Tutor C most often as the tutor with which they thought hardest (52 of 131, 𝜒 2(2)= 16.8,𝑝Holm =. 002) and did the most thinking themselves (49,𝜒 2(2)= 13.2,𝑝Holm =. 011)"
