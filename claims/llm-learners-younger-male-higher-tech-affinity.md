# LLM learners are younger, more likely male, and score higher on technology affinity than non-learners

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1`–`r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` LLM learners were significantly younger than non-LLM learners (M = 31 vs M = 35) and more likely to be male (53% vs 64% female among avoiders). [→ Terzimehić 2025](#terzimehic-2025)
`q2 i?` LLM learners scored significantly higher on the Affinity for Technology Interaction scale (M = 3.92) than those who avoided LLMs for learning. [→ Terzimehić 2025 (2)](#terzimehic-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Terzimehić 2025

Terzimehić, N., Bühler, B., & Kasneci, E. (2025). Conversational AI as a Catalyst for Informal Learning: An Empirical Large-Scale Study on LLM Use in Everyday Learning. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11789

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

ANOVA comparing age across three groups (non-LLM users, non-LLM learners, LLM learners) in the survey of 776 participants. The text reports "LLM learners tend to be younger on average (𝑀 = 31,𝑆𝐷 = 9.57), whereas those who did not use LLMs for learning were older (𝑀 = 35,𝑆𝐷 = 11)." An ANOVA confirmed significance (F = 6.68, p < 0.01).

> "LLM learners tend to be younger on average (𝑀 = 31,𝑆𝐷 = 9.57), whereas those who did not use LLMs for learning were older (𝑀 = 35,𝑆𝐷 = 11) on average"

### Terzimehić 2025 (2)

Terzimehić, N., Bühler, B., & Kasneci, E. (2025). Conversational AI as a Catalyst for Informal Learning: An Empirical Large-Scale Study on LLM Use in Everyday Learning. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11789

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey-based comparison of Affinity for Technology Interaction scores across groups. LLM learners scored highest (M = 3.92, SD = 0.55, α = 0.71), significantly above avoiders (p < 0.01). No standardised effect size is printed.

> "LLM-learners scored highest on the ATI scale (𝑀 = 3.92.𝑆𝐷 = 0.55, 𝛼 = 0.71 out of 5), significantly above those who avoided using LLMs (𝑝 < 0.01)"
