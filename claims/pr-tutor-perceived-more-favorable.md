# Students perceived the PR tutor as more effective than the SG tutor despite its weaker learning outcomes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` PR-condition students rated their tutor more favorably than SG students on perceived learning (p = 0.045, d = 0.5) and writing useful prompts (p = 0.045, d = 0.6), with a marginal difference in perceived task effectiveness (p = 0.09, d = 0.5). [→ Jérôme Brender 2026](#jerome-brender-2026)

## Evidence

### Jérôme Brender 2026

Jérôme Brender, Laila El-Hamamsy, Kim Uittenhove, Aitor Perez, Patrick Jermann, Francesco Mondada, and Engin Bumbacher. (2026). Reflective Dialogue or Prompt Refinement? Effects of Tutor Scaffolding on Students' Independent LLM Use for Programming. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1007/978-3-032-29763-1_37](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-29763-1_37)

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Post-course perception survey compared across tutor conditions using Kruskal–Wallis tests with corrected p-values; PR students rated their tutor more favorably on all three measures, with printed effect sizes d = 0.5, 0.5, and 0.6.

> "students in the PR condition reported more favorable perceptions of their tutor than students in the SG condition, with respect to perceived effectiveness for solving the task (Kruskal-WallisH= 482,p corrected = 0.09,d= 0.5), learning (H= 449,p corr =0.045,d= 0.5), and writing useful prompts (H= 440,p corr = 0.045,d= 0.6)."
