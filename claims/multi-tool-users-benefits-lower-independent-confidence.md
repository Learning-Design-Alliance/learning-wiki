# Multi-tool GenAI users report higher support, reliance, and benefit than single-tool users, but lower confidence working without GenAI

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Multi-tool users reported higher responses than single-tool users on nearly all significant items, with the largest difference for improved academic-work quality (rrb = −0.53). [→ Manikonda 2026](#manikonda-2026)
`q2 i?` Single-tool users reported greater confidence in completing tasks without GenAI than multi-tool users (rrb = +0.25). [→ Manikonda 2026 (2)](#manikonda-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Manikonda 2026

Manikonda, L.; Si, M.; Munira, S.; Seneviratne, O.; Bennett, K. (2026). The Uneven Impact of Generative AI on Student Learning: Examining the Roles of Reliance, Evaluation Literacy, and Course Policy in AI-related Courses. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18676

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Item-level group comparison using two-sided Mann–Whitney U tests with rank-biserial correlation effect sizes, FDR-corrected, in the N=118 survey. Multi-tool users scored higher, e.g. "improved academic-work quality (rrb =−0.53)".

> "The largest differences were for improved academic-work quality (rrb =−0.53), retention of course material (−0.49), using GenAI to evaluate work (−0.48), improving work quality (−0.45), and breaking down complex problems (−0.44)."
