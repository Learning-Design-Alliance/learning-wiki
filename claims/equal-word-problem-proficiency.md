## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Schwartz 2011

Schwartz, D. L., Chase, C. C., Oppezzo, M. A., & Chin, D. B. (2011). Practicing versus inventing with contrasting cases: The effects of telling first on learning and transfer. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://aaalab.stanford.edu/transfer.html. [doi:10.1037/a0025140](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0025140)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Word-problem test on Day 11 with three density and three speed problems, administered to verify that lack of transfer was not due to failure to learn the procedure.

> "Table 1 indicates that the T&P and ICC conditions exhibited similar percentages correct on the word problem test; F(1, 124) = 1.7, p = .20."

### Schwartz 2011 (2)

Schwartz, D. L., Chase, C. C., Oppezzo, M. A., & Chin, D. B. (2011). Practicing versus inventing with contrasting cases: The effects of telling first on learning and transfer. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://aaalab.stanford.edu/transfer.html. [doi:10.1037/a0025140](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0025140)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 ANCOVA on word-problem posttest performance with the achievement covariate; the printed d = .04 reflects no treatment difference.

> "For the word problems, there were no treatment differences, F(1, 111) = 0.21, MSE = 0.03, p = .65, d = .04; there was a very strong effect of achievement; F(1, 111) = 34.0, p < .001; but again, there was no interaction of treatment by achievement; F(1, 111) = 0.09, p = .76."
