# The effectiveness of the SGC methodology is consistent across different university contexts, with no significant differences in final SDG awareness, interest, or satisfaction between student groups.

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Kruskal-Wallis tests across six student groups found no significant differences in perceived ICT impact on SDGs, interest in including SDG content in CS studies, or satisfaction with SDG themes. [→ Blanco 2024](#blanco-2024)

## Evidence

### Blanco 2024

Blanco, J. M., Domínguez, C., Jaime, A., & Usandizaga, I. (2024). Connecting sustainability and computer science curricula through website learning projects embedding different types of student-generated content. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-024-10376-8

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Kruskal-Wallis tests comparing six student groups (across two universities and two years) on items 3–5 of the questionnaire. The study reports "no significant differences" for ICT impact, interest, and satisfaction items. No effect sizes are printed for these null results.

> "no significant differences are found in the final opinion on the impact of ICTs on SDGs, nor in the interest in introducing SDGs in CS studies, nor in their level of satisfaction with the choice of SDGs as a subject matter."
