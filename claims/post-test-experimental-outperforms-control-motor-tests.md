# At post-test the experimental group significantly outperformed the control group on all six motor tests, at significance levels varying from p<0.05 to p<0.001

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Independent t tests on post-test values showed statistically significant differences between the experimental and control groups in favour of the experimental group, at the .001 level for Agility, Controlling the Ball by Foot, and Footrace Coordination, the .01 level for Tossing a Tennis Ball, and the .05 level for Throwing a Ball into the Ring. ## Evidence

### Altinkok 2017

Altinkok, M. (2017). The Effect of Coordinated Teaching Method Practices on Some Motor Skills of 6-Year-Old Children. Eurasian Journal of Educational Research, 68, 49-61. https://doi.org/10.14689/ejer.2017.68.3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Independent-samples t tests comparing post-test means of the experimental (n=30) and control (n=30) groups on six motor tests. The article reports significant differences "in favour of the experimental group" at .001, .01, and .05 levels; no effect sizes are printed.

> "among the post-test values of the “independent t” test of the experimental and control groups, a statistically significant difference on the level of .001 (p<0.001) was found among the mean test values of Agility, Controlling the Ball by Foot, and Footrace Coordination"
