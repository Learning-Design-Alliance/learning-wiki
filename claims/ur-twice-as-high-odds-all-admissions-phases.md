## Evidence

### Orellana Minerva A. 2025

Orellana Minerva A., Beetler Danielle J., Silvano Carmen J., Wuertz Ryan, Weisbrod Jennifer L., Roberts Lewis R., Windebank Anthony J., Enders Felicity T., Walther-Antonio Marina R. (2025). Integrative review in PhD admissions: A case study of efficiently minimizing bias while maximizing the student narrative. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0323426

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Retrospective case study pooling admissions data from the traditional review era (n = 166) and algorithmic era (n = 54) into logistic effect modification models. The study reports "twice as high for under-represented students, a significant result (p < 0.05)" across all three admissions phases; odds ratios in Table 2 were UR 2.93 interviewed, 2.11 offered, 2.14 accepted.

> "The odds of receiving an interview, receiving an offer post interview, and notably, accepting that offer was twice as high for under-represented students, a significant result (p < 0.05)."
