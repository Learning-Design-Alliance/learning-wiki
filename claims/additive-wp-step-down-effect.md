# On additive word problems, a step-down effect occurred: additive WP intervention beat multiplicative WP intervention, which beat control

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` On the A-WP measure, A-WP students significantly outperformed both other conditions (ES = 1.40 over control; ES = 0.29 over M-WP), and M-WP also significantly outperformed control (ES = 1.10). [→ Fuchs et al. 2016](#fuchs-et-al-2016)

## Evidence

### Fuchs et al. 2016

Lynn S. Fuchs, Robin F. Schumacher, Jessica Long, Jessica Namkung, Amelia S. Malone, Amber Wang, Carol L. Hamlett, Nancy C. Jordan, Robert S. Siegler, and Paul Changas. (2016). Effects of Intervention to Improve At-Risk Fourth Graders' Understanding, Calculations, and Word Problems with Fractions. Elementary School Journal. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED566704

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Pairwise comparisons on the Additive Word Problems measure in the randomized trial. The article describes a step-down effect in which "A-WP students performed significantly better than both contrasting conditions," with ES = 1.40 over control, ES = 0.29 over M-WP, and M-WP over control ES = 1.10.

> "By contrast, on the A-WP measure, although A-WP students performed significantly better than both contrasting conditions, the M-WP condition also performed significantly better than the control condition. The ES favoring the A-WP condition over the control group was 1.40; the ES favoring the M-WP condition over the control group was 1.10; and the ES favoring the A-WP condition over the M-WP condition was 0.29."
