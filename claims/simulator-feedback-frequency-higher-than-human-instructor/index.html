Evidence¶

Sayed Azher 2024¶

Sayed Azher, Aralia Mills, Jinzhi He, Taliah Hyjazie, Junko Tokuno, Andrea Quaiattini, and Jason M. Harley. (2024). Findings Favor Haptics Feedback in Virtual Simulation Surgical Education: An Updated Systematic and Scoping Review. Surgical Innovation. https://doi.org/10.1177/15533506241238263

q2 · i? · causal · r?

The review reports, citing Yovanoff and colleagues, a study of internal jugular central venous catheterization training comparing a haptic robotic simulator with a manikin plus human instructor feedback. The review reports "the frequency of feedback to be significantly higher using a simulator rather than a human instructor".

"Interestingly, Yovanoff and colleagues also reported the frequency of feedback to be significantly higher using a simulator rather than a human instructor. 68 This adds to the potential benefit of utilizing VS simulators as they can provide feedback more frequently, and in doing so, potentially alleviate human resources for more personalized teaching and learning."
