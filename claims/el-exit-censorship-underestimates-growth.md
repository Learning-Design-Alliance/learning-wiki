# Longitudinal growth observed for current ELs likely underestimates total-cohort growth because students who reach English proficiency exit and are no longer assessed

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Because students who reach English proficiency no longer participate in the state language-proficiency assessment, observed longitudinal growth for current ELs is likely an underestimate relative to the total entering cohort. [→ Cook 2012](#cook-2012)

## Evidence

### Cook 2012

Cook, G., Linquanti, R., Chinen, M., & Jung, H. (2012). National Evaluation of Title III Implementation Supplemental Report—Exploring Approaches to Setting English Language Proficiency Performance Criteria and Monitoring English Learner Progress. American Institutes for Research, Washington, DC. http://www.ed.gov/about/offices/list/opepd/ppss/index.html

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Chapter I's Limitations section explains that faster-progressing, higher-attaining students exit the EL category sooner, so longitudinal results for current ELs "contains only the results of those EL students who continued not to meet exit criteria."

> "As a result, when looking at longer term, longitudinal results for current ELs, the observed growth is likely an underestimate relative to that of the total cohort of students who entered the system needing to learn English as a second language"
