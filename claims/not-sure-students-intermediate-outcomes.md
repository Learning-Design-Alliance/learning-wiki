## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students 'not sure' about being transgender or 'not sure yet' about sexual orientation reported lower supports and well-being than straight and non-transgender peers but higher than transgender, gay/lesbian, and bisexual peers. [→ Hanson 2019](#hanson-2019)

## Evidence

### Hanson 2019

Hanson, T., Zhang, G., Cerna, R., Stern, A., & Austin, G. (2019). Understanding the experiences of LGBTQ students in California. San Francisco, CA: WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED601909

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Key-findings summary of descriptive CHKS tabulations; the report treats 'not sure' respondents as a distinct category rather than aggregating them into a single LGBTQ sample.

> "Students who indicated being “not sure” if they were transgender or “not sure yet” about their sexual orientation were more likely to report lower levels of supports and well -being on all outcomes, compared to their straight and non- transgender peers, but reported higher supports and well -being than their transgender, gay/lesbian, and  bisexual peers"
