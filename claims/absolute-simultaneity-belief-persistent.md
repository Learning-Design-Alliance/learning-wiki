## Evidence

### Rachel E. Scherr 2002

Rachel E. Scherr, Peter S. Shaffer, and Stamatis Vokos. (2002). The challenge of changing deeply-held student beliefs about the relativity of simultaneity. https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1507792

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Findings from the group's previous detailed investigation of student conceptions after traditional instruction, in introductory through advanced courses, summarized in the introduction as motivation for the curriculum development.

> "Many seem to believe that the time of a di stant event is the time at which a signal from the event is received by an observer.  Thus, they treat the time ordering of two events as dependent on the location of an observer."

### Rachel E. Scherr 2002 (2)

Rachel E. Scherr, Peter S. Shaffer, and Stamatis Vokos. (2002). The challenge of changing deeply-held student beliefs about the relativity of simultaneity. https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1507792

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

The article reports these co-existing beliefs cause students to fail to recognize a profound implication of special relativity. Post-test commentary corroborates that the specific student difficulties are very persistent and resistant to change, holding strongly even among students at all levels.

> "Yet, many of these same students also have a deeply -held underlying belief that simultaneity is absolute and that when signal travel time is accounted for, all observers (in all reference frames) agree on the time order of any two events."
