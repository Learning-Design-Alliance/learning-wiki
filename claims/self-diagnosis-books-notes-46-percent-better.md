## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In introductory classical mechanics, students who diagnosed their mistakes by consulting their own books and notes performed 46% better on challenging follow-up exam problems than those provided a detailed solution. [→ Marshman 2015](#marshman-2015)

## Evidence

### Marshman 2015

Marshman, E. and Singh, C. (2015). A Framework for Understanding the Patterns of Student Difficulties in Quantum Mechanics. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020119](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020119)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review reports, citing Yerushalmi et al., a recitation-based self-diagnosis intervention in introductory classical mechanics with three intervention groups and an equivalent comparison group not asked to diagnose mistakes. Students in the books-and-notes group were 'generally more engaged and struggled more to diagnose their mistakes.'

> "the performance on challenging follow-up exam problems was 46% better for students who diagnosed their mistakes by consulting their books and notes compared to those who were provided a detailed solution"
