## Evidence

### Wang 2024

Wang, V., Leung-Gagné, M., Melnick, H., & Wechsler, M. E. (2024). Universal prekindergarten expansion in California: Progress and opportunities. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/597.103

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Statewide survey of 1,384 LEAs administering the UPK Planning and Implementation Grant. The report states "81% of all responding LEAs (74% of school districts and 92% of charter schools) reported offering Tk at all of their elementary sites"; among 9,000 reported sites, 88% offered TK in 2022–23 and 93% planned to in 2023–24.

> "In the 2022–23 school year, 81% of all responding LEAs (74% of school districts and 92% of charter schools) reported offering Tk at all of their elementary sites."

### Wang 2024 (2)

Wang, V., Leung-Gagné, M., Melnick, H., & Wechsler, M. E. (2024). Universal prekindergarten expansion in California: Progress and opportunities. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/597.103

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey findings on early-admittance TK (ETK), which LEAs may offer at their discretion. LEAs "do not receive state funding for educating these students until their 5th birthday" and must cover costs from existing budgets; the report describes the 50% participation and 48% continuation figures as descriptive survey results.

> "despite this added cost, half of all LEAs committed to expanding Tk more quickly than legislatively required and offered early-admittance Tk in the 2022–23 school year, and almost half (48%) of all LEAs planned to continue offering early-admittance Tk in 2023–24."
