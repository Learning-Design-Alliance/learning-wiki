## Evidence

### Chang Yu-Che 2016

Chang Yu-Che, Lee Ching-Hsing, Chen Chien-Kuang, Liao Chien-Hung, Ng Chip-Jin, Chen Jih-Chang, Chaou Chung-Hsien. (2016). Exploring the influence of gender, seniority and specialty on paper and computer-based feedback provision during mini-CEX assessments in a busy emergency department. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-016-9682-9

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The authors' interpretation (in the Discussion, illustrated in Fig. 1) of their finding that component completion decreased in order of presentation; they note it matches prior studies and offer cultural reluctance to criticise as an alternative explanation. No effect size printed.

> "This phenomenon in our study could be a primacy effect: in a busy ED setting, some assessors tend to complete only the first or second feedback component, especially in situations of clinical overload or high stress."
