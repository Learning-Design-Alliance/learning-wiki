# Doing a relevant computer tutorial in sequence with a patient encounter yields significantly higher post-test knowledge scores than doing the same tutorial out of sequence

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who completed a tutorial in sequence with a relevant patient scored higher on the post-test (z-score 1.06 vs 0.52) than out-of-sequence students, a statistically significant difference. [→ Pusic Martin V 2012](#pusic-martin-v-2012)

## Evidence

### Pusic Martin V 2012

Pusic Martin V, MacDonald Wendy A, Eisman Harley O, Black John B. (2012). Reinforcing outpatient medical student learning using brief computer tutorials: the Patient-Teacher-Tutorial sequence. BMC Medical Education, 12:70. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-70

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized trial ANCOVA of post-test z-scores for 92 McGill medical students (41 in sequence, 51 out of sequence), adjusted for pre-test score. The overall contrast shows in-sequence mean z-score 1.06 (SD 0.70) versus 0.52 (SD 1.1), "F = 4.8 (1,175); p = 0.03". No effect size printed for this contrast.
