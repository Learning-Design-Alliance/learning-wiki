# Spelling and reading test scores correlate moderately to highly (r = 0.5–0.9), and spelling instruction is associated with improved reading skills, though causality is unestablished

## Evidence

### Steven C. Pan 2021

Steven C. Pan, Timothy C. Rickard, Robert A. Bjork. (2021). Does Spelling Still Matter— and If So, How Should It Be Taught? Perspectives from Contemporary and Historical Research. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09611-y

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports a meta-analysis of 20 K-12 classroom studies comparing spelling instruction against no instruction or other interventions, finding spelling instruction "was associated with improved reading skills"; the review notes the meta-analytic conclusions have been criticized on methodological grounds (Bowers, 2020).

> "A recent meta-analysis of 20 experimental and quasi-experimental K-12 classroom studies (Graham & Santangelo, 2014"

### Steven C. Pan 2021 (2)

Steven C. Pan, Timothy C. Rickard, Robert A. Bjork. (2021). Does Spelling Still Matter— and If So, How Should It Be Taught? Perspectives from Contemporary and Historical Research. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09611-y

`q2 · i3` · `review · r3`

The review reports observational correlations across multiple studies: "Moderate-to-high correlations of r= 0.5–0.9 between spelling and reading test scores" and r = 0.4–0.5 for spelling and writing, but states a causal relationship cannot be inferred. Spelling instruction's effect on writing was modest and not statistically significant.

> "Moderate-to-high correlations of r= 0.5–0.9 between spelling and reading test scores have also been observed across multiple studies"
