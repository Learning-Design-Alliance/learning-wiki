Evidence¶

Rodrigo 2014¶

Rodrigo, V., Greenberg, D., & Segal, D. (2014). Changes in reading habits by low literate adults through extensive reading. Reading in a Foreign Language, 26(1), 73-91. http://nflrc.hawaii.edu/rfl

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Chi-square comparing native and non-native speakers within the ER group on number of books read; the article reports the pre-treatment difference as significant with "Eta = .341" but no standardized effect size in d, g, or r form, so no magnitude is coded. The authors interpret this as the intervention helping non-native speakers catch up.

"before treatment, the native speakers in the ER group had read more books than the non-native speakers (χ2 = 7.925, df = 3; n = 125; p = .048, Eta = .341), but, in the post assessment and in the follow-up, there is no difference in the amount of books read by native and non-natives in each group."

Rodrigo 2014 (2)¶

Rodrigo, V., Greenberg, D., & Segal, D. (2014). Changes in reading habits by low literate adults through extensive reading. Reading in a Foreign Language, 26(1), 73-91. http://nflrc.hawaii.edu/rfl

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Post-treatment Chi-square within the ER group on book completion by language background; the article reports the difference as significant with "Eta = .278" but no standardized effect size in d, g, or r form, so no magnitude is coded. The no-ER group showed no language difference on this item post-treatment.

"there is a group difference for the ER group after the treatment: in this group (χ2 = 5.804, df = 1, n = 75, p = .016, Eta = .278), 100% (n = 39) of the native speaker respondents versus 86% (n = 36) of the non-native readers finished the books they had picked."
