## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh and Guangtian Zhu 2016

Chandralekha Singh and Guangtian Zhu. (2016). Improving Students’ Understanding of Quantum Mechanics by Using Peer Instruction Tools. 2010 PERC Proceedings. Originally published 2010. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05461](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05461)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Classroom response data from the same class of 18 on CT1, a basic bound-state question about a finite square well; most students answered correctly and "Only 2 students incorrectly chose option A", identified by the authors as a specific misconception.

> "Only 2 students incorrectly chose option A which represents an incorrect conception that the energy eigenfunction for a given quantum system can be a bound state and a scattering state simultaneously."

### Chandralekha Singh and Guangtian Zhu 2016 (2)

Chandralekha Singh and Guangtian Zhu. (2016). Improving Students’ Understanding of Quantum Mechanics by Using Peer Instruction Tools. 2010 PERC Proceedings. Originally published 2010. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05461](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05461)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

On CT3, half the class of 18 "incorrectly believed that option (3) also allows both bound and scattering state wavefunctions", although only scattering states can exist for that potential. This documents a related difficulty the concept tests were designed to surface.

> "Only 33% of the students (out of 18) chose the correct answer C and 50% of them incorrectly believed that option (3) also allows both bound and scattering state wavefunctions."
