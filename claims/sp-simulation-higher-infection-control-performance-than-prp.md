# Among third-year nursing students, infection control performance after education was significantly higher in the simulation using standardized patient group (20.50 ± 3.03) than in the peer role-play group (14.81 ± 3.04).

## Evidence

### Kim Eunyoung 2020

Kim Eunyoung, Kim Sang Suk, Kim Sunghee. (2020). Effects of Infection Control Education for Nursing Students Using Standardized Patients vs. Peer Role-Play. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18010107

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

In this nonequivalent control group pretest-posttest study of 62 third-year nursing students (n = 29 SP, n = 33 PRP), blinded ICU nurses rated video-recorded infection control performance (ICC = 0.96). The SP group scored "significantly higher scores (20.50 ± 3.03) than the PRP group (14.81 ± 3.04)". No effect size was printed.

> "Regarding infection control performance, the simulation using SP group showed significantly higher scores (20.50 ± 3.03) than the PRP group (14.81 ± 3.04) (z = −7.354, p < 0.001)."
