## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After control for baseline reading, watching more than 2 hours of TV per day in grade 3 predicted a grade 5 reading score about 12.5 NAPLAN points lower than watching 1 hour or less. [→ Mundy LK 2020](#mundy-lk-2020)

## Evidence

### Mundy LK 2020

Mundy LK, Canterford L, Hoq M, Olds T, Moreno-Betancur M, Sawyer S, Kosola S, Patton GC. (2020). Electronic media use and academic performance in late childhood: A longitudinal study. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0237908

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal analysis of the CATS cohort (1239 children, multiple imputation), using linear generalised estimating equations adjusting for sex, age, SES and grade 3 reading; the heavy-TV group scored 12.5 points lower, which the authors call "a delay of approximately 4 months in learning".

> "children who watched more than 2 hours of TV per day in grade 3 on average had a lower grade 5 NAPLAN reading score (when adjusted for child sex, child age, SES, and baseline academic performance) compared with those who watched 1 hour or less (β = -12.5, 95% CI: -24.1 to -0.8)"
