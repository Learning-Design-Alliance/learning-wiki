## Evidence

### Burgess A 2020

Burgess A, van Diggele C, Roberts C, Mellis C. (2020). Feedback in the clinical setting. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02280-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing its authors' prior studies and peer-learning literature, that students perceive peer feedback as beneficial to knowledge, skills and professional attributes, that it can foster responsibility, and that some students report metacognitive gains. No effect size is printed.

> "The practice of providing feedback to peers is perceived by students as beneficial to development of knowledge, skills, and professional attributes [11]. Provision of feedback from peers can foster high levels of responsibility in students [11, 12], and some students report metacognitive gains [11–13]."

### Burgess A 2020 (2)

Burgess A, van Diggele C, Roberts C, Mellis C. (2020). Feedback in the clinical setting. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02280-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports concerns about the honesty and accuracy of peer feedback, attributing students' inability to provide constructive feedback to "both inadequate training, and social discomfort". Students are also concerned about providing negative feedback to peers. No effect size is printed.

> "However, unsurprisingly, there are real concerns regarding the honesty and accuracy of peer feedback [11–15]. The inability of students to provide constructive feedback to peers has been attributed to both inadequate training, and social discomfort [16]."
