# Phoneme discrimination training produces robust on-task learning across all 11 trained phoneme pairs, with the largest improvements in adults with the poorest initial thresholds

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Both training groups showed highly significant improvement in phoneme discrimination threshold for all 11 phoneme pairs after training. [→ Ferguson M. A. 2014](#ferguson-m-a-2014)

## Evidence

### Ferguson M. A. 2014

Ferguson M. A., Henshaw H., Clark D. P. A., Moore D. R. (2014). Benefits of Phoneme Discrimination Training in a Randomized Controlled Trial of 50- to 74-Year-Olds With Mild Hearing Loss. Ear and Hearing. https://doi.org/10.1097/AUD.0000000000000020

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

RCT of 44 adults aged 50–74 with mild hearing loss, randomized to Immediate or Delayed Training groups in a crossover design. The analysis of training blocks reports a highly significant improvement in phoneme discrimination threshold, "for all 11 phoneme pairs (F(1, 3931.1) = 479.1, p < 0.001)".

> "Across both groups of participants there was a highly significant improvement with training in phoneme discrimination threshold for all 11 phoneme pairs (F(1, 3931.1) = 479.1, p < 0.001)"

### Ferguson M. A. 2014 (2)

Ferguson M. A., Henshaw H., Clark D. P. A., Moore D. R. (2014). Benefits of Phoneme Discrimination Training in a Randomized Controlled Trial of 50- to 74-Year-Olds With Mild Hearing Loss. Ear and Hearing. https://doi.org/10.1097/AUD.0000000000000020

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Figure-based analysis of first versus last block thresholds per participant (Fig. 3) shows the regression line's shallower slope, so "the largest improvements occurred for those individuals who had the poorest initial thresholds"; printed partial η² values ranged from 0.10 to 0.25 across continua.

> "For each phoneme continuum, the regression line fitted to all the data points had a shallower slope than the diagonal, indicating the largest improvements occurred for those individuals who had the poorest initial thresholds"
