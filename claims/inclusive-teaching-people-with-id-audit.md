## Evidence

### Trollor JN 2018

Trollor JN, Eagleson C, Turner B, Tracy J, Torr JJ, Durvasula S, Iacono T, Cvejic RC, Lennox N. (2018). Intellectual disability content within tertiary medical curriculum: how is it taught and by whom? BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1286-z

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Survey of course coordinators at 12 Australian medical schools; people with intellectual disability acted as tutors, lecturers or symposium panel members for 24% of all units. The audit found "A majority of schools (n = 9, 75%) involved people with intellectual disability designing and teaching content".

> "A majority of schools (n = 9, 75%) involved people with intellectual disability designing and teaching content, but the extent to which this occurred was inconsistent."

### Trollor JN 2018 (2)

Trollor JN, Eagleson C, Turner B, Tracy J, Torr JJ, Durvasula S, Iacono T, Cvejic RC, Lennox N. (2018). Intellectual disability content within tertiary medical curriculum: how is it taught and by whom? BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1286-z

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison in the Discussion between the current audit and the 1999 Lennox and Diggens audit; the authors attribute the rise partly to increased awareness of the rights of people with disability championed in the UNCRPD.

> "Of note was a rise in the percentage of compulsory units that incorporated teaching strategies that were inclusive of people with intellectual disability (30% of schools in Lennox and Diggens [29])."
