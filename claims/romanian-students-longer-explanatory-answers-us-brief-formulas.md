# On an open-ended E&M survey, Romanian students wrote longer, more explanatory answers while American students answered briefly with mostly formulas

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` US students mostly exposed to MC exams wrote brief formula-based answers, while Romanian students mostly exposed to OE exams wrote more words and explained each step. [→ Cristian Raduta 2005](#cristian-raduta-2005)

`q2 i?` American students used the minimum number of formulas, words or symbols, while Romanian students explained each reasoning step in words. [→ Cristian Raduta 2005](#cristian-raduta-2005)

`q2 i?` In the word-count categorization of answers to force part c), Romanian answers fell more often in the rich-in-explanations category while American answers fell mostly in the some-words category. [→ Cristian Raduta 2005](#cristian-raduta-2005)

## Evidence

### Cristian Raduta 2005

Cristian Raduta, Gordon Aubrecht. (2005). Cultural differences in answering physics questions: Could they arise from the difference between reasoning expected in answering exam questions in those cultures?. Originally published 2002. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0503124](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0503124)

`q2 · i?`

Written short E&M survey given to 74 American (Ohio State University) and 52 Romanian (Bucharest University) students; the authors report the answering-style contrast and note it "held true for almost all Romanian and American students who gave reasonable good answers".

> "While the Romanian student (whose answer was presented) is writing more words, trying to be as explicit as he can answering each question, the American student is very brief in his answers, writing mostly formulas"
