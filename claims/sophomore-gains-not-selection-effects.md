## Evidence

### N.G. Holmes 2015

N.G. Holmes, Carl E. Wieman, D.A. Bonn. (2015). Teaching critical thinking. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1505329112

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subgroup re-analysis of the first-year measures restricted to students who continued into the sophomore lab (figures S1a-c). The authors interpret the similarity to whole-class trends as evidence against selection effects for the behavioural outcomes. No effect size printed.

> "This suggests that the students who continued into the the sophomore course were not exceptional in their behaviours in ﬁrst-year. This further suggests that the eﬀect seen in the Sophomore Lab experiment are not due to selection eﬀects."

### N.G. Holmes 2015 (2)

N.G. Holmes, Carl E. Wieman, D.A. Bonn. (2015). Teaching critical thinking. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1505329112

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Two-by-two comparison of end-of-first-year diagnostic scores by sophomore-lab participation and condition (table S6). Students who took the sophomore lab outperformed those who did not overall, consistent with expected selection bias on conceptual background, but experimental and control continuers did not differ significantly. No effect size printed.

> "Of the students who took the sophomore physics lab, however, there is a non-signiﬁcant diﬀerence between the experimental and control groups on both the MBT and BEMA."
