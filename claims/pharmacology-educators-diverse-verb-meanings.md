# Pharmacology educators attribute diverse meanings to common assessment action verbs (all except discuss) and to collocated words, including core pharmacology terms

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Kelly‐Laubscher Róisín 2025

Kelly‐Laubscher Róisín, Flaherty Caoimhe C., Herzel Lea, and Barry Órla P. (2025). Deciphering the Language of Assessment in Pharmacology Summative Written Assessments. Pharmacology Research & Perspectives 13, no. 6: e70116. https://doi.org/10.1002/prp2.70116

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Anonymous online survey of pharmacology educators at one Irish university, with 21 open-ended questions built on the four most common verbs; eight of twelve invited educators completed it.

> "Eight of 12 educators completed an online anonymous survey to identify the meaning(s) they associate with the common action verbs and their collocated words."

### Kelly‐Laubscher Róisín 2025 (2)

Kelly‐Laubscher Róisín, Flaherty Caoimhe C., Herzel Lea, and Barry Órla P. (2025). Deciphering the Language of Assessment in Pharmacology Summative Written Assessments. Pharmacology Research & Perspectives 13, no. 6: e70116. https://doi.org/10.1002/prp2.70116

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Content analysis of survey responses by two independent coders; "only one of the four actions verbs, “discuss”" had common meanings, while describe, outline and explain attracted diverse meanings including some respondents not distinguishing discuss from describe.

> "Interestingly post‐survey analysis identified that only one of the four actions verbs, “discuss”, had common educators' meanings associated with it when used in pharmacology summative assessments (Table 4)."

### Kelly‐Laubscher Róisín 2025 (3)

Kelly‐Laubscher Róisín, Flaherty Caoimhe C., Herzel Lea, and Barry Órla P. (2025). Deciphering the Language of Assessment in Pharmacology Summative Written Assessments. Pharmacology Research & Perspectives 13, no. 6: e70116. https://doi.org/10.1002/prp2.70116

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Same survey's collocated-word analysis; respondents split on whether physiological effects "should not be included under its mechanism of action", and also diverged on what the pharmacological management of requires.

> "While some respondents thought that the physiological effects of a drug's action should not be included under its mechanism of action, other participants felt they should be included (Table 5)."
