## Evidence

### Golle 2026

Golle, J., Lösche, P., Beißert, H., Wieland, A.-K., Rieber, R., Schult, J., Hasselhorn, M., Nagengast, B., & Trautwein, U. (2026). COVID-19-related school closures and students' achievement and motivation: Did teacher support make a difference. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00439-1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multiple regression with three contact-level indicator variables against the Cohort 1 reference; low contact meant occasionally or never; all pretest measures controlled. Printed coefficients in SDY units included b = −0.22 (reading intrinsic value) and b = −0.30 (reading self-concept); writing intrinsic value was marginal (p = 0.060).

### Golle 2026 (2)

Golle, J., Lösche, P., Beißert, H., Wieland, A.-K., Rieber, R., Schult, J., Hasselhorn, M., Nagengast, B., & Trautwein, U. (2026). COVID-19-related school closures and students' achievement and motivation: Did teacher support make a difference. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00439-1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same regression model. High contact (several times per week or almost every day) also showed negative self-concept effects, with printed coefficients b = −0.22 (reading) and b = −0.19 (writing). The authors note these negative effects on motivation did not differ substantially between low and high contact conditions; medium-contact coefficients were close to zero and not significant.

> "students who had high teacher- student contact during the pandemic compared with students taught under regular learning conditions also reported lower self-concept scores in reading and writing at the end of Grade 1, bread ¼/C0 0:22; p ¼ 0:033 and bwrite ¼/C0 0:19; p ¼ 0:050."
