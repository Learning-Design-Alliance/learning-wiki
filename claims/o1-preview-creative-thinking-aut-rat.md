## Evidence

### Latif 2025

Latif, E., Zhou, Y., Guo, S., Gao, Y., Shi, L., Nyaaba, M., Bewerdorff, A., Yang, X., & Zhai, X. (2025). Can OpenAI o1 outperform humans in higher-order cognitive thinking? arXiv preprint. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.21203/rs.3.rs-7435042/v1](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-7435042/v1)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Divergent thinking comparison on the Alternate Uses Task, reported in Section 3.5 and Table 9; originality was scored with an automated AI-based tool (Organisciak et al.) against 68 university students assessed by Urban et al.

> "o1-preview achieved an overall mean originality score of 2.98, higher than the human benchmark of 1.74 (see Tab. 9)."

### Latif 2025 (2)

Latif, E., Zhou, Y., Guo, S., Gao, Y., Shi, L., Nyaaba, M., Bewerdorff, A., Yang, X., & Zhai, X. (2025). Can OpenAI o1 outperform humans in higher-order cognitive thinking? arXiv preprint. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.21203/rs.3.rs-7435042/v1](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-7435042/v1)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Convergent thinking comparison on a 10-item Chinese Remote Associates Test, reported in Section 3.5 and Table 9, benchmarked against 54 Chinese bilingual university students from Xia et al.

> "The model was presented with 10 Chinese word association problems and achieved a total score of 7 out of 10, reflecting an accuracy rate of 70%. This performance surpasses the accuracy rate of 44.12% observed in human participants in similar tasks."
