Evidence¶

C.E. Holdich 2002¶

C.E. Holdich, R.G. Holdich and P.W.H. Chung. (2002). Assessing aspects of children's written grammar: automating the process. Computers and Education, Vol 39, pp37-50. http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-nd/2.5/

q2 · i? · design · r2

Harpin's (1976) type-token ratio technique applied to the first 100 words of each of the 12 stories; stories 10 and 11 (TTR 0.73 and 0.75) were especially praised by examiners while stories 1 and 2 (0.54 and 0.60) showed noted repetition.

"Broadly, the lower levels of attainment resulted in low TTR ratios whilst the higher levels of attainment resulted in higher TTR ratios, alt hough the results inevitably deviated on occasion depending on the strengths and weaknesses of the particular young writer and on the theme and organisation of the story."

C.E. Holdich 2002 (2)¶

C.E. Holdich, R.G. Holdich and P.W.H. Chung. (2002). Assessing aspects of children's written grammar: automating the process. Computers and Education, Vol 39, pp37-50. http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-nd/2.5/

q2 · i0 · associational · r1

The normalised TTR-drop curves plotted in Figure 1 showed only the Level 2 story dropping sharply; the article reports the drop-rate measure was not useful for quantitatively differentiating levels, a null result for Harpin's extension.

"Whilst the TTR for the story achieving Leve l 2 dropped sharply, th ere was little to discriminate between the other levels. Although Harpin was correct in his hypothesis that the rate of fall in the TTR would reveal the extent of variety of a child's vocabulary, the results did not prove useful in a quantitative analysis of the various levels."
