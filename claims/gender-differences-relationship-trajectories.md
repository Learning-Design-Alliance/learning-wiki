# Trajectory patterns of teacher-student warmth and conflict differ between boys and girls, with a high-stable conflict class found only for boys

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Multiple-group analyses showed trajectory patterns for warmth and conflict differed significantly for boys and girls, and the high-stable conflict trajectory emerged only among boys. [→ Spilt 2012](#spilt-2012)

## Evidence

### Spilt 2012

Spilt, J. L., Hughes, J. N., Wu, J.-Y., & Kwok, O.-M. (2012). Dynamics of Teacher-Student Relationships: Stability and Change across Elementary School and the Influence on Children's Academic Success. Child Development, 83(4), 1180-1195. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2012.01761.x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multiple-group GMM comparison (Results): freely estimated across-gender models fit significantly better than gender-fixed models for both warmth and conflict, supporting separate modeling; boys evidenced lower warmth and more conflict overall, with the high-stable conflict class found only for boys.

> "Freely estimated models across gender showed a significant better fit than fixed models for Warmth (∆BIC=36, ∆df =4, p<.001) and Conflict (∆BIC=248, ∆df =8, p<.001). Thus we concluded that trajectory patterns for Warmth and Conflict differed for boys and girls."
