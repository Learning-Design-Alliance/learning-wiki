# Generative AI systems carry strong potential for introduced and inherited bias

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Bias in AI models is defined as the systematic prevalence of untrue and/or harmful information leading to unfair, inaccurate, or discriminatory outcomes, and generative AI has strong potential for bias from human-designed algorithms, large training datasets, and lack of cultural knowledge. [→ Judi Fusco 2023](#judi-fusco-2023)

## Evidence

### Judi Fusco 2023

Judi Fusco, Pati Ruiz, Jeremy Roschelle. (2023). Ethics, Artificial Intelligence, and Digital Equity: Making Informed Decisions about the Integration of AI. https://digitalpromise.org/digital-equity/

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Definitional guidance quoted from Oregon's state guidance document in the Equity and AI: Spotlight on Bias section. It adds that "as generative AI uses large data sets, historical and systemic biases are introduced into the system" and that lacking cultural knowledge can cause misinterpretations of prompts.

> "Bias in AI models is deﬁned as the systematic prevalence of untrue and/or harmful information that leads to unfair, inaccurate, or discriminatory outcomes"
