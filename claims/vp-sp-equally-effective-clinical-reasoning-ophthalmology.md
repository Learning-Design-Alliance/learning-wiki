## Evidence

### Malik Tayyaba Gul 2024

Malik Tayyaba Gul, Mahboob Usman, Khan Rehan Ahmed, Alam Rabia. (2024). Virtual patients versus standardized patients for improving clinical reasoning skills in ophthalmology residents. A randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05241-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial at two tertiary care hospitals in Lahore, Pakistan, with 24 residents in the VP intervention arm and 23 in the SP control arm. Repeated-measures ANOVA showed significant gains over time in both arms (p value < 0.00), described in "respectively (p value < 0.00)". No effect size was printed.

> "For the SP group, the mean scores were 12.6 ± 3.08, 16.39 ± 3.01 and 15.39 ± 2.95, and for the VP group, the mean scores were 12.7 ± 3.84, 16.30 ± 3.19 and 15.65 ± 3.18 for the pretest, posttest and follow-up posttest, respectively (p value < 0.00)."

### Malik Tayyaba Gul 2024 (2)

Malik Tayyaba Gul, Mahboob Usman, Khan Rehan Ahmed, Alam Rabia. (2024). Virtual patients versus standardized patients for improving clinical reasoning skills in ophthalmology residents. A randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05241-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Between-group comparison in the same randomized trial: the VP and SP groups did not differ statistically on clinical reasoning scores, per "not statistically significant (p = 0.896)". The authors interpret this as equal effectiveness.

> "the difference between the VP and SP groups was not statistically significant (p = 0.896), indicating that both techniques were equally effective at acquiring clinical reasoning skills in ophthalmology residents."
