# The four-week summer school was associated with a small positive effect on English outcomes (effect size +0.17, about two additional months' progress)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Pupils allocated to the summer school made greater progress in English than control pupils, with a gain-score effect size of +0.17. [→ Gorard et al. 2014](#gorard-et-al-2014)

## Evidence

### Gorard et al. 2014

Stephen Gorard, Nadia Siddiqui, Beng Huat See. (2014). Future Foundations: Evaluation Report and Executive Summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED581128

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomised trial (435 pupils randomised in three waves; 310 with achieved gain scores) comparing treatment and control on gain from rescaled KS2 scores to the Progress in English post-test. Table 7 prints treatment mean gain 3.71 versus control 1.46, effect size +0.17; the report envisages "approximately two additional months' progress".

> "When the 'gain' scores from rescaled KS fine point scores to PiE or PiM raw scores are considered, there is again a positive result for English and a very small negative one for maths (Tables 7 and 8)."
