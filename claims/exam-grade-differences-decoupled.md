# Where final letter grades differed by gender, exam differences were not persistent across the course, and vice versa

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The study found that grade-level and exam-level gender differences did not persist together: courses with a final letter grade difference showed no persistent exam differences, and courses with persistent exam differences showed no grade difference. [→ Matthew Dew 2021](#matthew-dew-2021)

## Evidence

### Matthew Dew 2021

Matthew Dew, Jonathan Perry, Lewis Ford, William Bassichis, and Tatiana Erukhimova. (2021). Gendered Performance Diﬀerences in Introductory Physics: A Study from a Large Land-Grant University. arXiv:2009.09536. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010106](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010106)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Conclusion-level summary of the pooled t-test and ANOVA analyses across the four courses and more than a decade of data; the article states this decoupling as a new finding that exam-level differences need not affect final course grades.

> "Where diﬀerences in ﬁnal letter grades were found for a course, there were no persistent diﬀerences observed across that course's exams. Where persistent diﬀerences were observed on exams within a course, there were no diﬀerences for ﬁnal letter grades in that course."
