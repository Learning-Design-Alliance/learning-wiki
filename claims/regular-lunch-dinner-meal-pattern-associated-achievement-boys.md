# Regular lunch consumption is associated with increased odds of high academic achievement in both girls and boys, and regular dinner and an all-meals pattern in boys

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Regular lunch was associated with higher odds of high achievement in girls (AOR 1.44) and boys (AOR 1.43); regular dinner (AOR 1.44) and eating all four meals regularly (AOR 1.50) were associated with higher odds in boys only. [→ Stea TH 2014](#stea-th-2014)

## Evidence

### Stea TH 2014

Stea TH, Torstveit MK. (2014). Association of lifestyle habits and academic achievement in Norwegian adolescents: a cross-sectional study. BMC Public Health 2014, 14:829. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2458-14-829

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the same cross-sectional logistic regression analysis, regular lunch was associated with "AOR: 1.44 (1.08-1.93)" in girls and AOR 1.43 (1.09-1.89) in boys; dinner and the all-four-meals pattern reached significance in boys only. No standardized effect size was printed.

> "Increased odds of high academic achievement was also shown in boys who had a regular consumption of dinner (AOR: 1.44 (1.16-1.79)) and ate all four meals regularly (AOR: 1.50 (1.10 – 2.03))."
