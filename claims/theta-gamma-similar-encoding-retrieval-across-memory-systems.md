## Evidence

### Drew B. Headley and Denis Paré 2017

Drew B. Headley and Denis Paré. (2017). Common oscillatory mechanisms across multiple memory systems. npj Science of Learning 2:1. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-016-0001-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review's synthesis of human, macaque, and rodent studies concludes that "Theta and gamma oscillations have similar relationships with encoding and retrieval across multiple forms of memory and brain regions". No effect sizes are printed for this cross-regional regularity.

> "Theta and gamma oscillations have similar relationships with encoding and retrieval across multiple forms of memory and brain regions, despite regional differences in microcircuitry and information content."

### Drew B. Headley and Denis Paré 2017 (2)

Drew B. Headley and Denis Paré. (2017). Common oscillatory mechanisms across multiple memory systems. npj Science of Learning 2:1. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-016-0001-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports human intracranial and EEG findings in which "hippocampo- entorhinal gamma coherence during encoding is stronger for subsequently recalled words" and gamma power increases for recalled items, alongside theta-coherence effects for subsequently recalled declarative material.

> "In the human medial temporal lobe, hippocampo- entorhinal gamma coherence during encoding is stronger for subsequently recalled words, 80 and gamma power is increased across the medial temporal lobe for recalled words."
