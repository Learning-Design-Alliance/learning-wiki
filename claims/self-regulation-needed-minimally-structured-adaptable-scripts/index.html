Evidence¶

Lenka Schnaubert · Freydis Vogel 2022¶

Lenka Schnaubert · Freydis Vogel. (2022). Integrating collaboration scripts, group awareness, and self-regulation in computer-supported collaborative learning. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn (2022) 17:1–10. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-022-09367-9

q2 · i? · review · r2

Two experiments with university freshmen in mathematics learning in dyads with proof and argumentation tasks, comparing minimally structured, maximally structured, and adaptable collaboration scripts with heuristic worked examples. The editorial reports self-regulation "is particularly needed for learning with minimally structured or adaptable collaboration scripts". No effect size printed.

"the study focused on the role of learners’ self-regulation skills when learning with collaboration scripts and finds that self-regulation in CSCL is particularly needed for learning with minimally structured or adaptable collaboration scripts allowing learners to assess their own needs and select the script accordingly."

Related Claims¶

  • Learner-chosen adaptivity in a collaboration script was feasible as guidance design, but impacted regulatory actions only among students with more advanced self-regulation skills — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • In Experiment 2, self-regulation skills significantly positively predicted domain-specific MAS only in the minimal heuristic worked examples condition — related
  • In Experiment 1, the CSCL script condition significantly affected domain-specific MAS, with only the maximal script significantly outperforming the minimal script — related
  • In Experiment 1, self-regulation skills significantly predicted socio-discursive MAS only in the adaptable CSCL script condition — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • In Experiment 1, dyads that more often chose maximum CSCL scaffolding had lower post-test socio-discursive MAS — related
  • Effects of collaboration scripts combined with heuristic worked examples on mathematical argumentation skills depend on prior knowledge and working-memory capacity, consistent with a Matthew Effect — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Teacher guidance integrating argumentative scripts produces deeper whole-class collaborative argumentation than minimal teacher guidance in middle level science classrooms — related
