Evidence¶

Madeline B. Harms and Sherona D. Garrett-Ruffin 2023¶

Madeline B. Harms and Sherona D. Garrett-Ruffin. (2023). Disrupting links between poverty, chronic stress, and educational inequality. npj Science of Learning 8:50. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00199-2

q3 · i? · review · r2

The review reports this randomized trial, "considered the gold-standard for program efficacy research": MindUP versus social responsibility training in 4th-5th graders produced "steeper diurnal cortisol slopes at post-test" plus behavioral improvements. No effect sizes printed.

"Schnonert-Reichl and colleagues 52 found that 4th-5th grade children who received an SEL program that included mindfulness training (MindUP) showed steeper diurnal cortisol slopes at post-test (indicative of healthier HPA axis function), as well as improved outcomes in social-emotional functioning, self-reported school self-concept, and teacher-reported math achievement, relative to a control group of children who received a social responsibility training program."

Related Claims¶

  • Large-scale RCTs show school mindfulness training does not benefit overall well-being or social-emotional functioning and can harm those most at risk — reports the opposite
  • Prior studies support the experiential canalization framework: preschoolers in poverty are almost five times as likely to show disrupted diurnal cortisol patterns, and increased economic hardship is associated with significantly lower cognitive self-regulation — related
  • Social-emotional learning benefits persist at follow-up — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Universal school-based SEL programs improve students' behaviour, with a small and, in recent randomised trials, negligible effect on academic achievement — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Hypothesized chain linking implementation supports to teacher fidelity to student responsiveness to child outcomes in the MindUP trial — related
