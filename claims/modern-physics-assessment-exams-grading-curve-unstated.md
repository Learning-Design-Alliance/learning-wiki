## Evidence

### Buzzell 2025

Buzzell, A., Barthelemy, R., & Atherton, T. (2025). Modern physics courses: Understanding the content taught in the U.S. arXiv. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.21.010139](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.21.010139)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Syllabi were coded into three categories on exam use, with exam-using syllabi further divided into cumulative, non-cumulative, or unstated. Of the 90% with exams, 50% had a cumulative exam, 10% only noncumulative exams, and 40% did not state.

> "Of the 167 syllabi, 3% explicitly state exams would not be used as an assessment tool, 7% syllabi did not state if there were or were not exams, while 90% explicitly stated there would be exams (Figure 10)."

### Buzzell 2025 (2)

Buzzell, A., Barthelemy, R., & Atherton, T. (2025). Modern physics courses: Understanding the content taught in the U.S. arXiv. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.21.010139](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.21.010139)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Grading schemes were coded with an emergent coding method across the 167 syllabi, producing eight codes ranging from skills-based absolute scales to pass/fail and rounding policies.

> "78% of syllabi did not state if there would or would not be a curve (Figure 9). 7% stated there would be no curve, while 6% stated the grade would only curve for the students benefit."
