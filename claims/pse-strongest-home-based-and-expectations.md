# Parenting self-efficacy correlates most strongly with home-based participation and parental expectations and aspirations, and least with parent-teacher communication and relationships

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Home-based participation (r = .248) and parental expectations and aspirations (r = .248) showed the strongest associations; school-based participation (r = .124) and parent-teacher communication/relationships (r = .090) were weaker, with significant pairwise differences. ## Evidence

### Tianyi Ma 2024

Tianyi Ma, Cassandra L. Tellegen, Julie Hodges, Matthew R. Sanders. (2024). The Associations Between Parenting Self-Efficacy and Parents' Contributions to the Home-School Partnership Among Parents of Primary School Students: a Multilevel Meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09937-3

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Three-level mixed-effects model (M3) across four home-school partnership outcome categories; between-outcomes variation was significant, F(4) = 215.41, p < .001. Home-based involvement (r = .248) and expectations/aspirations (r = .248) did not differ from each other but both exceeded school-based participation and communication/relationships.

> "The association between parenting self-efficacy with home-based participation was statistically significantly stronger than the associations with school-based participation, r = .124, t(176) = 7.70, p < .001, and with parent-teacher communication and relationships, r = .090, t(176) = 9.36, p < .001."
