# Dependent versus autonomous cognitive offloading to GenAI shows opposite motivational and cognitive outcome patterns

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Dependent offloading (core thinking delegated to AI) is associated with transferred agency, lower intrinsic motivation, and poorer perceived cognitive outcomes; autonomous offloading shows the opposite pattern. [→ Fan Y 2026](#fan-y-2026)

## Evidence

### Fan Y 2026

Fan Y, Li X and Zhang R (2026). Family-school autonomy support for children's responsible use of generative artificial intelligence: a self-determination theory synthesis and developmental research agenda. Front. Psychol. 17:1969217. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1969217

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Zhu et al. (2026), a three-wave study of 589 university students and early-career knowledge workers distinguishing dependent from autonomous offloading. "Dependent offloading was associated with transferred agency, lower intrinsic motivation, and poorer perceived cognitive outcomes" while autonomous offloading showed the opposite pattern.
