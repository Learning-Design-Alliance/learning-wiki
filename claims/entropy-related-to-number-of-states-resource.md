# Graduate students used an entropy-is-related-to-number-of-states resource across every interview question, including those without microscopic cues

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` At least one student reasoned that entropy is related to a number of states on every question of the interview, including the Blocks and Expansions questions where no cue or need for microscopic thinking existed. [→ Nathan Crossette 2021](#nathan-crossette-2021)

## Evidence

### Nathan Crossette 2021

Nathan Crossette, Michael Vignal, and Bethany R. Wilcox. (2021). Investigating graduate student reasoning on a conceptual entropy questionnaire. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020119](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020119)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Cross-question theme analysis (Sec. IV A) of emergently coded interview transcripts from eight graduate students, using a resources framework. The article identifies a resource that 'entropy is related to a number of states in every single question on the interview,' phrased to reflect student language rather than the word multiplicity.

> "entropy is related to a number of states in every single question on the interview, even in the Blocks and Expansions questions where there was neither a cue nor a need to think about microscopic quantities"
