# Among language-only interventions, instructional practices and one-on-one or small-group delivery produced significantly larger language gains than curricula and large-group or whole-class delivery

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Interventions classified as instructional practices produced a significantly larger weighted effect size on language performance than curricula (0.43 versus 0.02). [→ Sarah Herrera 2021](#sarah-herrera-2021)
`q3 i2` Language instruction including some one-on-one or small-group implementation yielded a significantly larger weighted effect size than exclusive large-group or whole-class delivery (0.43 versus 0.10). [→ Sarah Herrera 2021 (2)](#sarah-herrera-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Sarah Herrera 2021

Sarah Herrera, Beth M. Phillips, Yi-Chieh Newton, Jennifer L. Dombek, and James A. Hernandez. (2021). Effectiveness of Early Literacy Instruction: Summary of 20 Years of Research. Regional Educational Laboratory Southeast, REL 2021-084. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Weighted effect size comparison among the 38 interventions that taught language only. Instructional practices yielded a weighted effect size of 0.43 versus 0.02 for curricula; the review notes 0.43 is "equivalent to a 17 percentile point increase in language performance."

> "interventions classified as instructional practices produced a significantly larger weighted effect size on language performance than interventions classified as curricula (0.43 versus 0.02; see table B6 in appendix B)"

### Sarah Herrera 2021 (2)

Sarah Herrera, Beth M. Phillips, Yi-Chieh Newton, Jennifer L. Dombek, and James A. Hernandez. (2021). Effectiveness of Early Literacy Instruction: Summary of 20 Years of Research. Regional Educational Laboratory Southeast, REL 2021-084. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Figure 2 comparison of 27 interventions using one-on-one or small-group instruction versus 11 using large-group or whole-class exclusively, among language-only interventions. Weighted effect sizes were 0.43 versus 0.10, and exclusive large-group or whole-class configurations did not improve language performance.

> "The weighted effect size on language performance was significantly larger when language instruction included some one-on-one or small-group implementation than when it used large-group or whole-class configurations exclusively (0.43 versus 0.10; figure 2)"
