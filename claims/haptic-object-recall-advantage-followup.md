# Haptic exploration of real objects improves long-term recall of those objects three weeks later, with vision-and-haptics outperforming only-vision

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` At the 3-week follow-up, haptics showed a significant main effect on object recall (ηp² = 0.20), and participants in the only-vision condition recalled significantly fewer objects than those in the vision-and-haptics condition. [→ Magdalena Novak & Stephan Schwan 2021](#magdalena-novak-stephan-schwan-2021)

## Evidence

### Magdalena Novak & Stephan Schwan 2021

Magdalena Novak & Stephan Schwan. (2021). Does Touching Real Objects Affect Learning? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09551-z

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

In the follow-up survey (n = 115 of 163), ANCOVA on delayed free recall (M = 3.15, SD = 1.52 of 6) controlling for prior knowledge and interest showed "significant main effects for vision" and haptics; post hoc tests showed only-vision recalled significantly fewer objects than vision-and-haptics.

> "we found significant main effects for vision, F(1,109) = 9.52, p =. 0 0 3 ,ηp2 = 0.08, and haptics, F(1,109) = 27.10, p <. 0 0 1 ,ηp2 = 0.20, but no significant interaction effect"
