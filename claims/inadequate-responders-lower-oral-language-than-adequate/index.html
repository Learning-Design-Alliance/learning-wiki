Evidence¶

Cho Eunsoo 2015¶

Cho Eunsoo, Roberts Garrett J., Capin Philip, Roberts Greg, Miciak Jeremy, Vaughn Sharon. (2015). Cognitive Attributes, Attention, and Self-Efficacy of Adequate and Inadequate Responders in a Fourth Grade Reading Intervention. Learning Disabilities Research & Practice. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4793275/

q3 · i3 · associational · r2

Planned pairwise contrasts from a MANOVA profile analysis of pretest cognitive measures in a randomized fourth-grade reading intervention study. Inadequate responders differed from adequate responders on "verbal knowledge, t (123) = 5.15, corrected p <.001, and listening comprehension, t (123) = 4.27, corrected p = .001". Hedges' g values from Table 3 show verbal knowledge g = 1.02 and listening comprehension g = .85 for the AR vs. IR contrast.

"Inadequate (IR-C and IR-CW) and adequate responders differed on verbal knowledge, t (123) = 5.15, corrected p <.001, and listening comprehension, t (123) = 4.27, corrected p = .001."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Adequate responders differ from typical readers on verbal knowledge, listening comprehension, working memory, and phonological awareness — related
  • Both inadequate responder groups show more teacher-rated attention problems and lower self-reported self-efficacy than typical readers — related
  • The multivariate omnibus test shows significant cognitive profile differences across the four reader groups — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Phonological awareness and rapid naming differentiate inadequate responders with word reading deficits from those without — related
  • Different responsiveness-determination approaches (status vs. slope, criteria, domains, cut points) identify different students as adequate and inadequate responders, with little or no overlap — related
  • Students with a learning disability started about one standard deviation lower in reading comprehension and did not catch up to typically developing peers by fourth grade, which the authors interpret as a developmental deficit — related
  • Responder Subgroup 1 improved across all measures during intervention and maintained gains long-term except reading comprehension, which regressed — related
  • When Tier 3 inadequate responders are compared with higher-responding peers, treatment groups show large negative effects, indicating they often fail to catch up — related
