# The six included systematic reviews were of medium to high quality, but only two described a synthesis method and meta-analysis proved impossible due to heterogeneity

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` AMSTAR 2 appraisal rated three reviews High Quality, two Moderate Quality and one Low quality; none was rated Critically Low. [→ Snelgrove Huon; Gabbott Ben 2020](#snelgrove-huon-gabbott-ben-2020)
`q2 i?` In the only two reviews describing meta-analysis, synthesis proved impossible because of heterogeneity of the primary studies. [→ Snelgrove Huon; Gabbott Ben 2020 (2)](#snelgrove-huon-gabbott-ben-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Snelgrove Huon; Gabbott Ben 2020

Snelgrove Huon; Gabbott Ben. (2020). Critical analysis of evidence about the impacts on surgical teams of ‘mental practice’ in systematic reviews: a systematic rapid evidence assessment (SREA). BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02131-3

`q2 · i?`

Two authors independently applied the AMSTAR 2 critical appraisal tool to the six included reviews, resolving discrepancies by consensus. The result: "three of the reviews were rated as ‘High Quality’... two of ‘Moderate Quality’ and one of ‘Low quality’".

`q2 · i?`

Quality-issues section of the SREA: in the two reviews describing meta-analysis, "this synthesis proved impossible because of the heterogeneity of the primary studies"; the authors instead used trend analysis and Jadad scoring. The remaining four reviews reported no synthesis method.

> "In the only two reviews where the synthesis method was described as meta-analysis, [37, 40] this synthesis proved impossible because of the heterogeneity of the primary studies."
