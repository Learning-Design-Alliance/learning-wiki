# An adapted SimCalc intervention in Florida produced strong learning gains (ES = 1.09), significantly higher posttests than predicted from the Texas control group

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Florida 7th-grade classrooms using the adapted Texas unit showed pretest-to-posttest gains of ES = 1.09, and an HLM found posttest scores significantly higher than Texas-control-based predictions (contrast coefficient 6.96, p < .001). [→ Roschelle 2018](#roschelle-2018)

## Evidence

### Roschelle 2018

Roschelle, J., Tipton, E., Shechtman, N., Vahey, P. (2018). Generalizability of a Technology-Based Intervention to Enhance Conceptual Understanding in Mathematics (SimCalc Technical Report 10). Menlo Park, CA: SRI International. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/7a123d3c-22ec-46db-b7cf-709c3aa65583

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of 13 Florida treatment teachers (no Florida control group) to the Texas control group, with an HLM (Table 5) showing a Florida/Texas contrast of 6.96, p = 0.000. The article reports "learning gains ... were strong in the Florida classrooms using the treatment (ES=1.09)."

> "As Figure 5 shows, learning gains from pretest to posttest were strong in the Florida classrooms using the treatment (ES=1.09)."
