The report's argumentative analysis of composite-indicator accountability systems cites prior research on gaming and test perversion (e.g., Booher-Jennings, 2005; Forte, 2010; Ho, 2008) and quotes Campbell's warning that quantitative social indicators used for decision making become subject to corruption pressures.

"Under test-based accountability, consequences and/or rewards are attached to outcomes, not behaviors. This is problematic because both desired behaviors (e.g., efforts to improve teaching practice) and undesired behaviors (teaching to the test, narrowing of the curriculum, cheating, etc.) are reinforced equally, so long as the outcome is the same"
