# Trained residents use more assessment and supportive utterances in simulated triadic breaking bad news consultations

> **Currency** · newest study 2013 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Trained residents showed significant increases in total assessment utterances (RR=1.83; P<0.001) and total support utterances (RR=1.58; P<0.001), including open directive questions, checking questions, other question types, and acknowledgements. [→ Merckaert I 2013](#merckaert-i-2013)
`q3 i?` The number of information utterances by residents remained unchanged after training (total information RR=0.95; P=0.612), with a significant decrease only in procedural information utterances (RR=0.83; P=0.047). [→ Merckaert I 2013 (2)](#merckaert-i-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Merckaert I 2013

Merckaert I, Liénard A, Libert Y, Bragard I, Delvaux N, Etienne A-M, Marchal S, Meunier J, Reynaert C, Slachmuylder J-L, Razavi D. (2013). Is it possible to improve the breaking bad news skills of residents when a relative is present? A randomised study. British Journal of Cancer. https://doi.org/10.1038/bjc.2013.615

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

LaComm content analysis of transcripts in the randomised study (n=95), analysed with GEE. Trained residents' assessment and support utterance rates rose significantly; total support utterances also increased (RR=1.58; P<0.001).

> "Regression analyses showed a significant increase in the rate of open directive questions (RR=2.14; P=0.001), checking questions (RR=1.66; P=0.034), ‘other types of questions' (RR=2.06; P<0.001), and total assessment types (RR=1.83; P<0.001) used by trained residents compared with untrained residents."

### Merckaert I 2013 (2)

Merckaert I, Liénard A, Libert Y, Bragard I, Delvaux N, Etienne A-M, Marchal S, Meunier J, Reynaert C, Slachmuylder J-L, Razavi D. (2013). Is it possible to improve the breaking bad news skills of residents when a relative is present? A randomised study. British Journal of Cancer. https://doi.org/10.1038/bjc.2013.615

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same LaComm/GEE analysis: total information utterances did not change significantly (RR=0.95; P=0.612); only procedural information decreased significantly (RR=0.83; P=0.047).

> "GEE analyses identified a significant decrease in the rate of procedural information utterances used by trained residents (RR=0.83; P=0.047)."
