# Probability ranking, consistent with novice fraction-comparison strategies such as numerator-denominator differences, is the most common incorrect justification students give for probability ratio answers

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among students not using the Boltzmann factor, roughly half at each school ranked probabilities as their primary justification, and none gave a physical explanation for the ranking. [→ Trevor I. Smith 2015](#trevor-i-smith-2015)

## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith 2015

Trevor I. Smith, Donald B. Mountcastle, and John R. Thompson. (2015). Student Understanding of the Boltzmann Factor. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020123](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020123)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Grounded-theory analysis of written PRQ explanations at both schools; roughly half of non-Boltzmann-factor users (15 of 26 at School 1, 13 of 28 at School 2) used probability ranking. "the most common incorrect justiﬁcation at both schools."

> "Using probability ranking, ei- ther explicit or implied, is the most common incorrect justiﬁcation at both schools, and no students provided a physical explanation for why the probabilities of the various energy levels would be ranked as they claimed."
