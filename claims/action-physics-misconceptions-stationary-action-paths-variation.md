## Evidence

### Lachlan P. McGinness and C. M. Savage 2016

Lachlan P. McGinness and C. M. Savage. (2016). Developing an Action Concept Inventory. arXiv:1605.01786. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010133](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010133)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

From think-aloud interview records and confidence-rated inventory answers, the article identifies the belief that "only minimum action paths are physical" as a misconception about the principle of stationary action; many students chose the associated distractor and assigned it high confidence.

> "One misconception about the principle of stationary action concept is the idea that only minimum action paths are physical. This is an example of where the associated distractor was assigned high conﬁdence by many students who chose it."

### Lachlan P. McGinness and C. M. Savage 2016 (2)

Lachlan P. McGinness and C. M. Savage. (2016). Developing an Action Concept Inventory. arXiv:1605.01786. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010133](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010133)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Interview and assessment analysis found students commonly misconceived mechanics paths as "spatial paths, rather than as space-time worldlines"; in optics paths are spatial but not in mechanics, a distinction the authors say challenges instructors.

> "Students commonly misconceived paths in mechanics as spatial paths, rather than as space-time worldlines. In the case of optics, the paths are spatial, but not in mechanics [31]."

### Lachlan P. McGinness and C. M. Savage 2016 (3)

Lachlan P. McGinness and C. M. Savage. (2016). Developing an Action Concept Inventory. arXiv:1605.01786. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010133](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010133)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Think-aloud interview records revealed the misconception that zero first-order variation at a stationary point means "there is no variation at all", e.g. that second-order variation is also zero.

> "One misconception was that if the ﬁrst order variation is zero, at a stationary point, then there is no variation at all, e.g. that the second order variation is also zero."
