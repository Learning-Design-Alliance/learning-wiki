## Evidence

### Ramjaun Aliya 2015

Ramjaun Aliya, Sudarshan Monisha, Patakfalvi Laura, Tamblyn Robyn, Meguerditchian Ari N. (2015). Educating medical trainees on medication reconciliation: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4373246/

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Experimental study (n=20) of internal medicine residents making a phone call to recently discharged patients within 72 hours; attitude and competence outcomes showed "no change" per Table 1, and the review reports "no appreciable impact on residents' competence".

> "Similar results were obtained by Weisman et al., where they found that having a resident make a phone call to recently discharged patient had no appreciable impact on residents' competence in performing medication reconciliation and related tasks [25]"

### Ramjaun Aliya 2015 (2)

Ramjaun Aliya, Sudarshan Monisha, Patakfalvi Laura, Tamblyn Robyn, Meguerditchian Ari N. (2015). Educating medical trainees on medication reconciliation: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4373246/

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Experimental Hospital to Home program (n=13) in which internal medicine residents visited recently discharged patients at home or nursing home; residents rated themselves able to perform discharge tasks fairly or quite well, but "their skills in conducting medication reconciliation had not significantly improved".

> "When comparing their ability to perform certain discharge tasks before and after the intervention however, participating residents stated that their skills in conducting medication reconciliation had not significantly improved"
