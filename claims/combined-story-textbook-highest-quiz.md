# In Experiment 2, reading the textbook-plus-story produced higher quiz scores than reading either version of the textbook alone, while the story outperformed the photocopied textbook

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` A four-condition ANOVA showed a significant main effect of text genre on quiz scores, with textbook-plus-story highest and typed and photocopied textbooks not differing. [→ Janit 2011](#janit-2011)

## Evidence

### Janit 2011

Janit, Adrian; Hammock, Georgina; and Richardson, Deborah. (2011). The Power of Fiction: Reading Stories in Abnormal Psychology. International Journal for the Scholarship of Teaching and Learning. https://doi.org/10.20429/ijsotl.2011.050116

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 with 86 undergraduates assigned to story, photocopied textbook, typed textbook, or textbook-plus-story conditions, tested immediately and three weeks later. The ANOVA showed "text genre, F (3, 82) = 8. 57, p = .001, η2 = .24"; means were 15.64, 14.94, 13.48, and 12.58.

> "Both of the m anipulated variables had an impact on the quiz scores: text genre, F (3, 82) = 8. 57, p = .001, η2 = .24, and time assessed, F (1, 82) = 45.03, p = .001, η2 = .35."
