# Reactive FOF is more time-consuming than proactive FOF, reducing opportunities to elaborate on preselected grammar points

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Naeimeh Bakshiri 2011

Naeimeh Bakshiri, Arshya Keyvanfar. (2011). Time Matters: Proactive vs. Reactive FOF. i-manager's Journal of English Language Teaching, Vol. 1 No. 2, April - June 2011. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1071093

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Authors' interpretive analysis (type e) drawing on the teacher-researcher's minute-to-minute classroom experience across the five classes; the 20-minute grammar period "was automatically extended in the reactive group." No effect size is printed for this mechanism account.

> "Since reactive focus on form involves a responsive teaching intervention in the form of occasional shifts to important errors (Long & Robinson, 1998), it inevitably becomes more time consuming, giving fewer opportunities to elaborate on key grammar points of the lesson."
