# Type of reflection and order of abstraction show no significant main effects or interaction on calculation scores

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Neither the interaction (F(1, 22) = 1.35) nor the main effects of reflection type (F(1, 22) = .003, p = .96) or order of abstraction (F(1, 22) = .83, p = .37) were significant for calculation scores. [→ DiBenedetto 2017](#dibenedetto-2017)

## Evidence

### DiBenedetto 2017

DiBenedetto, C. A., Blythe, J. M., & Myers, B. E. (2017). Effects of the Order of Abstraction and Type of Reflection on Content Knowledge when Teaching Experientially in a High School Classroom. Journal of Agricultural Education, 58(2), 67-82. https://doi.org/10.5032/jae.2017.02067

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

ANOVA on calculation scores in the same 2 x 2 randomized study found no significant main effects; the interaction (F(1, 22) = 1.35) was also non-significant. No effect sizes were printed.

> "There was no significant main effect of the type of reflection, F (1, 22) = .003, p = .96, or order of abstraction on the calculation scores F (1, 22) = .83, p = .37, resulting in a failure to reject the fifth and sixth null hypotheses."
