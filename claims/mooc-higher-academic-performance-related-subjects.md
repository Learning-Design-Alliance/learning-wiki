# Health sciences undergraduates who completed a MOOC achieved significantly higher academic performance in subjects related to the MOOC content

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Students who took the MOOC scored significantly higher in the four subjects whose content was related to the MOOC, with medium effect sizes (Cohen's d = 0.51, 0.53, 0.50, 0.66). [→ Martín-Valero Rocío 2021](#martin-valero-rocio-2021)

## Evidence

### Martín-Valero Rocío 2021

Martín-Valero Rocío, Pastora-Bernal José-Manuel, Ortiz-Ortigosa Lucía, Casuso-Holgado María Jesús, Pérez-Cabezas Veronica, Ruiz-Párraga Gema Teresa. (2021). The Usefulness of a Massive Open Online Course about Postural and Technological Adaptations to Enhance Academic Performance and Empathy in Health Sciences Undergraduates. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph182010672

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of means (Student's t-test for independent samples) between 176 MOOC participants and 205 controls across five subjects; Table 2 reports Cohen's d values of 0.51, 0.53, 0.50 and 0.66 for the significant contrasts, described as "a medium or high effect size power".

> "The results (Table 2) showed statistically significant differences in academic performance obtained between both groups in the four subjects whose content was related to the MOOC, in favor of those students who had realized the MOOC."
