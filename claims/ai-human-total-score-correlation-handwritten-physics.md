# AI total scores correlate strongly with official human scores on handwritten physics assessments (r = 0.91–0.97 in Round I; 0.93–0.96 in Round II)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r3` · `q2` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Across three assessments (OE1, OE2, QM), AI total scores tracked official human scores with Pearson correlations of 0.91–0.97 in Round I and 0.93–0.96 in Round II. [→ Praveen Pathak 2026](#praveen-pathak-2026)

## Evidence

### Praveen Pathak 2026

Praveen Pathak, Siddharth Tiwary, Charudatt Kadolkar, Vijay Singh, David Rakestraw, Shirish Pathare, and Anwesh Mazumdar. (2026). Large-scale AI grading of handwritten physics assessments: Score agreement and Olympiad team selection outcomes. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20521

`q2 · i3` · `design · r3`

Retrospective AI grading of 10,364 scanned pages from 520 handwritten submissions across OE1, OE2, and QM, each graded twice with official rubrics and no access to human marks. The article reports "The Pearson correlations are high in RI (r= 0.91–0.97) and remain high in RII (r= 0.93–0.96)."
