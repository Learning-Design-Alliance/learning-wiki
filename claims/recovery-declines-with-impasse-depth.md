# Next-turn recovery from impasses declines steadily as impasse depth increases, with help-seeking impasses declining most sharply

## Evidence

### Ahtisham 2026

Ahtisham, B., Vanacore, K., Napoli, A., Arens, J., Ionova, K., Cohn, C., Salehi, S., & Kizilcec, R. (2026). Examining Variation in How Guided AI Tutors Resolve Student Impasses. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38346 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.38346](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2609.38346)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive next-turn recovery rates by depth from 6,630 impasse turns, with 95% CIs from a session-level cluster bootstrap (Figure 1). A multivariable logistic model on N=6,163 turns found "each additional turn at an impasse was associated with 13% lower odds of recovery (AOR = 0.87, 95% CI [0.81, 0.94], p<.001)". No standardized effect size beyond the odds ratio is printed.

> "Across all impasse types, next-turn recovery fell steadily as impasses persisted, from 39.7% at onset to 32.6% at depth 2, 26.2% at depth 3, and 18.8% at depth 6 or more, less than half the onset rate"

### Ahtisham 2026 (2)

Ahtisham, B., Vanacore, K., Napoli, A., Arens, J., Ionova, K., Cohn, C., Salehi, S., & Kizilcec, R. (2026). Examining Variation in How Guided AI Tutors Resolve Student Impasses. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38346. [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.38346](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2609.38346)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Type-specific recovery trajectories from the same corpus show help-seeking impasses were most recoverable at onset but declined most sharply, while uncertainty remained comparatively recoverable through depth 4 (33.3% at depth 4).

> "Help-seeking was initially the most recoverable state (47.0% at onset) but declined most sharply once initial assistance had failed (25.3% at depth 3; 12.5% at depth≥6)."
