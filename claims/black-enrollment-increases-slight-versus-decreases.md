# Black undergraduate enrollment increased at 74% of selective private institutions from 2000 to 2020, but the average increase (2.3 percentage points) was smaller than the average decrease (3.7 percentage points) among institutions where enrollment declined.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Ninety institutions (74%) increased Black undergraduate enrollment between 2000 and 2020, with an average increase of 2.3 percentage points; no institution increased by more than 10 percentage points. [→ Gabriel Montague 2023](#gabriel-montague-2023)
`q2 i?` Among the 26.2% of institutions where Black enrollment decreased, the average decrease of 3.7 percentage points was larger than the average increase. [→ Gabriel Montague 2023](#gabriel-montague-2023)

## Evidence

### Gabriel Montague 2023

Gabriel Montague, Ed.M., Higher Education Research Analyst. (2023). 'Segregation Forever'?: The Continued Underrepresentation of Black Undergraduates at the Nation's 122 Most Selective Private Colleges and Universities. `q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Ed Trust analysis of IPEDS Fall 2000-2001 and Fall 2019-2021 enrollment data for 122 institutions. Of the 90 institutions with increases, 81 (90%) saw rises of less than 5 percentage points; "the average increase was 2.3 percentage points."

> "Ninety colleges and universities (74%) increased their Black undergraduate student enrollment; but the average increase was 2.3 percentage points."

### Gabriel Montague 2023 (2)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same IPEDS-based analysis of the 32 declining institutions: 22 had decreases under 2 percentage points and four schools (13%) had decreases exceeding 10 percentage points. The report states the "average decrease was 3.7 percentage points."

> "Among the 26.2% of institutions that had a decrease in Black undergraduate student enrollment, the average decrease was 3.7 percentage points, which is larger than the average percentage point increase."
