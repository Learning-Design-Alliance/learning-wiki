## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2015

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2015). Upper-division student difficulties with Separation of Variables. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020131)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple-response CUE data from CU E&M 1 students on the Cartesian general-solution question: "only two-thirds of CU students (66%, N=95 of 145) selected the correct expression", in contrast to the results on the exam question.

> "In contrast to the results on the exam question, only two-thirds of CU students (66%, N=95 of 145) selected the correct expression"

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2015 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2015). Upper-division student difficulties with Separation of Variables. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020131)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

The same multiple-response CUE question administered at external institutions showed a more pronounced trend, with "almost a quarter of students (23%, N=37 of 161) selecting either purely sinusoidal or purely exponential dependence".

> "This trend is even more pronounced in student populations at other institutions, with almost a quarter of students (23%, N=37 of 161) selecting either purely sinusoidal or purely exponential dependence."
