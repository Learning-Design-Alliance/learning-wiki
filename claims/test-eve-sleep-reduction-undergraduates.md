## Evidence

### Estevan I 2021

Estevan I, Sardi R, Tejera AC, Silva A, Tassino B. (2021). Should I study or should I go (to sleep)? The influence of test schedule on the sleep behavior of undergraduates and its association with performance. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0247104

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Survey #1: questionnaire completed immediately after a mid-term test by 97 first-year School of Sciences students. The article reports the test-eve sleep reduction and all-nighter rate: "Sleep duration was reduced in 2.1 ± 0.2 h".

> "Sleep duration was reduced in 2.1 ± 0.2 h (paired-samples t-test = 9.4, p < 0.001), with 13 students (13.4%) being all-nighters."

### Estevan I 2021 (2)

Estevan I, Sardi R, Tejera AC, Silva A, Tassino B. (2021). Should I study or should I go (to sleep)? The influence of test schedule on the sleep behavior of undergraduates and its association with performance. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0247104

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Survey #2: questionnaire after a Neurobiology mid-term answered by 252 School of Psychology students distributed across four test shifts; paired-samples tests per shift show sleep reduction, e.g. "sleep in 8:00 shift was reduced in 3.6 ± 0.3 h".

> "sleep in 8:00 shift was reduced in 3.6 ± 0.3 h (paired-samples t-test = 10.9, p < 0.001), in 9:45 shift sleep was reduced in 3.0 ± 0.3 h (paired-samples t-test = 9.1, p < 0.001)"
