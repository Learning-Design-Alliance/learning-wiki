# Having peer observers assess the team leader with a checklist during simulation-based ABCDE training yields higher in-training performance than training without peer assessment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Trainees whose peer observers assessed the team leader's performance scored significantly higher on overall in-training performance (checklist and global rating) than controls. [→ Krogh Charlotte Loumann 2014](#krogh-charlotte-loumann-2014)

## Evidence

### Krogh Charlotte Loumann 2014

Krogh Charlotte Loumann, Ringsted Charlotte, Kromann Charles B., Rasmussen Maria Birkvad, Todsen Tobias, Jørgensen Rasmus Lundhus, Jacobsen Rikke Borre, Dahl Jørgen B., Konge Lars. (2014). Effect of Engaging Trainees by Assessing Peer Performance: A Randomised Controlled Trial Using Simulated Patient Scenarios. BioMed Research International. https://doi.org/10.1155/2014/610591

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomised trial in which 84 final-year medical students were allocated to peer-assessment or control conditions during simulation-based ABCDE training; blinded expert raters scored videotaped performances. The "mean checklist score of 20.87 (SD 2.51) versus 19.14 (SD 2.65)" favoured the intervention group.

> "The intervention group obtained a significantly higher overall in-training performance score than the control group: mean checklist score of 20.87 (SD 2.51) versus 19.14 (SD 2.65) P = 0.003 and mean global rating of 3.25 SD (0.99) versus 2.95 (SD 1.09) P = 0.014."
