## Evidence

### Allensworth 2026

Allensworth, E.M., Antony, M., Delgado, W., & de la Torre, M. (2026). Connection, trust, and learning: Student attendance in the middle and high school grades following the COVID-19 pandemic. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/connection-trust-and-learning

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis linking CPS attendance records to test scores and GPAs for grades 6-11 in pre- and post-pandemic years. The brief reports correlations "somewhat larger than in pre-pandemic years" and similar absence relationships with test-score gains.

> "In post-pandemic years, the correlations of absences with standardized test scores ranged from -0.29 to -0.36 across the grade levels, while the correlations of absences with GPAs ranged from -0.41 to -0.68. These correlations were somewhat larger than in pre-pandemic years."

### Allensworth 2026 (2)

Allensworth, E.M., Antony, M., Delgado, W., & de la Torre, M. (2026). Connection, trust, and learning: Student attendance in the middle and high school grades following the COVID-19 pandemic. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/connection-trust-and-learning

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Figure 3 descriptive analysis of average test scores by absence rate for sixth and ninth grade; the 16-point difference is described as about one-half of an IAR standard deviation, similar to the 50th vs. 30th percentile difference.

> "the difference in average Illinois Assessment of Readiness (IAR) test scores for sixth-graders who missed 10% of days relative to those who missed no days of school (at the far left of the regression line) was about 16 points in both pre-pandemic years (732 to 716) and in post-pandemic years (734 to 718)."
