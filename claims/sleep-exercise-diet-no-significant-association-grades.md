## Evidence

### Alhroub Wasef 2026

Alhroub Wasef, Jabareen Maaweya, Fasfoos Ahmad, Bseiso Omair, Deeik Joana, Ahmoud Ahmad Hasan, Al-Manasrah Amro K., Thalji Ibraheem Mohammed, Arish Ahmad Basil Abu, Adawi Iyad Moayad, Alqawasmeh Mustafa Murad, Hroub Monther M., Hroub Musbah, Ahmad Jamal. (2026). Lifestyle behaviors during exam periods and their association with academic performance among Palestinian medical students: a cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09591-z

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Mann–Whitney U tests in the 403-student cross-sectional study found "no significant differences in average grades based on regular diet patterns (p = 0.874), exercise during exams (p = 0.756), or reported barriers to university attendance (p = 0.605)". No effect sizes were printed, so equivalence was not tested.

> "Results of Mann–Whitney U tests showed that there were no significant differences in average grades based on regular diet patterns (p = 0.874), exercise during exams (p = 0.756), or reported barriers to university attendance (p = 0.605)"

### Alhroub Wasef 2026 (2)

Alhroub Wasef, Jabareen Maaweya, Fasfoos Ahmad, Bseiso Omair, Deeik Joana, Ahmoud Ahmad Hasan, Al-Manasrah Amro K., Thalji Ibraheem Mohammed, Arish Ahmad Basil Abu, Adawi Iyad Moayad, Alqawasmeh Mustafa Murad, Hroub Monther M., Hroub Musbah, Ahmad Jamal. (2026). Lifestyle behaviors during exam periods and their association with academic performance among Palestinian medical students: a cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09591-z

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Spearman correlation analysis in the same cross-sectional study found "no significant correlations with average grades (all p > 0.05)" for energy drinks, fruit intake, fast food, snacks, sleep duration, and time alone. No effect sizes were printed for these null findings.

> "All other variables—including energy drinks, fruit intake, fast food, snacks, sleep duration, and time alone—showed no significant correlations with average grades (all p > 0.05)"
