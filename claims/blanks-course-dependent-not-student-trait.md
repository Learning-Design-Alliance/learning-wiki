# Uncollected exam item responses are a course-level rather than student-level variable: class rates range widely and blank-leaving in one course does not predict blank-leaving in the next

## Evidence

### Cassandra Paul 2026

Cassandra Paul, David J. Webb, and Mary Kate Chessey. (2026). Missing Data on Physics Exams: Demographic Patterns, Course-Level Predictions, and Implications for Equity. [doi:10.1103/b4q5-dkn5](https://doi.org/10.1103/b4q5-dkn5)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of all 108 CourseI and CourseII classes (Fig. 4), plotting the fraction of students leaving at least one blank against the class-average number of blanks. The authors found that "in 11 of these classes more than 74% of the students left at least one blank during the term," and since classes are essentially identical except for teachers and exams, the exam set likely plays a large part.

> "In 11 of these classes fewer than 10% of the students left a blank the entire term and, on the other end, in 11 of these classes more than 74% of the students left at least one blank during the term"

### Cassandra Paul 2026 (2)

Cassandra Paul, David J. Webb, and Mary Kate Chessey. (2026). Missing Data on Physics Exams: Demographic Patterns, Course-Level Predictions, and Implications for Equity. [doi:10.1103/b4q5-dkn5](https://doi.org/10.1103/b4q5-dkn5)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-course comparison for the subset of students with both CourseI and CourseII scores, correlating whether students left at least one blank in each course. The authors report "Pearson's correlation = 0.11 (P<10−3)" for leaving at least one blank, and a similarly weak 0.19 for fraction of blanks, concluding blank-leaving is not largely controlled by the student.

> "We find that there is very little correlation between these numbers, Pearson's correlation = 0.11 (P<10−3)"
