## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across 25 observations of five digital providers, technology was generally reliable and accessible (.78 average rating) but instructional software rarely adapted to student needs (.30) or was used for higher-order thinking skills (.16). [→ Burch 2016](#burch-2016)

## Evidence

### Burch 2016

Burch, P., Good, A., & Heinrich, C. (2016). Improving Access to, Quality, and the Effectiveness of Digital Tutoring in K–12 Education. Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis. https://doi.org/10.3102/0162373715592706

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Pilot digital-specific indicators rated on a standardized observation instrument across 25 observations of five digital providers in four districts (2012–2013). Ratings were recorded from 0 to 1, where a "1" would indicate a rating score of "2" in every observable instance.

> "As the data in Table 4 also indicate, there was little evidence of the use of technology to use higher order thinking skills. Often, the questions presented to students were simply “digitized worksheets” that did not require students to actually use technology to apply, evaluate, or create concepts."
