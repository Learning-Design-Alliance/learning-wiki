# Group performance on the QMFPS exceeded individual performance (72% vs 53%) after lecture-based instruction

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Upper-level undergraduate and first-year graduate students scored higher on the QMFPS when answering in groups (72%, SD 15%) than individually (53%, SD 18%). [→ Mary Jane Brundage 2023](#mary-jane-brundage-2023)

## Evidence

### Mary Jane Brundage 2023

Mary Jane Brundage, Alysa Malespina, and Chandralekha Singh. (2023). Peer interaction facilitates co-construction of knowledge in quantum mechanics. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020133](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020133)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Classroom study results: the same students took the QMFPS individually and then in groups. The printed averages show "the average score for groups was 72% (SD = 15%)" versus 53% (SD 18%) individually; no effect size is reported.

> "Overall, the average score for individual students on the QMFPS was 53% (standard deviation (SD) = 18%), and the average score for groups was 72% (SD = 15%)."

### Mary Jane Brundage 2023 (2)

Mary Jane Brundage, Alysa Malespina, and Chandralekha Singh. (2023). Peer interaction facilitates co-construction of knowledge in quantum mechanics. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020133](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020133)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Procedure as printed: "Students (N=78) first took the QMFPS individually" in a 50-minute period, then took it again in groups of two or three in the next class without instructor assistance.

> "Students (N=78) first took the QMFPS individually and were given a full 50-minute class period to complete the survey."
