# For post-test reflection on mistakes, a meaningful reward with only minimal scaffolding worked best, while students given detailed solutions did less well

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In post-test reflection interventions, the best support for learning from mistakes was a meaningful reward with minimal scaffolding. [→ Singh 2023](#singh-2023)
`q2 i?` Students given a detailed solution during reflection did less well than students restricted to their own resources. [→ Singh 2023 (2)](#singh-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Singh 2023

Singh, C., Maries, A., Heller, K., & Heller, P. (2023). Instructional Strategies that Foster Effective Problem-Solving. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1063/9780735425477_017](https://doi.org/10.1063/9780735425477_017)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports studies of students reflecting after a test (Yerushalmi et al., 2012a, 2012b): students could correct mistakes to regain 50% of lost points, and "a meaningful reward but only minimal scaffolding" was the best intervention. No effect size is printed.

> "In the studies of students after taking a test, the best intervention for students to learn from their mistakes was providing them a meaningful reward but only minimal scaffolding."

### Singh 2023 (2)

Singh, C., Maries, A., Heller, K., & Heller, P. (2023). Instructional Strategies that Foster Effective Problem-Solving. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1063/9780735425477_017](https://doi.org/10.1063/9780735425477_017)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports that in the same reflection experiments, students "given a detailed solution to the problem did less well" than those using only their own resources, textbook and notes; the review attributes this to superficial engagement without underlying concept work.

> "However, the students that were given a detailed solution to the problem did less well than student who could only use their own resources, textbook and notes)."
