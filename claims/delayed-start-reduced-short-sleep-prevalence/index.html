Evidence¶

Li Shenghui 2013¶

Li Shenghui, Arguelles Lester, Jiang Fan, Chen Wenjuan, Jin Xingming, Yan Chonghuai, Tian Ying, Hong Xiumei, Qian Ceng, Zhang Jun, Wang Xiaobin, Shen Xiaoming. (2013). Sleep, School Performance, and a School-Based Intervention among School-Aged Children: A Sleep Series Study in China. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0067928

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Post-intervention survey comparison across the three school groups in the intervention trial (Table 5, chi-square test). Short sleep prevalence was 44.27% (control), 22.88% (intervention 1), and 11.87% (intervention 2), a difference the table marks significant (χ2 = 23.04, p<0.001); no standardized effect size is printed.

Li Shenghui 2013 (2)¶

Li Shenghui, Arguelles Lester, Jiang Fan, Chen Wenjuan, Jin Xingming, Yan Chonghuai, Tian Ying, Hong Xiumei, Qian Ceng, Zhang Jun, Wang Xiaobin, Shen Xiaoming. (2013). Sleep, School Performance, and a School-Based Intervention among School-Aged Children: A Sleep Series Study in China. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0067928

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Authors' interpretation of the intervention trial outcome in the discussion: despite delayed start times, "only a minority of children got the recommended 10 or more hours of sleep" and about half still reported daytime sleepiness. This is the authors' stated caveat, not a separately tested result.

"it should be pointed out that only a minority of children got the recommended 10 or more hours of sleep and still approximately half of children continued to report daytime sleepiness after the change of the school start time."
