# Both PTEBL and traditional Stop the Bleed courses significantly increase self-reported willingness to help in a trauma emergency, with no between-group difference

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The proportion preferring to help rose significantly in both groups (PTEBL 50.6% to 96.1%; traditional 65.8% to 90.8%; P < 0.001 within each), with no significant between-group difference. [→ Zhao Wanchen 2024](#zhao-wanchen-2024)

## Evidence

### Zhao Wanchen 2024

Zhao Wanchen, Cao Yangbo, Hu Liangrong, Lu Chenxiao, Liu Gaoming, Gong Matthew, He Jinshen. (2024). A randomized controlled trial comparison of PTEBL and traditional teaching methods in “Stop the Bleed” training. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05457-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre-post questionnaire on rescue attitude in the RCT: within-group increases were significant (P < 0.001) in both groups, but "no statistically significant differences (P > 0.05) between the PTEBL group and the traditional group" in willingness to help.

> "There were also statistically significant differences (P < 0.001) in the traditional group. But no statistically significant differences (P > 0.05) between the PTEBL group and the traditional group."
