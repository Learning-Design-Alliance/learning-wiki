## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Relative to a mastery goal, a performance goal produced marginally greater utilization of high-quality information (ηp² = 0.05), and significantly greater utilization than no goal (ηp² = 0.10). [→ Poortvliet 2007](#poortvliet-2007)

## Evidence

### Poortvliet 2007

Poortvliet, P. M., Janssen, O., Van Yperen, N. W., & Van de Vliert, E. (2007). Achievement Goals and Interpersonal Behavior: How Mastery and Performance Goals Shape Information Exchange. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin. https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167207305536

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Study 1's Winter Survival Exercise experiment (N = 74, three goal conditions) measured utilization as the discrepancy between Spearman correlations of initial and final rankings with the experts' ranking; the quote reports the contrast statistics including the marginal p = .06 effect.

> "As expected, contrast analysis revealed that, relative to mastery goal manipulation, performance goal manipulation produced a marginally greater utilization of the high-quality information allegedly provided by the exchange partner, F(1, 69) = 3.67, p = .06, ηp² = 0.05. The performance goal condition also led to greater information utilization than the no-goal condition, F(1, 69) = 7.42, p = .01, ηp² = 0.10."
