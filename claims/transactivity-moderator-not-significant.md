# Whether a CSCL script prompts transactive activities does not significantly moderate its effects on domain-specific knowledge, and for collaboration skills the difference runs opposite to the expected direction

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The difference in effects on domain-specific knowledge between scripts prompting transactive activities and scripts not prompting them was not statistically significant; for collaboration skills, scripts not prompting transactive activities showed the larger effect (d = 2.15 vs. 0.62, from 3 and 9 effect sizes). [→ Freydis Vogel & Christof Wecker & Ingo Kollar & Frank Fischer 2016](#freydis-vogel-christof-wecker-ingo-kollar-frank-fischer-2016)

## Evidence

### Freydis Vogel & Christof Wecker & Ingo Kollar & Frank Fischer 2016

Freydis Vogel & Christof Wecker & Ingo Kollar & Frank Fischer. (2016). Socio-Cognitive Scaffolding with Computer-Supported Collaboration Scripts: a Meta-Analysis. Educ Psychol Rev (2017) 29:477–511. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-016-9361-7

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Meta-analytic moderator analysis comparing scripts prompting transactive activities (d = 0.22 per Table 1) with scripts not prompting them (d = 0.05). No significant difference; post hoc power was only 8.2%. For collaboration skills the authors report the subgroup difference as "not statistically significant in the expected direction, Q(df = 1) = 22.37, p < .001": scripts not prompting transactive activities had the larger mean effect (d = 2.15, k = 3 effect sizes) than scripts prompting them (d = 0.62, k = 9 effect sizes), the opposite of the hypothesis.

> "With respect to effects on domain-specific knowledge, the difference between the two subgroups was not statistically significant, Q(df = 1) = 0.28, p = .60. Post hoc power analysis indicated a statistical power of 8.2 %."
