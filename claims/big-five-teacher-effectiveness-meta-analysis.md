# Teacher extraversion, conscientiousness, emotional stability, and openness are each positively associated with teacher effectiveness, while agreeableness is not a significant correlate

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` In a random-effects meta-analysis, extraversion (r = .17), conscientiousness (r = .13), emotional stability (r = .10), and openness (r = .10) were significant positive correlates of teacher effectiveness, whereas agreeableness was not (r = .03, p > .05). [→ Lisa E. Kim 2019](#lisa-e-kim-2019)

## Evidence

### Lisa E. Kim 2019

Lisa E. Kim, Verena Jörg & Robert M. Klassen. (2019). A Meta-Analysis of the Effects of Teacher Personality on Teacher Effectiveness and Burnout. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9458-2

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 25 studies (total N = 6294) pooling Pearson r coefficients transformed via Fisher's z. The pooled correlations for the four significant domains are small, with extraversion largest at "r = .17, p < .05" and agreeableness non-significant at r = .03.

> "For teacher effectiveness (k = 14 to 17), all Big Five domains, except for agreeableness, were significant correlates, with the greatest effect sizes for extraversion (r = .17, p < .05), conscientiousness (r = .13, p < .05), emotional stability (r = .10, p < .05), openness (r = .10, p < .05), and lastly agreeableness (r = .03, p > .05)."
