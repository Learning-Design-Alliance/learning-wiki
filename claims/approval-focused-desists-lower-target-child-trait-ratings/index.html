Evidence¶

Rice 1973¶

Rice, W. K., Jr. (1973). Effects of Discipline Techniques on Children's Personality-Trait Inferences. Ph.D. dissertation, The University of Oklahoma. University Microfilms No. 74-12,322. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Effects+of+Discipline+Techniques+on+Children%27s+Personality-Trait+Inferences

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Randomized experiment: 32 boys and 32 girls randomly assigned to hear approval-focused or task-focused desist tapes. The ANOVA on personality-trait ratings of the child showed approval-focused desists produced "significantly lower trait- ratings to the target of the desists" (F = 14.79, p < .001); no standardized effect size was printed for this test.

"A 2 (sex of rater) x 2 (type of desist) ANOVA, using the dependent variable PTC revealed that children who heard the approval-focused desists gave significantly lower trait- ratings to the target of the desists than those who heard the task-focused desists (F = 14.79, ^ = l/60, g/C.OOl)."

Rice 1973 (2)¶

Rice, W. K., Jr. (1973). Effects of Discipline Techniques on Children's Personality-Trait Inferences. Ph.D. dissertation, The University of Oklahoma. University Microfilms No. 74-12,322. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Effects+of+Discipline+Techniques+on+Children%27s+Personality-Trait+Inferences

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Same 2x2 ANOVA on trait ratings of the child: the article reports the omega-squared for type of desist as .18, "a moderate amount of the total variance" accounted for by the type of desist. As a variance-accounted-for value it is reported as printed, not converted to a standardized effect size.

"The W for the type of desist was .18. Thus, a moderate amount of the total variance was accounted for by the type of desist."
