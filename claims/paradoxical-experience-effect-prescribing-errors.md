## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants reported a perceived paradoxical influence of experience on error likelihood: less-experienced doctors were considered more likely to err but also more likely to check information sources, while increased experience reduced checking and could increase error risk. ## Evidence

### Duncan Eilidh M 2012

Duncan Eilidh M, Francis Jill J, Johnston Marie, Davey Peter, Maxwell Simon, McKay Gerard A, McLay James, Ross Sarah, Ryan Cristín, Webb David J, Bond Christine. (2012). Learning curves, taking instructions, and patient safety: using a theoretical domains framework in an interview study to investigate prescribing errors among trainee doctors. Implementation Science. https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-7-86

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of interview data on learning curves. Participants described a “perceived paradoxical influence of experience,” reporting that with experience doctors feel more confident, consult external sources less, and may become careless about checking.

> "There was a perceived paradoxical influence of experience on the likelihood of making an error; both low and high levels of experience were reported to be associated with a greater chance of making an error."
