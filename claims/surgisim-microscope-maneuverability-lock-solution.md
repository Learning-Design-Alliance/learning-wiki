## Evidence

### de Lotbiniere-Bassett M 2022

de Lotbiniere-Bassett M, Volpato Batista A, Lai C, El Chemaly T, Dort J, Blevins N, Lui J. (2022). The user experience design of a novel microscope within SurgiSim, a virtual reality surgical simulator. Int J Comput Assist Radiol Surg. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11548-022-02727-8

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Feedback from the four-participant supplemental testing on the microscope boom. The weightless floating microscope head, hand-interaction physics, and two participants moving the head out of its locked position without grip buttons contributed to the maneuverability problem.

> "None of the participants reported that maneuvering the VR microscope felt realistic. This maneuverability presented such a problem that all of the participants reported that they would not use the VR platform, in its current state, for microscope training."

### de Lotbiniere-Bassett M 2022 (2)

de Lotbiniere-Bassett M, Volpato Batista A, Lai C, El Chemaly T, Dort J, Blevins N, Lui J. (2022). The user experience design of a novel microscope within SurgiSim, a virtual reality surgical simulator. Int J Comput Assist Radiol Surg. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11548-022-02727-8

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Design iteration addressing the maneuverability issue: a "lock" solution in which the ungrabbed microscope's simulated weight was set to 500 kg and dropped to around 50 kg when grabbed with the grip button, preventing hands from moving it unintentionally.

> "Whenever the microscope was not being grabbed by the handles, its weight would be set to 500 kg; therefore, the hands would not affect it because it was simply too heavy. Once the hands touched the handles and used the grip button, the simulated microscope weight would be set to around 50 kg"
