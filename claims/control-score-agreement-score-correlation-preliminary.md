## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Because few of the robot's utterances were one-up, a participant's one-up messages were more likely to pair with the robot's one-down messages to form complementary transactions, providing some support for a correlation between control and agreement scores. ## Evidence

### Amanda Buddemeyer 2022

Amanda Buddemeyer, Xiaoyi Tian, Erin Walker. (2022). Dominance as an Indicator of Rapport and Learning in Human-Agent Communication. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2212.02361](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2212.02361)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive analysis of coded transcripts from 14 students, where the agreement score is the percentage of transactions coded complementary. The authors report a "mean agreement score is 0.5757" and interpret the pairing of student one-up with robot one-down messages as the mechanism.

> "Table 2 also provides some support for a correlation between a student's control score and agreement score. The mean agreement score is 0.5757 and the median is 0.5646."
