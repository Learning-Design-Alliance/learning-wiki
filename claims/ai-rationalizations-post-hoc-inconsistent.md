# Students' moral rationalizations of AI use are typically post hoc and often internally inconsistent

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among 20 interviewed undergraduates, 15 exhibited back-and-forth, fluid, sometimes internally contradictory moral reasoning about AI use, and rationalizations typically explained behavior that had already occurred. [→ Kim 2026](#kim-2026)

## Evidence

### Kim 2026

Kim, Jiyoon; Toyama, Kentaro; Kim, Sangmi; Carroll, John. (2026). "It's OK Because...": The Wild West of Student Rationalization of AI Use in Academic Writing. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29090 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.29090](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2605.29090)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of semi-structured interviews with 20 undergraduates who self-reported AI use for writing, triangulated with chat logs and syllabi. The article reports that "Among the 20 participants, 15 exhibited this pattern" of evolving, contradictory reasoning, and that some participants only considered the ethics when interview questions raised it.

> "Their thinking was fluid, unfinished, and some- times internally contradictory. Among the 20 participants, 15 exhibited this pattern."
