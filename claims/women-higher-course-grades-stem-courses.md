`q3 i?` In a multilevel model, gender significantly predicted course grade, with women (M = 3.01) earning higher grades than men (M = 2.90) in both course types; no course-type effect and no interaction. [→ Bloodhart 2020](#bloodhart-2020)

## Evidence

### Bloodhart 2020

Bloodhart, B., Balgopal, M. M., Casper, A. M. A., Sample McMeeking, L. B., & Fischer, E. V. (2020). Outperforming yet undervalued: Undergraduate women in STEM. PLoS ONE 15(6): e0234685. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0234685

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear multilevel model of 2,866 course grades from nine undergraduate STEM courses, with classroom nesting, gender, and course type as predictors. Women's "slightly higher grades" were statistically significant (F = 4.22, p < .05); the difference is small and the article prints no standardized effect size.

> "Results indicated that gender was a significant predictor of course grade, F (1, 2794.9) = 4.22, p < .05, with women having slightly higher grades (M = 3.01, SE = 0.12) in both life and physical science courses than men (M = 2.90, SE = 0.11)."
