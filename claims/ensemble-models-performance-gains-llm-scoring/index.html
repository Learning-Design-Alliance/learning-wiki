Related Claims¶

  • Model self-confidence, weighted probabilistic scoring, and ensemble modeling each significantly improve LLM-based divergent thinking scoring, raising correlation with human judges from r = 0.781 to r = 0.823 — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Rank-averaged ensembling stabilized predictions but did not outperform the single base model — reports the opposite
  • A stacking ensemble of LG, SVM, NB, KNN and XGB achieved the best fine-tuning F1-score (0.88), outperforming individual models such as SVM (0.86) — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Both pedagogy-expertise-weighted and unanimous-vote ensembles frequently worsen LLM alignment with student learning — reports the opposite
  • Majority-vote ensembling across three runs produced only modest accuracy gains (1% to 5%) with no model showing degradation — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Ensembles of operational raters outperform the LLMs: two raters suffice on average across aspects, but larger ensembles are needed for individual aspects — related
  • Combining human and LLM-generated responses at a 1:1 ratio yields more accurate educational item calibration — related
  • Weighted probabilistic scoring improves LLM-based scoring by considering a broader range of completion possibilities — related
  • In a self-referential framework, LLMs, particularly GPT-4.1, tend to outperform a single operational human rater at identifying relative weaknesses (negative feedback) across several Language and Content aspects — related
  • An ensemble learner outperforms every individual base learner for predicting final exam score and course grade in a large introductory statistics course — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Each generalised plait ensemble (SVM, KNN, DCT) beats the single classifier, majority-vote and stacking ensemble of the same type in perceived task-difficulty classification (e.g., SVM plait error 14.54%, KNN plait 12.84%), though the DCT plait does not beat SVM stacking — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • An unweighted ensemble of LSA and n-gram co-occurrence achieves 96% overall accuracy in summary content assessment, outperforming LSA, n-gram, BLEU and ROUGE on five of six tests — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
