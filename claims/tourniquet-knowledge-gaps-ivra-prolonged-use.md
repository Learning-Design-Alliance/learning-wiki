## Evidence

### Gündoğdu Mert 2026

Gündoğdu Mert, Gülabi Deniz, Baysal Özgür. (2026). Knowledge, training, and practice patterns in pneumatic tourniquet use among orthopedic physicians: a national cross-sectional survey. Archives of Orthopaedic and Trauma Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00402-025-06176-1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive accuracy rates from the 19-item AORN-based knowledge questionnaire in the same 300-physician survey; the printed results show "only 72 participants (24.0%) correctly identified early anesthetic release".

> "For IVRA-related knowledge, only 72 participants (24.0%) correctly identified early anesthetic release as the most dangerous error, while 34% incorrectly selected cuff release as the primary error. Correct identification of the most common complication associated with prolonged tourniquet use was observed in 46.7% of the participants."

### Gündoğdu Mert 2026 (2)

Gündoğdu Mert, Gülabi Deniz, Baysal Özgür. (2026). Knowledge, training, and practice patterns in pneumatic tourniquet use among orthopedic physicians: a national cross-sectional survey. Archives of Orthopaedic and Trauma Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00402-025-06176-1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive results on preventive-measures and cuff-selection items from the same survey's overall knowledge responses section, reporting "255 participants (85.0%) selected all correct options".

> "Regarding preventive measures against tourniquet-related injury, 255 participants (85.0%) selected all correct options. Nearly all participants correctly identified the most important factor in cuff selection (n = 291, 97.0%)."
