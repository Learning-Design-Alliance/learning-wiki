## Evidence

### David S. Weiss 1983

David S. Weiss. (1983). The effects of text segmentation on children's reading comprehension. Discourse Processes, 6:1, 77-89. https://doi.org/10.1080/01638538309544555

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Scheffé follow-up contrasts (p < .05) on the Comprehension main effect in the first passage analysis of 648 grade 4 and 7 students, comparing three text formats. Both phrased formats outperformed prose: "significantly higher than the Comprehension scores obtained with the prose format (M = 33.04%)". No effect size was printed.

> "Comprehension scores for the pausal (M = 38.2%) and syntactic (Ai = 38.7%) phrase formats were significantly higher than the Comprehension scores obtained with the prose format (M = 33.04%). There was no statistical difference between the syntactic and pausal phrase formats."

### David S. Weiss 1983 (2)

David S. Weiss. (1983). The effects of text segmentation on children's reading comprehension. Discourse Processes, 6:1, 77-89. https://doi.org/10.1080/01638538309544555

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi F' analysis of variance (2x3x3x3 Grade by Reading Level by Passage Difficulty by Format design, with participants and passages as random effects) on first-passage comprehension scores. The quote reports the significant main effects; only p-values were printed, so no effect size is reported.

> "Significant Text Segmentation (p < .005), Reading Level (p < .005), and Passage Difficulty [p < .001) effects were evident."
