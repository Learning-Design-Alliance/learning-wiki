# Scores rose significantly from pre-test to post-test in both groups, with a larger mean gain in the flipped classroom group

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2024 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Paired t-tests showed significant pre-test-to-post-test score gains in the flipped classroom group (mean difference 13.4, t=6.09, p=0.001) and the traditional group (mean difference 11.4, t=6.34, p=0.001). [→ Mengesha Assefa Kebad 2024](#mengesha-assefa-kebad-2024)

## Evidence

### Mengesha Assefa Kebad 2024

Mengesha Assefa Kebad, Ayele Habtamu Semagne, Misker Melshew Fenta, Beyna Alemante Tafese. (2024). Assessing the effectiveness of flipped classroom teaching–learning method among undergraduate medical students at gondar university, college of medicine and health sciences: an interventional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06105-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Paired t-tests within each of the two 50-student groups in the quasi-experiment. The flipped group gained 13.4 points (t=6.09, df=49, p=0.001); the traditional group gained 11.4 points (t=6.34, p=0.001). No effect size is printed for either gain.

> "For the Flipped Classroom Group, the mean difference between the pre-test and post-test scores was 13.4 points, with a standard error of 2.2. The t-value for this difference was 6.09 with 49 degrees of freedom, and the p-value was 0.001."
