Evidence¶

Jennie Y. Jiang 2015¶

Jennie Y. Jiang, Susan E. Sporte, Stuart Luppescu. (2015). Analytic Memo: Evaluation Data from the First Year of REACH. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://www.cps.edu/Pages/reachstudents.aspx

q2 · i? · associational · r2

District-wide descriptive analysis of over 11,000 teachers' performance task scores in 2012-13, shown in Figure 3: "Over 20 percent of teachers with performance task scores" topped out at 4.0 and 74 percent received 3.0 or greater. No inferential test reported.

"Over 20 percent of teachers with performance task scores "topped out" at the highest performance level, receiving the highest possible score of 4.0; 74 percent received a 3.0 or greater"

Jennie Y. Jiang 2015 (2)¶

Jennie Y. Jiang, Susan E. Sporte, Stuart Luppescu. (2015). Analytic Memo: Evaluation Data from the First Year of REACH. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://www.cps.edu/Pages/reachstudents.aspx

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive analysis of missing performance task data in 2012-13: "Thirty-six percent of teachers did not receive an individual performance task score" and instead received the default score of 3.12. Causes cited include teacher-of-record changes, grading, and uploading difficulties.

"Thirty-six percent of teachers did not receive an individual performance task score and, instead, received CPS's default score of 3.12 due to incomplete or missing data."
