## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Lower secondary school students reported significantly longer total screen time (M = 6.38, SD = 4.15) than highest secondary school students (M = 4.79, SD = 3.29; b = 1.11, p < 0.001), driven by TV/video and game consoles. [→ Poulain T 2018](#poulain-t-2018)

## Evidence

### Poulain T 2018

Poulain T, Peschel T, Vogel M, Jurkutat A, Kiess W. (2018). Cross-sectional and longitudinal associations of screen time and physical activity with school performance at different types of secondary school. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-018-5489-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Cross-sectional linear regression at baseline (N = 850) with school type as independent variable, adjusted for gender, age, SES, year of assessment, BMI, and other media/activity indicators. The article reports significantly longer total screen time in lower secondary schools.

> "Adolescents attending a lower secondary school reported a significantly longer total screen time (M = 6.38, SD = 4.15) than adolescents attending the highest secondary school (M = 4.79, SD = 3.29)."

### Poulain T 2018 (2)

Poulain T, Peschel T, Vogel M, Jurkutat A, Kiess W. (2018). Cross-sectional and longitudinal associations of screen time and physical activity with school performance at different types of secondary school. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-018-5489-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Baseline regression analyses (N = 850) of physical activity by school type. The article reports school type "was not significantly related to the total-physical-activity-score", and detailed analyses found no difference for organized or non-organized activity.

> "The analyses furthermore showed that the type of school was not significantly related to the total-physical-activity-score (b = -0.09, p = 0.69)."
