## Evidence

### Chloë Marshall 2017

Chloë Marshall. (2017). Montessori education: a review of the evidence base. npj Science of Learning 2:11. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-017-0012-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review attributes this to Lillard and Else-Quest's cross-sectional study exploiting an existing school lottery to assign children to Montessori versus comparison groups, with post-hoc checks showing similar family income. Where differences were found, they favoured the Montessori group.

> "For 5-year olds, signi ﬁcant group differences were found for certain academic skills (namely letterword identi ﬁcation, phonological decoding ability, and math skills), a measure of executive function (the card sort task), social skills (as measured by social reasoning and positive shared play) and theory of mind (as measured by a false-belief task)."

### Chloë Marshall 2017 (2)

Chloë Marshall. (2017). Montessori education: a review of the evidence base. npj Science of Learning 2:11. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-017-0012-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Same lottery-based study, older cohort: the review reports significant Montessori advantages in story writing and social skills plus greater self-reported sense of community. Group differences were not found on all outcome measures.

> "For 12year olds, signiﬁcant group differences were found on measures of story writing and social skills. Furthermore, in a questionnaire that asked about how they felt about school, responses of children in the Montessori group indicated that they felt a greater sense of community."
