Evidence¶

Wanli Xing 2025¶

Wanli Xing, Nia Nixon, Scott Crossley, Paul Denny, Andrew Lan, John Stamper, Zhou Yu. (2025). The Use of Large Language Models in Education. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-025-00457-x

q2 · i? · review · r1

The editorial reports, citing Norberg et al. (2024), a study using GPT-4 to revise educational math content for readability in an adaptive learning system, finding improvement in "readability metrics, such as word frequency". No effect sizes are printed.

"The researchers found that GPT-4 could improve readability metrics, such as word frequency, sentence complexity, and semantic similarity"

Wanli Xing 2025 (2)¶

Wanli Xing, Nia Nixon, Scott Crossley, Paul Denny, Andrew Lan, John Stamper, Zhou Yu. (2025). The Use of Large Language Models in Education. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-025-00457-x

q2 · i? · review · r1

The editorial reports the same study's learning-outcome results: "some students demonstrated improved mastery" with GPT-4-revised problems while "higher error rates were observed in certain areas". The editorial itself characterizes the outcomes as mixed.

"While some students demonstrated improved mastery of mathematical concepts when using GPT-4-revised problems, higher error rates were observed in certain areas, suggesting mixed outcomes and the need for more comprehensive evaluation"
