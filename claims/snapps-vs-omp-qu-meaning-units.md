## Evidence

### Seki Masayasu 2016

Seki Masayasu, Otaki Junji, Breugelmans Raoul, Komoda Takayuki, Nagata-Kobayashi Shizuko, Akaishi Yu, Hiramoto Jun, Ohno Iwao, Harada Yoshimi, Hirayama Yoji, Izumi Miki (2016). How do case presentation teaching methods affect learning outcomes?-SNAPPS and the One-Minute preceptor. BMC Medical Education 16. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0531-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized comparative study of 71 junior residents presenting the same simulated low back pain case to a single preceptor; meaning units in recorded discussions were counted. The SNAPPS group used "significantly more meaning units related to QU than members of the OMP group" (P < 0.001).

> "Members of the SNAPPS group used significantly more meaning units related to QU than members of the OMP group did (P < 0.001)."
