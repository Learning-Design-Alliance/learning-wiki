## Subclaims
`q4 i3` Meta-analysis of 10 RCTs (n=856) shows gamified learning significantly improves skill performance (SMD=1.64, 95% CI [0.96, 2.32]), the largest effect among the outcome domains. [→ Chen W.-L. 2026](#chen-w-l-2026)

## Evidence

### Chen W.-L. 2026

Chen W.-L., Winitchayothin S., Cheng C.-H., Tonapa S.I., Chou F.-H., & Zimmerman P.-A. (2026). A systematic review and meta-analysis of the effect of gamified clinical skill learning on learning outcomes in undergraduate nursing education. The Journal of Nursing Research. https://doi.org/10.1097/jnr.0000000000000772

`q4 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 10 randomized controlled trials pooling 856 undergraduate nursing students. The review reports a very large pooled effect, SMD = 1.64, quoting "a standardized mean difference of 1.64 (95% CI [0.96, 2.32], p<.001)"; heterogeneity was very high (I2=95%).

> "A meta-analysis of 10 studies comprising 856 students was used to assess the impact of gamified learning on skill performance. The pooled analysis demonstrated a large effect size with a standardized mean difference of 1.64 (95% CI [0.96, 2.32], p<.001)."
