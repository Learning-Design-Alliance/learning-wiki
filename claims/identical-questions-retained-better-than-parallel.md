## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the only study directly comparing identical and parallel retention test forms, students retained identical questions better, and scores were higher on the second exam regardless of order. [→ Semb 1994](#semb-1994)

## Evidence

### Semb 1994

Semb, G. B., & Ellis, J. A. (1994). Knowledge Taught in School: What Is Remembered? Review of Educational Research, 64(2), 253-286. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543064002253

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Semb, Ellis, and Araujo (1993) directly compared identical and parallel retention test forms with counterbalanced test order at 4- and 11-month intervals; the reviewers report students "retained identical questions better than parallel questions" and scored higher on whichever exam came second.

> "The results showed that students retained identical questions better than parallel questions. They also found that, while the order of presentation of the exams (same-different versus different-same) did not affect performance, students scored higher on the second exam, regardless of whether it was the same or different"
