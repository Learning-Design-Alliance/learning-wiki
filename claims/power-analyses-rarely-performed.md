# Educational researchers rarely perform power analyses to determine sample size requirements

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Statistical power or effect size issues were considered in only 20 of 226 repeated-measures articles (8.8%), and in three of those no power assessments were actually performed despite authors noting potentially low power. [→ Keselman 1998](#keselman-1998)

## Evidence

### Keselman 1998

Keselman, H.J., Huberty, C.J., Lix, L.M., Olejnik, S., Cribbie, R.A., Donahue, B., Kowalchuk, R.K., Lowman, L.L., Petoskey, M.D., Keselman, J.C., & Levin, J.R. (1998). Statistical Practises of Educational Researchers: An Analysis of Their ANOVA, MANOVA and ANCOVA Analyses. https://doi.org/10.2307/1170601

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Content analysis of 226 repeated-measures design articles. The review reports "Issues of statistical power/effect size were considered in 20 of the 226 articles (8.8%) in the database." In 16 articles effect sizes were calculated, most commonly Cohen's d statistic; three articles mentioned potentially low power but performed no assessments.
