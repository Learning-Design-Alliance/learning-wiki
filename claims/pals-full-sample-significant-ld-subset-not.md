## Evidence

### What Works Clearinghouse 2012

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2012). Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies. WWC Intervention Report, Students with Learning Disabilities. http://kc.vanderbilt.edu/pals/

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

WWC table note on Fuchs et al. (1997), an RCT across three student types. The authors' full-sample analysis was significant, but WWC calculations on the 40-student learning-disability subset found no statistically significant difference between PALS and comparison students.

> "For the full sample, the authors found that PALS students scored significantly higher than comparison students on each of the two outcome measures. The study authors found no significant difference in effects between student types, leading them to conclude that PALS was effective for each of the three types of students."

### What Works Clearinghouse 2012 (2)

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2012). Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies. WWC Intervention Report, Students with Learning Disabilities. http://kc.vanderbilt.edu/pals/

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

WWC table note on Saenz et al. (2005), an RCT across four student types in ELL classrooms. Neither the authors' full-sample analysis nor WWC calculations on the 20-student learning-disability subset found statistically significant differences.

> "For the full sample, the authors found no significant difference between PALS students and comparison students on either of the two outcome measures. The study authors found no significant difference in effects between student types, leading them to conclude that PALS was not effective for any of the four types of students."
