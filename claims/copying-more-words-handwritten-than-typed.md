# Grades 4–5 students copy more words by hand than by keyboard in a 3-minute copying task

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Students in Grades 4–5 copied more words with pen-and-paper than with keyboard-and-computer in both Study 1 and the replication. [→ Broc Lucie 2025](#broc-lucie-2025)

## Evidence

### Broc Lucie 2025

Broc Lucie, Goudeau Sébastien, Colliot Tiphaine. (2025). Comparing the effects of typing and handwriting on spelling performance in school. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-03369-x

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Within-subject classroom study (replication, N = 167, Grades 4–5) copying the BHK text for 3 min in each medium, four days apart, spellchecker disabled. The printed effect size is η² = 0.49 and means show "students copied more words in the pen-and-paper condition".

> "The ANOVA conducted on the total number of words copied revealed a significant main effect of the medium used [F(1, 166) = 503, p < .001, η² = 0.49]. The students copied more words in the pen-and-paper condition (M = 40.06, SD = 9.37) than in the keyboard-and-computer condition (M = 22.22, SD = 9.07)."
