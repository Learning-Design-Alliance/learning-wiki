## Evidence

### Corbett 1995

Albert T. Corbett and John R. Anderson. (1995). Knowledge tracing: Modeling the acquisition of procedural knowledge. CMU-CS-95-129, School of Computer Science, Carnegie Mellon University. https://reports-archive.adm.cs.cmu.edu/anon/1995/CMU-CS-95-129.pdf

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Experiment 4 comparison of twenty-five knowledge-tracing students against twenty-one students completing only the 38 required exercises, using a strict 90%-correct operational definition of mastery on the test. The difference was "reliable, z = 2.21, p \<0.05"; no effect size is printed.

> "Figure 7 indicates that 56% of the students in this knowledge tracing condition reached the mastery performance level. A second group of twenty-one students in this study who completed just the 38 required exercises provides a contrast. Only 24% percent of these students reached this high level of test performance. This difference in the probability of success between groups is reliable, z = 2.21, p \<0.05"
