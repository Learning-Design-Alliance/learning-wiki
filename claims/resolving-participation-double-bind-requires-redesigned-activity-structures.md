# Resolving a participation double-bind requires redesigning activity structures, not just adding outreach

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

An embedded case study of a Chicago participatory budgeting process found that outreach plus sequential interpretation failed to sustain Latino immigrant participation (attendance dropped from 7 to 0 within two meetings), while a subsequent formative intervention that redesigned the activity's tools and roles — a dedicated Spanish-Language Committee with native-language deliberation — sustained participation through a full cycle and was later adopted by other wards.

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` All seven Latino volunteers who joined a standard committee (with English-language proceedings and sequential Spanish interpretation) attended the orientation, but participation dropped to one person by the second meeting and to zero thereafter, while Latino participants remained present only in the process's non-decision-making input phase. [→ Meléndez 2021](#melendez-2021)

`q2 i2` After a Spanish-Language Committee was created — offering native-language deliberation, flexible agenda norms, and standing as a full decision-making body — Latino participants sustained involvement through an entire budgeting cycle and began using claims-making language ("we also have the right to seek to improve...") that the discourse analysis coded as evidence of transformative civic agency. [→ Meléndez 2021](#melendez-2021)

`q1 i2` The Spanish-Language Committee model was subsequently integrated into the ward's Leadership Committee, inspired a parallel Youth Committee, and was adopted as a model by other Chicago wards implementing participatory budgeting — documented through researcher observation and practitioner reports rather than a systematic follow-up survey. [→ Meléndez 2021](#melendez-2021)

`q2 · embedded, interventionist single-case ethnography (the researcher co-designed the intervention, not a neutral observer) with before/after comparison across two iterations, discourse-analytic coding of 10 meetings; no comparison ward or control condition, no quantitative outcome measures beyond attendance counts` · `i2 · a dramatic, well-documented qualitative shift (7→0 then sustained participation) with a clearly articulated mechanism, plus system-level adoption evidence that is anecdotal rather than systematically tracked` · `n=7 Latino community volunteers (Iteration 1), Chicago's 49th Ward (21% Spanish-speaking at home, 30% foreign-born), fiscal years 2012-2014`

Meléndez conducted an embedded, interventionist ethnography of Chicago's Ward 49 participatory budgeting process (PB49) across two iterations, applying [Cultural-Historical Activity Theory](../theories/cultural-historical-activity-theory.md)'s expansive-learning framework and coding meeting transcripts for stabilizing/destabilizing discourse and claims-making language. The first iteration's naive-inclusion design (outreach + sequential interpretation into an English-run committee) failed to sustain Latino participation; a subsequent formative intervention — the [Differentiated Activity Structures for Inclusive Participation](../patterns/differentiated-activity-structures-for-inclusive-participation.md) pattern, instantiated as a Spanish-Language Committee — sustained participation and produced observable collective-level (claims-making) and system-level (adoption elsewhere) learning.

## Discussion
As a single embedded case with the researcher acting as co-designer of the intervention, positionality is a real limitation the author himself acknowledges; there is no comparison ward or control condition, so alternative explanations (e.g., general momentum from continued outreach, unrelated changes in ward leadership) cannot be fully ruled out. The dramatic before/after attendance shift (7→0→sustained) and the qualitative claims-making evidence are nonetheless a clear, well-documented pattern, and the system-level adoption by other wards — while not systematically measured — is a meaningful signal that the redesign was not merely locally effective. This is the empirical basis for [Cultural-Historical Activity Theory](../theories/cultural-historical-activity-theory.md)'s collective-vs-system-level distinction in expansive learning.
