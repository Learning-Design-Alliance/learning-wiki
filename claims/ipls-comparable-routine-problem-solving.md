# IPLS and standard-course students showed statistically indistinguishable performance on problems requiring a single previously-taught model and on calculation skills

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On fluids task part (a), which required applying a previously-taught simple model, the two groups showed no significant difference (p = 0.94, Wilcoxon test). [→ Benjamin D. Geller 2022](#benjamin-d-geller-2022)
`q2 i?` On the first two parts of the thermodynamics problem, which required implementing a model studied in class, the groups showed no significant difference (p = 0.13). [→ Benjamin D. Geller 2022 (2)](#benjamin-d-geller-2022-2)
`q2 i?` Total calculation-and-numerical-skill rubric scores across both problems were statistically indistinguishable between groups (p = 0.93; Bayes odds ratio 4.4 favoring the null). [→ Benjamin D. Geller 2022 (3)](#benjamin-d-geller-2022-3)

## Evidence

### Benjamin D. Geller 2022

Benjamin D. Geller, Maya Tipton, Brandon Daniel-Morales, Nikhil Tignor, Calvin White, and Catherine H. Crouch (2022). Assessing the impact of Introductory Physics for the Life Sciences on students' ability to build complex models. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010131)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Wilcoxon comparison of rubric scores on fluids part (a), a problem very similar to ones both groups had encountered: "no significant difference (p = 0.94)".

> "A Wilcoxon test (non-parametric test comparing two samples) indicated no significant difference (p = 0.94)."

### Benjamin D. Geller 2022 (2)

Benjamin D. Geller, Maya Tipton, Brandon Daniel-Morales, Nikhil Tignor, Calvin White, and Catherine H. Crouch (2022). Assessing the impact of Introductory Physics for the Life Sciences on students' ability to build complex models. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010131)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Rubric scores on the first two parts of the textbook thermodynamics problem: "again with no significant difference (p = 0.13)".

> "Likewise, the first two parts of the thermodynamics problem required implementing a model studied in class; Fig. 4b shows the total rubric scores for both groups, again with no significant difference (p = 0.13)."

### Benjamin D. Geller 2022 (3)

Benjamin D. Geller, Maya Tipton, Brandon Daniel-Morales, Nikhil Tignor, Calvin White, and Catherine H. Crouch (2022). Assessing the impact of Introductory Physics for the Life Sciences on students' ability to build complex models. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010131)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Comparison of total calculation-and-numerical-skill rubric scores across both problems: "statistically indistinguishable (p = 0.93, Wilcoxon test)" with Bayes odds ratio of 4.4.

> "The distributions are statistically indistinguishable (p = 0.93, Wilcoxon test); Bayes analysis gave an odds ratio of 4.4, corresponding to moderate confirmation of the null hypothesis (equivalent skill)."
