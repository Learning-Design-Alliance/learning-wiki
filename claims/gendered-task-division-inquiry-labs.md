# In inquiry-based physics labs, women's profiles cluster in laptop use and men's in equipment use, indicating gendered task division

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In inquiry lab sections, women's profiles are overrepresented in the Laptop cluster (44 ± 6% vs 25 ± 3% of men's) and underrepresented in the Equipment cluster (4 ± 2% vs 14 ± 2% of men's, p = 0.011). [→ Katherine N. Quinn 2019](#katherine-n-quinn-2019)

## Evidence

### Katherine N. Quinn 2019

Katherine N. Quinn, Kathryn L. McGill, Michelle M. Kelley, Emily M. Smith, and N. G. Holmes (2019). Who does what now? How physics lab instruction impacts student behaviors. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1119/perc.2018.pr.quinn](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2018.pr.quinn)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cluster analysis of 522 normalized behaviour profiles observed over 30 lab periods across two semesters of an honours mechanics course; students self-selected into labs. The inquiry-lab comparison shows "44 ± 6% of women’s proﬁles" in the Laptop cluster versus 25 ± 3% of men's, with a printed chi-squared p = 0.011 and no effect size reported.

> "there is a difference in the cluster composition with respect to men’s and women’s proﬁles in the inquiry labs (p = 0.011). Speciﬁcally, 44 ± 6% of women’s proﬁles in the inquiry labs are in the Laptop cluster compared to 25 ± 3% of men’s proﬁles, and 4 ± 2% of women’s proﬁles are in the Equipment cluster compared to 14 ± 2% for men."
