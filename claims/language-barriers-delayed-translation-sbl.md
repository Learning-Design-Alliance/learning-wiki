## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants describe internal translation between their native language and English, increasing cognitive strain and slowing real-time interaction during SBL. [→ Zhang 2025](#zhang-2025)
`q2 i?` Students hesitate to ask for clarification or contribute, fearing mistakes will make them appear less competent, and worry simulated patients simplify language based on perceived proficiency. [→ Zhang 2025](#zhang-2025)

## Evidence

### Zhang 2025

Zhang, L., Forbes, R., Patterson, F., & Penman, A. (2025). Becoming through simulation‐based learning: Lived experiences of occupational therapy students from culturally and linguistically diverse backgrounds. Australian Occupational Therapy Journal, 72(4), e70044. https://doi.org/10.1111/1440-1630.70044

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview study of 15 CALD occupational therapy students; reflexive thematic analysis. Participants reported "delayed response times in communication" from internal translation, difficulty with accents and slang, pronunciation-related misunderstanding, and pretending to understand rather than asking for clarification.

> "The participants described experiencing delayed response times in communication because of the internal translation process between their native language and English, which increased their level of cognitive strain during SBL"
