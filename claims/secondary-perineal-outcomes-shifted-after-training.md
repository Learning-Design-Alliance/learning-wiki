## Evidence

### Ali-Masri Hadil 2018

Ali-Masri Hadil, Hassan Sahar, Fosse Erik, Zimmo Kaled M., Zimmo Mohammed, Ismail Khaled M. K., Vikanes Åse, Laine Katariina. (2018). Impact of electronic and blended learning programs for manual perineal support on incidence of obstetric anal sphincter injuries: a prospective interventional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1363-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Whole-cohort comparison across the three phases (Table 2: episiotomy 35.6% → 26.5% → 18.0%; intact perineum 40.4% → 52.0% → 61.6%). The article reports "a significant reduction in episiotomy rates and a significant increase of intact perineum proportions"; no effect sizes are printed.

> "There was also a significant reduction in episiotomy rates and a significant increase of intact perineum proportions after implementing training-method 1 and 2 (Table 1)."

### Ali-Masri Hadil 2018 (2)

Ali-Masri Hadil, Hassan Sahar, Fosse Erik, Zimmo Kaled M., Zimmo Mohammed, Ismail Khaled M. K., Vikanes Åse, Laine Katariina. (2018). Impact of electronic and blended learning programs for manual perineal support on incidence of obstetric anal sphincter injuries: a prospective interventional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1363-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Regression analysis among primiparous women found the phase-3 rise in second-degree tears was "independently associated with training-method 2" (aOR 2.3) and episiotomy use (aOR 1.5). The authors argue this may reflect less severe lacerations from bPST use.

> "There was an increase in the incidence of second degree tears during phase-3 which was independently associated with training-method 2 (aOR: 2.3, CI; 1.4–3.7, p = 0.002) and episiotomy use (aOR:1.5, CI; 1.0–2.3, p = 0.033)."
