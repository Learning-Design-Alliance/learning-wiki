## Evidence

### Losiak Wladyslaw; Losiak-Pilch Julia 2020

Losiak Wladyslaw; Losiak-Pilch Julia. (2020). Cortisol Awakening Response, Self-Reported Affect and Exam Performance in Female Students. Applied Psychophysiology and Biofeedback. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10484-019-09449-9

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Repeated-measures ANOVA on PANAS ratings taken 30 min after waking in the 25-student sample; negative affect rose ("eta-squared = 0.438") and positive affect fell on the exam day.

> "Negative affect was significantly higher in the morning on the day of the exam when compared with the neutral day (F (1,24) = 19.486, p = .0001, eta-squared = 0.438) and the positive affect was significantly lower on the day of the exam (F (1,24) = 4.556, p = .04, eta-squared = 0.154)."

### Losiak Wladyslaw; Losiak-Pilch Julia 2020 (2)

Losiak Wladyslaw; Losiak-Pilch Julia. (2020). Cortisol Awakening Response, Self-Reported Affect and Exam Performance in Female Students. Applied Psychophysiology and Biofeedback. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10484-019-09449-9

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r1`

Same repeated-measures ANOVA on PANAS ratings in the 25-student sample; positive affect was significantly lower on the exam day, with "eta-squared = 0.154".

> "Negative affect was significantly higher in the morning on the day of the exam when compared with the neutral day (F (1,24) = 19.486, p = .0001, eta-squared = 0.438) and the positive affect was significantly lower on the day of the exam (F (1,24) = 4.556, p = .04, eta-squared = 0.154)."

### Losiak Wladyslaw; Losiak-Pilch Julia 2020 (3)

Losiak Wladyslaw; Losiak-Pilch Julia. (2020). Cortisol Awakening Response, Self-Reported Affect and Exam Performance in Female Students. Applied Psychophysiology and Biofeedback. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10484-019-09449-9

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis (Table 2) in the same sample found no significant cortisol-affect associations; the article reports they were "not significant and rather low". Authors attribute this to biases influencing self-reported affect ratings.

> "However, it should be noted that correlations between self-reported affect and cortisol measures were not significant and rather low."
