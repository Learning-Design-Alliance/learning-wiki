# In Experiment 1, the feedback intervention reduced pre-task signed metacognitive bias (underconfidence) but the group difference did not persist at post-task

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` At pre-task the feedback group was significantly less underconfident than the no-feedback group, but groups did not differ significantly in post-task metacognitive bias. [→ Ceri Ngai and Sam J. Gilbert 2026](#ceri-ngai-and-sam-j-gilbert-2026)

## Evidence

### Ceri Ngai and Sam J. Gilbert 2026

Ceri Ngai and Sam J. Gilbert (2026). Metacognitive training facilitates optimal cognitive offloading. Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-026-00714-0

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Pre-registered Experiment 1 (N = 164) comparing signed metacognitive bias between groups at pre- and post-task. The pre-task comparison showed the feedback group "significantly less biased than the no-feedback group" (d = .29); the post-task comparison was not significant (d = .022).

> "At pre-task, the feedback group was significantly less biased than the no-feedback group (t(137.4) = 1.9, p = .03, d = .29; note that this p value is for a one-tailed test, in accordance with our pre-registered hypothesis that feedback should reduce metacognitive bias)."
