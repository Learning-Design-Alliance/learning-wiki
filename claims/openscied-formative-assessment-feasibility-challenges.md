# Formative assessment practices coherent with OpenSciEd are constrained by grading expectations misaligned with formative goals and by the time cost of evaluating and giving feedback on three-dimensional tasks.

## Evidence

### McElhaney 2024

McElhaney, K., Urban, R., Kamdar, D., Baker, A., Tsai, K., and Roschelle, J. (2024). Practitioner-reported Needs for Enacting, Implementing, and Adopting OpenSciEd Curriculum Materials [White paper]. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/215

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Focus group interview finding; one teacher described voice-recording feedback taking two minutes per student for 200-plus students. The report concludes "the amount of time required to evaluate the response and provide constructive feedback for a large number of students can make certain formative assessment practices prohibitively time consuming."

> "the amount of time required to evaluate the response and provide constructive feedback for a large number of students can make certain formative assessment practices prohibitively time consuming"

### McElhaney 2024 (2)

McElhaney, K., Urban, R., Kamdar, D., Baker, A., Tsai, K., and Roschelle, J. (2024). Practitioner-reported Needs for Enacting, Implementing, and Adopting OpenSciEd Curriculum Materials [White paper]. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/215

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Focus group vignette in which teachers described difficulty fitting OpenSciEd work into letter-grade systems. The report states "assessment approaches that are coherent with OpenSciEd’s instructional model are at odds with typical grading expectations, which tend to emphasize summative (rather than formative) assessment."

> "assessment approaches that are coherent with OpenSciEd’s instructional model are at odds with typical grading expectations, which tend to emphasize summative (rather than formative) assessment."
