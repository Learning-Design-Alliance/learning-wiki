# Students without phonological input reported higher satisfaction with their pre-listening activity

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Students in Class 2 (no phonological input) were more likely to be satisfied with their assigned pre-listening activity than students in Class 1 (with phonological input). [→ Mihara 2015](#mihara-2015)

## Evidence

### Mihara 2015

Mihara, K. (2015). Effects of Phonological Input as a Pre-Listening Activity on Vocabulary Learning and L2 Listening Comprehension Test Performance. TESL-EJ 19(2). https://tesl-ej.org/pdf/ej74/a1.pdf

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

A one-item Likert-type survey of 40 students in the two experimental classes found higher satisfaction in Class 2 (M = 4.250, SD = 0.766) than Class 1 (M = 3.800, SD = 1.208). Post-interviews suggested this reflected personal preference and anxiety about unfamiliar test formats rather than actual effectiveness.

> "The descriptive statistics shown in Table 8 reveal that the students in Class 2, who did the pre-listening activity without phonological input, were more likely to be satisfied with their assigned pre-listening activity."
