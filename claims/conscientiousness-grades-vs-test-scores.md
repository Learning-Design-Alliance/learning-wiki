# Conscientiousness correlates more strongly with grades than with standardized test scores, across both language and STEM domains

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q4` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q4 i2` Conscientiousness correlations are larger for grades (ρ = 0.28) than for test scores (ρ = 0.13). [→ Meyer 2023](#meyer-2023)
`q4 i2` The grades-over-test-scores pattern holds within STEM (ρ = 0.26 vs. 0.11) and language (ρ = 0.27 vs. 0.16) domains, with no significant domain × measure interaction. [→ Meyer 2023 (2)](#meyer-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Meyer 2023

Meyer, J., Jansen, T., Hübner, N., & Lüdtke, O. (2023). Disentangling the Association Between the Big Five Personality Traits and Student Achievement: Meta-Analytic Evidence on the Role of Domain Specificity and Achievement Measures. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09736-2

`q4 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analysis of 305 conscientiousness effect sizes from 74 studies (N = 513,300), corrected for measurement error, found "larger correlations found for grades (ρ = 0.28) compared to test scores (ρ = 0.13)"; the domain moderation itself was not significant (p = 0.230).

> "we did find a moderating effect of measure (RQ2), with larger correlations found for grades (ρ = 0.28) compared to test scores (ρ = 0.13; z = 5.17; p < 0.001)"

### Meyer 2023 (2)

Meyer, J., Jansen, T., Hübner, N., & Lüdtke, O. (2023). Disentangling the Association Between the Big Five Personality Traits and Student Achievement: Meta-Analytic Evidence on the Role of Domain Specificity and Achievement Measures. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09736-2

`q4 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

In the individual domain × measure comparisons, grades exceeded test scores within both named domains; the overall interaction was not significant (t[60] = 1.58; p = 0.119), and no domain differences emerged within either measure.

> "we found larger correlations of grades compared to test scores with both STEM (ρ = 0.26 vs. 0.11; z = 4.54; p < 0.001) and language subject domains (ρ = 0.27 vs. 0.16; z = 3.27; p = 0.001)"
