The review reports the Ma et al. (2014) meta-analysis of ITS learning outcomes, finding that ITS "outperformed, in aggregate, the other modes of instruction" in evaluative studies; the authors caution this may partly reflect publication bias.

Related Claims¶

  • Learning with intelligent tutoring systems yields a moderate, statistically significant overall advantage in achievement over non-ITS modes of instruction — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Ma et al. (2014) meta-analysis: step-based ITSs outperformed no tutoring and large-group teacher-led instruction, but showed no differences against small-group or one-to-one human tutoring — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Intelligent tutoring systems show moderate learning benefits but are less effective than human tutoring — related
  • Intelligent tutoring systems improve achievement over non-individualised instruction — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Meta-analytic evidence indicates intelligent tutoring systems yield positive learning outcomes compared with non-ITS conditions — related
  • Kulik and Fletcher (2015) found similar-sized ITS improvements to other meta-analyses, with larger effect sizes when tests were tuned to the tutoring system than when standardised tests were used — related
  • GURU, a tutor embodying expert-teacher behaviour, produced learning gains similar to small-group human teaching and better than classroom teaching alone — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Small sample sizes and publication bias against non-significant findings may inflate meta-analytic effect size estimates in the chemistry teaching-method categories — related
