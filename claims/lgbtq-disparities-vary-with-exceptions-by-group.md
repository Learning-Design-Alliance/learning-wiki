# Disparities are not uniform: some gender- and sexual-minority groups report equal or better school experiences than majority peers (e.g., Black transgender students, Cambodian gay/lesbian students)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Black or African American transgender students reported more opportunities for meaningful participation (34% vs. 29–30%) than other Black or African American students, and Cambodian gay/lesbian students reported more positive supports and outcomes than their straight peers. [→ Cerna 2021](#cerna-2021)

## Evidence

### Cerna 2021

Cerna, R., Stern, A., Austin, G., Betz, J., Zhang, G., & Hashmi, S. (2021). Understanding the experiences of LGBTQ high school students in California by race/ethnicity. WestEd. https://www.wested.org/resources/lgbtq-students-in-california/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive exceptions reported in the Asian-group section of the CHKS 2017–19 analysis (Tables 13a–13c), alongside the report's finding that Black or African American transgender students reported meaningful participation at 34% versus 29–30% among other Black or African American students (Table 5). Percentages only.

> "Cambodian students who identify as gay/lesbian were more likely to report being held to high expectations, opportunities for meaningful participation at school, promotion of parental involvement at school, high GPA, academic motivation, and sense of school connectedness than their straight peers."
