# Critical appraisal identified beliefs about consequences and beliefs about capabilities as additional intervention targets despite failing relevance criteria

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A critical-appraisal stage, departing from previous TDF practice, identified beliefs about consequences and beliefs about capabilities as domains that should be targeted in an intervention even though participants did not link them to their own prescribing behavior. [→ Duncan Eilidh M 2012](#duncan-eilidh-m-2012)

## Evidence

### Duncan Eilidh M 2012

Duncan Eilidh M, Francis Jill J, Johnston Marie, Davey Peter, Maxwell Simon, McKay Gerard A, McLay James, Ross Sarah, Ryan Cristín, Webb David J, Bond Christine. (2012). Learning curves, taking instructions, and patient safety: using a theoretical domains framework in an interview study to investigate prescribing errors among trainee doctors. Implementation Science. https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-7-86

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Critical-appraisal stage of the interview analysis, in which participant responses were considered alongside published literature and other PROTECT study arms. The authors judged that “the discrepancy between objective evidence and trainee doctors’ beliefs” was itself an intervention target.

> "Therefore, the discrepancy between objective evidence and trainee doctors’ beliefs about consequences and their capabilities could itself be an appropriate target for intervention."
