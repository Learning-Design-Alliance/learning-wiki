## Evidence

### Haubruck P 2018

Haubruck P, Nickel F, Ober J, Walker T, Bergdolt C, Friedrich M, Müller-Stich BP, Forchheim F, Fischer C, Schmidmaier G, Tanner MC. (2018). Evaluation of App-Based Serious Gaming as a Training Method in Teaching Chest Tube Insertion to Medical Students: Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.9956

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary end point: indirect video observation by two independent blinded raters using the same OSATS tool in the same RCT. Both raters' medians favored the intervention group with "P<.001" for each; interrater correlation was 0.929.

> "rater 1: intervention group: 42.0 [I50=7.0] vs control group: 34.0 [I50=14.0]; P<.001; rater 2: intervention group: 40.0 [I50=9.0] vs control group: 33.5 [I50=10.0]; P<.001"

### Haubruck P 2018 (2)

Haubruck P, Nickel F, Ober J, Walker T, Bergdolt C, Friedrich M, Müller-Stich BP, Forchheim F, Fischer C, Schmidmaier G, Tanner MC. (2018). Evaluation of App-Based Serious Gaming as a Training Method in Teaching Chest Tube Insertion to Medical Students: Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.9956

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Combined-rating analysis in the same RCT confirmed the group difference, "40.0 [I50=7.2] vs control group: 32.3 [I50=10.1]; P<.001". Indirect ratings revealed smaller between-group differences than direct rating.

> "combined ratings of direct and indirect observation confirmed statistically significant differences (intervention group: 40.0 [I50=7.2] vs control group: 32.3 [I50=10.1]; P<.001; Figure 6)"
