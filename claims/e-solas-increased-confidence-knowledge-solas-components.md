## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a single-group pre-post study, physiotherapists' self-rated confidence in delivering all SOLAS intervention components increased significantly after e-learning training; knowledge rose significantly for some components but not others (physical-activity promotion P = .07, cycle of change P = .52, advice to patients P = .12). ## Evidence

### Hurley DA 2019

Hurley DA, Keogh A, Mc Ardle D, Hall AM, Richmond H, Guerin S, Magdalinski T, Matthews J. (2019). Evaluation of an E-Learning Training Program to Support Implementation of a Group-Based, Theory-Driven, Self-Management Intervention For Osteoarthritis and Low-Back Pain: Pre-Post Study. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/11123

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Single-group pre-post study of 13 physiotherapists trained via E-SOLAS; pre-post differences tested with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Bonferroni corrections. The article reports "significant increases in physiotherapists’ confidence and knowledge" with P<.05; no effect size is printed. The summary sentence overstates the knowledge result: Table 2 shows several knowledge comparisons that are not significant, while confidence rose for every component.

> "With regard to learning, there were significant increases in physiotherapists’ confidence and knowledge in delivery of all SOLAS intervention components (P<.05)."
