# Retained students struggled in their second time through the promotional policy, with only about half meeting cutoffs and eighth graders doing worst

## Evidence

### Roderick 2000

Roderick, M., Nagaoka, J., Bacon, J., & Easton, J. Q. (2000). Update: Ending Social Promotion: Passing, Retention, and Achievement Trends Among Promoted and Retained Students 1995-1999. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://www.consortium-chicago.org

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Cohort tracking of students retained in 1997 and 1998 through their retained year, including a new January retest chance in 1998-1999 that about one-quarter used. Performance was "only slightly better than in 1997."

> "After two years in the same grade and two summers in Summer Bridge, 53 percent of third graders re- tained in 1998 were able to meet the test score cutoffs, compared with 43 percent of third grad- ers retained in 1997."

### Roderick 2000 (2)

Roderick, M., Nagaoka, J., Bacon, J., & Easton, J. Q. (2000). Update: Ending Social Promotion: Passing, Retention, and Achievement Trends Among Promoted and Retained Students 1995-1999. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://www.consortium-chicago.org

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cohort tracking of retained and Transition Center eighth graders. The brief attributes the dismal progress partly to dropout: the one-year dropout rate for these students was roughly comparable in 1998 and 1999.

> "Only about 38 percent of eighth grad- ers who were retained or sent to T ransition Cen- ters in 1998 were able to raise their scores to the test cutoff by August."
