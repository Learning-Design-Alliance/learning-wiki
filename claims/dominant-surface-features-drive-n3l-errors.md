## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In N3L problems where one interacting object has a dominant surface feature irrelevant to interaction-force properties, novices focus on that feature and conclude the dominant object applies a larger force or causes the forces. [→ Lei Bao and Joseph C. Fritchman 2021](#lei-bao-and-joseph-c-fritchman-2021)

## Evidence

### Lei Bao and Joseph C. Fritchman 2021

Lei Bao and Joseph C. Fritchman. (2021). Development of a Conceptual Framework for Knowledge Integration in Learning Newton's Third Law. The Ohio State University, Department of Physics. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2104.05944](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2104.05944)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Framework-based analysis of documented student reasoning in the article. The incorrect reasoning is mapped as a pathway from surface features through dominance-based intermediate reasoning to the outcome that the dominant object applies a larger force than the secondary object.

> "novices lack the understanding of the central idea and often place focus on the dominant surface features, which lead to difficulties in answering these questions, especially when comparing magnitudes of forces"
