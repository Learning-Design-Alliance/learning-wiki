## Evidence

### Cress 2018

Cress, U., Stahl, G., Rose, C., Law, N., & Ludvigsen, S. (2018). Forming social systems by coupling minds at different levels of cognition: Design, tools, and research methods. International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning, 13, 235–240. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-018-9284-z

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

In the editorial's summary of Schneider et al.'s TinkerLamp study, dyads used mobile eye trackers and cross-recurrence graphs during a tangible warehouse-layout task; the indicator of joint visual interaction "correlates with collaboration quality" and unbalanced visual participation was negatively correlated with learning gains.

> "So, joint visual attention may be high also in highly coordinated – but ultimately ineffective groups – where one person takes on a visual leadership, and the other just follows and behaves like a free-rider."
