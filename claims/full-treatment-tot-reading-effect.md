## Evidence

### Borman Geoffrey D. 2026

Borman Geoffrey D., Yang Hyunwoo. (2026). Cumulative access to print books improves literacy achievement: Evidence from a five-year randomized trial in high-poverty schools. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2521416123

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Two-stage least squares instrumental-variables analysis using randomized assignment as the instrument for receipt of the full treatment, estimating the treatment-on-the-treated effect for the 1,805 students remaining in baseline schools all five years. The estimate was "equivalent to an effect size of d = 0.207" (P = 0.047).

> "For those students experiencing the full 5-y enrollment in a treatment school, the estimated second-stage coefficient is 0.219 (SE = 0.110, P = 0.047). Also, as seen in Fig. 2, this impact is equivalent to an effect size of d = 0.207."
