Evidence¶

Chunliang Yang 2018¶

Chunliang Yang, Rosalind Potts and David R. Shanks. (2018). Enhancing learning and retrieval of new information: a review of the forward testing effect. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0024-y

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports paired-associate forward testing experiments by Weinstein et al. and Yang et al. using four lists of face-name pairs with Interim Test and Interim Distractor conditions; the quote gives the doubled name recall, and the review adds that distractor groups showed substantially more PI and that the effect extends to Swahili-English and Euskara-English pairs.

Related Claims¶

  • When tested and new complementary information is presented together, interim testing on the tested material can impair learning of the new material (the borrowed time effect), which separate presentation can reverse — reports the opposite
  • In single item learning, interim testing of studied lists roughly doubles recall of new items compared with distractor or restudy control groups — related
  • Making JOLs with word stems enhances interim recall of new List-5 material compared to restudy — related
  • Making JOLs with complete words did not significantly enhance interim recall of new List-5 material compared to restudy — related
  • Overt retrieval practice enhances interim recall of new List-5 material compared to restudy, and does not differ from JOLs with word stems — related
