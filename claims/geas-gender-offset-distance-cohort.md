## Evidence

### Nicole P. Vogt & Amy Smith Muise 2015

Nicole P. Vogt & Amy Smith Muise. (2015). An Online Tutor for Astronomy: The GEAS Self-Review Library. [doi:10.1080/2331186x.2015.1037990](https://doi.org/10.1080/2331186x.2015.1037990)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

A comparative analysis (Figure 5) of average library scores and question counts by lecture module for distance learners split by gender. The printed difference of 7 ± 0.6% between the two named groups appears only in the distance cohort, not the in-class cohorts; no standardized effect size is reported.

> "In Figure 5 we divide the distance learners into two groups by gender, and observe that within this cohort men outscore women by 7 ± 0.6% on average across the board. We do not, however, observe this offset in either of the in-class cohorts under study."

### Nicole P. Vogt & Amy Smith Muise 2015 (2)

Nicole P. Vogt & Amy Smith Muise. (2015). An Online Tutor for Astronomy: The GEAS Self-Review Library. [doi:10.1080/2331186x.2015.1037990](https://doi.org/10.1080/2331186x.2015.1037990)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

A comparative analysis of pre-class concept inventory scores and weekly study time by gender across cohorts. Distant learners: men spend 5 minutes less per week (56 versus 61 minutes) answering the same number of questions; classroom students: men spend 11 minutes less (40 versus 51) and answer significantly fewer questions (44 versus 62 per week). No standardized effect size is reported.

> "The difference is 16 ± 3% for the distant cohort, but only half as much (7.9 ± 3%) for the classroom students. The amount of time the genders spend studying within the self-review also differs between cohorts."
