## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Wang Zi-Yue 2021

Wang Zi-Yue, Zhang Li-Jie, Liu Yu-Hong, Jiang Wei-Xi, Jia Jing-Yun, Tang Sheng-Lan, Liu Xiao-Yun. (2021). The effectiveness of E-learning in continuing medical education for tuberculosis health workers: a quasi-experiment from China. Infectious Diseases of Poverty. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40249-021-00855-y

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Final-evaluation questionnaire in the quasi-experiment, using self-reported perceived application as a proxy for behaviour change; for asynchronous training "57.3% of the 309 participants have expressed a similar view (177/309)".

> "56.0% (93/166) of the 166 synchronous E-learning session participants agreed that "the training knowledge can be applied to my work"."

### Wang Zi-Yue 2021 (2)

Wang Zi-Yue, Zhang Li-Jie, Liu Yu-Hong, Jiang Wei-Xi, Jia Jing-Yun, Tang Sheng-Lan, Liu Xiao-Yun. (2021). The effectiveness of E-learning in continuing medical education for tuberculosis health workers: a quasi-experiment from China. Infectious Diseases of Poverty. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40249-021-00855-y

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Univariate analysis of the survey data in the quasi-experiment; agreement with applying training knowledge to work varied by institutional level, education, and pilot status, with the printed P values as quoted. County-level and lower-educated staff agreed more often in asynchronous training.

> "medical staff at the provincial level (P < 0.001), with bachelor degree or above (P < 0.001), and from pilot area (P = 0.039) were more likely to agree with this argument."
