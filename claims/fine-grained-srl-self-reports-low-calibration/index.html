Evidence¶

Rovers 2019¶

Rovers, S. F. E., Clarebout, G., Savelberg, H. H. C. M., de Bruin, A. B. H., & van Merriënboer, J. J. G. (2019). Granularity matters: comparing different ways of measuring self-regulated learning. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-019-09188-6

q2 · i? · review · r3

Narrative review synthesis of ten included higher-education studies comparing self-report questionnaires with online measures of specific strategies (trace data, think-alouds, eye-tracking, micro-analytic questions), concluding there is "low calibration between the two forms of measurement".

"Overall, studies that focus on the use of specific strategies when comparing self-report questionnaires with behavioral measures indicate low calibration between the two forms of measurement"

Related Claims¶

  • AI tools collected and organized a previously unavailable granularity of data, including individual student exchanges and classroom discussion data, to generate actionable recommendations — related
  • When SRL is measured at a coarse grain size (global self-regulation), students are relatively well able to report on their strategy use — related
  • Low questionnaire–think-aloud correlations may have two alternative explanations besides poor self-report ability: the methods may measure different strategies or be aimed at different tasks — related
  • A task-specific questionnaire built item-by-item from a think-aloud coding taxonomy correlated r=.51 with think-aloud protocols, higher than the -.07 to .42 regularly reported — related
  • The SKT-SRL shows low, mostly non-significant correlations with SRL microanalysis (online measure) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Trace data tend to predict achievement better than self-reports, but self-reports of global SRL intensity can outperform traces — related
