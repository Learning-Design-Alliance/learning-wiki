### Peter Holtz & Joachim Kimmerle & Ulrike Cress 2018

Peter Holtz & Joachim Kimmerle & Ulrike Cress. (2018). Using big data techniques for measuring productive friction in mass collaboration online environments. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-018-9285-y

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports this from the same observational analysis of Wikipedia edit histories (Jirschitzka et al. 2017), where friction was operationalized as differences between authors' and articles' views. The articles the review attributes to Bakshy et al. and Del Vicario et al. frame the interpretation about echo chambers and polarization. No effect size is printed.

> "In the case of pro alternative medicine articles, the articles were more balanced when they were edited by more heterogeneous contributors, that is, contributors who displayed both pro and contra alternative medicine attitudes."
