## Evidence

### Anmarkrud 2022

Anmarkrud, Ø., Bråten, I., Florit, E., & Mason, L. (2022). The Role of Individual Differences in Sourcing: a Systematic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09640-7

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's synthesis of 35 studies examining prior knowledge in relation to sourcing found that "Less than half of these studies (n = 16) identified a statistically significant relationship". Illustrative findings include lower-knowledge students trusting biased sources more and higher-knowledge students trusting objective, balanced texts more.

> "Less than half of these studies (n = 16) identified a statistically significant relationship."

### Anmarkrud 2022 (2)

Anmarkrud, Ø., Bråten, I., Florit, E., & Mason, L. (2022). The Role of Individual Differences in Sourcing: a Systematic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09640-7

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Moderator analysis of measurement format: "Eleven of the 16 studies finding a statistically significant relationship... measured prior knowledge using multiple-choice measures", compared with only six of 19 null studies, which more often used open-ended questions, true/false questions, or self-reported perceived knowledge.

> "Eleven of the 16 studies finding a statistically significant relationship between prior knowledge and sourcing measured prior knowledge using multiple-choice measures, whereas this type of measure was used in only six of the 19 studies that did not find a statistically significant relationship."
