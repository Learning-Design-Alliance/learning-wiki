# CMPL simulation confirms component power-law predictions at the item level for RTs and SDs during a strategy shift, with identical within-strategy rate parameters

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the single-item simulation, power-function fits to algorithm and retrieval RTs and SDs were essentially exact, confirming component power-law predictions in the context of a strategy shift. [→ Rickard 1997](#rickard-1997)
`q2 i?` In the multi-item simulation of 18 participants x 12 items, component power functions held to a close approximation, with SD rate parameters slightly larger than RT rate parameters within each strategy. [→ Rickard 1997 (2)](#rickard-1997-2)

## Evidence

### Rickard 1997

Rickard, T. C. (1997). Bending the Power Law: A CMPL Theory of Strategy Shifts and the Automatization of Cognitive Skills. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 126(3), 288-311. https://psycnet.apa.org/record/xge126030288

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Simulation of 100 trials of practice on a single item generated 1,000 times with learning parameter c2 = 0.5; the article reports the fits "are essentially exact, confirming the component power-law predictions at the item level" for RTs and SDs (Figures 2 and 3).

> "The fits are essentially exact, confirming the component power-law predictions at the item level for both the RTs and SDs in the context of a strategy shift."

### Rickard 1997 (2)

Rickard, T. C. (1997). Bending the Power Law: A CMPL Theory of Strategy Shifts and the Automatization of Cognitive Skills. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 126(3), 288-311. https://psycnet.apa.org/record/xge126030288

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Second simulation mimicking Experiment 1 conditions (18 participants, 12 problems each) with c2 drawn from a beta distribution of mean .5; the component power functions "hold to a close approximation across most of the training interval" (Figures 5 and 6).

> "As shown in Figures 5 and 6, the component power-function predictions hold to a close approximation across most of the training interval for both the RTs and SDs."
