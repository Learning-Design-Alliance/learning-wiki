# Relative-Hards Solved Complex, a log measure of using complex code on personally difficult levels, predicts time spent on an unsolvable challenge task

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q2` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Relative-Hards Solved Complex correlated with challenge time (r = 0.67, p = .003), survived Benjamini-Hochberg correction and pretest control (partial r = 0.64, p < .01), and explained 44.7% unique variance in challenge time. [→ Malkiewich 2016](#malkiewich-2016)

## Evidence

### Malkiewich 2016

Malkiewich, L. J., Lee, A., Slater, S., & Chase, C. C. (2016). Tenacious Assessments: Using In-Game Behaviors to Measure Student Persistence and Challenge Navigation. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Tenacious+Assessments+Using+In-Game+Behaviors

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Step-wise regression with challenge time (minutes on an unsolvable task) as the classic persistence criterion; pretest did not explain challenge time (p = .87). Relative-Hards Solved Complex "explained 44.7% more unique variance in Challenge time than pretest alone". Raw correlation was r = 0.67, p = .003.

> "Relative-Hards Solved Complex, when added to the model, ultimately explained 44.7% more unique variance in Challenge time than pretest alone, F(1,14) = 11.36, p < .01."
