# Scaffolding for selecting and judging peer-created ELOs led students to engage more deeply with peer content and create better ELOs

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students who received scaffolding for deciding what to look for, selecting relevant ELOs, and judging ELO quality considered peer-created ELO contents in greater depth and created better ELOs of their own than unsupported students. [→ Ton de Jong 2012](#ton-de-jong-2012)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

A follow-up study reported in the article's student-experiences section compared students with and without scaffolding for using peer-created ELOs. The article reports that scaffolded students "considered the contents of peer-created ELOs in greater depth" and created better ELOs; no effect sizes are printed.

> "A follow-up study confirmed that students who received scaffolding for these processes considered the contents of peer-created ELOs in greater depth and therefore created better ELOs of their own than did students without additional support"
