# Students in project-based lab teams most often described functional team dynamics, though self-formed friend teams were not always functional

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Most project teams reported functional dynamics including clear expectations, good communication, fair task distribution, and shared responsibility; a small fraction reported poor communication and unfair task distribution. [→ Bei Cai 2019](#bei-cai-2019)
`q2 i?` Some teams felt functional due to prior experience together, while one non-functional team dynamic was caused by close friends working together, lowering pressure to attend and complete work. [→ Bei Cai 2019 (2)](#bei-cai-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Bei Cai 2019

Bei Cai, Lindsay Mainhood, and Robert G. Knobel. (2019). Using reflections to explore student learning during the project component of an advanced laboratory course. arXiv. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1119/perc.2018.pr.cai](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2018.pr.cai)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of the team-dynamics prompt in the 55 reflections found most teams "functioned quite well", with clear expectations, communication, fair task distribution and supportive relationships coded as common functional dynamics.

> "Most project teams felt that they functioned quite well. Common functional team dynamics included: having clear team expectations, good communication among team members, fair task distribution, friendly relationships that are supportive and trustworthy."

### Bei Cai 2019 (2)

Bei Cai, Lindsay Mainhood, and Robert G. Knobel. (2019). Using reflections to explore student learning during the project component of an advanced laboratory course. arXiv. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1119/perc.2018.pr.cai](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2018.pr.cai)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The analysis reports a student quote attributing a non-functional dynamic to a self-formed team of close friends; the authors note self-formed teams "might not necessarily lead to functional team dynamics".

> "Yet another student wrote that their non-functional team “dynamic was caused by having a group of close friends working together, which lowered the pressure of showing up very late to meetings or not doing the required work by the time it is due”."
