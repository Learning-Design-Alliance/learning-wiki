# The Chicago-versus-rest-of-Illinois gap in ISAT math also narrows in upper grades, but less than in reading

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` The math gap falls from 21 points (0.72 SDs) in third grade to 15 points (0.52 SDs) in eighth grade. [→ John Q. Easton 2007](#john-q-easton-2007)

## Evidence

### John Q. Easton 2007

John Q. Easton, Stuart Luppescu, Todd Rosenkranz. (2007). 2006 ISAT Reading and Math Scores in Chicago and the Rest of the State. Research Brief, Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/2006-isat-reading-and-math-scores-chicago-and-rest-state

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of 2006 ISAT math scale scores comparing CPS to the rest of Illinois across grades 3-8; the printed standardized effect sizes are d = 0.72, d = 0.60, and d = 0.52 for grades three, six, and eight.

> "There is a 21 point difference between Chicago students and students in the rest of the state in grade three (0.72 SDs), an 16 point difference in grade six (0.60 SDs), and a 15 point difference in grade eight (0.52 SDs)."
