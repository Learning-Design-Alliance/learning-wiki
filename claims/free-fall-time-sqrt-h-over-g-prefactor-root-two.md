# When a full bucket falls a height h with air ignored, its free-fall time has the form t = Q√(h/g) with dimensionless prefactor Q = √2, giving a characteristic time of about 1 s for h ≈ 10 m

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Dimensional analysis fixes the functional form t = Q√(h/g), and the exact solution gives the prefactor Q = √2, so the fall from a third story takes on the order of one second. [→ David W. Hogg 2007](#david-w-hogg-2007)

## Evidence

### David W. Hogg 2007

David W. Hogg. (2007). Real-world ballistics: A dropped bucket. https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.0107 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0709.0107](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0709.0107)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Dimensional analysis in the lecture: the only combination of h and g with dimensions of time is √(h/g), giving "this characteristic time is 1 s"; the exact prefactor is later identified as √2, and the mass m of the bucket drops out of the free-fall time.

> "With h ≈ 10 m and g ≈ 10 m s−2, this characteristic time is 1 s. And indeed, this probably agrees with your initial guess based on your memory of similar incidents you have experienced."
