Evidence¶

Sofia Magalhães 2020¶

Sofia Magalhães, Luísa Carneiro, Teresa Limpo & Marisa Filipe (2020). Executive functions predict literacy and mathematics achievements: The unique contribution of cognitive flexibility in grades 2, 4, and 6. Child Neuropsychology, 26(7), 934-952. https://doi.org/10.1080/09297049.2020.1740188

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Two-step hierarchical regression with 284 fourth graders predicting mathematics marks; Step 1 (R² = .28) included fluid intelligence, inhibitory control, working memory, and planning; Step 2 added flexibility, yielding "ΔR2 = .01, Fchange(1, 277) = 3.72, p = .05" and b = .12.

"When cognitive ﬂexibility was entered on Step 2, there was a signi ﬁcant increase in the amount of explained variance in mathematics achievement, ΔR2 = .01, Fchange(1, 277) = 3.72, p = .05. Cognitive ﬂexibility was found to be a signiﬁcant and unique predictor, above and beyond the other EF ( b = .12)."

Sofia Magalhães 2020 (2)¶

Sofia Magalhães, Luísa Carneiro, Teresa Limpo & Marisa Filipe (2020). Executive functions predict literacy and mathematics achievements: The unique contribution of cognitive flexibility in grades 2, 4, and 6. Child Neuropsychology, 26(7), 934-952. https://doi.org/10.1080/09297049.2020.1740188

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Two-step hierarchical regression with 203 sixth graders predicting mathematics marks; Step 2 added cognitive flexibility, yielding "ΔR2 = .06, Fchange(1, 196) = 20.32, p < .001"; the article reports flexibility as a significant unique predictor with b = .29 in Table 5.

"the introduction of cognitiveﬂexibility on Step 2 resulted in signiﬁcant increase in the amount of explained variance in mathematics achievement, ΔR2 = .06, Fchange(1, 196) = 20.32, p < .001."
