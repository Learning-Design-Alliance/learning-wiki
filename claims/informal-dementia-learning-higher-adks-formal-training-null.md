## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Alzahrani YA 2025

Alzahrani YA, Harbi MH, Almalki AG, Althomali RA, Alorabi RF, Basulyman BS, Alansari KA, Alzahrani JA, Alzahrani DA, Asuhaimi IA, Alzahrani AM, Alkatheeri AA. (2025). Evaluation of healthcare professionals' knowledge of Alzheimer's disease in government hospitals and healthcare centers: A cross-sectional study in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia. Medicine 2025;104:9(e41642). https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000041642

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cross-sectional survey analysis (N = 231) comparing ADKS means by informal dementia learning via t test; the "other forms of dementia learning were significantly associated with higher dementia knowledge (P < .05)". No effect size printed.

> "other forms of dementia learning were significantly associated with higher dementia knowledge (P < .05), with those having additional learning experiences about dementia achieving a mean score of 18.46 (SD = 3.11), compared to 17.34 (SD = 2.87) for those without such learning"

### Alzahrani YA 2025 (2)

Alzahrani YA, Harbi MH, Almalki AG, Althomali RA, Alorabi RF, Basulyman BS, Alansari KA, Alzahrani JA, Alzahrani DA, Asuhaimi IA, Alzahrani AM, Alkatheeri AA. (2025). Evaluation of healthcare professionals' knowledge of Alzheimer's disease in government hospitals and healthcare centers: A cross-sectional study in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia. Medicine 2025;104:9(e41642). https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000041642

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same cross-sectional survey (N = 231): the 24 participants with dementia training (mean 18.70) vs 207 without (mean 17.96) did not differ significantly; "dementia training did not significantly affect knowledge scores (P = .256)". No effect size printed; equivalence not tested.

> "dementia training did not significantly affect knowledge scores (P = .256)"
