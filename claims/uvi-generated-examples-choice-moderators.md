## Evidence

### Carol Sansone 2026

Carol Sansone, K. Ann Renninger, Judith M. Harackiewicz. (2026). Motivating Self-Regulation: Utility Value, Interest Development, and the Potential for Intervention. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10142-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review attributing to Canning and Harackiewicz (2015) the finding that self-generated utility examples make the intervention more effective than provided reasons; the review frames generating personal examples as a form of self-regulation.

> "research has found that the intervention is most effective when students generate examples of utility for themselves rather than being provided with reasons why course topics might useful (Canning & Harackiewicz, 2015)"

### Carol Sansone 2026 (2)

Carol Sansone, K. Ann Renninger, Judith M. Harackiewicz. (2026). Motivating Self-Regulation: Utility Value, Interest Development, and the Potential for Intervention. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10142-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review reporting that essay word count consistently mediates UVI effects on course outcomes, and that effects are stronger when students choose topics to write about or methods of generating utility (Asher & Harackiewicz, 2025; Priniski et al., 2019; Rosenzweig et al., 2019).

> "the degree of students' engagement when discussing utility value for course content (as reflected in the number of words in their essays) consistently serves as a mediator of the effects of the UVI on course outcomes (Asher et al., 2023; Harackiewicz et al., 2016)"
