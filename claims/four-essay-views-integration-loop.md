# Student explanations of an integration loop fall into four views: force-causal (38%), kinematic-observational (17%), iterative-local (65%), and unclassifiable (28%)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Essay explanations of the integration loop were captured by four distinct but not necessarily exclusive views, with 38% force-causal, 17% kinematic-observational, 65% iterative-local, and 28% unclassifiable. [→ John M. Aiken 2012](#john-m-aiken-2012)

## Evidence

### John M. Aiken 2012

John M. Aiken, Marcos D. Caballero, Scott S. Douglas, John B. Burk, Erin M. Scanlon, Brian D. Thoms, Michael F. Schatz. (2012). Understanding Student Computational Thinking with Computational Modeling. [doi:10.1063/1.4789648](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4789648)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Essay question completed by 29 of 32 students after the proctored assignment; students described the integration loop mathematically, physically, and programmatically. The article reports the printed percentages for each view, including 28% in no category with very short, incomplete responses.

> "Some ( 38%) students presented a "force -causal" view of the loop structure. This view  was characterized by a clear connection between force and motion."
