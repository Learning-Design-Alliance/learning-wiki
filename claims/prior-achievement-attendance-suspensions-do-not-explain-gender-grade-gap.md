# Prior achievement, attendance, and suspensions do not explain the gender difference in ninth-grade math grades

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2`–`r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Young women scored 0.27 standard deviation units above young men in ninth-grade math grades, and controlling for prior test scores, attendance, and suspensions left the difference essentially unchanged. [→ Easton 2023](#easton-2023)

## Evidence

### Easton 2023

Easton, J.Q. & Diaz, B. (2023). Lasting differences: Math grades and gender. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/lasting-differences

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Regression analysis of CPS administrative data for 29,229 students comparing young men and women with the same prior test scores, attendance, and suspensions. The unadjusted gap was "0.27 standard deviation units above young men in ninth-grade math classes."

> "Young women scored 0.27 standard deviation units above young men in ninth-grade math classes (equivalent to 0.30 of a grade point)."

### Easton 2023 (2)

Easton, J.Q. & Diaz, B. (2023). Lasting differences: Math grades and gender. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/lasting-differences

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The report states that after these comparisons "the difference after these comparisons was not statistically significantly different from the original difference," with regression outputs in Appendix Table A.4.

> "Yet when we used statistical techniques to compare young women and young men who had the same rates of prior test scores, attendance, and out-of-school suspensions, young women still had higher math grades."
