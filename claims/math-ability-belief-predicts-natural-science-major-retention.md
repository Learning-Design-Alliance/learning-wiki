# A one standard deviation increase in 10th grade perceived mathematics ability under challenge corresponds to a 1.62 times higher risk of staying in the natural sciences rather than never entering these fields in college

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Perceived mathematics ability under challenge predicted retention in the natural sciences (PEMC/biology), with a one standard deviation increase in the 10th grade measure corresponding to 1.62 times higher risk of staying in versus never entering these fields. [→ Perez-Felkner L 2017](#perez-felkner-l-2017)

## Evidence

### Perez-Felkner L 2017

Perez-Felkner L, Nix S and Thomas K (2017) Gendered Pathways: How Mathematics Ability Beliefs Shape Secondary and Postsecondary Course and Degree Field Choices. Front. Psychol. 8:386. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00386

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Major retention analyses compared intended postsecondary majors to declared majors among the ELS panel, distinguishing enterers, stayers, and leavers of the natural sciences from abstainers. The 10th grade measure remained most influential even with a 12th grade measure of the same indicator included, making the estimate more conservative.

> "a one standard deviation increase in 10th grade perceived mathematics ability corresponds to a 1.62 times higher risk of staying in the natural sciences as compared to never entering these ﬁelds in college."
