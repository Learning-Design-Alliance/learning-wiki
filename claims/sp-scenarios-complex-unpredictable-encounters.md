`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview finding from the Tackling Complexity theme: participants suggested role play and actors for communication skills with complex patients, including cases involving communication barriers and patients declining treatment, in a controlled and safe educational environment.

> "Many suggested that “using role play and actors [was] probably a good way to go” (P07) to provide opportunities for students refine their communication skills, especially when dealing with patients who present with complex issues"

### Mulhall Claire M 2025 (2)

Mulhall Claire M, Eppich Walter, Schulmann Katharine, Condron Claire, McDonough Suzanne B, O’Shea Orlagh. (2025). “It’s Still Exposure Just in a Slightly Different Way”—Understanding the Contribution of Simulation to Developing Physical Therapist Skills in Ireland: An Interpretive Description Study. Physical Therapy. https://doi.org/10.1093/ptj/pzaf102

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Participant quote framing simulation as practice for adapting communication approaches in real time rather than rehearsal of specific situations, reflecting the unpredictability educators described in clinical environments.

> "You’re dealing with a patient, you don’t know really what the response is going to be, it’s unpredictable, the workplace is unpredictable, patients are unpredictable and it’s really important that any simulation keeps up"
