# Stronger item misconception strength makes items harder to maintain or gain for students of teachers lacking KOSM, but easier for students of teachers with both SMK and KOSM

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The stronger an item's misconception strength, the more difficult it was on the posttest for students whose teachers had no SMK or only SMK without KOSM, and the easier it became for students whose teachers had both SMK and KOSM. [→ Chen 2020](#chen-2020)

## Evidence

### Chen 2020

Chen, C., Sonnert, G., Sadler, P. M., Sunbury, S. (2020). The Impact of High School Life Science Teachers’ Subject Matter Knowledge and Knowledge of Student Misconceptions on Students’ Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-08-0164

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

The article's own summary of a pattern it reports as consistent across three analytical approaches: the aggregate-level model, the item-level logistic models (M1/M2), and the fixed-pretest predictions of maintaining or gaining correctness shown in Figure 3.

> "The stronger the misconception strength of an item, the more difficult the item was in a posttest for students whose teachers had no SMK or only SMK without KOSM on that item. By contrast, for students whose teachers had both SMK and KOSM on the item, the stronger the misconception strength of the item, the easier it became in the posttest."
