# Sampling size is not an effective moderator of AR effects, with large samples (50+) at 0.647 and small samples (1-49) at 0.262

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The difference in AR effect sizes between large (50 and over) and small (1-49) sampling groups was not statistically significant (Q = 2.734 below the critical 3.841). [→ Ozdemir M. 2018](#ozdemir-m-2018)

## Evidence

### Ozdemir M. 2018

Ozdemir M., Sahin C., Arcagok S., & Demir M. K. (2018). The effect of augmented reality applications in learning process: A meta-analysis study. Eurasian Journal of Educational Research, 74, 165-186. https://doi.org/10.14689/ejer.2018.74.9

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Subgroup moderator analysis within the meta-analysis by sampling size. Pooled ES was 0.647 for large samples (10 studies) and 0.262 for small samples (6 studies); the Q value (2.734) fell below the critical value (3.841), and the article concluded the sampling size variable is not effective.

> "Table 8 indicated that the average effect size for the use of AR applications in a large sampling is 0.647, and the effect size in a small sampling is 0.262."
