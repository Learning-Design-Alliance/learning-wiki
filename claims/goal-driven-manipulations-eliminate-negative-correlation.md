# Goal-driven manipulations weaken the negative effort–monitoring correlation, which is nonsignificant under incentives, time pressure or self-agency ratings taken singly

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` With any goal-driven manipulation present, the negative effort–monitoring correlation was weaker but still significant (r = −.193, 95% CI [−.332, −.046], k = 21, against r = −.385 without); it became nonsignificant when incentives (r = −.174) or time pressure (r = −.117) were analysed separately, and self-agency effort ratings gave a nonsignificant positive association (r = .283, k = 3). ## Evidence

### Martine Baars & Lisette Wijnia & Anique de Bruin & Fred Paas 2020

Martine Baars & Lisette Wijnia & Anique de Bruin & Fred Paas. (2020). The Relation Between Students' Effort and Monitoring Judgments During Learning: A Meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09569-3

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Moderator analyses (mixed-effects model) comparing studies with versus without goal-driven manipulations found a significant moderation effect, Q(1) = 6.39, p = .011: with a manipulation present the correlation was still significantly negative but lower (r = −.193 against −.385). Analysed separately, the correlation was "nonsignificant when incentives or time pressure were used", and self-agency effort ratings gave a nonsignificant positive association (r = .283, k = 3).

> "In support of Hypotheses 3a and 3b, we found that the association between effort and monitoring judgments became nonsignificant when incentives or time pressure were used (see Table1)."
