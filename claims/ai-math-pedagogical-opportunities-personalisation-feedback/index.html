Omirzakova, F. N., & Tileubay, S. (2026). Artificial intelligence in mathematics education: A PRISMA-based systematic literature review (2021-2025). European Journal of STEM Education, 11(1), Article 43. https://doi.org/10.20897/ejsteme/19221

q2 · i? · review · r3

Thematic synthesis for RQ2 describing adaptive systems RadarMath, MathGPT, and Flexi 2.0 [G24, S13]. The review also reports teacher-facing uses: automating routine activities and assisting lesson preparation, e.g. MATH41 supporting rapid production of mathematics tasks.

"Their functions include diagnosing gaps in mathematical understanding, providing guided practice, and adjusting learning pathways in response to learners’ needs."

Omirzakova 2026 (2)¶

Omirzakova, F. N., & Tileubay, S. (2026). Artificial intelligence in mathematics education: A PRISMA-based systematic literature review (2021-2025). European Journal of STEM Education, 11(1), Article 43. https://doi.org/10.20897/ejsteme/19221

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Review synthesis of feedback mechanisms across included studies. The authors state the speed and convenience of generative tools "require careful monitoring of their pedagogical quality and methodological depth".

"large language models (LLMs) demonstrate faster response times than Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITS). However, this speed does not automatically translate into higher pedagogical quality."
