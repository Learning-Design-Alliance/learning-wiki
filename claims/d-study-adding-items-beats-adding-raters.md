# D Studies show adding items, not raters, is the best way to improve FASCI reliability: doubling items to ten raises relative reliability to about 0.71 (FA) and 0.64 (SCI)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2017 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For the FA dimension, doubling items from five to ten with three raters raises relative reliability from 0.54 to about 0.71; beyond two raters there is little gain. [→ Talbot 2017](#talbot-2017)
`q2 i?` For the SCI dimension, relative reliability at five items/three raters is 0.48 and could theoretically rise to about 0.64 with ten items; absolute reliability is only 0.30. [→ Talbot 2017 (2)](#talbot-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Talbot 2017

Talbot, R. M., III. (2017). A Multi-Faceted Approach to Scrutinizing the Reliability of a Measure of STEM Teacher Strategic Knowledge. University of Colorado Denver. Originally published 2014. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1702.06592](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1702.06592)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

D Study modeling all combinations of five to ten items and one to five raters for FA scores (Figure 9). The article reports baseline relative reliability of 0.54 versus alpha of 0.62, and that "doubling the number of items to ten and keeping the number of raters at three, relative reliability could be increased to about 0.71."

> "the results from this analysis indicate that the best way to improve FA score reliability comes from adding items to the instrument, rather than from adding raters. Beyond two raters, there is not much increase in reliability for a given number of items."

### Talbot 2017 (2)

Talbot, R. M., III. (2017). A Multi-Faceted Approach to Scrutinizing the Reliability of a Measure of STEM Teacher Strategic Knowledge. University of Colorado Denver. Originally published 2014. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1702.06592](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1702.06592)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

D Study for the SCI dimension (Figure 10). The article reports absolute reliability of 0.30 for five items and three raters, much lower than FA, indicating it "would be very difficult to make any absolute decisions on the basis of SCI scores."

> "For five items and three raters, relative reliability is estimated to be 0.48, which is lower than the value for Cronbach's alpha for these same scores (0.51). By increasing the number of items to ten and keeping the number of raters at three, relative reliability could theoretically be increased to about 0.64."
