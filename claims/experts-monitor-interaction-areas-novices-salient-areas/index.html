Evidence¶

Charlotte E. Wolff 2016¶

Charlotte E. Wolff, Halszka Jarodzka, Niek van den Bogert, Henny P. A. Boshuizen. (2016). Teacher vision: expert and novice teachers' perception of problematic classroom management scenes. Instr Sci 44:243-265. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-016-9367-z

q3 · i? · associational · r1

Confidence-interval analysis (99% or higher) of per-AOI revisits across all 64 AOIs in all four videos found experts revisited areas showing "physical and verbal interactions of or between students" while novices returned to regions with little or no student activity, e.g. an area with fluorescent green shoelaces.

"In sum, experts revisited more areas showing physical and verbal interactions of or between students, while novices revisited more regions lacking classroom interactions."

Charlotte E. Wolff 2016 (2)¶

Charlotte E. Wolff, Halszka Jarodzka, Niek van den Bogert, Henny P. A. Boshuizen. (2016). Teacher vision: expert and novice teachers' perception of problematic classroom management scenes. Instr Sci 44:243-265. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-016-9367-z

q3 · i3 · associational · r1

Mixed-design repeated measures ANOVA on total AOI revisits found a significant video-type effect with printed effect size g2 = 0.20 (the article's notation for eta-squared, in the large range); blatant escalating disruptions in Type 2 videos warranted more frequent monitoring. The between-subjects expertise effect was not significant, F(1, 60) = 3.187, p = 0.079, and there was no interaction, F < 1.

"There was a signiﬁcant effect for the type of video shown, F(1, 60) = 15.14, p \ 0.001, g2 = 0.20, with Type 2 videos (Video 3; Video 4) receiving more revisits than Type 1 videos(Video 1; Video 2)."
