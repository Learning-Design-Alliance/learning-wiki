Evidence¶

Azimi 2026¶

Azimi, S. (2026). AI Literacy over Tool Design: A Mixed-Methods Study of Scaffolded versus Unrestricted Generative AI in Programming Education. Delft University of Technology. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16784

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Thematic coding of six semi-structured interviews with students from both conditions; the article notes this finding "was not part of the design". Coach students also reported carrying hint-budget questioning into home use of unrestricted tools.

"In the interviews, students in both conditions said that the most useful outcome of the laboratory sessions had been becoming aware of how much they relied on AI."

Azimi 2026 (2)¶

Azimi, S. (2026). AI Literacy over Tool Design: A Mixed-Methods Study of Scaffolded versus Unrestricted Generative AI in Programming Education. Delft University of Technology. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16784

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Interview accounts from unrestricted-condition students who were "satisfied with their tools and, in the same interviews, uneasy about that reliance"; several said unprompted that there ought to be a way to keep AI's capacity while staying intellectually engaged.

"They described their engagement with the material as shallow, said that they felt their analytical skills slipping, and said that they nevertheless could not stop, because the tools were too efficient to give up."
