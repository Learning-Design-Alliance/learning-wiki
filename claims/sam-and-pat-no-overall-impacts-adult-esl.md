# Sam and Pat produced no statistically significant impacts on reading or English language outcomes for the overall sample of low-literate adult ESL learners

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r3` · `q4`
> **Currency** · newest study 2010 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` In a randomized trial, Sam and Pat group students did not differ significantly from control students on any of the seven reading or English language outcome measures at end of term. [→ Condelli 2010](#condelli-2010)

## Evidence

### Condelli 2010

Condelli, L., Cronen, S., Bos, J., Tseng, F., and Altuna, J. (2010). The Impact of a Reading Intervention for Low-Literate Adult ESL Learners (NCEE 2011-4003). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee/

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Randomized experiment with 1,344 low-literate adult ESL learners in 66 classes at 10 sites, tested at term start and after about 12 weeks; impact estimates compared randomly assigned Sam and Pat and control groups. "Effect sizes ranged from -0.06 to 0.01" and none were significant at the 0.05 level.

> "However, there were no statistically significant impacts of Sam and Pat on the reading and English language outcomes measured for the overall sample (Figure ES.2). Effect sizes ranged from -0.06 to 0.01."
