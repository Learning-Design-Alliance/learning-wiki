# Focus-group content analysis finds flexible e-learning video students describe cognition at four revised Bloom's taxonomy levels versus three for lecture students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Deductive content analysis of six focus groups (n = 36) identified ten categories spanning remember, understand, apply, and analyze in the video group versus five categories spanning understand, apply, and analyze in the lecture group. [→ Shikino Kiyoshi 2021](#shikino-kiyoshi-2021)

## Evidence

### Shikino Kiyoshi 2021

Shikino Kiyoshi, Rosu Claudia A., Yokokawa Daiki, Suzuki Shingo, Hirota Yusuke, Nishiya Katsumi, Ikusaka Masatomi. (2021). Flexible e-learning video approach to improve fundus examination skills for medical students: a mixed-methods study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-021-02857-8

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Qualitative content analysis of six focus groups (18 video-group and 18 lecture-group students) coded against the revised Bloom's taxonomy; interrater reliability was substantial (Cohen's kappa = 0.81). The control group contributed "five categories and six subcategories... corresponding to three levels."

> "In the intervention group, a total of ten categories and eighteen subcategories were identified corresponding to four levels of the revised Bloom's taxonomy [28]: remember, understand, apply, analyze."
