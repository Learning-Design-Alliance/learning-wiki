Yuhao Jia, Duantengchuan Li, Jinsong Chen, Zhongjie Mao, Mingwen Tong, Yue Li, Xiaoguang Wang. (2026). MBP-KT: Learning Global Collaborative Information from Meta-Behavioral Pattern for Enhanced Knowledge Tracing. Preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08697 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.08697

Performance comparison on three datasets (ASSISTments2009, EdNet-KT1, XES3G5M) with AUC, ACC, RMSE and F1 under a single-point prediction protocol; e.g., SAINT AUC rose from 0.7724 to 0.8050 on ASSISTments2009. The article reports "consistent performance improvements in almost all situations."

Related Claims¶

  • The survey reports, citing Pavlik et al., that no single knowledge tracing model was always the best, and that a better model must consider multiple student features and the learning context. — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it
  • There is no universal optimal injection position for the collaborative prior; effectiveness is an architecture-sensitive design choice — reports the opposite
  • Meta-behavioral pattern extraction outperforms knowledge-concept-based collaborative extraction, which fails to consistently boost performance due to data sparsity — related
  • Injecting the collaborative prior redistributes AKT's attention from local recency bias toward long-range dependencies — related
  • MBP-KT yields significantly greater improvements for learners with fewer interaction records than for learners with more records — related
  • MBP-KT performance is optimal with sliding-window size N between 2 and 4 and is not sensitive to pattern-set length K (variation within 2%) — related
  • Collaborative representations from meta-behavioral sequences are far less sparse than KC-based representations, which reach up to 99.3% sparsity — related
  • Symbolic knowledge injection improves temporal reliability of predictions, yielding lower early- and mid-sequence errors and the lowest prediction inconsistency rates across sequence lengths — related
