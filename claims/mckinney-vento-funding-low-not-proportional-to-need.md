# Federal McKinney-Vento funding is low and not allocated by homeless-student counts: $106.5 million in 2020–21, under $82 per identified student, reaching only a fraction of districts

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The McKinney-Vento program was funded at $106.5 million in 2020–21, equating to less than $82 per identified student, and funds are allocated to states based on Title I percentages rather than numbers of students experiencing homelessness. [→ Levin 2022](#levin-2022)

## Evidence

### Levin 2022

Levin, S., Espinoza, D., & Griffith, M. (2022). Supporting students experiencing homelessness: District approaches to supports and funding. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/557.894

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The report's policy analysis of federal funding mechanisms states the allocation formula is based on the percentage of Title I funding states receive, producing large variation across states and leaving many districts with no McKinney-Vento funds despite serving eligible students.

> "The McKinney-Vento program was funded at $106.5 million in 2020–21, which equates to less than $82 per student identified as experiencing homelessness, and only a fraction of districts with such students received funding."
