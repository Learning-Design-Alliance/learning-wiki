## Evidence

### Keith Weber 2013

Keith Weber, Matthew Inglis & Juan Pablo Mejia-Ramos. (2013). How mathematicians obtain conviction: Implications for mathematics instruction and research on epistemic cognition. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2013.865527

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Interview study with nine highly successful mathematicians from a top United States mathematics department (Weber & Mejia-Ramos) on why they read published proofs. One participant stated, "If it's in a journal, then I assume it is"; other participants expressed the opposite viewpoint.

> "Now notice what I did not say. I do not try and determine if a proof is correct. If it's in a journal, then I assume it is"

### Keith Weber 2013 (2)

Keith Weber, Matthew Inglis & Juan Pablo Mejia-Ramos. (2013). How mathematicians obtain conviction: Implications for mathematics instruction and research on epistemic cognition. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2013.865527

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Survey of 118 research-active mathematicians about their purposes in reading journal proofs. The article reports "74% of the 118 surveyed mathematicians agreed" they sometimes read proofs for understanding without checking correctness; no effect size is printed.

> "Indeed, 74% of the 118 surveyed mathematicians agreed that they sometimes did not check a published proof for correctness, but read the proof to increase their understanding"
