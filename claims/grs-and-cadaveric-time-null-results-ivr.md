## Evidence

### Lohre R 2020

Lohre R, Bois AJ, Pollock JW, Lapner P, McIlquham K, Athwal GS, Goel DP. (2020). Effectiveness of Immersive Virtual Reality on Orthopedic Surgical Skills and Knowledge Acquisition Among Senior Surgical Residents: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2020.31217

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Null result on the Global Ratings Scale in the same masked cadaveric assessment of the randomized trial; no effect size is printed and equivalence was not tested.

> "GRS total and individual domain scores were not significantly different between groups."

### Lohre R 2020 (2)

Lohre R, Bois AJ, Pollock JW, Lapner P, McIlquham K, Athwal GS, Goel DP. (2020). Effectiveness of Immersive Virtual Reality on Orthopedic Surgical Skills and Knowledge Acquisition Among Senior Surgical Residents: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2020.31217

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Cadaveric task completion time in the randomized trial: IVR 17.1 vs control 25.3 minutes, P = .13. The authors attribute the nonsignificant difference to control participants completing rapidly but incorrectly; equivalence was not tested.

> "Residents in the IVR group completed the cadaveric procedure faster than those in the control group, although the difference was not statistically significant (mean [SD] time, 17.1 [5.7] minutes vs 25.3 [32.5] minutes; difference, 8.2; 95% CI. −39.4 to 21.0; P = .13)."
