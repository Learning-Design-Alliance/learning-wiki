## Evidence

### O’Dea R. E. 2018

O’Dea R. E., Lagisz M., Jennions M. D. & Nakagawa S. (2018). Gender differences in individual variation in academic grades fail to fit expected patterns for STEM. Nature Communications. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-06292-0

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analysis of lnCV for girls by subject type (lnCV girls STEM–non-STEM diff: −0.101, CI −0.170 to −0.033; Supplementary Table 10). The article attributes the STEM similarity to girls' grades being "significantly more variable in STEM than non-STEM subjects".

> "The greater gender similarity in variability in STEM was due to girls’ grades being significantly more variable in STEM than non-STEM subjects"

### O’Dea R. E. 2018 (2)

O’Dea R. E., Lagisz M., Jennions M. D. & Nakagawa S. (2018). Gender differences in individual variation in academic grades fail to fit expected patterns for STEM. Nature Communications. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-06292-0

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analysis of lnCV for boys by subject type (lnCV boys STEM–non-STEM diff: −0.030, CI −0.102 to 0.042; CI spans zero). The article reports boys' grade variability "did not differ significantly between STEM and non-STEM subjects".

> "In contrast, the variability of boys’ grades did not differ significantly between STEM and non-STEM subjects"
