## Evidence

### Karel Van Acoleyen and Jos Van Doorsselaere 2018

Karel Van Acoleyen and Jos Van Doorsselaere. (2018). Captain Einstein: a VR experience of relativity. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1806.11085](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1806.11085)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

Authors' experience report in section IV: classroom use with smartphone VR viewers, in-class discussion and exercise session with headsets passed around and a projected point-of-view image, plus a home assignment. No learning outcome data are reported.

> "We have also used the movie in the context of the relativity course (third year bachelor physics and astronomy) at Ghent University. All students ( ≈ 50) viewed the movie on a smartphone VR viewer, either at home or in class."

### Karel Van Acoleyen and Jos Van Doorsselaere 2018 (2)

Karel Van Acoleyen and Jos Van Doorsselaere. (2018). Captain Einstein: a VR experience of relativity. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1806.11085](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1806.11085)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

Authors' interpretation (type e) in section IV, not a tested result: the educational value is their own estimation, with no measured outcomes. They refer to a separate study on the positive learning impact of the related Real Time Relativity game.

> "the immersive 360 ◦ experience of course gives the students a new handle on relativity, notably on the directional dependence of the eﬀects. But in our estimation the main advantage of the movie is actually simply that it gives a fun and concrete setting for studying the basic concepts of special relativity."
