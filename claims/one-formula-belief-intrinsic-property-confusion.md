## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Many students claimed there should be only one equation for a physical quantity, and incorrectly claimed resistance changes when voltage or current changes because of R=V/I, with similar confusions for capacitance and inductance. [→ Jing Li and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#jing-li-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)

## Evidence

### Jing Li and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Jing Li and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Students' Difficulties with Equations involving Circuit Elements. Originally published 2010. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.04872](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.04872)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Findings from written responses, informal discussions and formal paid interviews with six volunteers whose first midterm scores were near the class average. The article reports students "often incorrectly claimed that the resistance of an ohmic resistor at a given temperature should change when the voltage or current is changed."

> "They often incorrectly claimed that the resistance of an ohmic resistor at a given temperature should change when the voltage or current is changed because of the definition R=V/I. Similarly, they claimed that the capacitance of a capacitor must depend on the potential difference across it and on the charge on the capacitor plates."
