## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Students taught with algebra tiles achieved significantly higher posttest mean scores than students taught without such materials, with a very large effect (eta squared = 0.298). [→ Larbi and Mavis 2016](#larbi-and-mavis-2016)

## Evidence

### Larbi and Mavis 2016

Ernest Larbi, Okyere Mavis. (2016). The Use of Manipulatives in Mathematics Education. Journal of Education and Practice, Vol.7, No.36. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1126428

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental pretest-posttest nonequivalent group study of 56 JHS 2 students in two Ghanaian schools; experimental group taught with algebra tiles, control with traditional methods, over four weeks. The article reports "statistically significant difference" with "eta squared=0.298", meaning 29.8% of variance explained by instructional strategy.

> "there was statistically significant difference between the mean scores of the experimental group (M=32.16, SD=10.48) and that of the control group (M=20.35, SD=7.94); t (54) =4.797, p= 0.000"
