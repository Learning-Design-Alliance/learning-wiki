# Learning is a process of development in which the beginner's mode of working constantly changes and cannot use expert methods

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Even adults change their mode of performing a task constantly until they have completely learned it, so expert methods cannot be used in the earlier stage of learning. [→ E. A. Kirkpatrick 1910](#e-a-kirkpatrick-1910)

## Evidence

### E. A. Kirkpatrick 1910

E. A. Kirkpatrick. (1910). The Point of View of Genetic Psychology. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0074078

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The author's interpretive summary of existing learning research, citing penmanship, typewriting and telegraphy as areas where this 'has been clearly shown', plus tests of arithmetical prodigies. No specific studies or statistics are reported in the article itself.

> "All research concerning learning processes shows that, even in the case of adults, the beginner in any line does not continue to work in the same way, but that his mode of performing the task is constantly changing, until he has completely learned it."
