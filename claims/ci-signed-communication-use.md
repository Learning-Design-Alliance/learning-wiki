# A substantial proportion of children with cochlear implants used some form of signed communication despite parents' initial expectations otherwise

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Punch 2011

Punch, R., & Hyde, M. B. (2011). Communication, Psychosocial, and Educational Outcomes of Children with Cochlear Implants and Challenges Remaining for Professionals and Parents. International Journal of Otolaryngology. https://doi.org/10.1155/2011/573280

`q2 · i?`

Survey findings from parents (N = 247) and teachers (N = 151) on communication modes, elaborated by interviews in which 19 of 27 interviewed parents used signing. Parents valued signing for device-off communication, spoken language support, and Deaf community connection.

> "a substantial proportion (between 20% and 30%) of children were reported by both teachers and parents to be using some form of signed communication."

### Punch 2011 (2)

Punch, R., & Hyde, M. B. (2011). Communication, Psychosocial, and Educational Outcomes of Children with Cochlear Implants and Challenges Remaining for Professionals and Parents. International Journal of Otolaryngology. https://doi.org/10.1155/2011/573280

`q2 · i?`

From the step-wise regression analyses of parent survey data. The authors call for further research examining the impact of bimodal and bilingual communication with deaf students with CI.

> "The study found no negative predictor values for parents' use of signed communication or the use of signed communication in the educational setting."
