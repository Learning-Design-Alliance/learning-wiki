## Subclaims
`q2 i?` There is sometimes a gender gap on BEMA and CSEM, but it is usually much smaller than on mechanics inventories and sometimes zero or favors women, with weighted averages of 3.7% (pretest) and 8.5% (posttest). [→ Madsen 2013](#madsen-2013)

## Evidence

### Madsen 2013

Madsen, McKagan, and Sayre (2013). Gender gap on concept inventories in physics: What is consistent, what is inconsistent, and what factors influence the gap? Physical Review Special Topics - Physics Education Research 9, 020121. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.9.020121

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

The review reports E&M pretest gaps are small and variable, likely due to a 'floor effect' since all students score poorly from lack of familiarity with E&M topics, so the pretest gap may not be meaningful.

> "The weighted average pretest gender gap favoring men on the BEMA and the CSEM is 3.7%, which ranges from /C00:2% to 7.1% [16,21-23], much smaller than that on the FCI and FMCE. In some cases there is no gender gap at all."
