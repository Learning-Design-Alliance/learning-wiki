## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Active training strategies such as modeling, feedback, and practical exercises are indicated to be more effective than passive strategies such as reading manuals, with modeling the most important intervention in the cited meta-analysis. [→ Kühne Franziska 2020](#kuhne-franziska-2020)

## Evidence

### Kühne Franziska 2020

Kühne Franziska, Heinze Peter Eric, Weck Florian. (2020). Standardized patients in psychotherapy training and clinical supervision: study protocol for a randomized controlled trial. Trials 21, 276. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13063-020-4172-z

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

The protocol reports prior meta-analytic evidence in its background: the meta-analysis of helping skills training found conversational technique training "significantly more effective than no training at all (d = 0.89)" and modeling the most important intervention. The article notes the meta-analysis focused on counselling or exploration skills, not specific psychotherapeutic interventions.

> "A meta-analysis by Hill and Lent [21] revealed that training conversational techniques were significantly more effective than no training at all (d = 0.89) but that modeling was the most important intervention."
