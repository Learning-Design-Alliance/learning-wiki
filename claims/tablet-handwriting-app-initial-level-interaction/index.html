Evidence¶

Nathalie Bonneton-Botté 2020¶

Nathalie Bonneton-Botté, Sylvain Fleury, Nathalie Girard, Maëlys Le Magadou, Anthony Cherbonnier, Mickaël Renault, Eric Anquetil, Eric Jamet. (2020). Can Tablet Apps Support the Learning of Handwriting? An Investigation of Learning Outcomes in Kindergarten Classroom. Computers and Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2020.103831

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Between-groups quasi-experiment with 233 kindergarteners in 22 classes (tablet group n = 138, traditional n = 95), pre-test–12-week training–post-test. The 2x3x2 ANOVA "failed to reveal a main effect of experimental condition, F(1, 231) = 1.26, p = .261"; the three-way interaction was reported separately as significant, F(1, 231) = 3.95, p = .03. No effect sizes printed.

"This analysis failed to reveal a main effect of experimental condition, F(1, 231) = 1.26, p = .261, but there were significant main effects of time, F(1, 231) = 106.78, p < .001, and initial level, F(1, 231) = 114.14, p = .001."

Related Claims¶

  • Kindergarteners with a high initial handwriting level showed no learning effect on the transfer task in either the tablet-app or the traditional condition — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Kindergarteners with a low initial handwriting level improved significantly in both conditions, with no detectable advantage of tablet-app training over traditional instruction — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Kindergarteners with a medium initial handwriting level made significantly greater paper-based progress when handwriting training partially used the tablet app than when it was entirely traditional — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
