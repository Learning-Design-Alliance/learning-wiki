# Low-level feedback automation is appropriate only for sentence-level clarity; argumentative unclarity should trigger human review

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Clarity is not a single construct: sentence-level clarity is rule-based and automatable, while argumentative unclarity may signal incomplete understanding and requires human review rather than automated correction. [→ Venetsanos 2026](#venetsanos-2026)

## Evidence

### Venetsanos 2026

Venetsanos, D. T. (2026). A Tripartite Feedback Framework for AI-Assisted Assessment of Complex Reports in Higher Education. AI Educ. 2026, 2, 26. https://doi.org/10.3390/aieduc2030026

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical caveat in Section 3.1 of this conceptual paper. The article argues that where lack of clarity reflects lack of conceptual grasp, "the underlying issue is high-level, not low-level", and correcting prose without addressing reasoning leaves the actual problem untouched.

> "Low-level automation is therefore appropriate for sentence-level clarity, but flagged instances of argumentative unclarity should trigger human review rather than automated correction, since they may be diagnostic of a deeper high-level issue."
