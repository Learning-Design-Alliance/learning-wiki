# Student-reports of teacher personality predict subjective teacher-effectiveness measures more strongly than teacher self-reports

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For academic and personal support, student-reports showed significantly stronger associations than contextualized teacher self-reports for all five personality domains and both subjects. [→ Kim 2018](#kim-2018)
`q2 i?` For PSE the source moderation held for English teachers (all five domains) but not mathematics teachers, and it did not hold for academic achievement. [→ Kim 2018 (2)](#kim-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Kim 2018

Kim, Lisa, Dar-Nimrod, Ilan and MacCann, Carolyn (2018) Teacher Personality and Teacher Effectiveness in Secondary School: Personality Predicts Teacher Support and Student Self-Efficacy but not Academic Achievement. Journal of Educational Psychology. pp. 309-323. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000217

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Steiger's z-tests comparing level-2 correlations of mean student-reports versus contextualized teacher self-reports with teacher-effectiveness measures in the 13-school Australian sample. Support for H2 was "clearly supported for the effectiveness measures of academic and personal support".

> "Compared to contextualized teacher self-reports, student-reports showed significantly stronger associations with both academic support and personal support for all five personality domains and for both mathematics and English teachers (-3.43 ≤ z ≤ 6.20, p < .05)."

### Kim 2018 (2)

Kim, Lisa, Dar-Nimrod, Ilan and MacCann, Carolyn (2018) Teacher Personality and Teacher Effectiveness in Secondary School: Personality Predicts Teacher Support and Student Self-Efficacy but not Academic Achievement. Journal of Educational Psychology. pp. 309-323. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000217

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same comparison extended to PSE and achievement: for mathematics the only significant PSE difference favored contextualized self-reports (openness, r = -.26 vs r = -.02), while for English all five domains favored student-reports; no source differences emerged for achievement.

> "The results for PSE and academic achievement were less clear. There were differences in the results for PSE across subject areas, with H2 supported for English but not mathematics teachers."
