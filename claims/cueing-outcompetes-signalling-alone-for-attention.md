# Among two objects with the same signalling, participants focused more on the arrow-cued object, and the change occurred in the un-cued object

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Out of two objects with the same signalling, participants focused more on the object cued with an arrow; the change took place in the un-cued object and was detected when participants shifted to it. [→ Mutlu-Bayraktar 2018](#mutlu-bayraktar-2018)

## Evidence

### Mutlu-Bayraktar 2018

Mutlu-Bayraktar, D. & Bayram, S. (2018). Effects of cueing and signaling on change blindness in multimedia learning environment. World Journal on Educational Technology: Current Issues, 11(1), 128–139. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1205386

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Eye-tracking finding from the speaker scene, where two parts carried signalling but only one part received a visual cue; participants first focused on the cued object, then shifted to the un-cued object when the change occurred.

> "In addition to this, it was seen that out of two objects with the same signalling, the participants focused more on the object that was cued with an arrow, and they focused on the other object at the moment of change."
