# For a highly coherent text, taking a fill-in-the-blank practice test yields no retention benefit and does not slow forgetting compared to restudy

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Experiment 1 with a coherent black hole text, neither the main effect of learning condition nor the learning condition × retention interval interaction was significant, so testing did not slow the rate of forgetting relative to restudy. [→ de Jonge 2015](#de-jonge-2015)

## Evidence

### de Jonge 2015

de Jonge, M., Tabbers, H. K., & Rikers, R. M. J. P. (2015). The Effect of Testing on the Retention of Coherent and Incoherent Text Material. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-015-9300-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1, a 2×2 between-subjects design (restudy vs. testing; 5-min vs. 1-week interval) with 64 psychology students. Recall was 71% vs. 69% at 5 min and 48% vs. 50% at 1 week; "both F<1" for condition and interaction.

> "The main effect of learning condition and the learning condition × retention interval interaction did not reach the level of statistical significance (both F<1). Thus, we did not find a difference in the rate of forgetting between the restudy and the testing condition."
