## Evidence

### Petersen 2021

Petersen, F., & Groenewald, B. (2021). Students' Engagement in an Anonymous Peer Review: Using the Open-Source Sakai Platform. Proceedings of the 1st Virtual Conference on Implications of Information and Digital Technologies for Development, 2021. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2108.09955](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2108.09955)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Perceived-relatedness theme from the 179-student qualitative case study: students reported seeing how others tackled the problem, exposure to different perspectives, and understanding how peers thought and learnt.

> "The finding is supported by the majority of students (78 quotations) who indicated that the anonymous peer review exercise allowed them to learn from their peers."

### Petersen 2021 (2)

Petersen, F., & Groenewald, B. (2021). Students' Engagement in an Anonymous Peer Review: Using the Open-Source Sakai Platform. Proceedings of the 1st Virtual Conference on Implications of Information and Digital Technologies for Development, 2021. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2108.09955](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2108.09955)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Same case study: distrust of peer markers, uncertainty about doing the review correctly, perceptions that peers put in less effort, and non-completion by deadline meant some students did not receive results, negatively impacting perceived relatedness.

> "However, contrary evidence indicates that students did not trust their peers enough to review each other's work."
