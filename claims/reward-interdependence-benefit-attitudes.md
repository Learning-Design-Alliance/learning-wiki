# Reward interdependence raises agreement that group work was beneficial and generated better ideas among adult online learners

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants in reward groups agreed significantly more that they benefited from working with others, generated better ideas than they could individually, and that teammates earning high scores mattered. [→ Brewer 2003](#brewer-2003)

## Evidence

### Brewer 2003

Brewer, S. A., & Klein, J. D. (2003). Small Group Learning in an Online Asynchronous Environment. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED484997

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Attitude survey administered after the posttest in the four-condition experiment; Table 2 marks the relevant items significant at p < .01 (e.g., reward group mean 4.56 on importance of every member earning a high score vs 3.75 for no interdependence).

> "Participants in the reward condition had significantly higher agreement with several attitude statements that reflected benefit from working with others, being able to generate better ideas than they could have done as individuals, and the importance of fellow team mates earning a high score"
