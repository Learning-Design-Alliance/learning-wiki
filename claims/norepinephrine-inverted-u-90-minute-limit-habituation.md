# (Nor)epinephrine follows an inverted-U for learning: low levels aid learning, high levels trigger quitting, and production fades after about 90 minutes but habituates with repeated safe exposure

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Lower (nor)epinephrine levels produce calm alertness that improves learning rate, while high levels trigger a defensive impulse to quit that is incompatible with focusing on learning. [→ Lauren Margulieux 2025](#lauren-margulieux-2025)
`q2 i?` After about 90 minutes of sustained attention, uncertainty, or mistakes, (nor)epinephrine production diminishes and learning efficiency sharply declines until rest. [→ Lauren Margulieux 2025 (2)](#lauren-margulieux-2025-2)
`q2 i?` Tolerance for (nor)epinephrine can be increased through habituation: repeated safe exposure to the stressor returns elevated levels to baseline within about a week. [→ Lauren Margulieux 2025 (3)](#lauren-margulieux-2025-3)

Review narrative citing Ursin's (2012) paratrooper study as an extreme example of habituation through repeatedly experiencing safe stressful situations. The review links this to Kapur's finding that productive failure improves students' tolerance for uncertainty in later problem-solving.

> "in a study of paratroopers, Ursin (2012) found that on the first day of parachute training, (nor)epinephrine spiked to nearly double its baseline. However, it returned to normal within a week of daily jumps as the brain rapidly habituated to the stimulus."
