# Observing identifiable role models in VR scenarios supported residents' observational learning, self-efficacy and lifelong-learning needs, but limited scenario realism constrained this

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Resident doctors found observing experienced therapists in the VR scenarios meaningful for learning, picking up new approaches and strengthening self-efficacy, in line with observational learning principles. [→ Birkheim SL 2024](#birkheim-sl-2024)
`q2 i?` Scenario realism limits this benefit: scenarios depicting two therapists in conversation were described as exceptional in residents' experience, which may limit self-efficacy for handling situations alone. [→ Birkheim SL 2024](#birkheim-sl-2024)

## Evidence

### Birkheim SL 2024

Birkheim SL, Calogiuri G, Hvalstad M, Martinsen R. (2024). Exploring the experiences of resident doctors in child and adolescent psychiatry with virtual reality-based simulation training: a qualitative study. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-024-11941-w

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Focus-group data on the theme 'Observational learning contributing to lifelong learning': residents identified with the clinicians in the scenarios and valued seeing experienced professionals work, one stating "Seeing others work means you learn something new and pick up some tricks".

> "I think it’s great using VR-SIMI as one of the teaching methods in this residency program. Seeing others work means you learn something new and pick up some tricks. I’d like to experience many more scenarios."
