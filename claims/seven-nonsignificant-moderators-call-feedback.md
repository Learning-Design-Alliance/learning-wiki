# Seven moderators did not significantly affect CALL feedback: intervention length, modeling, status, language proficiency, measures of proficiency, publication type, and research setting

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Intervention length, intervention modeling, intervention status, language proficiency, measures of proficiency, publication type, and research setting showed no significant moderating effect on CALL feedback. [→ Adnan F. Saad Mohamed 2020](#adnan-f-saad-mohamed-2020)
`q3 i?` Research setting (foreign vs. second language) was not significant (QB(1) = 0.62, p > .05), with foreign settings (g = 0.59, k = 18) larger than second language settings (g = 0.35, k = 3), aligning with Li (2010) and differing from Kang and Han (2015). [→ Adnan F. Saad Mohamed 2020 (2)](#adnan-f-saad-mohamed-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Adnan F. Saad Mohamed 2020

Adnan F. Saad Mohamed. (2020). Feedback in Computer-Assisted Language Learning: A Meta-Analysis. TESL-EJ, 24(2). https://tesl-ej.org/wordpress/issues/volume24/ej84/ej84a1/

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random-effects Q-tests across the 21 studies for the seven remaining moderators (Table 5); none reached significance, e.g., intervention length QB(3) = 3.76, p > .05, and language proficiency QB(3) = 5.08, p > .05.

> "factors including intervention length, intervention modeling, intervention status, language proficiency, measures of proficiency, publication type, and research setting did not show an effect on feedback in CALL."

### Adnan F. Saad Mohamed 2020 (2)

Adnan F. Saad Mohamed. (2020). Feedback in Computer-Assisted Language Learning: A Meta-Analysis. TESL-EJ, 24(2). https://tesl-ej.org/wordpress/issues/volume24/ej84/ej84a1/

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Research-setting moderator analysis found no significant difference between foreign language (g = 0.59, k = 18) and second language (g = 0.35, k = 3) settings, though the foreign-language subgroup mean was larger, consistent with Li (2010) and contrary to Kang and Han (2015).

> "the research setting ( (1) = 0.62, p >.05) does not affect feedback in CALL on student language 
learning."
