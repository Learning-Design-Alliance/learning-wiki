# Students in online learning conditions performed better on average than those receiving face-to-face instruction (mean effect size +0.24, p < .01, across 51 contrasts).

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Across 51 independent effect sizes from experimental or controlled quasi-experimental studies, mean student learning outcomes favored online conditions over face-to-face instruction, with an average effect size of +0.24, statistically significant at p < .01. [→ Means 2009](#means-2009)

## Evidence

### Means 2009

Means, Toyama, Murphy, Bakia, & Jones. (2009). Evaluation of Evidence-Based Practices in Online Learning: A Meta-Analysis and Review of Online Learning Studies. U.S. Department of Education. https://www.ed.gov/about/offices/list/opepd/ppss/reports.html

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analysis of 51 independent effect sizes from studies with random-assignment or controlled quasi-experimental designs and objective learning measures; the pooled mean difference was significant at p < .01, with an "average effect size of +0.24 favoring online conditions."

> "Learning outcomes for students who engaged in online learning exceeded those of students receiving face-to-face instruction, with an average effect size of +0.24 favoring online conditions."
