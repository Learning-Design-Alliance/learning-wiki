## Evidence

### Braund H 2024

Braund H, Hall AK, Caners K, Walker M, Dagnone D, Sherbino J, Sibbald M, Wang B, Howes D, Day AG, Wu W, Szulewski A. (2024). Evaluating the Value of Eye-Tracking Augmented Debriefing in Medical Simulation—A Pilot Randomized Controlled Trial. Simulation in Healthcare. https://doi.org/10.1097/SIH.0000000000000825

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the RCT, four faculty debriefers rated their cognitive load after each 20-minute debrief; analysis used generalized estimating equations clustered by resident with faculty as a fixed effect. The adjusted difference of "−0.5 (−0.8 to −0.1, P = 0.005)" favored the eye-tracking condition.

> "Average debriefer CL was 3.2 (95% CI, 2.9 to 3.4) versus 3.6 (3.4 to 3.9) in the intervention and control conditions, respectively, with an adjusted between-arm difference of −0.5 (−0.8 to −0.1, P = 0.005)."
