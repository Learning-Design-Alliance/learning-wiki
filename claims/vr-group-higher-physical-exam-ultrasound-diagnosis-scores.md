`q3 i?` Undergraduate students taught with cardiac ultrasound virtual simulation scored significantly higher than the traditionally taught control group on physical examination, ultrasound manipulation, and diagnosis in the virtual simulation case assessment (P < 0.05 for each). ## Evidence

### Wang Qianyun 2025

Wang Qianyun, Wang Feifei, jiang Can, Guo Jun. (2025). The role of cardiac ultrasound virtual simulation technology in the construction of clinical diagnostic reasoning of structural heart diseases. BMC Medical Education 25:634. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07225-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled teaching experiment with 59 undergraduates (VR n=29, control n=30) randomized by student ID. The VR group "were higher in the VR group than in the control group" on all three online case-assessment components; means and SDs are printed but no effect size is reported.

> "The scores for physical examination(16.17 ± 1.74 VS 14.73 ± 2.55, P < 0.05), ultrasound manipulation (16.00 ± 2.82 VS 13.87 ± 3.56, P < 0.05) and diagnosis (16.07 ± 2.80 VS 14.07 ± 3.30, P < 0.05) were higher in the VR group than in the control group, and the difference was statistically significant."
