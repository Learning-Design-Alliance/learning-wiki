## Evidence

### Zahava Scherz · Asaf Salman · Giora Alexandron · Yael Shwartz 2023

Zahava Scherz · Asaf Salman · Giora Alexandron · Yael Shwartz. (2023). WhatsApp Discourse Throughout COVID-19: Towards Computerized Evaluation of the Development of a STEM Teachers Professional Learning Community. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-022-00320-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Chi-square comparisons of coded sub-category frequencies across the four cohorts found significantly higher Sharing tags in the pandemic cohorts; the authors attribute this to the increased need for remote teaching materials during COVID-19.

> "A significantly higher number of Sharing (SHAR) tags — which refers to shares in teaching and learning materials, instructional apps, and information of scientific contents — was noted in cohorts III and IV, as compared to cohorts I and II."

### Zahava Scherz · Asaf Salman · Giora Alexandron · Yael Shwartz 2023 (2)

Zahava Scherz · Asaf Salman · Giora Alexandron · Yael Shwartz. (2023). WhatsApp Discourse Throughout COVID-19: Towards Computerized Evaluation of the Development of a STEM Teachers Professional Learning Community. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-022-00320-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Follow-up comparison between the two pandemic cohorts showed the high volume of Sharing remained relatively steady between cohort III and cohort IV, which the authors interpret as Sharing having become a norm in the L-PLC.

> "After the outbreak, the high volume of Sharing was relatively steady, with no significant change between cohort III and cohort IV; indicating that Sharing became a norm in the L-PLC."
