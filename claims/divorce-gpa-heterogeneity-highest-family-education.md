## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The divorce–GPA association was significantly stronger when the highest parental education was at secondary school (ISCED 3–5) or Bachelor's (ISCED 6) levels compared to families where no parent exceeded ISCED 0–2. [→ Nilsen 2020](#nilsen-2020)

## Evidence

### Nilsen 2020

Nilsen, S. A., Breivik, K., Wold, B., Askeland, K. G., Sivertsen, B., Hysing, M., & Bøe, T. (2020). Divorce and adolescent academic achievement: Heterogeneity in the associations by parental education. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229183

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

OLS regression Model 2 with an education-by-divorce interaction term. The article reports significant interactions for ISCED 3–5 (b = -0.202, p < 0.05) and ISCED 6 (b = -0.245, p < 0.05) versus ISCED 0–2, shown in Fig 1A.

> "the results from these models showed that the associations between having divorced parents and the adolescents’ GPA were significantly stronger among adolescents where the highest parental education was at secondary school levels (ISCED 3–5) or Bachelor's levels (ISCED 6)"
