## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The amplifying effect of reading on math growth was strongest for below-average math performers with reading z > 0, and negligible or absent for children with reading z < −1. [→ Erbeli 2021](#erbeli-2021)

## Evidence

### Erbeli 2021

Erbeli, F., Shi, Q., Campbell, A. R., Hart, S. A., & Woltering, S. (2021). Developmental Dynamics Between Reading and Math in Elementary School. Developmental Science. https://doi.org/10.1111/desc.13004

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Statistical vector field plot (Figure 5) based on expected values from the best-fitting bivariate model, showing expected grade-to-grade change for combinations of reading and math scores. "Average and high reading performance levels appeared to have their strongest amplifying effects" for below-average math performers.

> "Average and high reading performance levels appeared to have their strongest amplifying effects (the steepest arrows) over time on math growth for below average math performing children, as indicated on the bottom right side of the vector field."

### Erbeli 2021 (2)

Erbeli, F., Shi, Q., Campbell, A. R., Hart, S. A., & Woltering, S. (2021). Developmental Dynamics Between Reading and Math in Elementary School. Developmental Science. https://doi.org/10.1111/desc.13004

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same vector field analysis: on the left side of the plot (reading z < −1), arrows were flat or almost horizontal, indicating "a negligible or no expected growth in math" at the lowest reading levels.

> "At the lowest levels of reading performance, there was a negligible or no expected growth in math."
