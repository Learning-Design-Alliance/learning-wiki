## Evidence

### F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry 2013

F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry. (2013). The benefits of completing homework for students with different aptitudes in an introductory physics course. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1305.2213](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1305.2213)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Observational analysis of plots of weighted-average exam scores versus homework completion for students in the USAFA E&M course in fall 2010 and spring 2011, with line fits. The authors report that "medium-low and low aptitude students had a negative benefit from doing homework for both semesters shown in Fig. 1".

> "Remarkably, medium-low and low aptitude students had a negative benefit from doing homework for both semesters shown in Fig. 1, indicating that, on average, the more homework that medium-low and low aptitude students did, the worse they performed on exams."

### F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry 2013 (2)

F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry. (2013). The benefits of completing homework for students with different aptitudes in an introductory physics course. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1305.2213](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1305.2213)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Line-fit and Pearson correlation analysis of exam scores versus homework completion for five semesters (spring 2009 to spring 2011) of the USAFA E&M course, summarized in Table IV. The negative correlations reached significance (p < 0.05) in "4 out of the 5 semesters".

> "The p-values from Table IV show that the negative correlations between homework and exam scores for medium-low and low aptitude students are statistically significant (p < 0.05) for 4 out of the 5 semesters."

### F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry 2013 (3)

F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry. (2013). The benefits of completing homework for students with different aptitudes in an introductory physics course. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1305.2213](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1305.2213)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Averaging of the Table IV line-fit slopes across the five semesters, plotted against aptitude in Fig. 2 with standard-deviation error bars. The authors state that medium-low and low aptitude students had a negative benefit "of more than one standard deviation".

> "Fig. 2 shows that, on average, medium-low and low aptitude students had a negative benefit from completing homework of more than one standard deviation."
