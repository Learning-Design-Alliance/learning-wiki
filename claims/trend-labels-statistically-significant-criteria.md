# Profile trends labeled up or down are determined by statistical criteria, so those labels denote statistically significant trends

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Each year's input, output, and gain trends are classified as up, down, or flat using statistical criteria, so that the up and down labels mark statistically significant trends. [→ Anthony S. Bryk 1998](#anthony-s-bryk-1998)

## Evidence

### Anthony S. Bryk 1998

Anthony S. Bryk, Yeow Meng Thum, John Q. Easton, Stuart Luppescu. (1998). Mining Productivity: How to Read Your Productivity Profile. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Reporting methodology described in the Your School Productivity Profiles section: school profiles label all three trends for each year as up, down, or flat, and the guide states it "used statistical criteria to make these determinations" so the labels carry statistical significance.

> "We have used statistical criteria to make these determinations so that "up" and "down" are statistically significant trends."
