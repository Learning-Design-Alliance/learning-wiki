# Force parameters decrease over trials for novices and experts but not intermediates

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Mean non-zero force and maximum impulse decreased significantly from first to sixth trial in the novice and expert groups, and force volume decreased in the expert group; the intermediate group showed no significant decrease on these parameters. [→ Rahimi A. Masie 2023](#rahimi-a-masie-2023)

## Evidence

### Rahimi A. Masie 2023

Rahimi A. Masie, Hardon Sem F., Willuth E., Lang F., Haney Caelan M., Felinska Eleni A., Kowalewski Karl-Friedrich, Müller-Stich Beat P., Horeman Tim, Nickel F., Daams Freek. (2023). Force-based assessment of tissue handling skills in simulation training for robot-assisted surgery. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-023-09905-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Wilcoxon signed-rank tests in the same study showed "a decrease in the mean non-zero force was observed in the novice group" and expert group, with printed medians and p-values; the intermediate group's decreases on these parameters were not statistically significant (e.g. p = 0.825 for mean non-zero force).

> "a decrease in the mean non-zero force was observed in the novice group (1.38 N vs. 1.22 N p = 0.040) and expert group (1.07 N vs. 1.00 N p = 0.017). Similarly, a decrease in max impulse was found in the novice group (27.01 Ns vs. 15.09 Ns p = 0.016) and expert group (14.74 Ns vs. 10.45 Ns p = 0.017). Lastly, the expert group were able to decrease the force volume (0.98 N3 vs. 0.66 N3 p = 0.019)"
