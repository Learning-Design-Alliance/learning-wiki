# Simulator use increased the proportion of students choosing the correct esophageal cancer diagnosis from 65% to 92%

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After simulator use, the proportion of students writing down the most probable diagnosis correctly rose from 65% (40/62) to 92% (57/62). [→ Kleinert R 2015](#kleinert-r-2015)

## Evidence

### Kleinert R 2015

Kleinert R, Heiermann N, Plum PS, Wahba R, Chang D, Maus M, Chon S, Hoelscher AH, Stippel DL. (2015). Web-Based Immersive Virtual Patient Simulators: Positive Effect on Clinical Reasoning in Medical Education. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.5035

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of pre- and postsimulator clinical reasoning files in the same single-group evaluation; students wrote down the most probable diagnosis and the tutor rated each answer right or wrong. Correct diagnosis rose from "65% (40/62)" to "92% (57/62)".

> "In the prequestionnaire, 65% (40/62) of students chose the right diagnosis whereas after simulator use, 92% (57/62) of students chose the right diagnosis."
