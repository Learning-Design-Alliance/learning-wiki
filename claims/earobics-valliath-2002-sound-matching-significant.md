# In a Chicago quasi-experiment, Earobics®'s overall effect was not significant, but the CTOPP Sound Matching subtest showed a significant positive effect

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Valliath (2002) found a positive, statistically significant effect on CTOPP Sound Matching (effect size 1.13), while the other five WWC-analyzed outcomes were not statistically significant. [→ What Works Clearinghouse 2009](#what-works-clearinghouse-2009)

## Evidence

### What Works Clearinghouse 2009

What Works Clearinghouse. (2009). WWC Intervention Report: Earobics®. U.S. Department of Education, What Works Clearinghouse. https://www.earobics.com

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental study of 30 first-graders with low reading ability in three Chicago-area schools; 15 used Earobics® games 20 minutes daily for 10 weeks versus 15 using math software. Appendix A3.1 prints an effect size of 1.13 for CTOPP Sound Matching, marked "Statistically significant".

> "The WWC found that the effect for one of the four phonological awareness tests (CTOPP: Sound Matching subtest) was positive and statistically significant."

### What Works Clearinghouse 2009 (2)

What Works Clearinghouse. (2009). WWC Intervention Report: Earobics®. U.S. Department of Education, What Works Clearinghouse. https://www.earobics.com

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Same quasi-experiment; the WWC analyzed four CTOPP phonological awareness subtests and two WRMT phonics subtests. The study average effect size was 0.60, judged substantively important but not statistically significant.

> "Valliath (2002) found that the overall intervention effect across the eight measures of beginning reading was not statistically significant."
