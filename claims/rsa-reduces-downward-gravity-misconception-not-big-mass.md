## Evidence

### Postiglione A and De Angelis I 2021

Postiglione A and De Angelis I. (2021). Students' understanding of gravity using the rubber sheet analogy: an Italian experience. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/abd1c4](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/abd1c4)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-questionnaire distractor analysis on the deformation item. The authors report "only the 3% of our sample choose the distractor" referring to downward gravity, attributing this to the opening discussion of the analogy's limitations.

> "our data show that it can indeed be fought using the RSA, since only the 3% of our sample choose the distractor “a force that points downward” when asked to specify what deforms the space-time."

### Postiglione A and De Angelis I 2021 (2)

Postiglione A and De Angelis I. (2021). Students' understanding of gravity using the rubber sheet analogy: an Italian experience. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/abd1c4](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/abd1c4)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Post-questionnaire item on the source of space-time deformation. The authors state the activity "does not solve it completely", with 34% still referring only to very big masses after the activity.

> "the data shows that our activity, even having faced this aspect, does not solve it completely, since there is a substantial part of students (34%) that after the activity refers only to very big masses when asked about the source of deformation."
