Evidence¶

Yang Gao and Barbara E. Hanna 2016¶

Yang Gao and Barbara E. Hanna. (2016). Exploring optimal pronunciation teaching: Integrating instructional software into intermediate-level EFL classes in China. CALICO Journal. https://doi.org/10.1558/cj.v33i2.26054

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Pearson correlation over all 60 participants between test-score increases and attitude-survey increases, reported in Table 11 as r = 0.778, significant at p < 0.01 (one-tailed): "The positive correlation of nearly 0.8 was significant at p < 0.01 (one-tailed)." The correlation is observational, not a causal test.

"The Pearson correlation coefficient was used to calculate the strength of association between increases of scores in tests on general quality of pronunciation and of scores in surveys on attitudes towards pronunciation (Table 11). The positive correlation of nearly 0.8 was significant at p < 0.01 (one-tailed)."
