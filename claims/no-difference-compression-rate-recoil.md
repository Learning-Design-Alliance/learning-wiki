## Evidence

### McCoy C. Eric MD 2019

McCoy C. Eric MD, MPH; Rahman Asif MD; Rendon Juan C. MD; Anderson Craig L. PhD, MPH; Langdorf Mark I. MD, MPHE; Lotfipour Shahram MD, MPH; Chakravarthy Bharath MD, MPH. (2019). Randomized Controlled Trial of Simulation vs. Standard Training for Teaching Medical Students High-quality Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation. Western Journal of Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2018.11.39040

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the randomized trial's primary outcomes, compression rate showed no statistically significant difference between groups; the article reports "The mean compression rate was 123.3 per minute" for SIM versus 116.1 for STD, p=0.06. No effect size was printed; equivalence was not tested.

> "The mean compression rate was 123.3 per minute (95% CI [117.9 – 128.4]) for the simulation (SIM) group and 116.1 per minute (95% CI [109.9 – 121.2]) for the STD group, p=0.06"

### McCoy C. Eric MD 2019 (2)

McCoy C. Eric MD, MPH; Rahman Asif MD; Rendon Juan C. MD; Anderson Craig L. PhD, MPH; Langdorf Mark I. MD, MPHE; Lotfipour Shahram MD, MPH; Chakravarthy Bharath MD, MPH. (2019). Randomized Controlled Trial of Simulation vs. Standard Training for Teaching Medical Students High-quality Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation. Western Journal of Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2018.11.39040

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The recoil proportion outcome in the same trial showed no significant difference between the SIM and STD groups (p=0.83). The article states "The mean percentage of chest compressions that were accompanied by full chest recoil was 0.954" for SIM versus 0.941 for STD. No effect size was printed.

> "The mean percentage of chest compressions that were accompanied by full chest recoil was 0.954 (95% CI [0.925 – 0.978]) for the SIM group and 0.941 (95% CI [0.874 – 0.985]) for the STD group, p=0.83"
