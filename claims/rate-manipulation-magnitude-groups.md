# Instructed slow reading ran about 40% below normal rate and rapid reading about 16% below normal time, fairly uniformly across groups except seventh graders

## Evidence

### Gilliland 1922

Gilliland, A. R. (1922). The effect of rate of silent reading on ability to recall. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1922-02093-001

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Manipulation check from the rate-of-reading experiment, reported from the reading-time data (Table I). The author states the slow and rapid instructions shifted rate as printed, with the seventh grade as the stated exception to the rapid-reading shift.

> "In general the rate of reading, when the subjects were instructed to read very slowly was about 40 per cent slower than their normal rate. This variation in rate was fairly constant for each of the four groups of subjects. The time required for reading rapidly was about 16 per cent less than the normal reading time."
