# Neural coding of consonants in noise strongly predicts phonological processing in 4-year-old pre-readers over and above demographic factors

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In 4-y-old children who had not yet learned to read, a regression model of neural coding of consonants in noise predicted phonological processing above demographics (ΔR2 = 0.488, p = 0.003). [→ White-Schwoch T 2015](#white-schwoch-t-2015)

## Evidence

### White-Schwoch T 2015

White-Schwoch T, Woodruff Carr K, Thompson EC, Anderson S, Nicol T, Bradlow AR, et al. (2015) Auditory Processing in Noise: A Preschool Biomarker for Literacy. PLoS Biol 13(7): e1002196. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1002196

`q3 · i?`

Hierarchical regression in a cohort of 37 4-y-olds (mean age 54.41 months) who had not yet learned to read; the model combined neural timing, trial-by-trial stability, and spectral-feature measures from a single FFR recording. The neural step added "ΔR2 = 0.488" over demographics, with total R2 = 0.684; no standardized effect size is printed, so impact is null.

> "We found that neural coding of consonants in noise strongly predicted phonological processing in pre-readers over and above demographic factors (CELF P-2 Phonological Awareness; ΔR2 = 0.488, F[9,24] = 4.121, p = 0.003; total R2 = 0.684, F[12,36] = 4.328, p = 0.001"
