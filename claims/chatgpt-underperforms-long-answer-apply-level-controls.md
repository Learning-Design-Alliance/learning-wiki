## Evidence

### Kwong Andrew C. 2026

Kwong Andrew C., Magnano Christopher, DeOliveira Cristina, Goglia Christine, Loparo Joseph J., Peters John Jacob. (2026). Designing life science assessments in the era of generative artificial intelligence. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0346127

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Blind grading by two teaching fellows and one postdoctoral fellow of GPT-4o responses against randomly selected student responses (n = 20) on Modules 1 and 5 long-answer worksheets. ChatGPT averaged "68% compared to 91%" for students.

> "ChatGPT achieved an average of 68% compared to 91% from a randomly selected student subset (Fig 2A). Doctoral students thus outperform GAI without prompt engineering, and ChatGPT importantly did not achieve the passing mark of 80%"

### Kwong Andrew C. 2026 (2)

Kwong Andrew C., Magnano Christopher, DeOliveira Cristina, Goglia Christine, Loparo Joseph J., Peters John Jacob. (2026). Designing life science assessments in the era of generative artificial intelligence. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0346127

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Bloom's-level breakdown of the same blind-graded long-answer comparison (Fig 2B). GAI matched students on understand and analyze but was "significantly worse on “apply”-level questions", which required rationalizing positive and negative controls.

> "When we examined the specific Bloom's levels associated with each rubric component, GAI performed similarly to doctoral students on the “understand” and “analyze” levels (Fig 2B). However, ChatGPT performed significantly worse on “apply”-level questions"
