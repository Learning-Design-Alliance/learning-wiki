# After the policy changes, students lived on average 0.6 miles closer to a school with full-day pre-k, with the largest gains for high-priority groups

## Evidence

### Ehrlich 2020

Ehrlich, S.B., Connors, M.C., Stein, A.G., Francis, J., Easton, J.Q., Kabourek, S.E., & Farrar, I.C. (2020). Closer to home: More equitable pre-k access and enrollment in Chicago. Chicago, IL: UChicago Consortium on School Research, NORC at the University of Chicago, and Start Early. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/closer-to-home

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive pre-post comparison of the two full-day access levers (Figures 10-11). Before the policy, students were unlikely to have even one full-day classroom near home; after, most had at least one. For example, 0.5 percent of Black students had one full-day classroom nearby pre-policy versus more than 2 percent after, while students in mostly-White neighborhoods saw a small drop.

> "on average, students were 0.6 miles closer to a school with full-day pre-k after policy changes, compared to before (see Figure 10). In particular, high-priority student groups (Black, Latinx, lowest-income, English Learners, and those living in high-priority neighborhoods) saw the largest decreases in distance to schools with full-day pre-k."
