# On the difficult problem, the notes-and-textbook group outperformed the worked-example group on the transfer (midterm) task despite lower self-diagnosis scores

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Group D (final answer plus notes and textbook) scored higher than group C (detailed worked example) on the midterm transfer problem for quiz 6 (0.47 vs 0.33, p-value=0.07), a marginal difference. [→ Andrew Mason 2009](#andrew-mason-2009)

`q2 i?` Group C, which received a worked-out example, scored lower on the midterm than group D, which received only notes and textbooks. [→ E. Yerushalmi 2008](#e-yerushalmi-2008)

## Evidence

### Andrew Mason 2009

Andrew Mason, Elisheva Cohen, Chandralekha Singh, and Edit Yerushalmi. (2009). Self-Diagnosis, Scaffolding and Transfer: A Tale of Two Problems. [doi:10.1063/1.3266736](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3266736)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Inter-group comparison of midterm (post) scores for quiz 6 reported in the Findings section: group D mean 0.47 versus group C mean 0.33, with p-value=0.07, despite group C receiving "the complete solution". The difference is marginal at the printed threshold.

> "Group D  fared better than group C on the midterm  (C-0.33, D-0.47, p-value=0.07) despite the fact that group C got the complete solution ."

### E. Yerushalmi 2008

E. Yerushalmi, A. Mason, E. Cohen, C. Singh. (2008). Effect of Self Diagnosis on Subsequent Problem Solving Performance. AIP Conf. Proc. 1064. [doi:10.1063/1.3021272](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3021272)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Classroom quasi-experiment, second TA's sections; Table 3 gives midterm physics means of 0.329 for group C versus 0.473 for group D; Table 4 reports the comparison p-value as 0.071.

> "However, despite actually seeing the complete solution, group C fared worse on the midterm than group D, who did not receive the solution during the recitation but had to try and figure it out on their own."
