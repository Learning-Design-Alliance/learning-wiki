## Evidence

### Sambeth A 2026

Sambeth A, Toth M, Blokland A (2026). Methylphenidate (Ritalin) does not improve exam performance in an experimental setting. Psychopharmacology 243(3):593–600. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-025-06864-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the randomized double-blind placebo-controlled between-subjects experiment (PLA-PLA n = 25, MPH-PLA n = 24, PLA-MPH n = 26), one-way ANOVAs compared the three groups per question category; the article reports "no difference between the treatment groups" with the printed η2 values of 0.05, 0.02 and 0.02.

> "there was no difference between the treatment groups on the performance of factual multiple choice questions (F(2,72) = 1.89, p > 0.10, η2 = 0.05), inference multiple choice questions (F(2,72) = 0.67, p > 0.10, η2 = 0.02), and inference open questions (F(2,72) = 0.73, p > 0.10, η2 = 0.02)"

### Sambeth A 2026 (2)

Sambeth A, Toth M, Blokland A (2026). Methylphenidate (Ritalin) does not improve exam performance in an experimental setting. Psychopharmacology 243(3):593–600. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-025-06864-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Bayesian complement to the per-category ANOVAs in the same randomized experiment; the printed Bayes factors of 0.025–0.080 support the null. Equivalence beyond the tested contrasts was not tested.

> "The Bayes factors ranged between 0.025 and 0.080, indicating that there was no evidence to reject the H0."
