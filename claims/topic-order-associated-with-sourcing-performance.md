# Task topic order was associated with sourcing performance and with individual change during the intervention

## Evidence

### Hämäläinen 2023

Hämäläinen, E. K., Kiili, C., Räikkönen, E., Lakkala, M., Ilomäki, L., Toom, A., & Marttunen, M. (2023). Teaching sourcing during online inquiry – adolescents with the weakest skills benefited the most. Instructional Science, 51, 135–163. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-022-09597-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-test comparison across the counterbalanced topic orders (vaccination vs. fats) in the quasi-experimental design; topic order was controlled in all regression analyses, and Table 4 shows topic order was a significant predictor of post-test sourcing in all four variables.

> "Additionally, in the post-test, the vaccination task students performed better in all the sourcing variables than the fats task students."

### Hämäläinen 2023 (2)

Hämäläinen, E. K., Kiili, C., Räikkönen, E., Lakkala, M., Ilomäki, L., Toom, A., & Marttunen, M. (2023). Teaching sourcing during online inquiry – adolescents with the weakest skills benefited the most. Instructional Science, 51, 135–163. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-022-09597-2

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Crosstabulation with χ2 tests and Cramer's V related topic order to RCI change classes among intervention students; the printed effect sizes were V = 0.22 and V = 0.18, and students who explored fats in the pre-test demonstrated a clear positive change significantly more often than those who explored vaccination.

> "topic order was associated with RCI classes in Sourcing in search queries ( χ2(2) = 15.32, p < .001, V = 0.22) and Sourcing in credibility judgments (χ2(3) = 10.59, p = .014, V = 0.18)."
