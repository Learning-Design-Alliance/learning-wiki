## Evidence

### Bugaj T. J. 2016

Bugaj T. J., Nikendei C. (2016). Practical Clinical Training in Skills Labs: Theory and Practice. GMS Journal for Medical Education 33(4) Doc63. [doi:10.3205/zma001062](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001062)

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Weyrich et al.'s randomized controlled trial and other studies, that peer-assisted learning by trained student tutors is equivalent to faculty-led skills lab training, attributed to 'eye level learning'. No effect size is printed.

> "This could be down to the fact that several studies have been able to show that PAL is equivalent to medical faculty instructed training [68], [69], [70] as it allows for "eye level learning" [58], [71]."

### Bugaj T. J. 2016 (2)

Bugaj T. J., Nikendei C. (2016). Practical Clinical Training in Skills Labs: Theory and Practice. GMS Journal for Medical Education 33(4) Doc63. [doi:10.3205/zma001062](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001062)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

The review attributes to an inventory study by Blohm et al. that PAL is used in about 90% of the skills labs of the surveyed German medical faculties. This is a descriptive survey finding.

> "according to a recent study published by Blohm et al. the principle of the PAL (peer-assisted learning) is used in about 90% of the skills labs of the surveyed German faculties."
