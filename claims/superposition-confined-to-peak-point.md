## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students explained that waves only add when amplitudes meet, or that amplitudes add when waves are on top of each other, focusing on single points rather than point-by-point addition. [→ Michael C. Wittmann 2002](#michael-c-wittmann-2002)

## Evidence

### Michael C. Wittmann 2002

Michael C. Wittmann. (2002). The object coordination class applied to wavepulses: Analysing student reasoning in wave physics. International Journal of Science Education 24:1, 97-118. [doi:10.1080/09500690110066944](https://doi.org/10.1080/09500690110066944)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview and written responses to the superposition question in figure 6, asked at a time when the peaks do not overlap. Another student explained 'since the waves are on top of each other, the amplitudes add'; both response types were repeated nearly verbatim by many students.

> "A student who drew a sketch like the one in figure 7-b explained, 'The waves only add when the amplitudes meet'. Unless the two points of the wavepulses that the student considers relevant overlap, these students assume there is no summation of displacements (superposition) in the region where the wavepulses do overlap."
