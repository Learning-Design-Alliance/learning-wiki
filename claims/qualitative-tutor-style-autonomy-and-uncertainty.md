## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Tutors observed high student autonomy in non-facilitatively tutored groups, particularly after the second PBL session. [→ Gerhardt-Szep Susanne 2016](#gerhardt-szep-susanne-2016)
`q2 i?` Students favored the facilitative style (n = 20 positive evaluations) while the primary effect of non-facilitative tutoring was uncertainty about the PBL procedure. [→ Gerhardt-Szep Susanne 2016 (2)](#gerhardt-szep-susanne-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Gerhardt-Szep Susanne 2016

Gerhardt-Szep Susanne, Kunkel Florian, Moeltner Andreas, Hansen Miriam, Böckers Anja, Rüttermann Stefan, Ochsendorf Falk. (2016). Evaluating differently tutored groups in problem-based learning in a German dental curriculum: a mixed methods study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0505-0

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Thematic analysis of semi-structured expert interviews with the four tutors in the qualitative part; tutors reported students "found out very many things on their own in the second session" and "needed less eye contact".

> "In the groups tutored non-facilitatively, tutors observed a high degree of student autonomy during the PBL sessions, particularly after the second session."

### Gerhardt-Szep Susanne 2016 (2)

Gerhardt-Szep Susanne, Kunkel Florian, Moeltner Andreas, Hansen Miriam, Böckers Anja, Rüttermann Stefan, Ochsendorf Falk. (2016). Evaluating differently tutored groups in problem-based learning in a German dental curriculum: a mixed methods study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0505-0

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Focused group discussion with eight students exposed to both styles, thematically analyzed; response frequencies reported in Table 8. Students described the facilitative tutor as a "guideline that leads you along".

> "the students rated PBL as practice-oriented learning (n = 6) and favored the facilitative tutoring style (n = 20), with only one student rating the two tutoring styles as equally effective."
