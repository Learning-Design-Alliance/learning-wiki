# Compensatory selection eliminates or tempers the positive correlation between valid standardized test scores and degree completion among accepted students

## Subclaims
`q2 i0` With correlated predictors and top-10% compensatory selection, a valid test showed an absent correlation with degree completion (r = .003). [→ Huber David E. 2022](#huber-david-e-2022)
`q2 i?` With uncorrelated, equally predictive predictors, including the test in top-10% selection tempered the positive correlation (R2 variance accounted for dropped from .92 to .71). [→ Huber David E. 2022 (2)](#huber-david-e-2022-2)
`q2 i0` Over-weighting the valid test in the selection sum produced little relationship between test scores and completion (r = .09). [→ Huber David E. 2022 (3)](#huber-david-e-2022-3)

## Evidence

### Huber David E. 2022

Huber David E., Cohen Andrew L., Staub Adrian. (2022). Compensatory selection with standardized tests. PLoS ONE 17(5): e0265459. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0265459

`q2 · i0` · `theoretical · r3`

Simulation with four latent characteristics and correlated predictors under a top-10% compensatory admissions policy; for this valid-test ground truth the article reports "an absent correlation (r = .003) between Test scores and degree completion".

> "This situation produced an absent correlation (r = .003) between Test scores and degree completion as seen in the middle graph."

### Huber David E. 2022 (2)

Huber David E., Cohen Andrew L., Staub Adrian. (2022). Compensatory selection with standardized tests. PLoS ONE 17(5): e0265459. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0265459

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Simulation with four equally predictive, uncorrelated predictors; including Test scores in top-10% selection "tempers this positive correlation (the R2 percent variance accounted for drops from .92 drops to .71)" relative to accepting all applicants.

> "the inclusion of Test scores in the selection process tempers this positive correlation (the R2 percent variance accounted for drops from .92 drops to .71)"

### Huber David E. 2022 (3)

Huber David E., Cohen Andrew L., Staub Adrian. (2022). Compensatory selection with standardized tests. PLoS ONE 17(5): e0265459. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0265459

`q2 · i0` · `theoretical · r3`

Same simulation with Test over-weighted in the summed selection score; over-weighting "eliminates accepted students with lower Test scores", producing "little relationship between Test scores and degree completion (r = .09)".

> "This eliminates accepted students with lower Test scores, as seen in the middle graph, resulting in little relationship between Test scores and degree completion (r = .09)."
