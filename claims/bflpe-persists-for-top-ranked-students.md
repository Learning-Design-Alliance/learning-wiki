## Evidence

### Herbert W. Marsh 2026

Herbert W. Marsh. (2026). Four Decades After the Big-Fish–Little-Pond Effect: Critical Boundary Tests and Integrative Models of Mean-Context, Ordinal-Standing, and Distributional-Scaling in 46 Countries. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10200-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Rank-fixed boundary analysis retaining exactly one top-ranked student per class (N = 11,684, one per class, unweighted): "the contextual effect remained negative (L2 = − 0.356, SE = 0.095)", contradicting the strong ordinal-standing prediction that the effect should approach zero at top rank.

> "In the top-student analysis, ordinal standing was fixed at the most favorable possible position, yet the contextual effect remained negative (L2 = − 0.356, SE = 0.095). Thus, even the student with no higher-achieving classmate still reported lower MSC in higher-achieving classrooms."
