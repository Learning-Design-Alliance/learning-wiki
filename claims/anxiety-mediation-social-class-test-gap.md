## Evidence

### Nele Claes 2024

Nele Claes, Annique Smeding, Arnaud Carré, Nicolas Sommet. (2024). The social class test gap: A worldwide investigation of the role of academic anxiety and income inequality in standardized test score disparities. Journal of Educational Psychology, 116(6), 871-888. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000881

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Mediation analyses within the PISA 2012 and 2015 multilevel models showed social class → anxiety → test performance mediations in the expected direction for all indicators; "Study 2a: all indirect effects ≥ .01, all ps <.001; Study 2b, all indirect effects ≥ 0.002, all p <.001".

> "The negative effect of anxiety accounted for between 10% and 19% of the positive association between social class indicators (i.e., parental education, family cultural capital, and family economic capital) and test performance"

### Nele Claes 2024 (2)

Nele Claes, Annique Smeding, Arnaud Carré, Nicolas Sommet. (2024). The social class test gap: A worldwide investigation of the role of academic anxiety and income inequality in standardized test score disparities. Journal of Educational Psychology, 116(6), 871-888. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000881

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Study 1 mediation analysis on PISA 2003 data (276,165 students, 41 countries) found a parental education → mathematics anxiety → mathematics performance mediation, "indirect effect = 0.02 [0.02, 0.03], p < .001" (Figure 1, upper panel).

> "Parental education was negatively associated with mathematics anxiety, β = - 0.09 [- 0.11, - 0.07], p < .001, which accounted for 17%7 of the positive association between parental education and mathematics performance"
