## Evidence

### Brijnath Bianca 2016

Brijnath Bianca, Bunzli Samantha, Xia Ting, Singh Nabita, Schattner Peter, Collie Alex, Sterling Michele, Mazza Danielle. (2016). General practitioners knowledge and management of whiplash associated disorders and post-traumatic stress disorder: implications for patient care. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-016-0491-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Knowledge-level results from the cross-sectional national survey of Australian GPs, scored from true/false/don't-know statements and grouped using means and standard deviations. The survey found 32.2 % of GPs were "deemed to have a high knowledge of WAD".

> "32.2 % (95 % CI: 27.9 %, 36.9 %) of GPs were deemed to have a high knowledge of WAD, whilst 58.2 % (95 % CI: 53.4 %, 62.9 %) and 9.6 % (95 % CI: 7.1 %, 12.8 %) of GPs were deemed to have moderate and low knowledge of WAD respectively."

### Brijnath Bianca 2016 (2)

Brijnath Bianca, Bunzli Samantha, Xia Ting, Singh Nabita, Schattner Peter, Collie Alex, Sterling Michele, Mazza Danielle. (2016). General practitioners knowledge and management of whiplash associated disorders and post-traumatic stress disorder: implications for patient care. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-016-0491-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Knowledge-level results from the same cross-sectional survey's PTSD scale: "The majority of GPs (59.4 %, 95 % CI: 54.6 %, 64.0 %) had moderate knowledge of PTSD", with 16.7 % low and 23.9 % high.

> "The majority of GPs (59.4 %, 95 % CI: 54.6 %, 64.0 %) had moderate knowledge of PTSD, and 16.7 % (95 % CI: 13.4 %, 20.6 %) had low and 23.9 % (95 % CI: 20.0 %, 28.2 %) had a high knowledge of PTSD."
