Evidence¶

Lasha Labadze 2023¶

Lasha Labadze, Maya Grigolia and Lela Machaidze. (2023). Role of AI chatbots in education: systematic literature review. International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-023-00426-1

q2 · i? · review · r2

Narrative synthesis in the educator-advantages section of the review. The review reports chatbots help educators "save time on routine tasks" and that educators can generate open-ended question prompts aligned with learning objectives, per Herft (2023).

"AI chatbot administrative support capabilities can help educators save time on routine tasks, including scheduling, grading, and providing information to students, allowing them to allocate more time for instructional planning and student engagement."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • The most significant reported benefit of AI in instructional design is assistance with administrative and repetitive tasks (18 of 28 studies, 64.2%) — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Teachers envisioned chatbots as extending teacher capacity and availability by providing additional access to support and redistributing planning workload — related
  • Nearly all educators most want AI to free up time in existing workflows and return capacity to overburdened staff — related
  • Participants reported that AdaPT reduced perceived preparation workload and aligned with their natural lesson adaptation workflows — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • AI assistance provides no significant overall time savings and can slow completion on easy task variants, with chat-interface friction (prompting time) dominating on trivial tasks — reports the opposite
  • People overestimate how much time AI assistance saves (speedup illusion), driven by miscalibration about AI-assisted completion time while independent completion time is well calibrated — reports the opposite
  • People overestimate how much mental effort AI assistance alleviates (offloading illusion), driven by overestimating the effort of independent completion while AI-assisted effort is accurately estimated — reports the opposite
