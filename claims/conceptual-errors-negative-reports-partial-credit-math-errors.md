## Evidence

### Corey McInerney 2026

Corey McInerney. (2026). A Thematic Analysis of A-level Physics Examiner Reports on Gravity. arXiv. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/ae806d](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/ae806d)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Cross-tabulation of 158 coded examiner-report phrases against overall sentiment in the thematic analysis. The results state that "it is conceptual errors which appear the most in 'negative' reports (59%)".

> "Yet despite the majority of lost marks being from mathematical errors, it is conceptual errors which appear the most in 'negative' reports (59%)."

### Corey McInerney 2026 (2)

Corey McInerney. (2026). A Thematic Analysis of A-level Physics Examiner Reports on Gravity. arXiv. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/ae806d](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/ae806d)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Sentiment analysis of coded examiner comments in the same dataset. The results report that "mathematical error comments were actually associated with a positive sentiment more often (45%) than a negative one (30%)", which the author reads as examiners awarding partial credit for calculation errors.

> "In contrast, mathematical error comments were actually associated with a positive sentiment more often (45%) than a negative one (30%)."
