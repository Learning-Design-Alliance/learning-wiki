# High-achieving students less often endorse AI-supported autonomy, effective learning, and active engagement, and more often agree that reliance on AI hinders critical thinking and independent problem solving

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` At item level, GPA band correlates negatively with feeling more autonomous when using AI (ρ = −0.285) and actively using AI for academic tasks (ρ = −0.269), and positively with agreeing that relying on AI hinders critical thinking and independent problem solving (ρ = 0.252). ### Jason Zagami 2026

International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education 23:47. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00625-6

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r1`

Item-level Spearman correlations with student-reported GPA band (Table 2), conducted because the grouped indices had weak internal consistency. Strongest item associations ranged from ρ = −0.285 (autonomy) to ρ = 0.252 (reliance hinders critical thinking), all p < 0.001.

> "High-achieving students were less likely to report that AI supported autonomy, helped them learn more effectively, or formed part of active academic engagement. They were also more likely to agree that reliance on AI hinders critical thinking and independent problem solving."
