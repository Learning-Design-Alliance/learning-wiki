## Evidence

### Turnbull 2019

Turnbull, S. M., Vanholsbeeck, F., Locke, K., & O'Neale, D. R. J. (2019). Bourdieu, networks, and movements: Using the concepts of habitus, field and capital to understand a network analysis of gender differences in undergraduate physics. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0222357](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0222357)

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Vertical-movement analysis of high school to stage-one course transitions using GPE percentile ranks; the study reports the enrolment percentages and odds ratio for top achievers entering AP1, quoting "Only 8.72% of male students and 5.06% of female students".

> "Only 8.72% of male students and 5.06% of female students who were top achievers from high school chose to enrol in AP1. These percentages also indicate that from this top achieving group, male students were 1.79 times more likely to go to AP1 (OR = 0.56, CI: 0.36-0.87)."

### Turnbull 2019 (2)

Turnbull, S. M., Vanholsbeeck, F., Locke, K., & O'Neale, D. R. J. (2019). Bourdieu, networks, and movements: Using the concepts of habitus, field and capital to understand a network analysis of gender differences in undergraduate physics. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0222357](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0222357)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same transition analysis comparing top achievers' choice of PLS, the physics course required for life-science students; the study reports "2.84 (CI: 2.10-3.83) times more likely" for females choosing this pathway, with no standardized effect size printed.

> "In contrast, 72.66% of male students, and 88.35% of female students from this top achieving group enrolled in PLS. Top achieving female students were thus 2.84 (CI: 2.10-3.83) times more likely than their male counter parts to follow this pathway."
