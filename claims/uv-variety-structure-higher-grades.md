# In a college biology course, students assigned a variety of self- and other-focused utility-value assignments earned higher course grades than students who wrote the same assignment type three times

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Writing a variety of self- and other-focused UV assignments produced higher course grades than writing the same type three times (β = 0.07, p = .024). [→ Priniski 2019](#priniski-2019)

## Evidence

### Priniski 2019

Priniski, S. J., Rosenzweig, E. Q., Canning, E. A., Hecht, C. A., Tibbetts, Y., Hyde, J. S., & Harackiewicz, J. M. (2019). The Benefits of Combining Value for the Self and Others in Utility-Value Interventions. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000343

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the RCT (N = 590), regression within a path-modeling framework tested five orthogonal contrasts on final course grades. The variety contrast was significant: "Œ≤ = 0.07, p = .024"; students writing varied assignments averaged M = 3.05 (SD = 0.69) versus M = 2.85 (SD = 0.80) for same-type writers.

> "There was a significant main effect of the variety contrast, which compared the two conditions in which students wrote a variety of self- and other-focused UV assignments to the two conditions in which students wrote only one type of assignment, on course grades, Œ≤ = 0.07, p = .024"
