# Practice effects in visual reproduction are specific: they transfer to new conditions only in proportion to identical ability-conditioning factors.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Increased ability depends on the particular material, time, instructions and conditions; tricks gained with dot-figures were valueless with other material, and the observers were not better observers or memorizers in general. [→ Foster 1910](#foster-1910)

## Evidence

### Foster 1910

Foster, W. S. (1910). The Effect of Practice upon Visualizing and upon the Reproduction of Visual Impressions. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0075685

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Theoretical conclusion drawn from the experiment's results and introspections. The conclusions section argues practice is specific, that dot-figure tricks were valueless elsewhere, and that no general habits of observing closely were furnished.

> "Practice, in other words, is specific. Practice with one set of conditions is valuable in a new set, only in proportion as the second set of conditions contain identical ability-conditioning factors."
