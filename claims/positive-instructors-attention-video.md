## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In Experiment 1, learners reported being more motivated to pay attention when the instructor displayed positive rather than negative emotions. [→ Lawson 2021](#lawson-2021)
`q3 i?` In Experiment 1, four of five self-report measures (effort, difficulty, enjoyment, wanting more lessons) showed no effect of instructor emotion. [→ Lawson 2021 (2)](#lawson-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Lawson 2021

Lawson, A. P., Mayer, R. E., Adamo-Villani, N., Benes, B., Lei, X., & Cheng, J. (2021). The positivity principle: do positive instructors improve learning from video lectures? Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-021-10057-w

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Item-level 2×2 ANOVAs on Experiment 1 postquestionnaire responses showed a large valence effect on the pay-attention item, "d = 1.07".

> "There was a significant effect of valence, F(1, 99) = 28.29, p < .001, d = 1.07, with participants indicating they were more motivated to pay attention when the instructor displayed positive emotions (M = 2.96, SD = 1.22) compared to when she displayed negative emotions (M = 1.84, SD = .903)"

### Lawson 2021 (2)

Lawson, A. P., Mayer, R. E., Adamo-Villani, N., Benes, B., Lei, X., & Cheng, J. (2021). The positivity principle: do positive instructors improve learning from video lectures? Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-021-10057-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Effort, difficulty, enjoyment, and wanting-more-lessons items showed no significant valence, activity, or interaction effects in Experiment 1; no effect sizes were printed for these null findings.

> "Overall, there is some support based on one of five subjective self-report learning meas- ures that the instructor’s level of positivity influenced the learner’s willingness to try hard to understand the lesson. However, ratings on four out of five measures were not consistent with hypothesis 3."
