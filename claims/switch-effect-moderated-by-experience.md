## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The article reports that the negative effect of switching from 3D to 2D was present in novices but not in moderately experienced surgeons on the same task, suggesting dependence on both task difficulty and inexperience. [→ Harada Hitoshi 2017](#harada-hitoshi-2017)

## Evidence

### Harada Hitoshi 2017

Harada Hitoshi, Kanaji Shingo, Nishi Masayasu, Otake Yoshito, Hasegawa Hiroshi, Yamamoto Masashi, Matsuda Yoshiko, Yamashita Kimihiro, Matsuda Takeru, Oshikiri Taro, Sumi Yasuo, Nakamura Tetsu, Suzuki Satoshi, Sato Yoshinobu, Kakeji Yoshihiro. `q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Discussion-level interpretation citing the authors' prior work: moderately experienced surgeons' performance on the straight rod transfer task (task 2 here) did not worsen after switching from 3D to 2D, whereas novices' performance on the same task did. This is a cross-study comparison, not a test within this study.

> "This suggests that the deleterious effect of loss with depth perception using 3D is dependent not only on the difficulty of the task but also on the lack of experience of the surgeon."
