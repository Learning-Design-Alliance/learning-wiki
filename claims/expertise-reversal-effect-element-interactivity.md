# Element interactivity and expertise reversal effects: cognitive load effects diminish and may reverse as expertise increases because known elements chunk into single elements

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` With increasing expertise, cognitive load effects may disappear and may even reverse; this reversal is the expertise reversal effect, a specific example of the element interactivity effect. [→ John Sweller 2023](#john-sweller-2023)

## Evidence

### John Sweller 2023

John Sweller. (2023). The Development of Cognitive Load Theory: Replication Crises and Incorporation of Other Theories Can Lead to Theory Expansion. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09817-2

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical statement in the article's review of element interactivity, attributed to Kalyuga et al. (2003) for the expertise reversal effect and Chen et al. (2017) for its relation to element interactivity. The article presents it as a construct explaining apparent replication failures.

> "Because of the element interactivity effect, with increasing expertise, cognitive load effects may disappear and may even reverse. This reversal is itself a cognitive load effect called the expertise reversal effect"

### John Sweller 2023 (2)

John Sweller. (2023). The Development of Cognitive Load Theory: Replication Crises and Incorporation of Other Theories Can Lead to Theory Expansion. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09817-2

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article reports, citing Chen et al. (2015, 2016a, b, 2017) and Kalyuga et al. (2001), that the reverse worked example effect occurs for low element interactivity information, in accordance with the expertise reversal effect. No statistics are printed in the quoted sentence.

> "a reverse worked example effect with problem solving being superior to studying worked examples is obtained for low element interactivity information"
