# Personalized Learning Effects Vary With Fidelity

The measured benefits of personalized learning depend heavily on how faithfully a program is implemented — effects observed in efficacy trials often shrink or disappear when implementation drifts from the intended design.

## Discussion

**Fidelity as a moderator.** Implementation research consistently distinguishes efficacy (ideal conditions) from effectiveness (typical conditions). Personalized learning programs are especially fidelity-sensitive because they bundle multiple components — adaptive technology, data-driven grouping, learner agency, revised teacher roles — and partial implementation can capture the cost of the program without its benefit. A school that adopts an adaptive platform but retains whole-class pacing, for example, may realize little of the intended differentiation [-M].

**What fidelity means here.** Fidelity has multiple dimensions: adherence (delivering the components as designed), dosage (sufficient time on the intervention), quality (skill of delivery), and learner responsiveness. For technology-mediated personalization, dosage and teacher use of the generated data are often the weakest links — dashboards that no one acts on do not personalize anything. This connects to the broader finding that [adaptive learning improves outcomes](adaptive-learning-improves-outcomes.md) only when usage is sustained and the system's recommendations are actually enacted [~M].

**Why multi-component models are fragile.** Because personalization bundles [adaptive difficulty](../elements/adaptive-difficulty.md), [competency-based pacing](../patterns/competency-based-learning.md), and revised teacher roles, each dropped component is a potential point of failure. Unlike single-mechanism interventions (e.g., [chunking reduces working memory load](chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md)), there is no one "active ingredient" that survives partial implementation. This mirrors the pattern seen in [blended learning improves outcomes](blended-learning-improves-outcomes.md), where the blend's enactment, not its label, drives effects [~M].

**Implications for evaluation and adoption.** Evaluations of personalized learning should measure and report fidelity — adherence checklists, usage logs, observation of teacher data practices — rather than treating "personalized learning" as a single undifferentiated treatment. Schools adopting such programs should budget for the implementation supports (professional development, scheduling, data routines) that efficacy trials typically include; without them, expected effects should be discounted accordingly. This is a constraint on interpretation, not a refutation: the claim is that effects are conditional, not that personalization cannot work.

**Open questions.** Because this page currently has no catalogued evidence entries, the specific magnitude of fidelity effects for personalized learning is not yet established in this wiki. Studies are needed that measure implementation fidelity directly and relate it to outcomes, ideally with dose–response designs that link fidelity scores to learner gains. Until then, treat claims of large average effects with caution.
