Evidence¶

Mollborn 2014¶

Mollborn, S., Lawrence, E., James-Hawkins, L., & Fomby, P. (2014). When Do Socioeconomic Resources Matter Most in Early Childhood? Advances in Life Course Research. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.alcr.2014.03.001

q3 · i? · associational · r3

In the ECLS-B growth curve models, the maternal education × age interaction was the largest of the three SES measures. The authors report the education–cognitive association "predicted an increase in cognitive/reading and math scores of 0.4 standard deviations at the peak of the relationship."

"An increase in maternal education equivalent to the change from a high school degree to a college degree predicted an increase in cognitive/reading and math scores of 0.4 standard deviations at the peak of the relationship"
