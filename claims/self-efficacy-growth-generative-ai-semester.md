# Students' self-efficacy for using generative AI increased over the semester, with current self-efficacy significantly higher after the fifth assignment than after the first

## Evidence

### Daniel M. Oppenheimer 2025

Daniel M. Oppenheimer, Trent N. Cash, Allison E. Connell Pensky. (2025). You've Got AI Friend in Me: LLMs as Collaborative Learning Partners. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-025-00521-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Survey study using a bespoke three-item self-efficacy scale (αs = 0.75 − 0.86) on 0–100% sliders; n = 81 for Assignment 1 and n = 98 for Assignment 5. Total self-efficacy means rose from 71.7 to 81.2 across the semester (Table 1). No effect size is printed.

> "students’ current self-efficacy was significantly higher after the fifth iteration of the assignment than after the first iteration of the assignment ( t(177) = 4.25, p =.006), suggesting growth throughout the semester."
