Evidence¶

Gnambs Timo 2020¶

Gnambs Timo. (2020). Limited evidence for the effect of red color on cognitive performance: A meta-analysis. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-020-01772-1

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Publication-bias analyses (rank correlation, PET/PEESE, regression, selection models, puniform*) on within-sample averaged effect sizes; the rank correlation test was significant (r = -.24, p = .036) and "no evidential value for an effect of red color" remained.

"an examination of the p-value distributions of the observed effect sizes (van Aert & van Assen, 2020) found no evidential value for an effect of red color on cognitive performance (about Δ = -0.02)."

Related Claims¶

  • Control color choice, color matching, presentation medium, and presentation timing showed no moderating effects on red color effects
  • Red color effects declined over time: initial studies found substantially larger effects than subsequent research
  • Red color effects, if they exist, may be limited to low-stakes laboratory research and may not generalize to applied testing settings
  • No significant difference between red and control color conditions for anagram tests or knowledge tests — related
  • The pooled effect of viewing red color on cognitive test performance is small (Δ = -0.13) — reports the opposite
  • Publication-bias checks indicate the pooled effect is unlikely to result from publication bias — reports the opposite
