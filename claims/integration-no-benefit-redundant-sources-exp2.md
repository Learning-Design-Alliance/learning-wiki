# Physical integration of text and diagrams yields no benefit when the sources of information are independently intelligible (redundant)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` When lighting-circuit diagrams could be understood without their accompanying text, conventional split-source and integrated instructions produced no significant difference in written or practical test performance. [→ Chandler 1991](#chandler-1991)

## Evidence

### Chandler 1991

Chandler, P. & Sweller, J. (1991). Cognitive Load Theory and the Format of Instruction. Cognition and Instruction, 8(4). http://ro.uow.edu.au/edupapers/128

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A 2 (Groups) x 2 (Test Periods) ANOVA on written lighting test scores of 28 first-year apprentices, using materials where diagrams were intelligible without reference to accompanying text, found the conventional group performed marginally better but "there was no significant difference between the groups." A Fisher Exact Test on the practical light-switch construction task (7 of 10 conventional vs 4 of 10 modified successful) also found no significant difference.

> "Nevertheless, there was no significant difference between the groups, F(l, 26) = 1.76, MSe = 64.29."
