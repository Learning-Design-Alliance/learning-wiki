# Explicit L2 grammar instruction leads to larger learning effects than implicit instruction, as reported in the reviewed synthesis literature

## Evidence

### Aydinbek C 2022

Aydinbek C. (2022). Contributions of cognitive theory to the problem of automatization of grammatical structures in teaching foreign language. Educational Research and Reviews. https://doi.org/10.5897/ERR2022.4230

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Norris and Ortega (2000), this meta-analytic finding on explicit versus implicit L2 instruction; the review narrates the result rather than computing it, and no effect size is printed here.

> "on average, instruction that incorporates explicit (including deductive and inductive) techniques leads to more substantial effects than implicit instruction"

### Norris & Ortega 2000

Norris, J. M., & Ortega, L. (2000). Effectiveness of L2 instruction: A research synthesis and quantitative meta-analysis. *Language Learning, 50*(3), 417–528. [doi:10.1111/0023-8333.00136](https://doi.org/10.1111/0023-8333.00136)

`q3 · research synthesis and meta-analysis (abstract only)` · `i?` · `n=49 studies`

Systematic research synthesis and meta-analysis of experimental and quasi-experimental studies of L2 instruction published between 1980 and 1998, comparing average effect sizes from 49 unique sample studies. The abstract reports that focused L2 instruction produces large target-oriented gains, that explicit types of instruction are more effective than implicit types, and that the effects are durable. Only the abstract was read: it prints no effect size, confidence interval or participant count, so the size of the explicit advantage is not established here, and the authors state that generalizability is limited because the field had not yet rigorously operationalized its type-of-instruction constructs. The Aydinbek (2022) entry above reports this same finding second-hand, so the two entries are not independent evidence.

> "explicit types of instruction are more effective than implicit types"

### Goo et al. 2015

Goo, J., Granena, G., Yilmaz, Y., & Novella, M. (2015). Implicit and explicit instruction in L2 learning: Norris & Ortega (2000) revisited and updated. In *Implicit and Explicit Learning of Languages* (Studies in Bilingualism, pp. 443–482). John Benjamins Publishing Company. [doi:10.1075/sibil.48.18goo](https://doi.org/10.1075/sibil.48.18goo)

`q3 · meta-analysis (abstract only)` · `i?` · `n=34 studies`

Meta-analytic review restricted to studies in which explicit and implicit instructional treatments were compared directly: 34 unique sample studies, 11 taken from Norris and Ortega's (2000) meta-analysis and 23 new studies published between 1999 and 2011. Overall, explicit instruction was more effective than implicit instruction, and moderator variables were also analysed. Only the abstract was read: it prints no effect size, no moderator results and no participant count. Because 11 of its 34 studies are Norris and Ortega's, this entry and theirs are partly the same evidence.

> "Overall, explicit instruction was found to have been more effective than implicit instruction."

### Spada & Tomita 2010

Spada, N., & Tomita, Y. (2010). Interactions between type of instruction and type of language feature: A meta-analysis. *Language Learning, 60*(2), 263–308. [doi:10.1111/j.1467-9922.2010.00562.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2010.00562.x)

`q3 · meta-analysis (abstract only)` · `i?` · `n=41 studies`

Meta-analysis of 41 studies of explicit and implicit instruction on the acquisition of simple and complex grammatical features in English, with features classed as simple or complex by the number of criteria applied to arrive at the correct form (Hulstijn & de Graaff, 1994) and treatments classed as explicit or implicit following Norris and Ortega (2000). Effect sizes were larger for explicit than implicit instruction for both simple and complex features, and explicit instruction contributed to both controlled knowledge and spontaneous use. Only the abstract was read: it prints no effect sizes or participant counts, and the target language in every study was English.

> "The results indicate larger effect sizes for explicit over implicit instruction for simple and complex features."
