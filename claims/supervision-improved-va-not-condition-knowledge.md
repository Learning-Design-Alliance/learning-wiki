## Evidence

### Kalua Khumbo 2014

Kalua Khumbo, Gichangi Michael, Barassa Ernest, Eliah Edson, Lewallen Susan, Courtright Paul. (2014). A randomised controlled trial to investigate effects of enhanced supervision on primary eye care services at health centres in Kenya, Malawi and Tanzania. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6963-14-S1-S6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Results of the cluster-randomised trial: changes in individual test items were analysed by a non-parametric (Kruskal-Wallis) test across the 137 facilities; no significant improvement for the four condition-knowledge items. No effect size printed.

> "Knowledge regarding cataract, conjunctivitis, presbyopia, and trauma did not improve significantly in the intervention facilities compared to non-intervention."

### Kalua Khumbo 2014 (2)

Kalua Khumbo, Gichangi Michael, Barassa Ernest, Eliah Edson, Lewallen Susan, Courtright Paul. (2014). A randomised controlled trial to investigate effects of enhanced supervision on primary eye care services at health centres in Kenya, Malawi and Tanzania. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6963-14-S1-S6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' summary of the trial's visual acuity component (Table 3 shows VA change 0.90 vs 0.09 points, p<0.001 all countries); the gain is described as modest and of questionable clinical relevance in the discussion.

> "With regular supervision based on skills transfer, we demonstrated a modest increase in the ability of PHCWs to measure VA."
