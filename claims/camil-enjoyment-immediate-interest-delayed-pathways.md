## Evidence

### Guido Makransky · Richard E. Mayer 2022

Guido Makransky · Richard E. Mayer. (2022). Benefits of Taking a Virtual Field Trip in Immersive Virtual Reality: Evidence for the Immersion Principle in Multimedia Learning. Educational Psychology Review (2022) 34:1771–1798. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-022-09675-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Structural equation model of the cognitive affective model of immersive learning fitted to the same 102-student experiment (CFI = 0.973, TLI = 0.965, SRMR = 0.046, RMSEA = 0.070). The quoted paths define the enjoyment pathway to the immediate posttest; only standardized path coefficients are printed, so no effect size is reported.

> "The path from enjoyment to immediate posttest score was also significant (β = 1.563, p = 0.002, se = 0.493), but the path from interest to immediate posttest score (β = 0.357, p = 0.452, se = 0.474) was not significant."

### Guido Makransky · Richard E. Mayer 2022 (2)

Guido Makransky · Richard E. Mayer. (2022). Benefits of Taking a Virtual Field Trip in Immersive Virtual Reality: Evidence for the Immersion Principle in Multimedia Learning. Educational Psychology Review (2022) 34:1771–1798. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-022-09675-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

The same SEM (Lavaan, maximum likelihood) also shows the interest pathway: "a significant path from interest to delayed posttest score" while enjoyment's path to the delayed posttest is not significant. The immediate posttest also predicted the delayed posttest (β = 0.739, p < 0.001, se = 0.071).

> "the model illustrates a significant path from interest to delayed posttest score (β = 1.294, p < 0.001, se = 0.340), but the path from enjoyment to delayed posttest score was not significant (β = -0.302, p = 0.409, se = 0.366)."
