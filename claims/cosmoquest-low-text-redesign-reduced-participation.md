# CosmoQuest's mandated low-text website redesign correlated with a significant reduction in project participation, reversed in 2019 by a text-heavy design that explained data use and credit

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After the 2016-2018 low-text redesign, the majority of existing users left and new users clicked through more often but did not stay, producing a significant reduction in project participation. [→ Reiheld 2019](#reiheld-2019)

## Evidence

### Reiheld 2019

Reiheld, A., & Gay, P. L. (2019). Coercion, Consent, and Participation in Citizen Science. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1907.13061](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1907.13061)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Observational account of the CosmoQuest project's experience after a third-party usability consultant's low-text design was mandated by funding requirements; no effect size is printed.

> "As a result, the majority of the project's existing users left, and new users, while clicking through to try projects more often, didn't stay. The combined result was a significant reduction in project participation."

### Reiheld 2019 (2)

Reiheld, A., & Gay, P. L. (2019). Coercion, Consent, and Participation in Citizen Science. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1907.13061](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1907.13061)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Observational report from the CosmoQuest project: the 2019 Bennu Mappers launch restored explanatory text, and the authors report a correlation with longer participation, not a tested causal effect.

> "This was reversed in 2019, with the launch of Bennu Mappers and a return to a more text-heavy design that delineated how data would be used and credit would be given. This correlated with a greater duration of project participation."
