Evidence¶

Ponniah 2011¶

Ponniah, R. J. (2011). The Effectiveness of the Comprehension Hypothesis: A Review on the Current Research on Incidental Vocabulary Acquisition. i-manager's Journal on English Language Teaching, 1(2), April-June 2011. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1071042 doi:10.26634/jelt.1.2.1452

q2 · i? · review · r1

Review reports Tekmen and Daloglu (2006): intermediate, upper-intermediate and advanced students read 'The Golden Fleece' (50 min, audio first reading, silent rereading, no dictionary); 30 target words tested at pre, post, and delayed post-test one week later. Gains: intermediate 3.12, upper-intermediate 4.00, advanced 5.34.

"Analysis of the data revealed that there was significant lexical gains on the raw scores for the subjects after the post test. The results of the delayed posttest showed that the subjects were able to retain the incidentally acquired words without much attrition."
