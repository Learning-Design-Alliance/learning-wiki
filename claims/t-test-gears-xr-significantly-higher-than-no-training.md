# Independent samples t-test shows significantly higher GEARS scores for XR-trained novices versus no additional training

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` An independent samples t-test found a significantly higher mean GEARS score for the XR-trained group (16.73) versus the no-training group (13.91), with a large effect size (d = 0.964, p < 0.001). [→ Bickford Michael 2025](#bickford-michael-2025)

## Evidence

### Bickford Michael 2025

Bickford Michael, Alruwaili Fayez, Ragab Sara, Rothenberg Hanna, Abedin-Nasab Mohammad (2025). Impact of extended reality on robot-assisted surgery training: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Robotic Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11701-025-02559-z

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Independent samples t-test comparing mean GEARS scores between XR-trained (n = 63) and no-training (n = 60) groups. The effect size was "strongly in favor of the VR trained group" at 0.964 with a 95% CI of [0.589, 1.336].

> "The independent samples t test yielded an effect size strongly in favor of the VR trained group; 0.964 (95% CI 0.589 to 1.336). The threshold for a large effect size is 0.8"
