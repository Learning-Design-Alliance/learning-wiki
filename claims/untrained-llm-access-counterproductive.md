## Evidence

### Benjamin M. Chen and Hong Bao 2026

Benjamin M. Chen and Hong Bao. (2026). Training for Technology: Adoption and Productive Use of Generative AI in Legal Analysis. [doi:10.2139/ssrn.6217459](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6217459)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Comparison of Group 2 (LLM access, no training) versus Group 1 (no LLM access) in the randomized examination; the decline in grade point was "not statistically significant" with a negligible printed effect size (d = -0.039).

> "On average, participants in Group 2 seemed to perform worse than participants in Group 1 on the grade point measure but this decline is not statistically significant (d = -0.039; p = 0.790, two-tailed t-test)."

### Benjamin M. Chen and Hong Bao 2026 (2)

Benjamin M. Chen and Hong Bao. (2026). Training for Technology: Adoption and Productive Use of Generative AI in Legal Analysis. [doi:10.2139/ssrn.6217459](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6217459)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Answer length in words: Group 1 mean 1059.673, Group 2 mean 895.737, Group 3 mean 1001.638. Untrained LLM answers were significantly shorter on two-tailed tests; these tests were not pre-registered. The printed d values are raw word differences, not standardized effect sizes.

> "If anything, answers in Group 2 were on the whole shorter than answers in Group 1 (d = -163.937; p = 0.019*, two-tailed t-test) and Group 3 (d = -105.901; p = 0.042*, two-tailed t-test)."

### Benjamin M. Chen and Hong Bao 2026 (3)

Benjamin M. Chen and Hong Bao. (2026). Training for Technology: Adoption and Productive Use of Generative AI in Legal Analysis. [doi:10.2139/ssrn.6217459](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6217459)

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Case Misstatements counted cited cases irrelevant to the preceding argument; Group 2 mean 0.193 versus Group 1 mean 0.020. The printed effect size d = 0.173 is below the small-effect threshold. The authors suggest "ineffective interaction with GenAI tools by Group 2 ultimately left participants with less time" to verify authorities.

> "Group 2 recorded a significantly higher number of Case Misstatements compared with Group 1 (d = 0.173; p = 0.017*, one-tailed t-test)."
