## Evidence

### McQuistan 2026

McQuistan, M. R., Young, L. B., & Pendleton, C. (2026). Poverty Simulations: Are the Learning Outcomes Consistently and Uniformly Positive? Journal of Dental Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/jdd.70090

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive analysis of attitude change from Table 1 for 162 students completing T1 and T2 measures. The article reports "21.6%, a substantial minority, reported poorer attitude scores at T2" with a median decline of 4.0 points.

> "although the majority reported an improvement in attitude scores from T1 to T2, 21.6%, a substantial minority, reported poorer attitude scores at T2."

### McQuistan 2026 (2)

McQuistan, M. R., Young, L. B., & Pendleton, C. (2026). Poverty Simulations: Are the Learning Outcomes Consistently and Uniformly Positive? Journal of Dental Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/jdd.70090

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Follow-up analysis at T3 (N = 159) shows the proportion reporting declined attitudes rose to 34.6%. The article states "those reporting poorer attitudes rose 13.0 percentage points to 34.6%" with a median decline of 4.0 points.

> "Two months later, students reporting improved poverty attitudes had dropped 11.8 percentage points compared to baseline, and those reporting poorer attitudes rose 13.0 percentage points to 34.6%."
