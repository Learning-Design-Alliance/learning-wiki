# Fine motor hand-function subtests correlate positively, though weakly, with specific basic nursing skills (nasogastric drug administration, blood collection, sterile glove wearing, subcutaneous injection) in first-year nursing students

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Left hand fine motor score correlates with nasogastric drug administration (r = 0.197), both-hand score with blood collection (r = 0.191), and assembly score with sterile glove wearing (r = 0.200) and subcutaneous injection (r = 0.277). [→ Ela Yılmaz Coşkun 2025](#ela-ylmaz-coskun-2025)

## Evidence

### Ela Yılmaz Coşkun 2025

Ela Yılmaz Coşkun, Aylin Yalçın Irmak, Merdiye Şendir. (2025). Examining the Relationship Between Basic Nursing Skills, Hand Function, and Anxiety Levels in First‐Year Nursing Students: A Correlational Study. Journal of Evaluation in Clinical Practice. https://doi.org/10.1111/jep.70191

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional correlational analysis (Pearson's correlations, N = 123 first-year nursing students) of hand skill subtest scores against basic nursing skill checklist scores. The article reports these as "significant positive low correlation" values, with r = 0.277 the largest.

> "A significant positive low correlation was found between the assembly skill score and the ability to wear sterile gloves (r = 0.200, p < 0.05) and subcutaneous injection skill (r = 0.277, p < 0.01)."
