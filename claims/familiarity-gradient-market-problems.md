## Evidence

### Banerjee 2025

Banerjee, A. V., Bhattacharjee, S., Chattopadhyay, R., Duflo, E., Ganimian, A. J., Rajah, K. & Spelke, E. S. (2025). Children's arithmetic skills do not transfer between applied and academic mathematics. Nature. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08502-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Delhi study 2a familiarity experiment: 400 working children answered five hypothetical market problems of increasing remove from their daily activity. The article reports 96% correct with own goods and price, "88% did so when the price was different", 69% with a second item, and 61% with unfamiliar pricing units.

> "96% of these children correctly calculated the amount due in a transaction that involved the same goods that they sold at the same price that they sold it, and 88% did so when the price was different"

### Banerjee 2025 (2)

Banerjee, A. V., Bhattacharjee, S., Chattopadhyay, R., Duflo, E., Ganimian, A. J., Rajah, K. & Spelke, E. S. (2025). Children's arithmetic skills do not transfer between applied and academic mathematics. Nature. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08502-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same Delhi study 2a sample, measuring solution approach as problems became less familiar. The article reports "only 4% of children used pen and paper" on familiar items, rising to 29% with two goods in familiar units, 41% in unfamiliar units, and 58% on context-free school-style problems.

> "When they worked with an item sold at their store, only 4% of children used pen and paper."
