## Evidence

### McManus IC 2013

McManus IC, Woolf Katherine, Dacre Jane, Paice Elisabeth, Dewberry Chris. (2013). The Academic Backbone: longitudinal continuities in educational achievement from secondary school and medical school to MRCP(UK) and the specialist register in UK medical students and doctors. BMC Medicine 11:242. https://doi.org/10.1186/1741-7015-11-242

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r3`

Correlational analysis of the UCLMS Cohort Study (729 clinical entrants, 2005-2006), reported in Table 1 covering GCSE, A-level, five medical school years and MRCP(UK) parts. The authors report that "All correlations were positive, with high correlations between the results in the 5 years of medical school"; e.g. fourth-to-fifth year r = 0.831, a large printed correlation.

> "All correlations were positive, with high correlations between the results in the 5 years of medical school. It is also striking that there were significant correlations of A-levels and even GCSEs with performance in medical school and at MRCP(UK)."

### McManus IC 2013 (2)

McManus IC, Woolf Katherine, Dacre Jane, Paice Elisabeth, Dewberry Chris. (2013). The Academic Backbone: longitudinal continuities in educational achievement from secondary school and medical school to MRCP(UK) and the specialist register in UK medical students and doctors. BMC Medicine 11:242. https://doi.org/10.1186/1741-7015-11-242

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Path analysis (Figure 1) of the UCLMS cohort using multiple regression with backwards elimination at P<0.05. The article reports that "all paths from one variable to the next are significant and large", and that GCSE points predicted clinical marks and BMS marks predicted Part 1 over and above intervening variables; no standardized effect size is printed for the path set as a whole.

> "With the sole exception of the path from Part 2 to PACES (where n is relatively small), all paths from one variable to the next are significant and large."
