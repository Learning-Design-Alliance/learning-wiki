## Subclaims
`q3 i1` In sex-stratified analyses, video game use was associated with lower grade 3 reading (OR, 0.67; 95% CI, 0.47-0.95; P = .02) and math (OR, 0.69; 95% CI, 0.51-0.93; P = .02) among female students, with no evidence of these associations among male students. [→ Li X 2025](#li-x-2025)

## Evidence

### Li X 2025

Li X, Keown-Stoneman CD, Omand JA, Cost KT, Gallagher-Mackay K, Hove J, Janus M, Korczak DJ, Pullenayegum EM, Tsujimoto KC, Vanderloo LM, Maguire JL, Birken CS. (2025). Screen Time and Standardized Academic Achievement Tests in Elementary School. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.37092

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Exploratory sex-stratified proportional odds analyses (Table 4) in 1608 female and 1714 male grade 3 children. Female students: printed effect sizes "OR, 0.67" (reading) and "OR, 0.69" (math); male students: no evidence of association (grade 3 reading OR 0.83, grade 3 math OR 0.97).

> "Video game time (any vs none) was associated with lower achievement levels in grade 3 reading (OR, 0.67; 95% CI, 0.47-0.95; P = .02) and math (OR, 0.69; 95% CI, 0.51-0.93; P = .02) among female students, but there was no evidence of these associations among male students (grade 3 reading: OR, 0.83; 95% CI, 0.64-1.08; P = .16; grade 3 math: OR, 0.97; 95% CI, 0.78-1.22; P = .80)"
