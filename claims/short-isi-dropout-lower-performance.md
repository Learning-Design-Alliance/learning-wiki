# A short-ISI dropout schedule (5-drop) used significantly fewer trials but yielded lower final test performance than conventional schedules in Experiment 1

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The 5-drop schedule was significantly less effective at final test than the 5-5-5 and 1-5-9 conventional schedules despite fewer trials. [→ Pyc 2007](#pyc-2007)

## Evidence

### Pyc 2007

Pyc, M. A., & Rawson, K. A. (2007). Examining the efficiency of schedules of distributed retrieval practice. Memory & Cognition, 35(8), 1917-1927. https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03193420

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1, 161 undergraduates, five schedule groups. The omnibus schedule main effect was not significant, F(4,156) = 1.76, p = .14; the interaction approached significance, F(4,156) = 2.33, p = .058, and follow-up t tests showed the 5-drop group lower (t(62) = 2.19 and t(61) = 2.10).

> "Follow-up tests showed that performance in the 5-drop group was lower than that in the 5-5-5 and 1-5-9 groups"
