Evidence¶

Poudyal 2026¶

Poudyal, B. (2026). Who Acts, Who Knows, Who Answers? A Corpus-Assisted Discourse Analysis of Agency, Epistemic Responsibility, and Accountability in Generative AI Higher Education Research. https://orcid.org/0000-0002-7210-5480

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Frequency analysis of the 366-document title/abstract corpus (91,405 tokens) using five actor dictionaries. The study reports that "AI expressions were the most frequent actor labels (2,050)" and that AI received the largest number of strict actor–predicate associations (447). No effect size is printed.

"AI expressions were the most frequent actor labels (2,050), followed by students (1,385), institutions (545), educators (389), and providers/developers (25). AI also received the largest number of strict actor–predicate associations (447)"

Poudyal 2026 (2)¶

Poudyal, B. (2026). Who Acts, Who Knows, Who Answers? A Corpus-Assisted Discourse Analysis of Agency, Epistemic Responsibility, and Accountability in Generative AI Higher Education Research. https://orcid.org/0000-0002-7210-5480

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Actor–predicate association analysis of the same corpus shows providers/developers were the least visible actor group. The authors interpret that actions shaping "data, model behavior, and platform accessibility" read as characteristics of AI rather than organizations' actions. Descriptive counts only; no effect size.

"Commercial developers/vendors appeared only 25 times and entered six strict predicate associations. As a result, the organizational actions that shape data, model behavior, and platform accessibility appear far less often to be organizations’ own actions than to be characteristics of “AI”."
