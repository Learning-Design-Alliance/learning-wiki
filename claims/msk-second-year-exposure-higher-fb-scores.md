## Evidence

### Khorsand Derek D 2018

Khorsand Derek D, Khwaja A, Schmale GA. (2018). Early musculoskeletal classroom education confers little advantage to medical student knowledge and competency in the absence of clinical experiences: a retrospective comparison study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1157-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Retrospective comparison using the online 24-question Freedman and Bernstein short-answer survey; the 2013 cohort taught under the new first-year curriculum scored 54% correct versus 61% for the earlier second-year cohorts, "p < 0.001". Response rate was 11% (112/986).

> "there was a statistically significant higher overall performance for students first exposed to musculoskeletal medicine during the second year of medical school compared to the those students first exposed to musculoskeletal medicine during the first year of medical school (61% correct combined overall average scores for students exposed to musculoskeletal medicine during their second years vs. 54% correct for students exposed to musculoskeletal medicine during their first years, p < 0.001)"
