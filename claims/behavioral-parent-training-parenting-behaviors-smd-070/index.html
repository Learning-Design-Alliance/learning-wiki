Related Claims¶

  • Psychosocial interventions for ADHD show a small positive overall weighted average effect (SMD = 0.26) in between-group studies, with substantial heterogeneity — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Behavioral classroom management improves teacher-rated ADHD symptoms (SMD = 0.66) and teacher-rated impairment (SMD = 0.72) in children with ADHD — related
  • Whether studies randomly allocated participants was not significantly related to effect size magnitude in the ADHD psychosocial treatment meta-analysis — related
  • Effect sizes across ADHD between-group intervention studies range from −0.76 to 1.12 with a median of 0.18 and a slightly positive skewed distribution — related
  • In a small randomized pilot study, children with ADHD showed better sociometric peer functioning when the MOSAIC classroom inclusion components were added to behavioral management and social skills training — related
  • In a pilot randomized trial, children whose parents received Parental Friendship Coaching were reported by parents and by blind teachers to show better social behaviors and greater peer acceptance than control children — related
  • Study year of publication was not significantly related to effect size magnitude in the ADHD psychosocial treatment meta-analysis — related
  • Existing child-focused treatments for ADHD, including the MTA's intensive behavioral management and medication, and clinic-based social skills training, have not improved peers' liking of children with ADHD — related
  • By parent report, 62% of intervention children versus 21% of controls responded or improved on ADHD symptom criteria — related
