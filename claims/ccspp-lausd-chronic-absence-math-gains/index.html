Evidence¶

Klevan 2026¶

Klevan, S., Spitzer, N., Hernández, L., Rubinstein, C., & Swain, W. (2026). Community schools in Los Angeles Unified: Transforming teaching and learning. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/677.130

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Quantitative comparison analysis of CCSPP Cohort 1 community schools against matched non-CCSPP LAUSD high-need schools, reported in the report's findings on grant impacts. The comparison schools served nearly identical low-income concentrations (92%), so the report attributes the differential gains to the community schools program.

"Compared to similar high-need schools that did not receive CCSPP grants, participating schools reduced chronic absence by approximately 9% more and improved math proficiency rates by approximately 10% more following grant implementation."

Klevan 2026 (2)¶

Klevan, S., Spitzer, N., Hernández, L., Rubinstein, C., & Swain, W. (2026). Community schools in Los Angeles Unified: Transforming teaching and learning. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/677.130

q2 · i? · causal · r2

The same CCSPP impact analysis found ELA proficiency improvements in CCSPP schools that were not statistically significant; the report prints no effect size for this outcome, so no magnitude is asserted.

"ELA proficiency also improved in CCSPP schools, though these gains were not statistically significant."
