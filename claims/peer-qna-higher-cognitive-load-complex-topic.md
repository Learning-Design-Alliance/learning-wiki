# LLM-driven peer question-asking significantly increases cognitive load in a complex VR lecture topic, on both NASA-TLX and pupil diameter

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the Double-Slit Experiment topic, NASA-TLX cognitive load was significantly higher in the Peer-QnA condition than in Peer-NoQnA. [→ Süleyman Özdel 2025](#suleyman-ozdel-2025)
`q2 i?` Mean pupil diameter was also significantly higher in Peer-QnA for the same topic. [→ Süleyman Özdel 2025 (2)](#suleyman-ozdel-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Süleyman Özdel 2025

Süleyman Özdel, Can Sarpkaya, Efe Bozkir, Hong Gao, Enkelejda Kasneci. (2025). Examining the Role of LLM-Driven Interactions on Attention and Cognitive Engagement in Virtual Classrooms. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2505.07377](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.07377)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Within-subjects VR experiment (19 participants) comparing Peer-QnA and Peer-NoQnA in the Double-Slit Experiment topic; NASA-TLX after each condition showed "significantly higher cognitive load scores in the Peer-QnA condition" with the printed means and p = .026.

> "We observed significantly higher cognitive load scores in the Peer-QnA condition (M = 60.50, SD = 18 .16) compared to the Peer-NoQnA ( M = 41 .73, SD = 15 .36)."

### Süleyman Özdel 2025 (2)

Süleyman Özdel, Can Sarpkaya, Efe Bozkir, Hong Gao, Enkelejda Kasneci. (2025). Examining the Role of LLM-Driven Interactions on Attention and Cognitive Engagement in Virtual Classrooms. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2505.07377](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.07377)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same within-subjects experiment; baseline-corrected pupil diameter served as an objective cognitive-load indicator and was "significantly higher mean pupil diameters in the Peer-QnA condition" for the Double-Slit topic.

> "This finding is further supported by pupil diameters, with significantly higher mean pupil diameters in the Peer-QnA condition ( M = .59, SD= .11) compared to the Peer-NoQnA condition ( M = .51, SD= .19), indicating a significant difference (p < .001)"
