# Examination of the trigone and ureteric orifices was the most difficult flexible cystoscopy step, with competence achieved only after 279 procedures and failure rates falling from 34% to 5%

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The trigone and ureteric orifice examination step showed repeated unacceptable control-line crossings and required 279 procedures to reach competence, longer than any other step. [→ MacKenzie Kenneth R. 2016](#mackenzie-kenneth-r-2016)
`q2 i?` Failure rate for this step fell in thirds: 34% in the first 128 attempts, 9% between the 128th and 254th, and 5% between the 254th and 383rd. [→ MacKenzie Kenneth R. 2016 (2)](#mackenzie-kenneth-r-2016-2)

## Evidence

### MacKenzie Kenneth R. 2016

MacKenzie Kenneth R., Aning Jonathan. (2016). Defining competency in flexible cystoscopy: a novel approach using cumulative Sum analysis. BMC Urology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12894-016-0143-9

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

CUSUM analysis of the trigone/ureteric orifice step (n = 383 procedures; acceptable failure rate 0.05, unacceptable 0.15). The curve shows "a further 15 unacceptable control lines continually crossed"; competence was achieved following 279 procedures, the latest of the five steps.

> "Figure 3 shows the unacceptable control line (horizontal line crossed in an upwards fashion) is first crossed following the 3rd attempt with a further 15 unacceptable control lines continually crossed."

### MacKenzie Kenneth R. 2016 (2)

MacKenzie Kenneth R., Aning Jonathan. (2016). Defining competency in flexible cystoscopy: a novel approach using cumulative Sum analysis. BMC Urology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12894-016-0143-9

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Descriptive tertile analysis of the same trigone/ureteric orifice CUSUM data, calculated because of the large number of control lines crossed. Failure rates are descriptive: "The initial failure rate for the first 128 attempts was 34 %", improving to 9% and then 5%.

> "The initial failure rate for the first 128 attempts was 34 %. Between the 128th and 254th procedures the failure rate improved to 9 % then continued to improve further with a failure rate of 5 % between the 254th and 383rd procedure."
