## Evidence

### Gerry Stahl & Friedrich Hesse 2011

Gerry Stahl & Friedrich Hesse. (2011). CSCL in Asia. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning 6:1–7. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-011-9112-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

A qualitative study by Pathak, Kim, Jacobson, and Zhang at the Singapore Learning Sciences Lab set up productive failure by giving some groups well-structured and others ill-structured physics problems, analyzing interactions qualitatively.

> "Since the ill-structured problems were more challenging, the groups with them had lower rates of solution, but gained experiences that helped them with later problems."

### Gerry Stahl & Friedrich Hesse 2011 (2)

Gerry Stahl & Friedrich Hesse. (2011). CSCL in Asia. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning 6:1–7. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-011-9112-1

`q2 · i?` · `review · r1`

The editorial defines productive failure as one of the most interesting findings from CSCL research, citing prior quantitative studies (Barron 2003; Kapur and Kinzer 2009; Schwartz 1995) that demonstrated the phenomenon.

> "Productive failure is the finding that student groups who score lower on individual post-tests may have actually experienced deeper learning (such as developing more abstract conceptualizations) that could eventually lead to superior performance in the future."
