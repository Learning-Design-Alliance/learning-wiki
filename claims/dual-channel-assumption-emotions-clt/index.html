Related Claims¶

  • Emotions may act as extraneous cognitive load by competing for limited working memory resources with task-irrelevant processing — related
  • Emotion may affect intrinsic cognitive load, for example when emotion regulation is part of the learning outcomes — related
  • Cognitive Load Theory lacks a motivational focus, which becomes problematic when interpreting interventions that foster effortful learning strategies — related
  • Emotion directly affects encoding, storage, and retrieval of information, including before awareness of the material, via broadening or narrowing cognitive resources and mood-dependent and mood-congruent processing — related
