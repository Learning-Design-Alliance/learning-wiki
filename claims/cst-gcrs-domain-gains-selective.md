## Evidence

### McCarrick CA 2025

McCarrick CA, Moynihan A, McEntee PD, Boland PA, Donnelly S, Heneghan H, Cahill RA. (2025). Impact of simulation training on communication skills and informed consent practices in medical students- a randomised controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07671-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Blinded GCRS scoring of the observed simulated-patient consent OSCE in the randomised trial; the significant domains were "a central focus of the communication skills training sessions". Paired t-tests compared domain scores between the 61 students per arm.

> "the intervention group performed significantly better than the control group in several domains: Verbal communication (8.30 vs. 6.70 out of 12), Initiation of the session (7.47 vs. 5.69 out of 8), Providing structure (3.36 vs. 2.58), Providing the correct amount of information for the patient (4.06 vs. 3.36 out of 6) and Aiding accurate understanding and recall (4.55 vs. 3.58 out of 6)"

### McCarrick CA 2025 (2)

McCarrick CA, Moynihan A, McEntee PD, Boland PA, Donnelly S, Heneghan H, Cahill RA. (2025). Impact of simulation training on communication skills and informed consent practices in medical students- a randomised controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07671-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same GCRS analysis of the trial's second assessment, Table 1 shows building the relationship (p = 0.55) and incorporating the patient's perspective (p = 0.37) did not differ significantly between groups at post-test.

> "Notably, there was no domain in which the intervention group scored lower than the control group."
