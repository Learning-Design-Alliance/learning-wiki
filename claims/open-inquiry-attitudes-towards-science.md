## Evidence

### Muhamad Dah 2024

Muhamad Dah, N., Mat Noor, M. S. A., Kamarudin, M. Z., & Syed Abdul Azziz, S. S. (2024). The impacts of open inquiry on students' learning in science: A systematic literature review. Educational Research Review, 43. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2024.100601

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Sadeh and Zion (2012), that "open inquiry students were more satisfied" and collaborated at a more advanced level; the review states the studies demonstrated statistically significant differences in attitudes.

> "According to Sadeh and Zion (2012), open inquiry students were more satisfied and thought they had obtained advantages from conducting the project. Furthermore, students take more initiative and collaborate with their project partners"

### Muhamad Dah 2024 (2)

Muhamad Dah, N., Mat Noor, M. S. A., Kamarudin, M. Z., & Syed Abdul Azziz, S. S. (2024). The impacts of open inquiry on students' learning in science: A systematic literature review. Educational Research Review, 43. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2024.100601

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Buck et al. (2014) and Moebius-Clune et al. (2011), that hands-on authentic tasks boosted self-confidence and ownership, and that open inquiry "has slowly improved the experiences of students with low self-esteem".

> "According to Buck et al. (2014), hands-on activities can boost students' self-confidence in science. These findings are also confirmed by Moebius-Clune et al. (2011), who highlighted that when students were given authentic tasks, they took ownership of their learning"
