## Evidence

### Omar Alsaiari 2025

Omar Alsaiari, Nilufar Baghaei, Hatim Lahza, Jason M. Lodge, Marie Boden, Hassan Khosravi. (2025). Emotionally Enriched Feedback via Generative AI. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1016/j.compedu.2025.105363](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2025.105363)

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

41-item adapted AEQ-S survey of 101 survey respondents in the RCT (49 control, 54 experimental minus 2 removed), analysed with Mann-Whitney U: anger medians 2.61 (control) vs 1.89 (experimental), "a notable reduction (p = 0.013∗, effect size |r| = 0.246)".

> "the experimental group exhibited significantly lower levels of anger, with a median of 1.89 compared to 2.61 in the control group, demonstrating a notable reduction (p = 0.013∗, effect size |r| = 0.246)."

### Omar Alsaiari 2025 (2)

Omar Alsaiari, Nilufar Baghaei, Hatim Lahza, Jason M. Lodge, Marie Boden, Hassan Khosravi. (2025). Emotionally Enriched Feedback via Generative AI. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1016/j.compedu.2025.105363](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2025.105363)

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Analysis across the four AEQ-S dimensions of anger in the same survey found the motivational-dimension item ("I get annoyed about having to address AI suggestions for improvements on my task") showed the notable result, significant at p <0.005 with |r| = 0.279.

> "students in the control group exhibited significantly higher levels of annoyance—an indicator of demotivation—when prompted to address AI-generated suggestions for improvements on their tasks. This difference was not only statistically significant (p <0.005), but also exhibited a moderate effect size (|r| = 0.279)."
