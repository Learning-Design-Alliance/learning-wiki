# The application's usability in two RCTs was similar to practicing the ABCDE approach with physical equipment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Two randomised controlled trials, one single- and one multiplayer, showed usability of the VR application similar to practicing with physical equipment, and the effect of VR was non-inferior to physical equipment with 578 first-year students. [→ Berg H 2023](#berg-h-2023)

## Evidence

### Berg H 2023

Berg H, Prasolova-Førland E, Steinsbekk A. (2023). Developing a virtual reality (VR) application for practicing the ABCDE approach for systematic clinical observation. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04625-2

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Two RCTs cited as the effect evaluation of the application: 578 first-year medical and nurse students participated, and the effect of the VR application was found non-inferior to practicing with physical equipment. This paper reports the development, not the trials' detailed results.

> "The usability of this application was tested in two randomized controlled trials (RCT), one single- and one multiplayer, both showing similar usability as practicing the ABCDE approach with physical equipment [19, 28]."
