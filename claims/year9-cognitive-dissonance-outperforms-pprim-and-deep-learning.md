## Evidence

### Athanasopoulos 2023

Athanasopoulos, P. (2023). Cognitive dissonance or p-prims? Towards identifying the best way to overcome misconceptions in physics. Prestatyn High School, UK. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2308.15601](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2308.15601)

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Pre-test/post-test comparison of two year 9 top-set classes (9p1 p-prim, 9p2 cognitive dissonance). Table 3 prints whole-test effect sizes of 0.73 (95% CI 0.11-1.34) for p-prim and 1.00 (95% CI 0.36-1.65) for cognitive dissonance; gains were 14% versus 15%, with the smaller standard deviation in 9p2 suggesting a real effect rather than fluctuation.

> "Once again, inducing cognitive dissonance appears to be superior and has a medium to high effect in the learning of the students."

### Athanasopoulos 2023 (2)

Athanasopoulos, P. (2023). Cognitive dissonance or p-prims? Towards identifying the best way to overcome misconceptions in physics. Prestatyn High School, UK. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2308.15601](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2308.15601)

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r1`

High-discriminator analysis for year 9: the cognitive dissonance group gained 10% (effect size 0.39, 95% CI -0.21, 1.00) while the p-prim group gained 0% (effect size 0.00, 95% CI -0.57, 0.57). The confidence interval for the cognitive dissonance group includes zero.

> "For the 9p2 group, cognitive dissonance also seems to have resulted to some deep learning, as demonstrated by the gain in the high discriminators, which is an interesting result by itself because this was not the case for any other group."
