## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a cluster-randomized controlled trial among Dutch physical therapists, the peer assessment groups increased guideline-consistent clinical reasoning by 8.4% while case discussion control groups declined 0.1%, an estimated group difference of 8.7% (P < 0.001). ## Evidence

### Maas MJM 2015

Maas MJM, van Dulmen SA, Sagasser MH, Heerkens YF, van der Vleuten CPM, Nijhuis-van der Sanden MWG, van der Wees PJ. (2015). Critical features of peer assessment of clinical performance to enhance adherence to a low back pain guideline for physical therapists: a mixed methods design. BMC Medical Education 15:203. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0484-1

`q3 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Cluster-randomized trial (10 communities of practice, PA n = 49 vs case discussion n = 41); vignettes completed by 78 participants. The article reports "an estimated group difference 8.7 %" favoring peer assessment with 95% CI 3.9 to 13.4; no standardized effect size is printed, so impact is left uncoded.

> "Multilevel analysis showed an increase in guideline-consistent clinical reasoning of 8.4 % in the peer-assessment groups whereas the control groups showed a decline of 0.1 % (estimated group difference 8.7 %; 95 % CI: 3.9 to 13.4; P < 0.001)."
