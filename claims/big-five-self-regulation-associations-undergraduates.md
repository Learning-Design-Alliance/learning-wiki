## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Conscientiousness shows the strongest association with self-regulation (r = 0.600 with perseverance, r = 0.587 with goals), and neuroticism shows significant negative associations, in a sample of 642 undergraduates. [→ de la Fuente Jesús 2020](#de-la-fuente-jesus-2020)

## Evidence

### de la Fuente Jesús 2020

de la Fuente Jesús, Paoloni Paola, Kauffman Douglas, Yilmaz Soylu Meryem, Sander Paul, Zapata Lucía. (2020). Big Five, Self-Regulation, and Coping Strategies as Predictors of Achievement Emotions in Undergraduate Students. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17103602

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Pearson bivariate correlation analysis in a sample of 642 Spanish undergraduates relating Big Five factors to self-regulation. The article reports conscientiousness "had the most significant association with self-regulation (SR)", with r = 0.600 (p < 0.001) for perseverance; extraversion showed r = 0.637 with SR total (Table 1).

> "the personality factor conscientiousness (C) had the most significant association with self-regulation (SR), both globally and in its constituent factors (especially in strength of association with perseverance (r = 0.600; p < 0.001)"
