## Evidence

### Stoet G 2013

Stoet G, Geary DC (2013) Sex Differences in Mathematics and Reading Achievement Are Inversely Related: Within- and Across-Nation Assessment of 10 Years of PISA Data. PLoS ONE 8(3): e57988. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0057988

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive secondary analysis (Figure S1) of public enrollment data from the British HEIDI database and Dutch Statistics Netherlands. The article reports the relative proportion of female to male mathematics students "stayed relatively constant"; in 2011 the first-year male-to-female ratio was 2.23:1 in the Netherlands and 1.63:1 in the UK.

> "Our own analysis (Figure S1) of the fraction of women that enrolled in a mathematics degree program at universities in the United Kingdom and The Netherlands (both countries have these numbers publicly available) shows that the relation between the fraction of female mathematics students as a proportion of all female students and the fraction of male mathematics students as a proportion of all male students has remained constant over the last 20 years in the Netherlands and the last 10 years in the United Kingdom."
