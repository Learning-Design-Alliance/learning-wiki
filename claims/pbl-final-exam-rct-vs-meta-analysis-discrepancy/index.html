Evidence¶

Chen Tu 2024¶

Chen Tu, Zhao Yi-Jing, Huang Feng-Qing, Liu Qun, Li Ying, Alolga Raphael N., Zhang Lei, Ma Gaoxiang. (2024). The effect of problem-based learning on improving problem-solving, self-directed learning, and critical thinking ability for the pharmacy students: A randomized controlled trial and meta-analysis. PLOS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0314017

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Randomized controlled trial of 57 pharmacy students (PBL n = 28, LBL n = 29) with a theoretical examination scored on a percentage basis; the article reports the printed means and a significant difference with no effect size.

"Furthermore, the PBL group obtained a significantly better final exam grade (79.86 ± 1.38) compared to the LBL group (68.1 ± 1.76)."

Chen Tu 2024 (2)¶

Chen Tu, Zhao Yi-Jing, Huang Feng-Qing, Liu Qun, Li Ying, Alolga Raphael N., Zhang Lei, Ma Gaoxiang. (2024). The effect of problem-based learning on improving problem-solving, self-directed learning, and critical thinking ability for the pharmacy students: A randomized controlled trial and meta-analysis. PLOS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0314017

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Meta-analysis of 8 randomized controlled trials involving 1819 students, analyzed with the metafor package in R. The article reports no significant final-exam difference with SMD = 0.23 and 95% CI = -0.08–0.53; equivalence was not tested.

"However, there was no significant difference in the final exam score between the PBL group and the LBL control group (SMD = 0.23, 95% CI = -0.08–0.53; Fig 2D)."
