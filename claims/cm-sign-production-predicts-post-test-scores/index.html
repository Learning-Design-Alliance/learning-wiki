Evidence¶

Rachel Sortino 2026¶

Rachel Sortino, Christina Kim, Thalia Guettler, Katie McClyman, Lorna Quandt, Alicia Wooten & Rachel Pizzie. (2026). Producing concept-motivated signs supports learning of STEM content in American Sign Language. npj Science of Learning 11:37. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00418-6

q3 · i? · causal · r?

LMM of sign counts in video-recorded signed summaries against test scores in the within-participant study; "More CM use was related to higher scores on both tests combined", with the printed coefficient and test statistic as shown.

"However, for CM lessons, there was a significant relationship between CM use and scores on both the pre- and post-test combined (X2(1) = 5.82, p = 0.016). More CM use was related to higher scores on both tests combined (β = 0.118,t(112.98) = 2.54)."

Rachel Sortino 2026 (2)¶

Rachel Sortino, Christina Kim, Thalia Guettler, Katie McClyman, Lorna Quandt, Alicia Wooten & Rachel Pizzie. (2026). Producing concept-motivated signs supports learning of STEM content in American Sign Language. npj Science of Learning 11:37. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00418-6

q3 · i? · causal · r?

Parallel LMM for EM lessons found no relationship between EM sign use and scores at either time point; separately, only post-scores were significantly associated with CM use (X2(1) = 7.24, p = 0.007).

"For the EM lessons, there was no significant relationship between EM use and scores (X2(1) = 0.05,p = 0.830), nor was there an interaction effect between EM use and time ( X2(1) = 1.32, p = 0.251)."

Rachel Sortino, Christina Kim, Thalia Guettler, Katie McClyman, Lorna Quandt, Alicia Wooten & Rachel Pizzie. (2026). Producing concept-motivated signs supports learning of STEM content in American Sign Language. npj Science of Learning 11:37. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00418-6
