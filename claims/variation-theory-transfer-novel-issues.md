# Variation-theory-based teaching significantly improves students' alternation of points of view on novel, untaught societal issues (transfer), with smaller effects than on the taught task

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Treatment students significantly outperformed control students on two novel post-test essay tasks (electronic ankle bracelet, life choices), with ηp2 = 0.08 and 0.07 — smaller than the taught-task effect. [→ Kristoffer Larsson · Klas Andersson 2025](#kristoffer-larsson-klas-andersson-2025)

## Evidence

### Kristoffer Larsson · Klas Andersson 2025

Kristoffer Larsson · Klas Andersson. (2025). Testing the use of variation theory in teaching critical thinking—a field experimental study. Instructional Science (2025) 53:1025–1046. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09721-y

`q3 · i2`

One-way independent ANOVAs on two post-test-only essay tasks not used in either intervention group, testing transfer to novel content. Effects were significant with ηp2 = 0.08 and 0.07; the article notes "even though the effects were smaller" than on the taught voting-age task.

> "The results show significant effects of the variation theory teaching intervention on students' ability to alternate between points of view on these tasks also, F (1, 90) = 7.38, MSE = 0.55, p < 0.05, ηp2 = 0.08; F (1, 90) = 7.28, MSE = 0.55, p < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.07."
