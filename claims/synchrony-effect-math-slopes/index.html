Evidence¶

Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante 2023¶

Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante, Andrea P. Goldin, Mariano Sigman and María Juliana Leone. (2023). A better alignment between chronotype and school timing is associated with lower grade retention in adolescents. npj Science of Learning 8:21. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00171-0

q3 · i0 · causal · r2

Slope comparisons in 5th-year math: the decrease per 1 h later chronotype is 0.535 points in the morning versus 0.250 and 0.238 elsewhere; morning vs afternoon d = −0.178, a negligible effect size. The authors read this as a synchrony effect that partially counteracts the chronotype effect.

"while in the morning a 1 h later chronotype is associated with a decrease of 0.535 points on math grades, in the afternoon and the evening the decrease is 0.250 and 0.238 points, respectively (morning vs. afternoon: t = −2.421, d = −0.178, 95% CI = −0.323 to −0.034"

Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante 2023 (2)¶

Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante, Andrea P. Goldin, Mariano Sigman and María Juliana Leone. (2023). A better alignment between chronotype and school timing is associated with lower grade retention in adolescents. npj Science of Learning 8:21. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00171-0

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

In 1st year, chronotype slopes differ from zero only for morning math (β = −0.200) and evening language (β = −0.185). The article states: "In the afternoon, later chronotypes were not associated with lower academic performance".

"At their 1st year, students with later chronotypes obtain lower performance than their earlier peers in math when attending school in the morning and in language when attending in the evening ( β = −0.200, 95% CI = −0.343 to −0.057, t = −2.746, P = 0.006"
