# In one small non-randomised waitlist-controlled study, MBSR reduced teachers' self-reported strain with large effects, with smaller and mixed effects on wellbeing and self-regulation competences

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` In a non-randomised waitlist-controlled study of 32 German teachers, the MBSR group reported significant reductions in cognitive and emotional strain on self-report scales, with large between-group effects at post-test (d = 1.51 and 1.34); effects on self-regulation competences were mixed (large for reduced resignation, medium for switching off from work, small for inner calm, near zero for proactive problem-solving), and small for general wellbeing (GHQ-12, d = 0.39). ## Evidence

### Rupprecht 2017

Rupprecht, S., Paulus, P. & Walach, H. (2017). Mind the teachers! The impact of mindfulness training on self-regulation and classroom performance in a sample of German school teachers. European Journal of Educational Research, 6(4), 565-581. https://doi.org/10.12973/eu-jer.6.4.565

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Pilot non-randomised waitlist-controlled study of 32 self-selected German school teachers (intervention n=18, control n=14; groups assigned by age and school type), with the quantitative outcomes below measured by teacher self-report questionnaires (the study also ran qualitative interviews). Post-test comparisons in Table 3 show Cohen's d of 1.51 for cognitive irritation and 1.34 for emotional irritation. The article reports "Between group effect sizes were large" for strain reductions in the intervention group versus waitlist controls. Self-regulation effects were mixed: d = 0.76 for ability to switch off from work, 1.09 for reduced resignation, 0.36 for inner calm and 0.03 for proactive problem-solving; general wellbeing (GHQ-12) d = 0.39.

> "At post -test, intervention group participants reported statistically significant reductions in cognitive and emotional strain. Control group teachers reported increases in these measures that missed statistical significance. Between group effect sizes were large."

### Rupprecht 2017 (2)

Rupprecht, S., Paulus, P. & Walach, H. (2017). Mind the teachers! The impact of mindfulness training on self-regulation and classroom performance in a sample of German school teachers. European Journal of Educational Research, 6(4), 565-581. https://doi.org/10.12973/eu-jer.6.4.565

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Same study and self-report measures as above. Follow-up comparisons in Table 4 show Cohen's d of 1.26 for cognitive irritation and 1.72 for emotional irritation. The article notes "most improvements in the experimental group were sustained or increased" at the 3-month follow-up before mid-term.

> "For example, teachers in the intervention group reported greater improvements in the ability to stop ruminating about work problems at hom e. At the same time, the control group reported being less able to switch off. Between group effect sizes were high."
