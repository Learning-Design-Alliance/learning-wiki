## Evidence

### Sayed Azher 2024

Sayed Azher, Aralia Mills, Jinzhi He, Taliah Hyjazie, Junko Tokuno, Andrea Quaiattini, and Jason M. Harley. (2024). Findings Favor Haptics Feedback in Virtual Simulation Surgical Education: An Updated Systematic and Scoping Review. Surgical Innovation. https://doi.org/10.1177/15533506241238263

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Vamadevan and colleagues, a randomized trial in which residents trained to proficiency in either a haptic or non-haptic simulator and were followed up 3-6 weeks later on the non-haptic simulator. The review reports "the non-haptic group reached proficiency faster during the follow-up test".

> "The haptic group required less instructor assistance and reached proficiency faster during the intervention, while the non-haptic group reached proficiency faster during the follow-up test. 81 Across both phases, the haptic group required significantly more instructor feedback during the follow-up than intervention while the non-haptic group required significantly less. 81 The authors concluded that acquired skills were not transferable to a conventional non-haptic setting."
