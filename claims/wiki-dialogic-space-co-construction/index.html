Evidence¶

Gerry Stahl & Friedrich Hesse 2011¶

Gerry Stahl & Friedrich Hesse. (2011). Let a hundred flowers bloom; let a hundred schools of thought contend. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning, 6, 139-145. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-011-9118-8

q2 · i? · causal · r?

The editorial's summary of the Pifarre and Kleine Staarman study, positioned in the small-group dialogicality vein and building on Mercer's and Wegerif's theoretic and analytic work. Students posted their own ideas first, used a "thinking together" approach based on "exploratory talk," and produced wiki content as a shared digital artifact.

"They investigate how a wiki environment can provide a “dialogic space” for group knowledge building"

Related Claims¶

  • Self-constructed collaborative concept maps trigger more deep exploratory talk and less non-dialogic talk than completing or sorting maps, which yield mostly superficial exploratory talk — related
  • Internalizing wiki information can yield emergent knowledge that was in neither the person's prior knowledge nor the wiki — related
  • Middle episodes of wiki text generation show a cumulative orientation, later with co-construction, in which students expand and re-elaborate each other's ideas — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Specific pedagogic design characteristics supported students' joint interaction in the wiki: initial text propositions, Thinking Together preparation, a collectively created artefact, and asynchrony — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Primary students' wiki negotiation contains collaboration and reasoning keywords distributed throughout the interaction — related
  • Wiki negotiation unfolds in four episodes with distinct intersubjective orientations, beginning with an exploratory orientation during content generation — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Collaborative wiki texts are longer, contain more t-units and more reasoning connectors than the initial pair-written texts — related
  • The final episode of wiki collaborative writing is characterised by a disputational orientation, possibly driven by time pressure to finish the text — related
