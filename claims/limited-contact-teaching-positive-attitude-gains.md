# Limited contact teaching produced moderate positive gains in expert-like thinking, concentrated in the same statements most harmed by remote learning

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Under limited contact teaching, most categories showed small to moderate increases in favorable answers, with the highest gains in conceptual connections and applied conceptual understanding. [→ Teemu Hynninen 2023](#teemu-hynninen-2023)
`q2 i?` Favorable answers rose significantly on statements 1, 5 and 40 (from 43%, 8% and 25% to 58%, 18% and 48%), the same statements most adversely affected in the no-contact cohort. [→ Teemu Hynninen 2023 (2)](#teemu-hynninen-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Teemu Hynninen 2023

Teemu Hynninen, Henna Pesonen, Olli Lintu, and Petriina Paturi. (2023). Ongoing eﬀects of pandemic-imposed learning disruption on student attitudes. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010101](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010101)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pre/post CLASS comparison for the 2021–2022 limited-contact cohort (N = 40); "the highest gains in expert-like thinking are seen in conceptual connections and applied conceptual understand-ing", though overall favorable gains were small with roughly equal increase in unfavorable answers.

> "In fact, the highest gains in expert-like thinking are seen in conceptual connections and applied conceptual understand-ing, where we saw the highest increase in non-expert-like thinking for the students with no contact teaching."

### Teemu Hynninen 2023 (2)

Teemu Hynninen, Henna Pesonen, Olli Lintu, and Petriina Paturi. (2023). Ongoing eﬀects of pandemic-imposed learning disruption on student attitudes. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010101](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010101)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same limited-contact cohort; favorable agreement on the three statements most harmed during remote learning rose significantly ("from 43 %, 8 % and 25 % to 58 %, 18 % and 48 %"), though the article prints no standardized effect size for this contrast.

> "These results can again be attributed to statement 1, 5 and 40, only now we see a signiﬁcant increase in favorable answers (from 43 %, 8 % and 25 % to 58 %, 18 % and 48 %, respectively)."
