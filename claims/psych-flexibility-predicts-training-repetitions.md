## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a regression on 34 novices' training repetitions, higher psychological flexibility and bimanual dexterity predicted fewer repetitions to proficiency, while higher perceived demands predicted more. [→ Beattie Kirsty L. 2022](#beattie-kirsty-l-2022)

## Evidence

### Beattie Kirsty L. 2022

Beattie Kirsty L., Hill Andrew, Horswill Mark S., Grove Philip M., Stevenson Andrew R. L. (2022). Aptitude and attitude: predictors of performance during and after basic laparoscopic skills training. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-021-08668-8

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical stepwise regression on training repetitions from 34 medical students in a proficiency-based box-trainer program. The model included viewing mode, perceived demands, psychological flexibility, and manual dexterity (bimanual), explaining "63.5% of the variance in total training repetitions". No standardised effect size is printed.

> "The final model was statistically significant, F(4, 29) = 15.33, p < 0.001, and explained 63.5% of the variance in total training repetitions, RAdj2=0.635 (large effect)."
