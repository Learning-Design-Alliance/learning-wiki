## Subclaims
`q2 i?` First-year medical students reported that HoloLens increased engagement and motivation, but engagement depended on smooth device function and physical tolerability, with eye strain, headaches, nausea, lagging and glitching reducing immersion. [→ Lin Joseph 2026](#lin-joseph-2026)

## Evidence

### Lin Joseph 2026

Lin Joseph, Ghazi Sarmad, Kuss Ethan, Shehata Sarah. (2026). Medical students’ experiences of mixed-reality-based anatomy instruction in a non-cadaveric curriculum: a qualitative case study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09881-6

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of semi-structured interviews with 12 volunteer first-year medical students (from an eligible cohort of 160) who had completed nine months of non-cadaveric anatomy instruction with HoloAnatomy on HoloLens 2. Students described engagement as "contingent on whether the device functioned smoothly and could be worn comfortably."

> "Sometimes if it wouldn’t work, it was kind of stressful seeing others’ HoloLens working while yours glitched or lagged, so you’re not getting the same smooth experience."
