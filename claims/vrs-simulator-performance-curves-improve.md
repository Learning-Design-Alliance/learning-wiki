# Repeated simulator attempts improve VRS performance, with expert groups showing no further improvement

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Multiple attempts on the simulator improved performance, reported as statistically significant by four of the included studies. [→ Rasmussen Rasmus Christian 2019](#rasmussen-rasmus-christian-2019)
`q2 i?` Expert groups showed no improvement across attempts in two studies, which the authors interpret as the simulator closely resembling real surgery. [→ Rasmussen Rasmus Christian 2019 (2)](#rasmussen-rasmus-christian-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Rasmussen Rasmus Christian 2019

Rasmussen Rasmus Christian, Grauslund Jakob, Vergmann Anna Stage. (2019). Simulation training in vitreoretinal surgery: a systematic review. BMC Ophthalmology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12886-019-1098-x

`q2 · i?`

Qualitative synthesis across the included studies; the review reports statistically significant improvement with repeated attempts in four studies, with no effect size printed.

> "Results were that multiple attempts did improve performance and was reported statistical significant by four of the included studies [22–25]."

### Rasmussen Rasmus Christian 2019 (2)

Rasmussen Rasmus Christian, Grauslund Jakob, Vergmann Anna Stage. (2019). Simulation training in vitreoretinal surgery: a systematic review. BMC Ophthalmology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12886-019-1098-x

`q2 · i?`

Authors' interpretation of two included studies' performance curves: surgeons' scores did not improve across sessions. The review also notes it could indicate the tested modules were too easy for the surgeons.

> "Solverson et al. [26] and Vergmann et al. [24] reported no improvement in the expert groups, which could be interpreted as the EyeSi® Surgical Simulator closely resembles real surgery."
