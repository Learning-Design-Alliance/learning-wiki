# Tier-2 intervention closed the target word learning gap between at-risk treatment students and not-at-risk reference students, but not the listening comprehension gap

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Treatment students outperformed the not-at-risk reference group on target word learning (g = 0.47), but scored lower on listening comprehension (g = –.34), with the comprehension gap depending on initial PPVT scores. [→ Coyne 2019](#coyne-2019)

## Evidence

### Coyne 2019

Coyne, M. D., McCoach, D. B., Ware, S., Austin, C. R., Loftus-Rattan, S. M., & Baker, D. L. (2019). Racing Against the Vocabulary Gap: Matthew Effects in Early Vocabulary Instruction and Intervention. Exceptional Children, 85(2), 163–179. https://doi.org/10.1177/0014402918789162

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive comparison of at-risk treatment students with the not-at-risk reference group (n = 741) on experimenter-developed measures. The article reports g = 0.47 favoring treatment on target words and g = –.69 for the lowest-PPVT treatment students on listening comprehension.

> "On average, treatment students had listening comprehension scores that were approximately one third of a standard deviation lower than the mean of the reference students (g = –.34). However, at-risk treatment students with initial PPVT scores 5 points above the mean scored comparably to the reference students (g = .05)."
