## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a binomial logistic regression, coded feedback quality was the only significant predictor of uptake (b = 1.84, SE = 0.56, z = 3.30, p = .001); actual source, recognition accuracy, their interaction, and aggregated perceptions were not significant (all ps ≥ .090). [→ Jacobsen 2025](#jacobsen-2025)

## Evidence

### Jacobsen 2025

Jacobsen, L. J., Mertens, U., Jansen, T., & Weber, K. E. (2025). AI, Expert or Peer? Provider Biases and Feedback Uptake among Pre-Service Teachers. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2507.16013](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2507.16013)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Binomial logistic regression of the correction rate (implemented suggestions relative to suggestions identified), based on 160 participants after exclusions; the model accounted for a small share of variation (Nagelkerke R² = .11).

> "The results showed that coded feedback quality was the only significant predictor of uptake (b = 1.84, SE = 0.56, z = 3.30, p = .001), with higher-quality feedback being more likely to be implemented."

### Jacobsen 2025 (2)

Jacobsen, L. J., Mertens, U., Jansen, T., & Weber, K. E. (2025). AI, Expert or Peer? Provider Biases and Feedback Uptake among Pre-Service Teachers. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2507.16013](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2507.16013)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive uptake analysis by actual source (Table 3): LLM 52%, expert 45%, peer 42% relative uptake; the article notes peer feedback typically flagged only one issue, limiting its uptake potential.

> "Feedback generated by the LLM identified the most mistakes and showed the highest absolute and relative uptake in participants, followed by expert feedback and, lastly, peer feedback."
