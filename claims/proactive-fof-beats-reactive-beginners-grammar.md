# Beginner EFL learners taught with proactive FOF scored significantly higher on a grammar posttest than learners taught with reactive FOF

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Beginner learners receiving proactive FOF outperformed those receiving reactive FOF on the grammar achievement posttest (t(40) = 2.38, p = .022). [→ Naeimeh Bakshiri 2011](#naeimeh-bakshiri-2011)

## Evidence

### Naeimeh Bakshiri 2011

Naeimeh Bakshiri, Arshya Keyvanfar. (2011). Time Matters: Proactive vs. Reactive FOF. i-manager's Journal of English Language Teaching, Vol. 1 No. 2, April - June 2011. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1071093

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental pretest-posttest nonequivalent-groups study of 42 beginner Iranian EFL learners (22 proactive, 20 reactive), 22 sessions with 20 minutes of grammar instruction. The independent-samples t-test showed a significant posttest difference, with the proactive group mean of "M= 23.45, SD = 2.89" exceeding the reactive group's 21.40.

> "The t-test conducted between the beginner proactive (M= 23.45, SD = 2.89) and reactive groups (M= 21.40, SD = 2.68) resulted in t(40) = 2.38. p =.022 (two-tailed), indicating that there was a significant difference between the means of the two groups at the end of the treatment."
