# Graphs drew visual attention away from text: proportion of dwell time on input and problem statement was significantly shorter when a graph was present

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students looked proportionally less at the input and problem statement when a graph was present, while spending about 19% of total dwell time on the graph. [→ Ögren 2017](#ogren-2017)

## Evidence

### Ögren 2017

Ögren, M., Nyström, M., & Jarodzka, H. (2017). There's more to the multimedia effect than meets the eye: is seeing pictures believing? Instructional Science, 45, 263–287. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-016-9397-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Eye-tracking AOI analysis across the 36 participants; students spent 19.0 ± 10.2% of total time viewing the graph, taken about equally from input and statement areas. The quotient between input and statement did not differ significantly (p > 0.05).

> "The proportion of total dwell time on both the input and problem statement was signiﬁcantly shorter when the graph was present, according to a two sample t test (p<0:001)"
