# Think-aloud interviews indicate QM experts consider whether operators are diagonal in a representation before choosing a basis, while students generally do not perform this metacognitive check

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` QM experts are more likely than students to consider whether different operators are diagonal in a given representation before choosing a basis for evaluating matrix elements. [→ Paul Justice 2020](#paul-justice-2020)

## Evidence

### Paul Justice 2020

Paul Justice, Emily Marshman, and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). Development and validation of a sequence of clicker questions for helping students learn addition of angular momentum in quantum mechanics. Physics Education Research Conference proceedings. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2006.07145](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2006.07145)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Think-aloud interviews with QM experts, who generally preferred the coupled representation because both Hamiltonians are diagonal in it. Interviews with students suggest they did not, in general, automatically perform this metacognition before selecting a basis.

> "based on think-aloud interviews, we find that QM experts are more likely to consider whether different operators are diagonal in a given representation before choosing a basis to evaluate the matrix elements of the two Hamiltonians."
