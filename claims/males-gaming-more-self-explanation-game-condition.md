# Male students game the system significantly more than female students in the self-explanation step of a digital learning game, across three studies

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the game condition, male students gamed the system significantly more than female students within the self-explanation steps (Study 1: F(1,68)=4.83, p=0.031; Study 2: F(1,156)=4.82, p=0.030). [→ Baker 2025](#baker-2025)

## Evidence

### Baker 2025

Baker, R. S., Richey, J. E., Zhang, J., Karumbaiah, S., Andres-Bray, J. M., Nguyen, H. A., Andres, J. M. A. L., & McLaren, B. M. (2025). Gaming the system mediates the relationship between gender and learning outcomes in a digital learning game. Instructional Science, 53, 203–238. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09679-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

ANOVA on Study 1 data (153 students after exclusions; 70 game, 83 non-game) comparing gaming rates by gender in the game condition. Male students gamed significantly more in the self-explanation step, "indicating that male students demonstrated more disengagement than female students in the game."

> "However, in the game condition, male students gamed the system significantly more than female students within the self-explanation steps (F(1,68) = 4.83, p = 0.031), indicating that male students demonstrated more disengagement than female students in the game."
