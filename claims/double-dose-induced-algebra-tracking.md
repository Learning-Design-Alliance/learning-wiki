# The double-dose policy led schools to track algebra classes by entering math skills, making classrooms more homogeneous in ability

## Evidence

### Takako Nomi and Elaine M. Allensworth 2008

Takako Nomi and Elaine M. Allensworth. (2008). “Double-dose” Algebra as an Alternative Strategy to Remediation: Effects on Students’ Academic Outcomes. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. http://www.informaworld.com/smpp/content~db=all?content=10.1080/19345740802676739

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive analysis of CPS course transcript data showing how the three structural guidelines (same teacher, sequential courses, same peers) induced sorting of above- and below-norm students into separate algebra courses. No effect size printed.

> "After the policy was implemented, math classes became more homogenous in terms of students‘ incoming math abilities. This decline in classroom heterogeneity occurred among both low- and high-ability students, but was largest among students with incoming test scores just below the national median."
