## Evidence

### Manher Jariwala 2018

Manher Jariwala, Jayson Nissen, Xochith Herrera, Eleanor W. Close, and Ben Van Dusen. (2018). Participation rates of in-class vs. online administration of low-stakes research-based assessments. PERC Proceedings. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/perc.2017.pr.044](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.044)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Hierarchical generalized linear model (logistic regression) of participation for the four measures, with instructor practices (0 to 4) as a section-level predictor. The article reports instructor practices explained 45% of the variance; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "The results of our HGLM model of the student data, shown in Table II, indicate that the more recommended practices instructors used, the higher the participation rates were for their CBTs."

### Manher Jariwala 2018 (2)

Manher Jariwala, Jayson Nissen, Xochith Herrera, Eleanor W. Close, and Ben Van Dusen. (2018). Participation rates of in-class vs. online administration of low-stakes research-based assessments. PERC Proceedings. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/perc.2017.pr.044](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.044)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Predicted participation rates from the HGLM (Figure 3), converted from logits to probabilities. The article states rates "of the CBT and PPT posttest were very similar" when all four practices were used; this is a model-predicted, not directly observed, comparison.

> "The number of recommended practices used by instructors dramatically increased predicted participation rates such that when instructors implemented all four recommended practices the participation rates of the CBT and PPT posttest were very similar."
