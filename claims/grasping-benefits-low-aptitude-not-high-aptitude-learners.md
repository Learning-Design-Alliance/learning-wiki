## Evidence

### Manuela Macedonia 2023

Manuela Macedonia, Brian Mathias, Astrid E. Lehner, Susanne M. Reiterer, Claudia Repetto. (2023). Grasping Virtual Objects Benefits Lower Aptitude Learners' Acquisition of Foreign Language Vocabulary. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09835-0

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Mixed-effects model interaction terms from the VR-cave study (LLA n = 21, HLA n = 25 by median split). The article reports an effect size of d = 0.42 for the significant AVOG × Aptitude interaction ("b = -0.70; p = .048, d = 0.42"); the AVO × Aptitude contrast was not significant.

> "The A VOG × Aptitude contrast was significant ( b = -0.70; p = .048, d = 0.42), and the A VO × Aptitude contrast was not significant ( b = -0.10; p = .77)."

### Manuela Macedonia 2023 (2)

Manuela Macedonia, Brian Mathias, Astrid E. Lehner, Susanne M. Reiterer, Claudia Repetto. (2023). Grasping Virtual Objects Benefits Lower Aptitude Learners' Acquisition of Foreign Language Vocabulary. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09835-0

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc Tukey comparisons in the same study. Grasping significantly benefited LLA learners ("t = -5.08; p < .001, d = 1.60") but not HLA learners (t = -2.64, p = .098); equivalence was not tested.

> "Post-hoc comparisons revealed a significant difference between test scores in the A VOG and A V learning conditions for LLA learners (t = -5.08; p < .001, d = 1.60), but not for HLA learners ( t = -2.64; p = .098)"
