# Microlearning produces more durable knowledge gains than webinars and routine training in EMTs at one-month follow-up

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Baranipour Jafar 2026

Baranipour Jafar, Hosseini Seyed Mohammad Reza, Sharifzadeh Gholam Reza, Poodineh Moghadam Mahdieh. (2026). Comparison of the effects of microlearning and webinars on knowledge and self-efficacy of emergency medical technicians (EMTs) in the diagnosis and treatment of cardiac arrhythmias. BMC Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12873-025-01428-0

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Results of a quasi-experimental pretest–posttest study with 75 EMTs cluster-randomized to microlearning, webinar, or control groups. At one-month follow-up the microlearning group's mean knowledge score of "16.7 ± 1.2" exceeded the webinar (13.2 ± 1.8) and control (7.8 ± 1.8) groups.

> "One month after the intervention, the mean knowledge scores were 16.7 ± 1.2, 13.2 ± 1.8, and 7.8 ± 1.8 in the microlearning, webinar, and control groups, respectively."

### Baranipour Jafar 2026 (2)

Baranipour Jafar, Hosseini Seyed Mohammad Reza, Sharifzadeh Gholam Reza, Poodineh Moghadam Mahdieh. (2026). Comparison of the effects of microlearning and webinars on knowledge and self-efficacy of emergency medical technicians (EMTs) in the diagnosis and treatment of cardiac arrhythmias. BMC Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12873-025-01428-0

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Tukey's post hoc test on knowledge scores in the same three-group quasi-experiment confirmed the one-month contrast between the two intervention groups: "a significant difference was observed between the microlearning and webinar groups" (P < 0.001).

> "Moreover, a significant difference was observed between the microlearning and webinar groups in the assessment one month after the intervention (P < 0.001, Table 1)."
