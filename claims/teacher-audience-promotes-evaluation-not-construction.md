# Writing to the teacher led students to treat writing as evaluation and reproduce transmitted content rather than construct their own knowledge

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students writing to the teacher perceived writing as an evaluation tool, iterated what the teacher transmitted, and avoided adding personal ideas and interpretations. [→ Kingir 2013](#kingir-2013)

## Evidence

### Kingir 2013

Kingir, S. (2013). Using Non-traditional Writing as a Tool in Learning Chemistry. Eurasia Journal of Mathematics, Science & Technology Education, 9(2), 101-114. https://doi.org/10.12973/eurasia.2013.922a

`q2 · i?`

Field notes taken during writing-sample assessment showed teacher-audience letters typically opened by stating what was understood from teaching and ended by asking the teacher to identify gaps, indicating evaluation-oriented writing.

> "These statements indicate that students perceived writing as an evaluation tool when they wrote to their teacher; they just iterated what their teacher transmitted to them in their writing."
