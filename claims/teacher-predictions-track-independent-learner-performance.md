## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After adjusting for time and random effects of teachers, learners, and questions, teachers' predictions correlated with learners' belief in the correct answer (β=0.02, t=4.6, P<0.001), with a time × prediction interaction (β=0.01, t=7.2, P<0.001). [→ Vélez 2023](#velez-2023)

## Evidence

### Vélez 2023

Vélez, N., Chen, A. M., Burke, T., Cushman, F. A., & Gershman, S. J. (2023). Teachers recruit mentalizing regions to represent learners’ beliefs. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2215015120

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Mixed-effects linear model on data from 140 online learners (Amazon Mechanical Turk) tested on teachers' examples, with learners, teachers, and problems as random intercepts. The article reports "a close relationship" with the printed coefficients; no standardized effect size is given.

> "After adjusting for these factors, we found a close relationship between teachers’ predictions about how likely learners would be to answer correctly and learners’ belief in the correct answer (Fig. 4C; main effect of teachers’ predictions: β=0.02, t=4.6, P< 0.001; interaction between time and teachers’ predictions: β = 0.01, t = 7.2, P < 0.001)."
