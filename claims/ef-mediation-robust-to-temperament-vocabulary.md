# The mediating role of executive functions remains significant when children's temperament and vocabulary are modeled as alternative mediators

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After adding negative affectivity, surgency/extraversion, effortful control, and Picture Vocabulary scores as additional mediators, the indirect paths from autonomy support via executive functions remained significant (Sobel Zs > 3.19, ps < .01), accounting for 60% (elementary) and 28% (high school) of the total effects. [→ Bindman 2015](#bindman-2015)

## Evidence

### Bindman 2015

Bindman, S. W., Pomerantz, E. M., & Roisman, G. I. (2015). Do Children's Executive Functions Account for Associations Between Early Autonomy-Supportive Parenting and Achievement Through High School? Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000017

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Supplemental SEM adding three CBQ temperament scales and Woodcock-Johnson Picture Vocabulary at 54 months as alternative mediators; the indirect paths via executive functions "remained significant" per Sobel tests.

> "When accounting for the three dimensions of temperament and the vocabulary scores, the links between children’s executive functions and achievement were reduced but remained significant (β =.39 for elementary school achievement and β = .32 for high school achievement, ts > 4.34, ps < .001)."
