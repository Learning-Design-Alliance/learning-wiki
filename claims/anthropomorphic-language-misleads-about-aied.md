## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Using anthropomorphic words such as 'intelligence' and 'learning' for AIED systems can mislead the public, policymakers, and researchers into assuming the tools have capabilities they lack, and the field should stop anthropomorphising. [→ Wayne Holmes 2024](#wayne-holmes-2024)

## Evidence

### Wayne Holmes 2024

Wayne Holmes. (2024). AIED—Coming of Age?. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-023-00352-3

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument in the Language section: the author contends no AI system today is intelligent or understands anything, and that anthropomorphic metaphors foster a false assumption of symmetric human-machine interaction. This is an argued position, not an empirical test.

> "continuing to use anthropomorphic words, confusing for example the appearance of intelligence with actual intelligence, can mislead people (the public, policymakers, and ourselves) into thinking that AIED tools can do more than they actually can"
