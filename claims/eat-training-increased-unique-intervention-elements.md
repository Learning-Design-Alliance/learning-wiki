# Training dietitians in the EAT Intervention significantly increased their delivery of unique motivational and behavioural intervention elements in real-world consultations

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Post-training consultations were significantly more likely to include the unique EAT elements (reasons for radiotherapy, eating as integral, written nutrition planner) than control consultations. [→ Beck A.K. 2020](#beck-ak-2020)

## Evidence

### Beck A.K. 2020

Beck A.K., Baker A.L., Carter G., Wratten C., Bauer J., Wolfenden L., McCarter K., Britton B. (2020). Assessing Adherence, Competence and Differentiation in a Stepped-Wedge Randomised Clinical Trial of a Complex Behaviour Change Intervention. Nutrients 12(8), 2332. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu12082332

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Differentiation analysis within a multi-site stepped-wedge RCT: 20% random stratified sample of audio-recorded dietetic consultations (control n = 196; intervention n = 194) coded by blind independent raters. Table 2 reports OR = 24.087 (p = 0.001) for reasons for radiotherapy, OR = 7.083 for eating as integral, and OR = 55.171 for the written nutrition planner, adjusted for site, radiotherapy interval and calendar time. The article states "dietitians were more likely to utilise both "unique" intervention elements".

> "Regression analyses confirmed that dietitians were more likely to utilise both "unique" intervention elements during consultations held after training, with exploration of patient reasons for undergoing radiotherapy increasing from largely absent, to almost a quarter of all sessions sampled (1 vs. 22.1%)"
