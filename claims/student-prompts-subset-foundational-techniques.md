## Evidence

### Amogh Sirnoorkar and N. Sanjay Rebello 2026

Amogh Sirnoorkar and N. Sanjay Rebello. (2026). Feedback That Clicks: Introductory Physics Students’ Valued Features in AI Feedback Generated From Self-Crafted and Engineered Prompts. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1103/v3pj-8491](https://doi.org/10.1103/v3pj-8491)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of 1235 qualified student prompts from an extra-credit assignment, coded dichotomously for four foundational techniques. The article reports "90.2% responses evidenced the technique of providing clear instructions"; for the second technique, "only 61.8% demonstrated both together" (context description plus explicit argument).

> "Overall, 90.2% responses evidenced the technique of providing clear instructions in their prompts by explicitly seeking feedback for their arguments."

### Amogh Sirnoorkar and N. Sanjay Rebello 2026 (2)

Amogh Sirnoorkar and N. Sanjay Rebello. (2026). Feedback That Clicks: Introductory Physics Students’ Valued Features in AI Feedback Generated From Self-Crafted and Engineered Prompts. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1103/v3pj-8491](https://doi.org/10.1103/v3pj-8491)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Coding of the same prompt corpus for the third and fourth techniques found these were "the least evidenced in students’ prompts": six role-based and three delimiter prompts among 1151 responses.

> "Out of 1151 responses, only six demonstrated role-based prompting, and only three included explicit use of delimiters such as quotes and asterisks."
