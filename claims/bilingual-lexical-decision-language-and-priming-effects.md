## Evidence

### Fei Gao 2023

Fei Gao, Lin Hua, Paulo Armada-da-Silva, Juan Zhang, Defeng Li, Zhiyi Chen, Chengwen Wang, Meng Du, Zhen Yuan. (2023). Shared and distinct neural correlates of first and second language morphological processing in bilingual brain. npj Science of Learning 8:33. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00184-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Linear mixed-effect modeling of reaction times from 30 adult Chinese-English bilinguals in a masked morphological priming task. The study found "Chinese items were recognized faster than English items" and morphological priming faster than semantic priming; averaged accuracy across participants was 94.90%.

> "RT results (Fig. 1b) indicated a signi ﬁcant main effect of language (estimate = 126.029, t = 3.721,p < 0.001, 95% CI = [0.06, 1.00]), such that Chinese items were recognized faster than English items. There was a signi ﬁcant priming type effect (estimate = 20.666, t = 2.864, p < 0.01, 95% CI = [0.00, 1.00]), where items of morphological priming were recognized faster than that of semantic priming."

### Fei Gao 2023 (2)

Fei Gao, Lin Hua, Paulo Armada-da-Silva, Juan Zhang, Defeng Li, Zhiyi Chen, Chengwen Wang, Meng Du, Zhen Yuan. (2023). Shared and distinct neural correlates of first and second language morphological processing in bilingual brain. npj Science of Learning 8:33. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00184-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Follow-up analysis of the same mixed-effect model showed the language-by-priming interaction was only marginal (p = 0.066), with "Chinese morphological priming condition was associated with faster responses than Chinese semantic priming"; no significant English priming difference is reported.

> "The interaction between language and priming type reached a marginal signi ﬁcance (estimate = −18.977, t = −1.841, p = 0.066, 95% CI = [0.00, 1.00]), whose following analysis showed that Chinese morphological priming condition was associated with faster responses than Chinese semantic priming ( p < 0.01)."
