## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Beattie 2021

Beattie, K. L., Hill, A., Horswill, M. S., Grove, P. M., & Stevenson, A. R. L. (2021). Laparoscopic skills training: the effects of viewing mode (2D vs. 3D) on skill acquisition and transfer. Surgical Endoscopy, 35(8), 4332–4344. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-020-07923-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Training-phase SSQ and NASA-TLX measures across the four groups; no statistically significant group differences were detected, so comfort and workload could not explain the training performance differences. No effect size was printed for these nulls.

> "There was no significant main effect of group on reported simulator sickness or experienced workload following the two training sessions, indicating that these variables could not account for the group differences in performance"

### Beattie 2021 (2)

Beattie, K. L., Hill, A., Horswill, M. S., Grove, P. M., & Stevenson, A. R. L. (2021). Laparoscopic skills training: the effects of viewing mode (2D vs. 3D) on skill acquisition and transfer. Surgical Endoscopy, 35(8), 4332–4344. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-020-07923-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Testing-phase NASA-TLX showed a significant main effect of group (p = 0.046); the single significant pairwise contrast was 2D → 3D vs 2D → 2D (p = 0.048). No effect size was printed for this finding.

> "Specifically, those who transferred from 2D to 3D reported significantly lower perceived workload in testing than those who used 2D only. All other comparisons were non-significant"
