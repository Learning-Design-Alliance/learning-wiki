# Example–problem pairs are more efficient than problem–example pairs for both gifted and nongifted primary school students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Studying example–problem pairs led to higher test performance with lower learning-phase effort investment than problem–example pairs, for gifted and nongifted students alike. [→ Coppens 2019](#coppens-2019)

## Evidence

### Coppens 2019

Coppens, L. C., Hoogerheide, V., Snippe, E. M., Flunger, B., & van Gog, T. (2019). Effects of problem–example and example–problem pairs on gifted and nongifted primary school students' learning. Instructional Science, 47, 279–297. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-019-09484-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experiment with 126 Dutch primary students quasi-randomly matched by gender and math score to EPEP or PEPE conditions; ANCOVAs on learning-phase effort and posttest performance. The discussion states "it was more efficient to study example–problem pairs" with similar test performance at lower effort.

> "although we did find that it was more efficient to study example–problem pairs, because the example–problem conditions attained that similar level of test performance with less effort investment during the learning phase than the problem–example conditions"
