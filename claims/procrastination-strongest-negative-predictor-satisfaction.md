## Evidence

### Liborius 2019

Liborius, P., Bellhäuser, H., & Schmitz, B. (2019). What makes a good study day? An intraindividual study on university students' time investment by means of time-series analyses. Learning and Instruction, 60, 310-321. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2017.10.006

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel analysis testing Hypothesis 4 in the 105-student diary study. The article reports the printed coefficient, a better-fitting random-slope model (χ²(6) = 349.27, p < .05), and a 24.3% reduction in residual variance when procrastination was added.

> "Procrastination negatively predicted students' daily study satisfaction above and beyond the previous predictors in the model (b procrastination = -0.53)."

### Liborius 2019 (2)

Liborius, P., Bellhäuser, H., & Schmitz, B. (2019). What makes a good study day? An intraindividual study on university students' time investment by means of time-series analyses. Learning and Instruction, 60, 310-321. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2017.10.006

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

The article's discussion of the diary data reports procrastination as "the factor most related to students' daily study satisfaction" with the printed correlation r = -.62, echoing Schmitz and Wiese's earlier finding of -.68.

> "Procrastination has been shown to be the factor most related to students' daily study satisfaction (r = -.62)."
