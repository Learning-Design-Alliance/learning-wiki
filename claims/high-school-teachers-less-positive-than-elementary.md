## Evidence

### Sebring 1995

Sebring, P. B., Bryk, A. S., Easton, J. Q., Luppescu, S., Thum, Y. M., Lopez, W. A., & Smith, B. (1995). Charting Reform: Chicago Teachers Take Stock. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/charting-reform-chicago-teachers-take-stock

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

School-level indicators were computed by averaging teacher responses within each school and displayed in box plots comparing elementary and high schools on recent changes and reform impact. The authors call these substantial elementary-high school differences a second major finding running throughout the report.

> "The average response among high schools is, however, roughly com- parable to responses among the lowest quarter of elementary schools."

### Sebring 1995 (2)

Sebring, P. B., Bryk, A. S., Easton, J. Q., Luppescu, S., Thum, Y. M., Lopez, W. A., & Smith, B. (1995). Charting Reform: Chicago Teachers Take Stock. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/charting-reform-chicago-teachers-take-stock

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bar graphs compared SIP assessments in the most and least positive elementary and high schools. The pattern holds for principal leadership, teacher influence, and LSC effectiveness as well; the authors conclude reform was a weaker treatment for high schools.

> "Only 37 percent of these high school teachers provide positive comments about the SIP and its role in local school improvement."
