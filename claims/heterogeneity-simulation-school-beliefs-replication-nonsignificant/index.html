Evidence¶

Baier-Mosch 2026¶

Baier-Mosch, Franziska; Dignath, Charlotte; Kunter, Mareike (2026). Effects of heterogeneity simulations on beliefs. Learning and Instruction 101, Art. 102258. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102258

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

Study 2, an exact replication with 152 pre-service teachers, same conditions, materials and instruments. The mixed ANOVA on school-student heterogeneity beliefs showed the "interaction between time and group was not significant, F(2, 149) =2.860, p =.060, partial η2 =.037".

"The results of a mixed ANOVA showed that the interaction between time and group was not significant, F(2, 149) =2.860, p =.060, partial η2 =.037."

Baier-Mosch 2026 (2)¶

Baier-Mosch, Franziska; Dignath, Charlotte; Kunter, Mareike (2026). Effects of heterogeneity simulations on beliefs. Learning and Instruction 101, Art. 102258. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102258

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Study 2 simple main effect in the simulation condition: "the effect of time for the heterogeneity simulation condition was not significant this time, F(1, 49) =3.968, p =.052, partial η2 =.075"; the article calls this a medium effect that only narrowly missed significance.

"A simple main effect test showed that the effect of time for the heterogeneity simulation condition was not significant this time, F(1, 49) =3.968, p =.052, partial η2 =.075."
