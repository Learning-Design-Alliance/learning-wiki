## Evidence

### Michael J. Obsniuk 2015

Michael J. Obsniuk, Paul W. Irving, and Marcos D. Caballero. (2015). A Case Study: Novel Group Interactions through Introductory Computational Physics. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.055](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.055)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Audio and video analysis of the debugging phase of the case-study group's work on the geostationary-orbit problem. Student A "read aloud and wrote down the line of code" for the momentum update "while the entire group confirmed on its correct form," after which similar confirmations followed for other lines.

> "That is, Student A (i) read aloud and wrote down the line of code ⃗ pf = ⃗ pi + ⃗Fnet∆t while the entire group conﬁrmed on its correct form."

### Michael J. Obsniuk 2015 (2)

Michael J. Obsniuk, Paul W. Irving, and Marcos D. Caballero. (2015). A Case Study: Novel Group Interactions through Introductory Computational Physics. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.055](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.055)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Video analysis of the group's gestures during the debugging phase, showing confirmation of the link between the position-update and momentum-update lines of code. The gesture sequence "demonstrated that the group understood" the role of the linking velocity equation.

> "Pointing at (iii), the ⃗ v in (ii), and the ⃗ pf in (i), demonstrated that the group understood that without this linking equation (iii), the velocity used in (ii) would not reﬂect the time updated velocity by means of (i)."
