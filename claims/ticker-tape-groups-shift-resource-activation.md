# Student groups working with ticker-tape timers shifted quickly between two mutually exclusive interpretations of the same dot spacing, drawing on different intuitive resources as the lesson's cues changed

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The same groups first interpreted shorter, more closely spaced tapes as taking less time and later reinterpreted strip length as proportional to speed, showing context-dependent reactivation of resources. [→ Edward F. Redish and Eric Kuo 2014](#edward-f-redish-and-eric-kuo-2014)

## Evidence

### Edward F. Redish and Eric Kuo 2014

Edward F. Redish and Eric Kuo. (2014). Language of physics, language of math: Disciplinary culture and dynamic epistemology. [doi:10.1007/s11191-015-9749-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11191-015-9749-7)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Observational case of groups of four students working with paper tapes from a Pasco Tape Timer in a recitation section; initially many said it takes less time to generate the shorter tape (more distance implies more time), then after a velocity prompt they reinterpreted the strips, and "multiple groups of students were seen to transition quickly from one interpretation to another."
