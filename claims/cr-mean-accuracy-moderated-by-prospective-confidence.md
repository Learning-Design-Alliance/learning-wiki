## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Double 2018

Double, K. S., & Birney, D. P. (2018). Reactivity to confidence ratings in older individuals performing the latin square task. Metacognition and Learning, 13, 309–326. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-018-9186-5

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Regression on Mean Accuracy (R2 = .35, F(3,85) = 15.56, p < .001) in the randomized CR vs No-CR experiment found a significant group × prospective confidence interaction, b = 29.82, p = .018, with the crossover shown in Fig. 3.

> "Notably, the group × prospective confidence interaction was also significant,b =2 9 . 8 2 ,t =2 . 4 1 ,p = .018. As shown in Fig. 3, participants high in prospective confidence performed better when confidence ratings were elicited, whereas participants low in prospective confidence performed worse when asked to provide confidence ratings."

### Double 2018 (2)

Double, K. S., & Birney, D. P. (2018). Reactivity to confidence ratings in older individuals performing the latin square task. Metacognition and Learning, 13, 309–326. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-018-9186-5

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Johnson-Neyman probing of the Mean Accuracy moderation showed the CR-versus-control difference was significant only for prospective confidence below 62.09%, where "participants in the CR group performed worse than controls".

> "The results indicated that there was a significant group difference between the CR and No-CR group for participants with prospective confidence below 62.09, such that participants in the CR group performed worse than controls within this region."
