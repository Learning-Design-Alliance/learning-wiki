## Evidence

### Al-Najafi Dheyaa 2026

Al-Najafi Dheyaa, Krause Katherine D., Wang Yundi, Zuo Qi Kang, Koblanski Maya E, Leong Cameron J., Schmidt Emma, Faran Muhammad, Verma Vanay, Vyas Ravi, Campbell Matthew, Hwang Jaehyun, Deng Jiawen, Palepu Anita. (2026). Psychometric performance and student perceptions of AI- versus student-generated multiple-choice questions: a single-center randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09671-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Primary ITT analysis of the 112-item mock exam scores; the article describes substantial overlap in score distributions and reads this as substantive validity evidence that both formats assessed similar constructs. Equivalence was not formally tested.

> "In the ITT population (N = 258), mean exam scores were 73.4% ± 12.3% for the Student-gen exam and 71.0% ± 9.0% for the AI-gen exam. This difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.083) and corresponded to a small effect size (Cohen's d = 0.22)"

### Al-Najafi Dheyaa 2026 (2)

Al-Najafi Dheyaa, Krause Katherine D., Wang Yundi, Zuo Qi Kang, Koblanski Maya E, Leong Cameron J., Schmidt Emma, Faran Muhammad, Verma Vanay, Vyas Ravi, Campbell Matthew, Hwang Jaehyun, Deng Jiawen, Palepu Anita. (2026). Psychometric performance and student perceptions of AI- versus student-generated multiple-choice questions: a single-center randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09671-0

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r3`

Per-protocol sensitivity analysis excluding 10 participants for pre-specified engagement and validity criteria; excluded participants scored lower overall (48.6% ± 28.3% versus 73.2% ± 8.3%). The article instructs interpreting the significant PP finding cautiously relative to the primary non-significant ITT result.

> "In the PP population, the Student-gen group demonstrated modestly higher mean scores than the AI-gen group (p = 0.005), though the effect size remained small (Cohen's d = 0.36)."
