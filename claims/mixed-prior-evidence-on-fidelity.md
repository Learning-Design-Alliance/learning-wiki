## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The review of prior work reports mostly equivalent effects of HF and LF simulation, but Conlon et al. found significantly better practical performance in an HF group of interns. [→ Massoth C 2019](#massoth-c-2019)

## Evidence

### Massoth C 2019

Massoth C, Röder H, Ohlenburg H, Hessler M, Zarbock A, Pöpping DM, Wenk M. (2019). High-fidelity is not superior to low-fidelity simulation but leads to overconfidence in medical students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1464-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Narrative review of prior trials in the Discussion. The article states an increasing body of high-quality randomized trials shows "equivalent effects", while attributing to Conlon et al. the exception that the HF group achieved significantly better expert-scored practical outcomes.

> "a study by Conlon et al. compared low-, mid- and high-fidelity training groups and found no differences with respect to the improvement of theoretical knowledge, but in the evaluation of practical performance, as scored by expert assessors, the HF group achieved significantly better outcomes"
