## Evidence

### Al‐Kaylani HM 2022

Al‐Kaylani HM, Reasoner EE, Loeffler BT, Mott SL, Madasu S, Liu A, Langbehn K, Conrad AL, Dickens D, Grafft A, Harshman L, Modi AJ, van der Plas E. (2022). Characterizing academic performance in pediatric acute lymphoblastic leukemia with population‐based achievement tests. Cancer Reports. https://doi.org/10.1002/cnr2.1560

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Univariate mixed effects model on school-based reading scores from 63 survivors. The article reports high-risk protocols "tended to be associated with lower reading scores" without reaching significance (β = −16.61, p = .07).

> "While not statistically significant at p < .05, univariate models indicated that exposure to high‐risk treatment protocols tended to be associated with lower reading scores (β = −16.61, 95% CI −34.57:1.35, p = .07)."

### Al‐Kaylani HM 2022 (2)

Al‐Kaylani HM, Reasoner EE, Loeffler BT, Mott SL, Madasu S, Liu A, Langbehn K, Conrad AL, Dickens D, Grafft A, Harshman L, Modi AJ, van der Plas E. (2022). Characterizing academic performance in pediatric acute lymphoblastic leukemia with population‐based achievement tests. Cancer Reports. https://doi.org/10.1002/cnr2.1560

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Mixed effects analysis of the same cohort; the article reports no significant association between age at diagnosis and academic outcomes, contrasting with prior studies. The discussion suggests effects of age may be modest in chemotherapy-only samples (80% of this sample) and that a more homogeneous sample may be needed.

> "By contrast, we observed no significant association between academic outcomes and age at diagnosis."
