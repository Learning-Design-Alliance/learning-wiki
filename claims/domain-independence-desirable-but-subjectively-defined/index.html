Evidence¶

Valdés Aguirre 2016¶

Valdés Aguirre, B., Ramírez Uresti, J. A., & du Boulay, B. (2016). An Analysis of Student Model Portability. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-016-0113-0

q2 · i? · theoretical · r2

Drawing on Kobsa's list of desirable GUMS traits, the article presents domain independence as the trait by which "a model can represent the aspect it is meant to represent, regardless of the domain it is in", illustrated with an affect model working equally in math and physics tutors.

"One of such traits is domain independence. This means that a model can represent the aspect it is meant to represent, regardless of the domain it is in, i.e., regardless of the context."

Valdés Aguirre 2016 (2)¶

Valdés Aguirre, B., Ramírez Uresti, J. A., & du Boulay, B. (2016). An Analysis of Student Model Portability. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-016-0113-0

q2 · i? · design · r2

The article argues domain definitions are subjective (is Calculus part of physics or math?) and states that "the answers of these questions lie with the maker of each tutoring system", defining domain as a conceptual label given by the tutor's creator. The article says it therefore excluded a domain independence factor as impractical to measure.

"Making a model domain independent is hard because the definition of domain can be subjective."
