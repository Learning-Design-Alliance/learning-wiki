# Testing condition significantly affects exam scores in introductory statistics, with student-prepared aids outperforming instructor-prepared aids and the open textbook

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Exam scores differed significantly across the four testing conditions, F(3,87) = 22.92, p < .001, partial η² = .441, with the student-prepared aids condition scoring significantly higher than the instructor-prepared aids and open-textbook conditions. ## Evidence

### Larwin et al. 2008

Karen H. Larwin, David A. Larwin, Jennifer Gorman. (2008). Mnemonic aids during tests: Worthless frivolity or effective tool in statistics education? Journal of Instructional Pedagogies. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Mnemonic+aids+during+tests+statistics+education

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Factorial ANOVA (2 gender × 2 instructor × 4 testing condition) on course exam scores of n = 94 undergraduates in four applied statistics sections, each section experiencing all four conditions in randomly determined order. The article prints the effect size "partial η² = .441" for the testing-condition effect, a large effect, with Scheffe post hoc mean differences of 5.73 and 13.19 favoring student-prepared aids (both p < 0.001).

> "The factorial analysis of variance also indicated that there were significant differences in student exam scores across the different testing conditions, F(3,87) = 22.92, p < .001, partial η² = .441."
