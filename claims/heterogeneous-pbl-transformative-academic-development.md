# Heterogeneous PBL group learning serves as a transformative academic development tool, including for ESL students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Collaboration across differing levels of academic preparedness bridges inequalities, addresses misconceptions, and motivates lower-level students. [→ Singaram Veena S. 2010](#singaram-veena-s-2010)
`q2 i?` Small-group collaboration creates a forum that pushes English second language students to engage in English dialogue, enhancing confidence and literacy skills. [→ Singaram Veena S. 2010 (2)](#singaram-veena-s-2010-2)

## Evidence

### Singaram Veena S. 2010

Singaram Veena S., van der Vleuten Cees P. M., Stevens Fred, Dolmans Diana H. J. M. (2010). “For most of us Africans, we don’t just speak”: a qualitative investigation into collaborative heterogeneous PBL group learning. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-010-9262-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Focus group thematic analysis: collaborating rather than competing "creates a safe environment that helps bridge inequalities" among students with different academic preparedness; misconceptions are addressed and lower-level students become motivated by higher-level peers.

> "Collaborating in PBL groups as opposed to being competitive creates a safe environment that helps bridge inequalities amongst students especially those who have different levels of academic preparedness."

### Singaram Veena S. 2010 (2)

Singaram Veena S., van der Vleuten Cees P. M., Stevens Fred, Dolmans Diana H. J. M. (2010). “For most of us Africans, we don’t just speak”: a qualitative investigation into collaborative heterogeneous PBL group learning. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-010-9262-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of student focus groups found tutorials "force" ESL students to converse in English, enhancing confidence and literacy; one student reported "I have improved my communication skills and the way that I associate with people."

> "The collaboration in the small group tutorial ‘forces’ and creates a forum for second language students to engage socially and academically by contributing, engaging and discussing in formal and informal English dialogue."
