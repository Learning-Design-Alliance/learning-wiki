Evidence¶

Haycock 1999¶

Haycock, Kati, Ed.; Barth, Patte, Ed.; Mitchell, Ruth, Ed.; Wilkins, Amy, Ed. (1999). Ticket to Nowhere: The Gap between Leaving High School and Entering College and High-Performance Jobs. Thinking K-16; v3 n2 Fall 1999. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED457262

q2 · i? · review · r1

The report cites national data (NCES Condition of Education 1997, from the NELS:88 longitudinal study) that "about three-quarters of high school graduates will go to college within two years of graduation," and separately projects that more than 80% of today's sixth graders will end up in college if present growth rates continue.

"Currently about three-quarters of high school graduates will go to college within two years of graduation."

Haycock 1999 (2)¶

Haycock, Kati, Ed.; Barth, Patte, Ed.; Mitchell, Ruth, Ed.; Wilkins, Amy, Ed. (1999). Ticket to Nowhere: The Gap between Leaving High School and Entering College and High-Performance Jobs. Thinking K-16; v3 n2 Fall 1999. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED457262

q2 · i? · review · r1

The report's data section, drawing on the NCES Condition of Education 1997 from a survey of remedial education at higher education institutions in Fall 1995, states that "Nearly half of all college students take at least one remedial course," with higher rates at minority colleges (e.g., reading 13% vs. 25%; mathematics 27% vs. 35%).

"Nearly half of all college students take at least one remedial course."

Haycock 1999 (3)¶

Haycock, Kati, Ed.; Barth, Patte, Ed.; Mitchell, Ruth, Ed.; Wilkins, Amy, Ed. (1999). Ticket to Nowhere: The Gap between Leaving High School and Entering College and High-Performance Jobs. Thinking K-16; v3 n2 Fall 1999. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED457262

q2 · i? · review · r1

The report's data page, citing Adelman in National Crosstalk (Summer 1998), presents that "Students Who Required Remediation Graduated Lower Rates": 54% earned a BA with no remedial courses, 45% with one remedial course, and 18% with three remedial courses.
