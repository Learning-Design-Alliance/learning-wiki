# BDNF sustains protein synthesis-dependent L-LTP at stage II but not stage I, converting E-LTP to L-LTP only when applied after tetanus

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` BDNF perfused 5–180 min after l-TBS reversed the L-LTP impairment caused by the protein synthesis inhibitor anisomycin, whereas BDNF before l-TBS did not. [→ Pang 2016](#pang-2016)
`q2 i?` BDNF applied at stage II, but not stage I, converted E-LTP (induced by short TBS) into L-LTP in wild-type slices. [→ Pang 2016 (2)](#pang-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Pang 2016

Pang, P. T., Nagappan, G., Guo, W. & Lu, B. (2016). Extracellular and intracellular cleavages of proBDNF required at two distinct stages of late-phase LTP. npj Science of Learning 1, 16003. https://doi.org/10.1038/npjscilearn.2016.3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Wild-type slice recordings with anisomycin (40 μM) throughout the recording; anisomycin alone prevented L-LTP, and BDNF reversed the deficit only when applied after l-TBS.

> "Perfusion of BDNF 60 min before to 5 min after l-TBS could not rescue L-LTP (Figure 3a, anisomycin, 101.4 ± 6.6%; anisomycin +BDNF, 102.6 ± 7.5%, P = 0.885), while BDNF perfused 5-180 min after l-TBS reversed the L-LTP impairment by anisomycin (Figure 3b, anisomycin, 101.4 ± 6.6%; anisomycin+BDNF, 164.5 ± 13.7%, Po 0.005)."

### Pang 2016 (2)

Pang, P. T., Nagappan, G., Guo, W. & Lu, B. (2016). Extracellular and intracellular cleavages of proBDNF required at two distinct stages of late-phase LTP. npj Science of Learning 1, 16003. https://doi.org/10.1038/npjscilearn.2016.3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Short TBS (3 bursts) induced only E-LTP in wild-type slices; BDNF at stage II raised fEPSP to 151.1 ± 5.0% while stage I application left it at 107.7 ± 108.5%, P = 0.638.

> "Application of BDNF at stage II converted E-LTP to L-LTP (Figure 3d, TBS 3 × , 112.5 ± 8.6%; TBS 3 × +BDNF, 151.1 ± 5.0%, P o0.005). In contrast, application of BDNF at the stage I failed to do the same"
