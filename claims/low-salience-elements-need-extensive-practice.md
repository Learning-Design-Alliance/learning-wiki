`q1 i?` Elements with low frequency or low salience, especially form-meaning mappings, are hard or impossible to acquire through mere communicative exposure, and proceduralizing them takes a very large amount of practice. [→ Robert DeKeyser 2010](#robert-dekeyser-2010)

## Evidence

### Robert DeKeyser 2010

Robert DeKeyser. (2010). Practice for Second Language Learning: Don't Throw out the Baby with the Bathwater. International Journal of English Studies, 10(1). https://www.um.es/ijes [doi:10.6018/ijes/2010/1/114021](https://doi.org/10.6018/ijes/2010/1/114021)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument citing SLA research (e.g. DeKeyser 2005; Pica 2009). The article states "Proceduralizing and (at least partially) automatizing such distinctions takes a very large amount of practice", giving examples like Romance aspectual distinctions and plural marking for speakers of Chinese, Japanese or Korean.

> "many have argued that a number of elements of any language are hard or impossible to learn through mere exposure and 'practice' in the mere sense of communicating in the target language, because these elements have low frequency or otherwise lack salience"
