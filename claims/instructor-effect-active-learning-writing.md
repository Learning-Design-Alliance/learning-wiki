## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Instructors differed in effectiveness with active-learning techniques, with significant effects on both in-class and exam writing scores controlling for aptitude and treatment. [→ Linton 2014](#linton-2014)

## Evidence

### Linton 2014

Linton, D. L., Pangle, W. M., Wyatt, K. H., Powell, K. N., & Sherwood, R. E. (2014). Identifying Key Features of Effective Active Learning: The Effects of Writing and Peer Discussion. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.13-12-0242

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

GLMM results across three instructors with differing experience (14 yr, 4 yr, first-time) implementing the same materials; the article reports "a strong effect of instructor" (χ2 = 13.3, p < 0.001) and, for exam writing, χ2 = 19.3, p < 0.0001. No effect size printed.

> "There was a strong effect of instructor on students' performance during the in-class writing (Figure 3; χ2 = 13.3, df = 2, p < 0.001), with some instructors achieving higher student scores than other instructors regardless of the treatment and controlling for student aptitude."
