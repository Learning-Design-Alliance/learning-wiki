## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Klejnstrup 2018

Klejnstrup, Buhl-Wiggers, Jones, & Rand. (2018). Early life malaria exposure and academic performance. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0199542

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Household fixed-effects estimation on 149,262 observations (households with at least two children aged 7–14); the English coefficient is -0.758 (SE 0.206), confirming the multivariate estimate of -0.903 (SE 0.161).

> "These results confirm those obtained in the previous multivariate models. This suggests that potential bias from omitted birth year by district-level factors is at most negligible."

### Klejnstrup 2018 (2)

Klejnstrup, Buhl-Wiggers, Jones, & Rand. (2018). Early life malaria exposure and academic performance. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0199542

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Robustness suite in Table 3: cohort-level pseudo-panel (-1.002), dropping pre-2002 cohorts, districts with initial PfPR rise, middle cohorts, and linear-probability models at each of five competency cutoffs, all significant except cutoff 1.

> "None of these raises concerns that the negative association between birth-year PfPR and performance on the English achievement test is fragile."
