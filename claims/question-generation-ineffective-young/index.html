Evidence¶

Garvin Brod 2021¶

Garvin Brod. (2021). Generative Learning: Which Strategies for What Age? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09571-9

q3 · i1 · review · r3

The review reports Rosenshine et al.'s meta-analysis of question-generation training interventions, with an overall effect size of 0.35 SDs on standardized tests and 0.82 SDs on non-standardized tests; college students consistently benefited but only one of four third-grade studies did, with mixed results for grades 4–9.

"revealed a positive overall effect on comprehension tested at the end of the intervention (0.35 SDs for standardized tests and 0.82 SDs for non-standardized tests)."

Garvin Brod 2021 (2)¶

Garvin Brod. (2021). Generative Learning: Which Strategies for What Age? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09571-9

q2 · i? · review · r3

A direct age-comparative study found self-generated questions inferior to provided questions and no better than rereading at all three grade levels, though relative effectiveness and the number of conceptual questions increased with age, attributed to better metacognitive abilities.

"It compared text comprehension in fifth-, eighth-, and eleventh-graders and found that self-generated questions were less effective than provided questions and not more effective than rereading in promoting comprehension across all age groups."
