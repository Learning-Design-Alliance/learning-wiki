# Under the 2025 norms, higher math growth expectations lower conditional growth percentiles for higher-growth students

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` For higher-growth students, the same RIT gain can rank lower in CGP under the 2025 norms because national math growth expectations are higher. [→ Hashim 2026](#hashim-2026)

## Evidence

### Hashim 2026

Hashim, A. K. (2026). How post-COVID norm shifts change the meaning of MAP Growth metrics for school accountability. NWEA White Paper. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/how-post%e2%80%91covid-norm-shifts-change-the-meaning-of-map-growth-metrics-for-school-accountability/

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The paper's illustrative math example in the teacher-evaluation section: a sixth-grade fall RIT of 240 with 8 points of growth ranks at the 50th CGP percentile under 2020 norms but the 34th under 2025 norms. Percentiles are descriptive, not effect sizes.

> "a sixth-grade student with a fall RIT score of 240 who grows 8 points by spring would place at the 50th percentile under the prior, 2020 norms. But under the 2025 norms, the same RIT score gain only corresponds to a CGP of the 34th percentile."
