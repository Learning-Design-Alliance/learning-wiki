## Evidence

### Lebert 2024

Lebert, A., & Vilarroya, Ó. (2024). The links between experiential learning and 4E cognition. Ann NY Acad Sci, 1541, 37–52. https://doi.org/10.1111/nyas.15238

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article reports the 2022 Kozanitis and Nenciovici meta-analysis of 104 studies comparing college students in humanities and social science programs under active instruction versus traditional lecturing. Assessment scores were higher under active instruction in a proportion similar to the Freeman et al. study; no effect size is printed.

> "Student performance on assessment scores was found to be higher under active instruction in a proportion similar to the Freeman et al. study."

### Lebert 2024 (2)

Lebert, A., & Vilarroya, Ó. (2024). The links between experiential learning and 4E cognition. Ann NY Acad Sci, 1541, 37–52. https://doi.org/10.1111/nyas.15238

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Moderator results of the same meta-analysis as reported by the article: benefits of active instruction were greater for smaller (≤20 students) classes or groups and for upper-level rather than introductory courses. No effect sizes are printed for these moderator contrasts.

> "The relative beneficial effect of active instruction was found to be higher for smaller (≤20 students) rather than larger class or group sizes, and for upper level rather than introductory courses."
