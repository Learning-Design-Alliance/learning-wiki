# Teachers who completed a scaffolding intervention program increased the contingency of their support far more than non-participating teachers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Contingency increased from premeasurement to postmeasurement significantly more in the scaffolding condition than in the nonscaffolding condition, with a large effect size. [→ van de Pol 2015](#van-de-pol-2015)

## Evidence

### van de Pol 2015

van de Pol, J., Volman, M., Oort, F., & Beishuizen, J. (2015). The effects of scaffolding in the classroom: support contingency and student independent working time in relation to student achievement, task effort and appreciation of support. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-015-9351-z

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVA manipulation check showed a significant condition by measurement occasion interaction on teachers' contingency, F(1,28) = 17.72, p = .00, with a large printed effect size (gp2 = .39). Contingency rose from about 50% to about 80% in the scaffolding condition.

> "The results of the repeated-measures ANOVA showed that there was a signiﬁcant interaction effect of condition and measurement occasion on teachers’ contingency (F(1,28) = 17.72, p = .00) (Fig. 1). The effect size can be considered large; gp2 was .39"
