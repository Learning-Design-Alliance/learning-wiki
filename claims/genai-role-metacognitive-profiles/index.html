Evidence¶

Cui 2026¶

Cui, Y., Yan, W., Chiu, T. K. F., & Nakajima, T. (2026). How motivation and roles influence metacognitive engagement in student-GenAI interaction. International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education, 23(34). https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00610-z

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Chi-square tests across four role groups (127 replacement-tool, 59 enhancement-tool, 74 instructor, 84 collaborator messages). The Collaborator group's M4 (35.3%), M5 (30.6%), and M6 (16.5%) together accounted for 82.4% of coded messages; the article reports M5 showing "the largest between-group effect (χ 2= 56.18, p < 0.001)". No standardized effect size is printed.

"The Replacement Tool group was almost entirely confined to M1 (31.2%) and M2 (46.9%), with M5 and M6 entirely absent."

Related Claims¶

  • Intrinsically motivated students show significantly higher proportions of evaluation and elaboration messages, while extrinsically motivated students concentrate in planning and orientation — related
  • Only the Collaborator group shows a complete significant sequential metacognitive chain from orientation through elaboration; the Replacement Tool group shows only self-loops — related
  • Motivational orientation is strongly associated with the role students assign to GenAI: intrinsically motivated students most often position it as an instructor, extrinsically motivated students as a replacement tool — related
  • Practitioners near-universally reject AI as a replacement for instructor expertise while affirming its supportive, collaborative role — related
  • Role metaphors positioned GenAI as helper, learning partner, and friend/foe, with a high proportion combining friend and foe — related
  • Post-training GenAI use patterns were unevenly distributed: efficient collaborators were the largest profile (47.5%), followed by over-revisers (26.2%) and mixed users (21.2%) — related
  • Students predominantly conceptualize the LLM as an Assistant rather than Peer or Expert, and role distributions differ significantly across assignments — related
  • Process mining shows the ChatGPT group's revision loop centred on consulting ChatGPT, while the human-expert group strengthened connections between revising and reading, orientation and evaluation — related
  • Human–GenAI collaborative interactions show promise for supporting metacognition and sociometacognition, based on an exploratory expert case study — related
  • Learners supported by ChatGPT, a human expert, checklist tools, or no support show no significant difference in post-task intrinsic motivation — related
  • Checklist tool use significantly increases Evaluation processes, an effect not observed in the ChatGPT and human-expert groups — related
