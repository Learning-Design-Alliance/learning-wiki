## Evidence

### McKagan 2010

McKagan, S. B., Perkins, K. K., & Wieman, C. E. (2010). The Design and Validation of the Quantum Mechanics Conceptual Survey. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.6.020121](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.6.020121)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Posttest analysis across course levels reported in the article's posttest section. The article states the level difference could reflect either a selection effect or later learning in the undergraduate curriculum; more research is needed.

> "More advanced students tend to score higher than less advanced students, with typical scores of 50-70% for students after modern physics courses, 80% for students after a junior-level quantum course, and 75-90% for graduate students."

### McKagan 2010 (2)

McKagan, S. B., Perkins, K. K., & Wieman, C. E. (2010). The Design and Validation of the Quantum Mechanics Conceptual Survey. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.6.020121](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.6.020121)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Gain analysis cited from the authors' earlier publications reporting pre/post QMCS administration: "students make signiﬁcant gains in modern physics courses" and zero gains in graduate quantum courses. No effect size is printed here.

> "When the QMCS is given pre and post, as we have reported elsewhere, students make signiﬁcant gains in modern physics courses 47, and zero gains in graduate quantum courses 48."

### McKagan 2010 (3)

McKagan, S. B., Perkins, K. K., & Wieman, C. E. (2010). The Design and Validation of the Quantum Mechanics Conceptual Survey. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.6.020121](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.6.020121)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Single junior quantum course with an abbreviated six-question pretest showed a printed normalized gain of 0.43. The authors caution this was "a limited subset of questions and an atypical course including many uncommon teaching reforms".

> "There was a signiﬁcant gain (0.43) on these six questions, which suggests that students learned concepts tested by the QMCS in this course."
