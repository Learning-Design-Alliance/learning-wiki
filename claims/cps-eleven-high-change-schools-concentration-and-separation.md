# Eleven struggling neighborhood high schools saw special education entry rise from 16 to 30 percent, and learning-disabled ninth graders were increasingly separated in English classrooms

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Eleven probation neighborhood high schools in low-income areas rose from 16 to 30 percent special education entry as very low achieving students plummeted (down 922) and area students with disabilities increasingly enrolled locally (64 to 69 percent). [→ Shazia Rafiullah Miller and Robert M. Gladden 2002](#shazia-rafiullah-miller-and-robert-m-gladden-2002)

## Evidence

### Shazia Rafiullah Miller and Robert M. Gladden 2002

Shazia Rafiullah Miller and Robert M. Gladden. (2002). Changing Special Education Enrollments: Causes and Distribution Among Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/changing-special-education-enrollments-causes-and-distribution-among-schools

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Case analysis of eleven high change schools (Austin, DuSable, Englewood, Gage Park, Manley, Orr, Phillips, Robeson, South Shore, Tilden, Wells), all on probation in 1999–00. On average 30 percent of entering ninth graders were special education eligible, driven by drops of 922 very low and 401 low achieving general education students.

> "By 1999–00, on average, 30 percent of entering ninth graders attending these schools was eligible for special education services"

### Shazia Rafiullah Miller and Robert M. Gladden 2002 (2)

Shazia Rafiullah Miller and Robert M. Gladden. (2002). Changing Special Education Enrollments: Causes and Distribution Among Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/changing-special-education-enrollments-causes-and-distribution-among-schools

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of ninth-grade English classroom composition, chosen because all ninth graders take English. The report finds learning-disabled ninth graders "increasingly took first-year English classes separately" from non-disabled peers, complicating the least restrictive environment mandate under IDEA.

> "ninth graders with learning disabilities increasingly took first-year English classes separately from their non-disabled peers, often in classrooms where 75 percent or more of the students were eligible for special education services"
