# Language proficiency significantly moderates the effect of learning motivation on oral proficiency improvement

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The interaction of learning motivation and language proficiency significantly predicts oral proficiency improvement. [→ Chen D 2026](#chen-d-2026)
`q2 i?` At low language proficiency, the effect of learning motivation on oral improvement is weak and not significant. [→ Chen D 2026 (2)](#chen-d-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Chen D 2026

Chen D, Zeng L, Ou W and Zhong S (2026) ASR technology in college English speaking instruction: the role of feedback internalization and metacognitive strategies. Front. Psychol. 17:1847238. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1847238

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multi-group SEM moderation analysis across the three proficiency groups (183/95/47) tested H11b. The interaction was significant (p = 0.0205); no standardized effect size is printed.

> "the interaction term between learning motivation and language pro-ficiency has a coefficient of 0.1200 (SE = 0.0515, CR = 2.3294, p = 0.0205)."

### Chen D 2026 (2)

Chen D, Zeng L, Ou W and Zhong S (2026) ASR technology in college English speaking instruction: the role of feedback internalization and metacognitive strategies. Front. Psychol. 17:1847238. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1847238

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Conditional effects analysis in the same model. At low proficiency the motivation effect was non-significant (p = 0.2938); at average and high proficiency the article reports significant positive conditional effects.

> "when language proficiency is low, the effect of learning motivation on oral improvement is weak and not significant (conditional effect estimate = 0.0698, SE = 0.0664, CR = 1.0516, p = 0.2938, 95% CI [−0.0608, 0.2003])."
