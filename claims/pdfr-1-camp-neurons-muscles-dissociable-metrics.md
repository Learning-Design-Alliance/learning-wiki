# PDFR-1 promotes habituation via cAMP signaling in both neurons and body wall muscle, with probability, duration, and latency as dissociable metrics

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Pan-neuronal PDFR-1 restoration fully rescued probability and latency but not duration; muscle restoration fully rescued probability and partially rescued latency and duration; even combined expression did not fully rescue duration. [→ Evan L. Ardiel 2017](#evan-l-ardiel-2017)
`q3 i?` Elevating cAMP via constitutively active ACY-1 in PDFR-1-positive cells normalized latency habituation of the pdf-1;pdf-2 double mutant without inducing a habituated state. [→ Evan L. Ardiel 2017 (2)](#evan-l-ardiel-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Evan L. Ardiel 2017

Evan L. Ardiel, Alex J. Yu, Andrew C. Giles and Catharine H. Rankin. (2017). Habituation as an adaptive shift in response strategy mediated by neuropeptides. npj Science of Learning 2:9. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-017-0011-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Intersectional Cre-lox promoter rescue experiments in the pdfr-1 mutant, testing PDFR-1 restoration in neurons (tag-168) and body wall muscle (myo-3) across three habituation metrics.

> "In contrast, restoring pdfr-1 expression in the body wall muscle ( myo-3 promoter) fully restored response probability and partially rescued the response latency (Fig. 3e) and duration phenotypes (Fig. 3f)"

### Evan L. Ardiel 2017 (2)

Evan L. Ardiel, Alex J. Yu, Andrew C. Giles and Catharine H. Rankin. (2017). Habituation as an adaptive shift in response strategy mediated by neuropeptides. npj Science of Learning 2:9. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-017-0011-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Transgenic overexpression of constitutively active adenylyl cyclase ACY-1(P260S) under the pdfr-1 promoter in the pdf-1;pdf-2 background; initial responses were unaffected, suggesting PDF signaling is permissive rather than instructive for habituation.

> "Stimulating cAMP synthesis compensated for loss of the PDF ligands, as response latency habituation of the pdf-1;pdf-2 double mutant expressing consti- tutively active ACY-1 was not signi ﬁcantly different from control"
