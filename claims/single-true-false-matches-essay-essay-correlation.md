## Evidence

### Arthur I. Gates 1920

Arthur I. Gates, Teachers College, Columbia University. (1920). The True-False Test as a Measure of Achievement in College Courses. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=true-false+test+as+a+measure+of+achievement+in+college+courses+gates

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis in Tables IV-VI with printed correlations: true-false with essay r = .33 (N = 33), true-false with written work r = .25 (N = 14), essay with written work r = .30 (N = 5). The article concludes a true-false test yields as high a correlation with an essay as another essay would.

> "A single True-False test gives a correlation with an essay examination which is low, but as high as the average inter-correlation of essay examinations. The correlation of written work with the essay is almost as high, while the correlation of true-false with written work is slightly less. The a (tr-obt. av.) indicates that real differ-ences between these coefficients are improbable."
