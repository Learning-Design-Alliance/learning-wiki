## Evidence

### Singh 2023

Singh, C., Maries, A., Heller, K., & Heller, P. (2023). Instructional Strategies that Foster Effective Problem-Solving. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1063/9780735425477_017](https://doi.org/10.1063/9780735425477_017)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Heller and Hollabaugh's finding that students "using explicit strategies in mixed ability collaborative groups" benefit more than when working alone, within a prescribed problem-solving framework with visualization and physics-description steps. No effect size is printed.

> "Heller and Hollabaugh found that when students work on problem-solving using explicit strategies in mixed ability collaborative groups, they benefit more than when working alone."

### Singh 2023 (2)

Singh, C., Maries, A., Heller, K., & Heller, P. (2023). Instructional Strategies that Foster Effective Problem-Solving. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1063/9780735425477_017](https://doi.org/10.1063/9780735425477_017)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports that group size three with roles making metacognitive practices explicit was chosen through "qualitative video research recording various size groups"; it also recommends changing group members about every 3 weeks to encourage individual learning and discourage free-riding.

> "They arrived at three as the optimal number of group members by conducting qualitative video research recording various size groups interacting while solving problems"
