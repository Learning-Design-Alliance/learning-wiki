# Students were least confident in critically appraising studies even though the bulk of the module focused on critical appraisal

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Postgraduate students reported lowest self-perceived confidence in appraising strengths and weaknesses of study designs, determining validity and interpreting results, despite critical appraisal dominating the module. [→ Rohwer Anke 2013](#rohwer-anke-2013)

## Evidence

### Rohwer Anke 2013

Rohwer Anke, Young Taryn, van Schalkwyk Susan. (2013). Effective or just practical? An evaluation of an online postgraduate module on evidence-based medicine (EBM). BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-77

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Anonymous electronic questionnaire after module completion, answered by 24 (56%) of 43 registered students, using an EPIC-adapted 0-100% confidence scale. Students were "least confident in their ability to critically appraise" and also wanted more information on statistical concepts.

> "Students' self-perceived confidence in EBM knowledge and skills showed that they were least confident in their ability to critically appraise a study on effects of interventions, even though the bulk of the module focused on this aspect of EBM."
