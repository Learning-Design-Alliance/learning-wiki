## Evidence

### Diego Ramírez 2023

Diego Ramírez, Eugenio J. Guzmán-Lavín, Javier Pulgar, Cristian Candia. (2023). Affinity-Based Groups in Secondary Education: Increased Stability at the Expense of Collaboration. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2309.15212](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2309.15212)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Regression models (9-11) predicting the number of original teammates (0 to 3) a student continued with, using the full sample of 90 students. The treatment coefficient ranged from 1.22 to 1.69 across models, all significant at p < 0.001.

> "The models in Table 4 reveal that the treatment is a strong positive predictor (p < 0.001) in all three models. Grade level also shows a positive and significant impact (p < 0.01)."

### Diego Ramírez 2023 (2)

Diego Ramírez, Eugenio J. Guzmán-Lavín, Javier Pulgar, Cristian Candia. (2023). Affinity-Based Groups in Secondary Education: Increased Stability at the Expense of Collaboration. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2309.15212](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2309.15212)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive flow analysis of group transitions shown in Figure 3: of 90 students, 38 joined completely new groups (84% from control classrooms) and 52 kept at least one original member (76.9% from experimental classrooms).

> "Out of 90 students, 38 ended up in new groups without any of their original members; 32 of these (84%) were from control (randomly assigned groups) classrooms C1 and C2. On the other hand, 52 students remained with at least one original group member in the second semester, with 40 of these (76.9%) being from the experimental condition (affinity-based groups)."
