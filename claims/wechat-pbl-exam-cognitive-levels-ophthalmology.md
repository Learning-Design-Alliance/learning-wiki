## Evidence

### Fang Fang 2026

Fang Fang, Bu Bing, Jiang Wenmin. (2026). Combining Problem-Based Learning Methods With the WeChat Platform in Teaching Ophthalmology: Randomized Controlled Trial. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/65279

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Blinded scoring of an identical closed-book examination administered 1 week after course completion in an RCT with 54 students per group. The memorization section was 50% of the total score; the WeChat group mean was 32.0 (SD 4.1) versus 37.6 (SD 2.8), a statistically significant difference ("t106=-8.426; P=.006").

> "students in the WeChat-based PBL group scored marginally lower in memorization compared to their peers in the traditional teaching group (traditional vs WeChat groups: 37.6, SD 2.8 vs 32.0, SD 4.1; t106=-8.426; P=.006; n=54)"

### Fang Fang 2026 (2)

Fang Fang, Bu Bing, Jiang Wenmin. (2026). Combining Problem-Based Learning Methods With the WeChat Platform in Teaching Ophthalmology: Randomized Controlled Trial. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/65279

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same blinded-scored examination in the RCT: the WeChat-based PBL group scored higher on understanding (28.0, SD 1.3 vs 24.1, SD 1.8; "t106=12.753; P=.007") and on knowledge application (27.6, SD 1.3 vs 24.3, SD 1.9; t106=10.899; P=.008). No standardized effect size is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "they exhibited markedly superior levels of understanding (traditional vs WeChat groups: 24.1, SD 1.8 vs 28.0, SD 1.3; t106=12.753; P=.007; n=54) and knowledge application (traditional vs WeChat groups: 24.3, SD 1.9 vs 27.6, SD 1.3; t106=10.899; P=.008; n=54)"
