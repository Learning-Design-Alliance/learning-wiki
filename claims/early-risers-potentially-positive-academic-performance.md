# Early Risers has potentially positive effects on academic performance, driven by a significant Academic Achievement Composite effect in one of two studies

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2`–`r3` · `q3` · `i0`–`i1`

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` August et al. (2002) found a positive, statistically significant effect on the Academic Achievement Composite (effect size 0.30) but not on the Concentration Problems Composite. [→ Early Risers 2012](#early-risers-2012)
`q3 i0` August et al. (2003) found no statistically significant difference on the Academic Achievement Composite (effect size 0.07). [→ Early Risers 2012 (2)](#early-risers-2012-2)

## Evidence

### Early Risers 2012

Early Risers. (2012). WWC Intervention Report: Children Classified as Having an Emotional Disturbance. What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. http://www.med.umn.edu/psychiatry/research/earlyrisers/home.html

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

In the 20-school randomized prevention trial analyzed after three years (199 students), the Academic Achievement Composite showed a statistically significant positive effect with effect size 0.30 and p-value 0.02, while the Concentration Problems Composite effect size was -0.12 (p = 0.19).

> "August et al. (2002) found, and the WWC confirmed, a positive and statistically significant difference between the intervention and comparison groups on the Academic Achievement Composite and no statistically significant difference between the intervention and comparison groups on the Concentration Problems Composite."

### Early Risers 2012 (2)

Early Risers. (2012). WWC Intervention Report: Children Classified as Having an Emotional Disturbance. What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. http://www.med.umn.edu/psychiatry/research/earlyrisers/home.html

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r3`

In the urban randomized effectiveness study (190 students analyzed after two years), the WWC confirmed no statistically significant difference on the Academic Achievement Composite; the effect size of 0.07 was not substantively important according to WWC criteria.

> "August et al. (2003) found, and the WWC confirmed, no statistically significant difference between the intervention and comparison group on the Academic Achievement Composite."
