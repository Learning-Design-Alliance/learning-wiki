Evidence¶

Steinbach 2022¶

Steinbach, S. (2022). Looking beyond vision: Supports for students who are blind or visually impaired in mathematics. NWEA. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/looking-beyond-vision-supports-for-students-who-are-blind-or-visually-impaired-in-mathematics/

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing Bell & Silverman (2019), Cryer et al. (2013) and Giesen et al. (2012), that BVI students are "falling behind in mathematics classes" and that the gap with non-disabled peers progressively widens with age. No effect sizes are printed.

"Students who are blind or visually impaired (BVIs) are falling behind in mathematics classes (Bell & Silverman, 2019; Cryer et al., 2013), and as they get older, the gap between them and their non-disabled peers progressively widens (Giesen et al., 2012)."

Steinbach 2022 (2)¶

Steinbach, S. (2022). Looking beyond vision: Supports for students who are blind or visually impaired in mathematics. NWEA. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/looking-beyond-vision-supports-for-students-who-are-blind-or-visually-impaired-in-mathematics/

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review attributes to Gulley et al. (2017) the statistics that "75% of BVI students are at least one grade behind" and 20% are four or more grades behind. These are descriptive percentages, not effect sizes.

"With regard to achievement, 75% of BVI students are at least one grade behind and 20% are four or more grades behind (Gulley et al., 2017)."
