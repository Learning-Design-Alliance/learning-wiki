Slomowitz R. F. 2025¶

Slomowitz R. F., Narayan A. J., & McGrath L. M. (2025). Resilience research in learning disabilities: Guiding principles from developmental psychopathology. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102090

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

Narrative review section of this theoretical paper: it lists reading resilience factors across cognitive, emotion/motivation, social/environmental, and neural levels of analysis, and math resilience factors including promotive (RAN, nonverbal reasoning, counting, spatial relations; SES) and protective (number concepts, oral language, nonverbal reasoning; home numeracy environment) candidates, attributed to cited primary studies the paper does not reanalyze.

"This resilience work is just emerging in the learning disabilities literature and so no clear consensus yet exists. As such we offer these findings for hypothesis generation."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Empirical evidence for protective factors in word-reading resilience is sparse and based on only a few studies — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Evidence for promotive and skill-enhancing factors in literacy resilience is underrecognized while the same evidence is overinterpreted towards protective effects — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Resilience should be conceptualized as a process rather than an outcome of development, with environmental factors playing an essential role alongside individual risk and resilience factors (Parrila's discussion, as reported by the editors) — related
  • In an at-risk preschool sample, 6% were identified as resilient, with maternal education a key protective factor and nonverbal intelligence, but not executive function, predicting resilience status (reported by the editors, citing Muñez et al.) — related
  • Semantic abilities act as the key protective factor against the negative effect of reading fluency impairments on reading comprehension, especially in low SES contexts (reported by the special issue editors, citing Lefèvre et al.) — related
  • Trait resilience had a promotive role in academic performance for immigrant and non-immigrant students alike, and an additional protective effect mitigating the negative effect of school discrimination specifically among first- and second-generation immigrant students (reported by the editors, citing Huang et al.) — related
  • Explicit, instructional home numeracy activities acted as protective (not promotive) factors for 1st–2nd grade math performance in Torppa (2025) — related
