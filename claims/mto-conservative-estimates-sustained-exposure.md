# The Moving to Opportunity experiment provides conservative estimates of neighborhood effects because it cannot capture the total effect of sustained exposure

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q1` argument or single case

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Because treatment and control groups were both exposed to high-poverty neighborhoods before the intervention, MTO estimates fail to capture the total effect of sustained exposure if effects are cumulative or lagged. [→ Wodtke 2011](#wodtke-2011)

## Evidence

### Wodtke 2011

Wodtke, Geoffrey T.; Harding, David J.; Elwert, Felix. (2011). Neighborhood Effects in Temporal Perspective. American Sociological Review. https://doi.org/10.1177/0003122411420816

`q1 · i?`

Authors' methodological critique of the MTO experiment: voucher families often moved to only slightly less poor segregated neighborhoods, results confound neighborhood poverty with residential mobility, and both groups shared pre-intervention exposure to high-poverty neighborhoods.

> "If the impact of neighborhood poverty on child development is cumulative or lagged, then estimates from MTO fail to capture the total effect of sustained exposure to disadvantaged neighborhoods."
