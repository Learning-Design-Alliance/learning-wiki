## Evidence

### Smith-Woolley 2018

Smith-Woolley, E., Ayorech, Z., Dale, P. S., von Stumm, S. & Plomin, R. (2018). The genetics of university success. Scientific Reports, 8, 14579. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-32621-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

ACE model fitting on the TEDS twin sample showed the shared-environment component fell across the transition to university: heritability decreased from 57% to 46% and shared environment was "no longer significant for university achievement". No effect size was printed for the non-significant component.

> "The heritability of achievement decreased from entrance exam achievement (57%) to university achievement (46%), as did the influence of the shared environment, which was no longer significant for university achievement."

### Smith-Woolley 2018 (2)

Smith-Woolley, E., Ayorech, Z., Dale, P. S., von Stumm, S. & Plomin, R. (2018). The genetics of university success. Scientific Reports, 8, 14579. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-32621-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

A liability threshold model decomposed the liability of going to university or not into A, C and E for the TEDS twin sample; the article reports "University enrolment showed the most shared environmental influence (36%)". This variance component is not a standardized effect size.

> "University enrolment showed the most shared environmental influence (36%)."
