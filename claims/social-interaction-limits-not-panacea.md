### Gherghel 2023

Gherghel, C., Yasuda, S., & Kita, Y. (2023). Interaction during online classes fosters engagement with learning and self-directed study both in the first and second years of the COVID-19 pandemic. Computers & Education, 200, 104795. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2023.104795

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Authors' discussion-section caution, citing prior work (Mehall, 2021; Kreijns et al., 2003; Chamorro-Premuzic et al., 2005; Hancock, 2004) rather than testing these boundaries in the present data. The article states satisfaction and engagement are fostered by the quality of interactions and that some students may find interactive courses emotionally draining.

> "Previous research suggests that social interaction during online classes leads to more satisfaction and better learning, after which it brings no additional benefits (Mehall, 2021). Therefore, “the more the better” is not always the case."
