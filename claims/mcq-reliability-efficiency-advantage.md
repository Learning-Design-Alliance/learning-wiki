## Evidence

### Hift Richard J 2014

Hift Richard J. (2014). Should essays and other “open-ended”-type questions retain a place in written summative assessment in clinical medicine? BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0249-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Lukhele et al.'s analysis of national university-entrance examinations containing both formats: 4–8 MCQ items equal one essay, essay efficiency under 10% of an MCQ item per minute, and a 75-minute 16-item MCQ as reliable as a three-hour open-ended assessment.

> "They found that 4-8 multiple-choice items provided the same amount of information as a single essay, and that the essay’s efficiency in providing information about the candidate’s ability per minute of testing was less than 10% of that of an average multiple-choice item."

### Hift Richard J 2014 (2)

Hift Richard J. (2014). Should essays and other “open-ended”-type questions retain a place in written summative assessment in clinical medicine? BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0249-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports marking-cost comparisons: open-ended items cost 300 times more to mark than multiple-choice items in one study, with scoring cost possibly exceeding a factor of 1000 for large examinations.

> "In one study, the cost of marking the open-ended items was 300 times that of the multiple-choice items [49]; the relative cost of scoring the papers may exceed a factor of 1000 for a large examination"
