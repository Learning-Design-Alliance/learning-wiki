# In knowledge-construction content analysis data from discussion groups, the largest share of variance lies at the message level, with only small between-group and between-student variance.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2007 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the three-level null model, 94.53% of total variance in knowledge construction is between messages, 2.84% between students within groups, and 2.63% between groups. [→ De Wever 2007](#de-wever-2007)
`q3 i?` In the four-level null model, 91% of variance arises from differences between messages, 7% at theme level, and 2% at group level, with no variance at the student level. [→ De Wever 2007 (2)](#de-wever-2007-2)

## Evidence

### De Wever 2007

De Wever, B., Van Keer, H., Schellens, T., & Valcke, M. (2007). Applying multilevel modelling to content analysis data: Methodological issues in the study of role assignment in asynchronous discussion groups. Learning and Instruction, 17, 436-447. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2007.04.001

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Random intercept null model from the three-level analysis (messages within students within groups) decomposing total variance; the group-level variance component was not significantly different from zero (χ² = 3.415, df = 1, p = .065).

> "The null model shows that respectively 2.63%, 2.84% , and 94.53% of the total variance in students' level of knowledge construction is lin ked to differences between groups, between students within groups, and between students' messages."

### De Wever 2007 (2)

De Wever, B., Van Keer, H., Schellens, T., & Valcke, M. (2007). Applying multilevel modelling to content analysis data: Methodological issues in the study of role assignment in asynchronous discussion groups. Learning and Instruction, 17, 436-447. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2007.04.001

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Four-level random intercepts null model (messages within themes within students within groups) for the analysis of all four discussion themes; the article reports no part of the total variance assigned to the individual student level.

> "91% of the variance arises from differences between messages within measurement occasions (χ² = 1440.268, df = 1, p < .001)."
