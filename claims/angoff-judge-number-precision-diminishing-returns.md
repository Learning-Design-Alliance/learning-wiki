## Evidence

### Shulruf Boaz 2016

Shulruf Boaz, Wilkinson Tim, Weller Jennifer, Jones Philip, Poole Phillippa. (2016). Insights into the Angoff method: results from a simulation study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0656-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Numerical simulation results shown in Fig. 2 across 49 judge-item combinations indicate precision gains from adding judges depend on test length, with diminishing returns beyond roughly 15–30 judges depending on item count.

> "For tests with 10 or fewer items, increasing the number of judges significantly improves the precision, although there is not more to be gained when the number of judges exceeds 30. For larger tests, increasing the number of judges beyond 15–20 has little effect on improving precision."

### Shulruf Boaz 2016 (2)

Shulruf Boaz, Wilkinson Tim, Weller Jennifer, Jones Philip, Poole Phillippa. (2016). Insights into the Angoff method: results from a simulation study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0656-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Simulation results across all 49 combinations (Fig. 1) show narrower confidence intervals for panels with more judges regardless of item count; no effect size is printed for this pattern.

> "However, increasing the number of judges was associated with narrower confidence intervals, irrespective of the number of items."
