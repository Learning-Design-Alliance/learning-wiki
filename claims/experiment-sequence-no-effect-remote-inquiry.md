# The order of video and virtual experiments (video-virtual vs. virtual-video) does not affect conceptual understanding in remote inquiry learning

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There were no differences between the Video-Virtual and Virtual-Video sequences for conceptual understanding, in either the post-test or the follow-up-test analyses. [→ Flegr 2023](#flegr-2023)

## Evidence

### Flegr 2023

Flegr, S., Kuhn, J., & Scheiter, K. (2023). How to foster STEM learning during Covid-19 remote schooling: Combining virtual and video experiments. Learning and Instruction, 86, 101778. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2023.101778

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Exploratory analyses (RQ1) in the randomized experiment found no sequence differences; the condition × time interaction in the pre-post mixed ANOVA was non-significant (F(2, 151) = 1.50; p = .23). The authors note this "corroborates the findings of the review of Wörner, Kuhn, and Scheiter (2022)".

> "there were no differences between the two learning sequences Video-Virtual and Virtual-Video for students' conceptual understanding, neither in the exploratory analyses of the post-test nor in the follow-up-test."
