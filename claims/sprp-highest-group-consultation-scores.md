# Scripted peer role-play students achieve the highest group-based history-taking assessment scores, ahead of non-scripted peer role-play and teacher standardized patients

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a randomized three-group study, the spRP group scored highest, followed by non-spRP and TSP, on total group-based consultation assessment (p < 0.05). [→ Wei Yingying 2025](#wei-yingying-2025)
`q3 i?` The spRP group scored significantly better than the non-spRP group on the overall group-based assessment of interrogation skills. [→ Wei Yingying 2025 (2)](#wei-yingying-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Wei Yingying 2025

Wei Yingying, Deng Na, Zheng Hongmei, Guo Haijun, Zhang Huiling, Li Bing, Wu Daiqin, Li Wei, Si Xiaoyun. (2025). Comparison of teacher-standardized patients and peer role-play in assessing medical students' history-taking skills. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07758-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized comparison of 630 third-year medical students assigned to TSP, spRP, or non-spRP conditions; one-way ANOVA with LSD-corrected post hoc tests showed the total score ordering "spRP group, non-spRP group, and TSP group having the highest to the lowest scores" (Table 4 total F = 8.116, p = 0.001).

> "The difference in the total score rate of group assessment was statistically significant (p < 0.05), with the spRP group, non-spRP group, and TSP group having the highest to the lowest scores in the given order."

### Wei Yingying 2025 (2)

Wei Yingying, Deng Na, Zheng Hongmei, Guo Haijun, Zhang Huiling, Li Bing, Wu Daiqin, Li Wei, Si Xiaoyun. (2025). Comparison of teacher-standardized patients and peer role-play in assessing medical students' history-taking skills. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07758-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same randomized study, the interrogation skills subscore differed significantly across groups (Table 4: F = 7.289, p = 0.001), with the spRP group scoring significantly better than the non-spRP group and the TSP group lowest.

> "Furthermore, the spRP group scored significantly better than the non-spRP group on the overall group-based assessment of interrogation skills."
