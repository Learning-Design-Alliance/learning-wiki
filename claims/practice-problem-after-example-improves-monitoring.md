## Evidence

### Tamara van Gog & Vincent Hoogerheide & Milou van Harsel 2020

Tamara van Gog & Vincent Hoogerheide & Milou van Harsel. (2020). The Role of Mental Effort in Fostering Self-Regulated Learning with Problem-Solving Tasks. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09544-y

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports the Baars et al. (2014a) primary-education experiment: an isomorphic practice problem after worked example study "reduced their overestimations of their performance on a future test" but did not improve which examples students chose to restudy. No effect size is printed.

> "providing students with an isomorphic practice problem after they had studied a worked example on how to solve a water jug problem improved their prospective monitoring accuracy (i.e., it reduced their overesti- mations of their performance on a future test). However, it did not improve their regulation accuracy"

### Tamara van Gog & Vincent Hoogerheide & Milou van Harsel 2020 (2)

Tamara van Gog & Vincent Hoogerheide & Milou van Harsel. (2020). The Role of Mental Effort in Fostering Self-Regulated Learning with Problem-Solving Tasks. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09544-y

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports the Baars et al. (2017) secondary-education study on heredity problems, in which receiving a practice problem after example study improved both monitoring judgments and restudy choices. No effect size is printed.

> "Adolescents who studied examples on how to solve heredity problems in biology and then received a practice problem to solve made more accurate monitoring judgments and were more accurate at indicating which examples they should restudy."
