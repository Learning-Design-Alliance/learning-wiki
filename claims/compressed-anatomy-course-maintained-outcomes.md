## Evidence

### Halliday N 2015

Halliday N, O'Donoghue D, Klump KE, Thompson B. (2015). Human Structure in Six and One-Half Weeks: One Approach to Providing Foundational Anatomical Competency in an Era of Compressed Medical School Anatomy curricula. Anatomical Sciences Education 8(2):149-157. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.1476

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Cohort comparison of two cohorts before (2008-2010) and two after (2010-2012) implementation of Human Structure, using overall course numeric grades. The article reports "similar results, with students earning an average of 88.0 in Gross Anatomy and 88.5 in Human Structure."

> "Analysis of final course grades two years before and two years after the implementation of Human Structure showed similar results, with students earning an average of 88.0 in Gross Anatomy and 88.5 in Human Structure."

### Halliday N 2015 (2)

Halliday N, O'Donoghue D, Klump KE, Thompson B. (2015). Human Structure in Six and One-Half Weeks: One Approach to Providing Foundational Anatomical Competency in an Era of Compressed Medical School Anatomy curricula. Anatomical Sciences Education 8(2):149-157. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.1476

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Cohort comparison of anatomy sub-scores from USMLE Step 1, calculated as standard deviations from the national mean, across two cohorts before and two after implementation. The article reports an increase of "almost half a standard deviation (SD ±0.50)" and notes no significant differences in matriculant quality (MCAT).

> "after implementation of Human Structure, USMLE Step 1 anatomy sub- scores increased by almost half a standard deviation (SD ±0.50) from before implementation to after."
