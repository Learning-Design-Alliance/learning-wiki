# Having a parent or sibling in medicine predicts research authorship among UK medical students but not overall extracurricular engagement

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A familial link to medicine was associated with higher research authorship (OR 1.38, CI 1.12 to 1.69, p=0.002), while overall extracurricular engagement was not significantly higher (OR 1.10, CI 0.97 to 1.25, p=0.12). [→ Ferreira Tomas 2025](#ferreira-tomas-2025)
`q3 i?` Excluding students with intercalated or pre-medicine degrees strengthened the family effect on coauthoring publications (OR 1.41, CI 1.10 to 1.80, p=0.006). [→ Ferreira Tomas 2025](#ferreira-tomas-2025)

## Evidence

### Ferreira Tomas 2025

Ferreira Tomas, Collins Alexander M, Handscomb Arthur, French Benjamin, Bolton Emily, Fortescue Amelia, Plumb Ella, Feng Oliver, The FAST Collaborative. (2025). Socioeconomic and demographic predictors of extracurricular achievements among UK medical students (FAST study). BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2025-103062

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Adjusted multivariable logistic regression in the FAST cross-sectional survey of 8,395 students. The article reports authorship OR 1.38 (p=0.002) but overall engagement "not significantly higher" (OR 1.10, p=0.12); a sensitivity analysis excluding intercalated/previous-degree students gave authorship OR 1.41 (p=0.006). No standardised effect size is printed.

> "This familial factor was associated with higher participation and attainment research authorship (OR 1.38, CI 1.12 to 1.69, p=0.002) but overall levels of extracurricular engagement were not significantly higher among such students (OR 1.10, CI 0.97 to 1.25, p=0.12)"
