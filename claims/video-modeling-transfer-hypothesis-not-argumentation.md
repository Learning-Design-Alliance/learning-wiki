# Video modeling examples improve hypothesis quality but not argumentation quality in a transfer task

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r3` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` VMP and VM participants produced higher-quality hypotheses than controls in the transfer task (d = 0.58). [→ Omarchevska 2022](#omarchevska-2022)
`q3 i?` The three groups did not differ significantly in argumentation quality in the transfer task. [→ Omarchevska 2022 (2)](#omarchevska-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Omarchevska 2022

Omarchevska, Y., Lachner, A., Richter, J., & Scheiter, K. (2022). Do Video Modeling and Metacognitive Prompts Improve Self-Regulated Scientific Inquiry? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09652-3

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r3`

Contrast analysis on the transfer task (Energy Conversion in a System simulation), all conditions working on the same task. The contrast favoring the video modeling conditions was significant: "t(124) = 3.06, p = 0.003, d = 0.58".

> "Contrast 1 indicated that VMP and VM had higher hypothesis quality than the control condition, t(124) = 3.06, p = 0.003, d = 0.58"

### Omarchevska 2022 (2)

Omarchevska, Y., Lachner, A., Richter, J., & Scheiter, K. (2022). Do Video Modeling and Metacognitive Prompts Improve Self-Regulated Scientific Inquiry? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09652-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Same transfer-task contrast analysis on argumentation quality: "The three groups did not differ in argumentation quality"; no contrast was significant. Equivalence was not tested; the article calls this partial support for H3a.

> "The three groups did not differ in argumentation quality (Contrast 1, t(124) = 0.09, p = 0.93, d = 0.02; Contrast 2, t(124) = 1.09, p = 0.28, d = 0.22)."
