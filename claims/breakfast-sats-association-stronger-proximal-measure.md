# The association between breakfast consumption and educational performance is stronger when breakfast is measured more proximal to the SATs tests (OR=2·02 at 6 months vs OR=1·61 at 18 months)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the nested cohort, the breakfast–SATs association was stronger for the follow-up measure taken 4–6 months before SATs (OR=2·02) than the baseline measure taken 16–18 months before (OR=1·61). [→ Littlecott Hannah J 2015](#littlecott-hannah-j-2015)

## Evidence

### Littlecott Hannah J 2015

Littlecott Hannah J, Moore Graham F, Moore Laurence, Lyons Ronan A, Murphy Simon. (2015). Association between breakfast consumption and educational outcomes in 9–11-year-old children. Public Health Nutrition. https://doi.org/10.1017/S1368980015002669

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Nested cohort analysis (n 1216) comparing baseline and follow-up dietary measures against SATs. For other dietary measures, associations were similar whether proximal or distal; all measures except unhealthy breakfast items were associated with performance.

> "Table 3 shows that among the nested cohort of children who provided measures at baseline and follow-up, the association of breakfast consumption with educational performance was stronger where the measure of breakfast consumption was more proximal to the collection of educational outcomes data (OR=2·02 for follow-up compared with OR=1·61 for baseline)."
