Evidence¶

Donoghue & Hattie 2021¶

Donoghue, G. M., & Hattie, J. A. C. (2021). A Meta-Analysis of Ten Learning Techniques. Frontiers in Education, 6, 581216. doi:10.3389/feduc.2021.581216

q3 · meta-analysis (242 studies, 1,619 effects) · i2 · medium effect, overall d = 0.56; 0.85 distributed practice, 0.74 practice testing · n=242 studies · quant-synthesis · r?

The authors meta-analysed the studies cited in Dunlosky et al.'s (2013) review of ten learning techniques: 242 studies, 1,619 effects and 169,179 participants. The ranking matched Dunlosky's high, moderate and low ratings: distributed practice (d = 0.85) and practice testing (0.74) highest; elaborative interrogation, imagery and self-explanation in the middle (0.54–0.56); mnemonics, re-reading, interleaved practice, underlining and summarization lowest (0.44–0.50). Heterogeneity was high (I² around 85%), so moderators matter more than the averages.

"The most effective techniques are Distributed Practice and Practice Testing and the least effective (but still with relatively high effects) are Underlining and Summarization."

Discussion¶

Even the lowest techniques average d = 0.44, but the outcomes were mostly surface, factual and near-transfer (see the related claims), and the low-rated interleaving effect here pools very different tasks: the wiki's interleaving principle records the conditions under which it helps or hurts.
