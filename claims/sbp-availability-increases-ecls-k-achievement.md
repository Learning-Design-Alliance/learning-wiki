## Evidence

### Frisvold David E 2015

Frisvold David E. (2015). Nutrition and Cognitive Achievement: An Evaluation of the School Breakfast Program. Journal of Public Economics. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2014.12.003

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Difference-in-differences first-stage estimate in the ECLS-K 5th-grade sample (3,040 students), using school-administrator reports of SBP availability. The study reports schools above the threshold are "33 percentage points (with a standard error of 0.141) more likely to offer breakfast through the SBP".

> "Schools where the percent of FRP students exceeds the state threshold, so that the state mandate binds, are 33 percentage points (with a standard error of 0.141) more likely to offer breakfast through the SBP than schools without a binding mandate"

### Frisvold David E 2015 (2)

Frisvold David E. (2015). Nutrition and Cognitive Achievement: An Evaluation of the School Breakfast Program. Journal of Public Economics. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2014.12.003

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Scaled ECLS-K difference-in-differences estimates dividing mandate effects by the first stage (0.329). The study reports "the availability of the SBP increases math achievement by 29.2 percent of a standard deviation" and notes the estimates are not precisely estimated, possibly due to the smaller sample.

> "After dividing the achievement estimates by 0.329, the availability of the SBP increases math achievement by 29.2 percent of a standard deviation, reading achievement by 36.2 percent of a standard deviation, and science achievement by 48.3 percent of a standard deviation"
