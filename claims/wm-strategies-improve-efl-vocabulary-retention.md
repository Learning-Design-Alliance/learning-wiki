## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After a 10-week intervention with working memory strategies, the experimental group (M = 3.46, SD = 0.99) scored significantly higher than the control group (M = 2.5, SD = 1.336) on the post-test, t(48) = -2.9, p = 0.005. [→ Ibarra Santacruz 2018](#ibarra-santacruz-2018)

## Evidence

### Ibarra Santacruz 2018

Ibarra Santacruz, D., & Martínez Ortega, D. (2018). Can working memory strategies enhance English vocabulary learning? HOW, 25(2), 29-47. https://doi.org/10.19183/how.25.2.410

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental study of two intact undergraduate EFL groups (28 experimental, 22 control) at a Colombian university; performance coded on a 4-point scale and compared with independent-samples t-tests. The article reports the experimental group "outperformed the control group" with t(48) = -2.9, p = 0.005.

> "The results of an independent-samples t-test (Table 4) demonstrated that the experimental group ( M = 3.46, SD = 0.99) outperformed the control group ( M = 2.5, SD = 1.336), t(48) = -2.9, p = 0.005 in the post-test."
