## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When prompts were distributed among four AIs, 11 of 20 MCQs (55%) were acceptable for both textbook-fragment and learning-objectives input. [→ Krushynska 2026](#krushynska-2026)

## Evidence

### Krushynska 2026

Krushynska, T., Ursin, J., & Heilala, V. (2026). AI-Assisted Model for Generating Multiple-Choice Questions. University of Jyväskylä. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2602.08383](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.08383)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Simulation distributing prompts among four AIs (ChatGPT concept map, MS Copilot questions, all four final MCQs) accepted "11 from 20 - 55%" for each input type (Table 6).

> "Involving multiple AIs gave better results than if a single AI was used (Tab.6)."

### Krushynska 2026 (2)

Krushynska, T., Ursin, J., & Heilala, V. (2026). AI-Assisted Model for Generating Multiple-Choice Questions. University of Jyväskylä. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2602.08383](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.08383)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Comparative simulation across the three prompt steps found step-specific strengths: "ChatGPT compiled the best concept maps, MS Copilot formulated the best real-world questions"; all AIs performed well on the final step.

> "Comparing AIs’ response to prompts 1- 3 showed that ChatGPT compiled the best concept maps, MS Copilot formulated the best real-world questions, while at the final stage, i.e., generating complete MCQ, all AIs performed well."
