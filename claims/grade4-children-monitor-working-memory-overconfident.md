# Grade 4 children can metacognitively monitor their working memory performance, tending to be over-confident as task difficulty increases

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Grade 4 children's confidence in their working memory decisions decreases with task difficulty, but less than accuracy does, indicating over-confidence at higher loads. [→ Freeman 2017](#freeman-2017)

## Evidence

### Freeman 2017

Freeman, Emily E.; Karayanidis, Frini; Chalmers, Kerry A. (2017). Metacognitive monitoring of working memory performance and its relationship to academic achievement in Grade 4 children. Learning and Individual Differences, Vol. 57. http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2017.06.003

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Repeated-measures experiment with 73 Grade 4 children completing the WMPT across five difficulty levels. The discussion reports confidence fell with difficulty but less than accuracy, so children "tend to be over-confident in their ability", especially on difficult tasks.

> "The expected decrease in children’s confidence in their decisions on the WMPT was also found, but this decrease was not as large as the decrease in accuracy, suggesting that while metacognitive monitoring of working memory is related to performance, children tend to be over-confident in their ability."
