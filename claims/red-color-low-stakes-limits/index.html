Evidence¶

Gnambs Timo 2020¶

Gnambs Timo. (2020). Limited evidence for the effect of red color on cognitive performance: A meta-analysis. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-020-01772-1

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

The article's constraints-of-generality discussion: most included studies had no individual consequences for respondents, and it is unclear whether effects would appear in high-stakes testing; the evidence cited is preliminary.

"Preliminary evidence (e.g., Arthur et al., 2016; see also Supplementary Online Material, Section D) indicates that red color effects (if they exist) might be limited to low-stakes, laboratory research and do not generalize to applied settings."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Control color choice, color matching, presentation medium, and presentation timing showed no moderating effects on red color effects
  • No significant difference between red and control color conditions for anagram tests or knowledge tests
  • After correcting for publication bias, no evidential value remains for an effect of red color on intellectual performance
  • Reasoning test performance was significantly lower in the red condition (Δ = -0.34), but the omnibus test across cognitive measures was not significant
  • Red color effects declined over time: initial studies found substantially larger effects than subsequent research — related
  • Optimal task design determined in laboratory studies may depend on learners' level of effort-by-allocation — a broader claim this one bears on
  • The pooled effect of viewing red color on cognitive test performance is small (Δ = -0.13) — related
