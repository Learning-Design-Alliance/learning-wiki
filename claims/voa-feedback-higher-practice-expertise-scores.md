## Evidence

### Fazlollahi AM et al 2022

Fazlollahi AM et al. (2022). Effect of Artificial Intelligence Tutoring vs Expert Instruction on Learning Simulated Surgical Skills Among Medical Students: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2021.49008

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Two-way mixed ANOVA (group × time) on ICEMS Expertise Scores from 350 practice resections by 70 randomized medical students, baseline performance as covariate. The "difference of 0.66 (95% CI, 0.55 to 0.77) points higher" vs instructor and 0.65 vs control were both P < .001; instructor and control did not differ significantly. No standardized effect size is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "Mixed ANOVA demonstrated that within-participant performance changes depended on the type of feedback, with the VOA feedback group achieving a difference of 0.66 (95% CI, 0.55 to 0.77) points higher compared with the instructor group (P < .001) and 0.65 (95% CI, 0.54 to 0.77) points higher compared with the control group (P < .001)"

### Fazlollahi AM et al 2022 (2)

Fazlollahi AM et al. (2022). Effect of Artificial Intelligence Tutoring vs Expert Instruction on Learning Simulated Surgical Skills Among Medical Students: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2021.49008

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Repeated-measures comparisons within the VOA group (n = 23) showed significant improvement over baseline in all four post-feedback trials; the article reports learning curves "plateaued at trials 3 and 4". No standardized effect size is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "Pairwise comparisons demonstrated that learners performed significantly better than baseline after AI tutoring feedback (mean difference vs baseline: trial 1, 0.37 [95% CI, 0.18 to 0.56] points; P < .001; trial 2, 0.51 [95% CI, 0.29 to 0.74] points; P < .001; trial 3, 0.65 [95% CI, 0.41 to 0.89] points; P < .001; trial 4, 0.61 [95% CI, 0.36 to 0.86] points; P < .001)"
