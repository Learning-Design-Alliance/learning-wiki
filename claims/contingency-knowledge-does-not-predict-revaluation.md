## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In mixed-effects models, contingency knowledge did not significantly predict response rates during training or testing, and did not significantly interact with Delay or Val. [→ Omar David Perez & Gonzalo P. Urcelay 2025](#omar-david-perez-gonzalo-p-urcelay-2025)

## Evidence

### Omar David Perez & Gonzalo P. Urcelay 2025

Omar David Perez & Gonzalo P. Urcelay. (2025). Delayed rewards weaken human goal directed actions. npj Science of Learning 10:36. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00325-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory mixed-effects model with Delay, contingency knowledge and their interaction as fixed effects on training response rates: knowledge coefficient −1.86, p = 0.216; Delay interaction 0.76, p = 0.596. A second model on test data likewise found no significant predictions (e.g., knowledge coefficient −0.47, p = 0.598). The authors conclude both lower knowledge and lower rates likely stem from a weakened action-outcome rate correlation.

> "The results indicated that contingency knowledge did not signiﬁcantly predict response rates (coefﬁcient = −1.86,p = 0.216), nor did it signiﬁcantly interact with Delay (coefﬁcient = 0.76,p = 0.596)"
