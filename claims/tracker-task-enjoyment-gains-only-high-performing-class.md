# In a Singapore Grade 9 physics cohort, only the high-performing mathematically-inclined class showed increased enjoyment of physics after the Tracker performance task, while the whole cohort showed practically no change

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The high-performing and mathematically-inclined class registered a strong positive change in enjoyment items (e.g. 'I look forward to physics lessons' pre=4.12, post=4.73) on a 6-point Likert perception survey. [→ Loo Kang Wee 2014](#loo-kang-wee-2014)
`q2 i?` For the whole cohort of 273 students there was practically no change in self-reported perception from pre- to post-survey. [→ Loo Kang Wee 2014](#loo-kang-wee-2014)

## Evidence

### Loo Kang Wee 2014

Loo Kang Wee, Tze Kwang Leong. (2014). Video Analysis and Modeling Performance Task to promote becoming like scientists in classrooms. American Journal of Educational Research. [doi:10.12691/education-3-2-13](https://doi.org/10.12691/education-3-2-13)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre- and post-perception survey of the performance task (Figure 7, N=273 whole cohort, N=30 for the high-performing class) on a 1-6 Likert scale. The class means rose, e.g. "pre=4.12, post=4.73" for looking forward to lessons, while the whole cohort showed "practically no change".

> "only the high-performing and mathematically-inclined class registered a strong positive change (Figure 7) in the affective domains like “I look forward to physics lessons”  pre=4.12, post=4.73"
