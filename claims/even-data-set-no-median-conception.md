## Evidence

### Amiruzzaman 2020

Amiruzzaman, M., & Kosko, K. W. (2020). Exploring students' understanding of median. In M. B. Wood, E. E. Turner, M. Civil, & J. A. Eli (Eds.), Proceedings of the 38th annual meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education. Tucson, AZ: The University of Arizona. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2004.10302](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2004.10302)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Episode 6 of a classroom-based teaching experiment with two focal sixth-grade students. Alice identified medians of odd-numbered data sets, but for an even-numbered data set "she claimed that there was no median", leaving the two middle numbers with no additional work.

> "However, when Alice was asked to find the median for data with an even number of elements, she claimed that there was no median."

### Amiruzzaman 2020 (2)

Amiruzzaman, M., & Kosko, K. W. (2020). Exploring students' understanding of median. In M. B. Wood, E. E. Turner, M. Civil, & J. A. Eli (Eds.), Proceedings of the 38th annual meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education. Tucson, AZ: The University of Arizona. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2004.10302](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2004.10302)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Same episode and tasks. Bob's work was nearly identical to Alice's: "when we have 10 numbers then we do not have a middle number…no median", while he identified a middle number for sets with 7 numbers.

> "Bob explained his position and said, "Median lives in the middle of all numbers…when there is 7 numbers then we have a middle number, but when we have 10 numbers then we do not have a middle number…no median"."
