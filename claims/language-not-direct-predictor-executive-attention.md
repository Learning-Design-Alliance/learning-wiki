## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the hybrid model, language-formatted arithmetic outcomes were not significant indicators of language, and the language–executive attention correlation was large (r=.80). [→ Rhodes Katherine T. 2017](#rhodes-katherine-t-2017)
`q2 i?` The authors interpret that language may play a facilitative role in reasoning during numeric processing via its strong relationship with executive attention. [→ Rhodes Katherine T. 2017](#rhodes-katherine-t-2017)

## Evidence

### Rhodes Katherine T. 2017

Rhodes Katherine T., Branum-Martin Lee, Washington Julie A., Fuchs Lynn S. (2017). Measuring arithmetic: A psychometric approach to understanding formatting effects and domain specificity. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000189

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Post-hoc hybrid full measurement model in the N=1314 confirmatory factor analysis: language loadings on language measures ranged .71 to .74, but no language-formatted arithmetic outcome loaded significantly on language; language correlated with executive attention at r=.80.

> "none of the language-formatted arithmetic outcomes were significant indicators of language, meaning that the auditory verbal factor is distinct from language"
