# Groups spent far more time getting to know each other than on task-related communication during the initial collaboration phase

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` On average, groups allocated a much larger share of initial-phase time to getting to know each other than to task-related communication, d = −1.98. [→ Natalia Reich-Stiebert 2023](#natalia-reich-stiebert-2023)

## Evidence

### Natalia Reich-Stiebert 2023

Natalia Reich-Stiebert, Jan-Bennet Voltmer, Jennifer Raimann, Stefan Stürmer. (2023). The role of first-language heterogeneity in the acquisition of online interaction self-efficacy in CSCL. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn 18:513–530. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-023-09411-2

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r3`

Paired comparison of group-level time-percentage reports collected after two weeks of collaboration; means show 50.45% on getting to know each other versus 9.07% on task-related communication, with a "significantly" larger allocation to getting to know each other, d = −1.98.

> "Groups spent significantly more time getting to know each other than on task-related communication, M get = 50.45 (SDget = 17.37), Mtask = 9.07 (SDtask = 7.35), t(1524) = −77.39, p < .001, d = −1.98, indicating that, overall, during the first phase of their collaboration, students focused more on getting to know each other than on task-related communication."
