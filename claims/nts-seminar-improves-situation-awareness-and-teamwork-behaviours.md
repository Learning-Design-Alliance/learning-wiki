# A single 90-minute NTS seminar improves medical students' situation awareness and team working behaviours in simulated emergencies, but not task management

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Situation awareness improved significantly from simulation I to II only in the NTS group (r=0.50). [→ Hagemann Vera 2017](#hagemann-vera-2017)
`q3 i3` Team working improved significantly from pre to post only in the NTS group (r=0.45), with a significant post-test difference between groups. [→ Hagemann Vera 2017](#hagemann-vera-2017)
`q3 i?` No significant effects were found for task management. [→ Hagemann Vera 2017](#hagemann-vera-2017)

## Evidence

### Hagemann Vera 2017

Hagemann Vera, Herbstreit Frank, Kehren Clemens, Chittamadathil Jilson, Wolfertz Sandra, Dirkmann Daniel, Kluge Annette, Peters Jürgen. (2017). Does teaching non-technical skills to medical students improve those skills and simulated patient outcome? International Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.58c1.9f0d

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVA and dependent-samples t-tests in a randomised, double-blind pre-test-post-test study of 77 fourth-year medical students, using ANTS-rated behaviour across two simulated scenarios. The results show "Situation awareness improved only in the NTS group (t(42) = -3.74, p<.01, r=0.50)" and team working improved significantly with η2p=0.13.

> "Situation awareness improved only in the NTS group (t(42) = -3.74, p<.01, r=0.50). Team working improved significantly from pre- to post-test (F(1,75) = 11.19, p<.01, η2p=0.13)."
