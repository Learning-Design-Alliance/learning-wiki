# MA is a significant regression predictor of mathematics performance for girls but not for boys

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In girls, MA significantly predicted performance (Beta = −.315, p <0.0001) while TA did not. [→ Devine A 2012](#devine-a-2012)
`q3 i?` In boys, neither MA nor TA emerged as significant predictors of performance. [→ Devine A 2012 (2)](#devine-a-2012-2)

## Evidence

### Devine A 2012

Devine A, Fawcett K, Szűcs D, Dowker A. (2012). Gender differences in mathematics anxiety and the relation to mathematics performance while controlling for test anxiety. Behavioral and Brain Functions. https://doi.org/10.1186/1744-9081-8-33

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Simultaneous multiple linear regression on standardized scores in the girls' subsample was highly significant, F(2,162) = 12.06, p <0.0001, accounting for 11.88% of variance (R2); MA predicted performance, Beta = −.315, p <0.0001, TA did not.

> "MA was a significant predictor variable of performance (Beta = −.315; p <0.0001). In contrast, test anxiety was not a significant predictor."

### Devine A 2012 (2)

Devine A, Fawcett K, Szűcs D, Dowker A. (2012). Gender differences in mathematics anxiety and the relation to mathematics performance while controlling for test anxiety. Behavioral and Brain Functions. https://doi.org/10.1186/1744-9081-8-33

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The boys' regression model reached significance, F(2,264) = 3.03, p <0.05, but accounted for only 1.5% of variance, and neither MA nor TA was a significant predictor of performance.

> "In boys the overall model reached significance (F(2,264) = 3.03; p <0.05). However, it accounted only for 1.5% of the variance and neither MA, nor test anxiety emerged as significant predictors of performance."
