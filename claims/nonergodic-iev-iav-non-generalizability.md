# For nonergodic processes, the structure of interindividual variation differs from the analogous structure of intraindividual variation, so IEV results do not generalize to single cases

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Classical ergodic theorems imply that only under very strict conditions can a structure of interindividual variation be generalized to the analogous structure of intraindividual variation. [→ Molenaar 2004](#molenaar-2004)
`q1 i?` Learning, developmental, and adaptive processes are nonstationary and hence nonergodic, so explicit analyses of IAV are required for valid results about individual learning performance. [→ Molenaar 2004](#molenaar-2004)

## Evidence

### Molenaar 2004

Molenaar, P. C. M. (2004). A Manifesto on Psychology as Idiographic Science: Bringing the Person Back Into Scientific Psychology, This Time Forever. Measurement, 2(4), 201-218. https://www.fmg.uva.nl/ml/publicaties_ml.cfm

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Mathematical-statistical argument from classical ergodic theory, presented as a formal theoretical result rather than an empirical study. The article states that for nonergodic processes "an analysis of the structure of IEV will yield results that differ" from analogous IAV analysis, and that the necessary ergodicity conditions "are rather strict and will not be obeyed by many psychological processes."

> "For nonergodic processes, an analysis of the structure of IEV will yield results that dif- fer from results obtained in an analogous analysis of IAV."
