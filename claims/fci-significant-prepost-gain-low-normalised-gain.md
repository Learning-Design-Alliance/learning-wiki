## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Mean FCI scores rose from 52% (pretest) to 62% (posttest), a significant increase (t-test p = 2.9×10−8), with an average normalised gain G = 0.20. [→ Allan L. Alinea and Wade Naylor 2016](#allan-l-alinea-and-wade-naylor-2016)

## Evidence

### Allan L. Alinea and Wade Naylor 2016

Allan L. Alinea and Wade Naylor. (2016). Gender gap and polarisation of physics on global courses. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1605.03302](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1605.03302)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Four-year pooled FCI results for 66 students across four cohorts (n = 11, 18, 18, 19). Table 1 reports per-year gains of 0.24, 0.23, 0.12 and 0.25, averaging "a relatively low value of G = 0.20".

> "The mean scores for the pretest and posttest are 52% and 62% respectively, corresponding to a rise of about 20%. Our t-test returns a p-value of 2.9×10−8."
