## Evidence

### Xiaoxuan Li · Julia Chatain · Fan Wang · Manu Kapur 2026

Xiaoxuan Li · Julia Chatain · Fan Wang · Manu Kapur. (2026). From Body to Word: A Three-Level Meta-Analysis of Embodied Vocabulary Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10194-9

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-regressions of amount of sensorimotor engagement found no significant moderation in either design (between-subject F = 0.15, p = .69; within-subject F = 0.10, p = .75). The authors state this challenges the assumption that greater bodily involvement necessarily leads to greater learning benefits.

> "activities involving larger bodily movements did not yield stronger effects than those involving more localized movements. This finding challenges the assumption that greater bodily involvement necessarily leads to greater learning benefits."

### Xiaoxuan Li · Julia Chatain · Fan Wang · Manu Kapur 2026 (2)

Xiaoxuan Li · Julia Chatain · Fan Wang · Manu Kapur. (2026). From Body to Word: A Three-Level Meta-Analysis of Embodied Vocabulary Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10194-9

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Between-subject meta-regressions found no significant moderation by target language, proficiency level, type of embodied interaction, immersion, or sensorimotor engagement. The authors note the absence of a target language effect contrasts with some previous findings.

> "Other examined variables did not significantly influence vocabulary learning outcomes, including target language ( F = 1.08, p = .58), proficiency level ( F = 1.83, p = .18), type of embodied interaction ( F = 1.12, p = .29), immersion ( F = 1.71, p = .63), and amount of sensorimotor engagement (F = 0.15, p = .69)."
