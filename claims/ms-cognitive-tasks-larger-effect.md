# Mental simulation benefits cognitive tasks more than motor or strength tasks

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` One meta-analysis found the effect of MS on cognitive tasks (M = 1.44) significantly larger than on motor (M = 0.43) or strength (M = 0.20) tasks, and MS effectiveness increases with the cognitive involvement of the task. [→ Meer 2009](#meer-2009)

## Evidence

### Meer 2009

Meer, J.P. van & Theunissen, N.C.M. (2009). Prospective educational applications of mental simulation: A meta-review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-009-9097-8

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Meta-analytic comparison reported in the reviewed literature: the effect on cognitive tasks (M = 1.44) exceeds motor (M = 0.43) and strength (M = 0.20). A second meta-analysis (Driskell et al. 1994) found "MS was more effective the more a task involved cognitive activities."

> "One meta-analysis (Feltz et al., 1983) found results indicating that the effect of MS on cognitive tasks (M = 1.44) is significantly larger than the effect on motor (M = 0.43) or strength (M = 0.20) tasks."
