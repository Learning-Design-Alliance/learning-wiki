Evidence¶

Allensworth 2020¶

Allensworth, E. (2020). High school GPAs and ACT scores as predictors of college graduation. Educational Researcher, 49(3), 198-211. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/high-school-GPAs-and-ACT-scores-as-predictors-of-college-completion-er-article

q2 · i? · associational · r?

The study's overview contrasts the two measures: "the relationship of HSGPAs with college graduation is strong and consistent and larger than school effects," whereas the ACT relationship is weak with school-varying slopes. No effect sizes are printed on this page.

"Yet, the relationship of HSGPAs with college graduation is strong and consistent and larger than school effects. In contrast, the relationship of ACT scores with college graduation is weak and smaller than high school effects, and the slope of the relationship varies by high school."

Related Claims¶

  • ACT scores show a weak relationship with college graduation, with slope variation across high schools larger than the average signal — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Both HSGPA and ACT scores leave large high school effects on college graduation unexplained, which the study recommends measuring and publishing directly — related
  • The linear slope of the HSGPA–college graduation relationship does not vary significantly across high schools, so HSGPA provides a consistent signal across schools — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • HSGPA has a strong, positive relationship with college graduation that does not vary significantly in its linear slope across high schools — related
  • ACT scores weakly predict college graduation, and the school-level variance in the ACT slope (0.192) exceeds the average slope (0.129), so school effects introduce more noise than the ACT signal — related
  • Adding ACT scores to models with HSGPA does not improve prediction of college graduation or reduce between-school variability — related
