# School growth estimates based on two years of data correlate around .55–.58 with estimates based on all five elementary years, below the .90 threshold at which ESSA accountability determinations would likely change

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Correlations between school contributions to growth estimated from spring of second through fourth grade versus spring of second through sixth grade are .584 in math and .556 in reading, suggesting different schools would be identified as low-performing under ESSA depending on the timespan. ## Evidence

### Soland 2019

Soland, J., Thum, Y.M., & King, G. (2019). Reconciling long-term education policy goals with short-term school accountability models: Evidence and implications from a longitudinal study. (The Collaborative for Student Growth at NWEA Working Paper). https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/reconciling-long-term-education-policy-goals-with-short-term-school-accountability-models-paper/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

CP-model comparison of two-year versus five-year between-year growth Z-scores for the same schools. The article reports correlations of .584 (math) and .556 (reading), no effect size, so impact is null; the .90 practical-significance cutoff comes from Koedel and Betts (2010) as cited by the authors.

> "Here, the correlations are .584 in math and .556 in reading. While these correlations are by no means small, they also likely have consequences for which schools would be deemed low-performing under ESSA."
