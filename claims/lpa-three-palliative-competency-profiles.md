## Evidence

### Guan Q 2025

Guan Q, Zhu X, Xue Z, Peng M. (2025). Core competency in palliative care among intensive care unit nurses: A latent profile analysis. Nursing in Critical Care. https://doi.org/10.1111/nicc.70021

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Latent profile analysis of 342 ICU nurses' PCNCC total scores fitted models with one to five profiles in Mplus 8.3. The discussion reports the three-class solution's proportions: "the 'medium palliative care core competency group' (47.2%)" was the largest, and low plus medium accounted for nearly 80%.

> "Using LPA, the study identified three groups according to ICU nurses' overall palliative care core competency ratings: the 'high palliative care core competency group' (31.2%), the 'medium palliative care core competency group' (47.2%) and the 'low palliative care core competency group' (21.6%)."

### Guan Q 2025 (2)

Guan Q, Zhu X, Xue Z, Peng M. (2025). Core competency in palliative care among intensive care unit nurses: A latent profile analysis. Nursing in Critical Care. https://doi.org/10.1111/nicc.70021

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Model selection compared AIC, BIC, adjusted BIC, entropy, LMR and bootstrapped likelihood ratio tests across one- to five-profile solutions. The three-profile model was chosen because "an entropy of 0.894" indicated high classification certainty; the five-profile model failed to improve on four profiles due to decreased entropy.

> "With an entropy of 0.894 and p‐values for both LMR and BLRT less than .05, the three‐profile model was found to be the best‐suited model with high classification certainty"
