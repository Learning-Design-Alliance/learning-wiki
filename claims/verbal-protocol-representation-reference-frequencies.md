# In verbal protocols, references concentrated on the model (44.4%) and graph (39.4%) with few to the equation (16.1%), and students referenced multiple representations on 42.6% of questions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across interviews, model references were most frequent (44.4%) and equation references least frequent (16.1%). [→ Mike Stieff 2011](#mike-stieff-2011)
`q3 i?` Participants referenced information in multiple representations directly on 42.6% of questions, with large individual differences. [→ Mike Stieff 2011 (2)](#mike-stieff-2011-2)

## Evidence

### Mike Stieff 2011

Mike Stieff, Mary Hegarty, Ghislain Deslongchamps. (2011). Identifying Representational Competence with Multi-Representational Displays. Cognition and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1080/07370008.2010.507318

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Verbal protocol frequency coding of nine transcribed interviews from the ten-participant study (one recording lacked audio). "the majority of references (44.4%) to a specific representation across the interviews were to the model".

> "the majority of references (44.4%) to a specific representation across the interviews were to the model and the fewest references (16.1%) were to the equation"

### Mike Stieff 2011 (2)

Mike Stieff, Mary Hegarty, Ghislain Deslongchamps. (2011). Identifying Representational Competence with Multi-Representational Displays. Cognition and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1080/07370008.2010.507318

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Protocol coding across the transcribed interviews; one student referenced multiple representations on 66.7% of questions, another on 16.7%, "differences in multiple representation use were evident and dramatic".

> "participants referred to information in multiple representations directly on 42.6% of questions. Individual differences in multiple representation use were evident and dramatic."
