# Feedback on numbers where logarithmic and linear representations are most discrepant (around 150) produces the greatest representational change in children's number-line estimation

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Second graders given feedback on 150 generated posttest estimates fitting the linear function significantly and substantially better than the logarithmic one, whereas other feedback conditions did not. [→ Opfer 2007](#opfer-2007)

## Evidence

### Opfer 2007

Opfer, J. E., & Siegler, R. S. (2007). Representational change and children's numerical estimation. Cognitive Psychology 55 (2007) 169–195. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2006.09.002

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Microgenetic experiment with 61 second graders whose pretest estimates fit a logarithmic function, randomly assigned to 150-, 5-, 725-, or no-feedback conditions. Only the 150-feedback group's posttest was significantly linear, with "lin R2 D .95, log R2 D .74" and d = .66.

> "children in the 150-feedback group generated estimates that Wt the linear function signiWcantly and substantially better than the logarithmic one (lin R2 D .95, log R2 D .74, t [21] D 2.40, p < .05 d D .66)"
