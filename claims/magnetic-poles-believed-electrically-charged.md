## Evidence

### Cristian Raduta 2005

Cristian Raduta. (2005). General Students' Misconceptions Related to Electricity and Magnetism. The Ohio State University. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0503132](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0503132)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Survey analysis by Maloney (Ref. 3) of particle-in-a-magnetic-field problems in two problem formats, summarized in Table 2. The - to N, + to S rule was most popular (28% Class of Spr 84, 29% Class of Sum 84), and the next most popular had both charges attracted to the stronger pole regardless of polarity. No effect size is printed.

> "The most popular individual strategy was the one that took the N-pole as being, or acting as though it were, positively charged. Discounting the people who were not consistent (the muddlers), the next most popular strategy had both types of charges being attracted to the stronger magnetic pole"

### Cristian Raduta 2005 (2)

Cristian Raduta. (2005). General Students' Misconceptions Related to Electricity and Magnetism. The Ohio State University. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0503132](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0503132)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Ranking-task survey by Maloney (Ref. 3) given to four classes, two without college electromagnetism instruction, summarized in Table 3. The N attr - strategy ranged from 42% to 59% across classes (n=58, 22, 114, 20). No effect size is printed.

> "What is important to notice here, is the similarity between the pre-instruction and post-instruction results. We notice also the similarities within the pre-test and post-test groups. For all classes the strategy that treats the N pole as being positive is the most popular"
