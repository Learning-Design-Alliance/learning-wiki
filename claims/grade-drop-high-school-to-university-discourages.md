## Evidence

### E. D. Jennings 1911

E. D. Jennings. (1911). The Elimination of Freshmen from the University of Texas. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=elimination+of+freshmen+from+the+university+of+texas

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Questionnaire of one freshman class of thirty-nine pupils: average study of four and three-quarter hours per day in high school versus six at the university; average grade 85 per cent in high school versus 70 at the university. The authors state data from so small a group were not considered conclusive.

> "Based on hours of work done, the university ranked them only two-thirds as high as the high school did."

### E. D. Jennings 1911 (2)

E. D. Jennings. (1911). The Elimination of Freshmen from the University of Texas. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=elimination+of+freshmen+from+the+university+of+texas

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Figure II plots registrar's scholarship standing by first-cause group; the text notes the 16 per cent gap "does not take into account the fact that students spend more time" studying at the university. The postal-card follow-up found a similar 16 per cent difference, and 13 per cent among 141 non-respondents.

> "It will be noted that the high school grades are about 16 per cent, higher than those of the university"
