# Most CPS completers who took longer than four years were enrolled more than eight terms: 67% finished in nine or ten terms and 12% in eleven or twelve

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among 2012–14 CPS graduates who completed in four to six years, about 79% were enrolled more than eight terms, with 67% needing one or two extra terms. [→ Nagaoka 2023](#nagaoka-2023)

## Evidence

### Nagaoka 2023

Nagaoka, J., Mahaffie, S., Usher, A., & Arneson, A. (2023). The four years fallacy: Four-year vs. six-year bachelor's degree completion rates. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/the-four-years-fallacy

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of terms enrolled for 5,447 2012–14 CPS immediate enrollees who completed a bachelor's degree in more than four but within six years (Figure 2). The report states "about 67% of students needed one or two extra terms" and 12% took 11 or 12 terms. No effect size is printed.

> "The majority of students took more than 8 terms to complete a bachelor's degree—about 67% of students needed one or two extra terms (completing in nine or 10 terms), and an additional 12% of students took considerably longer (11 or 12 terms)."
