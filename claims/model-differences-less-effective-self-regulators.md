# Six relationships in the model differ for less effective self-regulators, including performance-avoidance goals being unrelated to metacognition

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For less effective self-regulators (N=383), performance-avoidance goals were unrelated to metacognition (β=-.01), metacognition was unrelated to surface cognitive strategies (β=.09), and mastery and performance-approach goals had a large positive relationship (β=.52). ## Evidence

### Anneke Vrugt & Frans J. Oort 2008

Anneke Vrugt & Frans J. Oort. (2008). Metacognition, achievement goals, study strategies and academic achievement: pathways to achievement. Metacognition Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-008-9022-4

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Path model fitted to 383 less effective self-regulators (Model 3: CHISQ=8.98, df=7, p=0.254; RMSEA=0.027). Table 3 reports the standardized coefficients; two additional effects were a negative effect of avoidance goals on deep processing and a positive effect of mastery goals on resource management.

> "There are six relationships in this model that differ from those in the model of effective self-regulators. Mastery and performance-approach goals had a large positive relationship, whereas mastery goals and performance-avoidance goals were unrelated. Further, the pursuit of performance-avoidance goals was unrelated to metacognition. Moreover, metacognition was unrelated to the use of surface cognitive strategies."
