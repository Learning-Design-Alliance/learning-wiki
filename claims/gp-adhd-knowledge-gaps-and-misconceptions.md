## Evidence

### French B 2020

French B, Perez Vallejos E, Sayal K, Daley D. (2020). Awareness of ADHD in primary care: stakeholder perspectives. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-020-01112-1

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of the stakeholder interviews reports that GPs' knowledge was insufficient "to identify ADHD or give clear information on pathways and services", affecting patients and specialist services after referral. No effect size is printed.

> "GPs generally felt they knew enough so that when ADHD was first mentioned, they were happy to refer on to specialist services, yet not enough to identify ADHD or give clear information on pathways and services."

### French B 2020 (2)

French B, Perez Vallejos E, Sayal K, Daley D. (2020). Awareness of ADHD in primary care: stakeholder perspectives. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-020-01112-1

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

The misconceptions subtheme reports GPs raised SES and parenting as causal factors of ADHD, and GPs held biased views of lower-SES patients as seeking diagnosis for welfare benefits. No effect size is printed.

> "The main misconception related to the concepts of social economic status (SES) and parenting. These topics were often brought up by GPs as causal factors of ADHD."

### French B 2020 (3)

French B, Perez Vallejos E, Sayal K, Daley D. (2020). Awareness of ADHD in primary care: stakeholder perspectives. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-020-01112-1

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

The same subtheme reports that girls and high-functioning adults go "under the radar" because they are less hyperactive or disruptive and do not fit GPs' conceptions of ADHD. No effect size is printed.

> "High functioning individuals and girls seem to go under the radar as they often do not meet the GPs' beliefs about ADHD. They might be less hyperactive, less disruptive in class and therefore do not fit some conceptions attributed to ADHD."
