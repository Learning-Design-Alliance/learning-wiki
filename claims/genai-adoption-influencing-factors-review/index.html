This is the article's narrative review of prior studies, attributed to the cited works (e.g., Strzelecki 2024, Yilmaz et al. 2023, Lemke et al. 2023, Wang et al. 2023), not a new empirical analysis. The review organizes factors into motivational, cognitive, affective, contextual, and ethical categories.

"Motivational factors such as perceived value (Wigfield & Eccles, 2000), hedonic motivation (Kang et al., 2015; Kasneci et al., 2023; Strzelecki, 2024), and habit (Strzelecki, 2024) are a significant predictor of GenAI intention and utilization."

Related Claims¶

  • Contextual and moderating variables (instructor guidance, prior experience, instructor endorsement) show significant but weaker effects on GenAI acceptance than student-centered perceptual factors — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Hedonic motivation and habit were the strongest predictors of behavioral intention, and habit also substantially predicted use behavior — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Effort expectancy is associated with AI use only indirectly through competence, whereas hedonic motivation shows a direct positive association — related
  • Teachers' expectancy beliefs about successfully using AI applications shape their motivation to use them (theoretical argument). — related
  • No previously validated GenAI perception or readiness instrument was designed for both instructors and students in AI-enhanced higher education — related
  • Affective benefits are the strongest predictor of students' e-learning experience with AR learning applications — related
