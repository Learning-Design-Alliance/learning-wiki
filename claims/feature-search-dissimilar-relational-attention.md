# Feature search training among dissimilar distractors produces reward learning of both feature values and feature relationships

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2`–`i3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In Experiment 2a, the feature-match condition showed a significant main effect of reward on RTs, and the relation-match condition showed slower RTs for high- than low-reward relational distractors. [→ Oudeng Jia 2025](#oudeng-jia-2025)
`q3 i2` During feature search training itself, search RTs were faster for the high- than the low-reward-associated color. [→ Oudeng Jia 2025 (2)](#oudeng-jia-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Oudeng Jia 2025

Oudeng Jia, Qingsong Tan, Sihan Zhang, Ke Jia & Mengyuan Gong. (2025). The precision of attention selection during reward learning influences the mechanisms of value-driven attention. npj Science of Learning 10:49. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00342-1

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Controlled lab experiment (N = 40) using feature search training with dissimilar distractors. The feature-match test condition showed a significant reward main effect, "Experiment 2a: F(2, 78) = 16.20, p < 0.001", with printed effect sizes ηp2 = 0.293 in Experiment 2a and ηp2 = 0.222 in Experiment 2c.

> "A one-way repeated-measures ANOVA (reward history: high-, low- vs. no-reward) revealed a signiﬁcant main effect of reward on RTs (Experiment 2a: F(2, 78) = 16.20, p < 0.001, ηp 2 =0 . 2 9 3 ; Experiment 2c: F(2, 78) = 11.14, p < 0.001, ηp 2 =0 . 2 2 2 )."

### Oudeng Jia 2025 (2)

Oudeng Jia, Qingsong Tan, Sihan Zhang, Ke Jia & Mengyuan Gong. (2025). The precision of attention selection during reward learning influences the mechanisms of value-driven attention. npj Science of Learning 10:49. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00342-1

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Training-task analysis in Experiments 2a and 2c showed "reward facilitated search RTs for the high reward-associated color", with printed Cohen's d = -0.570 in Experiment 2a and d = -0.413 in Experiment 2c; accuracy remained high above 91% with no significant differences.

> "During the training task, we observ ed that reward facilitated search RTs for the high reward-associated color compared to the low reward- associated color (Experiment 2a: t(39) = -3.60, p < 0.001, Cohen’sd = -0.570, 95% CI = [-0.901, -0.232]; Experiment 2c: t(39) = -2.61, p = 0.013, Cohen’s d = -0.413, 95% CI = [-0.733, -0.087])."
