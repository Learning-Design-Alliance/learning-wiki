# The CPS test score gap between African-American students and other ethnic groups widened over the decade because Asian, white, and Latino scores improved at faster rates

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Although African-American students' average scores improved, the gap between them and other ethnic groups widened over ten years, and by 2001 Latino students averaged two to three months higher in reading and three to four months higher in math than African-American students. [→ Todd Rosenkranz 2002](#todd-rosenkranz-2002)

## Evidence

### Todd Rosenkranz 2002

Todd Rosenkranz. (2002). 2001 CPS Test Trend Review: Iowa Tests of Basic Skills. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/2001-cps-trend-review-iowa-tests-basic-skills

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Disaggregated trend analysis of mean ITBS scores by race/ethnicity, 1990-2001, reported in the race/ethnicity section and Figures 3 and Tables C-D. At the decade's start, Latino and African-American reading scores were quite similar.

> "The test score gap between African-American stu- dents and students of other ethnic groups in CPS has widened over the last 10 years. Although the average scores for African Americans has improved, the average scores for Asian, white, and Latino stu- dents have improved at a faster rate."
