# A dermatology E-learning programme produced a significant pre-to-post knowledge gain in GP residents (Cohen's d = 0.51)

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` GP residents randomized to the E-learning programme showed a statistically significant increase in dermatology knowledge test scores from pre- to post-test. [→ Verheijden Michelle J. H. 2021](#verheijden-michelle-j-h-2021)

## Evidence

### Verheijden Michelle J. H. 2021

Verheijden Michelle J. H., Martens Herm, Heeneman Sylvia. (2021). A dermatology E-learning programme is perceived as a valuable learning tool in postgraduate general practice training. International Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.612f.3d6c

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Randomized quantitative comparison in a mixed-methods study: 12 GP residents in the E-learning intervention group completed pre- and post-knowledge tests. The article reports the increase was significant, "t(11)= 2.258, p = 0.045, Cohen’s d = 0.51", a small-to-moderate effect.

> "The intervention group showed a significant increase in knowledge test scores from the pre- (M=58.92%, SD=9.55%) to the post-knowledge test (M=64.92%, SD=13.65%, t(11)= 2.258, p = 0.045, Cohen’s d = 0.51)"
