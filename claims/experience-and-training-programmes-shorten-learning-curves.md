## Evidence

### Soomro N. A. 2020

Soomro N. A., Hashimoto D. A., Porteous A. J., Ridley C. J. A., Marsh W. J., Ditto R. and Roy S. (2020). Systematic review of learning curves in robot‐assisted surgery. BJS Open. https://doi.org/10.1002/bjs5.50235

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's discussion of moderators, synthesizing five studies comparing operating-time learning curves by prior experience. The review notes studies generally did not report participants' specific grade or training experience, limiting interpretation.

> "in three of five studies comparing the operating time learning curves of robotic surgeons, those with greater experience required fewer procedures to overcome their learning curve16, 29, 38."

### Soomro N. A. 2020 (2)

Soomro N. A., Hashimoto D. A., Porteous A. J., Ridley C. J. A., Marsh W. J., Ditto R. and Roy S. (2020). Systematic review of learning curves in robot‐assisted surgery. BJS Open. https://doi.org/10.1002/bjs5.50235

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review attributes this finding to Guend and colleagues, describing it as based on a small sample size. The review notes few studies provided details of their training programmes, and mentions recent programmes using feedback loops to shorten time to adequate performance.

> "Guend and colleagues27 reported that a lower procedure volume was required to overcome the learning curve for robot‐assisted colorectal resections for three surgeons who had participated in an institutional training programme (25–30 procedures each), compared with the volume required for an earlier surgeon who joined the institution before the programme was established (74 procedures)."
