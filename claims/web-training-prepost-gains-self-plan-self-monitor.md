## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Experimental group posttest scores were significantly higher than pretest scores in self-plan, self-monitor, and total score after four weeks of web-based training, while the control group showed no significant change; but an ANCOVA comparing the groups found a significantly greater gain for the trained group only in self-plan (F(1, 50) = 6.49, p < .05), not in self-monitor (p = .856) or total score (p = .328). ## Evidence

### Chun-Yi Shen & Hsiu-Chuan Liu 2011

Chun-Yi Shen & Hsiu-Chuan Liu. (2011). Metacognitive Skills Development: A Web-Based Approach in Higher Education. The Turkish Online Journal of Educational Technology, 10(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ932234

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pretest-posttest quasi-experiment with 53 Taiwanese college students (experimental n=29, control n=24) measured with the 45-item MSEQ, a 6-point Likert self-report questionnaire. Paired-samples t-tests showed significant pre-post gains in the experimental group, e.g. self-plan "t=4.257, p<.001"; the control group showed no significant change on any dimension. The between-group test tells a narrower story: "students in experimental group made significantly greater gains compared to control group in self-plan (F (1, 50) = 6.4920, p < 0.05). However, there is no significant differences in self-monitor (F (1, 50) = 0.033, p = 0.856), self-modify (F (1, 50) = 0.089, p =0.767), self-evaluate (F (1, 50) = 0.037, p = 0.849), and total score (F (1, 50) = 0.976, p = 0.328)." So only the self-plan gain can be attributed to the training over the control condition.

> "the results of paired-samples t-test showed that experimental group’s posttest scores were significantly higher than the pretest scores in self-plan (t=4.257, p<.001), self-monitor (t=3.364, p <.01), and total score (t=3.753, p<.001), while there was no significance in the control group."
