## Evidence

### Chad T. Kishimoto 2018

Chad T. Kishimoto, Michael G. Anderson, and Joe P. Salamon. (2018). Flipping the Large-Enrollment Introductory Physics Classroom. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1807.03850](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1807.03850)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Archived-score comparison of 60 identical multiple-choice questions from seven flipped and ten traditional sections taught by the same instructors in different terms, with a stringent cut excluding similar, augmented, or isomorphic questions. "over half of the points are signiﬁcantly above the line" favoring flipped classes; no effect size printed.

> "While many of the questions are consistent with the 45 ◦ line, over half of the points are signiﬁcantly above the line, meaning that on these questions, students in ﬂipped classes performed signiﬁcantly better than those in traditional classes."
