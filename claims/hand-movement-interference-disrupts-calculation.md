## Evidence

### Maëlle Neveu 2023

Maëlle Neveu, Marie Geurten, Nancy Durieux, Laurence Rousselle. (2023). Finger Use and Arithmetic Skills in Children and Adolescents: a Scoping Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09722-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Within-subject randomized controlled trial in deaf and TD children comparing hand, foot, and control interference conditions; children were "less efficient in the hand interference condition than in the other two conditions." The review describes the difference as medium to large without a numeric value.

> "Crollen and Noël (2015) asked to their participants to solve problems in three different interference conditions (i.e., squeezing a ball with a hand, squeezing a ball with a foot and a control condition) and showed that children were less efficient in the hand interference condition than in the other two conditions, suggesting that gestures play a functional role in calculation skills."

### Maëlle Neveu 2023 (2)

Maëlle Neveu, Marie Geurten, Nancy Durieux, Laurence Rousselle. (2023). Finger Use and Arithmetic Skills in Children and Adolescents: a Scoping Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09722-8

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Correlational study (Kohen-Raz & Masalha, 1988) showing suppression of synkinetic finger movements is positively associated with arithmetic performance in deaf and TD children, with effect sizes described only as ranging from small to large.

> "ability to suppress finger synkinetic movements during arithmetic task is positively associated with arithmetic performance both in deaf and typically developing children, with effect sizes ranging from small to large (Kohen-Raz & Masalha, 1988)."
