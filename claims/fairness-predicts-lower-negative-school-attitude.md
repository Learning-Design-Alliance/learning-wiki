## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In regression analyses controlling for medication use, demographics, and co-occurring psychopathology, higher perceived fairness predicted significantly lower negative attitude to school, with no fairness × ADHD interaction. [→ Martinez 2026](#martinez-2026)

## Evidence

### Martinez 2026

Martinez, A. C., Green, C. D., Peugh, J. L., & Becker, S. P. (2026). Perceived School Fairness and Academic Functioning in Early Adolescents: Differential Associations for Adolescents with or Without ADHD? Research on Child and Adolescent Psychopathology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10802-025-01419-6

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression model for attitude to school (R² = 0.23) with fairness, ADHD status, and their interaction as predictors, covariates included; model-based multiple imputation in BLIMP. The fairness × ADHD interaction was not significant (estimate = 0.10, p = .170).

> "Higher levels of perceived fairness were associated with significantly lower negative attitudes toward school (estimate = −0.46, p <.001), whereas an ADHD diagnosis significantly predicted higher negative school attitudes (estimate = 0.13, p =.020)"
