## Evidence

### Chance-Revels 2026

Chance-Revels, R., Phan, Q., Crawford, K., Steiger, L., & Swan, B. A. (2026). Enhancing Community Health Worker Communication: Evaluating Simulation-Based Training in Motivational Interviewing. Journal of Community Health. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10900-025-01543-w

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Inductive grounded-theory coding of open-ended survey responses from CHW trainees identified realism and contextualization as a major theme. Trainees reported that authentic SP interactions helped build confidence, reduce anxiety, and make the simulation feel meaningful to their roles.

> "Trainees consistently highlighted the value of the real-world encounters reflected in the MI simulations. SPs were MI-trained actors who provided feedback and embodied the role of clients in the community. The authenticity of the SPs and their interactions with the CHWs helped build confidence, reduce anxiety, and make the simulation feel real, applicable, and meaningful to the CHWs' roles."
