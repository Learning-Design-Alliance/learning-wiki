## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors argue that experiencing hospitalization from the patient's role generates emotional resonance and helps nurses understand and meet patients' needs, improving care quality. [→ Liu Yingmin 2023](#liu-yingmin-2023)

## Evidence

### Liu Yingmin 2023

Liu Yingmin, Qie Dandan, Wang Mengmeng, Li Yuyuan, Guo Dongmei, Chen Ximin, Li Li, Yu Honglian, Wang Jingjing. (2023). Application of role reversal and standardized patient simulation (SPS) in the training of new nurses. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04294-1

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' interpretive argument in the Discussion, not a separately tested result: they claim personal experience of the patient role generates "emotional resonance, which then improves the quality of care", citing prior work. No empathy measure was reported in this trial.

> "The characteristic of role reversal combined with the SPS method is personal experience, which includes the personal experience of nurses and the personal experience of ‘heart’. Personal experience can better touch people’s hearts to generate emotional resonance, which then improves the quality of care"
