# Real-time display of open-format student responses in calculus revealed a constant-bounds misconception that dropped after immediate class discussion

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2013 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In an 87-student Honors Calculus III class, 32 of 83 responses to a triple-integral inequality question showed all constant bounds; after an immediate projected discussion of student responses, on a follow-up variation question all students responded, only 4 still showed the misconception in one dimension, and none provided all constant bounds. [→ F.V. Kowalski 2013](#fv-kowalski-2013)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Instructor report from a large lecture-based Honors Calculus III course of 87 first-term students using InkSurvey on pen-enabled devices. "32 out of 83 student responses showed all constant bounds"; the instructor then projected responses and discussed an appropriate approach in class. No statistical test or effect size is printed.

> "As in the sample above, 32 out of 83 student responses showed all constant bounds on these inequalities; four of the students didn’t respond at all."
