# Online RBA scores were 4–5% lower than historical in-class scores in the same courses, a non-significant difference driven by a larger lower tail

## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2020

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2020). Student behavior and test security in online conceptual assessment. arXiv:1912.05698v1 [physics.ed-ph]. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/perc.2019.pr.wilcox](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2019.pr.wilcox)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of online RBA scores with historical paper-based in-class scores from the same two courses with the same instructor. Online scores were 4–5% lower, a non-significant difference (two-tailed t-test, p > 0.05) largely driven by a larger lower tail online.

> "Comparisons of the online and in-class scores showed the online scores being 4-5% lower. This difference was not statistically signiﬁcant (two-tailed t -test, p > 0.05) and was largely driven by the presence of a larger lower tail of the distribution in the two online administrations."
