## Evidence

### Moustafa HAM 2024

Moustafa HAM, Hamid AE, Hassoub G, Kassem AB. (2024). Assessing the impact of critical care training on pharmacy students in Egypt: a pre-post study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06427-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Results section and Table 2 of the pre-post study (n = 72): pain and agitation 0.36 ± 0.25 to 0.98 ± 0.08, phenytoin 0.39 ± 0.27 to 1.0 ± 0.02, evidence based 0.40 ± 0.17 to 0.96 ± 0.09, all p < 0.001 by Wilcoxon signed ranks test.

> "All topics exhibited a statistically significant rise in students’ scores over the training course duration (p ≤ 0.05) (Fig. 1). The top 5 topics that showed the greatest increase in the total mean scores were: (a) pain and agitation; (b) phenytoin; (c) EBM"
