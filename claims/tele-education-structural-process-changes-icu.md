## Evidence

### Kovacevic Pedja 2019

Kovacevic Pedja, Dragic Sasa, Kovacevic Tijana, Momcicevic Danica, Festic Emir, Kashyap Rahul, Niven Alexander S., Dong Yue, Gajic Ognjen. (2019). Impact of weekly case-based tele-education on quality of care in a limited resource medical intensive care unit. Critical Care. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13054-019-2494-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Semistructured evaluation by 16 providers (10 physicians, 4 charge nurses, 2 physical therapists) who completed an evaluation of ICU structure and processes based on the CERTAIN platform, graded as fully, partially, or not implemented.

> "Process changes included daily sedation interruption, protocolized mechanical ventilation management and liberation, documentation of daily fluid balance with restrictive fluid and transfusion strategies, daily device assessment, and increased family presence and participation in care decisions."

### Kovacevic Pedja 2019 (2)

Kovacevic Pedja, Dragic Sasa, Kovacevic Tijana, Momcicevic Danica, Festic Emir, Kashyap Rahul, Niven Alexander S., Dong Yue, Gajic Ognjen. (2019). Impact of weekly case-based tele-education on quality of care in a limited resource medical intensive care unit. Critical Care. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13054-019-2494-6

`q2 · i?`

Provider evaluation of medication practice changes after two years of tele-education, reported in the Results section. Standardized nutrition, avoidance of polypharmacy, beta blocker use, and bone marrow biopsy were considered partially implemented by a minority of evaluators (25%).

> "Less effective (dopamine, thiopental, aminophylline) or expensive (low molecular weight heparin, proton pump inhibitor) medications were replaced with more effective (norepinephrine, propofol) or cheaper (unfractionated heparin, H2 blocker) alternatives. The vast majority of changes were evaluated as "fully implemented.""
