# True-false tests correlate with intelligence ratings (.406) more highly than essays (.344) or written work (.255), and combining true-false tests raises the intelligence correlation while combining essays does not

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Average correlations with intelligence are .406 for true-false, .344 for essays, and .255 for written work; the true-false-essay difference (.062) is about equal to its standard error (.067). [→ Arthur I. Gates 1920](#arthur-i-gates-1920)
`q2 i?` Combining several true-false tests yields a steadily increasing correlation with intelligence, whereas combining essays makes no noticeable increase. [→ Arthur I. Gates 1920 (2)](#arthur-i-gates-1920-2)

## Evidence

### Arthur I. Gates 1920

Arthur I. Gates, Teachers College, Columbia University. (1920). The True-False Test as a Measure of Achievement in College Courses. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=true-false+test+as+a+measure+of+achievement+in+college+courses+gates

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis in Tables XII-XIV of test scores against Army Alpha or Thorndike intelligence test ratings in the same classes. The printed correlations are r = .406 (true-false, N = 22), r = .344 (essays, N = 18), and r = .255 (written work, N = 9); the true-false-essay difference of .062 is about equal to its standard error of .067.

> "Correlations with intelligence tests are: for the True-False + .406, <j (tr.-obt. av.) -± .032, for the essay examinations, -f+ -314, a (tr.-obt. av.) ± .04 for the written work, -j+ .255, a (tr.-obt. av.) ± .034."

### Arthur I. Gates 1920 (2)

Arthur I. Gates, Teachers College, Columbia University. (1920). The True-False Test as a Measure of Achievement in College Courses. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=true-false+test+as+a+measure+of+achievement+in+college+courses+gates

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis in Tables XVI and XVII arranged by classes: true-false averages rise from .432 (2 tests) to .545 (5 tests) while essay averages stay near .34-.35. The article reports the increase as printed in these tables; no effect size for the increase is printed.

> "Combining several True-False tests produces a marked increase in the correlations with intelligence, which is not true of the essay examinations."
