Evidence¶

Nurit Paz-Baruch 2025¶

Nurit Paz-Baruch, Guy Grovas, Zemira R. Mevarech. (2025). The effects of meta-creative pedagogy on elementary school students' creative thinking. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09412-6

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Two-factor repeated-measures MANOVA on the London Bridge Task with N = 100 sixth-graders randomly assigned by class to CREATE (N = 49) or control (N = 51). The omnibus effect was "statistically significant, F(3, 96) = 6.47, p < .001. ηp² = 0.17"; the Time × Intervention interaction was also significant (ηp² = 0.16).

"The repeated measures MANOVA indicated that the intervention effect on all three creative thinking components simultaneously was statistically significant, F(3, 96) = 6.47, p < .001. ηp² = 0.17"

Related Claims¶

  • Students exposed to CREATE perceive their implementation of meta-creative processes to a greater extent than control students
  • CREATE benefits transfer to a new task on all three creativity components: fluency, flexibility, and originality
  • CREATE does not produce a significant immediate interaction effect on flexibility, though the experimental group improved pre-to-post — reports the opposite
  • CREATE improves students' fluency immediately after the intervention relative to control — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • CREATE improves students' originality immediately after the intervention across all three originality scoring methods — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Two or more semesters of AP art workshops significantly raised the graphical TTCT 13-forces creativity score in a doubly robust regression, but not total TTCT scores — related
