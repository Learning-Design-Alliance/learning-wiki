## Evidence

### Devine 2025

Devine, R. T., Gray, L., Edwards, M., Jess, M., Dempsey, C., Heng, J., Mehrotra, M., D'Souza, H., Fink, E., & Hughes, C. (2025). Changes in Children's Well-Being and Mental Health Across the Early School Years: Links With Academic and Social Competence. Developmental Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/dev0001962

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Structural equation models regressing Time 2 social competence (teacher and caregiver ratings) onto initial levels and changes in well-being and mental health, controlling for gender, SES, verbal ability, executive function, and prosocial behavior.

> "Social competence at Time 2 was uniquely predicted by initial levels of caregiver-rated child mental health and child well-being such that mental health difficulties at Time 1 predicted poorer social competence 1 year later and positive perceptions of child well-being were associated with better social competence 1 year later."

### Devine 2025 (2)

Devine, R. T., Gray, L., Edwards, M., Jess, M., Dempsey, C., Heng, J., Mehrotra, M., D'Souza, H., Fink, E., & Hughes, C. (2025). Changes in Children's Well-Being and Mental Health Across the Early School Years: Links With Academic and Social Competence. Developmental Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/dev0001962

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same SEM series for change predictors of Time 2 social competence. The article reports gains in caregiver-rated well-being were linked with better social competence, while child-rated well-being showed no unique association.

> "Specifically, increases in mental health difficulties from Time 1 to Time 2 were associated with poorer social competence. Likewise, gains in caregiver-rated well-being from Time 1 to Time 2 were associated with better social competence. In contrast, initial levels and changes in child-rated well-being were not uniquely associated with social competence at Time 2."
