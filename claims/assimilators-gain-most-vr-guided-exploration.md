# Assimilator learners gained significantly more in the VR (guided exploration) mode than in VR (non-guided exploration) or Non VR modes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` After adjusting for pre-test scores, learning mode had a large main effect on assimilator learners' gain scores (η2 = 0.284), with guided VR highest. [→ Chwen Jen Chen 2005](#chwen-jen-chen-2005)

## Evidence

### Chwen Jen Chen 2005

Chwen Jen Chen, Seong Chong Toh, & Wan Mohd Fauzy Wan Ismail. (2005). Are Learning Styles Relevant To Virtual Reality? Journal of Research on Technology in Education, 38(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ728898

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANCOVA (pre-test as covariate) on gain scores of assimilator learners across the three learning modes in the experimental study. The article reports η2 = 0.284, "which in Cohen's (1988) terms would be considered a large eﬀect size", with adjusted means of 31.727 (guided VR), 14.807 (Non VR) and 12.455 (non-guided VR).

> "After adjusting for the pre-test scores, there was a signiﬁcant diﬀerence between the assimilator learners of the three learning modes on the gain scores, F(2, 96) = 19.017, p = 0.000."
