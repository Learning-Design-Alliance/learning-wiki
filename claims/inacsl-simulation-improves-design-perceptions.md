## Evidence

### Tu Lewei 2026

Tu Lewei, Wu Huijie, Xia Xinyi, Zhu Xuehua, Mei Binbin, Zhang Yan, Xu Mengyan, Yang Yeqin. (2026). Effects of standards of best practice in simulation-based training among midwifery students in China. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08081-y

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

SDS results from the same quasi-experimental study: students rated the simulation's structural components after training. Both the "objective/information section" and the "guided reflection/feedback section" were significantly higher in the intervention group, each with P < 0.001 as printed; no standardized effect size appears in this results sentence.

> "The intervention group demonstrated significantly higher scores than the control group in two dimensions of the Simulation Design Scale: the objective/information section (median: 4.20 [P25 = 4.0, P75 = 4.40] vs. 4.0 [P25 = 3.60, P75 = 4.20], median difference:−0.200, 95% CI: −0.400 to −0.200, P < 0.001) and the guided reflection/feedback section(median: 4.38 [P25 = 4.25, P75 = 4.75] vs. 4.0 [P25 = 4.0, P75 = 4.25], median difference:−0.250, 95% CI: −0.500 to −0.250, P < 0.001)."

### Tu Lewei 2026 (2)

Tu Lewei, Wu Huijie, Xia Xinyi, Zhu Xuehua, Mei Binbin, Zhang Yan, Xu Mengyan, Yang Yeqin. (2026). Effects of standards of best practice in simulation-based training among midwifery students in China. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08081-y

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Total Simulation Design Scale score comparison in the same study (intervention n = 62, control n = 67). The intervention group's "significantly higher total SDS score" carried a printed mean difference of -0.163 (95% CI: -0.242 to -0.084), P < 0.001, with Cohen's d = -0.718 printed in Table 6.

> "The intervention group achieved a significantly higher total SDS score (4.20 ± 0.20) than the control group, which scored 4.03 ± 0.25, with a mean difference of −0.163 (95% CI: −0.242 to −0.084, P < 0.001)"
