Yamada Rie 2025¶

Yamada Rie, Futakawa Kaori, Kondo Satoshi, Xu Kuangzhe, Okazaki Satoshi. (2025). A scoping review on the use of virtual patients for enhancing empathy in medical students. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2025.2607825

q2 · i? · review · r3

Review's mapping of technological formats (RQ4). The review reports that "virtual patient simulations were delivered using two primary technology modalities: immersive systems and desktop-based platforms", with ten desktop-based and nine immersive studies, all delivered as single-session interventions.

"Across the 19 included studies, virtual patient simulations were delivered using two primary technology modalities: immersive systems and desktop-based platforms. Ten studies used desktop-based or non-immersive platforms, including standard monitors or laptop computers [25,26,31,32,56,63,65,68,69,71]. The remaining nine studies employed immersive modalities, such as head-mounted displays or room-scale VR environments [47,62,64,66,67,70,72–74]."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Virtual patient interventions improved medical students' empathy in all 19 included studies, with effects reported immediately after intervention in 17 studies — related
  • Virtual patient scenarios focused on psychiatric and chronic diseases, with major depressive disorder and cancer most common; acute, infectious, and rare conditions were absent — related
  • Immersion principle: people do not necessarily learn better in 3D immersive virtual reality than with a corresponding 2D desktop presentation (d = -0.10 across 9 tests) — related
  • In one meta-analysis, modality-effect studies using virtual reality head-mounted displays show larger effects than studies using computer screens or audio-tapes — related
  • All included VR training interventions used non-immersive desktop systems; no immersive VR studies met the review's inclusion criteria — reports the opposite
