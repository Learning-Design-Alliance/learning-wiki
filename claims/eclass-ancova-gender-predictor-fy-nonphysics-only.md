## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2016

Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski. (2016). Research-based assessment of students’ beliefs about experimental physics: When is gender a factor?. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.020130](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.020130)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Three-way ANCOVAs splitting by major found the adjusted postinstruction mean for men significantly higher than for women for non-physics majors (F-test, p < 0.01), but the trend did not hold for physics majors (F-test, p = 0.9).

> "For non-physics majors, the adjusted postinstruc- tion mean for men was signiﬁcantly higher than the ad- justed mean for women (F-test, p < 0.01); however, the same trend did not hold for physics majors (F-test, p = 0 .9)."

### Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2016 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski. (2016). Research-based assessment of students’ beliefs about experimental physics: When is gender a factor?. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.020130](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.020130)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The initial 4-way ANCOVA including all interaction terms revealed "a signiﬁcant inter- action between level and major (F-test [34], p = 0 .04)", violating homogeneity of regression slopes and requiring separate analyses by level and major.

> "the 4-way ANCOV A revealed a signiﬁcant inter- action between level and major (F-test [34], p = 0 .04). The existence of this interaction means that the variables course level and major must be analyzed independently."
