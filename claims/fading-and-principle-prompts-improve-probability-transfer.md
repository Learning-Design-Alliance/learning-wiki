# Backward fading and principle prompts supported transfer in probability learning

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q3` · n=78 (Experiment 1)

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` In Experiment 1, backward fading versus example–problem pairs and principle-identification prompts versus no prompts each improved near- and far-transfer performance; no interaction was detected. This does not establish a special combined synergy.

`q3 · peer-reviewed experiment` · `i? · no standardized effect recorded on this page` · `n=78 (Experiment 1)` · `causal · r?`

*Transitioning From Studying Examples to Solving Problems: Effects of Self-Explanation Prompts and Fading Worked-Out Steps*. Journal of Educational Psychology, 95(4), 774–783. [Full paper](https://mrbartonmaths.com/resourcesnew/8.%20Research/Making%20the%20most%20of%20examples/Fading%20out%20and%20Prompts.pdf), Experiments 1–2, pp. 775–781.

Experiment 1 randomized 78 university students in a 2×2 design. Tasks taught probability principles. Backward fading omitted later solution steps progressively; prompts asked learners to identify principles. Near/far transfer were study-defined probability tasks, not unrestricted real-world transfer. Experiment 2 compared prompting versus no prompting within fading, rather than repeating all four cells. Full paper checked for these contrasts; no adaptive learner-specific fading threshold or delayed safety outcome is established.
