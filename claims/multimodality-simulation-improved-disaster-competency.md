# A multimodality simulation disaster education program improved hospital nurses' disaster competencies across outcomes

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a quasi-experimental one-group pretest–posttest study of 40 emergency nurses, a 12-hour multimodality simulation program produced significant pre–post improvements in disaster perception, disaster skills with and without PPE, triage performance, crisis management, and problem-solving. ## Evidence

### Delbene Lara 2026

Delbene Lara, Mezini Sara, Cacciabue Camilla, Watson Roger, Aleo Giuseppe, Di Nitto Marco, Zanini Milko, Catania Gianluca, Sasso Loredana, Bagnasco Annamaria. (2026). Effective Educational Programs for Disaster Preparedness and Management: A Rapid Evidence Assessment. International Nursing Review. https://doi.org/10.1111/inr.70193

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Quasi-experimental one-group pretest–posttest study (Noh et al. 2020, South Korea) of a 12-hour multimodality simulation program (virtual simulation, tabletop exercises, part-task trainers, full-bodied mannequin) in 40 emergency nurses. The review reports significant pre–post improvements without standardized effect sizes.

> "Significant pre–post improvements across outcomes: disaster perception score increased; disaster skills (with/without PPE) increased markedly; triage performance improved in both VR and tabletop; crisis management improved (self‐assessment and observation); problem‐solving improved; high satisfaction (overall ∼9.54/10)."
