## Subclaims
`q2 i1` In algebra-based physics I, both male and female students in EBAE courses outperformed same-gender students in LB courses on the FCI posttest, with effect sizes ranging from 0.196 to 0.324. [→ Nafis I. Karim 2018](#nafis-i-karim-2018)
`q2 i2` In calculus-based physics II, both male and female students in EBAE courses outperformed same-gender students in LB courses on the CSEM posttest, with effect sizes ranging from 0.299 to 0.623. [→ Nafis I. Karim 2018 (2)](#nafis-i-karim-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Nafis I. Karim 2018

Nafis I. Karim, Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Do evidence-based active-engagement courses reduce the gender gap in introductory physics? https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.14.010103

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Between-course comparison of algebra-based FCI posttest scores (Table III), holding the instructor fixed and combining instructors. The article reports pretest performance was "similar to the EBAE courses" and posttest effect sizes of 0.196 to 0.324 favoring EBAE for both genders.

> "However, on the posttest both male (female) students in the EBAE courses outperformed male (female) students in the LB courses (effect sizes ranging from 0.196 to 0.324)."

### Nafis I. Karim 2018 (2)

Nafis I. Karim, Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Do evidence-based active-engagement courses reduce the gender gap in introductory physics? https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.14.010103

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Between-course comparison of calculus-based CSEM posttest scores (Table IV), same-instructor and combined-instructor conditions. Effect sizes of 0.299 to 0.623 favor EBAE for both male and female students; the article notes male effect sizes were slightly higher.

> "However, on the posttest, both male (female) students in the EBAE courses outperformed male (female) students in the LB courses (effect sizes ranging from 0.299 to 0.623)."
