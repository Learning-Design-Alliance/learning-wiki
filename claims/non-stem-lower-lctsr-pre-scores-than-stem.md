## Evidence

### Moore 2011

Moore, J. C., & Rubbo, L. J. (2011). Scientific reasoning abilities of non-science majors in physics-based courses. https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.2764 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.1110.2764](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1110.2764)

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Observational comparison of pre-instruction LCTSR scores: the authors' non-STEM population (N = 109, avg. = 54%) scored significantly lower than the STEM comparison sample (N = 1208, avg. = 75%) reported by Bao et al. No significant difference was found between physics and astronomy courses or between the two institutions.

> "Table I shows average LCTSR pre-instruction scores (N = 1208, avg. = 75%) for science and engineering majors enrolled in a calculus-based introductory physics course, as reported by Bao, et al."

### J. Christopher Moore and Louis J. Rubbo 2011

J. Christopher Moore and Louis J. Rubbo. (2011). Are we teaching students to think like scientists? Originally published 2009. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1101.1090](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1101.1090)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Assessment study comparing pre-instruction LCTSR scores of non-science majors in the authors' conceptual physics and astronomy courses over three years with freshman science and engineering majors. Non-science majors averaged 54% (N = 68) versus "avg = 74.2%" (N = 1061) reported by Bao, et al.

> "students in our conceptual physics and astronomy courses score significantly lower on Lawson's Classroom Test of Scientific Reasoning (LCTSR) compared to students enrolled in courses typically populated with science majors."
