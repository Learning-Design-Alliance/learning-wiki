# In a Terracotta study across classes, retrieval practice review outperformed restudy review on subsequent exams

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who reviewed material by taking a multiple-choice test (retrieval practice) outperformed students who reviewed by reading answers to quizzes (restudy), replicating an existing finding across course types. [→ motz-result](#motz-result)
`q3 i?` Students answered three-quarters of retrieval questions correctly, and when students got a question wrong and tried it again they were more likely to get it right. [→ motz-result](#motz-result)

## Evidence

### motz-result

Manai, J., & Roschelle, J. (2024, November). Connecting SEERNet and Improvement Science to Pursue Better Outcomes in Schools. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/234

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

The white paper reports the ManyClasses 2 study by Motz et al. (2024), conducted with the Terracotta platform in Canvas, which randomized retrieval-practice versus restudy review assignments. The authors report retrieval practice was better, with students able to answer "three-quarters of the questions correctly". No effect size is printed in this paper.

> "Motz et al. (2024) found that retrieval practice was better—as expected—and they also reported some interesting findings on the retrieval questions. For example, students were able to answer three-quarters of the questions correctly, suggesting they were neither too hard nor too easy."
