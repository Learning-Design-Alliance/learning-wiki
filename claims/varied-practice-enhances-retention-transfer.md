## Evidence

### Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork 2009

Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork. (2009). Making Things Hard on Yourself, But in a Good Way: Creating Desirable Difficulties to Enhance Learning. Psychology and the Real World. https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/research/

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The chapter reports, citing Smith, Glenberg, and Bjork (1978), that varying the environmental setting of study sessions enhanced recall, a result the authors note "flies in the face of" the common advice to study in one quiet place.

> "studying the same material in two different rooms rather than twice in the same room leads to increased recall of that material (Smith, Glenberg, & Bjork, 1978)"

### Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork 2009 (2)

Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork. (2009). Making Things Hard on Yourself, But in a Good Way: Creating Desirable Difficulties to Enhance Learning. Psychology and the Real World. https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/research/

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

The chapter reports, citing Kerr and Booth (1978), a study in which 8- and 12-year-olds practiced beanbag throwing with vision occluded at either a fixed distance or varied distances; after a delay all were tested at the fixed distance, and varied practice won for both age groups.

> "Common sense would suggest that the children who practiced at the tested distance would perform better than those who had never practiced at that distance, but the opposite was true for both age groups."
