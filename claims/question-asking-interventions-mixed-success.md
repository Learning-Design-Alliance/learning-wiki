# Explicit training in constraint-seeking questions showed only moderate, non-generalizing benefits, whereas prompting explanation supports better question asking

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Direct training on constraint-seeking questions achieved only moderate success with benefits that did not generalize or persist, but prompting children to explain previous observations promoted more effective question asking without extensive training. [→ Azzurra Ruggeri 2022](#azzurra-ruggeri-2022)

## Evidence

### Azzurra Ruggeri 2022

Azzurra Ruggeri. (2022). An Introduction to Ecological Active Learning. Current Directions in Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/09637214221112114

`q1 · i?`

The review reports, citing Courage (1989), that explicit training in generating constraint-seeking questions achieved only moderate success. It separately reports, citing Ruggeri, Xu, & Lombrozo (2019), that 'prompting them to explain previous observations' can promote identification of features applying to multiple objects, supporting more effective question asking.

> "Most children did not improve their performance, and the modest training benefits, when present, did not generalize to other sets of stimuli or domains and were no longer apparent just a few days later."
