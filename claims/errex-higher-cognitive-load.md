# Learning with erroneous examples often imposed higher cognitive load than correct examples or problem-solving

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` ErrEx students reported significantly higher cognitive load than CorrEx students in clinical-scenario studies. [→ Sonja Dieterich 2025](#sonja-dieterich-2025)
`q2 i?` Mental effort was higher for ErrEx and Con-ErrEx than for problem-solving in practice-problem studies. [→ Sonja Dieterich 2025 (2)](#sonja-dieterich-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Sonja Dieterich 2025

Sonja Dieterich, Stefan Rumann, Marc Rodemer. (2025). Conditions for Effective Learning from Erroneous Examples: A Systematic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10071-x

`q2 · i?`

Table 1 entry for Kopp et al. (2009a, b), an ErrEx vs CorrEx comparison in clinical scenarios with elaborated feedback, reports "ErrEx students had a significantly higher CL than students in the CorrEx group". Cognitive load was coded as high in this study.

Table 1 entry for van Peppen et al. (2021), an ErrEx vs PS comparison using practice problems, reports "Higher ME for ErrEx than PS". The same study reported higher mental effort for Con-ErrEx than PS, and no differences in mental effort in the ErrEx vs Con-ErrEx comparison.
