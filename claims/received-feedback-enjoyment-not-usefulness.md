## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Received feedback has an important effect on enjoyment but not on perceived usefulness (tRF→EN = 1.99 vs tRF→PUS = 0.56). [→ Acosta-Gonzaga 2016](#acosta-gonzaga-2016)
`q2 i?` The indirect effects of received feedback on attitude (t = 1.85) and usage intention (t = 1.64) are small, so receiving feedback is less crucial to acceptance than expected. [→ Acosta-Gonzaga 2016 (2)](#acosta-gonzaga-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Acosta-Gonzaga 2016

Acosta-Gonzaga, E., & Walet, N. R. (2016). The role of attitudinal factors in mathematical on-line assessments: a study of undergraduate STEM students. Originally published 2014. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1603.02313](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1603.02313)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

PLS-SEM bootstrap result from the 121-student survey; only t-values are printed, no effect size.

> "'Received feedback' has an important effect on 'enjoyment' but not on 'perceived usefulness', as can be seen by the direct t-values, tRF → EN = 1.99 and tRF → PUS = 0.56."

### Acosta-Gonzaga 2016 (2)

Acosta-Gonzaga, E., & Walet, N. R. (2016). The role of attitudinal factors in mathematical on-line assessments: a study of undergraduate STEM students. Originally published 2014. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1603.02313](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1603.02313)
