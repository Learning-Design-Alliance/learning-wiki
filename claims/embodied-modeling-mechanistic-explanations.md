## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a 3rd-grade classroom, students who cycled through embodied modeling, graphing, and NetLogo agent-based modeling developed progressively more complex mechanistic explanations of emergence, including backward and forward chaining. [→ Sengupta 2018](#sengupta-2018)

## Evidence

### Sengupta 2018

Sengupta, P., Dickes, A., & Farris, A.V. (2018). Toward a Phenomenology of Computational Thinking in STEM Education. In Computational Thinking in STEM Discipline: Foundations and Research Highlights (Khine, M.S., Ed.). Springer. [doi:10.1007/978-3-319-93566-9_4](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-93566-9_4)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Classroom study (Dickes et al., 2016) in a 3rd grade: an embodied foraging activity, bar-graph inscriptions, and two butterfly-bird-flower ABMs. The authors report students "were able to develop progressively more complex forms of mechanistic explanations of emergence," engaging in chaining as termed by Russ et al. (2008). No effect size is printed.

> "As students engaged iteratively in cycles of embodied modeling and graphing, and then modeled the same phenomena using multi-agent based NetLogo simulations, we found that they were able to develop progressively more complex forms of mechanistic explanations of emergence"
