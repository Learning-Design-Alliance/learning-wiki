## Evidence

### Shih-Yin Lin 2016

Shih-Yin Lin, John M. Aiken, Daniel T. Seaton, Scott S. Douglas, Edwin F. Greco, Brian D. Thoms, Michael F. Schatz. (2016). Exploring University Students’ Engagement with Online Video Lectures in a Blended Introductory Mechanics Course. Originally published 2013. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1603.03348](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1603.03348)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Analysis of fraction of in-video content accessed (Figure 8; FSE defined as accessing at least 99% of a video). "the mean FSE is 0.54 for lecture oriented videos and 0.56 for laboratory videos," so students completed videos only about half the time.

> "When averaged over all videos in the group, the mean FSE is 0.54 for lecture oriented videos and 0.56 for laboratory videos, suggesting that when students accessed videos, only half of the time they would access almost the entirety of the video"

### Shih-Yin Lin 2016 (2)

Shih-Yin Lin, John M. Aiken, Daniel T. Seaton, Scott S. Douglas, Edwin F. Greco, Brian D. Thoms, Michael F. Schatz. (2016). Exploring University Students’ Engagement with Online Video Lectures in a Blended Introductory Mechanics Course. Originally published 2013. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1603.03348](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1603.03348)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Figure 8 trendlines (y=-0.000447x+0.758 for lecture-oriented; y=-0.000167x+0.655 for lab). "the fraction of complete-accessing students did not decrease as much with video length" for lab videos, indicating length mattered less for lab content.

> "the fraction of students accessing almost the entire video decreases with a  slope of -0.00045 per second. For the lab videos, however, the fraction of complete-accessing students did not decrease as much with video length  (slope=-0.00016 per second)"
