## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Parenting efficacy increased significantly from baseline (M = 4.60, SD = 1.10) to posttest (M = 5.00, SD = 0.80) with a medium effect (partial r = .41), while the decrease in parental distress was not statistically significant (partial r = .34, p = .072). [→ Frey 2015](#frey-2015)

## Evidence

### Frey 2015

Frey, A. J., Small, J. W., Lee, J., Walker, H. M., Seeley, J. R., Feil, E. G., & Golly, A. (2015). Expanding the range of the First Step to Success intervention: Tertiary-level support for children, teachers, and families. Early Childhood Research Quarterly 30 (2015) 1–11. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecresq.2014.05.002

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Within-subject ANOVA on the Parenting Ladder self-efficacy measure for intervention-group parents. The article reports efficacy "increased from 4.60 (SD = 1.10) at baseline to 5.00 (SD = 0.80) at posttest" with partial r = .41, significant at p = .026. The parental distress decrease (partial r = .34) was not significant (p = .072).

> "Mean scores on parenting efﬁcacy increased from 4.60 (SD = 1.10) at baseline to 5.00 (SD = 0.80) at posttest. The effect size for increases in parental efﬁcacy was .41 (medium), and the differences from baseline to post-test were sta- tistically signiﬁcant (F[1,27] = 5.53, p = .026)"
