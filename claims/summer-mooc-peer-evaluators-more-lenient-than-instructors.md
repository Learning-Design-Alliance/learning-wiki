## Evidence

### Shih-Yin Lin 2014

Shih-Yin Lin, Scott S. Douglas, John M. Aiken, Chien-Lin Liu, Edwin F. Greco, Brian D. Thoms, Marcos D. Caballero, and Michael F. Schatz. (2014). Peer Evaluation of Video Lab Reports in an Introductory Physics MOOC. [doi:10.1119/perc.2014.pr.037](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.037)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of student and instructor ratings of hidden calibration videos in the Summer 2013 offering, shown in Figure 1. The authors conclude that "students had more lenient evaluation norms than instructors", with majorities of students often rating videos higher than instructors.

> "Comparison of student s’ ratings and instructor s’ ratings in Figure 1 suggests that in summer 2013, students had more lenient evaluation norms than instructors."

### Shih-Yin Lin 2014 (2)

Shih-Yin Lin, Scott S. Douglas, John M. Aiken, Chien-Lin Liu, Edwin F. Greco, Brian D. Thoms, Marcos D. Caballero, and Michael F. Schatz. (2014). Peer Evaluation of Video Lab Reports in an Introductory Physics MOOC. [doi:10.1119/perc.2014.pr.037](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.037)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same summer 2013 hidden-calibration analysis, Lab 4 item 3: instructors rated the video negatively while "60% of the students rated it positively", a stated example of majority student ratings exceeding instructor ratings.

> "For example, while instructors rated the Lab 4 video negatively on item 3 (relevant physics principles), 60% of the students rated it positively."
