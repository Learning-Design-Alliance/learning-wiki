## Evidence

### Kohlmyer 2009

Kohlmyer, M. A., Caballero, M. D., Catrambone, R., Chabay, R. W., Ding, L., Haugan, M. P., Marr, M. J., Sherwood, B. A., & Schatz, M. F. (2009). A Tale of Two Curricula: The performance of two thousand students in introductory electromagnetism. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.0022 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0906.0022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0906.0022)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

GT item analysis dividing the 30 BEMA items a priori into four categories (electrostatics, DC circuits, magnetostatics, Faraday's Law) and computing fractional contributions to the gain difference; the article reports magnetostatics accounts for 55% of the difference and electrostatics 28%.

> "in fact M&I student performance on mag-netostatics alone accounts for more than half (55%) of the diﬀerence in performance ∆ G relative to traditional students."

### Kohlmyer 2009 (2)

A Tale of Two Curricula: The performance of two thousand students in introductory electromagnetism. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.0022 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0906.0022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0906.0022)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same GT item analysis found positive fractional differences for DC circuits (12% of the total difference) despite the article noting it was tempting to think the traditional course would train students better for those traditional questions.

> "However, Fig-ure 11 demonstrates that in fact M&I students outper-form traditional students on traditional DC-circuit ques-tions."
