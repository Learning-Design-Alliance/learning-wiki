# Parental language input differs in quantity and quality across SES groups and relates to children's vocabulary, grammar, and processing development

## Evidence

### Schwab Jessica F. 2016

Schwab Jessica F., Lew-Williams Casey. (2016). Language learning, socioeconomic status, and child-directed speech. Wiley interdisciplinary reviews. Cognitive science. https://doi.org/10.1002/wcs.1393

`q2 · i?`

Narrative review of observational studies comparing parental speech across SES groups. The review reports that "middle- and upper-middle-class parents (relative to working class parents) tend to speak more, use more word types and word tokens," with differences also in topic-continuing responses. No effect sizes are printed.

> "middle- and upper-middle-class parents (relative to working class parents) tend to speak more, use more word types and word tokens, respond more often in a topic-continuing manner to children’s utterances, and focus less on directing children’s behavior."
