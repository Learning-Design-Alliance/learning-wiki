# Among out-of-sequence students, whether the tutorial was done immediately after the patient encounter or later in the shift did not significantly affect post-test scores

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Post-test raw scores did not differ educationally or statistically between immediate and later-in-shift tutorial timing (ORS 61% vs 63%; FWS 56% vs 55%). [→ Pusic Martin V 2012](#pusic-martin-v-2012)

## Evidence

### Pusic Martin V 2012

Pusic Martin V, MacDonald Wendy A, Eisman Harley O, Black John B. (2012). Reinforcing outpatient medical student learning using brief computer tutorials: the Patient-Teacher-Tutorial sequence. BMC Medical Education, 12:70. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-70

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Planned subgroup analysis of the 51 out-of-sequence students, 26 of 51 (49%) doing the tutorial immediately and the rest later in their 8-hour shift. Post-test raw scores were nearly identical across timing groups; no test statistic or effect size printed, so equivalence was not formally tested.

> "This timing difference did not affect post-test scores to an educationally or statistically significant degree (Post-test Raw Scores: ORS: Immediate 61% vs. Later in Shift 63%; FWS 56% vs. 55%)."
