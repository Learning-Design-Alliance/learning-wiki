# Frequency of AI use predicts AI feedback preference, while comfort with technology and frequency of human feedback predict relative preference for AI over human grading

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Yildirim-Erbasli 2026

Yildirim-Erbasli, S. N., Ilgun Dibek, M., Thomas, M. L., & Lesoway, N. (2026). When Do Undergraduate Students Prefer AI? Insights into AI Scoring and Feedback. Behavioral Sciences, 16(7), 1196. https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16071196

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Ordinal logistic regression predicting AI scoring/feedback preference from comfort, familiarity, and feedback-frequency variables in the survey sample. "Frequency of AI use was a significant predictor of AI preference", with OR = 8.24; other predictors were non-significant at α = 0.05.

> "students who rarely or never used AI had higher odds of being in a higher category of AI preference compared with the reference group, B = 2.11, SE = 1.03, t = 2.05, p = 0.040, OR = 8.24."

### Yildirim-Erbasli 2026 (2)

Yildirim-Erbasli, S. N., Ilgun Dibek, M., Thomas, M. L., & Lesoway, N. (2026). When Do Undergraduate Students Prefer AI? Insights into AI Scoring and Feedback. Behavioral Sciences, 16(7), 1196. https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16071196

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Second ordinal logistic regression predicting preference for AI relative to human grading. Somewhat-uncomfortable-with-technology students had lower odds (B = −1.37, p = 0.045, OR = 0.26), and "students who received human feedback frequently had higher odds" (OR = 3.45).

> "students who received human feedback frequently had higher odds of being in a higher category of preference for AI-based scoring and feedback compared with students receiving monthly feedback, B = 1.24, SE = 0.58, t = 2.13, p = 0.033, OR = 3.45."
