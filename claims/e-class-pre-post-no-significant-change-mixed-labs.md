# Pre/post E-CLASS scores showed a small decrease in expert-like responses and no statistically significant pre-post differences in a partially reformed lab course

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Average E-CLASS score decreased from 0.53 to 0.48, with no statistically significant differences between pre and post scores. [→ Doucette 2019](#doucette-2019)

## Evidence

### Doucette 2019

Doucette, D., Clark, R., and Singh, C. (2019). What's happening in traditional and inquiry-based introductory labs? An integrative analysis at a large research university. [doi:10.1119/perc.2018.pr.doucette](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2018.pr.doucette)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

E-CLASS survey administered in the second week (49 valid responses) and second-to-last week (33 valid responses) of the course, scored -1 to +1 against expert-like responses. The article reports "the average score decreasing from 0.53 to 0.48" and, for Fig. 1, "No statistically signiﬁcant differences are found between pre and post scores (Mann-Whitney U test, multiple comparison correction [12])."

> "We also compared E-CLASS pre and post scores. The overall effect is a decrease in expert-like responses, with the average score decreasing from 0.53 to 0.48 (on a scale from -1 to +1)."
