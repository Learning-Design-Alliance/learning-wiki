## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Reversing the Recognition-Interest arrow in the Kalender et al. SEM yields an equivalent graph in which the Gender-Recognition coefficient falls to a small, statistically insignificant value. [→ M. B. Weissman 2021](#m-b-weissman-2021)

## Evidence

### M. B. Weissman 2021

M. B. Weissman. (2021). On Policy Recommendations from Causal Inference in Physics Education Research. arXiv preprint, in press in Phys. Rev. Phys. Educ. Res. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020118](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020118)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical re-analysis (author's own Markov-equivalent graph, sample size of 559 stated by the original paper) showing the reversed-arrow graph changes which gender effect appears dominant. The path coefficients are unstandardized SEM coefficients, not effect sizes.

> "The coefficient between Interest and Recognition would increase slightly, from 0.64 to 0.67. The coefficient from Gender to Interest would increase from 0.16 to 0.33, and the coefficient from Gender to Recognition would fall from 0.27 to 0.05."

### M. B. Weissman 2021 (2)

M. B. Weissman. (2021). On Policy Recommendations from Causal Inference in Physics Education Research. arXiv preprint, in press in Phys. Rev. Phys. Educ. Res. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020118](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020118)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The author's conclusion about the Kalender et al. data: an equivalent graph with a Gender-Recognition coefficient one third the smallest included coefficient, with a printed 95% confidence interval of approximately (-0.05, 0.15), would conventionally be dropped as insignificant.

> "The data shown are exactly as compatible with a very small and statistically insignificant  effect of gender on perceived recognition as they are with the moderate-size effect presented."
