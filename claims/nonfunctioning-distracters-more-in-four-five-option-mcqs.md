## Evidence

### Vegada Bhavisha 2016

Vegada Bhavisha, Shukla Apexa, Khilnani Ajeetkumar, Charan Jaykaran, Desai Chetna. (2016). Comparison between three option, four option and five option multiple choice question tests for quality parameters: A randomized study. Indian Journal of Pharmacology. https://doi.org/10.4103/0253-7613.190757

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Distracter analysis (Table 4) of the randomized comparison found most questions had two functioning distracters regardless of format; "Nondiscriminating distracters were more in four option and five option groups as compared to three option groups."

> "Mean functioning distracters per items were 1.50, 1.77, and 2.37 for three option, four option, and five option groups, respectively. Nondiscriminating distracters were more in four option and five option groups as compared to three option groups [Table 4]."
