# LCFF-induced spending increases significantly improved math and reading achievement in every grade assessed, with the largest gains in districts receiving concentration grants

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` LCFF significantly improved math and reading achievement in all tested grades (3rd–8th and 11th), with students in districts receiving concentration grants showing the largest achievement boost. [→ Johnson 2023](#johnson-2023)
`q2 i?` Pre-LCFF placebo tests found no positive kink in achievement or revenues at the 55% disadvantage threshold, supporting a causal interpretation. [→ Johnson 2023 (2)](#johnson-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Johnson 2023

Johnson, R. C. (2023). School funding effectiveness: Evidence from California's Local Control Funding Formula. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/529.194

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental comparison of successive cohorts within the same schools as LCFF funding phased in. The kink at 55% high-need concentration shows larger achievement gains where concentration grants raised funding levels.

> "LCFF resulted in significantly improved achievement in math and reading for all grade levels tested. Students in districts that received LCFF concentration grants had the largest achievement boost."

### Johnson 2023 (2)

Johnson, R. C. (2023). School funding effectiveness: Evidence from California's Local Control Funding Formula. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/529.194

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Falsification test using pre-LCFF cohorts (Figures 7–8): the author reports no kink at the 55% disadvantage threshold before the reform, and no comparable pattern among Basic Aid districts ineligible for LCFF funds.

> "there is no positive kink relationship in either per-pupil revenues (at 55%) or student achievement (at 55%) for pre-LCFF cohorts; the relationship is indeed flat and statistically insignificant"
