# Reading a brief online female role model letter raised course grades for women in an introductory psychology course (d = .24)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small
> **Currency** · newest study 2016 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Female students randomly assigned to read the role model letter earned higher introductory psychology course grades than control students, a small effect (d = .24). [→ Herrmann et al. 2016](#herrmann-et-al-2016)

## Evidence

### Herrmann et al. 2016

Sarah D. Herrmann, Robert Mark Adelman, Jessica E. Bodford, Oliver Graudejus, Morris A. Okun, and Virginia S. Y. Kwan (2016). The Effects of a Female Role Model on Academic Performance and Persistence of Women in STEM Courses. Basic and Applied Social Psychology, 38(5), 258-268. https://doi.org/10.1080/01973533.2016.1209757

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Randomized experiment in an introductory psychology course: 258 female students were assigned to the role model letter or control after Exam 1; with consent (n à 240), semester grades were collected from the University Office of Institutional Analysis. "A d of .24" was reported as a small effect.

> "participants in the intervention condition had higher course grades (n à 120, M à 2.93, SD à 1.07) compared to participants in the control condition ( n à 120, M à 2.64, SD à 1.31). A d of .24 indicates that participants in the intervention group on average earned approximately one fourth of a standard deviation higher grade"
