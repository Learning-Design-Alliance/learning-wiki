## Evidence

### Lachlan P. McGinness and C. M. Savage 2016

Lachlan P. McGinness and C. M. Savage. (2016). Developing an Action Concept Inventory. arXiv:1605.01786. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010133](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010133)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

The ACI was administered to 61 ANU physicists (22 academics, 23 PhD students, 16 later-year undergraduates or masters students) as an adjunct to measuring student learning; the article reports the group did "signiﬁcantly better than the ﬁrst-year students" on most questions, with no effect size printed.

> "Hence, we administered it to 61 ANU physicists: 22 were academics, 23 Ph.D. students, and the remaining 16 were third or fourth year undergraduates, or masters students. For most questions this group did signiﬁcantly better than the ﬁrst-year students."

### Lachlan P. McGinness and C. M. Savage 2016 (2)

Lachlan P. McGinness and C. M. Savage. (2016). Developing an Action Concept Inventory. arXiv:1605.01786. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010133](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010133)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

On valid path-concept question 10, the physicists performed much worse than post-instruction Physics 2 students, many of whom had mastered the concept that path boundaries are events specified by a time and a place through repeatedly calculating actions along paths.

> "Interestingly, the physicists did much worse than post-instruction Physics 2 students on one of the questions concerning the path concept, even though the question was valid (question 10 [6])."

### Lachlan P. McGinness and C. M. Savage 2016 (3)

Lachlan P. McGinness and C. M. Savage. (2016). Developing an Action Concept Inventory. arXiv:1605.01786. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010133](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010133)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Comparison of physicist responses with prior post-test student interview findings showed convergence on two problematic questions, which the authors suggest could accelerate inventory development since physicists are available outside fixed class schedules.

> "Two of the questions they did worse on had already been identiﬁed as needing revision as a result of the post-test student interviews, suggesting that expert groups can help identify question validity."
