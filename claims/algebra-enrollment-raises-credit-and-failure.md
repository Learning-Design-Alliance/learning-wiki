# Taking Algebra I instead of remedial math raised Algebra credit attainment but increased failure rates and lowered math grades, with no test-score benefit

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A 20 percentage-point increase in Algebra I enrollment increased credit earning by about 10% but raised math failure rates among low- and average-ability students and lowered grades. [→ Allensworth 2008](#allensworth-2008)

## Evidence

### Allensworth 2008

Allensworth, E., Nomi, T., Montgomery, N., & Lee, V. E. (2008). College Preparatory Curriculum for All: Consequences of Ninth-Grade Course Taking in Algebra and English on Academic Outcomes in Chicago. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/college-preparatory-curriculum-all-consequences-ninth-grade-coursetaking-algebra-and

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Interrupted time-series model results shown as simulations in Table 2, holding enrollment change at 20 percentage points across ability groups. Credit earning rose (8.0% to 11.6% depending on ability), math grades declined most among average-ability students (0.18 grade points, p<.05), and math test scores were unaffected.

> "Math failure rates increased among low-ability students (3.0 percentage points, p<.01) and also among average-ability students (8.9 percentage points, p<.05)."
