`q2 i3` Achievement correlated positively with self-confidence, liking, usefulness, motivation and context (r=.80 to .88, p<.001) and negatively with anxiety (r=-.20); the male-domain correlation is reported inconsistently (r=.08, not significant, in Table 4; r=.57 in the text). ## Evidence

### Awofala 2014

Awofala, A. O. A. (2014). Examining Personalisation of Instruction, Attitudes toward and Achievement in Mathematics Word Problems among Nigerian Senior Secondary School Students. International Journal of Education in Mathematics, Science and Technology, 2(4), 273-288. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1059026

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Pearson Product Moment correlations on the total sample (n=350) reported in Table 4 and the results text. The five attitude dimensions correlated positively with achievement at r=.80 to .88 (p<.001); anxiety correlated negatively, r=-.20 (p<.001). For male domain the article contradicts itself: Table 4 gives r=.08 with no significance marker, while the results text quoted below gives r=.57, p<.001, so the male-domain correlation cannot be taken as established. Treatment (personalised instruction) was positively related to achievement (r=.22, p<.001). The correlations show association only, not that attitudes cause achievement.

> "results showed the existence of a positive correlation between achievement in mathematics word problem and self -confidence (r=.82, p<.001), liking (r=.80, p<.001), usefulness (r=.87, p<.001), motivation (r=.88, p<.001), context (r=.86, p<.001 ) and male domain (r=.57, p<.001). Anxiety toward mathematics word problem had a negative correlation with achievement in mathematics word problem (r= -.20, p<.001)."
