# Students from racial or ethnic groups historically marginalized in STEM reported less interest in quantum careers than white and Asian students, and this difference persisted when only physical science and computer science majors were analyzed

> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across all majors, students from historically marginalized racial/ethnic groups had less interest in quantum careers than white and Asian students. [→ Jessica L. Rosenberg 2024](#jessica-l-rosenberg-2024)
`q2 i?` Unlike the gender difference, the race/ethnicity difference in interest remained significant when analysis was restricted to computer science and physical science majors. [→ Jessica L. Rosenberg 2024 (2)](#jessica-l-rosenberg-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Jessica L. Rosenberg 2024

Jessica L. Rosenberg, Nancy Holincheck, Michele Colandene. (2024). Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics Undergraduates’ Knowledge and Interest in Quantum Careers: Barriers and Opportunities to Building a Diverse Quantum Workforce. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010138](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010138)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Independent samples t-test across all majors found historically marginalized students (M = 2.433) less interested than white and Asian students (M = 3.07), t(111) = -2.693, p=0.008; no effect size printed.

> "students with a racial or ethnic background that is historically marginalized in STEM (Black, Hispanic, and Native American; n = 30, M = 2.433, SD = 1.00) had less interest in pursuing a career in quantum than students who were not from a racial or ethnic group historically marginalized in STEM (white and Asian; n = 83, M = 3.07, SD = 1.13, t(111) = -2.693, p=0.008)"

### Jessica L. Rosenberg 2024 (2)

Jessica L. Rosenberg, Nancy Holincheck, Michele Colandene. (2024). Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics Undergraduates’ Knowledge and Interest in Quantum Careers: Barriers and Opportunities to Building a Diverse Quantum Workforce. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010138](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010138)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Repeating the t-test for computer science and physical science majors only, historically marginalized students (M = 2.532) still reported less interest than white and Asian students (M = 3.35), t(87) = -3.269, p<0.001.

> "For computer science and physical science majors, students from a group that is historically marginalized in STEM (Black, Hispanic, and Native American; n = 22, M = 2.532, SD = 1.09) had less interest in pursuing a career in quantum than students who were not from a racial or ethnic group historically marginalized in STEM (white and Asian; n = 67, M = 3.35, SD = 0.99, t(87) = -3.269, p<0.001)"
