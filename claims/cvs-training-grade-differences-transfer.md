# Within the Training-Probe condition, third- and fourth-graders' performance in later phases, including transfer, was higher than in Exploration, whereas second-graders' transfer performance was not significantly higher than their exploration performance, in one group with no comparison group.

## Evidence

### Chen 1999

Zhe Chen and David Klahr. (1999). All Other Things Being Equal: Acquisition and Transfer of the Control of Variables Strategy. Child Development, 70(5), 1098-1120. https://www.jstor.org/stable/1132052

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Grade-level ANOVAs within the Training-Probe condition of the hands-on study found that "only the third and fourth graders improved their performance over phases, ps \< .005", with paired comparisons showing each later phase better than Exploration, ps \< .01.

> "For children in the Training-Probe condition (Figure 4A), one-way ANOVAs revealed that only the third and fourth graders improved their performance over phases, ps \< .005."

### Chen 1999 (2)

Zhe Chen and David Klahr. (1999). All Other Things Being Equal: Acquisition and Transfer of the Control of Variables Strategy. Child Development, 70(5), 1098-1120. https://www.jstor.org/stable/1132052

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

For second graders in the Training-Probe condition, overall improvement was marginal, p = .10; the Assessment-Exploration difference was "marginally significant, p = .084", and "their transfer performance was not significantly higher than the exploration performance." No effect size printed.

> "Paired comparisons showed that the difference in the performance be- tween the Assessment and Exploration phases was marginally significant, p = .084, and that their trans- fer performance was not significantly higher than the exploration performance."
