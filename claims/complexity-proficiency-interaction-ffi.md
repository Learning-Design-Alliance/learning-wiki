# Grammatical complexity and L2 proficiency interact: complex features benefit more from input at low proficiency, while simple features benefit more from output at high proficiency

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Analysis of individual studies suggests complex grammatical features benefit more from input-based FFI when proficiency is low, whereas simple features benefit more from output-based instruction when proficiency is high. [→ Schenck 2024](#schenck-2024)

## Evidence

### Schenck 2024

Schenck, A. (2024). Learning How to Use Input-Based and Output-Based Form-Focused Instruction: A Meta-Analytic Comparison of Persian EFL Learners. Journal of Second Language Acquisition and Teaching, 30. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1457419

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Qualitative examination of individual studies, supported by two studies of intermediate learners (modals, conditionals) with contrasting results; the article reports this relationship "appears evident within past research of Corrective Feedback" as well.

> "Whereas complex grammatical features tended to benefit more from input-based FFI when proficiency was low, simplistic grammatical features tended to benefit more from output-based instruction when proficiency is high."
