## Evidence

### Carter 2016

Carter, B. E., and Wiles, J. R. (2016). Comparing effects of comedic and authoritative video presentations on student knowledge and attitudes about climate change. Bioscene, 42(1), 16-24. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1103782

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Paired t-tests on HICCK pre/post scores in the quasi-experiment showed a significant decrease for every group; the article calls it "a slight but significant decrease in actual understanding of climate change science for each group." Only p-values and mean differences are printed, no effect size.

> "For the Control group p = 0.00116 and mean of differences = -3.539; the IPCC group had p < 0.001, and mean of difference = -3.667, and the Comedy group p < 0.001 and mean of differences = -4.69."

### Carter 2016 (2)

Carter, B. E., and Wiles, J. R. (2016). Comparing effects of comedic and authoritative video presentations on student knowledge and attitudes about climate change. Bioscene, 42(1), 16-24. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1103782

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Political-leanings comparison on the same pre/post HICCK measure: both liberals and conservatives showed significant decreases with nearly identical mean differences. No effect size is printed.

> "Climate science knowledge significantly decreased pre- to post-intervention for both liberals (p < 0.001, mean of differences = -3.978) and conservatives (p < 0.001, mean of differences = -4.372)."
