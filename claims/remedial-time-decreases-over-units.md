# Evidence on remedial time across instructional units supports Bloom's claim that differences between fast and slow learners diminish under mastery learning

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Reanalysis of Arlin's (1984a) data shows remedial time needed decreased significantly over units, with unit 9 requiring only one-fourth the remedial time of unit 2, though the total-time ratio trend was not statistically significant. [→ Guskey 1985](#guskey-1985)

## Evidence

### Guskey 1985

Guskey, Thomas R.; Gates, Sally L. (1985). A Synthesis of Research on Group-Based Mastery Learning Programs. Paper presented at the Annual Meeting of the American Educational Research Association, Chicago, IL. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED262088

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Reanalysis of data from Arlin's (1984a) two-week study of elementary students across ten instructional units, plotted in Figures 2-5 and Table 5; the article reports a statistically significant linear reduction in remedial time, while the total-time ratio trend was not statistically significant but directionally supportive.

> "the amount of remedial time needed to bring students to a mastery criterion decreased over instructional units.In fact, the amount of remedial time needed in unit was only, one-fourth what was needed for unit 2"
