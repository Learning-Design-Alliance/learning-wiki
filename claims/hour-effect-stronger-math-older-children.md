## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The time-of-day effect is more pronounced for tests in mathematics and for older children, while evidence of heterogeneous break effects across subject and age is limited. [→ Sievertsen HH 2016](#sievertsen-hh-2016)

## Evidence

### Sievertsen HH 2016

Sievertsen HH, Gino F, Piovesan M. (2016). Cognitive fatigue influences students' performance on standardized tests. PNAS 113(10): 2621–2624. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1516947113

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subsample analyses of the full Danish test dataset split by subject (mathematics vs. reading) and age (young vs. old). The authors report "limited evidence of heterogeneous effect of breaks across subject... and age" but a more pronounced hourly effect for mathematics and older children. No effect sizes are printed.

> "we then conduct the same analyses for various subsamples but find limited evidence of heterogeneous effect of breaks across subject (i.e., mathematics vs. reading) and age (i.e., young vs. old). For hour of the day, the effect is more pronounced for tests in mathematics and older children."
