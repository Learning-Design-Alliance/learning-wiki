## Evidence

### Iordanou 2025

Iordanou, K., Fotiou, C., Manoli, A., & Zembylas, M. (2025). Examining myside bias on a controversial historical event after engagement in dialogic argumentation: Insights from a think aloud study. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102209

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

GLMM results comparing reasoning between the two text-protocols in the think-aloud study. The article attributes the asymmetry to greater familiarity: the events of the invasion are discussed in Greek-Cypriot schools and at home, so students' knowledge was reflected in associations and valid inferences.

> "participants used more valid elaborative inferences, relevant associations, statements referring to consequences for the present, and evaluative comments in the “own-side” text- protocol compared to the “other-side” text-protocol."

### Iordanou 2025 (2)

Iordanou, K., Fotiou, C., Manoli, A., & Zembylas, M. (2025). Examining myside bias on a controversial historical event after engagement in dialogic argumentation: Insights from a think aloud study. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102209

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

GLMM results on think-aloud code counts (Tables 1 and 2, Fig. 1) showing the reverse pattern for the other-side text-protocol: more invalid elaborative inferences and more other-side-supporting opinion statements than in the own-side protocol.

> "In contrast, participants made more invalid elaborative inferences and more opinion statements supporting the other-side in the “other-side” compared to the “own-side” text-protocol (see Tables 1 and 2, and Fig. 1)."
