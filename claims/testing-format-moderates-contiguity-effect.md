# Contiguity effects are larger under one-on-one testing (d = .89) than under group testing (d = .65), but remain educationally significant in both

## Evidence

### Ginns 2006

Ginns, P. (2006). Integrating information: A meta-analysis of the spatial contiguity and temporal contiguity effects. Learning and Instruction, 16(6), 511-525. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2006.10.001

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Moderator analysis of type of testing within the meta-analysis; the article reports the one-on-one versus group contrast as significant (zcontrast = 2.61, p = 0.009 in Results), with the large one-on-one effect "d = .89, 95% CI .75 – 1.03". The discussion notes group-testing effects still exceed benchmarks for educational significance.

> "Studies which used one -on-one testing had larger effect sizes ( d = .89, 95% CI .75 – 1.03) than studies which used group testing ( d = .65, 95% CI .54 – .76)."
