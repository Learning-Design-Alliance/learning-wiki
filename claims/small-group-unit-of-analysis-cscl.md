# CSCL should focus analytic attention on small-group interactions rather than individuals or society

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Theoretical argument: meaningful learning interactions and educational outcomes should be studied at the small-group level of analysis rather than at individual or societal levels in CSCL. [→ Gerry Stahl 2012](#gerry-stahl-2012)

## Evidence

### Gerry Stahl 2012

Gerry Stahl. (2012). Ethnomethodologically informed. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning 7:1–10. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-012-9142-3

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical/theoretical argument in Stahl's editorial: EM makes the meaning-making processes of group learning visible without inferring hidden mental states or social structures, supporting a small-group analytic focus in CSCL rather than individual or societal levels.

> "one does not have to make inferences about hidden changes in mental models or invisible social structures. Furthermore, EM studies can focus on the small-group unit of analysis, which seems most appropriate to analyzing collaborative learning."
