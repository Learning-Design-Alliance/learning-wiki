Evidence¶

Cui 2026¶

Cui, Y., Yan, W., Chiu, T. K. F., & Nakajima, T. (2026). How motivation and roles influence metacognitive engagement in student-GenAI interaction. International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education, 23(34). https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00610-z

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Lag sequential analysis of coded dialogues by role group (significant transitions at z > 1.96). The article states this complete M1 to M6 sequence was unique to the Collaborator group, suggesting students sustain high-order engagement once reached.

"A continuous, uninterrupted sequential chain was identified traversing all six categories: M1 →M2 (z = 2.00), M2→M3 (z = 2.97), M3→M4 (z = 2.36), M4→M5 (z = 2.22), and M5 →M6 (z = 3.53)."

Cui 2026 (2)¶

Cui, Y., Yan, W., Chiu, T. K. F., & Nakajima, T. (2026). How motivation and roles influence metacognitive engagement in student-GenAI interaction. International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education, 23(34). https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00610-z

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

LSA for the Replacement Tool group found a highly constrained sequential structure consisting exclusively of self-loops; the article reports no cross-category transitions and that Evaluation and Elaboration were entirely absent from the sequential map.

"Only two statistically significant transitions were iden -tified: Orientation (M1 →M1, z = 3.71) and Execution (M3 →M3, z = 3.29)."
