# No significant differences between STAC intervention and control groups in frequency of using the STAC strategies at 30-day follow-up

> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Chi-square analyses found no significant intervention-control differences for any of the four STAC strategies (stealing the show, turning it over, accompanying others, coaching compassion). [→ Midgett 2016](#midgett-2016)
`q3 i?` Coaching compassion was used least frequently by both groups, which the authors attribute to its greater risk-taking demands. [→ Midgett 2016](#midgett-2016)

## Evidence

### Midgett 2016

Midgett, A., Doumas, D., Trull, R., & Johnston, A. (2016). A Randomized Controlled Study Evaluating a Brief, Bystander Bullying Intervention with Junior High School Students. Boise State University. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1146195

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Dichotomized 2 x 2 chi-square analyses at 30-day follow-up in the randomized controlled study. For example, "76% in the intervention group reported using stealing the show, relative to 89% in the control group, χ2(1) = 0.63, p = .43, φ = .11"; no equivalence testing was reported.

> "Contrary to our hypothesis, results of the chi square analyses did not indicate significant differences between the intervention and control groups for frequency of using the STAC strategies at the 30-day follow-up."
