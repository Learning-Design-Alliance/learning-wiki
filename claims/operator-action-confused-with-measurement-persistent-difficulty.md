# Among students, the proportion confusing the action of an operator on a quantum state with measurement fell from pre-test to post-test after the CQS but remained around 40%, so the confusion remained a persistent student difficulty

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After the CQS, around 40% of students still mistakenly rated statements equating operator action with measurement as true; performance on subsequent CQS questions was higher than earlier in the sequence, and post-test scores were higher than pre-test scores, in one group with no comparison group. ## Evidence

### Peter Hu 2023

Peter Hu, Yangqiuting Li, and Chandralekha Singh. (2023). Challenges in addressing student difficulties with quantum measurement of two-state quantum systems using a multiple-choice question sequence in online and in-person classes. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020130](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020130)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Classroom pretest/post-test data (questions 1 and 2a) across all three implementations show students gravitated strongly to the incorrect operator-as-measurement idea after lecture, improved after CQS questions 1.1-1.3, yet "around 40%" still held the difficulty on the post-test.

> "This improvement was reflected on the post-test, but a significant fraction of students (around 40%) continued to mistakenly rate a statement such as 𝑄̂|𝜒⟩ = 𝑞1|𝑞1⟩ or 𝑄̂|𝜒⟩ = 𝑞2|𝑞2⟩ as true"
