# In Experiment 1, learners interleaved more only when high utility value of distinguishing was combined with instruction that differences matter, with the two factors interacting rather than showing main effects

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Differences-matter instruction yielded a main effect on informative between-switches, with instructed participants interleaving more frequently than uninstructed participants. [→ Roman Abel 2024](#roman-abel-2024)
`q3 i1` Utility value and differences-matter instruction interacted on informative between-switches: only participants learning poisonous and edible mushrooms interleaved more with the instruction. [→ Roman Abel 2024 (2)](#roman-abel-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Roman Abel 2024

Roman Abel, Anique de Bruin, Erdem Onan, Julian Roelle. (2024). Why Do Learners (Under)Utilize Interleaving in Learning Confusable Categories? The Role of Metastrategic Knowledge and Utility Value of Distinguishing. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09902-0

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

In the 2×2 between-subjects online experiment (N = 190 university students), the informative between-switches analysis showed the instruction main effect: "participants with instruction interleaved more frequently (M = 13.18, SE = 1.59) than participants without instruction (M = 6.07, SE = 1.61)"; ηp2 = 0.05.

> "Differences matter instruction yielded a main effect, F(1, 186) = 9.94, p = 0.002, ηp2 = 0.05, indicating that participants with instruction interleaved more frequently (M = 13.18, SE = 1.59) than participants without instruction (M = 6.07, SE = 1.61)."

### Roman Abel 2024 (2)

Roman Abel, Anique de Bruin, Erdem Onan, Julian Roelle. (2024). Why Do Learners (Under)Utilize Interleaving in Learning Confusable Categories? The Role of Metastrategic Knowledge and Utility Value of Distinguishing. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09902-0

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Simple-effects follow-up of the interaction (F(1, 186) = 5.19, p = 0.024, ηp2 = 0.03) in Experiment 1: "Participants who learned poisonous and edible mushrooms interleaved more when given the differences matter instruction compared to when given no instruction"; the low-utility groups were not affected (p = 0.541).

> "Participants who learned poisonous and edible mushrooms interleaved more when given the differences matter instruction compared to when given no instruction, p < 0.001, 95% CI [6.02, 18.47], MD = 12.25, SE = 3.16."
