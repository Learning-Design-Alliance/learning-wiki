### Meaders Clara L. 2025

Meaders Clara L., Mendez Lilyan, Aguilar Alfonso Godínez, Rivera Angelita T., Vasquez Izabella, Mueller Liam O., Owens Melinda T. (2025). An Asynchronous Chemistry-in-biology Intervention Improves Student Content Knowledge and Performance in Introductory Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education 24(1). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.24-05-0151

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Linear mixed modeling of prequiz scores (N = 1241 intervention-quarter students; conditional R squared = 0.145) identified significant opportunity gaps for women or nonbinary students, students with less chemistry coursework, and historically underserved students. Prequiz median was 61% (IQR = 34%).

> "The best fitting linear mixed model supported our hypothesis and explained 14.5% of the variation in student scores"

### Meaders Clara L. 2025 (2)

Meaders Clara L., Mendez Lilyan, Aguilar Alfonso Godínez, Rivera Angelita T., Vasquez Izabella, Mueller Liam O., Owens Melinda T. (2025). An Asynchronous Chemistry-in-biology Intervention Improves Student Content Knowledge and Performance in Introductory Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education 24(1). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.24-05-0151

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

From the same regression (Table 6), the no-prior-chemistry coefficient was −19.08 (p = 7.25e-10) and the PEER first-generation coefficient was −11.0 (p = 2.56e-14); these are unstandardized coefficients, so no standardized effect size is reported.

> "the model predicted that students without prior chemistry coursework would receive a score 19 percentage points lower on the prequiz than their classmates who had taken college-level chemistry"
