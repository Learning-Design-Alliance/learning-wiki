# Exposure to high-quality assignments predicted substantially higher IGAP reading, mathematics, and writing performance (effect sizes 0.43, 0.64, 0.52)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Two otherwise similar students would differ by 32 points on IGAP reading, 48 points on IGAP math, and 2.3 points on the IGAP writing rubric depending on exposure to high- versus low-quality assignments, corresponding to effect sizes of 0.43, 0.64, and 0.52. [→ Fred M. Newmann 2001](#fred-m-newmann-2001)

## Evidence

### Fred M. Newmann 2001

Fred M. Newmann, Anthony S. Bryk, Jenny K. Nagaoka. (2001). Authentic Intellectual Work and Standardized Tests: Conflict or Coexistence? Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/authentic-intellectual-work-and-standardized-tests-conflict-or-coexistence

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Value-added analysis of IGAP reading, mathematics, and writing outcomes for classrooms differing in assignment quality, controlling for demographics and prior-year ITBS scores. The illustrative contrast between two comparable students yields printed effect sizes of "0.43, 0.64, and 0.52, respectively."

> "student A would, according to our analyses, outperform his or her schoolmate, student B, by 32 points on the IGAP reading test and by 48 points on the IGAP math test. He or she would also be predicted to score 2.3 points higher on the IGAP writing rubric. These differences translate into standard effect sizes of 0.43, 0.64, and 0.52, respectively."
