Evidence¶

Kidd 2015¶

Kidd, Celeste and Hayden, Benjamin Y. (2015). The psychology and neuroscience of curiosity. Neuron. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.010

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports that in the Kang et al. (2009) trivia study, "curiosity enhances learning", with the learning effect particularly strong on trials where subjects' guesses were incorrect. The review presents this as support for curiosity's function being to motivate learning.

"Kang and colleagues also found that curiosity enhances learning, consistent with the theory that the primary function of curiosity is to facilitate learning."

Kidd 2015 (2)¶

Kidd, Celeste and Hayden, Benjamin Y. (2015). The psychology and neuroscience of curiosity. Neuron. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.010

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports the Gruber et al. (2014) fMRI study in which subjects answered trivia questions and rated curiosity while neutral faces were interleaved as a learning probe. The curiosity state "led to better learning, even for the things people weren't curious about", with memory correlated with midbrain and hippocampal activity.

"When tested later, subjects recalled the faces shown in high curiosity trials better than faces shown on low curiosity trials."
