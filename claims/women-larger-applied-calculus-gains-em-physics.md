Student gender modulates the intersection of calculus prof iciency and calculus self-efficacy in an introductory electricity and magnetism course. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.21.010130](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.21.010130)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Repeated survey analysis of matched General Physics II students earning an A (129 men, 61 women) across three semester deployments. Women's applied calculus scores rose from (13 ± 2)% to (36 ± 4)%, Z-statistic 4.74, versus men's rise from (20 ± 2)% to (27 ± 3)%, Z-statistic 1.92, as "Women thus disp layed a larger improvement in scores on the “applied” calculus questions".

> "Women thus disp layed a larger improvement in scores on the “applied” calculus questions and a smaller dec rease in scores on the “pure” calculus questions when compared to men."
