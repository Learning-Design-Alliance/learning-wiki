## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Overall performance scores for managing an unconscious infant in a low-fidelity simulation significantly differed across five groups differing in time since training (p < 0.0001), with the highest scores in the group trained less than 0.5 year prior. ## Evidence

### Ansquer Raihei 2019

Ansquer Raihei, Mesnier Thomas, Farampour Farnam, Oriot Denis, Ghazali Daniel Aiham. (2019). Long-term retention assessment after simulation-based-training of pediatric procedural skills among adult emergency physicians: a multicenter observational study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1793-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional observational comparison of 107 emergency physicians and residents in five groups by time since the PEP simulation course, assessed on a low-fidelity infant scenario with the TAPAS scale. "The highest scores were measured in G1: 85 [80; 90]." Kruskal-Wallis main effect p < 0.0001; no effect size printed.

> "Performance scores significantly decreased over time (p < 0.0001) (Fig. 1). The highest scores were measured in G1: 85 [80; 90]."
