# Biostatistics and research methodology coursework is associated with prior EBM training, while hospital clinical training is associated with lower EBM training

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Attendance at biostatistics or research methodology courses was significantly associated with prior EBM training (both p < 0.001). [→ Hamza K 2026](#hamza-k-2026)
`q2 i?` Participation in clinical training at hospitals was significantly associated with a lower proportion of students trained in evidence-based practice (p < 0.001). [→ Hamza K 2026 (2)](#hamza-k-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Hamza K 2026

Hamza K, Qawasmeh H, Ahmad Rasha K, Alkhateeb S, Awawdeh F, Douden B, Zrineh A, Akwan R. (2026). Knowledge, attitudes, and skills towards evidence-based medicine among Palestinian undergraduate medical students: a cross-sectional study. BMC Med Educ. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08750-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Chi-square comparisons in Table 1 of the 709-student survey; research methodology course attendance was likewise significant (p < 0.001). Printed percentages and p values only.

> "Received or attended any physical or online course in biostatistics (Yes)	327 (46.1%)	186 (56.9%)	141 (43.1%)	< 0.001"

### Hamza K 2026 (2)

Hamza K, Qawasmeh H, Ahmad Rasha K, Alkhateeb S, Awawdeh F, Douden B, Zrineh A, Akwan R. (2026). Knowledge, attitudes, and skills towards evidence-based medicine among Palestinian undergraduate medical students: a cross-sectional study. BMC Med Educ. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08750-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Chi-square comparison of clinical-training participation by EBM training status in the same survey; the authors interpret this as reflecting implementation constraints in busy clinical environments rather than lack of perceived value.

> "Nonetheless, participation in clinical training at hospitals was significantly associated with a lower proportion of students trained in evidence-based practice (p value < 0.001)."
