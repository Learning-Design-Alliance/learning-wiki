The review's coding of theoretical frameworks across the 156 included studies, extracted from each study's literature review. It reports 85.3% used no specific motivational theory.

"With regard to the motivational theories used to inform the application of AI, surprisingly, we found that 133 out of 156 studies (85.3%) did not rely on any specific motivational theory."

Jiajing Li, Ronnel B. King, Ching Sing Chai, Vivian WY Lee & Ruyi Li. (2026). AI and student motivation: A scoping review. Journal of Research on Technology in Education. https://doi.org/10.1080/15391523.2026.2672515

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review's breakdown of the 23 theory-grounded studies. Self-determination theory was most common (9.0%), followed by social cognitive theory (3.2%) and interest theory (1.9%); one study used the ARCS model.

"Specifically, 14 articles (9.0%) applied self-determination theory to design AI interventions (e.g., An, 2024; Chiu et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2024), five articles (3.2%) applied social cognitive theory (e.g., Tai & Chen, 2024), and three articles (1.9%) utilized interest theory"
