## Evidence

### Sunsundegui P 2026

Sunsundegui P, Llorente-Ortega M, Lucena F, Fernández-Ros N, Solas M, Alcaide AB, Landecho MF, Quiroga J, Iñarrairaegui M, Herrero JI. (2026). Reducing the Number of Distractors in Multiple-Choice Questions: A Randomized Study in Undergraduate Medical Education. Perspectives on Medical Education 15(1). https://doi.org/10.5334/pme.2583

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized comparison of 98 two-distractor and 100 three-distractor MCQs from second-year exams taken by 211 and 212 students. Median difficulty index was 0.68 in both groups (p = 0.931); mean point-biserial 0.29 in both (p = 0.708); preferred questions 38.8% versus 34% (p = 0.485).

> "No statistically significant differences were observed between items with two versus three distractors in terms of question type (factual versus clinical case-based), difficulty index, or discriminatory power, as measured by the point-biserial coefficient"
