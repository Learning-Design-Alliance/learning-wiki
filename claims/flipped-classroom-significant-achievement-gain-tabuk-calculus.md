# Flipped classroom instruction produced significantly higher calculus achievement than traditional instruction in a quasi-experiment at University of Tabuk

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Preparatory-year students taught Math2 via flipped classroom with Moodle-recorded videos scored significantly higher on a calculus achievement test than students taught traditionally, after ANCOVA adjustment. [→ Albalawi 2018](#albalawi-2018)

## Evidence

### Albalawi 2018

Albalawi, A.S. (2018). The effect of using flipped classroom in teaching calculus on students‟ achievement at University of Tabuk. International Journal of Research in Education and Science (IJRES), 4(1), 198-207. https://doi.org/10.21890/ijres.383137

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experiment with a control group (taught traditionally, no video access) and a treatment group (flipped instruction via Moodle videos), 92 students total, tested with one-way ANCOVA. The treatment mean (19.87) exceeded the control mean (14.96), and the article reports "The effect size was 0.522" for the group factor.

> "However, there were significant differences among students‟ means in favor of the treatmen t group which was taught by using flipped classroom. The effect size was 0.522, meaning that the effect of the independent variable (teaching with a flipped classroom) has an effect of 52% in student performance"
