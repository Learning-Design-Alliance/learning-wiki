## Evidence

### Robles M. J. 2019

Robles M. J., Miralles Ramón, Esperanza Ascension, Riera Mercedes. (2019). Different ways to present clinical cases in a classroom: video projection versus live representation of a simulated clinical scene with actors. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1494-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' stated limitation in the Discussion of the quasi-experimental seminar study; Table 1 prints the baseline contrast as 3.51 ± 1.47 vs 2.95 ± 1.50 with p = 0.036. Groups were assigned by alphabetical order and timetable compatibility, not individual randomisation.

> "Before the seminar, students in the clinical scene group had better scores on the theoretical questionnaire than the video group. Although this difference was slight, it could have influenced the higher score obtained by this group at the end of the seminar."
