# Black male students with low baseline attendance show the largest Future Forward attendance gains, though not statistically significant

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Black male students starting the year with low attendance improved their attendance by 6.0 percentage points (4.2 fewer days absent), an impact that was very large but not statistically significant (β = 0.59, p = .074). [→ Jones 2021](#jones-2021)

## Evidence

### Jones 2021

Jones, C. J., & Li, D. (2021). Testing the Impact and Scalability of the Future Forward Literacy Program. University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee. https://uwm.edu/sreed

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Intersectional subgroup analysis in the RCT. The model-adjusted attendance rate for this group was 86.2% for FF versus 80.1% for BAU, a 6.0 percentage point difference indicating 4.2 fewer days absent.

> "Although not statistically significant, the impact of FF on Black male students with low school attendance at the start of the FF program was very large (β = 0.59, SE = 0.33, p =.074)."
