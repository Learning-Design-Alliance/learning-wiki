# Pharmacy-related work is not significantly related to pharmacy students' GPA in either semester

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Compared to not being employed, pharmacy-related work showed no statistically significant association with GPA in fall or spring semesters. [→ Okogbaa John 2020](#okogbaa-john-2020)

## Evidence

### Okogbaa John 2020

Okogbaa John, Allen Rondall E., Sarpong Daniel F. (2020). Time Spent at Work and Its Impact on the Academic Performance of Pharmacy Students. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17020496

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Stratified regression analysis of the 384-student cohort. Fall pharmacy-related work coefficients were non-significant across models (e.g., Model IV 0.163 ± 0.107, p = 0.1284); spring Model IV was 0.148 ± 0.107 (p = 0.1696). No effect size was printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect); equivalence was not tested.

> "For both the fall and spring semesters, pharmacy-related work compared to not being employed was not significantly related to GPA."
