Jansen 2017¶

Jansen, R. S., Lakens, D., & IJsselsteijn, W. (2017). An integrative review of the cognitive costs and benefits of note-taking. Educational Research Review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2017.10.001

Narrative review synthesizing multiple studies (Aiken et al., 1975; Einstein et al., 1985; Fisher & Harris, 1973; Kiewra and colleagues; Peverly and colleagues) across text, audio, video, and live lecture formats, with free recall, summary, multiple choice, and true/false tests.

"These studies have consistently shown that better note quality is related to better performance on memory tests administered afterwards."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Lecture modality (written, audio, video, live) shows no consistent effect on memory for lecture content or on the note-taking effect — related
  • Spaced note-taking during lecture breaks outperformed parallel and no note-taking, and memory was worse at once-double lecture speed — related
  • Laptop note-takers transcribed more verbatim and did worse on conceptual questions than longhand note-takers in one set of experiments, but a direct replication found no consistent difference in test performance — related
  • Transcribed versus organised note-taking effects depend on lecture format: transcription won with audio lectures, summarising won with text — related
  • Learner highlighting of text gives a small memory benefit but no reliable comprehension benefit, and evidence on other forms of annotation is not yet recorded here — related
