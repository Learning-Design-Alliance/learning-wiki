## Evidence

### Seifert Lukas B. 2020

Seifert Lukas B., Schnurr Benedikt, Stefanescu Maria-Christina, Sader Robert, Ruesseler Miriam, Sterz Jasmina. (2020). Comparing video-based versions of Halsted's 'see one, do one' and Peyton's '4-step approach' for teaching surgical skills: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02105-5

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Retention OSCE 5–13 weeks after intervention with 95 students participating; the structured facial examination showed no significant difference (p < 0.616, η2 = 0.0055) while the 4-step group maintained its performance and the See One, Do One group significantly increased its performance.

> "At T2, no significant difference could be found between both groups (p < 0.616, η2 = 0.0055). Most (81.1%) of those in the video-based ‘4-step Approach’ group showed very good retention of the acquired knowledge"

### Seifert Lukas B. 2020 (2)

Seifert Lukas B., Schnurr Benedikt, Stefanescu Maria-Christina, Sader Robert, Ruesseler Miriam, Sterz Jasmina. (2020). Comparing video-based versions of Halsted's 'see one, do one' and Peyton's '4-step approach' for teaching surgical skills: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02105-5

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Delayed retention measurement for the tamponade in the same curricular OSCE; both groups significantly increased performance from T1 to T2, and the article reports no significant difference at T2, with the printed η2 of 0.0067.

> "At T2, no significant difference could be found (p < 0.362, η2 = 0.0067). Both groups were able to significantly increase their performance from T1 to T2 (Table 3; Fig. 3)."
