# Practitioners' use of father engagement strategies and their services' organizational use of father engagement strategies rose from pre-training to 2-month follow-up in both training formats, in two uncontrolled pre-post studies

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` From pre-training to 2-month follow-up, practitioners in both formats reported significant increases in their own frequency of using father engagement strategies and in their service's use of such strategies, with large effect sizes. [→ Burn M. 2019](#burn-m-2019)

## Evidence

### Burn M. 2019

Burn M., Tully L. A., Jiang Y., Piotrowska P. J., Collins D. A. J., Sargeant K., Hawes D., Moul C., Lenroot R. K., Frick P. J., Anderson V., Kimonis E. R., Dadds M. R. (2019). Evaluating Practitioner Training to Improve Competencies and Organizational Practices for Engaging Fathers in Parenting Interventions. `q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Study 2 repeated-measures MANOVA (assessed at pre-training and follow-up only) showed a multivariate effect for time, F (2, 101) = 29.42, p < 0.001, with univariate effects significant for both outcomes; partial eta squared values were 0.31 (practitioner strategy use) and 0.22 (service use). The same pattern with large effects was reported for Study 1 (partial eta squared 0.25 for both).

> "There were significant increases in practitioners’ ratings of their own frequency of use of father engagement strategies as well as organizational practices from pre-training to follow-up, with large effect sizes (see Table 3.)."
