# On a clicker question about gravitational force on falling bodies, half of students answered that equal speeds require equal pulls, while more than 80% answered the reversed horizontal version correctly

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In an algebra-based physics class using clickers, about half of students answered the falling-body force question by direct recall, while more than 80% answered the nearly equivalent horizontal pulling question correctly. [→ Edward F. Redish 2013](#edward-f-redish-2013)

## Evidence

### Edward F. Redish 2013

Edward F. Redish. (2013). Oersted Lecture 2013: How should we think about how our students think?. [doi:10.1119/1.4874260](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4874260)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Clicker responses in the author's algebra-based introductory physics class at the start of the second term, on two nearly equivalent questions (vertical falling vs. horizontal pulling). The article reports "half of the students" erred on the first and "more than 80%" answered the second correctly; no test statistic is printed.

> "half of the students answered the first question saying that creating equal speeds required equal pulls on different masses. But more than 80% realized that creating equal speeds for a heavy and light mass requires more force on the heavier."
