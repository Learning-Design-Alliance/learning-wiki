## Evidence

### Chacón-Cuberos 2018

Chacón-Cuberos, R.; Zurita-Ortega, F.; Martínez-Martínez, A.; Olmedo-Moreno, E.M.; Castro-Sánchez, M. (2018). Adherence to the Mediterranean Diet Is Related to Healthy Habits, Learning Processes, and Academic Achievement in Adolescents: A Cross-Sectional Study. Nutrients 10(11):1566. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu10111566

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Bivariate Pearson correlations (Table 5) in the 1059-adolescent sample: "engagement in PA was negatively associated with anxiety" (r = −0.070) and positively with three MSLQ-SF dimensions, all very small correlations significant at p < 0.05.

> "In addition, engagement in PA was negatively associated with anxiety (r = −0.070; p < 0.05) and positively associated with critical thinking (r = 0.075; p < 0.05), self-regulation (r = 0.077; p < 0.05), and time and study habits (r = 0.074; p < 0.05)."

### Chacón-Cuberos 2018 (2)

Chacón-Cuberos, R.; Zurita-Ortega, F.; Martínez-Martínez, A.; Olmedo-Moreno, E.M.; Castro-Sánchez, M. (2018). Adherence to the Mediterranean Diet Is Related to Healthy Habits, Learning Processes, and Academic Achievement in Adolescents: A Cross-Sectional Study. Nutrients 10(11):1566. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu10111566

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r1`

Group-mean comparison (Table 4) contrasting adolescents active more than three hours weekly with less active peers: active adolescents showed "lower scores for anxiety" and higher critical thinking, self-regulation, study habits and intrinsic goals (each p < 0.05).

> "Adolescents who engaged in PA also reported higher scores for critical thinking (3.75 ± 0.95 vs. 3.60 ± 1.02, p < 0.05), self-regulation (3.76 ± 0.87 vs. 3.61 ± 0.96, p < 0.05), time and study habits (3.89 ± 0.88 vs. 3.76 ± 0.94, p < 0.05), and intrinsically oriented goals (4.23 ± 0.84 vs. 4.12 ± 0.97, p < 0.05) and lower scores for anxiety (3.27 ± 1.08 vs. 3.42 ± 1.07; p < 0.05)."
