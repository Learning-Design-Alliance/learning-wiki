Physical exercise and academic performance among students in the medical field at Jordanian Universities. PLoS One 21(1): e0341310. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0341310

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Secondary logistic regression predicting physical activity engagement (Table 4) in the 1,209-student survey (55.6% female). The adjusted odds ratio for females was 0.5 (95% CI 0.4–0.6, p < 0.001). cGPA ≥ 3.5 was not significantly associated with activity (aOR = 1.3, 95% CI 1.0–1.6, p = 0.071). The authors attribute the gender gap likely to restrictions in the Middle East.

> "Female students were significantly less likely to engage in physical activity compared to males (aOR = 0.5; 95% CI: 0.4–0.6; p < 0.001). No other variables, including age, university, nationality, specialty, or cGPA, showed a statistically significant association with physical activity in this model."
