## Evidence

### Wong Ion Hong 2026

Wong Ion Hong, Gao Xiaoyu, Tan Zhengyi, Fang Yiping, Wang Qun, Lei Wai In, Van Iat Kio, Tan Meijing, Liu Ning, Wu Jianwei. (2026). The impact of experiential learning on healthcare students’ attitudes towards dementia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Geriatrics. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12877-026-07573-w

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Subgroup meta-analysis of delivery format, where face-to-face meant direct interaction with people with dementia and non-face-to-face meant online or simulated experiences. Both formats were significant; the between-mode difference was not (p = 0.38).

> "Similarly, both face-to-face (WMD = 10.92, 95% CI: 6.84–15.00, p < 0.001) and non-face-to-face interventions (WMD = 7.77, 95% CI: 2.04–13.50, p = 0.008) significantly improved attitudes, with no significant difference observed between delivery modes (p = 0.38)"

### Wong Ion Hong 2026 (2)

Wong Ion Hong, Gao Xiaoyu, Tan Zhengyi, Fang Yiping, Wang Qun, Lei Wai In, Van Iat Kio, Tan Meijing, Liu Ning, Wu Jianwei. (2026). The impact of experiential learning on healthcare students’ attitudes towards dementia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Geriatrics. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12877-026-07573-w

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Subgroup analysis of virtual versus non-virtual formats. Only two studies met inclusion criteria for virtual formats, so further subgroup synthesis was not conducted; the review attributes heterogeneity partly to methodological differences between the two virtual simulation studies.

> "When examining virtual versus non-virtual interventions, only the latter demonstrated a consistent and significant positive effect (WMD = 9.66, 95% CI: 6.33–12.98, p < 0.001), while the single study representing virtual learning yielded a non-significant result (WMD = 8.11, 95% CI: –7.33 to 23.54, p = 0.30) and exhibited high heterogeneity (I2 = 87.2%)"
