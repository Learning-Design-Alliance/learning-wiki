## Evidence

### Lucy Prior 2022

Lucy Prior, Clare Evans, Juan Merlo and George Leckie. (2022). Sociodemographic inequalities in student achievement: An intersectional multilevel analysis of individual heterogeneity and discriminatory accuracy (MAIHDA) with application to students in London, England. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2211.06321](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2211.06321)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Adjusted model (Model 2) adding age-in-year, gender, FSM, SEN and ethnicity main effects to the London cohort analyses: the proportional change in stratum variance shows additivity "describes the intersectional variation reasonably well" in these cohorts.

> "Comparing the variance positioned at stratum level in Model 2 and Model 1 we see a PCV of approximately 97% at age 11 and 96% at age 16."

### Lucy Prior 2022 (2)

Lucy Prior, Clare Evans, Juan Merlo and George Leckie. (2022). Sociodemographic inequalities in student achievement: An intersectional multilevel analysis of individual heterogeneity and discriminatory accuracy (MAIHDA) with application to students in London, England. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2211.06321](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2211.06321)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Model 2 variance partitioning for both London cohorts shows the interaction-effect VPC was "very low (1.3% at both age 11 and age 16)", so factors beyond the studied characteristics dominate residual variability.

> "These VPC statistics are very low (1.3% at both age 11 and age 16) suggesting these interaction effects are very small and that other factors beyond those considered here dominate in explaining the residual variability."
