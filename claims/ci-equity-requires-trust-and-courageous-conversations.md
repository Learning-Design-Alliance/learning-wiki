# Continuous improvement for equity requires trust and courageous conversations; without trusting relationships, the improvement process will not help

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Conversations about race and class within continuous improvement are emotionally sensitive, take time, and require trust and psychological safety. [→ Valdez 2020](#valdez-2020)

## Evidence

### Valdez 2020

Valdez, A., Takahashi, S., Krausen, K., Bowman, A., & Gurrola, E. (2020). Getting better at getting more equitable: Opportunities and barriers for using continuous improvement to advance educational equity. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED606089

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

A leader interviewed in the study stated that "if there is not trust or trusting relationships built within the team, then the continuous improvement process isn't going to help." Leaders noted equity conversations bring discomfort, guilt, and require psychological safety.

> "A continuous improvement process always involves difcult conversations and courageous conversations. There is a human element to the process — so if there is not trust or trusting relationships built within the team, then the continuous improvement process isn't going to help."
