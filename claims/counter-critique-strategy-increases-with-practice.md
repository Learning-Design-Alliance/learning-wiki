## Evidence

### Iordanou 2010

Kalypso Iordanou. (2010). Developing Argument Skills Across Scientific and Social Domains. Journal of Cognition and Development. https://doi.org/10.1080/15248372.2010.485335

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Repeated-measures ANOVA of coded dialogues for the Counter-C (counter-critique) strategy. The printed effect size is "partial g2 = .15" (partial η² = .15): "a three-way interaction, F(2, 53) = 4.59, p = .015". On the social topic SOC rose from 7.03% to 45.72% and SCI from 9.09% to 43.76%.

> "A 3 x 2 x 2 (Condition x Time x Topic) repeated-measures ANOVA for Counter-C revealed a three-way interaction, F(2, 53) = 4.59, p = .015, partial g2 = .15."

### Iordanou 2010 (2)

Kalypso Iordanou. (2010). Developing Argument Skills Across Scientific and Social Domains. Journal of Cognition and Development. https://doi.org/10.1080/15248372.2010.485335

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Two-way ANOVA on the science topic showed a significant Time x Condition interaction, F(2, 53) = 28.03, p \< .001, with printed effect size "partial g2 = .51" (partial η² = .51). The printed means show "the increase exhibited by participants in the SCI" was greater than the SOC's, and SOC still beat control (Bonferroni p = .019).

> "However, as shown in Figure 4, the increase exhibited by participants in the SCI, from 1.83% (SD = 4.97) to 41.48% (SD = 18.01), was greater than the one exhibited by participants in the SOC, from 1.71% (5.26) to 22.56% (18.36)."
