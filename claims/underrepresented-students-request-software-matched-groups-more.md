## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Women and gender-nonconforming/genderqueer students, and Black, Hispanic and White students, requested software-matched study groups at higher rates than men and Asian students respectively. [→ Sumer Kohli 2023](#sumer-kohli-2023)

## Evidence

### Sumer Kohli 2023

Sumer Kohli, Neelesh Ramachandran, Ana Tudor, Gloria Tumushabe, Olivia Hsu, and Gireeja Ranade. 2023. Inclusive Study Group Formation At Scale. In Proceedings of the 54th ACM Technical Symposium on Computer Science Education V. 1 (SIGCSE 2023). https://doi.org/10.1145/3545945.3569885

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Survey-based analysis of the Fall 2020 preference-survey responses of 477 consenting students in a 1000+ student remote introductory course, reported in Fig. 2 column (C) percentages by demographic group. The authors report that "women and gender-nonconforming/ genderqueer students were more likely to request software-matched study groups than men". No effect size is printed.

> "women and gender-nonconforming/ genderqueer students were more likely to request software-matched study groups than men, and Black, Hispanic and White students were more likely to request software-matched groups than Asian students (the largest demographic group)."
