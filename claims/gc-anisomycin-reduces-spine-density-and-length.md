# Anisomycin infusion into the GC reduces dendritic spine density and length in the gustatory cortex

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Six days after anisomycin infusion to the GC of naive rats, Golgi-stained neurons showed significantly reduced spine density and spine length compared with vehicle. [→ Levitan 2016](#levitan-2016)

## Evidence

### Levitan 2016

Levitan, D., Gal-Ben-Ari, S., Heise, C., Rosenberg, T., Elkobi, A., Inberg, S., Sala, C. & Rosenblum, K. (2016). The differential role of cortical protein synthesis in taste memory formation and persistence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/npjscilearn.2016.1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Histological experiment: anisomycin or vehicle was infused into the GC of naive animals and Golgi-stained brains analyzed 6 days later, showing "reduction of spine density and length" (density P = 0.0017, t = 3.299, d.f. = 57; length P = 0.0336, t = 2.141, d.f. = 56). No standardized effect size is printed.

> "Anisomycin infusion resulted in reduction of spine density and length as compared to vehicle (Figure 3. density: P = 0.0017, t = 3.299, degrees of freedom (d.f.) = 57; length: P = 0.0336, t = 2.141, d.f. = 56, n =2 8–32 neurons from four animals in each condition; t test with Welch correction)."
