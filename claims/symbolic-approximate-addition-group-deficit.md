# Children with MLD and LA are less accurate than TA children on symbolic but not non-symbolic approximate addition

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` TA children solved symbolic approximate addition more accurately than children with LA (d = 1.19) and MLD (d = 1.14), while no group differences appeared on the non-symbolic task. [→ Bert De Smedt 2011](#bert-de-smedt-2011)

## Evidence

### Bert De Smedt 2011

Bert De Smedt, Camilla K. Gilmore. (2011). Defective number module or impaired access? Numerical magnitude processing in first graders with mathematical difficulties. Journal of Experimental Child Psychology, 108, 278–292. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2010.09.003

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Repeated measures ANOVA on accuracy in symbolic and non-symbolic approximate addition after excluding below-chance performers. The "group × task interaction was also significant"; TA children outperformed LA and MLD only on the symbolic task, with no group differences on the non-symbolic task.

> "There was a main effect of group (F(2,60) = 7.09, p < .01, ηp² = .19) and the group × task interaction was also significant (F(2,60) = 5.05, p < .01, ηp² = .05) (Figure 3). Post-hoc t-tests revealed that TA children performed significantly more accurately on the symbolic tasks than children with LA (t(47) = 4.11, p < .01, d = 1.19) and children with MLD (t(43) = 3.50, p < .01, d = 1.14)"
