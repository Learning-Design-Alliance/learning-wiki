## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Women receiving active choice invitations were 3.8 percentage points more likely to attend training than the opt-in group, and enhanced active choice recipients 1.8 percentage points more likely, neither statistically significant. [→ Diwan Faizan 2014](#diwan-faizan-2014)

## Evidence

### Diwan Faizan 2014

Diwan Faizan, Makana Grace, McKenzie David, Paruzzolo Silvia. (2014). Invitation Choice Structure Has No Impact on Attendance in a Female Business Training Program in Kenya. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0109873

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Randomized field experiment: 1172 female business owners randomly assigned within market to opt-in, active choice, or enhanced active choice invitations; OLS with marketplace dummies. The article reports the active choice group was "3.8 percentage points more likely to attend training (p = 0.199)" and enhanced active choice 1.8 points (p = 0.556), both non-significant.

> "Those receiving the active choice treatment were 3.8 percentage points more likely to attend training (p = 0.199) than the opt-in group, while those receiving the enhanced active choice were 1.8 percentage points more likely to attend (p = 0.556)."
