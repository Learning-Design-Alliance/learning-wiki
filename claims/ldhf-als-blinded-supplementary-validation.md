## Evidence

### Moll Lisa 2026

Moll Lisa, Riessen Reimer, Dahlmann Philipp, Häske David. (2026). Effect of low-dose, high-frequency advanced life support training versus annual full-day training on simulation-based resuscitation performance: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09717-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Supplementary blinded assessment in which six EMS-education students, blinded to allocation and not involved in instrument development, rated all 13 video scenarios. Domain results: NTS 3.3 ± 1.3 vs. 2.6 ± 0.8 (p = 0.006); defibrillation 4.4 ± 0.6 vs. 4.0 ± 0.9 (p = 0.020); CPR-related 3.6 ± 0.9 vs. 2.9 ± 0.8 (p < 0.001).

> "The supplementary blinded outcome assessment also showed a significantly higher mean score in the intervention group than in the control group (3.9 ± 0.7 vs. 3.3 ± 0.6; p < 0.001), although the absolute scores were slightly lower than in the primary assessment."
