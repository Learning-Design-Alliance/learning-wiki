# General heuristics act as weak methods that yield weak results in complex domains, with a power-generality tradeoff

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### PERKINS 1989

D. N. Perkins and Gavriel Salomon. (1989). Are Cognitive Skills Context-Bound? Educational Researcher, 16-23. https://www.jstor.org/stable/1176259

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review narrates AI history as evidence: domain-specific programs succeeded where generic ones like the General Problem Solver failed, and investigators named general heuristics such as means-end analysis "weak methods."

> "But these generic programs seemed quite helpless in complex problem solving domains such as chess play, integrating mathematical expressions, or medical diagnosis. In contrast, programs designed specifically for those knowledge domains scored significant successes"

### PERKINS 1989 (2)

D. N. Perkins and Gavriel Salomon. (1989). Are Cognitive Skills Context-Bound? Educational Researcher, 16-23. https://www.jstor.org/stable/1176259

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports instructional attempts (citing Schoenfeld, 1985, pp. 71-74) to teach Polya's heuristics with little success, with students lacking the domain knowledge needed to apply them; the review attributes this to the cited work.

> "They understood the heuristics in broad terms but didn't seem to understand the mathematics well enough to apply them in the rather complex and context sensitive ways required."
