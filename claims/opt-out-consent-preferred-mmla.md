## Evidence

### Jin 2024

Jin, Y., Echeverria, V., Yan, L., Zhao, L., Alfredo, R., Tsai, Y.-S., Gašević, D., & Martinez-Maldonado, R. (2024). FATE in MMLA: A Student-Centred Exploration of Fairness, Accountability, Transparency, and Ethics in Multimodal Learning Analytics. Journal of Learning Analytics, 11(3), 6–23. https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2024.8351

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Interview study on ethics (RQ4) with 14 students. Eleven favoured opt-out for ease of participation; three preferred opt-in, valuing "autonomy" and "more control and more informed choice."

> "When discussing the preference between opt-out and opt-in consenting, 11 participants (P1–4, P6, P8–10, and P12–14) favoured the opt-out approach used in the study. In contrast, three participants (P5, P7, and P11) preferred the opt-in method."

### Jin 2024 (2)

Jin, Y., Echeverria, V., Yan, L., Zhao, L., Alfredo, R., Tsai, Y.-S., Gašević, D., & Martinez-Maldonado, R. (2024). FATE in MMLA: A Student-Centred Exploration of Fairness, Accountability, Transparency, and Ethics in Multimodal Learning Analytics. Journal of Learning Analytics, 11(3), 6–23. https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2024.8351

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Same ethics theme: reasons for not reading included length (the document was just over two pages), trust in the research, and skimming; reading did not guarantee full comprehension.

> "While all participants were aware of the statement and their rights to opt out, only five (P5, P9, P10, P12, P14) fully read it."
