## Evidence

### Edelsbrunner et al. 2018

Peter A. Edelsbrunner, Lennart Schalk, Ralph Schumacher, Elsbeth Stern. (2018). Variable control and conceptual change: A large-scale quantitative study in elementary school. Learning and Individual Differences 66. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2018.02.003

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Profile mean estimates on the seven-question transfer test included in the posttest questionnaire, estimated per posttest knowledge profile in the LTA (Table 3). The article labels these estimates descriptive.

> "Descriptively, on the transfer test score the low misconceptions proﬁle had the lowest estimate, followed by the high misconceptions proﬁle, then the fragmented and intermediate proﬁles, and the prescientiﬁc and scientiﬁc proﬁles had the highest estimates"

### Edelsbrunner et al. 2018 (2)

Peter A. Edelsbrunner, Lennart Schalk, Ralph Schumacher, Elsbeth Stern. (2018). Variable control and conceptual change: A large-scale quantitative study in elementary school. Learning and Individual Differences 66. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2018.02.003

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion of the high-misconceptions profile's transfer advantage, attributed to the co-existence of misconceptions with more advanced conceptions; misconceptions are not necessarily detrimental.

> "On the transfer tasks, students in this proﬁle outperformed those in a proﬁle with a lower number of misconceptions."
