## Evidence

### Shirazi 2019

Shirazi, F., & Heidari, S. (2019). The relationship between critical thinking skills and learning styles and academic achievement of nursing students. The Journal of Nursing Research, 27(4), e38. https://doi.org/10.1097/jnr.0000000000000307

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional survey of 139 nursing students classified with Kolb's Learning Styles Inventory; the study reports "Most participants (55.4%) used a “diverging” learning style." Descriptive distribution; no effect size printed.

> "Most participants (55.4%) used a “diverging” learning style, whereas 0.7% used a “converging” style."

### Shirazi 2019 (2)

Shirazi, F., & Heidari, S. (2019). The relationship between critical thinking skills and learning styles and academic achievement of nursing students. The Journal of Nursing Research, 27(4), e38. https://doi.org/10.1097/jnr.0000000000000307

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional survey; one-way analysis of variance compared mean critical thinking scores across the four Kolb learning-style groups. "The findings showed no statistically significant relationship between learning style and critical thinking or its subscales." No effect size printed.

> "The findings showed no statistically significant relationship between learning style and critical thinking or its subscales (Table 4)."
