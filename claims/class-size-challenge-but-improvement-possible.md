## Evidence

### Briseño-Garzón 2016

Briseño-Garzón, A., Han, A., Birol, G., Bates, S., & Whitehead, L. (2016). Faculty perceptions of challenges and enablers of effective teaching in a large research-intensive university: Preliminary findings. Collected Essays on Learning and Teaching, 9, 133-144. https://celt.uwindsor.ca/ceelt/vol9/brisenogarzon

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Analysis of open-ended survey responses found class size was the second most cited challenge (151 comments) and reducing class size the most recommended change (115 comments). Barriers included increased workload, difficulty meeting individual student needs, and difficulty implementing active learning in large classes.

> "Reducing class size emerged as the most frequently recommended change to help participants teach more effectively (115 comments) and, correspondingly, class size was identified as the second biggest challenge (151 comments)."

### Briseño-Garzón 2016 (2)

Briseño-Garzón, A., Han, A., Birol, G., Bates, S., & Whitehead, L. (2016). Faculty perceptions of challenges and enablers of effective teaching in a large research-intensive university: Preliminary findings. Collected Essays on Learning and Teaching, 9, 133-144. https://celt.uwindsor.ca/ceelt/vol9/brisenogarzon

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Despite class size challenges, a strong majority of survey participants believed teaching effectiveness could be improved without reducing class size: 40% strongly agreed and 36% agreed with this statement.

> "40% of the participants strongly agreed and 36% agreed with the statement that "Even without a smaller class size, I believe it is possible to improve the effectiveness of one's teaching.""
