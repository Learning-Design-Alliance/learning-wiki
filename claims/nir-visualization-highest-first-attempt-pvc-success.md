# Nursing students trained with NIR light visualization achieved the highest first-attempt PVC success rate (67.3%) compared to isometric exercise (40%) and control (29.2%) groups

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a randomized controlled study of 147 first-year nursing students, the NIR light visualization group had a significantly higher proportion of first-attempt PVC success (67.3%) than the isometric exercise group (40%) and the control group (29.2%). [→ Yılmaz Hülya 2025](#ylmaz-hulya-2025)

## Evidence

### Yılmaz Hülya 2025

Yılmaz Hülya, Yılmaz Dilek, Çinar Hava Gökdere. (2025). The effect of three different techniques used to improve vein prominence on the first peripheral venous catheterization experience in nursing students: a randomized controlled study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07350-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Parallel-group randomized controlled study of first-year nursing students (NIR n=49, isometric n=50, control n=48) performing PVC on peers; first-attempt success compared with chi-square. The article reports "c2 = 15.201; P <.001" with the NIR group most successful; no effect size is printed.

> "There was a statistically significant difference between the groups in the proportion of individuals who successfully mastered the PVC skill at the first attempt (c2 = 15.201; P <.001). It was seen that the most successful group was the NIR light visualisation group (67.3%) compared to the isometric exercise group (40%) and the control group (29.2%)."
