## Evidence

### Alber JM 2015

Alber JM, Bernhardt JM, Stellefson M, Weiler RM, Anderson-Lewis C, Miller MD, MacInnes J. (2015). Designing and Testing an Inventory for Measuring Social Media Competency of Certified Health Education Specialists. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.4943

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Field test of the SMCI with a random sample of CHES and MCHES (n=353) analyzed via classical test theory, CCFA, and Rasch RSM. Description reports the printed alphas: 'alpha=.98' for Social Media Self-Efficacy; Effort Expectancy alpha=.74 and Performance Expectancy alpha=.81. Facilitating Conditions and Social Influence reached .66.

> "Psychometric analyses of the data revealed acceptable reliability evidence for the following scales: Social Media Self-Efficacy (alpha=.98, item reliability=.98, item separation=6.76), Social Media Experience (alpha=.98, item reliability=.98, item separation=6.24)"

### Alber JM 2015 (2)

Alber JM, Bernhardt JM, Stellefson M, Weiler RM, Anderson-Lewis C, Miller MD, MacInnes J. (2015). Designing and Testing an Inventory for Measuring Social Media Competency of Certified Health Education Specialists. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.4943

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Second RSM analyses on the final scale items from the field test. Description quotes that 'item separation indices for each scale were above the cutoff value of 2.00'; printed item reliabilities ranged .93-.99 and item separations ranged 3.77-16.04 (Table 5).

> "Item reliabilities were above the recommended value of .80 or greater [59]. Likewise, the item separation indices for each scale were above the cutoff value of 2.00 [53,59]."
