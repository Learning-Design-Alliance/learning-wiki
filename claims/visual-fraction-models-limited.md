# Visual fraction models are limited as a main instructional approach: human vision restricts them to small integers, they are mathematically wrong in some aspects, and they have been obsolete as a main approach for centuries

## Evidence

### Frank Quinn 2013

Frank Quinn. (2013). Fractions in Elementary Education. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1311.2235](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1311.2235)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Analytical argument in the section on visual fraction models, one of four listed defects of the visual approach: some aspects are mathematically wrong, it is too clumsy for computation, "accuracy of human vision limits the method to small integers", and it poorly models mathematical reasoning.

> "Accuracy of human vision limits the method to small integers, and in the Common Core this shows up as severe limits on the size of denominators allowed in grade 4, for instance."

### Frank Quinn 2013 (2)

Frank Quinn. (2013). Fractions in Elementary Education. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1311.2235](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1311.2235)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Analytical argument about pie charts, the most common visual fraction model: their information concerns vertex angles, which connect with parts-of-a-whole and mixed-number forms, but "angles correspond to ratios" rather than fractions. Rectangles, by contrast, represent fractions sensibly including improper ones.

> "The problem is that angles correspond to ratios—more precisely oriented ratios, see§2.6—not fractions."
