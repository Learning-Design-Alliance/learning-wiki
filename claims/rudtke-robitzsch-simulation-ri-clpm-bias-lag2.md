# Simulation evidence shows RI-CLPM estimates are biased when the true model is a CLPM with lag-2 effects, and fit statistics cannot decide between the two

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r3` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In simulated data, the RI-CLPM led to biased estimates if the true model was a CLPM with lag-2 effects (FF-CLPM), and vice versa; model fit statistics were unsuitable for choosing between them. [→ Hübner 2023](#hubner-2023)

## Evidence

### Hübner 2023

Hübner, N., Wagner, W., Zitzmann, S., & Nagengast, B. (2023). How Strong Is the Evidence for a Causal Reciprocal Effect? Contrasting Traditional and New Methods to Investigate the Reciprocal Effects Model of Self-Concept and Achievement. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09724-6

`q1 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports a simulation study by Lüdtke and Robitzsch in which data were "generated ... based on a CLPM with three measurement occasions", showing RI-CLPM bias under a lag-2 true model and that "model fit statistics do not seem suitable" for model choice.

> "they generated data for two variables X and Y based on a CLPM with three measurement occasions and showed that the RI-CLPM leads to biased estimates if the true model is a CLPM with lag-2 effects"
