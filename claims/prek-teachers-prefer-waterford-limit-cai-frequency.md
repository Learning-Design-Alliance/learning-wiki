## Evidence

### Trotti 2017

Trotti, J., Hendricks, R., & Bledsoe, C. (2017). Emergent Literacy Development and Computer Assisted Instruction. SRATE Journal, 26(1), 30-39. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1134394

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Semi-structured focus group of five pre-K teachers after three months of implementation, responses coded via analytic induction. The article reports "Waterford was seen as the most helpful program by four of five teachers"; one teacher valued each equally.

> "Waterford was seen as the most helpful program by four of five teachers due to its use of higher order thinking and tools that help children stay on track. Imagine Learning was perceived as more of a skills -based program lacking authentic classroom connections for students."

### Trotti 2017 (2)

Trotti, J., Hendricks, R., & Bledsoe, C. (2017). Emergent Literacy Development and Computer Assisted Instruction. SRATE Journal, 26(1), 30-39. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1134394

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Focus-group suggestions for future use, categorized by the researchers as time, motivating students, literacy skill development, technological tools, and program choice. Teachers recommended "2-3 times per week in the computer lab to avoid distractions" and updated computers.

> "Summarily, teachers perceived daily use of either software program should be abandoned. T eachers recommended using either program 2-3 times per week in the computer lab to avoid distractions."
