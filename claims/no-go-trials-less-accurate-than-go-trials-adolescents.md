# Both maltreated and nonmaltreated adolescents are less accurate on no go trials than go trials, with a large trial-type effect

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Percentage of correct responses was higher on go trials (M = 97.8%) than no go trials (M = 64.7%) for both groups. [→ Bruce 2022](#bruce-2022)

## Evidence

### Bruce 2022

Bruce, J., & Kim, H. K. (2022). Behavioral and Electrophysiological Indices of Inhibitory Control in Maltreated Adolescents and Nonmaltreated Adolescents. Development and Psychopathology. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0954579420001819

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Repeated measures ANOVA of go/no go accuracy in 230 adolescents found a large trial-type main effect (partial η2 = .79 in Table 3): "a higher percentage of correct responses on the go trials (M = 97.8%) than the no go trials (M = 64.7%)". Simple effects were significant for both groups.

> "the main effect of trial type was significant, with a higher percentage of correct responses on the go trials (M = 97.8%) than the no go trials (M = 64.7%)."
