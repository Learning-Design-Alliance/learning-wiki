## Evidence

### Tanielian 2014

Tanielian, A. R. (2014). Foreign language anxiety in a new English program in Thailand. The International Education Journal: Comparative Perspectives, 13(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1035913

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

One-way ANOVA within the immersion group comparing FLCAS scores between students who passed and failed the question-and-answer portion of the oral exam. The article reports a statistically significant difference (p = .044).

> "Students who passed the question- and-answer portion had lower FLCAS scores (97.17 ± 14.74, n = 18) than students who failed the question- and-answer portion (108.58 ± 18.13, n = 19)."

### Tanielian 2014 (2)

Tanielian, A. R. (2014). Foreign language anxiety in a new English program in Thailand. The International Education Journal: Comparative Perspectives, 13(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1035913

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

One-way ANOVA within the immersion group comparing FLCAS scores between students who passed and failed the prepared speech portion of the oral exam. The difference was not statistically significant (p = .070).

> "Students who failed the prepared speech showed higher mean FLCAS scores (111.50 ± 22.04, n = 27) than students who passed the first portion of the oral exams (99.89 ± 14.49, n = 10). Those differences were not found by ANOVA to have been statistically significant (p = .070)."
