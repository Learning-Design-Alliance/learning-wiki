# Students report significantly higher accountability, preference for TBL, satisfaction, and total scores than neutral after the TBL module

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` End-of-semester survey results showed significantly higher accountability (p < 0.0001), preference for TBL over the previous cookbook approach (p < 0.001), satisfaction (p < 0.0001), and total score (p < 0.0001) than neutral. [→ Peterson 2015](#peterson-2015)

## Evidence

### Peterson 2015

Peterson, E.K. and Carrico, P. (2015). Laboratory Exercise in Behavioral Genetics Using Team-based Learning Strategies. Bioscene 41(2). https://visionandchange.org/files/2011/03/Revised-Vision-and-Change-Final-Report.pdf

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

End-of-semester survey using an instrument established by Mennenga (2010) with three subscales (accountability, preference, satisfaction), each compared to its neutral score via One-Sample T-Test (SPSS v. 22.0). All four comparisons were "significantly higher" than neutral as printed above.

> "Results from the survey (see Figure 5) showed that the use of TBL in this laboratory module resulted in significantly higher: 1) accountability (p < 0.0001), 2) preference for TBL over our previous “cookbook” approach (p < 0.001), 3) student satisfaction (p < 0.0001), and 4) total, a.k.a. the sum of all three categories (p < 0.0001)."
