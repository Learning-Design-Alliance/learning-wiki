## Evidence

### Herbert W. Marsh 2023

Herbert W. Marsh, Jiesi Guo, Philip D. Parker, Reinhard Pekrun, Geetanjali Basarkod, Theresa Dicke, et al. (2023). Peer Victimization: an Integrative Review and Cross-National Test of a Tripartite Model. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09765-x

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

The review reports that the updated Farrington meta-analysis "found weak effects (mean odds ratio = 1.22, CI = 1.09–1.38) marginally worse in 2019 than in 2009", indicating post-2009 studies were not stronger than earlier ones.

> "However, in updating their classic meta-analysis, Farrington and colleagues (Gaffney, Ttofi, & Farrington, 2019) found weak effects (mean odds ratio = 1.22, CI = 1.09–1.38) marginally worse in 2019 than in 2009."

### Herbert W. Marsh 2023 (2)

Herbert W. Marsh, Jiesi Guo, Philip D. Parker, Reinhard Pekrun, Geetanjali Basarkod, Theresa Dicke, et al. (2023). Peer Victimization: an Integrative Review and Cross-National Test of a Tripartite Model. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09765-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The review reports Ng et al.'s (2022) meta-analytic conclusion that intervention effects were "very small to small" and "marginally effective", with effects on victimization even weaker than those targeting bullying. No effect size is printed for this meta-analysis.

> "Ng et al.’s (2022) meta-analysis similarly concluded that intervention effects were “very small to small” and that these “marginally effective” results were generally consistent with previous meta-analyses."
