## Evidence

### Erdem 2026

Erdem, G., Kafescioğlu, N., & Başer, G. (2026). Are You Qualified, ChatGPT? Examining Clinical Skills and Competencies of ChatGPT in Delivering Systemic Interventions. Journal of Marital and Family Therapy. https://doi.org/10.1111/jmft.70168

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Authors' report from their own GPT training and pilot experience (limitations section), describing the phenomenon they call objective misalignment. The model "stayed overly engaging with continuous mirroring, validation, and quick responses" despite explicit behavioral rules.

> "When there was a conflict between our and base model instructions, the model reverted to its general behavior. For example, even though we have carefully designed our prompts for natural therapeutic dialogue (i.e., going with the pace of the client, being silent at times, challenging the client gently, not making quick assumptions, making an overall relational assessment and listening to the story) and gave clear behavioral examples to the GPT for each rule, the model stayed overly engaging with continuous mirroring, validation, and quick responses."
