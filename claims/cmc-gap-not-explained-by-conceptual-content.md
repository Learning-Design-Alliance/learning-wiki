## Evidence

### Nicholas T. Young 2023

Nicholas T. Young, Mark Mills, Rebecca L. Matz, Eric F. Bell, and Caitlin Hayward. (2023). Complex multiple-choice questions are inequitable for low-income and domestic students (and difficult for everyone). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2306.11068](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2306.11068)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of accuracy on a sampled subset of non-CMC questions categorized as conceptual or "plug-and-chug". Non-CMC conceptual accuracy exceeded CMC accuracy by "5.63 (95% CI 3.36, 7.90) percentage points"; no standardized effect size printed.

> "That is, students perform 5.63 (95% CI 3.36, 7.90) percentage points better on non-CMC conceptual questions than they do on CMC conceptual questions."

### Nicholas T. Young 2023 (2)

Nicholas T. Young, Mark Mills, Rebecca L. Matz, Eric F. Bell, and Caitlin Hayward. (2023). Complex multiple-choice questions are inequitable for low-income and domestic students (and difficult for everyone). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2306.11068](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2306.11068)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive comparison within sampled non-CMC questions. The article states the non-overlapping confidence intervals of the two gaps suggest the CMC gap "is not entirely due to CMC questions only being conceptual".

> "We found that on non-CMC questions, student accuracy on numerical questions was 3.0 percentage points higher than it was on conceptual questions."
