## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Two weeks after the intervention, the BAM game group's skill scores (16.4, SD 2.2) were significantly higher than the feedback lecture group's (11.8, SD 3.8). [→ Nasirzade Amirreza 2024](#nasirzade-amirreza-2024)

## Evidence

### Nasirzade Amirreza 2024

Nasirzade Amirreza, Deldar Kolsoum, Froutan Razieh, Shakeri Mohammad Taghi. (2024). Comparison of the effects of burn assessment mission game with feedback lecture on nursing students’ knowledge and skills in the burn patients’ assessment: a randomized clinical trial. BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-024-02558-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial of 42 fifth-semester nursing students randomized to the BAM game or feedback lecture; independent t-test showed the game group's skill total of 16.4 exceeded the lecture group's 11.8, with "Both differences were statistically significant (P < .001)".

> "Following the intervention, the skill scores were 16.4 (SD 2.2) for the intervention group and 11.8 (SD 3.8) for the control group. Similarly, the knowledge scores were 17.4 (SD 2.2) for the intervention group and 14.7 (SD 2.6) for the control group. Both differences were statistically significant (P < .001)."
