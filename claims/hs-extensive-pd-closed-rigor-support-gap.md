# In high schools, extensive-PD schools started with significantly lower rigor and teacher support but closed the gap by early implementation, while academic demand and clarity improved at rates comparable to limited-PD schools

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` High schools with extensive PD improved about 0.15 standard deviations in rigor and teacher support, closing a preexisting gap with limited-PD schools. [→ Julia A. Gwynne and Sarah Cashdollar 2018](#julia-a-gwynne-and-sarah-cashdollar-2018)

## Evidence

### Julia A. Gwynne and Sarah Cashdollar 2018

Julia A. Gwynne and Sarah Cashdollar. (2018). Changes in Math Instruction and Student Outcomes since the Implementation of Common Core State Standards in Chicago. UChicago Consortium working paper. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/changes-math-instruction-and-student-outcomes-implementation-common-core-state

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Difference-in-difference analysis of high school students' survey reports (grades 9-12) across three time periods; improvements among high schools with extensive PD were "0.15 standard deviations compared to approximately 0.10" for elementary schools.

> "During the pre-implementation period, schools in which teachers would eventually report extensive standards-related professional development in 2015 started out with signiﬁcantly lower levels of rigor and teacher support than schools with limited professional development, but by the early implementation period they had caught up and closed the gap."
