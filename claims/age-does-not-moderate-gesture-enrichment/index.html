Evidence¶

Andrä 2020¶

Andrä, C., Mathias, B., Schwager, A., Macedonia, M., & von Kriegstein, K. (2020). Learning Foreign Language Vocabulary with Gestures and Pictures Enhances Vocabulary Memory for Several Months Post-Learning in Eight-Year-Old School Children. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09527-z

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Exploratory multiple linear regression in Experiment 1 (n = 54, ages 8.5–10.2 years) compared null and moderator models via chi-square tests; no significant differences emerged (free recall p(χ2) = 0.46; L1-L2 translation p(χ2) = 0.82; L2-L1 translation p(χ2) = 0.92).

"These nonsignificant comparisons suggest that children's ages did not moderate the effects of gesture enrichment on vocabulary test performance."

Related Claims¶

  • Gesture enrichment specifically aided translation of abstract words in Experiment 1, but this word-type specificity did not replicate in Experiment 2 — related
  • Both gesture and picture enrichment enhanced children's test performance compared with non-enriched learning, with benefits persisting up to 6 months for concrete and abstract words — related
  • Gesture-enriched learning improves 8-year-olds' L2 vocabulary translation and free recall compared with non-enriched learning, persisting up to 6 months — related
  • Amount of sensorimotor engagement, target language, proficiency, and type of embodied interaction do not significantly moderate embodied vocabulary learning effects — related
  • Gesture effects on narrative comprehension are not moderated by total task difficulty or age when measured through free recall and specific questions — related
  • Concrete L2 words are learned better than abstract words overall, but this concreteness advantage appears only in 14-year-olds — related
  • Gesture-enriched L2 vocabulary learning benefits 14-year-olds more than picture enrichment, while 12-year-olds benefit equivalently from both — related
