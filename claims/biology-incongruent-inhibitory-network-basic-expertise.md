## Evidence

### Geneviève Allaire-Duquette 2021

Geneviève Allaire-Duquette, Lorie-Marlène Brault Foisy, Patrice Potvin, Martin Riopel, Marilyne Larose, Steve Masson. (2021). An fMRI study of scientists with a Ph.D. in physics confronted with naive ideas in science. npj Science of Learning 6:11. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00091-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Biology-domain t-contrasts (Tables 4, Fig. 6) in the 25-participant fMRI study; the areas more activated for incongruent statements included left IFG, bilateral ACC, bilateral caudate, right insula, bilateral superior frontal gyrus and right midcingulate gyrus; congruent statements recruited bilateral middle occipital gyrus and left precentral gyrus.

> "Our data revealed ﬁve brain regions that mediate the performance of physicists with a Ph.D. assessing naive science ideas in biology."

### Geneviève Allaire-Duquette 2021 (2)

Geneviève Allaire-Duquette, Lorie-Marlène Brault Foisy, Patrice Potvin, Martin Riopel, Marilyne Larose, Steve Masson. (2021). An fMRI study of scientists with a Ph.D. in physics confronted with naive ideas in science. npj Science of Learning 6:11. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00091-x

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Authors' interpretation in the discussion of the biology-domain activation pattern, linking the recruited regions to prior findings on inhibitory control; participants had a noticeable 17% error rate when evaluating biology statements.

> "The brain activation patterns associated with processing naive ideas in biology seem to largely overlap with the nodes of inhibitory control mechanisms."
