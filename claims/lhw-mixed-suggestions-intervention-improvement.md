## Evidence

### Puchalski Ritchie Lisa M. 2016

Puchalski Ritchie Lisa M., van Lettow Monique, Barnsley Jan, Chan Adrienne K., Schull Michael J., Martiniuk Alexandra L. C., Makwakwa Austine, Zwarenstein Merrick. (2016). Lay Health Workers experience of a tailored knowledge translation intervention to improve job skills and knowledge: a qualitative study in Zomba district Malawi. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0580-x

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative interview study; suggestions were "less consistent across participants" per the abstract. One participant complained onsite training was subject to disturbances; only increased HIV coverage and a drug dosage reference were noted by several participants as content additions.

> "Several participants felt the training period should be extended, with one suggesting it be extended from 6 weekly sessions to run several months. A number suggested that the training would be better conducted in the traditional manner, with LHWs taken off-site for several days to a week for training, and provided with stipends and/or refreshments as incentives for training."

### Puchalski Ritchie Lisa M. 2016 (2)

Puchalski Ritchie Lisa M., van Lettow Monique, Barnsley Jan, Chan Adrienne K., Schull Michael J., Martiniuk Alexandra L. C., Makwakwa Austine, Zwarenstein Merrick. (2016). Lay Health Workers experience of a tailored knowledge translation intervention to improve job skills and knowledge: a qualitative study in Zomba district Malawi. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0580-x

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Same study, contrasting views on onsite delivery and stipends. One participant said the meeting hour was convenient with no disturbances; another requested only that trainings take place regularly rather than stipends.

> "Others reported, the on-site training to work well, and indicated, that while stipends would be appreciated, they would prefer to have training without stipends, that to not have the training."
