# Spoken–written presentations do not outperform written-only presentations but do outperform spoken-only presentations

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Students did not learn more from spoken–written than from written-only presentations (g = –0.04), but spoken–written presentations enhanced learning compared with spoken-only presentations (g = 0.29). [→ Adesope 2012](#adesope-2012)
`q3 i?` Effect sizes differ significantly between comparison treatments (QB(1) = 22.77, p < .001), with spoken-only comparisons yielding larger effects than written-only comparisons. [→ Adesope 2012](#adesope-2012)

## Evidence

### Adesope 2012

Adesope, O. O., & Nesbit, J. C. (2012). Verbal Redundancy in Multimedia Learning Environments: A Meta-Analysis. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0026147

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Weighted mean analysis of comparison treatments in the meta-analysis: against text-only controls g = –0.04 (k = 23, CI crossing zero) and against audio-only controls g = 0.29 (k = 34), a small effect size, with a significant between-levels difference, QB(1) = 22.77, p < .001.

> "Table 2 shows that there is variability in the magnitude of effect sizes across comparison treatments, ranging from – 0.04 to 0.29. Prior to conducting moderator analyses, we examined the effect of learning with spoken–written presentations in comparison with written-only or spoken-only presentations. Table 2 shows that students did not learn more from spoken–written than written-only presentations. However, spoken–written presentations were found to enhance learning when compared with spoken-only presentations."
