# Poorer self-rated understanding of ethics and equity is associated with lower Knowledge Scores; importance of ethics education is also associated with KS

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Understanding of ethics (p = .026, mean difference 0.51) and equity (p = .001, mean difference 0.90) were each significantly associated with Knowledge Score, poor ratings scoring lower. [→ Laaksonen M 2025](#laaksonen-m-2025)
`q2 i?` Importance of ethics education was associated with KS (p = .003), with a significant Tukey mean difference of 1.22 (p = .004) between the important and neutral groups. [→ Laaksonen M 2025 (2)](#laaksonen-m-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Laaksonen M 2025

Laaksonen M, Paavilainen E, Koivisto A-M, Halkoaho A. (2025). Genomic competence among nurses: A spotlight on ethics. Nursing Ethics, 32(8). https://doi.org/10.1177/09697330251366594

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

T-tests linking ethics and equity self-ratings to Knowledge Score in the survey: nurses with poor understanding of ethics and equity "achieved lower scores in the KS than those in the excellent/good group" (Table 5 means 9.01 vs 9.52; 8.98 vs 9.88).

> "A T-test showed a statistically significant association between understanding of ethics and KS (p = .026, mean difference 0.51). The same was found between understanding of equity and KS (p = .001, mean difference 0.90)."

### Laaksonen M 2025 (2)

Laaksonen M, Paavilainen E, Koivisto A-M, Halkoaho A. (2025). Genomic competence among nurses: A spotlight on ethics. Nursing Ethics, 32(8). https://doi.org/10.1177/09697330251366594

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

ANOVA with Tukey pairwise comparison in the same survey: respondents rating ethics education as important scored 9.22 on KS versus 8.00 for the neutral group (mean difference 1.22, p = .004).

> "A statistically significant association between the importance of ethics education and KS (p = .003) was found. Based on Tukey's pairwise comparisons, a significant mean difference of 1.22 (p = .004) was noticed between two groups."
