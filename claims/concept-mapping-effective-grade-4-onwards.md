## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Meta-analytic evidence shows self-generated concept mapping improves achievement (0.72 SDs overall), with consistent beneficial effects for 4th–8th graders (0.68 SDs), 9th–12th graders (0.74 SDs), and college students (0.73 SDs). [→ Garvin Brod 2021](#garvin-brod-2021)

## Evidence

### Garvin Brod 2021

Garvin Brod. (2021). Generative Learning: Which Strategies for What Age? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09571-9

`q3 · i2` · `review · r2`

The review reports Schroeder et al.'s meta-analysis distinguishing self-generated from provided concept maps, finding a mean effect size of 0.72 SDs on achievement scores, with larger effects against passive controls (1.05 SDs) than active controls (0.48 SDs).

> "revealed that the mean size of the effect of self-generated concept mapping on students ’ achievement scores was 0.72 SDs"

### Garvin Brod 2021 (2)

Garvin Brod. (2021). Generative Learning: Which Strategies for What Age? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09571-9

`q3 · i2` · `review · r3`

The same meta-analysis examined grade as a proxy for age and found "consistent beneficial effects" across grade bands, with effect sizes of 0.68, 0.74, and 0.73 SDs respectively; no differences emerged between 4th–5th and 6th–8th grade studies.

> "Results indicated consistent beneficial effects across studies performed with 4th –8th graders2 (0.68 SDs), 9th–12th graders (0.74 SDs), and college students (0.73 SDs)."
