# Teacher technology use and school culture explain an additional 18 percent of school-by-school differences in high school student technology use

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Adding teacher use and school culture to a model with student and school characteristics reduces unexplained school-by-school variance in high school student use from 52 percent to 34 percent. [→ Stacy B. Ehrlich 2013](#stacy-b-ehrlich-2013)

## Evidence

### Stacy B. Ehrlich 2013

Stacy B. Ehrlich, Susan E. Sporte, and Penny Bender Sebring. (2013). The Use of Technology in Chicago Public Schools 2011: Perspectives from Students, Teachers, and Principals. UChicago CCSR Research Report. http://ccsr.uchicago.edu/surveys/documentation

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

HLM variance decomposition for high schools (Figure 12; Table C.3), showing unexplained school variance of 94 percent unadjusted, 52 percent after school characteristics, and 34 percent after teacher use and culture. The report states "another 18 percent of the original school-by-school differences are explained."

> "when we additionally take into account teacher use and school culture, another 18 percent of the original school-by-school differences are explained, leaving only 34 percent unexplained"
