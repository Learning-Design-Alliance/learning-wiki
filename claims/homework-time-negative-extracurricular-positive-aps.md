# Time spent on homework is negatively associated with academic performance, while attending extracurricular activities is positively associated

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After adjustment, homework time was negatively associated with teacher-reported APS whereas extracurricular activity attendance was positively associated, with the negative homework association pronounced above 2 hours per day. [→ Zhang Yunting 2015](#zhang-yunting-2015)

## Evidence

### Zhang Yunting 2015

Zhang Yunting, Zhang Donglan, Jiang Yanrui, Sun Wanqi, Wang Yan, Chen Wenjuan, Li Shenghui, Shi Lu, Shen Xiaoming, Zhang Jun, Jiang Fan. (2015). Association between Physical Activity and Teacher-Reported Academic Performance among Fifth-Graders in Shanghai: A Quantile Regression. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0115483

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Adjusted regressions on the Shanghai fifth-grade sample; the article reports homework time "was negatively associated with students’ academic performance, whereas attending extracurricular activities was positively associated".

> "Besides frequency of physical activities, time spent on homework was negatively associated with students’ academic performance, whereas attending extracurricular activities was positively associated with APS."

### Zhang Yunting 2015 (2)

Zhang Yunting, Zhang Donglan, Jiang Yanrui, Sun Wanqi, Wang Yan, Chen Wenjuan, Li Shenghui, Shi Lu, Shen Xiaoming, Zhang Jun, Jiang Fan. (2015). Association between Physical Activity and Teacher-Reported Academic Performance among Fifth-Graders in Shanghai: A Quantile Regression. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0115483

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The authors highlight the negative homework association "in particular more than 2 hours per day", relating it to study-load norms in Shanghai's public schools.

> "Our results showed that the average time spent on homework per day, in particular more than 2 hours per day, was negatively associated with academic performance, which may reflect the real situation in Shanghai’s public schools."
