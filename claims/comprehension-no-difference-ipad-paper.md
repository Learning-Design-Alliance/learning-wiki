## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Comprehension scores were marginally lower on iPad than paper but not statistically significant for the full sample or any experimental group. [→ Sackstein S 2015](#sackstein-s-2015)

## Evidence

### Sackstein S 2015

Sackstein S, Spark L & Jenkins A. (2015). Are e-books effective tools for learning? Reading speed and comprehension: iPad vs. paper. South African Journal of Education, 35(4), Art. #1202. https://doi.org/10.15700/saje.v35n4a1202

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Paired sample t-tests on 68 students' comprehension scores across iPad and paper conditions showed no significant differences (full sample t = -1.247, p = .217). All three experimental groups also showed non-significant differences.

> "Although statistically non -significant (t = -1.247, p = . 217) students comprehension scores from the iPad were only marginally lower on average than those on paper."
