# At 6 months, the simulation group reported significantly higher confidence than the theory-only group in diagnosis, management, transfusion and teamwork, except uterotonic use where confidence was higher in the theory-trained group

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` At 6 months the intervention group showed higher self-confidence in diagnosing PPH (p = 0.003), managing PPH (p = 0.013), blood transfusion (p = 0.001) and teamwork (p = 0.041), but confidence in uterotonic use was higher in the theory-trained group (p = 0.000). [→ Buhendwa Cikwanine Jean Paul 2025](#buhendwa-cikwanine-jean-paul-2025)
`q3 i?` There was no significant difference in confidence levels between the groups prior to training. [→ Buhendwa Cikwanine Jean Paul 2025 (2)](#buhendwa-cikwanine-jean-paul-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Buhendwa Cikwanine Jean Paul 2025

Buhendwa Cikwanine Jean Paul, Mukwege Denis, Mongwa Justin, shalamba Emile Mapatano, Maroyi Raha, Yoyu Jonathan Tunangoya, Martin Ludovic. (2025). An assessment of the effects of simulation training on management of postpartum hemorrhage: short- and long-term impact on knowledge retention, skills and teamwork in the Democratic Republic of Congo. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07393-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Paired-sample comparison of 4-item, 5-point Likert self-confidence ratings in the randomized trial; Table 4 shows no baseline differences (all p > 0.05) and significant 6-month differences, with uterotonic confidence "4.7(94.0)" in control versus "4.4(88.0)" in intervention.

> "At six months, the participants from interventional group demonstrated a higher level of self-confidence in their ability to diagnose PPH post training (p = 0.003), to administer uterotonic agents (p = 0.000), to carry out blood transfusions at six months post training (p = 0.012), and to work as part of a team at six months post training (p = 0.041)."

### Buhendwa Cikwanine Jean Paul 2025 (2)

Buhendwa Cikwanine Jean Paul, Mukwege Denis, Mongwa Justin, shalamba Emile Mapatano, Maroyi Raha, Yoyu Jonathan Tunangoya, Martin Ludovic. (2025). An assessment of the effects of simulation training on management of postpartum hemorrhage: short- and long-term impact on knowledge retention, skills and teamwork in the Democratic Republic of Congo. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07393-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Baseline equivalence of confidence ratings in the randomized groups, as printed in the results commentary on Table 4; all pre-training comparisons had p > 0.05.

> "Table 4 indicates no difference in confidence levels between the two groups regarding variables analyzed prior to training (p > 0.05) and a significant difference in confidence levels between the two groups at 6 months post training."
