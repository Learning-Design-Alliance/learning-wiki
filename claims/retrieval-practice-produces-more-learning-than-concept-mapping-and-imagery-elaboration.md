`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The chapter reports Karpicke and Blunt (2011) second-hand; the primary experiment is not recorded on this page, and the chapter's summary gives no sample size or effect size. In it, students read educational texts and practiced free recall or built concept maps; on a 1-week test, "Retrieval practice consistently produced more learning than elaborative studying with concept mapping did".

> "Retrieval practice consistently produced more learning than elaborative studying with concept mapping did (see also Lechuga et al., 2015 )."

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The chapter reports, second-hand, a series of vocabulary-learning experiments by Karpicke and Smith (2012), which are not themselves recorded on this page, that "showed advantages of repeated retrieval practice over elaborative imagery strategies", including the keyword mnemonic.

> "In a series of experiments, Karpicke and Smith (2012) had students learn vocabulary words and showed advantages of repeated retrieval practice over elaborative imagery strategies, including the keyword mnemonic ( Atkinson, 1975) and a mediator-generation strategy ( Pyc and Rawson, 2010 )."

## Discussion

Both entries are one review chapter's summaries of its author's own earlier experiments, so the page's evidence is second-hand: no sample size, design detail or effect size for Karpicke and Blunt (2011) or Karpicke and Smith (2012) is recorded. Adding those primary articles, and later studies that compared retrieval with concept mapping under other conditions (the chapter itself points to Lechuga et al. 2015), would let the claim be coded on its own evidence. Whether concept mapping helps on its own is a separate question; see [concept mapping](concept-mapping-improves-learning.md).
