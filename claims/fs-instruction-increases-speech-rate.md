# Explicitly taught FS treatment group significantly increased speech rate over 5 weeks while the control group did not (d = 1.3)

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` ESL students explicitly taught formulaic sequences showed a statistically significant pretest-to-posttest increase in speech rate (d = 1.3), whereas the control group showed no change (d = 0.06), and the between-group gain difference was significant (d = 1.3). [→ Michael McGuire & Jenifer Larson-Hall 2017](#michael-mcguire-jenifer-larson-hall-2017)

## Evidence

### Michael McGuire & Jenifer Larson-Hall 2017

Michael McGuire & Jenifer Larson-Hall. (2017). Teaching Formulaic Sequences in the Classroom: Effects on Spoken Fluency. TESL Canada Journal/Revue TESL du Canada, 34(3), 1–25. http://dx.doi.org/10.18806/tesl.v34i3.1271

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental 5-week classroom study with 19 completers (control n = 8, treatment n = 11) in a university Intensive English program. The between-group t-test on speech-rate gain scores found a statistical difference, "d = 1.3"; within-group, the treatment improved significantly (d = 1.3) while the control did not (d = 0.06).

> "An independent-samples t-test comparing the treatment group to the control group on speech rate (with equal variances not assumed) using the difference between posttest and pretest as the dependent variable found a statistical difference between groups (t11.1 = -2.7, p = .02, 95% CI: -39.7,-4.2, d = 1.3)."
