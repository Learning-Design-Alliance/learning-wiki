Evidence¶

Sunny Yu 2026¶

Sunny Yu, Myra Cheng, Ahmad Jabbar, Ilia Sucholutsky, Katherine M. Collins, Dan Jurafsky, Robert D. Hawkins. (2026). The efficiency-gain illusion: People underestimate the rate of AI use and overestimate its benefits on simple tasks. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22687

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Pre-registered Study 2 (prediction sample N=600, completion sample N=1001 on Prolific) compared predicted versus actual times for independent and AI-assisted completion. The speedup illusion held "across all four task categories and both difficulty levels" per the article.

"Specifically, participants expected AI to save more time (55.7 seconds) than it actually did (7.5 seconds) (Equation (1);β=−48.33,p<0.001). This difference primarily arises from miscalibration about AI-assisted performance, i.e., ˆtA(τ) versust A(τ): the actual completion time with AI (86.2 seconds) is nearly double the predicted time with AI (43.3 seconds) (β= 42.9,p<0.001)."

Related Claims¶

  • AI assistance provides no significant overall time savings and can slow completion on easy task variants, with chat-interface friction (prompting time) dominating on trivial tasks — related
  • Practicing with AI did not create an illusion of mastery: participants reported learning and skill levels similar to other conditions — related
  • People overestimate how much mental effort AI assistance alleviates (offloading illusion), driven by overestimating the effort of independent completion while AI-assisted effort is accurately estimated — related
  • People significantly underestimate AI-assisted completion times even though actual AI-assisted and independent completion times do not differ (the speedup illusion) — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • People are well-calibrated about their own independent completion times — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • People expect AI assistance to save far more time than assistance from another highly intelligent human participant — related
  • Participants more averse to thinking are more susceptible to the speedup illusion; AI familiarity measures do not predict calibration error — related
  • AI assistance sped up only difficult tasks and only a few individual tasks, not easy ones — related
