# Individual learning curves rise by irregular increments with occasional losses, with plateau-like periods attributed to changes in method and waning interest

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` No individual progresses uniformly; gains come by irregular increments with occasional actual losses, and plateau periods between the fifth-seventh and eighth-eleventh trials are attributed by the author to changes in method of work and waning enthusiasm. [→ Baldwin 1913](#baldwin-1913)

## Evidence

### Baldwin 1913

Baldwin, B. T. (1913). The learning of delinquent adolescent girls as shown by a substitution test. The Journal of Educational Psychology, IV(6), 317-332. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0069968

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Analysis of individual practice curves for the white girls in Chart I. The author interprets the plateau periods between the fifth and seventh and the eighth and eleventh trials as "due to changes in method of work, and to the lack of the enthusiasm and interest manifested at the first trials."

> "It will be noted that no individual progresses uniformly, but each gains by irregular increments, with an occasional actual loss. There is always a gain between the first and second trial, and at the last trial."
