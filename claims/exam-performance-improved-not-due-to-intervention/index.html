Evidence¶

Nederhand 2020¶

Nederhand, M. L., Tabbers, H. K., Jongerling, J., & Rikers, R. M. J. P. (2020). Reflection on exam grades to improve calibration of secondary school students: a longitudinal study. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-020-09233-9

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Two-level multilevel model of French exam grades (1–10 scale) over the school year in the quasi-experiment. The article reports a statistically detectable increase ("b = 0.05, t(215) = 3.19, p = .002") and a statistically detectable random effect of time.

"Our results showed that performance changed statistically detectable over exams, b = 0.05, t(215) = 3.19, p = .002. During the school year, students obtained better exam grades."

Related Claims¶

  • Providing grade estimates after each exam improved secondary students' calibration accuracy over a school year, regardless of the level of reflection support — related
  • Calibration accuracy improvements on French did not transfer to German and Math courses without the intervention, arguing against a maturation explanation — related
  • The level of reflection support (practice-only, grade comparison, or reflection prompts) did not differentially affect the improvement of secondary students' calibration accuracy over time — related
  • Students in the Reflection condition became more confident in their grade estimates over the school year, unlike students in the Practice and Grade comparison conditions — related
  • Incentives significantly improved calibration accuracy only among lower-achieving students in a college-course quasi-experiment — related
  • Bias scores did not improve over the school year, but school year level mattered: year 1 students became less overconfident while year 3 students improved their underconfidence — related
