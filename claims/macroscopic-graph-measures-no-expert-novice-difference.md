## Evidence

### Steven F. Wolf 2012

Steven F. Wolf, Daniel P. Dougherty, and Gerd Kortemeyer. (2012). An empirical approach to interpreting card-sorting data. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.8.010124](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.8.010124)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

KS test of 3-cycle distributions, a statistic 'related to how often a sorter categorizes cards in multiple piles', found 'no statistically signiﬁcant diﬀerence' (p = 0.1584) between expert and novice sorters.

> "A KS-test comparing the ECDFs of expert and novice 3-cycle distributions (see Figure 4) demonstrated no statistically signiﬁcant diﬀerence (p = 0.1584)."

### Steven F. Wolf 2012 (2)

Steven F. Wolf, Daniel P. Dougherty, and Gerd Kortemeyer. (2012). An empirical approach to interpreting card-sorting data. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.8.010124](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.8.010124)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

KS test of maximum clique size distributions — 'the size of the largest “pile” that a sorter has created' — showed that experts and novices 'are not distinguishable' (p = 0.0587).

> "A Kolmogorov–Smirnov test suggests that experts and novices are not distinguishable based on their maximum clique size distributions ( p = 0.0587)."

### Steven F. Wolf 2012 (3)

Steven F. Wolf, Daniel P. Dougherty, and Gerd Kortemeyer. (2012). An empirical approach to interpreting card-sorting data. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.8.010124](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.8.010124)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

KS test of graph diameter distributions from the same MSU sorting experiment found experts and novices 'are not distinguishable' (p = 0.6432); the authors note this was expected because diameter does not take problem identity into account.

> "experts and novices are not distinguishable based on their diameter distributions ( p = 0.6432)."

### Steven F. Wolf 2012 (4)

Steven F. Wolf, Daniel P. Dougherty, and Gerd Kortemeyer. (2012). An empirical approach to interpreting card-sorting data. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.8.010124](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.8.010124)

> "However, a KS-test comparing the ECDFs of expert and novice average path length distributions (see Figure 7) demonstrated no statistically signiﬁcant diﬀer- ence ( p = 0 .3906)."
