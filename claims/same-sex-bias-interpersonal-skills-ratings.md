# Ratings of interpersonal and communicative skills show same-sex bias: each student gender rates same-gender professors more positively

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The lecturer-sex by student-sex interaction on the interpersonal and communicative skills composite was significant, with female students favoring female lecturers and male students favoring male lecturers. [→ Graves 2017](#graves-2017)

## Evidence

### Graves 2017

Graves, A.L., Hoshino-Browne, E., & Lui, K.P.H. (2017). Swimming against the Tide: Gender Bias in the Physics Classroom. Journal of Women and Minorities in Science and Engineering 23(1), 15-36. [doi:10.1615/jwomenminorscieneng.2017013584](https://doi.org/10.1615/jwomenminorscieneng.2017013584)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A priori composite (Cronbach's alpha .71) analyzed by 2x2 ANOVA in the video-lecture experiment. The interaction was significant, "F(1, 117) = 7.41, p = .007"; simple main effect tests showed female students rated female lecturers' interpersonal skills more positively than male lecturers', and male students the reverse.

> "the evaluation of interpersonal and communicative skills showed a same-sex bias (Table 3 and Figure 4). That is, while main effects of lecturer sex and student sex were not significant, these variables interacted significantly, F(1, 117) = 7.41, p = .007."
