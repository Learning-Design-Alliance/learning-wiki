Evidence¶

Agarwal 2022¶

Agarwal, B., Urlings, C., van Lankveld, G., & Klemke, R. (2022). Identifying the Ethical Values and Norms of Artificial Intelligence in Education: a Systematic Literature Review. Open University of the Netherlands. https://orcid.org/0000-0002-7347-8465

q2 · i? · review · r2

Characteristics analysis of the 25 included articles in the systematic review, classifying publication types. The review reports fourteen perspective articles, five reviews, three reports, two methodology papers and one original research, and interprets the scarcity of empirical work as ethics being "discussed as an after-thought".

"Among the 25 articles selected for this SLR, it can be seen that methodology papers (two articles) and original research (one article) together make up only 12% of the selected literature. This implies that ethics is not integrated into the design of research but discussed as an after-thought instead."

Agarwal 2022 (2)¶

Agarwal, B., Urlings, C., van Lankveld, G., & Klemke, R. (2022). Identifying the Ethical Values and Norms of Artificial Intelligence in Education: a Systematic Literature Review. Open University of the Netherlands. https://orcid.org/0000-0002-7347-8465

q2 · i? · review · r2

Characteristics-table analysis of educational settings across the 25 included articles. The review reports that eight articles mention an educational setting, six focusing on tertiary education and two on K-12, and reads this as limited K-12 attention, citing Akgun and Greenhow (2021) and Adams et al. (2021).

"Among the eight articles that mention an educational level, only two of them address K-12 education. This suggests that there is limited attention paid to AI ethics in K-12 education, supporting the conclusions made by Akgun and Greenhow (2021) and Adams et al. (2021)."
