## Evidence

### Anastasia Sofroniou and Konstantinos Poutos 2016

Anastasia Sofroniou and Konstantinos Poutos. (2016). Investigating the Effectiveness of Group Work in Mathematics. Educ. Sci. 2016, 6, 30. https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci6030030

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of end-of-year exam results between an Experimental class (23 students, integration taught via group work) and a Control class (16 students, traditional arrangements). The article reports performance in integration questions improved "by around 109%" relative to the control class.

> "Average Performance Ratio for the Experimental Class(with group work) = 1.807 Average Performance Ratio for the Control Class(no group work) = 0.863"

### Anastasia Sofroniou and Konstantinos Poutos 2016 (2)

Anastasia Sofroniou and Konstantinos Poutos. (2016). Investigating the Effectiveness of Group Work in Mathematics. Educ. Sci. 2016, 6, 30. https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci6030030

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r1`

Effect-size analysis of the exam performance ratios from the two classes, computed as the difference in average performance ratio divided by the pooled standard deviation; the article prints a d-index of 0.3, with the average experimental student exceeding "the scores of 62% of the control class".

> "The analysis deduced an effect size of 0.3, meaning that the performance of the average student in the experimental class is 0.3 standard deviations above the average student in the control class and hence exceeds the scores of 62% of the control class"
