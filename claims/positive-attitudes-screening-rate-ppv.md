# More positive attitudes towards dementia care are associated with a lower positive screening rate and a higher positive predictive value

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In fully adjusted models, more positive attitudes towards dementia care were associated with a lower positive rate of screening (OR = 0.948, 95%CI: 0.905–0.994) and a higher PPV (OR = 1.114, 95%CI: 1.007–1.234); the article prints no standardised effect size. [→ Qiu Yanglin 2025](#qiu-yanglin-2025)

## Evidence

### Qiu Yanglin 2025

Qiu Yanglin, Fan Xinyu, Wu Yanjuan, Wang Shibin, Tan Wenyan, Liao Jing. (2025). Variation in dementia screening outcomes: the influence of primary care providers' occupations and knowledge, attitudes, skills. BMC Primary Care. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-025-02886-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Model 3 multilevel logistic regression on the positive rate among 2823 screened older adults nested in 252 PCPs, using the Dementia Care Attitudes Scale. The printed association is "OR = 0.948, 95%CI: 0.905–0.994" per unit of attitude score; no standardised effect size is printed.

> "The more positive attitudes the PCPs towards dementia care (OR = 0.948, 95%CI: 0.905–0.994), the less likely they were to screen older adults positive for dementia."

### Qiu Yanglin 2025 (2)

Qiu Yanglin, Fan Xinyu, Wu Yanjuan, Wang Shibin, Tan Wenyan, Liao Jing. (2025). Variation in dementia screening outcomes: the influence of primary care providers' occupations and knowledge, attitudes, skills. BMC Primary Care. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-025-02886-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Model 3 multilevel logistic regression on PPV among the 595 older adults completing the second-stage diagnosis. The printed association is "OR = 1.114, 95%CI: 1.007–1.234" per unit of attitude score; no standardised effect size is printed.

> "the more positive attitudes PCPs towards dementia care (OR = 1.114, 95%CI: 1.007–1.234), the more likely it was that older adults screened positive by PCPs would be truly positive."
