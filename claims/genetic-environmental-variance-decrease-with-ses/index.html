Genetic covariance structure modeling in a classical twin design (2479 MZ and 4450 DZ pairs), with means and variance components estimated per SES group and corrected for censoring; variance components decreased with increasing SES (Δ−2LL = 89.7, p < 0.001; Table 1).

"Noticeably, genetic and environmental variance differed across the groups and depended on SES ( Δ−2LL = 89.7, Δdf = 9, p < 0.001). Speci ﬁ-cally, the genetic and environmental variance components decreased with increasing SES."

Related Claims¶

  • Average educational achievement at age 12 increases with parental SES in the Netherlands — related
  • Educational achievement is highly heritable, and the common environmental variance is attributable to SES effects — related
  • A Classical Twin Design model of educational attainment in the TwinLife sample estimates shared environmental influence at 43% and additive genetic influence at 34% — related
  • Twin-specific shared environments account for a substantial portion of CTD shared-environment estimates for educational attainment and cannot be interpreted as between-family environmental differences — related
  • Under phenotypic assortment NTFD models, parental education shows no environmental effect on offspring educational attainment once genetic influences are accounted for — related
  • The best-fitting NTFD model estimates heritability of educational attainment at 51% and total shared environmental influence at 26%, disaggregated into twin-specific (16%) and sibling-shared (10%) components — related
  • Adjusting published twin-study ACE estimates for assortative mating raises mean heritability by about 17 points and lowers shared environmental estimates by about 16 points — related
  • SES does not moderate genetic, shared, or nonshared environmental variance in four-year-olds' learning motivation — related
  • Non-shared environmental variance in educational performance decreases with increasing school SES, but this moderation appears attributable to parental SES — related
  • School quality does not moderate genetic and shared-environmental influences on educational performance once moderation by school SES and parental SES is considered — related
  • Genetic variance in educational performance decreases with increasing school SES, partly independent of parental SES — related
  • In a genetically informed twin study, the effect of shared family environment was substantially reduced among twins whose tracking was delayed until a later age — related
  • High heritability does not imply that environmental interventions to modify a trait are doomed to failure — related
  • For childhood outcomes (academic achievement, ADHD symptoms), genetic-nurturing designs found only direct genetic effects and no genetic nurturing — related
