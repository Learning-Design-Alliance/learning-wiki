# The gap between Chicago Public Schools students and other Illinois students in ISAT reading is about half as large in eighth grade as in third grade

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The narrowing holds at the 25th percentile: the third-grade reading gap of 23 points shrinks to 7 points by eighth grade. [→ John Q. Easton 2007](#john-q-easton-2007)

## Evidence

### John Q. Easton 2007

John Q. Easton, Stuart Luppescu, Todd Rosenkranz. (2007). 2006 ISAT Reading and Math Scores in Chicago and the Rest of the State. Research Brief, Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/2006-isat-reading-and-math-scores-chicago-and-rest-state

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Box-plot analysis of the score distribution shows the narrowing is strongest for relatively low-performing students: at the 25th percentile the gap falls from 23 points to "seven points (229 vs. 236)" between third and eighth grade. No standardized effect size is printed for this percentile contrast.

> "In third-grade reading, for example, CPS students lag behind the rest of the state by 23 points (168 vs. 191). By eighth grade, that difference is down to seven points (229 vs. 236)."
