## Evidence

### Rusk A 2012

Rusk A, Smith N, Menya D, Obala A, Simiyu C, Khwa-Otsyula B, O'Meara W. (2012). Does anti-malarial drug knowledge predict anti-malarial dispensing practice in drug outlets? A survey of medicine retailers in western Kenya. Malaria Journal. https://doi.org/10.1186/1475-2875-11-263

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional survey analysis of open-ended stocking-motivation responses: MOH-guideline stockers "were 6 times more likely" to identify the recommended anti-malarial for children (OR = 6.00, 95% CI 1.90 – 18.99; p = 0.002) versus demand-driven stockers.

> "Those who said they stock according to the anti-malarials recommended by the Ministry of Health (n = 24) were 6 times more likely to correctly identify the MOH-recommended anti-malarial for children under 5 than those who stock according to what their customers demand (n = 74; OR = 6.00, 95% CI 1.90 – 18.99; 0.002)."

### Rusk A 2012 (2)

Rusk A, Smith N, Menya D, Obala A, Simiyu C, Khwa-Otsyula B, O'Meara W. (2012). Does anti-malarial drug knowledge predict anti-malarial dispensing practice in drug outlets? A survey of medicine retailers in western Kenya. Malaria Journal. https://doi.org/10.1186/1475-2875-11-263

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional survey analysis of open-ended dispensing-choice responses: MOH-guideline dispensers were "seven times more likely to recommend the appropriate first-line treatment to children" (OR = 7.00, 95% CI 1.24 – 39.49; p = 0.027).

> "Those who would dispense according to MOH guidelines (n = 11) were seven times more likely to recommend the appropriate first-line treatment to children than those who dispense according to customer demands (n = 41; OR = 7.00, 95% CI 1.24 – 39.49; 0.027)."
