# Students positioned as leaders thickened an “agents of change” identity by using surveys, data, and graphs to convince others, including teachers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Cathy and Iris used survey data and graphs to persuade audiences (peers, teachers, principal, school council), and their leader identities thickened into “agents of change.” [→ Takeuchi 2020](#takeuchi-2020)

## Evidence

### Takeuchi 2020

Takeuchi, M.A. (2020). Geopolitical configuration of identities and learning: Othering through the institutionalized categorization of “English language learners. Cognition and Instruction. Advance online publication https://doi.org/10.1080/07370008.2020.1825438

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Case analysis of two focal students in the ethnographic video study. Cathy presented a double bar graph comparing teacher and student responses to the principal; Iris presented survey results on recess activities. Their suggestions appeared as action items in school council minutes, and they later served as Peace Leaders and Play Leaders.

> "For Iris and Cathy, their “leader” identities were thickened through the transforming recess unit and they became “agents of change” as they used surveys, data, numbers, and graphs to convince others, including teachers."
