Evidence¶

Burnett Marketa 2022¶

Burnett Marketa, McBride Margarett, Green McKenzie N., Cooper Shauna M. (2022). “When I Think of Black Girls, I Think of Opportunities”: Black Girls' Identity Development and the Protective Role of Parental Socialization in Educational Settings. Frontiers in Psychology. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.933476

q2 · i? · qualitative · r?

Interview analysis found stereotypes related to "vocality and loudness of Black girls, policing of their bodies or suggestions about promiscuity, and Black girls' intelligence or academic potential." One participant described teachers placing her in different classes based on stereotypes.

"Many participants across developmental stage spoke of the stereotypes they were aware of or had encountered that were specific to Black girls."

Related Claims¶

  • Middle school girls framed Blackness as celebratory culture while high school girls emphasized individual efforts to overcome negative portrayals — related
  • In the ethnography the article analyzes, black girls bussed to an affluent suburban school were unpopular and socially excluded for being seen as loud and ghetto, while black boys were classified as incapable of academic achievement — related
  • Two gendered classroom cultures coexisted: rule-following vocal girls and disruptive boys receiving disproportionate time and space — related
  • In the analyzed school setting, black girls experience testimonial injustice as direct discrimination and hermeneutical injustice as indirect, structural discrimination that personal effort cannot erode — related
  • All participating girls reported parents helping them navigate school-based conflict, with parents advising silence or non-engagement as a protective strategy — related
  • Black girls' awareness of difficulties navigating educational settings is coupled with parental support that promotes positive gendered racial identities — related
  • In the analyzed school ethnography, stereotyping operates as a rationalization process that constructs uniformities, and the ghetto label privileges white culture's standards and evaluation networks — related
