# Design majors outperform matched peers on divergent thinking, though AUT originality shows no significant difference

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` The design group scored significantly higher on TTCT-figural fluency, originality, and combined scores (d = 0.79 to 0.86) and on AUT combined creativity (d = 0.50), while AUT originality did not differ (d = 0.19). [→ Jing Teng 2025](#jing-teng-2025)

## Evidence

### Jing Teng 2025

Jing Teng, Xinuo Qiao, Kelong Lu, Tuo Liu, Xinyue Wang, Zhenni Gao, Tingting Yu & Ning Hao (2025). Neural mechanisms underpinning the association between visual arts education and creativity. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00388-1

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Weighted independent-samples t-tests (FDR-corrected) on verbal (AUT) and figural (TTCT) divergent thinking in the propensity-score-matched sample of 78 participants. The quote reports the TTCT-figural effects (d = 0.79 to 0.86, i.e., medium effects). The article also reports AUT combined scores significant (d = 0.50), fluency marginal (d = 0.45), and originality non-significant (d = 0.19).

> "In the TTCT -figural task (see Fig. 3d), the design group scored significantly higher for fluency (t (72) = 3.47, pcorr = 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.79), originality (t (74) = 3.47, pcorr = 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.79), and combined creativity scores (t (68) = 3.80, pcorr < 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.86)."
