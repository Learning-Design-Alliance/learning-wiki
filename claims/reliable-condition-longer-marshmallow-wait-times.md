# Children in the reliable condition waited significantly longer on the marshmallow task than children in the unreliable condition (p < 0.0005)

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Kidd 2013

Kidd, C., Palmeri, H., & Aslin, R. N. (2013). Rational snacking: Young children's decision-making on the marshmallow task is moderated by beliefs about environmental reliability. Cognition, 126(1), 109-114. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2012.08.004

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized experiment with 28 children (ages 3;6-5;10) assigned to reliable or unreliable experimenter conditions before the marshmallow task. The unreliable group's mean wait was "M = 181.57 s" versus 722.43 s; Wilcoxon rank-sum W = 22.5, p < 0.0005. No effect size is printed.

> "Children in the unreliable condition waited without eating the marshmallow for a mean duration of 3 min and 2 s (M = 181.57 s). In contrast, children in the reliable condition waited 12 min and 2 s (M = 722.43 s). A Wilcoxon rank-sum test (also known as a Mann–Whitney Wilcoxon or a Mann–Whitney U) confirmed that this difference was highly significant (W = 22.5, p < 0.0005)."
