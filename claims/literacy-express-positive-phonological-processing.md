# Literacy Express has positive effects on phonological processing for preschool children, with significant effects in two of three studies

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Farver, Lonigan, and Eppe (2009) found a statistically significant and substantively important positive effect size of 0.54 across Blending and Elision subtests. [→ Literacy Express 2010](#literacy-express-2010)

## Evidence

### Literacy Express 2010

Literacy Express. (2010). WWC Intervention Report: Literacy Express. U.S. Department of Education, What Works Clearinghouse. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/288

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Los Angeles Head Start randomized trial (94 children analyzed): Pre-CTOPPP Blending and Elision subtests showed a printed effect size of 0.54, with "0.51 for Elision and 0.56 for Blending", favoring Literacy Express over High/Scope.

> "Their results showed, and the WWC confirmed, a statistically significant and substantively important positive effect of 0.54 across the two measures (0.51 for Elision and 0.56 for Blending)."

### Literacy Express 2010 (2)

Literacy Express. (2010). WWC Intervention Report: Literacy Express. U.S. Department of Education, What Works Clearinghouse. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/288

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Florida/California preschool randomized trial (722 children): printed effect size of 0.26 across Blending and Elision; per the report, driven primarily by a 0.38 effect on Elision, with Blending neither significant nor substantively important.

> "Their analyses showed, and the WWC confirmed, a statistically significant and substantively important positive effect of 0.26 across the two measures."

### Literacy Express 2010 (3)

Literacy Express. (2010). WWC Intervention Report: Literacy Express. U.S. Department of Education, What Works Clearinghouse. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/288

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

PCER Florida randomized trial (188 children analyzed) using the Pre-CTOPPP Elision subtest found no statistically significant or substantively important difference between Literacy Express and High/Scope.

> "The authors report, and the WWC confirms, that the difference between the Literacy Express group and the control group is not statistically significant or substantively important on this measure."
