## Evidence

### Tong Wan and Zhongzhou Chen 2024

Tong Wan and Zhongzhou Chen. (2024). Exploring Generative AI assisted feedback writing for students' written responses to a physics conceptual question with prompt engineering and few-shot learning. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010152](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010152)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Stage 2 usefulness ratings by the same four student researchers across 16 responses, shown in Figure 4. The article reports only descriptive distributions with no statistical test or effect size.

> "It appears all the student researchers rated the GPT-generated feedback as more useful. Student A showed the strongest preference toward GPT-generated feedback: three quarters of the GPT-generated feedback was rated more useful than human-written feedback."

### Tong Wan and Zhongzhou Chen 2024 (2)

Tong Wan and Zhongzhou Chen. (2024). Exploring Generative AI assisted feedback writing for students' written responses to a physics conceptual question with prompt engineering and few-shot learning. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010152](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010152)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Qualitative examination of the five feedback statements rated unanimously more useful (Table I), which came from the categories correct conclusion with correct explanation and no explanation. The authors infer the length and greater extent of addressing the response as the reason for the preference.

> "At a quick glance, all five GPT-generated feedback that were perceived as more useful are much longer than the corresponding human-written feedback. Taking a closer look, GPT-generated feedback addresses students' responses to a greater extent."
