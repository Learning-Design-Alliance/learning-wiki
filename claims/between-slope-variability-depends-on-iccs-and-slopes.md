# The variability of the between slope estimate in the multilevel latent covariate model decreases with more classes and students, with higher ICC(1) of the ratings and larger slopes, but increases with higher ICC(1) of the outcome

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The variability of the between slope decreases as the number of classes and the number of students per class increase. [→ Steffen Zitzmann 2021](#steffen-zitzmann-2021)
`q2 i?` The ICC(1) of the students' ratings decreases the variability of the between slope, whereas the ICC(1) of the achievement variable increases it. [→ Steffen Zitzmann 2021](#steffen-zitzmann-2021)

## Evidence

### Steffen Zitzmann 2021

Steffen Zitzmann, Wolfgang Wagner, Martin Hecht, Christoph Helm, Christian Fischer, Lisa Bardach, Richard Göllner. (2021). How Many Classes and Students Should Ideally be Sampled When Assessing the Role of Classroom Climate via Student Ratings on a Limited Budget? An Optimal Design Perspective. Educational Psychology Review (2022) 34:511–536. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09635-4

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical derivation using a first-order Taylor expansion of the variability of the between slope in Lüdtke et al.'s (2008) model, assuming standardized variables. The derivation shows "the variability will decrease when the number of classes (J ) gets larger" and that the two ICC(1) values have opposite effects on variability.

> "it shows that the variability is a function of the sample size, which means that the variability will decrease when the number of classes (J ) gets larger and all other quantities are held constant."
