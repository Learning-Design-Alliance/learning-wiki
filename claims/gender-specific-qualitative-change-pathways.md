## Evidence

### Meshesha Yeshitla Meaza 2026

Meshesha Yeshitla Meaza, Weldmeskel Fisseha Mikre. (2026). Gender differences in causal attributions, self-regulation, achievement motivation, and academic performance following an intervention. BMC Psychology, 14, 588. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-04357-0

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Thematic analysis of semi-structured interviews with 12 purposively selected students found gender-differentiated starting points; one male stated "Before it was luck, but after the training it is effort," while a female reported the training "cleared my hesitation about ability."

> "Before the intervention, male students predominantly attributed outcomes to luck, whereas female students tended to attribute outcomes to ability, difficulty of tasks, and personal limitations"

### Meshesha Yeshitla Meaza 2026 (2)

Meshesha Yeshitla Meaza, Weldmeskel Fisseha Mikre. (2026). Gender differences in causal attributions, self-regulation, achievement motivation, and academic performance following an intervention. BMC Psychology, 14, 588. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-04357-0

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Interview themes indicated males improved via study schedules and distraction management, while females reported mood-independent studying and self-monitoring; in motivation, males gained discipline and effort beliefs while females gained self-belief and future orientation.

> "males improved in structured behavioral control, while females showed greater growth in emotional regulation and autonomous self-monitoring"
