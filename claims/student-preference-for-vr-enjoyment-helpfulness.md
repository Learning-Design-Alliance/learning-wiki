## Subclaims

## Evidence

### J. R. Smith 2018

J. R. Smith, A. Byrum, T. M. McCormick, N. Young, C. Orban, and C. D. Porter. (2018). A controlled study of stereoscopic virtual reality in freshman electrostatics. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/perc.2017.pr.089](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.089)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Attitudinal questions administered to the same randomized treatment groups in the electric field study. On a scale from highly unenjoyable to highly enjoyable, the VR group's helpfulness mean of 1.06 exceeded the others' 0.70; enjoyment means were 1.1 versus 0.68 by a chi-squared test (χ2 = 153, p < 0.01).

> "Here also the VR treatment (mean = 1.06) scored signiﬁcantly higher than the others (mean = 0.70), withp< 0.01."
