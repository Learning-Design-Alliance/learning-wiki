Evidence¶

Brummelman 2024¶

Brummelman, E., van Atteveldt, N., Wolf, S. & Sierksma, J. (2024). Using social and behavioral science to address achievement inequality. npj Science of Learning, 9, 44. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00259-1

q2 · i? · review · r2

The editorial reports, citing Wolf and Lichand, a preregistered randomized controlled trial of SMS and audio nudges to parents and/or teachers in Cote d'Ivoire. The overall result was null: the intervention "did not reduce child labor and did not raise learning outcomes". No effect sizes are printed.

"Overall, the intervention did not reduce child labor and did not raise learning outcomes, regardless of whether the intervention targeted parents, teachers, or both."

Brummelman 2024 (2)¶

Brummelman, E., van Atteveldt, N., Wolf, S. & Sierksma, J. (2024). Using social and behavioral science to address achievement inequality. npj Science of Learning, 9, 44. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00259-1

q2 · i? · causal · r2

The editorial reports the same trial's subgroup finding: for children starting with low learning levels, the parent-focused arm "raised learning outcomes" and raised child labor, specifically domestic work such as cleaning, not cocoa farming. No effect sizes are printed.

"Among chil- dren who had started off with low learning levels, however, the parent-focused intervention raised learning outcomes and, seemingly paradoxically, also raised child labor."
