## Evidence

### Dalum Jesper 2024

Dalum Jesper, Paulsson Liselotte, Christidis Nikolaos, Andersson Franko Mikael, Karlgren Klas, Leanderson Charlotte, Sandborgh-Englund Gunilla. (2024). Consistency between inter-institutional panels using a three-level Angoff-standard setting in licensure tests of foreign-trained dentists in Sweden: A cohort study. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0313476

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Analysis of consistency between Angoff ratings and item difficulty (participant mean scores) across 267 items. Five disciplines showed "ρ 0.40–0.69, p≤0.001"; Table 2 reports total-item correlations of .493 (reference) and .448 (external).

> "There was a strong relationship between how the panels rated the exam items and how the participants performed in the dental disciplines of Cariology, Endodontology, Oral Radiology, Periodontology, and Paediatric Dentistry (ρ 0.40–0.69, p≤0.001)."

### Dalum Jesper 2024 (2)

Dalum Jesper, Paulsson Liselotte, Christidis Nikolaos, Andersson Franko Mikael, Karlgren Klas, Leanderson Charlotte, Sandborgh-Englund Gunilla. (2024). Consistency between inter-institutional panels using a three-level Angoff-standard setting in licensure tests of foreign-trained dentists in Sweden: A cohort study. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0313476

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same rating-versus-difficulty analysis, discipline-level results. Prosthodontics correlations were non-significant (p = .428 and .752 in Table 2); orofacial medicine reference correlation was non-significant (p = .074).

> "Prosthodontics showed the weakest correlation between panel ratings and participant scores(Reference = 0.148, External = 0.059). Orofacial Medicine had a moderate correlation(Reference = 0.306, External = 0.399)"
