# Adjusting for background variables and excluding exam-failure ARCP outcomes eliminated the difference in ARCP outcomes between UK overseas and home graduates

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In multivariable models excluding exam-failure ARCPs, the UK OG vs UKG difference was no longer statistically significant (OR 1.05, 95% CI 0.90 to 1.22, p>0.5). [→ Tiffin Paul A 2018](#tiffin-paul-a-2018)

## Evidence

### Tiffin Paul A 2018

Tiffin Paul A, Orr James, Paton Lewis W, Smith Daniel T, Norcini John J. (2018). UK nationals who received their medical degrees abroad: selection into, and subsequent performance in postgraduate training: a national data linkage study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2018-023060

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multivariable multilevel ordinal logistic regression (table 7) on the same 34,755-doctor dataset. UK OGs vs UKG excluding exam failures: "OR 1.05 (0.90 to 1.22)" with p>0.5; including exam failures OR 1.08 (p=0.3). The article reports the difference "disappearing entirely" after adjustment.

> "when the impact of differential postgraduate exam pass rates were also adjusted for (by excluding the relevant ARCP outcomes), the intergroup differences further reduced, disappearing entirely for UK OGs versus home medical graduates."
