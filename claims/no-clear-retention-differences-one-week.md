## Evidence

### Harris David J. 2017

Harris David J., Vine Samuel J., Wilson Mark R., McGrath John S., LeBel Marie-Eve, Buckingham Gavin. (2017). The effect of observing novice and expert performance on acquisition of surgical skills on a robotic platform. PLoS ONE 12(11): e0188233. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0188233

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc tests at the one-week retention test on errors/second in the randomized four-group experiment. The analysis "indicated no significant pairwise differences" after correction, with only trends (p = .07) for novice and expert versus mixed.

> "After correction for multiple comparisons, however, post-hoc analysis indicated no significant pairwise differences, although there was a trend towards both novice (p = .07, BF10 = 11.44) and expert (p = .07, BF10 = 9.00) groups showing a difference from the mixed condition."

### Harris David J. 2017 (2)

Harris David J., Vine Samuel J., Wilson Mark R., McGrath John S., LeBel Marie-Eve, Buckingham Gavin. (2017). The effect of observing novice and expert performance on acquisition of surgical skills on a robotic platform. PLoS ONE 12(11): e0188233. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0188233

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Bayes factor equivalence analysis at retention. The article reports "BF10 = 0.27 (p = .99) suggests that Expert and Novice groups were equivalent, as were Control and Mixed BF10 = 0.30", with inconclusive evidence for other control contrasts.

> "A Bayes factor of BF10 = 0.27 (p = .99) suggests that Expert and Novice groups were equivalent, as were Control and Mixed BF10 = 0.30 (p = .99), but there was inconclusive evidence for Control v Expert, (p = .27, BF10 = 0.78) and Control v Novice (p = .27, BF10 = 0.76)."
