## Evidence

### Doyle Patricia 2015

Doyle Patricia, VanDenKerkhof Elizabeth G, Edge Dana S, Ginsburg Liane, Goldstein David H. (2015). Self-reported patient safety competence among Canadian medical students and postgraduate trainees: a cross-sectional survey. BMJ Quality & Safety. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjqs-2014-003142

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

ANOVA comparisons by year on broader-aspects survey items among 255 medical students. Only 24% of students overall agreed system aspects were well covered and 35% agreed preceptor consistency existed.

> "significantly more first-year students than students in years 2–4 agreed that system aspects were well covered in their programme (1st year Mean (M)=3.4, 2nd=2.6, 3rd=2.6, 4th=2.7, Statistic (F)=9.3, p<0.01). First-year and second-year medical students also reported significantly more consistency in how patient safety issues are dealt with by different preceptors compared with students in years 3 and 4 (1st year M=3.4, 2nd=3.2, 3rd=2.9, 4th=2.7, F=6.1, p<0.01)."
