# Simulation-based instruction produced significantly higher post-test scores across all three test sections: fill in the blanks, matching, and short answer.

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Post-test section-level analysis showed statistically significant differences favoring the experimental group in fill in the blanks (p = 0.01), matching (p < 0.001), and short answer (p < 0.001) sections. [→ Sasikala 2016](#sasikala-2016)

`q2 i?` Experimental group students outscored control group students significantly in all three test sections: fill in the blanks (7.53 vs 5.5), matching (8.00 vs 4.91), and short answer (8.33 vs 5.75). [→ Reddy and Mint 2017](#reddy-and-mint-2017)

## Evidence

### Sasikala 2016

Sasikala, P., & Tanyong, S. (2016). A Study on Simulation Methods in Academic Success With Reference To Teaching Biology for Education Students. Journal of Education and Practice, 7(11). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1099628

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Section-level post-test analysis of the quasi-experimental study found experimental group means of 7.333 ± 1.271 (fill in the blanks), 7.833 ± 1.227 (matching), and 8.333 ± 0.857 (short answer) versus control means of 5.5 ± 1.101, 5 ± 0.939, and 5.333 ± 0.542, with "statistically significant variations" (p = 0.01, p < 0.001, and p < 0.001).

> "The results of the present study shows that statistically significant variations between the means of control and experimental group students of education (p= 0.01, p< 0.001, and p< 0.001)."
