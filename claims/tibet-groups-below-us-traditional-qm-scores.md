## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The average scores of all three Tibet University groups (1.54, 0.83, 0.92) were lower than the US traditional-course average of about 1.88 on the same six questions, and students' answers clustered in misconception-related distractors rather than being random. ## Evidence

### Guangtian Zhu 2024

Guangtian Zhu, Jing Hu, Chun Du. (2024). Assisting Tibetan Students in Learning Quantum Mechanics via Mathematica. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2406.18810](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2406.18810)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Benchmarking of Tibet University quiz scores against previously collected QMS data from US public research universities. The article reports the US averages of "about 1.88" (traditional) and "4.64" (PER tutorials) and that students' "choices fell in several distractors that reflect ed students' common misconceptions".

> "The average is about 1.88 for the students in traditional QM course and 4.64 for the students using PER -based tutorials [38]. None of the three groups in the Tibet University achieved a satisfactory average score on the quiz."
