## Evidence

### Josephine C. Meyer 2026

Josephine C. Meyer, Steven J. Pollock, Bethany R. Wilcox, and Gina Passante. (2026). Computer-generated QIS tutorial feedback is valued by students, but does not replicate in-class collaboration. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2607.15444](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.15444)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview reflection from Diana, a solo AcePhysics participant, on her nonuse of hints. The article reports her and Carlos's nonuse ran counter to the developers' intention of a friendly, formative atmosphere. Carlos said "I just didn't think to look at the hint."

> "I just really didn't want to use the hints because ... this is pretty basic starting stuff ... so I didn't want to click the hints unless I was really struggling with them."

### Josephine C. Meyer 2026 (2)

Josephine C. Meyer, Steven J. Pollock, Bethany R. Wilcox, and Gina Passante. (2026). Computer-generated QIS tutorial feedback is valued by students, but does not replicate in-class collaboration. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2607.15444](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.15444)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Observation from the solo AcePhysics interviews: both students dismissed the embedded timing cue meant to interrupt unproductive struggle, and one had not read it. The article notes such cues are only useful if students have sufficient awareness to recognize they need them.

> "Tellingly, both Carlos and Diana closed a system dialogue box advising them not to spend more than 5 minutes on the pretest warm-up and continued to persevere fruitlessly; Carlos remarked he hadn't even read it."
