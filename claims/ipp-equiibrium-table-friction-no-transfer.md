## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Effect of Misconception on Transfer in Problem Solving. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07686](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07686)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Classroom multiple-choice data comparing 115 students who answered only the table question with a different 272 students who answered the Arnold question first. The most common incorrect response on the table problem was µsN = 600 N (~40%), with or without the paired question, indicating the misconception dominated regardless of pairing.

> "Out of the 115 students, 22% provided a correct response when only question (8) was asked. Out of the 272 students, 69% provided a correct response for question (7) and only 24% for question (8)."
