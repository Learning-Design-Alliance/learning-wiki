## Evidence

### Alysa Malespina 2024

Alysa Malespina, Fargol Seifollahi, Chandralekha Singh. (2024). Bioscience students in physics courses with higher test anxiety have lower grades on high-stakes assessments and women report more test anxiety than men. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.3390/educsci14101092](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci14101092)

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Unpaired t-tests comparing male and female students in a two-semester bioscience physics sequence at a large US research university; Physics 1 sample was 67% women. The article reports large differences, "gender differences were large ( d ∼ 0.8) for test anxiety and self-efficacy" in Physics 1 (e.g., pre-test anxiety d = 0.83).

> "In general, men reported higher self-efficacy and lower test anxiety than women in both Physics 1 and Physics 2, for both the pre- and post-surveys. However, the magnitude of differences differed by construct and course. In Physics 1, gender differences were large ( d ∼ 0.8) for test anxiety and self-efficacy on both pre- and post-surveys."
