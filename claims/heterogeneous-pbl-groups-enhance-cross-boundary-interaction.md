## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Singaram Veena S. 2010

Singaram Veena S., van der Vleuten Cees P. M., Stevens Fred, Dolmans Diana H. J. M. (2010). “For most of us Africans, we don’t just speak”: a qualitative investigation into collaborative heterogeneous PBL group learning. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-010-9262-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Focus group study with a stratified sample of 20 second-year medical students in two groups of ten, each meeting twice, at a South African medical school. Thematic analysis found students "bond irrespective of race, colour and creed" during PBL tasks; one student described greeting people "you'd never think you'd greet."

> "The collaborative group setting creates a sense of familiarity and togetherness that leads to intergroup relations in mixed groups. Students get to know each other better and bond irrespective of race, colour and creed as they focus on the tasks and learning goals using the PBL steps."
