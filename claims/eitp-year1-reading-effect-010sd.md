# After the first year of the EITP pilot, cohort 1 schools improved reading achievement by 0.10 standard deviations relative to cohort 2 schools

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` A process-based teacher evaluation pilot produced a statistically significant 0.10 SD improvement in school-level reading achievement after one year. [→ Matthew P. Steinberg and Lauren Sartain 2015](#matthew-p-steinberg-and-lauren-sartain-2015)

## Evidence

### Matthew P. Steinberg and Lauren Sartain 2015

Matthew P. Steinberg and Lauren Sartain. (2015). Does Teacher Evaluation Improve School Performance? Experimental Evidence from Chicago's Excellence in Teaching Project. Association for Education Finance and Policy. https://doi.org/10.1162/EDFP_a_00173

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled comparison of 44 cohort 1 pilot schools and 49 cohort 2 comparison schools; intent-to-treat regression with area fixed effects, school covariates, and baseline reading. The printed effect size is 0.10 standard deviations in reading, significant at the 5 percent level in table 8.

> "net of school covariates and controlling for the schools' baseline reading achievement, reading achievement in cohort 1 schools improved by 0. 10 standard deviations compared with cohort 2 schools (see column 4 of table 8)."
