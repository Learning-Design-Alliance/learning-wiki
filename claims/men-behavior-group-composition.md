# Men behaved differently depending on group gender composition: men in single-gender groups were more often high Equipment users, while men in mixed-gender groups were more often high Other users

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Men's cluster membership differed significantly between mixed- and single-gender groups (χ²(3)=12.10, p=0.007, V=0.15), with men in single-gender groups more likely in the Equipment cluster and less likely in the Other cluster. [→ Katherine N. Quinn 2020](#katherine-n-quinn-2020)

## Evidence

### Katherine N. Quinn 2020

Katherine N. Quinn, Michelle M. Kelley, Kathryn L. McGill, Emily M. Smith, Zachary Whipps, and N.G. Holmes. (2020). Group roles in unstructured labs show inequitable gender divide. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010129](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010129)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Observational cluster analysis broken down by group composition in the inquiry labs. The chi-squared test compared men's cluster membership in mixed- versus single-gender groups, finding a statistically significant difference with Cramér's V = 0.15.

> "Statistically significant differences also existed between men in mixed- versus single-gender groups, shown in Fig. 6(c) (χ2(3) = 12 .10, p = 0.007, VCramer = 0.15)"

### Katherine N. Quinn 2020 (2)

Katherine N. Quinn, Michelle M. Kelley, Kathryn L. McGill, Emily M. Smith, Zachary Whipps, and N.G. Holmes. (2020). Group roles in unstructured labs show inequitable gender divide. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010129](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010129)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same cluster analysis restricted to mixed-gender inquiry-lab groups, comparing men's and women's cluster membership within those groups via chi-squared test (p = 0.02, V = 0.15).

> "Men in mixed-gender groups were more likely than their female group members to be in the Other cluster, and women in mixed-gender groups were more likely than their male group members to be in the Laptop cluster (χ2(3) = 10.34, p = 0.02, VCramer = 0.15)"
