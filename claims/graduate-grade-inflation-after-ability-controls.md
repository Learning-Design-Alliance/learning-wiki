## Evidence

### Lee V 2026

Lee V, Kuncel NR, Sackett PR. (2026). Graduate grade inflation at a U.S. research-intensive university: A 22-year longitudinal analysis. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0341315

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive statistics by admission year (Table 1) at one large US Midwestern university show rising mean GPAs for both degree levels, with printed standardized differences d = 0.56 (dmasters) and d = 0.36 (ddoctoral); GRE also rose more weakly (dmasters = 0.33; ddoctoral = 0.53).

> "The mean GPAs for both master’s and doctoral programs exhibit an increasing trend from 1999 to 2020 (dmasters = 0.56; ddoctoral = 0.36)."

### Lee V 2026 (2)

Lee V, Kuncel NR, Sackett PR. (2026). Graduate grade inflation at a U.S. research-intensive university: A 22-year longitudinal analysis. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0341315

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear mixed effects models (Model 6) on N = 40,516 students nested in programs added GRE total scores and a missingness indicator as level-1 covariates; the time effect remained significant, which the authors read as "indicating potential grade inflation". No effect size is printed for this adjusted trend.

> "Regarding the effect of time, results continued to show that time significantly and nonlinearly predicted GPA even after controlling for GRE scores, indicating potential grade inflation."
