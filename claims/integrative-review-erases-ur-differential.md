## Evidence

### Orellana Minerva A. 2025

Orellana Minerva A., Beetler Danielle J., Silvano Carmen J., Wuertz Ryan, Weisbrod Jennifer L., Roberts Lewis R., Windebank Anthony J., Enders Felicity T., Walther-Antonio Marina R. (2025). Integrative review in PhD admissions: A case study of efficiently minimizing bias while maximizing the student narrative. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0323426

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Before-after effect modification models comparing the integrative review era (n = 102) with its algorithmic predecessor (n = 54). The study reports the "differential effect for UR compared to non-UR students was no longer statistically significant" across all stages; UR odds ratios were 1.5, 3.17, and 3.17 with non-significant p-values.

> "the differential effect for UR compared to non-UR students was no longer statistically significant, unlike when we compared the algorithmic review process to a traditional review process. This was true at all admissions stages."
