Evidence¶

Condelli 2010¶

Condelli, L., Cronen, S., Bos, J., Tseng, F., and Altuna, J. (2010). The Impact of a Reading Intervention for Low-Literate Adult ESL Learners (NCEE 2011-4003). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee/

q4 · i? · causal · r3

Randomized experiment with 1,344 low-literate adult ESL learners in 66 classes at 10 sites, tested at term start and after about 12 weeks; impact estimates compared randomly assigned Sam and Pat and control groups. "Effect sizes ranged from -0.06 to 0.01" and none were significant at the 0.05 level.

"However, there were no statistically significant impacts of Sam and Pat on the reading and English language outcomes measured for the overall sample (Figure ES.2). Effect sizes ranged from -0.06 to 0.01."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Low-literate adult ESL learners in the study made statistically significant gains in reading and English language skills over one term, with effect sizes of 0.23 to 0.40 — reports the opposite
  • Sam and Pat classrooms devoted far more observed time to reading development instruction and far less to English language acquisition instruction than control classrooms — related
  • Sam and Pat showed no statistically significant impacts for subgroups defined by native-language background or study cohort — related
  • About two-thirds of observed Sam and Pat classes met the study's instructional fidelity criteria for using the materials and delivering reading instruction — related
  • Exposure to English language instruction was positively and exposure to reading instruction negatively correlated with ROWPVT scores, with very small standardized coefficients — related
  • Students made statistically significant gains in reading and English language skills over the term (effect sizes 0.23 to 0.40), but Sam and Pat had no statistically significant overall impacts (effect sizes -0.06 to 0.01) — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Sam and Pat showed no impacts for five of six subgroups, with a suggestive but non-significant positive effect on word attack decoding for lower-literacy students (effect size = 0.16) — related
