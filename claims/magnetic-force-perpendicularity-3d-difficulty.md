## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2017

Chandralekha Singh. (2017). Improving Students' Understanding of Magnetism. Originally published 2007. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1701.01523](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1701.01523)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Pre/post results for the greatest-force question with three charged particles in a uniform field: "no significant difference in the average performance on the pre-test and post-test"; the common error came from using redundant angle information and difficulty visualizing in three dimensions.

> "Table 1 shows that there is no significant difference in the average performance on the pre-test and post-test on question (5)."

### Chandralekha Singh 2017 (2)

Chandralekha Singh. (2017). Improving Students' Understanding of Magnetism. Originally published 2007. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1701.01523](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1701.01523)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre/post results for the question on which orientations of velocity, magnetic field and magnetic force are physically reasonable: performance "changed from 24% on pre-test to 43% on post-test", with option (a) the most common incorrect response.

> "The student performance on this question changed from 24% on pre-test to 43% on post-test."
