## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Secondary-school students who analyzed the blood cell problem before studying a text on osmosis recalled significantly more correct propositions overall than controls who analyzed an unrelated problem. [→ Schmidt 1989](#schmidt-1989)

## Evidence

### Schmidt 1989

Schmidt, H. G., De Volder, M. L., De Grave, W. S., Moust, J. H. C., & Patel, V. L. (1989). Explanatory Models in the Processing of Science Text: The Role of Prior Knowledge Activation Through Small-Group Discussion. Journal of Educational Psychology, 81(4), 610-619. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.81.4.610

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2: 88 Dutch secondary students (46 ninth-grade novices, 42 tenth-grade experts) randomly assigned to blood cell or airplane problem conditions, then read a 2,200-word text on osmosis and diffusion and completed free recall. Experts recalled more than novices overall, F(1, 82) = 82.47, p < .001.

> "The three-way ANOVA yielded the following results: A sig- nificant difference was found for the total number of propo- sitions correctly recalled, F(1, 82) = 6.15, MSe = 116.69,19 < .05."
