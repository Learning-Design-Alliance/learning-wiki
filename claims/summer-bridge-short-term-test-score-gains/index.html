Evidence¶

Melissa Roderick 2003¶

Melissa Roderick, Mimi Engel, Jenny Nagaoka, with Brian A. Jacob, Sophie Degener, Alex Orfei, Susan Stone, Jen Bacon. (2003). Ending Social Promotion: Results From Summer Bridge. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/ending-social-promotion-results-summer-bridge

q3 · i? · associational · r2

The report's evaluation of Chicago's mandatory Summer Bridge program (1997-2000) analyzed achievement of all participating students. It reports that sixth and eighth graders showed substantial gains, and in all three grades the rate of increase was above their school-year rate.

"Sixth and eighth graders had substantial test-score gains. In all three grades, the rate at which Summer Bridge students increased their test scores was above their rate during the regular school year."

Related Claims¶

  • Summer Bridge produced only small sustained effects: slightly larger two-year gains than comparable non-attendees, without changing learning trajectories — related
  • Summer Bridge gains were relatively uniform across demographic and achievement groups, with highest-risk third graders benefiting most — related
  • Mandatory summer curricula are not teacher proof: individualizing instruction and quality of teacher-student interactions were associated with larger gains — related
  • Passing rates under the Chicago promotional policy increased in all three grades from 1997 to 1999, mostly during the school year rather than Summer Bridge — reports the opposite
  • Students who failed both reading and math made up the majority of retained students, and fewer than 20% of them passed both subjects in Summer Bridge — related
  • Students who met the promotional cutoffs, including after Summer Bridge, maintained positive test trajectories two years after promotion — related
  • Retained students showed smaller three-year achievement gains than previously socially promoted students, though Rasch adjustment erased the gap for once-retained third graders — related
  • Summer Bridge's second chance substantially raised promotion rates: by August 1997, 68% of included third graders, 79% of sixth graders, and 83% of eighth graders had met the cutoff — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Sixth graders promoted in 1997 without meeting the test cutoff fared poorly at the eighth-grade gate, while Summer Bridge passers did relatively well — related

  • Students were significantly more positive about classroom academic environments and teacher attention in Summer Bridge than in the school year — related
