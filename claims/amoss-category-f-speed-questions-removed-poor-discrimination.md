## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the first pilot study, question II.F on apparent speed had a negative point-biserial value, indicating students who performed well overall tended to miss it, and none of the written explanations was correct even when the correct answer was ticked; the category F questions were therefore removed. [→ Bekaert 2020](#bekaert-2020)

## Evidence

### Bekaert 2020

Bekaert, H., Van Winckel, H., Van Dooren, W., Steegen, A., & De Cock, M. (2020). Design and validation of an instrument to test students' understanding of the apparent motion of the Sun and stars. Physical Review Physics Education Research, 16(2), 020135. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.16.020135

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Item analysis of the first pilot study (n = 42 secondary school students) showed question II.F had a point-biserial of -0.06, below the 0.15 threshold the authors consider satisfactory. The authors state this "confirrns the conclusion from the analysis of the written explanations that the category F questions should be removed" because apparent speed on the trajectory is too difficult for the participating students.

> "the negative value indicates that students who perforrned weil on the test as a whole tended to miss this question and students who did not perforrn as weil on the test as a whole got it right"
