# The perceived value of GenAI and XR varied across design stages: GenAI suited early exploratory prototyping, XR once a 3D artifact existed for spatial evaluation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Students viewed GenAI as more suitable for early exploratory prototyping and XR as more valuable once a 3D artifact could be evaluated spatially; very early ideation posed a prompting paradox, and later stages demanded precision students preferred to get from direct modeling tools. ## Evidence

### Xiao 2026

Xiao, Y., Chen, M., Li, Y., Powers, N., Wiesenfeld, M., Smith, G., Farzin, S., Liu, S. (2026). Generative AI and Extended Reality in Collaborative Architectural Design Education: An Exploratory Studio Study. arXiv preprint arXiv:2609.13494. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13494

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Interview-based thematic finding on design-stage needs. During very early ideation, one team "weren't quite at the stage of having ideas for what we wanted the specific building to look like yet," so they could not prompt meaningfully; later, GenAI struggled to follow encoded constraints, and students returned to SketchUp when precision mattered.

> "Students' reactions indicated that GenAI was generally viewed as more suitable for early exploratory prototyping, while XR was considered more valuable once there was a 3D artifact that could be evaluated spatially."
