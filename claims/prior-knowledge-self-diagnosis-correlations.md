## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Across all groups, physics quiz grade correlated marginally negatively with physics self-diagnosis grade (r = -0.172, p = 0.12). [→ E. Cohen 2008](#e-cohen-2008)
`q2 i2` Across all groups, presentation quiz grade correlated positively with presentation self-diagnosis grade (r = 0.277, p = 0.01). [→ E. Cohen 2008 (2)](#e-cohen-2008-2)

## Evidence

### E. Cohen 2008

E. Cohen, A. Mason, C. Singh and E. Yerushalmi. (2008). Identifying Differences in Diagnostic Skills between Physics Students: Students' Self-Diagnostic Performance Given Alternative Scaffolding. Proceedings of the Phys. Ed. Res. Conference. [doi:10.1063/1.3021284](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3021284)

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r1`

Correlational analysis (Table 8) across all groups: PHY quiz vs. SD r = -0.172 (p = 0.12), a "marginal negative correlation". The authors suggest students who did well had fewer mistakes and thus less chance to trace them.

> "Focusing on the PHY columns, one notes a marginal negative correlation between the grade of the physics part of the quiz, and the grade of the diagnosis of this part; namely, students who make more mistakes are better at diagnosing them."

### E. Cohen 2008 (2)

E. Cohen, A. Mason, C. Singh and E. Yerushalmi. (2008). Identifying Differences in Diagnostic Skills between Physics Students: Students' Self-Diagnostic Performance Given Alternative Scaffolding. Proceedings of the Phys. Ed. Res. Conference. [doi:10.1063/1.3021284](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3021284)

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Correlational analysis (Table 8) across all groups: PRE quiz vs. SD r = 0.277 (p = 0.01), a "positive correlation". The authors note the initial gap between students who did well and those who did not remained in the presentation part.

> "Focusing on the PRE columns, one notes a positive correlation between the grade of the presentation part of the quiz, and the grade of the diagnosis of this part; Students whose presentation was deficient were worse also at diagnosing it."
