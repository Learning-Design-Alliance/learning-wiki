Emotional intelligence correlates r = .16 with mathematics achievement (MacCann et al. 2020, k = 258, n = 9,750), the weakest of the aptitude variables. These are correlations compiled across meta-analyses: they describe how strongly the characteristic goes with mathematics achievement, not what causes it.

Evidence¶

Breit et al. 2025¶

Breit, M., Schneider, M., & Preckel, F. (2025). Mathematics achievement and learner characteristics: A systematic review of meta-analyses. Learning and Individual Differences, 118, 102621. doi:10.1016/j.lindif.2024.102621

q3 · systematic review of meta-analyses · i1 · small effect, r = .16 (k = 258) · n=30 meta-analyses, 66 correlations from 13,853 effect sizes (~4.66 million participants) · review · r3

The review compiles zero-order correlations from published meta-analyses (each with at least ten effect sizes, the largest one kept where several covered a variable), found by a systematic PsycINFO search in July 2024. It pools nothing further and controls for nothing: many of these characteristics correlate with one another, so a correlation here says how strongly the characteristic goes with mathematics achievement, not that it causes it. Emotional intelligence r = .16 (MacCann et al. 2020, 258 effect sizes, corrected for unreliability). MacCann et al. found the relation stronger for the humanities than for STEM (r = .38 against .21).

Discussion¶

The authors suggest emotional knowledge is more relevant to humanities content, which deals with people and their motives, than to mathematics.
