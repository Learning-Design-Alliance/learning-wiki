# Spelling and attentiveness are uniquely related to writing conventions of first-grade written composition, but instructional quality is not

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Children's spelling was uniquely related to the writing conventions dimension of written composition (p < .001) after accounting for all other variables in the model. [→ Kim 2013](#kim-2013)
`q3 i?` Children's attentiveness was uniquely related to the writing conventions dimension of written composition (p < .001). [→ Kim 2013](#kim-2013)
`q3 i?` None of the instructional quality variables were uniquely related to the writing conventions outcome (ps ≥ .09) after accounting for child-level predictors. [→ Kim 2013](#kim-2013)

## Evidence

### Kim 2013

Kim, Y.-S., Al Otaiba, S., Sidler Folsom, J., & Gruelich, L. (2013). Language, literacy, attentional behaviors, and instructional quality predictors of written composition for first graders. Early Childhood Research Quarterly, 28(3), 461–469. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecresq.2013.01.001

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multilevel model analysis of 527 first-grade children in 34 classrooms predicting the writing conventions outcome (sum of spelling, mechanics, and handwriting). Spelling and attentiveness were uniquely related; instructional quality variables were not.

> "For the writing conventions outcome, children's spelling (p < .001) and attentiveness (p < .001) were uniquely related after accounting for all the other variables in the model."

### Kim 2013 (2)

Kim, Y.-S., Al Otaiba, S., Sidler Folsom, J., & Gruelich, L. (2013). Language, literacy, attentional behaviors, and instructional quality predictors of written composition for first graders. Early Childhood Research Quarterly, 28(3), 461–469. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecresq.2013.01.001

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Null finding from the same multilevel model: instructional quality in responsiveness, individualization, organization, spelling, writing, and reading comprehension showed no unique relation to writing conventions.

> "For the writing conventions outcome, none of the instructional quality variables were uniquely related (ps ≥ .09) after accounting for child-level predictors."
