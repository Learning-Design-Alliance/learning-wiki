Evidence¶

Shute 2016¶

Shute, V. J., Wang, L., Greiff, S., Zhao, W., & Moore, G. (2016). Measuring problem solving skills via stealth assessment in an engaging video game. Computers in Human Behavior, 63, 106-117. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2016.05.047

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Multiple regression analysis from the validation study with Raven's entered first and MicroDYN second. The article reports R2 = 0.22, Raven's b = 0.25 (p = 0.11), MicroDYN b = 0.28 (p = 0.08), and that MicroDYN "explained an additional 5.7%" unique variance beyond Raven's.

"Results showed that, with both variables in the equation, R2 ¼ 0.22. Most of the problem solving variance was explained by MicroDYN (i.e., Raven 's b ¼ 0.25; p ¼ 0.1 1; MicroDYN b ¼ 0.28, p ¼ 0.08)."

Related Claims¶

  • MicroDYN is a stronger predictor of the in-game problem solving estimates than Raven's, with the rule-application facet more relevant than rule acquisition — related
  • Stealth assessment estimates of problem solving in Use Your Brainz correlate significantly with external measures (Raven's and MicroDYN) in middle-school students — related
  • Adding MicroDYN rule application significantly improves prediction of overall problem solving, planning, tool usage, and evaluating progress, but not analyzing givens — related
  • Students with higher general cognitive ability are more efficient in equation rearrangement — related
  • Stealth assessment facets show good internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha = 0.76) while MicroDYN reliabilities were low in this middle-school sample — related
