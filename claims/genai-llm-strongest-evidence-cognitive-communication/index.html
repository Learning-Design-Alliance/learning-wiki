q2 · i? · review · r3

Narrative subgroup synthesis of the four prespecified AI modalities (GenAI/LLM n=7, virtual patients/mannequins n=5, AI-VR/MR n=5, chatbots n=2) concluded "GenAI/LLM modalities provide the strongest evidence for cognitive and communication outcomes" and underscored aligning modality with learning objectives.

"GenAI/LLM modalities provide the strongest evidence for cognitive and communication outcomes; AI-driven mannequins show mixed effects with concerns about psychomotor skills; AI-VR/MR offers immersive potential limited by technical challenges; and chatbots serve primarily as adjunctive supports with weak independent effects"

Related Claims¶

  • AI-powered simulations show short-term gains in communication confidence and structured knowledge in RCTs, with limited or inferior effects for tactile psychomotor skills — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Simulation Based Education Improves Outcomes — a broader claim this one bears on
  • genAI-supported work can improve while the constructive processing underlying durable expertise erodes (the performance–metacognition dilemma) — related
  • Unguarded answer-giving AI harmed unaided exam performance while a guarded version of the same model erased the harm (Bastani et al., 2025, as reported) — related
  • High-frequency LLM users show higher minimum course outcomes and reduced score variability, though interaction level is not a dominant outcome predictor — related
  • Simulation effects vary by complex skill: highest for technical performance, lowest for communication skills — related
  • Purpose-built chatbot effects on educational performance are mixed, with some studies showing no benefits or worse outcomes than comparison conditions — related
  • AIED developments in personalized motor skills learning have lagged behind cognitive skills support despite procedural learning being considered since the 1980s — related
  • Affective outcomes (satisfaction, confidence, engagement) are consistently positive across ADS modalities but rest predominantly on subjective, short-term measures — related
  • Cognitive outcomes of AI-driven simulation are the most variable across modalities and designs, with generative AI gains often perceived rather than objectively validated — related
  • Immersive VR-haptic and ITS modalities are more often associated with objective psychomotor and cognitive gains, while generative AI platforms are more often associated with affective outcomes — reports the opposite
  • Spaced simulation reinforcement showed selective benefits: some psychomotor measures improved while written knowledge and time to shock did not — related
