# mPFC activity during schema-related encoding predicts normalized second-year course grades, with no such relation for MTL activity

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` mPFC activity for the schema subsequent memory contrast correlated positively with normalized second-year grades (effect size r = .43, p < .05), while MTL activity showed no such relation. [→ van Kesteren 2014](#van-kesteren-2014)

## Evidence

### van Kesteren 2014

van Kesteren, M. T. R., Rijpkema, M., Ruiter, D. J., Morris, R. G. M., & Fernández, G. (2014). Building on Prior Knowledge: Schema-dependent Encoding Processes Relate to Academic Performance. Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience, 26(10), 2250–2261. https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn_a_00630

`q2 · i3`

Correlational analysis of 25 of the 31 participants who supplied second-year grades, normalized by first-year grades. The printed effect size is r = .43 (r(24) = .43, p < .05) between mPFC schema subsequent memory activity and normalized grades; no relation was observed for MTL activity (r(24) = .09, ns).

> "Our analysis of this possibility revealed a positive relation between mPFC activity during learning of schema-related information and normalized grades (grades year 2/grades year 1) obtained in the subsequent course year ( r(24) = .43, p < .05; Figure 4)"
