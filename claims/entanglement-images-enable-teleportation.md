# Shared entanglement provides parties with multiple 'images' of an unknown state, and independently manipulating these images is what enables teleportation

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In the diagrammatic treatment, the entangled state of coins a and b produces two images of the unknown state S, and Alice's measurement acts differently on these images so that all of S is preserved and none repeated, allowing teleportation with two classical bits of communication. ## Evidence

### Scott M. Cohen 2008

Scott M. Cohen. (2008). Visualizing Teleportation. https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.0051 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0704.0051](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0704.0051)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical/derivational result (Section III B): the diagrams show that Alice's joint measurement "picks out diﬀerent parts of S from the diﬀerent images in a way such that all of S is preserved and none of it is repeated", after which Bob recovers the state using Alice's two-bit message.

> "The crucial observation is the presence of two images of the state S, resulting from the entanglement between coins a, b. Alice does a measurement that, while not acting independently on these two images, does act diﬀerently on them"
