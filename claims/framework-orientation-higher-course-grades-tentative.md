## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Average course grades show a clear stratification for orientation groups in favor of framework orientation (combined-semesters F vs. V p < 0.01), but the orientation categories are statistically differentiable for S14 students and not for S15 students. ## Evidence

### Andrew J. Mason 2014

Andrew J. Mason. (2014). Potential relationship of chosen major to problem solving attitudes and course performance. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.049](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.049)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparison of average course grades by orientation group (Table IV; combined-semesters Framework 3.35, Performance 2.84, Vague 2.48), with an incremental Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons. Combined F vs. V p < 0.01; S14 F vs. V p < 0.01, S15 p = 0.36.

> "the orientation categories are statistically differentiable for the S14 students, but not for the S15 students; particularly of note is the significantly better performance of vague students in the S15 semester than in the S14 semester (p < .05)"
