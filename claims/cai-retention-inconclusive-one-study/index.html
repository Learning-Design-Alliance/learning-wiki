Evidence¶

Viljoen Charle André 2019¶

Viljoen Charle André, Scott Millar Rob, Engel Mark E, Shelton Mary, Burch Vanessa. (2019). Is computer-assisted instruction more effective than other educational methods in achieving ECG competence amongst medical students and residents? A systematic review and meta-analysis. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2018-028800

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Only one included study (Fent et al) assessed retention three months after instruction without further teaching. Pooled "SMD=−0.24 (95% CI −1.05 to 0.58)" crossed zero, and the retention test had a 14% response rate, so equivalence was not established.

"While this study showed that there was no significant difference between the CAI and face-to-face teaching (SMD=−0.24 (95% CI −1.05 to 0.58)), the response rate was only 14% for the retention of knowledge test which was conducted three months after the educational intervention."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • CAI within a blended learning strategy outperforms face-to-face teaching alone for ECG competence acquisition
  • Specific CAI learning materials and activities — case scenarios, explanatory images, self-assessment with automated feedback, and chat rooms — are associated with better ECG competence than face-to-face teaching
  • CAI is not better than face-to-face teaching for acquiring ECG competence overall, with high heterogeneity among studies
  • Subanalyses by training level tend to favour CAI for both medical students and residents, though confidence intervals are imprecise
  • Distributed CAI instruction beats face-to-face teaching, but only when CAI is part of blended learning
  • Unrestricted 24/7 access to CAI materials is associated with better ECG outcomes, while restricted access is not
