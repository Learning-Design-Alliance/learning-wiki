## Evidence

### Benjamin A. Clegg 2024

Benjamin A. Clegg, Alex Karduna, Ethan Holen, Jason Garcia, Matthew G. Rhodes, & Francisco R. Ortega. (2024). Multimedia and Immersive Training Materials Influence Impressions of Learning But Not Learning Outcomes. 2022 Interservice/Industry Training, Simulation, and Education Conference (I/ITSEC), Paper No. 22459. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2407.05504](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2407.05504)

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Between-subjects Prolific experiment with 130 US participants (66 static, 64 video) studying eight assembly shapes for about 30 seconds each. The video condition produced higher judgments of learning with a printed effect size of "d = 0.54" (t(128 ) = 3.11, p < .005).

> "participants provided significantly greater ratings of learning from the video assembly instructions (Mean rating: 54.2, SD: 16.7) than the static diagrams (M: 44.0, SD:20.6), t(128 ) = 3.11, p < .005, d = 0.54"

### Benjamin A. Clegg 2024 (2)

Benjamin A. Clegg, Alex Karduna, Ethan Holen, Jason Garcia, Matthew G. Rhodes, & Francisco R. Ortega. (2024). Multimedia and Immersive Training Materials Influence Impressions of Learning But Not Learning Outcomes. 2022 Interservice/Industry Training, Simulation, and Education Conference (I/ITSEC), Paper No. 22459. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2407.05504](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2407.05504)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same Experiment 1 sample, three-alternative forced-choice recognition tests on assembly steps. Video and static conditions did not differ: "t(128) = 0.42, p > .05, d = 0.07"; equivalence was not tested.

> "scores on the memory test (see Figure 5) showed no difference between the video assembly instructions (mean percent correct: 71.8%, SD: 12.1) and the static diagrams (M: 72.8%, SD:15.3), t(128) = 0.42, p > .05, d = 0.07"
