# Hypothesized ATI effects of individualized explanations based on typical mistakes were not supported; exploratory benefits emerged for high-resource vocational-school students

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Neither the hypothesized benefit for low-prior-knowledge students nor the hypothesized moderation by sustained attention was found for individualized explanations. [→ Sarah I. Hofer 2025](#sarah-i-hofer-2025)
`q3 i?` Exploratively, individualized explanations were especially helpful for vocational school students with high interest, high self-concept, and high visual-spatial ability. [→ Sarah I. Hofer 2025 (2)](#sarah-i-hofer-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Sarah I. Hofer 2025

Sarah I. Hofer, Frank Reinhold. (2025). Scaffolding of learning activities: Aptitude-treatment-interaction effects in math?. Learning and Instruction 99 (2025) 102177. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102177

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

GLMM tests of the fifth and sixth hypotheses in the randomized experiment (N = 332) found no support for the expected interactions of individualized explanations with prior knowledge or sustained attention; the article reports no effect sizes for these null findings.

> "Figs. 6 and 7 showed that neither of these was true for our study."

### Sarah I. Hofer 2025 (2)

Sarah I. Hofer, Frank Reinhold. (2025). Scaffolding of learning activities: Aptitude-treatment-interaction effects in math?. Learning and Instruction 99 (2025) 102177. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102177

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory GLMM interaction tests in the VS subgroup showed significant interactions of individualized explanations with interest, self-concept, and visual-spatial ability; the article flags these as tentative findings requiring confirmation in larger samples.

> "However, we exploratively found individualized explanations to be especially helpful for VS students with high interest, X2(8,68) = 16.29, p < .05, high self-concept, X2(8,68) = 27.23, p < .001, and high visual- spatial ability, X2(8,68) = 23.21, p < .01 (Fig. 7)."
