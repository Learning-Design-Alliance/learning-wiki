# Security and technical risk perception exhibit threshold effects on perceived benefits, with positive associations becoming significant only above critical risk levels

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Security risk perception significantly promotes academic assistance only when risk exceeds the second threshold (coefficient 0.304, significant at the 1% level). [→ Du W 2026](#du-w-2026)
`q2 i?` Security risk perception's positive effect on skill development emerges only above the threshold (coefficient 0.166, p < 0.01). [→ Du W 2026 (2)](#du-w-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Du W 2026

Du W, Ning S, Shi Y and Chen Y. (2026). The impact of risk perception on perceived benefits of generative artificial intelligence users—an empirical study based on university students. Front. Psychol. 17:1866428. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1866428

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Threshold regression using Hansen's (2000) bootstrap method (5,000 samples) on the 814-student survey. Below the threshold, coefficients were positive but not statistically significant; only in the highest risk range was the effect significant.

> "When security risk exceed the second threshold and reach the highest range, the coeﬃcient becomes 0.304 and is signiﬁcant at the 1% level."

### Du W 2026 (2)

Du W, Ning S, Shi Y and Chen Y. (2026). The impact of risk perception on perceived benefits of generative artificial intelligence users—an empirical study based on university students. Front. Psychol. 17:1866428. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1866428

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Threshold regression on the same sample for skill development, with a single threshold value of 3 for security risk. The article reports the positive effect "emerges only when risk levels are relatively high".

> "When it exceeds the threshold, the regression coeﬃcient becomes 0.166 and is statistically signiﬁcant at the 1% level (p < 0.01)."
