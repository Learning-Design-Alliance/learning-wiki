# Community midwives' OSCE B (bag-mask ventilation and decision-making) pass rates rose from pre-test to post-test after each of three simulation-based high-frequency HBB training sessions, in one uncontrolled pre-post study

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2021 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In one uncontrolled pre-post study, OSCE B pass proportions rose significantly from pre-test to post-test after each of three trainings (p < 0.05 by McNemar's test), from 6 to 26 passes after training one, 20 to 29 after training two, and 23 to 32 after training three. [→ Mubeen Kiran 2021](#mubeen-kiran-2021)

## Evidence

### Mubeen Kiran 2021

Mubeen Kiran, Baig Marina, Abbas Sadia, Adnan Farzana, Lakhani Arusa, Bhamani Shireen Shehzad, Rehman Bushra, Shahid Shahnaz, Jan Rafat. (2021). Helping babies breathe: assessing the effectiveness of simulation-based high-frequency recurring training in a community-based setting of Pakistan. BMC Pediatrics. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12887-021-03014-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre-post analysis of 34 community midwives completing all three trainings, using exact McNemar's tests on Pass/Fail outcomes from the AAP HBB OSCE B checklist (17 of 23 items to pass). The study reports "a significant increase was found after the third training"; no effect size was printed.

> "The results were statistically different after each training for OSCE B (p-value < 0.05); specifically, after training one, the number of CMWs who passed increased from 6 to 26; after training two, the number of CMWs who passed increased from 20 to 29; and a significant increase was found after the third training pre- (n = 23) to post-test (n = 32)."
