# Fiber crafting develops proportional reasoning through nested, personally-defined units

Three youth engaging in knitting, crochet, and fabric pleating each developed and used multiplicative proportional reasoning specific to their craft's material structure — gauge in knitting, sector growth in crochet, and hidden-layer ratios in pleating — with material properties (tension, needle size, fabric spring-back) actively shaping what unit each child defined and discovered.

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` A 10-year-old knitter, after unraveling and restarting her project 12 times, discovered her stitch height did not equal her stitch width (i.e., her personal gauge was not 1:1), revised her row-count plan from 10 to 8 rows accordingly, and reframed the resulting extra dimension as an intentional design feature (a bag flap) rather than a mistake to fix. [→ Peppler et al. 2025](#peppler-et-al-2025)

`q2 i2` A 9-year-old crocheter distributing stitches across a magic-circle foundation to keep the shape flat (target ratio 1:6, 2:12, 3:18 stitches per row) produced an uneven distribution in one row (five double-stitches instead of six), then corrected the resulting shape by deliberately loosening the tension on one stitch — compensating for a proportional error using material tension rather than recounting stitches. [→ Peppler et al. 2025](#peppler-et-al-2025)

`q2 i2` An 11-year-old pleater produced six fabric segments corresponding to two pleats, articulating the underlying 3:1 unfolded-to-folded ratio (equivalently 6:2 segments) via the formula L=l+2np, and only succeeded in fixing this ratio into a persistent, buildable pattern unit after inventing ironing as a fixation strategy when pinning alone failed to hold the folds. [→ Peppler et al. 2025](#peppler-et-al-2025)

## Evidence

### Peppler et al. 2025

Peppler, K., Keune, A., Bender, S., & Yankova, N. (2025). Materialized Action: Reformulating the "Doing of" Math Through Fiber Crafting. *Cognition and Instruction, 43*(3), 175-200. [https://doi.org/10.1080/07370008.2025.2485070](https://doi.org/10.1080/07370008.2025.2485070)

`q2 · qualitative case study with rich multimodal data (40 hours of video across 3-4 cameras, 231 photographs, artifact reverse-engineering) and a deliberate design phase involving 5 adult crafters to align craft structure with the target mathematical concept; no control group, no pre/post outcome measure, small purposive sample` `i2 · a clear, well-triangulated qualitative pattern of proportional reasoning emerging through three distinct material pathways in three different learners, though with no quantified effect size and no test of how common this outcome is across the full 15-17 camp participants` `n=3 focal youth (ages 9-11) selected from a 17-participant, 3-day public-library summer camp`

Seventeen youth (ages 9-12) attended a 3-day, 4-hours-per-day summer camp in which each day was devoted to one fiber craft — knitting, crochet, and pleating — each designed by the research team (with input from adult crafters and mathematicians) to instantiate proportional reasoning through a different material structure. Three focal youth were analyzed in depth via video, photographs, and artifact analysis, using the [Materialized Action](../theories/materialized-action.md) framework's three nested levels (stitch/fold unit, pattern unit, project unit) to trace how each child's proportional reasoning emerged from their specific interactions with their craft's materials. This directly grounds [Fiber Crafting for Proportional Reasoning](../patterns/fiber-crafting-for-proportional-reasoning.md).

## Discussion
This is a small, purposive case study (3 focal youth from a 17-participant camp) with no pre/post assessment and no control condition; the authors explicitly frame it as demonstrating feasibility and mechanism ("possibilities for engaging deeply... in preparation for future work") rather than a tested intervention. All three examples come from a single camp implementation designed collaboratively with adult crafters and mathematicians specifically to elicit these mathematical relationships, so the craft activities themselves were engineered for this outcome — not naturally-occurring fiber-craft instruction. Generalization to typical craft instruction, larger cohorts, or different demographic contexts (the camp population and setting are not detailed in demographic terms) is untested.
