# In traditional guided-verification physics labs, no gender difference appears in the distribution of behaviour profiles

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` In traditional lab sections, there is no statistically significant difference in the fraction of men's and women's profiles in any of the five clusters (p = 0.65); equivalence was not tested. [→ Katherine N. Quinn 2019](#katherine-n-quinn-2019)

## Evidence

### Katherine N. Quinn 2019

Katherine N. Quinn, Kathryn L. McGill, Michelle M. Kelley, Emily M. Smith, and N. G. Holmes (2019). Who does what now? How physics lab instruction impacts student behaviors. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1119/perc.2018.pr.quinn](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2018.pr.quinn)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same observational cluster analysis of student behaviour profiles over 30 lab periods; the traditional-lab gender comparison of cluster membership shows no statistically significant difference with printed p = 0.65 and no effect size reported.

> "in the traditional labs, there is no statistically signiﬁcant difference in the fraction of men’s and women’s proﬁles in any of the clusters (p = 0.65)."

### Katherine N. Quinn 2020

Katherine N. Quinn, Michelle M. Kelley, Kathryn L. McGill, Emily M. Smith, Zachary Whipps, and N.G. Holmes. (2020). Group roles in unstructured labs show inequitable gender divide. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010129](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010129)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same observational cluster analysis of student behavior profiles, comparing cluster composition by gender in the traditional highly structured labs. The chi-squared test found no statistically significant gender difference; equivalence was not tested.

> "but none in the traditional labs ( χ2(3) = 3 .27, p = 0.65, VCramer = 0.08)"
