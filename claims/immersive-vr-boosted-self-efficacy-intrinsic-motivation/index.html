Evidence¶

Törmänen 2025¶

Törmänen, T., Ketonen, E., Lehtoaho, E., Turunen, M., Zabolotna, K., Shubina, T., & Järvenoja, H. (2025). Situational Motivation in Academic Learning: A Systematic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10036-0

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing Makransky et al. (2019), a comparison of three learning environments for laboratory safety training — immersive VR, desktop VR, and a conventional safety manual — in which "the immersive VR simulation increased self‐efficacy and intrinsic motivation more than the conventional safety manual."

"They found that the immersive VR simulation increased self‐efficacy and intrinsic motivation more than the conventional safety manual."

  • VR-based training received significantly higher ratings for medical safety awareness and radiation protection than conventional training (r=0.856) — related

  • In a piloted virtual reality WIL, most students reported improved crisis-problem-solving understanding and high enjoyment — related
  • VR improved movement-based transfer test performance versus reading in security training, while learning in VR without the possibility to act was detrimental — related
  • Immersion in virtual reality adds extraneous load yet can boost transfer, enjoyment, and motivation while leaving retention unaffected — related
  • Immersion principle: people do not necessarily learn better in 3D immersive virtual reality than with a corresponding 2D desktop presentation (d = -0.10 across 9 tests) — related
  • Mental simulation training increased children's reading motivation in both grades, while control-group motivation showed no significant change — related
