## Evidence

### Nabit Bajwa 2026

Nabit Bajwa, Seth B. Hunter, and Sanmay Das. (2026). Actionable Insights from Observational Data: The Case of Advanced Classes in K-12 Education. [doi:10.1145/3846167.3848599](https://doi.org/10.1145/3846167.3848599)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Causal forest subgroup CATE estimates (Fig. 4), with demographic and program characteristics entering as controls: "estimated gains are systematically smaller for students from historically underrepresented groups", including FRL-eligible, LEP, and SWD students, relative to peers.

> "estimated gains are systematically smaller for students from historically underrepresented groups, including lower socio-economic (SES) (characterized by free or reduced-price lunch (FRL) eligibility), English learners (LEP), and students with disabilities (SWD), relative to their peers."

### Nabit Bajwa 2026 (2)

Nabit Bajwa, Seth B. Hunter, and Sanmay Das. (2026). Actionable Insights from Observational Data: The Case of Advanced Classes in K-12 Education. [doi:10.1145/3846167.3848599](https://doi.org/10.1145/3846167.3848599)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same causal forest subgroup analysis (Fig. 4): "estimated effects are larger for students identified as gifted and talented"; gender differences are small, with similar estimated effects for male and female students.

> "At the same time, estimated effects are larger for students identified as gifted and talented."
