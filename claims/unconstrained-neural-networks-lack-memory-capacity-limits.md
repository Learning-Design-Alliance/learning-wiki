# Standard unconstrained neural networks do not naturally separate working memory from long-term memory or impose human-like capacity limits

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Standard neural networks omit the working-memory/long-term-memory distinction and sharp capacity limits central to educational cognition theories, though architectural constraints can add them. [→ Peng Wang 2026](#peng-wang-2026)

## Evidence

### Peng Wang 2026

Peng Wang, Olga Viberg, Effie Lai-Chong Law, Samuel Greiff. (2026). When Can AI Models Explain Learning? Validity Criteria for AI as Cognitive Models in Education. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10202-y

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Narrative review assertion about connectionist models used as virtual learners in education. The review states that without constraints, networks "can integrate and store far more information than a human learner could plausibly maintain at once", weakening their plausibility as process models, but notes this limitation is not inherent to the class.

> "Most importantly, standard networks do not naturally separate short-term working memory from long-term memory, nor do they impose the sharp capacity limits that constrain human information processing"
