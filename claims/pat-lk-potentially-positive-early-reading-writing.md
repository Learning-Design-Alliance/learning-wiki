# Phonological awareness training plus letter knowledge training has potentially positive effects on preschoolers' early reading/writing

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q2`–`q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In Gettinger (1986), the intervention group significantly outperformed the comparison group on four of five early reading/writing measures, with a study average effect size of 0.89 and improvement index +31. [→ What Works Clearinghouse 2006](#what-works-clearinghouse-2006)
`q2 i2` In Roberts and Neal (2004), the writing outcome was not statistically significant but was large enough to be rated substantively important and positive (effect size 0.52). [→ What Works Clearinghouse 2006 (2)](#what-works-clearinghouse-2006-2)

## Evidence

### What Works Clearinghouse 2006

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2006). WWC Intervention Report: Phonological Awareness Training plus Letter Knowledge Training. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/InterventionReport/376

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

RCT with 72 children; early reading/writing was measured after the skills training during subsequent reading instruction, with effects attributed to the initial PAT+LK skills training. Appendix A3.4 reports effect sizes from 0.53 to 1.25 across training words, transfer words, short vowel, and trials-to-criterion; discrimination (0.66) was not significant.

> "Gettinger (1986) reported statistically significant differences favoring the intervention group for four of five measures, and the WWC confirmed the statistical significance of these effects."

### What Works Clearinghouse 2006 (2)

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2006). WWC Intervention Report: Phonological Awareness Training plus Letter Knowledge Training. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/InterventionReport/376

`q2 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r1`

RCT with reservations (high attrition) of 33 Hmong- or Spanish-speaking three- and four-year-olds in a half-day state-funded preschool; the writing measure effect size was 0.52 with an improvement index of +20, not statistically significant.

> "Roberts and Neal (2004) found no statistically significant differences between the intervention group and the comparison group for a measure of writing; however, the finding was large enough to categorize the effect as substantively important and positive, according to WWC criteria."
