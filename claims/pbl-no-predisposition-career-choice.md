## Evidence

### Tsigarides Jordan 2017

Tsigarides Jordan, Wingfield Laura R., Kulendran Myutan. (2017). Does a PBL-based medical curriculum predispose training in specific career paths? A systematic review of the literature. BMC Research Notes. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5219658/

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Systematic review of 11 studies comparing PBL vs non-PBL medical curricula on career choice. The review reports that "PBL did not predispose a student to a career in a specific speciality" in 7 of 11 studies (64%).

> "The majority of studies showed that PBL did not predispose a student to a career in a specific speciality (n = 7 out of 11 studies, 64%)."

### Tsigarides Jordan 2017 (2)

Tsigarides Jordan, Wingfield Laura R., Kulendran Myutan. (2017). Does a PBL-based medical curriculum predispose training in specific career paths? A systematic review of the literature. BMC Research Notes. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5219658/

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Retrospective cohort study using Canadian Resident Matching Service data over 1993–2004 across 13 English-language Canadian medical schools (n = 14,370). The review reports "no significant differences" for pathology residency choice between PBL and non-PBL school graduates.

> "Most notably, Ford et al. [4] conducted a large study in Canada with 14,370 graduates, and this research group concluded that there were no significant differences specifically for choosing a pathology residency programme between graduates from medical schools with a predominantly PBL curriculum in comparison to those with a non-PBL curriculum."
