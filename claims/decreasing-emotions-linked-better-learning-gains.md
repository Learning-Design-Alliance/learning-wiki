## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Groups formed by individual emotion slopes did not differ at pre-test, but the decreasing-anxiety, decreasing-surprise and decreasing-enjoyment groups showed more positive change in concept-test scores; no associations were found for other emotions. [→ Ketonen 2026](#ketonen-2026)

## Evidence

### Ketonen 2026

Ketonen, E. E., Kruskopf, M., Lonka, K., & Mattlin, M. (2026). Dynamics of university students' epistemic emotions during game-based learning in an international relations course. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09771-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Mann-Whitney U comparisons of concept-test change scores between slope groups (n = 7–12 per group). Anxiety change: U = −2.07, p = 0.041; enjoyment: U = −2.46, p = 0.013; surprise: U = −2.01, p = 0.043. No effect sizes are printed.

> "those students showing a decreasing rather than stable trend in either anxiety or surprise demonstrated more positive change in their test scores. Interestingly, those students exhibiting a decreasing trend in enjoyment actually demonstrated more positive change in their test scores than those showing a more stable trend"
