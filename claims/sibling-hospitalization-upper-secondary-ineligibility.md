## Evidence

### Bortes C 2020

Bortes C, Strandh M, Nilsson K. (2020). Sibling Ill Health and Children's Educational Outcomes. Journal of School Health. https://doi.org/10.1111/josh.12887

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r3`

Logistic regression on the same Swedish register cohort shows adjusted odds of ineligibility of "OR = 1.42, 95% CI = 1.31‐1.52" for children with a repeatedly hospitalized sibling versus those without.

> "Both model 2 and model 3 show that despite being somewhat reduced after adjustments, sibling ill health still significantly increased the odds of ineligibility for upper secondary education (OR = 1.42, 95% CI = 1.32‐1.53, p < .001), (OR = 1.42, 95% CI = 1.31‐1.52, p < .001)."

### Bortes C 2020 (2)

Bortes C, Strandh M, Nilsson K. (2020). Sibling Ill Health and Children's Educational Outcomes. Journal of School Health. https://doi.org/10.1111/josh.12887

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r3`

Unadjusted logistic regression (model 1) on the same cohort shows "OR = 1.68, 95% confidence interval [CI] = 1.57‐1.80" for ineligibility among children with ill siblings.

> "The first unadjusted model shows that sibling ill health significantly increased the odds ratio (OR) of ineligibility for upper secondary education (OR = 1.68, 95% confidence interval [CI] = 1.57‐1.80, p < .001)."
