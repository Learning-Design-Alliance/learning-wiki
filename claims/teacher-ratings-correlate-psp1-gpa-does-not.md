# Teacher ratings of language and reading skills correlate significantly with PSP-1 total scores, whereas grade point average does not

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` Student grade point averages were not correlated with PSP-1 scores. [→ Iva I. Hrastinski 2023 (2)](#iva-i-hrastinski-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Iva I. Hrastinski 2023

Iva I. Hrastinski, Ana M. Bonetti, Mladen A. Hedjever. (2023). Relationship between auditory processing skills and academic achievement of elementary school children. Specijalna edukacija i rehabilitacija, 22(2), 95-116. https://doi.org/10.5937/specedreh22-37415

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis (Table 2) of teacher ratings against PSP-1 results for 412 children. The article reports the strongest correlations in "teachers' ratings of students' language skills (0.305, p ≤ 0.01) and reading ability (0.239, p ≤ 0.01)" with the PSP-1 total score.

> "The strongest correlation was evident in teachers' ratings of students' language skills (0.305, p ≤ 0.01) and reading ability (0.239, p ≤ 0.01) that were significantly correlated with the PSP-1 total score."

### Iva I. Hrastinski 2023 (2)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis of grade point average against PSP-1 subtest and total scores. The article reports that "student grade point averages were not correlated with PSP-1 scores," in contrast to the significant teacher-rating correlations.

> "However, student grade point averages were not correlated with PSP-1 scores."
