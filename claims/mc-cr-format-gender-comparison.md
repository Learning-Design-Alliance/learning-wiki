## Evidence

### Federer Meghan Rector 2016

Federer Meghan Rector, Nehm Ross H., Pearl Dennis K. (2016). Examining Gender Differences in Written Assessment Tasks in Biology: A Case Study of Evolutionary Explanations. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-01-0018

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

A subset of participants completed both the ACORNS CR items and the MC CINS; independent t tests (Table 6) compared gender subgroups. The article reports "no significant gender effects" for these scores; equivalence was not tested.

> "Comparisons of mean performance scores on the CINS MC and ACORNS CR items revealed no significant gender effects for the KC (CR) and CINS (MC) scores (Table 6)."

### Federer Meghan Rector 2016 (2)

Federer Meghan Rector, Nehm Ross H., Pearl Dennis K. (2016). Examining Gender Differences in Written Assessment Tasks in Biology: A Case Study of Evolutionary Explanations. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-01-0018

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

All possible male-female pairings were compared (e.g., 11,067 pairs in the majors-level sample). The pairwise advantage for females on CR items appeared related to biology experience/expertise, and the article suggests it may be an artifact of response-length differences on particular items.

> "Comparison of CINS scores with ACORNS KCD scores revealed that 1420 of these pairs were of type 1 (FCINS < MCINS but FACORNS > MACORNS), 1062 of type 2 (FCINS < MCINS but FACORNS = MACORNS), and 361 of type 3 (FCINS = MCINS but FACORNS > MACORNS), suggesting that females are more frequently ranked higher on CR items than males (i.e., types 1 and 3)."
