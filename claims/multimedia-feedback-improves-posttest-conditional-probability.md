# Multimedia feedback produces higher posttest performance than text-only feedback on conditional probability problems

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Undergraduates who received multimedia feedback (text plus diagrams) outperformed those who received text-only feedback on an immediate posttest. [→ Gök 2025](#gok-2025)
`q3 i2` The feedback advantage held for both familiar and novel (transfer) posttest problems. [→ Gök 2025 (2)](#gok-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Gök 2025

Gök, S., Zhang, T., Goldstone, R. L., & Fyfe, E. R. (2025). Multimedia Effects in Initial Instruction and Feedback on Problem Solving. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000943

`q3 · i2`

A 2x2 between-subjects ANCOVA on total posttest score with pretest as covariate, in the randomized experiment with 129 undergraduates, found a significant feedback modality effect (η p² = .08): "Those who received multimedia feedback outperformed those who received text-only feedback."

> "There was a significant main effect of feedback modality, F (1,124) = 10.54, p = .001, η p² = .08. Those who received multimedia feedback outperformed those who received text-only feedback."

### Gök 2025 (2)

Gök, S., Zhang, T., Goldstone, R. L., & Fyfe, E. R. (2025). Multimedia Effects in Initial Instruction and Feedback on Problem Solving. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000943

`q3 · i2`

An exploratory split of the same posttest into familiar and novel items showed the feedback effect generalized: for "novel posttest problems" the feedback main effect was significant, F (1, 124) = 8.63, p = .004, with printed effect size η p 2 = .07.

> "For novel posttest problems, there was also a significant main effect of the feedback condition, F (1, 124) = 8.63, p = .004, η p 2 = .07."
