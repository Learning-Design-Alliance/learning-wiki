## Evidence

### Tamblyn R 2007

Tamblyn R, Abrahamowicz M, Dauphinee D, et al. (2007). Physician Scores on a National Clinical Skills Examination as Predictors of Complaints to Medical Regulatory Authorities. JAMA. https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.298.9.993

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Poisson models for written examination components (Table 5): CDM score RR 1.51 (95% CI, 1.25-1.84, P<.001) for any retained complaint per 2-SD decline, versus multiple-choice RR 1.25 (P=.02), which was not significant for communication or quality-of-care complaints. Only relative risks were printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "The traditional written examination also was significantly associated with complaint rate, with the strongest association being for the clinical decision-making (CDM) score."

### Tamblyn R 2007 (2)

Tamblyn R, Abrahamowicz M, Dauphinee D, et al. (2007). Physician Scores on a National Clinical Skills Examination as Predictors of Complaints to Medical Regulatory Authorities. JAMA. https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.298.9.993

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Generalized additive model nonparametric extension of Poisson regression with smoothing splines (4 df) tested nonlinearity; the printed GAM test gave P=.02, and the score-range boundary is reported as printed with no effect size.

> "Statistically significant nonlinearity was found in the relationship between CDM scores and overall complaint rate (P = .02, for 3 df GAM test). The complaint rate increased with declining CDM scores between 600 and 450, with no systematic effect beyond this score range."
