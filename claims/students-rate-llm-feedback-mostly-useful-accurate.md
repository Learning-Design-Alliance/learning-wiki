## Evidence

### Winter Allen 2025

Winter Allen, Anand Shanker, N. Sanjay Rebello. (2025). Students' Perceptions to a Large Language Model's Generated Feedback and Scores of Argumentation Essays. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1119/perc.2025.pr.allen](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2025.pr.allen)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Delayed post-survey (1-2 weeks after writing arguments) with a six-point usefulness Likert scale and a four-point accuracy Likert scale, administered via the course LMS with bonus points. Ratings are shown in Figure 1; no effect size is printed.

> "Across both quizzes, where the average scores differed significantly, the majority of students found the feedback from the LLM to be "mostly useful" and "mostly accurate"."

### Winter Allen 2025 (2)

Winter Allen, Anand Shanker, N. Sanjay Rebello. (2025). Students' Perceptions to a Large Language Model's Generated Feedback and Scores of Argumentation Essays. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1119/perc.2025.pr.allen](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2025.pr.allen)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The same survey compared Likert ratings of students who got the MC question incorrect (orange bars in Fig. 1) versus correct (blue bars); the article reports no significant difference and prints no effect size.

> "There is no significant difference between how students who got the MC question incorrect versus correct scored the feedback."
