# Phonological awareness accounted for more unique variance in reading accuracy than RAN in the orthographies studied, while English was the only orthography where RAN added substantial unique variance (14.2%) and total explained variance was highest (39%)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2`–`r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The proximal predictors accounted for higher amounts of variance in reading accuracy in English (39%) than in all other orthographies (11.3 to 14.7%). [→ Moll 2014](#moll-2014)
`q2 i?` Phonological awareness accounted for more variance in reading accuracy (5.2 to 17.6%); only in English could RAN account for substantial variance beyond phonological processing (14.2%). [→ Moll 2014 (2)](#moll-2014-2)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Per-language stepwise regressions (age and IQ controlled in step 1; phonological memory, awareness and RAN in step 2) showed total proximal variance in reading accuracy was higher "in English (39 %) than in all other orthographies (11.3 to 14.7%)".

> "Table 4 shows that overall the proximal predictors phonological processing and RAN could account for higher amounts of variance in reading accuracy in English (39 %) than in all other orthographies (11.3 to 14.7%)."

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the same regression models "phonological awareness accounted for more variance in reading accuracy (5.2 to 17.6 %)", and "The only orthography in which RAN could account for a substantial amount of variance above and beyond phonological processing was English (14.2 %)".

> "While RAN was found the best unique predictor of reading speed, phonological awareness accounted for more variance in reading accuracy (5.2 to 17.6 %). The only orthography in which RAN could account for a substantial amount of variance above and beyond phonological processing was English (14.2 %)."
