# During the pandemic, Grade 4 students with SEN-L experienced less favorable home learning environments and lower parental support than non-SEN students, while Grade 9 group differences were largely non-significant

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Grade 4 SEN-L-R and SEN-L-S students had significantly less favorable home learning environments than non-SEN students, and parents of non-SEN students reported better support ability than parents of both SEN-L groups. [→ Rebecca Schneider 2025](#rebecca-schneider-2025)
`q3 i?` In Grade 9, group differences in pandemic learning conditions were not significant except for problems during distance learning, which non-SEN students faced more often than SEN-L-S students. [→ Rebecca Schneider 2025 (2)](#rebecca-schneider-2025-2)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Group comparisons of pandemic learning conditions in the 2022 Grade 9 assessment. The article reports that "group differences were not significant in Grade 9 with the exception of problems occurring during distance learning."

> "In contrast, group differences were not significant in Grade 9 with the exception of problems occurring during distance learning. Non-SEN students were more likely to face problems at home during distance learning than SEN-L-S students (Table 3)."
