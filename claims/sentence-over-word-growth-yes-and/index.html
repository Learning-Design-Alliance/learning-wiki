Evidence¶

DeMichele 2015¶

DeMichele, M. (2015). Improv and ink: Increasing individual writing fluency with collaborative improv. International Journal of Education & the Arts, 16(10). http://www.ijea.org/v16n10/

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Cross-study pattern reported in the Key Findings section, drawing on Tables 1-5 and Figures 1-5 from both studies. In pre-assessment phases word usage exceeded sentence usage; after improv the pattern reversed, which the author suggests means "Yes, and…" helped students "add consecutive thoughts".

"After exposure to the series of improv games, the growth in sentence usage exceeded the growth in word usage. This data suggests that the improv concept, “Yes, and…” did help students increase the length of their writing by helping them add consecutive thoughts."
