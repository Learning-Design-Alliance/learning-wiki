## Evidence

### Gaudet Andrew D. 2010

Gaudet Andrew D., Ramer Leanne M., Nakonechny Joanne, Cragg Jacquelyn J., Ramer Matt S. (2010). Small-Group Learning in an Upper-Level University Biology Class Enhances Academic Performance and Student Attitudes Toward Group Work. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0015821

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Qualitative thematic grouping of students' answers to the open-ended entrance/exit survey questions in the 2008 section; students reported, for example, that group work helped eradicate the illusion of comprehension.

> "The responses to the first question could be generally grouped into four categories: enhanced understanding, hearing different perspectives, increased motivation, and social support."

### Gaudet Andrew D. 2010 (2)

Gaudet Andrew D., Ramer Leanne M., Nakonechny Joanne, Cragg Jacquelyn J., Ramer Matt S. (2010). Small-Group Learning in an Upper-Level University Biology Class Enhances Academic Performance and Student Attitudes Toward Group Work. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0015821

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive comparison of entrance versus exit survey responses on perceived limitations; disparate motivation concerns dropped from 44% to 8% of students, which the article reads as satisfaction with group mates' effort. Communication difficulties were cited by about one-third of students on both surveys.

> "many students (44%) worried that "people do not do their work/fall behind/are not reliable" when working in groups. Substantially fewer students (8%) recognized student apathy and motivational issues as limitations on the exit survey"
