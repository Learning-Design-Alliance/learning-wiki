# Faculty in applied, hard, and life disciplines are more likely than colleagues in pure, soft, and nonlife disciplines to report publicly engaged scholarship overall

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Overall reporting of outreach and engagement differs significantly across the pure/applied Biglan dimension, with applied faculty reporting more (98.8% vs 88.4%). [→ Doberneck 2017](#doberneck-2017)

## Evidence

### Doberneck 2017

Doberneck, D. M., & Schweitzer, J. H. (2017). Disciplinary Variations in Publicly Engaged Scholarship: An Analysis Using the Biglan Classification of Academic Disciplines. Journal of Higher Education Outreach and Engagement, 21(1). https://portal.issn.org/resource/ISSN/1534-6104

`q2 · i1`

Interpretive content analysis of 171 RPT forms from one research-intensive land-grant institution; crosstabs of paired Biglan dimensions with frequency of reported publicly engaged scholarship. Table 3 reports overall outreach and engagement at 88.4% pure vs 98.8% applied, chi-square 7.758, significance 0.05.

> "Faculty members in the applied, hard, and life disciplines were overall more likely than their colleagues in pure, soft, and nonlife-fields to report publicly engaged scholarship."
