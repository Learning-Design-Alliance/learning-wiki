## Evidence

### Michael C. Wittmann 2010

Michael C. Wittmann. (2010). Using conceptual blending to describe emergent meaning in wave propagation. Originally published 2008. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1008.0216](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1008.0216)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The author's comparative theoretical argument, not an empirical test: in the resources framework the use of a resource in context is underdefined, while blending shows directly how an idea is used, and "modeling student responses with mental space integration provides a predictive power that is not possible in the resources framework."

> "Mental space integration provides a more complete description of student thinking than does the resources framework because it makes fewer assumptions and has greater explanatory power."

### Michael C. Wittmann 2010 (2)

Michael C. Wittmann. (2010). Using conceptual blending to describe emergent meaning in wave propagation. Originally published 2008. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1008.0216](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1008.0216)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Illustrative prediction from the Wave-Ball blending diagram (Figure 2): a larger rock causes a larger splash but not faster waves, and the blending account explains why the two situations are not connected, which the resource graph in Figure 4 does not capture.

> "Without a hand motion, the ball-space ideas simply won’t be connected to the water-wave situation and it seems reasonable to predict that students would not think that a larger splash leads to a faster wave in the pond."
