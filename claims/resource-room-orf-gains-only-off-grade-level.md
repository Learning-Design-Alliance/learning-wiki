# Students with LD made statistically significant gains in oral reading fluency on off-grade-level passages but no significant gains on word reading or comprehension standard scores

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Off-grade-level ORF scores increased significantly from pre- to posttest (t = 2.48), while on-grade-level ORF differences were not significant (t = 2.10, ns). [→ Swanson 2010](#swanson-2010)
`q2 i?` No statistically significant differences were detected on LWI, WA, or PC from pretest to posttest. [→ Swanson 2010 (2)](#swanson-2010-2)

## Evidence

### Swanson 2010

Swanson, E. A., & Vaughn, S. (2010). An Observation Study of Reading Instruction Provided to Elementary Students With Learning Disabilities in the Resource Room. Psychology in the Schools, 47(5). https://doi.org/10.1002/pits.20484

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pre–post assessment of 32 students over a 13-week period with t tests and Bonferroni adjustment. The results report "Mean off-grade-level ORF scores increased from pre- to posttest at a statistically signiﬁcant level ( t = 2.48, p>. 01)", with a mean gain of 0.37 words per week on off-grade-level passages.

> "Mean off-grade-level ORF scores increased from pre- to posttest at a statistically signiﬁcant level ( t = 2.48, p>. 01)."

### Swanson 2010 (2)

Swanson, E. A., & Vaughn, S. (2010). An Observation Study of Reading Instruction Provided to Elementary Students With Learning Disabilities in the Resource Room. Psychology in the Schools, 47(5). https://doi.org/10.1002/pits.20484

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same pre–post analysis using Woodcock Johnson III standard scores. The results state "No statistically signiﬁcant differences were detected on measures of LWI, W A, or PC"; scores remained at least 1 SD below the normative mean at posttest.

> "No statistically signiﬁcant differences were detected on measures of LWI, W A, or PC."
