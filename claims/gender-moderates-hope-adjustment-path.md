# Gender moderates the hope-to-adjustment path, which is stronger in men than women, but not the other model paths

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Multi-group analysis showed the path from academic hope to academic adjustment differed by gender (multi-group p = 0.007), with men β = 0.63 versus women β = 0.24. [→ Mehrali 2025](#mehrali-2025)

## Evidence

### Mehrali 2025

Mehrali, K., M. Mohsenpour, and A. Abdollahi. 2025. "The Mediating Role of Academic Engagement in the Relationship Between Academic Hope and Academic Adjustment of Undergraduate Students: The Role of Gender as a Moderator." Brain and Behavior 15, no. 11: e71079. https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.71079

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multi-group SEM by gender (N = 353; 174 men, 179 women), Results §3.5 and Table 4; Table 4 prints men β = 0.63, women β = 0.24, multi-group p = 0.007 for the AH→AA path.

> "the path from academic hope to academic adjustment is statistically significant (p < 0.05). This finding suggests that gender modifies this relationship, implying that the impact of academic hope on academic adjustment varies between male and female groups."

### Mehrali 2025 (2)

Mehrali, K., M. Mohsenpour, and A. Abdollahi. 2025. "The Mediating Role of Academic Engagement in the Relationship Between Academic Hope and Academic Adjustment of Undergraduate Students: The Role of Gender as a Moderator." Brain and Behavior 15, no. 11: e71079. https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.71079

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Null moderation results from the same multi-group analysis (Table 4): p = 0.59 for AH→AE and p = 0.47 for AE→AA, indicating comparable paths for men and women.

> "on the path from academic hope to academic engagement, the p‐value is greater than 0.05, suggesting that gender does not moderate this relationship"
