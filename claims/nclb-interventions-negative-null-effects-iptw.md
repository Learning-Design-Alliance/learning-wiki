## Evidence

### Lee 2013

Lee, J., Shin, H. & Amo, L. C. (2013). Evaluating the impact of NCLB school interventions in New York state: Does one size fit all? Education Policy Analysis Archives, 21 (67). http://epaa.asu.edu/ojs/article/view/1122

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

IPTW regression analysis of overall NCLB intervention effects on PI gains (Table 3), using pooled 2005-2009 New York State school data with propensity-score weighting and difference-in-differences. The article reports "small (d = -.14 in Grade 4 ELA) to moderate (d = -.69 in Grade 4 math)" negative effects; Grade 8 ELA was insignificant and Grade 8 math positive and small (d = .18).

> "schools in accountability treatment experienced significantly lower PI gains relative to schools in good standing in Grade 4 ELA (β = -3.238, p < .01) and Grade 4 math (β = -10.567, p < .05). The effect sizes10 for these negative treatment effects in standard deviation units are small (d = -.14 in Grade 4 ELA) to moderate (d = -.69 in Grade 4 math)."
