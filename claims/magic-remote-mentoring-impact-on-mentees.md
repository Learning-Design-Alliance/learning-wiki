## Evidence

### Khare 2013

Khare, R., Sahai, E., Pramanick, I. (2013). Remote Mentoring Young Females in STEM through MAGIC. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1304.7547](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1304.7547)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Five-year retrospective analysis of 23 MAGIC mentor-mentee pairs, with narrative data coded qualitatively. The authors report that "12 mentees reported feeling more confident about their skills and personal attributes" and that eight mentees felt significant STEM skill improvement. No effect sizes are reported.

> "Overall, 12 mentees reported feeling more confident about their skills and personal attributes as a result of the MAGIC interactions."

### Khare 2013 (2)

Khare, R., Sahai, E., Pramanick, I. (2013). Remote Mentoring Young Females in STEM through MAGIC. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1304.7547](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1304.7547)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

In the same retrospective study, the impact section reports career-awareness outcomes, including that "At least five mentees reported that they gained significant improvement in career awareness and advancement". Six mentees attended the Grace Hopper Conference, two won a 2013 NCWIT award, and three reported discontent with the remote format.

> "At least five mentees reported that they gained significant improvement in career awareness and advancement; one received a reference letter from her mentor for high school admission;"
