# Evidence that interim assessments benefit low-achievers more than other students was partial and not systematic

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The hypothesis that interim assessments promote achievement more for low-achievers was only partially supported, with significant effects at lower quantiles in some analyses but not others. [→ Konstantopoulos 2015](#konstantopoulos-2015)

## Evidence

### Konstantopoulos 2015

Konstantopoulos, S., Li, W., Miller, S., & van der Ploeg, A. (2015). Effects of Interim Assessments on the Achievement Gap: Evidence from an Experiment. SREE Spring 2015 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED562166

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Across quantile regression analyses of the cluster-randomized trial, significant lower-tail effects appeared in some grade ranges (e.g., 10th quantile mathematics in K-8 and K-6 analyses) but not consistently. The authors state the evidence "is not systematic."

> "The notion that interim assessments may promote achievement more for low -achievers than other students was partially supported. However, the evidence is not systematic, and thus we were not able to conclude definitively that interim assessments could reduce the achievement gap."
