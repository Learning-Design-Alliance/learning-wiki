# Former YPT participants show a lasting positive bias towards YPT, rating RPC and other Non-YPT activities as less useful

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students who participated in YPT activities earlier (but not those participating only this year) rate RPC and other Non-YPT activities as less useful for all hard-skill types than students who never participated. [→ Boyka Aneva 2022](#boyka-aneva-2022)

## Evidence

### Boyka Aneva 2022

Boyka Aneva, Sergej Faletič, Mihály Hömöstrei, Péter Jenei, Isza Éva, František Kundracik, Assen Kyuldjiev, Thomas Lindner, Hynek Němec, Harald Puhr, and Martin Plesch. (2022). Development of Scientific Skills via IYPT: How does YPT participation lead to hard-skill development? ELTE University Budapest. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2212.09076](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2212.09076)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Linear regressions on the student survey with never-participants as baseline; effects appear only for earlier participants, not recent ones, and no differences appear for perceived usefulness of YPT activities itself.

> "For all types of hard skills, we observe that students that participated in YPT-related activities consider RPC - and also other Non-YPT activities -   less useful to develop these hard skills."
