# Community college credential completion rates are low and have slightly declined, with markedly lower rates for black and Hispanic students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Roughly 21-23 percent of community college entrants earned a credential within three years, and rates for black and Hispanic students were about 12 and 16 percent respectively. [→ Nodine 2011](#nodine-2011)

## Evidence

### Nodine 2011

Nodine, T., Venezia, A., and Bracco, K. (2011). Changing Course: A Guide to Increasing Student Completion in Community Colleges. San Francisco, CA: WestEd. http://knowledgecenter.completionbydesign.org/knowledge-center

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

The guide reports national completion statistics for community college cohorts, stating that "roughly 23 percent earned a credential" for the 1999 cohort and roughly 21 percent for the 2005 cohort within three years. It separately reports about 12 percent for black and 16 percent for Hispanic students in the 2005 cohort.

> "Among students who began at a public community college in 1999, roughly 23 percent earned a credential ( that is, a certificate or an associate’s degree ) within three years. Among those who began in 2005 ( the most recent year for which data are available ), roughly 21 percent earned a credential after three years."
