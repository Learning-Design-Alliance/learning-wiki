## Subclaims
`q3 i2` The High Stable SE and Decreasing SE trajectories had higher initial math skills than the Low Stable and Increasing trajectories (ES .56–.80), and High Stable children developed math skills faster than Decreasing (d = 0.47) and Low Stable (d = 0.66) children. [→ Peura 2026](#peura-2026)

## Evidence

### Peura 2026

Peura, P., Koponen, T., Aro, T., Viholainen, H., & Aro, M. (2026). Dynamics of change in math self-efficacy, sources of self-efficacy, and math skills. Journal of Educational Psychology, 118(2), 209-223. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0001006

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Latent growth models of arithmetic fluency and basic arithmetic skills were linked to the four self-efficacy trajectories via BCH; the trajectories differed in level and rate of change in math skills (LR test < .01), with pairwise effect sizes ranging from .56 to .80 for initial skills.

> "Children in the High Stable SE trajectory developed their math skills faster than children in the Decreasing SE (d = 0.47) and Low Stable SE (d = 0.66) trajectories."
