## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For the subset of questions re-used between offerings differing only by removal of the least effective distractor, three of four courses showed no significant difference in marks, while A&P A percent score increased from 69.7 to 72.2%. [→ Fozzard Nicolette 2018](#fozzard-nicolette-2018)

## Evidence

### Fozzard Nicolette 2018

Fozzard Nicolette, Pearson Andrew, du Toit Eugene, Naug Helen, Wen William, Peak Ian R. (2018). Analysis of MCQ and distractor use in a large first year Health Faculty Foundation Program: assessing the effects of changing from five to four options. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1346-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Independent samples t-test comparison of marks on re-used questions between sequential cohorts. "Three out of the four courses showed no significant difference between offerings (p > .05, Table 5)"; in A&P A the percent score rose from 69.7 to 72.2% (p < .01).

> "Three out of the four courses showed no significant difference between offerings (p > .05, Table 5); that is, removal of the least effective distractor resulted in no significant change in student performance from first to second offering on those questions in three courses. In A&P A, the percent score on the repeated questions increased from 69.7 to 72.2% (p < .01)."
