# MACE participation increased students' use of daily (tier 1) geometrical words, but not academic (tier 2) geometrical words, which decreased in all conditions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Class differences in growth of daily geometrical words in the GAT were explained by MACE lesson series participation, but academic geometrical word use was not affected and declined over time. [→ Eveline M. Schoevers & Paul P. M. Leseman & Evelyn H. Kroesbergen 2019](#eveline-m-schoevers-paul-p-m-leseman-evelyn-h-kroesbergen-2019)

## Evidence

### Eveline M. Schoevers & Paul P. M. Leseman & Evelyn H. Kroesbergen 2019

Eveline M. Schoevers & Paul P. M. Leseman & Evelyn H. Kroesbergen. (2019). Enriching Mathematics Education with Visual Arts: Effects on Elementary School Students' Ability in Geometry and Visual Arts. International Journal of Science and Mathematics Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10763-019-10018-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multilevel analysis of geometrical vocabulary ratios in the GAT open questions. MACE lesson series participation explained class differences in tier 1 word growth; for tier 2 academic words neither condition explained differences (t(94) = 0.36, p = 0.72), and their proportion decreased at posttest in all conditions.

> "With regard to the daily geometrical words (tier1), the difference could be explained by the participation in the MACE program (t(94) = 2.60,p < 0.05), but not by participation in the MACE PD program (t(94) =− 1.42,p = 0.16)."
