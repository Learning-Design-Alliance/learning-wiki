Latent class analysis of parent-reported sleep health from 351 children in publicly-funded PreK programs; the two-class solution was selected as most parsimonious. The article states "87% of children in the sample were classified in Class 1, henceforth, the earlier, longer, consistent sleep health class."

Related Claims¶

  • The later, shorter, inconsistent sleep health class shows later bedtimes, shorter sleep duration, less consistent routines, and higher pre-bedtime caffeine consumption than the earlier, longer, consistent class — related
  • A greater proportion of children whose maternal educational attainment is some college, technical, or two-year degree fell in the later, shorter, inconsistent sleep health class — related
  • Children in the earlier, longer, consistent sleep health class showed stronger approaches to learning (PLBS total and T-CRS task orientation) than peers in the later, shorter, inconsistent class — related
  • Children in the earlier, longer, consistent sleep health class scored higher on expressive vocabulary than peers in the later, shorter, inconsistent class after covariate adjustment — related
  • Children in the earlier, longer, consistent sleep health class showed greater emotion regulation and assertiveness than peers in the later, shorter, inconsistent class after covariate adjustment — related
  • No statistically significant mean differences by sleep health class on literacy, mathematics, or executive functioning after covariate adjustment — related
  • A language-based bedtime routine at age 3 predicted longer nighttime sleep duration at age 5 and higher language abilities and receptive vocabulary at age 5 — related
