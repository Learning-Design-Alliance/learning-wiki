# Admissions selection induces a spurious negative correlation between admissions variables (GRE score and research experience) among admitted students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q1` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q1 i3` Among admitted students, GRE score and experience become strongly negatively correlated (r = -0.526 overall, r = -0.928 in the lower tier) despite being uncorrelated in the full pool. [→ Alexander Small 2017](#alexander-small-2017)

## Evidence

### Alexander Small 2017

Alexander Small. (2017). Range restriction, admissions criteria, and correlation studies of standardized tests. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1709.02895](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1709.02895)

`q1 · i3` · `theoretical · r3`

Simulation of admissions based on an accurate performance model; Table 1 reports the printed effect size r = -0.526 between GRE and experience among all admitted students and r = -0.928 in the lower half, versus -0.002 in the full pool. The article attributes this purely to the selection process, not the underlying sample.

> "range restriction leads to a negative correlation between them in the pools of admitted students. Admitted students who score poorly on one measure are essentially guaranteed to score well on the other, due to the selection process that they went through."
