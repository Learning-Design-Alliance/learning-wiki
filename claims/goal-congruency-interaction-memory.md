# Reading goal × congruency interaction on memory: study goal improves memory for congruent but not incongruent targets

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Moort 2022

Moort, M. L. van, Koornneef, A. W., Wilderjans, T. F., & Broek, P. W. van den. (2022). Validation processes and reading purpose: Is validation against knowledge and prior text influenced by reading goal? Journal of Educational Psychology, 114(7), 1533-1552. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000769

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r?`

Mixed-effects logistic regression on recognition memory scores (assessed ~24 hr after reading) revealed a significant goal × congruency interaction (b = -.34, z = -2.25, p = .025). Posthoc comparisons showed "memory for congruent targets was stronger when reading for study than when reading for comprehension" (OR = 1.59), while incongruent targets showed no goal effect (p = .440).

> "memory for congruent targets was stronger when reading for study than when reading for comprehension (Mstudy = .80, SEstudy = .01, Mcomp = .70, SEcomp = .01, b = .46, SE = .14, z = 3.39, p , .001, OR = 1.59) but memory for incongruent targets did not differ as a function of reading goals"
