# Classroom active breaks produce a small but statistically significant positive effect on working memory in schoolchildren aged 6–16

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Melguizo-Ibáñez 2026

Melguizo-Ibáñez, E., Zurita-Ortega, F., Tadeu, P., Ubago-Jiménez, J. L., & Alonso Vargas, J. M. (2026). Classroom Active Breaks as a Physical-Educational Intervention for Executive Functions and Mathematical Outcomes: a Systematic Review with Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10146-3

`q3 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analysis of 35 intervention studies (7701 students aged 6–16) using Robust Variance Estimation; the RVE model found "a small and statistically significant effect (g = 0.14, 95% CI [0.002, 0.288], t(11) = 2.23, p = 0.04)" for working memory.

> "The RVE model also presented a small and statistically significant effect (g = 0.14, 95% CI [0.002, 0.288], t(11) = 2.23, p = 0.04). Among cognitive outcomes, working memory demonstrated the most consistent evidence of potential benefit from active breaks."
