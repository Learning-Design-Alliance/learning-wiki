# Ontic and discursive relations progressively weaken from first year to Honours and postgraduate assessment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Assessment questions move from idealised single-concept problems with very strong ontic and discursive relations toward problems requiring application across fields, with relations substantially weaker at Honours and postgraduate levels. ## Evidence

### Alan S. Cornell and Kershree Padayachee 2021

Alan S. Cornell and Kershree Padayachee. (2021). Revealing shifts from mastery of knowledge to problem solving in assessments of a tertiary physics programme. arXiv. [doi:10.17159/2520-9868/i83a06](https://doi.org/10.17159/2520-9868/i83a06)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative exemplar analysis of assessment questions at each level: first-year questions are highly idealised one-idea problems where "Memorisation is probably enough to pass", second year introduces physical application, and Honours questions apply particle physics techniques to condensed matter, with substantially weaker relations.

> "Example 5 illustrates that in Honours, the problems used in assessments require students to draw on multiple concepts and procedures from one ﬁeld and to potentially apply these in a neighbouring or related ﬁeld. The ontic and discursive relations are thus, substantially weaker than in undergraduate assessments."
