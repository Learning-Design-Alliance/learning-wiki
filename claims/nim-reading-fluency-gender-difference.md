## Evidence

### Ayed H. Ziadat & Mohammad Soud A. AL-Awan 2018

Ayed H. Ziadat & Mohammad Soud A. AL-Awan. (2018). The Effectiveness of Neurological Impress Method on Reading Fluency of Students with Learning Disabilities in Amman, Jordan. International Education Studies, 11(1). https://doi.org/10.5539/ies.v11n1p165

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Two Way ANOVA on post-measurement reading fluency by group and gender (ten boys and ten girls per group, N = 40). Boys scored higher than girls in both groups: post-test means of 2.64 against 2.24 in the NIM group and 1.97 against 1.72 in the control group (gender F = 21.58, p < .05). The group-by-gender interaction was not significant (F = 1.058, p = .311), so the study does not show that NIM's effect differed by gender, although the authors conclude that NIM "was more effective with male's students". The gap is a difference between groups of students, not something gender causes. No effect size is printed.
