# Microgenetic case studies show learners translating across complementary inscriptions develop progressively refined explanations linking computational actions and physics concepts

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` As learners generate and translate across complementary inscriptional forms, they develop progressively refined explanations of semantic relationships (e.g., ViMAP step-size represents Surge's speed) and mathematical relationships (e.g., change in step-size represents acceleration). ## Evidence

### Sengupta 2016

Sengupta, P., & Clark, D. (2016). Playing Modeling Games in the Science Classroom: The Case for Disciplinary Integration. Educational Technology, 56(3), 16–22. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1607.05094](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1607.05094)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

In-depth microgenetic case studies of students using ViMAP interwoven with SURGE NextG game play showed progressively refined explanations, such as "the step-size of the ViMAP agent represents speed of Surge"; qualitative, no effect sizes.

> "In-depth microgenetic case studies demonstrated that as learners engaged with generating and translating across complementary inscriptional forms, they were able to develop progressively refined explanations about the semantic relationship between computational actions and physics concepts (e.g., the step-size of the ViMAP agent represents speed of Surge)"
