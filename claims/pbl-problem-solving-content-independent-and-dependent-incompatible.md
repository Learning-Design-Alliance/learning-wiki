# The problem-solving-skills and knowledge-acquisition approaches to PBL are epistemologically incompatible: problem-solving cannot be both content-independent and content-dependent

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Barrows's position (heuristics independent of content) and Schmidt's position (problem-solving anchored in prior knowledge) rest on opposing understandings of the role of knowledge in problem-solving and cannot both be true. [→ Servant-Miklos 2018](#servant-miklos-2018)

## Evidence

### Servant-Miklos 2018

Servant-Miklos, V. F. C. (2018). Problem solving skills versus knowledge acquisition: the historical dispute that split problem-based learning into two camps. Advances in Health Sciences Education, 24(3), 619–635. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-018-9835-0

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Theoretical argument from the article's historical analysis. Barrows's PBL problems could be "a written case, case vignette, standardized (also called simulated patient), computer simulation, video tape", while Schmidt's problems could be biomedical phenomena with no solution, requiring understanding of underlying mechanisms.

> "But problem-solving cannot be both content-independent and content-dependent—these two positions are therefore epistemologically incompatible with one another."
