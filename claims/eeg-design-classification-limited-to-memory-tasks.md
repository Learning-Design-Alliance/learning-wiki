## Evidence

### Jesus G. Cruz-Garza 2021

Jesus G. Cruz-Garza, Michael Darfler, James D. Rounds, Elita Gao, Saleh Kalantari. (2021). EEG-based Investigation of the Impact of Classroom Design on Cognitive Performance of Students. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2102.03629](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2102.03629)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The leave-one-out EEG classification analysis showed above-chance results for the DST, BT and VMT comparisons only; the article reports these changes were "found only during the cognitive tests that required short-term memory encoding". Hypotheses 2-4 were only partially supported.

> "the changes in EEG frequency band-power and connectivity associated with the design alterations were found only during the cognitive tests that required short-term memory encoding: the Digit Span Test, the Benton Test, and the Visual Memory Test."

### Jesus G. Cruz-Garza 2021 (2)

Jesus G. Cruz-Garza, Michael Darfler, James D. Rounds, Elita Gao, Saleh Kalantari. (2021). EEG-based Investigation of the Impact of Classroom Design on Cognitive Performance of Students. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2102.03629](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2102.03629)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same machine-learning analysis the VMT was the most robust test: all three design-vs-Neutral comparisons reached significance (p < 0.003), with reported test-set median accuracies of 55.0%, 57.5% and 61.3%; the Stroop and Arithmetic tests showed no robust classification.

> "When the participants were engaged in the VMT, the classifier could identify any of the three design conditions compared to the Neutral classroom with statistically significant success (p < 0.003)."
