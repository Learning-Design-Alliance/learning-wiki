# Pupils' pre-test attitudes toward social studies were negatively correlated with their pre-treatment attitudes toward ICT

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Pre-test social studies attitudes correlated negatively with pre-treatment ICT attitudes; a similar but statistically non-significant pattern appeared at post-test. [→ Emin Cener 2015](#emin-cener-2015)

## Evidence

### Emin Cener 2015

Emin Cener, Ismail Acun & Gokhan Demirhan. (2015). The Impact of ICT on Pupils' Achievement and Attitudes in Social Studies. Journal of Social Studies Education Research, 6(1), 190-207. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1105333

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Observational correlational observation reported in the findings discussion of the quasi-experiment; no correlation coefficient is printed. The authors add that pupils whose achievement test scores were higher statistically had slightly higher positive attitudes toward ICT as a result of the treatment.

> "One of the interesting points is that pupils' pre -test attitudes towards social studies are negatively correlated with their pre-treatment attitudes towards ICT. Although statistically not significant, similar result is observed with their post -test scores of social studies and ICT attitudes scales."
