## Evidence

### Stefan Küchemann 2023

Stefan Küchemann, Malte Ubben, David Dzsotjan, Sergey Mukhametov, Carrie A. Weidner, Carrie A. Weidner, Linda Qerimi, Jochen Kuhn, Stefan Heusler, and Jacob F. Sherson. (2023). The impact of an interactive visualization and simulation tool on learning quantum physics: Results of an eye-tracking study. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2302.06286](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2302.06286)

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Eye-tracking lab study of 28 novices and 8 experts solving Quantum Composer tasks. The article reports that "experts reach a higher score in Task 1 (p >0.05) and in Task 2 ( p = 0.040, Cohen's d = 0.76)", with scores ranging from -3 to 3 points per task.

> "We also found that experts reach a higher score in Task 1 (p >0.05) and in Task 2 ( p = 0.040, Cohen's d = 0.76, Fig. 3b). In each task, the scores ranged from -3 to 3 points. However, all diﬀerences are not statistically signiﬁcant."

### Stefan Küchemann 2023 (2)

Stefan Küchemann, Malte Ubben, David Dzsotjan, Sergey Mukhametov, Carrie A. Weidner, Carrie A. Weidner, Linda Qerimi, Jochen Kuhn, Stefan Heusler, and Jacob F. Sherson. (2023). The impact of an interactive visualization and simulation tool on learning quantum physics: Results of an eye-tracking study. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2302.06286](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2302.06286)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive timing data from the same study: novices needed 444 ± 187 s and experts 363 ± 95 s on Task 1, and 346 ± 128 s vs 294 ± 174 s on Task 2, with p >0.05 in both tasks; equivalence was not tested.

> "The novices needed 444 ± 187 s, and the experts needed 363 ± 95 s (p >0.05, Fig. 2b) to complete Quantum Composer Task 1. For Task 2, the novices required 346 ± 128 s, and the experts needed 294 ± 174 s (p> 0.05)."
