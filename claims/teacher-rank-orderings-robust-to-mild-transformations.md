# Rank orderings of teachers do not change radically under mild scale transformations

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` While value-added magnitudes shift with scale, the relative rank ordering of teachers is comparatively stable under mild transformations. [→ Soland 2017](#soland-2017)

## Evidence

### Soland 2017

Soland, J. (2017). Is teacher value added a matter of scale? The practical consequences of treating an ordinal scale as interval for estimation of teacher effects. Applied Measurement in Education, 30(1), 52–70. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/is-teacher-value-added-a-matter-of-scale-the-practical-consequences-of-treating-an-ordinal-scale-as-interval-for-estimation-of-teacher-effects/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Re-estimation of teacher value added after applying mild nonlinear transformations showed that "rank orderings of teachers do not change radically by transformation." No effect size is printed for this finding.

> "While rank orderings of teachers do not change radically by transformation, even mild departures from the original scale can change a teacher's odds of being considered high- or low-performing by a factor of 5."
