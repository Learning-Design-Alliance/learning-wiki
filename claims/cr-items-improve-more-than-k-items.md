## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The rate of change in correct-answer rates was significantly higher for clinical reasoning items (median +22.8%) than for knowledge items (median +4.2%), p < 0.008. [→ Watari Takashi 2020](#watari-takashi-2020)

## Evidence

### Watari Takashi 2020

Watari Takashi, Tokuda Yasuharu, Owada Meiko, Onigata Kazumichi. (2020). The Utility of Virtual Patient Simulations for Clinical Reasoning Education. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17155325

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Item-level McNemar's tests with Bonferroni correction on the 20 pre-post quiz items in the same 169-student pre-post study; 7 of 10 CR items and 4 of 10 K items showed significant increases. No standardized effect size was printed.

> "The rate of change between CR items (median = +22.8% IQR 11.8% to 39.1%, mean = 25.3, 95% CI (12.1–38.5)) was significantly higher than that between K items (median = +4.2% IQR −1.8% to 11.8%, mean = 3.84, 95% CI (−5.1 to 12.8)), with p-value < 0.008."
