Instructor feedback across classroom deployments in the Outcomes section: instructors could start with documentation, student support materials, instructor guidance, and assessment resources already in place. The authors state preparation time was not directly measured, so this is anecdotal, not a quantified comparison.

"Although we did not directly measure preparation time, anecdotal instructor feedback suggests that this packaging reduced the initial effort required to design, organize, adopt, and adapt PjBL activities."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • FORAP-packaged projects were deployed in 44 classroom trials across seven universities over four academic years, reaching about 2,000 students — related
  • In a one-year PBL deployment, the AI-assisted pipeline was associated with instructor preparation time falling from an average of eight hours to two hours per instruction (an estimated 75% reduction). — related
  • Instructors value design problems for assessing higher-order thinking in PjBL but cite creation effort and scalability as adoption barriers — related
  • Instructors engage LLM-generated suggestions as a controllable refinement process — related
  • Instructional materials authored by a single instructor via the pipeline were peer-reviewed and successfully deployed by six different professors, suggesting tacit knowledge was translated into reusable teaching assets. — related
  • University instructors' design process did not appear to draw on instructional design models — related
