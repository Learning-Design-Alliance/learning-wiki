Evidence¶

Steiner 2020¶

Steiner, M., van Loon, M. H., Bayard, N. S., & Roebers, C. M. (2020). Development of Children's monitoring and control when learning from texts: effects of age and test format. Metacognition and Learning, 15, 3–27. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-019-09208-5

q3 · i2 · causal · r1

Repeated measures ANOVA on open-ended question performance (N = 302). Overall gains from T1 to T2 (ηp2 = .07) were driven by second graders (ηp2 = .17 within grade), while fourth graders' performance did not change (p = .295). Omission errors also decreased over time (ηp2 = .09).

"children had more correct answers at T 2 compared to T 1, F (1, 297) = 23.73, p <. 0 0 1 ,ηp2 = .07, whereas no difference was found between second and fourth graders (p = .35). The significant interaction between measurement point and age, F (1, 297) = 12.73, p <. 0 0 1 ,ηp2 = .04, showed that second graders 'increased their performance from T 1 to T 2"

Steiner 2020 (2)¶

Steiner, M., van Loon, M. H., Bayard, N. S., & Roebers, C. M. (2020). Development of Children's monitoring and control when learning from texts: effects of age and test format. Metacognition and Learning, 15, 3–27. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-019-09208-5

q3 · i3 · causal · r1

Repeated measures ANOVA on true-false question performance. Children answered more true-false questions correctly at T2 than T1 (ηp2 = .20), with no significant grade difference (p = .06). Performance was above chance for both grades at both time points.

"For true-false questions, children had more correct answers at T 2 compared to T 1, F (1, 299) = 73.18, p <. 0 0 1 ,ηp2 = .20, whereas no difference was found between second and fourth graders ( p =. 0 6 ,ηp2 = .01)"

  • Children are considerably more overconfident about incorrect answers to true-false questions than about incorrect answers to open-ended questions — related
