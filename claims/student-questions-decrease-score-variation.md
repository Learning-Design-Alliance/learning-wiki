## Evidence

### Olive Ross 2026

Olive Ross, Meagan Sundstrom and N.G. Holmes. (2026). Predictive Modeling for High Impact Active Learning Classrooms. arXiv. [doi:10.21203/rs.3.rs-9955835/v1](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-9955835/v1)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Model prediction from the trained WLS regression on 69 classes. The article notes it "uniquely observe[s] the impact of individual, in-class, student questions on the learning outcomes of the whole class."

> "All classes spending more than 10% of class time on both group clicker questions and group worksheets are predicted to have larger learning gains if students are asking more questions"

### Olive Ross 2026 (2)

Olive Ross, Meagan Sundstrom and N.G. Holmes. (2026). Predictive Modeling for High Impact Active Learning Classrooms. arXiv. [doi:10.21203/rs.3.rs-9955835/v1](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-9955835/v1)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational finding shown in Fig. 5 of the Methods, plotting COPUS code values against changes in score standard deviations. The authors say this potentially indicates student questions can decrease variation in post-test scores.

> "We also find that more class time spent on student questions is correlated with a decrease in the width of the distribution of student scores from pre-test to post-test"
