# Short theoretical-practical CPR training significantly increased nursing professionals' knowledge and skills immediately after both trainings

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Immediately after each training there was a significant increase in theoretical knowledge (post-test means 21.6 and 21.9) and in practical skills (post-test means 17.9 after both), from pre-intervention means of 13.3 and 10.3. ## Evidence

### de Araujo Nyagra Ribeiro R 2022

de Araujo Nyagra Ribeiro R, de Araújo Raul Amaral H, Moretti Miguel Antonio A, Chagas Antonio Carlos Palandri C. (2022). Nursing training and retraining on cardiopulmonary resuscitation: a theoretical-practical intervention. Revista da Escola de Enfermagem da USP. https://doi.org/10.1590/1980-220X-REEUSP-2021-0521

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Within-group pre-post comparison in the same 56-professional cohort using a 25-item theoretical test and an 18-item practical checklist on a low-fidelity manikin, analyzed with the Wilcoxon test. The article reports "a significant increase in skills" with statistically equal post-test means of 17.9 (±0.4) after both trainings; pre-intervention skill mean was 10.3 (±3.7).

> "Immediately after T1 and T2, there was a significant increase in skills and the average of correct answers in the post-test of these two moments were statistically equal (17.9 (±0.4) vs 17.9 (±0.4), p = 0, 56)"
