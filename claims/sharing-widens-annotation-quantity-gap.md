# Pre-reading classes produced more annotations, and the sharing mechanism widened the annotation-quantity gap between pre-reading and non-pre-reading classes

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A significant difference in annotation quantity existed between classes with and without pre-reading in both phases (Table 5 t-values 2.496 and 4.374, p<.01). [→ Wu-Yuin Hwang & Guo-Liang Hsu 2011](#wu-yuin-hwang-guo-liang-hsu-2011)
`q3 i?` With sharing in phase 2, annotations increased in the pre-reading class and decreased in the class without pre-reading, enlarging the gap. [→ Wu-Yuin Hwang & Guo-Liang Hsu 2011 (2)](#wu-yuin-hwang-guo-liang-hsu-2011-2)

## Evidence

### Wu-Yuin Hwang & Guo-Liang Hsu 2011

Wu-Yuin Hwang & Guo-Liang Hsu. (2011). The Effects of Pre-reading and Sharing Mechanisms on Learning with the Use of Annotations. TOJET: The Turkish Online Journal of Educational Technology, 10(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ932242

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

T-tests on annotation counts among the 62 VPEN-group participants (Experiment and Control A); Table 5 prints phase means of 38.47 vs 27.13 (t=2.496) and 46.07 vs 25.28 (t=4.374), both p<.01.

> "According to Table 5, a significant difference exists between the classes with/without pre-reading in two phases."

### Wu-Yuin Hwang & Guo-Liang Hsu 2011 (2)

Wu-Yuin Hwang & Guo-Liang Hsu. (2011). The Effects of Pre-reading and Sharing Mechanisms on Learning with the Use of Annotations. TOJET: The Turkish Online Journal of Educational Technology, 10(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ932242

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of annotation quantities across phases in the VPEN group; the article reports the quantity "increased in the pre-reading class" and "decreased in another class without a request for pre-reading" once sharing was introduced.

> "By employing a sharing mechanism, as seen in Table 5, the quantity of annotations increased in the pre-reading class, while the quantity of annotations decreased in another class without a request for pre-reading."
