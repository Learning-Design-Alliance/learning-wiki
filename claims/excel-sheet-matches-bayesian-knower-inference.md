# The Excel sheet's knower-level inference closely matches fully Bayesian inference, with no discrepancies in posterior maxima

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On an independent data set of 56 children, the Excel approximation's posterior over knower levels differs from the full Bayesian posterior by a mean absolute difference of 0.2%, with no discrepancies in maxima. [→ Negen 2011](#negen-2011)

## Evidence

### Negen 2011

Negen, J., Sarnecka, B. W., & Lee, M. D. (2011). An Excel sheet for inferring children's number-knower levels from give-N data. Behavior Research Methods. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-011-0134-4

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Comparison of the Excel approximation with fully Bayesian (Markov chain Monte Carlo) inference on the 56-child data set from Lee and Sarnecka. The article reports "The mean absolute difference" of 0.2% (SD 0.68%) and a largest absolute difference of 4.8%, with identical posterior maxima.

> "The Excel sheet's inference is very close to the inference generated by fully Bayesian inference. In terms of maxima, there are no discrepancies. The mean absolute difference between the model's posterior over knower levels and the Excel sheet's approximate posterior is 0.2%, with a standard deviation of 0.68%."
