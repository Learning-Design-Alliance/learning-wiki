## Evidence

### Rebecca Y. Bayeck 2026

Rebecca Y. Bayeck. (2026). Exploring learning in classic African board games: a review of recent literature. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10733-9

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing de Voogt et al. (2010), an analysis of mancala-family games (Bao, Warri, Awelé) finding they engaged three of the five senses, making them conducive for players with visual impairments and creating inclusive learning environments.

> "The materiality of these games allowed players to touch, feel, and hear the sound of the playing materials, such as seeds."

### Rebecca Y. Bayeck 2026 (2)

Rebecca Y. Bayeck. (2026). Exploring learning in classic African board games: a review of recent literature. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10733-9

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing de Voogt (2017a, 2017b) and de Voogt et al. (2013), that similar games among the Nama, Ju'hoan, and Yeyi across Namibia and Botswana indicate cultural interaction, so players learn about community cultures through play.

> "The presence of these board games across borders, with limited transformation and/or innovation, demonstrates cultural transmission (de Voogt et al., 2013)"
