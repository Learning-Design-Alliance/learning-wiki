## Evidence

### Chen C and Liu T 2026

Chen C and Liu T (2026) Impact of automated scoring on interpreting performance and self-regulated learning: evidence from a pedagogical experiment. Front. Psychol. 17:1867463. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1867463

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

A preliminary criterion check scored 40 pilot recordings with both the Yunyi system and two trained raters; automated scores were generally lower than human scores (M = 53.32 vs. M = 80.40), and "the two sets of total scores had weak to moderate correlation" (r = 0.36).

> "Nevertheless, the two sets of total scores had weak to moderate correlation (Pearson r = 0.36, p = 0.022, 95% CI [0.06, 0.60]; Spearman ρ = 0.35, p = 0.026, 95% CI [0.04, 0.60])."

### Chen C and Liu T 2026 (2)

Chen C and Liu T (2026) Impact of automated scoring on interpreting performance and self-regulated learning: evidence from a pedagogical experiment. Front. Psychol. 17:1867463. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1867463

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Dimension-level agreement on the 40 pilot recordings was strongest for logical coherence (r = 0.79) and linguistic accuracy (r = 0.63), moderate for delivery fluency (r = 0.45), and weakest — non-significant — for information fidelity (r = 0.12), the dimension carrying the greatest rubric weight (35%).

> "Logical coherence ( r = 0.79, p < 0.001, 95% CI [0.64, 0.89]) and linguistic accuracy ( r = 0.63, p < 0.001, 95% CI [0.39, 0.78]) showed strong correlations with human ratings; delivery fluency (r = 0.45, p = 0.004, 95% CI [0.16, 0.67]) a moderate one; and information fidelity ( r = 0.12, p = 0.478, 95% CI [ −0.20, 0.41]) a weak, non-significant correlation."
