Evidence¶

Li Zhao 2024¶

Li Zhao, Junjie Peng, Shiqi Ke, Kang Lee. (2024). Effectiveness of Unproctored vs. Teacher-Proctored Exams in Reducing Students’ Cheating: A Double-Blind Randomized Controlled Field Experimental Study. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09965-z

q4 · i? · causal · r3

Double-blind randomized field experiment (Study 1, N = 177 second-year psychology students randomly assigned within class sections) using the Fake Question Paradigm in a midterm exam. The logistic regression showed "odds ratio (OR) = 3.46" for the exam type effect on cheating occurrence; cheating rates were 7.9% unproctored vs. 22.7% proctored.

"The exam type effect was significant, β = 1.24, SEβ = 0.47, Wald χ2(df = 1) = 7.00, p = 0.008, odds ratio (OR) = 3.46, 95% CI = 1.38–8.68, BF10 = 3.86."

Li Zhao 2024 (2)¶

Li Zhao, Junjie Peng, Shiqi Ke, Kang Lee. (2024). Effectiveness of Unproctored vs. Teacher-Proctored Exams in Reducing Students’ Cheating: A Double-Blind Randomized Controlled Field Experimental Study. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09965-z

q4 · i? · causal · r3

Study 2 replicated Study 1 with N = 158 students in Foundations of Education, same double-blind randomized design and Fake Question Paradigm. The exam type effect on cheating rate was significant with "odds ratio (OR) = 2.72"; rates were 11.5% unproctored vs. 26.3% proctored.

"The exam type effect was significant, β = 1.00, SEβ = 0.44, Wald χ2 (df = 1) = 5.23, p = 0.022, odds ratio (OR) = 2.72, 95% CI = 1.16 -6.40, BF10 = 1.32."
