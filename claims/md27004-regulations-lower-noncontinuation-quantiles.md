# Experiencing the MD 270/04 academic regulations is associated with lower Bachelor-Master non-continuation, an effect significant at lower quantiles that logistic regression does not detect

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2015 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The MD 270/04 regulation indicator has a negative effect on the latent non-continuation propensity, with significance and magnitude varying across quantiles and becoming more pronounced on the left tail. [→ Cristina Mollica and Lea Petrella 2015](#cristina-mollica-and-lea-petrella-2015)
`q2 i?` A Bayesian logistic regression with the same regressors estimates the MD effect as not significant, so the difference between the two regulation regimes is not captured by the mean-based model. [→ Cristina Mollica and Lea Petrella 2015 (2)](#cristina-mollica-and-lea-petrella-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Cristina Mollica and Lea Petrella 2015

Cristina Mollica and Lea Petrella. (2015). Bayesian binary quantile regression for the analysis of Bachelor-Master transition. [doi:10.1080/02664763.2016.1263835](https://doi.org/10.1080/02664763.2016.1263835)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

BQR forest plots (Figure 5) from the Sapienza cohort data show MD 270/04 estimates decreasing and becoming significant on quantiles below the median; students under MD 270/04 are less likely to drop out.

> "The MD 270/04 is estimated to have a negative eﬀect on the entire latent non-continuation propensity, but its magnitude and signiﬁcance considerably vary across quantiles."

### Cristina Mollica and Lea Petrella 2015 (2)

Cristina Mollica and Lea Petrella. (2015). Bayesian binary quantile regression for the analysis of Bachelor-Master transition. [doi:10.1080/02664763.2016.1263835](https://doi.org/10.1080/02664763.2016.1263835)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of the Bayesian logistic regression with the same regressors against the BQR on the same administrative data; the logistic model reports no significant MD effect, which the authors use to argue for quantile-level information.

> "it is possible to point out that the diﬀerence between the two MD is not captured by the latter model, since the corresponding eﬀect is estimated to be not signiﬁcant."
