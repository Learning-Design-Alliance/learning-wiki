# Brief signed video lessons produced learning gains, but the study could not rule out practice effects because it lacked a no-training control group

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors state that gains from pre-test to post-test could partly be attributed to taking the test twice rather than to the videos, since no no-training control group was included. [→ Rachel Sortino 2026](#rachel-sortino-2026)

## Evidence

### Rachel Sortino 2026

Rachel Sortino, Christina Kim, Thalia Guettler, Katie McClyman, Lorna Quandt, Alicia Wooten & Rachel Pizzie. (2026). Producing concept-motivated signs supports learning of STEM content in American Sign Language. npj Science of Learning 11:37. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00418-6

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

This is the authors' stated limitation, not a tested result. They note randomized question order, alternative question versions, and non-repeated measures (matching task, signed summaries) were used to reduce practice effects.

> "We did not incorporate a no-training control group to rule out the possibility of improvements in test scores coming from test-retest effects, which means the gains we saw from pre-test to post-test could be attributed to learning from taking the test twice in the same day instead of from the videos themselves."
