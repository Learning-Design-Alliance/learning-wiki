# Surface learning does not explain the normative goal link with achievement

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Normative and mastery goals have identical average correlations with surface strategies (rs = .20), and surface strategies are unrelated to achievement overall (mean r = -.01). [→ Senko 2011](#senko-2011)

## Evidence

### Senko 2011

Senko, C., Hulleman, C. S., & Harackiewicz, J. M. (2011). Achievement Goal Theory at the Crossroads: Old Controversies, Current Challenges, and New Directions. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2011.538646

`q3 · i2` · `review · r3`

The review's synthesis of 24 studies correlating goals, surface strategies, and achievement (Table 3) found "the average correlation with surface strategies was identical for normative and mastery goals (rs = .20)". The printed r = .20 is the basis for impact.

> "However, so too were mastery goals in over half of them (56%), and the average correlation with surface strategies was identical for normative and mastery goals (rs = .20)."

### Senko 2011 (2)

Senko, C., Hulleman, C. S., & Harackiewicz, J. M. (2011). Achievement Goal Theory at the Crossroads: Old Controversies, Current Challenges, and New Directions. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2011.538646

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports that in its tabulated studies "Surface learning strategies and achievement were unrelated overall (mean r = -.01)", undermining the first proposition of the depth of learning hypothesis.

> "Surface learning strategies and achievement were unrelated overall (mean r = -.01); the one study that did find a positive correlation between them did not observe a link between normative goals and surface learning strategies"
