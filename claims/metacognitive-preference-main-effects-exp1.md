# In Experiment 1, metacognitive preference for interleaving showed main effects of utility value and differences-matter instruction without an interaction

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Ordinal regression showed a significant impact of utility value on preference for interleaving. [→ Roman Abel 2024](#roman-abel-2024)
`q3 i?` Ordinal regression showed a significant impact of differences-matter instruction on preference for interleaving, with no interaction between the factors. [→ Roman Abel 2024 (2)](#roman-abel-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Roman Abel 2024

Roman Abel, Anique de Bruin, Erdem Onan, Julian Roelle. (2024). Why Do Learners (Under)Utilize Interleaving in Learning Confusable Categories? The Role of Metastrategic Knowledge and Utility Value of Distinguishing. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09902-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Ordinal regression on metacognitive preference in Experiment 1 (N = 190): "a significant impact of utility value, β = 0.77, SE = 0.38, Wald χ2 = 4.10, df = 1, p = 0.044"; only the test statistic is printed, no effect size.

> "We found a significant impact of utility value, β = 0.77, SE = 0.38, Wald χ2 = 4.10, df = 1, p = 0.044, indicating a higher preference for interleaving when the utility value of distinguishing is high."

### Roman Abel 2024 (2)

Roman Abel, Anique de Bruin, Erdem Onan, Julian Roelle. (2024). Why Do Learners (Under)Utilize Interleaving in Learning Confusable Categories? The Role of Metastrategic Knowledge and Utility Value of Distinguishing. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09902-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same ordinal regression showed "a significant impact of differences matter instruction, β = 0.79, SE = 0.38, Wald χ2 = 4.29, df = 1, p = 0.038"; the interaction was not significant (p = 0.172).

> "We also found a significant impact of differences matter instruction, β = 0.79, SE = 0.38, Wald χ2 = 4.29, df = 1, p = 0.038, indicating learners' higher preference for interleaving when being informed that differences matter for distinguishing."
