# An 'Exceeds Expectations' cohort with lower course grades significantly outperformed classmates on Discovery deliverables

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r3` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2020 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` EE students (≥1 SD grade differential in Discovery over course grade) obtained lower course grades but higher final Discovery grades than non-EE students. [→ Davenport Huyer 2020](#davenport-huyer-2020)
`q3 i?` EE students scored significantly higher than classmates on every deliverable except essays. [→ Davenport Huyer 2020 (2)](#davenport-huyer-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Davenport Huyer 2020

Davenport Huyer, L., Callaghan, N. I., Dicks, S., Scherer, E., Shukalyuk, A. I., Jou, M. & Kilkenny, D. (2020). Enhancing senior high school student engagement and academic performance using an inclusive and scalable inquiry-based program. npj Science of Learning 5:17. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-020-00076-2

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Subgroup comparison of the EE subset (N = 99 instances, defined as ≥1 SD [18.0%] grade differential in Discovery over final course grade) against 169 non-EE instances using Mann–Whitney U-tests; EE students obtained "lower course grades ( p < 0.0001" and higher final Discovery grades (p = 0.0004).

> "Discovery EE students (Fig. 3), roughly by de ﬁnition, obtained lower course grades ( p < 0.0001, Fig. 3a) and higher ﬁnal Discovery grades ( p = 0.0004, Fig. 3b) than non-EE students."

### Davenport Huyer 2020 (2)

Davenport Huyer, L., Callaghan, N. I., Dicks, S., Scherer, E., Shukalyuk, A. I., Jou, M. & Kilkenny, D. (2020). Enhancing senior high school student engagement and academic performance using an inclusive and scalable inquiry-based program. npj Science of Learning 5:17. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-020-00076-2

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Deliverable-level comparisons within the same five-term dataset showed EE students received significantly higher grades on each Discovery deliverable than classmates except introductory essays, where the difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.097).

> "This cohort of students exhibited program grades higher than classmates (Fig. 3c– h); these differences were signiﬁcant in every category with the exception of essays, where they outperformed to a signiﬁcantly lesser degree ( p = 0.097; Fig. 3c)."
