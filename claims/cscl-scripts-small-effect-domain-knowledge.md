# Learning with CSCL scripts yields a small positive effect on domain-specific knowledge compared to unstructured CSCL

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Collaborative learning with a CSCL script produces a statistically significant small positive average effect on domain-specific knowledge relative to collaborative learning without a script. [→ Freydis Vogel 2016](#freydis-vogel-2016)

## Evidence

### Freydis Vogel 2016

Freydis Vogel, Christof Wecker, Ingo Kollar, Frank Fischer. (2016). Socio-Cognitive Scaffolding with Computer-Supported Collaboration Scripts: a Meta-Analysis. Educ Psychol Rev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-016-9361-7

`q3 · i1`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 33 effect sizes from 21 articles comparing CSCL-script conditions to unstructured CSCL. The synthesis found a statistically significant positive, although small, average effect; Table 1 reports d = 0.20 with a statistically significant test (z = 2.08, p = .019).

> "As hypothesized, a statistically significant positive, although small, average effect was found (see Table 1). Post hoc power analysis revealed that the statistical power of this test was 87 %."
