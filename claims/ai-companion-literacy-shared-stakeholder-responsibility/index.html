Evidence¶

Qing Xiao 2026¶

Qing Xiao, Wenhan Xie, Ziyu Deng, Ruiwei Xiao, Ziyue Feng, Xie He, Shiyu Zhang, John Stamper, Hong Shen, and Xinying Hou. (2026). Exploring K-12 Teachers' Perceptions of Students' Relationships with AI Companions: Boundaries, Intervention Strategies, and Design Implications. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13479 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.13479

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Qualitative interview finding (RQ3). Teachers assigned counselors the relational depth, parents being informed and equipped, platforms making concerning patterns visible to adults, and policymakers protocols and training.

"Asked whose responsibility it was beyond teachers, nearly every teacher named several parties and assigned each a distinct part, with the teacher's own part being to notice, to teach the academic side, and to connect the student to whoever handles the rest."

Qing Xiao 2026 (2)¶

Qing Xiao, Wenhan Xie, Ziyu Deng, Ruiwei Xiao, Ziyue Feng, Xie He, Shiyu Zhang, John Stamper, Hong Shen, and Xinying Hou. (2026). Exploring K-12 Teachers' Perceptions of Students' Relationships with AI Companions: Boundaries, Intervention Strategies, and Design Implications. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13479

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

On stage ownership, the article reports 10 of 33 assigned primary responsibility to their own grade level, 12 to a different one, 8 to every level equally, one to no teachers at all, and two gave no clear answer; middle school was named the pivotal stage by every level yet claimed by few.

"Asked which grade level should own it, teachers agreed it must start early and be repeated, but disagreed sharply on where primary ownership lies."
