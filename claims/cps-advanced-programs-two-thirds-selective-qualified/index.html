Melissa Roderick 2009¶

Melissa Roderick, Jenny Nagaoka, Vanessa Coca, Eliza Moeller. (2009). From High School to the Future: Making Hard Work Pay Off — The Road to College for Students in CPS's Academically Advanced Programs. Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago, Urban Education Institute. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/high-school-future-making-hard-work-pay

q2 · i? · associational · r?

Descriptive quantitative analysis of CPS tracking data showing college qualifications of selective enrollment, IB, and AP graduates. The report also notes the average weighted GPA of neighborhood AP and IB students "is nearly a 4.0."

"Nearly two-thirds graduate from high school with access to a selective or very selective four-year college. Many students in academically advanced programs and schools graduate with ACT scores and grades that demonstrate to colleges that they have worked hard and done well in rigorous courses."

Related Claims¶

  • AP and IB programs in neighborhood high schools serve students with higher-than-average but below selective-enrollment achievement — related
  • College mismatch is common among Chicago graduates: only 38 percent of the most qualified students enrolled in a match college, and many mismatch by enrolling in two-year colleges or not enrolling — related
  • One-fifth of students in academically advanced programs do not apply to any four-year college — related
  • College undermatch is widespread among CPS students at all qualification levels, with only 27 percent of selective-access graduates matching — related
  • Among selective enrollment students, 64 percent are qualified to attend a selective or very selective college but only 37 percent enroll in one — related
  • Students with strong qualifications took themselves out of four-year college planning, choosing early two-year enrollment as a low-risk solution when searches felt overwhelming and costly — related
  • Only CPS graduates with access to very selective colleges (above 3.3 GPA and ACT 26+) have four-year college graduation prospects exceeding 50 percent — related
  • CPS neighborhood IB DP students were 40 percent more likely to attend a four-year college and 50 percent more likely to attend a more selective college than a matched comparison group — related
  • Even highly qualified students need strong parental support combined with structured school support to conduct an effective college search and enroll in a matched college — related
  • Among similarly qualified seniors, the high school attended largely determines coursetaking patterns — related
