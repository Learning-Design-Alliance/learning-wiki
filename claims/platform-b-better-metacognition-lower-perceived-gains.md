(2021). A Comparative Study of Learning Outcomes for Online Learning Platforms. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2104.07763](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2104.07763)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Self-assessment questionnaire administered after the post-quiz in the comparative study. The article reports the comprehension-rating/learning-gain correlation was "higher forPlatform B participants (r=0.41)" than Platform A (r=0.26), suggesting improved metacognition. For Q2 and Q3, no correlation with actual gains was found.

> "The correlation is higher forPlatform B participants (r=0.41) than for Platform A participants (r=0.26)."

### Francois St-Hilaire 2021 (2)

Francois St-Hilaire, Nathan Burns, Robert Belfer, Muhammad Shayan, Ariella Smofsky, Dung Do Vu, Antoine Frau, Joseph Potochny, Farid Faraji, Vincent Pavero, Neroli Ko, Ansona Onyi Ching, Sabina Elkins, Anush Stepanyan, Adela Matajova, Laurent Charlin, Yoshua Bengio, Iulian Vlad Serban, and Ekaterina Kochmar. (2021). A Comparative Study of Learning Outcomes for Online Learning Platforms. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2104.07763](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2104.07763)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same questionnaire data: Platform B mean comprehension rating was 3.65±0.29 versus 4.16±0.27 for Platform A, a significant difference (p<0.05). No effect size is printed. The authors attribute this to frustrating elements such as the AI tutor not always understanding answers.

> "on average, participants from Platform B reported lower levels of comprehension in absolute terms (Q1), and the diﬀerence from the Platform A average is signiﬁcant ( p<0.05)."
