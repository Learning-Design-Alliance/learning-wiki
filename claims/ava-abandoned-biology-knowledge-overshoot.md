## Evidence

### Benjamin W. Dreyfus 2014

Benjamin W. Dreyfus, Vashti Sawtelle, Chandra Turpen, Julia Gouvea, and Edward F. Redish. (2014). A Vision of Interdisciplinary Education: Students' Reasoning about "High-Energy Bonds" and ATP. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.10.010115](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.10.010115)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Analysis of exam essay responses identified an alternative, relatively common pattern favoring Kim's reasoning. Ava wrote that "Justin is not right because energy is not released when ATP is hydrolyzed," and the authors interpret that she "has abandoned her (correct) biology knowledge" in favor of correct physics reasoning about bond breaking.

> "Indeed she appears to have resolved the conflict, but in doing so has abandoned her (correct) biology knowledge in favor of using correct physics reasoning about breaking bonds."

### Benjamin W. Dreyfus 2014 (2)

Benjamin W. Dreyfus, Vashti Sawtelle, Chandra Turpen, Julia Gouvea, and Edward F. Redish. (2014). A Vision of Interdisciplinary Education: Students' Reasoning about "High-Energy Bonds" and ATP. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.10.010115](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.10.010115)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Essay-response analysis of Zeke's answer, which clearly described the O-P bond as requiring energy to break despite being relatively weak. The authors note that "when he describes Justin being right, it is unclear whether he thinks both" that breaking requires energy and that breaking releases energy.

> "However, when he describes Justin being right, it is unclear whether he thinks both that it requires energy to break the bond and that breaking the bond releases energy."
