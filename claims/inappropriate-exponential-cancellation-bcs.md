## Evidence

### Ryan 2017

Ryan, Q. X., Wilcox, B. R., & Pollock, S. J. (2017). Student Difficulties with Boundary Conditions in Electrodynamics. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020126](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020126)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Exam solutions (N=90) coded at the Execution component. Of the 50 missing exponentials, 23 showed evidence of explicit cancellation, suggesting an execution error rather than a construction error.

> "Over a half of the solutions (56%, 50 out of 90) missed the exponential terms in their ﬁnal answer (See Fig. 4), which was problematic when the boundary has a non-zero location."

### Ryan 2017 (2)

Ryan, Q. X., Wilcox, B. R., & Pollock, S. J. (2017). Student Difficulties with Boundary Conditions in Electrodynamics. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020126](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020126)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Exam solutions (N=128) coded at the Construction component (C3). Of the 57 omitting boundary application, 46 also lost the exponential terms.

> "We also observed that nearly half of the students (57 out of 128) omitted this step of applying the equation at the boundary."
