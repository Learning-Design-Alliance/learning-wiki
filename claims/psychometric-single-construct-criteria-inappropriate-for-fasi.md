## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Carl E. Wieman and Wendy K. Adams 2014

Carl E. Wieman and Wendy K. Adams. (2014). On the Proper Use of Statistical Analyses; a Comment on "Evaluation of Colorado Learning Attitudes about Science Survey" by Douglas et al. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.10.020128](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.10.020128)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The authors' methodological argument, grounded in the distinction between summative tests maximizing discrimination between students and formative assessments of instruction. They argue statistical measures "make sense only within a limited context and where certain fundamental assumptions hold."

> "the standard acceptable ranges of values for these statistical tests w ere determined for tests with different goals and design criteria , and are not appropriate for FASIs ."

### Carl E. Wieman and Wendy K. Adams 2014 (2)

Carl E. Wieman and Wendy K. Adams. (2014). On the Proper Use of Statistical Analyses; a Comment on "Evaluation of Colorado Learning Attitudes about Science Survey" by Douglas et al. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.10.020128](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.10.020128)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The authors argue that particular aspects of student perceptions were reflected in only one or two CLASS statements, deliberately kept to avoid redundancy and optimize student time and attention. They compare single-construct filtering to assuming all cows are spherical.

> "These “orphan” statements accurately capture important and oft-expressed views, and hence we believe they are important to keep, although these views are not well correlated with responses to other statements"
