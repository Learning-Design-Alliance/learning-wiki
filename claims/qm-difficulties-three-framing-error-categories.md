## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a secondary analysis, published QM difficulties map into three error categories: mismatch between student and problem framing (displacement), inappropriate or absent frame shifting (transition), and insufficient resource activation within an appropriate frame (content). [→ Bahar Modir 2019](#bahar-modir-2019)

## Evidence

### Bahar Modir 2019

Bahar Modir, John D. Thompson, and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2019). Framing difficulties in quantum mechanics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020146](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020146)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Secondary analysis of published difficulty descriptions from four papers in PER venues. The team coded problem-statement frames and student response frames with two independent raters reaching "an agreement rate of > 90% for both kinds of coding." The unit of analysis is the difficulty, not the student, so counts index difficulties, not students.

> "We found three categories of error: mismatches between students’ framing and problem statement framing; inappropriate or absent shifting between frames; and insuﬃcient resource activation within an appropriate frame."
