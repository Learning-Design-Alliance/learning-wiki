## Evidence

### Andrew J. Mason 2016

Andrew J. Mason, Charles A. Bertram. (2016). Epistemic Impact on Group Problem Solving for Different Science Majors. US-China Education Review B. Originally published 2014. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.08666](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.08666)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Classification of free-response end-of-semester survey answers from the 39-student IPLS cohort into framework-oriented, performance-oriented, and vaguely-defined groups (N = 14, 19, 9 per Table 1). "the survey responses were f ound to be classifiable into one of the three groups."

> "Responses of the students on the end-of-semester survey showed that 37 of 39 students found the learning exercise useful as a whole.  The remaining two students left comments about the portions of the exercise they did find useful, and so could still be analyzed.  After transcription, the survey responses were f ound to be classifiable into one of the three groups."

### Andrew J. Mason 2016 (2)

Andrew J. Mason, Charles A. Bertram. (2016). Epistemic Impact on Group Problem Solving for Different Science Majors. US-China Education Review B. Originally published 2014. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.08666](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.08666)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cross-tabulation of response categories with majors (Table 1). The authors note in discussion that "most framework-oriented students were biology majors," while Table 1 shows all eight chemistry/computer science majors as performance-oriented.

> "Of interest is an indication from Table 1 that most framework-oriented students were biology majors, accompanied by two health science majors and two non-science majors."
