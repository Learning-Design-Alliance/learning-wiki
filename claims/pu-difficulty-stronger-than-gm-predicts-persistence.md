# Physics understanding difficulty is a stronger predictor of in-game persistence than game mechanics difficulty in Physics Playground

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In HLM analyses, one unit increase in physics understanding (PU) difficulty raised predicted persistence by about 11%, versus 3% for game mechanics (GM) difficulty, and PU's standardized coefficient (.19) exceeded GM's (.04). [→ Rahimi 2021](#rahimi-2021)

## Evidence

### Rahimi 2021

Rahimi, S., Shute, V., & Zhang, Q. (2021). The effects of game and student characteristics on persistence in educational games: A hierarchical linear modeling approach. International Journal of Technology in Education and Science (IJTES), 5(2), 141-165. https://doi.org/10.46328/ijtes.118

`q2 · i?`

HLM analysis of 3,803 problem-level observations nested in 137 students found PU difficulty significantly predicted persistence (t(3,628) = 10.39, p < .001), a 11% increase per unit; "Comparing the standardized coefficients, PU (.19) is more effective than GM (.04) in predicting students' persistence."

> "The average effect of the PU difficulty on persistence was also significant: = .10 (t(3,628) = 10.39, p < .001). This result means that with one unit increase in the problem's PU, students' persistence increases by 11% on average controlling for all other predictors."
