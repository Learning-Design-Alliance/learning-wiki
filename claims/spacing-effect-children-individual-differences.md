The commentary attributes to Knabe and Vlach (2020) the prediction that developmental individual differences will moderate the spacing effect in children, and their warning that little classroom research on spaced practice for children exists. Attributed second-hand; no data printed in the commentary.

> "They predict, for example, that individual differences in visual attention, memory capacity, prior knowledge, and metamemory abilities—all of which are rapidly developing, but at different rates, during the early years of life—will play a major role in determining whether the learning of an individual child or age group can profit from a spaced versus a massed schedule of study."
