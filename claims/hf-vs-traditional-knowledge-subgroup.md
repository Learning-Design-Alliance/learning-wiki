# Knowledge benefit of HF simulation is greater against traditional training (SMD = 0.71) than against LF simulation (SMD = 0.22)

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Subgroup analysis found a knowledge benefit for HF simulation compared with LF simulation (SMD = 0.22; 95% CI: 0.09–0.35, P = 0.001, I2 = 0%) and a larger benefit compared with traditional training (SMD = 0.71; 95% CI: 0.51–0.97, P = 0.00001, I2 = 29%). [→ Zeng Qin 2023](#zeng-qin-2023)

## Evidence

### Zeng Qin 2023

Zeng Qin, Wang Kai, Liu Wei-xin, Zeng Jiu-zhi, Li Xing-lan, Zhang Qing-feng, Ren Shang-qing, Xu Wen-ming. (2023). Efficacy of high-fidelity simulation in advanced life support training: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04654-x

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Subgroup analysis of the knowledge meta-analysis splitting comparators. Against LF simulation the estimate was "SMD = 0.22; 95% CI: 0.09–0.35, P = 0.001, I2 = 0%"; against traditional training "SMD = 0.71; 95% CI: 0.51–0.97, P = 0.00001, I2 = 29%" (reported in Supplement 2).

> "Subgroup analysis revealed that a benefit in knowledge with HF simulation compared with LF simulation (SMD = 0.22; 95% CI: 0.09–0.35, P = 0.001, I2 = 0%) and traditional training (SMD = 0.71; 95% CI: 0.51–0.97, P = 0.00001, I2 = 29%)."
