# Averaged code fractions corroborate the cluster result: men handle equipment more and women use laptops more in inquiry labs

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In inquiry labs, men spent a larger fraction of coded time handling equipment than women (13 ± 1% vs 9 ± 1%, p = 0.0097), and women spent a larger fraction on laptops or personal devices than men (31 ± 3% vs 23 ± 2%, p = 0.0082). [→ Katherine N. Quinn 2019](#katherine-n-quinn-2019)

## Evidence

### Katherine N. Quinn 2019

Katherine N. Quinn, Kathryn L. McGill, Michelle M. Kelley, Emily M. Smith, and N. G. Holmes (2019). Who does what now? How physics lab instruction impacts student behaviors. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1119/perc.2018.pr.quinn](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2018.pr.quinn)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive averages of code fractions in inquiry labs, broken down by gender (Table III: Equipment 9 ± 1% vs 13 ± 1%, p = 0.0097; Laptop 31 ± 3% vs 23 ± 2%, p = 0.0082), run to check whether they support the cluster analysis. All other code comparisons had p > 0.2. No effect size printed.
