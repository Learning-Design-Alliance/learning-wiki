Fernandes Pimenta Isac Davidson Santiago et al 2025¶

Fernandes Pimenta Isac Davidson Santiago et al. (2025). Effectiveness of non-technical skills training in intensive care units: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07037-6

q2 · i? · review · r3

Systematic review of learning-dimension outcomes from two studies with 383 participants. The review reports "small improvements" with very low certainty for the NTS set and no significant change in situational awareness learning in the controlled study.

Related Claims¶

  • NTS training programs in ICUs are associated with improved teamwork, situational awareness, leadership and communication behaviors, with varying evidence certainty — related
  • The overall certainty of evidence for educational programme effects on nurses' knowledge and practice is very low (GRADE), driven by bias, inconsistency and imprecision — related
  • NTS training for emergency department professionals is associated with consistent pre- to post-training improvements in knowledge, teamwork attitudes, and self-confidence (low to moderate certainty). — related
  • Educational programmes are consistently associated with statistically significant improvements in nurses' knowledge of diabetic foot care across nine quasi-experimental studies, with very low certainty of evidence — related
  • In a low-certainty meta-analysis of 44 studies with very high heterogeneity (I² = 98%), undergraduate health professional students in flipped classes score higher than those in traditional classes (SMD = 0.57) — related
  • The certainty of evidence for the effects of teaching strategies on critical thinking ranges from very low to moderate, with no strategy rated high. — related
  • Certainty of evidence for e-learning effects on oral health outcomes is very low or low per GRADE assessment — related
