# Concurrent sleep problems at age 11–13 do not increase the risk of poor academic performance after controlling for mental health problems

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Concurrent DIMS was associated with poor academic performance in the crude model (OR 2.11, 95% CI 1.50–2.97) but not in the fully adjusted model (OR 1.19, 95% CI 0.82–1.73). [→ Stormark KM 2019](#stormark-km-2019)

## Evidence

### Stormark KM 2019

Stormark KM, Fosse HE, Pallesen S, Hysing M (2019) The association between sleep problems and academic performance in primary school-aged children: Findings from a Norwegian longitudinal population-based study. PLoS ONE 14(11): e0224139. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0224139

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression in the same longitudinal cohort, contrasting the Concurrent DIMS group (DIMS at T2 only) with the No DIMS group. The crude association "OR 2.11; 95% CI 1.50–2.97" became non-significant after full adjustment.

> "Children with concurrent DIMS were in the crude model found to have an increased risk of poor academic performance at T2 compared to no-DIMS children (OR 2.11; 95% CI 1.50–2.97), but in the fully adjusted model, this association did not longer remain significant (OR 1.19; 95% CI 0.82–1.73)."
