# State assessment design decisions inevitably shape classroom instruction, often narrowing curriculum and teaching practices

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Educators report that state assessments drive decisions about instructional scope and sequence, classroom practices, content selection, and local assessment purchases. [→ Badrinarayan 2024](#badrinarayan-2024)

## Evidence

### Badrinarayan 2024

Badrinarayan, A. (2024). Design principles for instructionally relevant assessment systems. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/111.528

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Educator insights gathered by the Learning Policy Institute in conversations with teachers, principals, and instructional coaches about how state assessment systems influence teaching and learning (Table 1). One teacher reported: "I decide which units to do a lighter touch on or skip altogether based on what will be covered on the test."

> "I cannot finish my full [high-quality instructional materials] within a given school year, so I decide which units to do a lighter touch on or skip altogether based on what will be covered on the test."

### Badrinarayan 2024 (2)

Badrinarayan, A. (2024). Design principles for instructionally relevant assessment systems. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/111.528

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Lessons from the field section reporting what leaders and educators told the institute across nearly every state: "instruction shifts to meet and reflect, not exceed, what is represented on assessments," which the authors argue functionally lowers the bar for students most in need of support.

> "teachers share that the reality is that in most cases, instruction shifts to meet and reflect, not exceed, what is represented on assessments."
