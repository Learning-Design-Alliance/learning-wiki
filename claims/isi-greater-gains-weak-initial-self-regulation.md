# For first graders with weak initial self-regulation, ISI is associated with greater self-regulation gains, increasing with teachers' A2i use

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i0` negligible

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` Students with HTKS scores in the lowest quartile (fewer than 25 of 40 points) in ISI classrooms showed significantly greater self-regulation gains than peers in control classrooms, with the difference growing as teachers' A2i use increased (d = .19 at 70 minutes; d = .96 at 285 minutes). [→ Connor Carol McDonald 2010](#connor-carol-mcdonald-2010)

## Evidence

### Connor Carol McDonald 2010

Connor Carol McDonald, Ponitz Claire Cameron, Phillips Beth M., Travis Q. Monét, Glasney Stephanie, Morrison Frederick J. (2010). First graders' literacy and self-regulation gains: The effect of individualizing student instruction. Journal of School Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsp.2010.06.003

`q3 · i0`

HLM analysis of the cluster randomized trial modeled spring HTKS controlling for fall scores. Comparing three children with fall HTKS of 5, fitted spring scores were approximately 26 (control), 27 (70 min A2i), and 31 (285 min A2i), translating to effect sizes of d = .19 and d = .96.

> "for students with HTKS scores in the lowest quartile (obtaining fewer than 25 points out of 40), participating in the ISI intervention had a significant positive effect on their HTKS score gains compared to peers in control classrooms. As hypothesized, this effect depended on teachers' A2i use"
