# Students who gamed the system more frequently also talked on-task with the teacher or other students more frequently (r = 0.37).

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` There was a fairly strong relationship between gaming frequency and talking about the subject matter, r = 0.37, which remained after controlling for prior knowledge and general academic achievement (partial correlation = 0.36). [→ Baker et al. 2004](#baker-et-al-2004)

## Evidence

### Baker et al. 2004

Ryan Shaun Baker, Albert T. Corbett, Kenneth R. Koedinger, Angela Z. Wagner. (2004). Off-Task Behavior in the Cognitive Tutor Classroom: When Students "Game the System". CHI 2004, Vienna, Austria. https://telearn.hal.science/hal-00190176v1

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Observational finding from the same classroom study: gaming frequency and on-task conversation were strongly positively related, r = 0.37, "a fairly strong relationship" that survived controls for prior knowledge and achievement (partial correlation = 0.36).

> "there was a fairly strong relationship between a student's frequency of gaming the system and that student's frequency of talking to the teacher or another student about the subject matter, F(1,68)=10.52,p<0.01, r=0.37."
