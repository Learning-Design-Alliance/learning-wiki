## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Novice anesthesia residents who performed guided mental imagery of a cognitive aid with spaced repetitions scored higher on non-technical skills (BARS) than control residents in a 6-month simulated cardiopulmonary resuscitation assessment. [→ Levy Manon 2025](#levy-manon-2025)

## Evidence

### Levy Manon 2025

Levy Manon, Dopff Cécile, Audibert Gerard, Bouquet Antoine, Gurda Thomas, Sy Mohammed, Bleuze Pauline, Michelet Daphné. (2025). Effect of mental imagery using cognitive aids on the performance of novice anesthesiology residents during a simulated cardiopulmonary resuscitation. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07782-8

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Prospective comparative study in two French university hospitals: 24 first-year anesthesia residents, 12 per group, assessed individually in high-fidelity simulation 6 months after initial training; BARS scored by two blinded raters on video, with an intra-class coefficient of concordance of 0.69. The article reports a significant difference in non-technical clinical performance "in favor of mental imagery".

> "Our results suggest that repeating mental imagery based on a cognitive aid at a fixed interval over 6 months leads to improved non-technical skills in simulated cardiopulmonary resuscitation. We found a significant difference in the non-technical clinical performance in favor of mental imagery."
