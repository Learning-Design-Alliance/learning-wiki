## Evidence

### Álvaro Suárez 2026

Álvaro Suárez, Richard González. (2026). Students’ Validation Criteria for AI-Generated Physics Solutions. The Physics Teacher (accepted for publication). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2606.16830](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2606.16830)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Classroom activity in which 8 of 20 pre-service teachers solved the charged-rod problem fully or in part while 12 "did not reach a consistent result"; the printed difficulty was "setting up and evaluating the integrals". Table 1 cross-tabulates these outcomes with first judgments (6/8 and 10/12 leaned toward valid).

> "Eight of the 20 students solved the problem fully or in part, while the remaining 12 did not reach a consistent result. The main difficulty among these 12 students was setting up and evaluating the integrals required to construct the field by superposition."

### Álvaro Suárez 2026 (2)

Álvaro Suárez, Richard González. (2026). Students’ Validation Criteria for AI-Generated Physics Solutions. The Physics Teacher (accepted for publication). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2606.16830](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2606.16830)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative analysis of the 12 non-solvers' written justifications: the obstacle was "tracing the intermediate steps it provided", exemplified by a student who could not accept the solution because they did not understand the integration steps.

> "The main difficulty was not identifying a specific error in the AI -generated result but tracing the intermediate steps it provided. One student wrote, “Since I do not understand the integration steps, I cannot accept the solution as valid because I do not know whether they are correc t.”"
