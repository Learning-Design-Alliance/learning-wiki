# Statewide, Universal Pre-K is associated with a small, statistically significant gain in fourth-grade math scores (2.7 percent of a standard deviation) but not reading, and the statewide gains are not robust to specification choice

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the baseline D-D model, Universal Pre-K increased fourth grade math scores by 2.7 percent of a standard deviation (statistically significant) and reading scores by 0.8 percent of a standard deviation (not significant). [→ Maria D. Fitzpatrick 2008](#maria-d-fitzpatrick-2008)

## Evidence

### Maria D. Fitzpatrick 2008

Maria D. Fitzpatrick (2008). Starting School at Four: The Effect of Universal Pre-Kindergarten on Children's Academic Achievement. The B.E. Journal of Economic Analysis & Policy: Vol. 8: Iss. 1 (Advances), Article 46. http://www.bepress.com/bejeap/vol8/iss1/art46

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Baseline differences-in-differences regression (equation 1) with state and year fixed effects, standard errors clustered at the state level, estimated on State NAEP plausible scores with NCES sample weights. The math effect is significant; the reading effect is not. No standardized effect size beyond the percent-of-SD figures is printed.

> "Universal Pre-K increased fourth grade math and reading scores of eligible children by 2.7 and 0.8 percent of a standard deviation respectively, though only the estimated effect on math scores is statistically significant."

### Maria D. Fitzpatrick 2008 (2)

Maria D. Fitzpatrick (2008). Starting School at Four: The Effect of Universal Pre-Kindergarten on Children's Academic Achievement. The B.E. Journal of Economic Analysis & Policy: Vol. 8: Iss. 1 (Advances), Article 46. http://www.bepress.com/bejeap/vol8/iss1/art46

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The article's own robustness assessment: statewide estimates are sensitive to specification, while subgroup gains for disadvantaged rural and small-town children persist. The article advises caution in drawing conclusions for other groups.

> "Though estimates showing statewide gains in math test scores and the probability of being on-grade are not robust to specification choice, there are gains in the academic achievement of some groups of children."
