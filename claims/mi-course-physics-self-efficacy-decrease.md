## Evidence

### Dou 2018

Dou, R., Brewe, E., Potvin, G., Zwolak, J. P., & Hazari, Z. (2018). Understanding the development of interest and self-efficacy in active-learning undergraduate physics courses. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1080/09500693.2018.1488088](https://doi.org/10.1080/09500693.2018.1488088)

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Dependent samples t-tests on SOSESC-P survey responses from three MI courses (N = 221) at the beginning and end of the semester. The article reports a "4.85 points" drop, "t(220) = 5.21, p < .001, Cohen's d = 0.35", a 7.35% drop based on the score range 59-125.

> "Mean physics self-efficacy scores decreased significantly from pre to post by 4.85 points. This indicated that students reported lower physics self-efficacy at the end of the course than they did at the beginning [t(220) = 5.21, p < .001, Cohen's d = 0.35]."
