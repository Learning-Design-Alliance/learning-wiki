## Evidence

### Ma Xiang 2025

Ma Xiang, Pan Wei, Yu Xiao-ning. (2025). Evaluating AI-generated examination papers in periodontology: a comparative study with human-designed counterparts. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07706-6

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Classical test theory discrimination analysis in the randomized trial, comparing high- versus low-scoring student groups (top/bottom 27%) per item. The human paper's discrimination (0.49) exceeded the AI paper's (0.35), Mann–Whitney U = 210.0, p = 0.004, "r = 0.36".

> "The discrimination index was significantly higher in the human-designed test paper compared to the AI-generated test paper (Human = 0.49 [95% CI: 0.45–0.53]; AI = 0.35 [95% CI: 0.31–0.39], Mann–Whitney U test: U = 210.0, Z=-2.87, p = 0.004, r = 0.36"

### Başaranoğlu Mert 2025

Başaranoğlu Mert, Akbay Erdem, Erdem Erim. (2025). AI-generated questions for urological competency assessment: a prospective educational study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07202-x

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Comparison of discrimination indices between AI-generated validated questions and traditional board examination questions reported in the Results. The article prints "p < 0.001" for the contrast; no standardized effect size is reported.

> "Traditional board examination questions showed significantly higher discrimination indices (mean: 0.42, 95% CI: 0.37–0.47) compared to AI-generated questions (mean: 0.28, 95% CI: 0.24–0.32; p < 0.001), though difficulty indices were similar."
