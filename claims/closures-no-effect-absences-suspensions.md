# School closures had no effect on absences or suspension rates for affected students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` Absences and suspension rates of students affected by closures showed trends similar to comparison students after the merger; the suspension decline difference was not significant. [→ Gordon 2018](#gordon-2018)

`q3 i?` There was no effect on absence or suspension rates for students in closed or welcoming schools, compared to students from similar schools. [→ Molly F. Gordon 2017](#molly-f-gordon-2017)

## Evidence

### Gordon 2018

Gordon, M.F., de la Torre, M., Cowhy, J.R., Moore, P.T., Sartain, L.S., & Knight, D. (2018). School closings in Chicago: Staff and student experiences and academic outcomes. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/school-closings-chicago-staff-and-student-experiences-and-academic-outcomes

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Administrative-data comparison of days absent and suspension rates for affected versus similar unaffected students. The report finds closures "did not affect the attendance rates" and that the slightly more pronounced suspension decline for closed-school students showed differences that were not significant.

> "Consequently, school closures did not affect the attendance rates of these students because absences changed at similar rates districtwide."

### Molly F. Gordon 2017

Molly F. Gordon, Marisa de la Torre, Jennifer R. Cowhy, Paul T. Moore, Lauren Sartain, and David Knight. (2017). School Closings in Chicago: Staff and Student Experiences and Academic Outcomes. UChicago Consortium Research Report. http://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/school-closings-chicago-staff-and-student-experiences-and-academic-outcomes

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Quantitative analysis of administrative data comparing closed and welcoming school students' attendance and suspension outcomes after the closings to their prior outcomes and to students in similar schools. No effect size is printed, so no magnitude is reported.

> "There was no effect on absence or suspension rates for students in closed or welcoming schools."
