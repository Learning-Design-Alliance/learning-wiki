## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Tau Equivalent Reliability for the binary+computational pair was 0.21 and Pearson r was 0.14, indicating the two question types were not testing the same construct and proficiency in one was almost completely uncorrelated with the other. [→ Abid H. Mujtaba 2016](#abid-h-mujtaba-2016)

## Evidence

### Abid H. Mujtaba 2016

Abid H. Mujtaba. (2016). Analyzing student conceptual understanding of resistor networks using binary, descriptive, and computational questions. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1611.06583](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1611.06583)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Statistical analysis of the 28 students' scores: Cronbach's alpha values (Table II) and Pearson correlations (Table III) across the three question types. The article reports "ρT = 0 .21 and r = 0.14" for the binary-computational pair.

> "In particular while comparing the binary and computational question s ρT = 0 .21 and r = 0.14 showed, surprisingly, that the binary and computational quest ions were not testing the same construct at all"
