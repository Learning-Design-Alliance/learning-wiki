# Relevance Theory-guided reading teaching produced higher reading scores than traditional reading teaching over one school year

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` English majors taught with Relevance Theory applications (analyzing texts before reading, setting reading tasks, raising relevant questions, providing background information, inferring from context) scored higher on a reading examination than a traditionally taught comparison class, with a statistically significant difference (P = 0.032). [→ He 2018](#he-2018)

## Evidence

### He 2018

Ping He. (2018). On Reading Comprehension Teaching for English Majors under Relevance Theory. English Language Teaching, 11(1). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v11n1p46

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

One-school-year quasi-experiment with two intact classes of English majors at Nanchang Normal College; Class One taught with Relevance Theory applications, Class Two with traditional reading models. The experimental class mean rose from 70.2 to 73.4 while the control rose from 69.7 to 70.5; the printed P-value of 0.032 was reported as statistically significant.

> "the average score in the experimental class is 73.4 and 3.2 higher than its original score; while the controlling class gets 70.5 on average, just increasing by 0.8"
