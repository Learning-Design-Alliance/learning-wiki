## Evidence

### Palanichamy Swetha 2025

Palanichamy Swetha, Malhotra Kashish, Sin Yun S, Philip Nevil C, Arshad Amynta, Balakrishnan Harshin, Johal Haneesh, Kauser-Malik Saima, Khan Sahrish, Moodalbyle Shreyas, Elhariry Maiar, Narayanan Lakshmi, Kempegowda Punith, DEVI Collaboration. (2025). Co-creation and delivery of a simulation-based learning model for locally employed doctors: Findings from the SIMBA-LED study. Clinical Medicine 25(6). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinme.2025.100524

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Serial cross-sectional survey study of 46 LEDs who completed both pre- and post-surveys; Wilcoxon signed-rank tests showed a "significant increase in confidence post-SIMBA" for all simulated, non-simulated and all cases overall, each p < 0.001.

> "There was a significant increase in confidence post-SIMBA for all simulated (p < 0.001), non-simulated (p < 0.001), and all cases overall (p < 0.001)."

### Palanichamy Swetha 2025 (2)

Palanichamy Swetha, Malhotra Kashish, Sin Yun S, Philip Nevil C, Arshad Amynta, Balakrishnan Harshin, Johal Haneesh, Kauser-Malik Saima, Khan Sahrish, Moodalbyle Shreyas, Elhariry Maiar, Narayanan Lakshmi, Kempegowda Punith, DEVI Collaboration. (2025). Co-creation and delivery of a simulation-based learning model for locally employed doctors: Findings from the SIMBA-LED study. Clinical Medicine 25(6). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinme.2025.100524

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Headline results as printed in the abstract of the same study: "Confidence increased by 32.4%", with pre/post percentages of 46.2% vs 84.8% for simulated and 41.5% vs 66.5% for non-simulated scenarios, both p < 0.0001; no effect size printed.

> "Confidence increased by 32.4%, with greater improvements in simulated (46.2% vs 84.8%, p < 0.0001) than non-simulated (41.5% vs 66.5%, p < 0.0001) scenarios."
