# Non-YPT extracurricular activities are perceived as significantly more useful than RPC for inquiry-oriented skills, and YPT versus Non-YPT usefulness differs by skill type

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1`–`r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2022 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students rate Non-YPT extracurricular activities (e.g., Physics Olympiad, IJSO, EUSO) significantly more useful than RPC for designing experiments, data analysis, theoretical modelling, simulations, literature research, and critical assessment. [→ Boyka Aneva 2022](#boyka-aneva-2022)
`q2 i?` YPT activities are rated more useful than Non-YPT activities for interpreting data (p = 0,003), developing theoretical models (p = 0,000), numerical simulations (p = 0,000), and critical assessment (p = 0,039), while Non-YPT is rated more useful for solving close-ended problems (p = 0,010) and independent literature research (p = 0,000). [→ Boyka Aneva 2022 (2)](#boyka-aneva-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Boyka Aneva 2022

Boyka Aneva, Sergej Faletič, Mihály Hömöstrei, Péter Jenei, Isza Éva, František Kundracik, Assen Kyuldjiev, Thomas Lindner, Hynek Němec, Harald Puhr, and Martin Plesch. (2022). Development of Scientific Skills via IYPT: How does YPT participation lead to hard-skill development? ELTE University Budapest. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2212.09076](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2212.09076)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Student survey t-tests comparing RPC and Non-YPT activity usefulness; the table also shows no significant differences for high school mathematics, physics, close-ended problems, or cookbook experiments.

> "Non-YPT extracurricular activities had a significantly stronger impact on hard skills than RPC: “Designing experiments” (p=0,000), “Int erpreting experimental data, data analysis” (p=0,032), “Developing own theoretical model” (p=0,000)"

### Boyka Aneva 2022 (2)

Boyka Aneva, Sergej Faletič, Mihály Hömöstrei, Péter Jenei, Isza Éva, František Kundracik, Assen Kyuldjiev, Thomas Lindner, Hynek Němec, Harald Puhr, and Martin Plesch. (2022). Development of Scientific Skills via IYPT: How does YPT participation lead to hard-skill development? ELTE University Budapest. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2212.09076](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2212.09076)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Student survey t-tests comparing YPT and Non-YPT activities; the article reports the complementary result that Non-YPT activities are considered more useful for solving close-ended problems (p = 0,010) and independent research in scientific literature (p = 0,000).

> "We also observed that YPT-related activities are perceived more useful than other activities to develop skills, e.g.  “Interpreting experimental data, data analysis” (p = 0,003), “Developing own theoretical model” (p = 0,000), “Numerical simulations” (p = 0,000), and “Critical assessment of others’ results” (p = 0,039)."
