# Learners' judgments of learning track actual test performance in single-evaluation mode but not in joint-evaluation mode

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Experiment 2, JOLs were significantly lower in the audio+instructor format, while in Experiment 4 JOLs were significantly higher for the instructor format. [→ Wilson 2018](#wilson-2018)
`q3 i?` In Experiment 3 (joint evaluation), JOLs did not differ between the instructor format and other formats. [→ Wilson 2018 (2)](#wilson-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Wilson 2018

Wilson, K. E., Martinez, M., Mills, C., D'Mello, S., Smilek, D., & Risko, E. F. (2018). Instructor presence effect: Liking does not always lead to learning. Computers & Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2018.03.011

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In Experiment 2, participants provided a JOL estimating their expected test grade before the comprehension test. The article reports "JOLs were lower in the audio+instructor format", mirroring actual comprehension differences.

> "Participants’ JOLs were lower in the audio+instructor format, F(1, 205) =  4.07, p = 0.05, η2 = .02,"

### Wilson 2018 (2)

Wilson, K. E., Martinez, M., Mills, C., D'Mello, S., Smilek, D., & Risko, E. F. (2018). Instructor presence effect: Liking does not always lead to learning. Computers & Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2018.03.011

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In Experiment 3's joint-evaluation design, the JOL main effect was driven by higher estimates for the text-only segment relative to audio only; no pairwise comparison involving the instructor format was significant.

> "However, none of the planned pair-wise comparisons were significant, all t < 1.7, p>0.09."
