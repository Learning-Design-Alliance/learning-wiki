# American Indian and Alaska Native students often do not willingly participate in the question-answer activity that constitutes most classroom discussions, because direct questioning in front of peers contradicts community norms

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Numerous studies show AI/AN students often do not willingly participate in question-answer discussion formats. [→ Nelson-Barber 2015](#nelson-barber-2015)
`q2 i?` Direct questioning of individual students in front of peers contradicts a norm of allowing people to demonstrate knowledge when they choose, and implicitly places students in competition with peers. [→ Nelson-Barber 2015 (2)](#nelson-barber-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Nelson-Barber 2015

Nelson-Barber, S., & Trumbull, E. (2015). The Common Core Initiative, Education Outcomes, and American Indian/Alaska Native Students: Observations and Recommendations [a report from the Center on Standards & Assessment Implementation (CSAI) at WestEd]. San Francisco: WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED570362

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The report's narrative review of multiple studies (Leap, Philips, McCarty, Swisher & Deyhle, Trumbull et al.) finds that "American Indian and Alaska Native students often do not willingly participate in the question-answer activity that constitutes most discussions."

> "numerous studies have shown that American Indian and Alaska Native students often do not willingly participate in the question-answer activity that constitutes most discussions"

### Nelson-Barber 2015 (2)

Nelson-Barber, S., & Trumbull, E. (2015). The Common Core Initiative, Education Outcomes, and American Indian/Alaska Native Students: Observations and Recommendations [a report from the Center on Standards & Assessment Implementation (CSAI) at WestEd]. San Francisco: WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED570362

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The report explains the mechanism: direct questioning "contradicts a norm of allowing people to demonstrate their knowledge when they decide to do so" and implicitly places students in competition with peers (Philips, 1983; Swisher & Deyhle, 1992).

> "Direct questioning of students in front of peers is not normally done in AI/AN communities because it contradicts a norm of allowing people to demonstrate their knowledge when they decide to do so"
