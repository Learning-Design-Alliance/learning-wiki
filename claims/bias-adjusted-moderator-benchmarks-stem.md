# Bias-adjusted posterior-predictive benchmarks for STEM moderator effect sizes (0.18, 0.38, 0.64 at the 25th, 50th, 75th percentiles) fall below both Cohen's conventional thresholds and raw empirical quartiles

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Empirical quartiles of observed moderator effect magnitudes were 0.29, 0.53, and 0.77, while publication-bias-adjusted posterior-predictive quartiles shifted downward to 0.18, 0.38, and 0.64. [→ Bicakci 2026](#bicakci-2026)
`q3 i?` Pronounced jumps in the raw distribution around Cohen's conventional thresholds disappeared after publication-bias adjustment, suggesting these thresholds may function as implicit reporting incentives. [→ Bicakci 2026 (2)](#bicakci-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Bicakci 2026

Bicakci, M., Heller, F., Stoeger, H., & Ziegler, A. (2026). From RAMSing to Rigor: Improving Moderator Analysis in STEM Education Meta-Research. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10172-1

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Benchmarking synthesis of 1,786 absolute g values from 89 meta-analyses (distribution right-skewed, mean 0.59, median 0.53) contrasted with 10,000 posterior-predictive draws from RoBMA-PSMA (Monte Carlo SE = 0.003).

> "The observed data yielded quartiles of 0.29, 0.53, and 0.77, whereas the fully adjusted posterior- predictive distribution shifted downward to 0.18, 0.38, and 0.64."

### Bicakci 2026 (2)

Bicakci, M., Heller, F., Stoeger, H., & Ziegler, A. (2026). From RAMSing to Rigor: Improving Moderator Analysis in STEM Education Meta-Research. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10172-1

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Histogram comparison of the raw observed distribution (Fig. 5) against the bias-adjusted posterior-predictive distribution (Fig. 6); the authors read the raw discontinuities as possible secondary RAMSing driven by magnitude thresholds.

> "Notably, the pronounced jumps around Cohen’s (1988) conventional thresholds in the raw distri bution (see Fig. 5) disappeared after publication-bias adjustment (Fig. 6)."
