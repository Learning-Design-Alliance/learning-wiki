# Students rated the e-learning module significantly higher than existing online resources on enjoyment, guidance and overall value, and ranked it the most useful resource for learning leukaemia

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Morgulis Yuri 2012

Morgulis Yuri, Kumar Rakesh K, Lindeman Robert, Velan Gary M. (2012). Impact on learning of an e-learning module on leukaemia: a randomised controlled trial. BMC Medical Education 12:36. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-36

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Five-point Likert scale questionnaire data compared between groups with Mann-Whitney U tests; "their rating of each aspect" including enjoyment, guide to study and overall value was significantly higher for the module (all P < 0.001).

> "The Study group’s online evaluations of the e-learning module were overwhelmingly positive, and their rating of each aspect (including enjoyment, guide to study and overall value for learning) was significantly higher compared with evaluations of the alternative e-learning resources by the Control group (all P < 0.001, Mann-Whitney U tests)"

### Morgulis Yuri 2012 (2)

Morgulis Yuri, Kumar Rakesh K, Lindeman Robert, Velan Gary M. (2012). Impact on learning of an e-learning module on leukaemia: a randomised controlled trial. BMC Medical Education 12:36. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-36

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Ranking question in the evaluation questionnaire: the module was "the most useful resource for learning about leukaemia" against lectures, tutorials, private study and clinical experience; the Control group's resources ranked a median third of five.

> "Of particular note, the Study group found the module to be the most useful resource for learning about leukaemia, compared with lectures, tutorials, private study and clinical experience, whereas the median ranking of the e-learning resources available to the Control group was third out of five."
