## Evidence

### Sengupta 2016

Sengupta, P., & Clark, D. (2016). Playing Modeling Games in the Science Classroom: The Case for Disciplinary Integration. Educational Technology, 56(3), 16–22. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1607.05094](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1607.05094)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

In the same SURGE NextG grapher vs. no grapher study, the article reports that grapher students reasoning about the meaning of distance–speed–acceleration relationships showed the highest gains of all, reported descriptively with no effect size printed.

> "Furthermore, the grapher students who framed their plan in a variable-focused way (e.g., by explicitly reasoning about the meaning of the relationships between distance, speed and acceleration evident in the graph) rather than superficially (e.g., by focusing only on the shape of the graph) showed the highest gains of all."
