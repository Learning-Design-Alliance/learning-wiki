## Evidence

### C. Efthimiou and R. Llewellyn 2004

C. Efthimiou and R. Llewellyn. (2004). "Physics in Films" A New Approach to Teaching Science. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0404064](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0404064)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of exam averages between two large sections of about 295 students each, taught by the same instructor on the same topics, one traditional and one Physics in Films. Table 1 prints averages of 49.3, 65.3, 58.2, 59.4 (traditional) versus 74.9, 67.7, 75.7, 72.8 (Physics in Films); no statistical test is reported.

> "The exam scores distribution in two classes of about the same size (2 95 students each) taught by the same instructor, one in traditional lecture mode, the other Physics in Films mode and covering the same topics are quite dramatically diﬀerent"

### Costas Efthimiou and Ralph Llewellyn 2004

Costas Efthimiou and Ralph Llewellyn. (2004). A revitalization of the traditional course by avatars of Hollywood in the physics classroom. Originally published 2003. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0404064](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0404064)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Quasi-experimental comparison of two sections of identical size taught by the same instructor in the same auditorium with similar exams; Table 5 reports Fall 2001 traditional averages of 49.34, 65.33, 58.18, 59.44 versus Fall 2002 Physics in Films averages of 74.92, 67.68, 75.68, 72.82. The authors call the differences "both dramatic and signiﬁcant" but print no test statistic or effect size.

> "we list the performance of UCF Physical Science students in two a lmost identical classes, one taught in the traditional way and one taught using mov ies. The important parameters in both classes are identical: the classes have the same size"
