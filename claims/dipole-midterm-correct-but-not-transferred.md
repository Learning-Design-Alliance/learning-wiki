## Evidence

### Charles Baily and Cecilia Astolfi 2014

Charles Baily and Cecilia Astolfi. (2014). Student Reasoning About the Divergence of a Vector Field. [doi:10.1119/perc.2014.pr.004](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.004)

`q3 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Midterm exam question asking students to state where the divergence and curl of an electric dipole field are zero or non-zero and explain reasoning. The article reports "59% of students received full credit" and "13% received zero points".

> "59% of students received full credit, and most of the rest gained some partial credit, primarily for correct responses with incomplete reasoning; 13% received zero points."

### Charles Baily and Cecilia Astolfi 2014 (2)

Charles Baily and Cecilia Astolfi. (2014). Student Reasoning About the Divergence of a Vector Field. [doi:10.1119/perc.2014.pr.004](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.004)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Authors' interpretation of the contrast between midterm and CURrENT performance: students understood divergence in the Gauss' law context but did not employ an epistemological framing that accessed the relevant knowledge in the wire context.

> "many did not access this same resource when later considering the current density inside a wire, even though they had all seen a similar homework problem"
