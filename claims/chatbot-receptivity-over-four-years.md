# Students remained receptive to chatbot communication over four years, with annual opt-out rates below 4% and average interactive-campaign engagement near 5%

## Evidence

### Mata 2026

Mata, Catherine, Emily Russell, and Lindsay C. Page. (2026). Sustaining AI-Enabled Student Support: A Four-Year Implementation and Impact Study. (EdWorkingPaper: 26-1409). Annenberg Institute at Brown University. https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1409

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive analysis of monthly opt-out rates from campaign-level chatbot records for AY2018-19 through AY2022-23, plotted in Figure 3. The article reports annual opt-out rates never exceed 4%, concentrated in September-November; an April 2023 campaign that explicitly mentioned opting out led about 2% of its targeted group to opt out.

> "Cumulatively, as shown in Figure 3, annual opt-out rates do not exceed 4%. We also observe that most students who opt out do so early in the fall semester (September-November), with very few opting out thereafter."

### Mata 2026 (2)

Mata, Catherine, Emily Russell, and Lindsay C. Page. (2026). Sustaining AI-Enabled Student Support: A Four-Year Implementation and Impact Study. (EdWorkingPaper: 26-1409). Annenberg Institute at Brown University. https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1409

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive analysis of response rates to interactive campaigns, aggregated by campaign domain and theme from campaign-level records. The article reports an average engagement rate of approximately 5%, with administrative campaigns exceeding 20% average response in AY2022-23 and a spring check-in campaign reaching nearly 46%.

> "Among campaigns that invite some level of engagement, the average engagement rate is approximately 5% (see Figure 4)."
