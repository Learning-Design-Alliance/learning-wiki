# After one year, DC's voucher program had no statistically significant effect, positive or negative, on public school test-score gains

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` None of 16 OLS regressions of public school Stanford-9 gains on voucher-competition measures (distance to nearest voucher school; number of voucher schools within one mile) produced a statistically significant finding. [→ Greene 2007](#greene-2007)

## Evidence

### Greene 2007

Greene, J. P., & Winters, M. A. (2007). An evaluation of the effect of DC's voucher program on public school achievement and racial integration after one year. Catholic Education: A Journal of Inquiry and Practice, 11(1), 83-101. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1005985

`q2 · i?`

Observational school-level analysis of 151 DC public schools using OLS regressions of one-year Stanford-9 test-score gains (2003-04 to 2004-05) on two proximity-based competition measures, controlling for percent White, free/reduced-price lunch, and baseline scores. The article reports "no statistically significant finding" in any of the 16 analyses; no effect sizes are printed.

> "None of our 16 regression analyses produces a statistically significant finding for the chosen measure of voucher competition—either distance to the nearest participating voucher school or the number of competing voucher schools within a one-mile radius (see Table 1)."
