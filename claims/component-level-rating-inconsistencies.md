# Principals rated some instruction components inconsistently, rating communicating with students, using assessment in instruction, and organizing physical space lower than observers, and student engagement higher

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On three components principals consistently gave lower ratings than external observers (communicating with students, using assessment in instruction, organizing physical space), while on engaging students in learning they consistently assigned higher ratings. [→ Lauren Sartain 2010](#lauren-sartain-2010)

### Lauren Sartain 2010

Lauren Sartain, Sara Ray Stoelinga, and Emily Krone. (2010). Findings From the First Year of the Excellence in Teaching Project in Chicago Public Schools: Rethinking Teacher Evaluation. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. http://ccsr.uchicago.edu/publications/Joyce_TE_yr1_finaldoc.pdf

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Matched-observation analysis in the year-one pilot found four challenging components: "Principals rated the following areas of teaching lower: communicating with students, using assessment in instruction, and organizing physical space," and were more likely to rate teachers higher on student engagement in learning than observers.
