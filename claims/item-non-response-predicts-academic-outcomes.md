# Item non-response predicts academic outcomes: a standard deviation increase is associated with roughly 0.2 standard deviation decreases in GPA, SAT attempting, end-of-year test scores, and college enrollment

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2020 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Controlling for cognitive ability and demographics, higher item non-response is significantly associated with lower high school GPA, lower probability of attempting the SAT, lower SAT scores, lower end-of-year math and reading scores, and lower probability of college enrollment one year after graduation. [→ Zamarro Gema 2020](#zamarro-gema-2020)

## Evidence

### Zamarro Gema 2020

Zamarro Gema, Nichols Malachi, Duckworth Angela L., D'Mello Sidney K. (2020). Validation of survey effort measures of grit and self-control in a sample of high school students. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0235396

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear regression and linear probability models (Table 4) predicting nine academic outcomes from item non-response with KBIT, age, ethnicity, gender, FRL, SPED, ELL, and income controls; the printed standardized GPA coefficient is "[-0.196]" and models used 392 to 458 observations.

> "we found that a standard deviation increase in item non-response led to an almost 0.2 standard deviation decrease in high school GPA, a 0.2 standard deviations decrease in the probability of attempting the SAT"
