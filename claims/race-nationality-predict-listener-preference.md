# Asian-American, underserved American, and international students are more likely than white American peers to prefer listening roles in peer discussions

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Asian-American students were 2.3 times less likely than white American peers to prefer leader/explainer and 2.4 times less likely to prefer collaborator, relative to the listener role. [→ Eddy SL 2015](#eddy-sl-2015)
`q2 i?` Predicted probability of preferring the listener role rose from 3.9% for white students to 13.4% (Asian-American), 21.6% (underserved American), and 32.7% (international). [→ Eddy SL 2015](#eddy-sl-2015)

## Evidence

### Eddy SL 2015

Eddy SL, Brownell SE, Thummaphan P, Lan M-C, Wenderoth MP. (2015). Caution, Student Experience May Vary: Social Identities Impact a Student's Experience in Peer Discussions. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-05-0108

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Study I model-averaged multinomial regression on preferred-role self-reports (n = 297). The article states Asian-American students "were more likely to prefer to be listeners than any other role" with the printed odds and p values, and reports parallel but not all-significant patterns for underserved and international students.

> "Asian-American students relative to their white American peers were more likely to prefer to be listeners than any other role (2.3 times less likely to prefer to be a leader/explainer, p = 0.037; 2.4 times less likely to prefer to be a collaborator, p = 0.041; 8.6 times less likely to prefer to be a recorder, p = 0.009)."
