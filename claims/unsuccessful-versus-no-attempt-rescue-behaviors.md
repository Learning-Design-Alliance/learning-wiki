## Evidence

### Rochlen LR 2017

Rochlen LR, Housey M, Gannon I, Mitchell S, Rooney DM, Tait AR, Engoren M. (2017). Assessing anesthesiology residents’ out-of-the-operating-room (OOOR) emergent airway management. BMC Anesthesiology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12871-017-0387-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Simulation checklist behavior for the unsuccessful-intubation group (n = 22), rated in real time by two independent raters; percentages as printed, no effect size reported.

> "Of the 22 residents, who were unsuccessful with intubation (U), 68% followed the DAA appropriately (i.e., completed any appropriate subsequent step). Despite an initial unsuccessful attempt, 3 (14%) residents made a second attempt with a different blade, 2 (9%) residents considered an alternative device, 10 (46%) placed a laryngeal mask airway (LMA) and 10 (46%) prepared for cricothyroidotomy."

### Rochlen LR 2017 (2)

Rochlen LR, Housey M, Gannon I, Mitchell S, Rooney DM, Tait AR, Engoren M. (2017). Assessing anesthesiology residents’ out-of-the-operating-room (OOOR) emergent airway management. BMC Anesthesiology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12871-017-0387-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Simulation checklist behavior for the never-attempted group (n = 19). Rescue steps were less frequent than in the unsuccessful group; percentages as printed, no effect size reported.

> "Of the 19 residents who did not attempt an intubation (NA), 74% followed the DAA appropriately. The majority (17; 89%) attempted initial bag mask ventilation; only 6 residents (31%) considered LMA placement, and 5 residents (26%) initiated cricothyroidotomy."
