Evidence¶

Elaine M. Allensworth and Stuart Luppescu 2016¶

Elaine M. Allensworth and Stuart Luppescu. (2016). Why do students get good grades, or bad ones? The influence of the teacher, class, school, and student. University of Chicago Consortium on School Research working paper. https://consortium.uchicago.edu

q3 · i? · associational · r3

The authors combine coefficients from Tables 2b and 2c into schedule-level illustrations: one-quarter AP classes with high-achieving peers lower GPA by 0.33 points, and four advanced math/science courses bring the total to 0.39, so "a very demanding schedule could make a difference of about 0.40 GPA points."

"taking only Honors classes with high-achieving peers (1 standard deviation above the mean) would likely reduce a student’s core course GPA by 0.25 of a GPA point (the class average effect of -0.173 plus the Honors class effect of -0.075)."
