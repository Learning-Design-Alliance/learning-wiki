# Parent spatial language is concurrently associated with child spatial language at 48 months, explaining substantial variance beyond controls

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Adding parent spatial language variables raised explained variance in child spatial language quantity to 80.3% and quality to 57.0%. [→ Farran 2025](#farran-2025)

## Evidence

### Farran 2025

Farran, E. K., Fink, E., Hughes, C., Gilligan-Lee, K. A., & The NewFAMS team. (2025). Limited sex differences in spatial language in parent-child dyads. Learning and Instruction, 95, 102006. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.102006

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional multiple regression (N = 107) with controls at step 1 and parent spatial language at step 2. Model 2 explained "80.3% of variance in children's spatial language quantity" and "57.0% of variance in children's spatial language quality"; no child-sex interactions were retained.

> "Model 2 (with step 2 parent spatial language variables
now added) explained 80.3% of variance in children's spatial language
quantity at 48 months (F(10, 96) = 44.11, p < .001) and 57.0% of variance in
children's spatial language quality at 48 months (F(10, 96)
= 15.04, p < .001)."

### Farran 2025 (2)

Farran, E. K., Fink, E., Hughes, C., Gilligan-Lee, K. A., & The NewFAMS team. (2025). Limited sex differences in spatial language in parent-child dyads. Learning and Instruction, 95, 102006. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.102006

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the final cross-sectional models (Table 2), father spatial language quantity (β = 0.780, p < .001) and mother quality (β = .305, p < .001) predicted child quantity; mother quality (β = .574) and father quality (β = .262, p = .022) predicted child quality.

> "for child spatial language quantity, mother spatial lan-
guage quality and father spatial language quantity were significant
predictors, whilst for child spatial language quality, both mother and
father spatial language quality were predictors (see Table 2)."
