### Amy E. Wenzel and David C. Rubin 1996

Amy E. Wenzel and David C. Rubin. (1996). One Hundred Years of Forgetting: A Quantitative Description of Retention. Psychological Review. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.103.4.734

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Narrative review of age-comparison studies, noting most researchers report no age difference or at most small differences. The counterevidence from Brainerd et al. (1990) required five conditions including recall tests, less memorable material, long retention intervals, learning to criterion, and a Markov model to equate initial learning.

> "Given these restrictions, the conservative conclusion to the existing data is that retention, as opposed to initial learning, is not affected by age over the range of ages tested or is at most affected under only specific conditions and assumptions."
