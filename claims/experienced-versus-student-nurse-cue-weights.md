# Experienced nurses and student nurses differ in the cues they emphasise, with experienced nurses weighting consciousness level and systolic blood pressure more and respiration rate less in paper cases

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In paper cases experienced nurses paid more attention to consciousness level and systolic blood pressure and less attention to respiration rate than students; in simulations they paid more attention to consciousness level and systolic blood pressure and less to temperature. [→ Yang Huiqin 2016](#yang-huiqin-2016)

## Evidence

### Yang Huiqin 2016

Yang Huiqin, Thompson Carl. (2016). Capturing judgement strategies in risk assessments with improved quality of clinical information: How nurses’ strategies differ from the ecological model. BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-016-0243-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Wilcoxon rank-sum comparisons of relative cue weights between 34 experienced nurses and 63 students in paper cases. Experienced nurses weighted consciousness level (Mdn 0.259 vs 0.240, P = 0.02) and systolic blood pressure (0.065 vs 0.054, P = 0.048) more, and respiration rate less (P = 0.005).

> "In paper cases experienced nurses paid more attention to consciousness level (median (Mdn) 0.259) than students (Mdn 0.240), z = −2.39, P = 0.02 and more attention to systolic blood pressure (Mdn 0.065) than students (Mdn 0.054), z = −1.97, P = 0.048."
