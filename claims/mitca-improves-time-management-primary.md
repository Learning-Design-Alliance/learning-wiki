# Twelve weeks of MITCA homework prescription produced higher time management than traditional homework in 5th–6th graders

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3` · `i0` negligible

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` Students whose teachers prescribed homework with the MITCA method scored significantly higher on behavioral and time management at post-test than students receiving traditional homework (d = 0.15). [→ Tania Vieites 2024](#tania-vieites-2024)

## Evidence

### Tania Vieites 2024

Tania Vieites, Eleftheria Gonida, Fátima Díaz-Freire, Susana Rodríguez, Antonio Valle. (2024). Effects of a homework implementation method (MITCA) on self-regulation of learning. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-024-09376-z

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r1`

Post-test independent-samples t-tests comparing the MITCA experimental group with the traditional-homework control group in a twelve-week intervention with 533 fifth and sixth graders. The article reports "behavioral and time management (t = 2.245, p < 0.05, d = 0.15)" in favor of the experimental group.

> "significant differences between the two groups in the post-test were found in behavioral and time management (t = 2.245, p < 0.05, d = 0.15), environment management (t = 3.243, p < 0.01, d = 0.22) and self-regulatory deficit (t = -2.557, p < 0.05, d = 0.17) in favor of the experimental group"
