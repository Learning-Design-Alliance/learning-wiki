# Undergraduate and graduate introductory QISE courses differ significantly in coverage only for three closely related advanced topics (Shannon entropy, von Neumann entropy, density matrices/mixed states), with graduate courses covering them more

## Evidence

### Josephine C. Meyer 2024

Josephine C. Meyer, Gina Passante, Steven J. Pollock, Bethany R. Wilcox. (2024). Introductory quantum information science coursework at US institutions: Content coverage. arXiv. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1140/epjqt/s40507-024-00226-0](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjqt/s40507-024-00226-0)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey comparison of NU = 46 undergraduate-listed and NG = 39 graduate-listed courses (22 both) using Rao-Scott modified χ2 with Hochberg-Bonferroni adjustment. Table 2 prints Shannon entropy 26% vs 49% (p <0.001), density matrices 63% vs 82% (p = 0.002), von Neumann entropy 26% vs 44% (p = 0.011).

> "Only for the three advanced topics shown in Table 2 were statistically significant differences detected between graduate and undergraduate courses (first-order Rao-Scott 3 modified χ2, pad j< 0.05 with Hochberg-Bonferroni adjustment 4 for multiple statistical tests)."

### Josephine C. Meyer 2024 (2)

Josephine C. Meyer, Gina Passante, Steven J. Pollock, Bethany R. Wilcox. (2024). Introductory quantum information science coursework at US institutions: Content coverage. arXiv. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1140/epjqt/s40507-024-00226-0](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjqt/s40507-024-00226-0)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The same survey analysis finds that outside the three advanced topics, "the percentage of undergraduate and graduate courses covering each topic were within an absolute difference of 12%", supporting generalization of the core list across levels.

> "Otherwise, the percentage of undergraduate and graduate courses covering each topic were within an absolute difference of 12%, giving us confidence that our findings in Sec. 3.1 generalize across both graduate and undergraduate levels."
