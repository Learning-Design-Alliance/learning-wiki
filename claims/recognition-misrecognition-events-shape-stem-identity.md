## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Preliminary phenomenological interviews found that students, regardless of major, recalled recognition and misrecognition events in STEM contexts that meaningfully shaped their STEM identity, with recognition events having a positive effect and misrecognition events a negative effect. ## Evidence

### Awsaf 2026

Awsaf, A.A., Giansanti, N., Sunbury, S., & Dou, R. (2025, March). Concurrent phenomenological analysis of STEM career aspirations in underrepresented youth: Role of experiences and identity [Presentation]. 2025 NARST Annual International Conference, National Harbor, MD. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2607.06035](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.06035)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Preliminary phenomenological analysis of the study's first five interviews with purposefully selected STEM and non-STEM aspirants at a Florida public university; the authors state that students "recall particular recognition and misrecognition events in STEM contexts that meaningfully shaped their STEM identity."

> "Our preliminary analysis suggests that undergraduate students, regardless of major, recall particular recognition and misrecognition events in STEM contexts that meaningfully shaped their STEM identity."
