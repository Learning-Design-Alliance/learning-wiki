## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across audited Australian medical schools, lectures were the most frequent method used to teach intellectual disability content in compulsory units, and method use varied markedly across schools. [→ Trollor JN 2018](#trollor-jn-2018)

## Evidence

### Trollor JN 2018

Trollor JN, Eagleson C, Turner B, Tracy J, Torr JJ, Durvasula S, Iacono T, Cvejic RC, Lennox N. (2018). Intellectual disability content within tertiary medical curriculum: how is it taught and by whom? BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1286-z

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Two-phase audit (2013-2014) of intellectual disability teaching across Australian medical schools; course coordinators from 12 participating schools (60% response rate) completed a staff survey, with descriptive analysis of 42 compulsory and 8 elective units. The audit found "the most frequent method was lectures (67% of compulsory units included some form of lecture)".

> "For compulsory units, the most frequent method was lectures (67% of compulsory units included some form of lecture), and the least frequent were other methods, such as a clinical assessment and a clinical day (each 2%)."
