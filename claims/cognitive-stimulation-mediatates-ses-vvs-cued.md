## Evidence

### Rosen Maya L. 2021

Rosen Maya L., Lurie Lucy A., Sambrook Kelly A., Meltzoff Andrew N., McLaughlin Katie A. (2021). Neural mechanisms underlying the income-achievement gap: The role of the ventral visual stream. Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2021.101025

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bootstrapped mediation analysis (Hayes) in the 62-child sample tested cognitive stimulation as mediator of the SES–VVS link, controlling for violence exposure. The article reports "a marginal indirect effect of income on activation in the right VVS during cued attention through cognitive stimulation (95% CI: [-.010,.183], 90% CI: [.002,.160]"; the 95% CI includes zero.

> "We found a marginal indirect effect of income on activation in the right VVS during cued attention through cognitive stimulation (95% CI: [-.010,.183], 90% CI: [.002,.160]; Table 4). No other significant indirect effects were found."

### Rosen Maya L. 2021 (2)

Rosen Maya L., Lurie Lucy A., Sambrook Kelly A., Meltzoff Andrew N., McLaughlin Katie A. (2021). Neural mechanisms underlying the income-achievement gap: The role of the ventral visual stream. Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2021.101025

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

ROI regressions in the same sample tested cognitive stimulation (HOME composite of language stimulation and caregiver engagement) against VVS activation. Right VVS during cued attention was "trend-level after FDR-correction (β = 0.238, p=.104"; no association was found during memory-guided attention.

> "The association between cognitive stimulation and activation in right VVS during cued attention was significant but become trend-level after FDR-correction (β = 0.238, p=.104; Fig. 5B) and was also marginal in left VVS (β = 0.202, p=.104). There was no significant association between cognitive stimulation and activation in left or right VVS during memory-guided attention (β = 0.095, p=.440; β = 0.138, p=.440)."
