## Evidence

### Le-Thi 2017

Le-Thi, D., Rodgers, M. P. H., & Pellicer-Sánchez, A. (2017). Teaching Formulaic Sequences in an English-Language Class: The Effects of Explicit Instruction Versus Coursebook Instruction. TESL Canada Journal/Revue TESL du Canada, 34(3), 111–139. http://dx.doi.org/10.18806/tesl.v34i3.1276

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated measures ANOVA on gain scores in the experimental conditions (n = 40) comparing low-salience (k = 7), low-salience-plus (k = 5), and explicitly taught (k = 42) items. The authors report "the low-salience-plus items did not score any higher than the low-salience ones," with significant main effects of exposure type (e.g., cloze pre-post F(1.58, 76) = 17.27, p < .001, partial η² = .312).

> "the explicitly taught items were always learned significantly better than the non-explicitly-taught items, regardless of whether the latter belonged to the low-salience or the low-salience-plus categories. Furthermore, the low-salience-plus items did not score any higher than the low-salience ones"

### Le-Thi 2017 (2)

Le-Thi, D., Rodgers, M. P. H., & Pellicer-Sánchez, A. (2017). Teaching Formulaic Sequences in an English-Language Class: The Effects of Explicit Instruction Versus Coursebook Instruction. TESL Canada Journal/Revue TESL du Canada, 34(3), 111–139. http://dx.doi.org/10.18806/tesl.v34i3.1276

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Paired sample t-tests in the control group (n = 20) comparing gain scores of low-salience (k = 41) and low-salience-plus (k = 13) items across cloze and translation pre-post and pre-delayed comparisons. The authors report "no significant difference emerged in any of the testing conditions" (t values ranged from -.14 to 1.79).

> "Table 10 presents the results of paired sample t-tests comparing the gain scores of low-salience and low-salience-plus items. As can be seen in the table, no significant difference emerged in any of the testing conditions."
