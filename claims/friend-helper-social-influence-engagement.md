# Friends' academic engagement socially influences adolescents' behavioral and emotional engagement; helpers influence behavioral engagement more clearly than emotional

## Evidence

### Shin 2023

Huiyoung Shin. (2023). The co-evolution of friend and help relationships and their different relationship formation and social influence. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-43346-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel SAOM influence results for the 20-classroom sample: adolescents became more similar to their friends in both "behavioral (η = 1.81, SD = 0.62, p > 0.99)" and "emotional ( η = 1.42, SD = 0.60, p > 0.99)" academic engagement over the semester.

> "We found evidence for the average similarity (social influence) effect being positive for both behavioral (η = 1.81, SD = 0.62, p > 0.99) and emotional ( η = 1.42, SD = 0.60, p > 0.99) academic engagement in friend networks."

### Shin 2023 (2)

Huiyoung Shin. (2023). The co-evolution of friend and help relationships and their different relationship formation and social influence. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-43346-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same SAOM analysis found helper influence positive for behavioral engagement (η = 2.53, p > 0.99) and only weak for emotional engagement (η = 1.06, p = 0.97, below the stated ≥ 0.975 threshold).

> "In terms of help networks, we found evidence for the average similarity effects being positive for behavioral (η = 2.53, SD = 0.74, p > 0.99) academic engagement and weak evidence for emotional (η = 1.06, SD = 0.59, p = 0.97) academic engagement."
