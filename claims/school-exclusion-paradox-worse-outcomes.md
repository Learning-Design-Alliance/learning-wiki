# School exclusionary policies paradoxically worsen dropout, delinquency, and achievement

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` School district policies emphasizing suspension and expulsion to address attendance problems lead paradoxically to more dropout, delinquency, academic lag, and juvenile justice involvement. [→ Kearney CA 2019](#kearney-ca-2019)

## Evidence

### Kearney CA 2019

Kearney CA, Gonzálvez C, Graczyk PA and Fornander MJ (2019) Reconciling Contemporary Approaches to School Attendance and School Absenteeism: Toward Promotion and Nimble Response, Global Policy Review and Implementation, and Future Adaptability (Part 1). Front. Psychol. 10:2222. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02222

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Suh et al. (2007), Stone and Stone (2011), and Monahan et al. (2014), that exclusionary policies backfire. Exclusion is also disproportionately assigned to low-income and diverse students. No effect sizes printed.

> "school district policies that emphasize suspension and expulsion to address school attendance problems lead paradoxically to more dropout, delinquency, lag in academic achievement, and student involvement with the juvenile justice system"
