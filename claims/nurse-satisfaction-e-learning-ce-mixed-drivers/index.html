Evidence¶

Rouleau G 2019¶

Rouleau G, Gagnon MP, Côté J, Payne-Gagnon J, Hudson E, Dubois CA, Bouix-Picasso J. (2019). Effects of E-Learning in a Continuing Education Context on Nursing Care: Systematic Review of Systematic Qualitative, Quantitative, and Mixed-Studies Reviews. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/15118

q2 · i? · review · r3

Narrative synthesis of level 1 (reaction) outcomes from 11 of 22 included SRs. The review reports positive reactions in "8 out of 22 SRs (36%)", citing satisfaction with content quality, social interactions, flexibility, and support.

"Positive outcomes were described in 8 out of 22 SRs (36%) [10,36,39,40,42,44,48,51], mostly in terms of nurse satisfaction with using e-learning for the following reasons: quality of content [44], importance of social interactions [40,48], active learning [48], flexibility [10,51]"

Rouleau G 2019 (2)¶

Rouleau G, Gagnon MP, Côté J, Payne-Gagnon J, Hudson E, Dubois CA, Bouix-Picasso J. (2019). Effects of E-Learning in a Continuing Education Context on Nursing Care: Systematic Review of Systematic Qualitative, Quantitative, and Mixed-Studies Reviews. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/15118

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Same narrative synthesis of reaction outcomes. The review reports dissatisfaction in "3 out of 22 SRs (14%)" attributed to technical difficulties, limited computer experience, and preference for face-to-face formats.

"In 3 out of 22 SRs (14%), nurse dissatisfaction with e-learning interventions was explained by the following reasons: technical difficulties [10,40], a lack of computer experience and internet literacy, slower information exchange [10], and a preference for face-to-face format [52]."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • E-learning improves nurses' knowledge in continuing education on many topics, but effects are mixed and classroom learning was superior for drug dose calculations
  • E-learning interventions for registered nurses in continuing education are evaluated almost exclusively at Kirkpatrick levels 1 and 2, with no reported outcomes on practice change and almost none on patient outcomes
  • E-learning improves nurses' skills in continuing education, but effects are mixed, with worse defibrillation performance under long-distance learning and lower first-attempt cannulation success with computer-based simulation
  • No included systematic review reported outcomes on nurses' practice change (Kirkpatrick level 3), so the transfer of e-learning to practice remains unknown — related
  • Expressed dissatisfaction with online technology, instruction, or content is not the primary reason for dropping — related
  • At Kirkpatrick Level 1, satisfaction, perceptions, anxiety, and learning flow did not differ significantly between roles, but emotional arousal favored active participants — related
  • Technical difficulties were the most frequently reported implementation limitation, mentioned in ten of the thirteen studies — related
