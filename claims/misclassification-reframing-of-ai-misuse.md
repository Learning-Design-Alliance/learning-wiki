# Learner AI over-reliance is reframed as misclassification: a failure of real-time metacognitive evaluation with two distinct pathways

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · theoretical `r2`–`r3` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Misclassification is defined as selecting a subzone-inappropriate AI interaction mode for a task at a given developmental stage, and implies a competency deficit rather than a norm violation. [→ Tsim 2026](#tsim-2026)
`q1 i?` Pathway one (Aid treated as Substitute) arises from effort minimization and an authority heuristic, eliminating the cognitive struggle through which skills would form. [→ Tsim 2026 (2)](#tsim-2026-2)
`q1 i?` Pathway two (Aid treated as Complement) arises from Dunning-Kruger-driven overconfidence, producing an illusion of competence where plausible AI errors pass unverified. [→ Tsim 2026 (3)](#tsim-2026-3)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

### Tsim 2026 (3)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument for the second path: a learner engages as a 'proficient' delegator without the task-specific knowledge Complement requires, so plausible AI errors pass through verification that feels rigorous but is not.
