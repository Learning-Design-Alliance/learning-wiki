# Gesture-enriched L2 vocabulary learning benefits 14-year-olds more than picture enrichment, while 12-year-olds benefit equivalently from both

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Eighth graders showed a significant gesture-over-picture benefit (d = 0.85), which sixth graders did not (d = 0.08), yielding a learning condition × grade interaction. [→ Brian Mathias 2022](#brian-mathias-2022)

## Evidence

### Brian Mathias 2022

Brian Mathias, Christian Andrä, Anika Schwager, Manuela Macedonia, Katharina von Kriegstein. (2022). Twelve- and Fourteen-Year-Old School Children Differentially Benefit from Sensorimotor- and Multisensory-Enriched Vocabulary Training. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09648-z

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Mixed effects modeling of composite vocabulary scores from 75 German high school children (39 sixth graders, 36 eighth graders) in a 3×3×1×2 mixed design. "gesture enrichment enhanced learning outcomes even more than picture enrichment for the eighth graders (β = 0.95, t = 3.56, p = 0.005, d = 0.85), which was not the case for the sixth graders (β = 0.09, t = 0.35, p = 0.99, d = 0.08)".

> "Mixed effects modeling of children's vocabulary test scores revealed an interaction between the children's grade level and the learning condition (χ2 (2, N = 75) = 5.70, p  =  0.04, v  =  0.19"
