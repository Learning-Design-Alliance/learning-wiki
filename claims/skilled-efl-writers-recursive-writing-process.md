# Skilled EFL writers undergo a recursive writing process in which planning, writing, and reviewing repeat and embed within each other during composing.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Interview analysis of nine skilled EFL writers shows the writing processes of planning, writing, and reviewing are interactive and recursive, with planning strategies re-used when writers run out of ideas mid-composition. [→ Yu-Ling You; Shih-Guey Joe 2001](#yu-ling-you-shih-guey-joe-2001)

## Evidence

### Yu-Ling You; Shih-Guey Joe 2001

Yu-Ling You; Shih-Guey Joe. (2001). Investigating the Metacognitive Awareness and Strategies of English-Majored University Student Writers. ERIC Document ED465281. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED465281

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Introspective interview study of nine English-majored Taiwanese university students deemed skilled writers, each of whom first wrote a one-hour English composition. Transcription analysis found the three processes "interactive and recursive"; one subject reported re-planning by brainstorming when resuming writing after a pause.

> "The findings of analyzing the transcriptions of interview are compatible with Flower and Hayes' and others'; that is, writing process can indeed be divided as planning, writing, and reviewing, and the three processes are interactive and recursive during the cognitive process of writing."
