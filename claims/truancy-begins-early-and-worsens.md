# Ninth grade attendance problems begin early in the year and generally worsen, with 89 percent of first-semester extreme truants ending the year at similar or worse levels

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Half of entering ninth graders missed two or more weeks in at least one major subject in the first semester, and 89 percent of first-semester extreme truants ended the year with similar or worse attendance. [→ Roderick 1997](#roderick-1997)
`q2 i?` Attendance problems spread: almost half of first-semester moderate truants became extreme truants by second semester, and over one-third of good attenders became truants by year's end. [→ Roderick 1997 (2)](#roderick-1997-2)

## Evidence

### Roderick 1997

Roderick, M., Arney, M., Axelman, M., DaCosta, K., Steiger, C., Stone, S., Villarreal-Sosa, L., & Waxman, E. (1997). Habits Hard to Break: A New Look at Truancy in Chicago's Public High Schools. Student Life in High Schools Project, School of Social Service Administration, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/habits-hard-break-new-look-truancy-chicagos-public-high-schools

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal transcript analysis across the two semesters of ninth grade for the 1995-96 CPS cohort. First semester, 50 percent missed two or more weeks in a major subject and 28 percent missed more than a month; early problems generally worsened by year's end.

> "Of the students who began ninth grade as extreme tru- ants, 89 percent ended the year with similar or worse levels of attendance."

### Roderick 1997 (2)

Roderick, M., Arney, M., Axelman, M., DaCosta, K., Steiger, C., Stone, S., Villarreal-Sosa, L., & Waxman, E. (1997). Habits Hard to Break: A New Look at Truancy in Chicago's Public High Schools. Student Life in High Schools Project, School of Social Service Administration, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/habits-hard-break-new-look-truancy-chicagos-public-high-schools

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same cohort analysis of attendance trajectories between first and second semester. The brief also reports over one-third of students who began as good attenders became truants by the end of ninth grade, indicating cutting spreads beyond initially disengaged students.

> "Almost half of the ninth graders who began the year with moderate absences—missing 2 to 4 weeks of a major subject—became extreme truants by the second semester."
