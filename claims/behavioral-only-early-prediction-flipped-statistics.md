# Behavioral-only metrics from an instrumented online textbook support early prediction of final course grades in flipped statistics courses

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Models using behavioral-only metrics engineered from students' textbook interactions were developed to detect at-risk students early in flipped introductory statistics courses. [→ Alice Xu 2026](#alice-xu-2026)
`q2 i?` Eleven chapter-level features (six reading-related, five formative-assessment-related) were constructed per chapter, yielding 132 features across 12 chapters. [→ Alice Xu 2026 (2)](#alice-xu-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Alice Xu 2026

Alice Xu, Icy (Yunyi) Zhang, Adam B. Blake and James W. Stigler. (2026). Modelability as a Strategy for Improving the Generalizability and Scalability of Predictive Models. Journal of Learning Analytics, 13(1). https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2026.9099

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Case study modeling goal (RQ1): predicting final grade category early in the term from behavioral-only metrics in a flipped classroom setting. Data came from two courses (183 and 60 completing students) at two public research universities; no effect size is printed.

> "we developed a model detecting at -risk students early in the courses , using behavioral -only metrics derived from students' uses of an online textbook in an introductory-level statistics course delivered in a flipped classroom setting."

### Alice Xu 2026 (2)

Alice Xu, Icy (Yunyi) Zhang, Adam B. Blake and James W. Stigler. (2026). Modelability as a Strategy for Improving the Generalizability and Scalability of Predictive Models. Journal of Learning Analytics, 13(1). https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2026.9099

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Feature engineering: reading sessions derived from enGauge timestamps yielded six features per chapter (e.g., session count, engagement ratio), plus five formative-assessment features; "In total, the model includes 132 features, with 11 features for each of the 12 chapters."

> "Specifically, we constructed 11 chapter-level features: six related to reading behavior and five related to formative assessment performance."
