## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Grades 3 and 5, serial digit naming became a significant unique predictor of both word-list and text reading fluency after accounting for discrete word reading. [→ Altani 2019](#altani-2019)

## Evidence

### Altani 2019

Altani, A., Protopapas, A., Katopodi, K., & Georgiou, G. K. (2019). From Individual Word Recognition to Word List and Text Reading Fluency. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000359

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple regression and commonality analyses (Tables 5 and 6) with discrete word reading and serial digit naming predicting serial word and text reading in each grade and language. The authors state "serial digit naming became a significant and unique predictor" in Grades 3 and 5; in Grade 1, discrete word reading predicted the larger share.

> "among the groups of intermediate (Grade 3) and more advanced (Grade 5) readers, serial digit naming became a significant and unique predictor not only for word list reading fluency, but also for text reading fluency ‒ beyond isolated word reading speed."
