## Evidence

### Nicole P. Vogt 2012

Nicole P. Vogt, Stephen P. Cook, and Amy Smith Muise. (2012). A New Resource for College Distance Education Astronomy Laboratory Exercises. [doi:10.1080/08923647.2013.795365](https://doi.org/10.1080/08923647.2013.795365)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparison of two general education astronomy courses taught by the same instructor, one classroom (Fall 2010) and one distance (Fall 2011), using an identical 30% of the two-hour final exam covering laboratory material. Table 2 reports classroom 24.7 ± 3.6 (n=85) versus remote 28.8 ± 1.8 (n=21) out of 30 points; the article reports a two-sample t-test with less than 1% probability and no effect size.

> "The distance learning cohort in fact did significantly better on this material, with a mean value more than one pooled standard deviation above that for on-campus students, and a two-sample t-test yields a less than 1% probability that the two results were drawn from the same parent sample."
