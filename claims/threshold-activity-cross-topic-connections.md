## Evidence

### Angela Little and Leslie Atkins Elliott 2018

Angela Little and Leslie Atkins Elliott. (2018). On the Importance of Engaging Students in Crafting Definitions. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/1.5051144](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.5051144)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The article offers this as an existence proof example: one small group of college physics students graphed physical quantities in a water-boiling example and a falling block to test their idea of crossing curves, linking mechanics and thermodynamics. No effect size is reported.

> "One small group of college physics students that author Little worked with explored an idea that thresholds occur at a point where two curves cross one another (see Figure 2). The group decided to check this idea of “crossing” with two specific examples: water boiling and ‘a block falling off the edge of a table.’"

### Angela Little and Leslie Atkins Elliott 2018 (2)

Angela Little and Leslie Atkins Elliott. (2018). On the Importance of Engaging Students in Crafting Definitions. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/1.5051144](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.5051144)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The authors report, from their experience facilitating the definitional activity, that one small group discussed thresholds in examples ranging from wolf-sheep predation to the stock market and human anger. This is an observational report from facilitation, not a measured comparison.

> "Not only do students see connections within physics, they see connections across STEM and beyond. One small group that Little worked with discussed examples as varied as wolf-sheep predation, the stock market, eating too many cookies until you get sick, and human anger and frustration."
