## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2015

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2015). Upper-division student difficulties with Separation of Variables. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020131)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Observational analysis of exam solutions to Cartesian SoV questions found "roughly a quarter of the solutions (26%, N=61 of 235) included only errors related to the elements in the Execution component"; the article notes this is high relative to prior work on multivariable integration and the Dirac delta function.

> "Ultimately, roughly a quarter of the solutions (26%, N=61 of 235) included only errors related to the elements in the Execution component (i.e., no previous mistakes in Activation or Construction)."

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2015 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2015). Upper-division student difficulties with Separation of Variables. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020131)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

For exam questions where the boundary condition allowed either strategy, "more of our students’ solutions utilized Fourier’s trick (44%, N=52 of 117) than term matching (36%, N=42 of 117)", even though term matching was considerably simpler for those boundary conditions.

> "Despite this, more of our students’ solutions utilized Fourier’s trick (44%, N=52 of 117) than term matching (36%, N=42 of 117)."
