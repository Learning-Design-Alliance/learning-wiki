# Revised visuals reduce cognitive processing of lesson text for low prior knowledge students but increase it for middle prior knowledge students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Total fixation duration on the text showed a condition by prior knowledge interaction, F(2, 51) = 23.63, p < .001, with low prior knowledge students processing the text more in the original condition and middle prior knowledge students processing it more in the revised condition. [→ Clinton 2017](#clinton-2017)

## Evidence

### Clinton 2017

Clinton, V., Cooper, J.L., Michaelis, J., Alibali, M.W., & Nathan, M.J. (2017). How Revisions to Mathematical Visuals Affect Cognition: Evidence from Eye Tracking. In Eye-tracking technology applications in educational research (pp. 195-218). IGI Global. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED574984

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Eye-tracking experiment measuring total fixation duration (log-transformed) on text regions. Low prior knowledge students had "greater total fixation duration on the text in the original conditions than the revised condition"; middle prior knowledge students showed the reverse, F(1, 15) = 9.82, p = .002.

> "There was an interaction between condition and prior knowledge group for total fixation duration on the text, F(2, 51) = 23.63, p < .001. Participants in the low prior knowledge group had greater total fixation duration on the text in the original conditions than the revised condition, F(1, 21) = 74.88, p < .001."
