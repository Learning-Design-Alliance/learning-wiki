Evidence¶

Janssen M 2014¶

Janssen M, Toussaint HM, van Mechelen W, Verhagen EALM. (2014). Effects of acute bouts of physical activity on children's attention: a systematic review of the literature. SpringerPlus. https://doi.org/10.1186/2193-1801-3-410

q2 · i? · review · r3

Review's discussion of Caterino & Polak (1999), a school experiment with 177 children aged 7–10 using the Woodcock-Johnson Test of Concentration after 15-min stretching or aerobic walking; significant only for 9–10 year olds (p = 0.05).

"Caterino and Polak (1999) found significant effect of PA only for 9 – 10 year old children, no effects were found for 7– 8 years old children, indicating the differences in out- comes between ages and the difficulty in comparing the various included studies."

  • Five of six laboratory studies found significant effects of acute PA on cognitive control of attention, mainly on accuracy not reaction time — related

  • Acute moderate aerobic exercise speeds children's reaction times without improving accuracy, with greater speed gains on choice than simple reaction time tasks — related
  • Acute physical activity has a positive effect on attention in preadolescent children (g = 0.43) — related
  • A single bout of open-skill exercise improves short-term memory performance in primary-school children of both age groups (7–8 and 9–10 years). — related
