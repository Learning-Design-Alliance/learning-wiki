## Evidence

### Poelman A. A. M. 2021

Poelman A. A. M., Cochet-Broch M., Beelen J., Wiggins B., Heffernan J. E., & Cox D. N. (2021). Teacher Evaluation of an Experiential Vegetable Education Program for Australian Primary Schools: Does Face-to-Face Training Add Value above Digital Training? Nutrients, 13(5), 1648. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu13051648

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Univariate ANOVA with training intensity (low/high) and state as factors on dimension ratings from 65 teachers implementing the program. The article reports that "The statistical analysis showed that the level of intensity of training (online or additional F2F) did not affect any of the acceptability ratings".

> "The statistical analysis showed that the level of intensity of training (online or additional F2F) did not affect any of the acceptability ratings of the vegetable education program by the teachers (Table 4)."

### Poelman A. A. M. 2021 (2)

Poelman A. A. M., Cochet-Broch M., Beelen J., Wiggins B., Heffernan J. E., & Cox D. N. (2021). Teacher Evaluation of an Experiential Vegetable Education Program for Australian Primary Schools: Does Face-to-Face Training Add Value above Digital Training? Nutrients, 13(5), 1648. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu13051648

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Survey ratings of the online training module from 51 of the 65 teachers who indicated having conducted it. The article reports that "There were no statistically significant differences in ratings as a factor of the intervention arm (training intensity) or state".

> "There were no statistically significant differences in ratings as a factor of the intervention arm (training intensity) or state (NSW/SA) (Table 3)."
