# Without explicit incentives, many advanced students do not automatically learn from their midterm mistakes even when provided correct solutions and graded exams

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` In the comparison group, overall gain on the repeated problems was only +3.6%, with a comparable number of students scoring lower on the final than on the midterm. [→ Benjamin R. Brown and Chandralekha Singh 2015](#benjamin-r-brown-and-chandralekha-singh-2015)

## Evidence

### Benjamin R. Brown and Chandralekha Singh 2015

Benjamin R. Brown and Chandralekha Singh, Andrew Mason. (2015). The effect of giving explicit incentives to correct mistakes on subsequent problem solving in quantum mechanics. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.012](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.012)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison-group outcome on the four repeated problems (Table II: pre 67.9, post 71.5, gain +3.6). The authors interpret the wide performance distribution as showing many advanced students do not automatically exploit mistakes for learning; this is their interpretation of the data.

> "The wide distribution of students’ perfor- mance on problems administered a second time suggests that many advanced students do not automatically exploit their mistakes as an opportunity for learning, and for repairing, ex- tending, and organizing their knowledge structure."

### Benjamin R. Brown 2016

Benjamin R. Brown, Andrew Mason, and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Improving performance in quantum mechanics with explicit incentives to correct mistakes. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010121](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010121)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

In the quasi-experimental study, the comparison group's posttest scores were not significantly different from their pretest scores ("t=-0.590 and p=0.557"), despite having been provided correct solutions and graded exams.

> "We ﬁnd that for the comparison group, the diﬀerence between the pretest and postest scores is not signiﬁcant (t=-0.590 and p=0.557) but for the inventivized group, the diﬀerence between the pretest and posttest scores is signiﬁcant (t=-3.966 and p< 0.001)."

### Benjamin R. Brown 2016 (2)

Benjamin R. Brown, Andrew Mason, and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Improving performance in quantum mechanics with explicit incentives to correct mistakes. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010121](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010121)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Among comparison group students, a comparable number regressed as improved on the repeated problems, indicating that many advanced students "do not automatically exploit their mistakes as an opportunity for learning".

> "while some advanced students performed equally well or improved compared to their performance on the midterm exam on the questions administered a second time, a comparable number of students obtained lower scores on the final exam than on the midterm exam."
