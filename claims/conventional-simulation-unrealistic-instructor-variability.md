# Students judge conventional simulation training unrealistic, confined to pre-established scenario behaviors, and variable in effectiveness across instructors

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · qualitative `r3` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2020 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students report that mannequin-based simulation is unrealistic: the model cannot converse, give feedback, or express higher-level pain signs, so students treat it carelessly. [→ Jeon Jaehee 2020](#jeon-jaehee-2020)
`q2 i?` Students report that training effectiveness differed according to the instructor's teaching method and competence, and they want standardized instruction. [→ Jeon Jaehee 2020 (2)](#jeon-jaehee-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Jeon Jaehee 2020

Jeon Jaehee, Kim Jin Hee, Choi Eun Hee. (2020). Needs Assessment for a VR-Based Adult Nursing Simulation Training Program for Korean Nursing Students: A Qualitative Study Using Focus Group Interviews. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17238880

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Focus group findings in the theme cluster 'Not so realistic'; participants said they were "less tense and we treat it more carelessly" with a model, and could not get direct feedback from the model patient.

> "Participants mentioned that one shortcoming of simulation training is that the role of the patient is played by a virtual model, which means that they cannot have a real conversation with it, thereby making them feel as if they were not delivering actual care."

### Jeon Jaehee 2020 (2)

Jeon Jaehee, Kim Jin Hee, Choi Eun Hee. (2020). Needs Assessment for a VR-Based Adult Nursing Simulation Training Program for Korean Nursing Students: A Qualitative Study Using Focus Group Interviews. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17238880

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Theme cluster 'Difference in effectiveness according to the instructor' from the same FGIs; one participant noted professors' acting skill varied, so "we kind of learn different things with different effectiveness."

> "Participants stated that they wanted the instructors to use a standardized method of instruction for simulation training and that they had learned different things with varying levels of effectiveness according to the instructor."
