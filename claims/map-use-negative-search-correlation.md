# Greater use of the navigation map is associated with less relevant searching in a hypermedia learning task

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` More visits to and more time on the navigation map correlated negatively with search ratio (rho = −0.33 and −0.31), meaning more map use accompanied less task-relevant information access. [→ Stanton 1997](#stanton-1997)

## Evidence

### Stanton 1997

Stanton, N., Correia, A. P. and Dias, P. (1997). Efficacy of a map on search, orientation and access behaviour in a hypermedia system. https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Efficacy+of+a+map+on+search+orientation+and+access+behaviour+in+a+hypermedia+system

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis of log-file data from 22 high school students using the tectonic-plates hypermedia prototype. The printed Spearman correlation coefficients are rho = −0.33 and rho = −0.31 (effect sizes |r| = .33 and |r| = .31, medium-range), showing "more visits to the map, less relevant search".

> "a negative correlation coefficient between frequency/time of visit to the map and search ratio (−0.33 and −0.31, respectively) that shows an inverse effort, that is, more visits to the map, less relevant search"
