# GPA combined with test scores was the best prediction of college degree attainment for all English Learner groups, but the test-score relationship partly reflects school composition

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For all English Learner groups, the combination of GPA with test scores best predicted which students eventually attained a college degree. [→ Allensworth 2025](#allensworth-2025)
`q2 i?` The strength of the test-score relationship with college outcomes weakens when comparing students with different test scores within the same high school. [→ Allensworth 2025 (2)](#allensworth-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Allensworth 2025

Allensworth, E.M., de la Torre, M., Franklin, K., & Xu, J. (2025). Identifying Indicators to Support Educational Attainment for Different Groups of English Learners in High School. (Working Paper). Providence, RI: Annenberg EdExchange. https://edworkingpapers.com/ai25-1241

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression models predicting degree completion for each English Learner subgroup, comparing McFadden pseudo-R2 across indicator models, found "The combination of GPA with test scores was the best prediction" of eventual college degree attainment for every group.

> "The combination of GPA with test scores was the best prediction of which students eventually attained a college degree for all English Learner groups."

### Allensworth 2025 (2)

Allensworth, E.M., de la Torre, M., Franklin, K., & Xu, J. (2025). Identifying Indicators to Support Educational Attainment for Different Groups of English Learners in High School. (Working Paper). Providence, RI: Annenberg EdExchange. https://edworkingpapers.com/ai25-1241

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

A footnote to the degree-prediction finding cautions that the test-score relationship is partly compositional: high-scoring students concentrate at particular high schools, and the relationship weakens in within-school comparisons (Table 5).

> "But the strength of relationship of test scores with college outcomes partially results because students with high test score s are more likely to be at particular high schools and when you compare students with different test scores in the same high schoo ls the relationship weakens (see Table 5 in the study)."
