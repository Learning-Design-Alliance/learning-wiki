# Among the 1424 students who gave Galilean answers to both Formulations 1 and 2, 76.4% gave Copernican answers and 3.8% gave Galilean answers on Formulation 3 in one uncontrolled pre-post study

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Of the 1424 students who gave Galilean answers to both Formulations 1 and 2, 3.8% gave Galilean answers on Formulation 3, while 76.4% gave Copernican answers, in a change measured within this group from pre-test to post-test. ## Evidence

### Estelle Blanquet and Éric Picholle 2019

Estelle Blanquet and Éric Picholle. (2019). Emergence of conformist thinking under strong paradigmatic pressure: the case of (Galilean) relativity. Physical Review Physics Education Research (preprint dated December 5, 2019). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1912.04110](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1912.04110)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Within-subject analysis in the SFP questionnaire: the 1424 previously 'Galilean' students (67.3% of 2115) "massively shift to a 'Copernican' stance" on Formulation 3 (Table V: 76.4% Copernican, 19.8% Other, 3.8% Galilean).

> "will massively shift to a 'Copernican' stance when confronted to a proposition involving the relative motion of the Earth and the Sun (Form. 3), with less than 4% maintaining a Galilean stance in this modified context, as shown in Table V."
