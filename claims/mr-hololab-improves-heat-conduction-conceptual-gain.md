# A mixed-reality holo.lab setting improves undergraduate students' gain in conceptual understanding of heat transfer and thermal conduction, with a small adjusted effect

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` In a quasi-experimental 2x2 pilot study, the treatment group using the HoloLens holo.lab showed significantly higher post-test scores than the control group after covariate adjustment (F = 5.61, p = 0.02, adjusted Cohen's d = 0.43). [→ M. P. Strzys 2018](#m-p-strzys-2018)

## Evidence

### M. P. Strzys 2018

M. P. Strzys, S. Kapp, M. Thees, P. Klein, P. Lukowicz, P. Knierim, A. Schmidt and J. Kuhn. (2018). Physics holo.lab learning experience: Using Smartglasses for Augmented Reality labwork to foster the concepts of heat conduction. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/aaa8fb](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/aaa8fb)

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental pilot study in the summer term 2017 at University of Kaiserslautern with N = 59 (NC = 43 control, NT = 16 treatment), comparing post-test HTCE scores via ANCOVA with pretest scores as covariates. The article reports "a statistically significant ( F = 5.61,p = 0.02)" effect with "adjusted Cohen’s d = 0.43".

> "This yields a statistically significant ( F = 5.61,p = 0.02) corresponding small eﬀect size with a tendency towards the medium range (adjusted Cohen’s d = 0.43)."
