# Experimental-group students reported positive perceptions of AR, with highest scores for enjoyment (M = 4.44) and ease of use (M = 4.29)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On a 16-item TAM-based Likert questionnaire, students rated ease of use (M = 4.29), perceived enjoyment (M = 4.44) and intent of use (M = 3.67) above the scale midpoint; perceived usefulness was lower (M = 3.33), and the item about getting better grades scored lowest (M = 2.69). ## Evidence

### Purificacion Toledo-Morales & Jose Manuel Sanchez-Garcia 2018

Purificacion Toledo-Morales & Jose Manuel Sanchez-Garcia. (2018). Use of Augmented Reality in Social Sciences as Educational Resource. Turkish Online Journal of Distance Education-TOJDE, 19(3), Article 4. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1183349

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Likert-type questionnaire (16 items, five TAM dimensions: utility, ease of use, enjoyment, attitude, intent of use) administered to the 25 experimental-group students; Cronbach's alpha was 0.858. Enjoyment reached M = 4.44 with "Using AR in class is fun" at M = 4.78; the grades item scored M = 2.69. No effect sizes are printed.
