# Students whose overall paradigm changes show significant pre-post differences in individual set-like responses, shifting about one S response on average

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students switching from mixed to set-like showed an average rise of one S response and fall of one P response from pre-test to post-test (average S shift 0.97 ± 0.09; P shift -1.09 ± 0.05), with significantly different pre-post S responses (p ≪ 0.01), in one group with no comparison group. [→ Benjamin Pollard 2017](#benjamin-pollard-2017)

## Evidence

### Benjamin Pollard 2017

Benjamin Pollard, Robert Hobbs, Jacob T. Stanley, Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer, and H. J. Lewandowski. (2017). Impact of an introductory lab on students’ understanding of measurement uncertainty. arXiv:1707.01979v1 [physics.ed-ph]. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1707.01979](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1707.01979)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Subpopulation analysis of the pre/post PMQ data from the Fall 2016 CU introductory lab cohort (Fig. 2 two-dimensional histogram of overall paradigm shifts). The switchers from mixed to set-like "had an average shift in the number of individual S responses of 0.97 ± 0.09" and significantly different pre-post S responses.

> "The students who switched from mixed to set-like had an average shift in the number of individual S responses of 0.97 ± 0.09 and an average shift in the number of P responses of -1.09 ± 0.05, gaining one S response and losing one P response on average."
