## Subclaims
`q1 i?` SPs gave dominantly positive perceptions of students (20/21 positive, 14 exclusively positive) compared with peer patients (13/22 positive, none exclusively positive). [→ Hecimovich Mark 2014](#hecimovich-mark-2014)

## Evidence

### Hecimovich Mark 2014

Hecimovich Mark, Volet Simone. (2014). Simulated learning in musculoskeletal assessment and rehabilitation education: comparing the effect of a simulation-based learning activity with a peer-based learning activity. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0253-6

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-test open-ended ratings by the trained SPs and peer patients of each student's professional and communication performance during the practical test. The article reports the 20/21 versus 13/22 contrast and offers professionalism as a possible explanation, not a tested result.

> "The dominantly positive perceptions provided by the SP (20/21, 14 of them exclusively positive) in comparison to the PP (13/22, none of them exclusively positive) suggests that although both groups did appear to be nervous and stressed, more than likely due to this being an assessment which formed their final mark, those students in the SPs condition may have developed a greater sense of professionalism"
