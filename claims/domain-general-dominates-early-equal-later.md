# Domain-general abilities are more important than domain-specific knowledge for mathematics learning in early grades, but the two are equally important in later grades

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Overall domain-general effects exceed domain-specific effects in grades 3 through 5, but by 8th grade domain-specific effects exceed domain-general effects and no overall differences are significant after 5th grade. [→ Geary David C. 2017](#geary-david-c-2017)

## Evidence

### Geary David C. 2017

Geary David C., Nicholas Alan, Li Yaoran, Sun Jianguo. (2017). Developmental Change in the Influence of Domain-General Abilities and Domain-Specific Knowledge on Mathematics Achievement: An Eight-Year Longitudinal Study. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000159

`q3 · i?`

Grade-by-grade contrasts from the longitudinal FDA model comparing summed domain-general and domain-specific beta estimates. Significant domain-general advantages in grades 4 (p = .014) and 5 (p = .023) give way to no significant differences after grade 5.

> "There is a trend for larger overall domain-general than domain-specific effects in grade 3 (p = .067) and significant differences in grades 4 (p =.014) and 5 (p = .023). As shown in Figure 4, the overall domain-specific effect exceed the domain-general effect by 8th grade, but none of the overall differences are significant after 5th grade (ps > .117)."
