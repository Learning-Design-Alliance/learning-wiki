# REACH observations consumed substantial administrator time—about six hours per observation cycle and roughly two to three full weeks per year

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Administrators reported about six hours per formal observation cycle; typical elementary administrators spent about 120 hours annually on observations and high school administrators about three full weeks. [→ Susan E. Sporte 2013](#susan-e-sporte-2013)

## Evidence

### Susan E. Sporte 2013

Susan E. Sporte, W. David Stevens, Kaleen Healey, Jennie Jiang, and Holly Hart. (2013). Teacher Evaluation in Practice: Implementing Chicago's REACH Students. UChicago CCSR Research Report. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/teacher-evaluation-practice-implementing-chicagos-reach-students

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Spring 2013 administrator surveys. Based on reported time and observation counts, "the typical elementary school administrator spent approximately 120 hours—or two full weeks—solely on observations," while the typical high school administrator spent approximately three full weeks.

> "Administrators reported spending about six hours per formal observation cycle, including the observation, pre- and post-observation conferences, and data management."
