## Evidence

### Huddlestone Lisa 2015

Huddlestone Lisa, Walker Gemma Michelle, Hussain-Mills Robana, Ratschen Elena. (2015). Treating tobacco dependence in older adults: a survey of primary care clinicians’ knowledge, attitudes, and practice. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-015-0317-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression analysis adjusting for significant factors, entered at p = 0.05. The article reports odds ratios of "2.4 times for medical" and "3.0 times for non-medical staff" for providing cessation support with training; no standardised effect size is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "the odds of respondents providing older patients with smoking cessation support increased with training by 2.4 times for medical (95 % CI 1.2-4.7, p = 0.009) and by 3.0 times for non-medical staff (95 % CI 1.4-5.6, p = 0.002)."

### Huddlestone Lisa 2015 (2)

Huddlestone Lisa, Walker Gemma Michelle, Hussain-Mills Robana, Ratschen Elena. (2015). Treating tobacco dependence in older adults: a survey of primary care clinicians’ knowledge, attitudes, and practice. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-015-0317-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive comparison of overall practice scores (maximum 18) between trained and untrained clinicians in the survey; the difference is described as "slightly higher" and no significance test is reported for it.

> "Overall, those clinicians who had received smoking related training achieved a slightly higher mean score in relation to their practice of addressing smoking among older adults when compared to those who had not attended smoking related training [12.79 (SD 3.2) vs 11.13 (SD 3.6), respectively]."
