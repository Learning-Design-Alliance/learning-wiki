Evidence¶

Lee 2013¶

Lee, J., Shin, H. & Amo, L. C. (2013). Evaluating the impact of NCLB school interventions in New York state: Does one size fit all? Education Policy Analysis Archives, 21 (67). http://epaa.asu.edu/ojs/article/view/1122

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Interaction terms from the IPTW regression models (Table 3) of NCLB intervention effects on PI gains. The article states this pattern (with the exception of Grade 8 ELA) "suggests that schools that were subject to interventions in later years produced better achievement gains."

"there was a positive interaction between intervention and year of implementation in Grade 4 ELA (β = 2.878, p < .01), Grade 4 math (β = 9.252, p < .001) and Grade 8 math (β = 5.524, p < .001)."

Related Claims¶

  • Differential analysis of the five NCLB intervention stages found null or negative effects, including significantly lower gains for SES schools in Grade 4 ELA and for restructuring schools in Grade 8 ELA — related
  • Treatment effects varied by propensity of treatment assignment: schools with better pre-treatment conditions (lower propensity) gained more, with a significant negative intervention-by-propensity interaction in Grade 8 math — related
  • After propensity-score matching, NCLB accountability interventions showed negative or null effects on New York schools' Performance Index gains, with only Grade 8 math showing a small positive effect — related
  • Unmatched comparisons suggested increasingly aggressive NCLB treatment groups had higher mean PI gains, but these advantages mostly disappeared after matching, indicating selection bias — related
  • Among treated schools, those that exited intervention status had better prior performance and more favorable schooling conditions than watch and fail groups — related
