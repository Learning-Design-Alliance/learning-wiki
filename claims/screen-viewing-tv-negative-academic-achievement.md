[doi:10.3389/fpubh.2024.1412634](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2024.1412634)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Adjusted regression models (Table 5, Model 2) in the GUSTO cohort printed small negative per-hour associations for total screen viewing and TV time with academic achievement; the age 5.5 estimates were marginal (p = 0.072 and 0.051) while the age 8 estimates were significant. No standardized effect size was printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "higher amount of time spent in total SVT and TV at age 5.5 years [−0.071 (−0.149, 0.006) and −0.131 (−0.262, 0.000) z-scores] and at age 8 years [−0.062 (−0.106, −0.018) and-0.091 (−0.167, −0.015) z-scores] showed lower mean academic achievement at age 9 years"

[doi:10.3389/fpubh.2024.1412634](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2024.1412634)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same adjusted models found no association of screen viewing measures with executive function at age 8.5 years; handheld devices also showed no significant association with academic achievement. No effect sizes were printed for these null findings.

> "Total SVT, TV and handheld screen device viewing time at ages 5.5 and 8 years were not associated with executive function (Table 5)."
