# How SES is operationalized moderates the SES-achievement correlation: home-resources measures yield the largest correlations and range restriction attenuates them

## Evidence

### Sirin 2005

Sirin, S. R. (2005). Socioeconomic Status and Academic Achievement: A Meta-Analytic Review of Research. Review of Educational Research, 75(3), 417-453. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543075003417

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Weighted ANOVA moderator analysis over 102 unique correlations; Qb(5, 79) = 587.14, p < .001. The printed effect sizes were mean correlations: r = .51 for home resources, r = .33 for lunch eligibility, r = .28 for occupation, r = .29 for income, r = .30 for education; pairwise comparisons among education, occupation, and income were nonsignificant.

> "SES measures based on "home resources" produced the highest mean ES (.51), followed by eligibility for free or reduced lunch programs (.33)."

### Sirin 2005 (2)

Sirin, S. R. (2005). Socioeconomic Status and Academic Achievement: A Meta-Analytic Review of Research. Review of Educational Research, 75(3), 417-453. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543075003417

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Moderator analysis of SES range restriction across 102 correlations; Qb(2, 102) = 238.65, p < .001, with all pairwise comparisons significant at p < .005. With no restriction the average ES was .35.

> "the average ES for the two-SES group only category (e.g., high versus low SES) was .24, while the average ES for the 3–7 SES groups category was .28."
