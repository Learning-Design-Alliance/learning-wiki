# Students appropriate the concept of tactics in their own post-game reasoning through thematization of general principles and framing performance in terms of creating dilemmas for the opponent

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · qualitative `r2`–`r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In student-led debriefings, students articulate general tactical themes such as concentration of force and the importance of creating a situational picture. [→ Sjöblom 2025](#sjoblom-2025)
`q2 i?` Students use the notion of creating dilemmas for the opponent as a frame for explaining and positively assessing their in-game decisions. [→ Sjöblom 2025 (2)](#sjoblom-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Sjöblom 2025

Sjöblom, B., & Lymer, G. (2025). Conceptual socialization in debriefing: tactics as an object of knowledge in wargame interactions. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09717-8

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Analysis of a student-led debriefing after a three-day game in which students raised concentration of force and the importance of creating a situational picture as tactical lessons; the article characterizes this as "thematization".

> "One way in which tactics is given meaning in debriefing conversations is through what might be called thematization; the articulation of general principles or considerations made salient by the gaming experience."

### Sjöblom 2025 (2)

Sjöblom, B., & Lymer, G. (2025). Conceptual socialization in debriefing: tactics as an object of knowledge in wargame interactions. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09717-8

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Analysis of a marine group's self-debriefing where a student described mine placement protected by missile groups and AA defense as exemplifying "the creation of dilemmas" for the opponent, framed as a positively assessed method.

> "In this excerpt, the notion of “creating dilemmas” serves as a frame of reference for understanding and motivating particular decisions and actions in the game."
