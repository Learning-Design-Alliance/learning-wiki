## Evidence

### Lieberoth 2015

Lieberoth, A., Pedersen, M. K., & Sherson, J. F. (2015). Play or Science? A Study of Learning and Framing in Crowdscience. Aarhus University, Centre for Community Driven Research (CODER). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1510.06841](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1510.06841)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Likert-scale Intrinsic Motivation Inventory responses from the 38-student post-play survey, plotted in Figure 2. Interest/enjoyment scored M = 3.67 (SD = .55), described as "above average scores for interest/enjoyment".

> "students' answers indicate above average scores for interest/enjoyment (the main intrinsic motivation measure) (M = 3.67, SD = .55), with slightly lower scores for perceived value/usefulness (M = 3.55, SD = .49)"

### Lieberoth 2015 (2)

Lieberoth, A., Pedersen, M. K., & Sherson, J. F. (2015). Play or Science? A Study of Learning and Framing in Crowdscience. Aarhus University, Centre for Community Driven Research (CODER). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1510.06841](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1510.06841)

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Validation-item correlations in the same survey correlation matrix. The game "was however not perceived as \"feeling like other good games\"", correlating r = .410** with interest/enjoyment, r = .345* with value/usefulness, and r = .46** with autonomy.

> "Quantum Dreams was however not perceived as "feeling like other good games" (a validation item used in Lieberoth, 2014b). This was reflected in medium correlations with both interest/ enjoyment, r = .410**, and value/usefulness, r = .345*, and most strongly autonomy r = .46**."
