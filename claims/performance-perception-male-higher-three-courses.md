## Evidence

### Matthew Dew 2021

Matthew Dew, Jonathan Perry, Lewis Ford, William Bassichis, and Tatiana Erukhimova. (2021). Gendered Performance Diﬀerences in Introductory Physics: A Study from a Large Land-Grant University. arXiv:2009.09536. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010106](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010106)

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Fall 2019 questionnaire (over 1,600 respondents, 63% response rate) with 5-point performance-perception items analyzed by t-test; the article reports male students rating performance significantly higher in the other three courses, with a medium effect size (d = 0.504) in algebra-based E&M.

> "For the other three courses, male students rated their performance as signiﬁcantly higher with eﬀect sizes ranging from weak to medium. In the calculus-based mechanics course, male students rated their performance a third of a point higher than their female classmates as shown in Table IX (p< 0.001); the eﬀect size was small (d = 0.389)."

### Matthew Dew 2021 (2)

Matthew Dew, Jonathan Perry, Lewis Ford, William Bassichis, and Tatiana Erukhimova. (2021). Gendered Performance Diﬀerences in Introductory Physics: A Study from a Large Land-Grant University. arXiv:2009.09536. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010106](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010106)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The same questionnaire found equal performance-perception ratings only in algebra-based mechanics (Table IX: t = -0.003, p = 0.998), the course where female students historically outperformed male students on grades.

> "We found that female students rated their performance perception equally to male students only in algebra-based mechanics (See Table IX)."
