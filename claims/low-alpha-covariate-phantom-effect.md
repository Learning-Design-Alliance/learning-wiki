# A low alpha for an error-prone covariate can attenuate, inflate, or artificially produce a spurious effect (phantom effect) in the coefficient of a perfectly reliable predictor

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In multiple regression with an error-prone covariate, the predictor's coefficient becomes a weighted sum that can be biased in either direction, including producing a phantom effect. [→ Steffen Zitzmann 2025](#steffen-zitzmann-2025)

## Evidence

### Steffen Zitzmann 2025

Steffen Zitzmann, Gabe A. Orona. (2025). Why We Might Still be Concerned About Low Cronbach's Alphas in Domain-specific Knowledge Tests. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10015-5

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical derivation for a multiple regression where only the covariate is error-prone: the authors show the predictor's coefficient is biased, and that "a low alpha can lead to its attenuation" or inflation or a spurious effect. A worked quasiexperimental example yields β* = 0.15 where the true effect is 0.00.

> "this means for an existing effect of the predictor that a low alpha can lead to its attenuation. Also, it can inflate the effect or even artificially produce a spurious effect (also referred to as phantom effect; e.g., Pokropek, 2015; Televantou et al., 2015), calling into question"
