# A 5-hour VR arthroscopy training program reduced standard-test completion time in inexperienced surgeons compared with untrained controls (p = 0.03)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Inexperienced surgeons who completed a 5-hour VR training program improved their average completion time from 720 seconds to 223 seconds, improving significantly more than inexperienced controls (p = 0.03). [→ Andersen C 2011](#andersen-c-2011)

## Evidence

### Andersen C 2011

Andersen C, Winding TN, Vesterby MS. (2011). Development of simulated arthroscopic skills: A randomized trial of virtual-reality training of 21 orthopedic surgeons. Acta Orthopaedica 82(1). https://doi.org/10.3109/17453674.2011.552776

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized trial at Silkeborg Regional Hospital: 7 interns with no arthroscopic experience received a 5-hour VR training program; 7 untrained interns served as controls. The intervention group improved "more than the inexperienced control group (p = 0.03)", moving from 720 s to 223 s average completion time.

> "The average improvement for the inexperienced control group was 65 seconds, whereas the experienced control group neither increased nor decreased their average time consumption, since the difference was only 1 second. The intervention group improved more than the inexperienced control group (p = 0.03)."
