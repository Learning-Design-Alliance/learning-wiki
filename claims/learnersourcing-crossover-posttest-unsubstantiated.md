# An AP Chemistry learnersourcing crossover design comparing student-written explanations with answer-only practice produced no substantiated results because the posttest was never assigned

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` A randomized AB crossover design in which students explained work for one topic and answered only for another was implemented, but its posttest was not assigned, so results were not substantiated. [→ Heffernan 2016](#heffernan-2016)

## Evidence

### Heffernan 2016

Heffernan, N. T., Ostrow, K. S., Kelly, K., Selent, D., Van Inwegen, E. G., Xiong, X., & Williams, J. J. (2016). The Future of Adaptive Learning: Does the Crowd Hold the Key? Int J Artif Intell Educ 26:615–644. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-016-0094-z

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

Proof-of-concept design study: AP Chemistry students were randomly assigned problem sets on two unrelated topics in an AB crossover, creating explanations for one topic and answers for the other, with teacher-selected explanations offered as hints; no outcome data exist because the posttest was dropped.

> "For this iteration of the study, the posttest was not ultimately assigned to the sample population and therefore results were not substantiated."
