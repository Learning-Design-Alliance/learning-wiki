## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Faced with rewriting T(x,y) = k(x2 + y2) in polar coordinates, physicists tend to treat T as a physical function of the point in space and answer T(r,theta) = kr2, while mathematicians treat it as a mathematical functional dependence and answer k(r2 + theta2). Theoretical illustration (anecdote from the Vector Calculus Bridge project) contrasting interpretations of one function across coordinate systems. The authors report "Physicists tend to think of T as a physical function" whereas mathematicians focus on functional form and treat variables as dummy variables; the roles are explicitly exaggerated to illustrate the point.

> "Physicists tend to think of T as a physical function - one that represents the temperature (in whatever units) at a particular point in space (in whatever coordinates)."
