## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students most commonly attributed science learning gains to iPads helping them learn science (71%), supporting personal learning needs (49%), and providing access to resources that let them act as researchers (44%). [→ Susan Nicholson-Dykstra 2013](#susan-nicholson-dykstra-2013)

## Evidence

### Susan Nicholson-Dykstra 2013

Susan Nicholson-Dykstra, Ben Van Dusen and Valerie Otero. (2013). Teaching to Learn: iPads as Tools for Transforming Physics Student Roles. [doi:10.1119/perc.2013.pr.053](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2013.pr.053)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Survey of the same AP Physics class (Table 2, N=14) with two emergent open-response categories: "iPads provide access to resources and allow student-directed learning and research" and "iPads support personal needs to optimize learning". Reported percentages are descriptive; no inferential test or effect size is printed.

> "Table 2 shows that a significant percentage of students felt that iPads made learning more fun and engaging, and helped them to learn science. Students also indicated that the iPad served as a tool for increasing their communication with both their instructor and their peers."
