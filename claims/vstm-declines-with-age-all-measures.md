## Evidence

### Daniel J. Mitchell 2018

Daniel J. Mitchell, Cam-CAN (Cambridge Centre for Ageing and Neuroscience), Rhodri Cusack. (2018). Visual Short-Term Memory Through the Lifespan: Preserved Benefits of Context and Metacognition. Psychology and Aging. https://doi.org/10.1037/pag0000265

`q3 · i?`

Cross-sectional analysis of the Cam-CAN population-based sample of 649 participants aged 18 to 89 on a continuous color-report VSTM task. The article reports statistically significant age-related worsening of raw error and K with no printed effect size in this passage.

> "Raw error magnitude significantly increased with age on average (see Figure 2e), and at all memory loads (see Figure 2a). K at each memory load reduced with age (see Figure 2b), but the relationship was weaker at lower loads due to a ceiling effect."

### Daniel J. Mitchell 2018 (2)

Daniel J. Mitchell, Cam-CAN (Cambridge Centre for Ageing and Neuroscience), Rhodri Cusack. (2018). Visual Short-Term Memory Through the Lifespan: Preserved Benefits of Context and Metacognition. Psychology and Aging. https://doi.org/10.1037/pag0000265

`q3 · i?`

Correlational analysis in which precision and K estimates from a no-memory perceptual control block were regressed out of every VSTM measure before recomputing age correlations. Declines persisted for all memory measures, indicating they are not attributable to poorer color perception or motor control.

> "Importantly, age-related declines remained for all memory measures (see Figures 2i through 2l)."
