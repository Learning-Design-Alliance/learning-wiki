## Evidence

### Permzadian 2024

Permzadian, V., & Shen, T. (2024). Assessing the predictive validity of expectancy theory for academic performance. BMC Psychology, 12. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-024-01935-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Null MLM result for grades in four courses (L1 N = 492, L2 = 123): valence model coefficient γ20 = 0.001, p = .59; equivalence was not tested, only non-significance reported.

> "These hypotheses were not supported as the valence model (γ20 = 0.001, p = .59) was not associated with grades and did not account for incremental variance (see Table 4, Model 3a and 4a)."

### Permzadian 2024 (2)

Permzadian, V., & Shen, T. (2024). Assessing the predictive validity of expectancy theory for academic performance. BMC Psychology, 12. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-024-01935-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same multilevel null result for the force model on self-reported final course grades: γ20 = 0.002, p = .25, no incremental validity when cognitive ability was added. Non-significance only; no effect size printed.

> "These hypotheses were also not supported as the force model (γ20 = 0.002, p = .25) was not associated with grades and did not account for incremental variance (see Table 4, Model 3b and 4b)."
