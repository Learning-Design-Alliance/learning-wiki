## Evidence

### Laninga-Wijnen 2018

Laninga-Wijnen, L., Ryan, A., Harakeh, Z., Shin, H., & Vollebergh, W. A. M. (2018). The Moderating Role of Popular Peers' Achievement Goals in 5th- and 6th-Graders' Achievement-Related Friendships: A Social Network Analysis. Journal of Educational Psychology, vol. 110, no. 2, pp. 289-307. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000210

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal social network analysis (RSiena) of 46 Grade 5 and 6 classrooms (N = 901 at Wave 1) comparing classrooms split by quartiles of performance goal popularity norms. The article reports students in high norm classrooms were "more likely (by 229% higher odds, which can be seen as a large effect) to select a friend who matched their" achievement.

> "the parameter for similarity-based selection related to achievement was significantly positive in classrooms with high performance goal popularity norms and significantly negative in classrooms with low performance goal popularity norms"
