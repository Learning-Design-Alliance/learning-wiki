# Children's inhibitory control moderates the association between parental number talk and children's math achievement, with a positive association only at higher inhibitory control

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The interaction between parental number talk and children's inhibitory control predicting children's math achievement was statistically significant, explaining an additional 3.2% of variance. [→ Silver 2022](#silver-2022)

## Evidence

### Silver 2022

Silver, A. M., Elliott, L., & Libertus, M. E. (2022). Parental math input is not uniformly beneficial for young children: The moderating role of inhibitory control. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000679

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Hierarchical regression in a sample of 123 children (Mage = 3.9 years) showed a significant number talk × inhibitory control interaction, "Cohen's f2 = .04", adding 3.2% of variance and ΔF(1,113) = 4.26, p = .041. The effect size printed is Cohen's f², so no standardized d-type impact value is coded.

> "This interaction was significant with a small to medium effect size with Cohen's f2 = .04, and resulted in an additional 3.2% of variance explained in children's math achievement, a significant increase from the first model, ΔF(1,113) = 4.26, p = .041."
