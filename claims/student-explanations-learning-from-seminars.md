# Students most often explained learning from seminars by comparing and discussing answers with peers, applying knowledge to pathophysiology cases, and being stimulated to prepare

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Of 91 respondents, 37% (N = 34) explained they learned from seminars because they could compare and discuss answers with peers, 24% cited applying knowledge in pathophysiology cases, and 21% cited being stimulated to prepare and study before class. [→ Bouwmeester Rianne A. M. 2016](#bouwmeester-rianne-a-m-2016)

## Evidence

### Bouwmeester Rianne A. M. 2016

Bouwmeester Rianne A. M., de Kleijn Renske A. M., van Rijen Harold V. M. (2016). Peer-instructed seminar attendance is associated with improved preparation, deeper learning and higher exam scores: a survey study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0715-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Deductive clustering of open-question answers from 91 students (73.4%) in the final questionnaire about why they learned from seminars; some students gave multiple explanations. The authors take these explanations as suggesting peer discussion and case application underlie the attendance–exam association.

> "approximately 37 % of the students (N = 34) explained they had learned from the seminars because they could compare and discuss their answers with answers given by peers"
