Evidence¶

Sten CW 2024¶

Sten CW, Söderström K, Stensrud B, Nylund IB, Siqveland J. (2024). The effectiveness of virtual reality training on knowledge, skills and attitudes of health care professionals and students in assessing and treating mental health disorders: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05423-0

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Single-group pretest–posttest study of 100 social work and nursing students using a VSP intervention, with an online pretest–posttest survey including Brief Substance Abuse Attitudes Scale items. The review reports "no significant effect of VR training on attitudes"; equivalence was not tested.

"This study found no significant effect of VR training on attitudes and was assessed as having a serious risk of bias."

Sten CW 2024 (2)¶

Sten CW, Söderström K, Stensrud B, Nylund IB, Siqveland J. (2024). The effectiveness of virtual reality training on knowledge, skills and attitudes of health care professionals and students in assessing and treating mental health disorders: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05423-0

q2 · i? · review · r3

The same single-group pretest–posttest study of 100 social work and nursing students assessed perceived competence in screening, brief intervention, and referral to treatment via online survey. The review reports "a significant increase in perceived competence"; no effect size value is printed.

"The study findings demonstrated a significant increase in perceived competence following the VSP intervention."

Related Claims¶

  • VR training improved mental health knowledge in two non-randomized studies but showed no statistically significant effect on diagnostic recognition in a cohort study — related
  • VR training interventions can promote knowledge and skills acquisition in health care professionals and students for assessing and treating mental health disorders, though the evidence base is uncertain — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Virtual standardized patient training improved alcohol screening and brief intervention skills and diagnostic accuracy for depression and PTSD in two RCTs, while a suicide risk assessment RCT found no significant effect — related
  • School-only substance use interventions such as HeadOn and SUPEP improved knowledge and attitudes without corresponding behavioural change — related
  • In the Harris RCT, neither e-learning strategy improved substance abuse management outcomes versus no training — related
  • An RCT using self-reported combined knowledge and skills found a significant within-group pretest-to-posttest increase with a large effect size, but reported no between-group comparison — related
