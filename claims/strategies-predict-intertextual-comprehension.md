# Self-reported accumulation negatively predicts intertextual comprehension while cross-text elaboration positively predicts it, after controlling for prior knowledge

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a hierarchical regression on the 71-student subsample, adding the two strategy scales to prior knowledge produced a statistically significant 11% increment in explained variance in intertextual comprehension. [→ Ivar Bråten & Helge I. Strømsø 2011](#ivar-bråten-helge-i-strømsø-2011)
`q2 i?` Accumulation was a significant negative predictor (β=−.26, p=.024) and cross-text elaboration a positive predictor that did not quite reach conventional significance (β=.22, p=.058). [→ Ivar Bråten & Helge I. Strømsø 2011 (2)](#ivar-bråten-helge-i-strømsø-2011-2)

## Evidence

### Ivar Bråten & Helge I. Strømsø 2011

Ivar Bråten & Helge I. Strømsø. (2011). Measuring strategic processing when students read multiple texts. Metacognition Learning 6:111–130. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-011-9075-7

`q2 · i?`

Hierarchical multiple regression with intertextual comprehension (InterVT) as the dependent variable in the subsample of 71 students; prior knowledge entered at Step 1 (R²=.06), the two strategy scales at Step 2, yielding a "statistically significant 11% increment" in explained variance.

> "The addition of scores on the two strategy measures in Step 2 of the equation resulted in a statistically significant 11% increment in the explained variance, Fchange(2, 64)=4.30, p=.018, R²=.17."

### Ivar Bråten & Helge I. Strømsø 2011 (2)

Ivar Bråten & Helge I. Strømsø. (2011). Measuring strategic processing when students read multiple texts. Metacognition Learning 6:111–130. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-011-9075-7

`q2 · i?`

Step 2 regression coefficients for the two MTSI scales predicting intertextual comprehension. The authors note the unique predictability of cross-text elaboration "did not quite reach a conventional level of statistical significance with this sample size."

> "self-reported use of accumulation negatively predicted intertextual comprehension, β=−.26, p=.024, whereas self-reported use of cross-text elaboration was a positive predictor, β=.22, p=.058"
