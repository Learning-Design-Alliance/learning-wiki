Related Claims¶

  • The gene-environment interplay in Dutch educational performance is an SES composition effect rather than a school quality effect — related
  • Non-parametric moderation analyses show more genetic variance in the lowest-quality schools, with only the first-versus-fourth quantile difference statistically significant — related
  • In Dutch twin data, 61–91% of variance in Cito educational performance is attributable to genetic variance, 0–15% to shared environmental and 9–24% to non-shared environmental variance, depending on the assumed genetic relatedness of same-sex twins — related
  • School quality does not moderate genetic and shared-environmental influences on educational performance once moderation by school SES and parental SES is considered — related
  • Genetic variance in educational performance decreases with increasing school SES, partly independent of parental SES — related
  • Delaying definite tracking is associated with a larger genetic influence and a lower shared-environmental influence on secondary educational attainment among Dutch twins — related
