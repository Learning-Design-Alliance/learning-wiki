# Teachers' motivating styles of autonomy support and structure correlate with lower extraneous cognitive load, while chaos and domineering control correlate with higher extraneous load, in a sinusoidal circumplex pattern

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Extraneous cognitive load showed the highest positive correlations with chaos (awaiting, abandoning) and the domineering subarea of control, and negative correlations with autonomy support (attuning, participative) and structure (clarifying, guiding). [→ Evans 2024](#evans-2024)
`q2 i?` The expected sinusoidal pattern of correlations was also observed for intrinsic cognitive load, load reduction instruction, motivation, engagement, disengagement, and school-reported grades. [→ Evans 2024](#evans-2024)

## Evidence

### Evans 2024

Evans, P., Vansteenkiste, M., Parker, P., Kingsford-Smith, A., & Zhou, S. (2024). Cognitive Load Theory and Its Relationships with Motivation: a Self-Determination Theory Perspective. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09841-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Correlational analysis of SIS circumplex subareas against cognitive load, motivation, engagement, and grades in the N = 1287 sample, plotted in Fig. 3 with 95% confidence intervals. "Extraneous cognitive load showed the highest positive correlations with the teaching style of chaos" and negative correlations with autonomy support and structure, as hypothesized.

> "Extraneous cognitive load showed the highest positive correlations with the teaching style of chaos (awaiting, abandoning) and the domineering subarea of control, and negative correlations with the styles of autonomy support (attuning, participative) and structure (clarifying, guiding)."
