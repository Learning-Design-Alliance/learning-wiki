## Evidence

### Qureshi Muhammad Fazal Hussain; Asif Fozia; Khan Shahmeer Raza; Akhtar Munazza; Shah Muzna; Altaf Ahmed; Latif Asad 2026

Qureshi Muhammad Fazal Hussain; Asif Fozia; Khan Shahmeer Raza; Akhtar Munazza; Shah Muzna; Altaf Ahmed; Latif Asad. (2026). Strengthening patient safety competencies among physician interns through a structured quality and safety course. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09875-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Subgroup analysis using paired t-tests comparing 23 interns with prior patient safety training and 143 without; the untrained group gained 2.57 points (12.15 to 14.72, p < 0.001) versus 0.95 (15.43 to 16.39, p = 0.05) for the trained group, "reflecting lower baseline knowledge in the untrained group".

> "Participants without previous training showed a larger gain in mean test scores (mean difference = 2.57; p < 0.001) compared with those with prior training (mean difference = 0.95; p = 0.05), reflecting lower baseline knowledge in the untrained group"

### Qureshi Muhammad Fazal Hussain; Asif Fozia; Khan Shahmeer Raza; Akhtar Munazza; Shah Muzna; Altaf Ahmed; Latif Asad 2026 (2)

Qureshi Muhammad Fazal Hussain; Asif Fozia; Khan Shahmeer Raza; Akhtar Munazza; Shah Muzna; Altaf Ahmed; Latif Asad. (2026). Strengthening patient safety competencies among physician interns through a structured quality and safety course. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09875-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

In the same stratified analysis, systems thinking improved significantly in both groups (0.20, p = 0.01 trained; 0.12, p < 0.001 untrained) and knowledge efficacy gains were "comparable gains in knowledge efficacy (mean difference = 0.67 vs. 0.66)".

> "Improvements in systems thinking and knowledge efficacy were statistically significant in both groups, with comparable gains in knowledge efficacy (mean difference = 0.67 vs. 0.66)"
