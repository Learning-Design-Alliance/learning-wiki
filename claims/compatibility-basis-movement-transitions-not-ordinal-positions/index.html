Evidence¶

Susanne Dyck & Christian Klaes 2025¶

Susanne Dyck & Christian Klaes. (2025). Faster implicit motor sequence learning of new sequences compatible in terms of movement transitions. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00296-4

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Post hoc test at the start of session 4 in experiment 1: "the Compatible and Incompatible groups showed no difference in performance (p = 1.)", despite their sequences sharing the same movement-transition overlap and differing in ordinal-position overlap. No effect size printed.

"The performance in the Same group was signiﬁcantly higher compared to all other groups (allp < 0.001). Only the Compatible and Incompatible groups showed no difference in performance (p = 1.)."

Susanne Dyck & Christian Klaes 2025 (2)¶

Susanne Dyck & Christian Klaes. (2025). Faster implicit motor sequence learning of new sequences compatible in terms of movement transitions. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00296-4

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion of experiment 1: "the determining factor of compatibility is not the ordinal position, but rather the movement transitions shared with the previously learned sequence", contrasting with King et al.'s explicit-MSL ordinal-position finding.

"At the same time, there was no difference in the performance between the Compatible and Incompatible group evident, thus the determining factor of compatibility is not the ordinal position, but rather the movement transitions shared with the previously learned sequence."

Susanne Dyck & Christian Klaes 2025 (3)¶

Susanne Dyck & Christian Klaes. (2025). Faster implicit motor sequence learning of new sequences compatible in terms of movement transitions. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00296-4

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Baseline (session 1 start) in experiment 2: "the performance of the Incompatible group being lower than in the Compatible group" (p = 0.012); all groups reached comparable performance by the end of session 1. No effect size printed.

"Post hoc tests revealed that only the performance in the Compatible and Incompatible group signiﬁcantly differed ( p = 0.012), with the performance of the Incompatible group being lower than in the Compatible group, while all other comparisons were not signiﬁcant"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • After three prior training sessions, implicit learning of sequences compatible in movement transitions (both Compatible and Incompatible groups) is faster than learning a completely new sequence in experiment 1. — related
  • By the end of the final training session, Compatible and Incompatible groups reach the performance level of the Same group, whereas the New group does not, in both experiments. — related
  • In experiment 1, novel movement transitions were performed better in the Compatible and Incompatible groups than in the New group, while initially learned transitions were retained better in the Same group. — related
  • Switching from a trained implicit motor sequence to a new, compatible, or incompatible sequence significantly reduces sequence-specific performance compared with continuing the same sequence, in both experiments. — related
