## Subclaims
`q2 i?` California collects enrollment and supply data from multiple sources but does not centrally aggregate them, and counties lack the staff, technological capacity, and funding to collect, share, and analyze data. [→ Melnick 2018](#melnick-2018)

## Evidence

### Melnick 2018

Melnick, H., Meloy, B., Gardner, M., Wechsler, M., & Maier, A. (2018). Building an early learning system that works: Next steps for California. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/early-learning-ca

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Report's data systems section, from county interviews and document review, finds fragmented data collection across programmatic and geographic lines that creates an unfunded burden for counties; workforce data are lacking while QRIS yields some program quality data.

> "The state collects enrollment and supply data from multiple sources, but these data are not centrally aggregated, thus limiting their utility. Through QRIS, county administrators have begun to provide valuable data on program quality, but workforce data are lacking."
