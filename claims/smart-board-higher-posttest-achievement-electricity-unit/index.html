Evidence¶

Aktas 2016¶

Aktas, S., & Aydin, A. (2016). The effect of the smart board usage in science and technology lessons. Eurasian Journal of Educational Research, 64, 125-138. http://dx.doi.org/10.14689/ejer.2016.64.7

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Control-group quasi-experiment with two intact 7th-grade classes (experimental N=37, control N=38). Post-test means were 19.11 (experimental) versus 16.63 (control); the article reports "a significant difference statistically was found between the achievement scores of the two groups" with t=2.431, p=.018.

"A significant difference statistically was found between the achievement scores of the two groups (t=2.431, p<.05). Therefore, the teaching methods with smart boards used in the experimental group increased the students' success more than the traditional Science and Technology Curriculum applied to the control group."
