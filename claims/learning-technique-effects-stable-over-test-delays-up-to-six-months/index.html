Evidence¶

Donoghue & Hattie 2021¶

Donoghue, G. M., & Hattie, J. A. C. (2021). A Meta-Analysis of Ten Learning Techniques. Frontiers in Education, 6, 581216. doi:10.3389/feduc.2021.581216

q3 · meta-analysis (moderator analysis by test delay) · i2 · medium effect at every delay, d = 0.51–0.59 · n=242 studies · quant-synthesis · r?

Most effects (68%) were measured within a day, usually immediately; the practice techniques (practice testing, distributed and interleaved practice) supplied most of the delayed tests. Mean effects were 0.58 under a day (1,073 effects), 0.59 from a day to a week (204), 0.56 from a week to a month (228) and 0.51 from one to six months (64); the authors report no overall differences.

"There were no overall differences relating to the effects less than a day (d = 0.58, SE = 0.025, N = 1,073), > 1 day and < 1 week (d = 0.59, SE = 0.057, N = 204), > 1 week and < 1 month (d = 0.56, SE = 0.058, N = 228), < 1 month and less than 6 months (d = 0.51, SE = 0.082, N = 64)."

Discussion¶

The long-delay effects come mostly from the practice techniques, whose benefits are known to grow with delay, so this is not evidence that every technique holds up over months. Only 64 effects were measured beyond a month.
