## Evidence

### McCaughan E.M. 2021

McCaughan E.M., Flannagan C., Parahoo K., Bingham S.L., Brady N., Connaghan J., Maguire R., Thompson S., Jain S., Kirby M., O'Connor S.R. (2021). Effects of a Brief E-Learning Resource on Sexual Attitudes and Beliefs of Healthcare Professionals Working in Prostate Cancer Care: A Pilot Study. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph181910045

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pairwise comparisons following repeated measures ANOVA (overall F = 12.192; P = 0.001) on the modified SABS in the 44-participant pilot, showing a maintained attitudinal shift toward involving partners in sexual wellbeing discussions.

> "Analysis of agreement with the statement that 'sexual issues should be discussed with partners' revealed significant differences between T0 and T1 (mean score/4 = 2.93 vs. 3.45, respectively: P = 0.001) and between T0 and T2 (mean score/4 = 2.93 vs. 3.39, respectively: P = 0.001)"
