## Evidence

### Josephine C. Meyer 2026

Josephine C. Meyer, Molly Griston, Gina Passante, Steven J. Pollock, and Bethany R. Wilcox (2026). Measuring student understanding in quantum computing: Development and validation of the Quantum Computing Conceptual Survey. [doi:10.1103/svp6-ffxn](https://doi.org/10.1103/svp6-ffxn)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

CTT item analysis of v2.2 pilot responses shows 'all difficulties falling between 0.2 and 0.9'; the authors note many items have structurally very low guessing floors, so slightly harder items are not a concern.

> "As such, we are not concerned about items that are slightly harder than the originally suggested threshold. Figure 4 shows the item difficulties, with all difficulties falling between 0.2 and 0.9."

### Josephine C. Meyer 2026 (2)

Josephine C. Meyer, Molly Griston, Gina Passante, Steven J. Pollock, and Bethany R. Wilcox (2026). Measuring student understanding in quantum computing: Development and validation of the Quantum Computing Conceptual Survey. [doi:10.1103/svp6-ffxn](https://doi.org/10.1103/svp6-ffxn)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

CTT discrimination analysis used the point-biserial correlation with auto-correlation dropped; all v2.2 items show discriminations 'between 0.26 and 0.51', which the authors describe as 'indicating sufficient discrimination based on CTT criteria'.

> "Figure 5 shows the item discriminations, with all items having a discrimination between 0.26 and 0.51, indicating sufficient discrimination based on CTT criteria."
