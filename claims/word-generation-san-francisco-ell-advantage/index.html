Evidence¶

Snow 2014¶

Catherine E. Snow. (2014). Extended discourse in first and second language acquisition: A challenge and an opportunity! Journal of Japanese Linguistics 30. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED571513

q3 · i? · review · r2

Multi-site experimental study of the full 24-week program with schools randomly assigned within three cities; San Francisco had a very high proportion of current and former English language learners. Program students learned more words on average than controls, and language minority students learned more than monolingual English speakers.

"students receiving t h e p r o g r a m l e a r n e d m o r e w o r d s , o n average, than students not in the program, and students from language minority households learned more than students who were monolingual English speakers."

Snow 2014 (2)¶

Catherine E. Snow. (2014). Extended discourse in first and second language acquisition: A challenge and an opportunity! Journal of Japanese Linguistics 30. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED571513

q3 · i? · theoretical · r2

Disaggregated analysis by years since reclassification as fully English proficient (Hwang, Lawrence, Mo, & Snow, 2014), possible because of the large number of ELLs in the San Francisco sample. Former ELLs outperformed English-only classmates for four years, the difference diminishing every year.

"Students who had been classified as ELL continued to outperform English-only classmates in their learning of words from the Word Generation program for four years – with the difference between bilingual and monolingual students diminishing every year."
