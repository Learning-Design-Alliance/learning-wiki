## Evidence

### Canning 2018

Canning, E. A., Harackiewicz, J. M., Priniski, S. J., Hecht, C. A., Tibbetts, Y., & Hyde, J. S. (2018). Improving Performance and Retention in Introductory Biology with a Utility-Value Intervention. Journal of educational psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000244

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Binary logistic regression on self-reported major at beginning and end of semester, conducted with the 534 students (93%) who initially reported a STEM major. The article reports "96.1% of students in the UV conditions remained in a STEM major" versus 89.2% of controls; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Among these students, we found that students in the UV conditions were more likely to persist in their STEM major, Wald = 4.44, B = .51, p = .035 (see Table 3). That is, 96.1% of students in the UV conditions remained in a STEM major from the beginning to the end of the semester, compared to 89.2% of students in the control condition."
