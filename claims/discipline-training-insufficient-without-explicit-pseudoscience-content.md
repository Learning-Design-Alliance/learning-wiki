## Evidence

### Franz 2013

Franz, T. M., & Green, K. H. (2013). The impact of an interdisciplinary learning community course on pseudoscientific reasoning in first-year science students. Journal of the Scholarship of Teaching and Learning, 13(5), 90–105. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1017127

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The article's interpretive argument, drawn from prior literature it cites (e.g., Farha & Steward, 2006, finding higher education does not necessarily lower paranormal beliefs); it concludes that without explicitly applying critical thinking to pseudoscience content, students may hold uncritical beliefs while developing strong reasoning skills.

> "Thus it seems that even further training within a scientific discipline, which one expects to focus on scientific reasoning, is not sufficient to eliminate a student's beliefs in paranormal (or more broadly, pseudoscientific) phenomena, suggesting that individuals possess the capacity to engage in different modes of thinking selectively even though the selection may not be made a conscious level."
