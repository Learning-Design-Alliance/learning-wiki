# Children begin applying racial stereotypes to out-groups around age 6, with out-group stereotyping increasing across age groups while in-group stereotyping shows no reliable age differences

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Out-group stereotyping increased with age (main effect of Age group, F(3, 85) = 6.95, p < .001, η2 = .20), with 6–7-year-olds (M = .60) and 7–10-year-olds (M = .67) exhibiting more out-group stereotyping than younger groups. [→ Pauker 2010](#pauker-2010)
`q3 i?` In-group stereotyping showed no reliable age group differences (F(3, 85) = .994, p = .40, η2 = .03). [→ Pauker 2010](#pauker-2010)

## Evidence

### Pauker 2010

Pauker, K., Ambady, N., & Apfelbaum, E. P. (2010). Race Salience and Essentialist Thinking in Racial Stereotype Development. Child Development, 81(6), 1799–1813. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2010.01511.x

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Cross-sectional ANOVA of stereotyping scores in 89 children ages 3–10 (four age groups: preschoolers n=21, kindergarteners n=36, 1st graders n=16, 2nd–4th graders n=16), recruited from suburban public elementary schools and the local community outside Boston, Massachusetts. Simple-effects tests on the Group × Age group interaction showed "age group differences were reliable for out-group" stereotyping with a large printed effect size, η2 = .30, but not in-group stereotyping (η2 = .03).

> "Follow-up simple-effects tests used to clarify this interaction indicated that age group differences were reliable for out-group, F(3, 85) = 12.38, p < .0001, η2 = .30, but not in-group stereotypes, F(3, 85) = .994, p = .40, η2 =.03."
