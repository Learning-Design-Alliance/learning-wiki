## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For the same cohorts at all three institutions, there is no statistically significant difference in end-of-course examination performance between male and female students. [→ Simon Bates 2012](#simon-bates-2012)

## Evidence

### Simon Bates 2012

Simon Bates, Robyn Donnelly, Cait MacPhee, David Sands, Marion Birch, Niels R. Walet. (2012). Gender differences in conceptual understanding of Newtonian mechanics: a UK cross-institution comparison. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/34/2/421](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/34/2/421)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of end-of-course examination results for the same matched cohorts at Edinburgh, Hull and Manchester found 'no statistically signiﬁcant diﬀerence in examination performance for male and female cohorts', by t-test at the 5% significance level; equivalence was not tested.

> "Nevertheless, for the same cohorts of students at each of the 3 institutions in this study, we ﬁnd that there is no statistically signiﬁcant diﬀerence in examination performance for male and female cohorts, as determined by a t-test at the 5% signiﬁcance level."
