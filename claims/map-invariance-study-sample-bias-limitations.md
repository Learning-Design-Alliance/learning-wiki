# The study's invariance findings may be biased by excluding students who missed a test and by sample heterogeneity

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 associational `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Students who missed one test were excluded from the longitudinal sample, which the authors state might introduce sample bias. [→ Wang 2013](#wang-2013)
`q1 i?` Samples were heterogeneous with respect to gender and ethnicity, and the impact of this heterogeneity on model parameter estimates is unknown. [→ Wang 2013 (2)](#wang-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Wang 2013

Wang, S., Jiao, H. and Zhang, L. (2013) 'Validation of longitudinal achievement constructs of vertically scaled computerised adaptive tests: a multiple-indicator, latent-growth modelling approach', Int. J. Quantitative Research in Education, Vol. 1, No. 4, pp.383–407. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/validation-of-longitudinal-achievement-constructs-of-vertically-scaled-computerised-adaptive-tests-a-multiple-indicator-latent-growth-modelling-approach/

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The authors' own stated limitation in the discussion: complete five-wave participation was required, so "students who missed one test were excluded from the sample". No effect size is printed; this is an author interpretation, not a tested result.

> "However, since the study was based on longitudinal data, students who missed one test were excluded from the sample, which might introduce sample bias."

### Wang 2013 (2)

Wang, S., Jiao, H. and Zhang, L. (2013) 'Validation of longitudinal achievement constructs of vertically scaled computerised adaptive tests: a multiple-indicator, latent-growth modelling approach', Int. J. Quantitative Research in Education, Vol. 1, No. 4, pp.383–407. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/validation-of-longitudinal-achievement-constructs-of-vertically-scaled-computerised-adaptive-tests-a-multiple-indicator-latent-growth-modelling-approach/

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Stated limitation: the pooled state samples were heterogeneous by gender and ethnicity, and the article states the impact on model parameter estimates is unknown. A cited prior study found statistically significant different rates of change by gender. No effect size is printed.

> "All samples used in this study are not homogeneous with respect to at least gender and ethnicity, and the impact of heterogeneity on model parameter estimates is unknown."
