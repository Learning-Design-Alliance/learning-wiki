## Subclaims
`q3 i?` As confidence increased, participants experienced more surprise, curiosity, and confusion after incorrect answers, and high-confidence errors aroused anger. [→ Berweger 2023](#berweger-2023)

## Evidence

### Berweger 2023

Berweger, B., Kracke, B., & Dietrich, J. (2023). Preservice Teachers' Epistemic and Achievement Emotions When Confronted With Common Misconceptions About Education. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000792

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bayesian multilevel analysis (Model 1) of 275 preservice teachers over 15 trials testing the accuracy x confidence interaction on emotions. The interaction was significant for surprise, curiosity, confusion, and anger, e.g., "surprise: .343 CI [.310, .377]"; coefficients are unstandardized and no effect size is printed.

> "That is, participants’ increasing confidence intensified their epistemic emotions after incorrect answers (surprise: .343 CI [.310, .377], curiosity: .096 [.061, .128], confusion: .313 [.276, .353]; solid lines in Figure 2)"
