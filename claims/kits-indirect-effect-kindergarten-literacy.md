# The KITS intervention has a significant indirect effect on kindergarten teacher-rated literacy through its effect on pre-kindergarten literacy skills (β = .09).

> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Improving pre-kindergarten literacy before school entry carries the intervention effect forward to better teacher-rated reading and writing skills in the fall of kindergarten. [→ Pears 2016](#pears-2016)

## Evidence

### Pears 2016

Pears, K. C., Kim, H. K., Fisher, P., & Yoerger, K. (2016). Increasing pre-kindergarten early literacy skills in children with developmental disabilities and delays. Journal of School Psychology, 57, 15-27. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsp.2016.05.004

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

SEM indirect-path test using the MODEL INDIRECT function in Mplus, with kindergarten literacy measured by teacher ratings on the CELF-P Pre-Literacy Rating Scale at T3. The article reports "a significant effect (β = .09, z = 3.06, p < .01)"; only teacher data were available at T3.

> "To test the hypothesis that the intervention has indirect effects on kindergarten literacy skills, the significance of the path from the intervention to kindergarten literacy through T2 pre-kindergarten literacy was tested. This was a significant effect (β = .09, z = 3.06, p < .01)."
