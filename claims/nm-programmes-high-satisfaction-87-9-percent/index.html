Remein Christy DiFrances C, Childs E, Pasco JC, Trinquart L, Flynn DB, Wingerter SL, Bhasin RM, Demers LB, Benjamin EJ. (2020). Content and outcomes of narrative medicine programmes: a systematic review of the literature through 2019. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2019-031568

The review's synthesis of satisfaction reporting across the 55 programmes. The authors calculated the combined agreement figure of 87.9% and note satisfaction "were reported to be high" across quantitative and qualitative evaluations.

"NM satisfaction scores were reported to be high, with the combined per cent agree or strongly agree to the satisfaction measures as 87.9% (our calculation)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • A systematic review through 2019 identified 55 unique narrative medicine programmes in academic health sciences, of which 75% reported some form of evaluation with inconsistent methods
  • A quarter of NM programmes (n=14, 25.5%) provided no evaluation details, and no programme used a well-described evaluation to assess gains in writing competence or confidence — related
  • Among well-described quantitative NM programme evaluations, statistically significant impacts were reported for increased empathy (three programmes) and increased perspective-taking/reflection (one programme) — related
  • Well-described qualitative evaluations of NM programmes reported positive impacts most frequently on relationship-building (15 programmes), empathy (10) and perspective-taking/reflection (10) — related
