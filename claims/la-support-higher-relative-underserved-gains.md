## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Although raw gaps were unchanged, LA-supported courses reduced relative gaps in gains because underserved students' gains were 87% of dominant students' gains, versus 80% without LAs. [→ Ben Van Dusen and Jayson M. Nissen 2018](#ben-van-dusen-and-jayson-m-nissen-2018)

## Evidence

### Ben Van Dusen and Jayson M. Nissen 2018

Ben Van Dusen and Jayson M. Nissen. (2018). Systemic inequities in introductory physics courses: the impacts of learning assistants. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/perc.2017.pr.095](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.095)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Secondary analysis of gains (rather than posttest levels) from the same HLM study of 4,365 students in 93 courses. The article reports LA courses have "higher relative gains for students who are from underserved groups, 87% versus 80%" of the dominant-background gain. These are printed descriptive percentages; no inferential test is reported for this specific contrast.

> "Courses with LAs have higher relative gains for students who are from underserved groups, 87% versus 80%, compared to the gain for students from dominant backgrounds."
