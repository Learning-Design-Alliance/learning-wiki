# Starting with an example strongly benefits first practice problem performance, with a larger effect for gifted students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` EPEP students outperformed PEPE students on the first practice problem for both gifted (d = 2.07) and nongifted (d = 1.06) students, with a significant Giftedness × Task Sequence interaction. [→ Coppens 2019](#coppens-2019)

## Evidence

### Coppens 2019

Coppens, L. C., Hoogerheide, V., Snippe, E. M., Flunger, B., & van Gog, T. (2019). Effects of problem–example and example–problem pairs on gifted and nongifted primary school students' learning. Instructional Science, 47, 279–297. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-019-09484-3

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA on first practice problem showed a significant interaction, F(1, 121) = 15.94, p < .001; Bonferroni-corrected t-tests showed "performance on the first problem was better for those in the EPEP condition than those in the PEPE condition" for both groups.

> "performance on the first problem was better for those in the EPEP condition than those in the PEPE condition, both for gifted students, t(63) = 7.94, p < .001, d = 2.07, and for nongifted students, t(59) = 2.89, p = .005, d = 1.06"
