## Evidence

### Kramer A 2025

Kramer A, Foley J, Hansen C, Teramoto M. (2025). Parent‐reported academic outcomes after a mild traumatic brain injury in the pediatric population. J Sch Health. https://doi.org/10.1111/josh.13502

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional survey of 285 parents of children with mTBI (2010-2013 ED/trauma cohort); contingency table analysis showed 43.4% of c-mTBI children had negative grade change versus 20.4% of mTBI children, "significantly more likely to suffer negative grade changes (p < .001)". No effect size printed for this contingency contrast; the regression below prints OR = 2.31.

> "Children sustaining c‐mTBI were significantly more likely to suffer negative grade changes (p < .001), whereas such observation wasn't found in children with mTBI or mTBI‐SF (p > .05)."

### Kramer A 2025 (2)

Kramer A, Foley J, Hansen C, Teramoto M. (2025). Parent‐reported academic outcomes after a mild traumatic brain injury in the pediatric population. J Sch Health. https://doi.org/10.1111/josh.13502

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Multiple logistic regression on negative grade changes (yes/no) adjusting for classification, recovery, years since injury, sex, age, and accommodations; the printed effect size was "OR = 2.31; 95% CI = 1.01, 5.29; p = .048", with predicted probability 0.34 for c-mTBI versus 0.23 for mTBI.

> "the odds of negative grade changes for children with c‐mTBI was more than twice the odds for those with mTBI (odds ratio [OR] = 2.31; 95% confidence interval [CI] = 1.01, 5.29; p = .048)"
