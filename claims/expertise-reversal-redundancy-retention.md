## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Experts who received additional information scored significantly worse on retention than experts without it (d = 0.72). [→ Rey 2011](#rey-2011)

## Evidence

### Rey 2011

Günter Daniel Rey, Florian Buchwald. (2011). The Expertise Reversal Effect: Cognitive Load and Motivational Explanations. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0022243

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Same experiment, expert contrast on retention in Results: experts with additional text scored worse, t(50) = 2.54, p = .01, and "The effect size (Cohen's d) was 0.72 for retention," reversing the novice pattern.

> "Contrary to novices, experts who received additional information scored significantly, t(50) ⫽ 2.54, p ⫽ .01 worse on the retention test (M ⫽ 8.04, SD ⫽ 2.03) than experts who did not receive additional information (M ⫽ 9.23, SD ⫽ 1.27)."
