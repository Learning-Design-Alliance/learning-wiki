# Global-fit evidence does not clearly favor unidimensional over multidimensional academic resilience measurement models, as fit-index pass rates diverge by index

## Evidence

### Könül Karimova 2026

Könül Karimova, Hsing-Fen Tu. (2026). Dimensionality, Reliability, and Validity of Academic Resilience Scales: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10207-7

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Systematic review synthesis of model-fit pass rates across included validation studies (Table 4). The review reports that "all available unidimensional models met the CFI criterion, compared with 11 of 24 multidimensional models," while RMSEA showed the opposite pattern, so conclusions depend on which fit index is emphasized.

> "Unidimensional models showed higher pass rates for CFI, TLI, and SRMR. Specifically, all available unidimensional models met the CFI criterion, compared with 11 of 24 multidimensional models."

### Könül Karimova 2026 (2)

Könül Karimova, Hsing-Fen Tu. (2026). Dimensionality, Reliability, and Validity of Academic Resilience Scales: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10207-7

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Exploratory Welch tests comparing reported global fit indices by dimensionality (Table 3). The review reports significant differences for CFI (t = −4.70) and TLI (t = −2.95) but not RMSEA or SRMR, and cautions these are descriptive, not formal meta-analytic comparisons.

> "The unidimensional and multidimensional model families differed for CFI, t = −4.70, df = 26.03, and TLI, t = −2.95, df = 13.69, but not for RMSEA, t = −0.26, or SRMR, t = 1.85, df = 13.64."
