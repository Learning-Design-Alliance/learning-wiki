Evidence¶

Harutyunyan and Poveda 2018¶

Liliya Harutyunyan & María Fernanda Poveda. (2018). Students’ Perception of Peer Review in an EFL Classroom. English Language Teaching, 11(4). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v11n4p138

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Questionnaire results on critical thinking indicators from 44 participants who used peer review: 34 students in the experimental groups of an EFL academic-writing course at a university in Ecuador, plus 10 academic and administrative staff from a similarly designed extensive course. The article reports "More than 75% of students responded positively"; its text says there were no option-D (totally negative) answers except on identifying a proper academic writing essay, but its table shows one option-D answer on that item and one on a second item near the top of the list (the extracted table aligns it with "Reevaluate my paper"). The article's prose says identifying proper academic writing "ranks slightly lower", but in its table that item has 38 of 44 positive (A or B) answers while two other items have 37. No comparison with the control group is reported for these perception items.

"More than 75% of students responded positively to the vast majority of critical thinking indicators. While the ability to identify proper Academic Writing ranks slightly lo wer, students’ comments reveal that progress is also being made in this area"
