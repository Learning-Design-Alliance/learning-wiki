# High-risk students (high stress plus elevated cortisol) show increased risk of lower GPA in both semesters (RR 3.10 and 2.54)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Compared with low-risk students, high-risk students had significantly increased risk of attaining lower GPA in semester I (adjusted RR = 3.10, 95% CI 1.77–4.51, p < 0.001) and semester II (RR = 2.54, 95% CI 1.34–4.10, p = 0.006). [→ Pathmavarthini G 2026](#pathmavarthini-g-2026)

## Evidence

### Pathmavarthini G 2026

Pathmavarthini G, Ughanthinii K, Shalini SB, Rubini SS, Trisha P, Devaashiny SV, Nursiati MT, Fatiah RMRZ, Dewi RR, Anwar ZK. (2026). Prediction of academic outcomes by serum cortisol, perceived academic stress, perceived memory performance, and physical activity among preclinical undergraduate medical students: a short-term observational study. BMC Psychology 14:557. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-04327-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Prospective observational cohort of 106 preclinical medical students followed across two semesters; multivariate ordinal regression adjusted for gender, ethnicity, student type, and academic year. Semester I adjusted RR = 3.10 (95% CI 1.77–4.51); the article reports a semester II RR of 2.54 (p = 0.006). No standardised effect size was printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "students classified within the high-risk group demonstrated a significantly increased risk of attaining a lower GPA, with an adjusted relative risk (RR) of 3.10 (95% CI = 1.77–4.51, p < 0.001)"
