## Evidence

### Tamara van Gog & Liesbeth Kester & Kim Dirkx & Vincent Hoogerheide & Joris Boerboom & Peter P. J. L. Verkoeijen 2015

Tamara van Gog & Liesbeth Kester & Kim Dirkx & Vincent Hoogerheide & Joris Boerboom & Peter P. J. L. Verkoeijen. (2015). Testing After Worked Example Study Does Not Enhance Delayed Problem-Solving Performance Compared to Restudy. Educ Psychol Rev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-015-9297-3

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 3 delayed-test ANOVA main effect of task format: "participants who tested/restudied isomorphic tasks performed better ( M=63.63 %, SD=30.20) on the delayed test than participants who tested/restudied identical tasks (M=52 .31 %, SD=31.8 2)". The authors caution the isomorphic testing condition's somewhat higher prior knowledge may have contributed, and suggest a variability effect is possible.

> "There was a significant main effect of task format, F(1,122)=4.40, p=0.038, ηp2=0.035, indicating that participants who tested/restudied isomorphic tasks performed better ( M=63.63 %, SD=30.20) on the delayed test than participants who tested/restudied identical tasks (M=52 .31 %, SD=31.8 2)"
