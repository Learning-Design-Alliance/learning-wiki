## Evidence

### Nicholas T. Young 2023

Nicholas T. Young, Mark Mills, Rebecca L. Matz, Eric F. Bell, and Caitlin Hayward. (2023). Complex multiple-choice questions are inequitable for low-income and domestic students (and difficult for everyone). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2306.11068](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2306.11068)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive comparison of accuracy by demographic group (Figure 5). All groups performed better on non-CMC questions; gaps varied between groups, with "about 11%" for B/H/M/N and female students. No standardized effect size printed.

> "the CMC-non-CMC performance gap was smallest for international students (4%) and largest for Black, Hispanic, Multiracial, and Native American students and female students (about 11% for both groups)."

### Nicholas T. Young 2023 (2)

Nicholas T. Young, Mark Mills, Rebecca L. Matz, Eric F. Bell, and Caitlin Hayward. (2023). Complex multiple-choice questions are inequitable for low-income and domestic students (and difficult for everyone). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2306.11068](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2306.11068)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Simple mixed effects logistic regression models, one per demographic variable. The sex-by-question-type interaction was significant (Table IV odds ratio 0.81, p < .05); no other demographic interaction reached significance in these models. No standardized effect size printed.

> "our model finds that female students are predicted to answer CMC questions correctly disproportionately less often than male students as compared to non-CMC questions."

### Nicholas T. Young 2023 (3)

Nicholas T. Young, Mark Mills, Rebecca L. Matz, Eric F. Bell, and Caitlin Hayward. (2023). Complex multiple-choice questions are inequitable for low-income and domestic students (and difficult for everyone). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2306.11068](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2306.11068)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Single full mixed effects model with all demographics, GPAO, ACT, and interactions (Table V). SES interaction OR 1.22 and international interaction OR 1.43 were significant; sex (0.85) and race (0.79) interactions were marginal (p < .10). No standardized effect size printed.

> "The interactions between socioeconomic status and question type and between international status and question type are statistically different from one while the interactions between sex and question type and between race and question type are marginally significant."
