## Evidence

### Phillips 2017

Phillips, Andrew J. K.; Clerx, William M.; O'Brien, Conor S.; Sano, Akane; Barger, Laura K.; Picard, Rosalind W.; Lockley, Steven W.; Klerman, Elizabeth B.; Czeisler, Charles A. (2017). Irregular sleep/wake patterns are associated with poorer academic performance and delayed circadian and sleep/wake timing. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-03171-4

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Diary-based comparison of the 12 Regular and 12 Irregular quintile groups in the 30-day observational study, quantifying daytime sleep episodes and compensatory sleep with rank-sum tests.

> "Irregular sleepers (Fig. 2B) averaged more daytime sleep ep﻿isodes (naps) per week than Regular sleepers (3.02 ± 1.47 vs. 0.75 ± 0.80; p < 0.002, rank-sum test) and obtained more daytime compensatory sleep per week (5.35 ± 2.82 h vs. 0.72 ± 0.65 h; p < 0.0005, rank-sum test)."

### Phillips 2017 (2)

Phillips, Andrew J. K.; Clerx, William M.; O'Brien, Conor S.; Sano, Akane; Barger, Laura K.; Picard, Rosalind W.; Lockley, Steven W.; Klerman, Elizabeth B.; Czeisler, Charles A. (2017). Irregular sleep/wake patterns are associated with poorer academic performance and delayed circadian and sleep/wake timing. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-03171-4

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Total sleep duration comparison between the same quintile groups showed no significant difference; the authors interpret this as homeostatic control forcing compensatory daytime sleep in Irregular sleepers.

> "There was no significant difference in average daily sleep duration between the Irregular (7.16 ± 0.64 h) and Regular (7.27 ± 0.59 h) groups (p = 0.68, t-test)."
