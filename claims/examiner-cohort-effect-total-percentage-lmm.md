# Examiner cohort explained 5.7% of total percentage score variance in a fully nested OSCE, with a maximal cohort adjustment difference of 5.7% (Cohen's d = 1.06) for the same student ability

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Linear mixed modelling showed examiner cohort explained 5.7% of total percentage score variance, with cohort adjustments ranging from −3.2% to +2.5%, a Cohen's d of 1.06. [→ Yeates P 2019](#yeates-p-2019)
`q3 i?` Five of seven remaining examiner cohorts were statistically different (p < 0.05) from the lowest-scoring cohort (cohort 4) in their scoring tendencies. [→ Yeates P 2019](#yeates-p-2019)

## Evidence

### Yeates P 2019

Yeates P, Cope N, Hawarden A, Bradshaw H, McCray G, Homer M. (2019). Developing a video‐based method to compare and adjust examiner effects in fully nested OSCEs. Medical Education, 53(3), 250–263. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.13783

`q3 · i3` · `design · r2`

Linear mixed modelling of total percentage scores for 116 students, with station and cohort as fixed effects and student ability as a random effect. "station explained 26.1%, examiner cohort 5.7% and student ability 68.1% of the total score variance"; marginal R2 0.09, conditional R2 0.17.

> "Score adjustments for the examiner cohorts (i.e. the scores the different examiner cohorts would give to the same student performance) ranged from −3.2% for examiner‐cohort 4 to +2.5% for examiner‐cohort 5 (relative to the mean cohort adjustment), a difference of 5.7%. Given that the standard deviation for the adjusted values for student's ability on this measure was 5.4%, this represents a Cohen's d effect size of 1.06."
