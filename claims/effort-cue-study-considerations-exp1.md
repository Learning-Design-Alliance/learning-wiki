# In Experiment 1, mental effort was negatively related to perceived effectiveness and (via perceived effectiveness) willingness to use interleaved studying, with a more influential role for interleaved than blocked studying

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In path analyses, the more effort invested during interleaved studying, the less effective students perceived it and the less willing they were to use it again. [→ Janssen 2023](#janssen-2023)
`q3 i?` For blocked studying, effort showed only a small indirect negative relationship with perceived effectiveness and no direct or indirect relationship with willingness to use it. [→ Janssen 2023 (2)](#janssen-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Janssen 2023

Janssen, E. M., van Gog, T., van de Groep, L., de Lange, A. J., Knopper, R. L., Onan, E., Wiradhany, W., & de Bruin, A. B. H. (2023). The Role of Mental Effort in Students' Perceptions of the Effectiveness of Interleaved and Blocked Study Strategies and Their Willingness to Use Them. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09797-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1 saturated path models (15 parameters, N = 150) tested direct and indirect effects of mental effort on considerations; the quote reports the indirect path via perceived effectiveness for interleaved studying. No effect sizes are printed for these paths.

> "The more effort students had to invest during interleaved studying, the less effective they perceived this study strategy to be and the less willing they were to use this strategy again."

### Janssen 2023 (2)

Janssen, E. M., van Gog, T., van de Groep, L., de Lange, A. J., Knopper, R. L., Onan, E., Wiradhany, W., & de Bruin, A. B. H. (2023). The Role of Mental Effort in Students' Perceptions of the Effectiveness of Interleaved and Blocked Study Strategies and Their Willingness to Use Them. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09797-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same Experiment 1 path model showed effort during blocked studying "was not directly related" to perceived effectiveness, with only a small indirect negative relationship via prospective judgments of learning.

> "The amount of mental effort students reported during blocked studying was not directly related to how effective they perceived blocked studying to be."
