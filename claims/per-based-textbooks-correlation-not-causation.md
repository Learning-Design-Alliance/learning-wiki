# PER-based textbooks (Matter and Interactions, Six Ideas) present Maxwell's equations as correlations, not cause-effect relationships

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Two introductory texts grounded in physics education research explicitly state that Faraday's law correlates the fields rather than having one create the other. [→ Álvaro Suárez 2022](#alvaro-suarez-2022)

## Evidence

### Álvaro Suárez 2022

Álvaro Suárez, Arturo C. Martí, Kristina Zuza, Jenaro Guisasola. (2022). Cause-effect relationships in Maxwell's equations and their implications in the teaching of electromagnetism in introductory physics courses. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2204.13479](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2204.13479)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article reports that Chabay and Sherwood's Matter and Interactions and Moore's Six Ideas that Shaped Physics expound the association view, quoting Moore's statement that electromagnetic fields "are created only by stationary or moving charged particles" and Chabay and Sherwood's remark that 'magnetic induction' language is "somewhat misleading".

> "So though E and B are correlated by Faraday's law in a given reference frame, correlation is not causation."
