### Dringoli 2024

Dringoli, B. J., Kolosova, K., Rademaker, T. J., Wray, J., Choquette, J., and Hilke, M. (2024). The impact of content sequencing on quantitative problem-solving: A case study in Electromagnetism using an online learning platform. arXiv version of November 2024, first posted 2019 as "Content Sequencing and its Impact on Student Learning in Electromagnetism: Theory and Experiment". [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1910.00145](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1910.00145)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Independent survey of 111 participants from the local STEM community (undergraduates, graduate students, faculty) on content-type and sequence preference (Fig. 8). The article reports preference "shifts from example (undergraduates) towards concept (graduates)".

> "With experience, content type preference shifts from example (undergraduates) towards concept (graduates), and a mix of concept and theory (faculty), consistent with the view that teachers may have beliefs on teaching science that are more traditional than constructivist [59]."
