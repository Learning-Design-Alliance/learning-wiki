## Evidence

### Emily Marshman 2018

Emily Marshman, Zeynep Y. Kalender, Christian Schunn, Timothy Nokes-Malach, and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). A longitudinal analysis of students' motivational characteristics in introductory physics courses: Gender differences. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1139/cjp-2017-0185](https://doi.org/10.1139/cjp-2017-0185)

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

MANOVA at the beginning of calculus-based Physics 1 comparing male and female students on six motivational factors. Follow-up univariate tests showed males higher on self-efficacy, fascination, value and physics intelligence mindset; Table III prints effect sizes including self-efficacy d = 0.75 and fascination d = 0.37.

> "For the calculus -based course, the resul ts of the MANOVA revealed that there was a statistically significant difference in male and female students' overall motivation, F(7,459)=10.344, p<0.001; Wilks' Lambda=0.864."

### Emily Marshman 2018 (2)

Emily Marshman, Zeynep Y. Kalender, Christian Schunn, Timothy Nokes-Malach, and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). A longitudinal analysis of students' motivational characteristics in introductory physics courses: Gender differences. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1139/cjp-2017-0185](https://doi.org/10.1139/cjp-2017-0185)

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

MANOVA at the beginning of algebra-based Physics 1 on the same six motivational factors. Follow-up tests showed males significantly higher on self-efficacy and fascination, and females significantly higher on grit (Table III prints grit effect size -0.26 favoring females).

> "For the algebra -based course, the results of the MANOVA revealed that there was a statistically sig nificant difference in male and female students' overall motivation , F(7,412)=10.213, p<0.001; Wilks' Lambda=0.852."
