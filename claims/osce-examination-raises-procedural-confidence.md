# Confidence in performing catheterisation rose significantly from pre-test to post-test in all teaching groups in a study reporting within-group changes without a comparison group

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Student confidence levels in performing catheterisation rose significantly from pre-test to post-test in all teaching groups, with the change measured within each group and without a comparison group (7.4 ± 0.96 v 8.1 ± 0.68, p = 0.005; 6.5 ± 1.03 v 7.54 ± 1.07, p = 0.01; 5.4 ± 0.79 v 6 ± 1.12, p < 0.05). ## Evidence

### Waters Peadar S 2014

Waters Peadar S, McVeigh Terri, Kelly Brian D, Flaherty Gerard T, Devitt Dara, Barry Kevin, Kerin Michael J. (2014). The acquisition and retention of urinary catheterisation skills using surgical simulator devices: teaching method or student traits. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0264-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated confidence questionnaires across teaching and examination stages in the 42-student study; pre-teaching confidence did not differ between groups (p = 0.59). All three groups rose significantly after the OSCE; no effect size printed.

> "Student confidence levels in performing catheterisation were found to be significantly higher for all teaching groups after they had undergone the examination process (7.4 ± 0.96 v 8.1 ± 0.68, p = 0.005, 6.5 ± 1.03 v 7.54 ± 1.07, p = 0.01 and 5.4 ± 0.79 v 6 ± 1.12 p < 0.05"
