Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • For Year 6 pupils, breakfast club provision produced around two months' additional progress on writing and English measures, while effects on KS2 reading and maths were positive but could have occurred by chance — related
  • Breakfast club provision improved school attendance, resulting in about 26 fewer half-days of absence per year for a class of 30 — related
  • Breakfast club provision produced larger attainment improvements for pupils not eligible for free school meals than for FSM-eligible pupils, though effects were positive for both groups — related
  • Teacher-reported pupil behaviour and concentration improved in breakfast club schools, suggesting benefits spillover to pupils who do not attend the club — related
  • Providing schools with support to run a free universal before-school breakfast club raised Year 2 (KS1) attainment by around two months' additional progress in maths, reading and writing — related
  • Robust RCTs of school breakfast programs failed to find behavioral benefits, with low and variable attendance and a shift from at-home to at-school consumption limiting impact — related
