## Evidence

### selfefficacy-yahaira

Wortman, A., and Schiner, N. (2024, June). Access to powerful technology as a catalyst for career pathway engagement [Project Report]. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/217

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

In the Engagement section, the authors interpret Yahaira's launch-event podcasting experience: her "participation in an authentic and challenging project in the Learning Studio helped develop her self-efficacy." She linked her genuine interest in podcasting to a shared, purposive project.

> "Yahaira's participation in an authentic and challenging project in the Learning Studio helped develop her self-efficacy and her belief that she could succeed as a podcaster in the future."

### selfefficacy-duarte

Wortman, A., and Schiner, N. (2024, June). Access to powerful technology as a catalyst for career pathway engagement [Project Report]. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/217

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

The authors describe Mr. Duarte's planned final project, in which students "will be asked" to find community "clients" and build a website or program solving the client's problem, and write that knowing they have the necessary skills "builds self-efficacy." This is the authors' expectation for a project not yet carried out; no self-efficacy outcome from it is reported.

> "Knowing that they have the skills necessary to complete the project and provide a useful service to someone else provides for a deeply engaging experience for the students and builds self-efficacy."
