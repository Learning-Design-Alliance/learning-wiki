## Evidence

### Thibault Asselborn 2021

Thibault Asselborn, Wafa Johal, Bolat Tleubayev, Zhanel Zhexenova, Pierre Dillenbourg, Catherine McBride and Anara Sandygulova. (2021). The transferability of handwriting skills: from the Cyrillic to the Latin alphabet. npj Science of Learning 6:6. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00084-w

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Spearman rank correlation analysis (Table 2) of 18 features across the two alphabets for grades 1–4; the table prints Mean Velocity correlations of r = 0.72 in grades 1 and 2 and Handwriting Density r = 0.73–0.75, large positive effects.

> "In general, the features seem to be highly positively correlated between the two alphabets. Indeed, 90% of the correlations we computed were signi ﬁcant. This is a very interesting result since it shows that children are able to transfer their handwriting speci ﬁcity from one alphabet to another."

### Thibault Asselborn 2021 (2)

Thibault Asselborn, Wafa Johal, Bolat Tleubayev, Zhanel Zhexenova, Pierre Dillenbourg, Catherine McBride and Anara Sandygulova. (2021). The transferability of handwriting skills: from the Cyrillic to the Latin alphabet. npj Science of Learning 6:6. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00084-w

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion of the correlation result: handwriting difficulties in one script predict similar relative difficulties in the other script. The article prints no effect size for this interpretive claim.

> "This correlation can also be interpreted as an indicator of transfer: A speci ﬁc pattern of pressure used to write one shape in an alphabet will be used to write a similar shape in another alphabet. Interestingly, this correlation means that a child suffering form severe dif ﬁculties in Cyrillic will likely experience similar dif ﬁculties in Latin."
