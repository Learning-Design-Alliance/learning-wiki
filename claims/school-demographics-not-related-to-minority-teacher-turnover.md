# After controlling for teacher and school background, school demographic characteristics were not significantly related to minority teacher turnover

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Poverty enrollment, minority enrollment, and proportion of minority faculty showed no consistent, statistically significant relationship with minority teacher departures. [→ Ingersoll 2017](#ingersoll-2017)
`q2 i?` In contrast, these same demographic characteristics predicted higher nonminority turnover. [→ Ingersoll 2017](#ingersoll-2017)

## Evidence

### Ingersoll 2017

Ingersoll, R., May, H., & Collins, G. (2017). Minority teacher recruitment, employment, and retention: 1987 to 2013. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/minority-teacher-recruitment

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Logistic regression models on SASS/TFS public school data (Tables 5a and 5b; minority teacher N 6,766–6,181; school N 2,977–3,304 depending on model). The report found "minority teachers, on average, did not depart at significantly different rates" across school demographic conditions, while nonminority turnover rose with poverty, minority enrollment, and minority faculty.

> "after controlling for the background characteristics of teachers and schools, minority teachers, on average, did not depart at significantly different rates from schools with different poverty levels, with different minority student levels, or with different proportions of minority faculty"
