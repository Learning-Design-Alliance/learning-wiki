# The mutual-attraction argument shows only what to expect, not a justification to intervene, because 'more women should be enrolled' does not imply 'we should enroll more women'

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Even if demographic and benefit arguments make greater female enrollment expected, they provide no justification for actively increasing enrollment if it does not happen spontaneously. [→ Mathieu Bouville 2006](#mathieu-bouville-2006)

## Evidence

### Mathieu Bouville 2006

Mathieu Bouville. (2006). Should there be more women in science and engineering?. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0611089](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0611089)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Philosophical argument (Section II.C) using a champagne-bottle analogy and Appiah's point that others have no reason to force better lives upon us. The author concludes the argument "tells us what to expect but does not provide us with any justiﬁcation to make it happen".

> "this argument by mutual attraction tells us what to expect but does not provide us with any justiﬁcation to make it happen if it does not spontaneously take place. In other words, ‘more women should be enrolled’ does not imply ‘we should enroll more women.’"
