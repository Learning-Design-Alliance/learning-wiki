# Displaced students in the highest-achieving receiving schools gained about a month in reading and over two months in math above expectations, while those in the weakest schools lost achievement

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the first year after displacement, students in top-quartile receiving schools scored almost a month above expected in reading and more than two months above in math, while bottom-quartile receiving school students lost over a month in reading and half a month in math. [→ Marisa de la Torre 2009](#marisa-de-la-torre-2009)

## Evidence

### Marisa de la Torre 2009

Marisa de la Torre, Julia Gwynne. (2009). When Schools Close: Effects on Displaced Students in Chicago Public Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago Urban Education Institute. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/when-schools-close-effects-displaced-students-chicago-public-schools

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Moderator analysis of first-year achievement by receiving-school academic quality quartile (Figure 9): reading -1.20 (Q1) to +0.92 (Q4), math -0.48 (Q1) to +2.76 (Q4), with reading significant at p < 0.05 and math at p < 0.01. The overall academic achievement of receiving schools affected math scores more than reading scores.

> "In reading, displaced students in the strongest receiving schools (those in the top quartile of the distribution) had an achievement level one year later that was almost a month above expected. However, students who attended some of the weakest schools in the system (those in the bottom quartile of the distribution) experienced a loss in achievement of over a month"
