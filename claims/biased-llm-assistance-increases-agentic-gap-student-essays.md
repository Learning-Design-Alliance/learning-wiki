# Students assisted by a gender-biased LLM produce career-plan essays with a significantly larger male-female agentic gap than students in neutral-LLM and no-AI control conditions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` In a between-subjects lab experiment, the biased-LLM condition showed an agentic gap of Δ=+0.242 (d=1.510, p<.001) versus Δ=+0.032 (d=0.219, p=.490) in the neutral condition. [→ Ariyan Hossain 2026](#ariyan-hossain-2026)

## Evidence

### Ariyan Hossain 2026

Ariyan Hossain, Kazi Kamruzzaman Rabbi, Farig Sadeque, S M Taiabul Haque. (2026). Contaminated Collaboration: Measuring Gender Bias Transfer in LLM-Assisted Student Writing. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15914

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Between-subjects experiment with 123 undergraduates writing career-plan essays under control, neutral, or biased LLM conditions; Welch's t-tests on within-condition agency gaps. The biased condition's gap was "the largest agentic gap (∆ = +0.242,d= 1.510 ,p< .001)"; the control condition showed an intermediate significant gap (Δ=+0.117, d=0.661, p=.045).

> "The biased LLM produced the largest agentic gap (∆ = +0.242,d= 1.510 ,p< .001), while the neutral LLM produced the smallest (∆ = +0.032,d= 0.219,p=.490)."
