# Effect sizes based on mathematics tests are significantly larger than effect sizes based on reading tests in targeted secondary interventions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Meta-regressions revealed that effect sizes based on mathematics tests were significantly larger than effect sizes based on reading tests, and QES showed significantly larger effect sizes than RCTs. [→ Dietrichson J 2020](#dietrichson-j-2020)

## Evidence

### Dietrichson J 2020

Dietrichson J, Filges T, Klokker RH, Viinholt BCA, Bøg M, Jensen UH. (2020). Targeted school‐based interventions for improving reading and mathematics for students with, or at risk of, academic difficulties in Grades 7–12: A systematic review. Campbell Systematic Reviews. https://doi.org/10.1002/cl2.1081

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-regression across instructional methods and moderators. Maths-test effect sizes averaged ES = 0.34 [0.169, 0.502] versus reading-domain effects of 0.14–0.22; QES effects exceeded RCT effects, a design-related moderator the review flags.

> "Including all instructional methods and moderators without missing observations in meta‐regressions revealed that effect sizes based on mathematics tests were significantly larger than effect sizes based on reading tests, and QES showed significantly larger effect sizes than RCTs."
