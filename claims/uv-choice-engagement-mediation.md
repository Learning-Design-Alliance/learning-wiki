### Priniski 2019

Priniski, S. J., Rosenzweig, E. Q., Canning, E. A., Hecht, C. A., Tibbetts, Y., Hyde, J. S., & Harackiewicz, J. M. (2019). The Benefits of Combining Value for the Self and Others in Utility-Value Interventions. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000343

In the RCT's motivational analyses, choice-condition students wrote longer assignments (Mwords = 518.26, SD = 122.90) and reported higher interest (M = 5.72, SD = 1.09) than fixed-type students (Mwords = 489.43, SD = 123.63; M = 5.60, SD = 1.12), per the quoted main effects.

### Priniski 2019 (2)

Priniski, S. J., Rosenzweig, E. Q., Canning, E. A., Hecht, C. A., Tibbetts, Y., Hyde, J. S., & Harackiewicz, J. M. (2019). The Benefits of Combining Value for the Self and Others in Utility-Value Interventions. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000343

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Path-model mediation analysis in the same RCT tested whether motivational variables carried the choice effect on grades. Task engagement, not interest, was the significant mediator, per the quoted indirect-effect results.

> "We found a significant indirect effect of the choice contrast on performance through task engagement (p = .029), but the indirect effect of the choice contrast through interest in biology was not significant (p = .051)"
