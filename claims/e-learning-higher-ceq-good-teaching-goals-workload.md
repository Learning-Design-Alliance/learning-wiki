## Evidence

### Abdelhai R 2012

Abdelhai R, Yassin S, Ahmad MF, Fors UGH. (2012). An e-learning reproductive health module to support improved student learning and interaction: a prospective interventional study at a medical school in Egypt. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-11

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of Course Experience Questionnaire (adapted from Ramsden) scale scores between 97 e-learning and 103 non-participating students: e-learning students had significantly higher Good Teaching (21.35 ± 5.36 vs 19.67 ± 4.47, P = 0.035), Clear Goals and Standards (P = 0.043) and Appropriate Workload (14.88 ± 2.38 vs 13.30 ± 2.96, P = 0.002) scores.

> "Students enrolled in the e-Learning course had higher significant mean scores for the Good Teaching Score. Similarly, they had significantly higher scores for the Clear Goals Scale and the Appropriate Workload Scale."

### Abdelhai R 2012 (2)

Abdelhai R, Yassin S, Ahmad MF, Fors UGH. (2012). An e-learning reproductive health module to support improved student learning and interaction: a prospective interventional study at a medical school in Egypt. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-11

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

On the remaining two CEQ scales the groups did not differ: Appropriate Assessment (9.29 ± 2.39 vs 9.23 ± 2.53, P = 0.893) and Generic Skills (21.82 ± 4.32 vs 21.59 ± 4.52, P = 0.77); no effect size was printed.

> "no difference between both groups was detected for the Appropriate Assessment Scale and the Generic Skills Scale [Table 4]."
