Evidence¶

Nagy 1984¶

Nagy, William E.; Herman, Patricia A.; Anderson, Richard C.; Pearson, P. David. (1984). Learning Words from Context. Center for the Study of Reading, Technical Report No. 319, University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED248470

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Extrapolation combining the study's own learning probabilities (about .10 or .11 at Interview Level 3), Anderson and Freebody's estimates of unknown words per 1000-word text, and reading-volume estimates: "between 750 and 5,500; the point-value estimate is 3,125" words per year. This is a modeled estimate, not a direct measurement.

"the number of new words the typical middle grade child learns in a year from context during reading is between 750 and 5,500; the point-value estimate is 3,125."

  • Single exposures yield partial word knowledge, and repeated encounters in varied contexts build complete word meaning (fast mapping then full mapping) — related
