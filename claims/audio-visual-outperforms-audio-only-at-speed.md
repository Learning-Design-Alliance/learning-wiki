## Evidence

### Ashley Chen 2024

Ashley Chen, Suchita E. Kumar, Rhea Varkhedi, Dillon H. Murphy. (2024). The Effect of Playback Speed and Distractions on the Comprehension of Audio and Audio-Visual Materials. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09917-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Experiment 2 assigned 200 undergraduates to audio-only or audio-visual clips (presentation slides plus instructor images) at 1x or 2x speed, then tested comprehension with 25 multiple-choice questions per topic. The multilevel model showed "Format was a significant predictor" favoring audio-visual content.

> "Format was a significant predictor, F(1, 192) = 11.90, p < 0.001, /u1D6FD = 0.08, demonstrating that those who were shown audio-visual content (M = 0.56, SD = 0.16) performed better on the test relative to those who listened to audio-only content (M = 0.49, SD = 0.14)."

### Ashley Chen 2024 (2)

Ashley Chen, Suchita E. Kumar, Rhea Varkhedi, Dillon H. Murphy. (2024). The Effect of Playback Speed and Distractions on the Comprehension of Audio and Audio-Visual Materials. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09917-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Tukey post-hoc comparisons in Experiment 2's discussion show the cross-format contrast: the 2x audio-only group scored significantly less than the 1x audio-visual group (ptukey = .005), while audio-visual groups at 1x vs 2x did not differ significantly (ptukey = .606).

> "Performance for the 2x speed audio-only group was significantly less than that of the 1x speed audio-visual group (about a 12% difference), ptukey = .005, whereas there were no significant differences in performance between the 1x and 2x speed audio-visual groups (about a 6% difference), ptukey = .606."
