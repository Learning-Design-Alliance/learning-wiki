Evidence¶

Gilliland 1922¶

Gilliland, A. R. (1922). The effect of rate of silent reading on ability to recall. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1922-02093-001

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Controlled experiment in which each subject read two paragraphs at each of three rates with order counterbalanced across subjects; comprehension scored as ideas reproduced. The author reports "very little difference for any of the groups" across rates, with a possible slight advantage for rapid reading in children and slow reading in older students.

"In comprehension there was very little difference for any of the groups resulting from the different rates of reading. If there was any advantage it seemed that it was in favor of the rapid reading with the children and in favor of slow reading for the older students."
