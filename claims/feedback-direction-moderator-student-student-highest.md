# Feedback direction moderates effects: student-to-student feedback is most effective (d = 0.85), teacher-to-student d = 0.47, student-to-teacher d = 0.35

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Feedback direction is a significant moderator, with student-to-student feedback showing the highest average effect, though based on only eight studies. [→ Wisniewski B 2020](#wisniewski-b-2020)

## Evidence

### Wisniewski B 2020

Wisniewski B, Zierer K and Hattie J (2020) The Power of Feedback Revisited: A Meta-Analysis of Educational Feedback Research. Front. Psychol. 10:3087. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Mixed-effects moderator analysis by feedback direction (Table 3). Student-to-student feedback (k = 16) averages d = 0.85 [0.59–1.11], teacher-to-student (k = 812) d = 0.47, student-to-teacher (k = 27) d = 0.35.
