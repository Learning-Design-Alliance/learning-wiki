# Effects of game-based learning in STEM education vary substantially across studies, with prediction intervals spanning trivial-to-negative through very strong effects

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Between-study heterogeneity is large (I2 76%-92%) and true effects disperse widely around the mean, including negative effects in some populations. [→ Michaela Arztmann 2023](#michaela-arztmann-2023)

## Evidence

### Michaela Arztmann 2023

Michaela Arztmann, Lisette Hornstra, Johan Jeuring & Liesbeth Kester (2023). Effects of games in STEM education: a meta-analysis on the moderating role of student background characteristics. Studies in Science Education, 59:1, 109-145. https://doi.org/10.1080/03057267.2022.2057732

`q3 · i?`

Random-effects meta-analysis reporting heterogeneity statistics: I2 ranges from 76% to 92% across the three outcomes, and the article states "the dispersion of effects around this mean is substantial", with prediction intervals of −0.11 to +1.12 for motivation and +0.08 to +1.78 for behaviour.

> "For cognition, the 95% prediction interval ranges from −0.38 to +1.76. This means, that in the population represented by the included studies, the true effect size in 95% of cases will fall somewhere in this range"
