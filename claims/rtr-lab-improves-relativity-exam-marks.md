# Students who completed the Real Time Relativity lab scored significantly higher on externally set relativity exam questions than classmates who did not

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among students from the same lecture course randomly assigned to do or not do the RTR lab, the lab group outperformed the non-lab group on relativity exam questions set and marked by people unassociated with the project. [→ C. Savage 2009](#c-savage-2009)

## Evidence

### C. Savage 2009

C. Savage, D. McGrath, T. McIntyre, M. Wegener, M. Williamson. (2009). Teaching Physics Using Virtual Reality. https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.5776 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0910.5776](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0910.5776)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized comparison within one lecture course: a class of 182 was assigned to a group of 132 who did the RTR lab or a group of 50 who did not. Exam questions were "set and marked by people unassociated with the RTR project"; mean marks were 5.4 versus 4.6 out of ten, significant at p < 5%. No effect size is printed.

> "The mean mark out of ten for these questions was 5.4 for the RTR group and 4.6 for the non-RTR group. This is a small but statistically significant difference with a probability of less than 5% of occurring by chance."
