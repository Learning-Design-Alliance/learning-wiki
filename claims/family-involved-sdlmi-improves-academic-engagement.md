## Evidence

### Kim 2012

Kim, N. H., & Park, J. (2012). The effects of the family-involved SDLMI on academic engagement and goal attainment of middle school students with disabilities who exhibit problem behavior. International Journal of Special Education, 27(1), 117-127. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ979719

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

A pretest-posttest control group design with 24 middle school students with disabilities compared checklist-rated academic engagement changes between groups after a seven-month family-involved SDLMI intervention. The article reports "a significant increase in academic engagement behavior in the experimental group."

> "There was a significant increase in academic engagement behavior in the experimental group compared to the control group (t=2.29, p<.05)."

### Kim 2012 (2)

Kim, N. H., & Park, J. (2012). The effects of the family-involved SDLMI on academic engagement and goal attainment of middle school students with disabilities who exhibit problem behavior. International Journal of Special Education, 27(1), 117-127. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ979719

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Direct observation of four engagement behaviors (attention, task participation, activity participation, presentation) using partial 15-second interval recording confirmed significantly greater engagement gains for the experimental group, with "the difference between the mean change in score of the experimental group and the control group ... significant."

> "The difference between the mean change in score of the experimental group and the control group was significant (t=5.54, p< .01)."
