Evidence¶

Vysniauske 2020¶

Vysniauske, R., Verburgh, L., Oosterlaan, J., & Molendijk, M. L. (2020). The Effects of Physical Exercise on Functional Outcomes in the Treatment of ADHD: A Meta-Analysis. Journal of attention disorders, 24(5), 644-654. https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054715627489

q3 · i2 · quant-synthesis · r2

Random-effects meta-analysis of studies on exercise effects on motor skills and executive functions in children with ADHD, identified through Cochrane, PsycInfo, PubMed and Web of Science; "Ten publications were selected." The pooled effect was "g = 0.627", a modest positive impact.

"There was a significant effect of exercise on ADHD functional outcomes (g = 0.627)."

Related Claims¶

  • Exercise effect sizes on ADHD functional outcomes are not related to exercise intensity, mean age of participants, or gender distribution
  • Longitudinal physical activity programs have a positive effect on executive functions in preadolescent children (g = 0.24) — related
  • Chronic cardio exercise programs improve executive functions, attention, and behavior in children with ADHD — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Small pilot studies provide preliminary evidence that exercise interventions improve behavioral symptoms and neuropsychological functioning in children with ADHD — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Acute cardio exercise improves executive functions, response time, and academic performance in children with ADHD — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Acute exercise produces an overall small positive effect on cognition, with executive functions benefiting most and effects lasting up to two hours post-exercise — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Reported benefits of physical activity programs on executive functions depend on program duration and the executive domain analyzed — related
