# In the PIRLS 2006 teaching-quality example, the optimal fixed-budget design was 395 classes with 12 students per class, and the actual PIRLS design achieved 83% power

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Applying the fixed-budget procedure to the Arens and Morin (2016) parameter values yielded optimal numbers of 395 classes and 12 students per class, with 73% power to detect a standardized between slope of .1. [→ Steffen Zitzmann 2021](#steffen-zitzmann-2021)
`q2 i?` The most cost-efficient design for 80% power required 469 classes and 12 students per class with a budget of 71,250€, and the actual PIRLS design had 83% power. [→ Steffen Zitzmann 2021 (2)](#steffen-zitzmann-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Steffen Zitzmann 2021

Steffen Zitzmann, Wolfgang Wagner, Martin Hecht, Christoph Helm, Christian Fischer, Lisa Bardach, Richard Göllner. (2021). How Many Classes and Students Should Ideally be Sampled When Assessing the Role of Classroom Climate via Student Ratings on a Limited Budget? An Optimal Design Perspective. Educational Psychology Review (2022) 34:511–536. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09635-4

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Illustrative application assuming a fixed budget of 60,000€, costs of 68.20€ per class and 7.05€ per student, ICC(1)s of .32 (achievement) and .12 (support ratings), a standardized within slope of 0.159, and a target between slope of .1. The optimal design had 19 classes and 6 students per class less than the actual PIRLS sample.

> "Applying our procedure for determining the design with the maximum power given a fixed budget to these values provided optimal numbers of 395 classes and 12 students per class."

### Steffen Zitzmann 2021 (2)

Steffen Zitzmann, Wolfgang Wagner, Martin Hecht, Christoph Helm, Christian Fischer, Lisa Bardach, Richard Göllner. (2021). How Many Classes and Students Should Ideally be Sampled When Assessing the Role of Classroom Climate via Student Ratings on a Limited Budget? An Optimal Design Perspective. Educational Psychology Review (2022) 34:511–536. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09635-4

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Cost-minimization application with the same parameter values and a target power of 80%. Only the number of classes increased (by 74) relative to the fixed-budget optimum, and the article concludes the actual PIRLS design was sufficiently powered because "the power was 83%."

> "we obtained an optimal design with 469 classes and 12 students using our procedure for finding the most cost-efficient design with a power of 80%. The required budget was 71,250€"
