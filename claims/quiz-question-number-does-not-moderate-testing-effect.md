# The number of quiz questions (five vs. ten) does not moderate the improvement in exam performance

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There was no significant difference in exam scores between the 5-question and 10-question quiz conditions. [→ Glodowski 2025](#glodowski-2025)

## Evidence

### Glodowski 2025

Glodowski, K. R., & Hayashi, Y. (2025). Toward cross‐disciplinary translation of the testing effect: A systematic replication. Journal of Applied Behavior Analysis, 58(3), 653–663. https://doi.org/10.1002/jaba.70012

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Bonferroni-adjusted post hoc comparisons in the within-subject study found no significant difference between the 5Q and 10Q conditions (5Q vs. 10Q Δ = −0.48, d = 0.07); "there was no significant difference between the 5Q" and 10Q conditions. Equivalence was not tested.

> "relative to the NQ condition (M = 76.67%, 95% CI [66.13, 87.21]), both quiz conditions significantly improved the exam scores, but there was no significant difference between the 5Q (M = 92.38%, 95% CI [86.76, 98.00]) and 10Q (M = 92.85%, 95% CI [88.21, 97.50]) conditions"
