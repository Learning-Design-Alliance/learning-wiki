## Evidence

### Melissa Roderick 2009

Melissa Roderick, Jenny Nagaoka, and Vanessa Coca. (2009). College Readiness for All: The Challenge for Urban High Schools. The Future of Children. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/college-readiness-all-challenge-urban-high-schools

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The authors' own analysis of Chicago Public Schools graduates used National Student Clearinghouse data on four-year enrollment and six-year graduation, modeled on unweighted GPA, honors/AP course-taking, and eleventh-grade test scores. GPA's association (15 percentage points) exceeded test scores' (7 percentage points); no standardized effect size is printed.

> "A one standard deviation increase in GPA, controlling for test scores and participation in advanced coursework, was associated with a 15 percentage point increase in the chances of four-year college graduation, while a one standard deviation increase in achievement test scores was linked with only a 7 percentage point increase in those chances."

### Melissa Roderick 2009 (2)

Melissa Roderick, Jenny Nagaoka, and Vanessa Coca. (2009). College Readiness for All: The Challenge for Urban High Schools. The Future of Children. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/college-readiness-all-challenge-urban-high-schools

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

In the same Chicago study, the authors report that "an unweighted GPA of 3.0" marked the cutoff at which students had a 50 percent or greater six-year four-college graduation likelihood. This is a descriptive benchmark, not a tested optimum.

> "The study identified an unweighted GPA of 3.0 as a key benchmark for college readiness—a cutoff that gave students a 50 percent or greater likelihood of graduating from a four-year college within six years."
