# Stop-and-go debriefing shows no significant difference from post-scenario debriefing in skill acquisition at four weeks in medical simulation training

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Medical students trained with stop-and-go debriefing performed equivalently to those trained with post-scenario debriefing on a repeat resuscitation scenario four weeks later, with no statistically significant difference in performance score. [→ Schober Patrick 2019](#schober-patrick-2019)

## Evidence

### Schober Patrick 2019

Schober Patrick, Kistemaker Kay R. J., Sijani Fereshte, Schwarte Lothar A., van Groeningen Dick, Krage Ralf. (2019). Effects of post-scenario debriefing versus stop-and-go debriefing in medical simulation training on skill acquisition and learning experience: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1772-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial: 50 medical students in simulation-based CPR training were randomly assigned to post-scenario or stop-and-go debriefing; 48 were analyzed after exclusions. The main regression analysis found "mean difference: − 0.35" with P = 0.75 and model R2 = 0.003.

> "No statistically significant difference was found between the groups for the performance score in the main analysis (post-scenario debriefing: 24.10; stop-and-go debriefing: 23.75; mean difference: − 0.35, 95% confidence interval: − 2.46 to 1.77, P = 0.75, model R2 = 0.003)."
