## Subclaims
`q4 i?` In Study 4, the cheating rate was 3.5% unproctored vs. 12.3% proctored, with a significant exam type effect (OR = 0.26) but only anecdotal BF10 evidence. [→ Li Zhao 2024](#li-zhao-2024)

## Evidence

### Li Zhao 2024

Li Zhao, Junjie Peng, Shiqi Ke, Kang Lee. (2024). Effectiveness of Unproctored vs. Teacher-Proctored Exams in Reducing Students’ Cheating: A Double-Blind Randomized Controlled Field Experimental Study. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09965-z

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Study 4 (N = 166, Foundations of Education, reminders present) used the same double-blind randomized design. The logistic regression showed a significant exam type effect with "odds ratio (OR) = 0.26"; the article characterizes the BF10 = 0.79 as anecdotal evidence for the lower unproctored rate. Cheating extent did not differ between formats.

> "The main effect of exam type was significant, β = –1.35, SEβ = 0.68, Wald χ2 (df = 1) = 3.94, p = 0.047, odds ratio (OR) = 0.26, 95% CI = 0.07 -0.98, BF10 = 0.79."
