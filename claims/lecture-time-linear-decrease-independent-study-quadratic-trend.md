# Over a full term, aggregated lecture time decreases linearly while independent study time follows a negative quadratic trajectory, rising until the end of the lecture period and then declining

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Aggregated time-series trend analysis shows a significant linear decrease in lecture time and significant linear and quadratic effects for independent study time over the 22-week term. [→ Liborius 2019](#liborius-2019)

## Evidence

### Liborius 2019

Liborius, P., Bellhäuser, H., & Schmitz, B. (2019). What makes a good study day? An intraindividual study on university students' time investment by means of time-series analyses. Learning and Instruction, 60, 310-321. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2017.10.006

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Aggregated time-series trend analysis across 105 students' daily diary records over 154 days. The article reports "a significant linear effect for lecture time" and significant linear and quadratic effects for independent study time, with the printed F statistics, β weights, and R² values as quoted.

> "The trend analysis shows a significant linear effect for lecture time (F(1, 152) = 57.97, p < .01, β = -.53, R² = .28) and a significant linear and quadratic effect for independent study time (F(2, 151) = 49.41, p < .01, β1 = -.16, β2 = -.61, R² = .40)."
