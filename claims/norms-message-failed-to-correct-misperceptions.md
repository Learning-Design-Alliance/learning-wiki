## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Even after receiving the social norms message stating most students drink 0-4 drinks, about two-thirds of participants believed fellow students consumed five or more drinks on their 21st birthday. [→ Glassman T 2010](#glassman-t-2010)

## Evidence

### Glassman T 2010

Glassman T, Dodd V, Kenzik K, Miller EM, Sheu JJ. (2010). Social norms vs. risk reduction approaches to 21st birthday celebrations. Am J Health Educ. 2010;41(1):38-45. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ871144

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Survey items asked participants to estimate the percentage of fellow students consuming five or more drinks; 66.8% believed peers did so, and SN and COMBO cohorts estimated 61.4% and 65.1% respectively, despite the norms message. The article reports this descriptively; no effect size printed.

> "in spite of the social norms intervention message, a high percentage (66.8%) of participants, believed that their fellow students consumed five or more drinks on their 21st birthday."
