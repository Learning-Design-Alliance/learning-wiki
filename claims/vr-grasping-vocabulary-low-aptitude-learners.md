# Grasping virtual objects in VR improves foreign language vocabulary retention for learners with low language aptitude, but not for those with high aptitude

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Adults trained in a VR environment who grasped virtual objects showed improved vocabulary retention if they had low language aptitude, while high-aptitude learners did not benefit. [→ de Koning 2025](#de-koning-2025)

## Evidence

### de Koning 2025

de Koning, B., Zhang, S., & Sepp, S. (2025). Integrating Human Movement in Learning: Advancements in Language Instruction, Multimedia, and Theory. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10027-1

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports the empirical study by Macedonia et al. (2023), in which adult participants trained in VR under three conditions: audiovisual, audiovisual with object observation, and audiovisual with grasping. The review reports that "grasping virtual objects improved vocabulary retention for learners with low language aptitude." No effect size is printed.

> "Results indicated that grasping virtual objects improved vocabulary retention for learners with low language aptitude, but not for those with high aptitude."
