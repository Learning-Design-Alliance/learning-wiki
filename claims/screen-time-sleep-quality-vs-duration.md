## Evidence

### Zeng Zhaolan 2026

Zeng Zhaolan, Shi Zeyao, Hou Shulin, Yan Jing, Yang Ru, Li Xiaowen. (2026). Comprehensive effects and dose–response relationship of screen exposure on university students' physical and mental health and academic performance in the digital age: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-026-27025-9

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 25 studies, primarily using the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index; after excluding 6 studies the pooled estimate was slightly attenuated but remained significant (OR = 1.12; 95%CI: 1.06–1.18), and trim-and-fill correction yielded OR = 1.073 (95%CI: 1.008–1.141).

> "A meta-analysis of 25 studies (29,213 university students) showed that longer daily screen time was associated with poorer sleep quality (OR = 1.28; 95%CI: 1.18–1.39; I2 = 79.30%)."

### Zeng Zhaolan 2026 (2)

Zeng Zhaolan, Shi Zeyao, Hou Shulin, Yan Jing, Yang Ru, Li Xiaowen. (2026). Comprehensive effects and dose–response relationship of screen exposure on university students' physical and mental health and academic performance in the digital age: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-026-27025-9

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Restricted cubic spline dose–response analysis of seven studies (10,149 students) showing an S-shaped curve; risk rose continuously above 1.6 h/day and the rate of increase slowed above 5 h/day. No standardized effect size is printed for this finding.

> "Notably, beyond 3 h of daily screen time, the risk of sleep quality deterioration increased significantly, with each additional hour of screen exposure associated with a more than 10% increase in the risk of sleep quality deterioration."
