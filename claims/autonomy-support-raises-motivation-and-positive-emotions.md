# Teacher autonomy support, compared with a controlling style, increases students' autonomy need satisfaction, autonomous motivation and positive academic emotions and lowers negative emotions in a simulated one-to-one teaching session

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Students taught with teacher autonomy support reported higher autonomy need satisfaction (d = 4.20), higher autonomous motivation (RAI, d = 1.17) and more positive academic emotions (d = 1.85) than students taught with a controlling style. [→ Wang Guoxia 2025](#wang-guoxia-2025)
`q3 i3` Students taught with teacher autonomy support reported lower negative academic emotions (d = −1.25) than students in the teacher control group. [→ Wang Guoxia 2025](#wang-guoxia-2025)
`q3 i?` In coded open-ended emotion responses, students in the teacher autonomy support group had about 70 times higher odds of positive emotions than the control group; no standardized effect size is printed for this regression. [→ Wang Guoxia 2025 (2)](#wang-guoxia-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Wang Guoxia 2025

Wang Guoxia, Zhang Qin, Wang Lumeng, Liu Yi. (2025). The impact of teachers’ autonomy support on students’ academic performance from the perspective of hyperscanning: the mediating role of autonomy need satisfaction. Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsaf115

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Experimental comparison of two manipulated teacher styles in a simulated one-to-one teaching session with 42 student datasets (support n = 20, control n = 22). Independent-samples t-tests showed "higher autonomy need satisfaction (t(40) = 13.70, P < .001, d = 4.20)" and the other printed group differences reported here, including d = 1.17, d = 1.85 and d = −1.25.

> "Compared to the control group, the teacher autonomy support group had higher autonomy need satisfaction (t(40) = 13.70, P < .001, d = 4.20), RAI (t(40) = 3.78, P = .001, d = 1.17, Fig. 4a) and positive emotions (t(40)=5.94, P < .001, d = 1.85; Fig. 4b), with lower negative emotions (t(40) = −4.03, P < .001, d = −1.25; Fig. 4c)."

### Wang Guoxia 2025 (2)

Wang Guoxia, Zhang Qin, Wang Lumeng, Liu Yi. (2025). The impact of teachers’ autonomy support on students’ academic performance from the perspective of hyperscanning: the mediating role of autonomy need satisfaction. Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsaf115

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Binary logistic regression on open-question emotion categories coded by two graduate raters (Kappa 0.934; 33 valid cases). The article reports students in the support group "had 70 times higher odds of positive emotions than the control group (b = 4.25, Wald = 12.27, P < .001, 95% OR 6.50–754.44)"; no standardized effect size is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "Results showed that students in the teacher autonomy support group had 70 times higher odds of positive emotions than the control group (b = 4.25, Wald = 12.27, P < .001, 95% OR 6.50–754.44)."
