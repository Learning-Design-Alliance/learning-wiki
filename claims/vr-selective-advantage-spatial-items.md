## Evidence

### Ito T 2026

Ito T, Kawasaki H, Yamazaki A, Mizoguchi Y, Yamaguchi K, Kaneko E, Tsutsumi T. (2026). Metaverse‐based virtual reality and spatial learning in cervicofacial anatomy: A curriculum‐embedded comparative study in undergraduate medical students. Anatomical Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.70277

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Between-cohort comparison of percent-correct on the 9 spatial–relational items of the 12-item test, analyzed with Welch's t-test in the nonrandomized curriculum-embedded study. The article reports a "mean difference 28.0" with Hedges' g = 1.44.

> "the VR advantage was pronounced for spatial items (mean ± SD: 68.6 ± 20.1 vs. 40.6 ± 18.9; mean difference 28.0 [95% CI 20.3–35.7], Welch's t‐test p < 0.0001; Hedges' g = 1.44)"

### Ito T 2026 (2)

Ito T, Kawasaki H, Yamazaki A, Mizoguchi Y, Yamaguchi K, Kaneko E, Tsutsumi T. (2026). Metaverse‐based virtual reality and spatial learning in cervicofacial anatomy: A curriculum‐embedded comparative study in undergraduate medical students. Anatomical Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.70277

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Between-cohort comparison of percent-correct on the 3 factual/topographic items of the same test; the Welch's t-test was not significant (p = 0.286, g = 0.20), so equivalence was not tested.

> "factual/topographic items were similar between groups (58.3 ± 25.9 vs. 52.5 ± 30.4; difference 5.8 [−4.9–16.6], p = 0.286; g = 0.20)"
