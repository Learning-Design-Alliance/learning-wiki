# Timing of the pediatrics clerkship shows no significant effect on final OSCE performance within individual years, with one pairwise difference across the pooled sample

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` One-way ANOVA found no significant effect of clerkship timing (first, second, third rotation) on final OSCE performance when analyzed within each academic year. [→ Al Rushood Maysoun 2020](#al-rushood-maysoun-2020)
`q3 i?` In the pooled three-year sample, students rotating third scored higher than those rotating second (79.17 vs 77.19, P = 0.029), but not higher than those rotating first. [→ Al Rushood Maysoun 2020 (2)](#al-rushood-maysoun-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Al Rushood Maysoun 2020

Al Rushood Maysoun, Al-Eisa Amal. (2020). Factors predicting students’ performance in the final pediatrics OSCE. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0236484

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

One-way ANOVA of final OSCE scores by rotation order (first/second/third) for 286 students. No significant timing effect was detected in any individual year or overall; equivalence was not tested.

> "Looking at the timing of their clerkships, there was no significant effect on the performance on the final OSCE (Table 3)."

### Al Rushood Maysoun 2020 (2)

Al Rushood Maysoun, Al-Eisa Amal. (2020). Factors predicting students’ performance in the final pediatrics OSCE. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0236484

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Post-hoc comparison in the pooled sample: rotation-3 students averaged "79.17 in rotation 3 compared to 77.19 in rotation 2 with a P = 0.029". This difference was not significant in individual years, nor for third vs first or second vs first.

> "The mean score was 79.17 in rotation 3 compared to 77.19 in rotation 2 with a P = 0.029."
