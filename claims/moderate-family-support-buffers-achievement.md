# Only moderate (not high or low) family support protects bullied adolescents from poor academic achievement

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For bullied students with moderate family support, bullying was not associated with lower achievement odds (OR 1.00), whereas high (OR 0.20) and low (OR 0.40) family support showed lower odds. [→ Rothon Catherine 2011](#rothon-catherine-2011)

## Evidence

### Rothon Catherine 2011

Rothon Catherine, Head Jenny, Klineberg Emily, Stansfeld Stephen. (2011). Can social support protect bullied adolescents from adverse outcomes? A prospective study on the effects of bullying on the educational achievement and mental health of adolescents at secondary schools in East London. Journal of Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.adolescence.2010.02.007

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multivariate model with a bullying × family social support interaction (homogeneity test p = 0.130, described as a very weak suggestion). "for students with moderate levels of support from family, bullying did not appear to be associated with lower odds" (OR 1.00); high support OR 0.20 (0.07–0.51), low support OR 0.40 (0.21–0.76).

> "for students with moderate levels of support from family, bullying did not appear to be associated with lower odds of achieving the academic benchmark (OR = 1.00, 95% CI 0.49–2.06)."
