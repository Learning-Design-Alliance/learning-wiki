# Bullying was not associated with lower academic achievement among adolescents with moderate family support, unlike those with high or low support, in a weakly supported interaction

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For bullied students with moderate family support, bullying was not associated with lower achievement odds (OR 1.00), whereas among those with high (OR 0.20) or low (OR 0.40) family support bullying was associated with lower odds; the interaction test was weak (p = 0.130) and the observational design cannot show that support protects. [→ Rothon Catherine 2011](#rothon-catherine-2011)

## Evidence

### Rothon Catherine 2011

Rothon Catherine, Head Jenny, Klineberg Emily, Stansfeld Stephen. (2011). Can social support protect bullied adolescents from adverse outcomes? A prospective study on the effects of bullying on the educational achievement and mental health of adolescents at secondary schools in East London. Journal of Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.adolescence.2010.02.007

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multivariate model with a bullying × family social support interaction (homogeneity test p = 0.130, described as a very weak suggestion). "for students with moderate levels of support from family, bullying did not appear to be associated with lower odds" (OR 1.00); high support OR 0.20 (0.07–0.51), low support OR 0.40 (0.21–0.76). This is a prospective observational association, not a test of whether family support protects bullied students.

> "for students with moderate levels of support from family, bullying did not appear to be associated with lower odds of achieving the academic benchmark (OR = 1.00, 95% CI 0.49–2.06)."
