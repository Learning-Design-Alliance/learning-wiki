## Evidence

### Lucy Prior 2022

Lucy Prior, Clare Evans, Juan Merlo and George Leckie. (2022). Sociodemographic inequalities in student achievement: An intersectional multilevel analysis of individual heterogeneity and discriminatory accuracy (MAIHDA) with application to students in London, England. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2211.06321](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2211.06321)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-cohort comparison of Model 1 stratum effects (Figure 2, top row) identifies the most negative shifts; the largest is "(-0.48 SD)" for the boy, FSM-eligible, Unclassified ethnicity, no-SEN, summer-born stratum, which the authors relate to disadvantaged boys falling behind during secondary schooling.

> "The biggest negative differences (where the age 16 cohort typically scores lower than the age 11 cohort) are seen by the strata typically defined by being a boy, FSM eligible, of an Unclassified ethnicity and without SEN. The largest difference (-0.48 SD) is seen for this group combined with being Summer born."

### Lucy Prior 2022 (2)

Lucy Prior, Clare Evans, Juan Merlo and George Leckie. (2022). Sociodemographic inequalities in student achievement: An intersectional multilevel analysis of individual heterogeneity and discriminatory accuracy (MAIHDA) with application to students in London, England. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2211.06321](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2211.06321)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

The same cross-cohort comparison shows the largest positive shifts for autumn-born girls with SEN of Asian ethnicity: "The largest shift (0.53 SD)" when not FSM eligible and "0.44 SD" when FSM eligible, described as older-in-year Asian girls gaining progress.

> "The largest shift (0.53 SD) was seen for these characteristics combined with not being eligible for FSM, whilst the second largest difference (0.44 SD) saw this combination with eligibility for FSM."
