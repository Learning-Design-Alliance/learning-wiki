# PTR-YC increases preschool children's directly observed engaged time more than business-as-usual

## Subclaims
`q4 i2` In an RCT, directly observed engaged time increased significantly more from pretest to posttest for the PTR-YC group than for the BAU group, with a small interaction effect. [→ Glen Dunlap 2018](#glen-dunlap-2018)

## Evidence

### Glen Dunlap 2018

Glen Dunlap, Phillip Strain, Janice K. Lee, Jaclyn Joseph, and Nancy Leech. (2018). A Randomized Controlled Evaluation of Prevent-Teach-Reinforce for Young Children. Topics in Early Childhood Special Education. https://doi.org/10.1177/0271121417724874

`q4 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Momentary time sampling of three 10-min video samples per child at pre- and posttest in the classroom-level RCT; mixed ANOVA showed a significant time-by-group interaction, "F(1, 151) = 7.56, p = .007", ηp² = .048, with similar pretest engagement and much higher PTR-YC posttest engagement.

> "Examining directly observed levels of engaged time revealed a statistically significant interaction effect between time and group, F(1, 151) = 7.56, p = .007, ηp 2 = .048."
