# Technical digital literacy measured with authentic software applications is not a unidimensional construct; a four-dimensional exploratory MIRT model fits best

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Unidimensional IRT models did not fit the ILPA technical DL data, whereas a four-dimensional exploratory MIRT model provided the best acceptable fit for P5, S2 and total samples. [→ Frank Reichert 2020](#frank-reichert-2020)

## Evidence

### Frank Reichert 2020

Frank Reichert, Nancy W. Y. Law, Gary K. W. Wong, Jimmy de la Torre. (2020). Exploring the structure of digital literacy competence assessed with authentic software applications. Education Tech Research Dev, 68, 2991–3013. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-020-09825-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Secondary IRT analysis of the ILPA technical DL assessment (830 P5 and 823 S2 students, tasks in authentic Office applications), reported in Results: unidimensional models "did not fit the data well"; the four-dimensional MIRT model was best (Table 4).

> "However, these measurement models did not fit the data well (see Table 4): technical DL as measured in ILPA was apparently not a unidimensional construct but may be comprised of several dimensions."

### Frank Reichert 2020 (2)

Frank Reichert, Nancy W. Y. Law, Gary K. W. Wong, Jimmy de la Torre. (2020). Exploring the structure of digital literacy competence assessed with authentic software applications. Education Tech Research Dev, 68, 2991–3013. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-020-09825-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Exploratory MIRT model comparison in Results across P5, S2 and total samples: the "four-dimensional exploratory MIRT model provided the best and acceptable model-data fit", with dimensions interpretable from item content and tools.

> "The four-dimensional exploratory MIRT model provided the best and acceptable model-data fit (rows printed in bold in Table 4) and we concluded that DL as measured in the ILPA study likely tapped four dimensions."
