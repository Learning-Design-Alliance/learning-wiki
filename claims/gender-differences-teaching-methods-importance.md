## Evidence

### Francisco González-Gómez 2011

Francisco González-Gómez, Jorge Guardiola, Óscar Martín Rodríguez, Miguel Ángel Montero Alonso (University of Granada, Spain). (2011). Gender differences in e-learning satisfaction. FEG Working Paper Series. http://www.ugr.es/~biblio/biblioteca_ugr/bibliotecas_centros/economicas/index.html

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

OLS regressions of questionnaire items on global satisfaction by gender (Table 3) show planning significant for female students (coefficient 0.10***, p 0.0000) but not males (p 0.1201), participation significant for females (0.05**, p 0.0161) but not males, and course pace significant for males (0.05***, p 0.0669) but not females.

> "Female students assign greater importance to planning and participation in their overall valuation of the subject. These two items are irrelevant for male students. Another difference observed is that male students consider the pacing of the course to be important, whereas female students do not."
