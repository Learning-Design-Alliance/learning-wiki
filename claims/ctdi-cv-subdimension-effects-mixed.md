## Evidence

### Yang Yuehang 2026

Yang Yuehang, Sheng Li, Huang Liang, Guo Qiannan, Hu Yuting, Shi Jiawei. (2026). Application of an AI-enabled personalized medical education model for improving clinical thinking among cardiovascular surgery trainees. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09993-z

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

CTDI-CV subdomain comparison in the randomized study (n = 79 per group); Table 2 reports Cohen's d of 0.68 (truth-seeking), 0.87 (open-mindedness), 1.01 (analyticity), 1.25 (systematicity), and 1.05 (inquisitiveness), all P < 0.001.

> "the experimental group scored significantly higher than the control group in the total score as well as in the sub-dimensions of truth-seeking, open-mindedness, analyticity, systematicity, and inquisitiveness (all P < 0.05)"

### Yang Yuehang 2026 (2)

Yang Yuehang, Sheng Li, Huang Liang, Guo Qiannan, Hu Yuting, Shi Jiawei. (2026). Application of an AI-enabled personalized medical education model for improving clinical thinking among cardiovascular surgery trainees. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09993-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same randomized study; Table 2 shows critical thinking self-confidence d = 0.21, P = 0.199 and cognitive maturity d = 0.25, P = 0.118, both non-significant. No formal multiple-comparison correction was applied, so subdomain findings were considered exploratory.

> "No statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups in the dimensions of critical thinking self-confidence and cognitive maturity"
