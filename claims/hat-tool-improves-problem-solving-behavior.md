The review reports the HAT study (Dufresne et al., 1992): a computer tutor guides students through choosing "broad principles" then subprinciples, with software giving consistent equations. It found "significant performance differences" between HAT users and traditionally solving students. No effect size or n is printed.

> "The researchers found significant performance differences in problem-solving between students who used the HAT tool and students who solved problems traditionally, and they conclude that this type of qualitative analysis results in a significant improvement in problem-solving behavior."
