# Increasing the frequency of teachers' story read-aloud with word-meaning explanations yields greater word-learning gains for EFL fourth graders, but gains stagnate after the third session

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Target-word scores significantly improved from the first to the third and fourth read-aloud sessions, with no significant difference between the third and fourth sessions. [→ Lin 2014](#lin-2014)

## Evidence

### Lin 2014

Lu-Chun Lin. (2014). Learning Word Meanings from Teachers' Repeated Story Read-Aloud in EFL Primary Classrooms. English Language Teaching, 7(7). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v7n7p68

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Repeated-measures ANOVA on target vocabulary scores of 45 Taiwanese EFL fourth graders across the first, third, and fourth read-aloud sessions. The article reports "F(2,88) = 7.48, p =.002, partial ŋ2 = .15" and post-hoc Bonferroni effect sizes of 0.55 (1st vs. 3rd) and 0.41 (1st vs. 4th).

> "A significant main effect of the number of sessions was found, F(2,88) = 7.48, p =.002, partial ŋ2 = .15. Subsequent post-hoc Bonferroni t tests showed that the children's knowledge of the target words significantly improved during the third and fourth read-aloud session as compared to the first one, but there was no significant difference between the third and fourth sessions."
