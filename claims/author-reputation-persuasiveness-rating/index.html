Evidence¶

Keith Weber 2013¶

Keith Weber, Matthew Inglis & Juan Pablo Mejia-Ramos. (2013). How mathematicians obtain conviction: Implications for mathematics instruction and research on epistemic cognition. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2013.865527

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Experiment by Inglis and Mejia-Ramos with 190 research-active mathematicians rating one argument's persuasiveness on a 0-100 scale, randomly assigned to anonymous or named (Littlewood) conditions. The named group's rating was "more than 17 points higher"; no effect size is printed.

"The named group's rating of the persuasiveness of the argument was more than 17 points higher than that of the anonymous group, a statistically significant difference (which was replicated in a subsequent study)."
