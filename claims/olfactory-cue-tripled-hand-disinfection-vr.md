## Evidence

### Junga A 2026

Junga A, Kockwelp P, Dabel J, Scherzer SH, Risse B, Holling M, Marschall B, Friederichs H. (2026). Olfactory Cues and Patient Safety Behavior in Virtual Reality Medical Simulation: Controlled Trial. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/93148

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Controlled trial of 95 medical students in a VR brain-death examination course, quasi-randomly allocated by room to scent or no-scent conditions, with hygiene behavior automatically tracked. The intervention group reached "20/52, 38.5%" versus 8/43 (18.6%) in controls, with a number needed to treat of 5.

> "The olfactory intervention nearly tripled the odds of hand disinfection (8/43, 18.6% vs 20/52, 38.5%; an absolute risk increase of 19.9 percentage points; OR=2.73, 95% CI 1.09‐7.40; P=.04; number needed to treat=5)"
