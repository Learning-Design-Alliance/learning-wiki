# Receiving two model answer diagrams improves secondary students' delayed-diagramming text comprehension relative to no model answers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who received two model answers (correct and partially correct) performed significantly better on the second comprehension test than students who received no model answers, with no other pairwise comparisons significant. [→ Hepner 2024](#hepner-2024)

## Evidence

### Hepner 2024

Hepner, S. L., Oudman, S., Carlson, T. E., van de Pol, J., & van Gog, T. (2024). Improving (meta)comprehension: Feedback and self-assessment. Learning and Instruction, 92, 101922. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.101922

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized 3×2 between-subjects experiment with 258 grade 11–12 students; linear mixed models on phase-2 comprehension showed a main effect of model answers, "F (2,252) = 3.45, p = .03", with no effect of self-assessment instructions or interaction. Pairwise, two model answers beat none, t(252) = 2.56, p = .03.

> "There was a significant main effect of model answers on students’ comprehension test performance in phase 2, F (2,252) = 3.45, p = .03. There was no significant main effect of Self-Assessment Instruction, F (1,252) = 1.52, p = .22, and no significant interaction effect, F (2,252) = 0.83, p = .44."
