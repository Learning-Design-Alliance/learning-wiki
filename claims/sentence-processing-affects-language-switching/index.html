Evidence¶

Mathieu Declerck & Andrea M. Philipp 2015¶

Mathieu Declerck & Andrea M. Philipp. (2015). A review of control processes and their locus in language switching. Psychon Bull Rev. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-015-0836-1

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports, citing Tarlowski, Wodniecka, et al. (2013) with Polish-English bilinguals describing pictures in present progressive or perfective phrases, that "language switch costs can be ob- tained when switching between sentences," with asymmetry depending on phrase type. No effect sizes are printed.

"Their results indicated that language switch costs can be ob- tained when switching between sentences (for a similar result within sentences, see Bultena, Dijkstra, & van Hell, 2015). Furthermore, asymmetrical switch costs were obtained with progressive phrases and symmetrical switch costs with perfec- tive phrases."

Mathieu Declerck & Andrea M. Philipp 2015 (2)¶

Mathieu Declerck & Andrea M. Philipp. (2015). A review of control processes and their locus in language switching. Psychon Bull Rev. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-015-0836-1

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports, citing Declerck and Philipp (2015a), that "no switch costs with the SBLS paradigm" occurred for word-to-word syntactically correct sentences, while substantial switch costs remained for scrambled or syntactically incorrect sentences, supporting syntactic (lemma-level) influence on switching.

"Their results indicated no switch costs with the SBLS paradigm when German –English bilinguals produced words in sentences that were syntactically correct when translating it word-to-word to the other language"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Concept preparation reduces L1 switch costs, indicating language control can initiate at the concept level
  • Phonological processing is affected by and affects language control in switching, though effects are not uniform across studies
  • Switch costs and mixing costs are common empirical observations across language switching paradigms, except voluntary switching under certain circumstances
  • Asymmetrical switch costs are not an unambiguous marker of inhibition: they depend on task type and bilingual profile, and admit non-inhibitory explanations — related
  • Language proficiency moderates switch cost asymmetry, but highly proficient bilinguals still show n-2 language repetition costs consistent with inhibition — related
