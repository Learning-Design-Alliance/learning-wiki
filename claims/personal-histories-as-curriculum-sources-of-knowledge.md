# Treating students' personal histories as sources of knowledge makes them part of the classroom curriculum

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q1` argument or single case

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` A teacher educator signals that personal histories and responses are sources of knowledge by telling her own stories and assigning students educational autobiographies, which become part of the classroom curriculum. [→ Hulsebosch 1993](#hulsebosch-1993)

## Evidence

### Hulsebosch 1993

Hulsebosch, Pat; Koerner, Mari. (1993). What Does Cultural Identity Have To Do with the Preparation of Teachers? Case Studies of "Culturally-Aware" Teachers. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED403553

`q1 · i?`

Mari Koerner's life-history narrative describes her classroom practice of valuing "personal histories and responses as sources of knowledge," including building on students' shared stories in class activities and serving as students' advocate beyond the semester.

> "By telling my own personal stories and making students' educational autobiographies an assignment, I try to show that I value personal histories and responses as sources of knowledge and that they therefore become part of the curriculum of the classroom."
