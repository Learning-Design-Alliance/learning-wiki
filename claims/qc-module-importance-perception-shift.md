## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The distribution of perceived importance of quantum computing changed after the course even though the median stayed at 4 on the 1-5 scale, reflecting recognition that media hype is hyperbole. [→ Ciaran Hughes 2020](#ciaran-hughes-2020)

## Evidence

### Ciaran Hughes 2020

Ciaran Hughes, Joshua Isaacson, Anastasia Perry, Ranbel Sun, and Jessica Turner. (2020). Teaching Quantum Computing to High School Students. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2004.07206](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2004.07206)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre/post survey item on perceived societal importance of quantum computing from the 45-student cohort, reported as distributions in the bottom left plot of Fig. 2. The medians are descriptive; no statistical test or effect size is printed.

> "Using our metric where 1 (5) indicated the minimal (maximal) importance of QC, the median of the before and after data is 4. The change in the distributions highlights that the students appreciated that QC technology is in its infancy, and much of the “hype” generated by popular media is hyperbole."
