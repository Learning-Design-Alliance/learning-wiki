Evidence¶

Brookman-Byrne A 2018¶

Brookman-Byrne A, Mareschal D, Tolmie AK, Dumontheil I (2018) Inhibitory control and counterintuitive science and maths reasoning in adolescence. PLoS ONE 13(6): e0198973. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0198973

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Hierarchical multiple regression (N = 81) predicting misconception accuracy; model 1b with Stroop RT cost added gave R2 = 31%, ΔR2 = 5.0%, β = -.22, p = .023. "Greater Stroop RT cost was associated with lower misconception accuracy" after controls.

"Stroop RT cost was selected using a stepwise approach in model 1b, uniquely accounting for 5% of the variance. Greater Stroop RT cost was associated with lower misconception accuracy."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Bilinguals show a marginally smaller Stroop effect and higher overall Stroop accuracy than monolinguals — related
  • Adolescents show lower accuracy and longer reaction times on counterintuitive misconception trials than on matched control trials in science and maths — related
  • Better response inhibition (complex No-Go accuracy) is associated with longer reaction times on counterintuitive misconception trials, not better performance — related
  • In discipline-specific exploratory regressions, neither Stroop RT cost nor complex No-Go accuracy significantly predicted science or maths misconception measures separately — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Smaller response-time differences between incongruent and congruent trials (better inhibition) correlate with higher mathematics scores — related
