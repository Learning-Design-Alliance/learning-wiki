## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The study crossed emoticon frequency (none, 1, 3, or 6) with valence (happy, sad, or confused) between groups using identical written feedback. [→ Moffitt 2020](#moffitt-2020)

## Evidence

### Moffitt 2020

Moffitt, RL; Padgett, C; Grieve, R. (2020). Accessibility and emotionality of online assessment feedback: Using emoticons to enhance student perceptions of marker competence and warmth. Computers and Education, 143. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2019.103654

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Design description of the experiment: 241 undergraduates viewed an online faux essay with associated written feedback typical of higher education, identical across conditions except for emoticons, assigned between groups to frequency and valence conditions.

> "Using a between-groups design, emoticons were manipulated in two ways: frequency (none, 1, 3, or 6) and valence (happy, sad, or confused)."
