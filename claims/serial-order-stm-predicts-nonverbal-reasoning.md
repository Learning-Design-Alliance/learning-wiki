# Serial order STM at Age 4 predicts later nonverbal reasoning, but serial order STM gain does not predict nonverbal reasoning gain

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i0` negligible

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Serial order reconstruction performance at Age 4 predicted 10% of independent variance in Raven's matrices scores at Age 5 after controls, while nonword repetition did not independently predict later Raven's scores. [→ Leclercq 2010](#leclercq-2010)
`q3 i0` Performance increase in serial order STM did not predict performance increase in nonverbal intelligence between Age 4 and Age 5 after control of vocabulary increase (r = .07, p = .67). [→ Leclercq 2010 (2)](#leclercq-2010-2)

## Evidence

### Leclercq 2010

Leclercq, A.-L., & Majerus, S. (2010). Serial Order Short-Term Memory Predicts Vocabulary Development: Evidence from a Longitudinal Study. Developmental Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018540

`q3 · i?`

Regression predicting Age 5 Raven's Coloured Progressive Matrices from Age 4 STM measures in the same longitudinal cohort; serial order STM predicted "10% of independent variance" while nonword repetition did not.

> "However, serial order reconstruction performance at Age 4 remained a significant predictor of Raven's matrices scores one year later, predicting 10% of independent variance, after control of initial differences in age, vocabulary knowledge, Raven's matrices scores, and nonword delayed repetition performance."

### Leclercq 2010 (2)

Leclercq, A.-L., & Majerus, S. (2010). Serial Order Short-Term Memory Predicts Vocabulary Development: Evidence from a Longitudinal Study. Developmental Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018540

`q3 · i0`

Difference-score partial correlation for the nonverbal reasoning outcome; the gain-gain association was "r = .07, p = .67", which the authors contrast with the robust vocabulary association.

> "Nevertheless, performance increase in the serial order STM task did not predict performance increase in the nonverbal intelligence task between Age 4 and Age 5 after control of vocabulary increase (r = .07, p = .67)."
