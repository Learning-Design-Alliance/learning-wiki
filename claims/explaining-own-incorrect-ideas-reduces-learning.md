## Evidence

### Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr 2017

Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr. (2017). Eliciting explanations: Constraints on when self-explanation aids learning. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 24, 1501–1510. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1079-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Kuhn & Katz (2009), where middle-school students on a scientific reasoning task were prompted to explain their own causal predictions or completed the task without explaining. Explainers showed reduced evidence-based claim success, attributed to focus on preexisting theories.

> "Prompting children to explain their own predictions, which were often incorrect, reduced their subsequent success at making evidence-based claims relative to a no-explanation c ondition."

### Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr 2017 (2)

Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr. (2017). Eliciting explanations: Constraints on when self-explanation aids learning. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 24, 1501–1510. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1079-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Siegler (1995), in which 5-year-olds were assigned to explain correct solutions after attempting problems, explain their own solutions before feedback, or solve with feedback only. The explain-correct condition produced the most correct transfer solutions.

> "Children who explained correct solutions solved substantially more transfer problems correctly than children who explained their own solutions, who did not differ from children who were not prompted to explain."

### Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr 2017 (3)

Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr. (2017). Eliciting explanations: Constraints on when self-explanation aids learning. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 24, 1501–1510. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1079-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports a set of studies (e.g., Siegler & Chen 2008 on water displacement) contrasting explaining correct information alone with explaining correct plus incorrect exemplars. Explaining both "gained better comprehension and transfer," though two studies found sufficient prior knowledge was needed.

> "In all of these studies, learners who were prompted to explain both correct and incorrect information gained better comprehension and transfer"
