# The Coach's hint budget was managed poorly: students split into early-spenders and savers, and within the Coach condition only those with a deliberate hint strategy scored higher

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Interaction logs showed two distinct hint-budget patterns: spending most hints early on first problems, or saving hints and finishing with most of the budget unused. [→ Azimi 2026](#azimi-2026)
`q2 i?` Within the Coach condition, students with a deliberate strategy for when to spend a hint scored higher on assignments than students who saved hints without a plan. [→ Azimi 2026 (2)](#azimi-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Azimi 2026

Azimi, S. (2026). AI Literacy over Tool Design: A Mixed-Methods Study of Scaffolded versus Unrestricted Generative AI in Programming Education. Delft University of Technology. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16784

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Log analysis of Coach interactions in the pilot, examining how students distributed the 25-hint budget over a session. The logs show students who "spent most of their budget early in a session" and others who saved hints; several complained about the limit.

> "Some students spent most of their budget early in a session, on the first problems they encountered, and had few hints left for the more demanding tasks at the end of the session."

### Azimi 2026 (2)

Azimi, S. (2026). AI Literacy over Tool Design: A Mixed-Methods Study of Scaffolded versus Unrestricted Generative AI in Programming Education. Delft University of Technology. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16784

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive within-Coach-condition comparison of assignment rubric scores against logged hint-use patterns. The article concludes the budget "rewarded a capacity for self -governance that some students already possessed; it did not develop that capacity"; no effect size is printed.

> "the students who had developed a deliberate strategy for when to spend a hint and when to hold back scored higher on the assignments than the students who saved their hints without a plan."
