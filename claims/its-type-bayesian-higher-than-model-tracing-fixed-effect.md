## Subclaims
`q4 i?` Under a random-effects model, model tracing, Bayesian network modeling, and other types of ITS produced statistically significant effect sizes while constraint-based modeling and expectation and misconception tailoring did not; the higher mean for Bayesian network modeling was significantly greater than model tracing and constraint-based modeling only under a fixed-effect model. [→ Wenting Ma 2014](#wenting-ma-2014)

## Evidence

### Wenting Ma 2014

Wenting Ma, Olusola O. Adesope, John C. Nesbit, and Qing Liu. (2014). Intelligent Tutoring Systems and Learning Outcomes: A Meta-Analysis. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037123

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Type-of-ITS moderator analysis (Table 2). Random-effects means: Bayesian network modeling g = .54 (k = 10), model tracing g = .35 (k = 21), expectation and misconception tailoring g = .34 (k = 3), constraint-based modeling g = .24 (k = 7). Fixed-effect between-levels difference was significant, QB(5) = 36.37, p < .001, with Bayesian network modeling significantly higher than model tracing and constraint-based modeling in post hoc analyses.
