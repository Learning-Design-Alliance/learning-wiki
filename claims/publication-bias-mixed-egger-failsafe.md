# Publication bias tests give mixed evidence: Egger's test indicates bias, but fail-safe N calculations lead the authors to conclude no publication bias

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Egger's regression intercept was -5.75921 with two-tailed p = 0.00009, which the authors interpret as the funnel plot indicating publication bias. [→ San 2018](#san-2018)
`q2 i?` Rosenthal's fail-safe N required 2780 additional studies and Orwin's fail-safe N 2089 studies to nullify the effect, leading the authors to conclude no publication bias. [→ San 2018 (2)](#san-2018-2)

## Evidence

### San 2018

San, I. & Kis, A. (2018). Effect of traditional methods in geometry and numbers learning domains on academic achievement: A Meta-analysis study. International Journal of Research in Education and Science (IJRES), 4(2), 544-554. https://doi.org/10.21890/ijres.428950

`q2 · i?`

Publication bias analysis of the meta-analysis; the authors also note "partial bias can be observed from the figure 2" but call visual funnel-plot detection subjective.

> "As a result of Egger’s regression intercept test, the intercept value was computed as -5,75921 and two -tailed p-value as 0.00009. According to these results, it can be in terpreted that visual interpretation of asymmetry is true, and funnel plot indicates publication bias."

### San 2018 (2)

San, I. & Kis, A. (2018). Effect of traditional methods in geometry and numbers learning domains on academic achievement: A Meta-analysis study. International Journal of Research in Education and Science (IJRES), 4(2), 544-554. https://doi.org/10.21890/ijres.428950

`q2 · i?`

Fail-safe N analyses; because all Turkish graduate theses are held in the CoHE National Thesis Center database, the authors argue it is impossible that this many inaccessible theses exist, concluding no publication bias.

> "Accordingly, for Rosenthal’s Fail -safe N, 2780 studies (nearly 90 times more), and for Orwin’s fail-safe N, 2089 studies (nearly 68 times more) are needed to decrease the overall effect size to “no effect” level."
