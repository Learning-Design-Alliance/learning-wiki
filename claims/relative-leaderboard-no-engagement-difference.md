## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Top-, middle-, and bottom-ranked learners on a relative leaderboard demonstrated an equal level of course engagement, supporting H2b; equivalence was not tested. [→ Bai 2021](#bai-2021)

## Evidence

### Bai 2021

Bai, Shurui, Khe Foon Hew, Michael Sailer, and Chengyuan Jia. 2021. "From top to bottom: how positions on different types of leaderboard may affect fully online student learning performance, intrinsic motivation, and course engagement." Computers & Education 173: 104297. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2021.104297.

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-intervention SCEQ in the 26-student relative leaderboard quasi-experiment. Descriptively bottom-ranked students perceived the highest engagement (M = 3.98, SD = 0.48), but the ANOVA found "no significant different in student course engagement among three groups".

> "However, the result of a one-way ANOVA test suggested there is no significant different in student course engagement among three groups, F (2, 21) = 0.82, p = .454."
