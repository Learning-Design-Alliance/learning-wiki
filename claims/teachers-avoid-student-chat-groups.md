## Evidence

### Juan J. Merelo 2023

Juan J. Merelo, Pedro A. Castillo, Antonio M. Mora, Francisco Barranco, Noorhan Abbas, Alberto Guillén and Olia Tsivitanidou. (2023). Chatbots and messaging platforms in the classroom: an analysis from the teacher's perspective. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1007/s10639-023-11703-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10639-023-11703-x)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Findings from the first survey's interaction-preference items (Table 7): 59.1% favoured chat interactions among students in the same course, while only 6.7% wanted interactions including teachers of the School/Faculty and 28.7% preferred not being part of the interaction at all.

> "The answer, then, to this research question, is that, in general, teachers do not want to have interaction with students in a chat group."

### Juan J. Merelo 2023 (2)

Juan J. Merelo, Pedro A. Castillo, Antonio M. Mora, Francisco Barranco, Noorhan Abbas, Alberto Guillén and Olia Tsivitanidou. (2023). Chatbots and messaging platforms in the classroom: an analysis from the teacher's perspective. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1007/s10639-023-11703-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10639-023-11703-x)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Second-survey item on chat-group composition, plotted in Fig. 15 by sector: the vast majority preferred course-only groups, described as more focused groups dedicated to course organization, and about 30% of teachers considered they should not be part of the chat group.

> "Regarding the social factor of chat groups that teachers use in class, according to Fig. 15 the vast majority prefer small groups only with the students from the same course."
