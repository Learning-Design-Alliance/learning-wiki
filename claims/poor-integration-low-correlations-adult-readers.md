# Correlations among reading and reading-related measures were much lower than expected, indicating poor integration of skills in struggling adult readers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Although most correlations among reading-related measures were statistically significant, the majority were small (64% at pretest and 55% at posttest at or below .30), far below the r > .60 expected among typically developing child readers. [→ Greenberg Daphne 2011](#greenberg-daphne-2011)

## Evidence

### Greenberg Daphne 2011

Greenberg Daphne, Wise Justin, Morris Robin, Fredrick Laura, Nanda Alice O., Pae Hye-K. (2011). A randomized control study of instructional approaches for struggling adult readers. Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3237050/

`q3 · i?`

Pearson correlations were conducted within and across time points among the study's language and reading measures. At pretest only two coefficients exceeded .60 (WJ Word Attack with TOWRE Phonemic Decoding Efficiency, r = .71; TOWRE Sight Word Efficiency with GORT Fluency, r = .66); at posttest only four did.

> "More importantly, however, the majority of the correlation coefficients (64%) were at or below .30; a level that represents small relationships."
