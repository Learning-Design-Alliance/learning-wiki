# TTI transfer teachers received less mentoring but provided more mentoring than control counterparts

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Treatment focal teachers were less likely to have a mentor (39 versus 66 percent, p = 0.007) but spent 25 more minutes per week mentoring colleagues, a statistically significant difference. [→ Glazerman 2012](#glazerman-2012)

## Evidence

### Glazerman 2012

Glazerman, S., A. Protik, B. Teh, J. Bruch, N. Seftor. (2012). Moving Teachers: Implementation of Transfer Incentives in Seven Districts (NCEE 2012-4052). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized comparison using the Teacher Background Survey (Figure ES.2) reports treatment focal teachers "reported having a mentor at a significantly lower rate than did control focal teachers (39 versus 66 percent, p- value = 0.007)." Time with mentor (33 vs 58 minutes) was not significant.

> "treatment focal teachers (made up of TTI transfers and whoever else filled the targeted vacancies) reported having a mentor at a significantly lower rate than did control focal teachers (39 versus 66 percent, p- value = 0.007)"
