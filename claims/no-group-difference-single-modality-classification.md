# Monolingual and bilingual children perform comparably on single-modality classification tasks in accuracy and speed

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For the single task, there were no significant effects of language group, modality, or their interaction on accuracy. [→ Bialystok Ellen 2011](#bialystok-ellen-2011)
`q3 i?` Reaction times showed no effect of language group in either single or dual conditions. [→ Bialystok Ellen 2011 (2)](#bialystok-ellen-2011-2)

## Evidence

### Bialystok Ellen 2011

Bialystok Ellen. (2011). Coordination of Executive Functions in Monolingual and Bilingual Children. Journal of Experimental Child Psychology. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3139691/

`q3 · i?`

Separate 2-way ANOVA on single-task accuracy in the same experiment with 63 children. "For the single task, there were no effects for any factor: language group, F (1, 61) = 1.86, n.s., modality, F (1, 61) = 1.44, n.s., or their interaction, F (1, 61) = 3.39, n.s."

> "For the single task, there were no effects for any factor: language group, F (1, 61) = 1.86, n.s., modality, F (1, 61) = 1.44, n.s., or their interaction, F (1, 61) = 3.39, n.s."

### Bialystok Ellen 2011 (2)

Bialystok Ellen. (2011). Coordination of Executive Functions in Monolingual and Bilingual Children. Journal of Experimental Child Psychology. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3139691/

`q3 · i?`

3-way ANOVA on reaction times for language group, condition, and modality. "There was no effect for language group, F < 1", so the bilingual accuracy advantage came with no speed cost.

> "There was no effect for language group, F < 1."
