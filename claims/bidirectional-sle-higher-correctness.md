# Students working in a Bidirectional QR-code SLE receive significantly higher teacher-assigned correctness scores than students in a Traditional SLE

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The Experimental Group using the Bidirectional SLE received significantly higher correctness scores (mean 3.74 vs 3.02 for the Control Group). [→ Pérez-Sanagustín 2015](#perez-sanagustin-2015)

## Evidence

### Pérez-Sanagustín 2015

Pérez-Sanagustín, M., Muñoz-Merino, P. J., Alario-Hoyos, C., Soldani, X., & Delgado Kloos, C. (2015). Lessons Learned from the Design of Situated Learning Environments to Support Collaborative Knowledge Construction. Computers & Education, 87, 70-82. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2015.03.019

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Mixed-method experiment comparing 19 Control teams (N=103, Traditional SLE) with 28 Experimental teams (N=150, Bidirectional SLE) over two academic years, teams assigned alphabetically by surname. A mixed ANOVA on teacher rubric scores found the group effect significant, F (1, 45) = 14.209, p=0.000, with means of 3.02 (CG) and 3.74 (EG); the 95% pairwise interval was "[0.334, 1.099]".

> "The mixed ANOVA analysis revealed that, in terms of correctness, the type of group has  a statistically significant effect, F (1, 45) = 14.209, p=0.000, being the difference in favor of the EG in  terms of  scores received from teachers."
