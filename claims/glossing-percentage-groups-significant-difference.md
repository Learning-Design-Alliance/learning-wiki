## Evidence

### Taylor 2014

Taylor, A. M. (2014). Glossing Frequency and L2 Reading Comprehension: The Influence of CALL Glossing. CALICO Journal, 31(3). https://doi.org/10.11139/cj.31.3.374-389

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Between-group comparison in the meta-analysis of studies grouped by percentage of text glossed (50% or more, 5-10%, less than 5%). The article reports a significant difference between groups, Q = 6.16, p = .04, with no effect size printed for this omnibus contrast.

> "There was a significant difference between groups (Q = 6.16; p = .04), which means that the difference between the groups was attributable to something other than random chance."

### Taylor 2014 (2)

Taylor, A. M. (2014). Glossing Frequency and L2 Reading Comprehension: The Influence of CALL Glossing. CALICO Journal, 31(3). https://doi.org/10.11139/cj.31.3.374-389

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Descriptive group means from the same meta-analysis: 1.13 for more than 50% glossing, .85 for less than 5%, and .20 for 5-10%. The discussion calls the .20 mean "barely within the parameters of having any practical importance" and asks whether a threshold is in play.

> "Average effect sizes were observed of 1.13 for studies with more than 50% of the text glossed, .85 for studies that had less than 5% of the text glossed and .20 for studies that had 5 -10% of the text glossed."
