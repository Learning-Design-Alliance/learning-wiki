## Evidence

### Plackett R 2020

Plackett R, Kassianos AP, Kambouri M, et al. (2020). Online patient simulation training to improve clinical reasoning: a feasibility randomised controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02168-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Acceptability survey completed by 98 intervention students at Time 1: 82% agreed eCREST helped them learn clinical reasoning skills to apply to clinical work; 86% would use it again without an incentive. "Most students (> 80%) agreed that eCREST helped them learn clinical reasoning skills."

> "Most students (> 80%) agreed that eCREST helped them learn clinical reasoning skills and that they would use it again without incentives (Table 2)."

### Plackett R 2020 (2)

Plackett R, Kassianos AP, Kambouri M, et al. (2020). Online patient simulation training to improve clinical reasoning: a feasibility randomised controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02168-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Chi-squared comparisons of acceptability between cohorts (n = 98): "those in cohort two were significantly more likely than cohort one to agree that: eCREST helped to improve their clinical reasoning skills (87.7% vs 64.0%)", χ2 (2) = 7.5, p = .024; use again without incentive 97.3% vs 52.0%, p = .000.
