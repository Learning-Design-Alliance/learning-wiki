# Students who see the class-response bar graph before peer discussion are 30% more likely to switch from a less common to the most common answer than students who do not see it

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a crossover experiment in eight introductory biology sections, students who saw the bar graph of initial class responses switched from a less common to the most common answer significantly more often than students who discussed without seeing the graph. [→ Perez Kathryn E. 2010](#perez-kathryn-e-2010)

## Evidence

### Perez Kathryn E. 2010

Perez Kathryn E., Strauss Eric A., Downey Nicholas, Galbraith Anne, Jeanne Robert. (2010). Does Displaying the Class Results Affect Student Discussion during Peer Instruction? CBE Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.09-11-0080

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Chi-square analysis of 4182 student-response combinations from a crossover design across eight lecture sections, comparing bar-graph-shown versus not-shown treatments on switching from less common (LC) to most common (MC) answers. The study found "a significant difference (χ2 = 42.87; p < 0.0001; N = 4182)". No effect size was printed.

> "If students saw the bar graph, they were 30% more likely to switch from LC to MC than were those who did not see the graph, a significant difference (χ2 = 42.87; p < 0.0001; N = 4182)"
