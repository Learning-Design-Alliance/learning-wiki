# The active-learning advantage is robust to publication bias and to variation in methodological rigor of the included studies

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` Fail-safe numbers were high (114 studies for examination performance, 438 for failure rate), and effect sizes showed no heterogeneity by quality of controls over student or instructor equivalence. [→ Freeman S 2014](#freeman-s-2014)

## Evidence

### Freeman S 2014

Freeman S, Eddy SL, McDonough M, Smith MK, Okoroafor N, Jordt H, Wenderoth MP. (2014). Active learning increases student performance in science, engineering, and mathematics. PNAS 111(23):8410-8415. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1319030111

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Sensitivity analyses of the examination-score dataset across four categories of control over student equivalence and two categories of instructor equivalence. No heterogeneity by student-equivalence quality (Q = 2.097, df = 3, P = 0.553) or instructor identity (Q = 0.007, df = 1, P = 0.934).

> "there was no heterogeneity based on methodological quality ( Q = 2.097, df = 3, P = 0.553): Experiments where students were assigned to treatments at random produced results that were indistinguishable from three types of quasirandomized designs"
