## Evidence

### Rob J. Nadolski 2021

Rob J. Nadolski, Hans G. K. Hummel, Ellen Rusman, Kevin Ackermans. (2021). Rubric formats for the formative assessment of oral presentation skills acquisition in secondary education. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-021-10030-7

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r?`

The mixed ANOVA (two oral presentation scores as within-subjects factor, three rubric-format conditions as between-subjects factor) revealed an "overall and significant improvement of oral presentation performance over time", F(1, 157) = 58.13, p < 0.01, ηp2 = 0.27.

> "A mixed ANOVA, with oral presentation performance as within-subjects factor (two scores) and rubric format as between-subjects factor (three conditions), revealed an overall and significant improvement of oral presentation performance over time, with F(1, 157) = 58.13, p < 0.01, ηp2 = 0.27."

### Rob J. Nadolski 2021 (2)

Rob J. Nadolski, Hans G. K. Hummel, Ellen Rusman, Kevin Ackermans. (2021). Rubric formats for the formative assessment of oral presentation skills acquisition in secondary education. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-021-10030-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Within the same mixed ANOVA, significant differences over time were found between conditions, F(2, 156) = 17.38, p < 0.01, ηp2 = 0.18; however, "only control group students showed signifi- cantly progress on performance scores over time (at the 0.01 level)". Experimental-group growth was not statistically significant.
