# Low TMS scores predicted study prolongation in both school-leaving grade groups (OR=0.890-0.853), whereas the school-leaving GPA did not predict continuity

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among best school leavers, lower TMS scores significantly predicted prolongation of pre-clinical studies (OR=0.89), while prolongation and dropout risk were not related to the school-leaving GPA. [→ Kadmon Guni 2016](#kadmon-guni-2016)
`q2 i?` Among mediocre school leavers, dropout was not related to the TMS score but low TMS scores predicted study prolongation (OR=0.85). [→ Kadmon Guni 2016 (2)](#kadmon-guni-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Kadmon Guni 2016

Kadmon Guni, Kadmon Martina. (2016). Academic Performance of Students with the Highest and Mediocre School-leaving Grades: Does the Aptitude Test for Medical Studies (TMS) Balance Their Prognoses? GMS Journal for Medical Education 33(1):Doc7. [doi:10.3205/zma001006](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001006)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression in the best school leavers group found GPA unrelated to prolongation and dropout risk, while "lower TMS scores significantly predicted the prolongation of the studies albeit with an odds ratio of only 0.89"; the authors attribute the weak OR partly to few TMS-taking withdrawals.

> "In contrast, lower TMS scores significantly predicted the prolongation of the studies albeit with an odds ratio of only 0.89 (see Table 3 (Tab. 3), Point A; OR<1 denotes the chance of not prolonging the studies)."

### Kadmon Guni 2016 (2)

Kadmon Guni, Kadmon Martina. (2016). Academic Performance of Students with the Highest and Mediocre School-leaving Grades: Does the Aptitude Test for Medical Studies (TMS) Balance Their Prognoses? GMS Journal for Medical Education 33(1):Doc7. [doi:10.3205/zma001006](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001006)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression in the mediocre school leavers group mirrored the best leavers: dropout was unrelated to TMS score, but "the risk of study prolongation by low TMS scores was determined at an odds ratio of 0.85"; interpretation is limited by age differences in the low-TMS subgroups.

> "By logistic regression dropout was not related to the TMS score whereas the risk of study prolongation by low TMS scores was determined at an odds ratio of 0.85 (see Table 5 (Tab. 5), Point A)."
