## Evidence

### Suzane F. Pinto 2020

Suzane F. Pinto, Ronan S. Ferreira. (2020). Analyzing course programmes using complex networks. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2005.00906](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2005.00906)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Numerical analysis of the three UFOP themes networks (Fig. 3, Table 1). Complementary cumulative in- and out-degree distributions were fitted with p(k) proportional to exp(-alpha k); printed exponents include alpha_in = 0.22(1) for EE, 0.28(1) for EP and 0.29(1) for EC. The article states "Our model was able to capture these differences using a quantitative approach."

> "Figure 3 shows the input and output distributions for the three UFOP engineers analyzed: Electrical En- gineering (EE), Computer Engineering (EC) and Pro- duction Engineering (EP) The trace curves are expo- nential adjustments and the values of the respective exponents are shown in table 1."

### Suzane F. Pinto 2020 (2)

Suzane F. Pinto, Ronan S. Ferreira. (2020). Analyzing course programmes using complex networks. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2005.00906](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2005.00906)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The authors' initial network construction connected only related themes without temporal dependence or accumulation of knowledge; this baseline yielded a random point distribution, in contrast to the structured distributions obtained with the proposed model.

> "In this case, what we found was an exclusively random distribution of points randomly ordered on thexy plane (data not shown)."
