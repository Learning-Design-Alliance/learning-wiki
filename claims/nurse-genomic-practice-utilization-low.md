## Evidence

### Calzone 2026

Calzone, K., Stokes, L., Peterson, C., Yee, L. M., Liewehr, D., and Badzek, L. (2026). Evaluation of the Integration of Genetics and Genomics Into Nursing Practice. Journal of Nursing Scholarship 58, no. 1: e70056. https://doi.org/10.1111/jnu.70056

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Utilization section of the 2023 GGNPS (Table 5), focusing on family history, which the article notes involves no cost, is not technology dependent, and is in the domain of all nurses. The article reports "less than half (34.8%) always collected" age at diagnosis.

> "Of those that collected family history information, less than half (34.8%) always collected one of the essential indicators of inherited predisposition to disease, the age in which the condition was diagnosed. Additionally, only half of nurses (50.7%) always collected both maternal and paternal lineages."

### Calzone 2026 (2)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same utilization analysis: 39.5% never used family history to facilitate a clinical decision, while 28.7% occasionally and 16.7% frequently did; the article reports "most (N = 586, 75.1%) never facilitated a referral to genetic services".

> "Despite this, most (N = 586, 75.1%) never facilitated a referral to genetic services."
