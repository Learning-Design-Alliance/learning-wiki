# Direct observation and video-based feedback formats differ structurally in timing: immediate short feedback versus delayed feedback after student self-review of a videotaped encounter

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The two formats differ in time frame: direct observation feedback occurs immediately after the performance within a short, rigid slot, whereas video-based feedback is delayed one week and follows student self-review, offering the supervisor more freedom and flexibility. [→ Junod Perron Noëlle 2016](#junod-perron-noelle-2016)

## Evidence

### Junod Perron Noëlle 2016

Junod Perron Noëlle, Louis-Simonet Martine, Cerutti Bernard, Pfarrwaller Eva, Sommer Johanna, Nendaz Mathieu. (2016). Feedback in formative OSCEs: comparison between direct observation and video-based formats. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.3402/meo.v21.32160

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Authors' interpretive (e) account in the Discussion of design differences: direct format is a 20-min encounter with 15-min immediate feedback; video format is a 20-min videotaped encounter with delayed 40-min feedback one week later after the student reviews and analyzes his or her own performance.

> "During the direct observation format, supervisors have a rather short and rigid time for feedback and teaching and may feel under pressure, preventing them to discuss and match their observations to students’ needs."
