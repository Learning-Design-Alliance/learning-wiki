Evidence¶

Stegemann 2014¶

Stegemann, K. C., & Grünke, M. (2014). Revisiting an Old Methodology for Teaching Counting, Computation, and Place Value: The Effectiveness of the Finger Calculation Method for At-Risk Children. Learning Disabilities: A Contemporary Journal, 12(2), 191-213. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1047461

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Within-group Wilcoxon Signed-Rank analysis of the quasi-experimental pre-post control group study: the grade 2 at-risk experimental group improved significantly in attitudes for math (Z = -2.210, p = .027) and Applied Problems, while "The control at-risk group made no statistically significant improvements in any area."

"only the experimental at-risk group demonstrated significant changes in Applied Problems (Z = -2.380, p = .018) and attitudes for math (TOMA) (Z = -2.210, p = .027). The control at-risk group made no statistically significant improvements in any area."

Stegemann 2014 (2)¶

Stegemann, K. C., & Grünke, M. (2014). Revisiting an Old Methodology for Teaching Counting, Computation, and Place Value: The Effectiveness of the Finger Calculation Method for At-Risk Children. Learning Disabilities: A Contemporary Journal, 12(2), 191-213. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1047461

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Authors' corrected effect size (Masendorf formula) for the grade 2 at-risk attitude gain: "a standardized mean difference of 1.86", described as very large. Quasi-experimental design, non-randomized existing classes; no standardized effect-size metric beyond the corrected value is printed.

"The Chisanbop method seemed to spark a noteworthy change in attitude in at-risk second graders. A calculation of a corrected effect size yielded a standardized mean difference of 1.86. Such a value can be considered very large (Ellis, 2010)."

Related Claims¶
