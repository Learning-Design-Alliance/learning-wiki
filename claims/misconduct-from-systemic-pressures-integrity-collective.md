# Academic misconduct emerges primarily from systemic pressures rather than individual moral failure, and academic integrity is a collective epistemic practice institutions must sustain

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Academic misconduct is generated primarily by systemic pressures, not individual moral failure; environments rewarding performance over learning, audit cultures, rankings, and precarity shape students' practical options. [→ Tristan B. Taylor & Travis LaCroix 2026](#tristan-b-taylor-travis-lacroix-2026)
`q1 i?` Academic integrity is not equivalent to the absence of misconduct; it entails positive epistemic virtues and cannot be achieved individually, functioning as a de facto social contract that institutions bear responsibility for sustaining.
