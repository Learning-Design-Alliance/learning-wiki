# Students using the Socratic AI Tutor perceived significantly greater support for critical and reflective thinking than students using an uninstructed chatbot

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Socratic-condition students rated the chatbot's responses as more thought-provoking (stimulated reflection), with a significant group effect. [→ Degen 2025](#degen-2025)
`q2 i?` Socratic-condition students reported significantly higher agreement that the chatbot fosters critical thinking. [→ Degen 2025 (2)](#degen-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Degen 2025

Degen, P.-B., & Asanov, I. (2025). Beyond Automation: Socratic AI, Epistemic Agency, and the Implications of the Emergence of Orchestrated Multi-Agent Learning Architectures. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2508.05116](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2508.05116)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multi-level analysis (random intercepts, 65 students, 123 observations) of the reflection item in the between-subjects feasibility study. The Socratic condition differed significantly from the uninstructed chatbot condition; the article prints a significant group effect (b = –0.66, p = .004) with a standardised coefficient of β = –0.54, which the article reports but does not label as an effect size magnitude.

> "revealed a statistically significant difference in participants' ratings of how thought-provoking the chatbot responses were, with model yielding a significant group effect (b = –0.66, SE = 0.22, t(62.48) = –2.97, p = .004)) and a standardised coefficient of β = –0.54, 95% CI [–0.94, –0.13]"

### Degen 2025 (2)

Degen, P.-B., & Asanov, I. (2025). Beyond Automation: Socratic AI, Epistemic Agency, and the Implications of the Emergence of Orchestrated Multi-Agent Learning Architectures. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2508.05116](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2508.05116)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Linear mixed-effects model on the critical-thinking item from the post-intervention survey (UTAUT2 and AI-chatbot perception scale adaptations). The Socratic group reported significantly higher perceived support (b = –0.61, p = .011) with a standardised effect of β = –0.54, which the article calls a moderate effect size but prints no d, r, or η² value.

> "we found statistically significant difference in students' agreement with the statement  "The chatbot fosters my critical thinking",  the Socratic group again reported significantly higher perceived support ( b = –0.61, SE = 0.23, t(65.27) = –2.62, p = .011), with a standardised effect of β = –0.54, 95% CI [–0.94, –0.13]"
