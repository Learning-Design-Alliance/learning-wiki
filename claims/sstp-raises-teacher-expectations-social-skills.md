# The SSTP raised teachers' expectations: after training, 14 social skills were rated as very important/critical for students with special needs by more than 50% of experimental teachers, versus one skill before training

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2013 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Before training only one skill was rated critical for students with special needs by more than 50% of experimental teachers; after training 14 skills (seven assertion, four self-control, three cooperation) exceeded the 50% threshold, and control teachers never rated any skill critical. [→ Elif SAZAK PINAR 2013](#elif-sazak-pinar-2013)
`q2 i?` Expectation gains were maintained at follow-up, with all assertion skills rated critical by more than 50% of experimental teachers. [→ Elif SAZAK PINAR 2013 (2)](#elif-sazak-pinar-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Elif SAZAK PINAR 2013

Elif SAZAK PINAR, Bülbin SUCUOGLU. (2013). The Outcomes of a Social Skills Teaching Program for Inclusive Classroom Teachers. Educational Sciences: Theory & Practice, 13(4), 2247-2261. https://www.edam.com.tr/kuyeb

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Percentage analysis of Social Skills Rating System importance ratings (Table 2) for students with special needs, before and after training and at follow-up, for 12 experimental and 17 control teachers; skills rated very important by more than 50% of teachers were deemed critical.

> "After the SSTP , the number of critical social skills rated by the experiment group increased, and 14 social skills (seven skills from assertion, four from self-control, and three from the cooperation subscales) were rated as very important/critical by more than 50% of the teachers in the experiment group."

### Elif SAZAK PINAR 2013 (2)

Elif SAZAK PINAR, Bülbin SUCUOGLU. (2013). The Outcomes of a Social Skills Teaching Program for Inclusive Classroom Teachers. Educational Sciences: Theory & Practice, 13(4), 2247-2261. https://www.edam.com.tr/kuyeb

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Follow-up percentages in Table 2 show experimental teachers' expectations were maintained: all assertion skills exceeded the 50% criticality threshold and two skills were rated very important by 75% of teachers.

> "According to the follow-up data, the expectations of the teachers in the experiment group were maintained for students with special needs. All assertion skills were rated as critical by more than 50% of the teachers, and two skills were viewed as very important in the follow-up stages by 75% of the teachers."
