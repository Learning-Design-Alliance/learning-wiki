# Visual causal models are perceived as helpful and complete, and belief in the information does not differ by presentation mode

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants in the dual-mode condition rated the visual causal models as helpful (M = 6.3) and complete (M = 6.0), with no patient–lay difference. [→ Kim 2013](#kim-2013)
`q3 i?` Belief ratings of the GAD information showed no main effects or interactions of presentation or participant type. [→ Kim 2013 (2)](#kim-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Kim 2013

Kim, N. S., Khalife, D., Judge, K. A., Paulus, D. J., Jordan, J. T., & Yopchick, J. E. (2013). Visual Causal Models Enhance Clinical Explanations of Treatments for Generalized Anxiety Disorder. Journal of Health Communication, 18(Suppl 1), 103-117. https://doi.org/10.1080/10810730.2013.829136

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Ratings from dual-mode participants on 7-point helpfulness and completeness scales; completeness was rated M = 6.0 (SE = 0.1, p = .40 for group difference).

> "Visual causal models were judged to be helpful overall (M = 6.3, SE = 0.1); again, lay people and patients did not differ (p = .78)"

### Kim 2013 (2)

Kim, N. S., Khalife, D., Judge, K. A., Paulus, D. J., Jordan, J. T., & Yopchick, J. E. (2013). Visual Causal Models Enhance Clinical Explanations of Treatments for Generalized Anxiety Disorder. Journal of Health Communication, 18(Suppl 1), 103-117. https://doi.org/10.1080/10810730.2013.829136

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Null result on belief ratings (overall M = 5.9, SE = 0.1 on a 7-point scale), suggesting participants received the clinicians' information without significant skepticism; equivalence was not tested.

> "A 2 (presentation) × 2 (participant type) univariate ANOVA, conducted on people's ratings of how much they believed the GAD information, revealed no main effects or interactions (all ps > .15)"
