# Iterative validation of ViMAP models against a real-world ramp scenario teaches students initial conditions and model refinement

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Through frequent comparison of programmed simulations with the real-world rolling-balls scenario, students in both elementary and high school studies learned to recognize constraints such as initial conditions and refined their models iteratively. [→ Pratim Sengupta 2012](#pratim-sengupta-2012)

## Evidence

### Pratim Sengupta 2012

Pratim Sengupta, Amy Voss Farris, Mason Wright. (2012). From Agents to Continuous Change via Aesthetics: Learning Mechanics with Visual Agent-based Computational Modeling. Technology, Knowledge & Learning (accepted for publication). [doi:10.1007/s10758-012-9190-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10758-012-9190-9)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Qualitative observations from both studies during Phase 2, where students modeled two balls rolling down ramps and one pushed ball. Nearly all students first modeled the blue ball at constant speed and only after repeated "validation of the simulated world with the real world" realized it should slow down each step.

> "This shows that validation of the simulated world with the real world played an important role in the iterative refinement of students' models."
