# Extensive reading reduced Japanese EFL learners' anxiety about foreign-language reading (r = .34)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Extensive reading significantly reduced Anxiety toward EFL reading, with a medium effect size (r = .34). [→ Junko Yamashita 2013](#junko-yamashita-2013)

## Evidence

### Junko Yamashita 2013

Junko Yamashita. (2013). Effects of extensive reading on reading attitudes in a foreign language. Reading in a Foreign Language, 25(2). http://nflrc.hawaii.edu/rfl [doi:10.64152/10125/66872](https://doi.org/10.64152/10125/66872)

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Bonferroni-corrected multiple comparisons after repeated-measures ANOVA showed a significant pretest–posttest decrease in Anxiety with a medium effect (r = .34). Table 1 shows the Anxiety mean fell from 3.17 to 2.84.

> "Effect size was large for Comfort (r = .54), medium for Anxiety (r = .34), close to medium but still small for Intellectual Value (r = .26), and negligible for Practical Value (r = .09)"

### Junko Yamashita 2013 (2)

Junko Yamashita. (2013). Effects of extensive reading on reading attitudes in a foreign language. Reading in a Foreign Language, 25(2). http://nflrc.hawaii.edu/rfl

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The omnibus main effect of time across all four attitude variables was not significant; the attitude changes emerged through the time × attitude interaction rather than a uniform shift.

> "the main effect of time was not significant, F(1, 60) = 0.364, p = .549, ηp2 < .001"
