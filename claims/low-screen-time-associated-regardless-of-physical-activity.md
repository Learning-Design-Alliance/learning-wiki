## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Japanese children with lower screen time had 2.0–2.7 times greater odds of high academic performance regardless of their physical activity level, indicating screen time is associated with academic performance independent of physical activity. [→ Ishii Kaori 2020](#ishii-kaori-2020)

## Evidence

### Ishii Kaori 2020

Ishii Kaori, Aoyagi Kenryu, Shibata Ai, Javad Koohsari Mohammad, Carver Alison, Oka Koichiro. (2020). Joint Associations of Leisure Screen Time and Physical Activity with Academic Performance in a Sample of Japanese Children. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17030757

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Authors' synthesis of the whole-sample logistic regression results from the longitudinal study of 261 Japanese children, with odds ratios OR = 2.04 and OR = 2.75: "2.0–2.7 times greater odds of having high academic performance, regardless of their physical activity level".

> "We found that Japanese children with lower screen time had 2.0–2.7 times greater odds of having high academic performance, regardless of their physical activity level."
