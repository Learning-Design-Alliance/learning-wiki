Evidence¶

Hiroyuki Yamada 2024¶

Hiroyuki Yamada. (2024). New Normal in Early Elementary Mathematics Learning: Part III - Learning from Variation in Grade 3. E4 Center, Northwestern University. https://e4.northwestern.edu/2024/12/19/new-normal-in-early-elementary-mathematics-learning-part-iii-learning-from-variation-in-grade-3/

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Cross-lagged panel model of domain scores across nine testing windows (fit: CFI = .98, TLI = .96, RMSEA = .05, SRMR = .06); Tables 3-4 print all path coefficients significant at p < .001, with AL autoregressive and AL-NO paths largest.

"Overall, AL could be considered as the strongest predictor of the subsequent domain-level performance, followed by NO. As shown in the lag 2 coefficients, those patterns observed in the strength of within-domain relationships and the associations between AL and NO remained in effect even when lagged by two testing windows."

Hiroyuki Yamada 2024 (2)¶

Hiroyuki Yamada. (2024). New Normal in Early Elementary Mathematics Learning: Part III - Learning from Variation in Grade 3. E4 Center, Northwestern University. https://e4.northwestern.edu/2024/12/19/new-normal-in-early-elementary-mathematics-learning-part-iii-learning-from-variation-in-grade-3/

q2 · i? · associational · r2

CLPM paths from Grade 3 winter domain scores to the spring overall Diagnostic (Table 4): AL→OV .24, NO→OV .19, MS→OV .14, GE→OV .09, all statistically significant with p < .001.

"The way those domain-level outcomes eventually contributed to the overall Mathematics performance in Spring in Grade 3 is delineated in Table 4. The strongest predictor was AL, followed by NO, MS, and GE in that order."
