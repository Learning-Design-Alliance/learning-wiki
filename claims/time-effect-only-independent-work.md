## Evidence

### Jennie K. Grammer 2021

Jennie K. Grammer, Keye Xu and Agatha Lenartowicz. (2021). Effects of context on the neural correlates of attention in a college classroom. npj Science of Learning 6:15. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00094-8

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

EEG analysis comparing the first versus last 2 minutes of each 10-minute activity, testing the widely cited 10–15 minute attention-span claim. A "signiﬁcant interaction effect with activity type" qualified the main effect; no effect size is printed for this omnibus test.

> "Alpha power was signiﬁcantly lower during the starting period ( MStart = 2.08) of the activity then the ending period of each activity ( Mend = 2.20), χ2(1) = 7.81, p = 0.005, and showed a signiﬁcant interaction effect with activity type, χ2(3) = 14.93, p = 0.002."

### Jennie K. Grammer 2021 (2)

Jennie K. Grammer, Keye Xu and Agatha Lenartowicz. (2021). Effects of context on the neural correlates of attention in a college classroom. npj Science of Learning 6:15. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00094-8

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Follow-up contrasts on the activity-type-by-time interaction from the same EEG analysis: "alpha was signiﬁcantly lower in the starting 2 min than the ending 2 min during the independent work only", r = 0.21.

> "Further comparisons indicated that alpha was signiﬁcantly lower in the starting 2 min than the ending 2 min during the independent work only, b = −0.43, t = −3.72, p < 0.001, r = 0.21, suggesting that time-related differences in student attention only occurred when students ﬁnished work independently."
