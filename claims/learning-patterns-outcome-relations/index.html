Evidence¶

Vermunt 2004¶

Vermunt, J. D. & Vermetten, Y. J. (2004). Patterns in Student Learning: Relationships Between Learning Strategies, Conceptions of Learning, and Learning Orientations. Educational Psychology Review. https://dspacelibrary.uu.nl/handle/1874/11958

q2 · i? · review · r2

Review synthesis of outcome studies (Beishuizen and Stoutjesdijk 1999; Busato et al. 1998; Lonka et al. 1997; Lindblom-Yläne and Lonka 1999; Vermunt 1992). Vermunt (1992) reported learning patterns explained 25-55% of variance in exam results by subject area. No standardized effect sizes printed.

"In general, studies show that meaning directed learning is associated positively with indicators for study achievements, even with scores on factual exam questions."

Vermunt 2004 (2)¶

Vermunt, J. D. & Vermetten, Y. J. (2004). Patterns in Student Learning: Relationships Between Learning Strategies, Conceptions of Learning, and Learning Orientations. Educational Psychology Review. https://dspacelibrary.uu.nl/handle/1874/11958

q2 · i? · review · r2

Review synthesis of outcome studies: the review reports undirected learning was consistently and negatively related to all types of exam results in both university types and all subject areas, while application-directed learning showed no relation to study success in most cases. No standardized effect sizes printed.

"Undirected learning mostly showed strong negative relations to exam performance, whereas application directed learning showed no relation to study success in most cases."

Related Claims¶

  • Meaning-directed learning is positively related to academic performance while undirected learning is consistently negatively related — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Cross-country meta-analysis: meaning-directed, reproduction-directed and undirected patterns are universal, while application-directed, passive-idealistic and passive-motivational patterns are context-specific — related
  • Four learning patterns (meaning-, reproduction-, application-, and undirected) form a replicable factor structure in first-year higher education students — related
