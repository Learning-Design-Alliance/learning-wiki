# High joint on-task engagement with high equality and mutuality of engagement did not necessarily mean cognitive engagement or ensure conceptual change

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In dyads with high joint on-task engagement high in equality and mutuality, one student achieved substantial conceptual change while the other showed none, indicating such engagement did not guarantee change. [→ Tao 1997](#tao-1997)

## Evidence

### Tao 1997

Tao, Ping-Kee; Gunstone, Richard F. (1997). Conceptual Change in Science through Collaborative Learning at the Computer. Paper presented at the Annual Meeting of the National Association for Research in Science Teaching (70th, Oak Brook, IL, March 21-24, 1997). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED406151

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Case-study analysis of the Sam/Mick and Nigel/Stan dyads, whose conversational interactions showed conceptual change during the FMM lessons but where Mick and Stan reverted to alternative conceptions after instruction. The dyads' engagement was high in equality and mutuality, yet only one member of each sustained change.

> "In two of the dyads with high joint on-task engagement (Sam/Mick and Nigel/Stan), one student achieved substantial change but the other student showed no change."
