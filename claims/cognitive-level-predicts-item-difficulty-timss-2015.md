# Item difficulty index moves toward zero as the item's cognitive level increases at both grade levels

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` At eighth grade, difficulty indices decreased across knowing, applying and reasoning levels (p̄ .45, .35, .30), a significant ANOVA result. [→ İlhan et al. 2020](#ilhan-et-al-2020)

## Evidence

### İlhan et al. 2020

Mustafa İlhan, Nagihan Boztunç Öztürk, Melek Gülşah Şahin. (2020). The Effect of the Item’s Type and Cognitive Level on Its Difficulty Index: The Sample of TIMSS 2015. Participatory Educational Research, 7(2), 47-59. http://dx.doi.org/10.17275/per.20.19.7.2

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

One-way ANOVA on eighth-grade TIMSS 2015 item difficulty indices across the three TIMSS cognitive levels; mean p̄ fell from .45 (knowing) to .30 (reasoning), "F(2-212)=12.48, p<.001".

> "when the differences between the difficulty indices of the items with different cognitive levels are tested by means of one-way ANOVA, the result is statistically significant [𝑝̅ values of the items at the level of knowing, applying and reasoning are .45, .35 and .30 respectively; F(2-212)=12.48, p<.001]"

### İlhan et al. 2020 (2)

Mustafa İlhan, Nagihan Boztunç Öztürk, Melek Gülşah Şahin. (2020). The Effect of the Item’s Type and Cognitive Level on Its Difficulty Index: The Sample of TIMSS 2015. Participatory Educational Research, 7(2), 47-59. http://dx.doi.org/10.17275/per.20.19.7.2

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Fourth-grade ANOVA across cognitive levels of TIMSS 2015 mathematics items; mean p̄ was .53, .42 and .36 for knowing, applying and reasoning, "F(2-175)=12.79, p<.001".

> "the differences between the difficulty indices of the items in terms of both cognitive levels [𝑝̅ values of the items at the level of knowing, applying and reasoning are .53, .42 and .36 respectively; F(2-175)=12.79, p<.001] and types [𝑝̅ values for multiple choice and open ended items are .51 and .40 respectively, t(176)=4.29, p<.001] are statistically significant"
