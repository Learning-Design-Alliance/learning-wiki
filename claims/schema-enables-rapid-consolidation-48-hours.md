# Prior schema learning allows one-trial paired associates to be recalled 24 hours later and consolidated within 48 hours, becoming hippocampus-independent

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Normal rats with a trained schema recalled new one-trial paired associates (PAs 7 and 8) in a probe test 24 hours later. [→ Tse et al. 2007](#tse-et-al-2007)
`q3 i?` After near-complete hippocampal lesions made 48 hours after training, rats still recalled both the original schema and the new one-trial pairs, implying storage outside the hippocampus. [→ Tse et al. 2007 (2)](#tse-et-al-2007-2)

## Evidence

### Tse et al. 2007

Dorothy Tse, Rosamund F. Langston, Masaki Kakeyama, Ingrid Bethus, Patrick A. Spooner, Emma R. Wood, Menno P. Witter, Richard G. M. Morris. (2007). Schemas and Memory Consolidation. Science. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1135935

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2: after about a month of schema training, a single session of six trials introduced two new paired associates, each rewarded only once. A nonrewarded probe test 24 hours later showed "Effective recall in PT4 of the location of the cued new PA" (repeated-measures F = 65.28, P < 0.001; cued above chance, t = 10.29, P < 0.001).

> "Effective recall in PT4 of the location of the cued new PA (solid bar), coupled with avoidance of the noncued new PA (gray bar) and the remaining original associates (open bar) 24 hours after a single session of training with only one trial of each new PA"

### Tse et al. 2007 (2)

Dorothy Tse, Rosamund F. Langston, Masaki Kakeyama, Ingrid Bethus, Patrick A. Spooner, Emma R. Wood, Menno P. Witter, Richard G. M. Morris. (2007). Schemas and Memory Consolidation. Science. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1135935

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Postoperative probe tests in experiment 2 after ~90% hippocampal lesions made 48 hours after training of the new pairs. Both sham (n = 8) and HPC-lesioned (n = 10) groups dug at cued locations of original and new associates above chance (ts > 8.07, P < 0.001; Group × Location F < 1, n.s.), implying rapid neocortical consolidation within 48 hours.

> "The hippocampal-lesioned group not only could successfully recall the original PAs learned over the previous month (Fig. 2D) but also, remarkably, could remember the newly acquired pairs PA7 and PA8"
