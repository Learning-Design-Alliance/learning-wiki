## Evidence

### Bursztyn Leonardo 2015

Bursztyn Leonardo, Jensen Robert. (2015). How Does Peer Pressure Affect Educational Investments? The Quarterly Journal of Economics 130(3). https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjv021

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of account login data for the full field experiment sample of 825 students, an outcome for which the article notes experimental control is lost because students could communicate afterward and the analysis may be underpowered. The article reports public treatment "reduces the rate of logging in to use the system by 8.2 percentage points" in nonhonors classes.

> "For the full sample of students, we find that in nonhonors classes, making the course public reduces the rate of logging in to use the system by 8.2 percentage points (from a base of 57 percent when sign-up is private; p-value = .051). In honors classes, as with the sign-up decision, there is no difference in login rates between public and private treatments (77 percent for private, 78 percent for public)."
