## Evidence

### Simone Lackner 2023

Simone Lackner, Frederico Francisco, Cristina Mendonça, André Mata, Joana Gonçalves-Sá. (2023). Some scientific knowledge is a dangerous thing: overconfidence grows non-linearly with knowledge. Nature Human Behaviour. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-023-01677-8

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The authors' own 2021 online survey (Lackner dataset) of 994 stratified respondents in Portugal, Germany and Norway, repeating the EB knowledge questionnaire and adding a direct self-reported confidence metric. The article reports confidence "grows very fast (and non-linearly) in the early knowledge bins" for both metrics and all countries; no effect size is printed.

> "Once again, it can be observed that confidence grows very fast (and non-linearly) in the early knowledge bins for both confidence metrics (Figure 3F and 3H), all calibration error measures (Supplementary Figure S7) and in all countries analysed"

### Simone Lackner 2023 (2)

Simone Lackner, Frederico Francisco, Cristina Mendonça, André Mata, Joana Gonçalves-Sá. (2023). Some scientific knowledge is a dangerous thing: overconfidence grows non-linearly with knowledge. Nature Human Behaviour. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-023-01677-8

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Reanalysis of the independently published Fernbach dataset (1559 participants, France, Germany, USA) on genetically modified foods, which originally reported evidence of the DKE. Applying the indirect metric, the article reports the incorrect-answer proportion "grows very fast in the lower knowledge bins"; no effect size is printed.

> "we observed that this proportion grows very fast in the lower knowledge bins so that the least knowledgeable remain the more likely to answer “Don’t know” instead of incorrectly."
