## Evidence

### Peter A. Edelsbrunner 2025

Peter A. Edelsbrunner, Bianca A. Simonsmeier, Michael Schneider. (2025). The Reliability, But Not the Cronbach's Alpha, of Knowledge Tests Matters: Response to Zitzmann and Orona (2025). Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10023-5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

The commentary reports, citing McCrae et al. (2011), a study of 34,108 participants in which "only retest reliability, not alpha, predicted validity indices for the NEO Personality Inventory." The reviewed study's design is not described beyond this.

> "A study of 34,108 participants found that only retest reliability, not alpha, predicted validity indices for the NEO Personality Inventory (McCrae et al., 2011)."

### Peter A. Edelsbrunner 2025 (2)

Peter A. Edelsbrunner, Bianca A. Simonsmeier, Michael Schneider. (2025). The Reliability, But Not the Cronbach's Alpha, of Knowledge Tests Matters: Response to Zitzmann and Orona (2025). Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10023-5

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The authors' stated boundary condition: knowledge changes through instruction or development could lower test-retest correlations even when measurement error is low, so retest reliability "is no panacea."

> "However, estimating the retest reliabil- ity of a knowledge test requires a testing interval during which no learning occurs."
