Evidence¶

Sifatul Anindho 2026¶

Sifatul Anindho, Videep Venkatesha, Jaclyn Ocumpaugh, and Nathaniel Blanchard. (2026). Ordered Network Analysis of Epistemic Emotions during Collaborative Problem Solving. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23317 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.23317

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Separate ONA models for self-caught and probe-caught reports (64% probe-caught vs. 36% self-caught) show "stronger self-loops among the core epistemic states" in probe-caught data, while self-caught reports showed more distributed transitions involving higher-arousal states such as frustration, surprise, and conflict.

"ONA models constructed from probe-caught reports exhibit stronger self-loops among the core epistemic states, indicating more sustained epistemic engagement over time. In contrast, ONA models constructed from self-caught reports showed more distributed transitions that involved aﬀective states typiﬁed by higher arousal, such as frustration, surprise, and conﬂict."

Related Claims¶

  • Curious, optimistic, and confused are the most frequently reported affective states, and self-caught reports are more evenly distributed than probe-caught reports — related
  • Affective experience during situated collaborative problem solving is organized around a stable epistemic core linking curiosity, optimism, and confusion with short-range persistence — related
  • Faster CPS groups show stronger transitions among curiosity and optimism, while slower groups show stronger transitions among confusion, conflict, and frustration — related
  • Confusion+frustration and curiosity+engagement were the only affective state pairs co-occurring above chance, and within each pair one state was more likely to imply the other — related
  • Low-learning groups tended to make stronger connections from features associated with confusion and uncertainty to individual exploration and inactivity, while high-learning groups exhibited stronger connections between features related to knowledge construction. — related
