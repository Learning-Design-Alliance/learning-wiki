# Non-core course failures in ninth grade were associated with decreases in four-year graduation rates similar to core course failures

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among 2011-14 freshman cohorts, students with one core failure and no other failures graduated at 70 percent; students with one non-core failure and no other failures graduated at 68 percent; students passing all courses graduated at 90 percent. [→ Seeskin 2018](#seeskin-2018)
`q2 i?` More than 3,500 students (6.7 percent of ninth-graders) were on-track but failed at least one non-core semester; the 326 students who failed one core semester and two non-core semesters had a 44 percent graduation rate. [→ Seeskin 2018 (2)](#seeskin-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Seeskin 2018

Seeskin, A., Nagaoka, J., & Mahaffie, S. (2018). Hidden risk: Changes in GPA across the transition to high school. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/hidden-risk-changes-gpa-across-transition-high-school

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Observational analysis of 2011-14 CPS freshman cohorts relating ninth-grade failure counts to four-year graduation (Table 1). "the graduation rate for students with one core course failure and no other failures in ninth grade was 70 percent" versus 68 percent for one non-core failure; students passing all courses had a 90 percent graduation rate.

> "the graduation rate for students with one core course failure and no other failures in ninth grade was 70 percent; for students with one non-core course failure and no other failures, the graduation rate was very similar, at 68 percent"

### Seeskin 2018 (2)

Seeskin, A., Nagaoka, J., & Mahaffie, S. (2018). Hidden risk: Changes in GPA across the transition to high school. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/hidden-risk-changes-gpa-across-transition-high-school

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of 2011-14 cohorts (Table 1). The report notes more than 3,500 students (6.7 percent) achieved on-track status while failing at least one non-core semester, and "the 326 students who failed only one semester of a core course but failed two semesters of non-core courses" graduated at only 44 percent.

> "the 326 students who failed only one semester of a core course but failed two semesters of non-core courses likely received an on-track status in the ninth grade, but only 44 percent of them went on to graduate from high school within four years"
