## Evidence

### C.J. Efthimiou and R.A. Llewellyn 2007

C.J. Efthimiou and R.A. Llewellyn. (2007). Cinema, Fermi Problems, & General Education. [doi:10.1088/0031-9120/42/3/003](https://doi.org/10.1088/0031-9120/42/3/003)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

The authors' observation from continuing development of the Physics in Films program: general education students 'readily accept and learn to emulate the Fermi calculat ion approach' to seemingly very difficult problems. This is an instructor report; no measures or effect sizes are printed.

> "it has been dis covered that general education students, who normally shudder at the thought of d oing calculations of any kind, readily accept and learn to emulate the Fermi calculat ion approach to dealing with seemingly very diﬃcult, if not impossible problems."

### C.J. Efthimiou and R.A. Llewellyn 2007 (2)

C.J. Efthimiou and R.A. Llewellyn. (2007). Cinema, Fermi Problems, & General Education. [doi:10.1088/0031-9120/42/3/003](https://doi.org/10.1088/0031-9120/42/3/003)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

Report from the Earth-speed example introduced at the first class meeting: after a Fermi-style determination of Earth's orbital speed students 'gain conﬁdence,' can solve the simple problems, and 'retain more, having 'done it themsel ves'.' No measures are printed.

> "they gain conﬁdence. They discover that (a) they can solve th e simple problems like those above and (b) they retain more, having 'done it themsel ves'."
