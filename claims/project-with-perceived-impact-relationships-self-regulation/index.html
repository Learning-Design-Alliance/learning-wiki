Evidence¶

Nakamoto 2025¶

Nakamoto, J., Carter, C., & Russo, S. (2025). Implementation and impact of the Project With curriculum in juvenile justice facilities and group homes. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED673580

q2 · i? · design · r2

Exit-survey impact items (sample sizes 171 to 172) on healthy relationships. "77 percent of the Project With participants reported that the curriculum made them" more likely to better understand what makes a relationship healthy; 63 percent said the same for talking to a trusted adult if someone made them uncomfortable or hurt them.

"77 percent of the Project With participants reported that the curriculum made them “much more likely” or “somewhat more likely” to better understand what makes a relationship healthy."

Nakamoto 2025 (2)¶

Nakamoto, J., Carter, C., & Russo, S. (2025). Implementation and impact of the Project With curriculum in juvenile justice facilities and group homes. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED673580

q2 · i? · design · r2

Exit-survey impact items on self-regulation and decision-making (sample sizes 170 to 171). "66 percent of the youth reported that they were" more likely to think about consequences before making a decision; 62 percent reported being more likely to manage their emotions in healthy ways.

"66 percent of the youth reported that they were “much more likely” or “somewhat more likely” to think about the consequences before making a decision as a result of participating in Project With."

Nakamoto 2025 (3)¶

Nakamoto, J., Carter, C., & Russo, S. (2025). Implementation and impact of the Project With curriculum in juvenile justice facilities and group homes. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED673580

q2 · i? · design · r2

Exit-survey item on talking with parents about sex (n = 171 to 172). "only 37 percent of the youth reported that the curriculum made them" more likely to have such conversations, which the authors attribute to the curriculum's focus on relationships rather than sex and to limited youth-parent contact in this population.

"only 37 percent of the youth reported that the curriculum made them “much more likely” or “somewhat more likely” to talk with their parent, guardian, or caregiver about sex."
