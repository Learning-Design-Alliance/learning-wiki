## Evidence

### Sidorkin 2025

Sidorkin, A. M. (2025). Form–Substance Discrimination: Concept, Cognition, and Pedagogy. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2504.00412](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2504.00412)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article's conceptual account of the developmental arc: beginners fuse form and substance, and only at advanced levels can practitioners "systematically evaluate substantive elements despite significant formal distraction." This is a theoretical claim, not a new empirical study.

> "Novice readers and writers tend to perceive text holistically, with form and substance fused into a single impression of quality."

### Sidorkin 2025 (2)

Sidorkin, A. M. (2025). Form–Substance Discrimination: Concept, Cognition, and Pedagogy. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2504.00412](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2504.00412)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article reports, citing research it attributes to Granello (2001), that regular analysis of argumentative texts builds internalized markers of rigor and more efficient discrimination. No effect sizes are printed in the article.

> "Research demonstrates that students who regularly analyze argumentative texts progre ssively internalize structural markers of intellectual rigor—logical coherence, evidential substantiation, counter -argument consideration —enabling increasingly efficient discrimination between superficial rhetorical flourish and genuine analytical depth"
