# GLAT scores predicted performance on GenAI-supported tasks better than perceived ChatGPT proficiency

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 1 quant-synthesis `r1`, 1 associational `r2` · `q2`–`q3`

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` GLAT, a 20-item objective GenAI literacy test validated with 355 higher-education students, predicted performance on GenAI-supported tasks better than perceived ChatGPT proficiency. [→ Daniele Verí 2026](#daniele-veri-2026)

`q2 i?` GLAT scores were significant predictors of learners' performance in GenAI-supported tasks and outperformed self-reported measures such as perceived ChatGPT proficiency. [→ Yueqiao Jin 2024](#yueqiao-jin-2024)

Criterion evidence from the GLAT validation study (Jin et al., 2025) as reported in the review's results section. The test showed internal consistency of α = .80 and ω = .81 with good 2PL IRT structural fit; the review reports no effect size for the criterion comparison.

### Yueqiao Jin 2024

Yueqiao Jin, Roberto Martinez-Maldonado, Dragan Gašević and Lixiang Yan. (2024). GLAT: The Generative AI Literacy Assessment Test. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00283 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2411.00283](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2411.00283)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The article reports that GLAT scores predicted performance on a GenAI chatbot-supported visual-analytics comprehension task better than the self-reported ChatGPT Literacy Scale, in an OLS regression controlling for baseline score and visualisation literacy (mini-VLAT) with 83 students. Detailed regression coefficients appear beyond the provided text.

> "GLAT scores were found to be significant predictors of learners’ performanceinGenAI-supportedtasks,outperformingself-reportedmeasuressuchasperceived ChatGPT proficiency and demonstrating external validity."
