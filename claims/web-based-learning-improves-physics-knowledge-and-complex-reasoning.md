## Evidence

### Chandra 2012

Chandra, Vinesh & Watters, James J. (2012). Re-thinking physics teaching with web-based learning. Computers and Education, 58(1), 631-640. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2011.09.010

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

MANCOVA of pre/post scores in a non-equivalent groups design with treatment (N=48) and comparison (N=32) Year 12 students. The article reports "The main effect is significant for Knowledge and Complex Reasoning but not for Science Processes", with Table 5 printing partial η² of .136 (Knowledge) and .144 (Complex Reasoning) for GROUP.

> "The main effect is significant for Knowledge and Complex Reasoning but not for Science Processes."

### Chandra 2012 (2)

Chandra, Vinesh & Watters, James J. (2012). Re-thinking physics teaching with web-based learning. Computers and Education, 58(1), 631-640. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2011.09.010

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

In the same MANCOVA, the GROUP effect on Science Processes was not significant (p = .247 in Table 5). The authors note "Since the website was not designed to enhance students‟ science process skills, the main effect was not significant for this domain."

> "Since the website was not designed to enhance students‟ science process skills, the main effect was not significant for this domain."
