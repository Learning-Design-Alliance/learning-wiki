# The cross-sectional ToM–achievement association remains significant after controlling for verbal ability, executive functions, non-verbal ability, and SES

## Evidence

### Lecce 2026

Lecce, S., Stagnitto, S. M., Mascheretti, S., Lampis, V., & Devine, R. T. (2026). Children's Theory of Mind and Academic Achievement: a Meta-analysis on Reading Comprehension and Math Skills. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10185-w

`q4 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analysis of partial correlations for reading comprehension (42 effect sizes controlling for verbal ability, 38 for executive functions, 13 for SES). Printed partial effect sizes rVA = .24, rEF = .33 and rSES = .28 all remained significant, and CIs overlapped the zero-order CI.

> "After controlling for each covariate, the weighted mean of the concurrent correlation between ToM and reading comprehension remained statistically significant, academic achievement ReadingComprehension: rVA = .24, 95% CI [.19, .30], p < .001; rEF = .33, 95% CI [.29, .40], p < .001; rSES = .28, 95% CI [.18, .37], p < .001."

### Lecce 2026 (2)

Lecce, S., Stagnitto, S. M., Mascheretti, S., Lampis, V., & Devine, R. T. (2026). Children's Theory of Mind and Academic Achievement: a Meta-analysis on Reading Comprehension and Math Skills. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10185-w

`q4 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analysis of partial correlations for math (20 effect sizes controlling for executive functions, 24 for non-verbal ability, 14 for SES). Printed partial effect sizes rEF = .32, rNVA = .26 and rSES = .35 remained significant, with CIs overlapping the zero-order CI.

> "After controlling for these covariates, the weighted mean of the correlation between ToM and math remained statistically significant, Academic Achievement Math: rEF = .32, 95% CI [.24, .40], p < .001; rNVA = .26, 95% CI [.20, .31], p < .001; rSES = .35, 95% CI [.27, .44], p < .001."
