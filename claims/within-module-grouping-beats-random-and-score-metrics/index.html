Evidence¶

Lechuga 2023¶

Lechuga, C. G., & Doroudi, S. (2023). Three Algorithms for Grouping Students: A Bridge Between Personalized Tutoring System Data and Classroom Pedagogy. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-022-00309-y

q2 · i? · design · r2

Mock-grouping evaluation on real ALEKS data: Class 1 had 27 students grouped in size-5 groups at the start of each of 11 modules, compared against all possible random groups of size 5 and deterministic score-based groups. The article reports the within-module method "obtained more favorable measures than the random and score methods on each module on average" on three outer-fringe overlap metrics; no effect sizes are printed.

"our results show that the within-module grouping method obtained more favorable measures than the random and score methods on each module on average"

Related Claims¶

  • In one module, a student with an outlier course score of 41 had outer-fringe readiness comparable to groupmates scoring 64–81, illustrating readiness not captured by a unidimensional score — related
  • Of 55 within-module groups formed across 11 modules, only 6 contained a pair of students sharing no module outer-fringe item — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Average shared-item metrics of within-module groups fell almost always above the 90th percentile of all possible size-5 groups, and above the 95th percentile half the time — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
