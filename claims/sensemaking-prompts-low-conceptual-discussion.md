## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Martinuk 2010

Martinuk, M. and Ives, J. (2010). Do Prescribed Prompts Prime Sensemaking During Group Problem Solving? Physics Education Research conference proceedings style manuscript, University of British Columbia. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1111.5400](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1111.5400)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Segment-level coding of six episodes (Table 3) shows the average proportion of time in Conceptual Discussion was "only 6%, which is very low compared to the upper limit estimate of 38%" for the sensemaking-intended prompts. Percentages are descriptive; no inferential test is printed.

> "In particular, prompts 1, 2, and 6 were intended to promote sensemaking, but at best half of the student groups engage in Conceptual Discussion during these segments and the average proportion of time spent in Conceptual Discussion was only 6%, which is very low compared to the upper limit estimate of 38%."
