# The review reports that students learning with serious games often perform better on content knowledge assessments than those in traditional settings

`q2 i?` Students who learn with serious games often perform better on content knowledge assessments than those in traditional settings, per meta-analyses the review cites. [→ Kelly McNeil 2026](#kelly-mcneil-2026)

## Evidence

### Kelly McNeil 2026

Kelly McNeil, Sharin Jacob, Quinn Burke, and Stefani Pautz Stephenson. (2026). Operation Outbreak: Exploring the Affordances and Challenges of Experiential Simulations in Education for Scalability. Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/5b9d16cb-7253-49d0-9d95-e510b6951d4b

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Wouters et al. (2013) and Clark et al. (2016), that serious games outperform traditional settings on content knowledge, "especially when games were aligned with clear learning objectives and given adequate time." No effect sizes are printed in the review.

> "Meta-analyses have found that students who learn with serious games often perform better on content knowledge assessments than those in traditional settings (Wouters et al., 2013; Clark et al., 2016)."
