# Non-native signers entering a signing school before age 6 had better ASL abilities than those entering later, but did not equal native signers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Non-native signers who entered before age 6 had better ASL abilities than those who entered after age 6; children entering between 6 and 12 did better than those entering after 12 but never caught up to native signers. [→ Henner J 2016](#henner-j-2016)

## Evidence

### Henner J 2016

Henner J, Caldwell-Harris CL, Novogrodsky R and Hoffmeister R (2016) American Sign Language Syntax and Analogical Reasoning Skills Are Influenced by Early Acquisition and Age of Entry to Signing Schools for the Deaf. Front. Psychol. 7:1982. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01982

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the discussion of the grouped age-of-entry results, non-native signers entering before age 6 had better ASL abilities than later entrants, and 6–12 entrants outperformed post-12 entrants without reaching native-signer levels.

> "Non-native signers who entered the school of the Deaf before age 6 had better ASL abilities than did those who entered after age 6. Deaf children who entered between ages 6 and 12 had better ASL skills than those who entered after age 12 but never equaled or caught up to the native signers."
