# Coached teachers were observed to use significantly more proactive behavior management and greater anticipation of and responsiveness to student problems than comparison teachers

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Observer ratings of proactive behavior management were higher among coached teachers (β = 0.34, p = .01; Δ = 0.45). [→ Bradshaw 2018](#bradshaw-2018)
`q3 i2` Observer ratings of anticipation of and responsiveness to student problems were higher among coached teachers (β = 0.29, p = .04; Δ = 0.37), though this effect did not survive false discovery rate adjustment. [→ Bradshaw 2018](#bradshaw-2018)

## Evidence

### Bradshaw 2018

Bradshaw, C. P., Pas, E. T., Bottiani, J. H., Debnam, K. J., Reinke, W. M., Herman, K. C., & Rosenberg, M. S. (2018). Promoting Cultural Responsivity and Student Engagement Through Double Check Coaching of Classroom Teachers: An Efficacy Study. School Psychology Review, 47(2), 118–134. https://doi.org/10.17105/SPR-2017-0119.V47-2

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Experimental contrast from the teacher-level RCT using external blinded ASSIST observations (15-minute live observations pre and post). Coached teachers were rated nearly one-half and over one-third of a standard deviation higher on these two global ratings than comparison teachers.

> "observer ratings of proactive behavior management (β = 0.34, p = .01; Δ = 0.45) and anticipation of and responsiveness to student problems (β = 0.29, p = .04; Δ = 0.37) were higher among those who received coaching."
