S., Alsalhani Belal, Alsalhani Anas B. (2025). Evaluating dental students’ understanding of oral complications and care management for head and neck cancer patients. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07698-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive item-level results from the 506-participant cross-sectional survey (Table 2): the article reports that "only 17.2% of the sample recognized" the paediatric developmental effects of high-dose therapy; interns answered this item correctly more often than students (24.1% vs. 14.1%, p = 0.006).

> "it was surprising that only 17.2% of the sample recognized that high doses of chemotherapy or head and neck radiation therapy can affect dental, craniofacial, or skeletal development in children."

### Alali Faisal Mehsen 2025 (2)

Alali Faisal Mehsen, Nassani Mohammad Zakaria, Tarakji Bassel, Raqe Alqhtani Nasser, Robaian Ali, Saad Alqahtahni Abdullah, Alhedyan Faisal S., bin nabhan Abdullah, Alenzi Adel, Ayidh Alqahtani Khalid, Alrafedah Ali, Alagla Maram, Abuelqomsan Mohammed A. S., Alsalhani Belal, Alsalhani Anas B. (2025). Evaluating dental students’ understanding of oral complications and care management for head and neck cancer patients. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07698-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive item-level results from the oral-care section of the same survey (Table 3): both the pre-radiotherapy oral-care item and the chewing-ice post-radiotherapy item were answered correctly by only 15.8% of the sample; interns outperformed students on the oral-care item (27.2% vs. 10.6%, p < 0.001).

> "Only 15.8% recognized that this increases the likelihood that the patient will be able to complete their optimal cancer treatment regimen. Similarly, only 15.8% correctly indicated that chewing ice post-radiotherapy can help alleviate mouth pain for a cancer patient."
