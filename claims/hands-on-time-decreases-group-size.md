## Evidence

### Noerholk LM 2022

Noerholk LM, Morcke AM, Kulasegaram K, Nørgaard LN, Harmsen L, Andreasen LA, Pedersen NG, Johnsson V, Vamadevan A, Tolsgaard MG. (2022). Does group size matter during collaborative skills learning? A randomised study. Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.14791

`q4 · i3` · `causal · r2`

ICAP video analysis of training sessions in the randomised trial. Mean hands-on time fell from 58.4% in singles to 13.7% in tetrads (F(3,90) = 252.54, p < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.89); post hoc tests showed significant differences between all smaller-versus-larger group pairs except triads versus tetrads.

> "The amount of hands-on time (percentage of the training time each participant was handling the ultrasound probe) decreased with increasing group size (mean per cent [SD]: singles: 58.4 [8.6], dyads: 27.7 [5.7], triads: 17.0 [5.3]; tetrads: 13.7 [4.0]; F(3,90) = 252.54, p < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.89)"

### Noerholk LM 2022 (2)

Noerholk LM, Morcke AM, Kulasegaram K, Nørgaard LN, Harmsen L, Andreasen LA, Pedersen NG, Johnsson V, Vamadevan A, Tolsgaard MG. (2022). Does group size matter during collaborative skills learning? A randomised study. Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.14791

`q4 · i3` · `causal · r3`

Pearson correlation across the four randomised groups showed a strong negative association between group size and hands-on time (r = −0.86, p < 0.001).

> "Group size and hands-on time were negatively correlated (Pearson's r = −0.86, p < 0.001)"
