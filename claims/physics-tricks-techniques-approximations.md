## Evidence

### Jeffrey M. Rabin 2024

Jeffrey M. Rabin, Andrew Meyertholen, Brian Shotwell. (2024). Students' Experience of Cultural Differences Between Mathematics and Physics. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Thematic analysis of interviews: the authors classify student-reported confusions in physics classes into techniques, which are mathematically justifiable manipulations students are unfamiliar with, and approximations, which "often require physical arguments specific to the physics problem at hand."

> "Such sources of confusion fall into t wo categories: “techniques” and “approximations.” Roughly speaking, the former are mathemat ical objects, structures, or manipulations that can be mathematically justiﬁed"

### Jeffrey M. Rabin 2024 (2)

Jeffrey M. Rabin, Andrew Meyertholen, Brian Shotwell. (2024). Students' Experience of Cultural Differences Between Mathematics and Physics. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview data on approximations: students cited Taylor series most often; one double major described the pendulum small-angle approximation as physics sweeping "a bit of stuff under the rug," and another noted real analysis provides error bounds that physics courses did not.

> "In general, students are uncomfortable contin uing with an approximate expression if they’re aware (or believe) that an exact expression e xists, if they don’t see how the approximation will help solve the problem at hand"
