# Feedback provision proceeded linearly through the text, while feedback processing was less linear and used two interchangeable strategies

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students provided feedback linearly, reading the text from start to end and commenting on elements at first encounter, then producing new remarks in a second round. [→ Jochem E. J. Aben 2024](#jochem-e-j-aben-2024)
`q2 i?` During processing, students alternated between taking the feedback remarks as starting point and taking their own text as starting point, and eight students postponed remarks they agreed with but could not yet act on. [→ Jochem E. J. Aben 2024](#jochem-e-j-aben-2024)

## Evidence

### Jochem E. J. Aben 2024

Jochem E. J. Aben, Mayra Mascareño Lara, Anneke C. Timmermans, Filitsa Dingyloudi, Jan-Willem Strijbos. (2024). The impact of interpersonal perceptions on the process of dealing with errors while providing and processing peer-feedback on writing. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09660-0

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative thematic analysis of think-aloud recordings (theme: working procedure). The article states "The processing of feedback, as conducted by the students in the sample, was less linear than the provision of feedback", with students making interchangeable use of two strategies and eight students repeatedly postponing feedback they agreed with but could not yet revise for.

> "The provision of feedback, as conducted by the students in the sample, could be characterized as linear."
