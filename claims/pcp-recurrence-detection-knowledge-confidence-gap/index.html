Vos Julien A. M. 2023¶

Vos Julien A. M., Wollersheim Barbara M., Cooke Adelaide, Ee Carolyn, Chan Raymond J., Nekhlyudov Larissa. (2023). Primary care physicians’ knowledge and confidence in providing cancer survivorship care: a systematic review. Journal of Cancer Survivorship. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11764-023-01397-y

q3 · i? · review · r3

Review synthesis of five included survey studies reporting PCP self-rated skills for recurrence detection; the review states "between 62 and 78% of PCPs “somewhat or strongly agreed”". Percentages are descriptive, no effect sizes pooled.

"In other studies, between 62 and 78% of PCPs “somewhat or strongly agreed” they had the necessary skills to initiate appropriate screening and detection of recurrences (n = 5)"

Vos Julien A. M. 2023 (2)¶

Vos Julien A. M., Wollersheim Barbara M., Cooke Adelaide, Ee Carolyn, Chan Raymond J., Nekhlyudov Larissa. (2023). Primary care physicians’ knowledge and confidence in providing cancer survivorship care: a systematic review. Journal of Cancer Survivorship. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11764-023-01397-y

q3 · i? · review · r3

Review synthesis of six studies measuring PCP confidence to screen for recurrence, described as "rated much lower across all the included studies", with 6–42% feeling very confident or prepared.

"Confidence to screen for cancer recurrence was rated much lower across all the included studies. Between 6-42% felt very confident or prepared to do so (n = 6) [21, 29, 31, 32, 34, 49]."

Related Claims¶

  • PCP confidence in managing physical effects varies by symptom: 79% felt confident managing fatigue versus 16% for chemobrain — related
  • Only 9–12% of PCPs felt at least somewhat familiar with surveillance guidelines, and lack of guideline knowledge was a reported barrier — related
  • A greater proportion of primary care physicians report confidence in managing psychosocial effects of cancer (24–47%) than physical effects (10–37%) — related
  • Qualitative studies consistently describe PCPs' lack of knowledge for survivorship care, with confidence depending on experience, sex, and survivor population — related
  • Most PCPs report inadequate formal survivorship training (up to 72%) and a desire for education, especially on physical effects (76–86%)
