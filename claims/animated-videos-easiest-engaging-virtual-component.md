# Animated videos were the easiest and most engaging Hero Elementary component to implement virtually, providing an accessible shared entry point for students with IEPs and English learners

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teachers consistently found the videos the easiest activity to implement and reported high student engagement, with episodes acting as an equalizer for special education and English learner students. [→ Li 2022](#li-2022)

## Evidence

### Li 2022

Li, L., Hayakawa, M., Freese, J., Daniels, B., Weiser, G., Luttgen, K., Lor, M. C., Schneider, M., Huang, C.-W., & Jensen, E. (2022). Pivoting an in-person transmedia science program to a virtual program during a pandemic: Lessons learned. Children, Youth and Environments, 32(3), 53-81. http://www.jstor.org/action/showPublication?journalCode=chilyoutenvi

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Teacher interview and survey data from Study 1's Results section: teachers "consistently found that the videos were the easiest activity to implement during distance learning," reported students connecting with characters across genders and ethnicities, and called the episodes "one of the strongest components of the program."

> "Another teacher agreed, characterizing the episodes as “a great equalizer” that “created the most equal platform for special education, English learners, and other abilities to all come together.”"
