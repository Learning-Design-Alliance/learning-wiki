# Group tests combined with teachers' estimates correlate .77 with the Stanford Revision, questioning the necessity of individual examination

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q2` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` The correlation of .77 between the Stanford Revision and group tests combined with teachers' estimates (30 cases) raised the question whether individual psychological examination is necessary for classification and promotion, though the authors still regard it as indispensable. [→ Chassell 1921](#chassell-1921)

## Evidence

### Chassell 1921

Chassell, C. F., & Chassell, L. M. (1921). A survey of the three first grades of the Horace Mann School by means of psychological tests and teachers' estimates, and a statistical evaluation of the methods employed. Part II. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 12(5), 243-252. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0068285

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis in the same survey, based on only 30 cases because prolonged absence of many children limited direct comparison; the printed correlation r = .77 codes as a large effect. The authors emphasize that r = .77 means a reduction in the ratio of variability around the regression line to only .64, and they "should still hesitate to say that it was not an essential instrument".

> "The correlation of .77 between these measures, based on only 30 cases, is sufficiently high to raise the question as to whether an individual psychological examination is really necessary for determining classification and promotion."
