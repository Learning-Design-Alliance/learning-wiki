# At impact, conservation of linear momentum together with the constraint that sandbag and lid momentarily move together leads to contradictions, so angular momentum conservation is used instead

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Evaluating post-collision velocities by normal momentum conservation produces more kinetic energy after the collision than before and cases where the sandbag bounces off upward; the article instead requires conservation of angular momentum. [→ U Schröter 2009](#u-schroter-2009)

## Evidence

### U Schröter 2009

U Schröter. (2009). Kermit's What-Happens-Next-Machine Reloaded. https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.0110 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0903.0110](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0903.0110)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical argument in section 3 on the impact treatment, which the author calls "the most diﬃcult, and to my opinion still somewhat ambiguous aspect of the problem". The footnote adds there is more kinetic energy after the collision than before under linear momentum conservation.

> "Attempting to evaluate velocities after the collision by normal momentum conservation together with the constraint that afterwards the sandbag on the seesaw and the lid at least momentarily move together does lead to contradictions, even if the anglesϕS(t) andϕL(t) do not show them."
