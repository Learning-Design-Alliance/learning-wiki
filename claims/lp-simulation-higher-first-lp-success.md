# Students who received LP simulation training had a higher first-LP success rate in real patients than untrained controls (67% vs 14%)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a prospective randomized study, the success rate of the first LP on a patient was significantly higher in the simulation group than the control group (67% versus 14%, p = 0.003). [→ Gaubert Sinead et al 2021](#gaubert-sinead-et-al-2021)

## Evidence

### Gaubert Sinead et al 2021

Gaubert Sinead et al. (2021). Positive effects of lumbar puncture simulation training for medical students in clinical practice. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02452-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Monocentric prospective controlled simple-blind randomized study during 2017–2018: 388 fifth-year students randomized, 41 performed a first LP (27 simulation, 14 control). Success defined as collecting enough CSF without supervisor assistance; Fisher's exact test, p = 0.003; no effect size printed.

> "The success rate of the first LP on a patient was significantly higher in the simulation group compared to the control group (67% versus 14%, respectively, p = 0.003) (Fig. 1c)"
