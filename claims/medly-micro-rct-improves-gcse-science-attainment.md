# Allocation to Medly improved post-test GCSE science attainment relative to business-as-usual self-directed revision (Hedges' g = 0.33)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` In the primary intention-to-treat analysis, students allocated to Medly achieved higher post-test attainment than students undertaking business-as-usual self-directed revision, g = 0.33 (95% CI 0.18 to 0.48). [→ Wayne Harrison 2026](#wayne-harrison-2026)

## Evidence

### Wayne Harrison 2026

Wayne Harrison, Rahil Khowaja, Emma Dobson, Germaine Uwimpuhwe, and Steve Higgins. (2026). Evaluating AI Tutoring at the Speed of Innovation: Practitioner-Led Micro-Randomised Trials of an AI Tutoring Platform in GCSE Science. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14789

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Multisite individually randomised controlled trial in English secondary schools; 929 students completed pre-test and 644 post-test. Adjusted mean post-test score was 13.95 in the intervention group and 11.39 in control, with "Hedges' g = 0.33 (95% CI 0.18 to 0.48)" in the combined primary intention-to-treat analysis.

> "The adjusted coefficient was 2.56 marks (95% CI 1.39 to 3.73), corresponding to Hedges' g = 0.33 (95% CI 0.18 to 0.48)."
