# Photogates do not determine the sign of velocity, cannot show zero velocity with non-zero acceleration at the turning point, and afford the Δv/Δx rather than the Δv/Δt definition of acceleration

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In the photogate lab, only speed and not the sign of velocity is directly determined, the equipment cannot provide data showing zero velocity but non-zero acceleration at the turning point, and Δv/Δx is afforded while Δv/Δt is not. [→ Jonte Bernhard 2018](#jonte-bernhard-2018)

## Evidence

### Jonte Bernhard 2018

Jonte Bernhard. (2018). What matters for students' learning in the laboratory? Do not neglect the role of experimental equipment! Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-018-9469-x

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Affordance analysis of the photogate lab (glider on an air track with two photogates), showing only speed is directly determined so "there is a risk that students make a sign mistake". The article adds that the equipment cannot show zero velocity with non-zero acceleration at the turning point and that the "wrong" definition of acceleration (Δv/Δx) is afforded, but not the "right" one.

> "since only speed—and not the sign of the velocity—is directly determined using the experimental equipment, there is a risk that students make a sign mistake when asked to perform this task."
