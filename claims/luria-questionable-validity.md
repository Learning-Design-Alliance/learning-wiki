# Luria's Uzbekistan cognition research is of questionable validity because its mediational means were culturally inappropriate

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The article argues Luria (1976) socially constructed his data in three ways—assuming speech as primary mediation, presenting decontextualized school-like tasks, and eliciting responses face-to-face—so the research is not mediational and of questionable validity. [→ Peter Smagorinsky 1995](#peter-smagorinsky-1995)

## Evidence

### Peter Smagorinsky 1995

Peter Smagorinsky. (1995). The Social Construction of Data: Methodological Problems of Investigating Learning in the Zone of Proximal Development. Review of Educational Research, 65(3), 191-212. https://doi.org/10.2307/1170682

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Interpretive critique in the essay: Luria tested Uzbekistani and Kirghizian peasants with decontextualized sorting tasks and judged them childlike against a telos valuing speech-mediated scientific concepts. The article concludes 'the research is not mediational and therefore of questionable validity', an authorial judgment rather than a tested result.

> "As a consequence, I believe, the research is not mediational and therefore of questionable validity (cf. Cole, 1985; Saxe, 1994)."
