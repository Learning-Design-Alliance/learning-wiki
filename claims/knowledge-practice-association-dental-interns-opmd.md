## Evidence

### Misra Satya Ranjan 2026

Misra Satya Ranjan, Das Rupsa, Das Subhra Jyoti. (2026). Preparedness of dental interns to identify oral potentially malignant disorders and oral cancer: a multi-state cross-sectional survey in India. BMC Oral Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-026-08681-0

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional survey correlating knowledge (8 items) with mean practice score (Q13–Q14) among 1220 interns. The printed correlation is "r = 0.42, p < 0.001", a moderate positive association.

> "Pearson’s correlation revealed a moderate positive relationship between total knowledge score and mean practice score (r = 0.42, p < 0.001)."

### Misra Satya Ranjan 2026 (2)

Misra Satya Ranjan, Das Rupsa, Das Subhra Jyoti. (2026). Preparedness of dental interns to identify oral potentially malignant disorders and oral cancer: a multi-state cross-sectional survey in India. BMC Oral Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-026-08681-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Simple linear regression of mean practice score on knowledge score. The authors caution that "because of the cross-sectional design, this association should not be interpreted as causal" and note modest variance explained.

> "Linear regression analysis showed that knowledge scores were significantly associated with practice frequency (β = 0.39, 95% CI: 0.29–0.49, p < 0.001), explaining 17.6% of the variance (adjusted R² = 0.175)."

### Misra Satya Ranjan 2026 (3)

Misra Satya Ranjan, Das Rupsa, Das Subhra Jyoti. (2026). Preparedness of dental interns to identify oral potentially malignant disorders and oral cancer: a multi-state cross-sectional survey in India. BMC Oral Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-026-08681-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Independent t-tests compared knowledge between "Always" and "Not always" responders for screening (t(1218) = 5.15) and counselling (t(1218) = 6.21), both p < 0.001, favouring the always groups.

> "Interns who always examined high-risk sites had significantly higher knowledge scores (5.59 ± 0.97, n = 1060) than those who did not (5.09 ± 1.17, n = 160; t(1218) = 5.15, p < 0.001)."
