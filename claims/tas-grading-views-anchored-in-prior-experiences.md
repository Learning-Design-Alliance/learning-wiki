# TAs' correctness-focused grading views are anchored in their former experiences of being graded on correctness alone

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors interpret TAs' resistance to a CA grading approach as anchored in their prior experiences: most TAs generally received full scores for correct answers without showing their solution process. [→ Edit Yerushalmi 2016](#edit-yerushalmi-2016)

## Evidence

### Edit Yerushalmi 2016

Edit Yerushalmi, Ryan Sayer, Emily Marshman, Charles Henderson and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Physics graduate teaching assistants' beliefs about a grading rubric: Lessons learned. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.097](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.097)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

The authors' interpretation, drawing on interviews and class discussions: "Most TAs, who had not shown the process of arriving at a final answer in their own solutions in the past, had generally managed to get full scores if their answers were correct", and many noted their own solutions were graded on correctness only throughout their education.
