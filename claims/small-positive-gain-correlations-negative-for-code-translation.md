# Correlations between individual gains in code practice and gains in the tests were generally small and positive, with the largest correlation for the code-translation test and a slight negative correlation for Test 5 suggesting interference between translating into and from code.

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Dearborn 1918

Dearborn, W. F., and Brewer, J. M. (1918). Methods and Results of a Class Experiment in Learning. The Journal of Educational Psychology, Vol. IX, No. 2. https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/edu/9/2/

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Spearman footrule rank correlations computed from the class score tables. The article reports "but little relation" between practice gains and test gains, the largest being with translation from code, and a slight inverse relation (R = -3, ±7) between final practice record and final Test 5 record.

> "The correlations under (I) show that there is, in general, but little relation between the individual percentages of gain made in the practice and those made in the tests. The largest correlation is with the translation from code tests."
