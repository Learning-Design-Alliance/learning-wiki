Evidence¶

Ross 2025¶

Ross, M., & Kaspar, K. (2026). Learning with pedagogical agents in extended reality: A conceptual research-based framework of agent-centered learning in immersive media environments (ACLIME). https://orcid.org/0000-0002-5092-6798 doi:10.31234/osf.io/e6smh_v1

q2 · i? · review · r?

The review reports, citing Hagiya et al. (2024) and Vladinov & Foronda (2024), that approximately 42% of participants are affected by cybersickness symptoms when using VR headsets; the underlying studies' designs are not described in the review.

"Recent studies still report substantial prevalence rates of cybersickness symptoms, with approximately 42% of participants affected when using VR headsets (Hagiya et al., 2024; Vladinov & Foronda, 2024)."

Ross 2025 (2)¶

Ross, M., & Kaspar, K. (2026). Learning with pedagogical agents in extended reality: A conceptual research-based framework of agent-centered learning in immersive media environments (ACLIME). https://orcid.org/0000-0002-5092-6798 doi:10.31234/osf.io/e6smh_v1

q2 · i? · theoretical · r2

The review reports, citing Kirollos and Merchant (2023), evidence that cybersickness occurs in AR and can exceed VR symptoms when rendered content is high enough; the review attributes this to recent findings without detailing the study design.

"In fact, when the amount of virtually rendered content is high enough, cybersickness symptoms in AR can even exceed those experienced in VR (Kirollos & Merchant, 2023)."
