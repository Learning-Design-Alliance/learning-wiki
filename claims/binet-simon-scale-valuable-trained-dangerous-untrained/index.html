Evidence¶

Lewis M. Terman and Clara Harbison Town 1917¶

Lewis M. Terman and Clara Harbison Town. (1917). Communications and Discussions, The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://www.jstor.org

q1 · i? · theoretical · r?

An expert clinical judgment from the symposium communication, stating the scale is 'a valuable tool when used by an adequately trained person, who knows when to use it and when to lay it aside.' No comparative data are reported in the article.

"In less expert hands, far from being a valuable tool, it is even a dangerous one, owing to the popular belief in its innate and almost uncanny power of detecting the subnormal child."

Related Claims¶

  • Scattering on the Binet scales does not support the claim that unequal development is the specific characteristic of the feeble-minded; normal pupils scatter most and imbeciles least — related
  • The Binet-Simon scale measured intelligence development from ages seven to eleven with at least 96 per cent efficiency under the study's conditions — related
  • The Binet-Simon and Yerkes Point Scale ratings of children run parallel and are practically identical up to age ten — related
  • Taylor's Sheffield study suggests the Binet scale is broadly suitable for English children, though tests for ages 6 and 7 seem too easy and tests above 11 seem unsatisfactory. — related
  • Applying the Stanford revision to selected children in four California cities, Williams classified 32% feeble-minded, 20% borderline, 22% dull normal, and 26% normal or superior. — related
  • In Wallin's St. Louis psycho-educational clinic, of 430 referred cases, 25.9% of 320 boys and 40% of 110 girls were found feeble-minded, with almost twice as many backward and retarded as feeble-minded. — related
  • The Binet-Simon metric scale works well with normal and slightly atypical children but is less satisfactory with imbeciles and idiots and almost worthless for dements — related
  • Binet-Simon measurement alone is insufficient for diagnosis except in low-grade feeble-mindedness, per Healy and Bronner — related
  • Kuhlmann argues the greater danger to the field is psychologists' destructive criticism of the Binet-Simon tests rather than errors from their use by untrained examiners — related
  • Trained assistants with extended institutional experience may differentiate feeble-minded children as well as, and sometimes better than, clinical psychologists on the Binet test — related
  • In expert hands the Binet Scale can be relied on to diagnose about 90 per cent of cases, with the remaining 10 per cent requiring other tests and criteria — related
  • Every pedagogically retarded child should receive a psycho-clinical examination to distinguish genuine subnormal development from retardation caused by late entrance, irregular attendance, illness, or poor teaching. — related
