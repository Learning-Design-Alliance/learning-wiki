# Slopes of the student-level predictors do not vary significantly between schools in Taiwan

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When slopes of home possessions, mother's education, and father's education were allowed to vary randomly, they showed no significant between-school variability (p > 0.05). [→ Cai 2018](#cai-2018)

## Evidence

### Cai 2018

Cai, J. (2018). Effects of Home Resources and School Environment on Eighth-Grade Mathematics Achievement in Taiwan. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1801.06049](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1801.06049)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Level-1 analysis (Model 3) testing random slopes for the three student-level predictors across schools. The predictors remained significant, but "their slopes do not vary between schools (p >0.05)", so slopes were fixed in the final model.

> "The results of this model show that hp, mo, and fa signiﬁcantly predict math achievement, but their slopes do not vary between schools (p >0.05)."
