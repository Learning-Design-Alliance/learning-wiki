## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh and Emily Marshman 2016

Chandralekha Singh and Emily Marshman. (2016). Student difficulties with determining expectation values in quantum mechanics. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.075](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.075)

`q3 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Written responses and interviews on open-ended questions Q2 and Q3. One student "incorrectly claimed that the eigenvalues 𝑞𝑛 of the operator 𝑄̂ were the expansion coefficients 𝑐𝑛"; Table III lists 3% UG and 5% G displaying this difficulty on the discrete question.

> "Some students wrote incorrect expansions of |Ψ〉, e.g., |Ψ〉 = ∑ |𝑞𝑛〉𝑛  or |Ψ〉 = ∑ 𝑞𝑛𝑛 |𝑞𝑛⟩."

### Chandralekha Singh and Emily Marshman 2016 (2)

Chandralekha Singh and Emily Marshman. (2016). Student difficulties with determining expectation values in quantum mechanics. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.075](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.075)

`q3 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Analysis of open-ended responses found students unable to distinguish identity from projection operators or leaving answers in terms of the operator; Table III lists 6% UG and 5% G (discrete) and 13% UG and 8% G (continuous) getting lost after inserting the identity, and 7% UG / 3% G writing incorrect expectation value expressions such as ⟨𝑞𝑛|𝑄̂|Ψ⟩ with mismatched bra and ket states.

> "Some students were aware of the fact that one could find the expectation value by inserting the identity operator in the expression for the expectation value, but they had difficulty with the procedure and/or got lost along the way."
