# Teacher-reported student sleep deficits were negatively associated with mathematics and science achievement in both the 2015 and 2019 TIMSS cycles

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2`–`r3` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2022 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Sleep deficits reported by teachers had a significant negative effect on mathematics achievement in both 2015 and 2019 before adjustment, with an increase in sleep deficits associated with about 10 fewer score points in 2015 and 9 in 2019. [→ Vik Frøydis N. 2022](#vik-frydis-n-2022)
`q2 i?` After controlling for SES, gender, and minority status, sleep deficits still accounted for approximately 8 points in mathematics in 2015 and 7 points in 2019, with similar results for science. [→ Vik Frøydis N. 2022 (2)](#vik-frydis-n-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Vik Frøydis N. 2022

Vik Frøydis N., Nilsen Trude, Øverby Nina C. (2022). Associations between sleep deficit and academic achievement - triangulation across time and subject domains among students and teachers in TIMSS in Norway. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-022-14161-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Regression analysis of teacher-reported sleep deficits on student achievement across the 2015 and 2019 TIMSS cycles; the effect of sleep deficits "reported by teachers" on mathematics achievement was significant in both cycles.

> "Before controlling for SES, gender, and minority status, the effect of the predictor (“Sleep deficits” reported by teachers) on mathematics achievement is significant in both 2015 and 2019."

### Vik Frøydis N. 2022 (2)

Vik Frøydis N., Nilsen Trude, Øverby Nina C. (2022). Associations between sleep deficit and academic achievement - triangulation across time and subject domains among students and teachers in TIMSS in Norway. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-022-14161-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Adjusted regression models for both cycles; sleep deficits "still account for approximately 8 points in 2015 and 7 points in 2019" in mathematics, and the article reports similar results for science achievement.

> "Sleep deficits have a smaller regression coefficient (after controlling for SES, gender, and minority status), but still account for approximately 8 points in 2015 and 7 points in 2019."
