# Self-efficacy significantly predicted engagement and inversely predicted disengagement, and mediated adaptability's links to outcomes

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Martin Andrew J. 2023

Martin Andrew J., Ginns Paul, Collie Rebecca J. (2023). University students in COVID-19 lockdown: The role of adaptability and fluid reasoning in supporting their academic motivation and engagement. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2022.101712

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Fully-forward SEM paths from self-efficacy (MES-UC, 4 items) to all four outcomes, with standardized betas as printed, controlling for lockdown, adaptability, fluid reasoning, and background covariates. The participation path was "medium effect"; the others large.

> "The model's mediator, self-efficacy, significantly predicted participation (β = 0.20, p = .05; medium effect), aspirations (β = 0.73, p ≤ .001; large effect), and enjoyment (β = 0.45, p ≤ .001; large effect)—and inversely predicted disengagement (β = −0.34, p ≤ .001; large effect)."

### Martin Andrew J. 2023 (2)

Martin Andrew J., Ginns Paul, Collie Rebecca J. (2023). University students in COVID-19 lockdown: The role of adaptability and fluid reasoning in supporting their academic motivation and engagement. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2022.101712

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Auxiliary test of indirect paths using parametric bootstrapping, reported in the Results narrative with details in Supplementary Materials. The mediation from lockdown or fluid reasoning through self-efficacy was not reported as significant.

> "self-efficacy significantly mediated the relationship between perceived adaptability and engagement and disengagement; and, moderation tests revealed a significant lockdown x fluid reasoning interaction effect for self-efficacy"
