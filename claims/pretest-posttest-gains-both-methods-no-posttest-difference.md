## Evidence

### Steenstra Ivan 2026

Steenstra Ivan, Flanagan Octavia, Benni Jyoti M, Schreckenbach Teresa, Gayan Mondeep MS, Shrivastava Sandeep MS, Sonowal Kiran MS, Gogoi Amilee MD, Goswami Hrishikesh MS, Pathak Prasenjit MS, Kumar Bhawesh MS. (2026). Standardized Patient vs Video Demonstration for Teaching Psychomotor Skills in Spinal Injury Management to Undergraduate Medical Students: Prospective Interventional Comparative Study. JMIR Formative Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/78701

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Paired 2-sided t tests on peer-validated pretest and immediate posttest questionnaires (out of 7 marks) administered via Google forms showed significant improvement in both arms, "P<.001" in each group.

> "Within the VD group, the mean pretest score was 3.43 and the mean posttest score was 5.23, representing a statistically significant improvement (P<.001). Similarly, within the SP group, the mean pretest score was 3.50 and the mean posttest score was 5.53, which is also statistically significant (P<.001)."

### Steenstra Ivan 2026 (2)

Steenstra Ivan, Flanagan Octavia, Benni Jyoti M, Schreckenbach Teresa, Gayan Mondeep MS, Shrivastava Sandeep MS, Sonowal Kiran MS, Gogoi Amilee MD, Goswami Hrishikesh MS, Pathak Prasenjit MS, Kumar Bhawesh MS. (2026). Standardized Patient vs Video Demonstration for Teaching Psychomotor Skills in Spinal Injury Management to Undergraduate Medical Students: Prospective Interventional Comparative Study. JMIR Formative Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/78701

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Unpaired comparison of posttest knowledge scores between the two arms found no significant difference ("P=.35"), indicating parity of the two methods on knowledge outcomes.

> "The mean posttest score of VD group was 5.23 and that of SP group was 5.53 out of the total of 7 marks. This difference was statistically not significant (P=.35) (Figure 6)."
