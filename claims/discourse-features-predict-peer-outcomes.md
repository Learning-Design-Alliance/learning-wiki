## Evidence

### Morris 2026

Morris, C., & Maes, P. (2026). When Peers Outperform AI (and When They Don't): Interaction Quality Over Modality. [doi:10.22318/cscl2026.989526](https://doi.org/10.22318/cscl2026.989526)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Pearson correlations at the individual participant level (n=24 peer participants) between coded discourse features and survey responses, reported in Table 1, including r=0.565 and r=0.642. The authors note the sample "limits statistical power."

> "Question asking Overall curiosity rate 0.565 0.0041** Question asking Social curiosity rate 0.642 0.0007*** Explanation giving Social curiosity rate 0.439 0.035*"

### Morris 2026 (2)

Morris, C., & Maes, P. (2026). When Peers Outperform AI (and When They Don't): Interaction Quality Over Modality. [doi:10.22318/cscl2026.989526](https://doi.org/10.22318/cscl2026.989526)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Individual-level correlations in the peer condition, with printed coefficients r=0.468, r=0.439 and r=0.417. The article notes the r=0.642 question-asking/social-curiosity correlation likely reflects overlapping constructs in the coding.

> "Students who expressed more social curiosity reported asking new questions beyond the lesson material (r=0.468, p=.022) and better understanding of graph theory concepts (r=0.439, p=.035). Additionally, explanation-giving behavior positively correlated with perceived value of collaboration (r=0.417, p=.039)."
