## Evidence

### Lee Hye-Yoon 2025

Lee Hye-Yoon, Kim Jundong, Choi Hyojae, Bae Hyojin, Jeong Aram, Choi Sungyoul, Kim Ji-Hwan, Kim Chang-Eop. (2025). Comparing AI chatbot simulation and peer role-play for OSCE preparation: a pilot randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08308-y

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive summary of free-text responses from the post-training surveys in the pilot RCT; the article reports these as qualitative explanations for the quantitative survey patterns, not as tested comparisons.

> "For the PRP group, realism was cited as a major advantage, with comments such as “practice in a real-life situation,” “experience real exam room atmosphere,” and “appropriate tension formation.”"

### Lee Hye-Yoon 2025 (2)

Lee Hye-Yoon, Kim Jundong, Choi Hyojae, Bae Hyojin, Jeong Aram, Choi Sungyoul, Kim Ji-Hwan, Kim Chang-Eop. (2025). Comparing AI chatbot simulation and peer role-play for OSCE preparation: a pilot randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08308-y

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive free-text analysis from the same surveys; chatbot-group students also suggested improvements such as improving Korean medicine terminology recognition, adding a time measurement function, and implementing pulse and tongue diagnosis simulations.

> "For the AI chatbot group, key advantages mentioned were “no spatio-temporal constraints”, “ability to practice alone”, “repeatable learning”, “structuring of the consultation flow”, “offer appropriate feedback with nice quality”."
