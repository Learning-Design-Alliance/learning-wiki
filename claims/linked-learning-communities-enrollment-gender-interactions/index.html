Evidence¶

What Works Clearinghouse 2014¶

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2014). Linked Learning Communities. WWC Intervention Report. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/630

q3 · i? · review · r3

WWC synthesis of six RCTs (about 7,400 students) on registration and enrollment. Site-level registration comparisons were small and non-significant, e.g., Sommo et al. enrolled 87% versus 85% and Baltimore 85% versus 84%.

"Thus, for the postsecondary enrollment domain, none of the six studies that met WWC group design standards showed either a statistically significant effect or an effect large enough to be considered substantively important."

What Works Clearinghouse 2014 (2)¶

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2014). Linked Learning Communities. WWC Intervention Report. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/630

q3 · i? · causal · r?

Houston Community College RCT (Weissman et al., 2011; 1,273 students) using transcript registration data. The gender-by-condition interaction was statistically significant and "large enough to be considered substantively important" in the WWC's words.

"These authors did observe a statistically significant interaction between student gender and learning communities on registration rates. Women in the intervention condition registered at a higher rate (88%) than women in the comparison condition (80%), while men in the intervention condition registered at a lower rate (78%) than men in the comparison condition (84%)."

What Works Clearinghouse 2014 (3)¶

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2014). Linked Learning Communities. WWC Intervention Report. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/630

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Hillsborough RCT (Weiss et al., 2010; 1,071 students). The report states this "difference in registration rates between men and women in the intervention group is not statistically significant, but is large enough to be considered substantively important."

"Women registered for courses at about the same rate irrespective of their condition (85% versus 84%), but men registered for courses at a lower rate in the intervention group (78%) than in the comparison group (83%)."
