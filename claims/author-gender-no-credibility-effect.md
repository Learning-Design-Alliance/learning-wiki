## Evidence

### Ulsperger 2026

Ulsperger, A., Pfannenschwarz, L., Schetla, C., Poletilo, K., Strecker, L., Egner, M., & Kimmerle, J. (2026). Authors' academic but not personal expertise or gender affect message credibility in science communication. Scientific Reports, 16, 18000. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-55148-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Study 1 two-way ANOVA (n = 203) on message credibility found no gender main effect and no expertise × gender interaction. A three-way ANOVA adding participant gender also found no significant main effects or interactions, so participant gender did not moderate ratings.

> "There was no main effect of gender, F(1, 199) = 0.10, p = 0.757, therefore rejecting H2. Regarding ERQ2, there was no significant interaction effect between the factors author expertise and author gender, F(1, 199) = 0.06, p = 0.809."

### Ulsperger 2026 (2)

Ulsperger, A., Pfannenschwarz, L., Schetla, C., Poletilo, K., Strecker, L., Egner, M., & Kimmerle, J. (2026). Authors' academic but not personal expertise or gender affect message credibility in science communication. Scientific Reports, 16, 18000. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-55148-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Study 2, a preregistered replication with a more population-representative Clickworker sample (n = 182), found no significant main effect of author expertise, no gender effect, and no interaction; the interaction p = 0.067 was not significant.

> "The two-way ANOVA showed neither a main effect of author expertise, F(1, 178) = 0.24, p = 0.626, nor a main effect of gender, F(1, 178) = 0.19, p = 0.662, or an interaction between the factors, F(1, 178) = 3.39, p = 0.067."
