# Sandwich feedback-based training produces higher observed perioperative performance (DOPS) than traditional training

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` FBL students obtained significantly higher total DOPS checklist scores than TBT students (P < 0.001; Cohen's d = 4.31), rated by a blinded rater. [→ Hosseinpour Azam 2024](#hosseinpour-azam-2024)

## Evidence

### Hosseinpour Azam 2024

Hosseinpour Azam, Nasiri Morteza, Keshmiri Fatemeh, Arabzadeh Tayebeh Tarabzadeh, Sharafi Hossein. (2024). Comparison of the effects of apprenticeship training by sandwich feedback and traditional methods on final-semester operating room technology students' perioperative competence and performance: a randomized, controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05598-6

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Blinded-rater DOPS assessment on the last intervention day in the RCT (n = 30). Table 3 reports total perioperative performance of 32.46 ± 2.74 (FBL) vs 20.86 ± 2.64 (TBT), with a printed Cohen's d of 4.31; all eight DOPS domains also differed significantly (P < 0.001).

> "the Mann-Whitney U test showed that the mean scores of the DOPS checklist recorded for the students in the FBL group were significantly higher than those recorded for the students in the TBT group (P < 0.001)."
