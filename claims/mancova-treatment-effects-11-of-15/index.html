Related Claims¶

  • Three months of daily computer-based MSL training in L3 English produced significant pre-post gains for the Test Dyslexic group on L2 and L3 rapid naming and L3 receptive vocabulary tasks, while control groups did not improve — related
  • L2 reading speed (SLRT-I) and L2 spelling showed no improvement as a function of the intervention, and no group made significant gains in L2 spelling — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Treatment and reading ability interacted significantly on several measures, including LISTEN (ηh2 = .446), PHON-L2 (ηh2 = .191) and SLS (ηh2 = .181), indicating the training effect depended on reading skill — related
  • The training proved effective irrespective of reading ability and was more effective for the Test Dyslexic group than for the Test Nondyslexic group, which made greater gains on rapid naming, L3 receptive vocabulary and L3 picture naming — related
  • Higher L2 vocabulary retrieval performance correlated strongly with higher L3 retrieval performance, and nondyslexic learners outperformed dyslexic learners on all stimulated vocabulary retrieval measures — related
