## Evidence

### Phenyo Phemelo Moletsane 2026

Phenyo Phemelo Moletsane, Michael W. Asher, Christine Kwon, Paulo F. Carvalho, and Amy Ogan. (2026). Inclusive Mobile Learning: How Technology-Enabled Language Choice Supports Multilingual Students. Proceedings of the 2026 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI '26). https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791139

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multinomial logistic regression on 2,924 registered learners with language choice as the dependent variable and Leb Lango as baseline. The model was significant (χ2 = 571.26, p<.001, McFadden's R2 = 0.091); "those in upper primary (P5-P7) were 2.60 times more likely to choose English over Leb Lango". Higher schooling levels showed even larger odds ratios.

> "Compared to learners in lower primary (P1-P4), those in upper primary (P5-P7) were 2.60 times more likely to choose English over Leb Lango (95% CI[ 1.73, 3.89], 𝑝<. 0001)"

### Phenyo Phemelo Moletsane 2026 (2)

Phenyo Phemelo Moletsane, Michael W. Asher, Christine Kwon, Paulo F. Carvalho, and Amy Ogan. (2026). Inclusive Mobile Learning: How Technology-Enabled Language Choice Supports Multilingual Students. Proceedings of the 2026 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI '26). https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791139

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same multinomial regression: neighborhood type was significantly associated with language choice, and "those in urban areas were 3.09 times more likely to select English" than village or settlement learners; urban learners were also more likely to choose Hybrid over Leb Lango.

> "compared to learners living in villages or settlements, those in urban areas were 3.09 times more likely to select English (95% CI[ 1.85, 2.87], 𝑝<. 0001)"
