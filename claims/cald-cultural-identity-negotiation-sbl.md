## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants struggled with implicit Australian health-care expectations such as rapport-building and informal conversation, and with cultural references unfamiliar to them. [→ Zhang 2025](#zhang-2025)
`q2 i?` Culturally shaped beliefs about independence versus family involvement influenced students' clinical reasoning about simulated patients' goals. [→ Zhang 2025](#zhang-2025)

## Evidence

### Zhang 2025

Zhang, L., Forbes, R., Patterson, F., & Penman, A. (2025). Becoming through simulation‐based learning: Lived experiences of occupational therapy students from culturally and linguistically diverse backgrounds. Australian Occupational Therapy Journal, 72(4), e70044. https://doi.org/10.1111/1440-1630.70044

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of interviews with 15 CALD occupational therapy students. Participants found Australian rapport-building norms confusing, were unfamiliar with "typical occupations" referenced by simulated patients, and reported cultural values shaping clinical reasoning, while also citing cultural sensitivity as a strength with CALD simulated patients.

> "Many participants reported difficulties understanding the implicit social expectations in Australian health‐care settings replicated in the simulation environment"
