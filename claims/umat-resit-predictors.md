# Likelihood of re-sitting the UMAT is raised by low initial UMAT-1 or UMAT-2 scores but reduced by low UMAT-3 scores, and predicted by male gender, younger age, non-English background, NZ origin and urban residence

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Lowest-quartile UMAT-1 and UMAT-2 scores increased the odds of re-sitting (OR 2.09 and 1.24) whereas lowest-quartile UMAT-3 reduced them (OR 0.61). [→ Puddey Ian B 2014](#puddey-ian-b-2014)
`q3 i?` Re-sitting was predicted by being male (OR 1.10), younger age, non-English language background, being from NZ (OR 3.59) and being non-ATSI, while rurally based Australian candidates were less likely to re-sit (OR 0.78). [→ Puddey Ian B 2014 (2)](#puddey-ian-b-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Puddey Ian B 2014

Puddey Ian B, Mercer Annette, Andrich David, Styles Irene. (2014). Practice effects in medical school entrance testing with the undergraduate medicine and health sciences admission test (UMAT). BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-14-48

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Binomial logistic regression of predictors of sitting twice or more (N = 17,089 re-sitters vs 110,295 single sitters). "the likelihood of re-sitting for those in the lowest quartile for UMAT-3 at the first test was reduced (OR 0.61, 95% CI 0.57, 0.64, P < 0.001)" while UMAT-1 lowest quartile carried OR 2.09.

> "For those in the lowest quartile for UMAT-1 or UMAT-2 percentile score at the first test, the likelihood of re-sitting was increased (OR 2.09, 95%CI 1.97, 2.23, P < 0.001, and OR 1.24, 95% CI 1.17, 1.31, P < 0.001, respectively). In contrast, the likelihood of re-sitting for those in the lowest quartile for UMAT-3 at the first test was reduced (OR 0.61, 95% CI 0.57, 0.64, P < 0.001)."

### Puddey Ian B 2014 (2)

Puddey Ian B, Mercer Annette, Andrich David, Styles Irene. (2014). Practice effects in medical school entrance testing with the undergraduate medicine and health sciences admission test (UMAT). BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-14-48

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Same logistic model of re-sitting; being from NZ (OR 3.59) was the strongest socio-demographic predictor. In a separate Australian-only model, rurally based candidates were less likely to re-sit (OR 0.78, 95% CI 0.71, 0.85, P < 0.001); the full model explained little variance (Nagelkerke R Square 0.066).

> "Increased likelihood of re-sitting the UMAT was predicted by being male (OR 1.10, 95% CI 1.06, 1.14, P < 0.001), being of younger age, being from a non-English language speaking background (Asian language background OR 1.18, 95% CI 1.13, 1.23, P < 0.001), (European language background OR 1.12, 95% CI 1.0, 1.26, P = 0.05) and all Other languages (OR 1.49, 95% CI 1.33, 1.67, P < 0.001), being from NZ (OR 3.59, 95% CI 3.43, 3.76) and being non-ATSI (OR 1.57, 95% CI 1.15, 2.13, P = 0.004)."
