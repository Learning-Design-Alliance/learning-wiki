## Evidence

### Razan Hamed and N. Sanjay Rebello 2026

Razan Hamed and N. Sanjay Rebello. (2026). Cognitive Load and Situational Interest in Physics Laboratories: A Comparative Study Across Three Instructional Modalities. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2602.06143](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.06143)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Authors' cross-experiment interpretation of the ANOVA patterns from the mechanics (N = 54) and circuits (N = 47) experiments; the article states higher cognitive load "tends to be associated with lower situational interest". This is an interpretation, not a computed correlation; no effect size is printed.

> "Taken together, these findings suggest an inverse relationship between cognitive load (both intrinsic and extraneous) and situational interest, such that higher cognitive load tends to be associated with lower situational interest, and vice versa."

### Razan Hamed and N. Sanjay Rebello 2026 (2)

Razan Hamed and N. Sanjay Rebello. (2026). Cognitive Load and Situational Interest in Physics Laboratories: A Comparative Study Across Three Instructional Modalities. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2602.06143](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.06143)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Cross-experiment pattern in the same two within-subject experiments: the article states the game modality "consistently yielded the lowest cognitive load (intrinsic and extraneous) and the highest situational interest" in both domains. No effect size is printed.

> "This was especially evident and significant in the game-based lab in both experiments since the game modality consistently yielded the lowest cognitive load (intrinsic and extraneous) and the highest situational interest."
