# Both simulation and case-based discussion improved knowledge from pre-test to post-test, and scores were maintained at 4–6-month retention

## Evidence

### Couto Thomaz Bittencourt I 2015

Couto Thomaz Bittencourt I, Farhat Sylvia C.L., Geis Gary L, Olsen Orjan, Schvartsman Claudio. (2015). High-fidelity simulation versus case-based discussion for teaching medical students in Brazil about pediatric emergencies. Clinics. [doi:10.6061/clinics/2015(06)02](https://doi.org/10.6061/clinics/2015(06)02)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Within-group pre-test to post-test comparisons in the crossover study of final-year medical students; both methods showed significant improvement (p<0.05) and scores were maintained over 4–6 months (p>0.05). No effect size is printed.

> "For both themes, there was improvement between the pre-test and the post-test (p<0.05), and no significant difference was observed between the post-test and the retention test (p>0.05)."
