## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The proportion of women decreases across degree levels, from majority among high-school and first-year undergraduate respondents to lower proportions finishing doctoral than master studies (the 'Scissors effect'). [→ Celia Anteneodo 2020](#celia-anteneodo-2020)

## Evidence

### Celia Anteneodo 2020

Celia Anteneodo, Carolina Brito, Alan Alves-Brito, Simone Silva Alexandre, Beatriz Nattrodt D'Avila, Débora Peres Menezes. (2020). Brazilian physicists community diversity, equity and inclusion: a first diagnostic. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010136](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010136)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive analysis of survey responses by highest degree attained (Fig. 3, bottom panel). The text reports that women's "relative presence tends to decrease" across stages, with the same trend at graduate level, and names this the "Scissors effect". No statistical test is reported.

> "women are the majority of re- spondents with a high-school degree and among ﬁrst-year undergraduate students, however their relative presence tends to decrease amongst those that ﬁnish the under- graduate stage"

### Celia Anteneodo 2020 (2)

Brazilian physicists community diversity, equity and inclusion: a first diagnostic. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010136](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010136)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive analysis of degree attainment by racial/ethnic group (Fig. 3, top panel). The article reports "a neat tendency of a lower proportion of black+parda people with the progression in the carrier", similar to trends reported for STEM in Brazil. No test statistic is printed.

> "This proﬁle puts into evidence the neat tendency of a lower proportion of black+parda people with the progres- sion in the carrier"
