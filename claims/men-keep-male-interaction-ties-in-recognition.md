## Evidence

### Sundstrom 2024

Sundstrom, M., Simpfendoerfer, L. N., Tan, A., Heim, A. B., & Holmes, N. G. (2024). Who and what gets recognized in peer recognition. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010127](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010127)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of the fraction of interaction-network edges kept in the recognition network, split by nominator and nominee gender, based on 859 of 1,000 nominations across all four courses; the authors report "men disproportionately 'keep' more of their interaction ties with men than with women".

> "In three out of the four courses (all but the spring offering of the lab course), we observe that men disproportionately "keep" more of their interaction ties with men than with women (see top panel of Fig. 6)."
