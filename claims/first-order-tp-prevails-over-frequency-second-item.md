# First-order transitional probability prevails over frequency in predicting the second item of a temporal sequence

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Response times to the second stimulus depended on first-order TP values rather than on the frequency of the pair, even when frequency and TP were fully separated across sequences. [→ Lazartigues Laura 2021](#lazartigues-laura-2021)

## Evidence

### Lazartigues Laura 2021

Lazartigues Laura, Mathy Fabien, Lavigne Frédéric. (2021). Statistical learning of unbalanced exclusive-or temporal sequences in humans. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0246826

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Touchscreen experiment with 38 psychology students; linear mixed-effect models on transition times compared sequence DBF (TP = .71, frequency .25) against ABC (TP = .62, frequency .4), with an interaction between first-order TP and block number (F(1,9557) = 31.8, p <.001). The article reports "This comparison shows that TT1 depends more on first-order TPs values within the two successive stimuli than on the frequency of the pair."

> "The RTTT1 were shorter for the highest value of first-order TP despite the lowest value of frequency (DBF, dashed blue line), compared to the first-order TP corresponding to the lowest value despite the highest value of frequency (ABC, dashed purple line)."
