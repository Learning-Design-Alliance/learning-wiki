# Home literacy environment is modestly and mostly positively associated with baseline reading scores across 12 low-resource sites, though not at every site

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` At baseline, home reading materials and literacy habits are modestly associated with students' reading achievement at most of the 12 sites, controlling for student and school characteristics; one site (the Philippines) shows significant negative coefficients on one index, and the association cannot show that the home environment causes the scores. [→ Dowd 2016](#dowd-2016)

## Evidence

### Dowd 2016

Dowd, A. J., Friedlander, E., Jonason, C., Leer, J., Sorensen, L. Z., D'Sa, N., Guajardo, J., Pava, C., & Pisani, L. (2016). Life-wide Learning and Early Reading Development in Twelve African and Asian Sites. SREE Spring 2016 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED567231

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Cross-country multilevel regression analysis (Model 1) fit separately for each of 12 sites, relating baseline literacy scores to home reading materials and habits indices. The article reports "statistically significant positive effect sizes ranging from 0.01 to 0.30", but its Table 2 also shows significant negative coefficients on one HLE index in the Philippines (-0.03 letters, -0.06 fluency, -0.04 comprehension) and no HLE coefficient at one Malawi site, so the relationship is positive at most sites rather than all. These are baseline correlations and cannot show cause.

> "Analyses reveal 1) a modest but consistent relationship between students' home literacy environments and their reading scores with statistically significant positive effect sizes ranging from 0.01 to 0.30"
