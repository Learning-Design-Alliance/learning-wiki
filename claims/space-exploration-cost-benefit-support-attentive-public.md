## Evidence

### Smith 2000

Smith, Howard A. (2000). Challenges in Affecting US Attitudes Towards Space Science. COSPAR 2000, Warsaw. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0008224](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0008224)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The S&EI survey cost-benefit question showed "48% in 1997" said space exploration benefits outweigh costs, relatively stable over ten years; support for scientific research generally averaged about 70%.

> "Nearly half of all adults sampled -- 48% in 1997 -- said the benefits of "space exploration" far outweighed or slightly outweighed the costs."

### Smith 2000 (2)

Smith, Howard A. (2000). Challenges in Affecting US Attitudes Towards Space Science. COSPAR 2000, Warsaw. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0008224](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0008224)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Comparing attentiveness and support in the S&EI data, the article reports the attentive public is the most supportive: a 2.5-to-1 positive-to-pessimistic ratio versus 1.5-to-1 among the neither-attentive-nor-interested, of whom about 43% are quite pessimistic.

> "Among the attentive population, two and one-half times as many think of science as positive and promising as compared to those whose attitudes are critical or pessimistic."
