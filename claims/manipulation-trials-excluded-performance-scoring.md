# Manipulation trials were excluded from performance scoring to preserve WAIS-III validity

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The authors did not use manipulation trials as performance measures because doing so would have violated WAIS-III administration guidelines and prevented valid standardized scoring. ## Evidence

### Test Boredom While Working on Difficult Versus Easy Tasks: Online Supplementary Materials 2022

Test Boredom While Working on Difficult Versus Easy Tasks: Online Supplementary Materials. (2022). https://www.google.com/search?q=%22Test+Boredom+While+Working+on+Difficult+Versus+Easy+Tasks%22

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Supplemental Material S3 (Study 2) gives three reasons manipulation trials were not used to calculate performance scores: adherence to "the guidelines for the WAIS-III to ensure validity," floor and ceiling effects of the very easy (2-3 digit) and very difficult (8-9 digit) tasks, and the impossibility of transforming resulting scores into standardized WAIS-III scores.

> "for the performance measure, we wanted to adhere as closely as possible to the guidelines for the WAIS-III to ensure validity. This would not have been possible with only using very easy or very difficult items, as included in the manipulation trials."
