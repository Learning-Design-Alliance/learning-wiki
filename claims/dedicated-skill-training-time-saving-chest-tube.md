# Dedicated chest tube insertion training was estimated to save attending consultants about 12 hours compared with conventional bedside ICU teaching

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Based on the authors' time accounting, the skill-training session (about 12 hours of fellow time, 4 hours of consultant time) was estimated to save attending consultants about 12 hours relative to an estimated 30 minutes of supervision per bedside chest tube insertion. [→ Zante Bjoern 2020](#zante-bjoern-2020)

## Evidence

### Zante Bjoern 2020

Zante Bjoern, Schefold Joerg C. (2020). Simulation training for emergency skills: effects on ICU fellows’ performance and supervision levels. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02419-4

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Descriptive time-expenditure comparison reported in the results section, contrasting the dedicated cadaver-lab session (about 2 hours per fellow, 6–8 insertions each) with estimated bedside teaching time. The figures are the authors' estimates, not a measured comparison.

> "Hence, six chest tube insertions for each fellow (about 30 min × 6 = 180 min), for six fellows (about 180 min × 6 fellows) require 1080 min (18 h) of time on supervision. During respective supervision activities, the attending consultant would be absorbed and unable to perform other activities. Hence, potential time saving for attending consultants might be about 12 h."
