# Peer pairing, peer tutoring, and small collaborative/cooperative groupings improved MLs' English phonological awareness, decoding, and fluency across grade levels

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Peer pairing, peer tutoring, and small collaborative/cooperative groupings were effective in improving MLs' English PA across grade levels, regardless of language of instruction, and also improved English decoding and fluency compared to teacher-mediated comparison conditions. [→ Kittle 2024](#kittle-2024)

## Evidence

### Kittle 2024

Kittle, J. M., Amendum, S. J., & Budde, C. M. (2024). What Does Research Say About the Science of Reading for K-5 Multilingual Learners? A Systematic Review of Systematic Reviews. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09942-6

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Synthesis of RQ2 findings from reviews including Pyle et al. (2017) and Tang et al. (2021). The review reports these groupings improved English PA regardless of language of instruction and across grade levels, and were often associated with improved decoding and fluency compared to teacher-mediated comparison conditions. No effect size is printed.

> "Peer pairing, peer tutoring, and small collaborative/cooperative groupings were effective in improving MLs’ English PA across grade levels. These types of pairings and groupings were effective when they include instructional activities such as previewing, predicting, paired read aloud, repeated reading, reciprocal reading, questioning, retelling, and error correction"
