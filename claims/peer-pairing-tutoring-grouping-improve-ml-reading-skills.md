# Peer pairing, peer tutoring, and small collaborative/cooperative groupings improved MLs' English phonological awareness across grade levels and were often associated with better English decoding and fluency than teacher-mediated instruction

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Peer pairing, peer tutoring, and small collaborative/cooperative groupings were effective in improving MLs' English PA, regardless of language of instruction (Pyle et al., 2017) and across grade levels (Tang et al., 2021). For English decoding, they significantly improved letter-word identification across grade levels (Tang et al., 2021) and were often associated with improved decoding compared to teacher-mediated comparison conditions (Pyle et al., 2017); for English oral reading fluency, they demonstrated gains and were often associated with improved fluency compared to teacher-mediated conditions (Pyle et al., 2017), with no across-grade statement for fluency. ## Evidence

### Kittle 2024

Kittle, J. M., Amendum, S. J., & Budde, C. M. (2024). What Does Research Say About the Science of Reading for K-5 Multilingual Learners? A Systematic Review of Systematic Reviews. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09942-6

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Synthesis of RQ2 findings from reviews including Pyle et al. (2017) and Tang et al. (2021). The review reports these groupings improved English PA regardless of language of instruction and across grade levels; for decoding, cooperative, collaborative and peer-tutoring strategies "significantly and positively impacted MLs' letter-word identification across grade levels" (Tang et al., 2021), and the groupings were "often associated with improved" English decoding and English reading fluency "compared to teacher-mediated comparison conditions" (Pyle et al., 2017). The fluency finding is not stated across grade levels. No effect size is printed for these practices.

> "Peer pairing, peer tutoring, and small collaborative/cooperative groupings were effective in improving MLs’ English PA across grade levels. These types of pairings and groupings were effective when they include instructional activities such as previewing, predicting, paired read aloud, repeated reading, reciprocal reading, questioning, retelling, and error correction"
