## Evidence

### Michaela Minárechová 2016

Michaela Minárechová. (2016). Using a concept cartoon© method to address elementary school students' ideas about natural phenomena. European Journal of Science and Mathematics Education, Vol. 4, No. 2, 214-228. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1107824

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Direct structured observation (adapted Fibonacci tools, Carulla 2012) of each concept-cartoon lesson, coded by frequency. Table 3 prints "Teacher insufficiently clarified to students work during the lesson" at 45%, teacher-suggested drafts at 56%, and no problems with classroom organization at 89%.

### Michaela Minárechová 2016 (2)

Michaela Minárechová. (2016). Using a concept cartoon© method to address elementary school students' ideas about natural phenomena. European Journal of Science and Mathematics Education, Vol. 4, No. 2, 214-228. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1107824

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Authors' interpretation (type e) of the observation findings: the teacher interrupted "scientific work of students" and tried to influence the right answer, which the authors argue prevents modification of imperfect ideas. This is an interpretation, not a tested result.

> "If the teacher presents his/her “correct answers” to students, so they will present the teacherʹs version. However they will constantly believe their own original imperfect ideas."
