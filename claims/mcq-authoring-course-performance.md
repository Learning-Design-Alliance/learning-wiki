## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Course performance differed significantly by level of question authorship, and quiz participation scored in combination with authorship was highly correlated with test performance. [→ Riggs C. Daniel 2020](#riggs-c-daniel-2020)

## Evidence

### Riggs C. Daniel 2020

Riggs C. Daniel, Kang Sohee, Rennie Olivia. (2020). Positive Impact of Multiple-Choice Question Authoring and Regular Quiz Participation on Student Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education 19(2):ar16. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-09-0189

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

ANCOVA on combined data from the two terms, in which each student was required to author two questions worth 4% of the final grade. The article reports "highly significant course performance differences by the different levels of authorship" controlling for incoming GPA.

> "ANCOVA revealed that there are highly significant course performance differences by the different levels of authorship after we control for previous academic performance (p = 2 × 10−16, F = 98.6; see Supplemental Table S1)."

### Riggs C. Daniel 2020 (2)

Riggs C. Daniel, Kang Sohee, Rennie Olivia. (2020). Positive Impact of Multiple-Choice Question Authoring and Regular Quiz Participation on Student Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education 19(2):ar16. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-09-0189

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Companion ANCOVA on the same combined two-year dataset, reporting quiz participation "highly correlated with test performance" with the printed F value of 187.6.

> "Similarly, when scored in combination with authorship, quiz participation was highly correlated with test performance (p < 2.2 × 10−16, F value = 187.6; see Supplemental Table S1-3)."
