Evidence¶

Nalline S. Baliram & Jeffrey J. Youde 2018¶

Nalline S. Baliram & Jeffrey J. Youde. (2018). A Meta-analytic Synthesis: Examining the academic impacts of feedback on student achievement. International Dialogues on Education, 5(2), 76-90. https://portal.issn.org/resource/ISSN/2198-5944

q3 · i3

Moderator analysis splitting the 26 data sets by feedback type: content-specific, praise, or objective (a numerical score, letter grade, or right/wrong indication). Content-specific feedback showed "Hedges’ g = .91" and praise was significant; objective feedback was not.

"The meta-analysis for content -specific feedback ( n = 8) indicated a summary overall effect of Hedges’ g = .91. Tests of statistical significance indicated support for the rejection of the null hypothesis ( p = .003). The meta-analysis for praise feedback (n = 2) indicated a summary overall effect of Hedges’ g = .42. Tests of statistical significance indicated support for the rejection of the null hypothesis ( p = .033). The meta-analysis for objective feedback ( n = 16) indicated a summary overall effect of Hedge s’ g = .13 with statistical non-significance indicated (p = .144)."
