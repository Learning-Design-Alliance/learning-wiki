# Active-learning meta-analyses compare broadly defined active learning with traditional lecture and do not show that named teaching methods are more effective

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · qualitative `r?` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2019 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Meta-analyses find that active learning / interactive engagement leads to larger learning gains than traditional lecture. [→ Strubbe 2019](#strubbe-2019)
`q2 i?` Because of broad definitions, these meta-analyses aggregate diverse non-lecture activities, so they do not show named teaching methods beat the broader definition of active learning. [→ Strubbe 2019 (2)](#strubbe-2019-2)
`q2 i?` The fraction of class time spent on activities in included studies spanned from 10% to 100%. [→ Strubbe 2019 (3)](#strubbe-2019-3)

## Evidence

### Strubbe 2019

Strubbe, L. E., Madsen, A. M., McKagan, S. B., & Sayre, E. C. (2019). Beyond teaching methods: highlighting physics faculty's strengths and agency. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1912.09586](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1912.09586)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

The article's review of meta-analytic literature: the analyses it cites "compare "active learning" or "interactive engagement" (IE) with "traditional lecture,"" finding larger gains for active learning — an aggregated contrast, not one among named methods.

> "Analyses of student learning gains across different teaching techniques compare "active learning" or "interactive engagement" (IE) with "traditional lecture," and find that active learning / IE leads to larger learning gains than traditional lecture [44–46]."

### Strubbe 2019 (2)

Strubbe, L. E., Madsen, A. M., McKagan, S. B., & Sayre, E. C. (2019). Beyond teaching methods: highlighting physics faculty's strengths and agency. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1912.09586](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1912.09586)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

The article notes that Freeman et al.'s broad definition of active learning means the "meta-analysis includes papers about a diverse range of non-lecture teaching activities and tools", many of which might not appear on named-method lists.

> "Because of this broad definition, Freeman et al.'s meta-analysis includes papers about a diverse range of non-lecture teaching activities and tools (e.g., in-class worksheets, problem-based learning, and clickers) [45]"

### Strubbe 2019 (3)

Strubbe, L. E., Madsen, A. M., McKagan, S. B., & Sayre, E. C. (2019). Beyond teaching methods: highlighting physics faculty's strengths and agency. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1912.09586](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1912.09586)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Dosage varied widely across included studies: class time spent on the activities "spanned a wide range, from 10% to 100%", so the meta-analyses measure an aggregated effect rather than a dose-response of named methods.

> "The fraction of class time spent on these activities also spanned a wide range, from 10% to 100%."
