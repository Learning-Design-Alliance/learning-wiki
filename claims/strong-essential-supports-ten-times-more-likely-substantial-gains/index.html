Evidence¶

Sebring 2006¶

Sebring, P. B., Allensworth, E., Bryk, A. S., Easton, J. Q., & Luppescu, S. (2006). The Essential Supports for School Improvement. Research Report, Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/essential-supports-school-improvement

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Observational analysis of Chicago public elementary schools, 1990-1996, using ITBS-based academic productivity measures and 1994 CCSR teacher and student surveys to measure the essential supports. The report found schools strong in most supports "at least ten times more likely" to show substantial gains, and very unlikely to stagnate.

"We found that schools strong in most of the essential supports were at least ten times more likely than schools weak in most of the supports to show substantial gains in both reading and mathematics."
