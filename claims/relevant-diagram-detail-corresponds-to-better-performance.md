# A higher level of relevant detail in a student's diagram corresponds to better problem-solving performance, but the detail gradient differs between the one-dimensional and two-dimensional problems

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` On the two-dimensional problem 2, students who drew relevant detail 2 diagrams (with force components) scored significantly better than relevant detail 1 drawers (p < 0.001, effect size = 0.87), and relevant detail 1 drawers beat unproductive-diagram drawers (p = 0.008, effect size = 0.61). [→ Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2018](#alexandru-maries-and-chandralekha-singh-2018)
`q3 i?` On the one-dimensional problem 1, drawers of relevant detail 1 or 2 diagrams significantly outperformed unproductive-diagram drawers (both p < 0.001 in the quiz), but the difference between relevant detail 1 and relevant detail 2 was not statistically significant in either the quiz or the midterm. [→ Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2018 (2)](#alexandru-maries-and-chandralekha-singh-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2018

Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Do students benefit from drawing productive diagrams themselves while solving introductory physics problems? The case of two electrostatics problems. Eur. J. Phys. 39 015703. https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/aa9038

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of diagram-detail levels on problem 2 in the quiz (table 7: unproductive N = 27, avg 4.1; RD1 N = 58, avg 5.7; RD2 N = 33, avg 8.0). The article notes "the effect sizes point to medium to large effects despite the large variation within each group".

> "students who drew relevant detail 1 diagrams performed signi ﬁcantly better than students who drew unproductive diagrams (p = 0.008, effect size = 0.61) and students who drew relevant detail 2 diagrams performed signi ﬁcantly better than students who drew relevant detail 1 diagrams (p < 0.001, effect size = 0.87)"

### Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2018 (2)

Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Do students benefit from drawing productive diagrams themselves while solving introductory physics problems? The case of two electrostatics problems. Eur. J. Phys. 39 015703. https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/aa9038

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

On problem 1 in both the quiz and the midterm, comparisons of RD1 or RD2 against unproductive diagrams were significant (p < 0.001 quiz, p = 0.003 midterm for UD-RD1), but the RD1-RD2 contrast was not significant (p = 0.284 quiz; p = 0.133 midterm). No effect size is printed for the null contrast.

> "the difference in performance between students who drew relevant detail 1 diagrams and students who drew relevant detail 2 diagrams is not statistically signi ﬁcant"
