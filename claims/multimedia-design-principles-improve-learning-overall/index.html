Evidence¶

Noetel 2022¶

Noetel, M., Griffith, S., Delaney, O., Harris, N. R., Sanders, T., Parker, P., del Pozo Cruz, B., & Lonsdale, C. (2022). Multimedia design for learning: An overview of reviews with meta-meta-analysis. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329

q3 · i1 · quant-synthesis · r3

Meta-meta-analysis of the 11 largest systematic reviews, pooling 808 effect sizes from 66,553 participants on a common Hedges's g metric. The article reports "The average effect of multimedia design principles on learning was moderate and statistically significant (g = 0.38" with CI [0.27, 0.49].

"the 11 largest reviews pooled a combined 808 effect sizes from 66,553 participants. The average effect of multimedia design principles on learning was moderate and statistically significant (g = 0.38, 95% CI [0.27, 0.49], k = 808)"

Noetel 2022 (2)¶

Noetel, M., Griffith, S., Delaney, O., Harris, N. R., Sanders, T., Parker, P., del Pozo Cruz, B., & Lonsdale, C. (2022). Multimedia design for learning: An overview of reviews with meta-meta-analysis. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Moderation analysis across 16 pooled effects found the design principle itself explained most variance in learning (R2 = 1.00, p < 0.001); Figure 3 shows principles ranging from moderate-large (captioning, contiguity, modality, signaling) to weak (verbal redundancy, pedagogical agents).

"The specific design principle explained most of the variance in learning outcomes (NPooledEffects = 16, R22 = 1.00, p < 0.001). As shown in Figure 3, some design principles had much larger effects than others"
