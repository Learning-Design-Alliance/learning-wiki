# Teacher's encouragement is more effective than modeling by a competent adult at raising children's adult encouragement barrier self-efficacy for recess physical activity

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a randomized three-group experiment, the encouragement group's adjusted post adult-encouragement barrier self-efficacy was significantly higher than the modeling group's (p = .001). [→ Efrat 2017](#efrat-2017)

## Evidence

### Efrat 2017

Merav W. Efrat. (2017). Exploring Strategies That Influence Children's Physical Activity Self-Efficacy. Contemporary Issues in Education Research, 10(2). https://clutejournals.com/index.php/cier

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized experiment with 161 fourth graders from three elementary schools assigned to modeling, encouragement, or comparison groups; 2 (gender) x 3 (group) ANCOVAs with pretreatment scores as covariates showed the encouragement-versus-modeling contrast was "significantly more effective" on adult encouragement barrier self-efficacy, p = .001.

> "Results indicated that a teacher's encouragement, compared to modeling, was significantly more effective at influencing a child's confidence in their ability to be less dependent on an adult's encouragement to prompt them to be active during recess, p =.001."
