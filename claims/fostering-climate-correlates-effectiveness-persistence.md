# Fostering and Collaborative classroom perceptions correlate positively, and Transmitting negatively, with perceived teacher effectiveness and with performance and persistence

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students' Fostering and Collaborative factor scores correlated positively with perceived teacher effectiveness while Transmitting scores correlated negatively, and students who saw classes as more effective had higher academic performance. [→ Dickie 2006](#dickie-2006)
`q2 i?` Classes perceived as fostering development positively impacted persistence and success, while classes perceived as transmitting had a negative impact, more so for women. [→ Dickie 2006 (2)](#dickie-2006-2)

## Evidence

### Dickie 2006

Dickie, L. O., Dedic, H., Rosenfield, S., Rosenfield, E., & Simon, R. A. (2006). Success and Persistence in Science: The Influence of Classroom Climate. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0610243](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0610243)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis of student factor scores from the validated three-factor perception model for high school and CEGEP samples (Table 2 reports correlations significant at p = .01, e.g. Fostering .819** for college students).

> "Students' factor scores on Fostering and Collaborative correlated positively with students' perception that the teacher was effective in making them learn while students' factor scores on Transmitting correlated negatively with their perception of teachers' effectiveness as shown in Table 2."

### Dickie 2006 (2)

Dickie, L. O., Dedic, H., Rosenfield, S., Rosenfield, E., & Simon, R. A. (2006). Success and Persistence in Science: The Influence of Classroom Climate. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0610243](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0610243)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of academic performance across perceived-effectiveness groups in the cohort. Students perceiving classes as more effective in promoting learning had significantly higher performance (means 83.0 vs. 81.3, F(1, 1302) = 16.7, p < .001).

> "there was also a significant difference in academic performance between students who saw their classes as more-effective/less-effective in promoting learning (means 83.0, 81.3), F(1, 1302) = 16.7, p < .001)."
