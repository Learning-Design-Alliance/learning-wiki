Evidence¶

Barth 2000¶

Barth, Patte, Ed. (2000). Honor in the Boxcar: Equalizing Teacher Quality. Thinking K-16; v4 n1 Spr 2000. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED458350

q2 · i? · review · r1

The article summarizes research findings previously published in its 'Good Teaching Matters' review, reporting that sequences of effective versus ineffective teachers produce divergent achievement trajectories, with students losing "significant ground which they may never recover."

"Students who have several effective teachers in a row make dramatic gains in achievement, while those who have even two ineffective teachers in a row lose significant ground which they may never recover."

Barth 2000 (2)¶

Barth, Patte, Ed. (2000). Honor in the Boxcar: Equalizing Teacher Quality. Thinking K-16; v4 n1 Spr 2000. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED458350

q2 · i? · review · r1

The same summary reports that teacher assignment quality can produce a gap of "as many as 50 percentile points" three years later among students who were similar in third grade. No standardized effect size is printed.

"Indeed, students who achieve at similar levels in the third grade may be separated by as many as 50 percentile points three years later depending on the quality of the teachers to whom they were assigned!"
