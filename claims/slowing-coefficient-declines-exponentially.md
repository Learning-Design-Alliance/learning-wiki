## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Slowing coefficients changed substantially and systematically with age, from approximately 3 for the youngest children to about 1.25 for 12- to 14-year-olds. [→ Kail 1991](#kail-1991)

## Evidence

### Kail 1991

Robert Kail. (1991). Developmental Change in Speed of Processing During Childhood and Adolescence. Psychological Bulletin, 109(3), 490-501. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.109.3.490

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Descriptive pattern in the fitted slowing coefficients from Table 2 across the 11 age sets: youngest children's coefficients "approximated 3" while adolescents' averaged 1.25, declining nonlinearly toward an asymptote of 1.

> "The youngest children (3- to 5-year-olds) had m, values that approximated 3, whereas the mean mi for 12- to 14-year-olds was 1.25."

### Kail 1991 (2)

Robert Kail. (1991). Developmental Change in Speed of Processing During Childhood and Adolescence. Psychological Bulletin, 109(3), 490-501. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.109.3.490

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Numerical curve fitting (STEPIT least squares) of Equation 6, with b = 5.16 and c = .20811 for the full set. Hyperbolic and power fits accounted for nearly identical variance (75.34% and 86.19% for the hyperbolic) but were rejected because negative c estimates "produce discontinuous developmental functions."

> "Equation 6 accounted for 76.92% of the variance in the slowing coefficients derived from the full set and 84.1% of the variance in the coefficients from the reduced data set."
