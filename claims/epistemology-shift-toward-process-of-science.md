# Instructors observed a shift in students' epistemology from memorization and grade-focus toward learning through experiments and applying models

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The authors report evidence, from interview transcripts and examination solutions, of a shift from viewing physics as something to endure and from memorization toward focusing on the process of science and applying developed models. [→ Thacker 2007](#thacker-2007)

## Evidence

### Thacker 2007

Thacker, B., Diaz, A., & Eligon, A. (2007). The Development of an Inquiry-based Curriculum Specifically for the Introductory Algebra-based Physics Course. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0702247](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0702247)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Qualitative evidence from student interviews on topics such as fluid dynamics and from students' exam solutions; the authors state "A detailed analysis is in progress," so the finding is preliminary and not quantified.

> "We have evidence of a shift in students’ epistemology from viewing physics class as something that has to be endured (which involves a focus on the process of getting a grade), to something more than tolerable, even enjoyable, which involves a focus on learning about the world around them through experiments they do themselves in class, on the process of science."
