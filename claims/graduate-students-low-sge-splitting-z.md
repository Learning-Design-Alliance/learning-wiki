## Evidence

### Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Improving Students' Understanding of Quantum Mechanics via the Stern-Gerlach Experiment. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06374](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06374)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Written survey administered at the beginning of graduate-level quantum mechanics instruction to more than 200 graduate students from different universities; the article reports "Only 41%" provided the correct response of two spots split along the z direction. Many students thought there would be only a single spot.

> "Only 41% of the more than 200 graduate students from diﬀerent universities enrolled in a quantum mechanics course pr ovided the correct response. These students were given this question as a part of a survey at th e beginning of graduate level quantum mechanics instruction."

### Singh 2016

Singh, C. (2016). Student Difficulties with Quantum Mechanics Formalism. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.075](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.075)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Written survey question part (a) on a Stern-Gerlach apparatus with a vertical magnetic field gradient in the -z direction. Even with lenient tallying, "Only 41% of the students provided the correct response"; many predicted a single spot on the phosphor screen.

> "All responses in which students noted that there will be a splitting along the z direction were considered correct even if they did not explain their reasoning. Only 41% of the students provided the correct response."
