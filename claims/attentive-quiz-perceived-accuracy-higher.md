Independent-samples t-test on the quiz-accuracy item of a two-item survey (the other item rated helpfulness), aggregated across three weeks; attentive group M=4.21 (SD=0.80) vs non-attentive M=2.94 (SD=1.25), a large printed effect size of Hedges' g = 1.157. Bayesian t-test supported the difference (BF10=14.28).

### Shi Liu 2026 (2)

Shi Liu, Martin Feick, Linus Bierhoff, and Alexander Maedche. 2026. AttentiveLearn: Personalized Post-Lecture Support for Gaze-Aware Immersive Learning. In Proceedings of the 2026 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI '26). ACM, New York, NY, USA. https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3790667

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Repeated measures correlation between per-section ADI and subjective quiz accuracy ratings, conducted to validate the ADI construct; printed effect size r_rm = -0.72, so sections with lower detected attention were rated as having more accurate quizzes. The non-attentive group showed no significant correlation (r_rm = 0.02, p=0.714).

> "For the attentive group, a repeated measures correlation indicated a significant negative association (𝑟𝑟𝑚 =− 0.72,𝑝< 0.001), confirming that lecture sections with lower detected attention were significantly more likely to be rated as having accurate quizzes."
