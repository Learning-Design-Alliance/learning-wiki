## Evidence

### Chao Jonathan 2010

Chao Jonathan, Chang Ellen T, So Samuel KS. (2010). Hepatitis B and liver cancer knowledge and practices among healthcare and public health professionals in China: a cross-sectional study. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2458-10-98

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariate linear regression (Table 3) on the 16-point knowledge score from the cross-sectional survey. The article reports the adjusted rural-versus-urban difference with its 95% CI.

> "After adjusting for other influences on HBV-related knowledge, individuals from rural provinces scored, on average, 1.0 point lower (95% CI: -1.6, -0.3 points) in HBV-related knowledge questions compared with those from urban provinces"

### Chao Jonathan 2010 (2)

Chao Jonathan, Chang Ellen T, So Samuel KS. (2010). Hepatitis B and liver cancer knowledge and practices among healthcare and public health professionals in China: a cross-sectional study. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2458-10-98

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Adjusted regression results from the same survey; the article notes the occupation result is "based on a small sample size" (5 nurses/midwives, 1 elementary school staff in Table 1).

> "nurse/midwives scored 2.4 points lower (95% CI: -4.3, -0.5) than physicians on HBV-related knowledge questions, while elementary school staff scored 3.9 points lower (95% CI: -7.4, -0.4)."
