# After the milq course, grade-13 high-school students reached a mean overall conception index of +55.8, significantly higher than first-year university physics students who had not yet attended quantum courses (+35.2, d = 0.97)

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` High-school students taught with the milq course scored on average 20.6 points higher on the overall quantum-conception index than a comparison group of first-year university physics students without university quantum instruction, a statistically highly significant large effect (d = 0.97). [→ Müller R and Mishina O 2020](#muller-r-and-mishina-o-2020)

## Evidence

### Müller R and Mishina O 2020

Müller R and Mishina O. (2020). milq – Quantum Physics in Secondary School. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2012.15162](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2012.15162)

`q2 · i3` · `design · r2`

Questionnaire evaluation study comparing high-school students after the milq course (N = 60) with 35 first-year physics students from the University of Munich without university quantum courses. The comparison group mean was "+35.2, i.e. 20.6 points lower than in the test group" with d = 0.97. The authors note a suitable control group was difficult to define because course content was far from the standard curriculum.

> "The mean value of the overall conception index in this comparison group was +35.2, i.e. 20.6 points lower than in the test group. The difference is statistically highly significant (p < 0.1%). The effect strength d is 0.97; it is therefore a large effect."
