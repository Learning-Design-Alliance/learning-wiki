# JOLs with word stems and retrieval practice reduce prior-list intrusions on the List-5 test, whereas JOLs with complete words do not

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Making JOLs with word stems decreased internal intrusions compared to restudy (d = 0.69), and retrieval practice did so as well (d = 0.57), while JOLs with complete words did not (d = 0.23, ns). [→ Veit Kubik 2022](#veit-kubik-2022)

## Evidence

### Veit Kubik 2022

Veit Kubik, Kenneth Koslowski, Torsten Schubert, Alp Aslan. (2022). Metacognitive judgments can potentiate new learning: The role of covert retrieval. Metacognition and Learning (2022) 17:1057–1077. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09307-w

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r3`

Between-subjects ANOVA on internal intrusions during interim List-5 recall showed a main effect of practice type, F(3, 157) = 2.97, p = .034, ω²p = 0.04; planned contrasts showed intrusions "decreased only for making JOLs with word stems, compared to restudy". Retrieval practice also reduced intrusions versus restudy, t(46.84) = 1.97, p = .027, d = 0.57.

> "the number of internal intrusions was decreased only for making JOLs with word stems, compared to restudy, t(42.83) = 2.26, p = .015, d = 0.69, 95% CI [0.17, ∞] (one-tailed), but again not for making JOLs with complete words, t(62.38) = 0.91, p = .365, d = 0.23, 95% CI [-0.27, 0.73]"
