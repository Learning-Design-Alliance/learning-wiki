## Subclaims
`q1 i?` If specific abilities are always summed into a single general rating, with marks averaged or teachers asked to estimate general ability, a general factor will appear as an artifact of the procedure. [→ Pressey 1920](#pressey-1920)

## Evidence

### Pressey 1920

Pressey, S. L. (1920). Intelligence and Its Measurement. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Intelligence+and+Its+Measurement+Pressey

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r?`

Theoretical argument, offered as a research-direction rationale rather than a tested result. Pressey argues that generalization procedures guarantee the appearance of generality: "there will appear to be a general factor, because everything has been generalized!" Yet such results are reused as evidence of innate general ability.

> "this would never be discovered if scores on tests were always summed into a single rating in "general intelligence", marks always averaged, or teachers asked to estimate general ability."
