# The home literacy–vocabulary and home literacy–word adaptation relations persist after controlling for parental reading skill and parental perception of the child's reading skill

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Parental perception of child reading skill was not correlated with home literacy practices (partial r(64) = 0.025) or word adaptation in the pIFs (partial r(42) = 0.115), and controlling for it did not eliminate the literacy-practice relations (vocabulary partial r(64) = 0.249, p = 0.022; pIFs adaptation partial r(42) = 0.302, p = 0.023). [→ Girard 2021](#girard-2021)

## Evidence

### Girard 2021

Girard, C., Bastelica, T., Léone, J., Epinat-Duclos, J., Longo, L. & Prado, J. (2021). Nurturing the reading brain: home literacy practices are associated with children's neural response to printed words through vocabulary skills. npj Science of Learning 6:34. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00112-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Control analysis addressing the reverse-causality possibility that parents read more with children they perceive as skilled readers; parental perception correlated with reading accuracy (r(64) = 0.386, p < 0.001) and speed (r(64) = 0.534, p < 0.001) but not with vocabulary or the key relations.

> "When controlling for this parental estimate of child 's reading skill, literacy practices remained related to vocabulary (controlling for parental income and education, partial r(64) = 0.249, p = 0.022) and word adaptation in the pIFs (controlling for parental income and education, partial r(42) = 0.302, p = 0.023)"
