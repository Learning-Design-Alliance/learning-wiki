# Class-level initial background knowledge does not moderate effects on content reading comprehension

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The interaction of class pretest content knowledge with treatment on content reading comprehension did not differ statistically from zero (p = .54), with no subgroup differences at other moderator values. [→ Jeanne Wanzek 2019](#jeanne-wanzek-2019)

## Evidence

### Jeanne Wanzek 2019

Jeanne Wanzek, Greg Roberts, Sharon Vaughn, Elizabeth Swanson, and Katherine Sargent. (2019). Examining the Role of Pre-Instruction Academic Performance Within A Text-based Approach to Improving Student Content Knowledge and Understanding. Exceptional Children, 85(2). https://doi.org/10.1177/0014402918783187

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same latent moderated SEM analysis of the class-randomized trial, the moderating effect of pretest ASK content knowledge on treatment effects for ASK content reading comprehension (controlling for pretest comprehension) was tested. The interaction "does not differ statis- tically from 0 (p  = .54)"; no effect size was printed for this model.

> "The interaction term, γ04z.jxj, does not differ statis- tically from 0 (p  = .54). Because PACT does not have a main effect on content reading comprehension (Vaughn et al., 2015), the absence of a moderating effect represents a less promising finding"
