# Relative performance information feedback improves final examination scores of low-performing students but has no significant impact on high-performing students

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For low-performing students the feedback effect was significantly positive (coefficient 5.553), while for high-performing students there was no significant difference between treated and control students. [→ Kajitani Shinya 2020](#kajitani-shinya-2020)

## Evidence

### Kajitani Shinya 2020

Kajitani Shinya, Morimoto Keiichi, Suzuki Shiba. (2020). Information feedback in relative grading: Evidence from a field experiment. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0231548

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Heterogeneity analysis (Eq 3) in the same RCT, interacting treatment with a dummy for midterm score of 60 or above, on 254 students. The low-performer treatment coefficient was "significantly positive and this magnitude is 5.553".

> "the coefficient for Di is significantly positive and this magnitude is 5.553. The relative performance information feedback for low-performing students on the midterm examination has a positive impact on their performance."

### Kajitani Shinya 2020 (2)

Kajitani Shinya, Morimoto Keiichi, Suzuki Shiba. (2020). Information feedback in relative grading: Evidence from a field experiment. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0231548

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same heterogeneity regression: the interaction Di × Hi was significantly negative (−8.074), and the F test of the sum of coefficients could not be rejected, so feedback showed no significant effect for high performers. Equivalence was not tested.

> "This indicates that, as for high-performing students, there is no significant difference between the final examination scores for students who received their rank information for the midterm examination and the scores for students who did not receive their rank information."
