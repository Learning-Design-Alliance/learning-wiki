## Evidence

### Iwatani 2019

Iwatani, E. (2019). How Rule Induction Data Mining Can and Cannot Be Useful for Education Research. Paper presented at the 2019 annual meeting of the American Educational Research Association. https://doi.org/10.302/1438441

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Ruleset re-analysis of the Byrnes & Miller (2007) hierarchical regression study on NELS:88 12th grade math achievement; subgroup contrasts contrasted higher versus lower 8th grade math achievers, reported in Table 10.

> "For example, rulesets in Study 2 suggested that Algebra 2 and math self-concept were positively related to 12th grade math scores, but only for those who were higher achieving in 8th grade math, while general math was negatively related to 12th grade math scores but only for those who scored lower on 8th grade math  (Table 10)."

### Iwatani 2019 (2)

Iwatani, E. (2019). How Rule Induction Data Mining Can and Cannot Be Useful for Education Research. Paper presented at the 2019 annual meeting of the American Educational Research Association. https://doi.org/10.302/1438441

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Association rule mining on income and parental education subgroups in the re-analysis of Thomas (2006); subgroup contrasts contrasted lower versus higher income and parental education groups, with results in Table 9.

> "In study 1, for example, several factors (e.g., participation in honors or gifted and talented programs, school safety) were more strongly associated with 12th grade achievement for lower income students, and students whose parents did not have a college degree (Table 9)."
