# Knowledge gains after the first VR training round predict later gains in behavioral intention; attitude and self-efficacy gains do not

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Within the experimental group, the knowledge change score from baseline to week 2 significantly predicted the intention change score from baseline to week 4 (β: 1.27, p = 0.010). [→ Lin Pei-Chao 2024](#lin-pei-chao-2024)
`q3 i?` Attitude and self-efficacy change scores from baseline to week 2 did not significantly predict intention change. [→ Lin Pei-Chao 2024](#lin-pei-chao-2024)

## Evidence

### Lin Pei-Chao 2024

Lin Pei-Chao, Wung Shu-Fen, Lin Pei-Chen, Lin Yi-Ching, Lin Cheng-Yu, Huang Hsiao-Ling. (2024). Virtual reality-based simulation learning on geriatric oral health care for nursing students: a pilot study. BMC Oral Health 24, Article 627. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-024-04249-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Simple linear regression among the 25 experimental-group participants predicting intention change from knowledge, attitude, and self-efficacy change scores. The article reports unstandardized βs only, so no standardized effect size is printed.

> "the change score of oral health care knowledge between Time 0 and Time 1 had a significant impact on the change scores of behavioral intentions between Time 0 and Time 2 [β: 1.27, 95% CI: 0.33, 2.20; p = 0.010]. The change scores of attitudes [β: -0.13, 95% CI: -0.81, 0.55; p = 0.690] and self-efficacy [β: 0.05, 95% CI: -0.23, 0.33; p = 0.719] between Time 0 and Time 1 did not have a significant impact on change scores of behavioral intentions between Time 0 and Time 2."
