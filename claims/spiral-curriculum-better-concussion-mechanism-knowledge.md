# Spiral-curriculum students more often correctly identified that concussion can be caused by a whiplash effect from an impact to any part of the body (90% vs 70%)

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Fraser S 2019

Fraser S, Wright AD, van Donkelaar P, Smirl JD. (2019). Cross-sectional comparison of spiral versus block integrated curriculums in preparing medical students to diagnose and manage concussions. BMC Medical Education 19(17). https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1439-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cross-sectional survey of 148 UBC medical students comparing spiral (n=78) and block (n=70) curricula. The results section reports the mechanism item: "90% of those in the spiral curriculum, compared to 70% of those in the block curriculum" answered correctly, chi-square significant.

> "Interestingly, when identifying the mechanism of concussion, 90% of those in the spiral curriculum, compared to 70% of those in the block curriculum, responded that a concussion could be caused by a whiplash effect to the brain caused by an impact to any part of the body (X21, 148 = 9.41, p = 0.002)."
