# In completers-only analyses, speechreading training shows large effects on everyday-questions speechreading that persist as medium effects at 11 months

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Among children who completed all training sessions, the ToCS Everyday Questions effect at T3 was large (d Items = 1.29, d Words = 0.78) and remained medium-sized at T4 (d Items = 0.59, d Words = 0.55). [→ Pimperton 2019](#pimperton-2019)

## Evidence

### Pimperton 2019

Pimperton, H., Kyle, F., Hulme, C., Harris, M., Beedie, I., Ralph-Lewis, A., Worster, E., Rees, R., Donlan, C., & MacSweeney, M. (2019). Computerized Speechreading Training for Deaf Children: A Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Speech, Language, and Hearing Research. https://doi.org/10.1044/2019_JSLHR-H-19-0073

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r3`

Exploratory completers-only analyses (speechreading n = 18; maths n = 19) comparing the same outcomes; the printed effect sizes were d Items = 1.29 and d Words = 0.78 at T3, persisting as d Items = 0.59 and d Words = 0.55 at T4, "larger in those participants who completed the intervention" than in intention-to-treat analyses.

> "There were large effects of speechreading training on the ToCS Everyday Questions task at T3 (d Items = 1.29, d Words = 0.78); these were still apparent as medium-sized effects in favor of the speechreading trained group at T4 (d Items = 0.59, d Words = 0.55)."
