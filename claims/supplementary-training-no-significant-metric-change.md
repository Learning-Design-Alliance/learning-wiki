# Technologists' segmentation similarity metrics and proportion of accurate segmentations did not differ significantly between the first and second batches in one uncontrolled pre-post study after supplementary training

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After supplementary multimedia training, DSC, JC, FP, and FN rates did not differ significantly between batches (DSC and JC p = 0.61; FP p = 0.07; FN p = 0.12), and the proportion of accurate segmentations did not differ significantly (62% vs 52%, p = 0.06). ## Evidence

### Suman Garima 2020

Suman Garima, Panda Ananya, Korfiatis Panagiotis, Edwards Marie E., Garg Sushil, Blezek Daniel J., Chari Suresh T., Goenka Ajit H. (2020). Development of a volumetric pancreas segmentation CT dataset for AI applications through trained technologists: a study during the COVID 19 containment phase. Abdominal Radiology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00261-020-02741-x

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Before-and-after comparison of two segmentation batches (188 and 159 CTs) using Kruskal–Wallis tests and a Chi-square test for proportions. "There was no difference in the proportion of accurate segmentations" between batches; the paper also reports no difference in DSC (p = 0.61), JC (p = 0.61), FP (p = 0.07), or FN (p = 0.12).

> "There was no difference in the proportion of accurate segmentations between the first and the second batch of technologists’ segmentations (62% in the first batch and 52% in the second batch, p = 0.06)."
