## Evidence

### Ashley A. August 2011

Ashley A. August, Kenneth C. Bretey, Bryant T. Cory, Elliott R. Finkley III, Robbie D. Jones, Dennis W. Marshall, Phillip C. Rowley, and W. Brian Lane. (2011). Enhancing Introductory Student Motivation with a Major-Managed Course Blog: A Pilot Study. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1108.1591](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1108.1591)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

End-of-semester interviews with the introductory students. The article reports "all of the students read the blog" and that many cited busyness and time constraints, which the authors interpret as managing time and cognitive load, similar to behavior observed by Harrison.

> "The student interviews revealed that all of the students read the blog, and that they all read more often than they posted comments."

### Ashley A. August 2011 (2)

Ashley A. August, Kenneth C. Bretey, Bryant T. Cory, Elliott R. Finkley III, Robbie D. Jones, Dennis W. Marshall, Phillip C. Rowley, and W. Brian Lane. (2011). Enhancing Introductory Student Motivation with a Major-Managed Course Blog: A Pilot Study. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1108.1591](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1108.1591)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

In the same interviews, "most students replied that their interest increased most signiﬁcantly when they had a sense of the applicability of the material," with aviation application most cited; one student reported lowest interest in topics requiring many laborious mechanical steps.

> "most students replied that their interest increased most signiﬁcantly when they had a sense of the applicability of the material, especially when that applicability extended to aviation."
