## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Pooled over the 6-month follow-up, the video-debriefing arm retained higher combined knowledge and skills scores than the control arm (adjusted mean difference 2.97, 95% CI 1.52–4.41, p < 0.001). [→ Odongkara B 2020](#odongkara-b-2020)

## Evidence

### Odongkara B 2020

Odongkara B, Tylleskär T, Pejovic N, Achora V, Mukunya D, Ndeezi G, Tumwine JK, Nankabirwa V. (2020). Adding video-debriefing to Helping-Babies-Breathe training enhanced retention of neonatal resuscitation knowledge and skills among health workers in Uganda: a cluster randomized trial. Global Health Action. https://doi.org/10.1080/16549716.2020.1743496

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mixed effects linear model for retention over 6 months in the cluster-randomized trial; the pooled competence row prints "2.97(1.52–4.41)" with p < 0.001, intervention mean 83.93 versus control 81.36. No standardized effect size is reported.

> "Adding video-debriefing to standard HBB training had significant effects on both skills and competence (knowledge and skills) retention over the 6-month period after controlling for differences in baseline characteristics (confounding) and clustering."
