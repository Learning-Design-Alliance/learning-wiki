## Evidence

### Harris Rebecca B. 2019

Harris Rebecca B., Grunspan Daniel Z., Pelch Michael A., Fernandes Giselle, Ramirez Gerardo, Freeman Scott. (2019). Can Test Anxiety Interventions Alleviate a Gender Gap in an Undergraduate STEM Course? CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.18-05-0083

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Best-fit regression model of exam points (GPA + anxiety + group) from the quasi-randomized two-section experiment with 779 analyzed students. The treatment coefficient was β = 4.249, p = 0.039, and "students in the treatment group earned more exam points"; the article reports a 1.06% boost in points earned. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "On average, students in the treatment group earned more exam points than equally matched students in the control group (treatment: β = 4.249, p = 0.039)."

### Harris Rebecca B. 2019 (2)

Harris Rebecca B., Grunspan Daniel Z., Pelch Michael A., Fernandes Giselle, Ramirez Gerardo, Freeman Scott. (2019). Can Test Anxiety Interventions Alleviate a Gender Gap in an Undergraduate STEM Course? CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.18-05-0083

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Stepwise backward model selection on exam points retained only treatment, test anxiety, and preparation; the article states "None of the interactions were retained in the final model", so benefits were not gender- or anxiety-specific. No effect size is printed.

> "None of the interactions were retained in the final model, demonstrating that, contrary to our expectations, the intervention did not differentially help women (hypothesis 6, Supplemental Figure S3) or students who are highly trait anxious (hypothesis 7)."
