## Evidence

### Scherer-Quenzer AC 2026

Scherer-Quenzer AC, Reinhart K, Diessner J, Altides A, Buechel J, Blau-Schneider B, Sauer ST, Deschler-Baier B, Woeckel A, Kiesel M. (2026). Comparative validation of a cost-effective in-house 3D-printed LLETZ simulator versus a commercial training model. Archives of Gynecology and Obstetrics. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00404-026-08413-3

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Friedman test across five attempts in the randomized student cohort (n = 60). The article prints η2 = 0.10 for the conventional and η2 = 0.227 for the in-house simulator, both statistically significant changes over time.

> "A statistically significant change in LEEP scores over time was observed for both simulators. The corresponding effect size for the conventional simulator was η2 = 0.10, whereas the in-house simulator demonstrated a larger effect size (η2 = 0.227)."

### Scherer-Quenzer AC 2026 (2)

Scherer-Quenzer AC, Reinhart K, Diessner J, Altides A, Buechel J, Blau-Schneider B, Sauer ST, Deschler-Baier B, Woeckel A, Kiesel M. (2026). Comparative validation of a cost-effective in-house 3D-printed LLETZ simulator versus a commercial training model. Archives of Gynecology and Obstetrics. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00404-026-08413-3

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Pairwise Friedman comparisons within each simulator group in the student cohort. Table 1 prints adjusted p values (e.g., 1 vs 5: < 0.001, d = 2.287 in-house; 1 vs 5: 0.553, d = 0.677 conventional).

> "After adjustment for multiple comparisons according to the Holm–Bonferroni method, no pairwise comparison remained statistically significant for the conventional simulator. In contrast, within the in-house simulator group, the differences between attempts 1 and 3, 1 and 4, 2 and 5, as well as 1 and 5 remained statistically significant following Holm correction and the corresponding effect sizes were large with all values exceeding 0.8"
