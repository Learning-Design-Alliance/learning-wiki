# In Experiment 1, posterior probabilities of norm existence increased with the number of norm-consistent behaviors while priors did not differ across conditions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Judgmental posterior probabilities increased with the number of norm-consistent behaviors observed, whereas prior probabilities did not differ across conditions. [→ Duan 2026](#duan-2026)

## Evidence

### Duan 2026

Duan, J., Guo, X., Zheng, L. & Yin, J. (2026). A Bayesian perspective on observers' inference of group norms. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00405-x

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Within-participants experiment (N = 32) using computer-animated events with five cartoon agents; participants judged the probability that a norm existed before and after observing 1–4 norm-consistent behaviors. The ANOVA showed a significant main effect, "F (1.48, 45.96) = 67.16, p < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.68", with significant adjacent-condition differences (ds > 0.72). Prior probabilities did not differ (F (2.06, 63.85) = 0.73, p = 0.491).

> "One-way repeated measures ANOVA revealed that the main effect of the number of norm-consistent behaviors was significant, F (1.48, 45.96) = 67.16, p < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.68."
