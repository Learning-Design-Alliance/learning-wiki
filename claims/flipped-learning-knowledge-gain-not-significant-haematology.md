# Flipped learning did not produce a significantly greater knowledge gain than traditional lectures in the haematology course, though the increase was significant within the flipped group

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The knowledge increase was significant in the intervention group (mean 1.46) but not in the control group (mean 0.67), and the between-group difference in knowledge increase was not statistically significant. [→ Algarni Abdulrahman Abdulrahman 2024](#algarni-abdulrahman-abdulrahman-2024)

## Evidence

### Algarni Abdulrahman Abdulrahman 2024

Algarni Abdulrahman Abdulrahman. (2024). Biomedical students’ self-efficacy and academic performance by gender in a flipped learning haematology course. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05421-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same quasi-experimental study, 30-item pre/post haematology tests produced knowledge-increase scores analysed by one-sample and independent-samples t tests. The intervention group's mean increase of 1.46 was significant (t40 = 3.08, p <.05) while the control group's 0.67 was not (t44 = 1.28, p =.21), yet "the knowledge increase of the intervention group and the knowledge increase of the control group" did not differ significantly (t84 = 1.12, p =.26). No effect size was printed.

> "No significant difference was found between the knowledge increase of the intervention group and the knowledge increase of the control group (t84 = 1.12, p =.26) (see Fig. 1)."
