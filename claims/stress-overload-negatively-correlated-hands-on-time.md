## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Across participants, overall perceived stress/overload correlated negatively with hands-on time (r=−0.18). [→ Hunziker S 2013](#hunziker-s-2013)

## Evidence

### Hunziker S 2013

Hunziker S, Pagani S, Fasler K, Tschan F, Semmer NK, Marsch S. (2013). Impact of a stress coping strategy on perceived stress levels and performance during a simulated cardiopulmonary resuscitation: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-227X-13-8

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Correlational analysis within the simulation trial relating participants' self-reported stress/overload (combined 'stressed' and 'overwhelmed' items, 1–20 scale) to hands-on time; the authors report "a significant negative correlation" (r=−0.18, p<0.05). No significant correlations were found with time to start CPR or leadership statements.

> "There was a significant negative correlation between the overall perceived stress/overload and hands-on time (r=−0.18, p<0.05) indicating that more stress/overload was associated with less hands-on time"
