## Subclaims
`q1 i?` May reported a positive affective response to equations and described them as universal codes scientists use to talk about and make sense of specific aspects of life such as diffusion. [→ Watkins 2010](#watkins-2010)
`q1 i?` May viewed equations as representing physical or biological concepts and saw their usefulness in simplicity, memorizing an equation and then working with its variables to understand relationships. [→ Watkins 2010](#watkins-2010)

## Evidence

### Watkins 2010

Watkins, J., Hall, K., Redish, E. F., & Cooke, T. J. (2010). Understanding How Students Use Physical Ideas in Introductory Biology Courses. University of Maryland, College Park. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1009.1049](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1009.1049)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Interview evidence from May, a student in Org Bio, contrasting with Ashlyn's response. May did not distinguish between scientists across disciplines and discussed equations' role in sense-making and communication, e.g., understanding that "the rate goes up because surface area went up."

> "It’s just scientists trying to understand one... like make sense of one specific aspect [of life], which is diffusion in this case… and we have to have universal codes for things, so that we can talk about them and make sense of them."
