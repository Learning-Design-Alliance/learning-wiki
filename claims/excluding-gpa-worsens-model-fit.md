## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Re-running forward regression without high school GPA produced worse-fitting models for all degrees; e.g. Mathematics dropped from 71.6% to 63.6% of API variance explained. [→ Dorta‐Guerra Roberto 2019](#dortaguerra-roberto-2019)

## Evidence

### Dorta‐Guerra Roberto 2019

Dorta‐Guerra Roberto, Marrero Isabel, Abdul‐Jalbar Beatriz, Trujillo‐González Rodrigo, Torres Néstor V. (2019). A new academic performance indicator for the first term of first‐year science degrees students at La Laguna University: a predictive model. FEBS Open Bio 9, 1493–1502. https://doi.org/10.1002/2211-5463.12707

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Sensitivity analysis repeating forward regression without GPA on the same five cohorts; variance explained also fell for Chemistry (64.3%→45.6%), Physics (70.2%→61.8%), Biology (42.8%→36.6%) and Environmental Sciences (54.2%→41.8%). The article reports the Mathematics contrast verbatim as quoted.

> "Concretely, for the Mathematics degree, the model including the high school grade point average accounted for 71.6% of the variance in the API, whereas the model that does not include the high school grade point average explained 63.6% of such a variance."
