Evidence¶

Nathan W. Whitmore 2024¶

Nathan W. Whitmore, Erika M. Yamazaki & Ken A. Paller. (2024). Targeted memory reactivation with sleep disruption does not weaken week-old memories. npj Science of Learning 9, 64. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00276-0

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Exploratory Spearman rank correlations with Benjamini-Hochberg corrections across sleep measures; cued-item forgetting ratio also showed no significant correlations with the sleep measures or with alpha and beta mean band power before and after cues.

Related Claims¶

  • Whether a cue provoked an EEG-scored arousal did not affect the forgetting ratio of cued objects — related
  • Greater sleep disruption in the present study rules out reduced sleep disruption as an explanation for the absent memory weakening — related
  • TMR benefits on name recall are negatively correlated with post-cue sleep disruption and post-cue beta-band EEG power, suggesting reactivation depends on undisturbed sleep — related
  • N3 duration, cuing, and sleep-disruption measures are intercorrelated but represent separable facets of sleep quality in predicting the recall cuing effect — related
  • The TMR cuing effect on name recall is positively correlated with the duration of stage N3 (slow-wave) sleep during the nap — related
  • The TMR cuing effect on face recognition accuracy is associated with N3 sleep duration, though cueing did not significantly improve recognition overall — related
  • Sleep Disruption Index classifies TMR responders: participants with an index below 0.13 showed a positive recall cuing effect, those above did not show a significant one — related
  • TMR-SD auditory cues presented during sleep did not reliably weaken week-old spatial memories compared to uncued objects — related
  • Sleep stage duration and architecture show no significant correlation with memory change in this nap paradigm — related
  • The TMR-SD effect did not differ for objects with low versus high pre-nap spatial error, ruling out memory strength as the explanation — related
  • The TMR-SD memory effect differed by post-learning delay: memory weakening occurred with a 1-hour delay but not with a 1-week delay — related
  • TMR efficacy depends on memory strength and task difficulty: benefits concentrate on moderately strong and challenging items, with limited advantage for easy (L1) items — related
