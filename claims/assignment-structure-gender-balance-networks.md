# Assignment submission structure relates to gender balance in interaction networks: contexts where assignments were submitted in groups showed proportionate interaction between men and women

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the three lab interaction networks plus the EM-Eng lecture network, where students completed and submitted assignments in small groups, men and women proportionately engaged in peer interactions; in the remaining three lecture networks, where work was submitted individually, men and women had significantly different numbers of connections. ## Evidence

### Meagan Sundstrom 2022

Meagan Sundstrom, Andy Schang, Ashley B. Heim, and N. G. Holmes. (2022). Understanding interaction network formation across instructional contexts in remote physics courses. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020141](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020141)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The authors' cross-context comparison of their ERGM gender results found that in contexts where students completed and submitted assignments in small groups, men and women "proportionately engaged in peer interactions".

> "In three out of four lab interaction networks and in the EM-Eng lecture interaction network, men and women proportionately engaged in peer interactions."

### Meagan Sundstrom 2022 (2)

Meagan Sundstrom, Andy Schang, Ashley B. Heim, and N. G. Holmes. (2022). Understanding interaction network formation across instructional contexts in remote physics courses. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020141](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020141)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the remaining three lecture networks, where students completed work in small groups but submitted it individually, the authors found that "men and women had signiﬁcantly different numbers of network connections".

> "We found in the remaining three lecture interaction networks, however, that men and women had signiﬁcantly different numbers of network connections."
