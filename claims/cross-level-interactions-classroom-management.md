# School characteristics moderate the self-efficacy–classroom management relationship: smaller slopes in schools with high collective efficacy or high collaboration, larger with more participation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Cross-level interactions predicting classroom management were (marginally) significant for collective efficacy in classroom management (B = –0.30), collaboration (B = –0.09), and participation (B = –0.12 for efficacy in instruction; B = 0.08 marginal for efficacy in classroom management). [→ Holzberger 2021](#holzberger-2021)

## Evidence

### Holzberger 2021

Holzberger, D., & Prestele, E. (2021). Teacher Self-Efficacy and Self-Reported Instructional Quality: A Multilevel Perspective on the Role of School Characteristics. Learning and Instruction, 76, 101513. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2021.101513

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Random slope models with school characteristics as Level 2 slope predictors on the TALIS 2018 Austrian sample. Only 4 of 16 tested cross-level interactions approached significance, all predicting classroom management; none predicted cognitive activation.

> "The cross-level interactions mean that in schools with a higher collective efficacious staff, the associations between teachers’ individual self-efficacy in classroom management and their self-reported classroom management were smaller than in schools with low collective efficacy"
