Interpretation of the visualized collaborative learning sequences (Figs. 5 and 6) in the discussion: the trend is described as plotted usage across stages, with the authors observing "it only peaked in the final stage of group B". The increase was gradual in both groups, the peak group-specific.

"Figs. 5 and 6 showed that while the usage of I–we gradually increased in both groups, it only peaked in the final stage of group B during which critical evaluation was done."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Group A, who failed the learning task, devoted most dialogues to orientation and conceptualization and asked yes-no questions at the personal level, while group B, who succeeded, distributed dialogue across investigation and discussion
  • Group B showed a shift toward intrapersonal voices (I–you/I–we) during its later empirical evaluation, while group A remained interpersonal (I–it/I–me) even when investigating, with un-negotiated inputs — related
  • Group A's two most common I-positions were I–it and I–me, while group B's were I–me and I–we, indicating different reliance on interpersonal versus intrapersonal voices — related
