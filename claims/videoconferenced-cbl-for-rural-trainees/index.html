Authors' interpretation in the discussion, grounded in the observation that NSW has many trainees in rural and remote regions and seven regional or rural accredited sites allowing a maximum of one trainee each. This is an untested proposal; no evidence for the intervention's effect on rural trainees is presented.

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Case-based discussions show evidence of reasoning and learning occurring and are perceived as useful for teaching and learning by oncology registrars and consultants — related
  • A simulated oncology MDT case-based learning session significantly improved trainee confidence, with a mean increase of 2 points on a 10-point scale (p < 0.01) — related
  • Only three studies met eligibility criteria for case-based learning in postgraduate medical oncology, totalling 111 participants, indicating a paucity of data in this field — related
  • Findings from other postgraduate specialties suggest PBL and CBL improve performance, knowledge and confidence, but overall RCT quality in this field is deemed unsatisfactory — a broader claim this one bears on
  • In a randomised trial of 80 first-year postgraduate oncology trainees, CBL produced significantly higher examination scores and satisfaction than traditional lecture-based learning, though confidence intervals crossed the null for examination results — related
  • All eligible postgraduate CBL studies had serious methodological flaws identified by CASP appraisal, limiting the validity and reliability of their conclusions — related
  • Case-based learning is viewed positively by practicing doctors, with all eligible postgraduate studies reporting favourable perceptions — related
  • AI in adult education risks reinforcing existing inequalities via the digital divide, particularly for rural, low-income, and marginalized learners — related
