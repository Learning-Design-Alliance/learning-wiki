## Evidence

### Sunny BS 2018

Sunny BS, DeStavola B, Dube A, Kondowe S, Crampin AC, Glynn JR. (2018). Does early linear growth failure influence later school performance? A cohort study in Karonga district, northern Malawi. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0200380

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Descriptive analysis of stunting prevalence by sex across three measurement points in the cohort. The article reports "more boys (11% and 23%) than girls (7.7% and 15.6%)" stunted at birth and early childhood, and higher levels in boys in late childhood.

> "Overall stunting prevalence increased from 9% at birth to 20% in early childhood, with more boys (11% and 23%) than girls (7.7% and 15.6%) being stunted at both points. However, in late childhood, stunting prevalence fell to 15%, with boys continuing to show higher levels of stunting than girls (16% and 13%)."
