## Evidence

### Sonlu 2025

Sonlu, S., Bendiksen, B., Durupinar, F., & Gudukbay, U. (2025). The Effects of Embodiment and Personality Expression on Learning in LLM-based Educational Agents. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1109/vr59515.2025.00094](https://doi.org/10.1109/vr59515.2025.00094)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pearson correlation analysis between LOES-S and BFI-2-XS factors reported in §4.3 and Table 2; positive correlations for all four positive traits, "highest correlations... for conscientiousness, particularly for Model D". The correlations are strongest for Model D and weakest for Model E.

> "Perceived openness, conscientiousness, extroversion, and agreeableness all positively correlate to the LOES-S scores, albeit some weakly. The highest correlations are for conscientiousness, particularly for Model D."

### Sonlu 2025 (2)

Sonlu, S., Bendiksen, B., Durupinar, F., & Gudukbay, U. (2025). The Effects of Embodiment and Personality Expression on Learning in LLM-based Educational Agents. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1109/vr59515.2025.00094](https://doi.org/10.1109/vr59515.2025.00094)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Correlation analysis of the neuroticism factor across models: negative and weak throughout, reaching statistical significance only "for learning in Model D and quality in Model A".

> "The expressed neuroticism is weakly inversely proportional to each parameter, but its only statistically significant correlations are for learning in Model D and quality in Model A."
