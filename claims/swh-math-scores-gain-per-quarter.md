# Students in SWH treatment schools gained about one-tenth of a standard deviation in Mathematics scores per quarter of exposure, erasing an initial disadvantage of roughly 0.4 SD after four semesters

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a cluster randomized study, each semester of SWH exposure was associated with a gain of one-tenth of a standard deviation in Mathematics scores relative to control schools, with a SWH slope coefficient of 0.1065 (p<.01). [→ Hand et al. 2013](#hand-et-al-2013)
`q3 i?` Treatment-school students started with a Mathematics disadvantage of roughly 0.4 of a standard deviation (SWH intercept -0.4096, p<.01), which disappeared after four semesters of exposure. [→ Hand et al. 2013](#hand-et-al-2013)

## Evidence

### Hand et al. 2013

Brian Hand, William Therrien, Mack Shelley. (2013). Examining the impact of using the Science Writing Heuristic approach in learning science: A cluster randomized study. SREE Fall 2013 Conference abstract. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED563211

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Cluster randomized experiment with 24 treatment and 24 control elementary schools; multilevel linear mixed effects model on standardized Iowa Assessments Mathematics scores for 3,801 students tested in 2009-10 and 2011-12. Table 1 reports SWH intercept -0.4096*** and SWH slope 0.1065***, so "students gained one-tenth of a standard deviation" per semester of exposure.

> "the students in schools assigned to teach using the SWH approach had a disadvantage in their test scores of roughly 0.4 of a standard deviation prior to the intervention. However, with each semester of exposure to the SWH approach, students gained one-tenth of a standard deviation against their peers in control schools."
