# High performance in old age is associated with higher education, foreign language use and IQ, but not physical activity or personality

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Old performance groups differed in IQ, education level and frequency of foreign language use, with old high performers highest. [→ Patrick D. Gajewski 2020](#patrick-d-gajewski-2020)
`q3 i?` No differences were found between performance groups in physical activity, BMI or personality dimensions. [→ Patrick D. Gajewski 2020 (2)](#patrick-d-gajewski-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Patrick D. Gajewski 2020

Patrick D. Gajewski, Michael Falkenstein, Sven Th€ones, Edmund Wascher. (2020). Stroop task performance across the lifespan: High cognitive reserve in older age is associated with enhanced proactive and reactive interference control. NeuroImage 207 (2020) 116430. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2019.116430

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

One-way ANOVAs on sociodemographic variables across the three old performance subgroups (n = 152). Groups differed in IQ (F(2,150) = 6.2, p < .005), education (F(2,150) = 4.5, p < .05) and foreign language use (F(2,146) = 5.5, p < .005), with "the most frequent usage in the OH".

> "the performance groups differed with respect to IQ (OL: 111.7 vs. OM: 117.4 vs. OH: 119.6; F(2,150) ¼ 6.2, p < .005). Post-hoc tests showed differences between OH vs. OL ( p < .001) and OM vs. OL, (p < .05), whereas no difference was observed between OH vs. OM ( p > .05)."

### Patrick D. Gajewski 2020 (2)

Patrick D. Gajewski, Michael Falkenstein, Sven Th€ones, Edmund Wascher. (2020). Stroop task performance across the lifespan: High cognitive reserve in older age is associated with enhanced proactive and reactive interference control. NeuroImage 207 (2020) 116430. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2019.116430

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

One-way ANOVAs on lifestyle variables across the three old performance subgroups found "No differences between performance groups were found regarding the current level of physical activity" or BMI, and no significant differences in the NEO-FFI personality dimensions, suggesting these factors did not contribute to performance differences.

> "No differences between performance groups were found regarding the current level of physical activity using the PWC-130 test, or regular physical activity in the last two years measured by the Lüdenscheid Activity Questionnaire or with respect to the Body Mass Index (all F's < 1)"
