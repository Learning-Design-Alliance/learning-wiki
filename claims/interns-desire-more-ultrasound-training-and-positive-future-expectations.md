## Evidence

### Gram P 2024

Gram P, Purup T, Eriksen EM, Søbye S, Wielandt SK, Astorp K, Jensen MB, Andersen CA. (2024). Ultrasound education, competencies and expected future use among medical interns in Denmark: a national cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06510-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive survey results on future expectations: 71.0% anticipated using ultrasound and 73.0% believed it should be a core competence for all interns. Competence gain was reported by 75.2% (medical school) and 55.3% (internship), mainly to lesser/some extent. No effect size is printed.

> "The majority (71.0%, 66.3–75.4) anticipated using ultrasound, 9.2% (6.5–12.4) did not, and 10.4% (7.6–13.8) were unsure. Most believed ultrasound should be a core competence for all interns (yes: 73.0%, 68.3–77.2"

### Gram P 2024 (2)

Gram P, Purup T, Eriksen EM, Søbye S, Wielandt SK, Astorp K, Jensen MB, Andersen CA. (2024). Ultrasound education, competencies and expected future use among medical interns in Denmark: a national cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06510-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the evaluation-of-education results, the efforts interns expected to improve scanning competence were ranked descriptively, led by "ultrasound courses during internships, scanning more patients, and more supervision". Proportions are descriptive; no inferential test is reported.

> "Efforts most frequently expected to improve scanning competence were ultrasound courses during internships, scanning more patients, and more supervision, accounting for 81.4% (77.2–85.1), 75.7% (71.2–79.8), and 61.8% (56.8–66.6), respectively."
