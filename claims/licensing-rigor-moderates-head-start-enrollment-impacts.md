# Head Start's impacts on ECCE enrollment are larger in states with less rigorous child care licensing regulations

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The impact on enrollment in formal ECCE was 31 percentage points in states with less rigorous licensing regulations versus 8 percentage points in states with more rigorous regulations. [→ Morris 2018](#morris-2018)

## Evidence

### Morris 2018

Morris, P. A., Connors, M., Friedman-Krauss, A., McCoy, D. C., Weiland, C., Feller, A., Page, L., Bloom, H., & Yoshikawa, H. (2018). New Findings on Impact Variation From the Head Start Impact Study: Informing the Scale-Up of Early Childhood Programs. AERA Open. https://doi.org/10.1177/2332858418769287

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subgroup multinomial logistic regression analysis (Connors & Friedman-Krauss, 2017; N = 4,385) comparing states with more versus less rigorous child care licensing regulations. Control-group children were 25 percentage points more likely to enroll in formal ECCE in more rigorous states versus 2 percentage points for treatment-group children.

> "The result is a much stronger impact on enrollment in states with less rigorous regulations (an effect size of 31 vs. 8 percentage points)."
