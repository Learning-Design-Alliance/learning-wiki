## Subclaims
`q2 i3` In a cross-sectional multi-group SEM, higher emotional e-regulation goes with fewer habitual digital behaviors for boys and girls (β = −0.39) and both age groups (β = −0.34), and e-self-control of impulsiveness has an equal negative weight by gender (β = −0.35) but a larger one for younger students (β = −0.45 vs. β = −0.24); these are statistical predictors, not causes. ## Evidence

### Ana Cebollero-Salinas 2022

Cebollero-Salinas, A., Cano-Escoriaza, J., & Orejudo, S. (2022). Are emotional e-competencies a protective factor against habitual digital behaviors (media multitasking, cybergossip, phubbing) in Spanish students of secondary education? *Computers & Education, 181*, 104464. [doi:10.1016/j.compedu.2022.104464](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2022.104464)

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Multi-group structural equation model (ADF estimation) on n = 776 students, with the three behaviors as one endogenous factor. The standardised path weights for both competencies are negative (higher competency, fewer habitual digital behaviors); the article describes both as having "a negative influence on habitual digital behaviors" and as protective, their correlations in Table 3 are negative, and the minus signs before each β are lost in the extracted text. E-self-control's predictive weight is smaller in older students, consistent with the authors' interpretation that impulse regulation becomes less discriminating as cognitive regulation matures. The design is cross-sectional, and the authors state it does not allow causal relations to be established.

> "The most important ones are emotional e-regulation, both for boys and for girls (β = −0.39), as well as for the two academic year age groups (β = −0.34), as well as e-regulation of impulsiveness, with the same factorial weight according to gender (β = −0.35), but with a greater relevance for 1st/2 nd year students (β = −0.45) than for 3rd/4th year students (β = −0.24)."
