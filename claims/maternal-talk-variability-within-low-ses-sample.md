## Evidence

### Hurtado Nereyda 2008

Hurtado Nereyda, Marchman Virginia A., Fernald Anne. (2008). Does input influence uptake? Links between maternal talk, processing speed and vocabulary size in Spanish-learning children. Developmental Science. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2898277/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive analysis of maternal speech from 12-minute play sessions at 18 months in the primarily low-SES sample (mean Hollingshead HI = 24.3; 70% in lowest two strata). The article reports children of more talkative mothers "heard on average seven times more words, five times more utterances, three times more different words"; maternal talk was uncorrelated with HI.

> "The children of mothers who talked relatively more heard on average seven times more words, five times more utterances, three times more different words, and sentences that were twice as long as those heard by children of less talkative mothers."
