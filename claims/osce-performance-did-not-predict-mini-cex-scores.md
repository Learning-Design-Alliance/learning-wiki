## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Low and medium OSCE performers received 'overall' mini-CEX scores only slightly lower than high OSCE performers, with confidence intervals including zero (−0.15 and −0.13), so OSCE performance was not a significant predictor. [→ Rogausch A 2015](#rogausch-a-2015)

## Evidence

### Rogausch A 2015

Rogausch A, Beyeler C, Montagne S, Jucker-Kupper P, Berendonk C, Huwendiek S, Gemperli A, Himmel W. (2015). The influence of students' prior clinical skills and context characteristics on mini-CEX scores in clerkships – a multilevel analysis. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0490-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

From the same multilevel regression of clerkship mini-CEX ratings: the contrast "low-performing OSCE group" vs high performers yielded −0.15 (95% CI −0.36 to 0.063) and medium vs high −0.13 (95% CI −0.28 to 0.021); neither interval excludes zero. No standardised effect size is printed.

> "In contrast, 'overall' mini-CEX scores for students from the low-performing OSCE group (or medium performers, respectively) were only slightly lower compared to the high-performing OSCE group (regression coefficient −0.15 [95 %-CI: −0.36–0.063] for low vs. high performers, for medium vs. high performers: −0.13 [95 %-CI: −0.28–0.021]; Table 2)."
