## Evidence

### Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork 2009

Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork. (2009). Making Things Hard on Yourself, But in a Good Way: Creating Desirable Difficulties to Enhance Learning. Psychology and the Real World. https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/research/

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The chapter reports, citing Simon and Bjork (2001), that participants who had learned three keystroke patterns predicted next-day test performance; blocked-practice learners misread good practice performance as evidence of long-term learning, mistaking retrieval strength for storage strength.

> "those given interleaved practice predicted their performance quite closely, whereas those given blocked practice were markedly overconfident (Simon & Bjork, 2001)."
