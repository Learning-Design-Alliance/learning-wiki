## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The article argues many prior studies supporting problem-solving first confounded order with other factors, and where order alone was varied via worked examples, "the example–problem sequence has proved superior to the problem–example sequence". [→ Ashman et al. 2020](#ashman-et-al-2020)

## Evidence

### Ashman et al. 2020

Greg Ashman & Slava Kalyuga & John Sweller. (2020). Problem-solving or Explicit Instruction: Which Should Go First When Element Interactivity Is High? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09500-5

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Narrative review of ordering studies (e.g. Hsu et al. 2015; Leppink et al. 2014; Van Gog et al. 2011) reported by the article as showing the example–problem sequence always superior. Second-hand report; the article also criticises designs varying more than order, such as the Jacobson et al. quasi-experiment with different teachers.

> "In all cases, the example–problem sequence has proved superior to the problem–example sequence, in contradiction of the assumption that problem-solving first is advantageous."
