## Evidence

### Babette Bühler 2024

Babette Bühler, Efe Bozkir, Hannah Deininger, Peter Gerjets, Ulrich Trautwein, and Enkelejda Kasneci. 2024. On Task and in Sync: Examining the Relationship between Gaze Synchrony and Self-Reported Attention During Video Lecture Learning. Proceedings of ACM Symposium on Eye Tracking Research & Applications (ETRA '24). [doi:10.1145/3655604](https://doi.org/10.1145/3655604)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Probe-level linear mixed-effects analysis of MultiMatch scanpath similarity for 84 participants; attentive reports showed "higher MM scanpath similarities" (Estimate = 0.38, p < 0.001), with the strongest subdimension effect for gaze positions (Estimate = 0.42, p < 0.001).

> "attentive learners demonstrated higher MM scanpath similarities (Estimate = 0.38, p < 0.001), indicating a greater degree of gaze synchronization though the effect size suggests these differences, while statistically significant, are modest in magnitude."

### Babette Bühler 2024 (2)

Babette Bühler, Efe Bozkir, Hannah Deininger, Peter Gerjets, Ulrich Trautwein, and Enkelejda Kasneci. 2024. On Task and in Sync: Examining the Relationship between Gaze Synchrony and Self-Reported Attention During Video Lecture Learning. Proceedings of ACM Symposium on Eye Tracking Research & Applications (ETRA '24). [doi:10.1145/3655604](https://doi.org/10.1145/3655604)

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Subdimension analysis of MultiMatch models in the same probe-level mixed-effects analysis found saccade length similarity "did not show a significant difference between attentive and inattentive groups" (Estimate = 0.02, p = 0.825); equivalence was not tested.

> "This finding was consistent across all subdimensions of the MM analysis, as displayed in Table 3, with the exception of saccade length similarity (Estimate = 0.02, p = 0.825), which did not show a significant difference between attentive and inattentive groups."
