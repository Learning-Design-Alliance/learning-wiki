## Evidence

### Ayush Gupta 2010

Ayush Gupta, Brian A. Danielak, Andrew Elby. (2010). Understanding students' difficulties in terms of coupled epistemological and affective dynamics. 40th ASEE/IEEE Frontiers in Education Conference. [doi:10.1109/fie.2010.5673256](https://doi.org/10.1109/fie.2010.5673256)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative case study of one undergraduate engineering student, based on a videotaped semi-structured interview analyzed with discourse, framing, and affective analysis tools. The authors argue that "Judy's deep annoyance at qualitative, conceptual homework problems, stabilizes her epistemological stance" and that disrupting the annoyance disrupts the stance.

> "We will argue that an affective stance, Judy's deep annoyance at qualitative, conceptual homework problems, stabilizes her epistemological stance.   Disrupting the annoyance disrupts the epistemological stance."

### Ayush Gupta 2013

Ayush Gupta, Brian A. Danielak, Andrew Elby. (2013). Toward affect-inclusive models of cognitive dynamics: Coupling epistemological resources and emotions. Pre-print for arxiv.org, under review at Physical Review Special Topics – Physics Education Research. https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.1333

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative case study of one undergraduate, Judy, based on a videotaped semi-structured clinical interview analyzed by the author group using discourse, framing, and affective analysis tools. The authors argue that "an affective stance—Judy’s deep annoyance at qualitative, conceptual homework problems—stabilizes her epistemological stance." No effect sizes are reported.

> "We will argue that an affective stance—Judy’s deep annoyance at qualitative, conceptual homework problems—stabilizes her epistemological stance. Our analysis aims to show how disrupting the affective stance co-occurs with the reversal of the epistemological stance – at least briefly."
