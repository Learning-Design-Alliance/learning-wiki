# Indicator performance differs across EL subgroups: former and late-arriving ELs had stronger ninth-grade GPAs and attendance than never ELs, while long-term ELs without IEPs had much lower GPAs

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Former and late-arriving ELs showed stronger ninth-grade GPAs and attendance than never ELs; long-term ELs without IEPs had much lower GPAs, though 22% of long-term ELs with IEPs had A-B GPAs. [→ Allensworth 2025](#allensworth-2025)
`q2 i?` Active ELs were far more likely to score low or very low on English-language standardized tests, ranging from 63% of late-arriving ELs to 93% of long-term ELs with IEPs. [→ Allensworth 2025 (2)](#allensworth-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Allensworth 2025

Allensworth, E.M., de la Torre, M., Franklin, K., & Xu, J. (2025). Predictive power of grades and other indicators for English Learners. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://edworkingpapers.com/ai25-1241

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive statistics (Table 1, fall 2008–2017 cohorts) on ninth-grade outcomes by EL subgroup. Former and late-arriving ELs had 33% with A-B GPAs versus 29% of never ELs; long-term ELs without IEPs had 14%.

> "Long-term English Learners without IEPs had much lower GPAs than other students (14% had A-B GPA), while 22% of long-term English Learners with an IEP did."

### Allensworth 2025 (2)

Allensworth, E.M., de la Torre, M., Franklin, K., & Xu, J. (2025). Predictive power of grades and other indicators for English Learners. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://edworkingpapers.com/ai25-1241

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive distribution of standardized test scores given in English by subgroup; 63% of late-arriving ELs also scored low or very low. These are descriptive percentages with no inferential tests printed.

> "Seventy-five percent of long-term English Learners without IEPs and 93% of long-term English Learners with IEPs scored in the low or very low range, while 30% of never English Learners and 27% of former English Learners scored in the low or very low range."
