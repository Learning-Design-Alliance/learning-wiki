# Behavior indicators show inconsistent predictive strength for high school graduation: several studies find them strongly predictive, but behavior was not predictive in Chicago above and beyond grades and attendance

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Differences in how behavior data is collected, in behavioral expectations and discipline policies across places, or in study methodologies may explain why behavior predicts dropout more strongly in some places than others. [→ Allensworth 2018](#allensworth-2018)

## Evidence

### Allensworth 2018

Allensworth, E.M., Nagaoka, J., & Johnson, D.W. (2018). High school graduation and college readiness indicator systems: What we know, what we need to know. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://ncs.uchicago.edu/freshman-on-track-toolkit

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review offers candidate explanations for the inconsistency, stating "differences in how behavioral data is collected in different places" or differing discipline policies may cause behavior data to be stronger predictors in some places. These are the authors' interpretations, not tested results.

> "It could be that differences in how behavioral data is collected in different places, or differences in behavioral expectations, discipline policies, or interventions for disciplinary infractions across different places, cause behavior data to be stronger predictors of dropout in some places than in others."
