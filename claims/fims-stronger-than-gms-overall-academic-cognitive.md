# Fine motor skills correlate more strongly with overall academic-cognitive skills (r = .302) than gross motor skills (r = .170) in children aged 3–16

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · quant-synthesis `r2`–`r3` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Across 59 studies measuring both motor skills in the same samples, FiMS showed a larger pooled correlation with overall academic-cognitive skills than GMS (r = .302 vs. r = .170, p < .001). [→ Karle 2026](#karle-2026)

## Evidence

### Karle 2026

Karle, V. L., Suggate, S. P., Winter, R., & Stoeger, H. (2026). When Fine is Large and Gross is Small: A Meta-Analysis of Links Between Fine and Gross Motor Skills with Academic-Cognitive Skills. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10161-4

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Mixed-effects meta-regression of 856 effect sizes from 59 studies (63 samples, 40,806 participants), controlling for study quality. Table 2 prints the pooled correlations: FiMS r = .302 (CI .252–.371) and GMS r = .170 (CI .122–.223), confirming that "FiMS resulted in greater effect sizes than GMS did".

> "As Table 2 shows, FiMS resulted in greater effect sizes than GMS did, a difference confirmed by a post-hoc ANOV A, p < .001."

### Karle 2026 (2)

Karle, V. L., Suggate, S. P., Winter, R., & Stoeger, H. (2026). When Fine is Large and Gross is Small: A Meta-Analysis of Links Between Fine and Gross Motor Skills with Academic-Cognitive Skills. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10161-4

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Discussion reports the pooled correlations: "FiMS showed an association of r = .302, and GMS of r = .170"; the authors interpret r = .30 as large and r = .17 as small by Gignac and Szodorai's empirically derived benchmarks.

> "First, FiMS showed an association of r = .302, and GMS of r = .170, with overall academic-cognitive skills."
