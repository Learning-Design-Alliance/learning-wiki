## Evidence

### Özsaban Aysel 2026

Özsaban Aysel, Bayram Aysun, Kılıç Çiğdem Torun, Calp Muhammed Hanefi, Öztürk Havva. (2026). The effect of scenario-based range of motion exercises animation on the knowledge, motivation, and attitude levels of nursing students: a randomized controlled study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08716-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled experiment with 104 first-year nursing students (52 per group) comparing a scenario-based animation video to a routine skill video. The study reports "did not show a statistically significant difference" between groups on knowledge, while within-group post-test gains were significant (p = 0.001).

> "The ROM exercises knowledge scores of the students in the experimental group compared to the control group did not show a statistically significant difference in the pre-test and post-test (p > 0.05). Post-test ROM exercises knowledge scores were statistically significantly higher than the pre-test within both groups (p = 0.001)"

### Özsaban Aysel 2026 (2)

Özsaban Aysel, Bayram Aysun, Kılıç Çiğdem Torun, Calp Muhammed Hanefi, Öztürk Havva. (2026). The effect of scenario-based range of motion exercises animation on the knowledge, motivation, and attitude levels of nursing students: a randomized controlled study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08716-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within both groups of the same randomized experiment, Wilcoxon comparisons showed post-test knowledge significantly above pre-test (p = 0.001 for each group), indicating both materials were instructive for ROM exercises.

> "Post-test ROM exercises knowledge scores were statistically significantly higher than the pre-test within both groups (p = 0.001)"
