# Combining reading, writing and spelling scores still leaves immense grade overlap: on average 33.2% of pupils reach the next grade's range and 35.3% fall below their own grade's range

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When equal-valued average scores for reading, writing and spelling were computed for 401 pupils, overlapping was somewhat less than for single subjects but still immense: an average of 33.2% of pupils in each grade reach or exceed the lower limit of the next grade, and 35.3% fall below the upper limit of their own grade. [→ Starch 1910](#starch-1910)

## Evidence

### Starch 1910

Starch, D. (1910). The Measurement of Efficiency in Spelling, and the Overlapping of Grades in Combined Measurements of Reading, Writing and Spelling. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=The+Measurement+of+Efficiency+in+Spelling

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Combined-measure analysis of 401 pupils: each pupil's reading speed and comprehension, writing speed and quality, and spelling score were reduced to a common scale assuming the grade-1-to-8 growth in each was of equal value, then plotted as per-grade distributions (Figure 3). The third-grade column includes pupils from grades 1 to 6.

> "A comparison of these curves reveals still an immense amount of overlapping. It is somewhat less than the overlapping of the performances in each subject separately, but it is not as much less as most teachers would anticipate."
