## Evidence

### Nan Li 2013

Nan Li, William W. Cohen, Kenneth R. Koedinger. (2013). Problem Order Implications for Learning. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-013-0005-5

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Simulation analysis of negative feedback (average number of times SimStudent received negative feedback per skill, shown in Fig. 8): interleaved ordering produced significantly more negative feedback in stoichiometry (p<0.05, 31.5%) and 10.0% more in fraction addition. The article argues negative feedback serves as negative training examples for when-learning.

> "the SimStudent given interleaved-o rdering problems receives significantly (p< 0.05, 31.5 %) more negative feedback than the SimStudent trained on blocked- ordering problems in stoichiometry, and 10.0 % more negative feedback in fraction addition."

### Nan Li 2013 (2)

Nan Li, William W. Cohen, Kenneth R. Koedinger. (2013). Problem Order Implications for Learning. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-013-0005-5

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Simulation analysis in equation solving: negative feedback amounts were approximately equal across order conditions (p = 1, −1.9%), yet the article shows negative examples from other problem types in interleaving were sometimes more informative, speeding skill acquisition.

> "In equation solving, SimStudent received approximately the same amount of neg- ative feedback (p = 1, −1.9 %) in the blocked problem order case and interleaved problem order case."
