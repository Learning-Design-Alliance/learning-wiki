# EWIMS did not have a detectable impact on school data culture, though the overall scale effect size (Hedges' g = 0.27) favored EWIMS

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Differences between EWIMS and control schools on the overall school data culture scale and its subscales were not statistically significant, but the overall effect size was Hedges' g = 0.27. [→ Faria 2017](#faria-2017)

## Evidence

### Faria 2017

Faria, A.-M., Sorensen, N., Heppen, J., Bowdon, J., Taylor, S., Eisner, R., & Foster, S. (2017). Getting students on track for graduation: Impacts of the Early Warning Intervention and Monitoring System after one year (REL 2017–272). Washington, DC: U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Regional Educational Laboratory Midwest. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory school-level analysis using the 2015 end-of-year school leader survey (66 schools). The printed effect size was Hedges' g = 0.27 for overall data culture; the quote states the effect size "(Hedges' g) for the overall school data culture scale was 0.27". Subscale differences were not statistically significant at p < .05.

> "However, the effect size (Hedges' g) for the overall school data culture scale was 0.27, suggesting that although not statistically significant, EWIMS schools reported modestly higher data culture than control schools"
