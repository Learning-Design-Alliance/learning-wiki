## Evidence

### Eric Matthew Hickok 2016

Eric Matthew Hickok. (2016). Teaching practices of graduate teaching assistants. San José State University Master's Project. Originally published 2015. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07740](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07740)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Normalized comparison of SJSU Physics 50 and 50W observations against West et al.'s UC Davis CLASP RIOT data (Fig. 5.1), normalized by total time in each mode since observation lengths differed. No effect sizes are printed.

> "Looking at “Small Group” time, it is clear that for both of the SJSU courses, the percentage of time spent Not Interacting is much higher than the Davis course."

### Eric Matthew Hickok 2016 (2)

Eric Matthew Hickok. (2016). Teaching practices of graduate teaching assistants. San José State University Master's Project. Originally published 2015. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07740](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07740)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparison of whole-class and small-group interaction distributions (Fig. 5.2) showing student-to-student interactions present in CLASP but absent or rare in both SJSU courses. No effect sizes are printed.

> "Students in both SJSU courses were never provided the opportunity to Share their Ideas , leading the conversation to collectively challenge their own ideas regarding physics concepts. They also never Presented their lab ﬁndings and rarely Presented to each other or Talked Serially to build each others’ ideas."

### Eric Matthew Hickok 2016 (3)

Eric Matthew Hickok. (2016). Teaching practices of graduate teaching assistants. San José State University Master's Project. Originally published 2015. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07740](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07740)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The author's interpretive conclusion from the UC Davis comparison, noting SJSU students are rarely encouraged to communicate ideas through discussion despite being engaged in prescribed activities. This is the author's assessment, not a tested result.

> "It seems fair to conclude that neither of the SJSU courses as they currently stand could fairly be classiﬁed as interactive courses."
