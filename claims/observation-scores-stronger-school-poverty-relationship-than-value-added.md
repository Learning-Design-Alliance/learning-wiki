# Observation scores have a stronger relationship with school poverty than value-added scores

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Differences in observation scores by school poverty level are more pronounced than differences in value-added scores, with a moderate negative correlation between observation scores and school economic disadvantage. [→ Jiang 2016](#jiang-2016)

## Evidence

### Jiang 2016

Jiang, J. Y., & Sporte, S. E. (2016). Teacher Evaluation in Chicago: Differences in Observation and Value-Added Scores by Teacher, Student, and School Characteristics. UChicago Consortium on School Research. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED562786

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive correlational analysis of 2013-14 CPS observation scores aggregated to the school level, finding a "moderate negative correlation (-0.33) between observation scores and the concentration of economically disadvantaged students in schools." Value-added differences by poverty are much smaller because value-added controls for student characteristics.

> "There is a moderate negative correlation (-0.33) between observation scores and the concentration of economically disadvantaged students in schools."
