## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The rotation-based transience of residents contrasted with stable AHCP roles, observable behavior shifts under continuous evaluation, and a persistent physician–AHCP divide act as barriers to MSF. [→ Yama Brie A. 2018](#yama-brie-a-2018)

## Evidence

### Yama Brie A. 2018

Yama Brie A., Hodgins Michael, Boydell Katherine, Schwartz Sarah B. (2018). A qualitative exploration: questioning multisource feedback in residency education. BMC Medical Education 18. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1270-7

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

From the Results' ward culture theme: an AHCP described frustration that new residents arrive yearly while AHCPs remain, and noted residents display a different attitude when the attending is on the ward.

> "Residents are trainees with temporally transient roles that contrast the consistent roles of AHCPs. This cultural dynamic, superimposed on the existing hierarchy, has the potential to create tensions affecting feedback."

### Yama Brie A. 2018 (2)

Yama Brie A., Hodgins Michael, Boydell Katherine, Schwartz Sarah B. (2018). A qualitative exploration: questioning multisource feedback in residency education. BMC Medical Education 18. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1270-7

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

From the Results: a junior resident reported the only negative feedback received went to chief residents rather than to the resident; the Discussion adds that informal information from across North America confirmed MSF can negatively impact relationships.

> "This separation, coupled with a lack of formal procedures for providing regular feedback, represents a barrier to feedback provision."
