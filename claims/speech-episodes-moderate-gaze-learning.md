# Speech episodes strengthen the relation between gaze similarity and posttest scores in both poster and game phases

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` During the poster phase, gaze similarity is higher for speech than no-speech segments (F (1,16) = 15.60, p = .001, d = 1.86), and the gaze similarity–posttest correlation is higher for speech segments (F (1,16) = 10.27, p = .003, d = 1.51). [→ Sharma Kshitij 2020](#sharma-kshitij-2020)
`q2 i3` During the game phase, the same interaction pattern holds for the second posttest (interaction F (1,16) = 11.96, p = .001, d = 1.63). [→ Sharma Kshitij 2020 (2)](#sharma-kshitij-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Sharma Kshitij 2020

Sharma Kshitij, Leftheriotis Ioannis, Giannakos Michail. (2020). Utilizing Interactive Surfaces to Enhance Learning, Collaboration and Engagement: Insights from Learners’ Gaze and Speech. Sensors (Basel). https://doi.org/10.3390/s20071964

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

ANCOVA with speech segment type (speech versus no-speech) as covariate, poster phase, 18 dyads. Two single effects and an interaction effect were observed on gaze similarity; the printed effect size for the speech-segment difference is "d = 1.86".

> "the gaze similarity is significantly higher for the speech segments than the gaze similarity for the no speech segments (F (1,16) = 15.60, p = .001, d = 1.86). Furthermore, the correlation between the gaze similarity and the average first posttest score is higher for the speech segments"

### Sharma Kshitij 2020 (2)

Sharma Kshitij, Leftheriotis Ioannis, Giannakos Michail. (2020). Utilizing Interactive Surfaces to Enhance Learning, Collaboration and Engagement: Insights from Learners’ Gaze and Speech. Sensors (Basel). https://doi.org/10.3390/s20071964

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

ANCOVA during the game phase with speech versus no-speech segments as covariate; the printed effect size for the interaction is "d = 1.63". Gaze similarity was also higher for speech segments (F (1,16) = 12.26, p = .001, d = 1.65).

> "the correlation between the gaze similarity and the average second posttest score is higher for the speech segments than that for the no speech segments (F (1,16) = 11.96, p = .001, d = 1.63, Figure 12b)"
