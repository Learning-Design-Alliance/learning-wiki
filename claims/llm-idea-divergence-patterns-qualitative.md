## Evidence

### Nguyen 2026

Nguyen, H., & Cao, J. (2026). Exploring the Capacity of Large Language Models to Simulate Students' Scientific Thinking: Insights for Responsive Teaching. Journal of Science Education and Technology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10956-026-10333-5

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Qualitative comparison of LLM-generated and lesson ideas, with GPT-5-assisted summaries verified by two researchers through two rounds of iterative coding. In an ecosystems lesson, LLMs gave generic ideas like "careless human activities" where lesson ideas cited local dams and gardening.

> "Although the generated ideas generally overlapped with the targeted concepts in the lesson ideas, we observed that LLM ideas showed less specific reasoning grounded in lesson contexts, evidence, and mechanisms (60.54%; see Table 2 for range across LLMs)."

### Nguyen 2026 (2)

Nguyen, H., & Cao, J. (2026). Exploring the Capacity of Large Language Models to Simulate Students' Scientific Thinking: Insights for Responsive Teaching. Journal of Science Education and Technology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10956-026-10333-5

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Table 2 reports divergence-theme percentages across LLMs (n = 294 clusters): broad reasoning 60.54%, misconception substance 17.69%, technical vocabulary 35.71%, less uncertainty 14.63%, analogy use 7.48%. Lesson ideas showed markers like "it seems like", "maybe" more often.

> "First, some LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, GPT-5-mini) produced longer sentences with more technical vocabulary than the lesson ideas (35.71%)."
