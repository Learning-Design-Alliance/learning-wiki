## Evidence

### Rice 1973

Rice, W. K., Jr. (1973). Effects of Discipline Techniques on Children's Personality-Trait Inferences. Ph.D. dissertation, The University of Oklahoma. University Microfilms No. 74-12,322. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Effects+of+Discipline+Techniques+on+Children%27s+Personality-Trait+Inferences

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Omnibus MANOVA with two factors, sex of rater and type of desist, on the four dependent measures; the article reports the desist main effect significant at U = .57591, p < .001, with sex and interaction effects non-significant (p > .10).

> "The Ss who listened to the task-focused desists differed significantly in their responses on the measures from the group that heard the approval-focused desists. Neither the sex of rater main effect nor the Sex of rater x Type of de­sist interaction was significant"

### Rice 1973 (2)

Rice, W. K., Jr. (1973). Effects of Discipline Techniques on Children's Personality-Trait Inferences. Ph.D. dissertation, The University of Oklahoma. University Microfilms No. 74-12,322. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Effects+of+Discipline+Techniques+on+Children%27s+Personality-Trait+Inferences

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Follow-up discriminant analysis on the same experimental data: "the PTC and PTT scores accounted for 97.6# of the total dispersion", with the confidence ratings (CRC and CRT) accounting for only a small proportion (2.4%).

> "A discriminant analysis revealed that the PTC and PTT scores accounted for 97.6# of the total dispersion."
