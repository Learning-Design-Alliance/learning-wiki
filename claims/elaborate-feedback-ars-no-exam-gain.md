# Adding elaborate spoken feedback to ARS questions in lectures does not significantly improve final exam performance compared with ARS questions without feedback

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2013 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Dental students who received elaborate immediate feedback after ARS questions scored no significantly higher on a 20-item final exam than controls who saw only the correct answer (ANCOVA p=0,362; T-test p=0,873). [→ Rahman Alexander 2013](#rahman-alexander-2013)

## Evidence

### Rahman Alexander 2013

Rahman Alexander, Jacker-Guhr Silke, Staufenbiel Ingmar, Meyer Karen, Zupanic Michaela, Hahnemann Merle, Lührs Anne-Katrin, Eberhard Jörg. (2013). Use of elaborate feedback and an audience-response-system in dental education. GMS Zeitschrift für Medizinische Ausbildung 30(3), Doc35. [doi:10.3205/zma000878](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma000878)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled study: 74 first-clinical-semester dental students randomized by age, sex and Physikum performance; study group (37) averaged 14.49 points, control group (36) 14.42 points on the 20-item final exam. The article reports "no significant difference" in both primary ANCOVA and secondary T-test (p=0,873).

> "The results of the final exam showed no significant difference between the study and control group (p=0,362) even with the application of a covariance analysis and taking the co-variable "score on the preliminary medical examination" into consideration."
