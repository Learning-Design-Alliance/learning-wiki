# Early College shows suggestive but not credible causal evidence of a positive effect on English Learners' on-time high school graduation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` DiD estimates show a significant 11.2 percentage point increase in on-time graduation, but event-study lead-year deviations caution interpretation. [→ Johnson 2020](#johnson-2020)

## Evidence

### Johnson 2020

Johnson, Angela, and Diana Mercado-Garcia. (2020). The Effects of Early College Opportunities on English Learners. (EdWorkingPaper: 20-232). Annenberg Institute at Brown University. https://edworkingpapers.com/ai20-232

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

DiD estimate on State of California on-time graduation codes among 15,090 ever-EL students; coefficient 0.112 (SE 0.031). Authors state there is only "suggestive evidence" and no "credible causal evidence" for a positive graduation impact.

> "The effect on on-time graduation is a significant 11.2 percentage point increase (column 2)."

### Johnson 2020 (2)

Johnson, Angela, and Diana Mercado-Garcia. (2020). The Effects of Early College Opportunities on English Learners. (EdWorkingPaper: 20-232). Annenberg Institute at Brown University. https://edworkingpapers.com/ai20-232

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Event-study estimates (Table 4) showed significant pre-program lead-year-3 coefficients for graduation and immediate enrollment, violating parallel trends in that year; similar estimates appeared in restricted smaller-school samples (Appendix Tables A1 and A2).

> "For high school graduation and immediate college enrollment, the significant estimates for lead year 3 suggest some deviation from the common trends in that year."
