## Evidence

### Nathan Hagen 2024

Nathan Hagen. (2024). Graduate education in optics in Japan and the United States: impact of funding levels on educational structure. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2401.08933](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2401.08933)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

The article's analogical argument compares optics for physics students to circuit design for physics students: both are useful but difficult as secondary subjects, and learning optical design well requires years of study outside a supportive peer cohort.

> "These physics students would not be surrounded by colleagues pursuing similar studies, as they would if they were studying in an optical design curriculum. As a result, reaching the same level of skill in optical design would take them more time than if they could consult with teachers, col- leagues, and friends at difficult points."

### Nathan Hagen 2024 (2)

Nathan Hagen. (2024). Graduate education in optics in Japan and the United States: impact of funding levels on educational structure. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2401.08933](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2401.08933)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The author argues the scale objection to independent optics departments is refuted by the track record of the named US centers, whose graduates companies actively recruit for their optical systems understanding and design ability.

> "The successes of OSC in Arizona, the Institute of Op- tics in Rochester, and CREOL in Florida, have demonstrated that this is not the case. Companies actively seek out grad- uates from these schools in order to take advantage of their understanding of optical systems and ability to do optical design."
