# The mean of the array of scores on one scale associated with a given score on another fallible measure tends to be nearer the overall mean than the truly corresponding value is, so the regression line does not show true correspondence

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` For imperfectly correlated measures of the same trait, the mean of every array of y values associated with a single value of x is nearer to the mean of all y values than is the value of y that truly corresponds to that x. [→ Arthur S. Otis 1922](#arthur-s-otis-1922)

`q2 i?` The regression line indicates the theoretical mean of associated second-form scores for a first-form score, including the error in that first score, so it does not show the correspondence between true scores. [→ Arthur S. Otis 1922](#arthur-s-otis-1922)

## Evidence

### Arthur S. Otis 1922

Arthur S. Otis. (1922). The Method for Finding the Correspondence Between Scores in Two Tests. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0075112

`q2 · i?`

A hypothetical thermometry demonstration with deliberate reading errors: two readers misread one scale half the time, one-fourth too high and one-fourth too low, and the plotted array means, in the article's words, "tend to be nearer to the mean" than the true corresponding values. No empirical data are involved.

> "The mean value of Centigrade readings found associated with each of the Fahrenheit readings tend to be nearer to the mean (10°) of all the Centigrade readings than are the Centigrade values to which these Fahrenheit readings correspond."

### Arthur S. Otis 1922

Arthur S. Otis. (1922). The Method for Finding the Correspondence Between Scores in Two Tests. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0075112

`q2 · i?`

Theoretical argument from a mental-testing illustration: a Grade XII pupil scoring 100 on Form A would most probably make 110 on Form B, while a Grade V pupil scoring 100 would most probably make 90, because each first score is, per the article, "most probably in error" toward the group mean.

> "The regression line therefore shows the most probable true score in a second test which a pupil would obtain who made a given score in a first test, the score in the first test being in error. The regression line therefore does not show the true correspondence between true scores in both scales."
