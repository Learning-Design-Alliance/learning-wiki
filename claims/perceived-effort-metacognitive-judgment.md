# Perceived effort ratings are metacognitive judgments based on heuristic cue processing and prone to bias

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Ratings of experienced effort are a type of metacognitive judgment resulting from heuristic processing and therefore prone to biases, inferred from cues such as effort beliefs, prior task experience, fatigue, interest, and processing fluency. ## Evidence

### Anique B. H. de Bruin 2023

Anique B. H. de Bruin, Felicitas Biwer, Luotong Hui, Erdem Onan, Louise David, Wisnu Wiradhany. (2023). Worth the Effort: the Start and Stick to Desirable Difficulties (S2D2) Framework. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09766-w

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Conceptual review claim, attributed to the recent review by Scheiter et al. (2020): self-reports of effort on Likert-type scales "should not be viewed as objective reflections of effort based solely on the difficulty of a task" but are inferred from cues of varying validity.

> "ratings of experienced effort are in fact a type of metacognitive judgment that result from heuristic processing and are therefore prone to biases"
