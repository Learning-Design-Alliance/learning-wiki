# Post-testing yields significantly better retention than pre-testing when total retrieval-practice time is held comparable (position effect)

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` The reading-quiz group outperformed the quiz-reading group on trained questions (d = 0.34) and on new untrained questions (d = 0.35). [→ Latimier 2019](#latimier-2019)

## Evidence

### Latimier 2019

Latimier, A., Riegert, A., Peyre, H., Ly, S. T., Casati, R. & Ramus, F. (2019). Does pre-testing promote better retention than post-testing? npj Science of Learning 4:15. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0053-1

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Direct comparison of the two quiz conditions in the 285-participant experiment: Table 4 reports reading-quiz vs. quiz-reading on trained questions "d = 0.34 [0.05, 0.63]", p = 0.019, and on new questions d = 0.35, p = 0.017.

> "planned t-test comparisons showed a post-testing effect, a pre-testing effect, and most interestingly, a position effect: retention was signiﬁcantly higher for the reading-quiz group compared with the quiz-reading group"
