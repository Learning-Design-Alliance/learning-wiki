# Year 5 pupils taught ReflectED made an average of four months' additional progress in maths compared to control pupils, but the difference was not statistically significant

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` In a class-randomised controlled trial of 1858 pupils in 30 schools, the ReflectED intervention group showed a moderate effect size of 0.30 (95% CI -0.04 to 0.63) on age-standardised InCAS mathematics scores, which was not statistically significant. [→ Motteram et al. 2016](#motteram-et-al-2016)

## Evidence

### Motteram et al. 2016

Gary Motteram, Sophina Choudry, Afroditi Kalambouka, Graeme Hutcheson and Amanda Barton. (2016). ReflectED: Evaluation report and executive summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/public/files/Projects/EEF_Project_Protocol_ReflectED.pdf

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Class-randomised controlled trial (38 intervention classes, 32 control classes) using CEM InCAS age-standardised mathematics scores with a two-level multi-level model. The complete-case Hedges g was 0.30 (-0.04, 0.63, p = 0.08) and the imputed analysis gave "0.27"; the report concludes there is "no statistically significant difference".

> "It is also evident that there is no statistically significant difference between students who have received the ReflectED treatment and students who have not. The intervention effect size is moderate, (i.e. 0.3 with the confidence interval -0.04 to 0.63)."
