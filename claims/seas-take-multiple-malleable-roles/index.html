Evidence¶

Dunn 2019¶

Dunn, L., & Ambroso, E. (2019). Balancing act: State and district roles in school improvement under ESSA. San Francisco, CA: WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED602966

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Qualitative interviews in 2018–19 with school improvement leads from 10 SEAs (e.g., Arizona, Alabama, Vermont). Interviewees described "shifting roles based on identified needs, capacity, and context considerations at the LEA and school levels."

"all interviewees described the state's role as largely malleable in order to address specific LEA and school needs. In particular, each SEA described ways they served in the roles of SEA Resource and SEA Oversight."

Related Claims¶

  • An analysis of 23 state ESSA plans identified four main categories of SEA and LEA roles in school improvement: LEA Independence, SEA Oversight, SEA Resource, and SEA–LEA Collaboration. — related
  • The SEA-as-resource-provider role was the most commonly emphasized approach, named in 10 of 23 state ESSA plans. — related
  • SEA staff describe their role as providing both support and accountability to LEAs, and describe struggling to balance the two. — related

  • SEAs are leveraging ESSA requirements and increased flexibility to specify SEA and LEA roles and push evidence-based, locally contextualized improvement strategies. — related
