## Evidence

### Jonathan D. Perry 2025

Jonathan D. Perry, Toni Sauncy, Susan White, Rachel L Ivie, John Tyler, and Tatiana Erukhimova. (2025). Impact of Outreach on Physics Student Development: Quantitative Results from a National Survey. [doi:10.1103/mj85-87vk](https://doi.org/10.1103/mj85-87vk)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression on the national survey with participation in outreach as the outcome; seniors were the most likely to have participated. Sophomores and juniors were also less likely but not at the study's significance level.

> "From the regression models, first year students were five times less likely (p <0.01) to have participated in outreach."

### Jonathan D. Perry 2025 (2)

Jonathan D. Perry, Toni Sauncy, Susan White, Rachel L Ivie, John Tyler, and Tatiana Erukhimova. (2025). Impact of Outreach on Physics Student Development: Quantitative Results from a National Survey. [doi:10.1103/mj85-87vk](https://doi.org/10.1103/mj85-87vk)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Null results from the same regression models: no significant participation differences by gender, race/ethnicity, international status, first-generation status, or LGBTQIA+ identity. The article cautions the data are insufficient to call outreach a great equalizer.

> "Students who did not identify as men were no more or less likely than men to have participated in outreach. The same lack of difference is also observed for non-white respondents compared to white respondents, international compared to domestic students"
