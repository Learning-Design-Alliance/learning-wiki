# Teachers of color leave schools or the profession at higher annual rates than White teachers, driven largely by school moving

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teachers of color move schools or leave the profession at a higher annual rate than White teachers (19% versus 15%), with similar leaver rates but much higher and widening mover rates. [→ Carver-Thomas 2018](#carver-thomas-2018)
`q2 i?` The turnover gap between teachers of color and White teachers disappears when comparing schools with the same proportion of students of color. [→ Carver-Thomas 2018 (2)](#carver-thomas-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Carver-Thomas 2018

Carver-Thomas, D. (2018). Diversifying the teaching profession: How to recruit and retain teachers of color. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/diversifying-teaching-profession

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of the federal Schools and Staffing Survey 2011–12 and Teacher Follow-up Survey (Figures 3 and 4) reported in the retention section. The report states mover rates for teachers of color rose from 7% to 10% between 1992 and 2013, widening the gap with White teachers.

> "Teachers of color move schools or leave the profession at a higher annual rate than do White teachers (19% versus 15%). While teachers of color and White teachers leave the workforce at similar rates over time, mover rates (transferring from one school to another) over time are much higher for teachers of color."

### Carver-Thomas 2018 (2)

Carver-Thomas, D. (2018). Diversifying the teaching profession: How to recruit and retain teachers of color. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/diversifying-teaching-profession

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The report states this moderation in the retention section: the overall turnover difference does not hold across school types, and because teachers of color tend to work in schools with higher concentrations of students of color, they face higher-turnover environments.

> "When teachers of color and White teachers work in schools with the same proportion of students of color, their turnover rates are statistically indistinguishable."
