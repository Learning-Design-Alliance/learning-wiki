# Exam-score associations with sleep parameters are pronounced on weekdays but absent on weekends

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Final exam score correlated significantly with weekday SE (r = 0.635) and weekday SOL (r = -0.463), and with weekday MADs of SE, SOL, and TST, but showed no significant weekend correlations except weekend TST MAD. [→ Lin Chen-Ta 2025](#lin-chen-ta-2025)

## Evidence

### Lin Chen-Ta 2025

Lin Chen-Ta, Liang Sheng-Fu & Shaw Fu-Zen. (2025). Better sleep is associated with higher academic performance from an actigraphy-based analysis of sleep consistency and grades in college students. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-33775-0

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Weekday/weekend correlation analysis (Table 4) in the same 33-participant sample. Weekday SE r = 0.635 (p < 0.001) and weekday SOL r = -0.463 (p = 0.006); "no such relationship was found for weekend SOL" and weekend SE was non-significant (r = 0.051).

> "The final exam score exhibited a significant positive correlation with weekday SE but not with weekend SE. A significant negative correlation was observed between final exam scores and weekday SOL, while no such relationship was found for weekend SOL."
