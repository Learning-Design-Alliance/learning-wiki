Evidence¶

Casey L. Roark and Bharath Chandrasekaran 2023¶

Casey L. Roark and Bharath Chandrasekaran. (2023). Stable, flexible, common, and distinct behaviors support rule-based and information-integration category learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00163-0

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Regression of success on learning speed (trials to reach group median, inverted) in both tasks and experiments; the article prints only unstandardized regression coefficients, so no standardized effect size is reported. The speed×task interaction was not significant (Exp 1: β = −0.017, p = 0.40; Exp 2: β = 0.014, p = 0.30).

"For both RB and II tasks and in both experiments, learning speed was positively related to learning success (Fig. 9a, d; Experiment 1: βspeed = 0.084, SE = 0.016, p < 0.0001; Experiment 2: βspeed = 0.046, SE = 0.010, p < 0.0001)"

Casey L. Roark and Bharath Chandrasekaran 2023 (2)¶

Casey L. Roark and Bharath Chandrasekaran. (2023). Stable, flexible, common, and distinct behaviors support rule-based and information-integration category learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00163-0

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Regression of success on OSPAN working memory score in both tasks; the article prints only unstandardized β coefficients, so no standardized effect size is reported. WM×task interactions were not significant (Exp 1: p = 0.25; Exp 2: p = 0.63).

"For both RB and II categories and in both experiments, WM capacity was positively related to learning success (Fig. 9c, f; Experiment 1: βWM = 0.34, SE = 0.069, p < 0.0001; Experiment 2: βWM = 0.19, SE = 0.069, p = 0.0067)"

Casey L. Roark and Bharath Chandrasekaran 2023 (3)¶

Casey L. Roark and Bharath Chandrasekaran. (2023). Stable, flexible, common, and distinct behaviors support rule-based and information-integration category learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00163-0

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Regression of success on learning stability (longest correct streak normalized by total correct responses) in both tasks; no statistically significant relationship in either experiment and no significant task difference, but equivalence was not tested and no standardized effect size is printed.

"For both RB and II tasks and in both experiments, learning stability was unrelated to learning success (Fig. 9b, e; Experiment 1: βstability = 6.12, SE = 14.7, p = 0.68; Experiment 2: βstability = −6.93, SE = 10.1, p = 0.50) and was not signi ﬁcantly different across RB and II tasks"
