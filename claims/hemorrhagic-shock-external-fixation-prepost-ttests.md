## Evidence

### Mogali Sreenivasulu Reddy 2025

Mogali Sreenivasulu Reddy, Yan Qi, Jiang Nan, Teng Peng, Xu Youran, Qian Kaoliang, Lu Ming, Hu Jun. (2025). Case-Based Virtual Reality Simulation for Severe Pelvic Trauma Clinical Skill Training in Medical Students: Design and Pilot Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/59850

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Paired 2-tailed t tests in the pilot (n=40 analyzed, df=39). Table 4 prints presimulation 39.7% (SEM 0.9) vs postsimulation 85.8% (SEM 0.8) for hemorrhagic shock, mean difference 46.1 (95% CI 43.6-48.6), t=37.6, Cohen d=8.5, P=.001.

> "Notably, there was a significant improvement in the overall scores for managing hemorrhagic shock (t39=37.6; 95% CI 43.6-48.6; P<.001) and performing external fixation of pelvic fractures (t39=24.1; 95% CI 53.4-63.3; P<.001) from pre- to postsimulation."

### Mogali Sreenivasulu Reddy 2025 (2)

Mogali Sreenivasulu Reddy, Yan Qi, Jiang Nan, Teng Peng, Xu Youran, Qian Kaoliang, Lu Ming, Hu Jun. (2025). Case-Based Virtual Reality Simulation for Severe Pelvic Trauma Clinical Skill Training in Medical Students: Design and Pilot Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/59850

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Discussion of the same pilot's paired analyses: Table 4 prints external fixation rising from 32.7% (SEM 2.3) to 91.1% (SEM 0.7), mean difference 58.4 (95% CI 53.4-63.3), t=24.1, Cohen d=5.3, P=.001. The authors add that external fixation scores "were not the highest due to the inherent complexity."

> "Quantitative outcome analyses revealed an overall improvement in pass rates at crucial assessment points posttraining with the digital simulation system, with external fixation of pelvic fractures displaying the most significant enhancement."
