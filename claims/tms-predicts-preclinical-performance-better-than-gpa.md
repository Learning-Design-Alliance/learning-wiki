# In both best and mediocre school leavers, the TMS predicted pre-clinical academic performance better than the school-leaving GPA (TMS β=0.442-0.446 vs GPA β=0.238-0.047)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among best school leavers (grade 1.0), the TMS score was a stronger regression predictor of mean pre-clinical examination results than the school-leaving GPA (β=0.442 vs β=0.238). [→ Kadmon Guni 2016](#kadmon-guni-2016)
`q2 i?` Among mediocre school leavers (grades 2.0-2.3), the school-leaving GPA showed no association with pre-clinical grades (β=0.047, ns) while the TMS predicted performance (β=0.446). [→ Kadmon Guni 2016 (2)](#kadmon-guni-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Kadmon Guni 2016

Kadmon Guni, Kadmon Martina. (2016). Academic Performance of Students with the Highest and Mediocre School-leaving Grades: Does the Aptitude Test for Medical Studies (TMS) Balance Their Prognoses? GMS Journal for Medical Education 33(1):Doc7. [doi:10.3205/zma001006](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001006)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple linear regression analysis of the best school leavers (grade 1.0) across four cohorts showed both predictors contributed, but "the predictive contribution of the school-leaving GPA (β=0.238) was weaker than the predictive value of the TMS (β=0.442".

> "However, the predictive contribution of the school-leaving GPA (β=0.238) was weaker than the predictive value of the TMS (β=0.442; see Table 2 (Tab. 2), Point A)."

### Kadmon Guni 2016 (2)

Kadmon Guni, Kadmon Martina. (2016). Academic Performance of Students with the Highest and Mediocre School-leaving Grades: Does the Aptitude Test for Medical Studies (TMS) Balance Their Prognoses? GMS Journal for Medical Education 33(1):Doc7. [doi:10.3205/zma001006](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001006)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Regression analysis of the mediocre school leavers (grades 2.0-2.3) found no GPA association (β=0.047, ns) while TMS scores predicted performance (β=0.446); the authors note the restricted TMS range made the regression "possibly more strongly susceptible to outliers".

> "Multiple linear regression analysis did not reveal an association between the school-leaving GPAs of the mediocre school leavers and their mean pre-clinical examination grades (β=0.047, ns). In contrast, their TMS scores appeared to predict their pre-clinical performance similarly to the group of the best school leavers (β=0.446; see Table 4 (Tab. 4), Point A)."
