# VPS and IPS produce no significant difference in overall sepsis resuscitation checklist performance among novices at delayed assessment

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Mean sepsis checklist scores did not differ significantly between VPS and IPS groups (difference 2.0; 95% CI, -1.4 to 5.4; Cohen's d=0.38). [→ Low Matthew Jian Wen 2024](#low-matthew-jian-wen-2024)

## Evidence

### Low Matthew Jian Wen 2024

Low Matthew Jian Wen, Chan Gene Wai Han, Li Zisheng, Koh Yiwen, Jen Chi Loong, Lee Zi Yao, Cheng Lenard Tai Win. (2024). Comparison of virtual and in-person simulations for sepsis and trauma resuscitation training in Singapore: a randomized controlled trial. Journal of Educational Evaluation for Health Professions. https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2024.21.33

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial in one Singapore emergency department: 19 VPS versus 21 IPS junior doctors, assessed on a different sepsis scenario with blinded raters after a median of over 40 days. Means "were not significantly different (2.0; 95% CI, -1.4 to 5.4), with a Cohen’s d of 0.38".

> "The mean±SD sepsis checklist scores for VPS (38.0±4.4) and IPS (36.0±6.1) were not significantly different (2.0; 95% CI, -1.4 to 5.4), with a Cohen’s d of 0.38"
