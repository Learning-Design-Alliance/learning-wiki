# Traditional coffee is significantly negatively correlated with grades in Spearman analysis but not significant in regression

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Bitar Anas 2024

Bitar Anas, Barakat Fady, Hawat Angie, Alsaid Bayan. (2024). Dietary and smoking habits during the exam period and their effect on the academic achievement among Syrian medical students. BMC Medical Education 24:60. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04950-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the Spearman analysis of exam-period habits among 608 students, daily traditional coffee consumption had a significant weak negative correlation with grades ("rs = -0.122, p = 0.003"). The article describes the correlation as weak; no standardised effect size beyond the coefficient is printed.

> "daily consumption of traditional coffee (rs = -0.122, p = 0.003)"

### Bitar Anas 2024 (2)

Bitar Anas, Barakat Fady, Hawat Angie, Alsaid Bayan. (2024). Dietary and smoking habits during the exam period and their effect on the academic achievement among Syrian medical students. BMC Medical Education 24:60. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04950-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

However, "traditional coffee consumption showed no significant association with grades (B = -0.033, 95% CI -0.346 – 0.280, p = 0.836)" in the adjusted multiple linear regression. The article reports this inconsistency between the two analyses.

> "traditional coffee consumption showed no significant association with grades (B = -0.033, 95% CI -0.346 – 0.280, p = 0.836)"
