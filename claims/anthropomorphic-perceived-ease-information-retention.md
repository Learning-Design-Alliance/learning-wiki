## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The anthropomorphic condition rated the overall experience of the two sewing tasks as significantly easier, rated on-screen information quantity significantly better, and had a significantly higher score for perceived ability to retain the learned stitch. [→ Murano 2011](#murano-2011)

## Evidence

### Murano 2011

Murano, P. & Sethi, T. (2011). Anthropomorphic User Interface Feedback in a Sewing Context and Affordances. International Journal of Advanced Computer Science and Applications, Vol. 2, No. 4. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1208.3323](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1208.3323)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Likert questionnaire results from the 40-participant between-users sewing experiment: overall task experience significantly easier (p < 0.05, table XVI), quantity of on-screen information significantly better (table XVII), and perceived ability to remember the stitch learned significantly higher in the anthropomorphic condition (table XV).

> "The participants in the anthropomorphic condition perceived the tasks overall to be significantly easier, than the non -anthropomorphic condition"
