## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The purely mathematical presentation fails to convey the origin of the formalism, its place in the history of science, and intuitive grasp of its physical significance, and its applications appear few and disconnected beside its overwhelming generality. ## Evidence

### Bruno Preziosi and Erasmo Recami 2007

Bruno Preziosi and Erasmo Recami. (2007). Ettore Majorana's Inaugural Lecture on Theoretical Physics (Neaples, Italy; Jan.13, 1938) with comments by the editors. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.0941 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0709.0941](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0709.0941)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Majorana's notes argue the mathematical method, which presents the formalism from the start in its most general and clearest structure, disappoints "the desire to somehow perceive by intuition its physical significance" and leaves its applications appearing rare and disconnected.

> "The second method, the purely mathematical one, presents ev en greater inconveniences. It does in no way allow to understand the ori gin of the formalism and as a consequence the place that quantum mechanics has in the history of science."
