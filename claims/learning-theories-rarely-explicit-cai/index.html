Viljoen Charle André 2019¶

Viljoen Charle André, Scott Millar Rob, Engel Mark E, Shelton Mary, Burch Vanessa. (2019). Is computer-assisted instruction more effective than other educational methods in achieving ECG competence amongst medical students and residents? A systematic review and meta-analysis. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2018-028800

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Narrative synthesis of the thirteen included studies' theoretical framing. The review reports theories were "infrequently mentioned or discussed in any detail", with self-directed learning the most frequent reference in CAI.

"Learning theories that underpin education were infrequently mentioned or discussed in any detail. The most frequent reference to learning theories was to self-directed learning in CAI."

Related Claims¶

  • Specific CAI learning materials and activities — case scenarios, explanatory images, self-assessment with automated feedback, and chat rooms — are associated with better ECG competence than face-to-face teaching
  • Four reviewed CAs explicitly integrate pedagogical theories, including the Vygotskian perspective, the 4C/ID model, scaffolding, and gamification
  • DL2F aligns with social and cognitive constructivist theories of learning
  • Contemporary SRL models largely omit the broader social context and implicitly assume all learners are available to learn — related
  • Direct SRL promotion in observed classrooms was dominated by implicit strategy instruction, metacognitive support, and metacognitive reflection, with explicit strategy instruction rare — related
