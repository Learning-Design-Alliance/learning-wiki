# Device-order effect on learning gains appeared in Experiment 1A but did not replicate in Experiment 1B

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In Experiment 1A, mean change in performance was greater for the VR-first group than the PC-first group (η2p = .075). [→ Karen B. Schloss 2021](#karen-b-schloss-2021)
`q3 i?` The device-order effect was not significant in the higher-powered Experiment 1B. [→ Karen B. Schloss 2021 (2)](#karen-b-schloss-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Karen B. Schloss 2021

Karen B. Schloss, Melissa A. Schoenlein, Ross Tredinnick, Simon Smith, Nathaniel Miller, Chris Racey, Christian Castro, and Bas Rokers. (2021). The UW Virtual Brain Project: An immersive approach to teaching functional neuroanatomy. Preprint. [doi:10.1037/tps0000281](https://doi.org/10.1037/tps0000281)

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

An exploratory mixed-design ANOVA in Experiment 1A (60 undergraduates) found "a main effect of device order" favoring the VR-first group (η2p = .075), with no device or system-device pairing effects.

> "there was a main effect of device order ( F(1, 56) = 4.56, p = .037, η2p = .075), in which the mean change in performance was greater for participants in the VR-ﬁrst group than the PC-ﬁrst group."

### Karen B. Schloss 2021 (2)

Karen B. Schloss, Melissa A. Schoenlein, Ross Tredinnick, Simon Smith, Nathaniel Miller, Chris Racey, Christian Castro, and Bas Rokers. (2021). The UW Virtual Brain Project: An immersive approach to teaching functional neuroanatomy. Preprint. [doi:10.1037/tps0000281](https://doi.org/10.1037/tps0000281)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In Experiment 1B's full ANOVA, "device order" was not significant (F(1, 116) = 2.01, p = .159), so the Experiment 1A order effect did not replicate with increased power.

> "The other main effects were also not significant: device order ( F(1, 116) = 2.01, p = .159, η2p = .017), system-device pairing ( F < 1)."
