## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Adding algebra and geometry to a regression model with number fact knowledge, calculation skills and conceptual understanding increased explained variance in mathematics achievement from 56% to 71%. [→ Spiller J 2023](#spiller-j-2023)

## Evidence

### Spiller J 2023

Spiller J, Clayton S, Cragg L, Johnson S, Simms V, Gilmore C. (2023). Higher level domain specific skills in mathematics; The relationship between algebra, geometry, executive function skills and mathematics achievement. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0291796

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical regression with 95 adolescents aged 12–14 using WIAT-II mathematical reasoning as outcome. Model 1 explained 56% of variance; adding algebra and geometry explained 71%, "an additional 15%". Algebra (β = .42) and geometry (β = .16) were significant unique predictors in model 2.

> "A hierarchical regression analysis using age, number fact knowledge, composite calculation skills, and understanding of arithmetic concepts as predictors (model 1) explained 56% of the variance in mathematics achievement. Including algebra and geometry as additional predictors explained 71% of the variance, an additional 15%"
