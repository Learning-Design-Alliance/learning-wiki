## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Differential Item Functioning fails for items unfair across all groups (e.g., vaguely worded items) and for items measuring two skills biased in opposite directions; the proposed method needs only one group. [→ Bakman 2012](#bakman-2012)

## Evidence

### Bakman 2012

Bakman, Y. (2012). Identifying Unfair Items in Educational Measurement. Originally published 2007. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1205.3380](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1205.3380)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument in the comparison section, not a tested result. The article adds that if an item measures two skills biased against male and female respectively, 'DIF is also helpless'.

> "Our method will identify such unfair item with a nd without group separation, while Differential Item Functioning cannot reveal any bias, because comparison of groups will not show any difference between them."
