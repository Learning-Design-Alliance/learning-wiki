# Race, ethnicity, and class affect teacher perceptions and teacher behavior, including persistence with struggling students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teacher persistence — how long a teacher waits when a child is figuring something out, and how many alternative explanations are offered — is significantly affected by race, ethnicity, and class cues. [→ Payne 2005](#payne-2005)

## Evidence

### Payne 2005

Payne, Charles. (2005). Still Crazy after All These Years: Race in the Chicago School System. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Illinois at Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/still-crazy-after-all-these-years-race-chicago-school-system

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Narrative review attribution: Payne reports, citing Ron Ferguson's 2003 review of research on race, expectations, and performance, that the body of research is not strong, but the most convincing line concerns teacher persistence with children who are struggling. Majority group children, he writes, "are going to get more chances."

> "If one has to make a call with the literature we have, then race, ethnicity, and class all affect teacher perception and all affect teacher behavior."
