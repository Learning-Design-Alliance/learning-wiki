## Evidence

### Maher 2014

Maher, C. A., Palius, M. F., Maher, J. A., Hmelo-Silver, C. E., & Sigley, R. (2014). Teachers Can Learn to Attend to Students' Reasoning Using Videos as a Tool. Issues in Teacher Education, 23(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1045688

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre-assessment analysis of the full sample: 22.8% of 127 experimental and 24.0% of 50 comparison participants provided a complete argument description of at least one student solution. The contingency table analysis tested "the null hypothesis of no difference in pre-assessment complete argument description rates".

> "The data in Table 2 indicate that there were no significant differences among groups at the p < 0.05 level (χ2 (3) = 4.97, p = 0.17)."
