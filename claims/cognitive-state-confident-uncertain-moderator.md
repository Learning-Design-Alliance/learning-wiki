## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Every uncertain rule user paired with a higher partner using a confident rule improved, whereas when the uncertain user was the higher partner paired with a confident lower partner, 6 of 13 declined and only 1 improved. [→ Tudge 1986](#tudge-1986)
`q2 i?` Uncertain rule users hearing lower-level arguments were likely to fall back; confident rule users were those not convinced by lower-level reasoning. [→ Tudge 1986 (2)](#tudge-1986-2)

## Evidence

### Tudge 1986

Tudge, Jon. (1986). Beyond Conflict: The Role of Reasoning in Collaborative Problem Solving. Paper presented at the Annual Symposium of the Jean Piaget Society. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED275395

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of the cognitive-state factor in the unequal-rule condition. The article reports the complementary case: all 8 children with an uncertain rule paired with a higher confident-rule partner improved, with improvement persisting for 6 at the second posttest.

> "On the other hand, when the "uncertain" rule user was the higher partner, andwas paired with a "confident" rule user, the situationwas reversed - 6 of 13 declined, while only 1 improved (only at the time of the second posttest)."

### Tudge 1986 (2)

Tudge, Jon. (1986). Beyond Conflict: The Role of Reasoning in Collaborative Problem Solving. Paper presented at the Annual Symposium of the Jean Piaget Society. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED275395

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The article describes an interaction between reasoning quality and cognitive state: confident rule users resisted lower-level arguments while uncertain rule users exposed to them were likely to regress.

> "The children who heard reasoning at a lower level but who were not convinced by it were, as one might expect from the discussion about types af rule, those who had used a "confident" rule at the time of the pretest."Uncertain" rule users, hearing arguments at a lower level, were likely to fall back."
