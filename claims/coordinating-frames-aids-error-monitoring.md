# Coordinating multiple frames helps students monitor calculations and find errors, while staying only in algorithmic math makes checking solutions difficult

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · qualitative `r3` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2017 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students who coordinate multiple frames can better monitor their calculation process and locate errors, whereas prolonged algorithmic math work without other quadrants makes it difficult to check whether a solution makes sense. [→ Bahar Modir 2017](#bahar-modir-2017)
`q2 i?` In one episode, a student transitioned from algorithmic math to algorithmic physics to check units via dimensional analysis and found the source of a sign error the group had made. [→ Bahar Modir 2017 (2)](#bahar-modir-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Bahar Modir 2017

Bahar Modir, John D. Thompson, and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2017). Students’ epistemological framing in quantum mechanics problem solving. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.020108](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.020108)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative episode analysis of a group solving for the wave function of a free particle, in which Adam shifted from algorithmic math to algorithmic physics to check units. The authors conclude that "coordinating multiple frames" supports monitoring and sense-making of final answers.

> "This episode shows that by coordinating multiple frames students can better monitor their calcu- lation process, saving time and/or making sense of their ﬁnal answers."

### Bahar Modir 2017 (2)

Bahar Modir, John D. Thompson, and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2017). Students’ epistemological framing in quantum mechanics problem solving. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.020108](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.020108)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Episode analysis of a group led through separation of variables in algorithmic math, where no discussion addressed whether the solution made sense. The authors describe algorithmic math as "a quick and powerful problem-solving mindset" that alone leaves solutions unchecked.

> "This episode shows that algorithmic math can be a quick and powerful problem-solving mindset, but without other quadrants it is quite diﬃcult to check if solutions makes sense."
