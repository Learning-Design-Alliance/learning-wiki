## Evidence

### Miller et al. 2017

Sarah Miller, Andy Biggart, Seaneen Sloan, Liam O'Hare. (2017). Success for All: Evaluation report and executive summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED581417

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Cluster randomised controlled trial in 54 English primary schools with 1,767 Reception-starting pupils, analysed intention-to-treat using multilevel models with BPVS pre-test as covariate. The report states there was "no evidence to suggest that the intervention group had better literacy skills" at mid-point, with neither post-test effect statistically significant.

> "There was no evidence to suggest that the intervention group had better literacy skills compared to the control group on the Woodcock at the mid-point (Hedges’ g=0.04, p=0.44). At post-test (end of Year 1) there was a small positive effect of the programme on the intervention group on both the Woodcock (Hedges’ g=0.07, p=0.14) and the phonics check (Hedges’ g=0.06, p=0.20); however neither effect was statistically significant."
