# In Experiment 1, the relationship between criterion level and final test performance was curvilinear (power function better than linear), showing diminishing returns

> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The power function fit final test performance across criterion levels better than the linear function at group and individual levels. [→ Pyc 2009](#pyc-2009)
`q3 i?` Diminishing returns from increasing criterion level appeared in three of four groups; the short ISI-long RI group showed a floor effect. [→ Pyc 2009 (2)](#pyc-2009-2)

## Evidence

### Pyc 2009

Mary A. Pyc, Katherine A. Rawson. (2009). Testing the retrieval effort hypothesis: Does greater difficulty correctly recalling information lead to higher levels of memory? Journal of Memory and Language 60 (2009) 437–447. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jml.2009.01.004

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Function-fit analyses of Experiment 1 final test data at group level compared linear, power and exponential fits; "the power function fit the data better than the linear function, accounting for 91% versus 74% of the variance". Individual-level fits also favored the power function (Md = .07, CI = .03, .11).

> "In each of the three groups, the best fitting curvilinear function (the power function) fit the data better than the linear function, accounting for 91% versus 74% of the variance across groups, respectively."

### Pyc 2009 (2)

Mary A. Pyc, Katherine A. Rawson. (2009). Testing the retrieval effort hypothesis: Does greater difficulty correctly recalling information lead to higher levels of memory? Journal of Memory and Language 60 (2009) 437–447. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jml.2009.01.004

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The diminishing-returns pattern held in three of the four Experiment 1 groups; in the short ISI-long RI group "performance was on the floor" and once versus ten correct retrievals did not significantly differ. The authors note this may warrant further investigation.

> "The only exception was the short ISI-long RI group, in which performance was on the floor; final test performance did not significantly differ after correctly retrieving an item once versus ten times during practice."
