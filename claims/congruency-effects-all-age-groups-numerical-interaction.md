## Evidence

### Camilla Gilmore 2015

Camilla Gilmore, Sarah Keeble, Sophie Richardson, Lucy Cragg. (2015). The role of cognitive inhibition in different components of arithmetic. ZDM Mathematics Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11858-014-0659-y

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Mixed-design ANOVA on accuracy for the dot comparison task with congruency as repeated-measures factor and age group as between-groups factor. The article reports "a significant interaction between age group and congruency (F(2,206) = 4.8, p = .009, ηp2 = .05)".

> "There was also a significant interaction between age group and congruency (F(2,206) = 4.8, p = .009, η p 2 = .05; see Fig. 2). Bonferroni-corrected post hoc tests revealed significant congruency effects for all three age groups (all ps < .001)."

### Camilla Gilmore 2015 (2)

Camilla Gilmore, Sarah Keeble, Sophie Richardson, Lucy Cragg. (2015). The role of cognitive inhibition in different components of arithmetic. ZDM Mathematics Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11858-014-0659-y

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Mixed-design ANOVA on RTs for the animal-size Stroop task. The article reports a large congruency main effect (ηp2 = .80) with "RTs faster for congruent than for incongruent trials" and no age-by-congruency interaction.

> "There was also a significant main effect of congruency (F(1,205) = 805.9, p < .001, ηp 2 = .80) with RTs faster for congruent than for incongruent trials. There was no interaction between age group and congruency (F(2,205) = 1.2, p = .302; see Fig. 2)."
