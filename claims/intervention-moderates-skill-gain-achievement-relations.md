# Relations between early number skills gains and first-grade mathematics achievement were moderated by ROOTS intervention participation

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Both informal and formal early number skills gains were positively and statistically significantly associated with first-grade mathematics achievement for intervention participants, but not for at-risk control participants. [→ Shanley 2017](#shanley-2017)

## Evidence

### Shanley 2017

Shanley, L., Clarke, B., Doabler, C. T., Kurtz Nelson, E., & Fien, H. (2017). Early number skills gains and mathematics achievement: Intervening to establish successful early mathematics trajectories. Journal of Special Education, 51(3), 177–188. https://doi.org/10.1177/0022466917720455

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

DIFFTEST model-invariance group difference tests comparing fixed versus free predictive paths for ROOTS and control participants in the SEM analysis. Follow-up tests found "statistically significant differences in the relationships between first-grade mathematics achievement and gains" for both skill factors; no effect size was printed.

> "These analyses revealed statistically significant differences in the relationships between first-grade mathematics achievement and gains on both informal early number skills, χ 2(1) = 4.69, p < .05, and formal early number skills, χ 2(1) = 4.30, p < .05, based on intervention participation."
