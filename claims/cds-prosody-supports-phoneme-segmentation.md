## Evidence

### Schwab Jessica F. 2016

Schwab Jessica F., Lew-Williams Casey. (2016). Language learning, socioeconomic status, and child-directed speech. Wiley interdisciplinary reviews. Cognitive science. https://doi.org/10.1002/wcs.1393

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports laboratory experiments in which infants detected phonemic differences only in CDS, and other work showing large pitch contours enable better vowel discrimination while speech with only higher mean pitch hinders it. No effect sizes are printed.

> "1- to 4-month-old infants were able to detect phonemic differences between subtly distinct syllable sequences with contrastive middle syllables (e.g., ‘marana’ vs ‘malana’) only in CDS, which had a combination of higher mean pitch, larger amplitude, and longer syllable duration than ADS."

### Schwab Jessica F. 2016 (2)

Schwab Jessica F., Lew-Williams Casey. (2016). Language learning, socioeconomic status, and child-directed speech. Wiley interdisciplinary reviews. Cognitive science. https://doi.org/10.1002/wcs.1393

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports an observational analysis from the Human Speechome Project, in which a single child's input and output was recorded between 9 and 24 months, showing prosodic characteristics of word forms associated with age of production. No effect sizes are printed.

> "Words that were louder, longer, and higher in pitch in parent speech were produced by the child at an earlier age."
