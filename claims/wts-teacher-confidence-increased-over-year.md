# Teacher confidence in teaching literacy and English learner students increased over the pilot year, especially with a local coach

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Average confidence in teaching literacy rose from 3.04 to 3.33 and confidence in teaching English learner students from 3.22 to 3.33 over the year (N = 20). [→ Patrick Rich 2025](#patrick-rich-2025)
`q2 i?` Teachers with a local coach gained more confidence than those without (0.45 vs. -0.08 for English learner student confidence), a difference the report calls statistically significant for English learner student confidence. [→ Patrick Rich 2025 (2)](#patrick-rich-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Patrick Rich 2025

Patrick Rich, Audrey Altieri, Rachel Garrett. (2025). A pilot study of Write to Succeed: An intervention to enhance writing instruction in New Mexico. Regional Educational Laboratory Southwest (REL 2025-013). https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/resource-library/report/descriptive-study/pilot-study-write-succeed-intervention-enhancing-writing-instruction-new-mexico

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Teacher surveys at beginning, middle, and end of year (N = 20) show literacy confidence rising from 3.04 to 3.33 and English learner confidence from 3.22 to 3.33 on a 1-4 agreement scale; analysis was of calculated averages.

> "Teacher confidence for teaching English learner students increased over the year and was at a similar level as confidence in teaching literacy by the year's end."

### Patrick Rich 2025 (2)

Patrick Rich, Audrey Altieri, Rachel Garrett. (2025). A pilot study of Write to Succeed: An intervention to enhance writing instruction in New Mexico. Regional Educational Laboratory Southwest (REL 2025-013). https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/resource-library/report/descriptive-study/pilot-study-write-succeed-intervention-enhancing-writing-instruction-new-mexico

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Survey-based comparison of average confidence gains from beginning to end of year (N = 14 local coach, N = 6 no local coach) shows gains of 0.18 versus -0.06 for literacy and 0.45 versus -0.08 for English learner student confidence; no effect size is printed.

> "Teachers who had a local coach had higher growth in self-efficacy than those who did not (these teachers had small decreases), especially for English learner student self-efficacy. The difference is statistically significant for confidence in teaching English learner students."
