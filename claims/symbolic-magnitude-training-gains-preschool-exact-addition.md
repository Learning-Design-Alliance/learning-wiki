## Subclaims
`q3 i3` After ten sessions of symbolic magnitude training, preschoolers showed significant improvement in exact addition performance, while non-symbolic and control groups did not. [→ Honoré Nastasya 2016](#honore-nastasya-2016)

## Evidence

### Honoré Nastasya 2016

Honoré Nastasya, Noël Marie-Pascale. (2016). Improving Preschoolers' Arithmetic through Number Magnitude Training: The Impact of Non-Symbolic and Symbolic Training. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0166685

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Randomized three-group training experiment with 56 preschoolers (19 non-symbolic, 19 symbolic, 18 control), pre- and post-tested on exact additions. The time × group interaction was significant, F(2,52) = 4.38; p = .017, η2 = 0.144, and "Improvement in performance over time was significant in the symbolic group only" (t(52) = 4.18; p < .001, η2 = 0.265).

> "More importantly, the interaction between time and group was significant, F(2,52) = 4.38; p = .017, η2 = 0.144 (Fig 9). Improvement in performance over time was significant in the symbolic group only, t(52) = 4.18; p < .001, η2 = 0.265"
