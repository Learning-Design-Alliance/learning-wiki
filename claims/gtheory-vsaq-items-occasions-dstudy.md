# Generalizability decision study: one occasion of ISF-VSAQ requires about 25 items for acceptable reliability (Phi = 0.72), while two occasions with ten items suffice (Phi = 0.71)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the two-facet crossed P×I×O decision study, Phi-coefficients rose from 0.60 to 0.79 as occasions increased from one to four with ten items; 25 items were needed on a single occasion for Phi = 0.72, but only ten items on two occasions (Phi = 0.71). ## Evidence

### Lertsakulbunlue Sethapong 2024

Lertsakulbunlue Sethapong, Kantiwong Anupong. (2024). Development and validation of immediate self-feedback very short answer questions for medical students: practical implementation of generalizability theory to estimate reliability in formative examination designs. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05569-x

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Generalizability theory decision study of the P×I×O design from the two ten-item ISF-VSAQ exams of 94 students, computed for varying item and occasion combinations. "For a single occasion, at least twenty-five items (Phi-coefficient = 0.72)" were required; two occasions needed only ten items (Phi = 0.71). No standardized effect size is printed.

> "For a single occasion, at least twenty-five items (Phi-coefficient = 0.72) are necessary for a reliable assessment. For two occasions, the Phi-coefficient ranges from 0.59 to 0.86, and only ten items (Phi-coefficient = 0.71) are sufficient for reliable assessment."
