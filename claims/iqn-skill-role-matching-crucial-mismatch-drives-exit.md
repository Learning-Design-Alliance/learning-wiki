## Evidence

### Kurup Chanchal 2025

Kurup Chanchal, Burston Adam Scott, Betihavas Vasiliki, Jacob Elisabeth Ruth. (2025). Exploring the Utilisation of Internationally Qualified Nurses' Specialty Skills: Analysis of Recruiting Managers' View Points. Nursing Open. https://doi.org/10.1002/nop2.70184

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Theme 1 (Right person, right opportunity) finding from interviews with eight recruiting managers. One manager explained that "Matching practice context to specialty skills would aid a successful transition as it is about transferable skills." Managers also noted assessing actual skills is difficult and structural limits delay suitable openings.

> "Managers noted that aligning an IQN's skills to a suitable position is crucial for effective specialty skill utilisation."

### Kurup Chanchal 2025 (2)

Kurup Chanchal, Burston Adam Scott, Betihavas Vasiliki, Jacob Elisabeth Ruth. (2025). Exploring the Utilisation of Internationally Qualified Nurses' Specialty Skills: Analysis of Recruiting Managers' View Points. Nursing Open. https://doi.org/10.1002/nop2.70184

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Sub-theme 'the effects of mismatching' from the same interview study. Managers described IQNs realising their area was not what they expected, with some transitioning to more suitable internal roles and others relocating outside the organisation to change specialties.

> "The consequences can be significant when IQNs are positioned in roles that do not utilise their specialty skills. Misalignment may prompt nurses to either transfer internally or leave the organisation entirely."
