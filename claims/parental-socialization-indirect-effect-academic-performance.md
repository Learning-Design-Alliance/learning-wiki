# Parental warmth and control relate to adolescents’ academic performance only indirectly, through psychological and school maladjustment, with no direct relationship with performance

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` No direct relationship was found between the parental socialization dimensions and academic performance, or between emotional symptomatology and academic performance, in either gender group. [→ Bully P. 2019](#bully-p-2019)
`q2 i?` The mediation path model (parental warmth and control → emotional symptoms and school maladjustment → teacher-rated academic performance) fit the entire sample well. [→ Bully P. 2019 (2)](#bully-p-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Bully P. 2019

Bully P., Jaureguizar J., Bernaras E., Redondo I. (2019). Relationship between Parental Socialization, Emotional Symptoms, and Academic Performance during Adolescence: The Influence of Parents’ and Teenagers’ Gender. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 16(12), 2231. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph16122231

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Path analysis of survey data from 823 Basque secondary students (12–16 years), with parental warmth and control as antecedents, BASC-S3 emotional symptoms and school maladjustment as mediators, and teacher-rated academic performance as criterion. The article reports that "No direct relationship was found between the parental socialization dimensions and academic performance".

> "No direct relationship was found between the parental socialization dimensions and academic performance or between emotional symptomatology and academic performance in either of the groups."

### Bully P. 2019 (2)

Bully P., Jaureguizar J., Bernaras E., Redondo I. (2019). Relationship between Parental Socialization, Emotional Symptoms, and Academic Performance during Adolescence: The Influence of Parents’ and Teenagers’ Gender. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 16(12), 2231. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph16122231

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same path model applied to the whole sample showed the printed fit indexes: "χ2(4) = 12.04, p = 0.017, χ2/df = 3.01, with SRMR = 0.020, IFI = 0.994, CFI = 0.994, and RMSEA = 0.049".

> "When the theoretical model was applied to the entire sample, the goodness-of-fit indexes were as follows: χ2(4) = 12.04, p = 0.017, χ2/df = 3.01, with SRMR = 0.020, IFI = 0.994, CFI = 0.994, and RMSEA = 0.049, 95% CI (0.019, 0.083)."
