## Evidence

### Dannaway Jasan 2016

Dannaway Jasan, Ng Heryanto, Schoo Adrian. (2016). Literature review of teaching skills programs for junior medical officers. International Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.5685.14da

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's appraisal of the 12 post-2008 studies found substantial internal validity threats: only five uncontrolled trials had pre and post measures, six had over 10% loss to follow-up, and only two used validated surveys or objective measures.

> "There were no randomised controlled trials (RCTs). Two were non randomised controlled trials27,34 and the rest were uncontrolled trials. Only six studies included a pre intervention outcome measure."

### Dannaway Jasan 2016 (2)

Dannaway Jasan, Ng Heryanto, Schoo Adrian. (2016). Literature review of teaching skills programs for junior medical officers. International Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.5685.14da

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's conclusion on the heterogeneous post-2008 group: subjective positive effects are well evidenced, but objective effects at higher Kirkpatrick levels and over time remain unevidenced, so these studies added little to the topic.

> "There is significant evidence that teaching skills programs have subjective positive effects on all Kirkpatrick outcome levels. Unfortunately, what is not present is strong evidence for objective positive effects for higher levels of outcomes (Kirkpatrick levels 2b, 3 or 4) and their effects over time."
