## Subclaims
`q2 i?` No significant correlations between quiz and transfer scores were found in any self-diagnosis group in either the challenging or typical problem setting, suggesting the interventions reduced the prior-knowledge gap. [→ Mason 2016](#mason-2016)

## Evidence

### Mason 2016

Mason, A., Yerushalmi, E., Cohen, E., & Singh, C. (2016). Learning from mistakes: The effect of students' written self-diagnoses on subsequent problem solving. Physics Education Research Conference proceedings. Originally published 2012. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07253](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07253)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Comparison across the three self-diagnosis intervention groups (I, W, S) versus control in an introductory physics course; no correlation coefficients are printed for the intervention groups, only the finding of "no significant correlations" and gap reduction.

> "we found no significant correlations between the quiz score and score on the transfer problem in any of the self-diagnosis groups in either setting, suggesting, as further evidence has confirmed, that all of the self-diagnosis interventions reduced the gap between students with low and high prior knowledge to some extent"
