## Evidence

### Richmond Helen 2017

Richmond Helen, Copsey Bethan, Hall Amanda M., Davies David, Lamb Sarah E. (2017). A systematic review and meta-analysis of online versus alternative methods for training licensed health care professionals to deliver clinical interventions. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1047-4

`q2 · i?`

TIDieR-guided description of the 14 included online interventions. All studies reported target group, topic and objectives, but the review found reporting of content, learning activity and delivery mode "insufficient for replication", and no studies reported how to access the interventions or formal pilot testing.

> "however, reporting of the course content, type of learning activity such as instruction or feedback, and mode of delivery such as text or video, was insufficient for replication. Moreover, no studies provided information on how to access the online interventions."

### Richmond Helen 2017 (2)

`q2 · i?`

Adherence reporting across the 14 included studies: only 6 reported mean time spent learning, 4 using self-report and 2 using online user analytics; in these studies adherence was reported as acceptable.

> "Only 6 studies reported adherence (mean time spent learning) to the online intervention, 4 of which used a self-reported measure of adherence"
