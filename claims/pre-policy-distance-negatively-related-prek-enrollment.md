# Before Chicago's 2013-14 policy changes, students living farther from a school with pre-k were less likely to enroll in school-based pre-k

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Pre-policy, greater distance to the closest school with any pre-k was associated with lower likelihood of enrolling in any school-based pre-k. [→ Ehrlich 2020](#ehrlich-2020)

## Evidence

### Ehrlich 2020

Ehrlich, S.B., Connors, M.C., Stein, A.G., Francis, J., Easton, J.Q., Kabourek, S.E., & Farrar, I.C. (2020). Closer to home: More equitable pre-k access and enrollment in Chicago. Chicago, IL: UChicago Consortium on School Research, NORC at the University of Chicago, and Start Early. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/closer-to-home

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Multilevel logistic regression on CPS student cohorts from 2010-11 through 2012-13 (pre-policy years) predicting enrollment in any school-based pre-k from distance to the closest school with pre-k. The article reports that living half a mile farther meant being "11 percentage points less likely to enroll in pre-k" than a peer living half a mile closer; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "In practical terms, a student who lived half a mile farther from a school with pre-k was 11 percentage points less likely to enroll in pre-k than a peer who lived half a mile closer to a school with pre-k."
