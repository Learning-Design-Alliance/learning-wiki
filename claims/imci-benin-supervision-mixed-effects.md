## Evidence

### Steinhardt Laura C. 2015

Steinhardt Laura C., Onikpo Faustin, Kouamé Julien, Piercefield Emily, Lama Marcel, Deming Michael S., Rowe Alexander K. (2015). Predictors of health worker performance after Integrated Management of Childhood Illness training in Benin: a cohort study. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-015-0910-4

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariable regression on register data found supervision visits significant for adequate fever treatment (OR 1.13), recommended anemia (OR 1.15), and negatively for recommended pneumonia (OR 0.87, p<0.05). The study reports supervision was "paradoxically associated with a lower odds of recommended treatment for pneumonia".

> "The number of supervision visits the health worker received in the previous six months was significantly associated with improved care for fever and anemia—although paradoxically associated with a lower odds of recommended treatment for pneumonia."
