## Evidence

### Andreas Lieberoth 2014

Andreas Lieberoth, Mads Kock Pedersen, Andreea Catalina Marin, Tilo Planke, Jacob Friis Sherson. (2014). Getting Humans to do Quantum Optimization - User Acquisition, Engagement and Early Results from the Citizen Cyberscience Game Quantum Moves. Human Computation. https://doi.org/10.15346/hc.v1i2.11

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

One-way ANOVA on average tutorial-level scores by gender in the beta cohort (Table 2 shows female mean 65.80, male mean 56.49). The article reports "females significantly outperforming their male competition (F(1,672)=5.55, p=0.019**)"; no effect size is printed.

> "there was a significant difference between the genders, with females significantly outperforming their male competition (F(1,672)=5.55, p=0.019**)."

### Andreas Lieberoth 2014 (2)

Andreas Lieberoth, Mads Kock Pedersen, Andreea Catalina Marin, Tilo Planke, Jacob Friis Sherson. (2014). Getting Humans to do Quantum Optimization - User Acquisition, Engagement and Early Results from the Citizen Cyberscience Game Quantum Moves. Human Computation. https://doi.org/10.15346/hc.v1i2.11

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Table 1 reports mean games played per active day of 73.18 for males (N=1528) versus 31.36 for females (N=371), with the gender ANOVA on play counts significant (F(1,582)=40.37, p<0.001). Means are descriptive with confidence bounds; no effect size printed.

> "Male 1528 73.18 62.37 84 Female 371 31.36 24.23 38.49"
