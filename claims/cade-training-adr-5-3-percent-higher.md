# Endoscopists trained with CADe achieved a 5.3% higher adenoma detection rate in their first 1000 AI-free colonoscopies than conventionally trained peers

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Orzeszko Z 2025

Orzeszko Z, Gach T, Necka S, Ochwat K, Major P, Szura M. (2025). The implementation of computer-aided detection in an initial endoscopy training improves the quality measures of trainees' future colonoscopies: a retrospective cohort study. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-025-11890-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Retrospective cohort comparison of 3000 patients examined by three AI-trained endoscopists versus 3000 patients examined by three conventionally trained endoscopists in their first 1000 AI-free procedures each. The study reports "AI-enhanced training improved ADR by 5.3%" with high-power analysis and a low missing-data rate of 0.6%; no standardized effect size was printed for this difference.

> "AI-enhanced training improved ADR by 5.3% and SDR by 5.4% (95% CI: 2.9–7.6%, and 3.8–7.0%, respectively) in high-power analysis (98.5–100%)."

### Orzeszko Z 2025 (2)

Orzeszko Z, Gach T, Necka S, Ochwat K, Major P, Szura M. (2025). The implementation of computer-aided detection in an initial endoscopy training improves the quality measures of trainees' future colonoscopies: a retrospective cohort study. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-025-11890-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression analysis on the same 6000-patient cohort identified AI-enhanced training as a factor of high-quality ADR performance: "OR 1.27, 95% CI: 1.14–1.42" with p < 0.05 and high data quality (missing data rate 0.6%).

> "Logistic regression confirmed that AI-trained endoscopists were more likely to meet high-quality standards in terms of ADR, SDR, and WT (OR 1.27, 95% CI: 1.14–1.42, p < 0.05; OR 1.93, 95% CI: 1.10–3.37, p < 0.05; OR 1.88, 95% CI: 1.69–2.09, p < 0.05, respectively), with no impact on CIR (Table 4)."
