# Family SES effects on children's reading are partially mediated in parallel by phonological awareness and vocabulary knowledge

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` SES has a significant direct effect on latent reading (β = 0.12) plus significant indirect effects through phonological awareness (β = 0.09) and vocabulary knowledge (β = 0.09), indicating partial mediation. [→ Li 2021](#li-2021)
`q3 i?` Both mediators show significant partial mediation for each specific reading outcome: accuracy, comprehension, and fluency models all show significant direct and indirect effects. [→ Li 2021](#li-2021)

## Evidence

### Li 2021

Li, J., Peng, P., Ding, N., Ma, X., & Zhao, J. (2021). How does family socioeconomic status influence children's reading ability? Evidence from meta-analytic structural equation modeling. https://www.frontiersin.org

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Two-stage MASEM (TSSEM) of the latent reading model (k = 458 independent samples) fit the data well (RMSEA = .014, CFI = .989). The study reports a "significant direct effect of SES on reading" plus parallel significant indirect effects through both mediators, i.e., partial rather than full mediation.

> "The direct effect of SES on reading was significant, β = 0.12, 95 % LBCI [0.09, 0.16], and the model revealed significant indirect effects of SES on reading through PA (β = 0.09, 95 % LBCI [0.08, 0.11]) and VK (β = 0.09, 95 % LBCI [0.08, 0.10])"
