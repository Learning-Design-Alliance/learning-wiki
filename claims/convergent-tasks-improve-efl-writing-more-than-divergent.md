# Convergent tasks improve EFL learners' writing significantly more than divergent tasks

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` EFL learners taught writing through convergent tasks scored significantly higher on a PET writing posttest than learners taught through divergent tasks, with a moderate effect size of 0.63. [→ Marashi 2015](#marashi-2015)

## Evidence

### Marashi 2015

Marashi, H., & Tahan-Shizari, P. (2015). Using convergent and divergent tasks to improve writing and language learning motivation. Iranian Journal of Language Teaching Research, 3(1), 99-117. https://www.urmia.ac.ir/ijltr

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Experimental study with 60 female intermediate EFL learners randomly assigned to convergent or divergent task groups (n=30 each) over 18 sessions. Independent samples t-test on PET writing posttest scores showed the convergent group significantly outperformed the divergent group (p = 0.002), with a moderate effect size of 0.63. The article reports "the learners in the convergent group benefited significantly more than those in the divergent group in terms of improving their writing."

> "The results led to the rejection of the first null hypothesis, thereby demonstrating that the learners in the convergent group benefited significantly more than those in the divergent group in terms of improving their writing."
