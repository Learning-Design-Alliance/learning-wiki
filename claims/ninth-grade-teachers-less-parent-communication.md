# Ninth-grade teachers report less communication with and academic support for parents than eighth-grade teachers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Ninth-grade teachers report significantly less emphasis on communicating and working with parents than eighth-grade teachers. [→ Roderick 1998](#roderick-1998)
`q3 i?` Ninth-grade teachers hold more negative views of parents and report lower trust than eighth-grade teachers. [→ Roderick 1998 (2)](#roderick-1998-2)

## Evidence

### Roderick 1998

Roderick, M., Stone, S., with Arney, M., Chiong, J., DaCosta, K., & Waxman, E. (1998). Changing Standards, Changing Relationships: Building Family-School Relationships to Promote Achievement in High Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://ccsr.uchicago.edu/publications/changing-standards-changing-relationships-building-family-school-relationships-promote

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Consortium teacher surveys in 1994 and 1997; the brief focuses on 1,000 eighth- and ninth-grade teachers. Fewer ninth-grade teachers strongly agreed they work closely with parents, communicate about learning, or invite parents to observe instruction (Figure 2).

> "ninth-grade teachers portrayed their schools as placing significantly less emphasis on communicating and working with parents than their eighth-grade counterparts"

### Roderick 1998 (2)

Roderick, M., Stone, S., with Arney, M., Chiong, J., DaCosta, K., & Waxman, E. (1998). Changing Standards, Changing Relationships: Building Family-School Relationships to Promote Achievement in High Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://ccsr.uchicago.edu/publications/changing-standards-changing-relationships-building-family-school-relationships-promote

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same teacher survey data (Figure 3). Ninth-grade teachers were less likely to see parents as supportive, felt less respected, and reported lower trust and partnership with parents than eighth-grade teachers.

> "Only 36 percent of the ninth-grade teachers versus 52 percent of the eighth-grade teachers believe that most or nearly all of the teachers in their school feel good about parents’ support for their work"
