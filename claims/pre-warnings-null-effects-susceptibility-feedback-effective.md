## Evidence

### Rau 2025

Rau, M. A., & Premo, A. E. (2025). Systematic Review of Educational Approaches to Misinformation. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10012-8

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's synthesis of 13 pre-warning articles found "null effects of general warnings based on comparisons to controls", with a misinformation effect occurring despite warnings in several studies; one study found warned participants exhibited a weaker misinformation effect than unwarned ones.

> "The results of most studies point towards null effects of general warnings based on comparisons to controls (Bailey et al., 2021; Luke et al., 2017) or because a misinformation effect occurred despite participants having been warned"

### Rau 2025 (2)

Rau, M. A., & Premo, A. E. (2025). Systematic Review of Educational Approaches to Misinformation. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10012-8

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

In Salovich and Rapp's approach reviewed here, participants were told their susceptibility would be monitored and received staged feedback; "negative feedback, informing participants that they were more susceptible than average, reduced their susceptibility." Specific warnings showed mixed results across studies.

> "Especially negative feedback, informing participants that they were more susceptible than average, reduced their susceptibility."
