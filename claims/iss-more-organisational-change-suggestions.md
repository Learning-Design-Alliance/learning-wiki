# In situ simulation participants generate more suggestions for organisational changes than off-site participants

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Qualitative analysis showed ISS participants suggested more ideas for organisational changes, especially concerning technology and tools in the delivery ward and operating theatre. [→ Sørensen JL 2015](#srensen-jl-2015)
`q3 i?` Quantitatively, the two groups scored equally on whether the simulations inspired changes to procedures or guidelines. [→ Sørensen JL 2015 (2)](#srensen-jl-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Sørensen JL 2015

Sørensen JL, van der Vleuten C, Rosthøj S, Østergaard D, LeBlanc V, Johansen M, Ekelund K, Starkopf L, Lindschou J, Gluud C, Weikop P, Ottesen B. (2015). Simulation-based multiprofessional obstetric anaesthesia training conducted in situ versus off-site leads to similar individual and team outcomes: a randomised educational trial. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2015-008344

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Organisational suggestions from open-ended evaluation questions and debriefings, categorised using part of the Systems Engineering Initiative for Patient Safety framework; the ISS group's advantage was concentrated in technology and tools.

> "A qualitative analysis showed that more ideas for organisational changes were suggested by ISS participants than OSS participants."

### Sørensen JL 2015 (2)

Sørensen JL, van der Vleuten C, Rosthøj S, Østergaard D, LeBlanc V, Johansen M, Ekelund K, Starkopf L, Lindschou J, Gluud C, Weikop P, Ottesen B. (2015). Simulation-based multiprofessional obstetric anaesthesia training conducted in situ versus off-site leads to similar individual and team outcomes: a randomised educational trial. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2015-008344

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Evaluation-questionnaire items 5 and 6 (Kruskal-Wallis, both p=0.70) showed equal self-reported inspiration to change procedures or guidelines, contrasting with the qualitative finding.

> "The quantitative analysis, however, showed that participants in the ISS and OSS groups scored equally concerning whether the simulations inspired making changes in procedures or guidelines (table 7, questions 5 and 6)."
