## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Bosch 2017

Bosch, N., & D'Mello, S. (2017). The Affective Experience of Novice Computer Programmers. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0069-5

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

One-sample t-tests of transition likelihoods against chance across affect sequences from 99 students, testing transitions among the four states specified by the D'Mello and Graesser model; five of six predicted transitions were significant (p<.05).

> "The results (see Fig. 4 and Table 5) indicated that five of the six predicted transitions, engagement ↔ confusion, confusion ↔ frustration, and frustration → boredom, were significant and aligned with the theoretical model."

### Bosch 2017 (2)

Bosch, N., & D'Mello, S. (2017). The Affective Experience of Novice Computer Programmers. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0069-5

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Transition likelihood analysis of the same affect sequences; the article attributes the unexpected boredom→engagement transition to the fast-paced session and to students' control over the environment via bottom-out hints, unlike the earlier AutoTutor study.

> "Interestingly, boredom was likely to transition to engagement (meanL=.260, p<.05) even though the boredom → engagement transition was not predicted by the theoretical model."
