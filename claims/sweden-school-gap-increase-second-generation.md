## Evidence

### Okuyama K 2025

Okuyama K, Lönn SL, Khoshnood AM, Assari S, Sundquist J, Sundquist K. (2025). School performance gap between non-immigrant and second-generation immigrant children in Sweden—time trends and contributing factors. Frontiers in Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1521387

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Linear mixed models on Swedish national registers covering 70,000–85,000 graduates per year (about 10% second-generation) tested the year-by-immigration-status interaction on ninth-grade scores. The year*status coefficient was −0.20 for females and −0.61 for males, indicating "the gap in school performance increased" more among non-immigrant children.

> "the gap in school performance increased by 0.20 points every year among females (−0.20, 95%CI: −0.37, −0.02) and 0.61 points among males (−0.61, 95%CI: −0.78, −0.44)."
