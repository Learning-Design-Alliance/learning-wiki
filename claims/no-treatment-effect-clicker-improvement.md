## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Improvement on repeated clicker questions did not differ significantly across the DO, WO, and DW treatments, suggesting improvement resulted from activity participation regardless of implementation method. [→ Linton 2014](#linton-2014)

## Evidence

### Linton 2014

Linton, D. L., Pangle, W. M., Wyatt, K. H., Powell, K. N., & Sherwood, R. E. (2014). Identifying Key Features of Effective Active Learning: The Effects of Writing and Peer Discussion. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.13-12-0242

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

GLMM on clicker improvement data (coded 0/1) across 10 question sets in three treatments; the article reports improvement "was not significantly different between treatments" (χ2 = 2.8, p = 0.24). No effect size is printed; equivalence was not tested.

> "Students scored significantly higher on the postactivity clicker questions than on the preactivity questions (V = 74,090, df = 1932, p < 0.0001) in all treatments. However, this improvement was not significantly different between treatments (Figure 2; χ2 = 2.8, df = 2, p = 0.24)."
