## Evidence

### Wójcik 2025

Wójcik, D., Adamiak, O., Czerepak, G., Tokarczuk, O. & Szalewski, L. (2025). Comparing the performance of ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude in English and Polish on medical examinations. Scientific Reports 15, 33083. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-17030-0

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

GLMM Model 2 contrast analysis by specialty and language (Table 11); the discussion notes these significant periodontology differences occurred "only for exam questions in Polish". No standardized effect-size value is printed for these contrasts, so no magnitude is coded.

> "In the specialty of periodontology, ChatGPT-4 showed 6.76 times higher probability of providing a correct answer compared to Gemini (p = 0.002). Gemini showed 85% lower probability of providing a correct answer compared to Claude (p = 0.002). Both effect sizes are large."

### Wójcik 2025 (2)

Wójcik, D., Adamiak, O., Czerepak, G., Tokarczuk, O. & Szalewski, L. (2025). Comparing the performance of ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude in English and Polish on medical examinations. Scientific Reports 15, 33083. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-17030-0

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Null contrasts in English (Table 11) for prosthetic dentistry, periodontology and orthodontics; in Polish, prosthetic dentistry and orthodontics likewise showed no significant differences. Equivalence was not tested, so this is a failure to detect, not evidence of no difference.

> "No statistically significant differences were detected among the chatbots in the specialties of prosthetic dentistry, periodontology, and orthodontics regarding the probability of accuracy to exam questions in English (Table 11; Fig. 5)."
