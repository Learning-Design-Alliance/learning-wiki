## Evidence

### Al‐Kaylani HM 2022

Al‐Kaylani HM, Reasoner EE, Loeffler BT, Mott SL, Madasu S, Liu A, Langbehn K, Conrad AL, Dickens D, Grafft A, Harshman L, Modi AJ, van der Plas E. (2022). Characterizing academic performance in pediatric acute lymphoblastic leukemia with population‐based achievement tests. Cancer Reports. https://doi.org/10.1002/cnr2.1560

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Mixed effects analysis of school-based math scores from 63 ALL survivors; the article reports no significant predictors of math performance. No effect size is printed, so no magnitude is asserted.

> "None of the predictor variables were significantly associated with math performance (Table S4)."

### Al‐Kaylani HM 2022 (2)

Al‐Kaylani HM, Reasoner EE, Loeffler BT, Mott SL, Madasu S, Liu A, Langbehn K, Conrad AL, Dickens D, Grafft A, Harshman L, Modi AJ, van der Plas E. (2022). Characterizing academic performance in pediatric acute lymphoblastic leukemia with population‐based achievement tests. Cancer Reports. https://doi.org/10.1002/cnr2.1560

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Univariate mixed effects analysis of school-based science scores from the same cohort. The article reports the lunch–science association "did not reach threshold of statistical significance" (β = −7.79, p = .09), and that treatment variables were not significantly associated with science scores.

> "Univariate analysis showed that the pattern of association between free/reduced lunch and science scores was similar to that of reading scores, but did not reach threshold of statistical significance at p < .05 (β = −7.79, 95% CI −16.78:1.19, p = .09)."
