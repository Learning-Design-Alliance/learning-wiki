## Evidence

### Pinheiro-Chagas Pedro 2014

Pinheiro-Chagas Pedro, Wood Guilherme, Knops André, Krinzinger Helga, Lonnemann Jan, Starling-Alves Isabella, Willmes Klaus, Haase Vitor Geraldi. (2014). In How Many Ways is the Approximate Number System Associated with Exact Calculation? PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0111155

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

ANCOVAs comparing 162 TA and 40 MD children on the three ANS acuity measures, controlling for intelligence and spelling. The article reports the TA group had "significant lower w the non-symbolic comparison task" while the estimation difference was "only marginally significant"; a significantly lower cv in non-symbolic addition was also found for TA children.

> "More importantly, the TA group presented higher ANS acuity, with significant lower w the non-symbolic comparison task. Moreover, TA children had lower cv in the non-symbolic estimation task; however, this difference was only marginally significant."

### Pinheiro-Chagas Pedro 2014 (2)

Pinheiro-Chagas Pedro, Wood Guilherme, Knops André, Krinzinger Helga, Lonnemann Jan, Starling-Alves Isabella, Willmes Klaus, Haase Vitor Geraldi. (2014). In How Many Ways is the Approximate Number System Associated with Exact Calculation? PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0111155

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same ANCOVA battery found the TA group "showed better performance in exact calculation when compared to the children with MD", while the simple reaction time task showed no group difference, indicating the difference was not general processing speed.

> "As expected, the TA group showed better performance in exact calculation when compared to the children with MD. No group difference was found in the simple reaction time task."
