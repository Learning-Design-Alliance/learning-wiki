## Evidence

### Ashish Gurung 2026

Ashish Gurung, Ge Gao, Jordan Gutterman, Danielle R. Thomas, Shivang Gupta, Lee Branstetter, Emma Brunskill, Vincent Aleven, Kenneth R. Koedinger. (2026). Improving Hybrid Human-AI Tutoring by Differentiating Human Tutor Roles Based on Student Needs. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11155

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-subject DiD analysis of IXL skill proficiency (SmartScore-based skills mastered) over the 10 weeks before each MAP test. Improvement was "significant improvement in student skill proficiency in both the IK bandwidth subsample (36%; +6.10 skills, p<.001)" and the full sample.

> "human-AI tutoring was associated with significant improvement in student skill proficiency in both the IK bandwidth subsample (36%; +6.10 skills, p<.001) and the full sample (42%; +6.75 skills, p <.001)."

### Ashish Gurung 2026 (2)

Ashish Gurung, Ge Gao, Jordan Gutterman, Danielle R. Thomas, Shivang Gupta, Lee Branstetter, Emma Brunskill, Vincent Aleven, Kenneth R. Koedinger. (2026). Improving Hybrid Human-AI Tutoring by Differentiating Human Tutor Roles Based on Student Needs. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11155

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

DiD comparison of proactive versus reactive groups on IXL skill proficiency (Table 6). The article reports the "proficiency gap widened by 3.69 skills (14.79%)" favoring the reactive group; the proactive group had lower baseline proficiency (-2.17, p=.067 in the IK bandwidth).

> "Following the introduction of human support, despite improvements for both groups, the proficiency gap widened by 3.69 skills (14.79%), with the reactive group demonstrating greater proficiency than their proactive peers."
