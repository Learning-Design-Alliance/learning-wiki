# Doctors' pre-simulation emotions are associated with their self-assessed performance but not with peer-assessed performance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r3` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2014 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Pre-assessment negative emotions (e.g. annoyance) were associated with more self-rated 'Avoids taking the lead' (r = .22*), and positive pre-mood (hedonic tone r = -.22* with avoidance; energetic arousal r = .35** with 'Focuses on colleagues') related to self-rated performance, while peer-assessed performance was unrelated to the assessed doctor's emotions. [→ Ferguson E 2014](#ferguson-e-2014)

## Evidence

### Ferguson E 2014

Ferguson E, Buttery A, Miles G, Tatalia C, Clarke DD, Lonsdale AJ, Baxendale B, Lawrence C. (2014). The Temporal Rating of Emergency Non-Technical skills (TRENT) index for self and others: psychometric properties and emotional responses. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0240-y

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Main-study zero-order correlations between UWIST MACL short-form mood scales and self-rated TRENT dimensions in 90 F1 doctors (Table 5; e.g. Energetic Arousal .35** with 'Focuses on colleagues not self'; Annoyed .22* with 'Avoids taking the lead'). Endnote g notes only 4 of 80 peer-assessment associations were significant, too few to signify a systematic effect.

> "The zero-order correlations (Table 5) between pre- and post-emotions and self-assessment confirm the prediction that the lead doctor's pre-simulation emotions are associated with their self-assessed performance, but not the peer-assessed performance"
