Won Ik Cho 2026¶

Won Ik Cho, Seong-hun Kim, Geunhye Kim. (2026). Position: Adopting AI in Practice Does Not Guarantee the Productivity Boost. Proceedings of the 43rd International Conference on Machine Learning, PMLR 306. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24688

q1 · i? · theoretical · r3

The position paper asserts this in its education section, citing Al-Obaydi & Pikhart (2025); it reports no new data of its own. It also cites Bastani et al. (2025) that "unrestricted generative AI access can harm learning outcomes even while improving immediate task performance."

"Students tend to develop tool usage skills rather than deploying existing expertise – AI assistance that substitutes for learning activities may improve immediate performance metrics while impeding long-term skill development (Al-Obaydi & Pikhart, 2025)."

Related Claims¶

  • AI tools can enhance short-term task performance while undermining durable learning (the learning-performance paradox) — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Students given AI assistance in peer feedback tended to rely on the AI-generated feedback and struggled to perform the task when the assistance was removed — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • In TBLT, teachers perceive scaffolding collapse, a shortcut paradox, invisible inequity, and task realism corrosion — related
  • Engagement-index EEG metrics show no significant differences across AI modes, and a possible short-term AI carryover effect remains unconfirmed — related
  • Students in an academic writing course developed a more nuanced understanding of AI's capabilities and limitations, engaging with it as a support tool rather than a substitute for original work — related
  • Unguarded answer-giving AI harmed unaided exam performance while a guarded version of the same model erased the harm (Bastani et al., 2025, as reported) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Review reports a field experiment in which GPT access improved supported practice but was followed by poorer unaided test performance, mitigated by a guarded tutor — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
