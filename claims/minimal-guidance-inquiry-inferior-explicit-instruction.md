## Subclaims

`q2 i1` The review reports, citing Alfieri et al. (2011), that inquiry with minimal or no guidance is less effective than explicit instruction (d = -0.38), whereas guided inquiry outperforms expository teaching of the same content (d = 0.30). [→ Lazonder 2016](#lazonder-2016)

`q2 i1` The article reports, citing Alfieri, Brooks, Aldrich and Tenenbaum, that random effects analyses of 580 comparisons favored explicit instruction over unassisted discovery, while 360 comparisons favored enhanced discovery learning over other forms of instruction (d = 0.30). ## Evidence

### Lazonder 2016

Lazonder, A. W., & Harmsen, R. (2016). Meta-analysis of inquiry-based learning: Effects of guidance. Review of Educational Research, 86(3), 681-718. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315627366

`q2 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Narrative review section reporting a prior meta-analysis; the review attributes to Alfieri et al. (2011) that "inquiry-based methods with minimal or no guidance are less effective than explicit instruction" while adequately guided inquiry beats expository methods. Design of the original meta-analysis is not described here.

> "Alfieri et al. (2011) found that across domains and settings, inquiry-based methods with minimal or no guidance are less effective than explicit instruction (d = −0.38). But when students receive adequate guidance during the inquiry, they learn more (d = 0.30) than students who are taught the same content by expository methods."

### Singaravelu 2012

Singaravelu, G. (2012). Discovery Learning Strategies in English. i-manager's Journal of English Language Teaching, Vol. 2 No. 1, January - March 2012. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1070208

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r1`

The article reports this second-hand from Alfieri et al.'s meta-analysis, citing it as showing enhanced discovery learning was favorable compared with other forms of instruction, with d = 0.30 and 95% CI [.23, .36].

> "In contrast, analyses of 360 comparisons revealed that outcomes were favorable for enhanced discovery learning when compared with other forms of instruction (d0.30,95% CI [.23, .36])."

### Singaravelu 2012 (2)

Singaravelu, G. (2012). Discovery Learning Strategies in English. i-manager's Journal of English Language Teaching, Vol. 2 No. 1, January - March 2012. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1070208

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The article attributes to the same meta-analysis the finding that explicit instruction was favorable compared with unassisted discovery under most conditions, and concludes that 'unassisted discovery does not benefit learners, whereas feedback, worked examples support the Discovery learning'.

> "Random effects analyses of 580 comparisons revealed that outcomes were favorable for explicit instruction when compared with unassisted discovery under most conditions"
