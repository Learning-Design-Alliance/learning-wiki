# Read, Write & Type!™ shows no statistically significant alphabetics advantages over the Auditory Discrimination in Depth® comparison

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Against the Auditory Discrimination in Depth® group, RWT showed no statistically significant differences on any of three phonological awareness measures or two phonics measures; the domain-average effect size was –0.14, favoring the comparison. ## Evidence

### What Works Clearinghouse 2007

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2007). WWC Intervention Report: Read, Write & Type!™. http://www.fcrr.org/TechnicalReports/RWTfullrept.pdf

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Randomized comparison of RWT (final analysis sample 52) with ADD (52) across five schools. Appendix A3.1 prints effect sizes of 0.02 (blending), –0.18 (elision), –0.15 (segmenting), –0.24 (word attack), and –0.14 (word identification), all ns; the alphabetics average for this comparison was –0.14, ns, improvement index –6.

> "the study authors found that there were no statistically significant differences between the groups on any of the three phonological awareness measures (phoneme blending, phoneme elision, and phoneme segmenting subtests of the Comprehensive Test of Phonological Processes) or the two phonics measures"
