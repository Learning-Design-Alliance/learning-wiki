# Teachers' job satisfaction is positively associated with the quality of teacher-student interactions, with the largest pooled correlation for general interactions (r = 0.28)

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Job satisfaction correlates positively with emotional support (r = 0.14), classroom management (r = 0.18), instructional support (r = 0.10), and general teacher-student interaction (r = 0.28). [→ Wartenberg 2023](#wartenberg-2023)

## Evidence

### Wartenberg 2023

Wartenberg, G., Aldrup, K., Grund, S., & Klusmann, U. (2023). Satisfied and High Performing? A Meta-Analysis and Systematic Review of the Correlates of Teachers' Job Satisfaction. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09831-4

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Random-effects meta-analyses across four interaction dimensions (k = 7 to 14 studies each), measured by teacher self-report, student ratings, classroom observations, and principal evaluation. All pooled correlations were positive and significant.

> "the relationships between teacher job satisfaction and teacher-student interactions were small but positive, with a correlation of r = 0.14, CIr = [0.08, 0.20] for emotional support, r = 0.18, CIr = [0.10, 0.25] for classroom management, r = 0.10, CIr = [0.05, 0.15] for instructional support, and r = 0.28, CIr = [0.21, 0.35] for general teacher-student interaction"
