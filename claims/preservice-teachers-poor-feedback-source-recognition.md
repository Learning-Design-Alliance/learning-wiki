## Evidence

### Jacobsen 2025

Jacobsen, L. J., Mertens, U., Jansen, T., & Weber, K. E. (2025). AI, Expert or Peer? Provider Biases and Feedback Uptake among Pre-Service Teachers. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2507.16013](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2507.16013)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive recognition-accuracy analysis in the Results section of the randomised experiment with 273 pre-service teachers, each assigned one of 30 messages. The article reports "Peer feedback was identified most accurately (46%)" and LLM detection "only slightly above chance (36%)".

> "Peer feedback was identified most accurately (46%), followed by expert feedback (40%). LLM feedback was detected only slightly above chance (36%)."

### Jacobsen 2025 (2)

Jacobsen, L. J., Mertens, U., Jansen, T., & Weber, K. E. (2025). AI, Expert or Peer? Provider Biases and Feedback Uptake among Pre-Service Teachers. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2507.16013](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2507.16013)

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANOVA comparing coded feedback quality across the three perceived sources among participants providing source judgements. Quality did not differ significantly across perceived sources (η² = .019), while message length was associated with judgements (b = 38.69, p = .008).

> "The difference was not significant, F(2, 259) = 2.54, p = .081, η² = .019, and none of the Bonferroni-adjusted pairwise comparisons reached significance (all ps ≥ .075), giving little indication that participants inferred the source from the quality of the message."
