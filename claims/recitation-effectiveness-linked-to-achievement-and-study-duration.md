# Perceived recitation effectiveness is strongly correlated with perceived learning achievement and rated higher by students who spent more than 6 years studying physics

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Perceived effectiveness of online recitations is highly correlated with perceived overall learning achievement (r = 0.66, p < 0.01). [→ P. Klein 2021](#p-klein-2021)
`q2 i?` Students who spent more than 6 years studying physics perceived the recitations as more effective than every other group of students [F(4, 389) = 5.04, p = 0.001]. [→ P. Klein 2021 (2)](#p-klein-2021-2)

## Evidence

### P. Klein 2021

P. Klein, L. Ivanjek, M.N. Dahlkemper, K. Jeličić, M.-A. Geyer, S. Küchemann, and A. Susac. (2021). Studying physics during the COVID-19 pandemic: Student assessments of learning achievement, perceived effectiveness of online recitations, and online laboratories. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010117](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010117)

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Effectiveness scale (seven 4-point Likert questions, α = 0.82, mean corresponding to 40.2%) collected from the 401 students who attended recitations. The article reports the correlation as "(r = 0.66, p< 0.01)".

> "It is worth noting that the perceived eﬀectiveness of the recitations is highly correlated with the perceived overall learning achievement (r = 0.66, p< 0.01)."

### P. Klein 2021 (2)

P. Klein, L. Ivanjek, M.N. Dahlkemper, K. Jeličić, M.-A. Geyer, S. Küchemann, and A. Susac. (2021). Studying physics during the COVID-19 pandemic: Student assessments of learning achievement, perceived effectiveness of online recitations, and online laboratories. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010117](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010117)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

ANOVA on perceived recitation effectiveness across duration-of-studies groups among recitation attendees. The article reports "F (4, 389) = 5 .04,p = 0.001" with the more-than-6-years group scoring "mean score: 2.95, corresponding to 65%"; no effect size is printed.

> "First, a signiﬁcant between-subjects eﬀect was found for the duration of studies [F (4, 389) = 5 .04,p = 0.001] and Bonferroni-corrected post-hoc comparisons revealed that students who spent more than 6 years studying physics perceived the recitations as more eﬀective than every other group of students (mean score: 2.95, corresponding to 65%; p< 0.01)."
