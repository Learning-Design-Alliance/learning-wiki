# Learners in the matched condition exhibited more meta-cognitive and cognitive activities—monitoring, preparation, and ending discussions—than learners in other conditions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Matched-condition learners showed more meta-cognitive activity overall and more monitoring specifically than simulation-only and mismatched learners. [→ B. Slof 2012](#b-slof-2012)

## Evidence

### B. Slof 2012

B. Slof, G. Erkens, P. A. Kirschner, J. Janssen, J. G. M. Jaspers. (2012). Successfully carrying out complex learning-tasks through guiding teams' qualitative and quantitative reasoning. Instr Sci 40:623–643. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-011-9185-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multilevel analysis of chat episodes (addressing non-independence of learner data) found matched learners more often discussed whether they had finished part-tasks on time (monitoring b = 3.96 and b = 4.17, P < 0.05); no effect sizes printed.

> "a category effect for meta-cognitive activities was found when comparing learners in the matched condition to learners in both the simulation-only (b = 5.37, P = 0.07) and mismatched conditions ( b = 6.17, P\ 0.05). Learners in the matched condition exhibited more meta-cognitive activities than learners in both other conditions."
