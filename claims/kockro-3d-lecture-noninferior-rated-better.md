# 3D virtual-reality lecture was statistically non-inferior to 2D lecture on knowledge and rated better on spatial understanding and enjoyment (Kockro et al.)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A randomised comparison of stereoscopic 3D and 2D lectures found no significant difference in mean MCQ scores, with 3D statistically non-inferior, and students rated the 3D method better in four experiential domains. [→ K Anniesmitha 2026](#k-anniesmitha-2026)

## Evidence

### K Anniesmitha 2026

K Anniesmitha, Rao K G Mohandas, Aithal P Ashwini, Bandekar Nikita N., Mandal Tatiyana, George Bincy M. (2026). Effectiveness of technology-enhanced learning in neuroanatomy education among health professionals - a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09764-w

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Randomised study of 169 second-year medical students comparing 3D VR and 2D lectures on a 10-question MCQ exam on third-ventricle anatomy. Means were "5.19 (± 2.12)" vs "5.45 (± 2.16)"; the 3D group was "not significantly inferior" (p < 0.0001).

> "The 2D group had a mean score of 5.19 (± 2.12), whereas the 3D group had a mean score of 5.45 (± 2.16). The findings of the 3D group were not significantly inferior to those of the 2D group (p < 0.0001)."
