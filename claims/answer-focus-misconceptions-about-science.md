## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Students' focus on the answer can exclude useful practices, make lab problems feel like personal failure, and, beyond class, support the dismissal of science because answers change. [→ Christopher M. Graney 2011](#christopher-m-graney-2011)

## Evidence

### Christopher M. Graney 2011

Christopher M. Graney. (2011). Teaching Galileo? Get to Know Riccioli! What a Forgotten Italian Astronomer Can Teach Students About How Science Works. [doi:10.1119/1.3670077](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.3670077)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

The author's interpretation of student behavior in his classes: students focus on "the answer" to the exclusion of practices like carefully drawing a free body diagram, and may acquire from the broader culture the idea that science should be dismissed when answers change.

> "Or, it leads to impatience in lab, as students don't view the problems naturally encountered in getting "the answer" in lab as normal and part of real science, but as signs that they are physics failures!"
