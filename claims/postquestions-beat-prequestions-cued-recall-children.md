## Evidence

### Lima 2020

Lima, N. K. d., & Jaeger, A. (2020). The Effects of Prequestions versus Postquestions on Memory Retention in Children. Journal of Applied Research in Memory and Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jarmac.2020.08.005

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1 phase-completion accuracy comparison among 27 fifth graders; postquestion completion accuracy (0.52) exceeded prequestion accuracy (0.34), a medium effect with "t(26) = 6.05, p < 0.001, d = 0.66".

> "children produced more correct answers to postquestions than to prequestions, a difference that was statistically significant, t(26) = 6.05, p < 0.001, d = 0.66"

### Lima 2020 (2)

Lima, N. K. d., & Jaeger, A. (2020). The Effects of Prequestions versus Postquestions on Memory Retention in Children. Journal of Applied Research in Memory and Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jarmac.2020.08.005

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 with 100 fourth graders reading a Sun text with 21 target-words, tested after 7 days; the cued recall advantage of postquestions over prequestions was significant but small ("t(99) = 2.78, p = 0.006, d = 0.24").

> "postquestions were more beneficial for perfor- mance in the cued recall test than prequestions, which in turn were more beneficial than restudy, postquestions vs. preques- tions, t(99) = 2.78, p = 0.006, d = 0.24"
