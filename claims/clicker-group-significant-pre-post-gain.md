# The experimental group's ESP scores improved significantly from pre-test to post-test under clicker use, while the control group's did not

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Wilcoxon Signed Rank Test results showed a statistically significant pre-to-post improvement in the experimental group (Z = -3.66, p = .000), with 17 of 19 students improving; the control group's change was not significant (Z = -1.707, p = .088). [→ Asmalı 2018](#asmal-2018)

## Evidence

### Asmalı 2018

Mehmet Asmalı. (2018). Integrating Technology into ESP Classes: Use of Student Response System in English for Specific Purposes Instruction. Teaching English with Technology, 18(3), 86-104. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1186385

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Within-group pre-post analysis in the same quasi-experiment: the experimental group's mean rose from 48.95 to 61.35 with "Z = -3.66, p = .000" and 17 of 19 students improving, while the control group's change from 42.70 to 44.45 was not significant (Z = -1.707, p = .088). No effect size was printed.

> "use of clickers in the experimental group for a 10-week period elicited a statistically significant change in students’ performance in ESP classes according to Wilcoxon Signed Rank Test results"
