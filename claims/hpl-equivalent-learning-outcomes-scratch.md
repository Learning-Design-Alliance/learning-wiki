## Evidence

### Brender 2025

Brender, J., El-Hamamsy, L., Giang, C., Mathex, L., Käser, T., & Mondada, F. (2025). The handwriting programming language for primary school: aligning computer science education with established teaching practices. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10503-z

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mixed-effects repeated measures ANOVA on pre-/post-test scores from the validated lean CT test (Wiebe et al., 2019), nesting students in groups and groups in classes, with the interaction between test time and modality. The model (n=108) found "an equivalent improve- ment in both conditions" and equivalent post-test scores across conditions.

> "the model (n=108, see Table 7) indicates that: (i) there is an equivalent improve- ment in both conditions ( /u1D6FD1 =− 0.94 , p < 0.0001 , and non-significant /u1D6FD2 and /u1D6FD3 ), (ii) HPL and Scratch students’ post-test scores are equivalent"

### Brender 2025 (2)

Brender, J., El-Hamamsy, L., Giang, C., Mathex, L., Käser, T., & Mondada, F. (2025). The handwriting programming language for primary school: aligning computer science education with established teaching practices. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10503-z

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Kruskal-Wallis test on normalised change scores for students answering both pre- and post-test. Although HPL students appeared to progress slightly more, the difference was not significant (H = 0.09, p = 0.7665), suggesting HPL is at least as effective as Scratch for learning.

> "While it would appear that the students in the HPL condition progressed slightly more than those in the Scratch condition, this difference is not significant (Kruskal-Wallis H = 0.09, p = 0.7665)"
