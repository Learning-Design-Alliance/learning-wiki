## Evidence

### Laura R. Novick and Kefyn M. Catley 2016

Laura R. Novick and Kefyn M. Catley. (2016). Fostering 21st-Century Evolutionary Reasoning: Teaching Tree Thinking to Introductory Biology Students. CBE—Life Sciences Education 15:ar66. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-06-0127

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Response-frequency analysis of the five-option multiple-choice evolutionary sequence question about Sellosaurus and Vulcanodon, on both pretest and posttest. The percentages are descriptive; no inferential test or effect size is reported for this contrast.

> "nearly half the students (47%) selected the incorrect alternative that included the three synapomorphies plus the two autapomorphies, approximately one-third (32%) chose the correct answer"

### Laura R. Novick and Kefyn M. Catley 2016 (2)

Laura R. Novick and Kefyn M. Catley. (2016). Fostering 21st-Century Evolutionary Reasoning: Teaching Tree Thinking to Introductory Biology Students. CBE—Life Sciences Education 15:ar66. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-06-0127

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

From the Discussion: for evolutionary sequence there were no significant effects in the ANOVA, and the authors note the question required students to go beyond what they had specifically been taught.

> "The one tree-thinking skill for which our experimental instruction did not lead to numerically larger improvement than business-as-usual instruction was evolutionary sequence."
