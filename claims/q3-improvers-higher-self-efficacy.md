## Evidence

### Christopher J. Fischer 2021

Christopher J. Fischer, Jennifer Delgado, Sarah LeGresley. (2021). Interplay between physics self-efficacy, calculus transfer ability, and gender. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2110.07364](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2110.07364)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Subset analysis of students who answered question 3 incorrectly on the pre-test (N = 183), comparing post-test correct (N = 58) versus incorrect (N = 125) groups' self-efficacy change; the article reports "the Z-statistic for this comparison is 2.19".

> "the average self-efficacy of the subset of these students who answered question 3 incorrectly on the post-test (N = 125) decreased over the semester while the average self-efficacy of the subset of these students who answered question 3 correctly on the post-test (N = 58) increased; the Z-statistic for this comparison is 2.19."
