## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Friendships were more likely to be maintained under mutual help than one-sided help, and under one-sided help than no help. [→ van Rijsewijk 2019](#van-rijsewijk-2019)

## Evidence

### van Rijsewijk 2019

van Rijsewijk, L. G. M., Snijders, T. A. B., Dijkstra, J. K., Steglich, C., & Veenstra, R. (2019). The Interplay Between Adolescents' Friendships and the Exchange of Help: A Longitudinal Multiplex Social Network Study. Journal of Research on Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1111/jora.12501

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bayesian random-effects RSiena analysis of 41 classrooms (N = 953 students, three waves) modeling friendship co-evolution with help. The article reports a positive effect of "mutual versus one‐sided help on friendship maintenance" (η = 1.14, SD = .22, p > .99), and also a positive effect of one-sided help versus no help (η = 0.92).

> "In line with our first hypothesis, there was a positive effect of mutual versus one‐sided help on friendship maintenance (parameter 4; η = 1.14, SD = .22, p = > .99)."
