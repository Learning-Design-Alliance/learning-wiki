# In an electric-potentials laboratory, students who used either a prebuilt simulation or an AI-generated simulation scored significantly higher on immediate conceptual questions than students using physical equipment (η² = 0.359).

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Median conceptual-question performance differed significantly across the three lab types, H(2)=58.718, p<.001, with a strong effect size (η² = .359). [→ Yossi Ben-Zion 2026](#yossi-ben-zion-2026)
`q2 i?` Dunn's post-hoc tests showed no significant difference between the AI and prebuilt simulation groups, but both simulation groups significantly outperformed the traditional physical laboratory group. [→ Yossi Ben-Zion 2026 (2)](#yossi-ben-zion-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Yossi Ben-Zion 2026

Yossi Ben-Zion, Turhan K. Carroll, Colin G. West, Jesse Wong, and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2026). Leveraging generative artificial intelligence for simulation-based physics experiments: A new approach to virtual learning about the real world. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1103/s8dy-kqy5](https://doi.org/10.1103/s8dy-kqy5)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Nonparametric omnibus test (Kruskal-Wallis) of lab-review conceptual-question scores across three quasi-experimental lab conditions in an IPLS course; the article reports the effect was strong (η2 =.359), with medians that "differed significantly across our three lab types".

> "We found that median performance on CQs differed significantly across our three lab types, HCQTot(2)=58.718, p<.001."

### Yossi Ben-Zion 2026 (2)

Yossi Ben-Zion, Turhan K. Carroll, Colin G. West, Jesse Wong, and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2026). Leveraging generative artificial intelligence for simulation-based physics experiments: A new approach to virtual learning about the real world. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1103/s8dy-kqy5](https://doi.org/10.1103/s8dy-kqy5)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Dunn's post-hoc analysis of the same conceptual-question scores reported adjusted p-values of 0.000 for both AI vs Lab and Sim vs Lab, and 0.258 for AI vs Sim; "both performed significantly better than students in the traditional lab section".

> "We can conclude that students in the AI and Sim lab groups both performed significantly better than students in the traditional lab section."
