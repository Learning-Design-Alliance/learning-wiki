## Evidence

### Catharina Tibken 2025

Catharina Tibken, Simon P. Tiffin-Richards. (2025). Reading behavior as an indicator of comprehension monitoring when reading expository texts. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09440-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Eye-tracking experiment with 96 adults reading 20 short expository texts; GLMM simple-slopes analysis of rereading time at the target word. The article reports that "longer rereading time of the target word was positively associated with consistency judgment accuracy in inconsistent texts" with no effect for consistent texts.

> "A longer rereading time of the target word was positively associated with consistency judgment accuracy in inconsistent texts (b = 0.96, SE = 0.18, t = 7.47, p <.001), while there was no such effect for consistent texts (b = -0.06, SE = 0.17, t = -0.35, p =.726)."

### Catharina Tibken 2025 (2)

Catharina Tibken, Simon P. Tiffin-Richards. (2025). Reading behavior as an indicator of comprehension monitoring when reading expository texts. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09440-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Same eye-tracking experiment, GLMM simple-slopes analysis of revisit probability at the target word. The article reports "a higher revisit probability of the target word in inconsistent texts was significantly associated with higher consistency judgement accuracy" with no effect for consistent texts.

> "Similarly, a higher revisit probability of the target word in inconsistent texts was significantly associated with higher consistency judgement accuracy (b = 1.47, SE = 0.18, t = 8.36, p <.001), while there was no such effect for consistent texts (b = -0.08, SE = 0.23, t = -0.34, p =.731)."
