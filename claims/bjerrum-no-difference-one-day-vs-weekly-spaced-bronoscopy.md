## Evidence

### Fahl Jan Torge 2023

Fahl Jan Torge, Duvivier Robbert, Reinke Laurens, Pierie Jean-Pierre E. N., Schönrock-Adema Johanna. (2023). Towards best practice in developing motor skills: a systematic review on spacing in VR simulator-based psychomotor training for surgical novices. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04046-1

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Randomized control trial (n=20 postgraduate medical students) included in the review, comparing three weekly sessions with three same-day sessions on a bronchoscopy simulator. The review reports "No main effect of group (p = n.s.)"; no effect size was printed.

> "No main effect of group (p = n.s.), thus no difference in test scores between one-day spaced training and weekly distributed training."

### Vieira Lais Meirelles Nicoliello 2022

Vieira Lais Meirelles Nicoliello, Camargos Paulo Augusto Moreira, Ibiapina Cássio da Cunha. (2022). Bronchoscopy simulation training in the post-pandemic world. Jornal Brasileiro de Pneumologia. https://doi.org/10.36416/1806-3756/e20210361

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Randomized study (Bjerrum et al., n = 20 pulmonology residents) with pre-test, post-test, and four-week retention test comparing single-day vs. one-week training; the quote reports equivalence with "p > 0.36". Equivalence was not formally tested; no effect size printed.

> "The effectiveness of bronchoscopy simulation training on a single day has been reported to be the same as that of training over one week (p > 0.36), a finding that suggests that bronchoscopy simulation training can be conducted in a format that best suits trainees and training centers."
