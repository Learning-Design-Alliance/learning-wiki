# Sequence order affected performance time and mistakes: later runs were faster, and switching to 3D reduced mistakes in the 4K-first sequence

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Parkour time was shorter in the second run within each sequence (e.g., 4K before 3D 1098.8 s ± 36.5 s vs 4K after 3D 900.1 s ± 34.5 s, P < 0.001), and mistakes dropped from 14.7 ± 1.0 to 11.8 ± 0.9 (P = 0.04) when switching from 4K to 3D. [→ Wahba R 2020](#wahba-r-2020)

## Evidence

### Wahba R 2020

Wahba R, Datta R, Bußhoff J, Bruns T, Hedergott A, Gietzelt C, Dieplinger G, Fuchs H, Morgenstern B, Möller D, Hellmich M, Bruns CJ, Stippel DL. (2020). 3D Versus 4K Display System – Influence of “State-of-the-art”-Display Technique on Surgical Performance (IDOSP-study) in Minimally Invasive Surgery: A Randomized Cross-over Trial. Annals of Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1097/SLA.0000000000004328

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Sequence analysis from the cross-over trial's results. The quote reports the printed means and P-values for both sequences; the 3D-before-4K time contrast is reported with P > 0.001 as printed.

> "The overall Parkour time for the sequence 3D before 4K versus 3D after 4K was 790.4 s ± 25.2 s versus 634.6 s ± 24.4 s (P > 0.001) and for the sequence 4K before 3D versus 4K after 3D it was 1098.8 s ± 36.5 s versus 900.1 s ± 34.5 s (P < 0.001). The overall number of mistakes for the sequences 3D before 4K versus 3D after 4K was 10.1 ± 0.8 versus 10.0 ± 0.8 (P = 0.96) and for the sequence 4K before 3D versus 4K after 3D it was 14.7 ± 1.0 versus 11.8 ± 0.9 (P = 0.04)."
