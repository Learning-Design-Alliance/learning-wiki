## Evidence

### Chang W 2025

Chang W, Lin C-C, Crilly J, Lee H-L, Chen L-C, Han C-Y. (2025). Virtual Reality Simulation for Undergraduate Nursing Students for Care of Patients With Infectious Diseases: Mixed Methods Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/64780

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Phase 1 quasi-experimental comparison of a VRS group (n=47) and a traditional-teaching control group (n=60) in a Taiwanese university infection control unit, measured with a 10-question written test at pretest (T0) and posttest (T1). The study reports a "significantly higher posttest scores compared with the control group (t98.13=2.70; P=.008)".

> "However, the VRS group achieved significantly higher posttest scores compared with the control group (t98.13=2.70; P=.008), suggesting that VRS teaching was more effective in enhancing students’ knowledge (Table 2 and Figure 3)."
