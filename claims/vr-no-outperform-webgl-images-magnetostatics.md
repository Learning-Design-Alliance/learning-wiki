## Evidence

### Porter 2020

Porter, C. D., Brown, J., Smith, J. R., Simmons, A., Nieberding, M., Ayers, A., & Orban, C. (2020). A controlled study of virtual reality in first-year magnetostatics. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/perc.2019.pr.porter](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2019.pr.porter)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated measures analysis in SPSS of the randomized three-treatment study (VR N=95, WebGL N=106, Images N=88) found "statistically significant differences between treatment groups" on neither all items nor the superposition subscale; no effect size is printed.

> "Adding the treatment type as a between -subjects factor in the repeated measures analysis again does not reveal statistically significant differences between treatment groups on all items ( p > 0.4), nor on the superpositions subscale (p > 0.8)."

### Porter 2020 (2)

Porter, C. D., Brown, J., Smith, J. R., Simmons, A., Nieberding, M., Ayers, A., & Orban, C. (2020). A controlled study of virtual reality in first-year magnetostatics. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/perc.2019.pr.porter](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2019.pr.porter)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated measures pre-post analysis of the 289-participant randomized study showed no treatment group made statistically significant gains on all items; the article reports p = 0.27 and no effect size.

> "None of the treatment groups showed statistically significant gains from the pretest to posttest when all items are included in the score (see Fig. 3). This was tested using a repeated measures analysis in SPSS which yielded p = 0.27."
