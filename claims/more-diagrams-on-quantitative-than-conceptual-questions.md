# Students draw more diagrams and do more scratchworks on quantitative than conceptual multiple-choice questions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 causal `r1` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2016 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Regardless of group, students were more likely to draw diagrams for quantitative questions than conceptual questions (p=0.000), and the same holds for scratchworks; no effect size is printed. [→ Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#andrew-mason-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)

`q2 i?` Students in both groups were more likely to draw diagrams or do scratch work for quantitative problems than for conceptual questions (e.g., traditional group: 4.3 vs 2.7 diagrams). [→ Andrew J. Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2015](#andrew-j-mason-and-chandralekha-singh-2015)

## Evidence

### Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Helping students learn effective problem solving strategies by reflecting with peers. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05628](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05628)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of diagram and scratchwork counts by question type in Table 2 for both groups; the corresponding scratchwork comparison also gives "statistically significant difference at the level of p = 0.000". No effect size is printed.

> "The comparison of the number of problems with diagrams for quantitative vs. conceptual problems for each of the traditional (N=107) and PR (N=93) groups displayed in Table 2 gives statistically signiﬁcant diﬀerence at the level of p = 0 .000."

### Andrew J. Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2015

Andrew J. Mason and Chandralekha Singh. (2015). Impact of guided reflection with peers on the development of effective problem solving strategies and physics learning. [doi:10.1119/1.4947159](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4947159)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive comparison from Table 1 by question type on the final exam (e.g., traditional diagrams 4.3 quantitative vs 2.7 conceptual; PR 5.1 vs 3.5). The article reports the trend descriptively with no statistical test or effect size printed.

> "Students in both groups were more likely to draw diagrams or do scratch work for quantitative problems than for the conceptual questions. While more scratch work is expected on quantitative problems, it is not clear a priori that more diagrams will be drawn for the quantitative problems"
