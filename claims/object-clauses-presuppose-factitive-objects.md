# Object clauses of verbs like know, think, believe and recommend presuppose factitive objects

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Verbs such as suggest and recommend have an appositional property: their object clauses are in apposition with an F object, so an underlying cognate string like 'He suggested the suggestion that they reconsider' must be posited. [→ Babcock 1967](#babcock-1967)

## Evidence

### Babcock 1967

Babcock, Sandra Scharff. (1967). Syntactic Dissimilation. ERIC. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED024020

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Analytical argument from topicalized and appositional alternants such as 'His suggestion was that they reconsider' and 'What he suggested was that they reconsider.' Since a noun clause can only be in apposition with another noun of the same category, an F noun must underlie these sentences.

> "It follows from this analysis that the object clauses of such verbs as know, think, believe, km paged, recommend, and an important list of others, presuppose the presence of Fobjects."
