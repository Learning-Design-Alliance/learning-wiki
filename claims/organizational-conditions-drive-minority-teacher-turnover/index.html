Evidence¶

Ingersoll 2017¶

Ingersoll, R., May, H., & Collins, G. (2017). Minority teacher recruitment, employment, and retention: 1987 to 2013. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/minority-teacher-recruitment

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Logistic regression Model 12 in Table 5b, controlling for teacher, school, and demographic characteristics. The report calls this "the strongest relationship we found": "a 40% difference in the odds of a minority teacher departing" per one-unit difference in classroom autonomy.

"A one-unit difference in reported teacher classroom autonomy (on a four-unit scale) was associated with a 40% difference in the odds of a minority teacher departing."

Ingersoll 2017 (2)¶

Ingersoll, R., May, H., & Collins, G. (2017). Minority teacher recruitment, employment, and retention: 1987 to 2013. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/minority-teacher-recruitment

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Logistic regression Model 11 in Table 5b. The report describes faculty decision-making influence as "one of the strongest relationships we found, and especially so for minority teachers," with a 37% decrease in departure odds per one-unit increase.

"A one-unit increase in reported faculty influence between schools (on a four-unit scale) was associated with a 37% decrease in the odds of a minority teacher departing."
