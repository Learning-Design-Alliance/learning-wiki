# Traditional classroom food safety training of managerial employees produced cumulative knowledge gains essentially no larger than those in untreated control stores

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Cumulatively, managerial employees in treatment stores gained 4.5 percentage points after interventional training while control-store managers gained 4.6 points, a difference the authors describe as one-tenth of a percent. [→ Caleb D. Dodd 2013](#caleb-d-dodd-2013)
`q2 i?` Non-managerial employees, who received no direct training, showed a cumulative change of 1.7% in treatment stores versus a 0.8% decrease in control stores. [→ Caleb D. Dodd 2013 (2)](#caleb-d-dodd-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Caleb D. Dodd 2013

Caleb D. Dodd, Scott Burris, Steve Fraze, David Doerfert, Abigail McCulloch. (2013). Evaluating the Effectiveness of Traditional Training Methods in Non-Traditional Training Programs for Adult Learners through a Pre-test/Post-test Comparison of Food Safety Knowledge. Journal of Agricultural Education. https://doi.org/10.5032/jae.2013.01018

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental pre-test/post-test study of managerial employees across three grocery chains; treatment managers received interventional classroom training while control stores received none. Scores are descriptive percentages; the article reports no significance test or effect size, so the near-identical changes (4.6 vs 4.5 points) are untested differences.

> "Cumulatively, the control group had lower pre-training (71.3%) and post-training (75.9%) scores than the treatment group (74.8%, 79.3%). However, the difference in the amount of change that occurred over time between both groups was one-tenth of a percent."

### Caleb D. Dodd 2013 (2)

Caleb D. Dodd, Scott Burris, Steve Fraze, David Doerfert, Abigail McCulloch. (2013). Evaluating the Effectiveness of Traditional Training Methods in Non-Traditional Training Programs for Adult Learners through a Pre-test/Post-test Comparison of Food Safety Knowledge. Journal of Agricultural Education. https://doi.org/10.5032/jae.2013.01018

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive comparison of non-managerial employees' pre/post knowledge scores in control versus treatment stores; non-managers received no additional training. The article prints only percentage changes, no test statistic or effect size.

> "A slight decrease of 0.8% was scored on the post-training score in the control group with a slight increase of 1.7% occurring in the treatment group."
