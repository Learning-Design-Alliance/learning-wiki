# Need-based grant aid improves persistence and completion while merit-only aid shows no detectable effect on these outcomes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Need-based grant aid programs raise year-to-year persistence by 2.5 percentage points with a precise standard error of .005. [→ Nguyen 2018](#nguyen-2018)
`q3 i?` Merit-only financial aid programs do not appear to affect persistence, though the standard error is sizable. [→ Nguyen 2018 (2)](#nguyen-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Nguyen 2018

Nguyen, T.D., Kramer, J.W., & Evans, B.J. (2018). The Effects of Grant Aid on Student Persistence and Degree Attainment: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of the Causal Evidence (CEPA Working Paper No. 18-04). Stanford Center for Education Policy Analysis. http://cepa.stanford.edu/wp18-04

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Subgroup analysis by eligibility type in the year-to-year persistence moderator results (Table 3); programs with a need-based component showed a 2.5 percentage-point effect with standard error .005, 95% CI 1.4 to 3.5 percentage points as printed.

> "In contrast, grant aid programs that include a need-based component seem to have a substantial impact on persistence at 2.5 percentage points with a precise standard error of .005."

### Nguyen 2018 (2)

Nguyen, T.D., Kramer, J.W., & Evans, B.J. (2018). The Effects of Grant Aid on Student Persistence and Degree Attainment: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of the Causal Evidence (CEPA Working Paper No. 18-04). Stanford Center for Education Policy Analysis. http://cepa.stanford.edu/wp18-04

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Subgroup analysis of merit-only programs in the same moderator table; the authors report merit-only aid "do not appear to affect persistence," with a fairly sizable standard error. This is a non-detected effect, not a tested equivalence.

> "Lastly, we find that merit-only financial aid programs do not appear to affect persistence although the standard error is fairly sizable."
