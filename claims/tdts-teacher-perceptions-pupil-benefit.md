# TDTS teachers reported more positive views of pupils' ability and engagement, and judged the approach beneficial across the ability range including pupils with special educational needs

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` TDTS teachers were much more likely than control teachers to agree with positive statements about their pupils' ability, confidence and motivation in science, especially enjoyment and engagement. [→ Pam Hanley 2015](#pam-hanley-2015)
`q2 i?` TDTS participants were more inclined than control teachers to say their approach benefited pupils with special educational needs (49% vs 24%) and low ability pupils (55% vs 14%). [→ Pam Hanley 2015](#pam-hanley-2015)

## Evidence

### Pam Hanley 2015

Pam Hanley, Robert Slavin, Louise Elliott. (2015). Thinking, Doing, Talking Science: Evaluation report and Executive summary. Education Endowment Foundation. http://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN54969918

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Teacher survey item asking intervention and control teachers which groups the approach benefited; percentages as printed. Around three-quarters of TDTS teachers agreed strongly they had used the strategies, found the training inspiring, and that it had benefited their pupils.

> "TDTS participants were more inclined to mention those with special educational needs (49% vs 24%). The intervention appeared to be judged effective across the ability range, with 55% (vs 14% control) saying it was beneficial for low ability pupils, 46% (vs 33% control) for middle ability, and 52% (vs 24% control) for high ability."
