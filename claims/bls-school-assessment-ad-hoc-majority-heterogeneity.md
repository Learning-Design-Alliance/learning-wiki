## Evidence

### Mecías‐Calvo M. 2026

Mecías‐Calvo M., Sobrido‐Prieto M., Navarro‐Patón R. (2026). Instruments for Assessing Basic Life Support Knowledge in Secondary School Students: A Scoping Review and Expert Panel. The Journal of School Health. https://doi.org/10.1111/josh.70223

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Scoping review synthesis of 36 studies assessing BLS knowledge in secondary school students. The review reports that "the majority of instruments (25/36) were ad hoc," attributing this to the absence of defined evaluation methods in secondary education.

> "Due to the absence of defined methods for evaluating BLS knowledge in secondary education, the majority of instruments (25/36) were ad hoc."

### Mecías‐Calvo M. 2026 (2)

Mecías‐Calvo M., Sobrido‐Prieto M., Navarro‐Patón R. (2026). Instruments for Assessing Basic Life Support Knowledge in Secondary School Students: A Scoping Review and Expert Panel. The Journal of School Health. https://doi.org/10.1111/josh.70223

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative synthesis in the review's conclusions. The authors state that ad hoc instruments "differ in the number of items, response formats, and content," and that this heterogeneity "limits comparability between studies" regarding program effectiveness.

> "Most instruments are ad hoc and differ in the number of items, response formats, and content. This limits comparability between studies and makes it difficult to draw conclusions about the effectiveness of educational programs."
