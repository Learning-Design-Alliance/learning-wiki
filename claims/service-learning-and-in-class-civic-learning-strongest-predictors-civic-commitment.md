# Service learning experiences and in-class civic learning opportunities have a larger impact on high school students' commitment to civic participation than other school, family, or community influences

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among the school, family, and community factors studied, service learning and in-class civic learning opportunities were most strongly related to students' civic commitments two years later, controlling for prior commitment. [→ Susan E. Sporte and Joseph E. Kahne 2007](#susan-e-sporte-and-joseph-e-kahne-2007)

## Evidence

### Susan E. Sporte and Joseph E. Kahne 2007

Susan E. Sporte and Joseph E. Kahne. (2007). Educating for Democracy: Lessons from Chicago. Consortium on Chicago School Research Research Brief. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/educating-democracy-lessons-chicago

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal survey study of 3,805 students in 47 Chicago Public Schools high schools, surveyed as freshmen and again as juniors, examining classroom civic learning opportunities after taking prior commitment into account. The study found that "service learning experiences and in-class opportunities to learn about civic issues have a larger impact" than all other influences studied.

> "service learning experiences and in-class opportunities to learn about civic issues have a larger impact on students' commitment to civic participation than all of the other influences included in the study"

Figure 1 plots the relative effects of classroom, family, neighborhood, and extracurricular factors on 2005 civic commitments; the text states that "in-class civic learning opportunities and service learning were most strongly related to students' civic commitments as juniors."

> "in-class civic learning opportunities and service learning were most strongly related to students' civic commitments as juniors"
