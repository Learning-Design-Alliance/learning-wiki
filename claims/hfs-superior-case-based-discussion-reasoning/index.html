Evidence¶

Eid Farida 2026¶

Eid Farida, Maryum Rida, Waugh Lucy, Kilian Paddy, Atiomo William, Al-Hammadi Suleiman. (2026). Simulation-Based Education (SBE) in paediatric undergraduate medical education: a scoping review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09730-6

q2 · i? · review · r2

Narrative review assertion attributed to the included primary studies (Moliterno et al., quasi-experimental, n = 33; Couto et al., quasi-experimental, n = 108). The review reports "superior knowledge retention and clinical performance compared to traditional case-based discussions" but does not aggregate effect sizes.

"High-fidelity simulation (HFS) has been shown to offer superior knowledge retention and clinical performance compared to traditional case-based discussions."

  • High-fidelity manikins and virtual reality were the most common paediatric SBE modalities across the reviewed studies — related

  • Thirty empirical studies of paediatric simulation-based education in undergraduate medical education were identified, predominantly randomized controlled trials and quasi-experimental designs — related
  • Virtual reality was particularly effective for procedural skill development and knowledge retention in paediatric undergraduate training — related
  • Manikins show anatomical discrepancies against human imaging data, yet may still support effective skill acquisition, suggesting educational fidelity can matter more than anatomical replication — related
  • High-fidelity simulation improves ECT procedural skills substantially but does not reliably improve theoretical knowledge or confidence — related
  • VR tools enhance acquisition and retention of clinical and procedural skills but face barriers including cybersickness, limited tactile feedback and variable long-term skill transfer — related
  • High-fidelity simulation methods (simulators, 3D models, cadaver-based practice) are associated with superior Acquisition retention, though some studies report contradictory findings — related
