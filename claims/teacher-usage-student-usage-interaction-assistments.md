# Teacher usage interacted with student usage: low- and non-using regular students performed better with high-usage teachers, with no overall teacher usage main effect

> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2010 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a 2 × 3 ANCOVA, the interaction between teacher usage and student usage was significant, F(2, 734) = 3.67, p = .03, while the teacher usage main effect was not, F(1, 734) = .395, p = .53. [→ Koedinger 2010](#koedinger-2010)
`q3 i?` Non-user students performed better with high usage teachers (M = 58.27) than low usage teachers (M = 53.93), F(1, 734) = 3.81, p = .05; the low-usage contrast was not significant and high users did marginally worse. [→ Koedinger 2010 (2)](#koedinger-2010-2)

## Evidence

### Koedinger 2010

Koedinger, K. R., McLaughlin, E. A., & Heffernan, N. T. (2010). A quasi-experimental evaluation of an on-line formative assessment and tutoring system. Journal of Educational Computing Research, 43(4), 489-510. https://doi.org/10.2190/EC.43.4.d

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

2 × 3 ANCOVA with teacher usage (high vs. low) and student usage (high vs. low vs. none) as factors and pre-test as covariate among regular students; the article interprets this as consistent with teachers adapting instruction from ASSISTments reports.

> "But, the interaction between teacher usage and student usage is significant, F(2, 734) = 3.67, p = .03."

### Koedinger 2010 (2)

Koedinger, K. R., McLaughlin, E. A., & Heffernan, N. T. (2010). A quasi-experimental evaluation of an on-line formative assessment and tutoring system. Journal of Educational Computing Research, 43(4), 489-510. https://doi.org/10.2190/EC.43.4.d

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Simple main effects: for low usage students the high-teacher advantage was not significant, F(1, 734) = .734, p = .39; high usage students with high usage teachers appeared to do worse, marginally significant, F(1, 734) = 3.12, p = .08.

> "Students who were non-users performed better with high usage teachers (M = 58.27, SE = 1.86) than with low usage teachers (M = 53.93, SE = 1.22), F(1, 734) = 3.81, p = .05."
