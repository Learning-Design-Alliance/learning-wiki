## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The Ebbinghaus forgetting curve—large losses at short retention intervals, leveling off to smaller incremental losses at longer intervals—has been found for meaningful as well as meaningless materials, with higher retention levels and a more expanded time scale for meaningful material. [→ Custers 2010](#custers-2010)

## Evidence

### Custers 2010

Eugène J. F. M. Custers. (2010). Long-term retention of basic science knowledge: a review study. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-008-9101-y

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Narrative review of laboratory retention studies beginning with Ebbinghaus's self-experiments on nonsense syllables; the review reports the curve form is the same for meaningless and meaningful materials but "the level of retention is considerably higher in the latter case". No effect size printed.

> "This function is characterized by large losses at short retention intervals, after which the curve levels off to smaller (but incremental) losses at longer intervals."
