### Haider and Frensch 2002

Hilde Haider & Peter A. Frensch. (2002). Why Aggregated Learning Follows the Power Law of Practice When Individual Learning Does Not: Comment on Rickard (1997, 1999), Delaney et al. (1998), and Palmeri (1999). Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.28.2.392

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Reanalysis of Haider and Frensch (1999a, Experiment 1): 45 participants received a transfer block in which errors could appear in the irrelevant string portion; error rate there served as the strategy-change indicator. The discriminant analysis "provided correct categorization of 37 out of 45 participants", with 17 of 18 linearity violators showing high error rates (chi-square(1, N = 45) = 20.37, p < .01, Table 3).

> "This analysis provided correct categorization of 37 out of 45 participants (see Table 3). More specifically, 17 out of 18 participants with violations of linearity also showed high error rates."
