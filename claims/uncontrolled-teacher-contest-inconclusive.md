# A head-to-head teaching contest between a primarily visual method and a combined method produced nearly identical averages (22.9 vs 23.0) and could not decide between the methods because of uncontrolled confounds

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The Head Master's visual-exposure method averaged 22.9 words correct and the assistant's combined method 23.0, a difference the author judged uninterpretable. [→ Winch 1913](#winch-1913)
`q1 i?` The author identifies four reasons the contest proved nothing: untested list equality, teachers of unequal experience and skill, unequal lesson placement (mornings vs afternoons), and unequally practised children. [→ Winch 1913 (2)](#winch-1913-2)

## Evidence

### Winch 1913

Winch, W. H. (1913). Experimental researches on learning to spell. Part II. *Journal of Educational Psychology, 4*(10), 579–592. [doi:10.1037/h0076021](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0076021)

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Subsidiary experiment, fourth method: Head Master and assistant teacher each taught 30 words for a week by their own method with Standard I boys, timing each other and keeping to stipulated methods, but without pre-testing list equality. Results were close and each teacher kept his own opinion.

> "On the following Monday the two lists were dictated. The average number of words spelt correctly by the Head Master's method was 22.9, and by the assistant's method was 23.0."

### Winch 1913 (2)

Winch, W. H. (1913). Experimental researches on learning to spell. Part II. *Journal of Educational Psychology, 4*(10), 579–592. [doi:10.1037/h0076021](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0076021)

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The author's own analysis of why the contest was inconclusive, listing untested word-list equality, unequal teacher skill, unequal lesson times, and unequally practised children; he concluded they "should have to work with fresh and unsophisticated children."

> "First of all, we did not know that the two sets of words just employed were equal in difficulty for the class. Secondly, the two methods were taught by two teachers not of equal experi- ence and skill."
