# Delegates' mOSATS scores for burr-hole skill rose from pre- to post-workshop assessment at a neurosurgical simulation workshop, in one single-group pre-post study

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Ten delegates' mean mOSATS score rose from 62.1% pre-workshop to 85.1% post-workshop; the pre-to-post rise was statistically significant on a paired-sample t test in one group with no comparison group. [→ Gough Melissa 2020](#gough-melissa-2020)

## Evidence

### Gough Melissa 2020

Gough Melissa, Solomou Georgios, Khan Danyal Zaman, Kamel Mohammed, Fountain Daniel, Kumaria Ashwin, Ashpole Richard, Sinha Saurabh, Mendoza Nigel. (2020). The evolution of an SBNS-accredited NANSIG simulated skills workshop for aspiring neurosurgical trainees: an analysis of qualitative and quantitative data. Acta Neurochirurgica. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00701-020-04325-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quantitative mOSATS assessment of a 10-delegate cohort (four foundation year doctors, six clinical medical students) at the 18 June 2019 workshop, scored by two assessors on burr-hole creation. Mean scores rose from "62.1% (21.75/35)" to "85.1% (29.8/35)"; the paired-sample t test was reported as significant (t(9) = −7.667, p = 3.105 as printed).

> "Delegates scored a mean total of 62.1% (21.75/35) and 85.1% (29.8/35) in pre- and post-workshop assessments, respectively (Table 1; Fig. 2). A 40.0% (8.05/35) average improvement was shown by the candidates between pre- and post-workshop assessment."
