Evidence¶

Schenck 2024¶

Schenck, A. (2024). Learning How to Use Input-Based and Output-Based Form-Focused Instruction: A Meta-Analytic Comparison of Persian EFL Learners. Journal of Second Language Acquisition and Teaching, 30. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1457419

q2 · i2 · quant-synthesis · r1

Collation of effect sizes across 18 Persian-L1 studies; Table 2 reports inter-phrasal input mean 3.5031 (n=12) vs output 2.7787 (n=9). The article calls the .72 difference "a medium difference in effect".

"At the inter-phrasal level, input-based instruction had a much larger impact than its output-based counterpart, yielding a difference in effect size of .72."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Input-based FFI is only slightly more effective than output-based FFI for intra-phrasal grammatical features in Persian EFL learners — related
  • Output-based FFI was more effective than input-based FFI for complex clausal grammatical features, a difference the article characterizes as a large effect — related
  • Grammatical complexity and L2 proficiency interact: complex features benefit more from input at low proficiency, while simple features benefit more from output at high proficiency — related
  • Input-based FFI was more effective than output-based FFI at both beginner and intermediate proficiency levels, and both types showed larger effects for intermediate learners — related
  • L1 transfer may moderate FFI effectiveness: features similar to Persian benefit more from input, while disparate features benefit more from output — related
