## Evidence

### Xusheng Dai 2025

Xusheng Dai, Zhaochun Wen, Jianxiao Jiang, Huiqin Liu, Yu Zhang. (2025). How Students Use AI Feedback Matters: Experimental Evidence on Physics Achievement and Autonomy. Preprint, under review. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2505.08672](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.08672)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Offline validation experiment: ten high school physics teachers and the GAI workflow each responded to ten flawed student responses; three expert teachers blind-reviewed the source. The article reports "no significant differences between the human-generated and AI-generated feedback"; no effect size is printed.

> "Results from independent samples t-tests revealed no significant differences between the human-generated and AI-generated feedback."

### Xusheng Dai 2025 (2)

Xusheng Dai, Zhaochun Wen, Jianxiao Jiang, Huiqin Liu, Yu Zhang. (2025). How Students Use AI Feedback Matters: Experimental Evidence on Physics Achievement and Autonomy. Preprint, under review. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2505.08672](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.08672)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive user-experience data collected from Group A participants during Experiment 1. Of 112 voluntary ratings, "82 ratings (73.2%) indicated "very helpful,"" which the authors take as evidence of high user satisfaction.

> "A total of 112 voluntary ratings were collected. Among them, 82 ratings (73.2%) indicated "very helpful," 28 ratings (25%) indicated "somewhat helpful," and 2 ratings (1.8%) indicated "not helpful.""
