## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across professions, one's own trac intensity negatively predicted stereotype change; the effect was significant for medical students (β=-.24, p<.05) and stronger for physiotherapy trainees (β=-.46, p<.01, R²a=.13). [→ Mette Mira 2021](#mette-mira-2021)

## Evidence

### Mette Mira 2021

Mette Mira, Hänze Martin. (2021). Arrogant or caring? Influence of transactive communication in interprofessional learning on knowledge gains and stereotype changes. GMS Journal for Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001462

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Multiple regression analyses with one's own trac and the other trac predicting stereotype change (posttest minus pretest). For physiotherapy trainees the model explained "13% of the variance" (Ra2=.13); for medical students only the own-trac predictor was significant within a non-significant model.

> "While the regression model as a whole was not significant for the medical students (F(2, 104)=2.36, p=.10, Ra2=.03), the predictor variable “intensity of one’s own trac” (β=-. 24, t(104)=2.10, p<.05) was significant. The model, however, was able to explain 13% of the variance in the physiotherapy trainees (F(2, 64)=5.95, p<.01, Ra2=.13, β=-.46, t(64)=-3.18, p<.01)."
