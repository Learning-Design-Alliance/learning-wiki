Review scheduled by a model of each student's memory improved end-of-semester retention in a middle-school language course more than massed or uniformly spaced review of the same duration

In a semester-long middle-school foreign-language course, personalized review chosen by a model combining memory theory with estimates of individual differences raised retention on a cumulative exam by 16.5% over massed study and 10.0% over one-size-fits-all spaced review, with review time matched.
