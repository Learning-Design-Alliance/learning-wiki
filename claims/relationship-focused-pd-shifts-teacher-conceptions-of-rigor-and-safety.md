# Relationship-focused PD shifts teacher conceptions of rigor and safety in argumentation instruction

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

Over four collaborative-autoethnography sessions across a school year, six secondary science teachers converged on and progressively deepened the view that trust, vulnerability, and safety are foundational to productive scientific argumentation, and several explicitly reframed their conception of instructional "rigor" from difficulty/workload toward discourse-based, equity-oriented engagement.

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` All six teachers, unprompted, connected relationships to argumentation in their very first reflection session, leading one teacher to remark: "I find it interesting that we all sort of jump to that relationship piece, which is a big part of argumentation but not something that you would ever talk about." [→ Chowning 2023](#chowning-2023)

`q2 i2` A teacher's account of a personal tragedy in Session 2 prompted a documented deepening of the vulnerability/safety theme across all six teachers' subsequent reflections, connecting emotional safety and willingness to take instructional risks ("there's something when you're not afraid to fail, they love it... I'm taking a lot more risks"). [→ Chowning 2023](#chowning-2023)

`q2 i2` By Session 3, at least three teachers explicitly reframed "rigor" away from a traditional definition (amount of homework, difficulty) toward a discourse-based definition centered on connecting content to students' own experiences and cultural backgrounds, with one teacher stating this reframing is "also more equitable because of its potential to be accessed by all students." [→ Chowning 2023](#chowning-2023)

Six secondary science teachers participated in [Collaborative Autoethnography for Teacher PD](../strategies/collaborative-autoethnography-for-teacher-pd.md) across four Saturday sessions over a school year, producing 25 written reflections and over 11 hours of recorded, transcribed discussion. Thematic analysis (with member-checking and peer review) traced how the relational theme first named in Session 1 deepened across subsequent sessions, ultimately connecting to some teachers' explicit reframing of instructional rigor. This directly evidences [Relationships as Foundation for Argumentation](../principles/relationships-as-foundation-for-argumentation.md).

## Discussion
This is a self-selected sample (6 of 21 eligible teachers volunteered for the extended engagement) and a qualitative case study without classroom observation data — the study documents what teachers said about their practice and beliefs, not their students' actual argumentation outcomes or a video-verified change in classroom behavior. The finding is nonetheless well-triangulated across written reflections, recorded discussion, and structured meta-reflection on the PD process itself, and the authors are transparent about the sample's self-selection and the first author's dual role as both a participant and the researcher.
