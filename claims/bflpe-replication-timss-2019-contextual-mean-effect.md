# Class-average achievement negatively predicts mathematics self-concept after controlling individual achievement (BFLPE replication in TIMSS 2019)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In TIMSS 2019 Grade 8 mathematics data from 46 countries, individual achievement positively predicted mathematics self-concept while class-average achievement negatively predicted it. [→ Herbert W. Marsh 2026](#herbert-w-marsh-2026)

## Evidence

### Herbert W. Marsh 2026

Herbert W. Marsh. (2026). Four Decades After the Big-Fish–Little-Pond Effect: Critical Boundary Tests and Integrative Models of Mean-Context, Ordinal-Standing, and Distributional-Scaling in 46 Countries. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10200-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel Model 2 (Table 1) predicting mathematics self-concept from student achievement (L1) and class-average achievement (L2) in TIMSS 2019 data (262,988 students, 11,684 classes, 46 countries), pooled across five plausible values. The contextual effect was "strongly negative (L2 = − 0.633, SE = 0.006)" in MSC SD units with country-cluster-robust standard errors.

> "Consistent with H1, individual achievement was strongly positive (L1 = 0.573, SE = 0.003), whereas the contextual class-average effect—the BFLPE—was strongly negative (L2 = − 0.633, SE = 0.006)."
