In the same experiment's RQ2 results, the ai draft condition's idea attribution (27%) exceeded expectations, indicating "the drafting stage is not necessarily one of simply “filling in” an outline, but a stage in which ideas are developed." The 27% figure is as printed.

"Additionally, an AI-generated draft based on participants’ own outline producedmoreideas than expected (27%); one might imagine that an outline could contain all the ideas necessary to write an essay but we found this was not necessarily the case."

Related Claims¶

  • Essay quality was highest for AI-generated drafts and lowest with no AI, negatively correlated with ownership, suggesting an ownership-quality tradeoff. — related
  • Planning-stage AI ideas were often redundant with or rejected in favor of writers' own ideas: idea attribution was 17.8% among users versus 2.9% among non-users, but ownership did not differ between users and non-users within ai plan. — related
  • In this experiment, drafts—human-written or AI-generated—anchored the final essay, with AI-generated drafts revised somewhat more but underlying phrases largely retained across conditions. — related
  • Any AI writing support decreases feelings of ownership compared to no AI support, with ownership following a gradient by stage (highest with no AI, then planning, revision, and AI-generated draft lowest). — related
  • AI support during drafting lowers ownership substantially more than support during planning or revision, and planning support preserves more ownership than revision support. — related
