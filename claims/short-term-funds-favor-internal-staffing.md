# LEAs most frequently used short-term behavioral health funds to expand internal staffing and direct services rather than partnerships and structural investments

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Analysis of SBHIP project plans found LEAs most frequently leveraged funds to expand behavioral health staffing and services provided directly by the LEA rather than investing in partnerships and structural investments. [→ Caparas 2024](#caparas-2024)
`q2 i?` Hiring behavioral health practitioners away from community-based organizations can exacerbate those organizations' workforce shortages and reduce their capacity to serve youth outside school time. [→ Caparas 2024 (2)](#caparas-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Caparas 2024

Caparas, R., Eisenberg, L., Krausen, K., & Lias, C. (2024). Schools can't do it alone: Developing sustainable systems of care for school-based behavioral health in California. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED655746

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Analysis of SBHIP project plans, corroborated by interviewed COE and MCP leaders, found LEAs favored internal capacity building; the brief notes this mirrors research on pandemic relief spending favoring direct services over systems change.

> "the analysis of SBHIP project plans found that LEAs most frequently reported leveraging their funds to expand behavioral health staffing and support services provided directly by the LEA rather than investing in partnerships and other structural investments."

### Caparas 2024 (2)

Caparas, R., Eisenberg, L., Krausen, K., & Lias, C. (2024). Schools can't do it alone: Developing sustainable systems of care for school-based behavioral health in California. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED655746

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The brief's qualitative analysis reports that hiring practitioners from community-based organizations may leave those organizations with exacerbated workforce shortages and less capacity to serve young people outside school settings.

> "One of the trade-offs of LEAs hiring new behavioral health practitioners is that those practitioners often come from community-based organizations."
