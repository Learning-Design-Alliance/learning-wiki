## Evidence

### Bartlett Richard D. 2017

Bartlett Richard D., Radenkovic Dina, Mitrasinovic Stefan, Cole Andrew, Pavkovic Iva, Denn Peyton Cheong Phey, Hussain Mahrukh, Kogler Magdalena, Koutsopodioti Natalia, Uddin Wasima, Beckley Ivan, Abubakar Hana, Gill Deborah, Smith Daron. (2017). A pilot study to assess the utility of a freely downloadable mobile application simulator for undergraduate clinical skills training: a single-blinded, randomised controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1085-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis across all 27 trial participants using a pre-test 10-point Likert confidence questionnaire. The authors characterise the association as a "poor correlate" of objectively assessed OSCE performance; the correlation did not reach the 5% significance threshold.

> "Overall, self-assessed student confidence was found to poorly correlate with examiner assessed performance in male urinary catheterisation (Pearson's co-efficient 0.367, p = 0.06, Fig. 4)."

### Bartlett Richard D. 2017 (2)

Bartlett Richard D., Radenkovic Dina, Mitrasinovic Stefan, Cole Andrew, Pavkovic Iva, Denn Peyton Cheong Phey, Hussain Mahrukh, Kogler Magdalena, Koutsopodioti Natalia, Uddin Wasima, Beckley Ivan, Abubakar Hana, Gill Deborah, Smith Daron. (2017). A pilot study to assess the utility of a freely downloadable mobile application simulator for undergraduate clinical skills training: a single-blinded, randomised controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1085-y

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multiple linear regression on the 27-student pilot sample comparing standardised beta coefficients for confidence, year group and examiner allocation. Confidence (0.357) outperformed year group (0.298) and examiner (0.156); the coefficients are unstandardised-standardised values as printed, with no effect-size test reported.

> "multiple linear regression analysis revealed that self-assessed student confidence was a better predictor of OSCE performance than year group or examiner allocation (standardised beta coefficients: 0.357 vs. 0.298 and 0.156, respectively)."
