# Group composition and educator cultural responsiveness shape CALD students' participation and sense of belonging in SBL

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Working with majority-local groups, students felt less confident, reported local students dominating discussion, and adopted passive observer roles. [→ Zhang 2025](#zhang-2025)
`q2 i?` Students reported more opportunities to contribute and stronger psychological connection when grouped with peers from similar cultural and linguistic backgrounds. [→ Zhang 2025](#zhang-2025)
`q2 i?` Participants feared biased evaluation compared with local peers and felt more understood by educators from CALD backgrounds who adapted their communication. [→ Zhang 2025](#zhang-2025)

## Evidence

### Zhang 2025

Zhang, L., Forbes, R., Patterson, F., & Penman, A. (2025). Becoming through simulation‐based learning: Lived experiences of occupational therapy students from culturally and linguistically diverse backgrounds. Australian Occupational Therapy Journal, 72(4), e70044. https://doi.org/10.1111/1440-1630.70044

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview study of 15 CALD occupational therapy students in high-fidelity SBL. Participants reported feeling excluded in majority-local groups, needing extra processing time, adopting passive roles, yet expressing themselves "more confidently" in groups mostly of international peers, and valuing culturally sensitive educators.

> "Luckily, the majority (of my group members) are foreigners, so I can express myself more confidently"
