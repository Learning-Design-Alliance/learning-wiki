## Evidence

### Gosavi A 2025

Gosavi A, Kanneganti A, Khoo ET, Singh K, Shen L, Rauff M, Liu C, Choolani M. (2025). Virtual reality simulation training for childbirth: A cluster randomized crossover study. Int J Gynecol Obstet. https://doi.org/10.1002/ijgo.70053

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Linear mixed modeling of the 14-item, six-domain feedback questionnaire compared modalities, with teaching modality, period, and group as main effects. The mannequin scored "61.0 versus 58.3". No effect size was printed.

> "The total feedback score was significantly higher for the mannequin simulator at 61.0 versus 58.3 with an adjusted mean difference of 2.45 (95% CI 0.95–3.95; P = 0.002)."

### Gosavi A 2025 (2)

Gosavi A, Kanneganti A, Khoo ET, Singh K, Shen L, Rauff M, Liu C, Choolani M. (2025). Virtual reality simulation training for childbirth: A cluster randomized crossover study. Int J Gynecol Obstet. https://doi.org/10.1002/ijgo.70053

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Domain-level comparison from the same crossover trial's feedback questionnaire; knowledge showed no significant difference (P = 0.981) while the other five domains favored the mannequin. No effect size was printed.

> "When comparing domains, the mannequin simulation had a significantly higher score than the VR simulation in all domains except knowledge (Table 4)."
