## Evidence

### Philip I. Pavlik Jr. 2005

Philip I. Pavlik Jr., John R. Anderson. (2005). Practice and Forgetting Effects on Vocabulary Memory: An Activation-Based Model of the Spacing Effect. Cognitive Science 29. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog0000_14

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVA on S2 correctness (repetitions × spacing × trial × retention interval, eight-repetition conditions excluded) in the authors' own Japanese–English paired-associate experiment with 40 participants. The "increasing benefit to spacing with more repetitions" interaction was significant.

> "we found a significant S1 repetition × S1 spacing interaction, F(4, 152) = 4.38, p < .005, reflecting an increasing benefit to spacing with more repetitions at a particular spacing."
