## Evidence

### Martin O. Archer 2021

Martin O. Archer, Jennifer DeWitt, Charlotte Thorley, and Olivia Keenan. (2021). Evaluating participants' experience of extended interaction with cutting-edge physics research through the PRiSE 'research in schools' programme. [doi:10.5194/gc-4-147-2021](https://doi.org/10.5194/gc-4-147-2021)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

From 2019 students (n = 68) rated teacher support on a 5-point scale; the average was "4.37 ± 0.08", significantly above the benchmark (p = 4×10−10). A separate article statement holds that teachers alone would likely not have been able to support the projects without university resources and mentoring.

> "The average response is 4.37 ± 0.08, which is considerably greater than the benchmark on teacher support reported by Vennix et al. (2017) of3.60±0.03 (p = 4×10−10)"

### Martin O. Archer 2021 (2)

Martin O. Archer, Jennifer DeWitt, Charlotte Thorley, and Olivia Keenan. (2021). Evaluating participants' experience of extended interaction with cutting-edge physics research through the PRiSE 'research in schools' programme. [doi:10.5194/gc-4-147-2021](https://doi.org/10.5194/gc-4-147-2021)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Authors' synthesis of qualitative comments from students and teachers across sections 4.1–4.2: teachers provided advice, encouragement and session arrangements, but lacked specific research knowledge and cited time pressure, indicating the university's support was necessary.

> "the comments from both students and teachers indicate that teachers alone would likely not have been able to successfully support these research projects in their schools without both the resources and external motivation/mentoring provided as part of the PRiSE framework"
