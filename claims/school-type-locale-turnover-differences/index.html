Tan, T., Wei, W., Carver-Thomas, D., & García, E. (2026). Teacher turnover in the United States: Who moves, who leaves, and why. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/248.479

q2 · i? · associational · r3

Descriptive analysis of turnover by type of public school (Table 3) using NTPS/TFS data. The report finds charter turnover of "17.8%" versus "14.9%" in traditional public schools, with movers accounting for most of the difference.

"turnover rates are higher in charter schools (17.8%) than in traditional public schools (14.9%), driven largely by a greater share of teachers moving schools (10.3% vs. 8.0%) and a slightly higher rate of teachers leaving the profession (7.4% vs. 6.9%) in charter schools"

Tan 2026 (2)¶

Tan, T., Wei, W., Carver-Thomas, D., & García, E. (2026). Teacher turnover in the United States: Who moves, who leaves, and why. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/248.479

q2 · i? · associational · r3

Descriptive analysis of turnover by school locale (Table 3). The report finds "city schools experience the highest turnover rates (17.7%)" relative to rural, suburban, and town schools.

"teachers in city schools experience the highest turnover rates (17.7%), those in rural schools have lower turnover rates (14.5%), and teachers in suburban or town schools have the lowest turnover rates (13.9% and 13.7%, respectively)."
