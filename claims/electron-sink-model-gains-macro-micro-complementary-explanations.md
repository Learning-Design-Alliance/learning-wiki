# Electron Sink group students showed large significant gains in macro-micro complementary explanations of electric current

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among Electron Sink participants, macro-micro complementary explanations rose from 5% to 60% in 5th grade and 10% to 60% in 7th grade; the increase was highly significant (5th: t(19) = 4.49, p = 0.0003; 7th: t(19) = 3.59, p = 0.002). [→ Pratim Sengupta and Uri Wilensky 2018](#pratim-sengupta-and-uri-wilensky-2018)

## Evidence

### Pratim Sengupta and Uri Wilensky 2018

Pratim Sengupta and Uri Wilensky. (2018). Understanding Electric Current Using Agent-Based Models: Connecting the Micro-level with Flow Rate. In: Proceedings of the Eighth International Conference on Computer Supported Education (CSEDU 2016). Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1802.04165](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1802.04165)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre/post written explanations in the Electron Sink group were coded for agent-aggregate complementarity; printed percentages are 5% to 60% (5th grade) and 10% to 60% (7th grade). Only test statistics are printed, no effect size.

> "Paired sample T-tests also showed that the increase in percentage of macro -micro complementary responses among participants in the Electron-Sink group was highly significant, both in 5th grade (t(19) = 4.49, p = 0.0003) and in 7th grade (t(19) = 3.59, p = 0.002)."
