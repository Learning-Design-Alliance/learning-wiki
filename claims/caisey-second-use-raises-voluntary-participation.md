# After a second assigned use of a voice-based AI discussion partner, students make about 31% more voluntary contributions in later class sessions

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` After a second assigned opportunity to use CAiSEY, students' voluntary contribution counts rose in the next session and across all future sessions, an effect equal to roughly 31% of the sample mean of 0.73 contributions per session. [→ Wang 2026](#wang-2026)

## Evidence

### Wang 2026

Wang, D. J., Jain, N. M., Burbano, V., Guzman, J., Keum, D., Kim, S., Kogut, B., & Wright, N. (2026). How assigned AI use before class shapes active student engagement in class. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.2139/ssrn.7585318](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7585318)

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Preregistered randomized field experiment in ten sections of a required first-year MBA strategy course (759 students); IV models of qualifying use (conversations of at least ten minutes) instrumented by random assignment. The article reports the qualifying-use estimate corresponds to "roughly 31% of the sample mean of 0.73 contributions per session"; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "The qualifying use estimate (Model 7) corresponds to about one additional voluntary contribution for every four to five sessions, or roughly 31% of the sample mean of 0.73 contributions per session (Supplementary Table S2, Panel A)."
