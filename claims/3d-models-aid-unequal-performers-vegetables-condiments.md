# Among unequal performers, interactive 3-D food models yielded higher within-±10% quantification accuracy than 2-D images, especially for vegetables and condiments

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2022 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For students who struggled to perform both formats equally, overall quantification accuracy within ±10% of calories was higher with 3-D than 2-D models (19% vs. 14%). [→ Ho Dang Khanh Ngan 2022](#ho-dang-khanh-ngan-2022)
`q2 i?` The 3-D advantage was significant for vegetables (2-D: 4% vs. 3-D: 20%, p < 0.001) and condiments (2-D: 6% vs. 3-D: 17%, p < 0.05). [→ Ho Dang Khanh Ngan 2022 (2)](#ho-dang-khanh-ngan-2022-2)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

First-semester within-group comparison of quantification accuracy by format among the 20 unequal performers, shown in Figure 3C. The article hedges that interactive 3-D food models "may help them more than 2-D models"; no effect size is printed for this contrast.

> "For those students who struggled to perform both 2-D and 3-D food viewing skills, interactive 3-D food models may help them more than 2-D models (within ±10% difference of total calorie estimation: 2-D: 14% vs. 3-D: 19%) (C),"

> "especially when estimating food portions of vegetables (2-D: 4% vs. 3-D: 20%, p < 0.001) and condiments (2-D: 6% vs. 3-D: 17%, p < 0.05) (D) (Figure 3)."
