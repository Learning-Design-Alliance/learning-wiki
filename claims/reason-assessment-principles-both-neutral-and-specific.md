# Principles of reason assessment are both subject-neutral and subject-specific

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q1` argument or single case

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Some principles of reason assessment, such as the prohibition on begging the question, are general and subject-neutral, while others, such as those linking a dead battery to a car that will not start, apply only in specialized domains and require specialized knowledge. [→ Weinstein 1989](#weinstein-1989)

## Evidence

### Weinstein 1989

Weinstein, Mark, Ed.; Oxman-Michelli, Wendy, Ed. (1989). Critical Thinking: Language and Inquiry across the Disciplines. Proceedings of the Annual Conference of the Institute for Critical Thinking (Upper Montclair, New Jersey, 1988). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED351305

`q1 · i?`

Theoretical argument in Siegel's plenary paper, illustrated with worked examples (the question-begging Bible argument; the dead battery and yellow skin examples). The paper concludes that 'critical thinking, consequently, is wrongly construed as either entirely subject-neutral or entirely subject-specific. It is both.'

> "Some principles of reason assessment, then, are general and subject-neutral. These are the principles studied by the field of logic--both formal and informal--and constitute one major type of principle of reason assessment. Other principles of reason assessment are subject-specific."
