## Evidence

### Ying Hu 2026

Ying Hu, Yuhang Shu & Xin Zhao. (2026). Children consider changes in performance over time when reasoning about academic achievements. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00401-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Study 2 change question (asking whose performance 'changed more'): a significant age effect (B = 1.04, p < 0.001) with Johnson–Neyman analyses showing above-chance selection of the improving character from 5.3 years; adults answered near ceiling (M = 0.97).

> "We found a signiﬁcant main effect of age ( B = 1.04, SE = 0.25, 95% CI [0.54, 1.54], p < 0.001; β = 1.66): with age, children increasingly answered that the improving character changed more."

### Ying Hu 2026 (2)

Ying Hu, Yuhang Shu & Xin Zhao. (2026). Children consider changes in performance over time when reasoning about academic achievements. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00401-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Study 2 decreasing-condition change question: children of all ages identified the decreasing character as having changed more (M = 0.91, p < 0.001), at a level comparable to adults (M = 0.95); there was no significant age effect (p = 0.786).
