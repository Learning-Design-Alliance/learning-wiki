Evidence¶

Pas P. 2021¶

Pas P., Hulshoff Pol H.E., Raemaekers M., Vink M. (2021). Self-regulation in the pre-adolescent brain. Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2021.101012

q2 · i1 · associational · r2

ROI regression in the 640-child cohort (Bonferroni-corrected across three ROIs) found frontal activation "was associated with self-regulation, F(1, 536) = 6.37, p = 0.01 (r = 0.11)". The small printed correlation corresponds to a small effect.

"activation in the right mid frontal cortex during proactive inhibition was associated with self-regulation, F(1, 536) = 6.37, p = 0.01 (r = 0.11), with no interaction effect for sex F(1, 536) = 0.07, p = 0.79"

Related Claims¶

  • Children aged 8–11 show significant activation in frontal-striatal-parietal regions during proactive inhibition and striatal activation during reactive inhibition, similar to regions reported in adults — related
  • Self-regulation scores correlate with functional coupling between the right striatum and left motor cortex during reactive inhibition, but not frontostriatal coupling during proactive inhibition — related
  • Children aged 8–11 with higher parent-rated self-regulation show more proactive response slowing on a stop-signal anticipation task — related
  • Functional coupling development during inhibition is sex-specific: striatal-motor coupling increases with age in girls, frontostriatal coupling increases with age in boys — related
  • Reactive inhibition speed and accuracy improve with age between 8 and 11 years, while proactive response slowing does not increase with age — related
  • Pre-adolescent children already exhibit proactive inhibitory control, slowing responses as stop-signal probability increases — related
