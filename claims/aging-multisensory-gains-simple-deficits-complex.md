# Older adults show larger multisensory gains for simple stimuli but multisensory deficits for complex speech stimuli

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Older individuals on average experience larger performance gains under multisensory conditions than younger adults for simple stimuli, but as stimulus and task complexity increases these gains disappear and multisensory performance deficits become the norm. [→ Wallace 2020](#wallace-2020)

## Evidence

### Wallace 2020

Wallace, M. T., Woynaroski, T. G., & Stevenson, R. A. (2020). Multisensory Integration as a Window into Orderly and Disrupted Cognition and Communication. Annual Review of Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010419-051112

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Laurienti et al. and Peiffer et al., that older adults show larger multisensory performance gains than younger adults, but notes these improvements appear limited to simple stimulus pairings such as flashes and beeps, with deficits emerging for speech-related audiovisual binding.
