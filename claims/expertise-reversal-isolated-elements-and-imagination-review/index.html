Evidence¶

Kalyuga 2003¶

Kalyuga, S., Ayres, P., Chandler, P., & Sweller, J. (2003). The Expertise Reversal Effect. Educational Psychologist, 38(1), 23-31. https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review attributes this to Cooper, Tindall-Ford, Chandler, and Sweller (2001) experiments with students learning to use a spreadsheet, calling it "a clear example of the exper- tise reversal effect."

"More knowledgeable students who held appropriate prerequisite schemas found imagining pro- cedures and relations more beneficial for learning compared with studying worked examples, whereas less knowledge- able students found imagining procedures and relations had a negative effect compared with studying worked examples."
