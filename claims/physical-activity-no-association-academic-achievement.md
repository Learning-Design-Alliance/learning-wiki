# Objectively measured physical activity shows no significant association with academic achievement in children with overweight/obesity

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Light, moderate, vigorous, and moderate-to-vigorous physical activity measured by accelerometry were not significantly associated with academic achievement in any model examined. [→ Cadenas-Sanchez 2020](#cadenas-sanchez-2020)

## Evidence

### Cadenas-Sanchez 2020

Cadenas-Sanchez, C., Migueles, J. H., Esteban-Cornejo, I., Mora-Gonzalez, J., Henriksson, P., Rodriguez-Ayllon, M., Molina-García, P., Löf, M., Labayen, I., Hillman, C. H., Catena, A., & Ortega, F. B. (2020). Fitness, physical activity, and academic achievement in overweight/obese children. Journal of Sports Sciences. https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2020.1729516

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional regression in 106 children with overweight/obesity (accelerometry subsample n=100) using wrist- and hip-worn GT3X+ accelerometers over 7 days. "Physical activity was not significantly associated with academic achievement in any of the models examined"; sensitivity analyses with different metrics and placements were consistent. Equivalence was not tested.

> "Overall, the results showed that physical activity was not significantly associated with academic achievement in any of the models examined"
