# In school racial/ethnic diversity research, mathematical diversity indices operationalizing dual-concept diversity are the dominant measure, with Simpson's D the most widely applied, and most studies assess static diversity at a single timepoint

## Evidence

### Chan 2023

Chan, M.-k., & Benner, A. D. (2023). A Consideration of Racial/Ethnic Diversity Conceptualization and Measurement: Clarifying Ambiguities and Advancing Scholarship. Educational Psychologist (accepted manuscript). https://orcid.org/0000-0001-6451-2417

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Systematic review (PRISMA, 1970–2023; 114 included studies) coding diversity measures. The review reports that "Mathematical indices of diversity are a prevalent measure in the existing school racial/ethnic diversity literature (n = 85)", with Simpson's D the most widely applied (n = 75).

> "Mathematical indices of diversity are a prevalent measure in the existing school racial/ethnic diversity literature (n = 85)."

### Chan 2023 (2)

Chan, M.-k., & Benner, A. D. (2023). A Consideration of Racial/Ethnic Diversity Conceptualization and Measurement: Clarifying Ambiguities and Advancing Scholarship. Educational Psychologist (accepted manuscript). https://orcid.org/0000-0001-6451-2417

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Coding of social spaces across the 114 reviewed studies found most used static diversity; "five studies incorporated the dynamic concept by taking the average of diversity index values across contexts or timepoints for each participant."

> "Among the reviewed studies, the vast majority of them employed static concept to capture school racial/ethnic diversity of a context at a particular timepoint"
