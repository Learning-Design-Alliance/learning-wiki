# Nunnery and Ross (2007) reported significant grade 5 but not grade 8 TAAS effects, which were non-significant in WWC recalculation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q2`–`q3`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The quasi-experiment's authors reported positive, statistically significant effects for grade 5 students but not for grade 8 students on the TAAS reading subtest. [→ Accelerated Reader™ 2010](#accelerated-readertm-2010)
`q3 i?` In WWC recalculations using untransformed scores accounting for clustering, none of the Nunnery and Ross effects were statistically significant and the average effect size fell below 0.25. [→ Accelerated Reader™ 2010 (2)](#accelerated-readertm-2010-2)

## Evidence

### Accelerated Reader™ 2010

Accelerated Reader™. (2010). WWC Intervention Report: Accelerated Reader™, Adolescent Literacy. What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. http://www.renlearn.com/ar/

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experiment in Texas comparing Accelerated Reader schools with matched comparison schools on performance, ethnicity, English proficiency, poverty, and mobility; two grade 5 cohorts (912 and 891 students) plus grade 8 students (482 intervention, 510 comparison).

> "Nunnery and Ross (2007) reported positive and statistically significant effects of the intervention for grade 5 students and did not find statistically significant effects of the intervention for grade 8 students on the reading subtest of the Texas Assessment of Academic Skills (TAAS) test."

### Accelerated Reader™ 2010 (2)

Accelerated Reader™. (2010). WWC Intervention Report: Accelerated Reader™, Adolescent Literacy. What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. http://www.renlearn.com/ar/

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

WWC recalculation of the same quasi-experiment's findings. The authors had not accounted for clustering within classrooms and used transformed test scores; WWC corrections changed the significance conclusions.

> "In WWC calculations, based on untransformed scores that account for clustering, none of these effects were statistically significant, and the calculated average effect size was not large enough to be considered substantively important according to WWC criteria (that is, an effect size of at least 0.25)."
