## Evidence

### Crosnoe 2010

Crosnoe, R., Morrison, F., Burchinal, M., Pianta, R., Keating, D., Friedman, S. L., Clarke-Stewart, K. A., & the NICHD Early Child Care Research Network. (2010). Instruction, teacher–student relations, and math achievement trajectories in elementary school. *Journal of Educational Psychology, 102*(2), 407–417. [doi:10.1037/a0017762](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0017762)

`q2 · observational longitudinal study with controls` · `i? · no standardized effect size for the interaction` · `n=587 children (low early skill group n = 71)` · `associational · r2`

Repeated-measures hierarchical linear models of NICHD Study of Early Child Care and Youth Development data on 587 children whose third- or fifth-grade classrooms were observed. Model 2 crossed classroom instruction and teacher–student relations with age and early skill group. The three-way interaction among teacher–student conflict, inference instruction and early skill group was statistically significant (F = 3.20, p < .05; unstandardized B = .10 for the low early skill group). In graphs of the model's predictions, low early skill children in classrooms with more inference instruction and low conflict had higher math achievement and faster gains than low early skill peers with less inference instruction or high conflict. The interaction is significant, but no standardized effect size is printed for it, so impact is coded `i?` (not reported, which is not the same as no effect). The authors note these leveling effects "were not large in magnitude" and that, because the data are observational, they "cannot establish causality".
