# The effect of inference-based instruction and teacher–student conflict on math achievement growth was significantly stronger for the low early skill group (three-way interaction)

`q3 i?` The three-way interaction among teacher–student conflict, inference instruction, and early skill group was statistically significant, with instruction and conflict predicting math achievement more strongly for the low early skill group. [→ Crosnoe 2010](#crosnoe-2010)

## Evidence

### Crosnoe 2010

Crosnoe, R., Morrison, F., Burchinal, M., Pianta, R., Keating, D., Friedman, S. L., Clarke-Stewart, K. A., & the NICHD Early Child Care Research Network. (2010). Instruction, Teacher–Student Relations, and Math Achievement Trajectories in Elementary School. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0017762

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Model 2 of the repeated measures HLM crossed instruction and teacher–student relations with age and early skill group. The omnibus three-way interaction was significant ("F = 3.20, p < .05"); no effect size was printed, so impact is null.

> "The three-way interaction among teacher–student conflict, inference instruction, and early skill group was statistically significant (F = 3.20, p < .05, with B = .10, p < .05, for low early skill group)."
