# In a Standard VII girls' class, the later-working group (11.25 A.M.) improved 21 per cent on preliminary records versus 15 per cent for the earlier-working group (10 A.M.).

## Evidence

### W. H. Winch 1911

W. H. Winch. (1911). Mental Adaptation During the School Day as Measured by Arithmetical Reasoning.—Part II. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Mental+Adaptation+During+the+School+Day+as+Measured+by+Arithmetical+Reasoning

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Fourth series: 30 girls of average age 13 years 2 months in a municipal girls' school in a poor district of southeast London were divided into equal groups on four preliminary tests; final tests were worked at 10 A.M. (Group A) or 11.25 A.M. (Group B). The article prints the improvement percentages but no effect size.

> "Group A improves 15 per cent, on its preliminary record, and Group B'improves 21 per cent. It is interesting to note that nearly the whole period between the preliminary and final tests was holiday."
