## Evidence

### Aliaga L 2024

Aliaga L, Bavolek RA, Cooper B, Mariorenzi A, Ahn J, Kraut A, Duong D, Burger C, Gisondi MA. (2024). Error Management Training and Adaptive Expertise in Learning Computed Tomography Interpretation: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.31600

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Participant-level mediation analysis (Hayes PROCESS macro) with learning strategy as exposure, number of training errors as mediator, and adaptive expertise score as outcome, comparing the two EMT cohorts. The analysis "explained by the number of errors made during training (P = .01)" at 87.2%; no effect size printed for the mediated path.

> "Taken together, 87.2% of the difficult EMT learning strategy's effect on improving adaptive expertise was explained by the number of errors made during training (P = .01)."

### Aliaga L 2024 (2)

Aliaga L, Bavolek RA, Cooper B, Mariorenzi A, Ahn J, Kraut A, Duong D, Burger C, Gisondi MA. (2024). Error Management Training and Adaptive Expertise in Learning Computed Tomography Interpretation: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.31600

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Two-tailed independent samples t test on training-case error rates between the two EMT cohorts in the randomized trial. Difficult-question answering produced far more errors than easy questions ("51.4% [95% CI, 47.7%-55.2%] vs 15.8% [95% CI, 12.6%-18.9%]"), Cohen d = 3.1.

> "The difficult EMT cohort made more errors on the training cases than the easy EMT cohort (51.4% [95% CI, 47.7%-55.2%] vs 15.8% [95% CI, 12.6%-18.9%], respectively; P < .001), with a large effect size (Cohen d = 3.1)"
