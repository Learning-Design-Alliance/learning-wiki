## Evidence

### Katherine M. Zinsser 2022

Katherine M. Zinsser, H. Callie Silver, Elyse R. Shenberger, and Velisha Jackson. (2022). A Systematic Review of Early Childhood Exclusionary Discipline. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211070047

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports that only two studies (Giordano, 2020; Zeng et al., 2019) could compute and report racial disproportionality because few collected demographics of enrolled children; per Table 2, Zeng et al. (2019) printed Male OR = 2.6 and Hispanic OR = 3.7 for suspension/pick-up.

> "However, as seen in K–12 settings (Welsh & Little, 2018), according to these two studies, boys, African American, and Hispanic children were at greater disproportionate risk for being excluded."

### Katherine M. Zinsser 2022 (2)

Katherine M. Zinsser, H. Callie Silver, Elyse R. Shenberger, and Velisha Jackson. (2022). A Systematic Review of Early Childhood Exclusionary Discipline. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211070047

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes this finding to Zeng et al. (2020), a secondary analysis of the National Survey of Early Care and Education; Table 2 prints 5.4% of children with disabilities versus 1.5% without for suspension/pick-up, with children with disabilities 3.7× children without.

> "Similarly, Zeng et al. (2020) reported that children with disabilities were overrepresented."
