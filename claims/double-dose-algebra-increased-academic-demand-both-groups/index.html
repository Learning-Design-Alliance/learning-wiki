Evidence¶

Takako Nomi 2010¶

Takako Nomi, Elaine M. Allensworth. (2010). The Effects of Tracking with Supports on Instructional Climate and Student Outcomes in High School Algebra. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/effects-tracking-supports-instructional-climate-and-student-outcomes-high-school

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Regression discontinuity with cross-cohort comparison of two CPS ninth-grade cohorts (6779 students in 55 schools). Above-norm students' demand rose "by 0.12 standard deviations (p<0.001)"; adding classroom average ability reduced the coefficient to 0.05, no longer significant (p>.05).

"Post-policy above-norm students reported greater academic demand, by 0.12 standard deviations (p<0.001), compared to their pre-policy counterparts."

Takako Nomi 2010 (2)¶

Takako Nomi, Elaine M. Allensworth. (2010). The Effects of Tracking with Supports on Instructional Climate and Student Outcomes in High School Algebra. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/effects-tracking-supports-instructional-climate-and-student-outcomes-high-school

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Cross-cohort discontinuity analysis at the 50th-percentile ITBS cutoff. Below-norm students' demand rose 0.12 SD (0.05+0.07) after adjusting for peer ability, suggesting instructional time and pedagogy, not composition, drove the gain.

"academic demand actually increased among below-norm students as much as among above-norm students with the policy; the post-policy difference compared to above-norm students was -0.02 SD and not statistically significant (p>0.1)."
