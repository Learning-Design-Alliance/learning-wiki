Evidence¶

Chun-Wei Huang 2016¶

Chun-Wei Huang, Jason Snipes, Neal Finkelstein, WestEd. (2016). Using assessment data to guide math course placement of California middle school students. SREE Spring 2016 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED567123

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Logistic regression analysis of 2,579 grade 8 algebra I students in SVRA districts (2011/12) predicted algebra I CST proficiency from grade 6 math CST scale scores; the study reports this approach "increases the accuracy of algebra I placement decisions in grade 8 from 69 percent to 75 percent."

"Using students’ grade 6 math CST scale score rather than proficiency status increases the accuracy of algebra I placement decisions in grade 8 from 69 percent to 75 percent."

Related Claims¶

  • When scale scores are used, prediction accuracy of grade 8 algebra I proficiency does not vary substantially across grade 6 CST, grade 7 CST, and MDTP assessments (75–78 percent) — related
  • Grade 6 math CST proficiency alone is not an effective indicator of algebra readiness: students need a scale score of 367, about 0.27 SD above the cutpoint, to reach a 50 percent probability of grade 8 algebra I proficiency — related
  • Revisiting grade 8 algebra placement with grade 7 math CST scores does not necessarily yield more accurate placement outcomes, because predictions from different assessments largely overlap — related
  • MDTP scores on five of seven topics significantly predict grade 8 algebra I proficiency after controlling for grade 6 CST scale score — related
