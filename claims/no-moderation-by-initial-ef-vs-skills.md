# Initial EF and VS skill levels do not significantly moderate intervention effects on classroom behavior

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Moderation analyses found no evidence that initial EF or VS levels moderated intervention impacts, with a non-significant trend for VS at school entry on learning-related behaviors (b = –0.02, p = .08). [→ Brock et al. 2018](#brock-et-al-2018)

## Evidence

### Brock et al. 2018

Laura L. Brock, William M. Murrah, Elizabeth A. Cottone, Andrew J. Mashburn, and David W. Grissmer. (2018). An after-school intervention targeting executive function and visuospatial skills also improves classroom behavior. International Journal of Behavioral Development, 42(5), 474–484. https://doi.org/10.1177/0165025417738057

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Moderation analyses entered EF and VS skills and their interaction terms in the same model to reduce false-positive risk (N = 87). No significant moderation emerged; the article reports a trend with "b ¼ –0.02, p ¼ .08" and no effect size.

> "Our results do not provide evidence for moderation of initial skill levels on interven- tion effect. A trend is noted where VS skills at school entry nearly moderate intervention impacts on learning-related behaviors (b ¼ –0.02, p ¼ .08)."
