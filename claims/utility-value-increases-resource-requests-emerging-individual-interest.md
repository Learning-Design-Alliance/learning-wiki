## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The directly-communicated utility value manipulation increased the odds of requesting regression resources by 1.35 times relative to control (OR = 1.35, p = .042); the choice manipulation had no influence (OR = 1.02, p = .885). [→ Asher 2025](#asher-2025)

## Evidence

### Asher 2025

Asher, M. W., & Harackiewicz, J. M. (2025). Using Choice and Utility Value to Promote Interest: Stimulating Situational Interest in a Lesson and Fostering the Development of Interest in Statistics. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000921

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Meta-analyzed logistic regression of the binary resource-request outcome across Studies 1–7. The utility value manipulation "increased the odds that participants requested regression resources by 1.35 times relative to control, OR = 1.35, p = .042"; 39.2% of utility value participants versus 33.2% of controls requested resources.

> "the directly-communicated utility value manipulation increased the odds that participants requested regression resources by 1.35 times relative to control, OR = 1.35, p = .042, suggesting that the manipulation promoted emerging individual interest in statistics."
