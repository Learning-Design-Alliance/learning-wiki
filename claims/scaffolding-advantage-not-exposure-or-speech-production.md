## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Scaffolding produced better word learning despite the instructor naming objects more often and children gazing at stimuli more in the Passive condition, and despite learning outcomes not correlating with speech-production measures. [→ Besser Ilan 2026](#besser-ilan-2026)

## Evidence

### Besser Ilan 2026

Besser Ilan, N., Shavit, H., Zohar, N., & Jaffe-Dax, S. (2026). Enhanced word learning and neural synchrony during children's storytelling. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00417-7

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Behavioral coding of the recorded in-person sessions showed the instructor repeated object names more in Passive yet scaffolding yielded better word learning; the naming-frequency difference was large (Cohen's d = 1.3). Children also looked at stimulus cards more in Passive (Cohen's d = -2.42).

> "words were better learned in the Scaffolding condition despite the instructor repeating the object names more often in the Passive condition [11.5 ± 3.5 vs. 19.1 ± 2.4 times, t(22) = -6.5, p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 1.3]"

### Besser Ilan 2026 (2)

Besser Ilan, N., Shavit, H., Zohar, N., & Jaffe-Dax, S. (2026). Enhanced word learning and neural synchrony during children's storytelling. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00417-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Turn-taking analysis of the Scaffolding sessions found no significant correlations between learning outcomes and child speech-production measures (all p ≥ 0.83); children rarely produced object names themselves (0.61 ± 1.1 times).

> "learning outcomes did not correlate with measures of child speech production, including average segment length [r(22) = 0.13, p = 0.83], speaking ratio [r(22) = 0.20, p = 0.83], or total speaking time [r(22) = 0.04, p = 0.85]"
