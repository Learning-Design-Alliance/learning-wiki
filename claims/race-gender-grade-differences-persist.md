# Systematic racial, ethnic and gender differences in grades persist among students with the same test scores, attendance, courses and teachers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Net of controls, Black and Latino students receive lower grades than White students (by 0.26 and 0.12 GPA points) and Asian students higher (by 0.26 points); boys receive about half a grade point lower than girls (0.31 after attendance controls). [→ Elaine M. Allensworth and Stuart Luppescu 2016](#elaine-m-allensworth-and-stuart-luppescu-2016)

## Evidence

### Elaine M. Allensworth and Stuart Luppescu 2016

Elaine M. Allensworth and Stuart Luppescu. (2016). Why do students get good grades, or bad ones? The influence of the teacher, class, school, and student. University of Chicago Consortium on School Research working paper. https://consortium.uchicago.edu

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Coefficients from Model 5a on the CPS grade dataset show these differences exist controlling for test scores, economic status, class type, school, and skills relative to classroom peers; attendance explains about one-third of the Asian difference, about 14 percent of the Black difference, and none of the Latino difference.

> "On average, Black and Latino students get lower grades than White students (by 0.26 and 0.12 GPA points, respectively), while Asian students get higher grades (by 0.26 points)."
