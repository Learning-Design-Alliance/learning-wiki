# For very young children, more abstract vocabulary terms such as afloat, haste, mellow, noticeable, outward and skill are relatively more difficult, while more concrete terms such as dungeon, envelope, eyelash, nerve, plumbing, pork and snip are learned earlier.

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The larger changes in word-difficulty order appear due to abstract terms being relatively more difficult at these early ages. [→ Cobb 1921](#cobb-1921)
`q2 i?` More concrete terms are learned earlier and are more likely to be known by young children relative to an older group with an equally small vocabulary. [→ Cobb 1921 (2)](#cobb-1921-2)

## Evidence

### Cobb 1921

Cobb, Margaret V. (1921). Tentative Order of Difficulty of the Terman Vocabulary with Very Young Children. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Educational_Psychology

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Authors' interpretation of the tabulated per-word credit data from kindergarten and Grade I examinations: "certain more abstract terms, such as afloat, haste, mellow, noticeable, outward and skill, are relatively more difficult at these early ages."

> "The larger changes in order seem to be due to the fact that certain more abstract terms, such as afloat, haste, mellow, noticeable, outward and skill, are relatively more difficult at these early ages"

### Cobb 1921 (2)
