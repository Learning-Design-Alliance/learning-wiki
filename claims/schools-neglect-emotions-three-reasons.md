### Henry 1916

Henry, T. S. (1916). The Education and Control of the Emotions. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/edu/

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument from the article's opening section on why schooling has ignored affective development. The author lists three reasons, including 'a lack of comprehension of the value of the emotional side of life' and the absence of any method applicable to training the emotions.

> "The school's neglect of this large and important part of our native equipment has been due to three reasons: (1) a mistaken conception of the school as an institution adapted primarily, if not solely, to the training of the intellectual"

### Henry 1916 (2)

Henry, T. S. (1916). The Education and Control of the Emotions. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/edu/

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument that educators now see thought and feeling as interdependent, which the author offers as a reason the neglect of emotion 'is beginning to lose its cogency'. No empirical data are reported; this is the article's own interpretive claim.

> "the mind is not composed of intellectual and emotional faculties set over against each other, but that there is a very vital connection and interplay of thoughts with feelings"
