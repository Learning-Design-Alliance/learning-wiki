# Graduate students were more facile than undergraduates at transforming ⟨x|p̂|p′⟩ and ⟨p|p̂|p′⟩, but interviews indicate this facility was largely due to memorized expressions rather than conceptual understanding

## Evidence

### Emily Marshman and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Emily Marshman and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Student difficulties with representations of quantum operators corresponding to observables. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.049](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.049)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Open-ended questions Q2 and Q3 administered to 68 first-year core graduate QM students at the University of Pittsburgh; 69% correctly transformed ⟨x|p̂|p′⟩ and 84% correctly transformed ⟨p|p̂|p′⟩ without using Dirac notation (Table III).

> "Table III shows that, in response to questions Q2 and Q3, 69% of the G students  were able to write ⟨𝑥|𝑝̂|𝑝′⟩ and 84% were able to write ⟨𝑝|𝑝̂|𝑝′⟩ without using Dirac notation."

### Emily Marshman and Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Emily Marshman and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Student difficulties with representations of quantum operators corresponding to observables. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.049](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.049)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Think-aloud interviews with graduate-student volunteers indicate that despite correct written transformations, many G students could not interpret ⟨𝑥|𝑝̂|𝑝′⟩ conceptually; the authors state their facility was "mainly due to the fact that they had memorized expressions for ⟨𝑥|𝑝′⟩ and ⟨𝑝|𝑝′⟩".

> "However,  in interviews, when asked about the interpretation of ⟨𝑥|𝑝̂|𝑝′⟩, many of them did not know that ⟨𝑥|𝑝̂|𝑝′⟩ is equivalent to representing 𝑝̂|𝑝′⟩ in the position representation."
