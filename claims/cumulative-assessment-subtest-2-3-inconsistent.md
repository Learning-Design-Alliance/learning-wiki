# Effects of cumulative assessment between subtests two and three were inconsistent across modules: significant in two modules (one in the expected direction, one opposite) and non-significant in two modules

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In module 3, initially low-scoring students' scores increased significantly more than those of high-scoring students between the combined subtests 1–2 and subtest 3. [→ Kerdijk Wouter 2013](#kerdijk-wouter-2013)
`q3 i?` In module 1, the decrease in scores of initially high-scoring students was significantly higher than the small increase of initially low-scoring students, contrary to expectation. [→ Kerdijk Wouter 2013 (2)](#kerdijk-wouter-2013-2)
`q3 i?` No significant differences in score change between subtests 2 and 3 were found in modules 2 and 4. [→ Kerdijk Wouter 2013 (3)](#kerdijk-wouter-2013-3)

## Evidence

### Kerdijk Wouter 2013

Kerdijk Wouter, Tio René A, Mulder B Florentine, Cohen-Schotanus Janke. (2013). Cumulative assessment: strategic choices to influence students’ study effort. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-172

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

t-test comparison of score change between combined subtests 1–2 and subtest 3 in module 3, where the third subtest was less difficult (p = .74); low scorers improved significantly more (t = 7.00, p = .00).

> "In line with our expectations, the scores of the low-scoring students increased significantly more than those of high-scoring students."

### Kerdijk Wouter 2013 (2)

Kerdijk Wouter, Tio René A, Mulder B Florentine, Cohen-Schotanus Janke. (2013). Cumulative assessment: strategic choices to influence students’ study effort. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-172

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

t-test comparison in module 1 with similar test difficulty; contrary to expectations, the high-scoring group's decrease was significantly larger than the low-scoring group's small increase (t = −3.24, p = .00).

> "In module 1, where test difficulty was similar between tests, the scores of low-scoring students increased whereas those of high-scoring students’ decreased."

### Kerdijk Wouter 2013 (3)

Kerdijk Wouter, Tio René A, Mulder B Florentine, Cohen-Schotanus Janke. (2013). Cumulative assessment: strategic choices to influence students’ study effort. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-172

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

t-tests comparing score change between combined subtests 1–2 and subtest 3 in modules 2 and 4 found no significant differences (module 2: t = .97, p = .33; module 4: t = -.02, p = .98); equivalence was not tested.

> "Against expectation, we found no significant differences in score change between subtests 2 and 3 in modules 2 and 4."
