## Evidence

### Chikeme Paulina Chigwara 2024

Chikeme Paulina Chigwara, Ihudiebube-Splendor Chikaodili Ndidiamaka, Ogbonnaya Ngozi Phoebe, Mbadugha Chisom Joy, Elodi Laurentia Onyinye. (2024). Flipped classroom model versus conventional teaching method: effects on nursing students’ self-directed learning readiness in a research methodology course. Pan African Medical Journal. https://doi.org/10.11604/pamj.2024.47.70.38359

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same 8-week quasi-experiment (n=64, 32 per group); Table 3 reports per-subscale mean differences with T values and p-values of 0.072, 0.069, and 0.898; no effect sizes are printed. Despite descriptive gains favoring FCM, the differences "were not statistically significant after the intervention".

> "Although the p-values for the self-management, desire for learning, and self-control variables were all greater than 0.05, the difference in averages between the two groups was not statistically significant after the intervention."

### Chikeme Paulina Chigwara 2024 (2)

Chikeme Paulina Chigwara, Ihudiebube-Splendor Chikaodili Ndidiamaka, Ogbonnaya Ngozi Phoebe, Mbadugha Chisom Joy, Elodi Laurentia Onyinye. (2024). Flipped classroom model versus conventional teaching method: effects on nursing students’ self-directed learning readiness in a research methodology course. Pan African Medical Journal. https://doi.org/10.11604/pamj.2024.47.70.38359

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Authors' interpretation in the discussion of the self-control finding, where the CTM group was at best; they suggest students may struggle to resist other online activities during pre-class audio-PowerPoint viewing. No data are reported for this explanation.

> "The fact remains that there are distractions with the online session of the flipped class. This means that while watching the lessons, it may have been difficult for the students to resist engaging in other activities like using Facebook, watching online football matches, or listening to music."
