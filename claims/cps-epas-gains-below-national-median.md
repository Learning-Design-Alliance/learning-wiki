## Evidence

### Elaine Allensworth 2008

Elaine Allensworth, Macarena Correa, Steve Ponisciak. (2008). From High School to the Future: ACT Preparation–Too Much, Too Late. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/high-school-future-act-preparation-too-much-too-late

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cohort analysis using ACT norming tables comparing CPS students' PLAN-to-ACT gains with matched national samples; "only about one-third or less of CPS students made the median ACT score in each of the four subject area tests."

> "improvements from the PLAN in the fall of tenth grade to the ACT in the spring of eleventh grade are well below median levels in all subjects."

### Elaine Allensworth 2008 (2)

Elaine Allensworth, Macarena Correa, Steve Ponisciak. (2008). From High School to the Future: ACT Preparation–Too Much, Too Late. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/high-school-future-act-preparation-too-much-too-late

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of EXPLORE benchmark-meeting rates for the 2005 ACT-taking cohort against national comparison figures shows CPS students entered high school at roughly national levels, yet those levels were insufficient for later benchmarks.

> "While the academic skills of CPS students who took the ACT were similar to students nationally when they entered high school and were also similar to state averages,19 these skill levels were not sufficient to meet most of the ninth-grade benchmark scores."
