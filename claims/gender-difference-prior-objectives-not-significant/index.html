Evidence¶

Mbah 2015¶

Mbah, B. A. (2015). Effects of prior knowledge of topics and the instructional objectives on students' achievement in Literature-in-English. Journal of Education and Practice, 6(20). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1079053

q2 · i? · causal · r1

ANCOVA of posttest scores by gender, with pretest as covariate, over all 120 students in the quasi-experiment (treatment and control groups together; residual df = 117). The gender effect was not significant at the .05 level (F = 1.059, table value 3.85). Descriptively, treatment-group males averaged 71.02 (SD 15.99, n = 39) and females 68.75 (SD 16.90, n = 24; the text gives 68.96), and control-group males 47.21 (n = 32) and females 48.96 (n = 28). The article reports no ANCOVA within the treatment group alone, although its discussion describes the Table 4 result as the treatment-group male-female difference. No effect size was printed. The article's own sentence under Table 4 restates the treatment-versus-conventional hypothesis rather than the gender one, an apparent error in the source.
