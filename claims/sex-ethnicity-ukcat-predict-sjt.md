## Evidence

### Garrud Paul 2018

Garrud Paul, McManus I. C. (2018). Impact of accelerated, graduate-entry medicine courses: a comparison of profile, success, and specialty destination between graduate entrants to accelerated or standard medicine courses in UK. BMC Medical Education 18:250. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1355-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple regression on UKMED graduate-entrant data predicting normalised FPAS SJT (pooled n = 750). Female sex (p = .012), BME ethnicity (p = .016) and UKCAT (p = .022) were significant; "female sex, white ethnicity, and higher UKCAT scores were significant predictors".

> "Multiple regression was then carried out using the significant univariate factors: this showed that female sex, white ethnicity, and higher UKCAT scores were significant predictors of the FPAS SJT score."
