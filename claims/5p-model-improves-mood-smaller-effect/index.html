Evidence¶

Srikoon et al. 2017¶

Srikoon, S., Bunterm, T., Nethanomsak, T., & Ngang, T. K. (2017). A comparative study of the effects of the neurocognitive-based model and the conventional model on learner attention, working memory and mood. Malaysian Journal of Learning and Instruction, 14(1), 83-110. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1150726

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

MANOVA multivariate test of mood (alertness, calmness, contentedness via the 16-item Bond-Ladder visual analogue scale) in the randomized Grade 9 study. The article reports the effect explained "the variance of 9.8 percent of mood" (partial eta-squared = 0.098).

"The instruction models significantly influenced mood at a 0.05 significance level (F(3,72)= 2.620, p=0.047) and can explain the variance of 9.8 percent of mood"
