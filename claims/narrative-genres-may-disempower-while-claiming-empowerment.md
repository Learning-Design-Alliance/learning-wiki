# Narrative and storying genres in educational inquiry risk disempowering the very people they claim to empower

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q1` argument or single case

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The new narrative genres have strengths but also weaknesses that may prove incapacitating, so sponsoring them in the name of empowerment may effectively disempower the people and causes they seek to work with. [→ Goodson 1994](#goodson-1994)

## Evidence

### Goodson 1994

Goodson, Ivor F. (1994). 'The Story So Far': Personal Knowledge and the Political. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED376160

`q1 · i?`

Theoretical argument in the paper's introduction: the author, sympathetic to narrative genres, nonetheless argues they require close scrutiny because their weaknesses may prove incapacitating. The article offers a reasoned argument, not empirical data, for this caution.

> "For whilst they have some obvious strengths, there are I think, some weaknesses which may prove incapacitating. If so, we may be sponsoring genres of inquiry in the name of empowerment, whilst at the same time, effectively disempowering the very people and causes we seek to work with."
