# Higher expressive vocabulary predicts membership in faster self-regulation trajectories

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q2` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Each 1 SD increase in expressive vocabulary raised odds of preschool-developer versus later-developer classification (OR = 1.40 MLSELD; OR = 1.23 Oregon) and of early versus intermediate classification (OR = 1.68 MLSELD; OR = 1.63 Oregon). [→ Montroy Janelle J. 2016](#montroy-janelle-j-2016)

## Evidence

### Montroy Janelle J. 2016

Montroy Janelle J., Bowles Ryan P., Skibbe Lori E., McClelland Megan M., Morrison Frederick J. (2016). The Development of Self-Regulation across Early Childhood. Developmental Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/dev0000159

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r3`

Logistic regression in the MLSELD preschool sample, controlling for age, race/ethnicity, and primary language, showed children were "1.40 times more likely" to be preschool versus later developers per 1 SD vocabulary increase (OR = 1.40). Oregon sample odds ratios were 1.23 (preschool vs. later) and 1.63 (early vs. intermediate); no other comparisons were significant.

> "For a 1 SD increase in expressive vocabulary, children in the MLSELD preschool sample were 1.40 times more likely to be classified as a preschool developer versus a later developer."
