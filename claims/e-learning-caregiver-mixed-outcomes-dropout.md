## Evidence

### Klimova Blanka 2019

Klimova Blanka, Valis Martin, Kuca Kamil, Masopust Jiri. (2019). E-learning as valuable caregivers’ support for people with dementia – A systematic review. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-019-4641-9

`q2 · i?`

Review-level finding across the six detected studies: the review reports a high drop-out rate in two studies and that the STAR e-learning portal RCT found a significant reduction in self-reported sense of competence in the experimental group.

> "In contrast, a high drop-out rate revealed the need to stimulate informal caregivers to participate in e-learning programs [25, 47]. One study [45] reported a reduction in self-reported sense of caring competence."

### Klimova Blanka 2019 (2)

Klimova Blanka, Valis Martin, Kuca Kamil, Masopust Jiri. (2019). E-learning as valuable caregivers’ support for people with dementia – A systematic review. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-019-4641-9

`q2 · i?`

Experimental study of 149 family caregivers using the guided self-help Internet intervention 'mastery over dementia' (MoD), assessed with the Informant Questionnaire on Cognitive Decline in the Elderly, CES-D, and Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale; the review reports the printed 55.7% non-completion figure.

> "MoD appears to be accessible for a broad range of family carers of people with dementia in terms of reach, adherence and user evaluation. The only drawback was a high percentage of carers who did not finish all lessons (55.7%)."
