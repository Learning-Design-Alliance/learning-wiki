## Evidence

### Doyle Patricia 2015

Doyle Patricia, VanDenKerkhof Elizabeth G, Edge Dana S, Ginsburg Liane, Goldstein David H. (2015). Self-reported patient safety competence among Canadian medical students and postgraduate trainees: a cross-sectional survey. BMJ Quality & Safety. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjqs-2014-003142

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Clinical-setting H-PEPSS responses from 141 postgraduate trainees; the response rate was only 32%, so the authors state results should be viewed with caution due to risk of bias in the sample.

> "The majority of postgraduate trainees reported confidence in the patient safety competencies gained in the clinical setting—clinical safety skills (84%), communicating effectively (81%), recognising and responding to adverse events (75%), working in teams (74%), managing safety risks (67%), culture of safety (63%) and understanding human and environmental factors (61%)."
