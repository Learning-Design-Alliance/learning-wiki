# Online father engagement training improves practitioner competencies, but confidence, competence and perceived effectiveness deteriorate from post-training to 2-month follow-up

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` After a 2-hour online training, practitioners' competencies increased from pre- to post-training and remained higher than pre-training at follow-up, yet scores significantly decreased from post-training to follow-up on all three competency measures. [→ Burn M. 2019](#burn-m-2019)

## Evidence

### Burn M. 2019

Burn M., Tully L. A., Jiang Y., Piotrowska P. J., Collins D. A. J., Sargeant K., Hawes D., Moul C., Lenroot R. K., Frick P. J., Anderson V., Kimonis E. R., Dadds M. R. (2019). Evaluating Practitioner Training to Improve Competencies and Organizational Practices for Engaging Fathers in Parenting Interventions. Child Psychiatry and Human Development. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10578-018-0836-2

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Single-group repeated-measures evaluation of online training (Study 2, N = 356) with pre, post and 2-month follow-up on the same FEQ measures. Follow-up scores remained "significantly higher than scores at pre-training" (e.g., competence t = 0.51, p < 0.001), but the post-to-follow-up deterioration was significant on all three scales; longer-term effect sizes ranged 0.47–0.67.

> "However, scores significantly decreased from post-training to follow-up on competence (t = − 0.13, p = 0.03), confidence (t = − 0.24, p < 0.001) and perceived effectiveness (t = − 0.37, p < 0.01), indicating a deterioration in improvements from post-training to follow-up."
