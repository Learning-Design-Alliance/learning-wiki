# Introducing human-AI tutoring improves relative MAP academic growth by 61% over an AI-only baseline within the IK bandwidth

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Within the IK bandwidth, human-AI tutoring was associated with a significant 61% improvement in relative MAP growth compared to the AI-only baseline period. [→ Ashish Gurung 2026](#ashish-gurung-2026)

## Evidence

### Ashish Gurung 2026

Ashish Gurung, Ge Gao, Jordan Gutterman, Danielle R. Thomas, Shivang Gupta, Lee Branstetter, Emma Brunskill, Vincent Aleven, Kenneth R. Koedinger. (2026). Improving Hybrid Human-AI Tutoring by Differentiating Human Tutor Roles Based on Student Needs. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11155 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.11155](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2605.11155)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental within-subject DiD analysis of 635 students in grades 5-8 comparing winter-to-spring (human-AI) with fall-to-winter (AI-only) relative MAP growth, using a multilevel model with grade random effects. The IK-bandwidth estimate was "significant improvements in relative MAP growth by an additional 61% (p = .003)"; the full-sample trend of 26% was not significant.

> "within the IK bandwidth, human-AI tutoring was associated with significant improvements in relative MAP growth by an additional 61% (p = .003). The full sample showed a similar but not significant trend of 26% improvement (p = .086)."
