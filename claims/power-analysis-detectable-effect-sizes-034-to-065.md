## Evidence

### Kersey 2018

Kersey, A. J., Braham, E. J., Csumitta, K. D., Libertus, M. E., & Cantlon, J. F. (2018). No intrinsic gender differences in children's earliest numerical abilities. npj Science of Learning, 3, 12. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0028-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Post-hoc power analysis across the five tasks. "we should have been able to detect small to medium effect sizes ranging from Cohen 's d = 0.34 to 0.65" at 80% power. Per-task detectable sizes: d = 0.65 (infant), d = 0.37 (Weber), d = 0.47 (How High), d = 0.52 (Give-N), d = 0.34 (TEMA).

> "Power analyses suggest that given the sizes of the samples analyzed here, we should have been able to detect small to medium effect sizes ranging from Cohen 's d = 0.34 to 0.65 (80% power, p = 0.05; Infant Numerosity Comparison (looking time): d = 0.65; Early Childhood Numerosity Comparison (w): d = 0.37"
