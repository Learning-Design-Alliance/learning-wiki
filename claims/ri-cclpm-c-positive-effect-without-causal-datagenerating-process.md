# The RI-CCLPM-C indicates a positive self-concept effect on achievement even when data are generated without any direct effects between self-concept and achievement

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In simulated data (SIM-NEW, N = 3527) generated with no direct effects between achievement and self-concept, the RI-CCLPM-C still yielded a statistically significant positive focal effect (β = 0.145), showing such effects can arise under non-causal data-generating processes. [→ Kimmo Sorjonen 2025](#kimmo-sorjonen-2025)

## Evidence

### Kimmo Sorjonen 2025

Kimmo Sorjonen, Bo Melin, Gustav Nilsonne. (2025). Still No Convincing Evidence for a Causal Effect of Academic Self-Concept on Achievement: A Reply to Núñez-Regueiro et al., 2025. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10083-7

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Simulation study using R (lavaan, MASS packages). SIM-NEW data (N = 3527, matching Marsh et al.'s sample) was generated with trait-like levels, autocorrelated state factors, and ability effects, but "no direct effects on each other". The RI-CCLPM-C focal effect was β = 0.145 [0.121; 0.168], p < 0.001, CFI = 0.997, RMSEA = 0.018.

> "The positive focal effect and good model fit (see Table 1) when fitting the RI-CCLPM-C to data generated as in Fig. 2 (SIM-NEW) shows that findings similar to those reported by Marsh et al. may be observed even if data has been generated without any direct effects between scores on self-concept and achievement."
