# Teachers qualitatively observed better retained experimental skills two to three years after the experiment-centered course, compared with students from classical tracks

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Teachers of third-year optics practicals observed that former students of the first-year course rapidly determined focal length, remembered self-collimation as a method to obtain a parallel beam, and were not afraid of experimenting, in contrast with students from more classical tracks. [→ C. Even 2017](#c-even-2017)

## Evidence

### C. Even 2017

C. Even, C. Balland, V. Guillet. (2017). Learning through experimenting: an original way of teaching geometrical optics. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/37/6/065707](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/37/6/065707)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Qualitative assessment by some course teachers who also taught third-year optics practicals and watched former first-year students practicing two or three years later. The article states this contrasts with students who followed a more classical way, in particular those from preparatory courses for Grandes Ecoles. No quantitative measures are printed.

> "basic experiments of general use for any optical layout were memorized (e.g., self -collimation, use of a lens, focal length determination). Students were able to rapidly determine the focal length of a given lens"
