# Inducing social presence in a chat-based writing condition did not improve comprehension compared with oral, standard written, or retrieval conditions

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` University students showed no significant differences in text-based or inference comprehension across oral, standard written, chat, and retrieval conditions, and Bayes factors favored the null hypothesis. [→ Jacob Leonie 2021](#jacob-leonie-2021)

## Evidence

### Jacob Leonie 2021

Jacob Leonie, Lachner Andreas, Scheiter Katharina. (2021). Does increasing social presence enhance the effectiveness of writing explanations? PLoS ONE 16(4): e0250406. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0250406

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r3`

MANCOVA on the four-condition randomized experiment (N = 137) with prior knowledge as covariate found no condition differences in comprehension; the article reports ηp2=.04 and adds Bayes factors (text-based BF01 = 9.43; inference BF01 = 128.04) supporting the null.

> "Results showed a main effect of prior knowledge, F(1, 132) = 14.59, p < .001, ηp2=.10, but no differences between conditions, F(3, 132) = 2.03, p = .062, ηp2=.04"
