## Evidence

### Watters Colm 2015

Watters Colm, Reedy Gabriel, Ross Alastair, Morgan Nicola J, Handslip Rhodri, Jaye Peter. (2015). Does interprofessional simulation increase self-efficacy: a comparative study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2014-005472

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison 2: cross-sectional postcourse comparison of FY1/2 doctors trained alone (UP; n=94) or interprofessionally (IP; n=62). Table 1 prints communication/teamwork post 78.7% (IP) vs 75.7% (UP), t=2; df 154; p<0.05.

> "Postcourse scores for doctors were higher (but not significantly so) for leadership, and significantly better for communication/teamwork in the IP group (p<0.05)."

### Watters Colm 2015 (2)

Watters Colm, Reedy Gabriel, Ross Alastair, Morgan Nicola J, Handslip Rhodri, Jaye Peter. (2015). Does interprofessional simulation increase self-efficacy: a comparative study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2014-005472

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same cross-sectional doctor comparison: the quote states the two-point difference was "not significantly so (t=1.4; df 154)". Leadership/management was also not significant (t=0.3; df 154).

> "Doctors' mean postcourse self-efficacy was higher by two percentage points (75–73%) in the IP group, but not significantly so (t=1.4; df 154)."
