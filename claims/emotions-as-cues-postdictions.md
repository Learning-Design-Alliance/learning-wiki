# Students also use achievement emotions as cues when making postdictions about their comprehension

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Stronger positive emotions were associated with higher postdictions and stronger negative emotions (including boredom) with lower postdictions, with small to medium correlations. [→ Prinz-Weiß 2023](#prinz-wei-2023)

## Evidence

### Prinz-Weiß 2023

Prinz-Weiß, A., Lukosiute, L., Meyer, M., & Riedel, J. (2023). The role of achievement emotions for text comprehension and metacomprehension. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09331-w

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analyses (N = 358) showed all achievement emotions significantly related to postdictions, with correlations of small to medium size (e.g., negative emotions r = -.29).

> "The positive emotions (i.e., enjoyment, hope, pride, and positive emotions overall) were positively correlated with postdictions, and the negative emotions (i.e., anger, anxiety, shame, boredom, hopelessness, and negative emotions overall) were negatively correlated with postdictions."
