## Evidence

### Chang Yu-Che 2016

Chang Yu-Che, Lee Ching-Hsing, Chen Chien-Kuang, Liao Chien-Hung, Ng Chip-Jin, Chen Jih-Chang, Chaou Chung-Hsien. (2016). Exploring the influence of gender, seniority and specialty on paper and computer-based feedback provision during mini-CEX assessments in a busy emergency department. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-016-9682-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Retrospective analysis of 1101 mini-CEX ratings of PGY1 residents collected over 26 months in a Taiwanese emergency department. The "overall completion rates" of the three written feedback components were reported descriptively, with no effect size.

> "The overall completion rates for the components of positive feedback, suggestions for development and agreed action plan were 85.3, 54.8, and 29.5 %, respectively."

### Chang Yu-Che 2016 (2)

Chang Yu-Che, Lee Ching-Hsing, Chen Chien-Kuang, Liao Chien-Hung, Ng Chip-Jin, Chen Jih-Chang, Chaou Chung-Hsien. (2016). Exploring the influence of gender, seniority and specialty on paper and computer-based feedback provision during mini-CEX assessments in a busy emergency department. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-016-9682-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same retrospective dataset of 1101 assessments; the article reports that "Only 22.9 % of the total mini-CEXs contained all three components of feedback" and 7.4% had none, attributing gaps to the busy ED setting. No effect size printed.

> "Only 22.9 % of the total mini-CEXs contained all three components of feedback, while 7.4 % of mini-CEXs were without any feedback."
