## Evidence

### Bolatli 2026

Bolatli, G., S. Birisci, and Z. Bolatli. 2026. An Alternative Approach in Anatomy Education: Design of a Learning Environment Based on Artificial Intelligence‐Supported Virtual Manipulatives and Investigation of Its Effectiveness. Clinical Anatomy 39, no. 1: 30–44. https://doi.org/10.1002/ca.70008

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Two-way ANOVA on pre/post Anatomy Achievement Test scores of 75 nursing students in three groups (C, SML, SML+AI) found a significant group effect, "F = 3.53; p < 0.05; η 2 = 0.047"; the experiment (pre-post) effect was larger (η2 = 0.246).

> "It was found that there was a statistically significant difference between the anatomy achievement scores obtained by the C, SML, and SML + AI groups (F = 3.53; p < 0.05; η 2 = 0.047)."

### Bolatli 2026 (2)

Bolatli, G., S. Birisci, and Z. Bolatli. 2026. An Alternative Approach in Anatomy Education: Design of a Learning Environment Based on Artificial Intelligence‐Supported Virtual Manipulatives and Investigation of Its Effectiveness. Clinical Anatomy 39, no. 1: 30–44. https://doi.org/10.1002/ca.70008

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

LCD post hoc results (Table 2) show SML+AI outscored C by 5.49 points (p = 0.018) and SML by 5.17 points (p = 0.033); C vs SML was not significant (p = 0.897).

> "a post hoc LCD test for multiple comparisons showed that the mean values of academic achievement scores differed significantly at the p < 0.05 level, confirming whether the SML + AI group exhibits superior posttest performance compared to both C and SML."
