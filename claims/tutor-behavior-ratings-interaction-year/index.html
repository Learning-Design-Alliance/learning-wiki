Evidence¶

Schmidt 1995¶

Schmidt, H., van der Arend, A., Kokx, I. & Boon, L. (1995). Peer versus staff tutoring in problem-based learning. Instructional Science 22: 279-285. https://doi.org/10.1007/bf00891781

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Student ratings on five-point Likert items administered at the end of each course, analyzed by ANOVA with tutor type and year of study. Staff tutors' greater use of subject-matter knowledge was significant, F(1,744) = 24.52, p < 0.001.

"Staff tutors made a more extensive use of their subject-matter knowledge than peer tutors: F(1,744) -- 24.52, MSe -- 0.56, p < 0.001."

Schmidt 1995 (2)¶

Schmidt, H., van der Arend, A., Kokx, I. & Boon, L. (1995). Peer versus staff tutoring in problem-based learning. Instructional Science 22: 279-285. https://doi.org/10.1007/bf00891781

q3 · i? · causal · r2

For relevance of contributions, the field experiment found both a main effect of tutor type, F(1,744) = 3.94, p < 0.05, and a significant interaction, F(3,742) = 6.97, p < 0.001; "peer tutors were rated higher in the first year, whereas staff were rated higher in subsequent years". A parallel interaction for asking stimulating questions, F(3,742) = 3.64, p < 0.05, had no main effect.

"Again, peer tutors were rated higher in the first year, whereas staff were rated higher in subsequent years."
