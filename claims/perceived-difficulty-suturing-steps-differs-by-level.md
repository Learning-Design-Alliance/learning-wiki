## Evidence

### Tang B 2020

Tang B, Zhang L, Alijani A. (2020). Evidence to support the early introduction of laparoscopic suturing skills into the surgical training curriculum. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-1986-z

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Survey responses from the 35 senior trainees who had practiced suturing during the course. The quoted counts are descriptive; the article reports a significant difference between groups without printing a test statistic.

> "Twenty-three (66%) senior trainees who had practiced the laparoscopic suturing indicated that laparoscopic needle manipulation was the most difficult skill to master, and 9 (26%) considered intracorporeal knot tying was difficult after having attended the training course"

### Tang B 2020 (2)

Tang B, Zhang L, Alijani A. (2020). Evidence to support the early introduction of laparoscopic suturing skills into the surgical training curriculum. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-1986-z

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Survey responses from the 36 junior trainees attending a 2-day basic laparoscopic course who could only guess at difficulty. The article suggests this indicates a need for clear description of knot-tying steps for juniors.

> "28 (78%) junior trainees who had not practiced the laparoscopic suturing predicted that the intra-corporeal knot tying would be the most difficult component of the laparoscopic suturing task"
