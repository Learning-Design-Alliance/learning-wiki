## Evidence

### Hoachlander 2020

Hoachlander, G. (2020). Building a system of college and career pathways in New Mexico. Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/new-mexico-education

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The report's analysis of 2018–19 New Mexico Public Education Department CTE enrollment data (Table 1, 104,220 duplicated course enrollments) shows "females were significantly underrepresented (19%, versus 49% in the total population)" in STEM, as were African American and Native American students.

> "in the state's second-largest program offering (science, technology, engineering, and mathematics, or STEM, which serves nearly 14,000 students), females were significantly underrepresented (19%, versus 49% in the total population), as were African Americans (0.7%, versus 2% in the total population) and Native Americans (6.4%, versus 10% in the total population)."

### Hoachlander 2020 (2)

Hoachlander, G. (2020). Building a system of college and career pathways in New Mexico. Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/new-mexico-education

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The report's analysis of CTE offerings found breadth often took priority over depth, "with relatively few students enrolling in the second or third course of a career pathway cluster," illustrated by the automotive and culinary sequences in a large-district school.

> "in a school of 1,800 students in one of the state's largest districts, 116 students enrolled in Automotive Technology I, but only 23 in Automotive Technology II, and just 11 in Automotive Technology III. Similarly, while 103 students enrolled in the introductory culinary arts course, only 15 enrolled in Pro Start I, and 17 in Pro Start II"
