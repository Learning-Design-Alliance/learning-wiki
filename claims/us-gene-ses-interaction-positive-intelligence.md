## Subclaims
`q4 i?` In US twin and sibling studies, the meta-analytic G × SES interaction on intelligence/achievement is significantly greater than zero (a′ = .074, SE = .020, p < .0005). [→ Tucker-Drob 2016](#tucker-drob-2016)

## Evidence

### Tucker-Drob 2016

Tucker-Drob, E. M., & Bates, T. C. (2016). Large Cross-National Differences in Gene × Socioeconomic Status Interaction on Intelligence. Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797615612727

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random-effects meta-regression of 43 effect sizes from 14 studies (k = 18 US, N = 10,831 pairs), using Purcell's (2002) biometric G × E model on re-analyzed or author-provided data. The US a′ estimate was "a′ = .074, SE = .020, p < .0005"; no standardized effect size beyond a′ is printed.

> "There was a significant G × SES effect for US studies (a′ = .074, SE = .020, p < .0005), but a trivial and non-significant G × SES effect for non-US studies (a′ = −.027, SE = .022, p = .223)."
