# The proportionality restrictions that the testlet model imposes on the bifactor model were implausible in the applied dataset, as specific-to-general loading quotients varied far from constant within testlets

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Quotients of specific-dimension loadings over general-dimension loadings were far from constant within each testlet, contradicting the testlet model's proportionality constraint in this application. [→ Rijmen 2009](#rijmen-2009)

## Evidence

### Rijmen 2009

Rijmen, F. (2009). Three Multidimensional Models for Testlet-Based Tests: Formal Relations and an Empirical Comparison. ETS Research Report RR-09-37. http://www.ets.org/research/contact.html

`q2 · i?`

Figure 6 plots the bifactor model's estimates of specific-dimension loadings divided by general-dimension loadings for the English assessment data. Were the testlet model to hold, these quotients should be approximately constant within each testlet, but "these quotients are far from constant within a testlet."
