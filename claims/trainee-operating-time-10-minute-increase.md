## Evidence

### Jabbar Salman AA 2019

Jabbar Salman AA, Ahmed Z, Mirza A, Nassar AHM. (2019). Laparoscopic Training Opportunities in an Emergency Biliary Service. JSLS: Journal of the Society of Laparoendoscopic Surgeons. https://doi.org/10.4293/JSLS.2019.00031

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Unadjusted analysis of operating time in the 2049-case series showed a mean of 84 minutes (SD, 57) for consultant-wholly-operated cases versus 87 minutes (SD, 36) for trainee cases (P = .22), with difficulty-stratified analyses yielding the overall ~10-minute difference. The article reports "a modest increase in the mean operating time of approximately 10 minutes".

> "Hence, overall trainee participation led to a modest increase in the mean operating time of approximately 10 minutes."

### Jabbar Salman AA 2019 (2)

Jabbar Salman AA, Ahmed Z, Mirza A, Nassar AHM. (2019). Laparoscopic Training Opportunities in an Emergency Biliary Service. JSLS: Journal of the Society of Laparoendoscopic Surgeons. https://doi.org/10.4293/JSLS.2019.00031

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Operating times stratified by Nassar difficulty grading in the same series: grade I-II cases showed a significant trainee time increase, while grade III-V cases showed no significant difference, attributed to case selection based on individual trainee ability. The article reports grade I-II times of "62 vs 72 minutes (P < .001)".

> "In the Grade I-II cohort (technically easier cases), the mean operating time of the principal surgeon vs trainee was 62 vs 72 minutes (P < .001). In the Grade III-V cohort, the mean operating time of the principal surgeon vs trainee was 108 vs 105 minutes (P < .38)."
