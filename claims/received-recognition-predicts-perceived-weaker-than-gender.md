## Evidence

### Meagan Sundstrom and N. G. Holmes 2025

Meagan Sundstrom and N. G. Holmes. (2025). Bias in physics peer recognition does not explain gaps in perceived recognition. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1038/s41567-025-02789-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-025-02789-w)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same linear mixed-model analysis of the 27-course survey dataset. The models show received recognition positively predicts perceived recognition in both contexts; the article adds that "gender has a larger effect than received recognition" and that a student would need three more nominations to close the average gender gap.

> "Received recognition also separately predicts perceived recognition: students receiving more recognition (and having the same gender, academic year, academic major, and race or ethnicity) report significantly higher perceptions of their recognition in both contexts (lab: b = 0.07 [0.01, 0.14], SE = 0.03, t = 2.25, p < 0.05; lecture: b = 0.11 [0.05, 0.17], SE = 0.03, t = 3.52, p < 0.001)."
