# A night of sleep improves auditory declarative memory in early adolescents, with a 20.6% advantage in paired-associate recall over an equal interval without sleep

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Adolescents tested 12 hours after learning following a night of sleep recalled more paired associates than those tested after the same interval without sleep (p<0.029). [→ Potkin KT 2012](#potkin-kt-2012)

## Evidence

### Potkin KT 2012

Potkin KT, Bunney WE Jr (2012) Sleep Improves Memory: The Effect of Sleep on Long Term Memory in Early Adolescence. PLoS ONE 7(8): e42191. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0042191

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized parallel-group experiment: 19 sleep and 18 no-sleep adolescents (outliers removed) learned 10 word pairs and were tested for cued recall 12 hours later. The sleep group scored 7.37 correct versus 6.11 (p = 0.029), "An increase of 20.6% in long-term memory".

> "An increase of 20.6% in long-term memory (Figure 1) was found as measured by the number correct in the paired-associate test following sleep, compared to the group which was tested at the same time interval, but without sleep (p ,0.029)."
