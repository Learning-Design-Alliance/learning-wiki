## Evidence

### Sabeti Fahimeh 2026

Sabeti Fahimeh, Ranjbar Azadeh, Kashaki Mandana, Khanjari Sedigheh, Banazadeh Marjan. (2026). Comparing between face-to-face and mobile learning for improving nurses’ performance in premature infant positioning: a quasi-experimental study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09855-8

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental nonequivalent control group study with 90 NICU nurses across four Tehran hospitals. One week post-intervention, total mean IPAT scores were 4.71 (control), 8.66 (face-to-face), and 8.57 (m-learning), with η² = 0.940. The quote confirms "the control group was significantly lower than in the face-to-face training (p < 0.001) and m-learning (p < 0.001) groups."

> "Tukey’s pairwise comparison showed the total mean score of positioning performance in the control group was significantly lower than in the face-to-face training (p < 0.001) and m-learning (p < 0.001) groups. There was no significant difference between face-to-face training and m-learning in terms of the total mean score of the IPAT (p = 0.71)"
