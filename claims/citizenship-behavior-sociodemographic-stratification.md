## Evidence

### Krijnen 2024

Krijnen, M. A., Wansink, B. G. J., van den Berg, Y. H. M., van Tartwijk, J., & Mainhard, T. (2024). Citizenship in the Elementary Classroom Through the Lens of Peer Relations. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09870-5

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review reports, citing several studies, that "higher levels of physical aggression and lower levels of relational aggression and prosocial behavior" were found for boys than girls, and offers gender-specific societal norms eliciting nominations as an alternative explanation.

> "Evidence of gender differences unequivocally found higher levels of physical aggression and lower levels of relational aggression and prosocial behavior for boys than for girls"

### Krijnen 2024 (2)

Krijnen, M. A., Wansink, B. G. J., van den Berg, Y. H. M., van Tartwijk, J., & Mainhard, T. (2024). Citizenship in the Elementary Classroom Through the Lens of Peer Relations. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09870-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Bukowski et al. (2017, 2020), that low-SES children "were more often seen as aggressive but less often as prosocial" and were less often nominated as accepted; researchers emphasized more research is needed as SES is infrequently addressed.

> "Children with a low socioeconomic status (SES) were more often seen as aggressive but less often as prosocial"
