## Evidence

### Philipp Bitzenbauer 2023

Philipp Bitzenbauer, Sarah Navarrete, Fabian Hennig, Malte S. Ubben, and Joaquin M. Veith. (2023). A cross-age study on secondary school students' views of stars. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020165](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020165)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Item-level analysis of domain 1 responses in the cross-age study: on the item stating "there are hundreds of stars in our solar system", 75.0% (lower), 70.9% (middle) and 62.7% (upper) of all participants agreed, which the authors read as "trouble with grasping the magnitude of our solar system".

> "item 1-5 which states that "there are hundreds of stars in our solar system." Here, 75.0% (lower), 70.9% (middle) and 62.7% (upper) of all participants agreed with the statement, indicating trouble with grasping the magnitude of our solar system."

### Philipp Bitzenbauer 2023 (2)

Philipp Bitzenbauer, Sarah Navarrete, Fabian Hennig, Malte S. Ubben, and Joaquin M. Veith. (2023). A cross-age study on secondary school students' views of stars. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020165](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020165)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Item-level analysis of domain 2 responses: on item 2-13, which states that "stars fade and disappear over time", an almost equal share of 63.5% (lower), 58.3% (middle) and 53.7% (upper) disagreed and "thus, perceive stars as permanent objects".

> "An almost equal share of 63.5% (lower), 58.3% (middle) and 53.7% (upper) disagreed with this statement and, thus, perceive stars as permanent objects."
