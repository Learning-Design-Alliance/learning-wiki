# Only about 9 percent of CPS students who start first grade without transferring out meet Illinois Learning Standards in reading and math by eleventh grade, and continuously enrolled students outperform returners

## Evidence

### Stephen M. Ponisciak 2005

Stephen M. Ponisciak. (2005). Understanding the Prairie State Achievement Exam: A descriptive report with analysis of student performance. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/understanding-prairie-state-achievement-exam-descriptive-report-analysis-student

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal cohort tracking of the 35,370 students enrolled as first-graders in CPS in spring 1994 through the 2004 PSAE. The report states "just 42 percent of the remaining group of students took the PSAE ten years later in 2004," with 18 percent meeting standards in reading or math and 9 percent in both.

> "In spring 1994, 35,370 students were enrolled as ﬁrst-graders in CPS. If we exclude students who transferred out of CPS from our calculations, we ﬁnd that just 42 percent of the remaining group of students took the PSAE ten years later in 2004. Of these students, only 18 percent met or exceeded standards in reading or math, and just 9 percent met or exceeded standards in both subjects."

### Stephen M. Ponisciak 2005 (2)

Stephen M. Ponisciak. (2005). Understanding the Prairie State Achievement Exam: A descriptive report with analysis of student performance. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/understanding-prairie-state-achievement-exam-descriptive-report-analysis-student

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of the 8,698 students progressing continuously through CPS from first grade 1994 to eleventh grade 2004 against students who left and returned. The report states continuously enrolled students were "less likely to have PSAE scores at the academic warning level in reading (9 versus 18 percent) and in math (16 versus 25 percent)."

> "The continuously enrolled students were less likely to have PSAE scores at the academic warning level in reading (9 versus 18 percent) and in math (16 versus 25 percent), and were more likely to meet or exceed standards on either test (40 versus 32 percent in reading, 30 versus 25 percent in math)."
