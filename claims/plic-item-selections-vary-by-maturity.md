# On a data-comparison item, expert responses are selected more often and novice responses less often with greater physics maturity

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On a pre-instruction question about comparing two k-values, the expert answer is picked increasingly more often with physics maturity, while the two novice answers are picked less frequently; the partial-expert response is picked almost equally by all students. [→ Cole Walsh 2019](#cole-walsh-2019)

## Evidence

### Cole Walsh 2019

Cole Walsh, Katherine N. Quinn, and N. G. Holmes. (2019). Assessment of critical thinking in physics labs. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1119/perc.2018.pr.walsh](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2018.pr.walsh)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Item-level analysis of pre-instruction PLIC responses (Fig. 2; N(FY)=584, N(BFY)=108, N(Grad)=34, N(Experts)=48) on comparing k-values via uncertainties. The article reports the expert answer "is picked increasingly more often with the physics maturity of the re- spondent".

> "As expected, the expert answer to this question is picked increasingly more often with the physics maturity of the re- spondent. Additionally, the two novice answers to this ques- tion are picked less frequently with additional physics matu- rity of the respondent."
