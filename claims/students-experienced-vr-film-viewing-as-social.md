# Students in an upper secondary classroom experienced the individually-oriented viewing of the VR film Travelling While Black as highly social, despite sitting silently in headset-wearing groups

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students experienced the VR film viewing as a highly social experience, characterized in terms of social and bodily relationships with the people in the film, despite the viewing itself appearing individual. [→ Kenneth Silseth 2024](#kenneth-silseth-2024)

## Evidence

### Kenneth Silseth 2024

Kenneth Silseth, Rolf Steier, Hans Christian Arnseth. (2024). Exploring students’ immersive VR experiences as resources for collaborative meaning making and learning. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-023-09413-0

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Cross-group review of transcribed reflective dialogues in a qualitative case study of one upper secondary class (30 students) showed the researchers' initial premise was overturned: "the students experienced the VR film as highly social." Students talked about being spoken to and seated among others in the film.

> "Our premise had been to use the reflective dialogues to compensate for the more individually oriented VR film viewing. However, we discovered that the students experienced the VR film as highly social."
