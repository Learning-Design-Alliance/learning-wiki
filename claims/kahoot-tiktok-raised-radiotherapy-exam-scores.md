## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students taught with Kahoot and TikTok added to traditional teaching scored significantly higher on the final exam than students taught traditionally (7.32 vs 6.02 points, p=0.0001). [→ Zapata-Martínez I 2025](#zapata-martinez-i-2025)

## Evidence

### Zapata-Martínez I 2025

Zapata-Martínez I, Rius-Diaz F, Lorenzo-Álvarez R, De la Peña-Fernández L. (2025). Radiation Oncology Active Learning in Undergraduate Medical Education: The Usefulness of Kahoot and TikTok. Journal of Cancer Education 40(6):847–853. https://doi.org/10.1007/s13187-025-02583-5

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Retrospective quasi-experimental cross-sectional comparison of two academic-year cohorts in one radiotherapy subject: control 2021/22 (n=161 examined, 6.02 ± 1.29) vs experimental 2022/23 (n=151 examined, 7.32 ± 1.31), "an increase in the mean grade of 12.9%" (p=0.0001). No effect size printed.

> "The students in the experimental group, in whose academic year the use of Kahoot and TikTok was implemented, obtained significantly higher grades than in the control group, with an increase in the mean grade of 12.9%"
