Evidence¶

Schmeck 2015¶

Schmeck, A., Opfermann, M., van Gog, T., Paas, F., & Leutner, D. (2015). Measuring cognitive load with subjective rating scales during problem solving: differences between immediate and delayed ratings. Instructional Science, 43, 93–114. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-014-9328-3

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Experiment 2 randomly assigned 107 students to sequences; Group 1 (n = 56) received increasing complexity. The quote shows the "delayed rating was signiﬁcantly higher than the average rating" for mental effort, d = 0.88; perceived difficulty showed the same pattern, d = 0.81.

"For mental effort, however, the delayed rating was signiﬁcantly higher than the average rating for Group 1 ( M = 4.34, SD = 1.17 vs. M = 3.56, SD = 0.60), t(55) = 5.69, p\ .001, d = 0.88"

Schmeck 2015 (2)¶

Schmeck, A., Opfermann, M., van Gog, T., Paas, F., & Leutner, D. (2015). Measuring cognitive load with subjective rating scales during problem solving: differences between immediate and delayed ratings. Instructional Science, 43, 93–114. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-014-9328-3

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Experiment 2 Group 2 (n = 51) received problems in decreasing-complexity order. The delayed mental effort rating was again significantly higher than the average rating, d = 0.71; perceived difficulty showed the same pattern (delayed M = 3.75 vs. average M = 3.11, d = 0.71).

"as well as Group 2( M = 4.25, SD = 0.89 vs. M = 3.72, SD = 0.63), t(50) = 5.85, p\ 0.001, d = 0.71."
