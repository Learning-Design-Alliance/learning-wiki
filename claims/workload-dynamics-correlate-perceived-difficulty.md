# Workload variability and slope metrics correlate strongly with perceived course difficulty

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` WTS course time-on-task correlates with perceived course difficulty at r=0.86, and variability measures such as WTS Time-on-Task Range (r=0.81) and LMS Time-on-Task SD (r=0.75) are key indicators of perceived difficulty. [→ Paul V. Sargent 2026](#paul-v-sargent-2026)

## Evidence

### Paul V. Sargent 2026

Paul V. Sargent, Isabel Hilliger, Jorge A. Baier. (2026). Beyond Time on Task: A Novel Analytical Framework for Assessing Student Workload and Its Relationship with Learning. Journal of Learning Analytics, 13(1), 110–129. https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2026.8725

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Course-level Spearman correlation analysis of workload features against perceived difficulty across 14 engineering courses. WTS course time-on-task correlated at "r=0.86" and LMS Course Average Sessions at r=0.82 with perceived difficulty.

> "Notably, measures of variability showed strong correlations with Perceived Difficulty, primarily regarding WTS Time-on-Task Range (r=0.81 ), LMS Time-on-Task SD (r=0.75 ), and WTS Time-on-Task SD (r=0.73 ). These relationships indicate that variability measures—from both LMS and WTS data—are key indicators of perceived course difficulty."

### Paul V. Sargent 2026 (2)

Paul V. Sargent, Isabel Hilliger, Jorge A. Baier. (2026). Beyond Time on Task: A Novel Analytical Framework for Assessing Student Workload and Its Relationship with Learning. Journal of Learning Analytics, 13(1), 110–129. https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2026.8725

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Week-level correlation analysis of weekly workload slopes against perceived difficulty. Positive slopes correlated at "r=0.45"; negative slopes correlated more mildly at r=0.23 (WTS) and r=0.17 (LMS).

> "Slope measures were also related to higher levels of perceived difficulty, most notably in the case of the WTS Time-on-Task positive slope ( r=0.45 ). Interestingly, negative slopes were also related to higher perceived difficulty, albeit with milder correlation coefficients (WTS:r=0.23 , p<0.001 ; LMS: r=0.17 , p<0.01 )."
