# Similarity attraction was not supported for self-efficacy matching: matched students did not show a more positive attitude towards their tutee

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who taught a tutee similar to them in SE did not show a more positive attitude towards the tutee than students with a mismatched tutee, contrary to the similarity attraction hypothesis. [→ Betty Tärning 2019](#betty-tarning-2019)

## Evidence

### Betty Tärning 2019

Betty Tärning, Annika Silvervarg, Agneta Gulz, Magnus Haake. (2019). Instructing a Teachable Agent with Low or High Self-Efficacy – Does Similarity Attract? International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-018-0167-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mann-Whitney U-test on the total attitude score (range 3–15) among 113 students in matched (n = 55) vs. mismatched (n = 58) conditions; no significant effect. Separate low-SE (p = .38) and high-SE (p = .49) student analyses, Holm corrected, also showed no effects.
