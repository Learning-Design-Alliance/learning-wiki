## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Study 1, one-way ANOVAs comparing post scores across the PT and two No PT sections found no significant differences on total scores or any individual rubric dimension. [→ Kruse 2013](#kruse-2013)
`q3 i?` The section x pre/post interaction for total scores failed to reach significance despite a descriptive pattern favoring the PT section. [→ Kruse 2013 (2)](#kruse-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Kruse 2013

Kruse, G. & Drews, D. (2013). Using Performance Tasks to Improve Quantitative Reasoning in an Introductory Mathematics Course. International Journal for the Scholarship of Teaching and Learning, 7(2), Article 19. https://doi.org/10.20429/ijsotl.2013.070219

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental Study 1 compared post scores across three sections (PT, No PT/Pre Test, No PT/Quant Skills) with one-way ANOVAs. The article reports the analyses "failed to reveal any significant differences between sections"; equivalence was not tested.

> "These analyses failed to reveal any significant differences between sections, on total scores or scores on any individual rubric dimension, all Fs<1.00 except for "complete," F(2,58) = 2.41, p = .098."

### Kruse 2013 (2)

Kruse, G. & Drews, D. (2013). Using Performance Tasks to Improve Quantitative Reasoning in an Introductory Mathematics Course. International Journal for the Scholarship of Teaching and Learning, 7(2), Article 19. https://doi.org/10.20429/ijsotl.2013.070219

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Two-way ANOVAs with repeated measures compared pre/post change in the PT and No PT/Pre Test sections. Descriptive means favored the PT section, but the article states "the interaction failed to reach significance" and no main effects were found.

> "the interaction failed to reach significance. In addition there was no overall pre/post difference or an overall difference between sections."
