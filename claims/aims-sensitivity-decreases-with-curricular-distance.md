## Evidence

### Romine William L. 2016

Romine William L., Miller Michele E., Knese Shawn A., Folk William R. (2016). Multilevel Assessment of Middle School Students' Interest in the Health Sciences: Development and Validation of a New Measurement Tool. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-02-0034

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Within-subjects MANOVA on Rasch subscales equated to a 0–10 scale, testing sensitivity (average magnitude of change) across the three subscales for 194 students measured pre and post; the null hypothesis of no difference was rejected at α = 0.05.

> "After translation of respective Rasch logit scales to a common scale between 0 and 10, we found significant differences in sensitivity with distance from the intervention (Λ = 0.915, F2192 = 8.86, p << 0.001)."

### Romine William L. 2016 (2)

Romine William L., Miller Michele E., Knese Shawn A., Folk William R. (2016). Multilevel Assessment of Middle School Students' Interest in the Health Sciences: Development and Validation of a New Measurement Tool. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-02-0034

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Post hoc Bonferroni contrasts showed Interest in Vision Care (M = 2.18, SD = 1.77) significantly more sensitive than Interest in Healthcare (M = 1.68) and Interest in Science (M = 1.70) at α = 0.01, with no significant difference between the latter two.

> "The close construct, Interest in Vision Care, had the highest sensitivity (M = 2.18, SD = 1.77, CI95% = 1.93–2.43)."

### Romine William L. 2016 (3)

Romine William L., Miller Michele E., Knese Shawn A., Folk William R. (2016). Multilevel Assessment of Middle School Students' Interest in the Health Sciences: Development and Validation of a New Measurement Tool. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-02-0034

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

The authors report this null contrast as conflicting with the expectation that sensitivity should decrease significantly with distance from the intervention, and suggest students may perceive science and healthcare careers as equidistant from the intervention.

> "However, we were surprised to find no significant difference in sensitivity between measures of interest in healthcare and science, which we labeled a priori as "proximal" and "distal," respectively."
