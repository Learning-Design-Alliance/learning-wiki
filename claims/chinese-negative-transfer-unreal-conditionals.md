## Subclaims
`q2 i?` English unreal conditionals involving backshifting of tense are challenging for Chinese learners because Chinese uses one verb-tense pattern. [→ Wu 2012](#wu-2012)

## Evidence

### Wu 2012

Mei Wu. (2012). The Probability Approach to English If-conditional Sentences. English Language Teaching, 5(5). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v5n5p37

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Literature-review analysis citing Zhang (2005) and Wu (1994): Chinese uses context, time reference and semantic logic rather than verb morphology to differentiate conditional types, making unreal conditionals with tense backshifting difficult for Chinese learners.

> "The Chinese language has only one verb-tense pattern, so Chinese speakers do not change tenses and modality in the verb phrase to indicate whether the conditional sentence is factual, hypothetical or counterfactual"
