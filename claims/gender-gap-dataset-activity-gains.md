## Evidence

### Schwortz 2020

Schwortz, A. C., & Burrows, A. C. (2020). Authentic Science Experiences with STEM Datasets: Post-secondary Results and Potential Gender Influences. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2004.12448](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2004.12448)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Gender subsample comparison in the matched pre/post study: male normalized gain 0.458 versus a female gain "significantly lower at 0.274", with ANOVA confirming the difference statistically significant.

> "The gain of male participants' was 0.458 (comparable to Hake's 1998 finding for active learning situations), while female participants' was significantly lower at 0.274 (comparable to Hake's lecture-based classrooms). ANOVA confirmed that these differences were statistically significant (Table 11)."
