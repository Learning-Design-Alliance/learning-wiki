## Evidence

### Rui Gomes Neves 2010

Rui Gomes Neves, Jorge Carvalho Silva & Vítor Duarte Teodoro. (2010). Computational Modelling with Modellus: An Enhancement Vector for the General University Physics Course. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1006.4662](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1006.4662)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Classroom observation reported in the vectors section (§3.1). The authors add that correcting the models while visualising the effect in the animation was "an essential advantage of the modelling process with Modellus" in solving this difficulty.

> "At ﬁrst, many students were not aware that the trigonometric functions are deﬁned with diﬀerent conventions and were frequently unable to make the vectors point in the right direction."

### Rui Gomes Neves 2010 (2)

Rui Gomes Neves, Jorge Carvalho Silva & Vítor Duarte Teodoro. (2010). Computational Modelling with Modellus: An Enhancement Vector for the General University Physics Course. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1006.4662](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1006.4662)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Reported in the Newton's equations section (§3.4) during the ball-throw modelling activity, where students used position and velocity time graphs to answer questions about the trajectory. The article presents this as a difficulty addressed through immediate visual feedback.

> "Again the choice of the appropriate time step was an important source of diﬃculties. This was resolved using the possibility of changing the mathematical model and immediately observe the eﬀect of the correction on the animation."
