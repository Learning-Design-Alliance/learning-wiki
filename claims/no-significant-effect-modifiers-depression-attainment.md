## Evidence

### Wickersham A. 2021

Wickersham A., Sugg H.V.R., Epstein S., Stewart R., Ford T., Downs J. (2021). Systematic Review and Meta-analysis: The Association Between Child and Adolescent Depression and Later Educational Attainment. Journal of the American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2020.10.008

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random-effects meta-regression across pooled studies tested mean baseline age and follow-up period as effect modifiers; neither was significant, and sensitivity analyses confirmed this. Stratification by study quality yielded similar pooled effect sizes.

> "Meta-regression did not show any statistically significant effects of follow-up period (p = .934) or age at baseline (p = .989), and this remained the case following sensitivity analyses"

### Wickersham A. 2021 (2)

Wickersham A., Sugg H.V.R., Epstein S., Stewart R., Ford T., Downs J. (2021). Systematic Review and Meta-analysis: The Association Between Child and Adolescent Depression and Later Educational Attainment. Journal of the American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2020.10.008

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Narrative synthesis of effect-modifier findings across included studies. The review also reports some evidence that school connectedness and ethnicity moderated the association, but "the modifying effect of ethnic variation was inconsistent."

> "The most commonly investigated effect modifier was gender, although findings were inconclusive, with some studies finding a significant association only in male participants,36,48 others only in female participants,43 and still more finding no effect of gender."
