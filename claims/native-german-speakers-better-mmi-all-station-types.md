## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Native German speakers outperformed non-native speakers at the MMI, and the effect did not depend on station type, contradicting the expectation that simulation stations would be less language-dependent. [→ Knorr M 2019](#knorr-m-2019)

## Evidence

### Knorr M 2019

Knorr M, Meyer H, Sehner S, Hampe W, Zimmermann S. (2019). Exploring sociodemographic subgroup differences in multiple mini-interview (MMI) performance based on MMI station type and the implications for the predictive fairness of the Hamburg MMI. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1674-z

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical linear model of the MMI sample (N = 1438 candidates; 88.5% German as first language). The language effect was uniform across simulation, interview and group stations; the authors state this "suggests that the language problem in the MMI is general rather than context-specific."

> "Candidates who spoke German as their first language performed better (b = 0.16, p < 0.001), irrespective of station type, while the effect of medical family background on MMI performance depended on station type (p = 0.004)."
