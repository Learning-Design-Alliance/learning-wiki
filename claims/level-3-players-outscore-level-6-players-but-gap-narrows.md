# The control group playing the easier States Level 3 outscored the experimental group playing States Level 6 on all three measures, though the gap narrowed across trials

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The control group (States Level 3) had better mean scores than the experimental group (States Level 6) on percent correct, distance error, and seconds on both first and last trials. [→ Prell et al. 2012](#prell-et-al-2012)
`q2 i?` The experimental group closed the first-trial gap on percent correct by 6.65 points (11.75 to 5.10) by the last trial. [→ Prell et al. 2012 (2)](#prell-et-al-2012-2)

## Evidence

### Prell et al. 2012

Jerry Prell, William R. Nelson, and John Foshay. (2012). The Effectiveness of Learning Geography Using Computer-based Games. Journal of Special Education Apprenticeship, Vol. 1, No. 2. https://sheppardsoftware.com/USA_Geography/USA_G2_double2.html

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Between-group comparison in the matched-pairs study: the control group outscored the experimental group on the first trial by 11.75 points in percent correct (69.90 vs. 58.15) and by 5.10 on the last trial (87.65 vs. 82.55), with similar patterns for distance error and seconds.

> "the control group, which played States Level 3, had better mean scores than the experimental group, which played States Level 6."

### Prell et al. 2012 (2)

Jerry Prell, William R. Nelson, and John Foshay. (2012). The Effectiveness of Learning Geography Using Computer-based Games. Journal of Special Education Apprenticeship, Vol. 1, No. 2. https://sheppardsoftware.com/USA_Geography/USA_G2_double2.html

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive gap analysis from the same study: the first-to-last-trial gap in percent correct narrowed by 6.65 points, and analogous gap reductions of 319.82 miles and 36.75 seconds occurred for distance error and time.

> "though the experimental group still scored lower on the last trial, they closed the gap that existed in the first trial by 6.65 (11.75, 5.10)."
