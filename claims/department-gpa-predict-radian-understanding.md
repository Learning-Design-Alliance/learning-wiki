# Academic department and GPA predicted correctness: engineering students had the highest odds of correct responses, and self-reported course grades mostly did not predict performance

## Evidence

### Yossi Ben-Zion 2025

Yossi Ben-Zion, Jianqiang Liu, Chun-Ming Wang, Atanu Rajak, and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2025). Global Blind Spot in Understanding Trigonometric Derivatives: A Multinational Analysis. [doi:10.1103/t2fv-2spd](https://doi.org/10.1103/t2fv-2spd)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Multivariate binary logistic regression predicting Q3 correctness in the 769-student survey (73.7% correct classification). The article also reports US vs. Israel OR = 0.387, p = 0.002, and no significant effects for GPA, mechanics, calculus, or academic year.

> "The analysis revealed that engineering students had significantly higher odds of answering correctly compared to chemistry students (OR = 3.253, 95% CI: 1.854–5.709, p < 0.001)."

### Yossi Ben-Zion 2025 (2)

Yossi Ben-Zion, Jianqiang Liu, Chun-Ming Wang, Atanu Rajak, and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2025). Global Blind Spot in Understanding Trigonometric Derivatives: A Multinational Analysis. [doi:10.1103/t2fv-2spd](https://doi.org/10.1103/t2fv-2spd)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariate binary logistic regression predicting Q4 correctness (63.0% correct classification). The article reports GPA 95+ OR = 2.361, p = 0.007; Engineering vs. Chemistry OR = 1.884, p = 0.015; confidence OR = 1.229, p = 0.010; no significant country or grade effects.

> "The analysis showed that students with a GPA of 95+ had significantly higher odds of answering correctly compared to those with lower GPAs (OR = 2 .361, 95% CI: 1.259–4.426, p = 0.007)."
