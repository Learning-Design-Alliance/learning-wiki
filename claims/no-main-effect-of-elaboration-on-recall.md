# Elaboration during the problem-based discussion had no significant main effect on immediate or delayed recall

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` On the immediate posttest, neither elaboration (F(1, 62) = 2.36, p = .13) nor prior knowledge (F(1, 62) = 1.81, p = .18) had a significant main effect on recall; the delayed-posttest elaboration effect was also non-significant (F(1, 62) = .69, p = .41). ## Evidence

### Floris M. Van Blankenstein 2013

Floris M. Van Blankenstein, Diana H. J. M. Dolmans, Cees P. M. Van der Vleuten, Henk G. Schmidt. (2013). Relevant prior knowledge moderates the effect of elaboration during small group discussion on academic achievement. Instructional Science, 41, 729-744. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-012-9252-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Main-effect tests from the 2×2 ANOVA on the immediate posttest; the delayed-posttest elaboration main effect was likewise non-significant (F(1, 62) = .69, p = .41, g2 = .01). The article prints no standardized effect size for these null findings.

> "On the immediate posttest, neither elaboration, F(1, 62) = 2.36, p = .13, g2 = .04 (small effect), nor prior knowledge, F(1, 62) = 1.81, p = .18, g2 = .03 (small effect), had a signiﬁcant effect on recall."
