## Evidence

### Owens Melinda T. 2017

Owens Melinda T., Tanner Kimberly D. (2017). Teaching as Brain Changing: Exploring Connections between Neuroscience and Innovative Teaching. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-01-0005

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The article's synthesis of psychology research on passive study versus retrieval, cited in its discussion of the hypothetical student Alessa. It reports that "mere passive exposure to knowledge, such as rereading a previously read passage, is not very effective at creating memories." No effect sizes are printed.

> "many psychology studies have confirmed that mere passive exposure to knowledge, such as rereading a previously read passage, is not very effective at creating memories (Roediger and Butler, 2011)."

### Owens Melinda T. 2017 (2)

Owens Melinda T., Tanner Kimberly D. (2017). Teaching as Brain Changing: Exploring Connections between Neuroscience and Innovative Teaching. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-01-0005

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article reports, citing three biology-education studies, that "assigning homework in college biology classes can increase performance," and links frequent active homework to course structure shown to reduce achievement gaps for underrepresented groups. No effect sizes are printed.

> "Studies suggest that assigning homework in college biology classes can increase performance (Lefcort and Eiger, 2003; Orr and Foster, 2013; Carnegie, 2015)."
