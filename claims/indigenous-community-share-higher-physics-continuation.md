## Evidence

### Eamonn Corrigan 2024

Eamonn Corrigan, Martin Williams, and Mary A. Wells. (2024). Beyond gender: The intersectional impact of community demographics on the continuation rates of male and female students into high school physics. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1139/cjp-2023-0319](https://doi.org/10.1139/cjp-2023-0319)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

RQ1 mixed-effects regression on Ontario school-level SCR data. Indigenous proportion was the most influential race/Indigeneity predictor, with printed estimates "β11 = 1.21±0.21; β12 = 1.89±0.30"; no standardised effect size is reported.

> "The proportion of Indigenous individuals within a community had the second-largest average positive effect size (β11 = 1.21±0.21; β12 = 1.89±0.30)."

### Eamonn Corrigan 2024 (2)

Eamonn Corrigan, Martin Williams, and Mary A. Wells. (2024). Beyond gender: The intersectional impact of community demographics on the continuation rates of male and female students into high school physics. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1139/cjp-2023-0319](https://doi.org/10.1139/cjp-2023-0319)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

RQ2 mixed-effects regression with the male-female SCR gap as outcome. Indigenous proportion significantly narrowed the gap in both grades ("β11 = −0.51 ± 0.25; β12 = −1.4 ± 0.40"); no standardised effect size is printed.

> "Conversely, the proportion of Indigenous people in a school’s community predicted a decrease in the gender gap for both grade 11 and 12 physics (β11 = −0.51 ± 0.25; β12 = −1.4 ± 0.40)."
