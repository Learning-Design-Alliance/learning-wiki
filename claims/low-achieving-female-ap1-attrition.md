# Low-achieving female AP1 students are less likely than male counterparts to progress to AP2, with no significant gender differences for middle and top achievers

## Evidence

### Turnbull 2019

Turnbull, S. M., Vanholsbeeck, F., Locke, K., & O'Neale, D. R. J. (2019). Bourdieu, networks, and movements: Using the concepts of habitus, field and capital to understand a network analysis of gender differences in undergraduate physics. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0222357](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0222357)

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Persistence analysis of AP1 to AP2 progression by achievement third; the study reports "40.38% of male students, and 28.29% of female students" and "OR =0.58, CI: 0.35-0.98" for low achievers.

> "Of the students from AP1 who ranked in the bottom third of achievers (low achievers), we found that 40.38% of male students, and 28.29% of female students progressed to AP2. Thus, female students from this low achieving group were around 1.717 (OR =0.58, CI: 0.35-0.98) times less likely to progress from AP1 to AP2."

### Turnbull 2019 (2)

Turnbull, S. M., Vanholsbeeck, F., Locke, K., & O'Neale, D. R. J. (2019). Bourdieu, networks, and movements: Using the concepts of habitus, field and capital to understand a network analysis of gender differences in undergraduate physics. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0222357](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0222357)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same persistence analysis for middle and top achievement thirds; the study reports non-significant odds ratios ("OR = 0.84, CI:0.56-1.24" and "OR = 0.77, CI: 0.49-1.21"), with no printed standardized effect size.

> "There were no significant gender differences in the middle (OR = 0.84, CI:0.56-1.24) and top achieving (OR = 0.77, CI: 0.49-1.21) AP1 students who went to AP2."
