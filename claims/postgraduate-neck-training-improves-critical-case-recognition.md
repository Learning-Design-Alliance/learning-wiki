## Evidence

### Nákity K 2025

Nákity K, Kasza BB, Tatár BB, Szűcs M, Kis D, Barzó P, Domján A. (2025). Assessment of differential diagnostic skills of physiotherapists related to the cervical spine - approaches to improving effectiveness: observational, cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education 25:1065. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07682-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression in the cross-sectional survey of 128 physiotherapists found "postgraduate training affected decision-making specific to the neck (95% CI 1.25–7.99, p = 0.014)" for critical medical cases; no point odds ratio is printed for this finding.

> "postgraduate training affected decision-making specific to the neck (95% CI 1.25–7.99, p = 0.014). In addition, all CM decisions were positively influenced by therapists working with orthopedic (95% CI 0.18–0.99, p = 0.047) and traumatic (95% CI 0.11–0.65, p = 0.004) patients."

### Nákity K 2025 (2)

Nákity K, Kasza BB, Tatár BB, Szűcs M, Kis D, Barzó P, Domján A. (2025). Assessment of differential diagnostic skills of physiotherapists related to the cervical spine - approaches to improving effectiveness: observational, cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education 25:1065. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07682-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Case-level logistic regressions in the same survey show neck-specific postgraduate training predicted correct decisions for the dens fracture, myelopathy, radiculopathy, and atlantoaxial instability cases, with printed confidence intervals and p-values as quoted; no point odds ratios are printed in the text.

> "Participants who had completed postgraduate training in the neck were significantly more likely to make appropriate decisions for cases CS 1 (95% CI 2.02–9.36, p = 0.001), CS 2 (95% CI 1.86-181.02, p = 0.030), CS 4 (95% CI 1.20–5.72, p = 0.016), and CS 7 (95% CI 1.21–8.89, p = 0.025) than participants who did not complete postgraduate training in the neck."
