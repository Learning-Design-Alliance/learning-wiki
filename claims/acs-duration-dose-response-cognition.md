### Phansikar 2019

Phansikar, M.; Ashrafi, S.A.; Khan, N.A.; Massey, W.V.; Mullen, S.P. (2019). Active Commute in Relation to Cognition and Academic Achievement in Children and Adolescents: A Systematic Review and Future Recommendations. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph16245103

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r1`

Review synthesis of dose–response evidence for ACS and cognition. One study categorized ACS duration as less than or greater than 15 min and found "girls (but not boys) active commuting for greater than 15 min performed significantly better"; another found better cognitive map accuracy with more than four days per week of walking to school.

> "They categorized the duration as either less than 15 min or greater than 15 min and found that girls (but not boys) active commuting for greater than 15 min performed significantly better on numeric tasks, reasoning tasks, and overall cognitive performance, as compared to girls who active commuted for less than 15 min [45]."
