## Evidence

### Sze Kai Ping 2026

Sze Kai Ping, Lim Jia Qing, Loke Yng Miin, Ding Teck Yong Gabriel, Wee Wei Chieh, Chen Tongyuan, Wu Jingkai Lawrence, Bansal Vivek, Fong Qi Wei, Chow Minyang. (2026). Human-Edited Generative AI-Assisted Multiple-Choice Questions in Postgraduate Family Medicine: Blinded Cross-Sectional Comparative Psychometric Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/100179

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Distractor-level analysis of 30 items per origin using Fisher exact tests; the authors report both comparisons "did not reach statistical significance (Fisher exact P=.46 and P=.41, respectively)". Fourteen GenAI-assisted items (46.7%) versus 9 educator-crafted (30.0%) had 1 or 2 negatively discriminating distractors.

> "Seventeen GenAI-assisted items (56.7%) had 3 or 4 nonfunctioning distractors, compared with 13 educator-crafted items (43.3%)"

### Sze Kai Ping 2026 (2)

Sze Kai Ping, Lim Jia Qing, Loke Yng Miin, Ding Teck Yong Gabriel, Wee Wei Chieh, Chen Tongyuan, Wu Jingkai Lawrence, Bansal Vivek, Fong Qi Wei, Chow Minyang. (2026). Human-Edited Generative AI-Assisted Multiple-Choice Questions in Postgraduate Family Medicine: Blinded Cross-Sectional Comparative Psychometric Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/100179

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Co-occurrence analysis of negative-discrimination items and distractor red flags in the same distractor analysis. The authors state this pattern "suggests that weak discrimination may be partly explained by option-set problems" but that the data cannot establish directionality.

> "Among the 6 GenAI-assisted items with negative corrected point-biserial correlations, 5 had at least 3 nonfunctioning distractors and 4 had 1 or 2 negatively discriminating distractors"
