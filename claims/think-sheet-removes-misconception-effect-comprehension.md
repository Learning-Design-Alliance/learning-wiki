# Under reading-only, students with more misconceptions achieved better comprehension, but completing a think sheet removed this effect of misconceptions on comprehension

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A significant overall interaction between number of misconceptions and instructional method emerged for comprehension (ΔR² = .10, p = .009). [→ Anja Prinz-Weiß & Elisa Röhm 2025](#anja-prinz-wei-elisa-rohm-2025)
`q3 i?` In the control group, more misconceptions predicted significantly better comprehension (b = 0.55, p = .012), whereas the effect was not significant in the think-sheet or think-sheet-reflection groups. [→ Anja Prinz-Weiß & Elisa Röhm 2025 (2)](#anja-prinz-wei-elisa-rohm-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Anja Prinz-Weiß & Elisa Röhm 2025

Anja Prinz-Weiß & Elisa Röhm. (2025). To what extent can think sheets with or without reflection prompts support self-regulated learning from text? Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09419-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multiple regression analysis on comprehension in the online experiment (N = 92 randomized to three conditions) showed a significant overall interaction, with the article reporting "Δ R2 = .10, F(2, 86) = 5.03, p = .009".

> "The analysis on comprehension showed a significant overall interaction between number of misconceptions before studying and instructional method, Δ R2 = .10, F(2, 86) = 5.03, p = .009."

### Anja Prinz-Weiß & Elisa Röhm 2025 (2)

Anja Prinz-Weiß & Elisa Röhm. (2025). To what extent can think sheets with or without reflection prompts support self-regulated learning from text? Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09419-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Conditional effects from the same regression showed that in the control group "the number of misconceptions had a significant positive effect on comprehension, b = 0.55"; effects in the think-sheet (b = -0.12, p = .430) and think-sheet-reflection (b = -0.30, p = .090) groups were not significant.

> "Unexpectedly, in the control group, the number of misconceptions had a significant positive effect on comprehension, b = 0.55, SE = 0.21, t(86) = 2.57, p = .012."
