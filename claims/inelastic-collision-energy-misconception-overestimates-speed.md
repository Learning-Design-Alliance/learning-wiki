## Subclaims
`q1 i?` A common student misconception ignores conversion of kinetic energy into thermal energy and sound in the inelastic collision, producing a prediction that is too large and visibly contradicts experimental data. [→ Andrew Ferstl and Nathan Moore 2007](#andrew-ferstl-and-nathan-moore-2007)

## Evidence

### Andrew Ferstl and Nathan Moore 2007

Andrew Ferstl and Nathan Moore. (2007). Measuring the 2D Vector Aspect of Momentum Using Only One Dimension. http://groups.physics.umn.edu/physed/Research/PSL/pslintro.html [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0803.4142](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0803.4142)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

The authors' analysis of the misconception-based prediction (their Eq 11) shows it is too large due to the absence of the cosine factor and missing a mass-ratio factor, leading to an over-estimation of the cart's final velocity; they state it "should visibly contradict experimental data."

> "One common student misconception is to ignore the conversion of kinetic energy into thermal energy and sound which is implicit in the box’s inelastic collision with the cart."
