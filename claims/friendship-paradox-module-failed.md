# A friendship-paradox module failed in practice because its verification calculations were tedious and it did not scale up

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Module 6 (Do Your Friends Have More Friends Than You Do?) was abandoned after one run: students understood the configuration-model arguments, but scaling up the discussion failed and the calculations were tedious even for small networks. [→ Harrington 2013](#harrington-2013)

## Evidence

### Harrington 2013

Harrington, H. A., Beguerisse Díaz, M., Rombach, M. P., Keating, L. M., & Porter, M. A. (2013). Teach Network Science to Teenagers. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1302.6567](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1302.6567)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Single-run module report (Appendix F). The authors state that "the calculations necessary to verify the key result are tedious even for small networks", that students were shy about reporting friend counts, and that the module needed to go back to the drawing board.

> "However, although we were able to get the students to understand the arguments with the conﬁguration model, our atte mpts to scale up from this point were unsuccessful."
