## Evidence

### Slepkov 2021

Slepkov, A. D., Van Bussel, M. L., Fitze, K. M., & Burr, W. S. (2021). A baseline for multiple-choice testing in the university classroom. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1177/21582440211016838](https://doi.org/10.1177/21582440211016838)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Empirical comparison of item-total point-biserial and item-excluded correlations across 11,246 items from 182 classroom tests, showing that "the overweighed correlation of rpb is never smaller than that of 𝑟′" and systematically overstates discrimination.

> "Overall, 𝑟!"−𝑟′ = 0.059 ± 0.025."

### Slepkov 2021 (2)

Slepkov, A. D., Van Bussel, M. L., Fitze, K. M., & Burr, W. S. (2021). A baseline for multiple-choice testing in the university classroom. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1177/21582440211016838](https://doi.org/10.1177/21582440211016838)

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Theoretical illustration from the Methods: for a test where every student guesses on every item, the mean point-biserial yields a non-zero value of 1/√n, while the mean item-excluded correlation is zero.

> "Given that the typical guideline for evaluating discrimination holds that a value of rpb above 0.20 is considered acceptable (Bodner, 1980; DiBattista & Kurzawa, 2011), a 20-item test comprising entirely randomly-scored items would be considered adeq uately discriminating when using the point-biserial correlation."
