## Evidence

### Cleland JA 2026

Cleland JA, Pienkowska A, Kitto SC. (2026). Endless justification: A scoping review of team‐based learning research in medical education. Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.70041

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's discussion contrasting TBL with the wave patterns observed in PBL research (Hung, Dolmans and van Merriënboer) and programmatic assessment (Pearce and Tavares), where research moved toward process-focused clarification.

> "However, TBL seems to be stuck. While the proportion of studies describing its perceived value has decreased in recent years, effectiveness (justification) studies continue to dominate as the spread and use of TBL increase over time and across different medical schools and contexts."

### Cleland JA 2026 (2)

Cleland JA, Pienkowska A, Kitto SC. (2026). Endless justification: A scoping review of team‐based learning research in medical education. Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.70041

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Authors' interpretation (type e) offered as a tentative explanation: TBL research is "highly situated and instrumental, rather than knowledge building", driven by the need to demonstrate local acceptability and effectiveness for buy-in.

> "We tentatively propose that this may be partly owing to local, instrumental concerns. Planning and implementing new learning approaches such as TBL represent a significant effort and investment in human and other resources, and those bringing in change have to bring onside other key stakeholders such as learners and administrators."
