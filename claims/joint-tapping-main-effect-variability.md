## Evidence

### Yunhee Kim 2026

Yunhee Kim, Doyeon Chae, Jaehwan Jang, Kyeong Taek Oh, Byeong-Sun Park & Min-gu Kim. (2026). Does tapping in sync reflect cognitive control? Links between sensorimotor synchronization, social context, and executive function in early childhood. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00443-5

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Extended LMM (REML) on log-transformed DTW values including the 50 bpm bimanual task; the pairwise reduction during joint tapping was significant (∆M = -0.05, SE = .02, p = .005), described as "a modest but reliable synchronization benefit across task types".

> "The main effect of Social Context was significant, F (1, 1656) = 7.81, p = .005, ηp2 = .005, such that synchronization variability was lower during Joint tapping (M = 2.31, SE = .27, 95% CI [1.14, 3.48]) than during Solo tapping condition"
