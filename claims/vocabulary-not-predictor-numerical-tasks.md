# Vocabulary is not a significant predictor of performance on the numerical tasks when Hearing Age is included

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across all three numerical measures, adding vocabulary to models with Hearing Age and Group did not significantly improve prediction, and vocabulary was not a significant predictor. [→ Stacee Santos 2023](#stacee-santos-2023)

## Evidence

### Stacee Santos 2023

Stacee Santos, Hiram Brownell, Marie Coppola, Anna Shusterman and Sara Cordes. (2023). Language experience matters for the emergence of early numerical concepts. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00202-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple regression and Bayesian analyses with Hearing Age, Group, and DVAP vocabulary as predictors of the three numerical measures. "Vocabulary was not found to be a signi cant predictor of performance on any of the numerical tasks" (e.g., verbal "more": β = 0.219, p = 0.733).

> "Bayesian analyses revealed Strong to Decisive Evidence in favor of Hearing Age and No Evidence for Group or Vocabulary as predictors of performance across any task."
