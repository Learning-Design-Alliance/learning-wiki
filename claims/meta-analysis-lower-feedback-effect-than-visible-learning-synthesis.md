# Meta-analysis yields a lower feedback effect (d = 0.48) than the Visible Learning meta-synthesis (d = 0.79), with three synthesized values overestimated

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The difference is attributed mainly to exclusion of duplets and studies lacking statistical detail or educational context, not to the random-effects model. [→ Wisniewski B 2020](#wisniewski-b-2020)

## Evidence

### Wisniewski B 2020

Wisniewski B, Zierer K and Hattie J (2020) The Power of Feedback Revisited: A Meta-Analysis of Educational Feedback Research. Front. Psychol. 10:3087. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion. They also note the random-effects model would predict a higher, not lower, mean given many small high-effect studies (fixed-effect mean would be 0.41), so model choice cannot explain the gap.

> "We assume that the diﬀerent results mainly stem from the fact that a number of studies used in the synthesis were excluded from our meta-analysis, either due to a lack of detailed information on the statistical data or due to content-related considerations"
