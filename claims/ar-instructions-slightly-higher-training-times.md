# AR instructions do not shorten training times; they are associated with slightly higher training times in both procedures

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Contrary to the authors' expectation of shorter training times, AR instructions were associated with slightly higher training times for both procedure 1 and procedure 2. [→ Wolf Julian 2021](#wolf-julian-2021)

## Evidence

### Wolf Julian 2021

Wolf Julian, Wolfer Viviane, Halbe Maximilian, Maisano Francesco, Lohmeyer Quentin, Meboldt Mirko. (2021). Comparing the effectiveness of augmented reality-based and conventional instructions during single ECMO cannulation training. International Journal of Computer Assisted Radiology and Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11548-021-02408-y

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Training-time analysis in the 21-participant counterbalanced simulator study; training times of both procedures were subject to a normal distribution (Shapiro–Wilk test). The article reports "slightly higher training times" with AR for P1 and likewise for P2.

> "For P1, AR instructions were associated with slightly higher training times and slightly lower error counts."
