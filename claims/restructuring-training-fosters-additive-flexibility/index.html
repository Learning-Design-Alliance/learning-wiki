Evidence¶

Laura Gabler 2024¶

Laura Gabler, Stefan Ufer. (2024). Training flexibility in dealing with additive situations. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.101902

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

Repeated measures ANOVA on flexibility scores of 115 second graders randomly assigned within classrooms to training or control. The time × treatment interaction was significant: "F(222.00, 2) = 3.17, p = 0.044, ηp2 = 0.03".

"A repeated measures ANOVA (Table 3) showed a significant interaction effect of measurement time and treatment group with a small effect size (F(222.00, 2) = 3.17, p = 0.044, ηp2 = 0.03)."

Laura Gabler 2024 (2)¶

Laura Gabler, Stefan Ufer. (2024). Training flexibility in dealing with additive situations. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.101902

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

Post-hoc contrasts on flexibility scores show the pretest-to-follow-up growth advantage of the experimental group: "b = 1.20, p = 0.03, d = 0.33"; the pre-to-post contrast was not significant.

"The performance growth between pretest and follow-up was significantly stronger in the experimental than in the control group (b = 1.20, p = 0.03, d = 0.33), but not from pretest to posttest (b = 0.87, p = 0.16, d = 0.24)."
