Evidence¶

Elaine Allensworth 2008¶

Elaine Allensworth, Takako Nomi, Nicholas Montgomery, & Valerie E. Lee. (2008). College Preparatory Curriculum for All: Consequences of Ninth-Grade Course Taking in Algebra and English on Academic Outcomes in Chicago. Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://ccsr.uchicago.edu

q2 · i? · causal · r?

RQ3 difference-in-difference comparisons of schools affected versus unaffected by the policy; credit increases of 8.8 and 7.4 percentage points (p<.01) for the lowest- and low-ability groups, alongside the failure increase.

"However, failures for lowest-ability students increased by 7.4 percentage points (p<.05) in the post- compared to the pre-policy period."

Elaine Allensworth 2008 (2)¶

Elaine Allensworth, Takako Nomi, Nicholas Montgomery, & Valerie E. Lee. (2008). College Preparatory Curriculum for All: Consequences of Ninth-Grade Course Taking in Algebra and English on Academic Outcomes in Chicago. Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://ccsr.uchicago.edu

q2 · i? · causal · r?

RQ3 long-term outcomes; a few pre/post comparisons were significant but inconsistent across ability groups, and the authors attribute them to possible Type 1 error from multiple comparisons.

"Thus, a policy that replaced remedial with college-prep coursework in the ninth grade for all students did not prepare more students for college—overall, students were no more likely to obtain credits in upper-level math courses, or attend a four-year college."
