Evidence¶

Stea TH 2014¶

Stea TH, Torstveit MK. (2014). Association of lifestyle habits and academic achievement in Norwegian adolescents: a cross-sectional study. BMC Public Health 2014, 14:829. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2458-14-829

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Cross-sectional survey of 2,432 Norwegian adolescents (1258 girls, 1187 boys) aged 15-17; logistic regression adjusted for BMI and parental education. Girls eating breakfast daily had "AOR: 3.30 (2.45-4.45)" and boys AOR 1.76 (1.32-2.34) for high grades; no standardized effect size was printed.

"The results showed increased odds of high academic achievement in girls and boys who had a regular consumption of breakfast (AOR: 3.30 (2.45-4.45) and AOR: 1.76 (1.32-2.34), respectively)"

Related Claims¶

  • The study's cross-sectional self-report design does not allow causal inference about lifestyle habits and academic achievement — related
  • High intake of fruit and berries is associated with increased odds of high academic achievement in girls and boys, and high vegetable intake in girls — related
  • Increased frequency of habitual breakfast is consistently positively associated with academic performance in children and adolescents — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Breakfast consumption shows a mainly positive effect on on-task behavior in the classroom in children — related
  • Regular lunch consumption is associated with increased odds of high academic achievement in both girls and boys, and regular dinner and an all-meals pattern in boys — related
  • High leisure time physical activity and active commuting to school are each associated with increased odds of high academic achievement in both girls and boys — related
  • Smoking and snuffing are inversely associated with high academic achievement in both adolescent girls and boys — related
  • High intake of lemonade, sugar-sweetened soft drinks and salty snacks is associated with decreased odds of high academic achievement, mainly in girls — related
  • Robust RCTs of school breakfast programs failed to find behavioral benefits, with low and variable attendance and a shift from at-home to at-school consumption limiting impact — related
  • Quality of habitual breakfast, in terms of variety of food groups and adequate energy, is positively related to school performance
  • A binding SBP mandate increases milk and fruit consumption and decreases soda consumption, but its effects on attendance are imprecise, suggesting the achievement effect operates through improved nutrition — related
  • School breakfast programs show suggestive positive effects on academic performance, with clearest effects on mathematics and arithmetic grades in undernourished children — related
