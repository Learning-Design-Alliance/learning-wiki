# Between-class comparisons showed no significant Chisanbop advantage: grade 2 classes did not differ on any measure, and the grade 5 control group outgained the experimental group on Calculations and Quantitative Concepts

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` There were no statistically significant differences in gain scores between experimental and control grade 2 classes on any measure. [→ Stegemann 2014](#stegemann-2014)
`q2 i?` Grade 5 classes differed significantly in gain scores on Calculations (U = 116.500, Z = -2.071, p = .038) and Quantitative Concepts (U = 113.000, Z = -2.355, p = .019), with the control group making the larger gains. [→ Stegemann 2014 (2)](#stegemann-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Stegemann 2014

Stegemann, K. C., & Grünke, M. (2014). Revisiting an Old Methodology for Teaching Counting, Computation, and Place Value: The Effectiveness of the Finger Calculation Method for At-Risk Children. Learning Disabilities: A Contemporary Journal, 12(2), 191-213. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1047461

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Mann-Whitney between-group tests of gain scores for whole grade 2 classes after the 10-month intervention; no measure reached significance. Equivalence was not tested.

> "There were no statistically significant differences in gain scores on any of the measures when comparing experimental to control grade 2 groups (Table 3)."

### Stegemann 2014 (2)

Stegemann, K. C., & Grünke, M. (2014). Revisiting an Old Methodology for Teaching Counting, Computation, and Place Value: The Effectiveness of the Finger Calculation Method for At-Risk Children. Learning Disabilities: A Contemporary Journal, 12(2), 191-213. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1047461

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mann-Whitney tests on grade 5 whole-class gain scores: significant differences favouring the control group on Calculations and Quantitative Concepts, contrary to the authors' hypothesis. Corrected effect sizes were small (dcorr -0.09 and -0.03).

> "there were significant differences between the gain scores for the grade 5 classes on the Calculations sub-test (U = 116.500, Z = -2.071, p = .038) and on Quantitative Concepts (U = 113.000, Z = -2.355, p =.019). Analysis of the raw data (Table 1) verifies that it was the grade 5 control group that made more significant gains than the experimental group."
