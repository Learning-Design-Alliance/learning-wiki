# Third grade is the notable exception where retained students did not make smaller gains, likely due to the ITBS Level 8 to Level 9 test format change

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The pattern of smaller gains among retained students does not hold in third grade, and the authors attribute the third-grade gain dip at least partly to the change in test format from ITBS Level 8 to ITBS Level 9. [→ Stuart Luppescu 1995](#stuart-luppescu-1995)

## Evidence

### Stuart Luppescu 1995

Stuart Luppescu, Anthony S. Bryk, Paul Deabster, John Q. Easton, and Yeow Meng Thum. (1995). School Reform, Retention Policy, and Student Achievement Gains. University of Chicago draft report. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/school-reform-retention-policy-and-student-achievement-gains

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' interpretation of the third-grade dip in gains in the Chicago gain comparisons. They note the dip is "hard to explain" but consistent with the test-format change, since repeaters taking Level 9 a second time show no dip. This is an interpretation, not a tested result.

> "It is probably at least partly attributable to the change in test format from ITBS Level 8 to ITBS Level 9. This explanation is consistent with the fact that the gains made by students who have repeated the grade, and thus are taking the Level 9 test for the second time, show no such dip."
