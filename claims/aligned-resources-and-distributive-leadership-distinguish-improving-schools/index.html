Evidence¶

Smylie 2003¶

Smylie, M. A., Wenzel, S. A., with Allensworth, E., Fendt, C., Hallman, S., Luppescu, S., & Nagaoka, J. (2003). The Chicago Annenberg Challenge: Successes, Failures, and Lessons for the Future. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Longitudinal field research on 12 Annenberg schools over five years. The report states "Teacher leaders make substantial contributions to school improvement, but this analysis highlights the 'make-or-break' role of the principal, even when people in different roles join principals in 'leadership work.'"

"However, what distinguished these schools from nonimproving schools was the ability to secure resources aligned with a particular development agenda and to employ these resources in an efficient and strategic manner. Finally, schools that were strong or that improved distinguished themselves from weak schools and nonimproving schools by the cultivation of strong, distributive leadership."
