# A progressive universal intervention improving school entry academic skills by 0.8 SD for disadvantaged children and 0.2 SD for others is estimated to reduce absolute socioeconomic inequality in poor educational achievement by 15.7% and overall risk by 4.5%

## Evidence

### Chittleborough 2014

Chittleborough, C. R., Mittinty, M. N., Lawlor, D. A., & Lynch, J. W. (2014). Effects of Simulated Interventions to Improve School Entry Academic Skills on Socioeconomic Inequalities in Educational Achievement. Child Development. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.12309

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Marginal structural model simulation on the imputed ALSPAC sample (n = 11,764) applying trial-derived effect sizes to the school entry assessment mediator. The article reports the reduction was "15.7% from 47.0 per 100 to 39.6 per 100" in excess risk, with overall risk reduced by 4.5% (49.3% to 47.1%).

> "When the school entry scores were improved more for socioeconomically disadvantaged children (intervention Scenario 2, Table4), the overall risk of poor educational achievement reduced by 4.5% and the excess risk experienced by the most disadvantaged children reduced by 15.7% from 47.0 per 100 to 39.6 per 100."
