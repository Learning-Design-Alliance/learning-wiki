## Evidence

### Costello Daniel M. 2021

Costello Daniel M., Huntington Isabel, Burke Grace, Farrugia Brooke, O'Connor Andrea J., Costello Anthony J., Thomas Benjamin C., Dundee Philip, Ghazi Ahmed, Corcoran Niall. (2021). A review of simulation training and new 3D computer-generated synthetic organs for robotic surgery education. Journal of Robotic Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11701-021-01302-8

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review's Results section on cadaver models states its comprehensive literature review "found no studies that validated the use of cadavers in robotic surgical training", despite cadavers being traditionally accepted as the gold standard for surgical simulation. This is a null finding with no effect size.

> "This comprehensive review of the literature regarding cadaver surgical simulation found no studies that validated the use of cadavers in robotic surgical training."

### Costello Daniel M. 2021 (2)

Costello Daniel M., Huntington Isabel, Burke Grace, Farrugia Brooke, O'Connor Andrea J., Costello Anthony J., Thomas Benjamin C., Dundee Philip, Ghazi Ahmed, Corcoran Niall. (2021). A review of simulation training and new 3D computer-generated synthetic organs for robotic surgery education. Journal of Robotic Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11701-021-01302-8

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, drawing on the 2011 Royal College of Surgeons of England systematic review of cadaveric workshops, that "no empirical evidence" demonstrated transfer of cadaver simulation skills to live operating rooms; one included study showed benefit for simple emergency procedures and one weak evidence for complex procedures.

> "There was no empirical evidence demonstrating the transfer of cadaver surgical simulations skill into the live operating room."
