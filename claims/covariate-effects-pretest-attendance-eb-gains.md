# Pretest, attendance, and EB status showed distinct covariate effects on specific literacy outcomes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Higher pretest early literacy skills predicted 10-12% lower growth on the three taught letter outcomes. [→ Vadasy 2023](#vadasy-2023)
`q3 i?` Each standard deviation increase in tutoring attendance predicted an 11% increase in word reading and spelling growth, and EB children were predicted to have 6% greater gains in letter sound writing. [→ Vadasy 2023 (2)](#vadasy-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Vadasy 2023

Vadasy, P. F., & Sanders, E. A. (2023). Cognitive flexibility + phonics intervention effects on reading gains. Reading Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1080/02702711.2023.2166636

`q3 · i?`

From the multilevel models of gains for 57 children, pretest negatively predicted the three taught letter outcomes; the article reports higher-skill students "were predicted to have 10-12% lower growth than average, all else held constant." These are printed percentage predictions, not standardized effect sizes.

> "Pretest was found to negatively predict the three taught letter outcomes: generally, students who started out with higher early literacy skills were predicted to have 10-12% lower growth than average, all else held constant."

### Vadasy 2023 (2)

Vadasy, P. F., & Sanders, E. A. (2023). Cognitive flexibility + phonics intervention effects on reading gains. Reading Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1080/02702711.2023.2166636

`q3 · i?`

Treatment attendance uniquely predicted word-level skill gains: "for each standard deviation increase in tutoring session attendance, there was an 11% predicted increase in word reading and spelling growth." The article also reports EB children were predicted to have 6% greater gains for letter sound writing, with no other EB patterns.

> "for each standard deviation increase in tutoring session attendance, there was an 11% predicted increase in word reading and spelling growth"
