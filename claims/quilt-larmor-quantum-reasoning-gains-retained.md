# QuILT work produces significant gains in correct quantum-mechanical reasoning that are retained, while classical reasoning shows no change or very small positive gain

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Average correct quantum reasoning scores on items 1–5 improved significantly from pre-test to post-test (all p-values < 0.05), with gains retained from post-test to final exam. [→ Benjamin R. Brown and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#benjamin-r-brown-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)
`q2 i?` Average correct classical reasoning showed either no change or a very small positive gain from pre-test to post-test and from post-test to final exam. [→ Benjamin R. Brown and Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)](#benjamin-r-brown-and-chandralekha-singh-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Benjamin R. Brown and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Benjamin R. Brown and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Development and Evaluation of a Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorial on Larmor Precession Of Spin. [doi:10.1119/perc.2014.pr.008](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.008)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Rubric-coded reasoning analysis of 72 students' pre-/posttest responses; a reasoning rubric scored quantum and classical explanations on items 1–5, with inter-rater agreement better than 90%. The article reports "significant gain in quantum mechanical reasoning from pre-test to post-test, and retention of these gains."

> "Comparison of students’ quantum reasoning in Table 2 shows signiﬁcant gain in quantum mechanical reasoning from pre-test to post-test, and retention of these gains (change from the post-test to the ﬁnal exam) more than two months later."

### Benjamin R. Brown and Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Benjamin R. Brown and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Development and Evaluation of a Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorial on Larmor Precession Of Spin. [doi:10.1119/perc.2014.pr.008](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.008)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Same reasoning-rubric analysis of the 72-student pre-/posttest data applied to classical reasoning (e.g., precession-based explanations). The article reports "either no change or a very small positive gain in average correct classical reasoning" across pre-test, post-test and final exam.

> "a similar analysis for each question shows either no change or a very small positive gain in average correct classical reasoning from the pre-test to post-test and from the post-test to the ﬁnal exam."
