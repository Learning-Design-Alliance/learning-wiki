## Evidence

### Alizadeh Maryam 2024

Alizadeh Maryam, Masoomi Rasoul, Mafinejad Mahboobeh Khabaz, Parmelee Dean, Khalaf Rusul Jasim, Norouzi Ali. (2024). Team-based learning in health professions education: an umbrella review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06147-x

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Umbrella review synthesis: four of 23 reviews (17.39%) included performance scores, all favoring TBL. The article links this to TBL's emphasis on the application phase.

> "All four reviews consistently reported higher clinical performance skills [28], skill scores [30], practice ability [31], and transfer of learning in real-world work environments [17] in favor of TBL."

### Alizadeh Maryam 2024 (2)

Alizadeh Maryam, Masoomi Rasoul, Mafinejad Mahboobeh Khabaz, Parmelee Dean, Khalaf Rusul Jasim, Norouzi Ali. (2024). Team-based learning in health professions education: an umbrella review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06147-x

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Umbrella review synthesis: engagement was reported in 7 of 23 reviews; one review identified three engagement types with learner-to-learner engagement most prevalent in TBL sessions.

> "Among these, 71.42% (5 out of 7) reported higher engagement in TBL compared with lecture [19, 28, 29, 36, 37]."
