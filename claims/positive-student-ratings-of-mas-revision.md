BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1085-y

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-test questionnaire data from the pilot trial's 27 students; MAS-group students also rated usefulness and user-friendliness highly (6.9/10 and 7.0/10). These are descriptive Likert means with no inferential test reported.

> "students thought app-based revision would make a useful addition to the clinical skills curriculum (mean Likert score 7.6 / 10)"

### Bartlett Richard D. 2017 (2)

Bartlett Richard D., Radenkovic Dina, Mitrasinovic Stefan, Cole Andrew, Pavkovic Iva, Denn Peyton Cheong Phey, Hussain Mahrukh, Kogler Magdalena, Koutsopodioti Natalia, Uddin Wasima, Beckley Ivan, Abubakar Hana, Gill Deborah, Smith Daron. (2017). A pilot study to assess the utility of a freely downloadable mobile application simulator for undergraduate clinical skills training: a single-blinded, randomised controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1085-y

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-test questionnaire confidence ratings after two assessed attempts, compared across the three trial groups. The article explicitly labels the MAS-group difference as non-significant; only descriptive Likert means are printed.

> "confidence was non-significantly higher in the MAS group relative to that of control or traditional resources (7.3 vs. 6.3 vs. 5.9 / 10 on Likert scale, respectively)."
