## Evidence

### Saumya Shivam and Kasey Wagoner 2020

Saumya Shivam and Kasey Wagoner. (2020). How Well do Remote Labs Work? A Case Study at Princeton University. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2008.04499](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2008.04499)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

E-CLASS pre/post comparison of the same cohort: fall 2019 with all in-person labs versus spring 2020 with remote labs for part of the semester. The overall score "remained nearly the same" despite the abrupt shift to remote instruction.

> "We see that despite the abrupt shift to remote instruction, the overall score for the spring semester remained nearly the same."

### Saumya Shivam and Kasey Wagoner 2020 (2)

Saumya Shivam and Kasey Wagoner. (2020). How Well do Remote Labs Work? A Case Study at Princeton University. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2008.04499](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2008.04499)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Instructor-created end-of-semester survey in which students compared the off-campus labs with in-person labs on a Likert scale; responses indicated remote labs were "roughly as instructive as in-person labs".

> "The upper panel indicates that students found remote labs to be roughly as instructive as in-person labs."

### Saumya Shivam and Kasey Wagoner 2020 (3)

Saumya Shivam and Kasey Wagoner. (2020). How Well do Remote Labs Work? A Case Study at Princeton University. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2008.04499](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2008.04499)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The interest question of the same instructor-created survey, on a Likert scale from far less to far more interesting, indicated remote labs were "slightly more interesting than in-person labs".

> "The lower panel indicates that students found remote labs to be slightly more interesting than in-person labs."
