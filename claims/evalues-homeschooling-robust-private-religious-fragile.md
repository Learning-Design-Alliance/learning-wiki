# E-value analyses indicate homeschooling associations are moderately robust to unmeasured confounding, whereas private and religious school associations are fragile

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Sensitivity analysis using E-values showed homeschooling associations (e.g., volunteering E-value 2.04) were at least moderately robust to unmeasured confounding, while private and religious school comparisons had CI E-values at most 1.24. [→ Chen Y 2021](#chen-y-2021)

## Evidence

### Chen Y 2021

Chen Y, Hinton C, VanderWeele TJ. (2021). School types in adolescence and subsequent health and well-being in young adulthood: An outcome-wide analysis. PLoS ONE 16(11): e0258723. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0258723

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

E-value sensitivity analysis (Table 3) quantifying the unmeasured confounding strength needed to explain away each school-type association. For homeschooling and volunteering, an unmeasured confounder associated with both by 2.04-fold each could suffice; the homeschooling comparisons were the only moderately robust ones.

> "for all comparisons of outcomes for public versus private independent schools, and all comparisons of public versus religious schools, the E-values for the confidence interval were at most 1.24, and often considerably smaller, suggesting modest amounts of confounding could suffice to explain away the observed difference"
