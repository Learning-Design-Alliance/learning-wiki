Evidence¶

Marie Geurten & Patrick Lemaire 2017¶

Marie Geurten & Patrick Lemaire. (2017). Metacognition for Strategy Selection during Arithmetic Problem Solving in Young and Older Adults. https://www.google.com/search?q=Metacognition+for+Strategy+Selection+during+Arithmetic+Problem+Solving+in+Young+and+Older+Adults

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Trial-by-trial phi correlations between RCJ (coded 0/1 around each participant's mean) and next-problem better-strategy selection in 66 adults: "φyoung = -.54 and φold = -.44" (selection-only) and "φyoung = -.31 and φold = -.49" (selection-execution), all ps < .01. No effect size beyond phi printed.

"The correlation between participants' confidence judgments on one problem and better strategy selection on the following problem significantly differed from 0 in both the selection-only task (φyoung = -.54 and φold = -.44) and in the selection-execution tasks (φyoung = -.31 and φold = -.49), all ps < .01"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Young and older adults make accurate retrospective confidence judgments but not prospective ease-of-selection judgments about their arithmetic strategy selections — related
  • Older adults show higher retrospective metacognitive accuracy than young adults in the selection-only task, especially on homogeneous unit-digit problems, but comparable accuracy in the selection-execution task — related
  • Retrospective metacognitive accuracy for strategy selection is higher in the selection-only task than the selection-execution task and on homogeneous than heterogeneous unit-digit problems — related
  • Collecting metacognitive judgments did not disrupt typical strategy selection and execution: both age groups selected the better strategy more on rounding-up homogeneous problems and executed rounding-down faster on homogeneous problems — related
  • Eliciting confidence ratings leads to poorer metacognitive monitoring in older adults — related
