## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a decision-tree partition analysis, Sleep Disruption Index was the strongest predictor of the Δrecall cuing effect; participants with an index <0.13 (n=15) had a positive cuing effect (mean=1.53 names, t(14)=2.16, p=0.049), while those above 0.13 did not have a significant one. ## Evidence

### Whitmore 2022

Whitmore, N. W., Bassard, A. M. & Paller, K. A. (2022). Targeted memory reactivation of face-name learning depends on ample and undisturbed slow-wave sleep. npj Science of Learning, 7:1. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00119-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory decision-tree partition analysis (JMP 15) classifying participants as TMR responders or non-responders based on number of spoken cues, N3 duration, or sleep disruption index. Lower-disruption participants showed superior recall for the cued class.

> "Participants with a Sleep Disruption Index < 0.13 ( n = 15) had a positive Δrecall cuing effect [mean = 1.53 names, SD = 2.75, t(14) = 2.16, p = 0.049], indicating superior recall for the cued class."

### Whitmore 2022 (2)

Whitmore, N. W., Bassard, A. M. & Paller, K. A. (2022). Targeted memory reactivation of face-name learning depends on ample and undisturbed slow-wave sleep. npj Science of Learning, 7:1. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00119-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same decision-tree analysis split participants by spoken-cue count for the recognition cuing effect; the high-cue group's positive effect was a nonsignificant trend while the low-cue group showed a significant negative cuing effect.

> "participants with >157 spoken cues had a nonsigni ﬁcant trend for a positive cuing effect [mean = 0.37, SD = 0.27, t(3) = 2.73, p = 0.072], whereas those with fewer spoken cues had a negative cuing effect [mean = −0.22, SD = 0.42, t(18) = 2.29, p = 0.03]"
