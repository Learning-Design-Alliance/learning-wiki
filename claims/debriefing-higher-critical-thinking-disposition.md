## Evidence

### Cao Xi 2026

Cao Xi, Wang Min, Huang Ye, Hu Qiu-Min, Tang Rong. (2026). Research on the application of debriefing in simulation-based education of surgical nursing. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09133-7

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Self-controlled before-and-after study of 340 nursing students using CTDI-CV. All seven dimensions and total score showed significant differences (all P < 0.001), with "the magnitudes of these differences were large (Cohen's ds ranging from 1.19 to 1.69)" for most dimensions.

> "The total score was 69.70 points higher (95% CI: 63.79 to 75.62), representing a 30.52% difference relative to the control phase. In the dimensions of Truth-seeking, Open-mindedness, and Maturity, the magnitudes of these differences were medium (Cohen's ds = 0.51, 0.68, and 0.74, respectively). In the other dimensions and the total score, the magnitudes of these differences were large (Cohen's ds ranging from 1.19 to 1.69)."

### Cao Xi 2026 (2)

Cao Xi, Wang Min, Huang Ye, Hu Qiu-Min, Tang Rong. (2026). Research on the application of debriefing in simulation-based education of surgical nursing. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09133-7

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Same study reporting that Truth-seeking, Maturity, and Open-mindedness dimensions showed moderate effects "lower than those observed in other dimensions", possibly reflecting cultural background and educational traditions.

> "However, the magnitudes of the differences for the Truth-seeking (Cohen's ds = 0.51), Maturity (Cohen's ds = 0.68) and Open-mindedness (Cohen's ds = 0.74) dimensions were moderate, lower than those observed in other dimensions."
