# Students made more evaluative comments after the dialogic argumentation intervention, but their evaluations still favored their own position

## Evidence

### Iordanou 2025

Iordanou, K., Fotiou, C., Manoli, A., & Zembylas, M. (2025). Examining myside bias on a controversial historical event after engagement in dialogic argumentation: Insights from a think aloud study. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102209

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

GLMM analysis of think-aloud codes from 30 participants at pre-test and 24 at post-test, with random intercepts for participants and school and refugeedom as covariate. The models showed "more evaluative comments (EC) post-assessment compared to pre-assessment" across both texts.

> "GLMM results comparing reasoning between the initial and final assessments showed that participants made more evaluative comments (EC) post-assessment compared to pre-assessment (see Table 2) across both texts."

### Iordanou 2025 (2)

Iordanou, K., Fotiou, C., Manoli, A., & Zembylas, M. (2025). Examining myside bias on a controversial historical event after engagement in dialogic argumentation: Insights from a think aloud study. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102209

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Thematic analysis of evaluative comments following Braun and Clarke (2006) found the same evaluation themes pre- and post-intervention, with quantity increasing but gist unchanged; evaluations were "mainly critical of the other side" at both assessment points.

> "Nevertheless, such evaluations were mainly critical of the other side in both the pre- and post-intervention assessment points."
