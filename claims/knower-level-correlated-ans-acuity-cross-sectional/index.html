Evidence¶

Shusterman Anna 2016¶

Shusterman Anna, Slusser Emily, Halberda Justin, Odic Darko. (2016). Acquisition of the Cardinal Principle Coincides with Improvement in Approximate Number System Acuity in Preschoolers. PLoS ONE 11(4): e0153072. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0153072

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Cross-sectional analyses of 42 children (S2) and 39 children (S5) in the six-month longitudinal study. Post-hoc tests showed subset-knowers differed from both CP-knower groups (p < .05 for all four comparisons), but CP-Mappers did not differ from CP-Non-Mappers; the authors call it "the first demonstration of a relationship between knower level and ANS acuity when controlling for age and sex."

"A one-way ANCOVA, with knower level (one-knower, two-knower, three-knower, four-knower, CP-Mapper, CP-Non-Mapper) as the between subjects variable, w as the dependent variable, and age and sex as covariates, revealed a significant effect of knower level on w both at S2, F(5, 37) = 4.00, p < .01, and at S5, F(5, 37) = 4.43, p < .01."

Related Claims¶

  • ANS acuity correlates with later number sets fluency but does not emerge as a unique predictor once symbolic knowledge is controlled — related
  • The Who Has More task showed moderate test-retest reliability (r = .46) in preschoolers, and the authors call for more stable ANS measures for this age — related
  • Female preschoolers showed significantly better ANS acuity than males at Session 5, and knower level increased significantly with age — related
  • The subset-to-cardinal-principle knower-level transition coincides with the largest longitudinal gains in ANS acuity, exceeding other transition types — related
  • ANS acuity (w) did not significantly improve within children over the six-month period in the session-level analysis, though a hierarchical linear model detected a linear increase in percent correct — related
  • ANS acuity improves throughout development, from w = 0.525 at age 3 to w = 0.179 at age 6 and w = 0.108 in adulthood — related
  • Both receptive and expressive vocabulary correlate with number-word knowledge independent of age, replicating Experiment 1. — related
  • Vocabulary adds significantly to predicting knower-level after age, but age does not add after vocabulary, suggesting age does not mediate the vocabulary–number-knowledge link. — related
  • Early ANS acuity (accuracy and RT) uniquely predicts preschoolers' math ability six months later, controlling for initial math ability, age, and expressive vocabulary — related
  • ANS acuity is a unique concurrent predictor of preschool math ability beyond expressive vocabulary, non-numerical attention RT, and memory span — related
