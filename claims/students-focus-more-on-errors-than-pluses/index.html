Evidence¶

Jochem E. J. Aben 2024¶

Jochem E. J. Aben, Mayra Mascareño Lara, Anneke C. Timmermans, Filitsa Dingyloudi, Jan-Willem Strijbos. (2024). The impact of interpersonal perceptions on the process of dealing with errors while providing and processing peer-feedback on writing. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09660-0

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Quantitative content analysis of think-aloud transcripts of twelve students providing peer-feedback. Seven students more often identified pluses and five more often identified errors, all hardly decoded errors or pluses, but "the majority of students said more often that they were evaluating errors than pluses" and nearly all encoded error-based feedback more often.

"During the procedure of providing feedback, the think-aloud utterances showed that students focused more on errors than on pluses."

Jochem E. J. Aben 2024 (2)¶

Jochem E. J. Aben, Mayra Mascareño Lara, Anneke C. Timmermans, Filitsa Dingyloudi, Jan-Willem Strijbos. (2024). The impact of interpersonal perceptions on the process of dealing with errors while providing and processing peer-feedback on writing. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09660-0

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Quantitative content analysis of think-aloud transcripts during feedback processing. All students voiced more utterances for error-related sub-phases (reading, evaluating, revising) than for pluses, "except for Sarah, who made more utterances regarding reading, evaluating, and revising for pluses than for errors".

"Also during the procedure of processing feedback, the think-aloud utterances showed that students focused more on errors than on pluses."
