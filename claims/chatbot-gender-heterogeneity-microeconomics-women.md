# Women assigned to the chatbot in Microeconomics earned final grades seven points higher than control women, with no treatment effects for men

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Microeconomics, treated women earned final grades seven points higher, were 11 percentage points more likely to earn a B or higher, and 10 percentage points more likely to earn a C or higher than control women; men showed no effects. [→ Meyer 2026](#meyer-2026)

## Evidence

### Meyer 2026

Meyer, Katharine, Lindsay C. Page, Catherine Mata, Eric N. Smith, B. Tyler Walsh, C. Lindsey Fifield, Michelle Tyson, Amy Eremionkhale, Michael Evans, Shelby Frost, and Eye Eoun Jung. (2026). Let's Chat: Leveraging Chatbot Outreach for Improved Course Performance (NBER Working Paper No. 35397). National Bureau of Economic Research. [doi:10.3386/w35397](https://doi.org/10.3386/w35397). Also released as EdWorkingPaper 22-564, Annenberg Institute at Brown University: https://edworkingpapers.com/ai22-564

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Subgroup ITT analysis in the Microeconomics RCT (915 randomized students) with formal tests of equality across gender. Treated women outperformed control women on all grade benchmarks; results "consistent even after multiple comparison corrections". Control women averaged 68.9 vs. men 70.5.

> "In Microeconomics, women assigned to treatment earn final grades that are seven points higher than control group women. They are 11 percentage points more likely to earn a B or higher, 10 percentage points more likely to earn a C or higher, and six percentage points less likely to drop the course than women in the control group."
