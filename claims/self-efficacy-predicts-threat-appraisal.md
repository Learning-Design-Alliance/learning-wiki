## Evidence

### Cassidy 2026

Cassidy, S. (2026). The Academic Threat Appraisal Ratio Scale (ATARS): Insights into attainment, academic progression, and retention in higher education. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 96, 90–107. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12780

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Multiple linear regression on the survey data; only self-efficacy was a significant predictor of DRES with "B = −.03, t(175) = 4.69, p < .001".

> "Multiple linear regression analysis used to predict DRES from academic self‐efficacy, academic resilience, grit, and mindset resulted in a significant model, explaining 18.8% of variance in DRES (F(4, 175) = 10.15, R 2 = .188, p < .001, effect size = medium, Cohen, 1988)."

### Cassidy 2026 (2)

Cassidy, S. (2026). The Academic Threat Appraisal Ratio Scale (ATARS): Insights into attainment, academic progression, and retention in higher education. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 96, 90–107. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12780

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Binary logistic regression predicting the dichotomy was significant (χ2(4) = 18.578, p < .001, Nagelkerke R2 = .132), with 65% classification accuracy; the odds of a challenge state increased with self-efficacy.

> "Again, only academic self‐efficacy was identified as a significant predictor of academic challenge‐threat dichotomy (B = .036, Wald = 9.23, p = .002, Exp(B) 1.037)."
