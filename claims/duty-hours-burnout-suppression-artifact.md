# Longer weekly duty hours are associated with lower odds of a positive burnout screen, an apparent reversal the authors attribute to statistical suppression

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Adjusted odds of burnout were lower for 60–79 duty-hours per week (aOR 0.786, p = 0.019) and ≥ 80 hours (aOR 0.646, p < 0.001) versus < 60 hours, a direction the authors interpret as a statistical artifact. [→ Ishizuka K 2026](#ishizuka-k-2026)

## Evidence

### Ishizuka K 2026

Ishizuka K, Nishizaki Y, Kataoka K, Shimizu T, Nojima M, Yamamoto Y, Shikino K, Fukui S, Nagasaki K, Kobayashi H, Ohta M, Tokuda Y. (2026). Impact of inpatient volume on residents' In-training examination scores and burnout in Japanese community hospitals: a nationwide cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08664-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Resident-level estimates from the multilevel multivariable logistic regression of burnout (Table 4): Category 2 (60–79 h) aOR 0.786 (p = 0.019); Category 3 (≥ 80 h) aOR 0.646 (p < 0.001) versus < 60 h. The authors call the reversal a statistical artifact, not a meaningful contradictory effect.

> "The apparent reversal in the burnout analysis likely reflects a statistical suppression effect from adjusting for correlated workload- and hospital-level covariates rather than true model instability"
