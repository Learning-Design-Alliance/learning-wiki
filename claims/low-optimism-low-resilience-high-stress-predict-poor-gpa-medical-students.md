## Evidence

### Popa-Velea Ovidiu 2021

Popa-Velea Ovidiu, Pârvan Irina, Diaconescu Liliana Veronica. (2021). The Impact of Self-Efficacy, Optimism, Resilience and Perceived Stress on Academic Performance and Its Subjective Evaluation: A Cross-Sectional Study. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18178911

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional linear regression on 118 medical undergraduates comparing lower GPA quartile (GPA below 8.62) to upper quartile students. The model with psychological predictors was significant, and the article reports "optimism (t = 2.819, p < 0.033), resilience (t = 2.727, p < 0.039) and perceived stress (t = −2.575, p < 0.018)" as predictors.

> "the second depicted model was statistically significant (F = 2.919, p < 0.017). The GPA predictors for this student category were optimism (t = 2.819, p < 0.033), resilience (t = 2.727, p < 0.039) and perceived stress (t = −2.575, p < 0.018)."

### Popa-Velea Ovidiu 2021 (2)

Popa-Velea Ovidiu, Pârvan Irina, Diaconescu Liliana Veronica. (2021). The Impact of Self-Efficacy, Optimism, Resilience and Perceived Stress on Academic Performance and Its Subjective Evaluation: A Cross-Sectional Study. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18178911

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The discussion reports that in low-performance students "a substantial percentage (34.1%) of GPA variance was predicted" by the psychological variables; no standardised effect size is printed, so no magnitude is coded.

> "a substantial percentage (34.1%) of GPA variance was predicted, in the case of low performance students, by psychological variables, specifically low optimism (p < 0.033), low resilience (p < 0.039) and high perceived stress (p < 0.018)."
