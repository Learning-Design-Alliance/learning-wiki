## Evidence

### Raison Nicholas 2021

Raison Nicholas, Harrison Patrick, Abe Takashige, Aydin Abdullatif, Ahmed Kamran, Dasgupta Prokar. (2021). Procedural virtual reality simulation training for robotic surgery: a randomised controlled trial. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-020-08197-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Skills transfer assessment on fresh frozen cadavers: performance data from 25 trained participants were compared to 9 control participants, with blinded post hoc GEARS scoring (maximum 30). The article reports a "significantly higher GEARS score than no training". No effect size is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "Any VR training (procedural or basic training) resulted in a significantly higher GEARS score than no training (mean GEARS score 11.3 ± 0.58 vs. 8.8 ± 2.9 p = 0.002)"
