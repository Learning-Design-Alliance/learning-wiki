# Secondary students exposed to CAI in either individualised or cooperative settings outperformed students taught biology by conventional classroom instruction

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` ANCOVA of post-test scores with pre-test as covariate showed a significant main effect of treatment (ICAI, CCAI, CI) on biology performance. [→ Yusuf and Afolabi 2010](#yusuf-and-afolabi-2010)

## Evidence

### Yusuf and Afolabi 2010

Mudasiru Olalere YUSUF and Adedeji Olufemi AFOLABI. (2010). EFFECTS OF COMPUTER ASSISTED INSTRUCTION (CAI) ON SECONDARY SCHOOL STUDENTS' PERFORMANCE IN BIOLOGY. The Turkish Online Journal of Educational Technology, 9(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ875764

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental pre-test/post-test study of 120 SSS I students from three private Nigerian schools; ANCOVA of BIOPET scores showed the treatment main effect was significant: "an F (2, 117) = 36.920, α = 0.000". No effect size was printed.

> "an F (2, 117) = 36.920, α = 0.000 for the main effect (treatment) was significant. This is because the significance of F = 0.000 is less than the 0.05 alpha level."

### Yusuf and Afolabi 2010 (2)

Mudasiru Olalere YUSUF and Adedeji Olufemi AFOLABI. (2010). EFFECTS OF COMPUTER ASSISTED INSTRUCTION (CAI) ON SECONDARY SCHOOL STUDENTS' PERFORMANCE IN BIOLOGY. The Turkish Online Journal of Educational Technology, 9(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ875764

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Scheffe follow-up on the same ANCOVA located pairwise differences; the ICAI mean (17.8750) exceeded the CCI mean (14.0500), and CCAI (20.0500) exceeded CCI (14.0500), both significant at the 0.05 level. Means are descriptive; no effect size printed.

> "Significant difference was established in the post-test scores of students exposed to ICAI (X=17.8750) and those exposed to CCI (X=14.0500) in favour of ICAI group."
