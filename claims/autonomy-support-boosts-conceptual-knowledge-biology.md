## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Students taught by an autonomy supportive teacher showed a significant time-dependent treatment effect favoring conceptual knowledge acquisition compared to students taught by a controlling teacher. [→ Hofferber 2014](#hofferber-2014)

## Evidence

### Hofferber 2014

Hofferber, N., Eckes, A., & Wilde, M. (2014). Effects of Autonomy Supportive vs. Controlling Teachers' Behavior on Students' Achievements. European Journal of Educational Research, 3(4), 177-184. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1085991

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Repeated measures ANOVA in a quasi-experimental pre/post study with 85 seventh graders found a significant interaction between time and treatment for the open-ended conceptual knowledge test, with η2 = 0.298 favoring the autonomy supportive group.

> "There was also a significant time-dependent treatment-effect (F1, 84 = 35.246; p < 0.001; η2 = 0.298; Figure 2). Autonomy supportive teacher behavior seemed to have a significant influence on learning at higher cognitive levels."
