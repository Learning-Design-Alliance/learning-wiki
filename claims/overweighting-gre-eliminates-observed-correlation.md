# Overweighting one admissions variable (GRE given twice the weight) produces near-zero or negative observed correlations between that variable and performance

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Alexander Small 2017

Alexander Small. (2017). Range restriction, admissions criteria, and correlation studies of standardized tests. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1709.02895](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1709.02895)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Simulation of admissions based on a linear model (Equation 6) with 2X weight for GRE; Table 3 reports P,GRE correlations of 0.027 (all admitted), -0.069 (top half), and r = -0.301 (lower half). The article attributes the negative correlation to admission of students with little prior research experience.

> "when the GRE receives disproportionate weight it exhibits no significant correlation with student performance in the full cohort and upper tier of admitted students, but shows a substantial negative correlation with performance in the lower tier of admitted students."
