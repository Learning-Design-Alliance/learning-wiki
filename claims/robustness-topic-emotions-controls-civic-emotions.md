# Climate-emotion associations and the enjoyment-participation indirect effect hold when controlling for topic emotions and discussion frequency, but some emotion-outcome paths do not

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Climate-emotion associations and indirect effects persist with controls, but the paths from class-related boredom to the outcomes and from class-related enjoyment to knowledge become non-significant. [→ Graf 2023](#graf-2023)

## Evidence

### Graf 2023

Graf, E., Stempfer, L., Muis, K. R., & Goetz, T. (2023). Classroom emotions in civic education: A multilevel approach to antecedents and effects. Draft version 1.0, University of Vienna / McGill University (not peer-reviewed preprint). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2023.101869

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Robustness checks adding topic emotions (enthusiasm, confusion, anxiety, boredom) and frequency of discussions as within-level controls. The article states: "The paths from class-related boredom to the outcome variables are in contrast to the models above, not significant if including these control variables. The same applies to the path from class-related enjoyment to knowledge."

> "Reported results on associations between classroom climate and emotions and indirect effects hold when including topic emotions and the frequency of discussions in the model"
