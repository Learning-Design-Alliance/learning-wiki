# Within the ICB course, students in the bottom quartile of online textbook use scored significantly lower on course exams than top-quartile users

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` ICB students in the bottom quartile of online textbook use scored significantly lower on each course exam than top-quartile users (exam 1: 76% vs. 87%; exam 2: 67% vs. 79%; exam 3: 62% vs. 71%; p < 0.05). [→ Luckie Douglas B. 2017](#luckie-douglas-b-2017)

## Evidence

### Luckie Douglas B. 2017

Luckie Douglas B., Hoskinson Anne-Marie, Griffin Caleigh E., Hess Andrea L., Price Katrina J., Tawa Alex, Thacker Samantha M. (2017). Integrating Concepts in Biology Textbook Increases Learning: Assessment Triangulation Using Concept Inventory, Card Sorting, and MCAT Instruments, Followed by Longitudinal Tracking. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-06-0204

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post hoc internal-control analysis using computer server logs of ICB students' online textbook usage correlated with course exam scores; the authors state this is not empirical comparative data and was excluded from statistical testing of the intervention.

> "ICB students identified in the bottom quartile for use of the online textbook, scored significantly lower on course exams than those in the top quartile (exam 1: 76% vs. 87%; exam 2: 67%, vs. 79%; exam 3: 62% vs. 71%; p < 0.05)."
