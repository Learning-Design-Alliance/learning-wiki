## Evidence

### Leslie M. Babinski 2018

Leslie M. Babinski, Steven J. Amendum, Steven E. Knotek, Marta Sánchez, Patrick Malone. (2018). Improving Young English Learners' Language and Literacy Skills Through Teacher Professional Development: A Randomized Controlled Trial. American Educational Research Journal. https://doi.org/10.3102/0002831217732335

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Intent-to-treat analysis of aggregated ratings from three classroom observations per teacher using the CQELL and the researcher-created DCCS Observation Tool, comparing 14 intervention and 16 control teachers. The article reports "Hedge's g = 2.02" for DCCS Strategies and g = 0.95 for Adapted Strategies; no group differences on Generic Lesson Elements or EL Support.

> "However, the intervention did lead to greater use of Adapted Strategies ( Mdiff = 0.57, 95% CI [.05, 1.10], p =.032, Hedge's g = 0.95) and DCCS Strategies ( Mdiff = 0.38, 95% CI [0.27, 0.50],p \.001, Hedge'sg = 2.02) relative to teachers in the control group."
