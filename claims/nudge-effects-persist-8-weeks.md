## Evidence

### Duckworth 2025

Duckworth, A. L., Ko, A., Milkman, K. L., et al. (2025). A national megastudy shows that email nudges to elementary school teachers boost student math achievement, particularly when personalized. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2418616122

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Preregistered durability analysis rerunning the primary weighted OLS models with lessons completed in weeks 5–8 and 9–12 as outcomes among the same 140,461 randomized teachers. The article reports the printed statistics quoted here.

> "the intervention messages had a significant impact during weeks 5 to 8 (additional lessons completed = 0.03, 1.81% increase; d = 0.01; P = 0.04) and during weeks 9 to 12 (additional lessons completed = 0.03, 2.42%, increase; d = 0.02; P = 0.01)"

### Duckworth 2025 (2)

Duckworth, A. L., Ko, A., Milkman, K. L., et al. (2025). A national megastudy shows that email nudges to elementary school teachers boost student math achievement, particularly when personalized. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2418616122

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Models 2 and 5 of the post-intervention durability analysis (SI Appendix, Table S12) for the Nudging Weekly Logins condition. The article reports a marginally significant BH-adjusted impact during weeks 9–12 (5.31% increase; unadjusted P = 0.004, BH-adjusted P = 0.06).

> "Nudging Weekly Logins showed a directional BH-adjusted impact on student progress during weeks 5 to 8 (additional lessons completed = 0.08, 4.49% increase; d = 0.02; unadjusted P = 0.01, BH-adjusted P = 0.11)"
