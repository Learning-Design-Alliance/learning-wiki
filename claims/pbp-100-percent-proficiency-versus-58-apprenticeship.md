## Evidence

### Puliatti Stefano 2026

Puliatti Stefano, Rodriguez Peñaranda Natali, Amato Marco, De Groote Ruben, Farinha Rui, Bunting Brendan, van Cleynenbreugel Ben, Mottrie Alexandre, Gallagher Anthony G. (2026). Randomised trial on the economic impact of proficiency‐based progression vs conventional robotic surgical training. BJU International. https://doi.org/10.1111/bju.70130

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Outcome of the OSSET prospective randomised trial: 47 Belgian medical students without prior robotic experience randomised 1:1:1:1 to four preparation conditions, assessed on a validated chicken bladder-urethra anastomosis task by blinded expert raters.

> "As shown in Fig. 2, Groups 1–3 (PBP‐based) achieved 100% proficiency, while only 58% of Group 4 (Apprenticeship) reached the benchmark."

### Puliatti Stefano 2026 (2)

Puliatti Stefano, Rodriguez Peñaranda Natali, Amato Marco, De Groote Ruben, Farinha Rui, Bunting Brendan, van Cleynenbreugel Ben, Mottrie Alexandre, Gallagher Anthony G. (2026). Randomised trial on the economic impact of proficiency‐based progression vs conventional robotic surgical training. BJU International. https://doi.org/10.1111/bju.70130

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same randomised trial, reporting mean training trials to proficiency: 5.92 for full PBP versus 15 for apprenticeship, with training time following the same pattern (2.96 h vs 7.75 h).

> "Group 1 (Full PBP) required the fewest attempts (mean 5.92 training trials), compared to Group 4, who required a mean of 15 training trials."
