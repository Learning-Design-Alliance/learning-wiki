Evidence¶

Adurangba Victor Oje 2026¶

Adurangba Victor Oje, Nathaniel J. Hunsu. (2026). Desktop virtual reality with scaffolded interaction and reflective explanation: effects on cognitive, motivational, and learning outcomes in engineering education. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10722-y

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

A one-way between-subjects ANOVA compared declarative knowledge during the initial exploration phase. No statistically significant difference was found between VR ("M = 6.58, SD = 3.22") and control (M = 5.89) groups; the effect size was small (d = 0.24) and the test non-significant, which does not show the groups were equal.

"The analysis revealed no statistically significant difference between the VR group (M = 6.58, SD = 3.22) and the control group (M = 5.89, SD = 2.47), F (1, 37) = 0.54, p >.05, partial η 2 =.01. The corresponding effect size was small (d = 0.24)"

Adurangba Victor Oje 2026 (2)¶

Adurangba Victor Oje, Nathaniel J. Hunsu. (2026). Desktop virtual reality with scaffolded interaction and reflective explanation: effects on cognitive, motivational, and learning outcomes in engineering education. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10722-y

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

A second ANOVA compared declarative knowledge after scaffolded guidance was introduced in the VR condition. The VR group scored higher ("M = 8.68, SD = 2.67") with a medium effect size (d = 0.63), but the difference was not statistically significant (p = .06), which the authors describe as marginal.

"The results revealed a marginally significant difference between the two groups, F (1, 37) = 3.78, p =.06, partial η 2 =.09. Students in the VR condition achieved higher mean scores (M = 8.68, SD = 2.67) than students in the control group (M = 7.05, SD = 2.51), with a medium effect size of d = 0.63"
