## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh and Emily Marshman 2015

Chandralekha Singh and Emily Marshman. (2015). Student difficulties with the probability distribution for measuring energy and position in quantum mechanics. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1509.04081](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1509.04081)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

In the same N = 210 multiple-choice survey, on questions 2 and 4 about position measurement at t > 0, "28% of the students agreed with statement (1)", i.e., that the probability density equals the squared initial wave function, indicating difficulty reasoning about how the wave function evolves according to the Hamiltonian.

> "Table I shows that on questions involving the probability density for measuring x at time 𝑡 > 0 (questions 2 and 4), 28% of the students agreed with statement (1)."

### Chandralekha Singh and Emily Marshman 2015 (2)

Chandralekha Singh and Emily Marshman. (2015). Student difficulties with the probability distribution for measuring energy and position in quantum mechanics. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1509.04081](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1509.04081)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of performance on question 5, which explicitly stated the expression |Ψ(x,t)|² but did not mention position measurement, versus question 2: 48% versus 28% made the time-independence error. Interviews suggest the explicit expression acted as a cue to square the initial wave function.

> "Table I shows that the percentage of students (48%) who incorrectly assumed that the probability density for measuring 𝑥 depends on time on question 5 is significantly higher than the percentage of students (28%) who made the same incorrect assumption on question 2 and selected statement (1)."
