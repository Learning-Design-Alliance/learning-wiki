## Evidence

### Maas MJM 2015

Maas MJM, van Dulmen SA, Sagasser MH, Heerkens YF, van der Vleuten CPM, Nijhuis-van der Sanden MWG, van der Wees PJ. (2015). Critical features of peer assessment of clinical performance to enhance adherence to a low back pain guideline for physical therapists: a mixed methods design. BMC Medical Education 15:203. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0484-1

`q3 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Online questionnaire ranking of program tasks after completion of the PA program; 44 PTs finished the program and two were excluded from quantitative analyses (response rate 86 %). Table 4 shows receiving peer feedback at mean 9.75, median 10.0; no effect size is printed.

> "Ranking results showed that participants committed the most to subtasks related to task performance in the PT role. Receiving peer feedback was perceived as the most valuable element, followed by receiving external coach feedback, performing the clinical task individually, and receiving simulated patient feedback."
