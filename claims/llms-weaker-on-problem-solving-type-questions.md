## Evidence

### Kim Seonghwan 2026

Kim Seonghwan, Ji Man, Kim Chung Young, Yun Young In, Lee Gahye, Yoon Chang Ho, Kim Mee Kum. (2026). Comparative performance of large language models in answering cornea and cataract surgery questions for resident training. BMC Ophthalmology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12886-026-04767-z

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Comparison on the 27 problem-solving-type questions. The article reports "none of the LLMs showed statistically significant differences compared to all trainees" (all p > 0.313 or > 0.999 in Table 1) and interprets the pattern as reflecting limits in advanced decision-making requiring nuanced prioritization, procedural judgment, or experience-based reasoning.

> "However, in problem-solving-type questions, ChatGPT-5 scored 85.19% and the other LLMs scored 81.48%, all showing lower performance than average fellows (87.65 ± 11.91%) and comparable performance to residents (83.95 ± 2.14%) (Table 1)."
