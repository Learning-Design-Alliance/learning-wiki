## Evidence

### Gunderson Elizabeth A. 2011

Gunderson Elizabeth A., Levine Susan C. (2011). Some types of parent number talk count more than others: Relations between parents' input and children's cardinal-number knowledge. Developmental Science. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-7687.2011.01050.x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression Model 6 (Results section, N=44) comparing the two present-object talk types head-to-head. The article states that "controlling for SES and the amount of talk about small numbers of present objects, additional talk about large numbers of present objects predicted children's cardinal-number knowledge."

> "We found that present-object talk about large sets remained a significant predictor of children's cardinal-value knowledge (β=.37, p<.05) while present-object talk about small sets did not (β=.07, n.s.)"

### Gunderson Elizabeth A. 2011 (2)

Gunderson Elizabeth A., Levine Susan C. (2011). Some types of parent number talk count more than others: Relations between parents' input and children's cardinal-number knowledge. Developmental Science. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-7687.2011.01050.x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression Model 7 (Results section) ruling out overall parental talkativeness as an alternative explanation. The article notes this talk type is "only 8.3 percent of number talk instances (SD=7.2 percent)" yet accounts for 15.7 percent of variance over SES.

> "controlling for SES and parents' overall amount of non-number talk, parents' large-number present-object talk remained significant (β=.38, p<.01)"
