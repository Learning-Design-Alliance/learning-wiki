# Flipped learning and conventional learning show no significant difference in CPR skills retention 6 months after training in time to first chest compression or total chest compressions

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Six months after training, there were no statistically significant differences between conventional and flipped learning groups in time to the first chest compression or number of total chest compressions. [→ Nakanishi Taizo 2017](#nakanishi-taizo-2017)

## Evidence

### Nakanishi Taizo 2017

Nakanishi Taizo, Goto Tadahiro, Kobuchi Taketsune, Kimura Tetsuya, Hayashi Hiroyuki, Tokuda Yasuharu. (2017). The effects of flipped learning for bystander cardiopulmonary resuscitation on undergraduate medical students. International Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.5a2b.ae56

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized post-test-only controlled trial of 108 first-year medical students at the University of Fukui; 50 conventional and 45 flipped learning completers assessed on mannequins 6 months after training. Medians were 33.0 vs 31.0 seconds and 101.5 vs 104.0 compressions, with "U=1171.0, p=0.73" and "U=1083.0, p=0.75"; no effect sizes printed.

> "There were no statistically significant differences 6 months after training between the two groups in our primary outcome measures, time to the first chest compression (33.0 [24.0-42.0] vs. 31.0 [25.0-41.0], U=1171.0, p=0.73) and the number of total chest compressions performed during the 2-minute test period (101.5 [90.8-124.0] vs. 104.0 [91.0-121.0], U=1083.0, p=0.75)."
