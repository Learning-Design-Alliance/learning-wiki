## Evidence

### Bailey 2018

Bailey, D. H., Duncan, G. J., Watts, T., Clements, D. H., & Sarama, J. (2018). Risky Business: Correlation and Causation in Longitudinal Studies of Skill Development. American Psychologist. https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0000146

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Correlational analysis of ECLS-K mathematics scores across waves. The article reports the printed correlations and SEs and presents this increasing stability as a testable prediction of the state-trait model's accumulating stable-factor effects, also established for cognitive ability and personality in prior work it cites.

> "Children’s mathematics achievement in the spring of kindergarten correlates .77 (SE= .01) with their mathematics achievement one year later in the spring of first grade, which correlates .80 (SE= .01) with their mathematics achievement two years later than that in the spring of third grade, which correlates .89 (SE= .01)"
