## Evidence

### Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2014

Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2014). Performance of Graduate Students at Identifying Introductory Physics Students’ Difficulties Related to Kinematics Graphs. 2014 PERC Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.039

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Quantitative results for the two translation questions, where the major introductory difficulty was matching the form of the graph with the verbal description superficially without careful examination of the y axis; introductory students with this difficulty were 37% on Q8 and 57% on Q9.

> "Table 5 shows that on both of these questions, few GSs  (8% and 28%) identified this major student difficulty."

### Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2014 (2)

Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2014). Performance of Graduate Students at Identifying Introductory Physics Students’ Difficulties Related to Kinematics Graphs. 2014 PERC Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.039

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Think-aloud interviews with six graduate students not from the class: on question 8 all interviewed graduate students selected an answer choice involving rolling down a hill, which Beichner's data shows was less common, with only 5% of introductory students selecting choice E.

> "However, Beichner’s data [1] indicates that, on this question, this difficulty is less common than the difficulty of matching  the graph with the verbal description superficially without regard for the axes (for example, only 5% of introductory students selected “E”, the incorrect answer choice favored by Tyler)."
