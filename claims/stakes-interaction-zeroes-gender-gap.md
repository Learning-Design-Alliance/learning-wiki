# The GPA-controlled in-term exam gender gap is about 0.23 standard deviations in medium-stakes non-retake classes but approximately zero in low-stakes retake classes

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In an HLM comparison of 3 retake classes with 254 non-retake classes, the female-student grade penalty on medium-stakes in-term exams was about 0.23 standard deviations, and the Female×Retake interaction (+0.234, p<10−3) reduced the low-stakes retake-class gap to approximately zero. [→ David J. Webb 2025](#david-j-webb-2025)

## Evidence

### David J. Webb 2025

David J. Webb, Cassandra A. Paul. (2025). High stakes exams inflate a gender gap and contribute to systematic grading errors in one introductory physics series. [doi:10.1103/ngrs-dxtf](https://doi.org/10.1103/ngrs-dxtf)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Three-level hierarchical linear model (students within classes within course series) controlling for incoming GPA, fit to term-averaged z-scored in-term exam scores from 257 classes (57,806 students with known GPA). Table II prints b_Female = -0.2262 and b_Female*Retake = 0.234 (z = 4.10, p<10−3); the article prints no standardized effect size, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "In the 254 non-retake classes which had medium-stakes in-term exams the average grade penalty (b F emale) that female students receive over the term is about 0.23 stan- dard deviations (of the exam average distribution). The interaction coefficient (b F emale∗Retake) for classes with low-stakes exams also has magnitude 0.23 but is posi- tive so that the low-stakes exams net gender gap is, as expected, approximately zero."
