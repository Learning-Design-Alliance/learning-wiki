# A blind engineering student autonomously measured the moment of inertia of an acrylic disk in a standard mechanics lab, obtaining a value 3.0% different from the ultrasonic-sensor calibration

## Evidence

### A. Lisboa and F. J. Peña 2024

A. Lisboa and F. J. Peña. (2024). Teaching labs for blind students: equipment to measure the inertia of simple objects. arXiv:2301.07033v1 [physics.ed-ph]. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1119/5.0152980](https://doi.org/10.1119/5.0152980)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Case-study measurement by a blind engineering student in a mechanics lab session; his tension-versus-acceleration data were fitted by linear regression, giving "a slope value of∼ 23.84±0.04 kg" and "Adj. R-Square was 0.9998", yielding a disc moment of inertia of 1.39×10⁻² kg m².

> "The results obtained gave a slope value of∼ 23.84±0.04 kg. The result of Adj. R-Square was 0.9998. The calculation of the disc’s moment of inertia with the data obtained by the student gives us a value of∼ (1.39× 10−2)kg m2."

### A. Lisboa and F. J. Peña 2024 (2)

A. Lisboa and F. J. Peña. (2024). Teaching labs for blind students: equipment to measure the inertia of simple objects. arXiv:2301.07033v1 [physics.ed-ph]. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1119/5.0152980](https://doi.org/10.1119/5.0152980)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Authors' conclusion reporting that the blind student "carried out the laboratory in an autonomous way" and that the disc's moment of inertia "diﬀers from the calibration data by 3 .0%" relative to the ultrasonic-sensor calibration measurements.

> "The blind student carried out the laboratory in an autonomous way, obtaining a result of the moment of inertia of the disk that diﬀers from the calibration data by 3 .0%."
