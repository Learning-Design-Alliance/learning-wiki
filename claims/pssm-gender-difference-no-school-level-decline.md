## Evidence

### Hagborg 1994

Winston J. Hagborg. (1994). An Exploration of School Membership among Middle- and High-School Students. Journal of Psychoeducational Assessment. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=An+Exploration+of+School+Membership+among+Middle-+and+High-School+Students

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Study 2 computed a 2 (gender) x 2 (school) ANOVA on PSSM scores across grades five to twelve; the gender effect was significant, consistent with Goodenow's earlier finding across seven of eight grades.

> "Female students reported significantly higher PSSM scores (F[1, 236] -7.94, p < .005)."

### Hagborg 1994 (2)

Winston J. Hagborg. (1994). An Exploration of School Membership among Middle- and High-School Students. Journal of Psychoeducational Assessment. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=An+Exploration+of+School+Membership+among+Middle-+and+High-School+Students

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same ANOVA found no significant middle- versus high-school difference; the article notes this contradicts expectations from school-climate studies that membership would decline among high school students.

> "School main effects (F[1, 236] = .02, ns) and the interaction between gender and school were not statistically significant (F[1, 236] = 1.17, ns)."
