## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants timing with only the dominant index finger showed no significant difference in mean response interval at the grand-mean target interval between short and long priors across trial bins, indicating generalisation into a single prior. [→ Yoshiki Matsumura 2024](#yoshiki-matsumura-2024)

## Evidence

### Yoshiki Matsumura 2024

Yoshiki Matsumura, Neil W. Roach, James Heron & Makoto Miyazaki. (2024). Body-part specificity for learning of multiple prior distributions in human coincidence timing. npj Science of Learning 9:34. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00241-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Experiment 1 (n=8) used only the dominant index finger for both priors across 640 trials. Paired t-tests with Holm correction on TR(Mpriors) found "No signi ﬁcant differences between the priors were found", and regression indices did not indicate concurrent learning; inferred prior means did not differ systematically.

> "No signi ﬁcant differences between the priors were found (pscor ≥ 0.27, ts(7) ≤ 1.35, Cohen’s ds ≤ 0.48, pairedt-test with Holm correction), indicating that participants learned a single prior by generalising over the two distributions."
