## Evidence

### Postiglione A and De Angelis I 2021

Postiglione A and De Angelis I. (2021). Students' understanding of gravity using the rubber sheet analogy: an Italian experience. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/abd1c4](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/abd1c4)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre/post item 'What is a gravitational lens?' (Figure 9). Before, 44% answered correctly and 41% did not know; after, "the correct answers rise to 93% of the total"; 98% correct at four months.

> "After the activity, the correct answers rise to 93% of the total, while the percentage of those who associate the phenomenon to a glass lens remains unchanged (3%)."

### Postiglione A and De Angelis I 2021 (2)

Postiglione A and De Angelis I. (2021). Students' understanding of gravity using the rubber sheet analogy: an Italian experience. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/abd1c4](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/abd1c4)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Item 'What are gravitational waves?' (Figure 10). Before, about 52% gave the correct definition, 21% did not know and 27% confused them with other topics; after, "the 83% gives the correct deﬁnition".

> "After the activity the 83% gives the correct deﬁnition. After four months we have a percentage of 81% of correct answers."

### Postiglione A and De Angelis I 2021 (3)

Postiglione A and De Angelis I. (2021). Students' understanding of gravity using the rubber sheet analogy: an Italian experience. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/abd1c4](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/abd1c4)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Item 'What is a black hole?' (Figure 11). Before, 57% did not feel confident to answer; after, "the percentage of correct answers rises to 79%", with only 7% choosing the downward-attraction distractor.

> "After the activity, the percentage of correct answers rises to 79%, giving that they associate black hole with something that attracts only the nearby matter."
