## Evidence

### Sutcher 2017

Sutcher, L., Podolsky, A., & Espinoza, D. (2017). Supporting principals' learning: Key features of effective programs. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/supporting-principals-learning

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The report's review section on learning opportunities focused on what matters cites research linking instructional leadership practices to student achievement and describes curricula emphasizing principals' complex instructional role, citing the McREL Balanced Leadership program's case-study goals.

> "Research has shown a positive link between instructional leadership—monitoring instruction, influencing curricula, assessing student learning, and other similar practices—and student achievement."

### Sutcher 2017 (2)

Sutcher, L., Podolsky, A., & Espinoza, D. (2017). Supporting principals' learning: Key features of effective programs. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/supporting-principals-learning

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The report states collaborative school environments are associated with improved achievement and reduced teacher turnover, and describes programs such as Bank Street's Principals Institute and the University of Connecticut's program teaching principals to organize time, share decision making, and practice collaborative leadership tasks.

> "A culture of collaboration does not spontaneously occur within schools. Principals need to learn to support the structures that foster collaboration"

### Sutcher 2017 (3)

Sutcher, L., Podolsky, A., & Espinoza, D. (2017). Supporting principals' learning: Key features of effective programs. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/supporting-principals-learning

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The report describes UIC's cycle of inquiry and the University of Connecticut's data-based planning cycles, and cites qualitative interviews in five STSP schools in Ohio where principals and teachers said the biggest contributing factor to success was increased use of student data to inform instruction.

> "High-quality programs train educators to use data and collective inquiry to identify problems and address needs, in collaboration with staff, parents, and community organizations."
