# Deepfake behaviours show gender patterns: males more likely to have shared deepfakes, females more likely to have reported them

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Males were significantly more likely to have shared a deepfake (23.2% vs. 10.0%, OR=0.34, p=.010), while females were significantly more likely to have reported one (14.1% vs. 24.0%, OR=2.14, p=.046). [→ Jake Renzella 2026](#jake-renzella-2026)
`q3 i?` Males trended toward creating deepfakes (10.1% vs. 4.0%) or being the subject of one (12.1% vs. 6.0%), though not statistically significant. [→ Jake Renzella 2026](#jake-renzella-2026)

## Evidence

### Jake Renzella 2026

Jake Renzella, Christian Bergh, Natasha Banks, and Alexandra Vassar. (2026). Gender Differences in AI Literacy Workshop Outcomes and Deepfake Engagement. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14718

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Pre-survey logistic regression (Table 2) controlling for year level and school on multi-select deepfake experience items. The article reports "OR=0.34" for sharing and "OR=2.14" for reporting; seen-deepfake rates were similar (71.7% vs. 74.0%).

> "While males were significantly more likely to haveshareda deepfake (23.2% vs. 10.0%, OR=0.34,p=.010), females were significantly more likely to havereporteda deepfake (14.1% vs.24.0%, OR=2.14,p=.046)."
