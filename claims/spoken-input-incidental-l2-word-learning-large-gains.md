# Meaning-focused exposure to spoken L2 input yields large vocabulary gains (g = 1.05) in incidental word learning

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Across 32 studies, meaning-focused exposure to spoken L2 input produced a significant mean learning gain of g = 1.05. [→ de Vos 2018](#de-vos-2018)

## Evidence

### de Vos 2018

de Vos, J. F., Schriefers, H., Nivard, M. G., & Lemhöfer, K. (2018). A Meta-Analysis and Meta-Regression of Incidental Second Language Word Learning from Spoken Input. Language Learning. https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12296

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 105 effect sizes from 32 primary studies with 1,964 participants with typical cognitive functioning. The weighted average effect was "g = 1.05, 95% conﬁdence interval (CI) [0.81, 1.28]", significantly larger than zero, indicating generally large vocabulary gains from spoken input.

> "Model 1 yielded a weighted average effect-size estimate of g = 1.05, 95% conﬁdence interval (CI) [0.81, 1.28], SE = 0.12, z = 8.77, p < .0001"
