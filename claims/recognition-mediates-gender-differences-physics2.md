# Gender differences in physics 2 grade and self-efficacy are mediated by perceived recognition rather than predicted directly by gender

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the SEM with gender mediation, gender did not directly predict physics 2 grade or post self-efficacy; the gender differences at the end of physics 2 were mediated by perceived recognition in physics 2 plus physics 1 grade and self-efficacy. [→ Yangqiuting Li 2020](#yangqiuting-li-2020)
`q3 i?` Perceived recognition had a large standardized effect on post self-efficacy in physics 2 (β = 0.40), roughly comparable to the β = 0.58 between post self-efficacy in physics 1 and physics 2, and its β to physics 2 grade exceeded that of high school GPA. [→ Yangqiuting Li 2020](#yangqiuting-li-2020)

## Evidence

### Yangqiuting Li 2020

Yangqiuting Li, Kyle Whitcomb, and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). How perception of being recognized or not recognized by instructors as a “physics person” impacts male and female students’ self-efficacy and performance. Department of Physics and Astronomy, University of Pittsburgh. [doi:10.1119/10.0002067](https://doi.org/10.1119/10.0002067)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Structural equation modeling with gender mediation on matched students (233 female, 464 male), controlling for SAT-Math, HS-GPA, physics 1 grade and post self-efficacy 1; model fit reported as CFI = 0.945, TLI = 0.925, RMSEA = 0.061, SRMR = 0.035. The article prints "β=0.40" for recognition to post SE 2 and "β=0.58" for SE 1 to SE 2.

> "we note that gender does not directly predict students’ grade and post self-efficacy in physics 2, even though there are statistically significant gender differences in students’ grade and post self-efficacy in physics 2 as shown in Table I. This means that the gender differences at the end of physics 2 were actually mediated by students’ perceived recognition in physics 2"
