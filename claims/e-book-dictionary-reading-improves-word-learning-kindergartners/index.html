Evidence¶

Korat et al. 2017¶

Korat, O., Kozlov-Peretz, O., & Segal-Drori, O. (2017). Repeated E-book Reading and Its Contribution to Learning New Words among Kindergartners. Journal of Education and Training Studies, Vol. 5, No. 7. https://doi.org/10.11114/jets.v5i7.2498

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Randomized two-group intervention: 78 LSES kindergartners divided into experimental (n=40) and control (n=38); three two-way ANCOVA analyses compared groups over pretest, posttest 1, and posttest 2. The first set of analyses reports differences "favoring the experimental group" on all three measures. For word production, though, the experimental group made no significant progress from pretest to posttest 1 (only from posttest 1 to posttest 2 and pretest to posttest 2), the word-type analysis reports "no difference in word production" between the groups in change across the test points, and the discussion says the authors "cannot give a definite answer to the research question about the effectiveness of promoting the production of words." The firm finding is for receptive comprehension and explanation.

"significant differences were found between the pre- and post-intervention scores in all three measures (receptive word l earning, explanation and produc tion of words) between the experimental and the control groups, favoring the experimental group."
