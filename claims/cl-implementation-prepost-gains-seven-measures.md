## Evidence

### Hall Troy E. 2018

Hall Troy E., Piso Zachary, Engebretson Jesse, & O'Rourke Michael. (2018). Evaluating a dialogue-based approach to teaching about values and policy in graduate transdisciplinary environmental science programs. PLoS ONE 13(9): e0202948. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0202948

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Wilcoxon signed-rank comparison of pre- and post-course interview scores for the implementation-year students. "There were seven cases where measures improved significantly (p ≤ .10) from pre-test to post-test", spanning all three domains.

> "Finally, among students receiving the curriculum, there were seven cases where measures improved significantly (p ≤ .10) from pre-test to post-test (Table 7)."

### Hall Troy E. 2018 (2)

Hall Troy E., Piso Zachary, Engebretson Jesse, & O'Rourke Michael. (2018). Evaluating a dialogue-based approach to teaching about values and policy in graduate transdisciplinary environmental science programs. PLoS ONE 13(9): e0202948. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0202948

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same implementation-year pre-post analysis; the authors label the effect sizes "medium" for most changes, with a "small effect size" for sociopolitical constraints (conceptualization) and a "large effect size" for expertise (analysis and interpretation).

> "All but two of these changes had medium effect sizes: the domain of sociopolitical constraints in the conceptualization phase had a small effect size and the expertise domain in the analysis and interpretation phase had a large effect size [65]."

### Hall Troy E. 2018 (3)

Hall Troy E., Piso Zachary, Engebretson Jesse, & O'Rourke Michael. (2018). Evaluating a dialogue-based approach to teaching about values and policy in graduate transdisciplinary environmental science programs. PLoS ONE 13(9): e0202948. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0202948

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Table 7 row for the expertise question in the analysis and interpretation phase: Wilcoxon signed-rank z = -3.84, p < .01, r = -.52; the mean rose from 1.56 to 2.93 (n = 27), a large effect.
