# Undergraduate health professional students report more positive satisfaction with flipped class learning than with traditional class learning (SMD = 0.48), at low certainty

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Across eight studies (n = 1696), student satisfaction with flipped class learning was positive compared to traditional class learning (SMD = 0.48). [→ Naing 2023](#naing-2023)

## Evidence

### Naing 2023

Naing, C., Whittaker, M. A., Aung, H. H., Chellappan, D. K., & Riegelman, A. (2023). The effects of flipped classrooms to improve learning outcomes in undergraduate health professional education: A systematic review. Campbell Systematic Reviews, 19, e1339. https://doi.org/10.1002/cl2.1339

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random-effects meta-analysis of eight studies (n = 1696) measuring student satisfaction with the method of learning at immediate post-test via self-report scales. The review reports satisfaction "was positive compared to traditional class learning" with SMD = 0.48 (95% CI 0.15 to 0.82), I² = 89%.

> "Overall, student satisfaction with flipped class learning was positive compared to traditional class learning (SMD = 0.48, 95% CI = 0.15 to 0.82, τ 2: 0.19, I 2:89%, p < 0.00001, 8 studies n = 1696); all being low certainty of evidence."
