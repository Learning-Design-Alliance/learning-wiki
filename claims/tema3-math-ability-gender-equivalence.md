## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Ninety-seven children completed the TEMA-3; mean ability and variance were statistically equivalent across gender, and the gender × age interaction was not significant. [→ Kersey 2019](#kersey-2019)

## Evidence

### Kersey 2019

Kersey, A. J., Csumitta, K. D., & Cantlon, J. F. (2019). Gender similarities in the brain during mathematics development. npj Science of Learning 4:19. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0057-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Behavioral assessment of math ability in 97 children (50 girls, 47 boys) aged roughly 3–9 using the TEMA-3. The t test showed no mean difference (t(95) = 0.57, p = 0.57), equivalence tests were significant, and the regression showed no Gender × Age Interaction (b = 0.26, p = 0.84).

> "Regression of TEMA-3 on gender and age: R2 = 0.79, F(3,93) = 118.5, p < 0.0001, Gender: b = 1.22, t(93) = 0.163, p = 0.871; Age: b = 11.74, t = 13.97, p < 0.0001; Gender × Age Interaction: b = 0.26, t = 0.21, p = 0.84."
