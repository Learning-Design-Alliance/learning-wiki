## Evidence

### Michael Brown 2026

Michael Brown, Rachel Smith, Jennifer Tipton, Gabrielle Leitner. (2026). The Threads of Campus Life: A Review of the Literature on the Use of Social Network Analysis in Research Articles on Undergraduate Students. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543261438444

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's corpus-level analysis of network types across 104 studies reports that "sociometric studies were much more common than egocentric ones", attributing this to trace data availability and organizational boundary definitions. Counts are descriptive, from Figure 2.

> "As an overview, sociometric studies were much more common than egocentric ones. We attribute this, in part, to the significant number of studies that used online interaction trace data as well as the studies that used an organizational boundary to define study participation."

### Michael Brown 2026 (2)

Michael Brown, Rachel Smith, Jennifer Tipton, Gabrielle Leitner. (2026). The Threads of Campus Life: A Review of the Literature on the Use of Social Network Analysis in Research Articles on Undergraduate Students. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543261438444

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review identifies latent networks as a distinct analytic form: "Eight studies measured what we refer to as latent networks" using institutional data such as co-enrollment or dining swipes; these were always sociometric and capture potential rather than enacted ties.

> "Eight studies measured what we refer to as latent networks—that is, instances of interaction where students had the potential to interact but may have not actually connected—using institutional data."
