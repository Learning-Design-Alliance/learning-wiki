## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Compared with low risk + high MET students, both high risk + high MET (RR 3.33 semester I; 3.87 semester II) and high risk + low MET (RR 3.24; 3.91) sub-groups showed further increased risk of lower GPA in both semesters (p < 0.01), indicating PA moderated but did not remove the risk. ## Evidence

### Pathmavarthini G 2026

Pathmavarthini G, Ughanthinii K, Shalini SB, Rubini SS, Trisha P, Devaashiny SV, Nursiati MT, Fatiah RMRZ, Dewi RR, Anwar ZK. (2026). Prediction of academic outcomes by serum cortisol, perceived academic stress, perceived memory performance, and physical activity among preclinical undergraduate medical students: a short-term observational study. BMC Psychology 14:557. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-04327-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariate ordinal regression across four risk-by-PA sub-groups over two semesters in the prospective cohort of 106 students. Both high-risk sub-groups showed elevated GPA risk relative to the low risk + high MET reference, corresponding to approximately 6% (semester I) and 53% (semester II) increases over baseline risk. No standardised effect size was printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "Compared with the "low risk + high MET" group, both "high risk + high MET" (RR = 3.33 and 3.87, respectively, p < 0.01) and "high risk + low MET" (RR = 3.24 and 3.91, respectively, p < 0.05) showed further increased risks of lower GPA in both semesters"
