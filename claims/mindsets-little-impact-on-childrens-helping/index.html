Evidence¶

Sierksma 2023¶

Sierksma, J. (2023). Children perpetuate competence-based inequality when they help peers. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00192-9

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Across Studies 2 and 3, preregistered mindset analyses (vignette items and mean scores) yielded no significant main effects or interactions with target competency; the article reports p-values ranging widely without significant effects.

"Overall, however, there was little indication that children’s mindsets had an impact on the type of help they provided."

Sierksma 2023 (2)¶

Sierksma, J. (2023). Children perpetuate competence-based inequality when they help peers. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00192-9

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Study 3 found a barely significant interaction of math self-mindset with competency (β = −0.22, p = .049); growth-mindset children differentiated more, opposite to expectations. The article says it "should thus be interpreted with caution".

"Simple slope analyses showed that when children endorsed a growth mindset about their math abilities (i.e., 1 SD below the mean), their tendency to provide more hints was stronger for competent compared to incompetent targets (β = 0.62, SE = 0.15, p < .001, 95% CI = [0.33, 0.93])."
