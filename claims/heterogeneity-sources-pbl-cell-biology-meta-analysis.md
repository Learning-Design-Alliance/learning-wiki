## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The pooled SMDs showed strong heterogeneity (I2 = 89.7%, P < .001), and subgroup analysis attributed it to different teachers, similar or same examination papers, and student numbers over 100. [→ Xu Wei 2021](#xu-wei-2021)

## Evidence

### Xu Wei 2021

Xu Wei, Ye Tingjie, Wang Xiaoling, Hayashi. Shogo. (2021). The effectiveness of the problem-based learning in medical cell biology education: A systematic meta-analysis. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000027402

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Heterogeneity assessment of the pooled examination-score SMDs using Cochran Q and I2; the article reports I2 = 89.7% with P < .001, indicating strong heterogeneity among the included studies. No effect-size magnitude is attached to the heterogeneity itself.

> "The heterogeneity of pooled SMDs in these 10 studies was tested and I2 value obtained was 89.7%, Pheter < .001, which revealed there was heterogeneity among these 9 studies (Fig. 2)."

### Xu Wei 2021 (2)

Xu Wei, Ye Tingjie, Wang Xiaoling, Hayashi. Shogo. (2021). The effectiveness of the problem-based learning in medical cell biology education: A systematic meta-analysis. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000027402

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Subgroup analysis (Table 3) classified studies by teacher sameness, examination paper sameness, and student number; the authors state these 3 factors "contributed to the heterogeneity in the combined effect", while meta-regression on 6 covariates found all P values over .05.

> "We demonstrated that 3 factors, including different teachers, similar or same examination paper, and over 100 students, contributed to the heterogeneity in the combined effect through subgroup analysis."
