# In water-jug problems, presenting information about available moves and their consequences did not enhance planning, though looping moves reliably decreased

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Providing move-availability information did not result in dramatically reduced legal moves, indicating additional information did not enhance the likelihood of planning. [→ Ahmad Tarmizi 1991](#ahmad-tarmizi-1991)
`q2 i?` A reliable reduction in looping moves was detected for the move-availability group, serving as a sensitive measure of planning. [→ Ahmad Tarmizi 1991 (2)](#ahmad-tarmizi-1991-2)

## Evidence

### Ahmad Tarmizi 1991

Ahmad Tarmizi, Rohani. (1991). Guidance During Mathematical Problem Solving. Doctoral thesis, University of New South Wales. http://hdl.handle.net/1959.4/69310

`q2 · i?`

The thesis reports, citing Atwood, Masson and Polson's experiment on water-jug problems comparing control, move-availability and memory condition groups, that "the additional information did not result in dramatically reduced legal moves". No effect size is printed.

> "Comparisons between the control and move-availability condition groups showed that the additional information did not result in dramatically reduced legal moves in finding solution to the problem."

### Ahmad Tarmizi 1991 (2)

Ahmad Tarmizi, Rohani. (1991). Guidance During Mathematical Problem Solving. Doctoral thesis, University of New South Wales. http://hdl.handle.net/1959.4/69310

`q2 · i?`

In the same Atwood, Masson and Polson experiment as reported in the thesis, "a reliable reduction in looping moves was detected for the move-availability group", which the authors treated as a sensitive measure of planning. No effect size is printed.

> "However, a reliable reduction in looping moves was detected for the move-availability group. This suggested that the numbers of looping moves could be used as a reliable measure"
