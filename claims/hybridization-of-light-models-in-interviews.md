## Evidence

### Martin Erik Horn 2004

Martin Erik Horn, Antje Leisner, Helmut F. Mikelskis. (2004). Difficulties of Model Construction in Optics. German Physical Society Spring Conference Leipzig 2002. Originally published 2002. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0402080](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0402080)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Evaluation of student interviews conducted after the holography course with individual students of all learning groups; one student's sketch and interview show the ray model as cognitive anchor extended by postulating an enlarged light ray carrying waves made of particles.

> "The spectrum of the discovered perspectives reaches from the partial overlapping of models to the integration of one model into an- other and to the complete hybridization of all mod- els known by the student, as exemplified by fig- ure 5."

### Martin Erik Horn 2004 (2)

Martin Erik Horn, Antje Leisner, Helmut F. Mikelskis. (2004). Difficulties of Model Construction in Optics. German Physical Society Spring Conference Leipzig 2002. Originally published 2002. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0402080](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0402080)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Authors' interpretation of the double-slit interview scene in which the student switches between photon, ray and wave conceptions mid-explanation; the authors note he "works with several models in a cognitive and synchronous way".

> "The missing distinction between the model conceptions has to thus be con- sidered one of the most important causes for a fail- ure of attempts of explanation of the test student in optics."
