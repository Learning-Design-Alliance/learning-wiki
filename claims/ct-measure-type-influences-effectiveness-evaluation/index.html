The review's comparison of CT outcomes across its 33 studies (Appendix D) reports non-standardized researcher-made measures were associated with significant improvement more frequently (93%) than standardized measures (55%). Items plausibly related to instruction tasks may explain the difference.

"Studies which employed th e non-standardized CT meas ures reported significant improvement on the posttest or between the experimental and control conditions more frequently (93% of the studies) than the standardized CT measures (55% of th e studies)."
