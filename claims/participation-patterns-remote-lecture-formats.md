# Student participation in polls and question-asking stayed similar to face-to-face levels, but problem-set submission and voting behaviour shifted under remote formats

## Evidence

### Schwarz G 2022

Schwarz G, Bleiner D, Günther D. (2022). On video lectures during remote teaching and beyond. Analytical and Bioanalytical Chemistry. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00216-022-03983-y

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Observational comparison of voting participation across the remote-teaching semesters against previous face-to-face years in the Element Analysis course; descriptive, no statistics. Questions via email, LMS, response system, or office hours were "just as sparse as before."

> "Overall, student participation was on par with previous face-to-face sessions for both asynchronous and synchronous lectures. That is, the number of voting participants was similar to that of previous years."

### Schwarz G 2022 (2)

Schwarz G, Bleiner D, Günther D. (2022). On video lectures during remote teaching and beyond. Analytical and Bioanalytical Chemistry. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00216-022-03983-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Observational account of submission-channel changes: email submission in spring 2020 caused a sharp drop; moving submissions to the learning management platform in autumn 2020 raised the percentage above previous years. The article reports significance without a statistic.

> "The number of students submitting problem sets dropped sharply after the launch of distance learning in spring 2020, when we asked for submissions via email rather than on paper. Subsequently, the percentage of students submitting problem sets via the learning management platform in autumn 2020 increased significantly compared to that of previous years."

### Schwarz G 2022 (3)

Schwarz G, Bleiner D, Günther D. (2022). On video lectures during remote teaching and beyond. Analytical and Bioanalytical Chemistry. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00216-022-03983-y

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Observational note from the synchronous spring 2021 semester: classroom response system voting fell to about 20% of videoconference participants. The authors state no explanation and report no test.

> "Finally, for no apparent reason, participation in voting via the classroom response system dropped to only about 20% of lecture videoconference participants in spring 2021."
