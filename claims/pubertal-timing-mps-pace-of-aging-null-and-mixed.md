## Evidence

### Raffington 2024

Raffington, L. (2024). Utilizing epigenetics to study the shared nature of development and biological aging across the lifespan. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00239-5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

The perspective reports the author's Texas Twin Project analysis quantifying MPSs of biological aging in children and adolescents to examine relations to pubertal development; no statistically significant association was found. Equivalence was not tested, and no effect size is printed.

> "In 8-18-year-olds from the Texas Twin Project, we found no associations between pubertal timing and accelerated biological age"

### Raffington 2024 (2)

Raffington, L. (2024). Utilizing epigenetics to study the shared nature of development and biological aging across the lifespan. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00239-5

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The same Texas Twin Project analyses found a somewhat faster pace of aging in post-menarcheal girls compared with same-aged girls who had not, and higher chronic inflammation MPSs in more pubertally advanced children after accounting for socioeconomic disadvantage. No effect sizes are printed.

> "We did, however,ﬁnd a somewhat faster pace of aging in girls who had experienced theirﬁrst menses compared with same-aged girls who had not"
