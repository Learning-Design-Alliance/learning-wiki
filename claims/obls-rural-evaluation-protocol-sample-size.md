`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

This is a planned prospective evaluation described in the protocol's Methods, not a completed result. The article prints pilot-based power calculations: 24 hospital participants and 20 prehospital participants after adjusting for a 20% dropout rate, at 95% power and 5% significance (two-sided).

> "A minimum of 44 HCWs will be trained at four implementation sites (two hospital-based and two prehospital). The sample size calculation is based on cognitive test score data from the original OBLS pilot study"

### Cunningham Shayna D. 2026 (2)

Cunningham Shayna D., Carandang Rogie Royce, Lowe W. Andy, Day Ann Marie, Trimarco Thomas, Becker Les R., Balakumar Poorna, Phelps John, Sutton Benjamin, Mosesso Vincent, Murphy Makayla, Grady James J., Vidosh Jacqueline, Shields Andrea. (2026). Optimizing obstetric life support training in rural healthcare settings in New England: protocol for a multisite prospective study. BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12884-026-08715-z

`q2 · i?` · `design · r?`

The protocol specifies the analysis plan: knowledge as percentage correct, self-efficacy on a 100-point scale, megacode performance as percentage of correct items, and retention assessed via least squares means from generalized estimating equations across three time points. All tests two-tailed.

> "Pre- and post-course cognitive scores will be analyzed using general linear mixed models, with results reported as mean changes (95% CI) and associated p-values. McNemar's test will be used to compare pre- and post-course self-efficacy scores"
