## Subclaims

## Evidence

### O'Neill Lotte Dyhrberg 2014

O'Neill Lotte Dyhrberg, Norberg Karen, Thomsen Maria, Jensen Rune Dall, Brøndt Signe Gjedde, Charles Peder, Mortensen Lene Stouby, Christensen Mette Krogh. (2014). Residents in difficulty—just slower learners? a case–control study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0276-z

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Unadjusted conditional logistic regression in the matched case–control study (89 cases, 343 controls). The article reports a 28% odds increase per extra year; Table 3 shows completion time was the only predictor surviving backwards elimination (OR 1.30, 1.06–1.58, p = 0.011).

> "For an increase in time to complete medical school of one year, the odds of ending up as a resident in difficulty increased 28% (OR = 1.28; Table 4)."

### O'Neill Lotte Dyhrberg 2014 (2)

O'Neill Lotte Dyhrberg, Norberg Karen, Thomsen Maria, Jensen Rune Dall, Brøndt Signe Gjedde, Charles Peder, Mortensen Lene Stouby, Christensen Mette Krogh. (2014). Residents in difficulty—just slower learners? a case–control study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0276-z

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Exposure–response analysis in the same case–control study, comparing late finishers (+3 years) with on-schedule finishers; the article prints unadjusted OR = 2.12 (1.15–3.90), rising to 12.23 (1.60–93.48) at +10 years.

> "The odds of ending up being a resident in difficulty were more than twice as large for a student who finished medical school nine years from study start (+3 years) compared to students who finished on schedule, i.e. after six years (OR = 2.12; Table 4)."
