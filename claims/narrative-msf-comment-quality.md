## Evidence

### Holm Ellen Astrid 2021

Holm Ellen Astrid, Al-Bayati Shaymaa Jaafar Lafta, Barfod Toke Seierøe, Lembeck Maurice A, Pedersen Hanne, Ramberg Emilie, Klemmensen Åse Kathrine, Sorensen Jette Led. (2021). Feasibility, quality and validity of narrative multisource feedback in postgraduate training: a mixed-method study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2020-047019

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Directed content analysis of all comments from the 48 MSF reports coded in NVivo; Table 1 prints mean constructive comments per MSF of 3.8 (3.9% of comments covered), behaviour related 26.5 (27.5%), specific 26.2 (27.2%).

> "Out of 1935 comments containing a positive or negative statement, 89% had positive valence and 11% had negative valence. Only 185 comments were coded as being constructive (giving suggestions for change); 1289 comments described behaviour and 1275 comments were specific i.e. giving an answer that was more specific than the question."

### Holm Ellen Astrid 2021 (2)

Holm Ellen Astrid, Al-Bayati Shaymaa Jaafar Lafta, Barfod Toke Seierøe, Lembeck Maurice A, Pedersen Hanne, Ramberg Emilie, Klemmensen Åse Kathrine, Sorensen Jette Led. (2021). Feasibility, quality and validity of narrative multisource feedback in postgraduate training: a mixed-method study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2020-047019

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion of the low amount of negative and constructive feedback, noting this finding is similar to previous studies on MSF and that raters have been shown to be reluctant to give negative feedback.

> "All respondents used a very polite language, indeed so polite that a hint of criticism could easily remain unnoticed."
