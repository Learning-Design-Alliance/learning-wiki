# The negative priming effect on probe RTs was statistically significant in both adolescents (Cohen's d = 1.07) and adults (Cohen's d = 0.73)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` The negative priming effect on probe response times reached statistical significance in both age groups, with Cohen's d = 1.07 for adolescents and d = 0.73 for adults. [→ Rossi 2019](#rossi-2019)

## Evidence

### Rossi 2019

Rossi, S., Vidal, J., Letang, M., Houdé, O., & Borst, G. (2019). Adolescents and Adults Need Inhibitory Control to Compare Fractions. Journal of Numerical Cognition, 5(3), 314-336. https://doi.org/10.5964/jnc.v5i3.197

`q3 · i3`

Paired t-tests on probe RTs in the negative priming paradigm showed significant negative priming effects in both groups: "t(19) = 2.08, p < .025, Cohen's d = 1.07 for adolescents and t(29) = 1.77, p < .05, Cohen's d = 0.73 for adults". Both effect sizes are medium-to-large.
