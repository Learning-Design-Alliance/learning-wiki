## Evidence

### Orr Robin M. 2020

Orr Robin M., Cohen Bruce S., Allison Stephen C., Bulathsinhala Lakmini, Zambraski Edward J., Jaffrey Mark. (2020). Models to predict injury, physical fitness failure and attrition in recruit training: a retrospective cohort study. Military Medical Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40779-020-00260-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Retrospective cohort analysis of 19,769 Australian Army recruits in 28-day and 80-day courses (2006–2011), with injury data from the Defence Injury Prevention Program surveillance tool. The study reports "17.6% vs. 34.3%" per course (P < 0.001) and "2.29 vs. 1.56 injuries per person-year" when exposure time was accounted for. Overall any-type injury incidence was 27.8% in both courses combined.

> "The proportion injured at least once was significantly lower in the 28-day course (17.6% vs. 34.3% in the 80-day course; P < 0.001). Accounting for exposure time, however, the any-type incidence rate was higher in the 28-day course (2.29 vs. 1.56 injuries per person-year in the 80-day course)."
