## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among 143 children aged 2–5.5 years, boys and girls did not differ in the highest number they could count to on the How High task, and showed marginal statistical equivalence. [→ Kersey 2018](#kersey-2018)

## Evidence

### Kersey 2018

Kersey, A. J., Braham, E. J., Csumitta, K. D., Libertus, M. E., & Cantlon, J. F. (2018). No intrinsic gender differences in children's earliest numerical abilities. npj Science of Learning, 3, 12. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0028-7

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional study of 143 children (71 girls, 72 boys; mean age = 4.10 years) asked to count aloud up to 100. "boys and girls did not show a difference in their ability to memorize the verbal counting sequence" with t(141) = 1.48, p = 0.14; equivalence was marginal (t2 p = 0.06).

> "An independent-samples t test revealed that boys and girls did not show a difference in their ability to memorize the verbal counting sequence ( t(141) = 1.48, p = 0.14, boys' mean = 30 girls mean = 23, difference = 7, 95% CI = −2 to 16), and Schuirmann 's tests of equivalence found marginal statistical equivalence"
