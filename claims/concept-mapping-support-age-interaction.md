# Concept-map generation requires age-appropriate instructional support: younger learners profit from high-coherence, pre-structured maps

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A map coherence × age group interaction showed university students profited more from low-coherence maps requiring self-organization, while high-school students profited more from high-coherence maps. [→ Garvin Brod 2021](#garvin-brod-2021)

## Evidence

### Garvin Brod 2021

Garvin Brod. (2021). Generative Learning: Which Strategies for What Age? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09571-9

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

A study comparing college and high-school students found the interaction; university students benefited more from low-coherence maps calling for self-organization, high-school students from high-coherence maps providing structure. Prior knowledge differences were not assessed.

> "a study comparing concept mapping among college and high-school students, which revealed a map coherence × age group interaction"

### Garvin Brod 2021 (2)

Garvin Brod. (2021). Generative Learning: Which Strategies for What Age? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09571-9

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

A study with late-elementary children varied support during concept mapping across three experiments: no-support children generated entire maps with poor results, while fill-in children outperformed both no-support and no-mapping controls on retrieval of key concepts.

> "Children who only had to fill in parts of an existing concept map (experiments 2 and 3) did significantly better than children in the no-support condition and children who did not work on concept maps at all."
