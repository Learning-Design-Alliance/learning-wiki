Evidence¶

Adolphus 2013¶

Adolphus, K., Lawton, C. L., and Dye, L. (2013). The effects of breakfast on behavior and academic performance in children and adolescents. Frontiers in Human Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2013.00425

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review's synthesis of cross-sectional studies using food-group-based breakfast quality classifications (e.g., AGHE core food groups). It states "quality of habitual breakfast, in terms of providing a greater variety of food groups and adequate energy, was positively related to school performance," with a possible dose-response relationship in one cohort.

"Some evidence suggested that quality of habitual breakfast, in terms of providing a greater variety of food groups and adequate energy, was positively related to school performance."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Increased frequency of habitual breakfast is consistently positively associated with academic performance in children and adolescents
  • School breakfast programs show suggestive positive effects on academic performance, with clearest effects on mathematics and arithmetic grades in undernourished children
  • Regular breakfast consumption is associated with increased odds of high academic achievement in Norwegian adolescent girls and boys, most strongly in girls
  • A mid-morning snack benefits on-task behavior only for children who consumed a small breakfast, correcting the energy deficiency — related
  • Breakfast consumption shows a mainly positive effect on on-task behavior in the classroom in children — related
  • A low glycaemic load breakfast increases time on-task in young children, but the effect is short-lived and specific to the first observation period — related
  • Acute experimental studies show inconsistent effects of breakfast on classroom behavior, with three of seven showing an on-task advantage — related
  • Robust RCTs of school breakfast programs failed to find behavioral benefits, with low and variable attendance and a shift from at-home to at-school consumption limiting impact — related
  • Breakfast After the Bell shows benefits for attendance and behavior, but mixed evidence on test scores — related
