## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Within the subgroup of 1168 referred doctors, male sex (HR 2.01), PLAB part 2 score at first sitting (HR 0.96) and multiple part 2 attempts (HR 1.37) significantly predicted progression to censure, while PLAB part 1 score was only weakly predictive. ## Evidence

### Tiffin Paul A. 2017

Tiffin Paul A., Paton Lewis W., Mwandigha Lazaro M., McLachlan John C., Illing Jan. (2017). Predicting fitness to practise events in international medical graduates who registered as UK doctors via the Professional and Linguistic Assessments Board (PLAB) system: a national cohort study. BMC Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-017-0829-1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Univariable survival analyses repeated on the restricted dataset of 1168 PLAB international medical graduates who had been referred at least once, to identify predictors of progression from referral to censure. Hazard ratios with confidence intervals are printed in the Results text and Additional file 1: Table S6.

> "the variables that were significantly predictive (P < 0.05) of progressing from a referral to censure were male sex (HR, 2.01; 1.41 to 2.88), PLAB part 2 score at first sitting (HR, 0.96; 0.93 to 0.99) and multiple attempts at part 2 (HR, 1.37; 1.01 to 1.85)."
