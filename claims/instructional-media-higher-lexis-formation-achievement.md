## Evidence

### Arulselvi 2011

Evangelin Arulselvi. (2011). Effect of Instructional Media in the Learning of English Grammar on the Achievement of Teacher Training Students at Namakkal District. i-manager's Journal of English Language Teaching, Vol. 1 No. 3 July - September 2011. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1071032

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Parallel-group experiment with 80 teacher training students; the experimental group (n=40) learned grammar via instructional software while the control group (n=40) used conventional methods. The article reports "higher mean scores (M = 34.25)" for the experimental group versus "(M = 24.15)" for controls, with "'t' value is 7.1" significant at .05.

> "But after the treatment, it was found that experimental group students were achieving higher mean scores (M = 34.25) in comparison to control group students (M = 24.15). The computed 't' value is 7.1 which is significant at .05 level of confidence."

### Arulselvi 2011 (2)

Evangelin Arulselvi. (2011). Effect of Instructional Media in the Learning of English Grammar on the Achievement of Teacher Training Students at Namakkal District. i-manager's Journal of English Language Teaching, Vol. 1 No. 3 July - September 2011. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1071032

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre-test equivalence check reported in the results section: both groups scored nearly equal on lexis formation before treatment, with a non-significant t value of 0.3, establishing that "both the groups were more or less same in lexis formation" prior to instruction.

> "Before the treatment both experimental and control groups were attaining nearly equal mean scores (M = 29, 29.45) for the grammar item lexis formation. The calculated t value is 0.3 which is not significant at the acceptable level of confidence."
