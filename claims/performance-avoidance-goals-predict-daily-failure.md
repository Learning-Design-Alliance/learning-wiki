# Morning performance-avoidance goals predict same-day academic failure, though not robustly across sensitivity analyses

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2` · `i0` negligible

## Subclaims
`q2 i0` Performance-avoidance goals, but not performance-approach or mastery goals, predicted same-day academic failure (β = .086, a small standardized coefficient); this link was no longer significant in a sensitivity model with all random effects covariances estimated. [→ Neubauer 2022](#neubauer-2022)

## Evidence

### Neubauer 2022

Neubauer, A. B., Schmidt, A., Schmiedek, F., & Dirk, J. (2022). Dynamic Reciprocal Relations of Achievement Goals with Daily Experiences of Academic Success and Failure: An Ambulatory Assessment Study. https://osf.io/gfvja/?view_only=b87fe179cf134471948726cb0e482a80

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r2`

DSEM of 108 Grade 5 students' daily reports. Performance-avoidance goals significantly predicted same-day failure ("β = .086, [.023, .149]"); the other goals did not. The standardized coefficient is small (below d = 0.2 equivalent).

> "In line with our second hypothesis, performance-avoidance goals, β = .086, [.023, .149], but not performance-approach goals, β = .050, [-.012, .112], and mastery goals, β = -.022, [-.088, .042], significantly predicted daily experiences of academic failure."

### Neubauer 2022 (2)

Neubauer, A. B., Schmidt, A., Schmiedek, F., & Dirk, J. (2022). Dynamic Reciprocal Relations of Achievement Goals with Daily Experiences of Academic Success and Failure: An Ambulatory Assessment Study. https://osf.io/gfvja/?view_only=b87fe179cf134471948726cb0e482a80

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Sensitivity analysis (alternative Model 2 with covariances among all random effects) found the performance-avoidance-failure link no longer significant. The authors call the effect comparatively weak and advise cautious interpretation.

> "Further, the link between performance-avoidance goals and same-day academic failure was no longer statistically significant either, β = .066 [-.006, .138]."
