## Evidence

### Baier-Mosch 2026

Baier-Mosch, Franziska; Dignath, Charlotte; Kunter, Mareike (2026). Effects of heterogeneity simulations on beliefs. Learning and Instruction 101, Art. 102258. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102258

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Study 1, a randomized 1x3 between-subjects online experiment with pre- and posttest measures (156 pre-service teachers). The mixed ANOVA on beliefs about responding to school students' heterogeneity showed a "significant interaction between time and group, F(2, 152) =4.996, p =.008, partial η2 =.062".

> "The results of a mixed ANOVA showed that there was a significant interaction between time and group, F(2, 152) =4.996, p =.008, partial η2 =.062."

### Baier-Mosch 2026 (2)

Baier-Mosch, Franziska; Dignath, Charlotte; Kunter, Mareike (2026). Effects of heterogeneity simulations on beliefs. Learning and Instruction 101, Art. 102258. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102258

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Study 1 simple main effects within the heterogeneity simulation condition (50 participants): "a significant effect of time for the heterogeneity simulation condition, F(1, 49) =11.107, p =.002, partial η2 =.185", a large within-condition increase from pretest to posttest.

> "Simple main effects tests revealed that there was a significant effect of time for the heterogeneity simulation condition, F(1, 49) =11.107, p =.002, partial η2 =.185."
