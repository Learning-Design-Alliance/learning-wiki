## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In focus groups, core internal medicine faculty described setting barriers (large auditorium, rows of seats, interruptions), faculty barriers (formulating questions, reworking lectures, giving up control, lack of training), and resident barriers (culture and buy-in) to active learning. ## Evidence

### Sawatsky Adam P. 2015

Sawatsky Adam P., Zickmund Susan L., Berlacher Kathryn, Lesky Dan, Granieri Rosanne. (2015). Understanding the challenges to facilitating active learning in the resident conferences: a qualitative study of internal medicine faculty and resident perspectives. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.3402/meo.v20.27289

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative analysis of three focus groups with 20 core internal medicine faculty (12 general internists, 8 subspecialists), coded with an editing approach within grounded theory (mean kappa 0.78). Faculty described "the auditorium layout and resident behaviors" as "physical barriers to facilitating active learning".

> "Faculty members discussed the auditorium layout and resident behaviors (sitting in the back of the room, being interrupted by pagers) as physical barriers to facilitating active learning."
