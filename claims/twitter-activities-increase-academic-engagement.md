## Evidence

### Smaragdi Loutou 2018

Smaragdi Loutou, Nikolaos Tselios, Panagiota Altanopoulou. (2018). The effect of twitter-mediated activities on learning outcome and student engagement: A case study. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1803.01343](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1803.01343)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

One-group pretest-posttest comparison of Greek-translated NSSE engagement scores for 17 students after four weeks of Twitter activities. The article reports means of 2.11 and 2.26 and a "statistically significant (paired-sample T-test, p = 0,02 <0,05, s)" increase. No effect size is printed.

> "In the initial test, the average was 2.11 and the standard deviation of 0.29, while in the final test, the average score was 2.26, and the standard deviation 0.26. Therefore, there is a positive differentiation at NSSE questionnaire and was statistically significant (paired-sample T-test, p = 0,02 <0,05, s)."

### Smaragdi Loutou 2018 (2)

Smaragdi Loutou, Nikolaos Tselios, Panagiota Altanopoulou. (2018). The effect of twitter-mediated activities on learning outcome and student engagement: A case study. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1803.01343](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1803.01343)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Subgroup analysis of NSSE change per academic-community relation (Table 2). The article attributes the largest improvement to faculty participation in the activities, "which amounts to 22.8%", with smaller changes for administrative staff (0.12 points) and other students (6.27%).

> "The fact that the teaching of the course participated in the activities, led the students to make the greatest improvement, which amounts to 22.8%."
