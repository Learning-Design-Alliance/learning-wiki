# Gender and baseline performance show no significant main effects or interactions with time on long-term training aftereffects, though descriptively the sustainability of effects was higher in the male group

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Bootstrap two-way MD-ANOVAs found neither a Gender main effect nor a Gender × Time interaction on behavioral and EEG measures, and neither a Baseline performance main effect nor interaction, though low performers showed significantly lower accuracy and higher response time overall. ## Evidence

### Linlin Dong 2025

Linlin Dong, Yufeng Ke, Xiaodong Zhu, Shuang Liu & Dong Ming. (2025). Long-term cognitive and neurophysiological effects of mental rotation training. npj Science of Learning 10:16. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00309-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Exploratory moderator analyses splitting 34 participants into Female/Male (17 each) and low-performance (20) vs. high-performance (14) groups via K-means clustering on pre-test 75° performance. The MD-ANOVAs found "no Baseline performance × Time interaction effect" and no Gender effects; the quoted contrast is the only significant group difference reported.

> "Post-hoc group comparisons showed signiﬁcantly lower ACC and higher RT in the low-performance group compared to the high-performance group, except for the post1-test’s ACC in the 75° task"
