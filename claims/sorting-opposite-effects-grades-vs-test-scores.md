## Evidence

### Elaine Allensworth and Takako Nomi 2014

Elaine Allensworth and Takako Nomi. (2014). Skill-Based Sorting in the Era of College Prep for All. University of Chicago Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/skill-based-sorting-era-college-prep-all-costs-and-benefits

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

The report's comparison of sorted versus de-sorted algebra classes found a divergence between outcome types: "the grades and pass rates of high-skilled students decline, while the grades of low-skilled students improve." No statistics are printed in the release.

> "However, sorting by ability has different effects on test scores than on grades and pass rates; the grades and pass rates of high-skilled students decline, while the grades of low-skilled students improve."

### Elaine Allensworth and Takako Nomi 2014 (2)

Elaine Allensworth and Takako Nomi. (2014). Skill-Based Sorting in the Era of College Prep for All. University of Chicago Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/skill-based-sorting-era-college-prep-all-costs-and-benefits

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

The report identifies a middle group harmed by sorting: "students with slightly above-average achievement" become the lowest-skilled in their class, and the release states this makes a big difference for their performance, with no effect size printed.

> "Sorting causes students with slightly above-average achievement to be the lowest-skilled students in their class, which makes a big difference for their performance."
