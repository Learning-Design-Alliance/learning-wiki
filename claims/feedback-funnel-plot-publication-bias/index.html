Effect of face-to-face verbal feedback compared with no or alternative feedback on the objective workplace task performance of health professionals: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2019-030672

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Visual examination of funnel plots (figure 4A all studies; 4B excluding high risk of bias) created to explore reporting bias; the review reports asymmetry "indicating some potential for publication bias". No quantitative test statistic is printed.

"Both funnel plots are asymmetrical, with a paucity of small studies with negative effect sizes that are less likely to be published, indicating some potential for publication bias."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • The likely effect of verbal feedback in any individual new study ranges widely, from a very small detrimental effect to a very large beneficial effect (prediction interval −0.06 to 1.46)
  • Across all 11 included trials, verbal face-to-face feedback versus no feedback yielded a pooled SMD of 1.09 before excluding high-risk-of-bias studies
  • Publication bias analyses suggest the pooled effect may be underestimated: trim-and-fill raises the mean effect from g = 0.24 to g = 0.29, with no evidence of funnel plot asymmetry — related
  • Funnel-plot asymmetry suggests small studies with negative or null results may be missing from the simulation-versus-clinical meta-analysis — related
