## Evidence

### Rhodes 2018

Rhodes, K. T., Lukowski, S., Branum-Martin, L., Opfer, J., Geary, D. C., & Petrill, S. A. (2018). Individual Differences in Addition Strategy Choice: A Psychometric Evaluation. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11360214/

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Individual differences analysis of item-level EIRT slope factor scores (Model 2), displayed in Figures 3C and 3D. The authors interpret the plotted slopes descriptively, reporting the item 29 example statistics of 14.61 seconds and success probability .56.

> "In general, using min-counting was particularly maladaptive as problem sizes increased. For example, min-counting was a particularly poor choice for item 29 (65 + 28), one of the most difficult problems on the assessment. Use of min-counting to solve this problem increased solution times by an average of 14.61 seconds with a probability of success = .56."
