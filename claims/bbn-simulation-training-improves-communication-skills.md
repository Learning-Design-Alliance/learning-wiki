# A four-hour simulation-based breaking bad news training significantly improves communication skills measured with the mBAS versus a traditional ED rotation alone

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The training group reduced non-effective communication skills by 23.53% (mBAS, reversed scoring) while the control group showed 0% change, with a significant group-by-time effect. [→ Servotte J-C 2019](#servotte-j-c-2019)

## Evidence

### Servotte J-C 2019

Servotte J-C, Bragard I, Szyld D, Van Ngoc P, Scholtes B, Van Cauwenberge I, Donneau A-F, Dardenne N, Goosse M, Pilote B, Guillaume M, Ghuysen A. (2019). Efficacy of a Short Role-Play Training on Breaking Bad News in the Emergency Department. Western Journal of Emergency Medicine. [doi:10.5811//westjem.2019.8.43441](https://doi.org/10.5811//westjem.2019.8.43441)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Communication skills were assessed with the modified Breaking Bad News Assessment Schedule (reversed, 1 = very good to 5 = very poor) by blinded raters in the same randomized simulation assessment; the difference in gains between groups was statistically significant.

> "we found a significant group-by-time effect (p<0.001) of the training on communication skills. There was a 23.53% reduction in the non-effective communication skills of the TG participants, but 0% in the CG."
