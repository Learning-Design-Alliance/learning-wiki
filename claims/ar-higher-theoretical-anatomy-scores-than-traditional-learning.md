# Medical students learning cranial nerve anatomy with hologram-based AR achieve significantly higher theoretical knowledge test scores than students using traditional learning methods

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` In a randomized controlled trial, the AR group achieved significantly higher theoretical knowledge test scores (median 18.8 [IQR 16.6–20.0]) than the TL group (median 9.4 [IQR 7.7–11.3]; P < .001). [→ Zingg L 2025](#zingg-l-2025)

## Evidence

### Zingg L 2025

Zingg L, Häusler M, Hein J, Jecklin S, Kottner S, Gascho D, Cavalcanti N, Voinov P, Götschi T, Carrillo F, Lagler F, Fürnstahl P, Farshad M. (2025). Using Hologram-Based Augmented Reality in Anatomy Learning: The TEACHANATOMY Randomized Trial. Academic Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/ACM.0000000000006012

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial (TEACHANATOMY) with 48 first- and second-year medical students randomized to AR (n=24) or TL (n=24). The AR group's theoretical median of 18.8 [IQR = 16.6–20.0] exceeded the TL group's 9.4 [IQR = 7.7–11.3] with P < .001; the article reports large effect sizes but prints no numeric effect size value.

> "Participants in the AR group achieved significantly higher final scores on both the theoretical (AR: median = 18.8 [IQR = 16.6–20.0]; TL: median = 9.4 [IQR = 7.7–11.3]; P < .001) and practical (AR: median = 14.0 [IQR = 12.3–14.7]; TL: median = 5.0 [IQR = 4.0–6.0]; P < .001) sections of the knowledge test compared to participants in the TL group (Figure 3)."
