# Achievement goal instructions show no main effects on achievement emotions in game-based learning

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Neither mastery-approach nor performance-approach goal instructions had statistically significant main effects on enjoyment, pride, anger, boredom, or shame. [→ Hu 2024](#hu-2024)

## Evidence

### Hu 2024

Hu, Y., Wouters, P., van der Schaaf, M., & Kester, L. (2024). The effects of achievement goal instructions in game-based learning on students' achievement goals, performance, and achievement emotions. Learning and Instruction, 93, 101943. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.101943

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the randomized experiment (N = 450), robust regression found no significant main effects of either instruction type on the measured achievement emotions (enjoyment, pride, anger, boredom, shame); anxiety was removed due to low reliability (greatest lower bound = 0.57).

> "Robust regression revealed no statistically significant main effects on achievement emotions."
