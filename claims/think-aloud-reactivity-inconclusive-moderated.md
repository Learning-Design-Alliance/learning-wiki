# Think-aloud reactivity effects on reading outcomes are inconclusive, moderated by report type, task language, and participant age

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · review `r2`–`r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` One study found think-aloud participants showed better comprehension monitoring and test performance than silent readers, suggesting positive reactivity. [→ Hu 2017](#hu-2017)
`q2 i?` Two studies found metacognitive verbalization did not significantly alter reading outcomes compared to non-verbalization. [→ Hu 2017 (2)](#hu-2017-2)
`q2 i?` Thinking aloud significantly increased the time spent completing reading tasks. [→ Hu 2017 (3)](#hu-2017-3)

## Evidence

### Hu 2017

Hu, Jingjing; Gao, Xuesong (Andy). (2017). Using think-aloud protocol in self-regulated reading research. Educational Research Review, v.22, pp. 181-193. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2017.09.004

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Baumann, Seifert-Kessell, and Jones (1992) with fourth-grade L1 students, that the think-aloud group outperformed silent readers in comprehension monitoring and reading tests.

> "The former group was found to be more skillful in comprehension monitoring and to perform better in the reading tests, suggesting that think-aloud might have altered the monitoring process and learning outcomes in a positive way."

### Hu 2017 (2)

Hu, Jingjing; Gao, Xuesong (Andy). (2017). Using think-aloud protocol in self-regulated reading research. Educational Research Review, v.22, pp. 181-193. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2017.09.004

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports null reactive effects on outcomes in Bannert and Mengelkamp (2008) and Yoshida (2008), both with university students; no equivalence test was reported.

> "Bannert and Mengelkamp (2008) found that reading outcomes of university students who were involved in metacognitive verbalization was not significantly different from those of students who were involved in cognitive verbalization and non verbalization activities"

### Hu 2017 (3)

Hu, Jingjing; Gao, Xuesong (Andy). (2017). Using think-aloud protocol in self-regulated reading research. Educational Research Review, v.22, pp. 181-193. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2017.09.004

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Yoshida (2008), the only study examining latency: think-aloud significantly increased task completion time, supporting the recommendation to avoid think-aloud when reaction time is the dependent variable.

> "The results showed that the think-aloud group took considerably more time than the non-think-aloud group, which suggested that reporting meta-cognition aloud had a significant effect on the amount of time spent for completing reading tasks."
