# Free ChatGPT use boosts originality on the assisted creative task but the advantage disappears when assistance is removed

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 1 causal `r3`, 1 theoretical `r2` · `q2`–`q3` · `i0` negligible

## Subclaims

`q3 i0` On the product improvement task with ChatGPT available, the general-AI group produced more original solutions than the human-only group (d = 0.79), but on the subsequent unassisted product invention task the general-AI group did not differ from the human-only group (d = 0.04); the `i0` code reads this unassisted result. [→ Wong 2026](#wong-2026)

`q2 i?` The paper reports, citing Wong and Qiu (2026), that university students using ChatGPT freely produced the most creative work on the assisted task, but on a later task with no ChatGPT the think-first group outperformed the free-ChatGPT group. [→ Halani 2026](#halani-2026)

## Evidence

### Wong 2026

Wong, S. S. H., & Qiu, S. X. (2026). Think First, ChatGPT Later: Guiding Human–AI Collaboration for Learning Gains in Independent Human Creativity. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10118-7

`q3 · i0 · negligible difference on the unassisted task, d = 0.04 (the assisted-task d = 0.79 is not what this code reads)` · `causal · r3`

A 3 (Learning Group) × 2 (Creative Task) mixed ANOVA on originality scores from 196 university students randomly assigned to human-only, general-AI, or regulated-AI groups. The general-AI group "produced more original solutions than both the human-only" and regulated-AI groups on the assisted task. However, "the general-AI group did not differ from the human-only group in their originality after the intervention, p = 1.00, d = 0.04."

> "the general-AI ( M = 4.08, SD = 1.22) group produced more original solutions than both the human-only ( M = 3.06, SD = 1.37) and regulated-AI (M = 3.30, SD = 1.30) groups, p <.001 and p =.002, d = 0.79 and − 0.62"

### Halani 2026

Halani, A. (2026). Designing for Reach: Seven Levers and the Student Alone with AI. Tang Institute at Phillips Academy. https://doi.org/10.35542/osf.io/t4cd2_v1

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The paper reports this secondhand, citing Wong and Qiu (2026): university students improved a creative product alone, with free ChatGPT, or under a think-first regimen; on the assisted task the free-ChatGPT group produced the most creative work and the think-first group showed no advantage.

> "On a later task with no ChatGPT, the pattern reversed: the free-ChatGPT group fell back to the level of those who had worked alone, while the think-first group produced more creative responses than the other groups (Wong & Qiu, 2026)."
