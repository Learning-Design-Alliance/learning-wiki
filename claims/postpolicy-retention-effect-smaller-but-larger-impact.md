# The postpolicy retention effect on dropout was smaller than the prepolicy retention relationship, but its systemwide impact was larger

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Prepolicy retained students' dropout rates were 25.3 percentage points larger than similar nonretained prepolicy students, a much stronger relationship than the postpolicy retention effect. [→ Allensworth 2004](#allensworth-2004)

## Evidence

### Allensworth 2004

Allensworth, E. (2004). Ending Social Promotion: Dropout Rates in Chicago after Implementation of the Eighth-Grade Promotion Gate. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/ending-social-promotion-dropout-rates-chicago-after-implementation-eighth-grade

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same statistical models estimating the postpolicy effect were applied to prepolicy cohorts. The report explains prepolicy retainees were a very select group (less than 2 percent), whereas many more postpolicy students were retained, giving retention a larger total systemwide impact.

> "retained students in prepolicy cohorts had dropout rates that were 25.3 percentage points larger than nonretained prepolicy students with similar demographic and achievement characteristics (55.7 percent, compared to 30.4 percent)."
