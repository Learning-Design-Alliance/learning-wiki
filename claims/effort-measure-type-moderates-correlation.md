# Effort measurement type moderates the correlation: self-agency ratings show a positive association and subjective ratings a stronger negative one than objective measures

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Mental effort ratings promoting self-agency yield a nonsignificant positive association, while other subjective mental effort ratings correlate more negatively than objectively logged measures. [→ Martine Baars & Lisette Wijnia & Anique de Bruin & Fred Paas 2020](#martine-baars-lisette-wijnia-anique-de-bruin-fred-paas-2020)

## Evidence

### Martine Baars & Lisette Wijnia & Anique de Bruin & Fred Paas 2020

Martine Baars & Lisette Wijnia & Anique de Bruin & Fred Paas. (2020). The Relation Between Students' Effort and Monitoring Judgments During Learning: A Meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09569-3

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Moderator analysis of effort measurement type (k reported in Table 1: objective 89, self-agent 3, other ME rating 31) found self-agency ratings positive and nonsignificant (r = .283) and other mental effort ratings most negative (r = −.502).

> "In support of Hypothesis 4a, mental effort ratings that promote a sense of self-agency result in a nonsignificant positive association between effort and monitoring judgments, in line with goal-driven self-regulation."
