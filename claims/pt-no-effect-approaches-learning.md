# Progress testing produced no significant changes in medical students' deep or surface approaches to learning compared with traditional assessments

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a repeated-measures ANCOVA controlling for age and gender, neither surface nor deep approach scores changed significantly from Time 1 to Time 2 in either the progress testing or the traditional cohort. [→ Chen Yan 2015](#chen-yan-2015)

## Evidence

### Chen Yan 2015

Chen Yan, Henning Marcus, Yielder Jill, Jones Rhys, Wearn Andy, Weller Jennifer. (2015). Progress testing in the medical curriculum: students' approaches to learning and perceived stress. BMC Medical Education 15:147. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0426-y

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Repeated-measures ANCOVA comparing the PT cohort (Years 2 and 4) with the traditional assessment cohort (Years 3 and 5) at two time points, controlling for age and gender, which "showed no significant changes in either surface or deep approach scores from Time 1 to Time 2".

> "A 2 × 2 × 2 (approaches to learning × time × cohort) repeated-measures Analysis of Covariance (ANCOVA), while controlling for the effect of age and gender, showed no significant changes in either surface or deep approach scores from Time 1 to Time 2, regardless of cohorts."
