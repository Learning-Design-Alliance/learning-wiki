# No statistically significant differences in nomophobia scores across academic level, residential status, marital status, college, or student habits

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` One-way ANOVA and independent t-tests found no statistically significant differences between nomophobia scores and academic level, residential status, marital status, or colleges. [→ Essel Harry Barton 2021](#essel-harry-barton-2021)
`q2 i?` Nomophobia scores did not differ significantly by habits such as sleep duration or eating behaviour. [→ Essel Harry Barton 2021 (2)](#essel-harry-barton-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Essel Harry Barton 2021

Essel Harry Barton, Vlachopoulos Dimitrios, Tachie-Menson Akosua. (2021). The relationship between the nomophobic levels of higher education students in Ghana and their academic achievement. PLoS ONE 16(6): e0252880. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0252880

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

One-way ANOVA and independent-samples t-tests across sociodemographic variables in the N = 670 sample; all reported tests were non-significant, e.g. academic level F(3, 644) = 1.120, p = 0.342 and colleges F(5, 642) = 2.16, p = 0.057.

> "The study found no statistically significant difference between nomophobia scores and academic level [F(3, 644) = 1.120, p = 0.342], residential status [t(646) = -1.120; p = 0.306], marital status [t(646) = -0.345; p = 0.731), and the colleges [F(5, 642) = 2.16; p = 0.057], as illustrated in Table 7."

### Essel Harry Barton 2021 (2)

Essel Harry Barton, Vlachopoulos Dimitrios, Tachie-Menson Akosua. (2021). The relationship between the nomophobic levels of higher education students in Ghana and their academic achievement. PLoS ONE 16(6): e0252880. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0252880

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Additional between-group tests of nomophobia scores across student habits in the same sample; sleep duration p = 0.891 and eating p = 0.557, with the article reporting the score "was not statistically significant". Equivalence was not tested.

> "The nomophobia score was not statistically significant (p = 0.235) among students’ habits such as sleep duration (p = 0.891) and eating (p = 0.557)."
