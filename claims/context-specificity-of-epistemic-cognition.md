## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Epistemic cognitions, including aims, often vary from situation to situation, so predicting learning in a given situation requires knowing the specific cognitions operative there. [→ CHINN et al. 2011](#chinn-et-al-2011)

## Evidence

### CHINN et al. 2011

Clark A. Chinn, Luke A. Buckland & Ala Samarapungavan (2011). Expanding the Dimensions of Epistemic Cognition: Arguments From Philosophy and Psychology. Educational Psychologist, 46(3), 141-167. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2011.587722

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument with illustrative cases: the article argues epistemic aims vary by context and can shift over a single conversation, citing Rosenberg et al. (2006) on a teacher comment prompting students to shift from listing facts to constructing a coherent explanation. It recommends situation-specific questionnaires and discourse observation. No effect sizes are printed.

> "In one class period, a student may adopt the aim of developing a good scientific explanation of photosynthesis. The next day the same student may adopt a simpler aim of simply learning a list of facts about plant growth."
