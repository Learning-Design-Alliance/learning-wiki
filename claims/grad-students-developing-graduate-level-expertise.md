## Evidence

### Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Surveying Graduate Students' Attitudes and Approaches to Problem Solving. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/37/5/055704](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/37/5/055704)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey comparison (Figure 2) of faculty and graduate students across problem levels; graduate students' graduate-level responses resembled introductory students' responses about introductory problems, suggesting developing expertise.

> "while solving physics problems they could often tell when their work and/or answer is wrong even without external resources but only approximately 50% of the graduate students could do so while solving graduate level problems"

### Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Surveying Graduate Students' Attitudes and Approaches to Problem Solving. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/37/5/055704](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/37/5/055704)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey item (Figure 1): graduate students were far less likely to see structural similarity between graduate-level conservation-of-energy problems. In contrast, all of the physics faculty noted that both problems can be solved using similar methods.

> "close to 90% of the graduate students agreed that two introductory level problems, both of which involve conservation of energy, can be solved using similar methods whereas only approximately 55% of them agreed"
