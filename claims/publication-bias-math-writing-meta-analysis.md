# Publication bias is present in the mathematics–writing literature, though the pooled estimate is robust in sensitivity analyses

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Funnel plot asymmetry tests indicate publication bias exists (intercept 0.76, bias −3.5, p < 0.001). [→ Young-Suk Grace Kim 2024](#young-suk-grace-kim-2024)
`q4 i3` The average effect size remains stable (0.49) when small studies are excluded, and did not differ by publication status or region. [→ Young-Suk Grace Kim 2024 (2)](#young-suk-grace-kim-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Young-Suk Grace Kim 2024

Young-Suk Grace Kim, Dandan Yang, Jinkyung Hwang. (2024). Are Mathematics and Writing Skills Related? Evidence from Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09960-4

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Egger's regression test and funnel plot inspection (Fig. 2) across the 564 effect sizes. The intercept significantly deviated from 0, and the authors conclude publication bias exists, with missing articles to the right of the mean near the bottom.

> "We also conducted a linear regression test of funnel plot asymmetry, and the result revealed an intercept of 0.76 and a bias of − 3.5 (t = − 7.97 and p < 0.001)."

### Young-Suk Grace Kim 2024 (2)

Young-Suk Grace Kim, Dandan Yang, Jinkyung Hwang. (2024). Are Mathematics and Writing Skills Related? Evidence from Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09960-4

`q4 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Sensitivity analyses across the 211 included studies. The pooled estimate stayed at 0.49 as rho varied from 0 to 1, and excluding studies with 20 or fewer participants yielded r = 0.49 (95% CI [0.46, 0.52]); publication status and region did not significantly predict the correlation.

> "Sensitivity analysis results showed that the average effect size is relatively robust with coefficients remaining consistent at 0.49 as rho increased from 0 to 1."
