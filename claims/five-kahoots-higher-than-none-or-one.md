# Playing all five Kahoot quizzes was associated with significantly higher exam scores than playing none or only one

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2025 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students who played all 5 Kahoot quizzes (n=26) scored significantly higher than those who played none (7.96 vs 6.93, p=0.018) or only 1 (vs 6.87, p=0.035), with an overall ANOVA difference across number of Kahoots played (p=0.007). [→ Zapata-Martínez I 2025](#zapata-martinez-i-2025)

## Evidence

### Zapata-Martínez I 2025

Zapata-Martínez I, Rius-Diaz F, Lorenzo-Álvarez R, De la Peña-Fernández L. (2025). Radiation Oncology Active Learning in Undergraduate Medical Education: The Usefulness of Kahoot and TikTok. Journal of Cancer Education 40(6):847–853. https://doi.org/10.1007/s13187-025-02583-5

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Bonferroni post-hoc comparisons after a significant ANOVA (p=0.007) on mean exam score by number of Kahoots played (0–5) in the experimental group; the all-5 group (7.96 ± 1.14) beat the none and one-Kahoot groups. No effect size printed.

> "students who played all 5 Kahoot (n = 26) scored significantly higher than those who played none or only 1 Kahoot (7.96 vs. 6.93 (p = 0.018) and 6.87 (p = 0.035), respectively)"
