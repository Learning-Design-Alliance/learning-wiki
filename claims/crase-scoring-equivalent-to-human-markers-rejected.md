## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The CRASE automated scoring engine produced writing scores with consistency and reliability equivalent to very experienced human markers, but was not used because of a conflict between commercial confidentiality and the need for explainability. [→ Webb 2021](#webb-2021)

## Evidence

### Webb 2021

Webb, M. E., Fluck, A., Magenheim, J., Malyn-Smith, J., Waters, J., Deschênes, M., & Zagami, J. (2021). Machine learning for human learners: opportunities, issues, tensions and threats. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-020-09858-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The article reports Lazendic et al.'s investigation of CRASE, an AI system for assessing school student writing in a national Australian test. It found "consistency and reliability equivalent" to experienced markers, was resilient to manipulation attempts, and had latent structure matching human markers, yet was not deployed. No effect size is printed.

> "They found it provided scoring outcomes that had consistency and reliability equivalent to those produced by independent groups of very experienced markers."
