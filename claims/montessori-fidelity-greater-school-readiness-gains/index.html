Evidence¶

Chloë Marshall 2017¶

Chloë Marshall. (2017). Montessori education: a review of the evidence base. npj Science of Learning 2:11. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-017-0012-7

q2 · i? · review · r3

Longitudinal comparison of 3–6-year-olds in high-fidelity Montessori, supplemented Montessori and conventional classes, tested at two time points and matched on parental education. The review reports that high-fidelity children showed "significantly greater gains" on the five listed measures, though groups were not randomly assigned.

"Children in the high- ﬁdelity Montessori school, as compared with children in the other two types of school, showed signi ﬁcantly greater gains on measures of executive function, reading, math, vocabulary, and social problem-solving."

Chloë Marshall 2017 (2)¶

Chloë Marshall. (2017). Montessori education: a review of the evidence base. npj Science of Learning 2:11. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-017-0012-7

q2 · i? · review · r3

Within-Montessori analysis in the same longitudinal study: classroom-level percentage of children using Montessori materials predicted end-of-year school readiness skills. The review reports significant prediction for executive function, reading and vocabulary, interpreting this as evidence that treatment fidelity mattered.

"the degree to which children were engaged with Montessori materials signiﬁcantly predicted gains in executive function, reading and vocabulary"

Related Claims¶

  • Removing supplementary materials from Montessori classrooms improved letter-word identification and executive function but not vocabulary, theory of mind, maths or social problem-solving — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Implementation fidelity matters: classrooms using only Montessori materials produced greater gains than classrooms mixing in other materials — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Children admitted by random lottery to high-fidelity Montessori schools performed better or the same as waitlisted controls on academic, executive function, and social measures, never worse — related
  • Low-income children in high-fidelity Montessori significantly closed the achievement gap with higher-income children by the end of a longitudinal lottery study — related
  • In a lottery-based comparison, Montessori children showed advantages on academic, executive function, social and theory-of-mind measures, with no differences on all measures — related
