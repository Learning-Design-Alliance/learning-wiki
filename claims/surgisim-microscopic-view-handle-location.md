## Evidence

### de Lotbiniere-Bassett M 2022

de Lotbiniere-Bassett M, Volpato Batista A, Lai C, El Chemaly T, Dort J, Blevins N, Lui J. (2022). The user experience design of a novel microscope within SurgiSim, a virtual reality surgical simulator. Int J Comput Assist Radiol Surg. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11548-022-02727-8

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Feedback from the four-participant testing of the microscopic view. The problem was solved by adding a small OR layout map at the bottom of the microscopic view so users could easily locate the microscope handles (Fig. 5).

> "All of the participants provided feedback that it was difficult to find the microscope handles once they entered the microscopic view. All of the participants frequently needed to exit the microscopic view to locate the handles in space."

### de Lotbiniere-Bassett M 2022 (2)

de Lotbiniere-Bassett M, Volpato Batista A, Lai C, El Chemaly T, Dort J, Blevins N, Lui J. (2022). The user experience design of a novel microscope within SurgiSim, a virtual reality surgical simulator. Int J Comput Assist Radiol Surg. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11548-022-02727-8

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Because Oculus controllers remain in the user's hands, tactile contact with static handles is absent; vibratory feedback was added. The mismatch between felt vibration and inability to move the microscope was addressed by enlarging the sphere of space in which the buttons can move the microscope.

> "All of the participants provided feedback that experienced a learning curve as they adapted to this different kind of haptic feedback. During microscope testing, all of the participants noted that the vibration had occurred and yet they were still unable to move the microscope."
