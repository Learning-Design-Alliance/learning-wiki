# In NAEP regression discontinuity estimates, exceeding the state threshold increases math achievement by 2.6 points (9.1% of a standard deviation) and reading achievement by 4.4 points (12.2% of a standard deviation)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A fuzzy regression discontinuity design on NAEP data finds exceeding the state threshold raises math achievement by 2.6 points (9.1 percent of a standard deviation) and reading achievement by 4.4 points (12.2 percent of a standard deviation). [→ Frisvold David E 2015](#frisvold-david-e-2015)

## Evidence

### Frisvold David E 2015

Frisvold David E. (2015). Nutrition and Cognitive Achievement: An Evaluation of the School Breakfast Program. Journal of Public Economics. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2014.12.003

`q3 · i?`

Fuzzy RD using local linear regression with a triangle kernel and bandwidth of 5 (optimal per Imbens and Kalyanaraman), comparing schools just above and just below state FRP thresholds in the 2003 NAEP sample. The study reports "exceeding the state threshold increases math achievement by 2.6 points" and reading by 4.4 points; no standardised effect size (d, g, r) is printed, so impact is null.

> "exceeding the state threshold increases math achievement by 2.6 points (with a standard error of 1.242) or 9.1 percent of a standard deviation and reading achievement by 4.4 points (with a standard error of 1.820) or 12.2 percent of a standard deviation"
