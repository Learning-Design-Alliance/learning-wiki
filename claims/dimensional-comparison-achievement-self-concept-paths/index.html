Evidence¶

Jansen 2020¶

Jansen, M., Becker, M., & Neumann, M. (2020). Dimensional Comparison Effects on (Gendered) Educational Choices. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 113(2), 330-350. https://nbn-resolving.org/urn:nbn:de:0111-pedocs-220459

q3 · i3 · associational · r2

SEM path analysis of the longitudinal sample (N = 519) testing the I/E pattern. The article reports "strong positive within-domain paths from grades to self-concept" with math β = 0.85 and German β = 0.75, plus negative cross-domain effects on self-concept and value for three of four paths.

"As expected, we found strong positive within-domain paths from grades to self-concept (math: β = 0.85, p < .01; German: β = 0.75, p < .01; see Table 3, Model 1) and value."

Related Claims¶

  • Dimensional comparisons: achievement–self-concept paths are positive in similar subjects but negative in dissimilar subjects — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Dimensional comparison effects extend to subjective task values, with contrast effects strongest for intrinsic value and costs and lowest for utility value, often mediated by self-concept — related
  • Grades show mediated dimensional comparison effects on course choices via self-concept and value — related
  • Subject similarity moderates dimensional comparison effects: far comparisons (e.g., math vs. verbal) yield strong contrast effects, near comparisons yield weaker contrast or assimilation effects — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Negative frame-of-reference effects are hypothesized to be truncated in pure self-efficacy measures relative to self-concept measures — related
  • Prior longitudinal SEM studies of self-concept versus achievement causality show heterogeneous causal predominance, varying with design, self-concept operationalization, domain, and achievement measure — a broader claim this one bears on
