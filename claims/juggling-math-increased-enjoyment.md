## Evidence

### van den Berg V. 2019

van den Berg V., Singh A.S., Komen A., Hazelebach C., van Hilvoorde I., Chinapaw M.J.M. (2019). Integrating Juggling with Math Lessons: A Randomized Controlled Trial Assessing Effects of Physically Active Learning on Maths Performance and Enjoyment in Primary School Children. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health 16(14): 2452. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph16142452

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mixed-model analysis in the cluster-randomized trial, with the same covariates as for performance, showed children in the intervention group "reported to have enjoyed the lessons significantly more than children who followed the sedentary math group", using a post-test evaluation questionnaire.

> "However, children in the intervention group reported to have enjoyed the lessons significantly more than children who followed the sedentary math group (see Table 4)."

### van den Berg V. 2019 (2)

van den Berg V., Singh A.S., Komen A., Hazelebach C., van Hilvoorde I., Chinapaw M.J.M. (2019). Integrating Juggling with Math Lessons: A Randomized Controlled Trial Assessing Effects of Physically Active Learning on Maths Performance and Enjoyment in Primary School Children. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health 16(14): 2452. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph16142452

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Table 4 reports post-intervention enjoyment of 7.0 (SD 2.5) in the intervention group versus 4.7 (SD 2.3) in the control group on a 0-10 visual analog scale, with an adjusted effect of "−2.2 [−3.2; −1.1]".

> "Enjoyment 7.0 (2.5) 4.7 (2.3) −2.2 [−3.2; −1.1]"
