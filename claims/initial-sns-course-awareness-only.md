## Evidence

### Vanderhoven 2015

Vanderhoven, E., Schellens, T., Vanderlinde, R., & Valcke, M. (2015). Developing educational materials about risks on social network sites: A design-based research approach. Ghent University. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-015-9415-4

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Pretest-posttest mixed-method survey intervention study with 1035 pupils (average age 15.14), control vs. experimental condition, multilevel analysis. The course raised awareness but, per Table 4, attitude and behavior differences were not significant (p > .02).

> "Quantitative results showed that the awareness of contact risks on SNSs increased when the pupils were involved in the course, compared with those of the control group"

### Vanderhoven 2015 (2)

Vanderhoven, E., Schellens, T., Vanderlinde, R., & Valcke, M. (2015). Developing educational materials about risks on social network sites: A design-based research approach. Ghent University. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-015-9415-4

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Open-question data from the same first intervention study: 17% vs. 7% reported profile changes, yet "83% of the teenagers reported not having changed anything".

> "in the experimental condition significantly more pupils reported having changed something on their profile (17% vs. 7% in the control condition), mostly their privacy settings (75%) and personal information (14%; including contact information)"
