# In survey data, moral identity moderated the curvilinear praise–antisocial behavior association: more praise was associated with more antisocial behavior only among students with low moral identity

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The interaction between the praise square and moral identity negatively predicts antisocial behavior. [→ Zhu Xiaobo 2024](#zhu-xiaobo-2024)

## Evidence

### Zhu Xiaobo 2024

Zhu Xiaobo, Jiang Wenyi, Shi Weijin, Shi Junfeng. (2024). Good learners or trouble makers? Study on the relationship between academic performance and antisocial behavior of junior high school students. PLOS ONE 19(1): e0295705. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0295705

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Moderated curvilinear regression (Model 9) on the CEPS sample. Fig 5 plots a fall-then-rise pattern for low moral identity and a stably negative relationship for high moral identity.

> "The results of model 9 in Table 3 show that the interactive term between the square of the praise and moral identity is negatively related to antisocial behavior (β = -. 030, p < 0.05), which shows that moral identity moderates the relationship between the praise and antisocial behavior."

### Zhu Xiaobo 2024 (2)

Zhu Xiaobo, Jiang Wenyi, Shi Weijin, Shi Junfeng. (2024). Good learners or trouble makers? Study on the relationship between academic performance and antisocial behavior of junior high school students. PLOS ONE 19(1): e0295705. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0295705

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Simple slope tests (Table 8): low moral identity slopes at 1SD and 2SD are significant and positive ("β = .043, p < 0.01; β = .122, p < .001"), whereas at high moral identity slopes are negative throughout, significant only at -SD and 0.

> "In other words, in the case of a low level of student moral identity, the praise will be positively related to the antisocial behavior after it exceeds a certain degree, and the positive effect is stronger than the main effect of the praise"
