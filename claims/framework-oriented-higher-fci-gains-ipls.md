## Evidence

### Andrew J. Mason 2016

Andrew J. Mason, Charles A. Bertram. (2016). Epistemic Impact on Group Problem Solving for Different Science Majors. US-China Education Review B. Originally published 2014. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.08666](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.08666)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pre-post observational analysis of 39 students in a first-semester algebra-based IPLS course, comparing averaged individual modified FCI gains across three survey-response categories. The framework-oriented group showed the highest gain: "a borderline significant difference existed in average gains" (p = 0.06 and p = 0.056; group gains g = +0.22, +0.13, +0.04).

> "Despite small sample size, a borderline significant difference existed in average gains of framework-oriented and performance-oriented students (p = 0.06). The framework-oriented group also showed borderline significance in FCI gains from the vaguely -defined group ( p = 0.056)."
