# Learner-perceived quality and educational impact of a low-cost pediatric simulator did not differ from a high-fidelity simulator for difficult airway management training in continuing medical education

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Anesthetists and emergency or ICU physicians randomized to a low-cost simulator rated the educational quality (EQS), simulation quality (SQS), and non-technical skills (ANTS) of the workshop the same as those randomized to a high-fidelity SimBaby simulator, with no difference at T0, T3, or T6. [→ Lejus-Bourdeau Corinne 2021](#lejus-bourdeau-corinne-2021)

## Evidence

### Lejus-Bourdeau Corinne 2021

Lejus-Bourdeau Corinne, Pousset Florence, Magne Cécile, Bazin Olivier, Grillot Nicolas, Pichenot Vincent. (2021). Low-cost versus high-fidelity pediatric simulators for difficult airway management training: a randomized study in continuing medical education. Brazilian Journal of Anesthesiology. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bjane.2021.05.006

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Open randomized single-center study of 128 physicians in teams of 4 to 6, randomized to HF (n = 65) or LC (n = 63) simulators for two difficult intubation scenarios. The article reports "There was no difference in T0, T3, and T6 between LC and HF groups" on the primary endpoint (T0 EQS) and secondary endpoints; equivalence was not formally tested.

> "There was no difference in T0, T3, and T6 between LC and HF groups for the primary and secondary endpoints."
