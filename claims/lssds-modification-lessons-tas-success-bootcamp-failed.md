# Among LSSDS program modifications, alumni teaching assistants succeeded while a pre-school coding bootcamp and highly specialized lectures failed

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Recruiting former LSSDS students as teaching assistants was an extremely positive modification, retained in subsequent in-person editions. [→ A. Bayo 2024](#a-bayo-2024)

## Evidence

### A. Bayo 2024

A. Bayo, V. Mesa, G. Damke, M. Cerda, M. J. Graham, D. Norman, F. Förster, C. Ibarlucea and N. Monsalves. (2024). La Serena School for Data Science and the Spanish Virtual Observatory Schools: Initiatives Based on Hands on Experience. [doi:10.1017/s1743921324003223](https://doi.org/10.1017/s1743921324003223)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

Organizer retrospective in Sec. 4.1 on the virtual editions held during the pandemic. Alumni TAs supported new students and faculty, and the feature was kept in the last two in-person editions. No comparative evaluation data are printed.

> "during the virtual editions, former LSSDS students (alumni) were recruited to help running the school as TAs. This experience was extremely positive for all parties"

### A. Bayo 2024 (2)

A. Bayo, V. Mesa, G. Damke, M. Cerda, M. J. Graham, D. Norman, F. Förster, C. Ibarlucea and N. Monsalves. (2024). La Serena School for Data Science and the Spanish Virtual Observatory Schools: Initiatives Based on Hands on Experience. [doi:10.1017/s1743921324003223](https://doi.org/10.1017/s1743921324003223)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Sec. 5 Lessons Learned reports the pre-school coding bootcamp as a modification that did not provide satisfactory results, based on the organizers' general feeling rather than measured outcomes. Specialized lectures were morphed into on-demand lectures during project time.

> "The former was offered to level the coding skills of the participants, however, the general feeling was that in fact exacerbated the differences in skills rather than level."
