# Gaze similarity is significantly higher in the collaborative than the competitive game version, while scores do not differ

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Gaze similarity was significantly higher in the collaborative version than the competitive version (F (1,16) = 4.79, p = .02, d = 1.02). [→ Sharma Kshitij 2020](#sharma-kshitij-2020)
`q2 i?` Score differences between the two players did not correlate with their average collaborative score (r = -0.10, p = .66). [→ Sharma Kshitij 2020 (2)](#sharma-kshitij-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Sharma Kshitij 2020

Sharma Kshitij, Leftheriotis Ioannis, Giannakos Michail. (2020). Utilizing Interactive Surfaces to Enhance Learning, Collaboration and Engagement: Insights from Learners’ Gaze and Speech. Sensors (Basel). https://doi.org/10.3390/s20071964

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Comparison of the two game modalities in the within-group design with 18 dyads; the printed effect size for the modality difference is "d = 1.02". The authors attribute the difference to the need to discuss answers jointly in the collaborative phase.

> "we observed a significant difference in the gaze similarity of the two players for the two different versions of the game (F (1,16) = 4.79, p = .02, d = 1.02). The gaze similarity was significantly higher for the collaborative version of the game than the gaze similarity for the competitive version"

### Sharma Kshitij 2020 (2)

Sharma Kshitij, Leftheriotis Ioannis, Giannakos Michail. (2020). Utilizing Interactive Surfaces to Enhance Learning, Collaboration and Engagement: Insights from Learners’ Gaze and Speech. Sensors (Basel). https://doi.org/10.3390/s20071964

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Null correlational result from the game-phase analysis of 18 dyads; the printed value is "r (16) = -0.10, p = .66". Equivalence was not tested.

> "we did not observe any significant correlation between the difference in scores of the two players and their average score in the collaborative version of the game (r (16) = -0.10, p = .66)"
