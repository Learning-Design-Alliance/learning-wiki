## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across multiple domains, providing opt-out options enhances the accuracy of the answers learners choose to provide (output-bound accuracy), from preschool age through adulthood. [→ Yael Sidi · Rakefet Ackerman 2024](#yael-sidi-rakefet-ackerman-2024)

## Evidence

### Yael Sidi · Rakefet Ackerman 2024

Yael Sidi · Rakefet Ackerman. (2024). Opting Out as an Untapped Resource in Instructional Design: Review and Implications. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09879-w

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing multiple studies, that adults, primary school children (ages 7–12), and preschoolers (ages 3–5) "can exploit the advantage of opting out for enhancing their output-bound accuracy". The review is explorative, not systematic.

> "Research has demonstrated that adults (Pansky et al., 2009; Rhodes & Kelley, 2005; Undorf et al., 2021), as well as primary school children (ages 7–12; Koriat & Ackerman, 2010; Krebs & Roebers, 2012), and even preschoolers (ages 3–5, Lyons & Ghetti, 2013), can exploit the advantage of opting out for enhancing their output-bound accuracy."
