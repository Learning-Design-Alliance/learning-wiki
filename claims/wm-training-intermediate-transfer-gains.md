## Subclaims
`q4 i1` Immediately after training there are significant, moderate effects on verbal working memory (g = 0.31) and visuospatial working memory (g = 0.28) versus treated controls, with true heterogeneity between studies. [→ Melby-Lervåg 2016](#melby-lervag-2016)

## Evidence

### Melby-Lervåg 2016

Melby-Lervåg, M., Redick, T. S., & Hulme, C. (2016). Working Memory Training Does Not Improve Performance on Measures of Intelligence or Other Measures of “Far Transfer”: Evidence From a Meta-Analytic Review. Perspectives on Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616635612

`q4 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analysis of intermediate-transfer outcomes. Table 1 reports verbal working memory g = 0.31 [0.19, 0.42] across 60 treated-control comparisons and visuospatial working memory g = 0.28 [0.16, 0.40] across 40 comparisons, both significant, with significant heterogeneity on verbal working memory.

> "These effects are significant and moderate in size, and there is evidence of true heterogeneity between studies (see Table 1)."
