## Evidence

### Stephanie L. Day 2024

Stephanie L. Day, Jin K. Hwang, Tracy Arner, Danielle McNamara, Carol M. Connor. (2024). Choose Your Own Adventure: Interactive E-Books to Improve Word Knowledge and Comprehension Skills. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2403.02496](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2403.02496)

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

In the Hurricane! HLM the total times read coefficient was γ70 = 0.459, p = 0.035 (Table 3). The association is observational, derived from user-log read counts, not a manipulated condition; no effect size was printed.

> "Finally, students who read Hurricane! two or more times also tended to have higher scores on the Hurricane! posttest."

### Stephanie L. Day 2024 (2)

Stephanie L. Day, Jin K. Hwang, Tracy Arner, Danielle McNamara, Carol M. Connor. (2024). Choose Your Own Adventure: Interactive E-Books to Improve Word Knowledge and Comprehension Skills. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2403.02496](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2403.02496)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

User-log read counts for Dragon's Lair (mean 2.20 readings) were entered in the HLM; the coefficient was γ60 = 0.245, p = 0.236 (Table 2), a non-significant association with no printed effect size, so equivalence was not tested.

> "On average, students read the story approximately two times (Table 1), however reading the story more than once was not associated with higher posttest outcomes.  (Table 2)."
