Evidence¶

Baker 2022¶

Baker, D.L., Alberto, P.C., Macaya, M.M., García, I., & Gutiérrez-Ortega, M. (2022). Relation Between the Essential Components of Reading and Reading Comprehension in Monolingual Spanish-Speaking Children: a Meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-022-09694-1

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Moderator analysis within the random-effects meta-analysis of component–comprehension relations. The review reports "a large heterogeneity across studies" explained in part by age, country, and the reading comprehension tests used; no heterogeneity statistics are printed in the available text.

"(3) there is a large heterogeneity across studies which is explained, in part, by factors such as age, country where the study was conducted, and the reading comprehension tests used."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • The evidence base comprises 33 studies yielding 146 effect sizes on reading components and comprehension in monolingual Spanish-speaking children (2000–2021). — related
  • Most predictor effect sizes showed high heterogeneity; only morphological awareness, working memory, and language comprehension fell in the moderate I² range — related
  • Six component reading skills show strong average relationships with reading comprehension in struggling adult readers (rs ≥ .50) — related
  • Phonological awareness and fluency show the largest effect-size relations with reading comprehension among Spanish-speaking children's essential reading components. — related
  • Most studies of Spanish-speaking children's reading have focused on phonological awareness or alphabetic principle in relation to reading comprehension. — related
  • Prior knowledge research is conducted mostly with adults (40.71%) and in postsecondary education (46.31%), based on available database records — related
  • Poor readers are highly heterogeneous and do not all display the same type of reading impairment — related
