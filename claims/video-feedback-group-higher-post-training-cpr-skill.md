## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Nursing students trained with video feedback scored significantly higher on post-training CPR skill than the infographic group (15.5 ± 2.4 vs. 13.5 ± 3.2, P = 0.004). [→ Miri Kheizaran 2024](#miri-kheizaran-2024)

## Evidence

### Miri Kheizaran 2024

Miri Kheizaran, Yaghoubi Ali, Kholousi Sadaf, Yousofzadeh Mahdi, Zanganeh Alireza, Gharayi Mehdi, Namazinia Mohammad. (2024). Comparative study on the impact of ‘Infographic versus video feedback’ on enhancing students’ clinical skills in basic life support. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05763-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled comparison of 38 students per group; CPR skills assessed by a 17-item questionnaire plus a mannequin demonstration scored on a 21-point checklist. The video feedback group scored "15.5 ± 2.4" versus 13.5 ± 3.2, P = 0.004; no effect size is printed.

> "the post-training CPR skill score was also significantly higher in the Video Feedback group (15.5 ± 2.4) compared to the Infographic group (13.5 ± 3.2, P = 0.004)"
