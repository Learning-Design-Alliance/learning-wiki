# Self-regulation and metacognition correlate strongly with mathematics achievement (r = .42 and .37)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r3` · `q3` · `i3` large · n=30 meta-analyses, 66 correlations from 13,853 effect sizes (~4.66 million participants)

Self-regulation correlates r = .42 with mathematics achievement in children (Robson et al. 2020, k = 22) and metacognition .37 in adolescents (Muncer et al. 2022, k = 74). These are correlations compiled across meta-analyses: they describe how strongly the characteristic goes with mathematics achievement, not what causes it.

## Evidence

### Breit et al. 2025

Breit, M., Schneider, M., & Preckel, F. (2025). Mathematics achievement and learner characteristics: A systematic review of meta-analyses. *Learning and Individual Differences, 118*, 102621. [doi:10.1016/j.lindif.2024.102621](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2024.102621)

`q3 · systematic review of meta-analyses` · `i3 · large effect, r = .42 and .37` · `n=30 meta-analyses, 66 correlations from 13,853 effect sizes (~4.66 million participants)` · `review · r3`

The review compiles zero-order correlations from published meta-analyses (each with at least ten effect sizes, the largest one kept where several covered a variable), found by a systematic PsycINFO search in July 2024. It pools nothing further and controls for nothing: many of these characteristics correlate with one another, so a correlation here says how strongly the characteristic goes with mathematics achievement, not that it causes it. Self-regulation r = .42 (Robson et al. 2020, k = 22, n = 7,076 children), stronger in younger samples; metacognition r = .37 (Muncer et al. 2022, k = 74, n = 570,575 adolescents, I² = .99). Both correlate more strongly with mathematics than in Hattie's (2023) cross-domain synthesis.

## Discussion

The authors call these promising targets because they are malleable, citing a separate meta-analysis of self-regulation interventions (Pandey et al. 2018, d = 0.42); that intervention evidence is not part of this review.
