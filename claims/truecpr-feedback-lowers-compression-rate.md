# Feedback device use lowers chest compression rate toward the guideline-recommended range in simulated CPR

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Without the device trainee doctors compressed at 129 cpm (above the 100–120 guideline range); with TrueCPR the rate was 112 cpm, a statistically significant difference. [→ Majer Jolanta 2019](#majer-jolanta-2019)

## Evidence

### Majer Jolanta 2019

Majer Jolanta, Jaguszewski Miłosz J., Frass Michael, Leskiewicz Marcin, Smereka Jacek, Ładny Jerzy R., Robak Oliver, Szarpak Łukasz. (2019). Does the use of cardiopulmonary resuscitation feedback devices improve the quality of chest compressions performed by doctors? A prospective, randomized, cross-over simulation study. Cardiology Journal. https://doi.org/10.5603/CJ.a2018.0091

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same randomized cross-over simulation study (65 trainee doctors, 2-min compression cycles); the device condition produced a significantly lower rate, "112 cpm (IQR 103–113)", vs 129 cpm manual, p = 0.002.

> "The frequency of CCs when the TrueCPR device was used resulted in 112 cpm (IQR 103–113) and achieved statistical significance which was lower than when not using the device during CCs, resulting in 129 cpm (IQR 122–135)"
