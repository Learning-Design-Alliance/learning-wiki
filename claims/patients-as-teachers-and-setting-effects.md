## Evidence

### Klimas J. 2017

Klimas J., Small W., Ahamad K., Cullen W., Mead A., Rieb L., Wood E., McNeil R. (2017). Barriers and facilitators to implementing addiction medicine fellowships: a qualitative study with fellows, medical students, residents and preceptors. Addiction Science & Clinical Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-017-0086-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In the patient-concerns domain of the interview study, the analysis showed patients "taught" learners lessons regarding addiction medicine; a nursing fellow reported learning "more and more, just from my participants and the patients that I see".

> "Our analysis demonstrated that patients "taught" learners lessons regarding addiction medicine, and thus facilitated learning implementation. Physicians learned that trust in the therapeutic relationship was critical to patient engagement and treatment success."

### Klimas J. 2017 (2)

Klimas J., Small W., Ahamad K., Cullen W., Mead A., Rieb L., Wood E., McNeil R. (2017). Barriers and facilitators to implementing addiction medicine fellowships: a qualitative study with fellows, medical students, residents and preceptors. Addiction Science & Clinical Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-017-0086-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

A clinical fellow described being verbally assaulted and having trays hurled in the inpatient setting, "whereas an out-patient setting, people are dying to see a doctor for this". The article notes this resonated with a perceived need for outpatient clinical rotation.

> "Sometimes, the learning was difficult and confrontational, probably varying as a result of setting—inpatient versus outpatient—and help seeking"
