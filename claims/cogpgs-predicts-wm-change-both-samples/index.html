Evidence¶

Bruno Sauce 2021¶

Bruno Sauce, John Wiedenhoeft, Nicholas Judd and Torkel Klingberg. (2021). Change by challenge: A common genetic basis behind childhood cognitive development and cognitive training. npj Science of Learning 6:16. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00096-6

q3 · i? · associational · r2

A general linear model ('WM change model') on 344 children combining a developmental sample (n = 160) and a training sample (n = 184), with WM change as outcome. The article reports "β = 0.15, p = 0.04" and "2.2% of variance explained" for cogPGS; no standardised effect size beyond this partial variance is printed.

"The WM change model showed that cogPGS signiﬁcantly explained interindividual differences in WM change ( β = 0.15, p = 0.04), with 2.2% of variance explained after accounting for the effect of gender, age, sample, and the interactions."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Age interacts with sample type in predicting WM change: younger participants changed more during 2-year development, while older participants improved more during 25-day training (interaction p = 0.04). — related
  • cogPGS significantly explains baseline WM variation (β = 0.10, p = 0.03; 1.0% of variance after accounting for gender and age). — related
  • The effect of cogPGS on WM change does not depend on whether gains came from typical development or from training (no cogPGS × sample interaction, p = 0.46). — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Gender shows no significant effect on baseline WM (p = 0.44) or on WM change (p = 0.09) in the combined samples. — related
  • Both samples showed significant WM gains: a 14% mean increase after 2 years of development (β = 0.60, p < 0.001) and a 34% mean increase after 25 days of training (β = 2.10, p < 0.001). — related
