# Paying ninth graders for course grades increases GPA but not achievement test scores in Chicago

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Chicago grade incentives raised GPA by .093 (.057) ITT and .131 (.078) TOT, with no effect on math or reading test scores. [→ Roland G. Fryer 2010](#roland-g-fryer-2010)

## Evidence

### Roland G. Fryer 2010

Roland G. Fryer, Jr. (2010). Financial Incentives and Student Achievement: Evidence from Randomized Trials. NBER Working Paper No. 15898. http://www.nber.org/papers/w15898

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Chicago randomized trial in 20 low-performing high schools: ninth graders paid $50 per A, $35 per B, $20 per C in five core courses. GPA increased but test scores on the PLAN assessment did not.

> "The estimate of the impact of incentives on grades is .093 (.057) [ITT] and .131 (.078) [TOT]."
