## Evidence

### Talsma 2018

Talsma, K., Schüz, B., Schwarzer, R., & Norris, K. (2018). I believe, therefore I achieve (and vice versa): A meta-analytic cross-lagged panel analysis of self-efficacy and academic performance. Learning and Individual Differences, 61, 136-150. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2017.11.015

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Moderator analysis comparing separate CLPA path models for adult and child subgroups of the k=11 pooled studies. The article reports "significantly stronger cross lagged effects for adults than for children" for both cross-lagged paths (Table 5).

> "the reciprocal relationship between self-efficacy and academic performance varied depending on participant age, with significantly stronger cross lagged effects for adults than for children."

### Talsma 2018 (2)

Talsma, K., Schüz, B., Schwarzer, R., & Norris, K. (2018). I believe, therefore I achieve (and vice versa): A meta-analytic cross-lagged panel analysis of self-efficacy and academic performance. Learning and Individual Differences, 61, 136-150. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2017.11.015

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Subgroup path model for the child studies (two studies; n=1506 children vs n=1182 adults). The child model's self-efficacy → performance path was near-zero and non-significant, while all other paths in the child model remained significant.

> "the βSE1P2 cross-lagged path was not statistically significant for children: self-efficacy did not have a net effect on subsequent performance for children in this sample."
