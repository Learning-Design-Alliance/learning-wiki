## Evidence

### Jessica Watkins 2011

Jessica Watkins, Janet E. Coffey, Edward F. Redish, & Todd J. Cooke. (2011). Disciplinary authenticity: Enriching the reforms of introductory physics courses for life-science students. Originally published 2010. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1109.4983](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1109.4983)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

The article cites pre-course survey results from the studied course context, attributing the finding that many introductory biology students do not initially perceive biology-math-physics connections; it also notes textbook treatments often set math apart in side boxes.

> "Pre-course survey results suggest that many introductory biology students do not see the connection between biology and math or physics"

### Jessica Watkins 2011 (2)

Jessica Watkins, Janet E. Coffey, Edward F. Redish, & Todd J. Cooke. (2011). Disciplinary authenticity: Enriching the reforms of introductory physics courses for life-science students. Originally published 2010. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1109.4983](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1109.4983)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative case analysis of one videotaped interview with a sophomore ecology major who earned a B in the honors course. She reported being "really amazed at how many different physics principles" were involved and described physics as linked to the course's unity-and-diversity framework. The article presents her as representative of students after traditional instruction whose ideas shifted.

> "What also made this really different from the AP biology course is that they've used physics and math a lot more."
