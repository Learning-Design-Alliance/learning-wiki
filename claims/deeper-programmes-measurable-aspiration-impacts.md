## Evidence

### Martin Archer 2021

Martin Archer, Jennifer DeWitt, Carol Davenport, Olivia Keenan, Lorraine Coghill, Anna Christodoulou, Samantha Durbin, Heather Campbell, Lewis Hou. (2021). Going beyond the one-off: How can STEM engagement programmes with young people have real lasting impact? Manuscript in preparation for Research for All. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.14324/rfa.05.1.07](https://doi.org/10.14324/rfa.05.1.07)

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes this to research on the Science Capital Teaching Approach (L. Archer et al., 2018), co-developed with 43 secondary science teachers over four years and experienced by students ongoing across a school year. Only significance is printed, no effect size.

> "Research on the approach found a statistically significant increase in the science capital score of young people whose teachers had participated in the project, as well as an increase in aspirations to study science post-16 and improved attitudes to science."

### Martin Archer 2021 (2)

Martin Archer, Jennifer DeWitt, Carol Davenport, Olivia Keenan, Lorraine Coghill, Anna Christodoulou, Samantha Durbin, Heather Campbell, Lewis Hou. (2021). Going beyond the one-off: How can STEM engagement programmes with young people have real lasting impact? Manuscript in preparation for Research for All. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.14324/rfa.05.1.07](https://doi.org/10.14324/rfa.05.1.07)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes this finding to the NUSTEM primary partnerships evaluation (Emembolu et al., 2020), an ongoing collaboration with 33 schools delivering regular classroom activities, teacher CPD and family engagement. No effect size is printed.

> "Over the first three years of the partnership the gender difference in career aspirations between girls and boys decreased significantly, with more children saying that they would be interested in a broader range of careers (Emembolu et al., 2020)."
