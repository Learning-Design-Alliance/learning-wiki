## Evidence

### Andrew J. Mason 2020

Andrew J. Mason. (2020). Learning goals and perceived irrelevance to major within life science majors in introductory physics. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/perc.2019.pr.mason](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2019.pr.mason)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Survey of 218 students over six semesters of an algebra-based first-semester physics course at a primarily undergraduate institution, using an end-of-semester written survey question about which material related to their major. As printed: "50 out of 218 students, or about 23% of the course population, declared that nothing within the course related to their majors".

> "In this data set, 50 out of 218 students, or about 23% of the course population, declared that nothing within the course related to their majors , thus expressing a perception of irr elevance"

### Andrew J. Mason 2020 (2)

Andrew J. Mason. (2020). Learning goals and perceived irrelevance to major within life science majors in introductory physics. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/perc.2019.pr.mason](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2019.pr.mason)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive distributions in Table 1 of irrelevance percentages by major (Biology 16.5%, Health 22.4%, Other Sci 40.0%) and by orientation (Framework 22.4%, Performance 20.3%, Vague 27.0%). The article reports "there is no statistical separation between groups for either majors or orientations".

> "Biology majors were slightly less likely to perceive irrelevance than were health science majors, and Vague-oriented students were slightly more likely to perceive irrelevance than were other learning orientations.  However, there is no statistical separation between groups for either majors or orientations."

### Andrew J. Mason 2020

Andrew J. Mason. (2020). Perceived irrelevance and mastery vs. performance achievement goals: Two mindset variables within attitudinal experiences of life science majors in introductory physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2012.09900](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2012.09900)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Post-test feedback survey responses across six semesters of a single instructor's algebra-based course, in which students answered which course sections related to their major coursework; "50 out of 218 total students" reported no relevance. Table 1 gives the 22.9% overall irrelevance rate.

> "50 out of 218 total students stated that they saw no relevance  whatsoever to their perceived majors"
