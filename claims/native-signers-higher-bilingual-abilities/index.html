Evidence¶

Freel 2011¶

Freel, B. L., Clark, M. D., Anderson, M. L., Gilbert, G. L., Musyoka, M. M., & Hauser, P. C. (2011). Deaf Individuals’ Bilingual Abilities: American Sign Language Proficiency, Reading Skills, and Family Characteristics. Psychology, 2(1), 18-23. https://doi.org/10.4236/psych.2011.21003

q3 · i? · associational · r1

Independent-samples t-test in the same 55-participant sample comparing native signers (deaf parents, ASL acquired before age three) with non-native signers on the combined Bilingual Ability score. The study reports "t (53) = 4.12, p = 0.000" with the printed group means; no effect size is printed.

"A t-test indicated that native signing of ASL was significantly related to participants’ bilingual abilities , with native signers (M = 3334.51, SD = 618.59) exhibiting more proficient bilingual abilities than non-nativ e signers (M = 2327.35, SD = 779.33), t (53) = 4.12, p = 0.000."

Related Claims¶

  • Native signers (children of at least one Deaf parent) outperform non-native signers on ASL syntactic judgment and analogical reasoning tasks — related
  • Deaf adults in this sample read on average at a 5.8 grade level, ranging from grade 1.7 to post-college
  • Family communication mode and hearing status at birth were not significantly related to bilingual abilities
  • Native signing and maternal education jointly predict bilingual ability, accounting for 38.4% of variance — related
  • All three groups differed significantly on expressive vocabulary, with non-native signers scoring lowest — related
  • The advantage of signing from birth holds for Deaf learners of all ages tested and all age-of-entry groupings — related
  • Higher maternal education relates to more proficient bilingual abilities in deaf adults — related
  • Deaf parental hearing status, sign language use at home, and attendance at a school for the deaf are each associated with more positive perceptions of deaf children's social success — related
  • First graders taught sight words with ASL signs achieved higher posttest accuracy than students taught without ASL — related
