# Students who omitted direction from E-Field diagrams could largely draw accurate directional diagrams when prompted, pointing to activation or choice rather than diagramming ability

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who did not include directional information in unprompted E-Field diagrams often drew correct directions and directional labels in the prompted diagramming task, which the authors attribute to activation or choice rather than diagramming ability. [→ Vignal 2022](#vignal-2022)

## Evidence

### Vignal 2022

Vignal, M., & Wilcox, B. R. (2022). Investigating Unprompted and Prompted Diagrams Generated by Physics Majors During Problem Solving. arXiv. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010104](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010104)

`q3 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Comparison of unprompted and prompted diagrams for the E-Field problem-task pair showed three of five students with sign errors correctly drew direction and included directional labels when prompted; the authors call this an "issue of activation or choice, rather than diagramming ability".

> "We believe that this was either an issue of activation or choice, rather than diagramming ability, since these students were largely capable of drawing accurate diagrams when prompted in the paired diagramming task."
