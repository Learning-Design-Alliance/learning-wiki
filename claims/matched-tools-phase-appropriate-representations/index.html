Evidence¶

B. Slof 2012¶

B. Slof, G. Erkens, P. A. Kirschner, J. Janssen, J. G. M. Jaspers. (2012). Successfully carrying out complex learning-tasks through guiding teams' qualitative and quantitative reasoning. Instr Sci 40:623–643. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-011-9185-2

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Content analysis of phase-related representations (coded against expert representations via MEPA) compared matched and mismatched teams; the quote reports the t-tests for relationship counts and errors in each phase, with no effect sizes printed.

"Compared to teams in the mismatched condition, teams in the matched condition signiﬁcantly represented (1) more relationships during the problem–solution phase ( t(14) = 2.77, P = 0.03) but made more errors representing them (t(14) = 4.18, P = 0.00), (2) fewer relationships during the solution–evaluation phase ( t(14) =- 2.29, P = 0.05) but made fewer errors representing them (t(14) =- 3.59, P = 0.00)."

B. Slof 2012 (2)¶

B. Slof, G. Erkens, P. A. Kirschner, J. Janssen, J. G. M. Jaspers. (2012). Successfully carrying out complex learning-tasks through guiding teams' qualitative and quantitative reasoning. Instr Sci 40:623–643. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-011-9185-2

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Content analysis comparing the two partly matched conditions found causal-only teams represented more concepts and relationships than simulation-only teams; only t and p values are printed, no effect sizes.

"Compared to teams in the simulation-only condition, teams in the causal-only condition represented signiﬁcantly more concepts ( t(16) = 2.56, P = 0.02) and relationships (t(16) = 4.24, P = 0.00)."
