## Evidence

### Jeffrey M. Rabin 2024

Jeffrey M. Rabin, Andrew Meyertholen, Brian Shotwell. (2024). Students' Experience of Cultural Differences Between Mathematics and Physics. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Written survey of 20 math/physics double majors or majors-with-minors at UCSD. Students cited dissatisfaction especially with Fourier analysis, Hilbert spaces, and differential equations, and did not enjoy the hand-waviness of manipulations and approximations in physics classes.

> "15 out of 20 students cited at least some dissatisfaction, especially with Fourier anal- ysis, Hilbert spaces, and diﬀerential equations."

### Jeffrey M. Rabin 2024 (2)

Jeffrey M. Rabin, Andrew Meyertholen, Brian Shotwell. (2024). Students' Experience of Cultural Differences Between Mathematics and Physics. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Survey question 7 response counts as printed: 7 students reported negative effects from lower rigor in physics, 6 reported positive complementary effects, and 5 reported no effect on learning. Other answers were unclear or blends.

> "(7) Yes: proofs and justiﬁcations are more rigorous in math classe s than in physics classes, which has had a negative eﬀect on my understanding in phys ics classes due to lack of clarity; (6) Yes: the two complement one another nicely, w hich has had a positive eﬀect on my learning; (5) No: I recognize there are diﬀerences, but I don’t think this has aﬀected my learning overall."
