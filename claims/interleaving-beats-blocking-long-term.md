# Interleaved practice produces superior long-term retention and transfer compared with blocked practice, despite blocked practice yielding faster gains during training

## Evidence

### Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork 2009

Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork. (2009). Making Things Hard on Yourself, But in a Good Way: Creating Desirable Difficulties to Enhance Learning. Psychology and the Real World. https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/research/

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The chapter reports, citing Shea and Morgan (1979), that participants practiced three movement patterns in interleaved or blocked conditions; blocked practice improved performance faster during training, but at a 10-day retest the interleaved group far outperformed the blocked group under random testing.

> "Under interleaved/random testing conditions, participants who had practiced under interleaved conditions performed far better than did the blocked-practice participants, who appeared, when tested under a random schedule, to have learned virtually nothing."

### Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork 2009 (2)

Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork. (2009). Making Things Hard on Yourself, But in a Good Way: Creating Desirable Difficulties to Enhance Learning. Psychology and the Real World. https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/research/

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The chapter reports, citing Rohrer and Taylor (2007), that participants learning formulas for calculating volumes of solids in blocked or interleaved fashion showed a large delayed-test advantage for interleaving: "63 percent versus 20 percent of new problems worked correctly a week later." No standardized effect size is printed for this finding.

> "The size of the long-term advantage of interleaved practice was striking: 63 percent versus 20 percent of new problems worked correctly a week later (Rohrer & Taylor, 2007)."

### Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork 2009 (3)

Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork. (2009). Making Things Hard on Yourself, But in a Good Way: Creating Desirable Difficulties to Enhance Learning. Psychology and the Real World. https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/research/

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The chapter reports, citing Kornell and Bjork (2008), that participants learned the styles of 12 artists from six paintings each; interleaved presentation enhanced later identification of new paintings' artists, extending interleaving benefits to inductive learning.

> "interleaving a given artist's paintings among the paintings by other artists—versus presenting that artist's paintings one after another (blocking)—enhanced participants' later ability to identify the artist responsible for each of a series of new paintings."
