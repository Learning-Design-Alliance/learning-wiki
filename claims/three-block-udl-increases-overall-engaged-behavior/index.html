Evidence¶

Katz 2013¶

Jennifer Katz. (2013). The Three Block Model of Universal Design for Learning (UDL): Engaging students in inclusive education. Canadian Journal of Education, 36(1). https://journals.sfu.ca/cje/index.php/cje

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Quasi-experimental control group pretest-posttest study in 10 Manitoba schools; 631 students Grades 1-12, 94 observed students. MANCOVA controlling for teacher years of experience and education gave partial η = .549 for overall engaged behavior; Table 3 shows treatment means rose from 41.54 to 54.52 per 60 minutes while control means fell from 50.18 to 43.64.

"Overall engaged behavior was significantly different between the treatment and control groups post intervention. Students in the treatment classes were significantly more engaged than students in control classes, with treatment group students’ scores increasing overall, and control group students’ scores decreasing."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • UDL treatment students were significantly more actively engaged while control students were significantly more passively engaged and spent more time not engaged — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Grade level moderated engagement effects: students in higher grades spent more time passively engaged, with secondary control students most disengaged — related
  • UDL implementation shifted instructional grouping: treatment classes spent significantly more time in small groups while control classes spent more time in whole class and independent structures — related
  • UDL treatment students interacted more frequently with peers and adults and spent less time not interacting — related
  • UDL instructional practices alone raised reported inclusivity and student autonomy but not classroom or school climate, with very small effect sizes on social variables — related
