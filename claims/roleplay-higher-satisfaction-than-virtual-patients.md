# Students report higher satisfaction with role-play than with linear virtual patients

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` The role-play (control) group reported significantly higher satisfaction than the virtual patient group (median 5.65 vs 5.06, p = .001, r = .35). [→ Pers Michał 2026](#pers-micha-2026)

## Evidence

### Pers Michał 2026

Pers Michał, Kupis Robert, Kononowicz Andrzej A., Cebula Grzegorz, Szopa Magdalena. (2026). Linear virtual patients versus student role-plays in teaching patient-centeredness: a mixed-method study. International Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.6a0d.902f

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Satisfaction questionnaire (adapted from Cook and colleagues, 7-point scale) completed by 106 students; role-play median 5.65 (IQR 5.00–6.09) vs virtual patients median 5.06 (IQR 4.24–5.62), "with a moderate effect size (r = .35)".

> "The between-group difference was supported by the Mann–Whitney U test (U = 1023.0, p = .001), with a moderate effect size (r = .35)."
