## Evidence

### Hayward Mark F 2014

Hayward Mark F, Curran Vernon, Curtis Bryan, Schulz Henry, Murphy Sean. (2014). Reliability of the Interprofessional Collaborator Assessment Rubric (ICAR) in Multi Source Feedback (MSF) with post-graduate medical residents. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0279-9

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r1`

Two-way repeated measures ANOVA across the 17 ICAR items in the MSF field test; the article reports "F = 2.79, p = .002, η 2 = .02" as a small effect. No standardised effect size beyond η2 was printed.

> "Analysis revealed there was a significant main effect on the means of the individual 17 items (F = 2.79, p = .002, η 2 = .02), indicating a small effect size accounting for 2% of the total variance."

### Hayward Mark F 2014 (2)

Hayward Mark F, Curran Vernon, Curtis Bryan, Schulz Henry, Murphy Sean. (2014). Reliability of the Interprofessional Collaborator Assessment Rubric (ICAR) in Multi Source Feedback (MSF) with post-graduate medical residents. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0279-9

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same two-way repeated measures ANOVA; the rater-group by item interaction was non-significant ("F = 0.807, p = .713, η 2 = .012"), indicating the groups did not differ in scoring patterns across items.

> "However, rater groups did not differ in their scores across items as indicated by a non-significant interaction effect (F = 0.807, p = .713, η 2 = .012)."
