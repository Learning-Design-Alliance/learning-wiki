# In Study 1, a greater seductive detail amount linearly worsened recall performance (b = −0.11, p = .030)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Study 1, the greater the amount of SD content, the worse the recall of the learning material, with each 1% more SDs worsening the recall score by 0.11% of the maximum achievable score. [→ Lukas Wesenberg 2026](#lukas-wesenberg-2026)

## Evidence

### Lukas Wesenberg 2026

Lukas Wesenberg, Sebastian Jansen, Felix Krieglstein, Felicia Meusel, Sascha Schneider, Georg Valtin, Günter Daniel Rey. (2026). The more, the worse? The influence of the seductive detail amount on learning. Instructional Science (2026) 54:29. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09781-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Online regression analysis (PROCESS Model 2) with 191 students randomly assigned to 0%, 25%, or 50% SD conditions with a 600-word solar-cell text; the overall recall model was significant, F(5, 185) = 8.41, p <.001, R² = 0.19, and "the greater the amount of SD content, the worse the recall".

> "In line with H5, the SD amount significantly affected learning performance as measured by recall, b = −0.11, p =.030. The greater the amount of SD content, the worse the recall of the learning material."
