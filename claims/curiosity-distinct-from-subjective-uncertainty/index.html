Metcalfe 2023¶

Metcalfe, J., Vuorre, M., Towner, E., & Eich, T. S. (2023). Curiosity: The effects of feedback and confidence on the desire to know. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, vol. 152, no. 2, pp. 464-482. https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0001284

Experiment 4 between-participants comparison (42 subjective-uncertainty, 56 curiosity participants) of the two rating types on the same items. Related Claims¶

  • Second-order metacognitive judgments of subjective uncertainty show a small high-confidence error/correct difference, smaller than curiosity ratings show — related
  • Without feedback, people are more curious about high-confidence errors than about equally high-confidence correct answers, replicated across experiments — related
  • Curiosity about trivia answers is a U-shaped function of confidence, supporting information-gap theory — related
  • Yes/no feedback that participants were correct nearly quenched curiosity for correct answers, whereas without feedback curiosity peaked at moderate-to-high confidence — related
  • Curiosity and confidence show an inverted U-shaped relationship, peaking at an intermediate confidence level — related
  • Yes/no feedback that one was wrong increased curiosity selectively for high-confidence errors, leaving curiosity about low-confidence errors largely unchanged — related
  • The more confident a learning partner's answers, the more competent the partner is perceived — related
  • Low and medium Knowledge Confidence heighten curiosity, contradicting the predicted inverted U-shaped relationship — related
  • Correctness Confidence linearly predicts curiosity, with higher confidence in a guess associated with greater curiosity — reports the opposite
  • Higher confidence reduced knowledge exploration after correct answers, and high-confidence errors showed no effect on exploration — related
