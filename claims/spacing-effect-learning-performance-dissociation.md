## Evidence

### Soderstrom 2015

Nicholas C. Soderstrom and Robert A. Bjork. (2015). Learning Versus Performance: An Integrative Review. Perspectives on Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691615569000

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review of Bloom and Shuell (1981), in which high school students studied French–English pairs for 30 consecutive minutes or 10 minutes on each of three consecutive days. The review reports equivalent immediate performance and superior 7-day recall for spaced study.

> "On an initial test that was administered immediately following each practice schedule—after the 30-min study session for the massed group and after the third 10-min study session for the spaced group—virtually identical short-term performance was observed. However, on a long-term retention test administered 7 days later, participants who had spaced their study recalled more pairs than participants who had massed their study (Bloom & Shuell, 1981)."

### Soderstrom 2015 (2)

Nicholas C. Soderstrom and Robert A. Bjork. (2015). Learning Versus Performance: An Integrative Review. Perspectives on Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691615569000

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Narrative review of Balota, Duchek, and Paullin (1989), a continuous cued-recall study with younger adults ages 18–25 and older adults ages 61–76 studying paired associates under massed or spaced schedules, tested after 2 or 20 intervening items.

> "both age groups exhibited a spacing-by-retention-interval interaction— that is, short-term retention (i.e., performance) favored the massed items, whereas long-term retention (i.e., learning) favored the spaced items (Balota, Duchek, & Paullin, 1989)."
