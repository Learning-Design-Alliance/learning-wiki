## Evidence

### Hecht 2022

Hecht, C. A., Dweck, C. S., Murphy, M. C., Kroeper, K. M., & Yeager, D. S. (2022). Efficiently exploring the causal role of contextual moderators in behavioral science. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America, 120(1). https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2216315120

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Preregistered replication (study 2) fielded via TESS on the NORC AmeriSpeak panel with 803 adolescents aged 13–17 (n = 411 growth mindset teacher condition, n = 392 fixed), a probability sample described as generalizing well to US adolescents. The reported "probability of difference in βs between conditions = 0.94" supports the replication.

> "A growth mindset framework predicted choice of the challenging assignment positively and more strongly in the growth mindset teacher condition relative to the fixed mindset teacher condition (probability of difference in βs between conditions = 0.94; Fig. 3 A and B)."

### Hecht 2022 (2)

Hecht, C. A., Dweck, C. S., Murphy, M. C., Kroeper, K. M., & Yeager, D. S. (2022). Efficiently exploring the causal role of contextual moderators in behavioral science. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America, 120(1). https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2216315120

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Subgroup analyses of the same preregistered nationally representative experiment tested growth mindset framework × condition × subgroup interactions; each subgroup comparison fell "< 0.70", below the article's 75% interpretability threshold, so no demographic heterogeneity was interpreted.

> "Surprisingly, we did not find meaningful heterogeneity in these results across participant gender, underrepresented racial/ethnic minority status, income, or parent education (probability of the growth mindset framework × condition × subgroup interaction < 0.70 for each subgroup comparison)."
