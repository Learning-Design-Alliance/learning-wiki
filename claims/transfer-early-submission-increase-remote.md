# Remote learning increased transfer students' early submission frequency, closing a gap that had disadvantaged them under face-to-face instruction.

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` During F2F instruction in SP20, transfer students had significantly lower early submission fractions than non-transfer students (p = 0.01), but this difference was not observed in FA20 remote instruction. [→ Zhongzhou Chen and Tom Zhang 2021](#zhongzhou-chen-and-tom-zhang-2021)
`q2 i?` Transfer students had significantly higher early submission frequencies during FA20 compared to SP20 (p = 0.02), with no significant difference for non-transfer students. [→ Zhongzhou Chen and Tom Zhang 2021 (2)](#zhongzhou-chen-and-tom-zhang-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Zhongzhou Chen and Tom Zhang 2021

Zhongzhou Chen and Tom Zhang. (2021). Analyzing the Heterogeneous Impact of Remote Learning on Students' Ability to Stay on Track During the Pandemic. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2108.00601](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2108.00601)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Mann-Whitney U comparison of early submission fractions between demographic groups (Table IV): transfer vs non-transfer not significant in FA20 (p = 0.93) but significant in SP20 first 41 modules (p = 0.01), suggesting remote instruction removed the gap.

> "During the F2F period of the SP20 semester, transfer students had signiﬁcantly lower fraction of early submissions compared to non-transfer students, yet the same diﬀerence is not observed between transfer and non-transfer students in FA20."

### Zhongzhou Chen and Tom Zhang 2021 (2)

Zhongzhou Chen and Tom Zhang. (2021). Analyzing the Heterogeneous Impact of Remote Learning on Students' Ability to Stay on Track During the Pandemic. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2108.00601](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2108.00601)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Within-group between-semester comparison of the first 41 modules (Table III, Early column): transfer students significant at p = 0.02, non-transfer students not significant (p = 0.68); Figure 6(b) shows the FA20 increase for transfer students. No effect size printed.

> "transfer students had signiﬁcantly higher early submission frequencies during FA20 compared to SP20 (Figure 6(b)), while no signiﬁcant diﬀerence is detected for non-transfer students."
