## Evidence

### Mocevic 2025

Mocevic, H., and B. Gniewosz. 2025. "Stereotype Threat and a Multiple Identity Intervention Among First‐ and Second‐Generation Students of Migrant Descent." Journal of Community Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1002/jcop.70065

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Moderation analysis (PROCESS Model 1) predicting test performance with stereotype activation as independent variable and migrant generation as moderator in the classroom quasi-experiment (N = 452), controlling gender, parental education, and domain importance. The "significant interaction between stereotype activation and second‐generation student status" supports Hypothesis 1; no effect size was printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "The analysis revealed a significant interaction between stereotype activation and second‐generation student status, b = –2.50, SE = 1.16, 90% CI [–4.42, –0.58], but not for first‐generation status, b = –2.60, SE = 1.79, 90% CI [–5.55, 0.35]."

### Mocevic 2025 (2)

Mocevic, H., and B. Gniewosz. 2025. "Stereotype Threat and a Multiple Identity Intervention Among First‐ and Second‐Generation Students of Migrant Descent." Journal of Community Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1002/jcop.70065

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Estimated marginal means from the same moderation analysis, adjusted for gender, parental education, and domain importance (Table 3). Means are descriptive; only the second-generation interaction reached significance.

> "Mean comparisons indicated that nonmigrant students performed slightly higher under stereotype activation (M = 25.73 vs. M = 26.77), whereas first‐generation students showed lower scores (M = 21.52 vs. M = 19.96), as did second‐generation students (M = 23.02 vs. M = 21.56)."
