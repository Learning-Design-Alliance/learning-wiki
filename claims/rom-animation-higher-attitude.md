# Scenario-based animation training produced higher post-test e-learning attitudes than routine skill video training, which alone did not change attitudes

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The animation group's post-test attitude scores exceeded the control group's (p < 0.01); the control group's pre-post attitude change was not significant (p = 0.687). [→ Özsaban Aysel 2026](#ozsaban-aysel-2026)

## Evidence

### Özsaban Aysel 2026

Özsaban Aysel, Bayram Aysun, Kılıç Çiğdem Torun, Calp Muhammed Hanefi, Öztürk Havva. (2026). The effect of scenario-based range of motion exercises animation on the knowledge, motivation, and attitude levels of nursing students: a randomized controlled study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08716-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Between- and within-group attitude comparisons in the randomized experiment (n = 104): the animation group's post-test attitude was higher than the skill-video group's (p < 0.01), and only the animation group showed a significant within-group attitude gain (p < 0.001); the control group's change was not significant (p = 0.687).

> "In contrast, the attitude scores of the students in the experimental group were higher in the post-test than in the control group (p < 0.01). The post-test attitude scores of the experimental group were statistically significantly higher than the pre-test (p < 0.001), and the post-test attitude scores of the control group were higher than pre-test scores, but the difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.687)"
