## Evidence

### Losiak Wladyslaw; Losiak-Pilch Julia 2020

Losiak Wladyslaw; Losiak-Pilch Julia. (2020). Cortisol Awakening Response, Self-Reported Affect and Exam Performance in Female Students. Applied Psychophysiology and Biofeedback. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10484-019-09449-9

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical regression of number of correct answers on exam-day CAR and affect measures (Table 3); CARauc entered first with "Beta -.446*", R2 = .19. The relationship was negative; the beta is a regression coefficient, so no standardised effect size is claimed.

> "Only one variable—the integrated volume of cortisol over the waking period (CARauc)—on the examination day was a significant predictor of exam performance. The relationship was negative: the higher the CARauc on the day of the exam, the lower the exam performance."

### Losiak Wladyslaw; Losiak-Pilch Julia 2020 (2)

Losiak Wladyslaw; Losiak-Pilch Julia. (2020). Cortisol Awakening Response, Self-Reported Affect and Exam Performance in Female Students. Applied Psychophysiology and Biofeedback. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10484-019-09449-9

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the same hierarchical regression, CARi and both affect scales added no significant explained variance; the article notes "the amount of explained variance was generally low". Equivalence was not tested.

> "Neither the increase of cortisol following waking (CARi) nor negative or positive self-reported affect on the day of the exam contributed to a significant increase of explained variance in the regression."
