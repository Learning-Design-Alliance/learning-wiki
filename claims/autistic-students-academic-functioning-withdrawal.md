## Evidence

### Gurbuz Emine 2018

Gurbuz Emine, Hanley Mary, Riby Deborah M. (2018). University Students with Autism: The Social and Academic Experiences of University in the UK. Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-018-3741-4

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same questionnaire comparison; the academic functioning component (adjustment, satisfaction, thoughts of withdrawing) was significantly lower in the autism group (p < 0.001). Item-level data show similar reported grades and academic skills across groups. No effect size printed.

> "Compared to the non-autistic students, those with autism had significantly lower scores both in social (p < 0.001) and academic components (p < 0.001)."

### Gurbuz Emine 2018 (2)

Gurbuz Emine, Hanley Mary, Riby Deborah M. (2018). University Students with Autism: The Social and Academic Experiences of University in the UK. Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-018-3741-4

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Authors' summary of the questionnaire findings: 56% of autistic students had considered withdrawing or taking a break, significantly more than non-autistic students (15.3% agreement in Table 3). No effect size printed.

> "A significant proportion of the autistic students reported that they had difficulty adjusting to their institution and 56% reported that they had considered withdrawing or taking a break from their studies, which was significantly more than reported by the non-autistic students."
