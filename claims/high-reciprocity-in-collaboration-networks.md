## Evidence

### Nathan Crossette 2023

Nathan Crossette, Lincoln D. Carr, and Bethany R. Wilcox. (2023). Correlations between student connectivity and academic performance: a pandemic follow-up. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010106](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010106)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Reciprocity analysis of the three measured course networks reported in the results section and Table III (Mines .990, CU fall .861, CU spring .848). The article states these values are "higher than typ- ically seen in social networks". No effect size is printed.

> "Using the method proposed by Squartini et. al for calculating reciprocity in weighted networks [18], we found that all networks were highly reciprocal."

### Nathan Crossette 2023 (2)

Nathan Crossette, Lincoln D. Carr, and Bethany R. Wilcox. (2023). Correlations between student connectivity and academic performance: a pandemic follow-up. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010106](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010106)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Simulation comparison using four random-network methods (DM, MH, MD, DD); the DM and MH networks showed low reciprocity despite the logical OR combination, so the article concludes the observed reciprocity is "a real signal that student collaborat ion tends to be highly reciprocal".

> "this ﬁnding indicates that the reciprocity seen in the real networks is n ot a consequence of combining student reports using a logical OR operation, but rather a real signal that student collaborat ion tends to be highly reciprocal"
