# African American identification moderates first-grade effects: TRIAD-FT significantly reduces the negative main effect for African American students (g = .31)

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` African American students scored significantly worse overall (g = .39), but African American students in TRIAD-FT scored significantly higher than African American students in control (g = .31). [→ Clements 2013](#clements-2013)

## Evidence

### Clements 2013

Clements, D. H., Sarama, J., Wolfe, C. B., & Spitler, M. E. (2013). Longitudinal Evaluation of a Scale-Up Model for Teaching Mathematics With Trajectories and Technologies: Persistence of Effects in the Third Year. American Educational Research Journal. https://doi.org/10.3102/0002831212469270

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Moderator analysis within the intent-to-treat HLM model (N = 1,079). The article also reports a significant negative main effect for African American (b = -.252, g = .39) and concludes random assignment to TRIAD-FT "significantly reduces the negative main effect."

> "Children identifying as African American within the TRIAD-FT condition scored significantly higher than children identifying as African American within the control condition (relative to children identifying with another ethnic group), yielding a moderate effect size (g = .31)."
