## Evidence

### Mahembe GR 2025

Mahembe GR, Mash R. (2025). The competency of nurses in basic life support at district hospitals in Cape Town, South Africa. S Afr Fam Pract. https://doi.org/10.4102/safp.v67i1.6056

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Inferential analysis of the composite competency score in the cross-sectional survey. The quote reports the training association (p < 0.001), the worse performance of never-trained nurses, and the certification effect (p = 0.001); no effect size is printed.

> "There was also a significant association between BLS training and BLS competency scores (p < 0.001). Post hoc analysis found that nurses who ‘never’ had any form of BLS training performed significantly worse. While having a BLS certification conferred a statistically significant better performance (p = 0.001)"

### Mahembe GR 2025 (2)

Mahembe GR, Mash R. (2025). The competency of nurses in basic life support at district hospitals in Cape Town, South Africa. S Afr Fam Pract. https://doi.org/10.4102/safp.v67i1.6056

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Spearman's rank correlation for years in practice and a paired samples t-test for CPR frequency against the competency score, in the same survey. Both tests were non-significant as printed; equivalence was not tested and no effect size is given, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "there was no significant relationship between performing CPR frequently (p = 0.083) or the number of years in practice (r = 0.025, p = 0.702)."
