## Evidence

### Van der Wath 2015

Van der Wath, A.E. & Du Toit, P.H., 2015, 'Learning end-of-life care within a constructivist model: Undergraduate nursing students' experiences', Curationis 38(2), Art. #1537. https://doi.org/10.4102/curationis.v38i2.1537

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Theme analysis of written reflections in the 'I clarified my values' theme; participants reported the "existential intelligence they obtained through the learning opportunities", including one student's realisation about fear of talking about death.

> "Participants shared the existential intelligence they obtained through the learning opportunities:'What I should do differently is to talk about death. I was afraid of talking about it previously.'"

### Van der Wath 2015 (2)

Van der Wath, A.E. & Du Toit, P.H., 2015, 'Learning end-of-life care within a constructivist model: Undergraduate nursing students' experiences', Curationis 38(2), Art. #1537. https://doi.org/10.4102/curationis.v38i2.1537

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Same 'I clarified my values' theme in the reflection analysis; participants reported gaining "interpersonal intelligence on how to appreciate and respect others' values with regards to death and dying", illustrated by commitments not to judge grieving people.

> "They also obtained interpersonal intelligence on how to appreciate and respect others' values with regards to death and dying"
