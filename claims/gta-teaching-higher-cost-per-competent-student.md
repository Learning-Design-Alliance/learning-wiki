## Evidence

### Janjua Aisha 2018

Janjua Aisha, Roberts Tracy, Okeahialam Nicola, Clark T Justin. (2018). Cost-effective analysis of teaching pelvic examination skills using Gynaecology Teaching Associates (GTAs) compared with manikin models (The CEAT Study). BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2017-015823

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Base-case cost-effectiveness analysis alongside the TARGET RCT, costs in 2013/2014 prices from a bottom-up resource-use approach. The results report ICERs of "£640.20 per competent student and £274.37 per student competent at merit level and £274.37 at distinction level".

> "The incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs) demonstrated that it costs an additional £640.20 per competent student and £274.37 per student competent at merit level and £274.37 at distinction level compared with conventional manikin-based teaching (table 4)."

### Janjua Aisha 2018 (2)

Janjua Aisha, Roberts Tracy, Okeahialam Nicola, Clark T Justin. (2018). Cost-effective analysis of teaching pelvic examination skills using Gynaecology Teaching Associates (GTAs) compared with manikin models (The CEAT Study). BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2017-015823

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

One-way deterministic sensitivity analyses varying room hire, interest rate, GTA session length, GTA pay (£20/hour) and inclusion of GTA training costs, plus a probabilistic sensitivity analysis with CEACs. The article reports GTA teaching "remained more expensive but more effective in all the scenarios considered".

> "As shown in table 5, GTA teaching remained more expensive but more effective in all the scenarios considered."
