## Subclaims
`q3 i1` When parts were performed concurrently in the whole task (fractionation), PTT showed a strong transfer cost (g = –0.35). [→ Wickens 2013](#wickens-2013)
`q3 i?` When parts were created from sequential elements (segmentation), PTT showed neither cost nor benefit on either measure. [→ Wickens 2013 (2)](#wickens-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Wickens 2013

Wickens, C. D., Hutchins, S., Carolan, T., & Cumming, J. (2013). Effectiveness of Part-Task Training and Increasing-Difficulty Training Strategies: A Meta-Analysis Approach. Human Factors, 55(2), 461-470. https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720812451994

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Concurrence moderator analysis within the PTT meta-analysis, coding how parts were created. Fractionation studies showed "a strong cost (TR = 0.71*, k† = 25; g = –0.35*, k† = 10)" against whole-task control; the printed effect size g = –0.35 is small.

> "However, fractionation, whereby the two parts were performed concurrently during the whole task, showed a strong cost (TR = 0.71*, k† = 25; g = –0.35*, k† = 10)."

### Wickens 2013 (2)

Wickens, C. D., Hutchins, S., Carolan, T., & Cumming, J. (2013). Effectiveness of Part-Task Training and Increasing-Difficulty Training Strategies: A Meta-Analysis Approach. Human Factors, 55(2), 461-470. https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720812451994

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Same concurrence moderator analysis: studies whose parts came from sequential task elements "showed neither cost nor benefit by either measure", localizing the overall PTT cost in fractionation. No effect size printed for this null.

> "Segmentation, whereby the parts were created out of sequential elements within the task, showed neither cost nor benefit by either measure."
