# Practitioner confidence, competence and perceived effectiveness rose from pre-training to post-training after face-to-face father engagement training and were maintained at 2-month follow-up in one group with no comparison group

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` After a 4.5-hour face-to-face training, practitioners' confidence, competence and perceived effectiveness in engaging fathers increased from pre- to post-training and were maintained at 2-month follow-up, with large effect sizes. [→ Burn M. 2019](#burn-m-2019)

## Evidence

### Burn M. 2019

Burn M., Tully L. A., Jiang Y., Piotrowska P. J., Collins D. A. J., Sargeant K., Hawes D., Moul C., Lenroot R. K., Frick P. J., Anderson V., Kimonis E. R., Dadds M. R. (2019). Evaluating Practitioner Training to Improve Competencies and Organizational Practices for Engaging Fathers in Parenting Interventions. Child Psychiatry and Human Development. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10578-018-0836-2

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Single-group repeated-measures evaluation of face-to-face training (Study 1, N = 233) with pre, post and 2-month follow-up assessments on the Father Engagement Questionnaire. Follow-up scores were "significantly higher than pre-training scores" on all three competency scales; effect sizes ranged from 0.37 to 0.57 in the longer term.

> "Planned comparisons revealed that follow-up scores were significantly higher than pre-training scores for competence (t = 0.65, p < 0.001), confidence (t = 0.67, p < 0.001) and perceived effectiveness (t = 0.55, p < 0.001), and there were no significant differences between post-training and follow-up scores."
