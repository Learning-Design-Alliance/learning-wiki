# Lecture modality (written, audio, video, live) shows no consistent effect on memory for lecture content or on the note-taking effect

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across reviewed experiments, no main effect of lecture modality on memory for lecture content was observed, and modality did not interact with note-taking. [→ Jansen 2017](#jansen-2017)

## Evidence

### Jansen 2017

Jansen, R. S., Lakens, D., & IJsselsteijn, W. (2017). An integrative review of the cognitive costs and benefits of note-taking. Educational Research Review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2017.10.001

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review synthesis of experiments by Peters (1972), Peper and Mayer (1978, study 2), and Schoen (2012), which manipulated written versus spoken/video lecture modality with and without note-taking. The review reports "no indication that lecture modality influences" memory, while cautioning that low statistical power warrants larger samples.

> "Overall, based on the available research, there is no indication that lecture modality influences the amount of lecture material students remember, nor is there any indication modality effects depend on whether students take notes or not."
