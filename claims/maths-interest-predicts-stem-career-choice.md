## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the joint model, maths interest significantly and independently predicted STEM career choice (OR = 1.75), and remained significant after adjusting for maths achievement and SES (OR = 1.65). [→ Ferdinand 2024](#ferdinand-2024)

## Evidence

### Ferdinand 2024

Ferdinand, R., Malanchini, M. & Rimfeld, K. (2024). Mathematics interest, self-efficacy, and anxiety predict STEM career choice in emerging adulthood. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00275-1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Binary logistic regression in the TEDS cohort jointly testing maths anxiety, self-efficacy and interest at ages 16–18 on STEM career choice at ~21. The article reports "maths interest (OR = 1.75" was a significant independent predictor; attenuated to OR = 1.65 after adjusting for achievement and SES.

> "maths anxiety (OR = 0.78, 95% CI [0.64, 0.96], p =0 . 0 2 ) and maths interest (OR = 1.75, 95% CI [1.44, 2.67],p < 0.001) signiﬁcantly and independently predicted STEM career choice"

### Ferdinand 2024 (2)

Ferdinand, R., Malanchini, M. & Rimfeld, K. (2024). Mathematics interest, self-efficacy, and anxiety predict STEM career choice in emerging adulthood. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00275-1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Sex-stratified logistic regressions. Maths interest predicted STEM career choice in every model in the female sample (ORs 1.63–1.77) and in the male sample the article reports ORs "ranging from 1.71 to 1.88".

> "In the female sample, maths interest signiﬁcantly predicted STEM career choice across all models (ORs ranging from 1.63 to 1.77)"
