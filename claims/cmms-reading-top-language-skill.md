## Evidence

### Javid 2014

Javid, C. Z. (2014). EMP Needs of Medical Undergraduates in a Saudi Context. Kashmir Journal of Language Research, 14(1), 89-110. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1410.6469](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1410.6469)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Structured semi-open-ended interviews with 34 second- and third-year students who had secured distinction in content-subject final examinations; question 3 responses were computed and analyzed with SPSS 10, showing "a majority of 58.8% ranked reading skills".

> "Third question captured samples' perception about the most important language skill in the context of CMMS and a majority of 58.8% ranked reading skills while 20.6% rated speaking skills as the most important skill for their academic needs."

### Javid 2014 (2)

Javid, C. Z. (2014). EMP Needs of Medical Undergraduates in a Saudi Context. Kashmir Journal of Language Research, 14(1), 89-110. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1410.6469](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1410.6469)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Faculty interviews, class observations, and student interviews, triangulated in the Discussion section, uniformly pointed to reading as the most important skill; the author notes the questionnaire alone ranked listening first, and that students may have "over-generalized the importance of different language skills".

> "Data generated by the three remaining tools offered uniform findings that reading was the most important skill to carry out their studies effectively at CMMS."
