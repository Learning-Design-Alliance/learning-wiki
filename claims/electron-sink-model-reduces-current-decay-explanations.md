# After interacting with the NIELS Electron Sink model, the percentage of 5th and 7th graders giving the incorrect 'current decays' (Type A) explanation dropped significantly

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the Electron Sink group, incorrect Type A responses fell to 10% (5th grade) and 15% (7th grade) at post-test, a statistically significant reduction (5th: t(19) = 4.49, p = 0.0001; 7th: t(19) = 2.65, p = 0.02). [→ Pratim Sengupta and Uri Wilensky 2018](#pratim-sengupta-and-uri-wilensky-2018)

## Evidence

### Pratim Sengupta and Uri Wilensky 2018

Pratim Sengupta and Uri Wilensky. (2018). Understanding Electric Current Using Agent-Based Models: Connecting the Micro-level with Flow Rate. In: Proceedings of the Eighth International Conference on Computer Supported Education (CSEDU 2016). Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1802.04165](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1802.04165)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre-/post-test written responses of Electron Sink group classrooms (20 students per section). The percentage of Type A "incorrect responses" stating current decays through the wire dropped significantly in both grades; only test statistics are printed, no effect size.

> "Paired sample T-tests revealed that the reduction in the percentage of these incorrect responses is statistically significant for both 5th grade (t(19) = 4.49, p = 0.0001) and 7th grade participants (t(19) = 2.65, p = 0.02)."
