# Children with EAL who underachieve in reception are more likely than comparable monolingual peers to reach Year 2 targets

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Among children performing below a good level of development in reception, EAL status was associated with significantly higher odds of performing on target in Year 2, with no significant interaction. [→ Whiteside 2017](#whiteside-2017)

## Evidence

### Whiteside 2017

Whiteside, Katie E.; Gooch, Debbie; Norbury, Courtenay F. (2017). English Language Proficiency and Early School Attainment Among Children Learning English as an Additional Language. Child Development, 88(3), 812-827. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.12615

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r3`

Hierarchical logistic regression on children below a good level of development in reception predicting on-target Year 2 performance; the unadjusted EAL odds ratio was 2.48 (95% CI 1.15–5.36, p = .021) and the adjusted odds ratio was 2.94 (95% CI 1.35–6.42, p = .007).

> "This indicates that children with EAL, who were academically underachieving in reception year, were more likely to go on and meet academic targets in Year 2 relative to monolingual peers with comparable language proficiency and academic attainment in reception year."
