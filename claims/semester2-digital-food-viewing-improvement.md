## Evidence

### Ho Dang Khanh Ngan 2022

Ho Dang Khanh Ngan, Lee Yu-Chieh, Chiu Wan-Chun, Shen Yi-Ta, Yao Chih-Yuan, Chu Hung-Kuo, Chu Wei-Ta, Le Nguyen Quoc Khanh, Nguyen Hung Trong, Su Hsiu-Yueh, Chang Jung-Su. (2022). COVID-19 and Virtual Nutrition: A Pilot Study of Integrating Digital Food Models for Interactive Portion Size Education. Nutrients 14(16):3313. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu14163313

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Second-semester exam (n = 65) using eleven Taiwanese meal sets, each presented as a pair of 2-D images and a 3-D model on the online platform. Table 3 prints overall identification 91.50%, quantification within ±10% 42.90%, under-estimation 35.00%, omission 6.0%.

> "Table 3 shows that overall accuracies of digital food viewing skills of food identification and portion size estimation improved to 91.5% and 42.9%, respectively."

### Ho Dang Khanh Ngan 2022 (2)

Ho Dang Khanh Ngan, Lee Yu-Chieh, Chiu Wan-Chun, Shen Yi-Ta, Yao Chih-Yuan, Chu Hung-Kuo, Chu Wei-Ta, Le Nguyen Quoc Khanh, Nguyen Hung Trong, Su Hsiu-Yueh, Chang Jung-Su. (2022). COVID-19 and Virtual Nutrition: A Pilot Study of Integrating Digital Food Models for Interactive Portion Size Education. Nutrients 14(16):3313. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu14163313

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Within-group over-time comparisons reported in Figure 4 for the second semester (n = 65). The article prints p values only; the discussion gives descriptive group accuracies of 58% (amorphous), 52% (fillings), 38% (condiments) and 6% (vegetables), untested as such.

> "At 9 months, repeated training in digital food viewing skills also revealed a greater improvement in the estimation accuracy of amorphous foods, fillings/stuffing, and condiments (all p < 0.0001), but not vegetables (B), although the estimated error was substantially reduced compared to the first semester (C) (p < 0.05) (Figure 4)."
