# Model type (embodied vs dialogue-only) showed no statistically significant effect on learning outcomes or perceived personality

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` No statistically significant effects of model type on LOES-S learning, quality, or engagement scores were observed, so hypothesis H1 was rejected. [→ Sonlu 2025](#sonlu-2025)
`q2 i?` The initial significant effect of model type on perceived neuroticism (F = 3.681, p = 0.027) did not remain significant after the Hommel procedure (adjusted p = 0.417). [→ Sonlu 2025](#sonlu-2025)

## Evidence

### Sonlu 2025

Sonlu, S., Bendiksen, B., Durupinar, F., & Gudukbay, U. (2025). The Effects of Embodiment and Personality Expression on Learning in LLM-based Educational Agents. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1109/vr59515.2025.00094](https://doi.org/10.1109/vr59515.2025.00094)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Discussion of the two-way ANOVA results (n = 210): "no statistically significant effects of model type on LOES-S scores were observed", meaning the presence of a visual representation did not impact learning outcomes, so H1 was rejected. Equivalence between models was not tested, only non-significance.

> "Regarding RQ2, we found no statistically significant effect of model type on perceived personality. Similarly, for RQ3, no statistically significant effects of model type on LOES-S scores were observed."

### Sonlu 2025 (2)

Sonlu, S., Bendiksen, B., Durupinar, F., & Gudukbay, U. (2025). The Effects of Embodiment and Personality Expression on Learning in LLM-based Educational Agents. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1109/vr59515.2025.00094](https://doi.org/10.1109/vr59515.2025.00094)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Results section, ANOVA reporting: the model-type effect on perceived neuroticism was "initially significant" but dropped to adjusted p = 0.417 after multiple-testing correction.

> "The main effect of the model type was initially significant for neuroticism, but the effect did not remain significant after the Hommel procedure ( p = 0.417)."
