# Making socioeconomic identity salient or presenting a test as diagnostic of intelligence leads low-SES students to perform significantly worse and report lower self-confidence than low-SES participants in non-threatening conditions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` When socioeconomic identity is made salient before a test, or the test is presented as diagnostic of intelligence, low-SES students perform significantly worse than low-SES participants in non-threatening conditions. [→ Spencer 2007](#spencer-2007)
`q3 i?` Under those threatening conditions low-SES students also report much lower self-confidence than low-SES participants in non-threatening conditions. [→ Spencer 2007](#spencer-2007)

## Evidence

### Spencer 2007

Spencer, B; Castano, E (2007). Social class is dead, long live social class! Stereotype threat among low-socioeconomic individuals. Social Justice Research. https://dx.doi.org/10.1007/s11211-007-0047-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Experimental study in which "Participants were placed in one of four conditions that varied in level of ''threat'' related to socioeconomic status." The article reports that threatened low-SES students "perform significantly worse, and report much lower self- confidence"; no effect size is printed.

> "Results show that when socioeconomic identity is made salient before taking a test, or when the test is presented as diagnostic of intelligence, low-SES students perform significantly worse, and report much lower self- confidence, than low-SES participants in the non-threatening conditions."
