# The teaching-associate advantage in head and skull examination skills persists at 4 months on a summative OSCE with a small-to-moderate effect size

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Four months after training, students taught by teaching associates remained significantly better than controls on the summative OSCE (rater 1 p = .009, rater 2 p = .015; Cohen's d = .396 and .421), though the effect size was smaller than at time point 1. [→ Hoefer 2017](#hoefer-2017)

## Evidence

### Hoefer 2017

Hoefer, S. H., Sterz, J., Bender, B., Stefanescu, M.-C., Theis, M., Walcher, F., Sader, R., Ruesseler, M. (2017). Conveying practical clinical skills with the help of teaching associates—a randomised trial with focus on the long term learning retention. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0892-5

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Summative surgical OSCE four months after the skills lab week; all rated students were scored by two blinded attending examiners. Printed effect sizes at time point 2 were d = .396 and .421, which the authors place within what they call the Zone of desired effects.

> "The effect size (Cohens d) for raters 1 and 2 at time point 1 was 1.422 and 1.201, whereas at time point 2 the effect size was .396 and .421, respectively."
