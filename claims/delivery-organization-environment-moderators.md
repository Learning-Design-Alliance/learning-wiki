# Individual treatment organization and delivery form significantly moderate scaffold effects; classroom settings and Web-based environments show the largest effects

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Individual treatment (ES = 0.496) significantly outperformed group treatment (ES = 0.116; Q = 7.079, df = 1, p = 0.008), and delivery form significantly moderated effects (Q = 10.43, df = 2, p = 0.005), with combined direct-and-indirect delivery highest (ES = 1.200). [→ Lanqin Zheng 2016](#lanqin-zheng-2016)
`q3 i1` Type of computer-based learning environment significantly moderated effects (Q = 19.21, df = 5, p = 0.002), with Web-based learning environments showing the greatest effect size (ES = 0.676). [→ Lanqin Zheng 2016 (2)](#lanqin-zheng-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Lanqin Zheng 2016

Lanqin Zheng. (2016). The effectiveness of self-regulated learning scaffolds on academic performance in computer-based learning environments: a meta-analysis. Asia Pacific Education Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12564-016-9426-9

`q3 · i1`

Moderator analysis of treatment organization: 24 studies implemented scaffolds individually (2127 subjects) versus five in groups (521 subjects); the difference was statistically significant. Delivery form also differed significantly (Q = 10.43, df = 2, p = 0.005).

> "a higher effect size was associ- ated with the individual treatment (ES = 0.496) and a lower effect size was associated with groups. Moreover, a signiﬁcant difference was found between the two types of organization type ( Q = 7.079, df = 1, p = 0.008)"

### Lanqin Zheng 2016 (2)

Lanqin Zheng. (2016). The effectiveness of self-regulated learning scaffolds on academic performance in computer-based learning environments: a meta-analysis. Asia Pacific Education Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12564-016-9426-9

`q3 · i1`

Moderator analysis across six types of computer-based environments in the 29 studies; Web-based environments (15 studies, 1580 subjects) had the largest effect (ES = 0.676), while mobile and inquiry learning environments had negative effect sizes. The between-group test was significant.

> "The greatest effect size was asso- ciated with the Web-based learning environment (ES = 0.676) (Table 14). This suggested that scaffolding in a Web-based learning environment is more effective than for other learning environments."
