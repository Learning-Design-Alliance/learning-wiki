## Evidence

### Fahlman M 2011

Fahlman M, McCaughtry N, Martin J, Shen B. (2011). School health educators' self-efficacy, intentions to teach, and implementation of nutrition education increases after in-service training. Am J Health Educ. 2011;42(3):181-190. https://doi.org/10.1080/19325037.2011.10599191

`q2 · quasi-experiment` · `i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental design (30 intervention, 29 control teachers); repeated-measures ANOVA on the 59-teacher sample for the intention-to-teach subscale; total intention rose from 18.2 to 33.2 in the intervention group while controls stayed near 17.6-17.9. The article reports a significant time main effect rather than a group-by-time interaction for this outcome (unlike self-efficacy and outcome expectations, where the interaction was significant), so the pre-post rise is not shown to differ from controls statistically.

> "Table 4 represents pre- and post-scores for intentions to teach nutrition content. There was a significant time main effect from pre-post (F = 7.14, P = 0.001) for the intervention teachers."

### Fahlman M 2011 (2)

Fahlman M, McCaughtry N, Martin J, Shen B. (2011). School health educators' self-efficacy, intentions to teach, and implementation of nutrition education increases after in-service training. Am J Health Educ. 2011;42(3):181-190. https://doi.org/10.1080/19325037.2011.10599191

`q2 · quasi-experiment` · `i?` · `causal · r2`

Item-level analyses showed significant pre-post intention gains on six measures (e.g., interpreting food labels P = 0.030; product claims P = 0.026), while intentions to teach the food pyramid (P = 0.123) and food groups (P = 0.218) showed no significant differences. The article gives no reason; its Table 4 shows pre-test means on those two items already near the top of the 5-point scale (4.0 and 4.3), against 1.5-3.4 on the other six.

> "Specifically, changes occurred in six out of the eight measures: interpreting food labels, the meaning of product claims, the influences on body image, factors that contribute to weight control, eating healthy in a fast food restaurant, and developing a healthy body image. There were no significant differences in their intentions to teach the food pyramid or food groups."
