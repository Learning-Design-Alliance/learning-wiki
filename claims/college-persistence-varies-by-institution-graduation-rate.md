# The review reports that equally qualified Chicago graduates' college persistence differed systematically depending on the institutional graduation rates of the colleges they attended

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among CPS graduates with comparable grades and test scores, college persistence differed depending on the institutional graduation rates of the colleges attended. [→ Nagaoka 2014](#nagaoka-2014)
`q2 i?` A CPS graduate with a 3.5 GPA had roughly a 20 percent to over 90 percent chance of earning a college degree depending on which of the 20 most-attended colleges she attended. [→ Nagaoka 2014 (2)](#nagaoka-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Nagaoka 2014

Nagaoka, J., Farrington, C. A., Ehrlich, S. B., with Johnson, D. W., Dickson, S., Heath, R., & Mayo, A. (2014). A Framework for Developing Young Adult Success in the 21st Century: White Paper — Defining Young Adult Success. University of Chicago Consortium on Chicago School Research Working Paper. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/framework-developing-young-adult-success-21st-century

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

The white paper attributes to ongoing University of Chicago analyses of successive CPS graduate cohorts that the "same sets of grades and test scores produced systematic differences in college persistence" by college institutional graduation rates. Reported second-hand; no effect size printed.

> "Their recent analyses of college outcomes among successive cohorts of Chicago Public School (CPS) graduates indicate that the same sets of grades and test scores produced systematic differences in college persistence depending on the institutional graduation rates of the colleges students attended (Roderick, Holsapple, Kelley-Kemple, Johnson, & Moeller, forthcoming)."

### Nagaoka 2014 (2)

Nagaoka, J., Farrington, C. A., Ehrlich, S. B., with Johnson, D. W., Dickson, S., Heath, R., & Mayo, A. (2014). A Framework for Developing Young Adult Success in the 21st Century: White Paper — Defining Young Adult Success. University of Chicago Consortium on Chicago School Research Working Paper. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/framework-developing-young-adult-success-21st-century

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r?`

The review attributes to Roderick, Nagaoka, Coca, & Moeller (2008) that a CPS graduate with a 3.5 GPA faced a roughly 20 percent to over 90 percent degree-attainment chance across the 20 most-attended colleges. Percentages are descriptive, as printed.

> "This confirmed their earlier findings that showed that a CPS graduate with a 3.5 GPA had between a roughly 20 percent chance of earning a college degree to an over 90 percent chance, depending on which college he or she attended among the 20 colleges most attended by CPS graduates (Roderick, Nagaoka, Coca, & Moeller, 2008)."
