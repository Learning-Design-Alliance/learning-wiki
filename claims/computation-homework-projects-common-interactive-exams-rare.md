## Evidence

### Marcos D. Caballero and Laura Merner 2018

Marcos D. Caballero and Laura Merner. (2018). On the Prevalence and Nature of Computational Instruction in Undergraduate Physics Programs across the United States. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020129](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020129)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey results on the nature of computational tasks (Figure 4): "slightly more than 50% (106 departments)" report computational homework among the 195 departments; the article reports 98 departments for computational projects at the same threshold.

> "From Fig. 4, we ﬁnd that out of the 195 departments with at least 50% of surveyed faculty reporting that computation is taught, slightly more than 50% (106 departments) report that some form of computational home-work is used."

### Marcos D. Caballero and Laura Merner 2018 (2)

Marcos D. Caballero and Laura Merner. (2018). On the Prevalence and Nature of Computational Instruction in Undergraduate Physics Programs across the United States. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020129](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020129)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey results on computational assessment (Figure 4): "Less than 25% departments have 50% or more of their faculty report assessing computation on exams", rising to 35% at the one-faculty threshold. The article separately reports fewer faculty using interactive engagement activities than homework or projects.

> "Less than 25% departments have 50% or more of their faculty report assessing computation on exams. This fraction increases to 35%, as the inset indicates, when considering the responses of a single faculty member."
