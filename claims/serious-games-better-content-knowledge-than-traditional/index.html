q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing Wouters et al. (2013) and Clark et al. (2016), that serious games outperform traditional settings on content knowledge, "especially when games were aligned with clear learning objectives and given adequate time." No effect sizes are printed in the review.

"Meta-analyses have found that students who learn with serious games often perform better on content knowledge assessments than those in traditional settings (Wouters et al., 2013; Clark et al., 2016)."

Related Claims¶

  • The review reports that simulation games support near transfer of knowledge but evidence for long-term or far transfer is less robust — related
  • The survey reports, citing Long and Aleven, that students who used DragonBox enjoyed the experience more, while students who used the Lynnette intelligent tutoring system performed significantly better on the test. — related
  • Extramural English gaming relates to better L2 proficiency, but effects vary by activity type and strategy — related
  • Quizizz-based gamification improved word memorization over traditional methods for intermediate learners — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • The FTS proposes that fading distinguishes expert from non-expert peers via a game-progress threshold, and peer-tutoring collaboration is expected to improve non-expert game progress and knowledge gained — related
  • Guided Play Improves Academic Outcomes — related
  • The review reports that participants in simulation-game conditions had significantly higher skill-based knowledge outcomes than those in non-game conditions — related
  • Immediate Elaborated Feedback in a formative assessment game significantly improved students' programming knowledge, interest, and problem-solving versus systems without immediate feedback — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Simulation games are effective as a supplement to other instruction but not as standalone instruction, where comparison groups learned slightly more (a difference not statistically significant) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Traditional assessments are commonly perceived to suffer from student motivation deficits that gameful assessments propose to address — related
  • Digital game-based learning supports long-term retention of second-language vocabulary — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
