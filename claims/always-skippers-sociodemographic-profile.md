## Evidence

### Sincovich A 2025

Sincovich A, Sechague Monroy N, Smithers LG, Brushe M, Boulton Z, Rozario T, Gregory T. (2025). Breakfast skipping and academic achievement at 8–16 years: a population study in South Australia. Public Health Nutr 28(1), e20. https://doi.org/10.1017/S1368980024002258

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive stratification (Table 1) of breakfast skipping categories by student, family and community characteristics in the 28,651-student sample. For example, mean sleep frequency was 2·5 for always skippers versus 5·0 for never skippers, and 47·8% of always skippers reported poor/fair health.

> "A greater percentage of students who always skip breakfast reported poorer overall health, higher levels of sadness and worries and less frequent good night’s sleep, compared to sometimes and never skippers. Further, always skippers tended to have less educated parents and live in more socioeconomically disadvantaged areas."
