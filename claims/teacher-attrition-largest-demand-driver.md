# High teacher attrition, near 8% annually, is the largest share of teacher demand, and halving it could virtually eliminate shortages

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Attrition of nearly 8% of the workforce annually accounts for the largest share of annual teacher demand, and reducing attrition by half could virtually eliminate shortages. [→ Sutcher 2016](#sutcher-2016)
`q2 i?` U.S. attrition hovers near 8% while high-achieving jurisdictions like Finland, Singapore, and Ontario lose only about 3% to 4% of teachers yearly. [→ Sutcher 2016 (2)](#sutcher-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Sutcher 2016

Sutcher, L., Darling-Hammond, L., & Carver-Thomas, D. (2016). A coming crisis in teaching? Teacher supply, demand, and shortages in the U.S. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/coming-crisis-teaching

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The report's demand modeling, decomposing demand into enrollment growth, pupil-teacher ratio changes, retirement, and preretirement attrition, found "attrition... responsible for the largest share of annual demand" and states that changing attrition would reduce projected shortages more than any other single factor.

> "High levels of attrition, estimated to be nearly 8% of the workforce annually, are responsible for the largest share of annual demand."

### Sutcher 2016 (2)

Sutcher, L., Darling-Hammond, L., & Carver-Thomas, D. (2016). A coming crisis in teaching? Teacher supply, demand, and shortages in the U.S. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/coming-crisis-teaching

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

International comparison reported in the report: U.S. attrition "hovering near 8% over the last decade" versus about 3% to 4% in Finland, Singapore, and Ontario; the report states matching those rates would eliminate overall U.S. teacher shortages.

> "Compared to high-achieving jurisdictions like Finland, Singapore, and Ontario, Canada—where only about 3% to 4% of teachers leave in a given year—U.S. attrition rates are quite high, hovering near 8% over the last decade"
