# UR trainees trained at PWIs had significantly higher first-semester Ph.D. GPAs and GRE scores than UR trainees from HBCUs, but undergraduate GPA did not differ

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` PWI-trained UR trainees had higher first-semester Ph.D. GPA mean rankings (22.22 vs 13.71) than HBCU-trained trainees, significant at p = 0.02. [→ Williams Tremaine B. 2021](#williams-tremaine-b-2021)
`q2 i?` Undergraduate GPA did not differ significantly between HBCU- and PWI-trained trainees (mean rankings 18.29 and 19.43; α level 0.77). [→ Williams Tremaine B. 2021](#williams-tremaine-b-2021)

## Evidence

### Williams Tremaine B. 2021

Williams Tremaine B., Prince Latrina Y., Allen Antiño R., Sterba Kristen M., Thomas Billy R., McGehee Robert E. (2021). Performance measures of racially underrepresented Ph.D. students in biomedical sciences: The UAMS IMSD Program Outcomes. PLoS ONE 16(2): e0246683. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0246683

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Mann-Whitney U test comparing 23 PWI-trained and 14 HBCU-trained trainees among 37 IMSD students. The article reports "The p-value of 0.02 reflected in Fig 1 is below the α-level of .05" for first-semester GPA; GRE verbal, quantitative, and writing differences were also significant, while undergraduate GPA was not.

> "UR trainees who were trained at PWIs had higher grade point averages during their first semester in the Ph.D. program than UR trainees from HBCUs. This difference is represented by the 22.22 and 13.71 mean rankings in Fig 1."
