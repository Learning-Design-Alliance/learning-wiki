## Evidence

### Michael D. Mrazek 2026

Michael D. Mrazek, Dustyn S. Ransom, Amy R. Texter, Calvin V. Tower, Bryce A. Rutila, Thomas F. Kennedy, Elijah J. Woodson, Jonathan W. Schooler, Alissa J. Mrazek. (2026). Theory-driven design and validation of a tool to enhance academic focus. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10730-y

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Study 1, a naturalistic single-group pre-post study across five public high schools (N = 322 pre-test), used hierarchical linear modeling of Focus Coach check-in data. The article reports that “students’ real-time focus tended to increase within a session” with b = 0.03, p < .001, against the typical vigilance decrement.

> "However, while using the tool, students’ real-time focus tended to increase within a session, with higher focus reported as the session progressed, b = 0.03, SE = 0.006, t (3985) = 4.42, p < .001 (Fig. 2)."

### Michael D. Mrazek 2026 (2)

Michael D. Mrazek, Dustyn S. Ransom, Amy R. Texter, Calvin V. Tower, Bryce A. Rutila, Thomas F. Kennedy, Elijah J. Woodson, Jonathan W. Schooler, Alissa J. Mrazek. (2026). Theory-driven design and validation of a tool to enhance academic focus. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10730-y

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Study 2, a pre-registered direct replication with new participants (N = 695 pre-test; 615 with check-in data), replicated the within-session effect with b = 0.03, p < .001. No effect size was printed for this slope, so impact is uncoded.

> "Students’ real-time focus increased within a session, b = 0.03, SE = 0.005, t(7716) = 6.52, p < .001 (Fig. 2)."
