2022/23 analysis comparing kindergarten risk identification at BOY, MOY, and EOY by type of early childhood program experience, using EC experience indicators defined in the report.

"more kindergarten students without EC experience were identified as significantly below benchmark within any time period and multiple times during the school year than were students with any EC experience."

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Analysis of K–3 students' risk identification by formal versus informal early childhood experience, including likelihood models controlling for race/ethnicity and program type reported in the appendices.

"For all students in grades K–3, students with formal EC experience were less often significantly below benchmark than were students with informal EC experience. Effects of formal EC experience were most pronounced among English learner students and among Black English learner students in particular."

Lemke 2024¶

Lemke, M., Murphy, D., Soo Ping Chow, A., & Acuña, A. (2024). Intersections: Student background and early literacy performance. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED663858

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Figure 2 of the 2022/23 analysis (females shown; males show the same patterns) shows formal EC experience reduces risk across ethnoracial groups; low income students showed about a 1 or 2 percentage point decrease. Formal EC experience was a statistically significant predictor for all grades K–3.

"The likelihood of Black English learner students being identified as significantly at risk is reduced by about 20 percentage points when they have attended formal EC programs (24 percent, compared to 44 percent, for females; 21 percent, compared to 40 percent, for males)."
