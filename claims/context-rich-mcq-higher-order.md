## Evidence

### Hift Richard J 2014

Hift Richard J. (2014). Should essays and other “open-ended”-type questions retain a place in written summative assessment in clinical medicine? BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0249-2

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article presents an illustrative context-rich multiple-choice item in internal medicine, arguing it demands analysis, application and evaluation and thereby tests "5 of Bloom’s 6 levels". This is the author's worked example, not an empirical study.

> "Such a question requires analysis (What is the underlying problem?), application (How do I apply what I know to the treatment of this patient?) and evaluation (Which of several possible treatments is the most appropriate?), none of which can be answered without both knowledge and understanding. Thus 5 of Bloom’s 6 levels have been tested."

### Hift Richard J 2014 (2)

Hift Richard J. (2014). Should essays and other “open-ended”-type questions retain a place in written summative assessment in clinical medicine? BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0249-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r1`

The review reports studies of the effect of stimulus on thought processes in open questions, showing the stimulus format is more important than the response format; correlations approach 100% for context-rich items on the same material. No effect sizes printed.

> "Scores on questions in open-ended format and multiple-choice format correlate highly (approaching 100%) for context-rich questions testing the same material. In contrast, low correlations are observed for different content using the same question format"
