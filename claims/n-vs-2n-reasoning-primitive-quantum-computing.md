# Students after lecture-based instruction commonly overgeneralize an N vs 2^N replacement reasoning primitive, incorrectly claiming 2^N qubits must be initialized and 2^N bits are output on a quantum computer

> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After traditional lecture-based instruction, students commonly reason that quantities associated with N for a classical computer should be replaced with 2^N for a quantum computer, e.g., that 2^N qubits must be initialized and 2^N bits of information are obtained as output. [→ Hu 2024](#hu-2024)
`q2 i?` This reasoning primitive led many students to incorrectly think there are only N distinctly different states available on a classical computer. [→ Hu 2024](#hu-2024)

## Evidence

### Hu 2024

Hu, P., Li, Y., & Singh, C. (2024). Investigating and improving student understanding of the basics of quantum computing. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020108](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020108)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Analysis of student responses to open-ended exam and pre-test questions after lecture-based instruction in undergraduate QM and QISE courses. The authors identify the replacement primitive as common and trace its origins to overgeneralization of ideas about superpositions of 2^N states and exponential quantum advantage.

> "one common response was that a major difference between an 𝑁-bit classical and 𝑁-qubit quantum computer is that various things that are 𝑁 for a classical computer should be replaced with 2𝑁 for a quantum computer"
