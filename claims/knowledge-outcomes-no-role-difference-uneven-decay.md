# Knowledge acquisition and retention generally did not differ significantly between observer and active participant roles, though decay patterns differed across studies

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r2`–`r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In 8 studies of immediate knowledge acquisition, 7 found no significant between-role difference; one found the active participant-observer group outperformed the observer-only group (P=0.001). [→ Amechghal A 2026](#amechghal-a-2026)
`q3 i?` Knowledge decay was not uniform across roles or time points in the 3 interpretable delayed follow-up studies. [→ Amechghal A 2026 (2)](#amechghal-a-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Amechghal A 2026

Amechghal A, Naciri A, Takhdat K, Loubbairi S, Mellouki A, Lahlou L, Nassik H. (2026). The impact of the learner role in healthcare simulation-based education on learning outcomes: a systematic review. J Educ Eval Health Prof. https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2026.23.24

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Review synthesis of 8 studies measuring knowledge immediately after simulation; the exception (Blanie et al. [17]) found the hybrid active participant-observer group outperformed observers only (P=0.001).

> "All 8 studies demonstrated significant within-role improvement; 7 studies did not identify statistically significant between-role differences."

### Amechghal A 2026 (2)

Amechghal A, Naciri A, Takhdat K, Loubbairi S, Mellouki A, Lahlou L, Nassik H. (2026). The impact of the learner role in healthcare simulation-based education on learning outcomes: a systematic review. J Educ Eval Health Prof. https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2026.23.24

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Review synthesis of delayed follow-up in 4 studies (one uninterpretable owing to insufficient power); Blanie et al. found a significant 3-month decline only in the active participant-observer group (P=0.001) with no between-group difference (P=0.277).

> "knowledge decay was not uniform across roles or time points: Johnson [23] reported a significant 4-week decline in both roles with no between-role difference (P=0.593); de Moraes et al. [19] identified a selective significant 14-day decline among active participants (P<0.05)."
