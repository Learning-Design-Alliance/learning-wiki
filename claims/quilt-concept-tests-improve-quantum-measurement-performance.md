# Students using research-based learning tools (QuILT and concept tests) performed significantly better on quantum measurement assessments than students in traditionally taught comparison classes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r3` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2016 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a comparison across two similar universities, the experimental group taught with concept tests and the QuILT outperformed the comparison group taught with traditional lectures and homework on a quantum measurement assessment (26% vs 91% class averages). [→ Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#guangtian-zhu-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)

## Evidence

### Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Improving Students Understanding of Quantum Measurement Part 2: Development of Research-based Learning Tools. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06926](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06926)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Preliminary evaluation in a junior-senior level quantum mechanics course: the comparison group with "traditional lectures in clas s and regular homework problems" averaged 26% (25 students), while the experimental group averaged 76% on the pre-test (31 students) and 91% on the post-test (29 students) after the QuILT. No statistical test or effect size is reported.

> "The comparison group students only had traditional lectures in clas s and regular homework problems from the textbook. Students in the comparison group took the test at the end of their ﬁrst semester quantum mechanics course when all the topics about quantum measurement had been taught. The class average (25 students) was 26% including both Test A and Test B."
