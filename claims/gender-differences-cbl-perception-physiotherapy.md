# Perception of individual CBL features did not differ significantly by gender, but female students' total perception score was significantly higher than males' (median 100.0 vs 96.5, P = 0.049)

`q3 i?` No significant gender-based differences were observed in perception levels for the individual CBL features (P > 0.05 on each item). [→ Elgheit Rehab E. Abo 2025](#elgheit-rehab-e-abo-2025)
`q3 i?` Total perception score was significantly higher among females (median 100.0) than males (median 96.5; Mann–Whitney Z = −1.966, P = 0.049). [→ Elgheit Rehab E. Abo 2025 (2)](#elgheit-rehab-e-abo-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Elgheit Rehab E. Abo 2025

Elgheit Rehab E. Abo, Nashat Nagwa. (2025). Bringing theory to life: integrating case-based learning in applied physiology for undergraduate physiotherapy education. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06725-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Fisher's exact tests on each of the 20 perception items among the 238 respondents found no significant gender differences on any individual feature; the article prints no effect size.

> "Although female students constituted the majority of the participants (72.3%) compared to males (27.7%), no significant gender-based differences were observed in perception levels regarding the various features of CBL, as indicated by each question (P > 0.05)."

### Elgheit Rehab E. Abo 2025 (2)

Elgheit Rehab E. Abo, Nashat Nagwa. (2025). Bringing theory to life: integrating case-based learning in applied physiology for undergraduate physiotherapy education. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06725-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Mann–Whitney U test of total perception score by gender (Table 4): female median 100.0 vs male 96.5, mean ranks 124.65 vs 106.08, Z = −1.966, P = 0.049. No effect size printed.

> "Table 4 indicates a significantly higher perception level among female participants compared to males (P < 0.05), with a median perception score of 100.0 for females compared to 96.5 for males."
