# Video-based feedback on real consultations is associated with a significant group-by-time increase in residents' self-perceived empathy on the Jefferson Scale

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3` · `i2` medium
> **Currency** · newest study 2020 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` First-year medical residents receiving VF of real consultations increased their Jefferson Scale of Physician Empathy total scores after the intervention while the control group's scores decreased, producing a significant time-by-group interaction with a moderate effect size. [→ Dohms M. C. 2020](#dohms-m-c-2020)

## Evidence

### Dohms M. C. 2020

Dohms M. C., Collares C. F., Tibério I. C. (2020). Video-based feedback using real consultations for a formative assessment in communication skills. BMC Medical Education 20:57. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-1955-6

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Repeated-measures ANOVA of 54 first-year primary care medical residents in a pre/post study with a control group, comparing intervention (VF) and control groups on the Jefferson Scale of Physician Empathy before and after the communication program. The Jefferson Time*Group interaction showed F = 5.494, p = 0.024, partial η² = 0.113 (Table 1), described as "a significant interaction with moderate effect size".

> "the intervention group had higher mean total scores after the intervention (from 80,26 to 83,63). As a result, a significant interaction with moderate effect size arose between group and time of application."
