# Relevancy of emphasized text directs attention and influences test performance

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` High relevant material emphasis leads to higher scores on high relevant questions. [→ Nist 1985](#nist-1985)
`q3 i2` Low relevant material emphasis leads to higher scores on low relevant questions. [→ Nist 1985 (2)](#nist-1985-2)

## Evidence

### Nist 1985

Nist, S. L., & Hogrebe, M. C. (1985). The Effects of High and Low Relevant Text Underlining on Test Performance. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED265520

`q3 · i2`

In a controlled experiment with 67 college freshmen, a weighted means ANOVA showed a significant main effect for relevancy, F (1, 49) = 13.56, 2 < .01, for the "high relevant score".

> "The main effect for relevancy was significant, F (1, 49) = 13.56, 2 < .01 when the dependent measure was the high relevant score."

### Nist 1985 (2)

Nist, S. L., & Hogrebe, M. C. (1985). The Effects of High and Low Relevant Text Underlining on Test Performance. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED265520

`q3 · i2`

In the same study, the main effect for relevancy was also significant for the "low relevant score", F (1, 40) = 6.98, 2 < .05.

> "For the low relevant score, F (1, 40) = 6.98, 2 < .05."
