Meta-analysis of 107 independent effect sizes comparing ITS to non-ITS instruction; effect sizes weighted by inverse variances and computed with Hedges's correction for small-sample bias. Related Claims¶

  • ITS significantly outperform large-group human instruction, individual computer-based instruction, and textbooks or workbooks, but not small-group or individual human tutoring — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Intelligent tutoring systems improve achievement over non-individualised instruction — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Bayesian network modeling ITS showed a higher weighted mean effect size than model tracing and constraint-based modeling, but only under a fixed-effect model — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • AI-enabled personalized STEM education yields a statistically significant overall effect of g = 0.455, which its authors class as small, compared with traditional non-AI instruction in K-12 schools — related
  • Intelligent tutoring systems show moderate learning benefits but are less effective than human tutoring — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Meta-analytic evidence indicates intelligent tutoring systems yield positive learning outcomes compared with non-ITS conditions — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • AI-supported instruction has a moderate positive effect on student achievement in STEM education (g = 0.670 under a random effects model) — related
  • Ma et al. (2014) meta-analysis: step-based ITSs outperformed no tutoring and large-group teacher-led instruction, but showed no differences against small-group or one-to-one human tutoring — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Nesbit et al. (2014) found a significant advantage of ITSs over teacher-led classroom instruction and non-ITS computer-based instruction in teaching programming — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • A meta-analysis found that Intelligent Tutoring Systems outperformed, in aggregate, the other modes of instruction they were compared against, though publication bias may partly explain this — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Mathematical modelling produces a moderate positive effect on high school and college students' mathematics achievement compared with traditional instruction — related
  • Adaptive web-based system treatment showed a small, statistically non-significant advantage over control on elementary algebra post-test scores (Hedges' g = .12) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
