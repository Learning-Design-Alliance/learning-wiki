## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Children who enjoyed school at age 6 scored on average 14.41 more GCSE points at age 16 in the fully adjusted model, equivalent to about two grades. [→ Tim T. Morris 2021](#tim-t-morris-2021)

## Evidence

### Tim T. Morris 2021

Tim T. Morris, Danny Dorling, Neil M. Davies and George Davey Smith. (2021). Associations between school enjoyment at age 6 and later educational achievement: evidence from a UK cohort study. npj Science of Learning 6:18. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00092-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear regression of age 16 GCSE capped points score on school enjoyment in the multiple imputation ALSPAC sample (n = 12,135). The unadjusted difference was "29.34 (95% CI: 19.4, 39.29) more points at GCSE"; adjustment for socioeconomic position and cognitive ability attenuated it to 14.41 (6.9, 21.93).

> "Children who enjoyed school at age six scored on average 29.34 (95% CI: 19.4, 39.29) more points at GCSE and those had mixed enjoyment scored on average 12.53 ( −2.42, 22.65) more points compared to children who did not enjoy school."
