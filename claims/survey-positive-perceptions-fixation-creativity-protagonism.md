# Students report that the active lab methodology supports content fixation, creativity and protagonism

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a Likert-scale survey of students who experienced the methodology, majorities totally or partially agreed that it favoured content fixation (40% partially, 60% totally), fostered creativity (20% partially, 80% totally) and stimulated the student's protagonism versus pre-determined-script methodologies (20% partially, 80% totally). [→ Suzane F. Pinto 2020](#suzane-f-pinto-2020)

## Evidence

### Suzane F. Pinto 2020

Suzane F. Pinto, Ronan S. Ferreira, Miguel M. Costa, and Agmael M. Silva. (2020). A proposal for an active methodology for physics labs. arXiv:2005.06532 [physics.ed-ph]. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2005.06532](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2005.06532)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Anonymous Likert-scale perception survey (1-5 attitude scale) given to a Classical Mechanics (Physics vol.1) class at the end of the methodology, with a second group surveyed the following semester to minimize grade-related social effects. The article reports "40% of the students answering that they agree par- tially, while 60% of them agree totally" for content fixation; items 2 and 3 showed 20% partially agree and 80% totally agree for creativity and protagonism.
