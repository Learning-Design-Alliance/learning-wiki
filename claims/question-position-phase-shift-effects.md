## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Placing the question at the beginning shortened fixation durations on situational text in the first phase, shortened fixation durations on numbers in the first phase, and lengthened fixation durations on situational text in the second phase. [→ Strohmaier 2025](#strohmaier-2025)

## Evidence

### Strohmaier 2025

Strohmaier, A. R., Schons, C., Knabbe, A., Vogel, M., Saado, K., & Obersteiner, A. (2025). The relation between text comprehension and computation in mathematical word-problem solving. Learning and Instruction, 101, 102235. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102235

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Linear mixed-model results from the eye-tracking experiment (n = 47); question-first reduced first-phase situational text (p = .037) and numbers (p < .001), and increased second-phase situational text (p < .001). Question position also showed a trend toward lower solution probability, OR = 0.71, p = .056. No standardized effect size printed.

> "Placing the question at the beginning of the problem led to a significantly shorter fixation duration on situational text during the first phase. In addition, fixation durations on numbers were significantly shorter in the first phase and significantly longer in the second solution phase when the question was placed at the beginning of the problem."
