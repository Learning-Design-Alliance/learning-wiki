# Contextualized heuristic instruction with metacognitive control yields dramatic problem-solving gains, and reciprocal teaching yields large reading gains with in-class transfer

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Schoenfeld's heuristic instruction produced dramatic gains in college students' mathematical problem solving, with gains directly depending on students' active use of the heuristics. [→ PERKINS 1989](#perkins-1989)
`q2 i?` Reciprocal Teaching produced dramatic reading comprehension gains, transfer to in-class reading in science and social studies, and long-term retention among poor readers. [→ PERKINS 1989 (2)](#perkins-1989-2)

## Evidence

### PERKINS 1989

D. N. Perkins and Gavriel Salomon. (1989). Are Cognitive Skills Context-Bound? Educational Researcher, 16-23. https://www.jstor.org/stable/1176259

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Schoenfeld's teaching experiments; protocol analysis showed students who used the heuristics performed better while those who did not failed to show gains. Success required teaching heuristics contextualized to the domain plus a general metacognitive control level.

> "Schoenfeld, in extensive work on teaching mathematical problem solving, has demonstrated that heuristic instruction can yield dramatic gains in college students' mathematical problem solving"

### PERKINS 1989 (2)

D. N. Perkins and Gavriel Salomon. (1989). Are Cognitive Skills Context-Bound? Educational Researcher, 16-23. https://www.jstor.org/stable/1176259

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review describes the Reciprocal Teaching intervention (modeling, guiding, group participation around questioning, clarifying, summarizing, predicting) and its reported gains; transfer across subject-matter reading is treated as within a 'tool domain.'

> "Palincsar and Brown's approach yielded dramatic gains in the students' reading comprehension, transfer to in-class reading in science and social studies, and long-term retention."
