# Self-assessed poor simulation performance is associated with more negative mood after the simulation, controlling for pre-simulation mood

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Doctors who self-rated more 'Avoids taking the lead' behaviours reported higher post-simulation tense arousal (r = .37***) and annoyance (r = .34**) and lower hedonic tone (r = -.52***) and energetic arousal (r = -.28**); effects remained significant in 4th-order partial correlations controlling all four pre-emotions. ## Evidence

### Ferguson E 2014

Ferguson E, Buttery A, Miles G, Tatalia C, Clarke DD, Lonsdale AJ, Baxendale B, Lawrence C. (2014). The Temporal Rating of Emergency Non-Technical skills (TRENT) index for self and others: psychometric properties and emotional responses. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0240-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Main-study correlational analysis of 90 F1 doctors' post-simulation mood against self-rated performance. Table 5 prints post-mood correlations including Hedonic Tone -.52*** with 'Avoids taking the lead' (partial -.48***) and Tense Arousal .37*** (partial .31**). The article reports this as the first evidence that simulator performance can influence mood after assessment.

> "To control for the effect of pre-emotions on the association between self-assessments of performance and post-emotions, 4th order partial correlations (controlling for all 4 pre-emotion levels) were calculated and are reported in Table 4 in parentheses. All effects remained significant: self-assessed performance in the simulation environment thus has a strong effect on emotions after leaving the simulation."
