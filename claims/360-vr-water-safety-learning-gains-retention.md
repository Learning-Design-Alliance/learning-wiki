## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Children aged 10–12 showed improved water-safety knowledge after a workshop and retained it at one and eight weeks, with no significant difference across the three instructional mediums. [→ Araiza-Alba 2020](#araiza-alba-2020)

## Evidence

### Araiza-Alba 2020

Araiza-Alba, P., Keane, T., Matthews, B., Simpson, K., Strugnell, G., Chen, W. S., & Kaufman, J. (2020). The potential of 360-degree Virtual Reality videos to teach water-safety skills to children. https://www.lsv.com.au/vr

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Two-way ANOVA on 115 randomly assigned children (360° VR n = 46, TV n = 28, Poster n = 41) found a significant effect of Test but not Condition, indicating "all students learned water safety and hazard identification and retained their knowledge during the subsequent test at one and eight weeks".

> "Results show the learning outcome was significantly different across the four tests conducted (F(3, 448) = 91.187, p <.001, Partial η2=.38), but not across different conditions (F(2, 448) = 2.102, p =.123, Partial η2=.009)."
