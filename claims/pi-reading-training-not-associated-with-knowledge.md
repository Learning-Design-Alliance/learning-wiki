## Evidence

### Kocaslan Toran Meryem 2026

Kocaslan Toran Meryem, Özgür Su, Aslan Fatma Eti, Toygar İsmail (2026). Knowledge of Pressure Ulcers Amongst Nurses and Associated Factors: A Cross‐Sectional Study. International Wound Journal. https://doi.org/10.1111/iwj.70888

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Non-significant Kruskal-Wallis comparisons (KW = 6.400 and KW = 7.380, per Table 2) in the 346-nurse survey. The article reports no significant association for either recency variable; no effect sizes are printed, so this is not evidence of no effect.

> "the time since the last reading of an article (p = 0.171) and the time since the last training on pressure injuries (p = 0.117) did not demonstrate a significant association with knowledge scores."

### Kocaslan Toran Meryem 2026 (2)

Kocaslan Toran Meryem, Özgür Su, Aslan Fatma Eti, Toygar İsmail (2026). Knowledge of Pressure Ulcers Amongst Nurses and Associated Factors: A Cross‐Sectional Study. International Wound Journal. https://doi.org/10.1111/iwj.70888

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same survey, separate Kruskal-Wallis test of time since last training (KW = 7.380). The printed medians show never-trained nurses at 44.50 versus 44.50 for training within the last year; no effect size is printed.

> "the time since the last reading of an article (p = 0.171) and the time since the last training on pressure injuries (p = 0.117) did not demonstrate a significant association with knowledge scores."

### Kocaslan Toran Meryem 2026 (3)

Kocaslan Toran Meryem, Özgür Su, Aslan Fatma Eti, Toygar İsmail (2026). Knowledge of Pressure Ulcers Amongst Nurses and Associated Factors: A Cross‐Sectional Study. International Wound Journal. https://doi.org/10.1111/iwj.70888

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Mann-Whitney U comparison of ever versus never having read a pressure-injury article (U = 14 549, p = 0.851) in the same survey. The article reports no significant difference; no effect size is printed.

> "the median knowledge scores on pressure injuries were found to be not significantly different according to gender (p = 0.803), education level (p = 0.390), current role (p = 0.900), years of nursing experience (p = 0.103), wound care certification status (p = 0.659) or previous reading of articles on pressure injuries (p = 0.851)."
