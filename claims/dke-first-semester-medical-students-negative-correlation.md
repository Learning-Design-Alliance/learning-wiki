# First-semester medical students show a pronounced Dunning-Kruger effect: the correlation between self-assessment ability and actual oral anatomy exam performance is strongly negative (ρ = -0.590)

## Evidence

### Knof Harald 2024

Knof Harald, Berndt Markus, Shiozawa Thomas. (2024). Prevalence of Dunning-Kruger effect in first semester medical students: a correlational study of self-assessment and actual academic performance. BMC Medical Education. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11515314/

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Spearman correlational analysis of N = 426 first-semester medical students' self-assessed versus actual oral anatomy exam scores, collected before results were announced across three semester cohorts. The study reports "The correlation was ρ = -0.590 (p < 0.001)" overall, with gender-separated coefficients nearly identical. No effect size beyond the correlation is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "The correlation was ρ = -0.590 (p < 0.001); separated by gender for females ρ = -0.591 (p < 0.001) and for males ρ = -0.580 (p < 0.001), indicating that the higher the overestimation, the lower the exam performance and the higher the underestimation the higher the actual exam performance"
