# Readers with a study goal generate more forward/predictive inferences during reading than readers with an entertainment goal

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` College students instructed to read expository text for study produced more predictive inferences than students reading for entertainment. [→ P. van den Broek 2001](#p-van-den-broek-2001)

## Evidence

### P. van den Broek 2001

P. van den Broek, R. F. Lorch, Jr., T. Linderholm, & M. Gustafson. (2001). The effects of readers' goals on inference generation and memory for texts. Memory & Cognition, 29(8), 1081-1087. https://link.springer.com/journal/13414

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same think-aloud experiment (71 analyzed undergraduates, four expository texts, study vs. entertainment goal). A separate ANOVA for the predictive category confirmed "more predictive inferences were made in the study condition than in the entertainment condition," reliable against both participant and item variability.

> "Second,more predictive inferences were made in the study condition than in the entertainment condition [ F1(1,55) = 6.80, MSe = .0080; F2(1,135) = 21.87, MSe = .0199]."
