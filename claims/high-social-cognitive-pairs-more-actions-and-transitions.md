`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The study's headline comparison, stated in the article's report of results, contrasting pairs with at least one high social and high cognitive member against the other pairs on network measures (numbers of nodes, density, degree centralization). The authors "interpreted" this as "more productive CPS than the other pairs."

> "The results showed that pairs having at least one member with high social and high cognitive CPS skills conducted more actions and demonstrated a higher average frequency of action transitions with a higher tendency to conduct actions for different number of times, implying that they attempted more paths to solve the problem than the other pairs."
