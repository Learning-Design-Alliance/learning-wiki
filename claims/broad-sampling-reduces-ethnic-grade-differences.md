## Evidence

### van Andel C E E 2019

van Andel C E E, Born M P, Themmen A P N, Stegers‐Jager K M. (2019). Broadly sampled assessment reduces ethnicity‐related differences in clinical grades. Medical Education, 53(3), 264–275. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.13790

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Retrospective cohort logistic regression in 1667 medical students (818 global, 849 broadly sampled cohorts) predicting good clinical evaluation. The article reports the significant ethnicity × assessment interaction: "were smaller in the broadly sampled assessment than in the global assessment".

> "Furthermore, the interaction between students’ ethnicity and type of assessment was significant (Wald χ 2 (4) = 15.77, p< 0.01). The score differences between Surinamese/Antillean students and native Dutch students (Wald χ 2 (1) = 11.37, p< 0.01) and between Asian students and native Dutch students (Wald χ 2 (1) = 4.18, p< 0.05) were smaller in the broadly sampled assessment than in the global assessment."
