## Evidence

### Camp 2010

Camp, P. J. (2010). U-shaped Development of Newtonian Concepts: Implications for Pedagogical Design and Research Practice. Spelman College. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1007.1647](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1007.1647)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pattern-of-incorrect-answers analysis of second law items at RB, where about 2/3 of incorrect responses used velocity as an indicator of acceleration. The author writes that "students respond as if they correctly associate force with acceleration, but are unable to correctly infer acceleration in situations where it is not provided to them explicitly".

### Camp 2010 (2)

Camp, P. J. (2010). U-shaped Development of Newtonian Concepts: Implications for Pedagogical Design and Research Practice. Spelman College. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1007.1647](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1007.1647)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cross-cluster comparison of decline timing between acceleration and second law clusters, which the author reads as evidence that "the acceleration cluster showed a performance decline before second law, which decline in turn persisted through the decline in second law".

> "This, in turn, is consistent with the fact that the acceleration cluster showed a performance decline before second law, which decline in turn persisted through the decline in second law."
