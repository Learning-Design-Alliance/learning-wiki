# In the ECLS-K: 2010 dataset, a single latent EF factor model fits EF–math associations better than a specific-components model at all three time points

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across fall kindergarten, spring kindergarten, and spring first grade, the latent EF model (Model 1) was preferred over the component-specific model (Model 2) by BIC and fit the data exceptionally well. [→ Nguyen 2019](#nguyen-2019)

## Evidence

### Nguyen 2019

Nguyen, T., Duncan, R.J., & Bailey, D.H. (2019). Theoretical and methodological implications of associations between executive function and mathematics in early childhood. Contemporary Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cedpsych.2019.04.002

`q3 · i?`

Secondary analysis of the ECLS-K: 2010, a nationally representative sample of 18,170 kindergartners, comparing BIC fit of a latent EF factor model against a saturated component-specific model at three waves. The article reports "CFI/TLI=1.00, RMSEA=0–0.02, and SRMR=0–0.01" for Model 1.

> "For the three time points in the ECLS-K dataset, Model 1 was preferred each time and fit the data exceptionally well: CFI/TLI=1.00, RMSEA=0–0.02, and SRMR=0–0.01."
