Evidence¶

Negar Kamali 2026¶

Negar Kamali, Candice Rockell Gerstner, Jessica Hullman, Matthew Groh. (2026). Generative AI Literacy Training Improves Intelligence Analysts' Discrimination of Real and AI-Generated Images. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28510

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Stimulus-level analysis plotting image-ranked changes in accuracy from pre- to post-training against a null model's expected band, following Simonsohn et al.'s visualization approach. The article reports positive changes appear throughout the ranked distribution rather than in isolated outliers.

"We find that the training effect is distributed across images and is not concentrated in a narrow subset, which indicates that performance gains do not hinge on a few highly responsive stimuli."

Negar Kamali 2026 (2)¶

Negar Kamali, Candice Rockell Gerstner, Jessica Hullman, Matthew Groh. (2026). Generative AI Literacy Training Improves Intelligence Analysts' Discrimination of Real and AI-Generated Images. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28510

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Stimulus-level tally reported in the Discussion, cross-referencing the ranked-delta plots. The article reports 65 of 97 images gained while 32 declined, with the aggregate effect strongly positive but heterogeneous across stimuli.

"Of the 97 stimulus images, 65 (67%) showed post-training accuracy gains and 32 (33%) showed declines (Fig. 3). Of the 65 gain images, 47 fell above the null expectation under a uniform user-level effect, indicating gains larger than sampling variability alone would produce."
