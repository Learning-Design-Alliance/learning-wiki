# Student explanations of the radian requirement fell into seven categories, with the incorrect Units Equivalence reasoning most common (23.9%) and correct Derivative Distinction least common among substantive categories (6.4%)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Open-ended explanations (Q5) were blindly coded into seven categories by three raters (Cohen's Kappa 0.56–0.76); Units Equivalence was the most common explanation at 23.9%, and Derivative Distinction the least common at 6.4%. [→ Yossi Ben-Zion 2025](#yossi-ben-zion-2025)
`q3 i2` Confidence differed significantly across reasoning categories (F(2,766) = 21.70, p < .001, η2 = 0.054), with meaningful and misguided reasoning both showing higher confidence than meaningless reasoning. [→ Yossi Ben-Zion 2025 (2)](#yossi-ben-zion-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Yossi Ben-Zion 2025

Yossi Ben-Zion, Jianqiang Liu, Chun-Ming Wang, Atanu Rajak, and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2025). Global Blind Spot in Understanding Trigonometric Derivatives: A Multinational Analysis. [doi:10.1103/t2fv-2spd](https://doi.org/10.1103/t2fv-2spd)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Qualitative coding of Q5 open-ended responses from the survey, conducted blindly by three coders with majority-rule decisions. The article reports Units Equivalence at 23.9% (184), Dimensional Analysis 17% (131), and Derivative Distinction 6.4% (49).

> "The most common explanation, given by 23.9% of participants (184), was the Units Equivalence category, which claims that both methods can be used and emphasizes the possibility of converting between them."

### Yossi Ben-Zion 2025 (2)

Yossi Ben-Zion, Jianqiang Liu, Chun-Ming Wang, Atanu Rajak, and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2025). Global Blind Spot in Understanding Trigonometric Derivatives: A Multinational Analysis. [doi:10.1103/t2fv-2spd](https://doi.org/10.1103/t2fv-2spd)

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

ANOVA of confidence across the three main reasoning groupings (meaningful, misguided, meaningless). The article reports F(2, 766) = 21.70, p < .001, η2 = 0.054; means were 4.04 (meaningful), 3.94 (misguided), 3.48 (meaningless), with no significant meaningful-versus-misguided difference (p = .95).

> "An analysis of confidence levels across reasoning categories revealed a significant effect (F (2, 766) = 21.70, p < .001, η2 = 0.054)"
