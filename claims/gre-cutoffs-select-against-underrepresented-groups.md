## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Roughly 25% of physics PhD programs publicize a minimum GRE-P score around 700, and above that threshold the representation of Hispanics, Blacks, and women drops sharply relative to their share of test takers. [→ Casey W. Miller 2019](#casey-w-miller-2019)

## Evidence

### Casey W. Miller 2019

Casey W. Miller, Benjamin M. Zwickl, Julie R. Posselt, Rachel T. Silvestrini, Theodore Hodapp. (2019). Typical Physics PhD Admissions Criteria Limit Access to Underrepresented Groups but Fail to Predict Doctoral Completion. Draft preprint; journal version in Science Advances. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1126/sciadv.aat7550](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aat7550)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Descriptive analysis of ETS test-taker data (test years 2009-2015) showing demographic composition shifts above a 700 GRE-P cutoff used by about 25% of programs. Percentages are descriptive; no effect size is printed.

> "Hispanics and Blacks are 6.2% and 1.8% of test takers, but only 4.1% and 0.6% of those whose scores exceed 700; Asians are 7.8% of test takers, but their above-700 representation is 11.4%; the representation of Whites is unchanged at 78%; women are 20% percent of test takers, but only 11% of those scoring above 700."
