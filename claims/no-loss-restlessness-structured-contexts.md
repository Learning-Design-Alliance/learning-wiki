# Loss-restlessness is not observed even in structured environments, though restlessness is generally higher against exploiting opponents

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Restlessness showed a main effect of outcome (ηp² = 0.275; lower after losses) and of opponent (higher during exploiting, 35.62%, than exploitable, 33.00%) but no interaction, so loss-restlessness was not in evidence. ## Evidence

### Benjamin James Dyson and Ahad Asad 2021

Benjamin James Dyson and Ahad Asad. (2021). A micro-genesis account of longer-form reinforcement learning in structured and unstructured environments. npj Science of Learning 6:19. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00098-4

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Mixed ANOVA on shift-shift behaviour across Experiments 5–10 (n = 157 retained); restlessness was again significantly less likely following losses relative to wins and draws (ηp² = 0.275), replicating the reversed pattern seen against unexploitable opponents.

> "a signi ﬁcant main effect of outcome: F (2,310) = 58.67, MSE = 0.002, p < 0.001, ƞp 2 = 0.275, but no interaction: F(2,310) = 2.42, MSE = 0.002, p = 0.091, ƞp 2 = 0.015. Here, restlessness was greater during exploiting opponents (35.62%) relative to exploitable opponents (33.00%)"
