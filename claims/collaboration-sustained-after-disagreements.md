## Evidence

### Zhou 2025

Zhou, J., Hmelo-Silver, C. E., Ryan, Z., Stiso, C., Murphy, D., Danish, J., Chinn, C. A., & Duncan, R. G. (2025). Disagreeing softly: Supporting students in managing disagreement in peer critique. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-024-09438-z

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Episode-by-episode review of subsequent turns after disagreements in the gallery walk corpus. The authors note this links to Chiu's (2008a) caution that rude disagreements can kill collaboration, but their data show sustained collaboration after both disagreement types.

> "Our analysis of each disagreement episode and their subsequent turns revealed that collaboration consistently sustained following both direct and soft disagreements across all episodes."

### Zhou 2025 (2)

Zhou, J., Hmelo-Silver, C. E., Ryan, Z., Stiso, C., Murphy, D., Danish, J., Chinn, C. A., & Duncan, R. G. (2025). Disagreeing softly: Supporting students in managing disagreement in peer critique. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-024-09438-z

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Observational finding from the full disagreement corpus. The authors attribute the climate partly to social norms emphasized through whole-class instruction, such as respecting peers' contributions and showing appreciation of each other's work.

> "Notably, we did not observe any occurrences of rude behaviors such as insults or shouting during disagreements, highlighting a positive and polite atmosphere within the groups."
