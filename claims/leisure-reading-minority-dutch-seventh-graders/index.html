Evidence¶

Mol SE and Jolles J 2014¶

Mol SE and Jolles J (2014) Reading enjoyment amongst non-leisure readers can affect achievement in secondary school. Front. Psychol. 5:1214. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01214

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Survey of 1,071 Dutch seventh graders (548 boys, 523 girls) who selected activities from a list of ten; students checking reading were categorized as leisure readers. The article reports "a minority of only 19.5% of students in the lower and 32.5% in the higher educational track."

"First, we found that a minority of only 19.5% of students in the lower and 32.5% in the higher educational track indicated that they read during their leisure time."

Related Claims¶

  • Leisure readers are far more likely than non-leisure readers to enjoy reading and to engage in mental imagery, in both educational tracks — related
  • Boys read for leisure and enjoy reading less than girls, but no sex differences appear in mental imagery — related
  • A majority of non-leisure readers report enjoying reading in general (59% lower track, 69% higher track) — related
  • In the lower track, reading enjoyment relates to school achievement for girls but not for boys among non-leisure readers — related
  • In the higher track, reading enjoyment and sex show main effects on non-leisure readers' school achievement, with enjoyers of both sexes scoring higher — related
  • Mental imagery shows no direct relation to school achievement among non-leisure readers, though a ceiling effect limits this null finding — related
