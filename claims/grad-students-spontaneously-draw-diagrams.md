# Most physics graduate students spontaneously chose the diagrammatic approach, and graduate students outperformed introductory students on the quiz problem

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` 76% of first-year physics graduate students elected to draw a diagram and ignored the provided equations, and graduate students using the diagrammatic approach scored better than introductory students using the same approach (p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 1.07). ## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh and Alexandru Maries 2023

Chandralekha Singh and Alexandru Maries. (2023). Helping students become proficient problem solvers Part II: An example from waves. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2304.06188](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2304.06188)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

The quiz problem was given to 26 first-year graduate students in a TA training course with equations provided. Pairwise t-tests showed graduate students outperformed introductory students on the diagrammatic approach (p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 1.07) and overall (p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.574).

> "we found that 76% of them elected to draw a diagram to solve the problem (and ignored the equations provided to them completely), thus confirming our hypothesis that experts are more likely to follow the diagrammatic approach to solve this problem"
