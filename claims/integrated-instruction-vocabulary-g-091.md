## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Students receiving integrated literacy and content-area instruction showed significant improvement in vocabulary, with an overall effect of g = 0.91 across 36 outcome measures. [→ HyeJin Hwang 2021](#hyejin-hwang-2021)

## Evidence

### HyeJin Hwang 2021

HyeJin Hwang, Sonia Q. Cabell & Rachel E. Joyner (2021). Effects of Integrated Literacy and Content-area Instruction on Vocabulary and Comprehension in the Elementary Years: A Meta-analysis. Scientific Studies of Reading. https://doi.org/10.1080/10888438.2021.1954005

`q3 · i3`

Random-effects meta-analysis (Robumeta, robust variance estimation) of 35 (quasi)experimental studies involving 13,289 students in K-5. The pooled vocabulary effect was "g = 0.91" with CI [.34, 1.45], significant at p < .01; heterogeneity was high (I2 = 91.82).

> "Students who received integrated literacy and content-area instruction showed improvement in vocabulary (the number of the outcome measures [n] = 36, g = 0.91, 95% confidence interval [CI] = [.34, 1.45]). The effect size was significant (p < .01) and large based on Kraft's (2020) benchmarks"
