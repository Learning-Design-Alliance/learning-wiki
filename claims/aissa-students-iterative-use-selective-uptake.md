# Interviewed students adopted an iterative upload–review–revise workflow and perceived the AI feedback as fair and highly useful, though some disregarded recommendations conflicting with their design intent

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Alvaro Becerra 2026

Alvaro Becerra, Diego Gomez, Ruth Cobos. (2026). AISSA: Implementation and Deployment of an AI-based Student Slides Analysis tool for Academic Presentations. LASI Spain 26: Learning Analytics Summer Institute Spain 2026. https://orcid.org/0009-0003-7793-2682

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Qualitative semi-structured interviews with a purposefully sampled subset of 20 students, sampled for gender parity and varying performance and interaction levels, using personalized inquiries based on activity logs. Students "perceived the AI-generated feedback as fair and highly useful", praising precision on font size, visual consistency, and bibliography formatting.

> "the interviews revealed that participants found the platform highly intuitive, adopting an iterative workflow of uploading drafts, refining work, and resubmitting without requiring external technical support."

### Alvaro Becerra 2026 (2)

Alvaro Becerra, Diego Gomez, Ruth Cobos. (2026). AISSA: Implementation and Deployment of an AI-based Student Slides Analysis tool for Academic Presentations. LASI Spain 26: Learning Analytics Summer Institute Spain 2026. https://orcid.org/0009-0003-7793-2682

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

From the same interview study, the article reports selective uptake of feedback: some students ignored recommendations that conflicted with their own design or presentation intentions. The authors flag this selective uptake as a target for future analysis.

> "several students reported intentionally disregarding some recommendations when they considered that those suggestions conflicted with their planned visual design or presentation strategy."
