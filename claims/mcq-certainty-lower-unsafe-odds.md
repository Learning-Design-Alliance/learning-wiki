## Evidence

### Tweed M.J. 2017

Tweed M.J., Stein S., Wilkinson T.J., Purdie G., Smith J. (2017). Certainty and safe consequence responses provide additional information from multiple choice question assessments. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0942-z

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Mixed-effects logistic regression across all 6600 responses (correct and incorrect) found "Students were less likely to give an unsafe response with moderate or high certainty than low certainty" (OR 0.71 and 0.65 vs low certainty).
