# Retained students showed smaller three-year achievement gains than previously socially promoted students, though Rasch adjustment erased the gap for once-retained third graders

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After Rasch equating, retained third graders gained about the same as social promotes (1.6 logits each; 1.3 for retained twice), but retained sixth graders, especially double-retained, still showed smaller gains. [→ Roderick 2000](#roderick-2000)

## Evidence

### Roderick 2000

Roderick, M., Nagaoka, J., Bacon, J., & Easton, J. Q. (2000). Update: Ending Social Promotion: Passing, Retention, and Achievement Trends Among Promoted and Retained Students 1995-1999. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://www.consortium-chicago.org

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Rasch-equated three-year growth analysis (Figures 12 and 14) adjusting for test-level effects. The brief states it "cannot yet conclude that retention is having significant negative effects on achievement, but in neither comparison are retained students doing better."

> "socially promoted students in 1995 gained 1.6 logits, compared to 1.6 for third graders retained once and 1.3 for third graders retained twice."
