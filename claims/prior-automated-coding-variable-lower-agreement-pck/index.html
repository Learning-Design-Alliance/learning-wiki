The review reports the printed kappa ranges; the reviewed studies' designs are not described here beyond what the article states.

Related Claims¶

  • The multi-agent LLM GradeOpt achieved substantial agreement with human coders on overall PCK items (κ = .68, QWK = .79) and outperformed RoBERTa, SBERT, and naïve GPT-4o prompting — related
  • LLM agreement with human examiners is limited in fast mode and improves in reasoning or thinking modes — related
  • Comparison models (RoBERTa, SBERT, naïve GPT-4o prompting) failed to reach acceptable agreement on PCK items, with Cohen's κ at or below .55 — related
  • All automated models, including GradeOpt, were most challenged by knowledge of teaching mathematics items (GradeOpt κ = .59) and least challenged by knowledge of students' mathematical thinking (κ = .79) — related
  • On CK items, GradeOpt showed near-perfect alignment with human codes (κ = .88 and .85; QWK = .89 and .94), comparable to human–human agreement — related
  • Human-LLM agreement remains low across constructs (κ = 0.000 to 0.419), with self-efficacy showing the best alignment and Prior KSAs none — related
  • Perceptions of feedback characteristics vary between researcher and teacher coders, with agreement shaped by professional background — related
  • Cohen's kappa exhibits a paradox in which high observed agreement yields low kappa, making it unsuitable for pass/fail decision consistency — related
  • Studies of teachers' SRL knowledge use heterogeneous, often implicit definitions, limiting comparability — related
  • Human–GPT coding agreement varied sharply by code, with near-chance or negative Kappa for co-ordination codes despite near-perfect agreement on others — related
