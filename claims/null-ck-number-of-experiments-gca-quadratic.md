# Content knowledge does not significantly predict the number of experiments conducted; general cognitive ability shows a quadratic association and self-concept a negative linear association

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Neither the linear nor quadratic component of content knowledge significantly predicted the number of experiments, contradicting hypothesis H2.1. [→ Gerdesmann 2026](#gerdesmann-2026)
`q3 i?` General cognitive ability showed a significant quadratic association with number of experiments (β = −1.10, p = .038), reversing about half an SD above the mean; self-concept showed a negative linear effect (β = −1.40, p = .011). [→ Gerdesmann 2026 (2)](#gerdesmann-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Gerdesmann 2026

Gerdesmann, D., Schwichow, M., & Koerber, S. (2026). Factors that explain simulation-based inquiry performance in physics learning. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09806-2

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

A second-order polynomial regression on the number of experiments performed, excluding two outlier participants with more than 60 experiments, tested hypothesis H2.1. "neither the linear component (β = 0.23, p =.760) nor the quadratic component (β = −0.79, p =.105) of content knowledge was significant".

> "neither the linear component (β = 0.23, p =.760) nor the quadratic component (β = −0.79, p =.105) of content knowledge was significant"

### Gerdesmann 2026 (2)

Gerdesmann, D., Schwichow, M., & Koerber, S. (2026). Factors that explain simulation-based inquiry performance in physics learning. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09806-2

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the same polynomial regression, "the quadratic component of general cognitive ability (β = −1.10, p =.038) was significant", reversing at about half a standard deviation above the mean; self-concept showed a negative linear effect (β = −1.40, p = .011).

> "the quadratic component of general cognitive ability (β = −1.10, p =.038) was significant. Participants with lower general cognitive ability conducted fewer experiments, while those with higher ability initially conducted more."
