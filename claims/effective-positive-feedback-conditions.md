`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Conditions for effective positive feedback stated in the Supportive section of this review, drawing on cited work. The review lists five conditions, including that feedback be "perceived by the learner as honest" and that "success is attributed to effort and strategy rather than innate ability". No effect sizes are printed.

> "Positive feedback is considered to be most effective when: (i) it is perceived by the learner as honest, (ii) success is attributed to effort and strategy rather than innate ability, (iii) it reinforces improvement and learning rather than social comparison, (iv) is delivered privately rather than publicly (where possible), and (v) the criteria needed to gain positive feedback are specific and achievable, and are made explicit to learners beforehand [79]"
