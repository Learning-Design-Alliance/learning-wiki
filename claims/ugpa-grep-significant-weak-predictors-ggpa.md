# UGPA and GRE-P are weak but statistically significant predictors of graduate GPA, while GRE-Q and GRE-V are not, in a multiple regression on 1,955 US physics PhD students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a multiple linear regression predicting graduate GPA, UGPA (β = 0.24) and GRE-P (β = 0.15) are significant predictors while GRE-Q and GRE-V are not. [→ Mike Verostek 2021](#mike-verostek-2021)

## Evidence

### Mike Verostek 2021

Mike Verostek, Casey Miller and Benjamin Zwickl. (2021). Analyzing admissions metrics as predictors of graduate GPA and whether graduate GPA mediates PhD completion. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020115](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020115)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple linear regression on 1,955 US students modeling graduate GPA from UGPA, GRE-P, GRE-Q, GRE-V, gender and race; adjusted R-squared 0.11. The article reports "Signiﬁcant predictive eﬀects" for UGPA and GRE-P; GRE-Q and GRE-V coefficients were not statistically significant.

> "Signiﬁcant predictive eﬀects at the 95% threshold were found for the numerical metrics UGPA (β = 0.24, t = 8.88, p < .01) and GRE-P ( β = 0.15, t = 5.02, p < .01)."
