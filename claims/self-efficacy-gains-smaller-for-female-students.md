## Evidence

### Jared P. Canright and Suzanne White Brahmia 2024

Jared P. Canright and Suzanne White Brahmia (2024). Modeling novel physics in virtual reality labs: An affective analysis of student learning. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010146](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010146)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Gender-split Wilcoxon analysis of paired PhIS self-efficacy responses in the 100-level population, divided into male-identifying and female-identifying students. Positive shifts occur for all items for both genders, but "female-identifying students consistently started lower on each item and saw a smaller increase."

> "While there are positive shifts for all items for both genders, female-identifying students consistently started lower on each item and saw a smaller increase."

### Jared P. Canright and Suzanne White Brahmia 2024 (2)

Jared P. Canright and Suzanne White Brahmia (2024). Modeling novel physics in virtual reality labs: An affective analysis of student learning. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010146](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010146)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Significance tests for the female-identifying subgroup on the team-contribution and data-defense items. The increases on the last two items are "notably small"; the PhIS4 shift "barely meets the traditional significance threshold p <0.05, and the shift for PhIS5 does not."

> "Female-identifying students' increases on the last two items are notably small; the shift in responses to PhIS4 barely meets the traditional significance threshold p <0.05, and the shift for PhIS5 does not."
