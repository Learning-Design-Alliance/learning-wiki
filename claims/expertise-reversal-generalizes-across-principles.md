## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The isolated-interactive elements principle helps novices but has no or negative effects for experts, while the variability and imagination principles help experts but reduce novices' outcomes. [→ Rey 2011](#rey-2011)

## Evidence

### Rey 2011

Günter Daniel Rey, Florian Buchwald. (2011). The Expertise Reversal Effect: Cognitive Load and Motivational Explanations. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0022243

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Narrative synthesis in the Expertise Reversal Effect section, citing prior experiments rather than new data. The article adds that the variability principle and the imagination principle "can increase the learning performance of experts, but can reduce the learning outcome of novices concurrently."

> "the isolated- interactive elements principle (i.e., initially presenting individual elements of information sequentially rather than in a fully inter- active form, e.g., Blayney, Kalyuga, & Sweller, 2010) has positive learning effects for novices, but either no effect or a negative effect, on learning performance for experts"
