## Evidence

### Varano 2023

Varano, S., and Zanella, A. (2023). Design and evaluation of a multi-sensory representation of scientific data. Frontiers. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.3389/feduc.2023.1082249](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2023.1082249)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Qualitative questionnaire and debrief evaluation of the first multi-sensory prototype (plywood tablet with bolts connected via a Makey Makey board) with the 27-user sample. "The stimuli were easily identified during the autonomous exploration" and the code was clear after declaration.

> "The feedback collected from questionnaires showed that the stimuli were easily identified during the autonomous exploration and that the representation was clearly understandable after the code declaration."

### Varano 2023 (2)

Varano, S., and Zanella, A. (2023). Design and evaluation of a multi-sensory representation of scientific data. Frontiers. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.3389/feduc.2023.1082249](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2023.1082249)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Same preliminary multi-sensory phase: the authors adopted white noise for sources without distance data because users recognized it as "the lack of information about the represented feature", a choice carried into the final exhibit.

> "We adopted white noise to represent the unknown distances, as this is generally recognizable as the lack of information about the represented feature, as confirmed by our experiment."
