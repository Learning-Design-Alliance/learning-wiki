# For standardized mean changes, rubric effects on performance decrease with mean age, yet secondary-education studies show larger effects than primary and middle school — contradictory moderator results

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the standardized-mean-change meta-analysis, each one-unit increase in mean age predicted a 0.062-unit decrease in effect, based on only 14 effect sizes from two studies. [→ Panadero 2023](#panadero-2023)

## Evidence

### Panadero 2023

Panadero, Ernesto; Jonsson, Anders; Pinedo, Leire; Fernández-Castilla, Belén. (2023). Effects of Rubrics on Academic Performance, Self-Regulated Learning, and self-Efficacy: a Meta-analytic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09823-4

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-regression within the standardized-mean-change analysis (12 studies). The article cautions the result is "based on only 14 effect sizes from two studies, and there were only two different values for the mean age."

> "The mean age of the participants significantly predicted the variability of the standardized mean changes: for a one unit increase in the average age, there was a 0.062 unit decrease in the standardized mean changes"

### Panadero 2023 (2)

Panadero, Ernesto; Jonsson, Anders; Pinedo, Leire; Fernández-Castilla, Belén. (2023). Effects of Rubrics on Academic Performance, Self-Regulated Learning, and self-Efficacy: a Meta-analytic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09823-4

`q3 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Moderator analysis of standardized mean changes by educational level; secondary education also exceeded middle school (g = 0.14, difference = 0.85, p < 0.001). The printed category effects (g = 0.99, g = 0.03, g = 0.14) are the article's only effect-size values for this contrast.

> "the overall effect for studies from secondary education was higher (g  = 0.99, number of effects = 10) than the overall effect of studies from primary education (g = 0.03, number of effects = 10, difference = 0.96, p = 0.004)"
