## Evidence

### Owens Melinda T. 2017

Owens Melinda T., Tanner Kimberly D. (2017). Teaching as Brain Changing: Exploring Connections between Neuroscience and Innovative Teaching. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-01-0005

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative synthesis of stress-neuroscience studies in the article's section on factors affecting plasticity. The article reports a nonmonotonic pattern: "mild elevations in cortisol can boost performance on memory tests" while high levels inhibit encoding and retrieval in both animals and humans. No effect sizes are printed.

> "While mild elevations in cortisol can boost performance on memory tests, high levels inhibit both the encoding and the retrieval of memory in both animals and humans (Lupien and McEwen, 1997; de Kloet et al., 1999)."

### Owens Melinda T. 2017 (2)

Owens Melinda T., Tanner Kimberly D. (2017). Teaching as Brain Changing: Exploring Connections between Neuroscience and Innovative Teaching. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-01-0005

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review of chronic-stress studies cited by the article, reporting that "chronic stress can impair learning and memory" and is associated with shrinkage of certain brain structures in humans; the article states this shrinkage is reversible. No effect sizes are printed.

> "Many different studies using all sorts of stressors have found that chronic stress can impair learning and memory and is even associated with the shrinkage of certain brain structures in humans (Conrad, 2010)."
