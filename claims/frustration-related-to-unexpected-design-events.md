# Frustration during the design process was related to unexpected events, with more unexpected events yielding greater frustration and worse perceived performance

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The case study indicates frustration is related to unexpected events: a larger number of unexpected events affecting the design process and proposed solution yielded greater frustration, and designers perceived their performance as worse when frustrated. [→ Christopher Nikulin 2019](#christopher-nikulin-2019)

## Evidence

### Christopher Nikulin 2019

Christopher Nikulin, Gabriela Lopez, Eduardo Piñonez, Luis Gonzalez, Pia Zapata. (2019). NASA-TLX for predictability and measurability of instructional design models: case study in design methods. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-019-09657-4

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Interpretive analysis combining NASA-TLX data with tape recordings in the case study; the authors state that "a larger number of unexpected events affecting the design process and proposed solution yields greater frustration", and that frustrated designers perceived worse performance.

> "A larger number of unexpected events affecting the design process and proposed solution yields greater frustration. For example, when frustration emerged during the decision process, the designers perceived that their performance was worse."
