## Evidence

### Mittal Monica 2019

Mittal Monica, Supramaniam Prasanna Raj, Lim Lee Nai, Hamoda Haitham, Savvas Mike, Narvekar Nitish. (2019). Is the clinician an independent variable in embryo transfer outcomes under standardized direct and indirect supervision? A 5-year observational cohort study. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001215](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001215)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational comparison of catheter selection across first-50 and last-50 transfers in the cohort database. In Group A, experienced clinicians used soft catheters in 53.3% of transfers versus 36.0% for trainees (p<0.05), with trainee soft-catheter use rising over time.

> "an experienced clinician was more likely to opt for a soft catheter than a trainee (p<0.05), with an increasing level of confidence seen over time in trainees with a greater proportion of the transfers performed by them using a soft catheter in the last 50 ET procedures undertaken."

### Mittal Monica 2019 (2)

Mittal Monica, Supramaniam Prasanna Raj, Lim Lee Nai, Hamoda Haitham, Savvas Mike, Narvekar Nitish. (2019). Is the clinician an independent variable in embryo transfer outcomes under standardized direct and indirect supervision? A 5-year observational cohort study. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001215](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001215)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Within-cohort comparison of pregnancy rates by catheter type in this study's own data, which the authors note showed "varying results to previous studies", since a meta-analysis by Abou-Setta et al. (2005) found soft catheters yielded significantly higher pregnancy rates.

> "No significant difference was found in pregnancy success rates for transfers performed using a soft catheter or a firm catheter with a stylet: first 50 ET procedures, p=0.38 (soft, 44.8%; firm, 39.8%); last 50 ETs, p=0.66 (soft, 43.2%; firm, 38.4%)."
