# Information allegedly from a practitioner elicits moderately higher topic-specific multiplism than information from experts or scientists

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Study 2's experiment, student teachers held moderately higher multiplistic beliefs about content allegedly from a practitioner than the same content allegedly from an expert or a scientist, whose conditions differed negligibly. [→ Merk 2018](#merk-2018)

## Evidence

### Merk 2018

Merk, S., Rosman, T., Muis, K. R., Kelava, A., & Bohl, T. (2018). Topic specific epistemic beliefs: Extending the Theory of Integrated Domains in Personal Epistemology. Learning and Instruction, 56, 84–97. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2018.04.008

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Split-plot experiment in which 365 student teachers read four content-invariant texts on educational topics allegedly authored by a practitioner, an expert, or a scientist (source manipulated between persons); multi-group multilevel CFA latent mean contrasts showed "moderately higher multiplistic beliefs" for the practitioner condition, with latent mean differences of −0.314 and −0.336 reported as parameter estimates rather than standardized effect sizes.

> "Parameter estimates (MExpert = −0.314, p = .042; MScientiﬁc Study = −0.336, p = .023) indicate that student teachers have moderately higher multiplistic beliefs regarding information given by practitioners in contrast to information given by experts or scientists"
