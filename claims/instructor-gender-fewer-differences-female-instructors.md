## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Significant gender differences on the first calculus-based mechanics exam were observed under both male instructors (d = 0.229) and female instructors (d = 0.243), and fewer significant differences were seen for female instructors overall. [→ Matthew Dew 2021](#matthew-dew-2021)

## Evidence

### Matthew Dew 2021

Matthew Dew, Jonathan Perry, Lewis Ford, William Bassichis, and Tatiana Erukhimova. (2021). Gendered Performance Diﬀerences in Introductory Physics: A Study from a Large Land-Grant University. arXiv:2009.09536. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010106](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010106)

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Comparison by instructor gender in calculus-based mechanics: 11 male instructors (N=4,227) and 3 female instructors (N=1,222). Other significant differences on Exams 3 and 4 were noted for male instructors only, with weak effect sizes.

> "Signiﬁcant diﬀerences in student performance based on gender were observed for instructors of both genders on the ﬁrst midterm exam, with a small eﬀect size for male (d = 0.229) and female (d = 0.243) instructors."
