## Evidence

### Henry 2025

Henry, L. N., Gross, R. A., & Hinshaw, S. P. (2025). Mathematics Achievement in Women with and without ADHD: Childhood Predictors and Developmental Trajectories into Adulthood. Journal of Learning Disabilities, 58(6). https://doi.org/10.1177/00222194241301044

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Table 1 reports percentages meeting three math-difficulty criteria (1, 1.5, and 2 SD below the national mean) at each wave in the ADHD and comparison samples across the four waves.

> "At Wave 1, 1–15% of girls with ADHD exhibited childhood math difficulties, which increased to 22–48% at Wave 4. In the comparison sample, 0–7% of girls had childhood math difficulties at Wave 1, which increased to 4–15% at Wave 4."

### Henry 2025 (2)

Henry, L. N., Gross, R. A., & Hinshaw, S. P. (2025). Mathematics Achievement in Women with and without ADHD: Childhood Predictors and Developmental Trajectories into Adulthood. Journal of Learning Disabilities, 58(6). https://doi.org/10.1177/00222194241301044

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Incidence analysis among girls without Wave-1 difficulties who were retained through Wave 4. Comparison girls showed "4–14% revealed math difficulties by Wave 4" over the same criteria (1SDN = 11/81, 1.5SDN = 8/85, 2SDN = 3/85).

> "of the girls with ADHD without math difficulties at Wave 1 who were retained through Wave 4, 22–39% revealed math difficulties by Wave 4 (39/99 girls with the 1 SD criterion; 37/110 with 1.5 SD criterion; 25/116 with 2 SD criterion)"
