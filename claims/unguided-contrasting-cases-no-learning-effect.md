# Contrasting cases without explicit guidance on their use do not improve conceptual understanding or solution-attempt quality

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Contrasting cases in the problem-solving material did not significantly affect conceptual understanding posttest scores or the quality of solution attempts. [→ Loibl 2020](#loibl-2020)

## Evidence

### Loibl 2020

Loibl, K., Tillema, M., Rummel, N., & van Gog, T. (2020). The effect of contrasting cases during problem solving prior to and after instruction. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-020-09504-7

`q3 · i?`

ANCOVA on quality of the best solution attempt in the 2×2 experiment (N = 181). Contrasting cases showed no significant effect on quality, and the conceptual posttest contrast PScc-I vs. PS-I was "not significant (p = 0.60)".

> "In contrast to Hypothesis 3c, the effect of contrasting cases, F(1, 176) = 0.53, p = 0.47, η2 < 0.01, and the interaction effect, F(1, 176) = 2.21, p = 0.14, η2 = 0.01, were not significant"
