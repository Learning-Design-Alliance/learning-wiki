# A fixed raw or Hake gain corresponds to different proficiency progressions depending on students' initial pre-test score

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For a fixed learning increase Δθ, the Hake gain is an increasing function of pre-test score, so a gain of 0.34 corresponds to Δθ = 2 at S = 8%, Δθ = 1 at S = 30% and Δθ = 0.5 at S = 80%. [→ Jean-François Parmentier and Brahim Lamine 2015](#jean-francois-parmentier-and-brahim-lamine-2015)
`q2 i?` The raw gain and the Hake gain are not interval scales and should not be used to compare progression of students with different pre-test scores. [→ Jean-François Parmentier and Brahim Lamine 2015](#jean-francois-parmentier-and-brahim-lamine-2015)

## Evidence

### Jean-François Parmentier and Brahim Lamine 2015

Jean-François Parmentier and Brahim Lamine. (2015). Interpreting gains and losses in conceptual test using Item Response Theory. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1509.03878](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1509.03878)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Numerical illustration using a hypothetical logistic true-score test (Fig. 7); the article concludes "the Hake gain should not be used to compare student's progression when they have diﬀerent pre-test score, even in test of type C".

> "a fixed value of the gain – for instance 0.34 – correspond to a strong learning for low pre-test score (∆θ = 2 for S=8%), a medium learning for medium pre-test score (∆ θ = 1 for S=30%) and a low learning for high pre-test score (∆ θ = 0.5 for S = 80%)."
