Evidence¶

Moores 2011¶

Moores, E., Cassim, R. and Talcott, J.B. (2011). Adults with dyslexia exhibit large effects of crowding, increased dependence on cues, and detrimental effects of distractors in visual search tasks. Frontiers in Psychology. doi:10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2011.10.005

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Correlation analyses (n=31, approximate critical r=0.35) relating summary crowding measures to psychometric scores. Both literacy measures correlated significantly with crowding, while the crowding-IQ association was not statistically significant, suggesting a direct rather than IQ-mediated relationship.

"As can be seen in Table 2, both spelling and reading ability increased significantly as the influence of crowding decreased. In contrast, the association between crowding and IQ was not statistically significant, suggesting that the effects of crowding impact on reading ability directly rather than through a third variable such as general cognitive ability."

Moores 2011 (2)¶

Moores, E., Cassim, R. and Talcott, J.B. (2011). Adults with dyslexia exhibit large effects of crowding, increased dependence on cues, and detrimental effects of distractors in visual search tasks. Frontiers in Psychology. doi:10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2011.10.005

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Correlation analyses of pre-cue, post-cue and set size summary measures with literacy and IQ scores. Pre-cue use and set size effects correlated significantly with spelling, reading and IQ; post-cue use showed no significant associations with any variable.

"The increased use of pre-cues and the influence of set size were both significantly associated with decreases in spelling scores, reading scores and IQ. The use of post-cues was not associated with reading, spelling or IQ variables."
