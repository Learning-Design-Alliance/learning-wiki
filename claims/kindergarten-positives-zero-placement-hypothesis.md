# Kindergarteners did not improve on placing positive integers, possibly because zero was marked in the middle rather than at the edge of the line

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Unlike prior preschooler studies, kindergarteners here did not improve on positive placements; the authors attribute this to zero being in the middle of the line and to the added load of learning negatives. [→ Bofferding 2015](#bofferding-2015)

## Evidence

### Bofferding 2015

Bofferding, L., Hoffman, A., Suazo, E., & Lisy, N. (2015). Number Line Estimation with Negatives. Proceedings of the 37th annual meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED584204

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' interpretation of the kindergarten null result for positives, contrasting with Ramani and Siegler (2008): students often marked 0 and 1 near the left edge, left of zero. The article also suggests the time spent on negatives may have taken away from positive-number practice.

> "A likely reason for this is that students were given space to mark positive numbers before 0 (as opposed to having zero at the edge of the page)."
