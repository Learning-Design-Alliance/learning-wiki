Evidence¶

Daches Cohen L and Rubinsten O 2017¶

Daches Cohen L and Rubinsten O (2017) Mothers, Intrinsic Math Motivation, Arithmetic Skills, and Math Anxiety in Elementary School. Front. Psychol. 8:1939. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01939

q2 · i3 · associational · r1

Hierarchical regression in a correlational study of 27 sixth graders and their mothers, with math fluency entered first as predictor of AMAS scores. The article reports "math ﬂuency scores signiﬁcantly explained 17.6% of the variance" with F(1,25)= 5.34, p < 0.05. Math fluency also correlated with AMAS at r=−0.42.

Related Claims¶

  • Implicitly measured math anxiety (attentional bias) in children is predicted by environmental factors, specifically mothers' numerical task anxiety and maternal pressure — related
  • Preliminary evidence of intergenerational transmission of math anxiety: mothers' math anxiety relates to children's lower intrinsic math motivation and stronger attentional bias toward math stimuli — related
  • Higher levels of maternal help, press for intellectual development, and monitoring are associated with poorer children's arithmetic skills — related
  • Math anxiety's direct effect on math performance emerges in grade 4 but not grade 3, suggesting a developmental trajectory — related
  • Math anxiety is not significantly correlated with trait anxiety in young children — related
