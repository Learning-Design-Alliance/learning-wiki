# Achievement groups differ significantly in Attention, Relevance, and Confidence but not Satisfaction on the IMMS

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` ANOVA showed significant between-group differences in Attention (F = 4.162, p = 0.023), Relevance (F = 5.746, p = 0.007), Confidence (F = 4.527, p = 0.017), and overall IMMS (F = 4.700, p = 0.015). [→ Wan Chong Choi & Iek Chong Choi 2022](#wan-chong-choi-iek-chong-choi-2022)
`q2 i?` Satisfaction showed no statistically significant difference between achievement groups (F = 1.705, p = 0.196). [→ Wan Chong Choi & Iek Chong Choi 2022 (2)](#wan-chong-choi-iek-chong-choi-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Wan Chong Choi & Iek Chong Choi 2022

Wan Chong Choi & Iek Chong Choi. (2022). The Influence and Relationship between Computational Thinking, Learning Motivation, Attitude, and Achievement of Code.org in K-12 Programming Education. [doi:10.1145/3578837.3578871](https://doi.org/10.1145/3578837.3578871)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

One-way ANOVA comparing IMMS scores across the Low, Medium, and High PAT groups. Attention, Relevance, and Confidence were each significant; pairwise comparisons showed the High group's Attention mean of 4.34 exceeded Low (3.77) and Medium (3.84).

> "The overall IMMS score indicated a statistically significant difference between the groups, as shown by an F-ratio of 4.700 and a p-value of 0.015."

### Wan Chong Choi & Iek Chong Choi 2022 (2)

Wan Chong Choi & Iek Chong Choi. (2022). The Influence and Relationship between Computational Thinking, Learning Motivation, Attitude, and Achievement of Code.org in K-12 Programming Education. [doi:10.1145/3578837.3578871](https://doi.org/10.1145/3578837.3578871)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same ANOVA analysis for the Satisfaction dimension. The article reports no statistically significant difference between achievement groups at the 0.05 level; no effect size is printed.

> "The results showed an F -ratio of 1.705 with a p -value of 0.196 for the dimension of Satisfaction. This indicates that, although there is some variance in satisfaction among the groups, the differences were not statistically significant at the 0.05 level"
