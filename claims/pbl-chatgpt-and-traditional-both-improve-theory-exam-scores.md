# Both PBL-ChatGPT and traditionally taught urology interns significantly improved their theoretical knowledge exam scores from before to three days after the course

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Post-course theoretical exam scores were significantly higher than pre-course scores in both the PBL-ChatGPT group and the traditional teaching group. [→ Zeng H 2025](#zeng-h-2025)

## Evidence

### Zeng H 2025

Zeng H, Zhu Z, Hu J, Cui Y. (2025). Application of ChatGPT-assisted problem-based learning teaching method in clinical medical education. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06321-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled study of 42 fifth-year medical interns on a two-week urology rotation, with a 100-point theoretical exam administered before the course and three days after it ended. Both within-group improvements were reported significant at "p < 0.01". No effect size was printed.

> "The scores of the two groups of students who took exams three days after the course ended were significantly higher than their scores before the course (PBL-ChatGPT group: p < 0.01;Traditional teaching group: p < 0.01)."
