## Evidence

### McManus I C 2013

McManus I C, Elder Andrew T, Dacre Jane. (2013). Investigating possible ethnicity and sex bias in clinical examiners: an analysis of data from the MRCP(UK) PACES and nPACES examinations. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-103

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Table 1 summarises examiner significance categories: 34 (1.9%) PACES and 35 (2.3%) nPACES examiners were hawkish at corrected p < .05, with doveishness in similar proportions.

> "It is clear that some examiners are significantly more hawkish or doveish than their co-examiners, as has been found previously [3]."

### McManus I C 2013 (2)

McManus I C, Elder Andrew T, Dacre Jane. (2013). Investigating possible ethnicity and sex bias in clinical examiners: an analysis of data from the MRCP(UK) PACES and nPACES examinations. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-103

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational re-analysis of 1204 examiners with estimates in both examination formats. The article reports r = 0.402 (p < .001) but cautions this does not mean hawkishness is a fixed trait.

> "Hawkishness was calculated twice in 1204 examiners, and the correlation was 0.402 (p < .001), indicating strong stability."
