# Frequent two-thumb smartphone typing was associated with significantly better Glass Movement on the simulator

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants who never or very rarely type with two thumbs performed significantly poorer in Glass Movement (p = 0.006); means fell from 7.583 (very rarely/never) through 3.575 (occasionally) to 1.947 (all the time). [→ Szabó DI 2024](#szabo-di-2024)

## Evidence

### Szabó DI 2024

Szabó DI, Vereczkei A, Papp A. (2024). From gaming to surgery: the influence of digital natives on robotic skills development. Journal of Robotic Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11701-024-02178-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Group comparison of Glass Movement by self-reported two-thumb typing frequency (15 all-the-time, 8 occasional, 6 very rarely/never); those who "performed significantly poorer in this metric (p = 0.006)". No effect size printed.

> "our results suggest that Glass Movement (GM) depends on how often and quickly both of the thumbs are used for typing on smartphones, those who never or very rarely do it performed significantly poorer in this metric (p = 0.006)"
