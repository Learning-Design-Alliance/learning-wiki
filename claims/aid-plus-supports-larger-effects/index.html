Evidence¶

Nguyen 2018¶

Nguyen, T.D., Kramer, J.W., & Evans, B.J. (2018). The Effects of Grant Aid on Student Persistence and Degree Attainment: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of the Causal Evidence (CEPA Working Paper No. 18-04). Stanford Center for Education Policy Analysis. http://cepa.stanford.edu/wp18-04

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Subgroup (moderator) analysis of year-to-year persistence studies in the random-effects meta-analysis (Table 3); studies whose programs added supports such as faculty advising showed an effect size "nearly doubled" relative to the overall estimate. Subgroup, not meta-regression.

"In terms of additional treatment, we find that the effect size is nearly doubled suggesting that both aid and the other forms of support such as faculty advising, peer support, or academic support have substantial effects on postsecondary persistence."

Nguyen 2018 (2)¶

Nguyen, T.D., Kramer, J.W., & Evans, B.J. (2018). The Effects of Grant Aid on Student Persistence and Degree Attainment: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of the Causal Evidence (CEPA Working Paper No. 18-04). Stanford Center for Education Policy Analysis. http://cepa.stanford.edu/wp18-04

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Subgroup analysis of degree completion studies (Table 4); programs with additional treatments showed on-time and delayed completion estimates "more than 50 percent higher than the overall estimates," reinforcing the persistence moderator result. No standardized effect size is printed.

"In terms of additional treatment, the estimates for both on-time and delayed completion for studies with additional treatment are more than 50 percent higher than the overall estimates, which reinforces our earlier results in Table 3 indicating that additional supports likely help students to persist and complete their degrees."

Related Claims¶

  • An additional $1,000 of grant aid improves year-to-year persistence by a statistically significant 1.2 percentage points, with smaller statistically insignificant effects on degree completion — related
  • Need-based grant aid improves persistence and completion while merit-only aid shows no detectable effect on these outcomes — related
  • Grant aid increases postsecondary persistence, raising within-year persistence by 3.2 and year-to-year persistence by 1.8 percentage points in U.S. causal studies — related
  • Grant aid increases degree completion, raising on-time completion by 2.4 and delayed completion by 2.5 percentage points in U.S. causal studies — related
  • Effects of grant aid on persistence and completion are heterogeneous across studies, justifying random-effects meta-analysis — related
