## Evidence

### Wu et al. 2021

Huimin Wu, Yiqun Guo, Yingkai Yang, Le Zhao and Cheng Guo. (2021). A Meta-analysis of the Longitudinal Relationship Between Academic Self-Concept and Academic Achievement. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09600-1

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Moderator analysis of the ASC-to-achievement path, which showed significant heterogeneity (Q(61) = 372.51, I2 = 85.20%). Age was the first significant moderator: "t (49) = 2.85, p = 0.006", opposite in direction to the moderation of the reverse path.

> "The first was student age, t (49) = 2.85, p = 0.006. The effect of ASC on achievement was stronger for adolescents (β = 0.11, p \< 0.01) than for children (β = 0.06, p \< 0.01)"

### Wu et al. 2021 (2)

Huimin Wu, Yiqun Guo, Yingkai Yang, Le Zhao and Cheng Guo. (2021). A Meta-analysis of the Longitudinal Relationship Between Academic Self-Concept and Academic Achievement. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09600-1

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Achievement level significantly moderated the ASC-to-achievement path: "t (10.2) = − 4.51, p = 0.01". High-achieving samples could not be estimated (NA in Table 4).

> "The second significant moderator was student achievement level, t (10.2) = − 4.51, p = 0.01. The effect sizes for low-achieving students (β = 0.01, p \< 0.01) were significantly weaker than typical-achieving students (β = 0.11, p \< 0.01)."

### Wu et al. 2021 (3)

Huimin Wu, Yiqun Guo, Yingkai Yang, Le Zhao and Cheng Guo. (2021). A Meta-analysis of the Longitudinal Relationship Between Academic Self-Concept and Academic Achievement. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09600-1

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Measurement type significantly moderated the ASC-to-achievement path: "F (2, 4.88) = 6.74, p = 0.04", consistent with the hypothesis that motivation-laden measures (grades, teacher-rating) show stronger associations than low-stake standardized tests.

> "types of achievement measurement significantly moderated the effect of ASC on achievement, F (2, 4.88) = 6.74, p = 0.04. In line with our hypothesis, when achievement measurement used standardized tests, the effect of ASC on achievement was minimal (β = 0.06, p \< 0.01), compared to grades (β = 0.13, p \< 0.01) and teacher-rating (β = 0.10, p \< 0.01)."
