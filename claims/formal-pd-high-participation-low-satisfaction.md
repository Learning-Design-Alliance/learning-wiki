# Teachers participate almost universally in formal professional development (averaging 30 required hours per year) but report much lower satisfaction with it than participation

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Ninety-nine percent of teachers participate in formal professional development, averaging 30 required hours per year, yet satisfaction lags far behind participation (e.g., 84 percent participate in in-service days but only 20 percent are satisfied with them). [→ Grunwald Associates LLC and Digital Promise 2015](#grunwald-associates-llc-and-digital-promise-2015)

## Evidence

### Grunwald Associates LLC and Digital Promise 2015

Grunwald Associates LLC and Digital Promise. (2015). Making Professional Learning Count: Recognizing Educators' Skills with Micro-credentials. https://www.digitalpromise.org/page/-/dpdocuments/microcredentials/making_professional_learning_count.pdf?nocdn=1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Nationally representative online survey of 856 U.S. K-12 teachers asked about participation in and satisfaction with professional development in the past 12 months. The report states "Ninety-nine percent of teachers participate in formal professional development" and prints satisfaction of 20 percent for in-service days versus 84 percent participation.

> "Ninety-nine percent of teachers participate in formal professional development . More than 80 percent participate in in-service days, and more than half in educator workshops . On average, teachers are required to complete 30 hours of professional development per year ."
