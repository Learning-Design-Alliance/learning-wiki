# Giving both problems of a car-on-incline isomorphic problem pair did not improve performance on the friction problem

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 causal `r1` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2016 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Students who worked the tension problem before the friction problem performed similarly on the friction problem (17% correct) to students who only saw the friction problem (16% correct). [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016)

`q2 i?` Students who solved both the tension and friction problems performed no significantly differently on the friction problem than students who solved only the friction problem (28% vs. 20%). [→ Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#shih-yin-lin-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)

## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Effect of Misconception on Transfer in Problem Solving. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07686](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07686)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Classroom multiple-choice data comparing two groups: 115 students answering only the static-friction question and a different 272 students answering the tension question then the friction question. The paper reports "only 17% provided a correct response for the case with friction" after the tension problem, versus 16% without it; common incorrect answers were µsN = 11,700 N (~40%) and µkN = 10,400 N (~20%).

> "Out of the 115 students, 16% provided a correct response when only question (4) was asked. Out of the 272 students, 61% provided a correct response for question (2) with the tension force holding the car at rest but only 17% provided a correct response for the case with friction."

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Challenges in Analogical Reasoning. Department of Physics and Astronomy, University of Pittsburgh. Originally published 2008. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06423](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06423)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison across course cohorts: 81 students answered only the friction problem while 479 students answered both problems. Scores were "an average of 20%" versus 28% on friction and 67% on tension; the authors state there was no significant difference on the friction problem.

> "The 81 students who only solved the problem with friction obtained an average of 20% whereas 479 students who solved both problems obtained 28% and 67% on the problems involving friction and tension, respectively."
