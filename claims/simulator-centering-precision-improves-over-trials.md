## Evidence

### Joseph David 2014

Joseph David, Jehl Jean-Philippe, Maureira Pablo, Perrenot Cyril, Miller Neal, Bravetti Pierre, Ambrosini Pascal, Tran Nguyen. (2014). Relative Contribution of Haptic Technology to Assessment and Training in Implantology. BioMed Research International. https://doi.org/10.1155/2014/413951

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-group analysis of 20 third-year dental students performing eight simulator trials; the article reports a trial-dependent improvement in centering, quoting "after 8 exercises the deviation was reduced to 0.54 ± 0.06 mm". Only t and P values are printed, so no effect size is reported.

> "The mean initial position was off the mark by 0.86 ± 0.12 mm, but after 8 exercises the deviation was reduced to 0.54 ± 0.06 mm (t = 2.247, P = 0.0310 versus the 1st trial)."
