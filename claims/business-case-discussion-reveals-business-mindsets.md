# The Business Case Discussion gives admissions readers evidence of applicants' business mindsets, defined as cognitive and intrapersonal skills rather than business expertise

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Readers evaluate developmentally appropriate application of business mindsets rather than prior business expertise; technical jargon or elite internships may distract from critical thinking. [→ Willis 2022](#willis-2022)

## Evidence

### Willis 2022

Willis, L., & Martinez, M. R. (2022). Authentic student work in college admissions: Lessons from the Ross School of Business. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/756.774

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview findings from admission staff reported in the Business Case Discussion section. Readers look for critical thinking applied to the applicant's immediate context, not business knowledge; one reader said it is "not really about business knowledge."

> "Admission staff reported that the use of technical jargon or detailed descriptions of elite internships may even distract from demonstrating the critical thinking and inquiry skills at the heart of the question. Rather than expertise, they evaluate the extent to which applicants apply developmentally appropriate business mindsets to their immediate context."
