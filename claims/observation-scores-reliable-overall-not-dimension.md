## Evidence

### Garet 2017

Garet, M.S., Wayne, A.J., Brown, S., Rickles, J., Song, M., and Manzeske, D. (2017). The Impact of Providing Performance Feedback to Teachers and Principals (NCEE 2018-4001). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Reliability analysis of the study's two classroom observation rubrics, reported in the executive summary; year-averaged overall scores showed reliabilities of ".53 to .61 and .70 to .77" for the two rubrics, with similar Year 1 reliabilities.

> "scores averaged over the year provided some reliable information to distinguish teacher performance (with Year 2 reliabilities of .53 to .61 and .70 to .77 for the two observation rubrics used)."

### Garet 2017 (2)

Garet, M.S., Wayne, A.J., Brown, S., Rickles, J., Song, M., and Manzeske, D. (2017). The Impact of Providing Performance Feedback to Teachers and Principals (NCEE 2018-4001). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

The same reliability analysis found dimension-level score differences had limited reliability for identifying areas for improvement, with Year 2 reliabilities of ".35 to .43 and .18 to .30"; observation score reliabilities were similar in Year 1.

> "Differences in teachers' observation ratings across dimensions, however, had limited reliability to identify areas for improvement, even when averaged over the year (with Year 2 reliabilities of .35 to .43 and .18 to .30 for the two observation rubrics)."
