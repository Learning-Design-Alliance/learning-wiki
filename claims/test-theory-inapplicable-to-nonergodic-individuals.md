# Classical test theory's validity and reliability claims cannot be generalized to individual assessment of nonstationary processes such as development and learning

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Because classical test theory defines true and error scores in terms of between-subjects variation, its validity and reliability claims do not transfer to individual assessments when the underlying process is nonergodic. [→ Molenaar 2004](#molenaar-2004)
`q1 i?` A test theory based on the original within-subject (IAV) true-score definition of Lord and Novick is needed for individual counseling applications. [→ Molenaar 2004](#molenaar-2004)

## Evidence

### Molenaar 2004

Molenaar, P. C. M. (2004). A Manifesto on Psychology as Idiographic Science: Bringing the Person Back Into Scientific Psychology, This Time Forever. Measurement, 2(4), 201-218. https://www.fmg.uva.nl/ml/publicaties_ml.cfm

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument built on a close reading of Lord and Novick (1968): the original true-score definition uses propensity distributions over repeated measurements of the same person (IAV), but test theory is developed for IEV because obtaining a person's propensity distribution was deemed practically impossible. The article offers this as argument, not empirical test.

> "claims based on classical test theory that a test is valid and reliable cannot be gen- eralized to individual assessments of development, learning, or any other non- stationary process."
