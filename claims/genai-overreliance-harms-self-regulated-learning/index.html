  • A randomized controlled trial with 86 K-12 students found a significant gain in self-regulated-learning strategy use from agentic coaching systems — related
  • Perceived AI-adaptive learning environments are negatively associated with college students' self-regulated learning in a three-wave longitudinal survey — related
  • Reaching the qualitative SOLO phase in GenAI interaction required repeated meta-level learner interventions, demanding the very self-regulatory skills the tool was expected to support — related
  • Student-agent interaction featured a significant Query-Evaluation-Query verification loop (RM-QR-EA), distinct from the linear QR-UN path of student-instructor interaction — related
  • Within the AI-assisted group, the frequency of checking the AI's claims against sources predicted decision-making gains, with no students flagged for over-reliance — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • HelpCoach led to greater knowledge retention one week later than pre-task help-seeking training alone — reports the opposite
  • LLMs can deliver scalable, personalized, interactive feedback almost instantly, reducing dependency on teacher availability and supporting self-regulated learning in large courses — reports the opposite
  • Reflective strategies such as cognitive forcing, pre-commitment, and uncertainty communication reduce overreliance on AI and improve decision quality, but have been evaluated only as short-term single-session interventions — related
  • GenAI use collapses SRL cycle transitions, with metacognitive activity restructuring around the tool — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Self-regulated learning ability significantly moderates Gen-AI's effect on HOT: high-SRL students show ES = 0.863 versus ES = 0.284 for low-SRL students — related
  • Effort regulation negatively predicts, and general academic help seeking positively predicts, reported AI dependency; time management is non-significant — related
  • HelpCoach did not significantly outperform Baseline in improving or retaining self-reported self-regulation — related
  • In both interaction-log and think-aloud records from a GenAI-assisted instructional-design task, regulation was the most frequent operational label and monitoring the least frequent — related
  • Learners using a GenAI system issued prompts that offloaded tasks that may have been important for them to perform themselves, such as differentiating concepts and deciding what to learn — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Unscaffolded GenAI access did not significantly change pre-service teachers' self-regulated learning relative to traditional learning — related
  • Advanced learners may benefit from learner-controlled selection of learning tasks and levels of generality — related
  • Computer-based metacognitive scaffolding shows limited evidence of long-term retention or transfer of the scaffolded skills — related
