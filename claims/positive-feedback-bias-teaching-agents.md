## Evidence

### Matilda Knierim 2024

Matilda Knierim, Sahil Jain, Murat Han Aydoğan, Kenneth Mitra, Kush Desai, Akanksha Saran, and Kim Baraka. (2024). Prosody as a Teaching Signal for Agent Learning: Exploratory Studies and Algorithmic Implications. INTERNATIONAL CONFERENCE ON MULTIMODAL INTERACTION (ICMI '24). https://doi.org/10.1145/3678957.3685735

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Analysis of teacher audio from a mixed-participant Wizard-of-Oz interactive RL study with 28 participants in 14 sessions on a grid-world task. The population-level chi-square test showed "significantly more positive than negative feedback over all participants (p≤ 0.001)"; five of 14 trainers individually showed the bias.

> "A population level chi-square goodness of fit test showed that there was significantly more positive than negative feedback over all participants (p≤ 0.001)."

### Matilda Knierim 2024 (2)

Matilda Knierim, Sahil Jain, Murat Han Aydoğan, Kenneth Mitra, Kush Desai, Akanksha Saran, and Kim Baraka. (2024). Prosody as a Teaching Signal for Agent Learning: Exploratory Studies and Algorithmic Implications. INTERNATIONAL CONFERENCE ON MULTIMODAL INTERACTION (ICMI '24). https://doi.org/10.1145/3678957.3685735

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pilot study of 10 minutes of audio per game from a single demonstrator playing Ms. Pac-Man, Seaquest, and Space Invaders. Utterance durations were compared across games; "yes" utterances significantly outnumbered "no" utterances (p < 0.05) in all three games.

> "We find that the demonstrator uses significantly more "yes" utterances compared to "no" utterances (Fig. 17) for all three games (𝑝 < 0.05)."
