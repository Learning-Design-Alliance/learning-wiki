# The replay item in JEU SERAI indicated Realistic type affiliation regardless of the anticipated RIASEC type of the mini game replayed

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Only two mini games out of 18 had their best replay correlation with the right RIASEC type, contrary to the preference hypothesis. [→ Guardiola 2015](#guardiola-2015)
`q2 i?` The replay item correlated highest with the Realistic type in 12 of 18 mini games, regardless of the mini game's anticipated type. [→ Guardiola 2015 (2)](#guardiola-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Guardiola 2015

Guardiola, E., & Natkin, S. (2015). A Game Design Methodology for Generating a Psychological Profile of Players. In Serious Games Analytics, Methodologies for Performance Measurement, Assessment, and Improvement. Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-05834-4_16

`q2 · i?`

Correlation analysis of the JEU SERAI test data showed the replay item, originally seen as a preference measure, failed as such: "only two mini games out of 18 have the best correlation coefficient with the right RIASEC type".

> "But the correlations tables demonstrate the contrary: for the “Replay” item, only two mini games out of 18 have the best correlation coefficient with the right RIASEC type."

### Guardiola 2015 (2)

Guardiola, E., & Natkin, S. (2015). A Game Design Methodology for Generating a Psychological Profile of Players. In Serious Games Analytics, Methodologies for Performance Measurement, Assessment, and Improvement. Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-05834-4_16

`q2 · i?`

The same analysis found the replay item's highest correlation was with Realistic-type subjects in "12 times out of 18" cases, suggesting total replay frequency across any mini game may indicate Realistic type affiliation rather than per-game preference.

> "the “Replay” item has its highest correlation coefficient 12 times out of 18 with the Realistic type of subjects, regardless of the anticipated RIASEC type of the mini game."
