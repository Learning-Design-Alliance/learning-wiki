# Five years after receiving CSR awards, schools did not demonstrate larger achievement growth than matched comparison schools

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Matched non-CSR schools had larger statistically significant increases in middle school mathematics and reading than CSR schools, though only middle school mathematics showed a marginally significant difference. [→ Orland 2010](#orland-2010)

## Evidence

### Orland 2010

Orland, M., Hoffman, A., & Vaughn, III, E. S. (2010). Evaluation of the Comprehensive School Reform Program Implementation and Outcomes: Fifth-Year Report. U.S. Department of Education. https://www.ed.gov/about/offices/list/opepd/ppss/reports.html#title

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental matched comparison of Title I CSR and non-CSR schools using school-level achievement data from 2002–03 to 2006–07. The report finds that "matched non-CSR schools had larger statistically significant increases in middle school mathematics and reading of 2.8 and 2.5 percentage points." Only middle school mathematics showed a marginally significant difference.

> "matched non-CSR schools had larger statistically significant increases in middle school mathematics and reading of 2.8 and 2.5 percentage points, respectively. Nevertheless, only middle school mathematics had a marginally statistically significant difference between CSR schools and their matched non-CSR schools."
