## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Higher youth economic stress about needs is associated with lower academic achievement (β = −.41, p = .03), while youth stress about wants is unrelated to educational outcomes. [→ Mistry 2021](#mistry-2021)

## Evidence

### Mistry 2021

Mistry, R. S., & Elenbaas, L. (2021). It’s All in the Family: Parents’ Economic Worries and Youth’s Perceptions of Financial Stress and Educational Outcomes. Journal of Youth and Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-021-01393-4

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Path-analytic SEM of cross-sectional survey data from 136 youth and their parents in a northeastern U.S. city found "higher levels of youth stress about needs was associated with lower parent-reported academic achievement, β = −.41, p = .03". No standardized effect size beyond the path coefficient is printed, so impact is coded null.

> "Supporting Hypothesis 3, higher levels of youth stress about needs was associated with lower parent-reported academic achievement, β = −.41, p = .03, and youth SSS was marginally associated with youth-reported academic motivation, β = .17, p = .10."
