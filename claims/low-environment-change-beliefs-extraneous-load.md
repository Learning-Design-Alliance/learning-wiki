# Low environment-change beliefs lead students to allocate cognitive resources to monitoring for breakdowns, creating extraneous load

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` When environment-change beliefs are low, students may allocate more cognitive resources to monitoring for sensory threat, participation penalties, and accommodation failures rather than to comprehension and integration. [→ Srinivasan 2026](#srinivasan-2026)

## Evidence

### Srinivasan 2026

Srinivasan, H. (2026). Possibility Mindset: A Theory of Motivation Under Constraint, with Neurodivergence as a Revealing Interpretive Case. Preprint (v1) posted to PsyArXiv on January 18, 2026. https://osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical prediction in the possibility mindset and learning processes section, drawing on cognitive load theory (Sweller, 1988). The article argues that under low environment-change beliefs the environment itself functions as an ongoing source of extraneous load; no empirical test is reported.

> "the environment becomes an ongoing source of extraneous load, making it harder to sustain attention, encode material, and coordinate strategies even when motivation is intact"
