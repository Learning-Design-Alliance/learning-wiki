## Evidence

### Tamara van Gog & Vincent Hoogerheide & Milou van Harsel 2020

Tamara van Gog & Vincent Hoogerheide & Milou van Harsel. (2020). The Role of Mental Effort in Fostering Self-Regulated Learning with Problem-Solving Tasks. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09544-y

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports the Kostons et al. (2012) experiment in which secondary students observed video modeling examples of the performance-assessment-selection cycle; training "enhance[d] self-assessment accuracy" and task selection accuracy versus a control receiving only the performance part. No effect size is printed.

> "The training was shown to enhance self-assessment accuracy (i.e., the absolute deviation between the judged and actual perfor- mance) and task selection accuracy (i.e., the deviation [number of columns in Fig. 1] between the chosen task and the recommended task)."

### Tamara van Gog & Vincent Hoogerheide & Milou van Harsel 2020 (2)

Tamara van Gog & Vincent Hoogerheide & Milou van Harsel. (2020). The Role of Mental Effort in Fostering Self-Regulated Learning with Problem-Solving Tasks. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09544-y

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports the Kostons et al. (2012) second experiment (pretest, training, self-regulated learning phase with eight chosen tasks, posttest): training-condition students "outperformed students in the control condition on the posttest problems"; replicated by Raaijmakers et al. (2018b) with a general heuristic. No effect size is printed.

> "Students in the training condition outperformed students in the control condition on the posttest problems."

### Tamara van Gog & Vincent Hoogerheide & Milou van Harsel 2020 (3)

Tamara van Gog & Vincent Hoogerheide & Milou van Harsel. (2020). The Role of Mental Effort in Fostering Self-Regulated Learning with Problem-Solving Tasks. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09544-y

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports the Raaijmakers et al. (2018a) follow-up: biology-trained students "did not show better math posttest performance than students who did not receive the training" on linear equation problems. No effect size is printed; equivalence was not tested. The review itself says "it is as yet unclear" whether the training effects can transfer to self-regulated learning in other problem contexts.

> "students who first received the self-assessment and task selection training in the context of the biology problems and then had to engage in a self-regulated learning with math problems (linear equation problems consisting of 3 steps; organized in a database with 5 complexity and 2 support levels) did not show better math posttest perfor- mance than students who did not receive the training."
