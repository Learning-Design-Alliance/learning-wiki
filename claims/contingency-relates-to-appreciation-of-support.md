# Higher levels of contingent support were related to higher appreciation of support, regardless of measurement occasion or independent working time

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The main effect of contingency on appreciation of support was significant (small effect size), with no interaction with occasion or independent working time. [→ van de Pol 2015](#van-de-pol-2015)

## Evidence

### van de Pol 2015

van de Pol, J., Volman, M., Oort, F., & Beishuizen, J. (2015). The effects of scaffolding in the classroom: support contingency and student independent working time in relation to student achievement, task effort and appreciation of support. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-015-9351-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multilevel analysis of the appreciation-of-support questionnaire (Cronbach's a = .90) in the experimental classroom study; the article reports "only the main effect of contingency was signiﬁcant (small effect size of R 2 = .04)" and higher contingency related to higher appreciation.

> "For students’ appreciation of support, only the main effect of contingency was signiﬁcant (small effect size of R 2 = .04; Cohen 1992). Regardless of the measurement occasion or the independent working time, higher levels of contingency were related to higher appreciation of support"
