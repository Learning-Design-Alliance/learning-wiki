## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In Experiment 3, comprehension under interruption with a time-out did not differ from the no-interruption control, while interruption without time-out was significantly poorer. [→ Foroughi 2015](#foroughi-2015)

## Evidence

### Foroughi 2015

Foroughi, C. K., Werner, N. E., Barragán, D., & Boehm-Davis, D. A. (2015). Interruptions Disrupt Reading Comprehension. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General. https://dx.doi.org/10.1037/xge0000074

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 3, a 2 x 3 repeated-measures ANOVA with 24 students across interruption, control, and time-out-with-interruption conditions. The significant interaction (F(2,46) = 18.47, p < .001, η2∂ = .45) showed the time-out removed the disruption; no differences existed within recognition questions.

> "significantly poorer performance in the interruption condition (M = 4.54, SD = 1.47, 95% CI [3.92, 5.16]) compared to the no interruption condition ((M = 5.63, SD = 1.61, 95% CI [4.95, 6.30]), t(23) = 7.42, p < .001, d = 1.51) and the interruption with a time-out condition ((M = 5.58, SD = 1.38, 95% CI [5.00, 6.17]), t(23) = 6.81, p < .001, d = 1.39)"
