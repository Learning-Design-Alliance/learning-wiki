# There were no significant differences in pupils' attitude scores toward the courseware across the three presentation modes

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Attitude scores did not differ significantly between AI and TI (p = 0.270) or between TI and AIT (p = 0.655). [→ Aldalalah and Fong 2010](#aldalalah-and-fong-2010)

## Evidence

### Aldalalah and Fong 2010

Osamah (Mohammad Ameen) Ahmad Aldalalah & Soon Fook Fong. (2010). Effects of Modality and Redundancy Principles on the Learning and Attitude of a Computer-Based Music Theory Lesson among Jordanian Primary Pupils. International Education Studies, 3(3). https://www.ccsenet.org/ies

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

t-tests of attitude questionnaire scores (20-item adapted instrument, Cronbach Alpha 0.79) between treatment groups of third-grade pupils. The TI-AIT comparison was also non-significant (p = 0.655); pupils showed positive attitudes in all modes.

> "The mean of post-test scores for group with TI (38.0567) was higher than the mean of post-test scores for group with AI (37.7891), and p = 0.270. This shows that there is no significant difference."
