## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The effect of school diversity climate on performance is positive and significant for first-generation students, corresponding to a 6-point increase in mathematics, 10 in science, and 12 in reading per one-SD increase in climate. [→ Anatolia Batruch 2026](#anatolia-batruch-2026)

## Evidence

### Anatolia Batruch 2026

Anatolia Batruch, Nicolas Sommet, Eva G.T. Green. (2026). Teachers' immigration attitudes and students' performance. Learning and Instruction 102 (2026) 102303. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102303

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Fixed-effects model with controls on PISA 2018 plausible values. The article reports the climate effect on first-generation students' performance is "positive and significant"; Table 2 prints SDC × 1G coefficients of 10.08 (science), 6.29 (math), 11.74 (reading). No standardized effect size is printed.

> "a one–standard-deviation increase in school-level teachers' immigration attitudes is associated with an average performance increase of 6 points in mathematics, 10 in science and 12 in reading."
