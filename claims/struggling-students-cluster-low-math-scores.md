## Evidence

### Xia et al. 2022

Qi Xia, Hongbiao Yin, Ruonan Hu, Xiuhan Li, and Junjie Shang. (2022). Motivation, Engagement, and Mathematics Achievement: An Exploratory Study Among Chinese Primary Students. SAGE Open. https://doi.org/10.1177/21582440221134609

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

K-mean clustering on centered means of the four integrative factors, with a three-cluster solution suggested by the elbow method, classified 681 'active', 441 'passive', and 416 'struggling' students among the 1,538 surveyed.

> "The third category comprised 416 students (27.0%) who had above-average levels of both adaptive motivation and engagement and maladaptive motivation and engagement. These students were des- ignated as ''struggling students.''"

### Xia et al. 2022 (2)

Qi Xia, Hongbiao Yin, Ruonan Hu, Xiuhan Li, and Junjie Shang. (2022). Motivation, Engagement, and Mathematics Achievement: An Exploratory Study Among Chinese Primary Students. SAGE Open. https://doi.org/10.1177/21582440221134609

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of mean mathematics exam scores across the three clusters (Table 4): active students scored highest (85.1 overall), passive students lowest (78.7), and struggling students 78.9, nearly as low as passive students despite high adaptive engagement.

> "In contrast to previous findings that students with adaptive engagement could get high scores in mathematics (Putwain et al., 2018), this study found that for the ''struggling students'' who had a high adaptive engagement, their mathematics scores were almost as low as those of the ''passive students.''"
