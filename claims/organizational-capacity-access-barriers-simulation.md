### Niyongombwa Irenee 2025

Niyongombwa Irenee, Sibomana Isaie, Wallace Lorraine S., Stein David. (2025). Exploring medical students’ simulation learning experience in Rwanda: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07981-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview finding from Theme 3, organizational-challenges sub-theme of the qualitative study: participants reported that "time constraints, curriculum structure, trainer shortages, and poor coordination limited learning opportunities."

> "“Simulation-based educational activities are planned, but the plan is rarely executed…sometimes until the rotation ends.” (Student 6)."

### Niyongombwa Irenee 2025 (2)

Niyongombwa Irenee, Sibomana Isaie, Wallace Lorraine S., Stein David. (2025). Exploring medical students’ simulation learning experience in Rwanda: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07981-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview finding from the limited-capacity sub-theme: shortages of space, equipment, and staff were reported to constrain learning, with some students only able to observe during sessions.

> "“There are not enough materials…some perform, and others observe because there are not enough mannequins…” (Student 11)."

### Niyongombwa Irenee 2025 (3)

Niyongombwa Irenee, Sibomana Isaie, Wallace Lorraine S., Stein David. (2025). Exploring medical students’ simulation learning experience in Rwanda: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07981-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview finding from the restricted-access sub-theme; however, the article reports not all participants agreed—another student said centers are open to all for individual practice, but no one is permanently assigned to guide self-directed users and supplies are sometimes out of stock.

> "“We access simulation facilities only in case of planned group sessions. If people could go even at another time, or could borrow materials to practice on independently, that would be helpful.” (Student 9)."
