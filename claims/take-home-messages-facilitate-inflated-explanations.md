# Figures providing take-home message verbiage facilitate inflated explanations in which students mimic legend text without understanding the data

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Students frequently restated phrases from figure legends or labels, sounding convincing while admitting non-understanding, and this pattern was facilitated by figures that supply take-home messages. [→ Finby B 2021](#finby-b-2021)

## Evidence

### Finby B 2021

Finby B, Heyer LJ, Malcolm Campbell A. (2021). Data‐rich textbook figures promote core competencies: Comparison of two textbooks. Biochem Mol Biol Educ. https://doi.org/10.1002/bmb.21488

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative analysis of the eight coded think-aloud transcripts, in which students freely admitted using text rather than the graph (e.g., "So the text is providing a lot more help as opposed to the actual graph does"). The authors concluded "Inflated explanation is facilitated by figures that provide take‐home message verbiage in their legends and/or labels."
