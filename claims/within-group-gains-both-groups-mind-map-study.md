## Evidence

### Polat 2017

Polat, O., Yavuz, E., A. & Tunc, A., B., O. (2017). The effect of using mind maps on the development of mathematics and science skills. Cypriot Journal of Educational Science. 12(5), 32-45. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1140786

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Wilcoxon signed-rank analysis of the 15 mind map children's pre- and post-test scores; the article reports "z= 3.424 p<.01" for Maths Skills and "z= 3.214 p<.01" for Science Skills, with mean ranks favouring the post-test. No effect size is printed.

> "Table 2 shows that there was a meaningful difference between the scores obtained by the children in the experiment group on the sub-scales of the MRPS Maths Skills (z= 3.424 p<.01) and Science Skills (z= 3.214 p<.01) before and after the study."

### Polat 2017 (2)

Polat, O., Yavuz, E., A. & Tunc, A., B., O. (2017). The effect of using mind maps on the development of mathematics and science skills. Cypriot Journal of Educational Science. 12(5), 32-45. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1140786

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Wilcoxon analysis of the 15 control children's scores after the regular 2013 pre-school curriculum activities; printed statistics are "z= 3.413 p<.01" (Maths) and "z= 2.746 p<.01" (Science), favouring the post-test. No effect size is printed.

> "The results of Table 3 show that there was a meaningful difference between the pre-study and post-study scores obtained on the MRPS sub-scales of Maths Skills (z= 3.413 p<.01) and Science Skills (z= 2.746 p<.01) by the children who solely did the activities presented in the daily syllabi prepared in line with the 2013 pre-school education curriculum."
