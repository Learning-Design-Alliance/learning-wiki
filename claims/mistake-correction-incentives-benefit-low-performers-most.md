## Evidence

### Benjamin R. Brown 2016

Benjamin R. Brown, Andrew Mason, and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Improving performance in quantum mechanics with explicit incentives to correct mistakes. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010121](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010121)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

In the quasi-experimental study, low-performing students (pretest scores below 50%) in the incentivized group gained an average of "44.9%" compared to "16.2%" for the comparison group on the repeated problems.

> "The students in the Low category on the pretest in the incentivized and comparison groups had an average gain of 44.9% and 16.2%, respectively (Table I)."

### Benjamin R. Brown 2016 (2)

Benjamin R. Brown, Andrew Mason, and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Improving performance in quantum mechanics with explicit incentives to correct mistakes. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010121](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010121)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The study found that self-diagnosis activities with grade incentives reduced the performance gap between high and low performers on repeated problems, while the gap remained for the comparison group.

> "Therefore, the gap between the High and Low performers on the midterm exam was reduced for this incentivized group on the repeated problems on the final exam."
