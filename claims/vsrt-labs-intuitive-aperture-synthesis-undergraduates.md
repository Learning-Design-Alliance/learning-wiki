# Table-top VSRT interferometer labs give undergraduates hands-on, intuitive understanding of aperture synthesis without Fourier-transform mathematics

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Undergraduates completing the VSRT lab sequence ended with first-hand experience showing that data from pairs of antennas at many separations yields a function from which source size or source separation can be inferred, without the high-level mathematics. [→ J. M. Marr 2011](#j-m-marr-2011)

## Evidence

### J. M. Marr 2011

J. M. Marr, A. Pere, K. Durkota, A. E. E. Rogers, V. Fish, M. B. Arndt (2011). Laboratory Exercises Using the Haystack VSRT Interferometer To Teach the Basics of Aperture Synthesis. Submitted to the American Journal of Physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1109.3816](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1109.3816)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Report of a Fall 2010 independent-study group of undergraduate students at Union College who completed all four labs; the authors report that these students "ended the term with ﬁrst-hand experience" of inferring source structure from baseline data. No quantitative outcome measure is printed.

> "these students ended the term with ﬁrst-hand experience that demonstrated that obtaining data between pairs of antennas at many diﬀerent separation distances leads to a function from which one could infer the size of a single source or the separation of a pair of sources."
