# Simulation shows balanced investment across childhood outperforms adolescent-only remediation: 91% high school graduation and 37% college enrollment versus 35% higher cost for late-only policy

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In simulations, a Perry-like early intervention raises high school graduation to more than 65% and college enrollment to 12%, and cuts welfare use in half relative to no intervention. [→ Cunha 2007](#cunha-2007)

## Evidence

### Cunha 2007

Flavio Cunha and James Heckman. (2007). The Technology of Skill Formation. NBER Working Paper No. 12840. http://www.nber.org/papers/w12840

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Simulation of the estimated nonlinear skill formation model for children from first-decile parental skills backgrounds: the Perry-like early policy moves children from first to fourth decile of cognitive skills and boosts graduation above 65% and college enrollment to 12%.

> "This policy increases high school graduation and college enrollment rates to more than 65% and 12%, respectively. It reduces participation in crime. It makes the children more productive when they are adults. It cuts in half the probability that the child collects welfare benefits in his/her early adult years."

### Cunha 2007 (2)

Flavio Cunha and James Heckman. (2007). The Technology of Skill Formation. NBER Working Paper No. 12840. http://www.nber.org/papers/w12840

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Simulation comparing a postponed adolescent remediation policy (college tuition, adolescent literacy, mentoring programs) with the Perry-like early policy: matching Perry-like high school graduation rates via adolescent investment costs more than 35% more in present value.

> "To achieve Perry-like outcomes for this population through adolescent investment, it is necessary to move adolescent investment to the top of the parental investment distribution. The present value of the costs of the investment in this adolescent remediation program is more than 35% larger than in the Perry Preschool program."

### Cunha 2007 (3)

Flavio Cunha and James Heckman. (2007). The Technology of Skill Formation. NBER Working Paper No. 12840. http://www.nber.org/papers/w12840

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Simulation of a balanced policy distributing the adolescent-only program's funds optimally over the child's life cycle: 91% graduation, 37% college enrollment, and 2.6% welfare use. Raising early skills increases the marginal productivity of later investment.

> "For a balanced program, high school graduation and college enrollment rates are, respectively, 91% and 37%. The reduction in conviction and probation rates is better than what is obtained from an adolescent-only policy, and welfare use is reduced to a low 2.6% rate."
