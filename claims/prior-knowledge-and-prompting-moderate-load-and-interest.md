# Prior knowledge and intervention condition predict repeated cognitive load and interest measures: prior knowledge lowers effort and difficulty and raises interest and understanding, and prompting groups perceive tasks as more difficult

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Intervention groups without prompting perceive the tasks as less difficult than prompting groups, and prior knowledge influences perceived difficulty regardless of group. [→ Katrin Schuessler 2026](#katrin-schuessler-2026)
`q3 i?` Prior knowledge lowers reported invested mental effort, while the intervention group has no decisive effect on effort. [→ Katrin Schuessler 2026 (2)](#katrin-schuessler-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Katrin Schuessler 2026

Katrin Schuessler, Jenna Koenen, Elke Sumfleth, Marvin Rost. (2026). Variations in Repeated Measures of Cognitive Load and Interest During Complex Learning Tasks. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10105-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Bayesian multilevel ordinal regression results for perceived task difficulty (best-fitting model G1, global non-linear trend) in the N = 360 sample; prior knowledge and intervention group enter that model as fixed effects (main effects), not as moderators; no standardized effect size is printed for them.

> "Overall, regardless of the intervention group, prior knowledge has an influence on the perceived difficulty of the task. The intervention groups with prompting differ from the groups without prompting in terms of perceived difficulty, that is, intervention groups without prompting perceive the tasks as less difficult."

### Katrin Schuessler 2026 (2)

Katrin Schuessler, Jenna Koenen, Elke Sumfleth, Marvin Rost. (2026). Variations in Repeated Measures of Cognitive Load and Interest During Complex Learning Tasks. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10105-4

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bayesian multilevel ordinal regression for invested mental effort (best-fitting model M5) in the N = 360 sample; the article adds that the interventional group has no decisive effect on effort. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "Less influential but significant is the effect of prior knowledge, which lowers the reported invested mental effort."
