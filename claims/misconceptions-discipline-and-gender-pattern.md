## Evidence

### Olde Bekkink M 2016

Olde Bekkink M, Donders ART, Kooloos JG, de Waal RM, Ruiter DJ. (2016). Uncovering students’ misconceptions by assessment of their written questions. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0739-5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Subgroup analysis by gender in the same 185-question cohort; the article reports no difference in prevalence and prints no effect size or test statistic for this contrast.

> "There was no difference in the prevalence of questions containing misconceptions among male and female students."

### Olde Bekkink M 2016 (2)

Olde Bekkink M, Donders ART, Kooloos JG, de Waal RM, Ruiter DJ. (2016). Uncovering students’ misconceptions by assessment of their written questions. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0739-5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Subgroup analysis by discipline in the same cohort; the article reports all misconceptions came from medical students (160 medical, 25 biomedical students included). The authors attribute the difference possibly to biomedical students’ science-methodology background or to selection bias.

> "All questions containing misconceptions were derived from medical students; questions written by biomedical science students did not reveal misconceptions."
