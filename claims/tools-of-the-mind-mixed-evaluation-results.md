# Tools of the Mind curriculum effects are mixed across evaluations: null in prekindergarten, substantial in high-poverty kindergarten schools

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r3` · `q2`–`q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` A large cluster randomized trial of the kindergarten Tools of the Mind curriculum found substantial effects (ES = 0.30–0.80) on vocabulary, reasoning, and attention in high-poverty schools, but a prekindergarten evaluation found no effects. [→ Blair 2015](#blair-2015)
`q2 i?` A randomized evaluation of the prekindergarten version with a larger, more diverse sample found no effects on academic ability or self-regulation. [→ Blair 2015 (2)](#blair-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Blair 2015

Blair, Clancy & Raver, C. Cybele. (2015). School Readiness and Self-Regulation: A Developmental Psychobiological Approach. Annual Review of Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010814-015221

`q3 · i2` · `review · r3`

Large-scale cluster randomized-controlled trial of the kindergarten version of Tools of the Mind, spanning low to high socioeconomic status schools. The article reports printed effect sizes ES = 0.30–0.80 on vocabulary, reasoning, and attention in high-poverty schools, plus effects on salivary cortisol and alpha amylase and sustained reading and vocabulary growth into first grade.

> "Findings from this evaluation, which included schools spanning the range of socioeconomic status from low to high, indicated substantial effects (ES = 0.30–0.80) of the curriculum on vocabulary, reasoning, and attention among children in high-poverty schools as indicated by the percent of children eligible for free or reduced-price lunch."

### Blair 2015 (2)

Blair, Clancy & Raver, C. Cybele. (2015). School Readiness and Self-Regulation: A Developmental Psychobiological Approach. Annual Review of Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010814-015221

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports that a randomized evaluation of the prekindergarten version "found no effects of Tools on any aspect of academic ability or any aspect of self-regulation." A small-scale randomized evaluation with Spanish-speaking English-language learners had found effects on executive function measures. No effect size is printed for the null result.

> "However, a randomized evaluation of the prekindergarten version of the curriculum with a larger, more diverse sample found no effects of Tools on any aspect of academic ability or any aspect of self-regulation, including direct assessments of executive functions and teacher ratings of emotion regulation (Wilson & Farran 2012)."
