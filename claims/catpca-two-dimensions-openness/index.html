Evidence¶

van Heijst 2019¶

van Heijst, H., de Jong, F. P. C. M., van Aalst, J., de Hoog, N., & Kirschner, P. A. (2019). Socio-cognitive openness in online knowledge building discourse: does openness keep conversations going? International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning, 14, 165-184. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-019-09303-4

q2 · i? · associational · r2

CATPCA in SPSS version 24 on the coded openness components of the 594 analyzed units. Dimension 1 explained 20.9% of variance (loadings .36-.75) and dimension 2 explained 15.2% (loadings .49-.70), with "two extracted dimensions show strong similarities with the theoretical framework"; transactivity was not included, though the authors maintain its theoretical importance.

"Principle components analysis with Promax Kaiser rotation yielded two dimensions in the model with Eigen values exceeding 1, accounting for 36.2% of the total variance. Dimension 1 explained 20.9% of the variance (loadings ranging from .36–.75), while dimension 2 explained 15.2% of the variance (loadings ranging from .49–.70)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • In Knowledge Forum discourse, three components (connecting knowledge, transactivity, authority) dominated notes, and cognitive openness components predicted follow-up while social openness components predicted slightly reduced follow-up
  • Component frequencies: elaboration, other transactivity and expert authority are frequent; multiple justification, personal positioning and questioning are rare
  • Contributions to online knowledge building discourse show moderate socio-cognitive openness, with higher social than cognitive openness
  • Social openness components transactivity and authority are associated with less follow-up, and dimension-level openness shows no significant association with follow-up
  • At the discourse-act level, notes expressing uncertainty are associated with more follow-up and notes open on community orientation with less, and notes lacking building-knowledge openness receive less follow-up
  • Context characteristics (community type, trimester, teacher-student role) have little impact on openness; teachers contribute more social openness and receive less follow-up
