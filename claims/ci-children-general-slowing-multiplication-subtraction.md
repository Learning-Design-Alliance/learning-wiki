# Children with cochlear implants are generally slower than hearing peers in multiplication and subtraction but show comparable error profiles

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Children with CI had significantly longer response latencies than NH controls in the multiplication verification task (3516 ms vs. 2537 ms) with no group differences in error rates or error-type profiles. [→ Pixner 2014](#pixner-2014)
`q3 i?` Children with CI were generally slower in the subtraction choice reaction task (6691 ms vs. 5265 ms), with no reliable borrowing or group×borrowing effects on latencies. [→ Pixner 2014 (2)](#pixner-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Pixner 2014

Pixner, S., Leyrer, M., and Moeller, K. (2014). Number processing and arithmetic skills in children with cochlear implants. Frontiers in Psychology 5:1479. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01479

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

PC-administered multiplication verification task with 80 one-digit problems in 45 CI and 49 NH children; ANOVA on latencies with CE functioning as covariate showed "latencies of children with CI were longer than those of NH controls". Error-rate ANOVAs revealed no significant effects (all F < 1).

> "the reliable main effect of participant group [F(1,91) = 9.45, p < 0.01] indicated that latencies of children with CI were longer than those of NH controls (3516 ms vs. 2537 ms, respectively"

### Pixner 2014 (2)

Pixner, S., Leyrer, M., and Moeller, K. (2014). Number processing and arithmetic skills in children with cochlear implants. Frontiers in Psychology 5:1479. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01479

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Two-digit subtraction choice reaction task (40 problems) in the same matched groups; ANOVA on latencies with CE covariate found only the group main effect reliable, with "responses of children with CI responded were generally slower". Borrowing and interaction effects on latencies were not reliable (both F < 1.26).

> "For response latencies only the main effect of participant group was reliable [F(1,91) = 12.82, p < 0.01]. This indicated that responses of children with CI responded were generally slower as compared to the responses of the control group (6691 ms vs. 5265 ms, respectively)."
