## Evidence

### Ben Van Dusen 2011

Ben Van Dusen, Mike Ross, and Valerie Otero. (2011). Changing Identities and Evolving Conceptions of Inquiry through Teacher-Driven Professional Development. [doi:10.1063/1.3680073](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3680073)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Analysis of the group's emails across early, middle, and late periods; total emails per month ranged from eight to fifty-four. With the exception of the final month, the percentage of teacher-originated emails increased. In the middle period teachers primarily asked for assistance or acted as resources for one another.

> "Initially, the researchers sent the majority of the emails, but over time the teachers began to send a larger share of the total number of emails."

### Ben Van Dusen 2011 (2)

Ben Van Dusen, Mike Ross, and Valerie Otero. (2011). Changing Identities and Evolving Conceptions of Inquiry through Teacher-Driven Professional Development. [doi:10.1063/1.3680073](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3680073)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Video coding of forty-two hours of meetings, with primary agenda items weighted one point and secondary items 0.5 points. In the late period "the teachers provided the majority of the agenda items." A focus group interview confirmed teachers were aware of these role changes.

> "researchers provided all of the meeting agenda items during the early time period. The middle time period shows substantial growth in the percentage of agenda items provided by teachers."
