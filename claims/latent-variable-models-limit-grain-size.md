# Multidimensional latent variable models rarely support more than about four to six latent variables, limiting fine-grained SKIVE modeling

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The literature on multidimensional latent variable models shows it is numerically very challenging to differentiate multiple latent dimensions; successfully estimated models contain at most about four to six latent variables, and highly correlated within-domain dimensions may produce unreliable scores. [→ Rupp 2010](#rupp-2010)

## Evidence

### Rupp 2010

Rupp, A.A., Gushta, M., Mislevy, R.J., & Shaffer, D.W. (2010). Evidence-centered Design of Epistemic Games: Measurement Principles for Complex Learning Environments. Journal of Technology, Learning, and Assessment, 8(4). http://www.jtla.org

`q2 · i?`

The paper's evidence-model section reports, citing Haberman (2008) and Sinharay, Haberman, and Puhan (2007), that multidimensional models estimated with real data sets rarely exceed four to six latent variables and that highly correlated dimensions yield unreliable scores.

> "Most latent variable models that are estimated successfully with real data sets contain between four and six latent variables at the most. Moreover, when the latent variables represent multiple latent characteristics within a single domain, they are often highly correlated and may also produce relatively unreliable scores (e.g., Haberman, 2008; Sinharay, Haberman, & Puhan, 2007)."
