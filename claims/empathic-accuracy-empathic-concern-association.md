## Evidence

### Kral 2018

Kral, T. R. A., Stodola, D. E., Birn, R. M., Mumford, J. A., Solis, E., Flook, L., Patsenko, E. G., Anderson, C. G., Steinkuehler, C. & Davidson, R. J. (2018). Neural correlates of video game empathy training in adolescents: a randomized trial. npj Science of Learning 3:13. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0029-6

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Baseline correlational analysis across the randomized trial sample confirmed a cross-sectional relationship between the behavioral empathic accuracy task and the Interpersonal Reactivity Index empathic concern subscale, r = 0.28, p = 0.016.

> "participants with higher empathic accuracy had higher self-reported empathic concern ( t(72) = 2.47, p = 0.016, b = 4.64, CI = [0.90, 8.39], r = 0.28)"

### Kral 2018 (2)

Kral, T. R. A., Stodola, D. E., Birn, R. M., Mumford, J. A., Solis, E., Flook, L., Patsenko, E. G., Anderson, C. G., Steinkuehler, C. & Davidson, R. J. (2018). Neural correlates of video game empathy training in adolescents: a randomized trial. npj Science of Learning 3:13. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0029-6

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Change-score correlational analysis found support for the hypothesized association between gains in empathic accuracy and gains in self-reported empathic concern, r = 0.30, p = 0.03; the group difference in empathic concern change itself was non-significant (t(68) = 0.15, p = 0.88).
