# In mathematics tutoring, the support dimension was positively associated with satisfaction with school, while structure and challenge did not predict grades

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Structural equation modelling showed support in mathematics tutoring positively associated with school satisfaction (small effect) and tutoring intensity negatively associated, with no significant effects of quality on grades. [→ Guill Karin 2019](#guill-karin-2019)

## Evidence

### Guill Karin 2019

Guill Karin, Lüdtke Oliver, Köller Olaf. (2019). Assessing the instructional quality of private tutoring and its effects on student outcomes: Analyses from the German National Educational Panel Study. The British Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12281

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Structural equation model on NEPS mathematics tutoring data (N = 1,722; model fit CFI = .931, RMSEA = .035), regressing end-of-year grades and school and family satisfaction on the latent quality factors with prior satisfaction and grades controlled. Only the support path to school satisfaction was significant and "the effects were small".

> "Support in private tutoring was positively associated with satisfaction with school, while tutoring intensity was negatively associated with satisfaction with school. However, the effects were small."
