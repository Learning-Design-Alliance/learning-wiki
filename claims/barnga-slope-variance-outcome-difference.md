# Peer-evaluated effectiveness shows significant slope variance across participants, whereas self-reported effectiveness shows no significant development across rounds

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For self-reported effectiveness, mean slope and slope variance were non-significant, indicating no significant development across rounds. [→ Hofhuis 2020](#hofhuis-2020)
`q3 i?` For peer-evaluated effectiveness, slope variance was significant (σ² = .42, p < .001), showing development differs between participants and can be positive or negative. [→ Hofhuis 2020 (2)](#hofhuis-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Hofhuis 2020

Hofhuis, J., Schilderman, M. F., & Verdooren, A. (2020). Multicultural personality and effectiveness in an intercultural training simulation: The role of stress and pro‐active communication. International Journal of Psychology, 55(5), 812–821. https://doi.org/10.1002/ijop.12647

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Model construction analysis of intercept and slope variance before hypothesis testing among 116 participants. Self-reported effectiveness showed "no significant development across rounds on this outcome variable".

> "The mean slope was not significant (M slope = .21; SE = .65; p = .748), and neither was its variance (σ 2 slope = .02; SE = .09; p = .858), indicating no significant development across rounds on this outcome variable."

### Hofhuis 2020 (2)

Hofhuis, J., Schilderman, M. F., & Verdooren, A. (2020). Multicultural personality and effectiveness in an intercultural training simulation: The role of stress and pro‐active communication. International Journal of Psychology, 55(5), 812–821. https://doi.org/10.1002/ijop.12647

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same variance decomposition found significant slope variance for peer-evaluated effectiveness ("σ 2 slope = .42; SE = .11; p < .001"), making individual improvement trajectories a meaningful outcome for predictor analysis.

> "we did find significant slope variance (σ 2 slope = .42; SE = .11; p < .001), which shows that the degree to which other‐rated effectiveness develops over time, differs between participants, and can be both positive and negative."
