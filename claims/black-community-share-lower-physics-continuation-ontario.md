# The proportion of Black residents in a school's community is the only demographic factor that significantly predicts lower physics continuation rates for both grade 11 and grade 12

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Eamonn Corrigan 2024

Eamonn Corrigan, Martin Williams, and Mary A. Wells. (2024). Beyond gender: The intersectional impact of community demographics on the continuation rates of male and female students into high school physics. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1139/cjp-2023-0319](https://doi.org/10.1139/cjp-2023-0319)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Mixed-effects linear regression (RQ1) on eleven years of Ontario school-level enrolment data linked to Census demographics via Forward Sortation Area. The Black-proportion estimate was negative and significant for both grades: "β11 = −1.06 ± 0.24" and "β12 = −0.81 ± 0.24"; no standardised effect size is printed.

> "Finally, the proportion of Black individuals within a community was the the sole predictor variable which was negative and statistically significant for both grade 11 ( β11 = −1.06 ± 0.24) and grade 12 physics (β12 = −0.81 ± 0.24)."
