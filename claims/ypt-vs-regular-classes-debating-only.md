## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A t-test on students' self-reported usefulness found YPT-related activities significantly more useful than regular physics classes only for debating skills (t = -2.188, p = 0.031); differences for the other five soft skills were not statistically significant. [→ Aneva 2022](#aneva-2022)

## Evidence

### Aneva 2022

Aneva, B., Faletič, S., Hömöstrei, M., Jenei, P., Kundracik, F., Kyuldjiev, A., Lindner, T., Němec, H., Puhr, H., & Plesch, M. (2022). Development of Soft Skills via IYPT. Wirtschaftsuniversität Wien. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2210.09648](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2210.09648)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Paired t-tests on student survey ratings compared regular classes vs. YPT activities per soft skill; the printed table shows for Debating skills t = -2.188, df = 99, p = 0.031, and non-significant results for the other five skills.

> "Only in the case of “Debating skills”, we find that students perceived YPT-related activities as significantly more useful than regular physics classes. For all other types of soft skills, we did not find statistically significant differences (p ≤ 0.10)."
