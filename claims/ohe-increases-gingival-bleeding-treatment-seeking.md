# Choices for treatment-seeking behaviours for gingival bleeding rose from pre-test to post-test after both courses among non-dental students, in one uncontrolled pre-post study, with visiting a dentist becoming the top choice

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After the traditional and e-learning courses, non-dental students significantly increased choices to visit the dentist, use functional toothpaste and rinse for gingival bleeding. [→ Wu Zhiwu 2021](#wu-zhiwu-2021)

## Evidence

### Wu Zhiwu 2021

Wu Zhiwu, Li Mingming, Zhu Fangzhi, Lei Lei, Cheng Ran, Hu Tao. (2021). The effects of oral health education regarding periodontal health on non-dental undergraduates in southwestern China—exploring the feasibility of an e-learning course for oral health promotion. BMC Oral Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-021-01476-5

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Chi-square/Fisher's exact comparisons of treatment choices for gingival bleeding before and after each course (Fig. 2). The article reports a "significant increase" in choosing to visit the dentist, use functional toothpaste and rinse in both course groups, with the printed P values as listed.

> "After the course, there was a significant increase in the number of non-dental students who chose to visit the dentist (traditional course: P < 0.001; e-learning course: P < 0.001), use functional toothpaste (traditional course: P < 0.001; e-learning course: P < 0.001) and rinse (traditional course: P = 0.001; e-learning course: P < 0.001)."
