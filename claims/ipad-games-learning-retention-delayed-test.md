## Evidence

### Najmuldeen 2017

Najmuldeen, H. A. (2017). The Impact of Educational Games-Based iPad Applications on the Development of Social Studies Achievement and Learning Retention among Sixth Grade Students in Jeddah. World Journal of Education, 7(3). https://doi.org/10.5430/wje.v7n3p21

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Delayed achievement test applied to both groups (24 each) with ANCOVA pre-control. The article is inconsistent on its timing: the abstract and procedural definition say four weeks after the post-test, but the procedure says "Six weeks later" and the appendix schedule dates the post-test 22/2/2016 and the delayed test 4/4/2016, six weeks apart. Adjusted delayed total means were 15.16 (experimental) versus 10.59 (control); the article reports the difference as statistically significant with no effect size in this sentence.

> "Table (8) shows statistically significant differences among means of both groups' delayed achievement test at the levels of (memorizing, comprehension and application)"

### Najmuldeen 2017 (2)

Najmuldeen, H. A. (2017). The Impact of Educational Games-Based iPad Applications on the Development of Social Studies Achievement and Learning Retention among Sixth Grade Students in Jeddah. World Journal of Education, 7(3). https://doi.org/10.5430/wje.v7n3p21

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Total-test row of the delayed-test ANOVA table (Table 8) prints F = 43.09, significance 0.001, and Eta Square 0.49 (η² = 0.49) for the between-groups difference, a large printed effect size favoring the experimental group. The delayed test was given six weeks after the post-test by the article's procedure and dated schedule (four weeks by its abstract).
