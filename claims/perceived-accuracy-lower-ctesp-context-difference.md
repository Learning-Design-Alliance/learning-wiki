## Evidence

### Mendonça PC 2026

Mendonça PC, Quintal F, Figueiredo M and Mendonça F (2026) Perceived usefulness and intention to use large language model-generated feedback across three educational levels: a user-centred study in programming. Front. Educ. 11:1934069. https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2026.1934069

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Kruskal–Wallis comparison of the three educational contexts on response-level dimensions; only accuracy was significant [H(2) = 10.90, p = 0.022], with Dunn comparisons showing "accuracy was rated higher in secondary education (M = 4.63) than in CTeSP". A gender-adjusted sensitivity analysis confirmed the CTeSP difference (b = −0.40).

> "accuracy was rated higher in secondary education (M = 4.63) than in CTeSP (M = 4.20; z = 3.07, p = 0.007, rank-biserial = 0.49) and higher in undergraduate education (M = 4.42) than in CTeSP (z = 2.63, p = 0.017, rank-biserial = 0.28)"

### Mendonça PC 2026 (2)

Mendonça PC, Quintal F, Figueiredo M and Mendonça F (2026) Perceived usefulness and intention to use large language model-generated feedback across three educational levels: a user-centred study in programming. Front. Educ. 11:1934069. https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2026.1934069

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Kruskal–Wallis comparisons of the six report-level dimensions across the three contexts found no significant differences after Holm correction; the smallest adjusted p-value was for cognitive load [H(2) = 8.32, p = 0.094].

> "At the report level, no dimension differed across contexts after the Holm correction, as shown in Table 6, and the Welch analysis of variance agreed with this result."
