# About 27 percent of 2018 graduates statewide met all CCR graduation requirements, with gaps by socioeconomic status, English learner status, race/ethnicity, and prior achievement

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The percentage of on-time graduates meeting all CCR requirements rose from 15 percent in 2013/14 to 27 percent in 2017/18, and no student group exceeded 36 percent. [→ Hanson 2020](#hanson-2020)

### Hanson 2020

Hanson, H., & Fantz, T. (2020). Implementation of career- and college-ready requirements for high school graduation in Washington (REL 2020–020). Washington, DC: U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Regional Educational Laboratory Northwest. https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/resource-library/report/descriptive-study/implementation-career-and-college-ready-requirements-high-school-graduation-washington

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Descriptive analysis of transcript and graduation data for 365,485 students who began grade 9 between 2010/11 and 2014/15, shown in figure 4 for graduating classes 2013/14 through 2017/18. The share meeting all CCR requirements rose from 15 to 27 percent.

> "The percentage of on-time graduates who met all the CCR graduation requirements increased steadily with each graduating class, from 15 percent in 2013/14 to 27 percent in 2017/18 (figure 4)."

### Hanson 2020 (2)

Hanson, H., & Fantz, T. (2020). Implementation of career- and college-ready requirements for high school graduation in Washington (REL 2020–020). Washington, DC: U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Regional Educational Laboratory Northwest. https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/resource-library/report/descriptive-study/implementation-career-and-college-ready-requirements-high-school-graduation-washington

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Descriptive analysis of 2017/18 graduate data by student group (figure 5). The report also prints 16 percent of current English learner students versus 28 percent of never English learner students, 29 percent of White students versus 20–27 percent of students of color, and 21 versus 30 percent for bottom- versus top-quartile grade 8 assessment scorers.

> "Gaps were most evident by socioeconomic status — 36 percent of students in low- poverty schools met all the requirements compared with 10 percent of students in high-poverty schools. Similarly, 31 percent of students who had never been eligible for the national school lunch program met all the requirements compared with 23 percent of students who had ever been eligible."
