# After retrieval practice, undergraduates score significantly higher on lower-order thinking questions than on higher-order thinking questions about a studied passage

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Regardless of retrieval format, reading comprehension was significantly better for lower-order thinking questions (M = 2.93) than higher-order thinking questions (M = 2.62), F(1, 98) = 9.88, p = .002. [→ Nero 2021](#nero-2021)

## Evidence

### Nero 2021

Nero, C. A., & Zulkiply, N. (2021). The Effects of Retrieval Practice Across Levels of Thinking and Retention Interval on Reading Comprehension. Asian Journal of University Education (AJUE), 17(4). https://doi.org/10.24191/ajue.v17i4.16222

`q3 · i?`

Within-subjects levels-of-thinking factor of the classroom experiment (100 undergraduates; five lower-order and five higher-order items per format, validated by the course instructor). Results: "significantly better for lower-order thinking questions (M = 2.93) compared to higher-order thinking questions (M = 2.62)", F(1, 98) = 9.88, p = .002.

> "results showed that there was a significant difference, F(1, 98) = 9.88, p = .002, indicating that by using retrieval practice during the retrieval practice phase regardless of the formats used, students’ performance in reading comprehension was significantly better for lower-order thinking questions (M = 2.93) compared to higher-order thinking questions (M = 2.62)."
