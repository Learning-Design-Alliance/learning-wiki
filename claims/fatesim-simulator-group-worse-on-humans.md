## Evidence

### Weimer Johannes Matthias 2024

Weimer Johannes Matthias, Sprengart Franziska Marietta, Vieth Thomas, Göbel Sebastian, Dionysopoulou Anna, Krüger Rebecca, Beer Jan, Weimer Andreas Michael, Buggenhagen Holger, Kloeckner Roman, Pillong Lukas, Helfrich Johanna, Waezsada Elias, Wand Philipp, Weinmann-Menke Julia. (2024). Simulator training in focus assessed transthoracic echocardiography (FATE) for undergraduate medical students: results from the FateSim randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06564-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the randomized trial's practical test at T2, the Direct Observation of Procedural Skills test on human subjects showed the human-trained control group "achieved significantly better results" than the simulator-trained study group (p < 0.01).

> "The control group achieved significantly better results in the DOPSHuman (study: 59 ± 10 vs. control: 64 ± 9; p < 0.01)."

### Weimer Johannes Matthias 2024 (2)

Weimer Johannes Matthias, Sprengart Franziska Marietta, Vieth Thomas, Göbel Sebastian, Dionysopoulou Anna, Krüger Rebecca, Beer Jan, Weimer Andreas Michael, Buggenhagen Holger, Kloeckner Roman, Pillong Lukas, Helfrich Johanna, Waezsada Elias, Wand Philipp, Weinmann-Menke Julia. (2024). Simulator training in focus assessed transthoracic echocardiography (FATE) for undergraduate medical students: results from the FateSim randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06564-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The study's multivariate linear regression across the practical examinations found that only performance on human subjects was significantly influenced by group membership, with "membership of the control group" (β = 4.76; p = 0.005) the sole significant predictor; the β is unstandardised, so no effect size is reported.

> "In the linear regression analysis of DOPSSim, DOPSSimPatho, and DOPSHuman, only DOPSHuman was significantly influenced by “membership of the control group” (β = 4.76; p = 0.005), with no other influencing factors detected."
