## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In expert-rated parameter tuning of 21 skip-gram/t-SNE visualizations, the highest rated visualization came from window size five and vector size 12; low vector sizes yielded excessively sequential plots and low window sizes yielded plots with almost no connections. ## Evidence

### Pardos 2019

Pardos, Z. A., & Horodyskyj, L. (2019). Analysis of Student Behaviour in Habitable Worlds Using Continuous Representation Visualization. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.18608/jla.2019.61.1](https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2019.61.1)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Parameter tuning study in which 21 visualizations varying window size (1-5) and vector size (2-32) were rated on a five-point usefulness scale by the course co-creator (Figure 4). The article reports "the highest rated visualization was produced by a skip-gram with a window size of five and a vector size of 12." Ratings are a single expert's judgments, not a statistical test.

> "In summary, the highest rated visualization was produced by a skip-gram with a window size of five and a vector size of 12. Other high vector sizes, paired with high window sizes, also yielded visualizations with useful information."
