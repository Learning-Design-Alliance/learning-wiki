# Delay discounting is negatively correlated with children's addictive use of digital devices, with the LDR proportion the only significant predictor in a controlled regression

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Children who chose the larger delayed reward less often (steeper discounting) showed more addictive use of digital devices (r = -0.28, p = 0.016), and the LDR proportion was the only significant predictor of DASC score (β = -0.27, p = 0.032) after controls. [→ Tim Schulz van Endert 2021](#tim-schulz-van-endert-2021)

## Evidence

### Tim Schulz van Endert 2021

Tim Schulz van Endert. (2021). Addictive use of digital devices in young children: Associations with delay discounting, self-control and academic performance. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0253058

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis of questionnaire data from 75 children aged 10–13 recruited from a public elementary school in Berlin; DASC and Monetary Choice Questionnaire scores correlated. "the more often children chose the larger delayed reward, the less they addictively used digital devices" (r = -0.28, p = 0.016).

> "A negative correlation (r = -0.28, p = 0.016) between the two variables was found. On average, the more often children chose the larger delayed reward, the less they addictively used digital devices."
