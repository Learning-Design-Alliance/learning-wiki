# Learners request additional information mainly on items where their (adjusted) confidence is low, indicating confidence-based metacognitive regulation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Within-subject correlations between post-adjustment confidence and information requests were strongly negative (mean coefficient − 0.44; d = 1.57). [→ Schnaubert 2021](#schnaubert-2021)

## Evidence

### Schnaubert 2021

Schnaubert, L., Krukowski, S., & Bodemer, D. (2021). Assumptions and confidence of others: the impact of socio-cognitive information on metacognitive self-regulation. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-021-09269-5

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Within-subject experiment testing hypothesis 3a via point-biserial rank correlations between post-adjustment confidence and information requests. The mean regulation coefficient was − 0.44 (SD = 0.28) and the t-test showed learners "regulate metacognitively by requesting information more frequently when their confidence is comparably low" (t(50) = − 11.21, p < 0.001, d = 1.57).

> "A one sample t-test (tested against zero) showed a highly significant result (t(50) = − 11.21, p < 0.001, d = 1.57) indicating that learners—on average—regulate metacognitively by requesting information more frequently when their confidence is comparably low."
