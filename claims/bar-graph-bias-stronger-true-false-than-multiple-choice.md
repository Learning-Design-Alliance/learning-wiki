# The biasing effect of seeing the bar graph is stronger for two-choice (true/false) questions (38%) than for four-choice multiple-choice questions (28%)

## Evidence

### Perez Kathryn E. 2010

Perez Kathryn E., Strauss Eric A., Downey Nicholas, Galbraith Anne, Jeanne Robert. (2010). Does Displaying the Class Results Affect Student Discussion during Peer Instruction? CBE Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.09-11-0080

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Chi-square analyses of the same crossover study split by question format found significant treatment differences for both two-choice (χ2 = 16.30; p = 0.001; N = 1926) and four-choice questions (χ2 = 26.22; p ≤ 0.0001; N = 2256). The text reports "38% more changing from LC to MC when given two choices" versus 28% for four choices.

> "The influence of seeing the bar graph was stronger for the two-choice than for the four-choice questions, with 38% more changing from LC to MC when given two choices (Figure 5C) versus a 28% increase when given four choices (Figure 5D)."
