## Evidence

### Farrokhi-Khajeh-Pasha Yasin 2012

Farrokhi-Khajeh-Pasha Yasin, Nedjat Saharnaz, Mohammadi Aeen, Rad Elaheh Malakan, Majdzadeh Reza, Monajemi Farshid, Jamali Ehsan, Yazdani Shahryar. (2012). The validity of Iran’s national university entrance examination (Konkoor) for predicting medical students’ academic performance. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-60

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Pearson correlation analysis of the national 2003 cohort (2126 matriculants; CBSE scores for 1759), relating preadmission Konkoor scores to centralized exam results. The article classifies r = 0.473 as "medium predictive validity coefficient effect size" per Cohen's calibration.

> "Konkoor total score had a medium predictive validity coefficient effect size for the CBSE (r = 0.473), the CPIE (r = 0.326) and msGPA(r =0.339)."

### Farrokhi-Khajeh-Pasha Yasin 2012 (2)

Farrokhi-Khajeh-Pasha Yasin, Nedjat Saharnaz, Mohammadi Aeen, Rad Elaheh Malakan, Majdzadeh Reza, Monajemi Farshid, Jamali Ehsan, Yazdani Shahryar. (2012). The validity of Iran’s national university entrance examination (Konkoor) for predicting medical students’ academic performance. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-60

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Stepwise multiple linear regression on the same national cohort using eight predictor sets; model A4 entered all eight Konkoor crude sub-scores, model A7 the total score alone, predicting CBSE performance.

> "All Konkoor sub-scores combined (A4) and Konkoor total score alone (A7) explained 23.3% and 22.3% of the variation in students’ performance on the CBSE, respectively."
