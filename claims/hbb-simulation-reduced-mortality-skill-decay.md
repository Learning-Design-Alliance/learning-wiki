## Evidence

### Robinson SJA 2024

Robinson SJA, Ritchie AMA, Pacilli M, Nestel D, McLeod E, Nataraja RM. (2024). Simulation-Based Education of Health Workers in Low- and Middle-Income Countries: A Systematic Review. Glob Health Sci Pract. https://doi.org/10.9745/GHSP-D-24-00187

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Vossius et al., on HBB low-technology mannequin and scenario-based simulation for neonatal resuscitation in Tanzania: "a 42% reduction in neonatal mortality" across 8 facilities. Reported as a review-narrated study result.

> "The program had success in 8 Tanzanian facilities, resulting in a 42% reduction in neonatal mortality."

### Robinson SJA 2024 (2)

Robinson SJA, Ritchie AMA, Pacilli M, Nestel D, McLeod E, Nataraja RM. (2024). Simulation-Based Education of Health Workers in Low- and Middle-Income Countries: A Systematic Review. Glob Health Sci Pract. https://doi.org/10.9745/GHSP-D-24-00187

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

The review attributes to Arlington et al. the finding of skill decay 4–6 months after HBB training, which "influenced other HBB initiatives to incorporate maintenance training." A maintained HBB program at Haydom Lutheran Hospital showed ongoing mortality improvements over 5 years.

> "However, Arlington et al. demonstrated skill decay 4–6 months after HBB training."
