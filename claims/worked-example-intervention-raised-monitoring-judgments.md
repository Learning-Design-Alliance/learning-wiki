# Adults randomly assigned to a worked-example educational intervention made higher post-intervention monitoring judgments on health math problems than control participants

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants in the intervention condition reported higher monitoring judgments than the control condition on all three post-intervention problems, controlling for gender and pretest monitoring judgments. [→ Scheibe 2022](#scheibe-2022)

## Evidence

### Scheibe 2022

Scheibe, D. A., Fitzsimmons, C. J., Mielicki, M. K., Taber, J. M., Sidney, P. G., Coifman, K., & Thompson, C. A. (2022). Confidence in COVID problem solving: What factors predict adults’ item-level metacognitive judgments on health-related math problems before and after an educational intervention? Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09300-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVAs in a randomized experiment with 1,177 Qualtrics-panel adults compared intervention vs. control monitoring judgments controlling for gender and pretest judgments. The intervention group was higher on all three posttest problems; e.g., "F(1, 1173) = 6.30, p = 0.012, partial η2 = 0.01" on problem 1.

> "On posttest problem 1, the intervention group reported higher monitoring judgments ( M = 83.89, SE = 0.84) than the control group ( M = 80.88, SE = 0.85), F(1, 1173) = 6.30, p = 0.012, partial η2 = 0.01."
