# SES shows a medium correlation with academic achievement at the student level and a much larger correlation when SES is aggregated at the school or district level

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` At the student level, the average corrected correlation between SES and achievement was about .27-.28, significantly different from zero. [→ Sirin 2005](#sirin-2005)
`q3 i3` At the aggregated school/district level, the average correlation was much larger (.64-.67) than at the student level. [→ Sirin 2005 (2)](#sirin-2005-2)

## Evidence

### Sirin 2005

Sirin, S. R. (2005). Socioeconomic Status and Academic Achievement: A Meta-Analytic Review of Research. Review of Educational Research, 75(3), 417-453. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543075003417

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analysis of 74 independent samples from 58 journal articles (1990-2000); student-level data included 101,157 students. The printed effect size was a mean correlation of r = .28 (fixed effects, 95% CI .28-.29); random-effects mean r = .27, described as "significantly different from zero".

> "For the samples with the student-level data, the average ES for the fixed effects model was .28 with a 95% confidence interval of .28 to .29, and it was significantly different from zero"

### Sirin 2005 (2)

Sirin, S. R. (2005). Socioeconomic Status and Academic Achievement: A Meta-Analytic Review of Research. Review of Educational Research, 75(3), 417-453. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543075003417

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Aggregated-level data covered 6,871 schools and 128 school districts; correlations ranged from .11 to .85 with a mean of .60. The printed effect size was a mean correlation of r = .67 (fixed effects) and r = .64 (random effects), both "significantly different from zero".

> "The average ES for the fixed effects modelwas .67 with a 95% confidence inter- val of .66 to .67, and it was significantly different from zero (z = 147.56, p \< .001)."
