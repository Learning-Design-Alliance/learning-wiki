# Individual predictive correlations are weak (mostly 0.16–0.24) but the overall pattern of significant relationships exceeds chance (51 of 380, 13.4%)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The majority of statistically significant correlations were in the range 0.16 to 0.24, with only a few exceeding 0.30, yet 51 of 380 possible relationships (13.4%) were significant at the 5% level versus a 5% chance proportion. [→ Adam Jane 2012](#adam-jane-2012)

## Evidence

### Adam Jane 2012

Adam Jane, Bore Miles, McKendree Jean, Munro Don, Powis David. (2012). Can personal qualities of medical students predict in-course examination success and professional behaviour? An exploratory prospective cohort study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-69

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The authors' summary of the correlation matrices from the cohort study reports correlations "in the range 0.16 to 0.24" and only a few above 0.30, "accounting for only 9% of the variance"; no standardised effect size statistic was printed, so no magnitude code is assigned.

> "The individual predictive ability of any of the measures (of cognitive skills, non-cognitive traits and of behaviour) appears weak, the majority of the statistically significant correlations being in the range 0.16 to 0.24 and only a few exceeded 0.30 (accounting for only 9% of the variance)."

### Adam Jane 2012 (2)

Adam Jane, Bore Miles, McKendree Jean, Munro Don, Powis David. (2012). Can personal qualities of medical students predict in-course examination success and professional behaviour? An exploratory prospective cohort study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-69

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The authors counted significance rates against chance to argue the pattern is real: "51 (or 13.4%) were significant at the 5% level or better" of 380 possible relationships, exceeding the 5% chance proportion. Supplementary regression and resampling checks added no stronger support.

> "of the 380 possible relationships that could have been included in these tables (19 personality scales by 20 tutor ratings), 51 (or 13.4%) were significant at the 5% level or better; this becomes 18.2% of 280, if only the 14 personality scales actually appearing in the tables are considered."
