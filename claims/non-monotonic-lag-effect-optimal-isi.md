# The lag effect is non-monotonic: beyond an optimal ISI, further increases in ISI reduce final-test accuracy for a given retention interval

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For a given retention interval there is a non-zero ISI that optimizes accuracy; longer ISIs in proportion to retention interval reduce accuracy. [→ Cepeda 2006](#cepeda-2006)

## Evidence

### Cepeda 2006

Cepeda, Nicholas J., Pashler, Harold, Vul, Edward, Wixted, John, & Rohrer, Doug. (2006). Distributed Practice in Verbal Recall Tasks: A Review and Quantitative Synthesis. Psychological Bulletin. https://escholarship.org/uc/item/3rr6q10c. [doi:10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354)

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Synthesis across the meta-analysis's difference, absolute, and within-study lag analyses of verbal recall studies; the article reports that beyond the optimum "further increases in ISI reduced accuracy", characterizing the function as non-monotonic.

> "When ISI was increased, participants retained more information. However, for long ISIs, in proportion to retention interval, further increases in ISI reduced accuracy. Thus, for a given retention interval, there was a non-zero value of ISI that optimized accuracy. (This is known as a non-monotonic lag effect.)"
