# Cyberbully-victims did not differ significantly from pure cyberbullies or pure cybervictims in perceptions or reported feelings, nor did their own perceptions differ from their reported feelings.

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Cyberbully-victims did not differ from pure cyberbullies on expected victims' feelings (t (34) = 1.59, p = .120), from pure cybervictims on reported feelings (t (82) = 0.52, p = .607), or between their own perceptions and feelings (t (15) = -1.28, p = .221). ## Evidence

### Ana M. Giménez Gualdo 2014

Ana M. Giménez Gualdo, Simon C. Hunter, Kevin Durkin, Pilar Arnaiz, and Javier J. Maquilón. (2014). The emotional impact of cyberbullying: Differences in perceptions and experiences as a function of role. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2014.11.013

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Three non-significant t-test comparisons involving the cyberbully-victim subgroup in the survey sample, including their within-group comparison of perceived versus experienced impact, t (15) = -1.28, p = .221. No effect sizes are printed; equivalence was not tested.

> "Cyberbully-victims did not differ from pure cyberbullies on how they thought victims would feel, t (34) = 1.59, p = .120 (two-tailed). Neither did cyberbully-victims differ from pure cybervictims when reporting how they actually felt when cyberbullied, t (82) = 0.52, p = .607 (two-tailed)."
