# Parasympathetic stress relief marker (HF) is significantly lower in peer simulation than individual simulation during the first patient assessment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` During the first patient assessment phase, the HF component of heart rate variability was significantly decreased in the peer simulation group relative to the individual simulation group (p = .015), indicating greater stress in the peer group. [→ Nakayama N 2021](#nakayama-n-2021)

## Evidence

### Nakayama N 2021

Nakayama N, Ejiri H, Arakawa N, Makino T. (2021). Stress and anxiety in nursing students between individual and peer simulations. Nursing Open. https://doi.org/10.1002/nop2.680

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Observational comparison of Holter ECG heart rate variability in third-year nursing students (individual group n = 50; peer group n = 59) during the first patient assessment phase. The HF component, an indicator of parasympathetic activity, was "significantly decreased relative to that of participants in the individual simulation (p = .015)"; no effect size was printed.

> "During the first patient assessment phase, no significant difference was found in HR between the two groups (p = .263) (Figure 1), but the HF component of participants in the peer simulation was significantly decreased relative to that of participants in the individual simulation (p = .015)"
