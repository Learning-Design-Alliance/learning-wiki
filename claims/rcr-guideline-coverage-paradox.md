# Not teaching ethical guidelines predicted better combined learning outcomes, while guideline coverage supported only knowledge

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Johannes Katsarov 2021

Johannes Katsarov, Roberto Andorno, André Krom, Mariëtte van den Hoven. (2021). Effective Strategies for Research Integrity Training—a Meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review (2022) 34:935–955. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09630-9

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Pooled meta-regression of all 75 effect sizes (F = 8.62, p = 0.000); Table 4 reports a coefficient of 0.35* [0.09, 0.61] for not teaching ethical guidelines versus superficial coverage. The coefficient is a regression coefficient, not a standardized effect size.

> "Significant positive effects were found for a practical engagement with conc rete cases, for courses that did not teach ethical guidelines, and for courses of more than 5 h (in comparison to shorter courses)."

### Johannes Katsarov 2021 (2)

Johannes Katsarov, Roberto Andorno, André Krom, Mariëtte van den Hoven. (2021). Effective Strategies for Research Integrity Training—a Meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review (2022) 34:935–955. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09630-9

`q1 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Authors' interpretive explanation offered in the Discussion for why guideline coverage did not benefit higher-order outcomes; it is a theoretical interpretation attributed to reactance theory, not a tested result in this article.

> "An explanation for this paradoxical finding could be that attitudinal and behavioral learning is hampered through reactance when people are expected to adopt evaluations that they have not concluded autonomously (Worchel and Brehm, 1971)."
