# Chicago's double-dose algebra policy raised algebra test scores for both low-skill and high-skill students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students in double-dose algebra classes showed significantly higher algebra test scores after the policy, despite declines in peer skill levels. [→ Claire Durwood 2010](#claire-durwood-2010)
`q3 i?` Higher-skill students in single-period algebra also improved, benefiting from more homogeneous classrooms with fewer behavioral and attendance problems. [→ Claire Durwood 2010 (2)](#claire-durwood-2010-2)

## Evidence

### Claire Durwood 2010

Claire Durwood, Emily Krone, and Christopher Mazzeo. (2010). Are Two Algebra Classes Better Than One? The Effects of Double-Dose Instruction in Chicago. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. http://ccsr.uchicago.edu/content/page.php?cat=3&content_id=34

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental study (regression discontinuity and time series designs) of five cohorts of first-time ninth-graders in Chicago Public Schools, 2000-2004. The brief reports that "Test scores rose significantly for students in double-dose algebra classes" on the fall tenth-grade PLAN exam.

> "Test scores rose significantly for students in double-dose algebra classes despite declines in peer skill levels and greater concentrations of students with attendance and disciplinary problems."

### Claire Durwood 2010 (2)

Claire Durwood, Emily Krone, and Christopher Mazzeo. (2010). Are Two Algebra Classes Better Than One? The Effects of Double-Dose Instruction in Chicago. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. http://ccsr.uchicago.edu/content/page.php?cat=3&content_id=34

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same CCSR study reports gains for higher-skill students in single-period classes: "the more homogenous classroom environment also produced more demanding classes," with fewer students having behavioral and attendance problems creating a better learning environment.

> "For higher-skill students, the more homogenous classroom environment also produced more demanding classes, with teachers covering more difficult material than in the past."
