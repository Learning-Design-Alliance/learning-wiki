# In introductory physics courses, the learning gap favors dominant students without LAs but is significantly reversed in courses with LAs (pooled across concept inventories)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In courses without LAs, the learning gap was significantly negative: dominant students outperformed their non-dominant peers (p<0.005). [→ Van Dusen 2016](#van-dusen-2016)
`q2 i?` In courses with LAs, the learning gap was significantly positive: non-dominant students outperformed dominant students (p<0.025). [→ Van Dusen 2016 (2)](#van-dusen-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Van Dusen 2016

Van Dusen, B., White, J.-S. S., & Roualdes, E. A. (2016). The impact of learning assistants on inequities in physics student outcomes. 2016 Physics Education Research Conference Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.085

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Paired t-tests with Bonferroni corrections on cleaned LASSO data (2,868 pre-post pairs from 67 courses in 16 institutions) compared the learning gap, defined as non-dominant students' mean effect size minus dominant students' mean effect size, in courses without LAs. The "learning gap was significantly negative" (t597.6=3.67; p<0.005). No effect size for the gap itself is printed.

> "The learning gap was significantly negative (i.e. dominant students outperformed their non-dominant peers) in courses without LAs (Fig. 2a: t597.6=3.67; p<0.005)."

### Van Dusen 2016 (2)

Van Dusen, B., White, J.-S. S., & Roualdes, E. A. (2016). The impact of learning assistants on inequities in physics student outcomes. 2016 Physics Education Research Conference Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.085

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same paired t-test analysis of the LASSO dataset, testing the learning gap in LA-supported physics courses. The gap was "significantly positive (i.e. non-dominant students outperformed dominant students)" (t2068.2=-2.63; p<0.025). The article prints no effect size for this contrast.

> "The learning gap was significantly positive (i.e. non-dominant students outperformed dominant students), however, in courses with LAs where (t2068.2=-2.63; p<0.025)."
