### Daniel V. Schroeder 2015

Daniel V. Schroeder. (2015). Interactive molecular dynamics. Am. J. Phys. 83 (3), 210–218. https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4901185

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

The author's design argument in the model section: a two-dimensional simulation lets the student see all simulated atoms at once and interact with them naturally with a mouse, requires fewer particles, and runs at a faster animation rate than a three-dimensional one. This is a reasoned design argument, not an empirical comparison.

> "However, a two-dimensional simulation can demonstrate most of the important physical principles equally well, and is preferable for many educational purposes."
