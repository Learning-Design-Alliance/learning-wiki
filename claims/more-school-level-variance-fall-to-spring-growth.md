# More of the variance in linear growth lies at the school level for fall-to-spring than for spring-to-spring estimates, in some cases twice or three times as much

## Evidence

### Soland 2019

Soland, J. & Thum, Y.M. (2019). School effectiveness, summer loss, and federal accountability: Applying the compound polynomial model in a program evaluation context. The Collaborative for Student Growth at NWEA Working Paper. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/school-effectiveness-summer-loss-and-federal-accountability-applying-the-compound-polynomialmodel-in-a-program-evaluation-context/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Intraclass correlation coefficients computed from the school- and student-level variance components of CP models (Table 3), including linear-only adaptations omitting the quadratic terms. The article reports the three-times result for reading centered at second grade.

> "These ICCs suggest that, across models and subjects, more of the variance in linear growth is at the school level when estimates use within-year rather than between-year growth.  Differences are most pronounced when the models do not include quadratic terms.  For example, for reading centered at second grade, three times as much of the variance is at the school level for fall-to-spring growth than spring-to-spring."
