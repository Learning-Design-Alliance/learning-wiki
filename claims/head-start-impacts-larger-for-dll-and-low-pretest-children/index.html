Evidence¶

Morris 2018¶

Morris, P. A., Connors, M., Friedman-Krauss, A., McCoy, D. C., Weiland, C., Feller, A., Page, L., Bloom, H., & Yoshikawa, H. (2018). New Findings on Impact Variation From the Head Start Impact Study: Informing the Scale-Up of Early Childhood Programs. AERA Open. https://doi.org/10.1177/2332858418769287

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

Subgroup moderation analysis with center fixed effects (Bloom & Weiland, 2015) of the HSIS randomized trial, with a printed vocabulary effect size of 0.26 SD for DLLs versus 0.10 SD for non-DLL peers. Compliance rates across the four subgroups ranged from 74.1% to 84.7%, judged too small to explain the subgroup differences.

"the effect size of Head Start random assignment on vocabulary was 0.26 SD (p < .01) for DLLs and 0.10 SD (p < .01) for their non-DLL peers, a 0.16-SD difference in impacts across groups."

Morris 2018 (2)¶

Morris, P. A., Connors, M., Friedman-Krauss, A., McCoy, D. C., Weiland, C., Feller, A., Page, L., Bloom, H., & Yoshikawa, H. (2018). New Findings on Impact Variation From the Head Start Impact Study: Informing the Scale-Up of Early Childhood Programs. AERA Open. https://doi.org/10.1177/2332858418769287

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Site-level predictor analysis of receptive vocabulary, the one outcome with substantial cross-site variability and a sizable differential effect for low-pretest DLLs. For sites where all students were low-pretest DLLs, the grand mean ITT effect was 0.40 SD versus 0.15 SD at the mean concentration.

"Bloom and Weiland (2015) estimated that about 15% of the cross-site effect size variance was explained by cross-site variation in the representation of low-pretest DLLs."

Discussion¶

The low-pretest advantage is shown within dual language learners: low-pretest DLL children had substantially larger effects than other DLL children on receptive vocabulary and early numeracy, with no such pattern by pretest score among English-only children. Across all children, the article's Table 4 shows a larger effect for low-pretest performers on early numeracy (0.20 vs 0.06 SD), but on receptive vocabulary it prints 0.02 SD for low-pretest performers against 0.09 SD for other performers, so the vocabulary advantage is not established for low-pretest children as a whole.
