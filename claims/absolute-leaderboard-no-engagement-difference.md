# On an absolute leaderboard, course engagement does not differ significantly across top, middle, and bottom ranked students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` There was no statistically significant difference in course engagement across the three position levels on an absolute leaderboard; equivalence was not tested. [→ Bai 2021](#bai-2021)

## Evidence

### Bai 2021

Bai, Shurui, Khe Foon Hew, Michael Sailer, and Chengyuan Jia. 2021. "From top to bottom: how positions on different types of leaderboard may affect fully online student learning performance, intrinsic motivation, and course engagement." Computers & Education 173: 104297. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2021.104297.

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-intervention SCEQ survey in the 24-student absolute leaderboard quasi-experiment. Descriptively the middle-ranked students were most engaged (M = 4.18, SD = 0.42) and top-ranked lowest (M = 3.96, SD = 0.31), but the ANOVA found "no significant difference in student course engagement across the three position levels".

> "The result showed that there was no significant difference in student course engagement across the three position levels, F (2, 17) = 0.55, p = .59. This result does not support hypothesis (H2a )."
