# AS and NS JOSs reliably predicted later solving outcomes, with AS JOSs predicting success and NS JOSs predicting failure

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r3` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Anagrams that received AS JOSs were more frequent among solved than not-solved anagrams, and NS JOSs were more frequent among not-solved than solved anagrams. [→ Burton 2023](#burton-2023)

## Evidence

### Burton 2023

Burton, O. R., Bodner, G. E., Williamson, P., & Arnold, M. M. (2023). How accurate and predictive are judgments of solvability? Explorations in a two-phase anagram solving paradigm. Metacognition and Learning, 18, 1–35. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09313-y

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r3`

Experiment 1 solved-versus-not-solved outcome analysis: AS JOS effect η²p = .37; NS JOSs showed the reverse pattern, F(1, 93) = 56.40, p < .001, η²p = .38. "Anagrams that had received an AS JOS were more frequent among solved anagrams than among not-solved anagrams".
