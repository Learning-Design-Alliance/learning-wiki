## Evidence

### Tsikas Stefanos A. 2024

Tsikas Stefanos A., Afshar Kambiz, Fischer Volkhard. (2024). Does voluntary practice improve the outcome of an OSCE in undergraduate medical studies? A Propensity Score Matching approach. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0312387

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive statistics from the questionnaire of 245 OSCE participants: 89% reported at least one free practice visit, averaging 3.22 visits (SD 2.68), and 90% emphasized the exam's role in their decision for FP.

> "In total, 89% of respondents reported visiting the Skills Lab for Free Practice (FP) at least once before the OSCE, with an average of 3.22 visits (SD: 2.68). The primary motivation behind utilizing the Skills lab was preparation for the OSCE, as highlighted by 90% of respondents"

### Tsikas Stefanos A. 2024 (2)

Tsikas Stefanos A., Afshar Kambiz, Fischer Volkhard. (2024). Does voluntary practice improve the outcome of an OSCE in undergraduate medical studies? A Propensity Score Matching approach. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0312387

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Authors' interpretation (type e) of the pattern across stations: significant effects for complex skills like PVC but not venipuncture, which they attribute to PVC entailing more steps that have to be considered. This is an explanation, not a tested result.

> "We think that the complexity of a task might explain (besides statistical explanations) the presence or absence of a positive effect of FP."
