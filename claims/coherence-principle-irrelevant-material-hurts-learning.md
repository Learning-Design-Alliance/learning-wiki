# Coherence Principle Irrelevant Material Hurts Learning

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

Adding interesting-but-irrelevant text, images, sounds, or music to instructional materials typically impairs learning by consuming working-memory resources without supporting the learning goal.

## Discussion

**Mechanism.** The coherence principle follows from [cognitive load theory](../theories/cognitive-load-theory.md): working memory has severely limited capacity, and seductive details — emotionally interesting but tangential material — compete for those resources, diverting attention away from essential content and disrupting the construction of a coherent mental model. This connects directly to [Cognitive overload degrades learning](cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) and [Chunking reduces working-memory load](chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md).

**Moderators and boundary conditions.** The effect is not uniform. Learners with higher prior knowledge may be less harmed by, or even benefit from, interesting extraneous material because they can more easily separate essential from inessential content — consistent with the [expertise reversal effect](../theories/expertise-reversal-effect.md). The harm also appears strongest when seductive details are inserted adjacent to (rather than separated from) core explanations, and when the material is high in perceptual salience (e.g., background music, decorative animation) rather than merely thematically related.

**Open questions.** Whether "interesting" adjuncts can ever pay for their cognitive cost through increased motivation and engagement remains contested; some researchers argue motivational gains may offset load costs under certain conditions, but the weight of experimental evidence favors removal of irrelevant material for novices.

**Design implication.** The constructive counterpart to deleting the irrelevant is cueing the relevant: rather than adding decorative content, use [signaling](signaling-improves-learning.md) to direct attention to essential material. In multimedia design this means pruning decorative images, background audio, and verbose anecdotes that do not serve the stated learning objective — see [Multimedia learning improves outcomes when aligned with cognitive theory](multimedia-principle-improves-learning.md).
