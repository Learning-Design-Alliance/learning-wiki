## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Subjective learning outcome ratings were significantly higher for Live-CCD than for Video-CCD and Paper-Cases (partial η2=0.236), which did not differ from each other. [→ Weidenbusch M 2019](#weidenbusch-m-2019)

## Evidence

### Weidenbusch M 2019

Weidenbusch M, Lenzer B, Sailer M, Strobel C, Kunisch R, Kiesewetter J, Fischer MR, Zottmann JM. (2019). Can clinical case discussions foster clinical reasoning skills in undergraduate medical education? A randomised controlled trial. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2018-025973

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Nine-item Likert questionnaire (Cronbach's α=0.95) administered at post-test in the RCT. Live-CCD rated highest, "F(2,85)=13.16, p<0.001, partial η2=0.236"; Video-CCD and Paper-Cases did not differ (Duncan test p=0.48).

> "Experimental groups differed significantly with respect to subjective learning outcomes (see table 1), F(2,85)=13.16, p<0.001, partial η2=0.236. Participants of the Live-CCD group (M=4.20; SD=0.63) assigned better ratings to their course format than participants in the Video-CCD group (M=3.18; SD=1.24) and the Paper-Cases group (M=3.00; SD=0.99)."
