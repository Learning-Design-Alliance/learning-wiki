# CACE estimators based on correct variance formulas and commonly used simplified formulas yield very similar significance findings across 10 education RCTs

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 causal `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Across 10 large-scale education RCTs, significance findings for the CACE estimator were very similar whether based on correct variance formulas or formulas ignoring certain sources of error. [→ Peter Z. Schochet 2011](#peter-z-schochet-2011)

`q2 i?` Empirical comparison across ten large-scale education RCTs indicates that the variance correction terms for the CACE estimator matter little for significance findings. [→ Peter Z. Schochet 2009](#peter-z-schochet-2009)

## Evidence

### Peter Z. Schochet 2011

Peter Z. Schochet, Hanley S. Chiang. (2011). Estimation and Identification of the Complier Average Causal Effect Parameter in Education RCTs. Journal of Educational and Behavioral Statistics, vol. 36, no. 3. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Estimation and Identification of the Complier Average Causal Effect Parameter in Education RCTs

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Comparative analysis of CACE significance findings across 10 large-scale education RCTs, contrasting correct variance formulas with commonly used formulas that ignore certain sources of error. The article reports the formulas "lead to very similar significance findings."

> "The authors use data from 10 large-scale RCTs to compare significance findings for the CACE estimator based on correct variance formulas with those based on commonly used formulas that ignore certain sources of error. These formulas lead to very similar significance findings."

### Peter Z. Schochet 2009

Peter Z. Schochet, Hanley Chiang. (2009). Technical Methods Report: Estimation and Identification of the Complier Average Causal Effect Parameter in Education RCTs. Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/technical-methods-report-estimation-and-identification-of-the-complier-average-causal-effect-parameter-in-education-rcts

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Empirical comparison across "ten large-scale RCTs" comparing CACE significance findings using correct versus commonly used variance formulas, in nominal and standard deviation units. The report states "the variance correction terms matter little".

> "The authors used data from ten large-scale RCTs to compare significance findings for the CACE estimator measured in nominal and standard deviation units using the correct variance formulas with those that are typically used in practice. The empirical results indicate that the variance correction terms matter little."
