## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Mallick R. 2020

Mallick R., Henderson T., Lahue B. J., Kafal A., Bassett P., Scalchunes C. (2020). Subcutaneous immunoglobulin in primary immunodeficiency – impact of training and infusion characteristics on patient-reported outcomes. BMC Immunology. [doi:10.1186/s12865-020-00371-y](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12865-020-00371-y)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression on the IDF survey cohort tested training characteristics against TSQM side effects tertile membership. "a higher confidence after training was associated with higher odds of being in the best tertile" (p = 0.004); Table 5 shows OR = 2.04 for confidence rated 6–7.

> "For side effects: a higher confidence after training was associated with higher odds of being in the best tertile (p = 0.004). The linear model revealed that fewer training sessions translated to a better side effect score (p = 0.05)."

### Mallick R. 2020 (2)

Mallick R., Henderson T., Lahue B. J., Kafal A., Bassett P., Scalchunes C. (2020). Subcutaneous immunoglobulin in primary immunodeficiency – impact of training and infusion characteristics on patient-reported outcomes. BMC Immunology. [doi:10.1186/s12865-020-00371-y](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12865-020-00371-y)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression on the same cohort tested training characteristics against TSQM convenience tertile membership. "higher confidence after training and absence of training barriers were associated with higher odds of being in the best tertile" (p = 0.001 and p = 0.04).

> "For convenience: higher confidence after training and absence of training barriers were associated with higher odds of being in the best tertile (p = 0.001 and p = 0.04, respectively)."
