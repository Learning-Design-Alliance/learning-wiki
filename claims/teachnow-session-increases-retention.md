# A single teacher-initiated TeachNow session increases student course progression and retention in a MOOC

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who received a single TeachNow session showed sustained 40% higher course progression than a propensity-matched control group, driven by reduced dropout. [→ Ali Malik 2024](#ali-malik-2024)

## Evidence

### Ali Malik 2024

Ali Malik, Juliette Woodrow, Chao Wang, and Chris Piech. 2024. TeachNow: Enabling Teachers to Provide Spontaneous, Realtime 1:1 Help in Massive Online Courses. In Proceedings of the 2024 Innovation and Technology in Computer Science Education V. 1 (ITiCSE 2024). https://doi.org/10.1145/3649217.3653629

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In a randomised control trial embedded in a six-week online CS1 course with around 9,000 students, 35% of students were eligible to be nudged during Weeks 2-3 and helped students were compared to propensity-matched controls working on the same assignment within ±1% course progress. The article reports "a sustained 40% higher course progression than the control group". No standardised effect size is printed.

> "These results indicate that students who received a single TeachNow session had a sustained 40% higher course progression than the control group."
