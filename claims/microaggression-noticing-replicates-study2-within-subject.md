## Evidence

### Civitillo 2025

Civitillo, S., S. Glock, L. A. Naudszus, L. Venhoff, D. Lieck, and P. Jugert. (2025). Investigating Preservice Teachers' Competence to Notice Ethnic Microaggressions in the Classroom. Journal of Community Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1002/jcop.70056

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r3`

Preregistered within-subject online experiment (Study 2, N = 184) using only the school performance vignettes. Low-ambiguity (M = 6.20, SD = 1.14) exceeded high-ambiguity (M = 3.93, d = 1.89), which exceeded control (M = 1.71, d = 1.85); the belonging effect was also significant, ηp² = 0.58.

> "The results showed that ambiguity had a significant main effect on perceived microaggressions, F(2, 366) = 603.72, p < 0.001, η p 2 = 0.77, supporting H1a."
