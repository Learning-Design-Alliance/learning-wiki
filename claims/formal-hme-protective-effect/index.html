Latent interaction SEMs (Models 2A–4H series) testing formal HME at ages 2, 5 and Grade 1 as moderator of parental skill effects on children's Grade 1 and 2 arithmetic fluency; the significant standardized parent skill × formal HME interaction estimates were negative, ranging from -.26*** to -.60*** (Tables 4–6; -.60*** in Table 6). "Of the nine interaction models, seven suggested significant negative interaction effects. These included all of the models where Grade 1 skill was the outcome as well as the Grade 2 prediction model with HME- formal at age 5. Visual inspection of the slopes (Fig. 3) revealed that the risk due to lower parental math skill levels was weaker when a more formal HME was provided."

Related Claims¶

  • HME-everyday activities at age 5 showed a protective effect, in contrast to other informal HME models, possibly because items were partially instructional for 5-year-olds — related
  • Formal math activities in the home environment mitigated the risk of parental math difficulties for children's math development (reported by the editors, citing Torppa et al.) — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Among lower-risk children, higher formal HME was associated with worse arithmetic performance, a reversed association already present at ages 2 and 5 — related
  • Explicit, instructional home numeracy activities acted as protective (not promotive) factors for 1st–2nd grade math performance in Torppa (2025) — related
  • None of the HME measures served as a significant promotive factor; the only significant correlation between children's skills and HME factors was negative (Grade 1 HME-formal) — related
  • Home work in arithmetic can be done as well as school work, with overall differences close to zero — related
