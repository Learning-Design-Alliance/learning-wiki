# Overall choice frequency did not differ by condition or prior knowledge, but the condition pattern reversed across prior-knowledge groups

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Two-way ANOVA found no significant main effects of condition or prior knowledge, nor an interaction, on students' frequency of engaging with optional tasks (overall M = 55% of opportunities). [→ Man Su 2025](#man-su-2025)
`q2 i?` Descriptively, choice frequency was higher for LPK students in the Non-Agent condition but higher for HPK students in the Agent condition. [→ Man Su 2025 (2)](#man-su-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Man Su 2025

Man Su, Belle Dang, Andy Nguyen & Tomohiro Nagashima. (2025). Choice-making in an adaptive learning system with motivational pedagogical agents. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00366-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Two-way ANOVA on system-logged choice frequency (proportion of optional task opportunities completed, averaging 55% overall) in the 49-student study. None of the three effects was statistically significant, and post-hoc comparisons showed no significant group differences.

> "two-way ANOVA showed no significant main effect of condition, F(1, 45) = 0.39, p = 0.537, or prior knowledge, F(1, 45) = 0.01, p = 0.924 on their choice frequency, and no significant interaction effect between condition and prior knowledge, F(1, 45) = 0.93, p = 0.341."

### Man Su 2025 (2)

Man Su, Belle Dang, Andy Nguyen & Tomohiro Nagashima. (2025). Choice-making in an adaptive learning system with motivational pedagogical agents. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00366-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A descriptive pattern visualized in Fig. 1d and reported from Table 2: the LPK-over-HPK ordering in the Non-Agent condition reversed in the Agent condition; these contrasts were not statistically tested.

> "choice frequency was higher for LPK students in the Non-Agent condition, but this pattern reversed in the Agent condition, where HPK students showed higher choice frequency."
