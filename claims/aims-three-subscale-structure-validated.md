# The AIMS three-subscale measurement structure fits the data and yields reliable Rasch measures for middle school students

## Evidence

### Romine William L. 2016

Romine William L., Miller Michele E., Knese Shawn A., Folk William R. (2016). Multilevel Assessment of Middle School Students' Interest in the Health Sciences: Development and Validation of a New Measurement Tool. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-02-0034

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Ordinal CFA (LISREL 8.80) of the final 26-item AIMS administered pre–post to middle school students at an urban midwestern school; the three-dimension model showed close fit, with "RMSEA = 0.058" and little pre–post change in loadings (largest change: item H2, 0.39 to 0.27).

> "The hypothesized measurement structure of the AIMS, containing three moderately correlated dimensions (interest in science, healthcare, and vision care) adequately fitted the data (RMSEA = 0.058)."

### Romine William L. 2016 (2)

Romine William L., Miller Michele E., Knese Shawn A., Folk William R. (2016). Multilevel Assessment of Middle School Students' Interest in the Health Sciences: Development and Validation of a New Measurement Tool. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-02-0034

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Rasch rating scale analysis of the 26-item AIMS (194 students measured at both time points) found residual PCA first eigenvalues "well below the value of 2" used to indicate unidimensionality, corroborating the CFA finding of single-construct subscales.

> "First eigenvalues from PCA on standardized residuals with respect to the Rasch model sat at 1.12, 1.13, and 1.08 items of variance for interest in science, healthcare, and vision care, respectively."

### Romine William L. 2016 (3)

Romine William L., Miller Michele E., Knese Shawn A., Folk William R. (2016). Multilevel Assessment of Middle School Students' Interest in the Health Sciences: Development and Validation of a New Measurement Tool. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-02-0034

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Rasch analysis of the final validation sample (226 pretest, 212 posttest students) found person reliabilities above the 0.50 What Works Clearinghouse minimum, which the authors say indicates "sufficient precision for comparisons of individual middle school students in the context of health interventions."

> "This is corroborated by Rasch person-measurement reliabilities of 0.87, 0.90, and 0.86 for interest in science, healthcare, and vision care, respectively."
