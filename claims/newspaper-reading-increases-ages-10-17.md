# Newspaper reading frequency increases systematically between ages 10 and 17 but remains below adult levels

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Frequency of newspaper reading shows a systematic linear increase across age groups 10-17 at both measurement waves, though 17-year-olds still read less than parents. [→ Tims 1983](#tims-1983)

## Evidence

### Tims 1983

Tims, Albert R., Jr. (1983). Development of Public Affairs Media Use. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED238044

`q2 · i2`

Two-wave statewide telephone survey of Wisconsin parent-child pairs; age-group ANOVA of newspaper reading days per week. The article reports a "systematic linear increase in the frequencyof newspaper reading" with winter F = 44.8 and fall F = 26.8, both p < .001.

> "Inspection of the patterns across the age groupsfor both the winter and fall measurements reveals asystematic linear increase in the frequencyof newspaper reading(winter:F = 44.8, d.f. = 7, 488, P <.001; fall:F = 26.8, d.f.= 7, 491, Il<.001)."
