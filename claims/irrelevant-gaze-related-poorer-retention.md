## Evidence

### Harris David J. 2017

Harris David J., Vine Samuel J., Wilson Mark R., McGrath John S., LeBel Marie-Eve, Buckingham Gavin. (2017). The effect of observing novice and expert performance on acquisition of surgical skills on a robotic platform. PLoS ONE 12(11): e0188233. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0188233

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Spearman correlation analysis linking eye-tracking gaze metrics during video observation to subsequent performance. The article reports a significant relationship with retention performance, r(79) = .31, p = .006, a printed correlation of r = .31, but not post-intervention.

> "There was a significant relationship between irrelevant fixation time (away from instruments or ring) and performance at retention, r(79) = .31, p = .006, but not immediately post intervention, r(79) = .18, p = .13."

### Harris David J. 2017 (2)

Harris David J., Vine Samuel J., Wilson Mark R., McGrath John S., LeBel Marie-Eve, Buckingham Gavin. (2017). The effect of observing novice and expert performance on acquisition of surgical skills on a robotic platform. PLoS ONE 12(11): e0188233. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0188233

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Spearman correlation between irrelevant fixation time and accelerometry-derived movement entropy. The article reports "increased entropy in instrument movements post-intervention, r(79) = .35, p = .002", a printed correlation of r = .35.

> "Irrelevant fixations also related to increased entropy in instrument movements post-intervention, r(79) = .35, p = .002."
