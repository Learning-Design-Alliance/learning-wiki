# In initial dyad-versus-individual simulation training, individuals show larger increases in anxiety and stress than dyads

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Medical students training individually had a significantly larger increase in anxiety (19.6 vs 7.6 on 80-point scale, p = 0.02) and stress (1.8 vs 0.9, p = 0.045) than students training in dyads. [→ Abbott Eduardo F. 2021](#abbott-eduardo-f-2021)

## Evidence

### Abbott Eduardo F. 2021

Abbott Eduardo F., Laack Torrey A., Licatino Lauren K., Wood-Wentz Christina M., Warner Paul A., Torsher Laurence C., Newman James S., Rieck Katie M. (2021). Comparison of dyad versus individual simulation-based training on stress, anxiety, cognitive load, and performance: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-021-02786-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized trial of 41 fourth-year medical students in a simulated bradycardia scenario (individuals n=17, dyads n=24). "larger mean increases in anxiety" and stress ratio were significantly greater for individuals (p = 0.02; p = 0.045).

> "Individuals compared to dyads had larger mean increases in anxiety, (19.6 versus 7.6 on 80-point scale, p = 0.02) and stress ratio (1.8 versus 0.9, p = 0.045)."
