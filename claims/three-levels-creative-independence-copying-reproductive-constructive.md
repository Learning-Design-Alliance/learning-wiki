# Creative independence develops through three levels: copying, reproductive-and-creative, and constructive-and-creative

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The article defines three developmental levels of creative independence, distinguished by the degree of transfer: elementary transfer on similar tasks, complicated transfer in similar conditions, and difficult transfer to new tasks. [→ Shangaraeva et al. 2016](#shangaraeva-et-al-2016)

## Evidence

### Shangaraeva et al. 2016

Liya F. Shangaraeva, Alfiya A. Yarkhamova, Zubayda A. Biktagirova, Dorice Agol. (2016). The Formation of Students' Creative Independence at the English Language Classes. International Journal of Environmental & Science Education, 11(6), 1267-1274. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1114326

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Definitional statement from the methods section describing the middle of three levels of creative independence; the copying level is doing tasks according to samples and the constructive-and-creative level is difficult transfer to the solution of new tasks. No empirical test of the level taxonomy itself is reported.

> "Reproductive and creative level is characterized by ability to transform, expand a speech sample, to choose one of the acquired sample and apply it solving a similar task, i.e. to carry out the complicated transfer in similar conditions."
