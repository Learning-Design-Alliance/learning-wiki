q3 · i? · associational · r2

Multilevel model results from the population-wide MoBa sample of 23,471 students in 2578 schools, with schools explaining achievement variance at different EA-PGI values. Figure 3 caption states "schools explain ~4% of the phenotypic variance" for students 2 SD below versus ~2% for students 2 SD above the mean.

"School effects on achievement vary across values of the within-family EA-PGI. For students with within-family EA-PGI that are 2 SD below the mean, schools explain ~4% of the phenotypic variance, whereas for students with PGI 2 SD above the mean, schools explain ~2%."

Rosa Cheesman 2022 (2)¶

Rosa Cheesman, Nicolai T. Borgen, Torkild H. Lyngstad, Espen M. Eilertsen, Ziada Ayorech, Fartein A. Torvik, Ole A. Andreassen, Henrik D. Zachrisson and Eivind Ystrom. (2022). A population-wide gene-environment interaction study on how genes, schools, and residential areas shape achievement. npj Science of Learning 7:29. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00145-8

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Result from the best-fitting multilevel model (Model 3a) in the Results section, shown in Fig. 2: the slope-intercept correlation was negative, meaning EA-PGI variation is less influential in higher-performing schools, interpreted by the authors as compensation.

"the slope-intercept correlation was negative, meaning that variation in students' EA-PGI is less influential for achievement in the schools with overall higher student achievement."

Related Claims¶

  • Neighbourhood, district, and municipality variation contributes little to achievement (under 2% of variance collectively) and does not interact with student EA-PGI — related
  • Five measured school sociodemographic characteristics do not explain the EA-PGI-by-school interaction — related
  • Between-school variation in EA-PGI effects is substantial: the average EA-PGI effect is 0.22 with slope SD 0.034, and variance explained by EA-PGI ranges from ~2% in the weakest-effect schools to ~8% in the strongest — related
  • The EA-PGI-by-school interaction is stronger for maths and reading than for English — related
