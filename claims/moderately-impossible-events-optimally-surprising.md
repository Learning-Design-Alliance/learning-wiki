# Core knowledge violations may be 'moderately impossible,' aligning with the Goldilocks effect on attention allocation

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Stahl Aimee E. 2019

Stahl Aimee E., Feigenson Lisa. (2019). Violations of Core Knowledge Shape Early Learning. Topics in Cognitive Science. https://doi.org/10.1111/tops.12389

`q1 · i?` · `review · r3`

Theoretical speculation (type e) linking the authors' events to the Goldilocks Effect (Kidd et al., 2012, 2014) and children's preferential recall of minimally counterintuitive concepts (Banerjee et al., 2013). The article explicitly labels this suggestion tentative.

> "Although physically impossible, the events were structured such that observers would be likely to generate a prediction about their outcome. Just one aspect of that prediction was then violated"
