Evidence¶

Duckworth Angela L. 2016¶

Duckworth Angela L., White Rachel E., Matteucci Alyssa J., Shearer Annie, Gross James J. (2016). A Stitch in Time: Strategic Self-Control in High School and College Students. Journal of educational psychology, 108(3), 329-341. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000062

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Field experiment with N = 159 University of Pennsylvania undergraduates randomly assigned to situation modification (n = 52), response modulation (n = 52), or control (n = 55), replicating Study 2 in an older, more autonomous sample. Response modulation did not differ from the no-treatment group, p = .97; hours studied was a covariate due to baseline differences.

"A one-way ANCOVA revealed that students' success at achieving their goals differed by condition, F(2, 155) = 6.48, p < .01, ηp2 = .08. As shown in Figure 5a, pairwise comparisons revealed that participants in the situation modification group (M = 3.56, SD = 1.05) accomplished their goal more often than participants in either the response modulation (M = 3.06, SD = 1.04), p < .01, d = 0.60, or the no-treatment group (M = 2.98, SD = 1.13), p < .01, d = 0.63."
