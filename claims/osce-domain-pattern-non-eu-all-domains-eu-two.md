## Evidence

### Huhn D. 2017

Huhn D., Lauter J., Roesch Ely D., Koch E., Möltner A., Herzog W., Resch F., Herpertz S. C., Nikendei C. (2017). Performance of International Medical Students In psychosocial medicine. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0950-z

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Domain-level ANOVAs on the same retrospective OSCE data showed significant group effects in all four domains; for relationship building, "non-EU students scoring significantly poorer than German students (p < .001; d = .39)". Non-EU differences were also significant for anamnesis (d = .20) and psychopathological findings (d = .47).

> "Regarding the ‘relationship with the patient’ items, groups also differed significantly from each other (F(2, 1030) = 8.471: p < .001; η 2 = .016), with non-EU students scoring significantly poorer than German students (p < .001; d = .39). No significant results emerged for students from EU countries (p = .106; d = .20)"

### Huhn D. 2017 (2)

Huhn D., Lauter J., Roesch Ely D., Koch E., Möltner A., Herzog W., Resch F., Herpertz S. C., Nikendei C. (2017). Performance of International Medical Students In psychosocial medicine. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0950-z

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

For the psychopathological findings and decision-making domain, only non-EU students (d = .47) differed significantly from German students; for EU students "No significant results emerged" (p = .054), so the EU gap held only in conversational skills and anamnesis.

> "Students from non-EU countries scored poorer than German students (p < .001; d = .47). No significant results emerged for EU students (p = .054; d = .24) (see Table 2)."
