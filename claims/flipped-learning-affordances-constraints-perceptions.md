# Students perceived flipped learning as having a distinct set of affordances (flexible video access, preparation, interaction) and constraints (video length, lack of guidance while watching)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students reported affordances including being well prepared, accessing materials anywhere, controlling playback (rewind, pause, stop, replay), and increased communication with peers and the instructor. [→ Almodaires et al. 2019](#almodaires-et-al-2019)
`q2 i?` Students reported constraints: needing more time to watch videos, wanting guidance while watching online, and finding videos longer than 10 minutes time-consuming. [→ Almodaires et al. 2019 (2)](#almodaires-et-al-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Almodaires et al. 2019

Abdullah M. Almodaires, Ghaida M. Alayyar, Tareq O. Almsaud & Faisal M. Almutairi. (2019). The Effectiveness of Flipped Learning: A Quasi-Experimental Study of the Perceptions of Kuwaiti Pre-Service Teachers. International Education Studies, 12(1). https://doi.org/10.5539/ies.v12n1p10

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Thematic analysis (Braun and Clarke, 2006 procedures) of open-ended questionnaire responses from the EG students, coded within the bounds of affordances and constraints of technologies (Gaver, 1991). Students reported being well prepared, controlling playback, and increased communication with peers and the instructor.

> "Moreover, the students perceived that they were able to rewind, pause, stop, and replay the video to enhance their understanding of the concepts."

### Almodaires et al. 2019 (2)

Abdullah M. Almodaires, Ghaida M. Alayyar, Tareq O. Almsaud & Faisal M. Almutairi. (2019). The Effectiveness of Flipped Learning: A Quasi-Experimental Study of the Perceptions of Kuwaiti Pre-Service Teachers. International Education Studies, 12(1). https://doi.org/10.5539/ies.v12n1p10

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same thematic analysis of open-ended responses: constraints included needing more time to watch videos, wanting guidance when watching online before class, and videos "lasted for more than 10 minutes", which slowed progress through the steps and quiz questions.

> "The students also reported that some of the videos lasted for more than 10 minutes, which they co nsidered to be time-consuming."
