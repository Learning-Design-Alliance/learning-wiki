## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The main effect of imagined activity was not statistically significant on any of the three ASQ components (positive regard, anxiety/avoidance, similarity). [→ Eleni Fleva 2014](#eleni-fleva-2014)

## Evidence

### Eleni Fleva 2014

Eleni Fleva. (2014). Imagined Contact Improves Intentions towards a Hypothetical Peer with Asperger Syndrome but not Attitudes towards Peers with Asperger Syndrome in General. World Journal of Education, 5(1). https://doi.org/10.5430/wje.v5n1p1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA on the self-generated ASQ in the 416-participant experiment found no significant imagined-activity effect on any component; equivalence was not tested. The authors suggest the ASQ "may have been a not sufficiently sensitive instrument" and note possible order effects.

> "The main effect of imagined activity was found not to be statistically significant for neither component 1 (Positive Regard) (p= .12), nor for component 2 (Anxiety/Avoidance) ( p= .90) or for component 3 (Similarity) ( p= .78)"
