# Longitudinal PRIMR data suggest pupils transferred literacy skills from English to Kiswahili, a direction opposite to that generally suggested by the Western literature.

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In some skills, pupils transferred literacy skills from English to Kiswahili over time, which the authors describe as unexpected based on Western literature suggesting transfer in the other direction. [→ Piper 2015](#piper-2015)

## Evidence

### Piper 2015

Piper, B., Bulat, J., & Johnston, A. (2015). Reading Skill Transfer Across Languages: Outcomes from Longitudinal Bilingual Randomized Control Trials in Kenya and Haiti. SREE Spring 2015 Conference Abstract. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED562423

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Longitudinal analysis of the PRIMR tracer sample of Grade 1 pupils assessed in English and Kiswahili at baseline, midterm, and endline. The analysis suggests "pupils are transferring literacy skills from English to Kiswahili," hedged as holding only in some skills.

> "The longitudinal analysis is able to determine how the language skills interact over time and across languages, and suggest that in so me skills, pupils are transferring literacy skills from English to Kiswahili. This is unexpected based on the Western literature, which generally suggests language transfer in the other direction."
