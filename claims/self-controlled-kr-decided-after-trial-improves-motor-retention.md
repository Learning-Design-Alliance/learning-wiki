## Evidence

### Carter 2014

Carter, M. J., Carlsen, A. N., & Ste-Marie, D. M. (2014). Self-controlled feedback is effective if it is based on the learner’s performance: a replication and extension of Chiviacowsky and Wulf (2005). Frontiers in Psychology, 5, 1325. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01325

`q3 · i? · partial η² only, no standardised mean difference` · `causal · r1`

Laboratory experiment in which 48 volunteers (mean age 21.35, run at the University of Ottawa) learned to propel a slider to a target distance under six knowledge-of-results (KR) conditions (n = 8 each), with retention and transfer tests on the next day; retention absolute error (AE) was analysed with a 2 (Choice) × 3 (Decision) ANOVA. The article reports "a significant Choice × Decision interaction, F(2,42) = 7.13, p = 0.002, ηp2 = 0.25".

> "In retention, the main effects for Choice and Decision were superseded by a significant Choice × Decision interaction, F(2,42) = 7.13, p = 0.002, ηp2 = 0.25."

### Carter 2014 (2)

Carter, M. J., Carlsen, A. N., & Ste-Marie, D. M. (2014). Self-controlled feedback is effective if it is based on the learner’s performance: a replication and extension of Chiviacowsky and Wulf (2005). Frontiers in Psychology, 5, 1325. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01325

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post hoc comparisons of retention AE among the three self-controlled groups in the same experiment: "they were both significantly more accurate in retention than the Self-Before group (M = 29.18, SE = 4.08)". No pairwise effect size is printed.

> "Post hoc comparisons revealed that although the Self-After (M = 10.04, SE = 1.89) and Self-Both (M = 12.45, SE = 3.58) groups did not differ significantly, they were both significantly more accurate in retention than the Self-Before group (M = 29.18, SE = 4.08)."

### Carter 2014 (3)

Carter, M. J., Carlsen, A. N., & Ste-Marie, D. M. (2014). Self-controlled feedback is effective if it is based on the learner’s performance: a replication and extension of Chiviacowsky and Wulf (2005). Frontiers in Psychology, 5, 1325. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01325

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Self versus yoked post hoc comparisons on retention AE: "both the Self-After and Self-Both groups had significantly less AE than their Yoked counterparts"; the Self-Before and Yoked-Before groups did not differ. No pairwise effect size is printed.

> "(1) both the Self-After and Self-Both groups had significantly less AE than their Yoked counterparts (Yoked-After: M = 24.87, SE = 3.04; Yoked-Both: M = 35.66, SE = 3.55); and (2) the Self-Before and the Yoked-Before (M = 27.35, SE = 3.66) groups did not differ significantly."
