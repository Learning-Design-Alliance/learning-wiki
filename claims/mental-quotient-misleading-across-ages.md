# The mental quotient is misleading as a general expression of mental development and is valuable only when cases are of the same age

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Stern's proposed mental quotient (mental age divided by chronological age) may be quite misleading because different grades of defect can yield the same quotient and the quotient changes with age without any change in mental capacity. [→ E. A. Doll 1912](#e-a-doll-1912)
`q1 i?` The mental quotient is valuable only when the cases under consideration are all of the same age. [→ E. A. Doll 1912](#e-a-doll-1912)

## Evidence

### E. A. Doll 1912

E. A. Doll. (1912). Inexpert Binet Examiners and Their Limitations. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://www.jstor.org

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r?`

A theoretical argument against Stern's (1912) mental quotient: the author shows by example that an idiot, an imbecile and a moron "would all have the same mental quotient of .50", and that the quotient falls with age while mentality stays constant.

> "there might be all grades of mental defect finding expression in the same quotient. Thus, for instance, a 4-year old idiot of 2 years mentality, a 10-year old imbecile of 5 years mentality, and an 18-year old moron of 9 years mentality would all have the same mental quotient of .50."
