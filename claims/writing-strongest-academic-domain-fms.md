# Writing shows the strongest FMS correlation among academic domains, exceeding reading

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` FMS correlated more strongly with writing (r = .343) than with reading (r = .310; p = .029), and more strongly with mathematics than with cognition (p = .006); writing and mathematics did not differ significantly (p = .805). [→ Sebastian P. Suggate 2025](#sebastian-p-suggate-2025)

## Evidence

### Sebastian P. Suggate 2025

Sebastian P. Suggate, Viktoria L. Karle, Tanja Kipfelsberger, Heidrun Stoeger. (2025). Keep the hands in mind: A meta-analysis of correlations between fine motor skills and reading, writing, mathematics, and cognitive development in children and adolescents. Educational Research Review 49 (2025) 100748. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2025.100748

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Post-hoc comparisons across academic-cognitive domains in the multilevel meta-regression (Table 1 lower section: writing r = .343, maths r = .337, reading r = .310, cognition r = .291). The article reports "FMS correlated lower with reading than with writing (p = .029)".

> "FMS correlated lower with reading than with writing (p = .029). FMS correlations with writing did not differ significantly from those with mathematics (p = .805), but FMS linked more strongly to maths than to cognition (p = .006) in the post-hoc analyses."
