# Estimated success declines with learner age for both friends and relatives, with relatives rated descriptively higher at every age

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` As learner age increased, success estimates significantly decreased for both friend and relative conditions, and relatives' means exceeded friends' means at all ages. [→ Harvey J. GINSBURG 2016](#harvey-j-ginsburg-2016)

## Evidence

### Harvey J. GINSBURG 2016

Harvey J. GINSBURG, Rebecca CAMERON, Roque V. MENDEZ & Michael WESTHOFF. (2016). Helping others use social media: age stereotypes when estimating learner's success. Psychology, Society, & Education, 8(1), 1-12. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1123456

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive means across the six scenarios: 20-year-old relative M =7.80 vs friend M =7.15; 40-year-old relative M =5.36 vs friend M =4.77; 60-year-old relative M =3.74 vs friend M =3.49. The article notes "relatives always showed higher M than friends", though the acquaintance main effect was not significant.

> "For all ages and the total scores, relatives always showed higher M than friends."
