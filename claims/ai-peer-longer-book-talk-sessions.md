# In elementary book talk, students spent significantly more time interacting with a fixed-role AI peer than with their homeroom teacher in both grades

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students spent significantly more time on average interacting with the AI than with their human teacher, for Grade 4 (t(11)=2.58, p=.026) and Grade 5 (t(6)=3.97, p=.007). [→ Liao 2026](#liao-2026)

## Evidence

### Liao 2026

Liao, C.-Y. (2026). Beyond a single role: Justifying a role-adaptive framework for ai companions through a comparative study in elementary book talk. Education and Information Technologies. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10639-026-13958-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Within-subjects quasi-experiment with 19 elementary students (12 Grade 4, 7 Grade 5), each completing two AI-led and two teacher-led sessions. Paired-samples t-tests on student-level means showed longer AI sessions: "t(11) = 2.58, p =.026" for Grade 4 and "t(6) = 3.97, p =.007" for Grade 5. No effect size printed.

> "We found that students spent significantly more time, on average, interacting with the AI than with their human teacher. This was true for both Grade 4 students ( t(11) = 2.58, p =.026) and was even more pronounced for Grade 5 students (t(6) = 3.97, p =.007)."
