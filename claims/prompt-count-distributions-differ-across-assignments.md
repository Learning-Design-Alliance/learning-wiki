### Nizamani 2026

Nizamani, S. B., Lee, Y., Donekal Chandrashekar, N., Ellis, M., & Ramakrishnan, N. (2026). Understanding Student Use of Large Language Models Across Computer Science Subfields. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01158 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2610.01158](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2610.01158)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Kruskal–Wallis test on ordinal prompt-count ranges (0, 1–2, 3–5, 6–10, 10+) reported by 211 students across seven assignments. The article reports "H = 71.18, df = 6, p<0.001"; no effect size is printed.

> "A Kruskal–Wallis test confirmed that prompt count distributions differed significantly across assignments (H = 71.18, df = 6, p<0.001)."

### Nizamani 2026 (2)

Nizamani, S. B., Lee, Y., Donekal Chandrashekar, N., Ellis, M., & Ramakrishnan, N. (2026). Understanding Student Use of Large Language Models Across Computer Science Subfields. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01158. [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2610.01158](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2610.01158)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive distribution of prompt counts among LLM users across the seven assignments. The article reports that "3–5 prompts accounted for approximately 29-46% of users" and that 10+ prompts was "rare except in Algorithms (18.0%)"; minimal 1–2 prompt use was more common in Machine Learning and Software Engineering.

> "Across assignments, 3–5 prompts accounted for approximately 29-46% of users, while 6-10 prompts accounted for 13-34%. Extended prompting of 10+ prompts was rare except in Algorithms (18.0%)."
