Den Heijer 2017¶

Abramovitch et al. (2013) as tabulated: 30 men with ADHD divided by leisure-time activity questionnaire into high (n = 10) and low (n = 20) physical activity groups; correlational, no intervention. "ADHD patients who frequently engage in physical exercise showed a healthier psychological well- being"; social and health worrying and venturesomeness were similar across groups.

"ADHD patients who frequently engage in physical exercise showed a healthier psychological well- being than ADHD patients who do not often exercise actively. They reported less meta- worrying, less problems with intrusive thoughts"

Related Claims¶

  • Acute exercise produces an overall small positive effect on cognition, with executive functions benefiting most and effects lasting up to two hours post-exercise — related
  • Daily moderate-to-vigorous physical activity is associated with better executive functioning in boys with ADHD — related
  • Small pilot studies provide preliminary evidence that exercise interventions improve behavioral symptoms and neuropsychological functioning in children with ADHD — related
  • Adolescents with high weekly physical exercise hours (more than 4 h/week) show significantly better selective attention, concentration, and processing speed than lower-exercise groups, with no differences between low and moderate groups — related
  • Physical activity, including brief aerobic exercise sessions, improves children's selective attention — related
  • A 10-minute bout of moderate-intensity exercise improves Stroop inhibitory control with increased left dorsolateral prefrontal activation in young adults — related
