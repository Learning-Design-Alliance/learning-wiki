## Evidence

### Jones JS 2020

Jones JS, Milton F, Mostazir M, Adlam ALR. (2020). The academic outcomes of working memory and metacognitive strategy training in children: A double‐blind randomized controlled trial. Developmental Science. https://doi.org/10.1111/desc.12870

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mixed-model analysis of the RCT's 3-month follow-up found the metacognitive-plus-Cogmed group improved more on the AWMA composite than Cogmed alone: "improvements on the AWMA were significantly greater in the MetaCogmed group compared to the Cogmed group at the 3 month outcome (p = 0.030)".

> "Furthermore, improvements on the AWMA were significantly greater in the MetaCogmed group compared to the Cogmed group at the 3 month outcome (p = 0.030)."

### Jones JS 2020 (2)

Jones JS, Milton F, Mostazir M, Adlam ALR. (2020). The academic outcomes of working memory and metacognitive strategy training in children: A double‐blind randomized controlled trial. Developmental Science. https://doi.org/10.1111/desc.12870

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Task-level mixed models at 3 months showed the metacognitive group's advantage concentrated on Backwards Digit Recall, where it beat both control and Cogmed alone; the authors interpret this as better consolidation of strategies for retrieval.

> "At the 3‐month outcome, MetaCogmed significantly improved performance on the Digit Recall task compared to the Control group (p = 0.002) and on the Backwards Digit Recall task compared to the Control (p < 0.001) and Cogmed groups (p < 0.001)."
