## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After two months, interns randomised to PBL combined with comparative nursing rounds scored higher than the traditional teaching group on a 38-item, 5-point Likert health education questionnaire; this is a questionnaire score, not a performance test, and the effect cannot be credited to PBL alone. ## Evidence

### Xue Huarong 2025

Xue, H., Lu, Y., & Liu, L. (2025). Efficacy of problem-based learning in enhancing health education skills, self-directed learning, and critical thinking among nursing interns: a prospective cohort study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07936-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The PBL group received "PBL combined with comparative nursing rounds teaching", so the two are not separated. Measured with a four-section, 38-item, 5-point Likert health education evaluation questionnaire (assessment, planning, implementation, evaluation) scored 38–190. Post scores: traditional 124.45 ± 4.98 vs. PBL "127.26 ± 5.96, t = 3.048, P = 0.003"; pre-assessment scores did not differ significantly (P = 0.748).

> "the post-assessment scores revealed a statistically significant difference (124.45 ± 4.98 vs. 127.26 ± 5.96, t = 3.048, P = 0.003)"
