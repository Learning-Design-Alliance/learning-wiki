# Medical undergraduates who participated in research training report higher research skills than untrained peers

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Undergraduates with at least three months of research training scored higher on self-rated scientific literature reading, research data analysis, and scientific paper writing than untrained undergraduates. [→ Jiang Juan 2025](#jiang-juan-2025)

## Evidence

### Jiang Juan 2025

Jiang Juan, Deng Pengbo, Li Ruijia, Yang Qian, Hu Chengping, Li Yuanyuan. (2025). The effect of research training on research skills, academic performance, and stress level among medical undergraduates in China: a cross-sectional survey. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07809-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Single-center cross-sectional survey of 123 medical undergraduates (95 trained, 28 untrained) using a structured online questionnaire. The trained group "demonstrated significant advantages in self-evaluation scores" on all three research-skill items; medians and P values as printed.

> "The trained group demonstrated significant advantages in self-evaluation scores on scientific literature reading ability (6.0 [5.0–7.0] vs. 5.0 [4.0–6.0], P = 0.001), research data analysis ability (5.0 [4.0–7.0] vs. 5.0 [2.0–5.0], P = 0.014), and scientific paper writing ability (6.0 [5.0–7.0] vs. 3.0 [1.0–5.0], P < 0.001)."
