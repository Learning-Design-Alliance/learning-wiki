# EPM-based learning shows better 6-week delayed recall of recognition and pronunciation than traditional content, though group differences narrow

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` At the 6-week follow-up, the EG scored significantly higher than the CG on both recognition recall (t (69, 2) = 5.18) and pronunciation recall (t (69, 2) = 4.57). [→ Iqbal Tassawar 2018](#iqbal-tassawar-2018)
`q2 i?` The EG–CG mean differences at recall were smaller than at immediate post-test, which the authors attribute to a six-week lapse without practice. [→ Iqbal Tassawar 2018 (2)](#iqbal-tassawar-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Iqbal Tassawar 2018

Iqbal Tassawar, Iqbal Saqib, Hussain Syed Sajid, Khan Iftikhar Ahmed, Khan Hikmat Ullah, Rehman Attiqa. (2018). Fighting adult illiteracy with the help of the environmental print material. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0201902

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Delayed recall assessment six weeks after the learning sessions: 42 of 54 EG participants and 27 of 53 CG participants returned; recognition and pronunciation were re-collected with the same assessment setup.

> "Significant differences of the recall (recognition (t (69, 2) = 5.18, p < = 0.0001) and pronunciation (t (69, 2) = 4.57, p < = 0.0001)) appeared as a result thus confirming hypothesis H3 as well."

### Iqbal Tassawar 2018 (2)

Iqbal Tassawar, Iqbal Saqib, Hussain Syed Sajid, Khan Iftikhar Ahmed, Khan Hikmat Ullah, Rehman Attiqa. (2018). Fighting adult illiteracy with the help of the environmental print material. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0201902

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The authors' interpretation of the narrowed EG–CG gap at follow-up (e.g., recognition EG μ = 18.2 vs CG μ = 10.94, versus 20.50 vs 14.99 at post-test); this is an untested explanation, not a measured result.

> "The reason might be the time lapse of 6 weeks would have corroded the learning process as the participants did not practice the content again in the elapsed time."
