## Evidence

### Lieu 2017

Lieu, R., Wong, A., Asefirad, A., & Shaffer, J. F. (2017). Improving Exam Performance in Introductory Biology through the Use of Preclass Reading Guides. CBE—Life Sciences Education. [doi:10.1187/cbe.16-11-0320](https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-11-0320)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Validation study in which two teaching assistants unannounced examined reading guides of 62 students in discussion sections 2–4 hours before lecture and compared completion levels with clicker self-reports. "69.4% of the teaching assistant observations matched the students’ self-reports."

> "Analysis of these data showed that 69.4% of the teaching assistant observations matched the students’ self-reports, 14.5% of the students overreported their completion of the reading guides, and 16.1% underreported their completion of the reading guides."

### Lieu 2017 (2)

Lieu, R., Wong, A., Asefirad, A., & Shaffer, J. F. (2017). Improving Exam Performance in Introductory Biology through the Use of Preclass Reading Guides. CBE—Life Sciences Education. [doi:10.1187/cbe.16-11-0320](https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-11-0320)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Authors' sensitivity analysis carrying the ~30% self-report error through the regression estimates. The reduced exam 3 estimate of "0.95 ± 0.26 percentage points" yields a predicted 4.1–7.3 point advantage for completing all six guides.

> "In the most conservative example, a 30% reduction in the lowest model estimate (that from exam 3; Table 4) would be 0.95 ± 0.26 percentage points (out of 100) on the exam, which is still nearly a full percentage point gained by fully completing one reading guide before class."
