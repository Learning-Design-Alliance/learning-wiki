Evidence¶

Brezovnik 2015¶

Brezovnik, A. (2015). The Benefits of Fine Art Integration into Mathematics in Primary School. c e p s Journal, 5(3). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1128967

q2 · i? · causal · r1

A non-probability pedagogical experiment with 210 Slovenian fifth-graders (105 control, 105 experimental) taught four topics simultaneously; four tests scored 0–12. The multivariate test showed a statistically significant difference in academic achievements, with the experimental group higher on all four tests.

"The multivariate test shows that we obtained a statistically significant difference in academic achievements, F (4.205) = 26.70, p < 0.0005; Wilk’s Λ = 0.657."

Brezovnik 2015 (2)¶

Brezovnik, A. (2015). The Benefits of Fine Art Integration into Mathematics in Primary School. c e p s Journal, 5(3). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1128967

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Tests of between-subject effects in the same pedagogical experiment show the integration of fine art had a statistically significant effect on every topic, with the largest F for Powers (55.89) and the smallest for Perimeter (22.30).

"From Table 9 we can see that the integration of fine art into mathematics has a statistically significant effect on all of the research topics: Equations (F (1. 208) = 45.73; p < 0.0005), Inequations (F (1. 208) = 55.37; p < 0.0005), Powers (F (1. 208) = 55.89; p < 0.0005), Perimeter (F (1. 208) = 22.30; p < 0.0005)."
