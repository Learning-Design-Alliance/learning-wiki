## Evidence

### Kupf Irma 2024

Kupf Irma, Thanner Gabriele, Gerken Michael, Crispin Alexander, Braess Jan. (2024). How accurate is clinical prognostication by oncologists during routine practice in a general hospital and can it be improved by a specific prognosis training programme: a prospective interventional study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2023-081661

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Subgroup analysis of the same prospective interventional study, reported in the discussion; residents (entry level to 1 year of oncology) and board-certified oncologists (13–34 years) are compared on printed accuracy values, with no effect size printed.

> "This effect was especially pronounced in residents who started from a lower level (60.7% in phase 1) and then achieved 81.1% in the final phase (+20.4%). The experienced oncologists started from a significantly higher level of accuracy (75.7%) but also benefited from training (85.6% in the final phase)—however, to a less pronounced degree in absolute terms (+9.9%)."
