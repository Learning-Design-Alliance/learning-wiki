## Evidence

### Alina Hase 2024

Alina Hase, Poldi Kuhl. (2024). Teachers’ use of data from digital learning platforms for instructional design: a systematic review. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-024-10356-y

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Qualitative synthesis answering the review's third research question on purposes and derived actions. Purposes included understanding students' learning processes, identifying misconceptions, and improving feedback; the review reports "Teachers took actions for differentiation as well as actions for individualization."

> "Within the identified studies, the purposes of using learning data can be divided into two areas: First, all n = 11 studies focused on the added value of the data for teachers and their teaching. Second, n = 6 studies also addressed the potential of data for students and, in some cases, for their parents."

Same synthesis section on whole-class actions. Examples printed include using class-progress information as a starting point for designing the following lesson's introduction, differentiating instructions, and spending more class time on tasks.

> "Teachers also made some instructional changes for the whole learning group in n = 7 studies (Bonham, 2018; Edmunds & Hartnett, 2014; Hinkle, 2021; Michaeli et al., 2020; Molenaar & Knoop-van Campen, 2018; Xhakaj et al., 2016, 2017)."
