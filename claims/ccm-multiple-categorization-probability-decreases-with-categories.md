# The best-fitting Cognitive Categorization Model has a multiple-categorization probability that decreases with the number of categories, matching observed 'stacker' and 'spreader' sorter behaviors

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In the best-fitting CCM variant, the probability of categorizing a single problem in multiple categories differs by person and decreases with the number of categories created. [→ Steven F. Wolf 2012](#steven-f-wolf-2012)
`q1 i?` Sorters were observed to fall into two behaviors, 'stackers' who make as few piles as possible and 'spreaders' who spread problems out. [→ Steven F. Wolf 2012 (2)](#steven-f-wolf-2012-2)

## Evidence

### Steven F. Wolf 2012

Steven F. Wolf, Daniel P. Dougherty, and Gerd Kortemeyer. (2012). An empirical approach to interpreting card-sorting data. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.8.010124](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.8.010124)

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Model-fitting result from the authors' CCM simulations: among three variants of the multiple-categorization probability, the best fit was the variant depending on the number of categories (Equation 6), and the fitted 'probability actually decreases with the number of categories that are created'.

> "the probability to categorize a single problem in multiple categories is diﬀerent for each person and that probability actually decreases with the number of categories that are created."

### Steven F. Wolf 2012 (2)

Steven F. Wolf, Daniel P. Dougherty, and Gerd Kortemeyer. (2012). An empirical approach to interpreting card-sorting data. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.8.010124](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.8.010124)

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Qualitative observation of sorters at work during the categorization task: 'We observed two diﬀerent sorter behaviors', with some people 'resolved to make as few piles as possible', whom the authors call stackers; the others are called spreaders.

> "We observed two diﬀerent sorter behaviors while they worked on the categorization task. It seemed like some people were resolved to make as few piles as possible, we will call these people “stackers.”"
