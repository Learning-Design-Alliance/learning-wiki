## Evidence

### Graell M. et al 2026

Graell M. et al. (2026). Towards disentangling the polygenic contribution of dyslexia to school performance. Translational Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41398-026-04118-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Ordinal mixed-effect models in the INSchool cohort (n = 4274) tested the discordant partition (variants with opposite direction of effect in dyslexia and EA) against school grades. The discordant partition had the strongest negative effect on school performance of all partitions.

> "PGSDYS_discordant was associated with worse performance in all subjects (OR < 0.78, adj-P ≤ 8.8E–15) and explained the largest proportion of variance on school performance of all genetic partitions, ranging from 1.76–2.65%"

### Graell M. et al 2026 (2)

Graell M. et al. (2026). Towards disentangling the polygenic contribution of dyslexia to school performance. Translational Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41398-026-04118-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Linear mixed-effect models tested PGS associations with CBCL-6/18 subscales in n = 4084 individuals with completed clinical information. The discordant partition was positively associated with somatic complaints, social and attentional problems, and externalizing behavior.

> "the SNP subset with discordant direction of the effect between dyslexia and EA was also associated with externalizing behavior, indicating that this genomic partition is associated with the majority of behavioral and emotional problems assessed"
