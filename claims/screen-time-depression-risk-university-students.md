## Evidence

### Zeng Zhaolan 2026

Zeng Zhaolan, Shi Zeyao, Hou Shulin, Yan Jing, Yang Ru, Li Xiaowen. (2026). Comprehensive effects and dose–response relationship of screen exposure on university students' physical and mental health and academic performance in the digital age: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-026-27025-9

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 17 cross-sectional and longitudinal studies in a PROSPERO-registered review. Initial pooled "OR = 1.51; 95%CI: 1.36–1.68" with I2 = 94.40%; after excluding 5 heterogeneity-contributing studies the pooled effect increased to OR = 1.93 (95%CI: 1.75–2.12).

> "Seventeen studies (involving 30,352 university students) reported on the association between screen time and depression risk. The pooled results indicated a significant positive correlation between daily screen time and depression risk (OR = 1.51; 95%CI: 1.36–1.68)."

### Zeng Zhaolan 2026 (2)

Zeng Zhaolan, Shi Zeyao, Hou Shulin, Yan Jing, Yang Ru, Li Xiaowen. (2026). Comprehensive effects and dose–response relationship of screen exposure on university students' physical and mental health and academic performance in the digital age: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-026-27025-9

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

One-stage restricted cubic spline dose–response analysis of six studies (19,070 students) fitted with weighted least squares, showing an S-shaped curve; risk rose continuously above 1 h/day and the rate of increase slowed above 5 h/day. No standardized effect size is printed for this finding.

> "Notably, beyond 2.5 h of daily screen time, the risk of depression increased significantly, with each additional hour of screen exposure associated with a more than 5% increase in depression risk."
