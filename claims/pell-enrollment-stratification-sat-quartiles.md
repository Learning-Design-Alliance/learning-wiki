## Evidence

### Andrew Howard Nichols 2015

Andrew Howard Nichols. (2015). The Pell Partnership: Ensuring a Shared Responsibility for Low-Income Student Success. The Education Trust. https://www.edtrust.org/resource/pellgradrates

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of enrollment and graduation rates by SAT quartile across 1,002 institutions (Figure 4). The report finds "only 12 percent" of non-Pell students attend the lowest quartile versus 26 percent of Pell recipients, and 41 percent of non-Pell versus 25 percent of Pell students attend the highest quartile.

> "Consider that very few non-Pell students — only 12 percent — attend institutions in the lowest SAT quartile (where graduation rates are lower), while double that percentage (26 percent) of Pell recipients do."

### Andrew Howard Nichols 2015 (2)

Andrew Howard Nichols. (2015). The Pell Partnership: Ensuring a Shared Responsibility for Low-Income Student Success. The Education Trust. https://www.edtrust.org/resource/pellgradrates

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of student composition within SAT quartiles (Figure 5). The report states "Nearly 83 percent of students attending institutions in the highest SAT quartile are non-Pell recipients, while just 17 percent are Pell recipients", versus 42 percent Pell in the lowest quartile.

> "Nearly 83 percent of students attending institutions in the highest SAT quartile are non-Pell recipients, while just 17 percent are Pell recipients."
