Evidence¶

Hernández 2022¶

Hernández, L. E., & Darling-Hammond, L. (2022). Creating identity-safe schools and classrooms. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/165.102

q2 · i? · review · r1

The report's synthesis of restorative practices evaluations (citing RAND and WestEd reviews). It states the approach "reduces the use of exclusionary discipline" and improves climate and achievement; no effect sizes are printed.

"Accumulating evidence suggests that shifting to restorative practices, with robust supports to enable the adults to do so, reduces the use of exclusionary discipline, resulting in fewer and less racially disparate suspensions and expulsions, while making schools identity-safe and improving school climate, relationships, and academic achievement."

Hernández 2022 (2)¶

Hernández, L. E., & Darling-Hammond, L. (2022). Creating identity-safe schools and classrooms. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/165.102

q2 · i? · review · r1

A district-level example described in the report's restorative practices section: a continuum from proactive exchanges to restorative conferences. The report states disparities "were reduced every year for each racial group" with achievement gains.

"a continuum model including proactive restorative exchanges, affirmative statements, informal conferences, large group circles, and restorative conferences substantially changed school culture and outcomes rapidly in one major district, as disparities in school discipline were reduced every year for each racial group and gains were made in academic achievement across all subjects in nearly every grade level."
