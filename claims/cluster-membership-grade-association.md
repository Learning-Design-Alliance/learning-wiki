# Cluster membership is significantly associated with grade level but not gender

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Chi-square tests showed a significant association between cluster membership and grade level (χ²(8) = 79.32, p < .001, Cramér's V = 0.31). [→ Bautista 2026](#bautista-2026)

## Evidence

### Bautista 2026

Bautista, G. Jr., Cacuyong, R., Lavicza, Z., Sabitzer, B., & Emara, M. (2026). Function Art: Understanding the Artistic Transformation of Mathematical Functions through Learning Analytics. Journal of Learning Analytics, 13(1). https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2026.9037

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Chi-square tests of independence on the full sample of 400 students examined associations between cluster membership and demographic variables. The grade-level association was significant with a moderate effect size (Cramér's V = 0.31).

> "the chi -square test for grade level was significant , χ2(8) = 79.32, p <.001, Cramér’s V = 0.31, as was the test for function type, χ2(4) = 20.18, p <. 001, Cramér’s V = 0.15."

### Bautista 2026 (2)

Bautista, G. Jr., Cacuyong, R., Lavicza, Z., Sabitzer, B., & Emara, M. (2026). Function Art: Understanding the Artistic Transformation of Mathematical Functions through Learning Analytics. Journal of Learning Analytics, 13(1). https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2026.9037

`q2 · i0` · `design · r2`

Chi-square test of independence examining gender distribution across the three clusters found no significant association. The small effect size (Cramér's V = 0.07) indicates negligible differences in gender distribution across profiles.

> "gender was not significantly associated with cluster membership, χ 2(2) = 2.33, p =.31, Cramér’s V = 0.07, suggesting that the distribution of gender did not differ across the three clusters."
