# Subtle prejudice is unexpectedly positively related to perceiving microaggressions in normative scenarios (Study 1)

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Both subtle prejudice subscales positively predicted perceived microaggressions in the school normative scenario, contrary to hypothesis. [→ Civitillo 2025](#civitillo-2025)

## Evidence

### Civitillo 2025

Civitillo, S., S. Glock, L. A. Naudszus, L. Venhoff, D. Lieck, and P. Jugert. (2025). Investigating Preservice Teachers' Competence to Notice Ethnic Microaggressions in the Classroom. Journal of Community Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1002/jcop.70056

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Study 1 regression for the school normative scenario. Contrary to expectation, "participants with high levels of subtle prejudice seemed to be aware that the teacher in the vignette was biased." Exaggeration of cultural differences also positively predicted projected belonging (β = 0.33, p = 0.01).

> "both subscales of subtle prejudice (i.e., the exaggeration of cultural differences and the negation of positive emotions) were positively related to perceived microaggressions in the normative scenario (β = 0.25, p < 0.05; β = 0.23, p < 0.05; respectively)"
