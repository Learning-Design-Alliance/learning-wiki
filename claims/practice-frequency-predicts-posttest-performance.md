# Practice frequency, not cumulative usage time, was strongly associated with posttest presentation performance after controlling for baseline and demographics

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a multiple regression on posttest Overall Rating (N = 204), usage frequency showed a strong positive association (β = .90, p < .001) while cumulative usage time did not reach conventional significance (β = −.11, p = .066). ## Evidence

### Hung-Yue Suen and Kuo-En Hung 2026

Hung-Yue Suen and Kuo-En Hung. (2026). An Interpretable Closed-Loop Intelligent Tutoring System for Multimodal Affective Feedback in Asynchronous Presentation Training. IEEE Transactions on Learning Technologies. https://doi.org/10.1109/TLT.2026.3693864

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple regression with posttest Overall Rating as outcome, pretest rating, two log-based practice indicators, and six demographic covariates as predictors; the model was significant, F(9, 194) = 37.72, p < .001, with "usage frequency showed a strong positive association (β = .90, p < .001)".

> "The model was statistically significant, F(9, 194) = 37.72, p < .001, explaining 63.6% of the variance in posttest scores (adjusted R² = .62). Pretest performance remained a reliable predictor (β = .16, p = .002), and usage frequency showed a strong positive association (β = .90, p < .001)"
