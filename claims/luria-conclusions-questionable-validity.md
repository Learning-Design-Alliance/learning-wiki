# Luria's conclusions about Uzbek and Kirghiz peasants' cognitive development are of questionable validity because his assumptions about speech, task context, and face-to-face elicitation socially constructed his data

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · theoretical `r3` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The author argues Luria (1976) contributed to the social construction of his data by assuming speech was primary to development and using speech to elicit data regardless of its appropriateness to the culture studied. [→ Peter Smagorinsky 1995](#peter-smagorinsky-1995)
`q1 i?` A second and third contribution to the social construction of Luria's data were his use of decontextualized school-like tasks for people whose thinking centered on situated activity, and his elicitation of responses in face-to-face social transactions. [→ Peter Smagorinsky 1995 (2)](#peter-smagorinsky-1995-2)

## Evidence

### Peter Smagorinsky 1995

Peter Smagorinsky. (1995). The Social Construction of Data: Methodological Problems of Investigating Learning in the Zone of Proximal Development. Review of Educational Research, 65(3), 191-212. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543065003191

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical critique in the article's concluding section, re-examining Luria's (1976) experimental protocols (e.g., the Rakmat hammer-saw-log-hatchet grouping) through the neo-Vygotskian lens; the author concludes the research is not mediational and therefore of questionable validity.

> "Fundamentally, then, Luria contributed to the social construction of his data by assuming that speech was primary to development; he then used speech as the means through which to elicit his data, regardless of its appropriateness to the culture under study."

### Peter Smagorinsky 1995 (2)

Peter Smagorinsky. (1995). The Social Construction of Data: Methodological Problems of Investigating Learning in the Zone of Proximal Development. Review of Educational Research, 65(3), 191-212. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543065003191

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Continuation of the same critique: the article states the tasks Luria presented were decontextualized problems such as those found in school, unlikely to be responsive to the higher mental processes functional in the peasants' way of life, and that elicitation was face-to-face.

> "Luria (1976) contributed to the social construction of his data in a third crucial way, by eliciting responses from his participants in face-to-face social transactions."
