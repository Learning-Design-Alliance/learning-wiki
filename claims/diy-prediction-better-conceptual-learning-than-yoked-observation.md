# Children who actively generate their own predictions show better conceptual learning about water displacement than children who observe matched predictions from another learner

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r3` · `q3` · `i1`–`i2`

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Children aged 5-7 who generated their own predictions before seeing outcomes performed significantly better on a posttest of water displacement concepts than children who observed predictions matched to their own prior beliefs (d = 0.45). [→ Brod 2025](#brod-2025)
`q3 i1` The DIY advantage extends to transfer performance (d = 0.36), indicating deeper conceptual internalization rather than mere memory for trained items. [→ Brod 2025 (2)](#brod-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Brod 2025

Brod, G., Holstein, E., Weindorf, L., Colantonio, J., Bonawitz, E. & Theobald, M. (2025). Do it yourself: discerning the effects of self-directed activity on conceptual learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00364-9

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r3`

Preregistered experiment (N = 95, ages 5–7) comparing children who generated predictions themselves (active) to children who observed predictions of a Bayesian-matched fictitious peer (yoked). "children in the active condition performed signiﬁcantly better than children in the yoked con- dition" on the posttest (d = 0.45).

> "children in the active condition performed signiﬁcantly better than children in the yoked con- dition ( t(93) = 2.21, one-tailed p = 0.015, d = 0.45; active: M = 0.91, SD = 0.18; yoked: M = 0.81, SD = 0.24)"

### Brod 2025 (2)

Brod, G., Holstein, E., Weindorf, L., Colantonio, J., Bonawitz, E. & Theobald, M. (2025). Do it yourself: discerning the effects of self-directed activity on conceptual learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00364-9

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r3`
