## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Beyond directive functions on content or language structure, online feedback was perceived as a personal, private relationship (e.g., an email only the recipient can read, a mother, an invisible microphone). [→ Ding 2019](#ding-2019)

## Evidence

### Ding 2019

Ding, S. L., & Chew, E. (2019). Thy word is a lamp unto my feet: A study via metaphoric perceptions on how online feedback benefited Chinese learners. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-019-09651-w

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interpretation of metaphors such as 'email' ("Once you receive it, only you can read it; others can't") and 'mother' in the case study. The authors conclude online feedback "allows for a personal relationship" suited to introverted students. No effect size is printed.

> "It is also valued for its relational and expressive role in providing praise, encouragement and positive opinions. It allows for a personal relationship, which works particularly well for more introverted students, such as Chinese and East Asians."
