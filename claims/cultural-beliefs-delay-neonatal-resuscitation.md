## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Mothers and attendants may insist on suctioning or shaking the newborn upside down, practices midwives are trained to abstain from because they can delay resuscitation. [→ Myrnerts Höök Susanna 2025](#myrnerts-hook-susanna-2025)

## Evidence

### Myrnerts Höök Susanna 2025

Myrnerts Höök Susanna, Abrahamsson Marielle, Namusoko Sarah, Byamugisha Josaphat, Bergström Anna. (2025). Midwives' knowledge and perspectives on neonatal resuscitation and survival before and after Helping Babies Breathe training: a qualitative study in Uganda. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2024-094012

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Focus group data in the qualitative study; a participant quoted mothers believing babies "will swallow the secretions if not tilted ('ocumira')". These practices are sometimes perceived as essential, potentially leading to blame directed at the midwife.

> "cultural beliefs often emphasise practices such as suctioning or shaking the newborn upside down. For example, the mother or attendant in-laws may insist that the MW perform these actions"
