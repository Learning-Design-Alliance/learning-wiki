The review's cross-study synthesis of the included literature found modality–outcome patterns: "High-fidelity simulations were particularly associated with enhanced clinical judgment and decision-making accuracy" while VR and games favoured empathy and reflective thinking.

"High-fidelity simulations were particularly associated with enhanced clinical judgment and decision-making accuracy [17, 29, 30]. Virtual reality and game-based simulations appeared more effective in fostering empathy and reflective thinking, especially among early-year students"

Related Claims¶

  • Simulation-based education improves nursing students' clinical decision-making, judgment, and self-confidence — related
  • The review reports that high-fidelity simulation outperformed case-based discussion for clinical reasoning and leadership in paediatric emergencies — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Review syntheses: VR/AR most often improved academic achievement and motivation, but sometimes performed no better than conventional technologies — related
  • Immersion in virtual reality adds extraneous load yet can boost transfer, enjoyment, and motivation while leaving retention unaffected — related
  • Virtual reality was particularly effective for procedural skill development and knowledge retention in paediatric undergraduate training — related
  • Very high majorities of students rated high-fidelity simulation as enhancing learning, clinical reasoning, decision-making, and value — related
  • High-fidelity simulation is the most commonly used simulation type in studies of critical thinking — related
  • The included studies do not reflect which intervention is better or which favours the acquisition and retention of knowledge more effectively. — reports the opposite
  • The review's authors interpret structured pre-briefing as supporting nursing students' metacognition, clinical judgment, and meaningful simulation learning — related
