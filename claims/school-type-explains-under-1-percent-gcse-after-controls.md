## Evidence

### Smith-Woolley 2018

Smith-Woolley, E., Pingault, J.-B., Selzam, S., Rimfeld, K., Krapohl, E., von Stumm, S., Asbury, K., Dale, P. S., Young, T., Allen, R., Kovas, Y. & Plomin, R. (2018). Differences in exam performance between pupils attending selective and non-selective schools mirror the genetic differences between them. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0019-8

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Analysis controlling EduYears GPS for the heritable selection factors (ability, achievement, SES) in the TEDS subsample with full selection-factor data. Similar null results emerged for state non-selective schools in areas of varying selectivity.

> "We found that, after accounting for the variance explained by heritable selection factors, there were no significant EduYears GPS differences between students of the three school types: state non-selective, grammar and private"

### Smith-Woolley 2018 (2)

Smith-Woolley, E., Pingault, J.-B., Selzam, S., Rimfeld, K., Krapohl, E., von Stumm, S., Asbury, K., Dale, P. S., Young, T., Allen, R., Kovas, Y. & Plomin, R. (2018). Differences in exam performance between pupils attending selective and non-selective schools mirror the genetic differences between them. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0019-8

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Hierarchical linear regression with dummy coding tested school type effects on GCSE after controls. The article reports grammar 'M = 9.14; t = 2.35, p < 0.019' and private 'M = 9.32, t = 6.16, p < 0.001' versus non-selective M = 8.96; standardised betas of 0.03 and 0.07. No standardised effect size is printed for this finding.

> "One of our main findings was that after accounting for the variance explained by the selection factors and EduYears GPS, the variance in GCSE explained by school type dropped from 7.1% to only 0.5%"
