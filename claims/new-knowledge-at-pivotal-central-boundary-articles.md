Peter Holtz & Joachim Kimmerle & Ulrike Cress. (2018). Using big data techniques for measuring productive friction in mass collaboration online environments. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-018-9285-y

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports a social network analysis using snapshots of the link structure among approximately 11,000 Wikipedia articles in psychology and education for the years 2006 to 2012. Betweenness was used as a proxy for productive friction and centrality for within-domain importance. No effect size is printed.

> "A first analysis (Halatchliyski and Cress 2014) revealed that new knowledge in the form of edits or newly created articles did indeed center either around articles with high levels of centrality within the respective domains, or around articles with high betweenness scores."
