# Lower secondary students are more likely to study vocabulary on days they receive a smartphone study reminder than on days they do not

## Subclaims

`q3 i1` Within the Reminder Group, receiving a reminder on a given day increased the probability of studying vocabulary that day (OR = 1.77). [→ Lea Nobbe 2024](#lea-nobbe-2024)

`q3 i?` In an intensive longitudinal micro-randomized study, 130 children aged 9–13 were more likely to use a vocabulary app on days with a reminder prompt than on days without one. [→ Breitwieser 2024](#breitwieser-2024)

## Evidence

### Lea Nobbe 2024

Lea Nobbe, Jasmin Breitwieser, Daniel Biedermann & Garvin Brod. (2024). Smartphone-based study reminders can be a double-edged sword. npj Science of Learning 9:40. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00253-7

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Within-person encouragement design in a preregistered intervention with 85 students aged 10–12; logistic mixed-effects model on daily log-file data from a vocabulary learning app compared reminder and no-reminder days. The study found "a signi ﬁcant positive effect of Reminder" with OR = 1.77, 95% CI [1.32, 2.37].

> "we found a signi ﬁcant positive effect of Reminder on students ’ likelihood to study vocabulary on a given day (b = 0.57, 95% CI [0.28, 0.826], χ2(1) = 14.39, p < 0.001). On average, stu- dents in the Reminder Group were more likely to study on days they received a reminder ( OR = 1.77, 95% CI [1.32, 2.37])."

### Breitwieser 2024

Breitwieser, J., Neubauer, A. B., Schmiedek, F. & Brod, G. (2024). Realizing the potential of mobile interventions for education. npj Science of Learning, 9, 76. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00289-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Intensive longitudinal micro-randomized study in which 130 children used the PROMPT app and a vocabulary app over 36 days, answering daily experience-sampling questions with reminders randomly delivered on some days. Findings reported from Fig. 2b.

> "Our results showed that children were more likelyto study with the vocabulary app on days with a prompt compared to days without prompt (Fig. 2b). These findings suggest that the prompt had a causal effect on study behavior."
