## Evidence

### Noetel 2022

Noetel, M., Griffith, S., Delaney, O., Harris, N. R., Sanders, T., Parker, P., del Pozo Cruz, B., & Lonsdale, C. (2022). Multimedia design for learning: An overview of reviews with meta-meta-analysis. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-meta-analytic pooling of 68 effect sizes on cognitive load outcomes across included reviews; the pooled effect was small (g = 0.22, CI [0.04, 0.40]) and moderated by design principle (R2 = 1.00, p = 0.01).

> "Overall, multimedia design interventions led to small improvements in management of cognitive load (g = 0.22, 95% CI [ 0.04, 0.40], k = 68). As with effects on learning, effects on cognitive load were moderated by the multimedia design principle"

### Noetel 2022 (2)

Noetel, M., Griffith, S., Delaney, O., Harris, N. R., Sanders, T., Parker, P., del Pozo Cruz, B., & Lonsdale, C. (2022). Multimedia design for learning: An overview of reviews with meta-meta-analysis. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329

`q2 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

The article reports that "a small number of primary studies found large effects" for personalisation (k = 4) and modality (k = 2) on cognitive load, both g = 0.62, while larger reviews found only small effects for segmentation, signaling, and pedagogical agents.

> "a small number of primary studies found large effects personalisation (g = 0.62, 95% CI [ 0.13, 1.11], k = 4; Ginns et al., 2013) and the modality effect (g = 0.62, 95% CI [ 0.27, 0.96], k = 2; Ginns, 2005)"
