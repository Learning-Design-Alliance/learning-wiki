## Evidence

### C. E. Ferree and Gertrude Rand 1917

C. E. Ferree and Gertrude Rand. (1917). The Power of the Eye to Sustain Clear Seeing Under Different Conditions of Lighting. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0072369

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Results of the acuity-sustain tests across the tested lighting situations. The authors rank surface-brightness extremes above the angle of incident light as a factor, reporting they are "the most important causes of the eye's discomfort and loss of efficiency."

> "Extremes of surface brightness in the field of vision seem to be the most important causes of the eye's discomfort and loss of efficiency in lighting systems as we have them at the present time."

### C. E. Ferree and Gertrude Rand 1917 (2)

C. E. Ferree and Gertrude Rand. (1917). The Power of the Eye to Sustain Clear Seeing Under Different Conditions of Lighting. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0072369

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Brightness measurements made with a Sharp-Millar illuminometer, calibrated against a magnesium oxide surface, in the tested installations. The printed ratios describe the brightness extremes the authors' tests show are "very fatiguing to the eye."

> "In lighting from exposed sources it is not infrequent to find the brightest surface from 1,000,000 to 2,500,000 times as brilliant as the darkest; and from 300,000 to 600,000 times as brilliant as the reading page."
