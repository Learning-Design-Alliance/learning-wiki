## Evidence

### Pedraza 2024

Pedraza, F., Farkas, B. C., Vékony, T., Haesebaert, F., Phelipon, R., Mihalecz, I., Janacsek, K., Anders, R., Tillmann, B., Plancher, G. & Németh, D. (2024). Evidence for a competitive relationship between executive functions and statistical learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00243-9

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Linear mixed model and correlation analysis of the 157-participant Hungarian university sample re-analysed from a prior dataset. The quote reports the replicated negative association "Pearson's r = −0.170, 95% CI = [-0.319, -0.014], p = 0.033".

> "EF factor 1 scores significantly negatively correlated with average implicit SL learning scores, and with a similar effect size, as in Study 1 (Fig. 4b, Pearson's r = −0.170, 95% CI = [-0.319, -0.014], p = 0.033"

### Pedraza 2024 (2)

Pedraza, F., Farkas, B. C., Vékony, T., Haesebaert, F., Phelipon, R., Mihalecz, I., Janacsek, K., Anders, R., Tillmann, B., Plancher, G. & Németh, D. (2024). Evidence for a competitive relationship between executive functions and statistical learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00243-9

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same Study 2 sample and models; the null association for the short-term-memory factor was not significant: "Pearson's r = −0.034, 95% CI = [-0.190, 0.123], p = 0.672". Equivalence was not tested; no effect size beyond the small r is interpretable.

> "EF factor 2 scores did not correlate significantly with average implicit SL learning scores (Factor 2: Pearson's r = −0.034, 95% CI = [-0.190, 0.123], p = 0.672, Spearman's r h o = −0.023, 95% CI = [-0.179, 0.134], p = 0.771, BF-0 = 0.145)."
