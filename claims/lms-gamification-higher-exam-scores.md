## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who played three different gamified activities to review key concepts scored significantly higher on exams than students in the control group. [→ Chen 2018](#chen-2018)

## Evidence

### Chen 2018

Chen, C.-C., Huang, C., Gribbins, M., & Swan, K. (2018). Gamify online courses with tools built into your learning management system (LMS) to enhance self-determined and active learning. Online Learning, 22(3), 41-54. https://doi.org/10.24059/olj.v22i3.1466

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experiment with 80 graduate students randomly assigned to gamified (n = 44) or control (n = 36) conditions in an online biostatistics course. Welch's independent t-test on average exam scores (maximum 300 points) showed "the experimental group (M = 272.40, SD = 8.91, n = 44) had higher scores than the control group (M = 251.44, SD = 10.56, n = 36)".

> "the experimental group (M = 272.40, SD = 8.91, n = 44) had higher scores than the control group (M = 251.44, SD = 10.56, n = 36), t (68.68) = 4.73, p < .001, two-tailed"
