## Evidence

### Gur Windmiller 2021

Gur Windmiller, Philip Blanco, and William F. Welsh. (2021). Developing Virtual Reality Activities for the Astro 101 Class and Lab. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2109.01592](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2109.01592)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison in general education astronomy: pre- and post-VR quizzes administered to the VR group and to a "control" group taught by traditional methods, across a cohort of N=116. Figure 1 reports the change in quiz score for the VR group (N = 48) versus the control group (N = 68).

> "We administered pre- and post-VR experience quizzes to test students' understanding, and did the same with a "control" group taught by traditional methods."

### Gur Windmiller 2021 (2)

Gur Windmiller, Philip Blanco, and William F. Welsh. (2021). Developing Virtual Reality Activities for the Astro 101 Class and Lab. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2109.01592](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2109.01592)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

In the authors' summary of the Welsh (2020) assessment results, students who participated in the VR activity "did at least as well as students who were taught with traditional methods"; no effect size is printed, so equivalence beyond non-impediment was not tested.

> "we found that students who participated in the VR activity did at least as well as students who were taught with traditional methods, as found by Madden (2020)."
