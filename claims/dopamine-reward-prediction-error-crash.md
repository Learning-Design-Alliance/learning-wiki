# Reward prediction error: achieving less than expected causes a dopamine dip below baseline that discourages future effort, while external reward surges are followed by compensatory dips

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Dopamine release on reward depends on the difference between expected and actual reward: achieving less than expected produces a below-baseline dip that discourages future efforts. [→ Lauren Margulieux 2025](#lauren-margulieux-2025)
`q2 i?` After external rewards are received, dopamine compensates by dipping below baseline, which can cause amotivation and psychological pain. [→ Lauren Margulieux 2025 (2)](#lauren-margulieux-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Lauren Margulieux 2025

Lauren Margulieux, James Prather, Masoumeh Rahimi. (2025). The Biological Benefits of Failure on Learning and Tools to Manage the Fallout. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10013-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Conceptual review presenting reward prediction error (citing Watabe-Uchida et al., 2017; Holroyd & Coles, 2002) as three outcomes: expected achievement releases no additional dopamine, better-than-expected releases extra dopamine, and worse-than-expected causes a below-baseline dip.

> "We achieve less than expected and experience a dopamine dip below baseline that discourages us from future efforts (e.g., we achieve a lower grade than expected)."

### Lauren Margulieux 2025 (2)

Lauren Margulieux, James Prather, Masoumeh Rahimi. (2025). The Biological Benefits of Failure on Learning and Tools to Manage the Fallout. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10013-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Review narrative citing Lembke (2021): the post-reward crash can cause amotivation and psychological pain, which the review links to feeling low after a big achievement such as the post-dissertation slump. The review argues the internal, pursuit part of the dopamine system should be preferentially leveraged over external rewards.

> "After external rewards are received, dopamine levels compensate by dipping below baseline (Lembke, 2021)."
