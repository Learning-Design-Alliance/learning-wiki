# Galileo's observation that all things fall at the same rate holds only when gravity is the dominant force and the bodies are close in space relative to the gravitating body

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The statement that all objects fall alike is a regime-limited claim: it requires the air not to matter and requires the falling bodies to be much closer to each other than to the source of the gravitational acceleration. [→ David W. Hogg 2007](#david-w-hogg-2007)

## Evidence

### David W. Hogg 2007

David W. Hogg. (2007). Real-world ballistics: A dropped bucket. https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.0107 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0709.0107](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0709.0107)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Conceptual argument in the lecture: the qualifying conditions are illustrated with a newspaper sheet that drifts, a helium balloon that rises, and bricks dropped in New York versus Paris or far from sea level, where accelerations differ in direction or magnitude.

> "The statement of Galilean gravity—that all things fall at the same rate—ought to be—more precisely—“under conditions in which gravity is the dom- inant force, all bodies that are close in space move with the same accelera- tion.”"
