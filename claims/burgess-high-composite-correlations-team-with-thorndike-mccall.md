## Subclaims
`q3 i3` The Burgess test's correlations with the rate and comprehension composites (0.82 and 0.80) are higher than those of any other test; its lowest correlation except Brown's comprehension is with the Thorndike-McCall (0.48), indicating the two measure different but correlated functions. ## Evidence

### Arthur I. Gates 1921

Arthur I. Gates. (1921). An Experimental and Statistical Study of Reading and Reading Tests (Continued). The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0068367

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis of the Burgess test against rate and comprehension composites and fourteen separate tests across grades III-VIII (Table XI). Printed correlations with the composites are r = 0.82 (rate) and r = 0.80 (comprehension), higher than any other test; the article also reports r = 0.48 with the Thorndike-McCall and calls the two "an excellent team".

> "On the whole, the correlation with the composites are high, higher than those yielded by any other test and equally high with rate and comprehension; 0.82 and 0.80 respectively."
