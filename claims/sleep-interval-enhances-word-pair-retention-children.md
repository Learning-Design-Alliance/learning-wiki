## Evidence

### Backhaus et al. 2008

Jutta Backhaus, Ralf Hoeckesfeld, Jan Born, Fritz Hohagen, Klaus Junghanns. (2008). Immediate as well as delayed post learning sleep but not wakefulness enhances declarative memory consolidation in children. Neurobiology of Learning and Memory 89, 76-80. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2007.08.010

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-subject experiment with 27 children (aged 9-12) learning word pairs in Sleep-Wake and Wake-Sleep conditions. The 3 x 2 ANOVA showed "significant main effects of the retention condition (F1,26 = 8.0, p = 0.009)", plus a significant condition x time interaction (F1,26 = 4.3, p = 0.024).

> "Children retained a signiﬁcantly greater number of previously learned word pairs after sleep than after wakefulness. This diﬀerence was conﬁrmed in an 3 · 2 ANOVA with the within-group factors ‘time’ (immediate recall at learning, ﬁrst and second delayed recall) and ‘retention condition’ (Sleep–Wake vs. Wake–Sleep) indicating signiﬁcant main eﬀects of the retention condition (F1,26 = 8.0, p = 0.009)"
