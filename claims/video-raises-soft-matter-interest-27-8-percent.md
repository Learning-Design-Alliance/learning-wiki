# In one uncontrolled pre-post study, respondents' self-reported interest and awareness of soft matter rose by an average of 2.78 points out of 10 (27.8%) after watching the homemade soft matter video

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After watching the video, respondents' levels of interest in soft matter related research, applications, and careers increased by 1.35 to 3.51 points, averaging 2.78 points out of 10 (27.8%). [→ Yen-Chen Chen 2020](#yen-chen-chen-2020)

## Evidence

### Yen-Chen Chen 2020

Yen-Chen Chen. (2020). Boost the Public Demand for Soft Matter Education and Career Opportunities with a Homemade Video. Worcester Polytechnic Institute Interactive Qualifying Project. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2006.09852](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2006.09852)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Online before/after survey (Google Form) comparing page 3 and page 5 ratings for five soft matter awareness and interest questions. The article reports increases of "1.35 to 3.51 points" with an "average increment of 2.78 points out of 10; 27.8 %"; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "the respondents’ levels of interests in soft matter related research, applications, and careers have increased by 1.35 to 3.51 points (with average increment of 2.78 points out of 10; 27.8 %), which shows that the video has stimulated people’s awareness on soft matter."

### Yen-Chen Chen 2020 (2)

Yen-Chen Chen. (2020). Boost the Public Demand for Soft Matter Education and Career Opportunities with a Homemade Video. Worcester Polytechnic Institute Interactive Qualifying Project. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2006.09852](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2006.09852)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same survey analysis, Figure 4.1.1: the article reports that standard deviations decreased after watching and interprets this as knowledge gaps between respondents shrinking. Descriptive statistics only; no test statistic is printed.

> "We can also see that the standard deviations on people’s interest or awareness to soft matter have decreased after watching the video. This indicates that the soft matter knowledge gaps between each respondent have been shrank by watching the video."
