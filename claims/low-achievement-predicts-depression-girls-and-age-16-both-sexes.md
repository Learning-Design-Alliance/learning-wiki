## Evidence

### López-López José A. 2021

López-López José A., Kwong Alex S.F., Washbrook Liz, Tilling Kate, Fazel Mina S., Pearson Rebecca M. (2021). Depressive symptoms and academic achievement in UK adolescents: a cross-lagged analysis with genetic covariates. Journal of Affective Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2021.01.091

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

RI-CLPM results for girls (N=1,938) in the ALSPAC cohort. The unadjusted model was inconclusive on achievement-to-depression paths, but adjusted models suggested "negative associations around the later stages of compulsory education", with higher achievement associated with lower later depressive symptoms. No standardised effect size is printed for these paths.

> "the estimates from the adjusted models suggest negative associations around the later stages of compulsory education (in the fully adjusted model, these were estimated to be -0.07, -0.13 to -0.01 between 14-16 years and -0.05, -0.11 to 0.01 between 16-18 years)."

### López-López José A. 2021 (2)

López-López José A., Kwong Alex S.F., Washbrook Liz, Tilling Kate, Fazel Mina S., Pearson Rebecca M. (2021). Depressive symptoms and academic achievement in UK adolescents: a cross-lagged analysis with genetic covariates. Journal of Affective Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2021.01.091

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

RI-CLPM results for boys (N=1,871). Adjusted models strengthened the evidence that achievement at the end of compulsory education (age 16) was negatively associated with depressive symptoms at 18, consistent with the authors' sensitive-period hypothesis. No standardised effect size is printed for this path.

> "Comparison between the unadjusted and the adjusted models showed differences mainly at the cross-lagged paths between ages 16 and 18, with stronger evidence of a negative association between academic achievement at 16 and later depressive symptoms (-0.09, -0.17, -0.01 in the fully adjusted model)"
