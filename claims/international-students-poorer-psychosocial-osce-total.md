# International medical students score significantly poorer than German students on a psychosocial medicine OSCE total score, with non-EU students scoring poorest

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Both non-EU (d = .61) and EU (d = .44) students scored significantly worse than German students on the psychosocial OSCE total score, with a significant group effect (η 2 = .044). [→ Huhn D. 2017](#huhn-d-2017)

## Evidence

### Huhn D. 2017

Huhn D., Lauter J., Roesch Ely D., Koch E., Möltner A., Herzog W., Resch F., Herpertz S. C., Nikendei C. (2017). Performance of International Medical Students In psychosocial medicine. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0950-z

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Retrospective analysis of OSCE scores for 1033 students (840 German, 73 EU, 120 non-EU) tested 2012–2015. ANOVA showed a significant group effect ("F(2, 1030) = 23.524: p < .001; η 2 = .044"); contrasts showed non-EU d = .61 and EU d = .44 poorer than German students.

> "The comparison between German students, students with an EU migration background and students with a non-EU migration background showed a significant group effect (F(2, 1030) = 23.524: p < .001; η 2 = .044)."
