## Evidence

### Diener 2026

Diener, J. P. W., Frick, B. L., & Kriek, J. (2026). Determining the degree of randomness in multiple-choice question distractors. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2610.01431](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.01431)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Analysis of FMCE item-level response data from two cohorts (N=48 and 206 submissions) in an introductory physics course in Botswana. The study reports "a mean of 89% (cohort I) and 88% (cohort II) of all incorrect responses" matching coded misconception options, replicated across cohorts (Table IV).

> "Across the cluster of low-scoring items highlighted above, a mean of 89% (cohort I) and 88% (cohort II) of all incorrect responses landed on exactly the answer options coded to that item's named misconception"

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`
