## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across reviewed studies, children with stronger topic knowledge comprehend texts better than when reading similarly cohesive texts on unfamiliar topics, for both skilled and low-skilled readers. [→ Reid Smith 2021](#reid-smith-2021)

## Evidence

### Reid Smith 2021

Reid Smith, Pamela Snow, Tanya Serry & Lorraine Hammond (2021) The Role of Background Knowledge in Reading Comprehension: A Critical Review, Reading Psychology, 42:3, 214-240, https://doi.org/10.1080/02702711.2021.1888348

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Critical review synthesis of 23 empirical studies (quasi-experimental and correlational) with children ages 6-12. The review reports this as a consistent finding across studies (Marr & Gormley, 1982; Reutzel & Morgan, 1990; Taft & Leslie, 1985).

> "We consistently found that higher levels of background knowledge enable children to better comprehend a text. Readers who have a strong knowledge of a particular topic, both in terms of quantity and quality of knowledge, are more able to comprehend a text than a similarly cohesive text for which they lack background knowledge."
