## Evidence

### Ghimire 2024

Ghimire, A. and Singh, C. (2024). How often does unguided peer interaction lead to correct response consensus? An example from Conceptual Survey of Electricity and Magnetism. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ad336c](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ad336c)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same graduate CSEM study; co-construction was defined as groups scoring 00,1 (both members incorrect individually, group correct). The article reports "in 19% of eligible cases across all questions, co-construction happened".

> "We find that in 19% of eligible cases across all questions, co-construction happened."

### Ghimire 2024 (2)

Ghimire, A. and Singh, C. (2024). How often does unguided peer interaction lead to correct response consensus? An example from Conceptual Survey of Electricity and Magnetism. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ad336c](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ad336c)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The same analysis notes that "15 out of 32 questions had an opportunity for co-construction", because in the remaining items at least one group member was already correct individually, limiting opportunities to co-construct knowledge.

> "We note that 15 out of 32 questions had an opportunity for co-construction since in other cases, the groups had at least one student in each group who had the correct answer individually before working in a group."
