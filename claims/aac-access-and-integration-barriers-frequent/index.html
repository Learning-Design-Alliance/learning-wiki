Parent-reported benefits, barriers, and experiences of augmentative and alternative communication use among children with autism in Nablus, Palestine: a cross-sectional study. BMC Pediatrics. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12887-026-07161-2

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Item-level results from the same cross-sectional parent survey in Nablus. Barriers were reported "by 53/75 parents/caregivers (70.7%)" for daily-routine integration and 51/75 (68.0%) for program access, despite high benefit scores.

"Difficulty integrating AAC into the child’s daily routine was reported by 53/75 parents/caregivers (70.7%), and difficulty accessing AAC programs was reported by 51/75 (68.0%)."

Discussion¶

Learner Variables¶

  • Autism Spectrum Disorder — population: instruction studied with autistic learners, not a comparison with non-autistic learners

Related Claims¶

  • Parents more often report initial difficulty accepting AAC than continued difficulty after time has passed — related
  • Parents of children with autism using AAC commonly report perceived benefits, most frequently increased communication opportunities — related
  • Parental awareness of alternative AAC systems is limited, and only about one-third agree AAC systems are suitable for all children — related
  • Exploratory subgroup and correlation analyses show mostly no significant differences in benefit or experience scores by child and family characteristics
  • Perceived AAC benefit score is positively correlated with the seven-item AAC implementation experience score
  • Many children with communication needs who require AAC lack access to appropriate supports, and embedding AAC interventions in the classroom is challenging — a broader claim this one bears on
  • AAC users report device useability difficulties, high cognitive load when learning communication systems, and poor physical and sensory access — related
  • Parental factors including emotional unreadiness, feeling overwhelmed, and unrealistic expectations can contribute to AAC device abandonment — related
  • Systems-level barriers to school AAC use include limited clinical expertise, need–system mismatch, funding difficulty, and unfamiliar communication partners — related
  • Family engagement programs can integrate mathematical thinking and activities across a range of settings — related
  • Sync feature uptake declined between survey and interview, with non-users citing unawareness, technical difficulties, or single-device viewing — related
  • Pre-study survey found most art teachers spend 1-3 hours daily planning and struggle to integrate and assess teaching resources across platforms — related
  • Learning spaces are positively perceived and well accepted by the educational community, with benefits outweighing barriers — related
