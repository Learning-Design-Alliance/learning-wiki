# Sense of mathematics belonging predicts algebra learning, even after controlling for prior knowledge and background factors

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Middle school students' competency expectations and sense of mathematics belonging predicted algebra learning after accounting for prior algebra knowledge and self-concept; in a follow-up, belonging was the only significant predictor of pre/post algebra gains. [→ Fitz 2025](#fitz-2025)
`q2 i?` Non-Asian racial and ethnic minority students reported lower mathematics belonging than Asian and White peers despite no differences in prior algebra knowledge. [→ Fitz 2025 (2)](#fitz-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Fitz 2025

Fitz, J. (with Price, H.). (2025). Positive conditions for mathematics learning: An overview of the research. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/967.472

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

A follow-up study of middle school students found "sense of belonging was the only significant predictor" of pre/post algebra skill learning, controlling for socioeconomic status, perceived importance of math, incremental ability views, and interest; no effect size printed.

> "A follow-up study found that sense of belonging was the only significant predictor of student learning as measured on a pre/post-test of algebra skills, even when considering students’ socioeconomic status, perceptions of the importance of math, incremental view of mathematical ability, and interest in math."

### Fitz 2025 (2)

Fitz, J. (with Price, H.). (2025). Positive conditions for mathematics learning: An overview of the research. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/967.472

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The same follow-up study found "non-Asian racial and ethnic minority students reported lower levels of belonging than their Asian and White peers" despite equivalent prior algebra knowledge, consistent with stereotype-internalization accounts.

> "Notably, this study also found that non-Asian racial and ethnic minority students reported lower levels of belonging than their Asian and White peers, even though there were no differences in their prior algebra knowledge."
