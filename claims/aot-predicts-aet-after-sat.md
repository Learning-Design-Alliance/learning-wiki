## Evidence

### Stanovich 1997

Keith E. Stanovich and Richard F. West. (1997). Reasoning Independently of Prior Belief and Individual Differences in Actively Open-Minded Thinking. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.89.2.342

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical regression analyses (Table 2) predicting the argument-quality beta weight, with SAT total entered first. The article concludes the linkage between thinking dispositions and AET performance "is not entirely due to covariance with cognitive ability."

> "the AOT composite score accounted for significant variance in performance on the AET even after SAT scores were entered into the equation—as did scores on the superstitious thinking, outcome bias (a measure of decontextualization), and counterfactual thinking (a mea- sure of perspective switching) measures."

### Stanovich 1997 (2)

Keith E. Stanovich and Richard F. West. (1997). Reasoning Independently of Prior Belief and Individual Differences in Actively Open-Minded Thinking. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.89.2.342

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Stepwise regression with the same variables; outcome bias entered third (1.7% additional variance, p < .05) and counterfactual thinking last (1.4%, p < .05). The article reports SAT entered fifth in the second hierarchical analysis accounted for 7.8% unique variance (p < .001).

> "The first variable to enter was SAT scores, which accounted for 12.4% of the variance (p \< .001). The next variable to enter the equation was the AOT composite, which accounted for 3.7% additional variance (p \< .001)."
