## Evidence

### Prell et al. 2012

Jerry Prell, William R. Nelson, and John Foshay. (2012). The Effectiveness of Learning Geography Using Computer-based Games. Journal of Special Education Apprenticeship, Vol. 1, No. 2. https://sheppardsoftware.com/USA_Geography/USA_G2_double2.html

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Matched-pairs quasi-experiment: 80 seventh-graders (40 pairs) played States Level 3 (control) or Level 6 (experimental) for 3 trials per day over 3 days; paired t-tests compared first and last trials, with Sig. (2 tailed) reported as .000 for all three measures in both groups.

> "The Sig. (2 tailed) score of less than .05 means that a statistical level of significance existed between initial and final scores for percent correct, distance error in miles and number of seconds needed to play for both the control and experimental groups."

### Prell et al. 2012 (2)

Jerry Prell, William R. Nelson, and John Foshay. (2012). The Effectiveness of Learning Geography Using Computer-based Games. Journal of Special Education Apprenticeship, Vol. 1, No. 2. https://sheppardsoftware.com/USA_Geography/USA_G2_double2.html

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive mean comparisons from the same matched-pairs study (Table 3): control percent correct rose from 69.90 to 87.65 and experimental from 58.15 to 82.55; distance error and completion time also decreased for both groups.

> "the control group improved their mean score for percent correct by 17.75 percentage points and the experimental group increased by 24.40 percentage points."
