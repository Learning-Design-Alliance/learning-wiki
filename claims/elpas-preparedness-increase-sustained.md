# E-learning on patient safety increased medical students' perceived preparedness for safe patient practice, sustained at one year

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` The intervention group felt better prepared for safe patient practice after the intervention and at follow-up (η² = .137), while control students felt less prepared the longer they were enrolled. [→ Gaupp R 2019](#gaupp-r-2019)

## Evidence

### Gaupp R 2019

Gaupp R, Dinius J, Drazic I, Körner M. (2019). Long-term effects of an e-learning course on patient safety: A controlled longitudinal study with medical students. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0210947

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Repeated-measures ANCOVA in the same controlled quasi-experimental study. Preparedness means in the intervention group rose from 4.32 (T0) to 4.88 (T1) and 4.65 (T2), while control means declined from 4.72 to 4.46. The printed effect size is "η2 = .137".

> "there was also a statistically significant effect of the e-learning on perceived preparedness for safe patient practice (F(2, 85) = 6.743, p < .002, η2 = .137), supporting the preparation hypothesis (ii)."
