# In an initial eye-tracking study, time on action predicted self-explanation (16-second threshold) and gaze shifts were most reliable at detecting its absence

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2`–`r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Actions accompanied by self-explanation took significantly longer on average than actions without it, and ROC analysis determined 16 seconds as the sufficient-time threshold. [→ Christina Merten and Cristina Conati 2005](#christina-merten-and-cristina-conati-2005)
`q2 i?` Among gaze shifts only, time only, and both combined, gaze shifts proved most reliable at detecting absence of self-explanation, while gaze shifts and time together performed best at identifying self-explanation when it occurred. [→ Christina Merten and Cristina Conati 2005 (2)](#christina-merten-and-cristina-conati-2005-2)

## Evidence

### Christina Merten and Cristina Conati 2005

Christina Merten and Cristina Conati. (2005). Eye-Tracking to Model and Adapt to User Meta-cognition in Intelligent Learning Environments. https://www.cs.ubc.ca

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

User study with 18 university students using ACE with gaze tracked by an Eyelink I eye-tracker; two experts independently coded self-explanation from audio/video, and only fully agreed actions were used, generating 149 data points. ROC analysis set the sufficient-time threshold at 16 seconds.

> "We found a statistically significant difference between the average time taken for actions accompanied by self-explanation and actions that were not, indicating that time can be a predictor of self-explanation behavior"

### Christina Merten and Cristina Conati 2005 (2)

Christina Merten and Cristina Conati. (2005). Eye-Tracking to Model and Adapt to User Meta-cognition in Intelligent Learning Environments. https://www.cs.ubc.ca

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

In the same 18-student study, three predictors were compared: gaze shifts only, time only, and both combined. Gaze shifts were "the most reliable at detecting the absence of self-explanati on", while gaze shifts and time together were best at identifying self-explanation when it occurred.

> "Gaze shifts proved to be the most reliable at detecting the absence of self-explanati on. However, gaze shifts and time performed best together at ide ntifying self- explanation when it occurred."
