## Evidence

### Orji 2021

Orji, F. A., Greer, J., & Vassileva, J. (2021). Evaluating a Persuasive Intervention for Engagement in a Large University Class. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-021-00260-4

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

A repeated-measures ANOVA with time as within-subject factor and condition type (experimental vs. control) as between-subject factor compared SARA engagement between the 228 surveyed students. The article reports "a significant main effect of condition type on students’ engagement" with η2 = .056, and pairwise comparison showed the intervention group more engaged (p < .05).

> "The results reveal that there was a significant main effect of condition type on students’ engagement (F1, 226 =2 0 . 5 5 ,p =. 0 0 0 1 ,η2 = .056). There was a significant difference in students ’ engagement between the experimental and control group over time."

### Orji 2021 (2)

Orji, F. A., Greer, J., & Vassileva, J. (2021). Evaluating a Persuasive Intervention for Engagement in a Large University Class. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-021-00260-4

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

An RM-MANOVA on MindTap data from 208 students compared the experimental and control groups over time. The article reports "a significant effect of condition type on students’ engagement" for number of logins (η2 = .644), time spent (η2 = .440), and engagement score (η2 = .948), with the experimental group more engaged (p < .05).

> "The results reveal that there was a significant effect of condition type on students’ engagement for number of logins (F1,206 = 371.947, p =. 0 0 0 1 ,η2 = .644), for the time they spent in the system (F 1,206 = 162.181, p = .0001, η2 = .440), and for their engagement score (F1,206 = 3748.788, p = .0001, η2 = .948)."
