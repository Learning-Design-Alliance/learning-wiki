# Self-perceived eye and ear anatomy comprehension significantly improved after the VR learning session in pooled data (effect sizes 0.54 and 0.62), but cohort-level significance was limited to MSc ear comprehension

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Pooled paired t-tests showed significant improvements in self-perceived comprehension with medium effect sizes of 0.54 (eye) and 0.62 (ear). [→ Alturkustani S 2025](#alturkustani-s-2025)
`q2 i?` In cohort-based analysis, only the MSc cohort showed a significant improvement in self-perceived ear comprehension (p = 0.014); the Neuroscience cohort showed no significant improvement for either structure. [→ Alturkustani S 2025 (2)](#alturkustani-s-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Alturkustani S 2025

Alturkustani S, Durfee A, O'Leary OF, O'Mahony SM, O'Mahony C, Lone M, Factor A. (2025). Measuring students' perceptions of virtual reality for learning anatomy using the general extended technology acceptance model for E‐learning. Anatomical Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.70045

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Two-tailed paired samples t-test comparing pre- and post-VR self-perceived comprehension (3-point scale) for 17 students. Pooled eye means rose from 2.38 (±0.53) to 2.7 (±0.39) and ear from 2.32 (±0.53) to 2.76 (±0.4).

> "Analysis of pooled data shows significant improvements in self‐perceived anatomy comprehension (p < 0.05) with a medium effect size (0.54) and (0.62) for the eye and ear, respectively."

### Alturkustani S 2025 (2)

Alturkustani S, Durfee A, O'Leary OF, O'Mahony SM, O'Mahony C, Lone M, Factor A. (2025). Measuring students' perceptions of virtual reality for learning anatomy using the general extended technology acceptance model for E‐learning. Anatomical Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.70045

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Cohort-based paired t-tests (Table 5): Neuroscience eye p = 0.153, ear p = 0.334; MSc eye p = 0.053, ear p = 0.014. The authors suggest high initial Neuroscience ear comprehension (mean 2.51) may have masked improvement.

> "However, the cohort‐based analysis showed that only the MSc cohort reported significant improvements in self‐perceived ear comprehension (p = 0.014)."
