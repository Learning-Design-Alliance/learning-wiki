# Student-instructor rating agreement in peer evaluation of video lab reports increased over the semester and was not solely an artifact of the distribution of instructor ratings

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The gain in agreement held within every instructor rating category, so it was not solely due to a nonuniform distribution of instructor ratings. [→ Scott S. Douglas 2014](#scott-s-douglas-2014)

## Evidence

### Scott S. Douglas 2014

Scott S. Douglas, Shih-Yin Lin, John M. Aiken, Brian D. Thoms, Edwin F. Greco, Marcos D. Caballero and Michael F. Schatz. (2014). Peer Evaluation of Video Lab Reports in a Blended Introductory Physics Course. [doi:10.1119/perc.2014.pr.015](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.015)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Within-expert-rating analysis excluding the single "excellent" instructor rating; Kolmogorov-Smirnov two-sample tests comparing Lab 1 to later labs were "statistically significant", p « 0.05, showing at least a small gain in agreement within every instructor rating.

> "The differences between all student rating distributions in Lab 1 and subsequent labs were determined to be statistically significant with a Kolmogorov-Smirnov two-sample test, p « 0.05"
