### Banerjee 2025

Banerjee, A. V., Bhattacharjee, S., Chattopadhyay, R., Duflo, E., Ganimian, A. J., Rajah, K. & Spelke, E. S. (2025). Children's arithmetic skills do not transfer between applied and academic mathematics. Nature. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08502-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Study 3a wave 1 randomized experiment with 400 working children in four cross-randomized conditions (market problem first, encouragement). Treatments reduced stress by 0.17-0.32 SD, but the article reports "we did not observe significant improvements in performance of solving abstract subtraction questions across any of the treatments".

> "However, we did not observe significant improvements in performance of solving abstract subtraction questions across any of the treatments"

### Banerjee 2025 (2)

Banerjee, A. V., Bhattacharjee, S., Chattopadhyay, R., Duflo, E., Ganimian, A. J., Rajah, K. & Spelke, E. S. (2025). Children's arithmetic skills do not transfer between applied and academic mathematics. Nature. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08502-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Study 3 randomized incentive experiment: a random sample of school children received tokens exchangeable for gifts. The article reports "We did not find significant differences in performance across groups" (β = 0.10, 95% CI -0.14 to 0.34, P = 0.406).

> "We did not find significant differences in performance across groups (β = 0.10, s.e.m. = 0.12, 95% CI = –0.14 to 0.34, P = 0.406"

### Banerjee 2025 (3)

Banerjee, A. V., Bhattacharjee, S., Chattopadhyay, R., Duflo, E., Ganimian, A. J., Rajah, K. & Spelke, E. S. (2025). Children's arithmetic skills do not transfer between applied and academic mathematics. Nature. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08502-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hint experiments embedded in study 3: working children received anchoring and rounding hints on abstract problems (effects of -2 and 1 percentage points, both non-significant); non-working children received breakdown and rounding hints on the word problem (β = 0.00, P = 0.881). The article reports "Neither anchoring nor rounding hints had a significant impact".

> "Neither anchoring nor rounding hints had a significant impact on how accurately the children answered these problems"
