# IRC analysis classified FCI items 4, 9 and 21 as inefficient for Ugandan students, while item 1 was effective

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Item 4's response options appealed across ability levels and it was classified inefficient; items 9 and 21 showed flat IRCs with many incorrect answers at all abilities. [→ Kirya 2025](#kirya-2025)
`q2 i?` Item 1's correct-response sigmoid curve and discriminating distractor curves matched the behaviour expected of an effective item. [→ Kirya 2025 (2)](#kirya-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Kirya 2025

Kirya, K. R., Mashood, K. K., & Yadav, L. L. (2025). Use of item response curves to evaluate the validity of the force concept inventory in secondary schools in Uganda and comparison with other educational contexts. European Journal of Mathematics and Science Education, 6(2), 79-95. https://doi.org/10.12973/ejmse.6.2.79

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

IRC analysis (Figures 4–7) of selected FCI items, plotting percentage of each response option against total score. For item 4, only 11% chose the correct option E; IRCs for items 9 and 21 were nearly flat and "did not exhibit the anticipated discriminating pattern".

> "As a result, the response options appear to appeal to students o f various ability levels. This could be due to students guessing and selecting responses at random. Hence, FCI item 4 is classified as an inefficient item."

### Kirya 2025 (2)

Kirya, K. R., Mashood, K. K., & Yadav, L. L. (2025). Use of item response curves to evaluate the validity of the force concept inventory in secondary schools in Uganda and comparison with other educational contexts. European Journal of Mathematics and Science Education, 6(2), 79-95. https://doi.org/10.12973/ejmse.6.2.79

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

IRC analysis of item 1: the correct response C showed a sigmoid curve, distractors were chosen mainly by low-ability students, and each choice drew at least 14% of students, matching Morris et al.'s effective-item pattern.

> "That means the pattern is indicative of an effective item because it conforms to the behaviour expected of an effective item (Morris et al. 2006)."
