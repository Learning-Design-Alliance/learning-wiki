# Knowledge of high-quality CPR principles improves from a mean 68% to 85% after the RCDP simulation, with improvement significant by role for advanced practice providers and resident/fellows but not reaching significance for nurses

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Overall mean knowledge scores on four multiple-choice questions improved from 68% preparticipation to 85% postparticipation (p < .01). [→ Songer K 2025](#songer-k-2025)
`q3 i?` When stratified by role, advanced practice providers and resident/fellows showed significantly improved knowledge scores, but improvement in the nurse group did not reach statistical significance, likely due to smaller sample size. [→ Songer K 2025 (2)](#songer-k-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Songer K 2025

Songer K, Fiero M, Roberts J. (2025). First 3 Minutes: A Rapid Cycle Deliberate Practice Pediatric Resuscitation Simulation for Multidisciplinary Staff. MedEdPORTAL. https://doi.org/10.15766/mep_2374-8265.11529

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Paired pre/post multiple-choice knowledge test (four questions on high-quality CPR principles) analyzed with a one-tailed paired t test. The article reports the overall mean score of "68% preparticipation and 85% postparticipation (p < .01)"; no effect size is printed.

> "Scores for the correct answer on these multiple-choice tests improved after the simulation session, with an overall mean score of 68% preparticipation and 85% postparticipation (p < .01; Table 3)"

### Songer K 2025 (2)

Songer K, Fiero M, Roberts J. (2025). First 3 Minutes: A Rapid Cycle Deliberate Practice Pediatric Resuscitation Simulation for Multidisciplinary Staff. MedEdPORTAL. https://doi.org/10.15766/mep_2374-8265.11529

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Role-stratified analysis of the same paired knowledge test. The article attributes the nurses' non-significant result to smaller sample size; no effect size is printed.

> "the advanced practice providers and resident/fellow groups showed significantly improved knowledge scores; however, improvement in scores in the nurse group did not reach statistical significance despite having pre- and postparticipation scores similar to those of the other groups"
