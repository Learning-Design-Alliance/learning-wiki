## Evidence

### Sukkurwalla 2024

Sukkurwalla, A., Zaidi, S.J.A., Taqi, M., Waqar, Z., & Qureshi, A. (2024). Exploring medical educators’ perspectives on teaching effectiveness and student learning. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06465-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Survey of 93 educators using the ATI in Karachi; Mann-Whitney U comparison of teaching experience by gender. "The mean rank for teaching experience was higher for male educators than for female educators" yet "not statistically significant (p = 0.154)"; equivalence was not tested.

> "The results indicate that the difference in teaching experience between male and female educators was not statistically significant (p = 0.154), suggesting no significant difference in teaching experience between male and female educators"

### Sukkurwalla 2024 (2)

Sukkurwalla, A., Zaidi, S.J.A., Taqi, M., Waqar, Z., & Qureshi, A. (2024). Exploring medical educators’ perspectives on teaching effectiveness and student learning. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06465-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same survey sample (n = 43 male, n = 50 female); Mann-Whitney U test of ITTF teacher-centric scores by gender reported "The p-value obtained from the test was 0.401". No effect size printed, so the null is not an equivalence finding.

> "This suggests that there was no statistically significant difference in teacher-centric scores between male and female teachers. The Mann-Whitney U value was 966.50."
