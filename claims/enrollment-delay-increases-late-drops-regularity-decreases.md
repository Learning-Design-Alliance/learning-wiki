# Later enrollment is associated with more late dropped course units, while later dropping and more regular planning activity are associated with fewer late dropped units

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Each SD increase in enrollment delay is associated with a 6% higher incidence rate of late dropped units (IRR = 1.06). [→ Borchers 2025](#borchers-2025)
`q2 i?` Later dropping (IRR = 0.93) and higher activity regularity (IRR = 0.79) are associated with lower incidence rates of late dropped units. [→ Borchers 2025](#borchers-2025)

## Evidence

### Borchers 2025

Borchers, C., Xu, Y., & Pardos, Z. A. (2025). Workload Overload? Late Enrollment Leads to Course Dropout. Journal of Educational Data Mining, Volume 17, No 1. https://github.com/CAHLR/enrollment-procrastination-edm

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Generalized linear mixed models of late dropped units per student semester with a random student intercept, fitted to 629,200 student semesters; the dual delay model had the lowest BIC (2,763,638). The article reports "a 6% increase in the expected incidence rate" per SD of enrollment delay, and 7% and 21% decreases for drop delay and regularity. No standardized effect sizes are printed.

> "Conversely, “Enrolled Later” yields a coefficient of 1.06, signifying a 6% increase in the expected incidence rate for each unit increase in the predictor. “Dropped Later” and “Higher Regularity in Activity” contribute coefficients of 0.93 and 0.79, respectively, suggesting a 7% decrease and a 21% decrease in the expected incidence rate perSD increase in their respective predictors."
