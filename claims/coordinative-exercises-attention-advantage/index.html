Evidence¶

Janssen M 2014¶

Janssen M, Toussaint HM, van Mechelen W, Verhagen EALM. (2014). Effects of acute bouts of physical activity on children's attention: a systematic review of the literature. SpringerPlus. https://doi.org/10.1186/2193-1801-3-410

q2 · i? · review · r3

Review's discussion of Budde et al. (2008), a school experiment with 115 adolescents (13–16 yrs) using heart-rate monitoring; significant improvement after coordinative exercises (p < 0.01) on the D2 test.

"Budde et al. (2008) compared a PA bout consisting of coordinative exercises to a normal PE lesson of the same intensity level and found a significant difference between the two PA bouts in favour of the coordinative exercise condition."

Related Claims¶

  • Evidence for effects of acute bouts of physical activity on children's attention is weak and inconclusive — related
  • School-setting studies show no overall significant difference between active and classroom lessons, but subgroup effects emerge — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Duration and intensity of PA bouts may moderate attention effects, consistent with an inverted-U hypothesis — related
  • Age may moderate acute PA effects on attention: effects appeared for 9–10 year olds but not 7–8 year olds — related
  • Five of six laboratory studies found significant effects of acute PA on cognitive control of attention, mainly on accuracy not reaction time — related
  • Exercise intensity differentially moderates acute exercise cognitive benefits, with moderate intensity favoring executive functions and high intensity favoring information processing — related
  • Physical activities combining exercise with character development or mindfulness improved children's EFs more than exercise alone: tae kwon do outperformed standard PE, and a yoga pilot improved planning — related
  • Self-chosen exercise intensity produced the greatest mood benefits in a small study, suggesting enjoyable workouts may best improve mood — related
  • Acute physical activity has a positive effect on attention in preadolescent children (g = 0.43) — related
