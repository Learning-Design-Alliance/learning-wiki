# Clinically relevant attention problems in ADHD cause writing problems without impairing reading

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Children with ADHD made more spelling errors in morphological categories and showed poor time utilization in writing, while their reading abilities were similar to the comparison group. [→ Döhla D and Heim S 2016](#dohla-d-and-heim-s-2016)

## Evidence

### Döhla D and Heim S 2016

Döhla D and Heim S (2016). Developmental Dyslexia and Dysgraphia: What can We Learn from the One About the Other? Front. Psychol. 6:2045. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.02045

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports Adi-Japha et al. (2007)'s study of ADHD children: "clinically relevant attention problems cause problems in writing", attributed to an impaired graphemic buffer and kinematic motor production rather than linguistic problems. Reading abilities of the ADHD group were similar to the comparison group, and fluency results were similar for both groups.

> "they determined that clinically relevant attention problems cause problems in writing. They suggested that the deﬁcits in writing do not necessarily have underlying linguistic problems but an impaired graphemic buﬀer and impairments in kinematic motor production."
