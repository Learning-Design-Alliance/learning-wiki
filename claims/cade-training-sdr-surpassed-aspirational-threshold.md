## Evidence

### Orzeszko Z 2025

Orzeszko Z, Gach T, Necka S, Ochwat K, Major P, Szura M. (2025). The implementation of computer-aided detection in an initial endoscopy training improves the quality measures of trainees' future colonoscopies: a retrospective cohort study. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-025-11890-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Group comparison from the retrospective cohort: the AI-trained group's sessile lesion detection rate of 12.5% exceeded the conventionally trained group's 7.1%, "surpass[ing] aspirational SDR threshold of 10%"; the 5.4% difference (95% CI 3.8–7.0%) was reported in high-power analysis. No standardized effect size was printed for this difference.

> "AI-trained endoscopists also surpassed aspirational SDR threshold of 10%, achieving an optimal level of 12.5%, compared to 7.1% in conventionally trained practitioners."

### Orzeszko Z 2025 (2)

Orzeszko Z, Gach T, Necka S, Ochwat K, Major P, Szura M. (2025). The implementation of computer-aided detection in an initial endoscopy training improves the quality measures of trainees' future colonoscopies: a retrospective cohort study. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-025-11890-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression on the 6000-patient cohort identified AI-enhanced training as a factor of high-quality SDR performance with "OR 1.93, 95% CI: 1.10–3.37" and p < 0.05; the authors interpret this as AI-trained endoscopists being almost twofold more likely to achieve high SDR.

> "Logistic regression confirmed that AI-trained endoscopists were more likely to meet high-quality standards in terms of ADR, SDR, and WT (OR 1.27, 95% CI: 1.14–1.42, p < 0.05; OR 1.93, 95% CI: 1.10–3.37, p < 0.05; OR 1.88, 95% CI: 1.69–2.09, p < 0.05, respectively), with no impact on CIR (Table 4)."
