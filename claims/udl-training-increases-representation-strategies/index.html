Evidence¶

Davies 2013¶

Davies, P. L., Schelly, C. L., & Spooner, C. L. (2013). Measuring the Effectiveness of Universal Design for Learning Intervention in Postsecondary Education. Journal of Postsecondary Education and Disability, 26(3), 195-220. http://accessproject.colostate.edu/udl

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Two-way mixed ANOVA (PrePost within, Group between) on student questionnaire ratings in nine intervention sections (386 paired respondents) versus six control sections (204 paired respondents); a priori t comparisons showed intervention gains, e.g., Q1 t = 3.66, p < .002, control NS.

"four questions yielded significant a priori PrePost change for the intervention group, but there was no significant change for the control group. Those questions are Q1 (presenting material in multiple formats), Q17 (highlighting key information after showing videos), Q22 (course content outside of class is delivered using technology), and Q24 (course materials are accessible, clearly organized and easy to use)."

Related Claims¶

  • The control group improved more than the intervention group on only two items, instructor enthusiasm and helpful feedback, without surpassing intervention post-ratings — related
  • Student-reported engagement, instructor enthusiasm, and helpful feedback improved across a semester in both trained and untrained sections, suggesting rapport effects independent of UDL training — related
  • For several UDL items the intervention group showed significantly greater pre-to-post change than the control group (significant PrePost x Group interactions) — related
  • Both groups increased in beginning lectures with an outline, but the intervention group showed significantly more change (Q7) — related
