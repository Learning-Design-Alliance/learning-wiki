# Translation features in mobile apps enabled direct communication between educators and parents who speak languages other than English

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Spanish-speaking caregivers and educators used built-in app translation features to communicate directly, replacing a prior practice of routing communication through Spanish-speaking support staff. [→ Orta 2022](#orta-2022)

## Evidence

### Orta 2022

Orta, D., & Gutiérrez, V. (2022). Improving school-family communication and engagement: Lessons from remote schooling during the pandemic. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/improving-school-family-communication-and-engagement

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Finding from focus groups with Spanish-speaking parents, who described pre-remote communication mediated by a bilingual secretary and times when "we didn't talk to the teacher." The brief attributes the shift to advancement in the accuracy of translation technologies.

> "During remote schooling, with the increased use of apps and virtual platforms, educators and caregivers were able to use built-in translation features to allow them to communicate directly with one another."
