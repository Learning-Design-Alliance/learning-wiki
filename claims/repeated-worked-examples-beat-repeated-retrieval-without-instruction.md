## Evidence

### Meng Cao & Paulo F. Carvalho 2026

Meng Cao & Paulo F. Carvalho. (2026). Striking the Balance: How Variability Shapes Retrieval Practice and Worked Examples for Transfer Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10169-w

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 replicated Experiment 1's procedure with the initial instruction omitted (74 participants after exclusions). The mixed-effects ANOVA on final-test accuracy showed a worked example advantage with printed effect size ηp² = .11 ("F(1,72) = 8.72, p = .004, ηp 2 = .11") — the opposite pattern from Experiment 1.

> "participants who studied worked examples performed significantly better on the final test than those who engaged in retrieval practice, F(1,72) = 8.72, p = .004, ηp 2 = .11."

### Meng Cao & Paulo F. Carvalho 2026 (2)

Meng Cao & Paulo F. Carvalho. (2026). Striking the Balance: How Variability Shapes Retrieval Practice and Worked Examples for Transfer Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10169-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A post hoc simple-effects analysis within the repeated-items condition of Experiment 2 found repeated worked examples (M = 0.69) outperformed repeated retrieval practice (M = 0.49), "t(72) = −4.34, p = < .001"; no standardized effect size was printed for this contrast.

> "Participants who studied repeated worked examples (M = 0.69, SE = 0.04) performed better on the final test than those who engaged in repeated retrieval practice ( M = 0.49, SE = 0.04), t(72) = −4.34, p = < .001."
