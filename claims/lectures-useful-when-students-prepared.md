## Subclaims
`q1 i?` After flip-JiTT preparation, students were more attentive during a lecture-mode explanation than the authors recalled in any recent lecture, suggesting preparation makes lectures useful. [→ Nathaniel Lasry 2012](#nathaniel-lasry-2012)

## Evidence

### Nathaniel Lasry 2012

Nathaniel Lasry, Michael Dugdale & Elizabeth Charles. (2012). Just in Time to Flip Your Classroom. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1309.0852](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1309.0852)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

Anecdotal classroom observation during a problem-solving session in the authors' E&M course, when one instructor spontaneously lectured on the electric field near an infinite sheet of charge. The authors attribute the engagement to prior preparation, citing the idea of A Time-For-Telling. No measures are reported.

> "Surprisingly, students were more attentive than ever. Questions and classroom discussions arose. Nowhere in recent memory could we find an instance of this level of engagement in any of our lectures."
