# Trainees accept Peyton's approach well: Peyton-group students felt they learned more and control-group women preferred the Peyton group

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` On the course evaluation, Peyton-group students reported learning more (e.g., 'In this course I learned a great deal': intervention F(1,19) = 5.1, p = 0.04), and control-group women especially reported preferring the Peyton group (p = 0.01 intervention, p = 0.003 gender). ## Evidence

### Gradl-Dietsch G 2016

Gradl-Dietsch G, Lübke C, Horst K, Simon M, Modabber A, Sönmez TT, Münker R, Nebelung S, Knobe M. (2016). Peyton's four-step approach for teaching complex spinal manipulation techniques – a prospective randomized trial. BMC Medical Education 16:284. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0804-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Paper-based 38-item survey; complete questionnaires from 23 participants (26% response rate). Multivariate analysis found no overall significant effect of intervention (p = 0.4) or gender (p = 0.7), but univariate items 'I learned a great deal' and 'would have rather been in the other group' reached significance.

> "Evaluation results showed that students in the Peyton Group had the impression that they had learned a lot and students in the Control Group, especially women, reported that they would have rather been in the Peyton Group."
