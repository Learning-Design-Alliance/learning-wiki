# The NGSS genetics standards move away from rigid Mendelian and Central Dogma inheritance models toward modeling, probability, and evidence-based claims, and drop the ethical-ramifications standard as developmentally inappropriate

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors interpret the NGSS as replacing rigid inheritance schemes with standards focused on modeling and on many possible inheritance schemes, shifting from possibility to probability defended by evidence-based claims. [→ Larry L Bowman 2014](#larry-l-bowman-2014)
`q1 i?` The authors judge the exclusion of CLE 3210.4.7 (ethical ramifications of emerging genetic technologies) appropriate because high school students cannot resolve moral debates about genetic engineering. [→ Larry L Bowman 2014 (2)](#larry-l-bowman-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Larry L Bowman 2014

Larry L Bowman, Jr. and Aimee L Govett. (2014). Becoming the Change: A Critical Evaluation of the Changing Face of Life Science, as Reflected in the NGSS. Submitted to The Science Educator. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1401.5681](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1401.5681)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

Interpretive analysis of the genetics guidemap (Figure 2): the authors report the NGSS drops Punnett-square/Mendelian and Central Dogma standards (CLE 3210.4.1-4) and instead focuses on "modeling and clarifying the fact that many schemes are not only possible but likely" (HS-LS3-1-3).

> "We see a distinct movement away from the very rigid models of inheritance proposed by the Punnet t square and Mendelian genetics (CLE 3210.4.3-4) and the Central Dogma (CLE 3210.4.1 -2, Figure 2)."

### Larry L Bowman 2014 (2)
