# Teacher experience and education explain little (never more than 5%) of the variance in teacher effects

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Neither teacher experience nor teacher education explained much variance in teacher effects (never more than 5%); experience effects on gains were significant only for grade-2 reading and grade-3 mathematics, and education only for grade-3 mathematics gains. [→ Nye 2004](#nye-2004)

## Evidence

### Nye 2004

Nye, B., Konstantopoulos, S., & Hedges, L. V. (2004). How Large Are Teacher Effects? Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis, 26(3), 237-257. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=How+Large+Are+Teacher+Effects

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

HLM fixed-effects analyses of the randomized STAR data adding dichotomous teacher experience (more than three years) and teacher education (graduate degree) at level 2. Positive experience effects on gains ranged from 0.06 to 0.19 standard deviations but were "statistically significant only for grade-2 reading and grade-3 mathematics achievement."

> "Neither teacher experience, nor teacher education explained much variance in teacher effects (never more than 5%)."

### Nye 2004 (2)

Nye, B., Konstantopoulos, S., & Hedges, L. V. (2004). How Large Are Teacher Effects? Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis, 26(3), 237-257. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=How+Large+Are+Teacher+Effects

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Significance results from the same level-2 HLM analyses of teacher experience on achievement gains in the STAR experiment. Effects on achievement status were generally smaller, significant only for grade-2 reading; no education effect on status was significant.

> "However, the effects of teacher experience on achievement gains were statistically significant only for grade-2 reading and grade-3 mathematics achievement."
