# Educational researchers rarely examine the validity assumptions of their ANOVA-type analyses, with under 20% of reviewed articles mentioning assumption violations

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In content analyses of published educational research, fewer than 20% of articles in between-subjects univariate designs and fewer than 16% of repeated-measures articles referenced distributional assumption violations. [→ Keselman 1998](#keselman-1998)

## Evidence

### Keselman 1998

Keselman, H.J., Huberty, C.J., Lix, L.M., Olejnik, S., Cribbie, R.A., Donahue, B., Kowalchuk, R.K., Lowman, L.L., Petoskey, M.D., Keselman, J.C., & Levin, J.R. (1998). Statistical Practises of Educational Researchers: An Analysis of Their ANOVA, MANOVA and ANCOVA Analyses. https://doi.org/10.2307/1170601

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Content analysis of 61 between-subjects univariate design articles from prominent educational journals (1994/1995 issues). The review reports that "In 12 articles (19.7%), the author(s) indicated some concern for distributional assumption violations." Normality was assessed by descriptive measures only; variance homogeneity was evaluated in five articles.
