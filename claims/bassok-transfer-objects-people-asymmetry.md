# Transfer of a permutation algorithm succeeds for objects-assigned-to-people problems but fails for people-assigned-to-objects problems

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After training on an objects-to-people assignment problem, participants transferred the solution successfully to other objects-to-people problems (89%) but not to people-to-objects problems (0%), because world knowledge about assignment roles shaped the interpreted structure. [→ Hippolyte Gros 2020](#hippolyte-gros-2020)

## Evidence

### Hippolyte Gros 2020

Hippolyte Gros, Jean-Pierre Thibaut, Emmanuel Sander. (2020). Semantic Congruence in Arithmetic: A New Conceptual Model for Word Problem Solving. Educational Psychologist. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2019.1691004

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article reports, citing Bassok et al. (1995), a transfer experiment in which participants received a short lesson and a training problem with its solving equation before solving a transfer problem using the same algorithm. Success was "89% of success" for O→P transfer versus 0% for P→O transfer; no effect size is printed.

> "participants who were first trained on a problem involving an assignment of objects to people (O→P) had a dramatically higher success rate when they transferred the solution to other O→P problems (89% of success) than those who had to transfer the solution to “people assigned to objects” (P→O) problems (0% of success)."
