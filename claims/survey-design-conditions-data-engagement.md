# Survey design and management conditions influenced how schools engaged with climate data

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Schools engaged better with climate data when surveys were accessible, short, and engaging, scheduled to allow same-year action, disaggregated by student group, and supported by structured discussion time, ownership, and external support. [→ Klevan 2024](#klevan-2024)

## Evidence

### Klevan 2024

Klevan, S., Leung-Gagné, M., & Nakajima, T. M. (2024). Using data to improve school climate: Insights from three California schools. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/918.411

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Cross-case finding identifying factors that hindered or helped data use across the three schools; the report notes "all three schools faced challenges related to survey design and staff capacity."

> "Findings suggest that schools can better engage and use climate data when surveys are accessible, short, and engaging; surveys are scheduled with ample time to analyze and act upon them in the same school year; climate reports disaggregate for student groups; schools set aside structured time to examine and discuss survey results with students and staff; district- and school-level personnel have ownership over the process; and schools have external support to bolster their capacity."
