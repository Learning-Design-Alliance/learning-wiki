## Evidence

### Mwilike Beatrice Erastus 2026

Mwilike Beatrice Erastus, Philbert Ruchius, Rimoy Martha, Mabada Lucy, Komba Nicodem, Okade Tom, Mwanga Feddy, Gosse Rashid, Ambikile Joel. (2026). Long-term knowledge and skills retention following Helping Mothers Survive and Helping Babies Survive training in Tanzania: a mixed-methods follow-up study. Frontiers in Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1824835

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Paired t-tests comparing post-test and follow-up knowledge scores among 210 providers in the mixed-methods cross-sectional study. All four domains showed statistically significant declines, e.g., pre-eclampsia "t = 20.276, p < 0.001".

> "For all knowledge areas assessed, there was a significant decrease in knowledge scores. Specifically, statistically significant drops were observed in mean knowledge scores for controlling bleeding after birth (t = 15.404, p < 0.001), pre-eclampsia (t = 20.276, p < 0.001), helping babies breathe (t = 8.580, p < 0.001), and essential care for small babies (t = 19.620, p < 0.001)"
