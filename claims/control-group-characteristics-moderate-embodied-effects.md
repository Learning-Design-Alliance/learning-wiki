# In exploratory between-subject analyses, embodied learning showed larger effects against passive non-embodied controls (g = 0.43) than against active non-embodied controls (g = 0.86)

## Evidence

### Xiaoxuan Li · Julia Chatain · Fan Wang · Manu Kapur 2026

Xiaoxuan Li · Julia Chatain · Fan Wang · Manu Kapur. (2026). From Body to Word: A Three-Level Meta-Analysis of Embodied Vocabulary Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10194-9

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Exploratory between-subject meta-regression of control group characteristics. Effects were larger against active non-embodied conditions (g = 0.86) than passive controls (g = 0.43), significant at F = 5.89, p = .02.

> "only control group characteristics significantly moderated effect sizes ( F = 5.89, p = .02). Specifically, larger effects were observed when embodied learning was compared with active non-embodied learning conditions ( g = 0.86, 95% CI [0.60, 1.12]) than when it was compared with passive non-embodied control conditions ( g = 0.43, 95% CI [0.16, 0.69])."
