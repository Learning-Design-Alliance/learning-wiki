Evidence¶

Leat et al. 2017¶

Susan J. Leat, Francis Fengqin Si, Deborah Gold, Dawn Pickering, Keith Gordon, and William Hodge. (2017). The Experience of a Randomized Clinical Trial of Closed-Circuit Television Versus Eccentric Viewing Training for People with Age-Related Macular Degeneration. Journal of Visual Impairment & Blindness. https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT00971464

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Randomized parallel-arm trial at CNIB with 10 completers (4 eccentric viewing training, 6 CCTV), all previously given basic low vision care and optical devices. The primary outcome showed "a significant difference between the groups ( p /H110050.04)" in pre-post change in 1.3M reading speed.

"Within the CCTV group, there was a significant improvement at the outcome visit compared to the baseline (paired t-test, p /H110050.005), but not within the eccentric viewing training group ( p /H110050.28). A two-sample t-test of the changes (pre-post) showed a significant difference between the groups ( p /H110050.04)."
