# Correlational meta-analyses of learning styles and achievement average r = 0.24 but do not test or support the matching hypothesis

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Thirteen correlational meta-analyses summarizing 559 studies show an average correlation of r = 0.24 (d = 0.50) between learning style scores and achievement, but these correlations say nothing about matching styles to optimize achievement. [→ John Hattie 2025](#john-hattie-2025)

## Evidence

### John Hattie 2025

John Hattie, Timothy O'Leary. (2025). Learning Styles, Preferences, or Strategies? An Explanation for the Resurgence of Styles Across Many Meta-analyses. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10002-w

`q3 · i2` · `review · r2`

Synthesis of the 13 correlational meta-analyses in the MetaX database (559 studies, 82,853 people per Table 1). The review reports the pooled "correlation of r = 0.24 (d = 0.50)" and stresses that causality and directionality are lost; the correlation is a moderate effect size as printed.

> "Secondly, the correlation-based meta-analyses have an overage correlation of r = 0.24 (d = 0.50) and say nothing about matching styles to optimize achievement outcomes. Causality and directionality are lost in these correlations."
