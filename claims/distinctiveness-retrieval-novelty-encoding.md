## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Making an item distinctive does not increase encoding strength but eases retrieval, and several studies found novelty exposure enhances encoding of subsequently learned material up to thirty minutes later. [→ van Kesteren 2020](#van-kesteren-2020)

## Evidence

### van Kesteren 2020

van Kesteren, M. T. R. & Meeter, M. (2020). How to optimize knowledge construction in the brain. npj Science of Learning 5:5. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-020-0064-y

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review of encoding studies: distinctiveness acts via lessened interference at retrieval (a study where items became distinctive only after study still showed better retrieval), while novelty "can create a time window for strong learning in your hippocampus", probably mediated through release of dopamine.

> "Several studies have found that exploring a novel environment 83,84 or seeing a set of novel pictures 85 enhances encoding of subsequently learned material, up to thirty minutes later."
