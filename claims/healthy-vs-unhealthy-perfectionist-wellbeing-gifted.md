# Healthy perfectionist groups show better emotional well-being than unhealthy perfectionist groups among gifted students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across seven group-based studies, healthy perfectionist groups reported more positive outcomes (happiness, positive adjustment, self-esteem) and fewer negative outcomes than unhealthy perfectionist groups. [→ Grugan 2021](#grugan-2021)

## Evidence

### Grugan 2021

Grugan, M. C., Hill, A. P., Madigan, D. J., Donachie, T. C., Olsson, L. F., & Etherson, M. E. (2021). Perfectionism and Academically Gifted Students: A Systematic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09597-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Synthesis of seven group-based studies (n = 2088 gifted students) examining emotional intelligence, life satisfaction, self-esteem, and coping. Healthy groups "reported more positive outcomes (e.g., increased happiness, positive adjustment, and self-22 esteem)" than unhealthy groups; comparisons with non-perfectionists varied by outcome.

> "the findings show that the healthy perfectionist groups 21 reported more positive outcomes (e.g., increased happiness, positive adjustment, and self-22 esteem) and less negative outcomes (e.g., maladaptive psychological symptoms and 23 dysfunctional coping strategies) than unhealthy perfectionist groups."
