## Evidence

### Schwortz 2020

Schwortz, A. C., & Burrows, A. C. (2020). Authentic Science Experiences with STEM Datasets: Post-secondary Results and Potential Gender Influences. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2004.12448](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2004.12448)

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Video-recorded observation of 20 groups during the activity: among "the nine mixed-gender groups, three had only males controlling the computer" and three had males controlling it most of the time; two had only a female controlling the computer.

> "In the nine mixed-gender groups, three had only males controlling the computer, three had males controlling the computer the majority of the time (with females only touching the mouse occasionally), two had only a female controlling the computer"

### Schwortz 2020 (2)

Schwortz, A. C., & Burrows, A. C. (2020). Authentic Science Experiences with STEM Datasets: Post-secondary Results and Potential Gender Influences. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2004.12448](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2004.12448)

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Discussion-section observation of the same recordings: "when female undergraduates worked in mixed-gender groups, they were never observed to control the computer mouse or keyboard"; the only female sole controllers were one female science educator who participated twice.

> "In this study, when female undergraduates worked in mixed-gender groups, they were never observed to control the computer mouse or keyboard."
