Evidence¶

Allensworth 2005¶

Allensworth, E. M., & Easton, J. Q. (2005). The On-Track Indicator as a Predictor of High School Graduation. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/track-indicator-predictor-high-school-graduation

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Observational analysis of the fall 2000 CPS cohort by number of semester F's, reported with Figure 5. Fewer than one-third of freshmen with three or more semester F's graduated in four years; the relationship held equally among high- and low-elementary-achieving students.

"The vast majority of students (83 percent) who received no semester F's in core courses during the freshman year graduated within four years. Students who received just one semester F had a graduation rate more than 20 percentage points lower than than students who passed all their courses."

Allensworth 2005 (2)¶

Allensworth, E. M., & Easton, J. Q. (2005). The On-Track Indicator as a Predictor of High School Graduation. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/track-indicator-predictor-high-school-graduation

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Correlational analysis of 1999 CPS freshmen by subject of the failed course. The report found the relationship between course failure and graduation was similar across all subjects, so no single subject's failure is uniquely predictive.

"graduation was correlated with English failure, math failure, and science failure at -0.49, and with social science failure at -0.48"
