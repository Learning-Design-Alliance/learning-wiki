# Learning style and form of teaching show no statistically significant interaction effect on university students' academic achievement

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a quasi-experiment with pre-service English teachers, the two-way ANOVA found no significant interaction between learning style and form (online vs in-class) on academic achievement. [→ Cimermanová 2018](#cimermanova-2018)

## Evidence

### Cimermanová 2018

Cimermanová, I. (2018). The Effect of Learning Styles on Academic Achievement in Different Forms of Teaching. International Journal of Instruction, 11(3), 219-232. https://doi.org/10.12973/iji.2018.11316a

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Two-way ANOVA in a one-semester pedagogical quasi-experiment (online experimental group n=27, in-class control n=55) with GRSLSS-measured styles. The article reports "no significant interaction" for form, F(1, 72) = .27, p>.05, and for learning style, F(4, 72) = .06.

> "For the form, the ANOVA result (table 1) indicated no significant interaction, F(1, 72) = .27, p>.05. Similarly, no interaction effect was recorded for learning style, F(4, 72) = .06, p>05."
