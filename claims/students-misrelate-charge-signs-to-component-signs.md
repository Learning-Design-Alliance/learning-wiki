# Some students incorrectly relate the signs of electric field or force components to the signs of the charges, e.g., attaching a negative sign to any component of a negative charge's field or taking absolute values of all charges

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students either automatically attach negative signs to components of vectors associated with negative charges, or place absolute value signs around all charges, rendering all quantities positive. [→ Colin S. Wallace 2024](#colin-s-wallace-2024)

## Evidence

### Colin S. Wallace 2024

Colin S. Wallace, Liam Jones, Alex Lin. (2024). Four errors students make with inverse-square law vectors. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ad2391](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ad2391)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative analysis of E&M exam responses only; the article states this error "only occurs in problems about electric fields or forces" and that prevalence was not quantified because incorrect procedures often yield mathematically correct results.

> "Some will automatically include a negative sign with any component of any vector associated with a negative charge, regardless of whether or not that vector points in a direction they (implicitly or explicitly) treat as positive elsewhere in their solution."
