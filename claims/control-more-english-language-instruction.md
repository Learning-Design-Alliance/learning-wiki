## Evidence

### Condelli 2010

Condelli, L., Cronen, S., Bos, J., Tseng, F., and Altuna, J. (2010). The Impact of a Reading Intervention for Low-Literate Adult ESL Learners—Executive Summary (NCEE 2011-4004). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Classroom observation analysis in the same randomized study reporting the complementary instructional emphasis: "English language acquisition instruction" dominated control classrooms. Control classrooms also spent more time on functional reading, writing and math instruction (18 percent vs. 5 percent).

> "Conversely, the control classrooms spent more time on English language acquisition instruction than did Sam and Pat classrooms (68 percent vs. 27 percent of observed time intervals, respectively), and this difference was statistically significant."
