# A student's epistemological wall between formal and everyday reasoning is associated with between-type reconciliation in only 13% of opportunities versus 46% within type

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In coded clinical interviews, Jan reconciled conflicting lines of reasoning about 46% of the time when both lines were of the same type (formal or everyday/intuitive) but only 13% of the time when the lines were of different types, supporting an epistemological barrier between the two reasoning types. [→ Lising 2005](#lising-2005)

## Evidence

### Lising 2005

Lising, L., & Elby, A. (2005). The impact of epistemology on learning: A case study from introductory physics. Submitted to the American Journal of Physics. [doi:10.1119/1.1848115](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1848115)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Coding of 36 reconciliation opportunities from 106 coded reasoning lines across five interviews with one case-study student. Within type (28 opportunities) Jan reconciled 13 times; between types (8 codings) she reconciled once. The authors note the small number of codings "does not give very reliable statistics."

> "the dramatic difference between her within-type and between-type reconciliation rates, 46% versus 13%, supports our attribution of a barrier between formal and everyday/intuitive thinking in Jan's epistemology."

### Lising 2005 (2)

Lising, L., & Elby, A. (2005). The impact of epistemology on learning: A case study from introductory physics. Submitted to the American Journal of Physics. [doi:10.1119/1.1848115](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1848115)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Reliability check on the interview coding: an external education researcher coded a subset including deliberately difficult cases, and "The external coder's codings matched our codings 80% of the time," with the same general trends in reconciliation rates.

> "To test the reliability of our coding, we trained an education researcher not previously involved with the project and then had the coder apply our four coding steps to a subset of our data"
