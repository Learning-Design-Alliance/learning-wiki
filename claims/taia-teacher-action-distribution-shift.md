## Evidence

### Fan Chen 2026

Fan Chen, Gaowei Chen. (2026). How a technology-augmented inquiry approach affects teacher practice and student learning in disadvantaged science classrooms. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09788-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Frequency and chi-square analysis of video-coded teacher support actions across six recorded inquiry sessions (three per group); statistics are as printed, no effect size reported.

> "A total of 839 discrete teacher support actions were coded, with a higher frequency observed in the EG ( n = 454) than in the CG ( n = 385). This difference suggests that the technology-augmented approach elicited more frequent instructional interactions. A chi-square test confirmed a significant difference in the distribution of teacher support actions between the groups [χ²(8) = 119.914, p < 0.001]"

### Fan Chen 2026 (2)

Fan Chen, Gaowei Chen. (2026). How a technology-augmented inquiry approach affects teacher practice and student learning in disadvantaged science classrooms. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09788-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparative action counts from the video analysis (final coding at Cohen’s kappa = 0.86): A1 83 vs 14, B2 89 vs 38, and Content Delivery (C1) more frequent in the CG (156 vs 62), C2 98 vs 41. The article concludes TAIA reoriented the teacher's role toward scaffolding inquiry.

> "Hypothesis & Planning (A1) occurred approxi- mately six times more frequently in the EG (83 instances vs. 14 in the CG), indicating a greater emphasis on monitoring and guiding students’ initial inquiry tasks."
