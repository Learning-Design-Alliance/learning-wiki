# Algorithm aversion persists even among participants who saw the algorithm outperform the human forecaster

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants in the model-and-human condition who saw the model beat the human were still less likely to choose the model than participants who had not seen the model perform. [→ Berkeley J. Dietvorst 2014](#berkeley-j-dietvorst-2014)

## Evidence

### Berkeley J. Dietvorst 2014

Berkeley J. Dietvorst, Joseph P. Simmons, and Cade Massey. (2014). Algorithm Aversion: People Erroneously Avoid Algorithms After Seeing Them Err. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General. https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000033

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Chi-square tests across Studies 1, 3a, 3b, and 4 restricted to model-and-human participants who saw the model outperform the human (83%, or 610 of 741, across the five studies) confirmed the aversion; Study 3a's test was not significant in that subsample.

> "This result is not limited to the minority who saw the human outperform the model, as even those who saw the model outperform the human were less likely to choose the model than were participants who did not see the model perform."
