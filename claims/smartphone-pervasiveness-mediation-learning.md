## Evidence

### Gui Marco 2026

Gui Marco, Respi Chiara, Abbiati Giovanni, Paladini Cristiana, Ercolanoni Sofia, Milzani Francesca, Pirola Tiziana, Vezzoli Giovanni. (2026). Longitudinal effect of early social media use on standardized learning outcomes during school career. Nature Human Behaviour. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-026-02522-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory mediation analysis (Table 2) using matching estimators with school track as control; pervasiveness was associated with lower grade-10 scores (a one-s.d. increase corresponding to −0.11 Italian, −0.14 mathematics) and with earlier account opening. The authors stress the evidence is purely correlational because mediators lacked longitudinal data.

> "By contrast, smartphone pervasiveness explains a meaningful portion of the effect—between 33% and 47% in mathematics, and between 15% and 34% in Italian."
