# Across 309 students, median normalized gain was 0.42 overall, with a female–male difference (0.38 vs 0.45) that was not statistically significant

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For 309 learners from 21 non-lab physics and astronomy courses over 12 semesters, the median normalized gain was 0.42±0.18 overall, 0.38±0.19 for female learners and 0.45±0.18 for male learners; the difference was not statistically significant. [→ Cooksey 2022](#cooksey-2022)
`q2 i?` Learners with higher final letter grades tended to show larger normalized gains, but the authors state it is difficult to disentangle causation from correlation because grades were assigned with knowledge of normalized gains. [→ Cooksey 2022 (2)](#cooksey-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Cooksey 2022

Cooksey, K. L. & Jonsson, P. (2022). Using Pre-/Post-Quizzes Intentionally in Curriculum Development and Evaluation. UC Santa Cruz: Institute for Scientist & Engineer Educators. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2210.01823](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2210.01823)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Secondary observational analysis of the instructor's own course records (Figure 2), described as very limited; the sex labels are the instructor's perceptions, not students' self-identification. No effect size or test statistic is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "For the entire sample in Figure 2, the median gain is 0.42±0.18 overall, 0.38±0.19 for female learners, and 0.45±0.18 for male, where the uncertainties are the median absolute deviations. There is a difference, but it is not statistically significant."

### Cooksey 2022 (2)

Cooksey, K. L. & Jonsson, P. (2022). Using Pre-/Post-Quizzes Intentionally in Curriculum Development and Evaluation. UC Santa Cruz: Institute for Scientist & Engineer Educators. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2210.01823](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2210.01823)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The same Figure 2 analysis examined gain as a function of letter grade; the correlation is confounded because higher-gain students tended to be bumped up a letter grade. No effect size is printed.

> "Learners with higher final grades tended to learn more. It is difficult to disentangle causation from correlation since letter grades were assigned with knowledge and at- tention to normalized gains"
