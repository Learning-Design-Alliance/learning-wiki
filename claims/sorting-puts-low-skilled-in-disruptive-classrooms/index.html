Evidence¶

Elaine Allensworth and Takako Nomi 2014¶

Elaine Allensworth and Takako Nomi. (2014). Skill-Based Sorting in the Era of College Prep for All. University of Chicago Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/skill-based-sorting-era-college-prep-all-costs-and-benefits

q2 · i? · causal · r?

The report examines classroom environments under sorted versus de-sorted conditions in Chicago algebra classes, finding "low-skilled students are at higher risk of being in disruptive classrooms" under sorting. The release prints no effect size for this finding.

"When classes are sorted by skill level, low-skilled students are at higher risk of being in disruptive classrooms. Thus, sorting can lead students with low entering skills to have a weaker instructional environment."
