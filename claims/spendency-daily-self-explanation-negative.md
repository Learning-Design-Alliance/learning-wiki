# Spendency is negatively related to daily self-explanation quality during training but not to posttest or retention self-explanation quality

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · associational `r1`–`r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Spendency correlated negatively with daily self-explanation quality on training days 3 through 8, but not on days 1 and 2. [→ Snow 2015](#snow-2015)
`q2 i?` In hierarchical regressions controlling pretest self-explanation ability, spendency significantly predicted daily self-explanation quality on each of the six significant training days. [→ Snow 2015 (2)](#snow-2015-2)
`q2 i?` Spendency was not related to self-explanation quality at posttest or at the delayed retention test. [→ Snow 2015 (3)](#snow-2015-3)

## Evidence

### Snow 2015

Snow, E. L., Allen, L. K., Jackson, G. T., & McNamara, D. S. (2015). Spendency: Students' Propensity to Use System Currency. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education, 25, 407–427. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0044-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Pearson correlations between spendency and daily self-explanation scores across the eight training sessions of the 11-session study. Relations were negative on days 3–8 and "no significant relation between spendency and self-explanation quality on days 1 and 2 of training."

> "Results from this analysis indicated that there was a negative correlation between students ' spendency and self-explanation quality on days 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, and 8. However, there was no significant relation between spendency and self-explanation quality on days 1 and 2 of training."

### Snow 2015 (2)

Snow, E. L., Allen, L. K., Jackson, G. T., & McNamara, D. S. (2015). Spendency: Students' Propensity to Use System Currency. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education, 25, 407–427. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0044-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

"significant models and R2 change values for session 3, F(1,37)=6.79" with R2 change=0.13; significant models were also reported for sessions 4 through 8.

### Snow 2015 (3)

Snow, E. L., Allen, L. K., Jackson, G. T., & McNamara, D. S. (2015). Spendency: Students' Propensity to Use System Currency. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education, 25, 407–427. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0044-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pearson correlations between spendency and self-explanation quality at posttest and at the one-week delayed retention test in the same 40-student sample. Spendency "was not related to self-explanation quality at posttest" (r=−0.195, p=0.227) "or retention" (r=−0.231, p=0.187).

> "This analysis revealed that spendency was not related to self-explanation quality at posttest (r=−0.195, p=0.22 7) or retention (r=−0.231, p= 0.187)."
