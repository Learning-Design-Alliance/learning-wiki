## Evidence

### Lund Frederike 2012

Lund Frederike, Schultz Jobst-Hendrik, Maatouk Imad, Krautter Markus, Möltner Andreas, Werner Anne, Weyrich Peter, Jünger Jana, Nikendei Christoph. (2012). Effectiveness of IV Cannulation Skills Laboratory Training and Its Transfer into Clinical Practice: A Randomized, Controlled Trial. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0032831

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Video-recorded clinical performance of IV cannulation by both randomized groups; time to successful cannulation was measured in seconds from the processed video material. The skills laboratory group was faster, "IG: 595.4 SD(188.1)s; CG: 692.7 SD(247.8)s".

> "Additionally, IG needed a significantly shorter time for the performance on a patient (IG: 595.4 SD(188.1)s; CG: 692.7 SD(247.8)s; 95% CI 23.5 s to 45.1 s; p = 0.049)."

### Lund Frederike 2012 (2)

Lund Frederike, Schultz Jobst-Hendrik, Maatouk Imad, Krautter Markus, Möltner Andreas, Werner Anne, Weyrich Peter, Jünger Jana, Nikendei Christoph. (2012). Effectiveness of IV Cannulation Skills Laboratory Training and Its Transfer into Clinical Practice: A Randomized, Controlled Trial. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0032831

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

In the same video-rating outcome analysis, the number of successful attempts was counted; the intervention group achieved significantly more successful attempts, "IG: 36; CG: 26; p = 0.015", while mean total attempts did not differ (2.39 vs 2.43, p=0.850).

> "Students of the IG, however, made significantly more successful attempts than students of the CG (IG: 36; CG: 26; p = 0.015)."
