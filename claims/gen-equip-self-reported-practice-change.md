## Evidence

### Jackson L 2018

Jackson L, O'Connor A, Paneque M, Curtisova V, Lunt PW, Pourova RK, Macek M Jr, Stefansdottir V, Turchetti D, Campos M, Henneman L, Godino L, Skirton H, Cornel MC. (2018). The Gen-Equip Project: evaluation and impact of genetics e-learning resources for primary care in six European languages. Genetics in Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41436-018-0132-3

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Self-reported practice change (Kirkpatrick level 3) from surveys and interviews. The article notes many changes in practice behavior are self-reported and qualitative; no effect size is printed.

> "When asked how the Gen-Equip modules had influenced their practice and teaching, many cited that they had made them more likely to "be suspicious" of a genetic condition where this was possible, to ask more questions about the family history, to seek advice from genetics colleagues, and to refer to specialist services (Fig. 1)."

### Jackson L 2018 (2)

Jackson L, O'Connor A, Paneque M, Curtisova V, Lunt PW, Pourova RK, Macek M Jr, Stefansdottir V, Turchetti D, Campos M, Henneman L, Godino L, Skirton H, Cornel MC. (2018). The Gen-Equip Project: evaluation and impact of genetics e-learning resources for primary care in six European languages. Genetics in Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41436-018-0132-3

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Phase 4 qualitative interview data (21 interviews, thematic analysis coded independently by three researchers). One interviewee's account of changed practice regarding patient choice about testing is quoted verbatim.

> "Others paid more attention to the patient's perspective:"I do leave options open more and I talk to people about why they might not want to be tested, [stating that] they don't have to do this but this is an option for you…it has changed my practice with each patient." (interview 3)"
