# Greater anger, hopelessness, and negative emotions overall during reading are associated with poorer text comprehension, while positive emotions show no relation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Among high school students reading an expository text, greater anger, hopelessness, and negative emotions overall correlated with poorer text comprehension (small correlations), whereas none of the positive emotions were related to comprehension. [→ Prinz-Weiß 2023](#prinz-wei-2023)

## Evidence

### Prinz-Weiß 2023

Prinz-Weiß, A., Lukosiute, L., Meyer, M., & Riedel, J. (2023). The role of achievement emotions for text comprehension and metacomprehension. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09331-w

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Correlational field study of N = 358 senior high school students in regular biology classes reading a 580-word expository text; Spearman correlations showed negative emotions related to poorer comprehension. The article notes the correlations were "of rather small size" (e.g., hopelessness r = -.19).

> "Anger and hopelessness were nega- tively correlated with text comprehension, as were negative emotions overall."
