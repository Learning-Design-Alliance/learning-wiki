# For the more difficult chain-stitch task, the anthropomorphic condition completed the task significantly faster and viewed the tutorial significantly fewer times than the non-anthropomorphic condition

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The anthropomorphic condition was significantly faster at completing the difficult chain-stitch task and revisited the tutorial significantly fewer times, which the authors interpret as a more immediate and confident understanding of the stitch. [→ Murano 2011](#murano-2011)

## Evidence

### Murano 2011

Murano, P. & Sethi, T. (2011). Anthropomorphic User Interface Feedback in a Sewing Context and Affordances. International Journal of Advanced Computer Science and Applications, Vol. 2, No. 4. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1208.3323](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1208.3323)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

MANOVA on task 2 timing in the same 40-participant between-users sewing experiment: the anthropomorphic group was significantly faster (p < 0.01, table III). A parallel significant result for "number of tutorial visits for task 2" shows the anthropomorphic condition visited the tutorial significantly fewer times (table VI).

> "For the variables Time taken for task 2, experimental condition, age range and gender, there is a significant (p < 0.01) difference. The anthropomorphic condition completed the task significantly faster than the non -anthropomorphic condition"
