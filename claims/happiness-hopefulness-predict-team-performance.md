# Post-simulation happiness and hopefulness positively predict perceived team performance in team-based medical simulations

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Higher post-simulation happiness and hopefulness ratings predict higher perceptions of team performance (b = .22, η² = .08). [→ Grewal 2026](#grewal-2026)

## Evidence

### Grewal 2026

Grewal, K., Azher, S., Moreno, M., Pekrun, R., Wiseman, J., Flake, J., Lajoie, S., Sun, N.‐Z., Fried, G. M., Khalil, E., & Harley, J. M. (2026). Medical trainees' emotions and their effects on perceptions of performance and team mood in team‐based simulations. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 96, 306–336. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.70017

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Multi-level model of post-simulation emotions predicting perceived team performance, controlling for presimulation emotions, role, PGY and specialty, showed that as the happiness and hopefulness variable increased, "perceptions of team performance increased" (b = .22, p = .001, η² = .08).

> "From the model, the post‐simulation happiness and hopefulness variable was a significant predictor at the individual level for perceptions of team performance. As scores for the post‐simulation happiness and hopefulness variable increased, perceptions of team performance increased, b = .22; p = .001; η 2 = .08; power > .99."
