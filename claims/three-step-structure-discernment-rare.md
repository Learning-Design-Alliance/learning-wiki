## Evidence

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2012

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2012). Using an Isomorphic Problem Pair to Learn Introductory Physics: Transferring from a Two-step Problem to a Three-step Problem. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.9.020114](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.9.020114)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Categorization of written work from the two introductory courses (Table 12) into structural mistake categories; the percentage showing good understanding of the three-step solution structure is reported for each condition. Percentages are descriptive; no effect size is printed.

> "As shown in Table 12, only 44~50% of the calculus based students and less than 25% of the algebra-based students were able to figure out the 3 step nature of the quiz problem."

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2012 (2)

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2012). Using an Isomorphic Problem Pair to Learn Introductory Physics: Transferring from a Two-step Problem to a Three-step Problem. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.9.020114](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.9.020114)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Examination of control-group written work in both courses found omitting the conservation of momentum principle was among the most common mistakes, especially in the algebra-based course (50% versus 24% in calculus-based). Descriptive percentages only.

> "Twenty four percent of the calculus-based control group students and fifty percent of the algebra-based control group students made this mistake ."
