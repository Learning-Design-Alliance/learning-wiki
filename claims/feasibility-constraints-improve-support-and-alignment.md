## Evidence

### Chenguang Pan 2026

Chenguang Pan, Yuxuan Li, Thomas Harris, and Youmi Suk. (2026). Estimating Optimal Dynamic Treatment Regimes for Personalized Education: A Tutorial and Applications with Machine Learning. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2610.10843](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.10843)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multi-stage ODTR analysis of HSLS:09 with four decision points (Grades 9–12), evaluating recommendation plausibility via the empirical support rate. Constrained models reached 92.7% and 94.1% versus 53.7% and 56.2% unconstrained.

> "The math achievement model under the feasibility constraints yields an empirical support rate of 92.7%, compared with 53.7% for its unconstrained counterpart. For college enrollment, the constrained model attains 94.1%, compared with 56.2% for the standard model."

### Chenguang Pan 2026 (2)

Chenguang Pan, Yuxuan Li, Thomas Harris, and Youmi Suk. (2026). Estimating Optimal Dynamic Treatment Regimes for Personalized Education: A Tutorial and Applications with Machine Learning. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2610.10843](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.10843)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same multi-stage analysis, comparing expert-alignment rates against an expert-informed set of math pathways based on math and STEM identity. The feasible ODTR "increases this proportion to 43.9%" for math achievement and to 38.7% for college enrollment.

> "the observed course-taking patterns have a rate of 28.9%, whereas the feasible ODTR increases this proportion to 43.9%. For college enrollment, the corresponding proportion increases from 23.5% in the observed data to 38.7% under the feasible model."
