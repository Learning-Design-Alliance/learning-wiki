## Evidence

### Aaron Fisher 2014

Aaron Fisher, G. Brooke Anderson, Roger Peng, Jeff Leek. (2014). A randomized trial in a massive online open course shows people don't know what a statistically significant relationship looks like, but they can learn. arXiv. [doi:10.7717/peerj.589](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.589)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized trial embedded as an ungraded component of a statistics MOOC with 2,039 participating students, each classifying eight scatterplots. The study reports "47.4% (95% CI: 45.1%-49.7%)" sensitivity and 74.6% specificity, showing subjects tended to be conservative in classifying significance.

> "Subjects correctly classified only 47.4% (95% CI: 45.1%-49.7%) of statistically signiﬁcant relationships, and 74.6% (95% CI: 72.5%-76.6%) of non-signiﬁcant relationships."
