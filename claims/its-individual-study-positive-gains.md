## Evidence

### Létourneau 2025

Létourneau, A., Martineau, M. D., Charland, P., Karran, J. A., Boasen, J. & Léger, P. M. (2025). A systematic review of AI-driven intelligent tutoring systems (ITS) in K-12 education. npj Science of Learning 10:29. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00320-7

`q2 · i3` · `review · r3`

Nehring et al.'s quasi-experiment across five schools (100 twelfth-graders) in a 2x2 mixed ANOVA (F(1, 98) = 19.16, η² = 0.16, p < 0.001) found the ALEKS intervention group significantly increased exam scores with "d = 0.87"; the control group's change was not significant.

> "students in the intervention group signiﬁcantly increased their exam scores between October and May (M_diff = 13.55, SE = 1. 7 2 ,p < 0.001, d = 0.87, 95% CI [10.14, 16.96]), whereas the control group exhibited no statistically signiﬁcant change in mean perfor- mance"

### Létourneau 2025 (2)

Létourneau, A., Martineau, M. D., Charland, P., Karran, J. A., Boasen, J. & Léger, P. M. (2025). A systematic review of AI-driven intelligent tutoring systems (ITS) in K-12 education. npj Science of Learning 10:29. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00320-7

`q2 · i2` · `review · r3`

Roscoe et al.'s no-control study of Writing Pal (113 students, average 16 h over six months) found essay scores "increased signiﬁcantly ( t(112) = 5.85, p = 0.001, d = 0.71)"; Ökördi et al.'s eDia study similarly reported gains of one-third of a standard deviation for students completing more than half the sessions.

> "Both pre-and post-studyessays were graded, and their essay scores increased signiﬁcantly ( t(112) = 5.85, p = 0.001, d = 0.71)27.T h e authors also noted that positive changes were observed in essay structure and lexical sophistication27."
