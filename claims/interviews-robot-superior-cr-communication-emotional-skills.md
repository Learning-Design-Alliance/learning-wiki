# Interviewed students experienced the social robotic platform as superior to the computer-based platform for interactivity, engagement, clinical reasoning and communication practice, while emotional-skill practice fell short of real encounters

> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · causal `r1` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Students described the robotic platform as highly interactive and engaging, superior to VIC. [→ Borg A 2025](#borg-a-2025)
`q1 i?` Students perceived the robot as effective for practicing clinical reasoning and communication skills through active dialogue and follow-up questions. [→ Borg A 2025 (2)](#borg-a-2025-2)
`q1 i?` Students perceived emotional-skill practice (e.g., empathy) as inferior with both platforms compared with real-life patient encounters. [→ Borg A 2025 (3)](#borg-a-2025-3)

## Evidence

### Borg A 2025

Borg A, Georg C, Jobs B, Huss V, Waldenlind K, Ruiz M, Edelbring S, Skantze G, Parodis I. (2025). Virtual Patient Simulations Using Social Robotics Combined With Large Language Models for Clinical Reasoning Training in Medical Education: Mixed Methods Study. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/63312

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative thematic analysis of in-depth interviews with 8 medical students, theme "realism and immersion", subtheme interactivity and engagement; students reported the robot required active participation versus passively clicking.

> "The social robotic platform facilitated a highly interactive and engaging experience, which was described to be superior to VIC."

### Borg A 2025 (2)

Borg A, Georg C, Jobs B, Huss V, Waldenlind K, Ruiz M, Edelbring S, Skantze G, Parodis I. (2025). Virtual Patient Simulations Using Social Robotics Combined With Large Language Models for Clinical Reasoning Training in Medical Education: Mixed Methods Study. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/63312

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Interview theme "skill acquisition and development"; students also reported practicing communication skills by thinking about how to phrase questions in real time, while the computer case was seen as more informative.

> "Students reported that the robotic platform facilitated the development of CR skills by engaging them in active dialogue, requiring them to think critically about the patient's symptoms, and allowing them to ask relevant follow-up questions."

### Borg A 2025 (3)

Borg A, Georg C, Jobs B, Huss V, Waldenlind K, Ruiz M, Edelbring S, Skantze G, Parodis I. (2025). Virtual Patient Simulations Using Social Robotics Combined With Large Language Models for Clinical Reasoning Training in Medical Education: Mixed Methods Study. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/63312

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Interview subtheme on emotional skills; students said you do not really see the pain in someone through a robot, a limit applying to both platforms.

> "students perceived that the practice of "emotional skills," such as empathy, was inferior with both platforms compared to real-life patient encounters"
