# Students who progressed furthest in the tournament achieved higher pharmacology marks than the rest of the class

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Pharmacology question marks varied with tournament progression (p = 0.0221), with final-stage participants scoring higher than the rest of the class (p = 0.0057). [→ Delage Clément 2024](#delage-clement-2024)
`q2 i?` Non-pharmacology question marks did not vary according to tournament progression (p = 0.3553). [→ Delage Clément 2024](#delage-clement-2024)

## Evidence

### Delage Clément 2024

Delage Clément, Palayer Maeva, Lerouet Dominique, Besson Valérie C. (2024). “Pharmacotrophy”: a playful tournament for game- and team-based learning in pharmacology education - assessing its impact on students’ performance. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05157-z

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Combined-ratio analysis of participants split by tournament progression (nGroup stage = 10, nQuarter-final = 13, nSemi-final = 9, nFinal = 7), analysed with Kruskal-Wallis and post-hoc tests. Final-stage students’ ratio of “1.290 ± 0;107 vs 0.986 ± 0.024” for the rest of the class on pharmacology questions.

> "Students reaching the final stage obtained better marks than the rest of the class (1.290 ± 0;107 vs 0.986 ± 0.024; p = 0.0057b)."
