# ToGRA subscale gains favored the treatment group on careers, enjoyment and leisure, with enjoyment showing the greatest change

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` On all three ToGRA subscales the control group declined while the treatment group rose; the enjoyment subscale showed the greatest mean change for both groups (post difference 0.35, p=0.003). [→ Richardson 2013](#richardson-2013)

## Evidence

### Richardson 2013

Richardson, Ronald; Brouillette, Liane. (2013). Historic and Ethnic Music Found to Increase Geographic Understanding: A Quasi-Experimental Study. Journal for Learning through the Arts, 9(1). http://escholarship.org/uc/item/71g4x3cr

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subscale analysis (Table 7) of attitudes toward geography-related careers, enjoyment, and leisure activities for 84 control and 131 treatment students. Enjoyment post difference was 0.35 (p=0.003); career post difference 0.16 was not significant (p=0.083).

> "In all cases (including both the “total” ToGRA mean scores as well as the subscales), the control group went down whereas the treatment group scores went up. Comparisons of the change scores indicate statistically significant mean differences. The greatest mean change for both treatment and control groups was the “enjoyment” category."
