## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Immediate feedback tutors let students learn from their errors by correcting them, but students are not given the opportunity to learn from the downstream consequences of their errors. [→ Kenneth R. Koedinger 2007](#kenneth-r-koedinger-2007)

## Evidence

### Kenneth R. Koedinger 2007

Kenneth R. Koedinger, Vincent Aleven. (2007). Exploring the Assistance Dilemma in Experiments with Cognitive Tutors. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-007-9049-0

`q1 · i?` · `review · r?`

The authors' interpretive note (type e) qualifying the feedback debate: immediate feedback tutors do support error correction without further assistance, yet withhold the opportunity to learn from errors' downstream consequences. No effect size is printed.

> "In fact, immediate feedback tutors do allow students to learn from their errors—after all students are required to correct their errors and can and often do so without further assistance. However, they are not given the opportunity to learn from the downstream consequences of their errors."
