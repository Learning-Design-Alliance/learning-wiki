# Physical activity interventions in childhood show no overall benefit for executive functions or academic achievement

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3` · `i0` negligible

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` Combined across sub-domains, physical activity interventions did not significantly benefit executive function measures (g = 0.09) or academic achievement (g = 0.14). [→ Vasilopoulos 2023](#vasilopoulos-2023)

## Evidence

### Vasilopoulos 2023

Vasilopoulos, F., Jeffrey, H., Wu, Y., & Dumontheil, I. (2023). Multi-Level Meta-Analysis of Physical Activity Interventions During Childhood: Effects of Physical Activity on Cognition and Academic Achievement. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09760-2

`q3 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Three-level meta-analysis combining EF sub-domains (127 effect sizes, 45 studies) and AA sub-domains (114 effect sizes, 47 studies) in the Results, showing negligible summary effects of "g = 0.09" for executive functions and "g = 0.14" for academic achievement, both non-significant. Equivalence was not tested.

> "When combining across sub-domains, three-level meta-analysis indicated that PA interventions did not benefit executive function measures (g = 0.09, p = 0.24 (95% CI: − 0.06–0.25), very low-certainty evidence) or academic achievement (g = 0.14, p = 0.14 (95% CI: − 0.04–0.31), moderate-certainty evidence)"
