# Same-rater associations between teacher behavior ratings and emotions are unlikely to result from common-method bias only

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The association between teacher/student perceptions and teacher/student emotions cannot be completely attributed to common-method bias, because overlap between perspectives also accounted for part of the variance. [→ Donker 2021](#donker-2021)

## Evidence

### Donker 2021

Donker, M. H., van Vemde, L., Hessen, D. J., van Gog, T., & Mainhard, T. (2021). Observational, student, and teacher perspectives on interpersonal teacher behavior: Shared and unique associations with teacher and student emotions. Learning and Instruction, vol. 73, 101414. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2020.101414

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The novel correction method applied to the regression results showed "about 25% of the explained variance in positive emotions originally attributed to the students’ perspective only could be attributed to the overlap between students and teachers", likely due to shared classroom history.

> "This study confirmed that the association between teacher/student perceptions and teacher/student emotions cannot be completely attributed to common-method bias. We showed that the student perspective on communion was correlated with the teacher perspective, and that about 25% of the explained variance in positive emotions originally attributed to the students’ perspective only could be attributed to the overlap between students and teachers"
