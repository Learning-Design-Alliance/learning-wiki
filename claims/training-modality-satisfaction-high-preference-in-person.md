## Evidence

### Geissler Mark Enrik 2024

Geissler Mark Enrik, Bereuter Jean-Paul, Geissler Rona Berit, Bökkerink Guus Mattheus Johannes, Egen Luisa, Kowalewski Karl-Friedrich, Haney Caelan. (2024). Comparison of distance versus in-person laparoscopy training using a low-cost laparoscopy simulator—a randomized controlled multi-center trial. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-024-11069-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-test self-designed questionnaire on training perception in the randomized trial. Ratings of skill development, teaching, tutor support, and instrument visibility were all non-significant between groups; overall over 90% of participants rated the training as good or very good.

> "The vast majority of students in both groups found their skill improvement to be very good or good (control: 71.4% vs. intervention: 94.7%, p value = 0.249)."

### Geissler Mark Enrik 2024 (2)

Geissler Mark Enrik, Bereuter Jean-Paul, Geissler Rona Berit, Bökkerink Guus Mattheus Johannes, Egen Luisa, Kowalewski Karl-Friedrich, Haney Caelan. (2024). Comparison of distance versus in-person laparoscopy training using a low-cost laparoscopy simulator—a randomized controlled multi-center trial. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-024-11069-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Questionnaire item on preferred training modality. Despite comparable satisfaction, preference significantly favored in-person training (p < 0.001): all in-person participants and 42.1% of digital participants chose in-person.

> "Regarding the choice of training groups, there were significant differences between the groups with the majority of students wanting to be trained in-person (control: 100% vs. intervention: 42.1%, p value < 0.001)"
