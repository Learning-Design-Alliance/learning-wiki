Book 1908¶

Book, William Frederick. (1908). The Role of the Teacher in the Most Expeditious and Economic Learning. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=The+Role+of+the+Teacher+in+the+Most+Expeditious+and+Economic+Learning

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Introspective and objective records from the typewriting learning study showed adaptations were "never planned but made quite unintentionally." The study further determined the pre-condition was extreme effort on a good day after old associations were revived, and that less effort accompanied plateau stages.

"It was found that the new adaptations or forward steps in the development of these higher-order habits were never planned but made quite unintentionally so far as the learners were concerned."

Related Claims¶

  • In natural learning, elemental and higher-order habits develop together, so the most economic method is to practice with the highest-order habits possible — related

  • Practice when learners were nervous or tired was worthless for strengthening associations and positively injurious, favoring wrong habits — related
