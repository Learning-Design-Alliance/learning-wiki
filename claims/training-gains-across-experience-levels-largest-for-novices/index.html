Evidence¶

Negar Kamali 2026¶

Negar Kamali, Candice Rockell Gerstner, Jessica Hullman, Matthew Groh. (2026). Generative AI Literacy Training Improves Intelligence Analysts' Discrimination of Real and AI-Generated Images. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28510

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Exploratory subgroup analysis of the counterbalanced experiment, splitting participants by self-reported digital-forensics experience (roughly ten per subgroup). The no-experience group also had the lowest pre-training accuracy (62.7%) and the highest false-positive rate (43.9%).

"The training effect, estimated by the BA×Post interaction, was largest among participants with no prior forensics experience, corresponding to a 9.2 percentage point increase in accuracy (95% CI: [3.5, 14.2]). Participants with less than one year and more than one year of experience showed smaller estimated gains of 5.4 percentage points (95% CI: [0.5, 10.0]) and 2.0 percentage points (95% CI: [-3.9, 8.3]), respectively."

Negar Kamali 2026 (2)¶

Negar Kamali, Candice Rockell Gerstner, Jessica Hullman, Matthew Groh. (2026). Generative AI Literacy Training Improves Intelligence Analysts' Discrimination of Real and AI-Generated Images. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28510

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Exploratory subgroup analysis splitting participants by self-reported generative AI familiarity. The article reports the training "improved participant accuracy across most generative AI familiarity groups", with the frequent-use group gaining the most.

"The estimated training effect was largest among participants reporting frequent or daily prior use of generative AI tools, corresponding to a 7.2 percentage point increase in accuracy (95% CI: [3.9, 10.0]). Participants reporting occasional and rare use exhibited smaller estimated gains of 6.0 percentage points (95% CI: [-1.3, 13.2]) and 2.9 percentage points (95% CI: [-3.3, 9.0]), respectively."
