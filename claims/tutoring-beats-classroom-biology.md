# Both the ITS and human tutoring conditions outperformed classroom-only instruction on immediate and delayed tests

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` ITS students were significantly more likely than Class students to answer post-test items correctly (OR = 2.24, d = .50) and delayed-test items correctly (OR = 2.04, d = .45). [→ Olney 2025](#olney-2025)
`q3 i2` Human-tutored students were significantly more likely than Class students to answer post-test items correctly (OR = 2.04, d = .44) and delayed-test items correctly (OR = 2.11, d = .47). [→ Olney 2025](#olney-2025)

## Evidence

### Olney 2025

Olney, A.M., D'Mello, S.K., Person, N., Cade, W., Hays, P., Dempsey, C.W., Lehman, B., Williams, B., Graesser, A. (2025). Efficacy of a Computer Tutor that Models Expert Human Tutors. [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-98462-4_38](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-98462-4_38)

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Between-condition Tukey-adjusted contrasts from the logistic mixed model in the Results section of the 9-week study (N = 34). The article reports ITS vs Class post-test "OR = 2.24, d= .50", Human vs Class post-test OR = 2.04, d= .44, and delayed-test contrasts ITS OR = 2.04, d= .45 and Human OR = 2.11, d= .47.

> "revealed significant differences between ITS and Class,z = 4.24, p < .001, and between Human and Class,z = 3.84, p < .001, such that ITS was more likely to answer questions correctly on the post-test than Class,OR = 2.24, d= .50"
