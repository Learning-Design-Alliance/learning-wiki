## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Two specific forms of collective reflexivity, fractured and restricted, were linked to disengagement in relation to joint tasks such as group projects and dissertation approval processes. [→ Peter Kahn 2017](#peter-kahn-2017)

## Evidence

### Peter Kahn 2017

Peter Kahn, Lucy Everington, Kathleen Kelm, Iain Reid, Francine Watkins. (2017). Understanding student engagement in online learning environments: the role of reflexivity. Education Tech Research Dev, 65, 203-218. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-016-9484-z

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Concluding analysis of the multiple case study of online postgraduate students. Fractured collective reflexivity arose where students felt control was limited, and restricted collective reflexivity where practices worked against mutual understanding.

> "We identiﬁed two speciﬁc forms of collective reﬂexivity that were linked to student disengagement in relation to joint tasks, and these can be contrasted with the development of mutual insight that resulted from the exercise of collective reﬂexivity in relation to the discussion boards."

### Peter Kahn 2017 (2)

Peter Kahn, Lucy Everington, Kathleen Kelm, Iain Reid, Francine Watkins. (2017). Understanding student engagement in online learning environments: the role of reflexivity. Education Tech Research Dev, 65, 203-218. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-016-9484-z

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview analysis of the eight-student sub-group; only four of the eight participants offered indications of such reflexivity, which the authors partly attribute to structural constraints in the different disciplines.

> "Participants exhibited concerns on 19 occasions that we identiﬁed as typical of fractured reﬂexives, in which concerns were focused on frustrations rather than on constructive ways forward."
