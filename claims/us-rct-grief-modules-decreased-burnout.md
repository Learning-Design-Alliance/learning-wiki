# A US randomized controlled trial found significantly decreased burnout after physicians attended four modules on stress, grief and burnout in medicine

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Attending four modules on stress, grief and burnout in medicine was followed by significantly decreased levels of burnout, operationalized as emotional exhaustion and depersonalization. [→ Sikstrom L 2019](#sikstrom-l-2019)

## Evidence

### Sikstrom L 2019

Sikstrom L, Saikaly R, Ferguson G, Mosher PJ, Bonato S, Soklaridis S. (2019). Being there: A scoping review of grief support training in medical education. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0224325

`q3 · i?`

The review reports a US randomized controlled trial (cited as reference 34, Medisauskaite and Kamau) of four modules on stress, grief and burnout. It notes "significantly decreased levels of burnout (i.e., emotional exhaustion and depersonalization)"; no effect size is printed.

> "An RCT in the US noted significantly decreased levels of burnout (i.e., emotional exhaustion and depersonalization) after attending four modules on stress, grief and burnout in medicine [34]."
