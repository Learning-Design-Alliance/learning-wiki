# Some students mentally convert between numerical and pictorial representations, and their reasoning can change with representation

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Interviewed students sometimes mentally converted numerical data into a pictorial error-bar representation to solve the numerical item, and individual students gave different-quality reasoning on the two isomorphic items. [→ Gayle Geschwind 2023](#gayle-geschwind-2023)

## Evidence

### Gayle Geschwind 2023

Gayle Geschwind, Michael Vignal, and H. J. Lewandowski. (2023). Representational differences in how students compare measurements. [doi:10.1119/perc.2023.pr.geschwind](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2023.pr.geschwind)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Qualitative analysis of 27 think-aloud interviews conducted during Fall 2022, in which students completed SPRUCE and explained each answer choice. One student "essentially converted the numeral data into pictorial data in their mind"; another gave nonexpert reasoning on the NRI but expert-like reasoning on the PRI.

> "mentally switching from a numeric to a pictorial repre- sentation easily and using this skill to solve the numeric item"
