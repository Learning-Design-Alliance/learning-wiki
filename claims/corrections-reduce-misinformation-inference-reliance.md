## Evidence

### Swire-Thompson 2025

Swire-Thompson, Briony, Butler, Lucy, & Rapp, David N. (2025). The truth sandwich format does not enhance the correction of misinformation. Journal of Educational Psychology, 117(6), 851–862. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000961

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r3`

One-way ANOVA on post-test inference ratings in Experiment 1 showed a significant main effect of condition, ηp2 = .30, with "inference ratings in both the refutation and truth sandwich conditions lower than in the control" (ps < .001).

> "There was a significant main effect of condition, F(1.93, 997.37) = 216.89; p < .001, MSE = 2.73, ηp2 = .30, with inference ratings in both the refutation and truth sandwich conditions lower than in the control, t(516) = 16.65, p < .001; t(516) = 17.84, p < .001 respectively."

### Swire-Thompson 2025 (2)

Swire-Thompson, Briony, Butler, Lucy, & Rapp, David N. (2025). The truth sandwich format does not enhance the correction of misinformation. Journal of Educational Psychology, 117(6), 851–862. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000961

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Post-hoc contrast on Experiment 1 inference ratings showed "no significant difference between the refutation and truth sandwich correction conditions" (p = .556); equivalence was not tested beyond this contrast.

> "As predicted, there was no significant difference between the refutation and truth sandwich correction conditions (p = .556), see Figure 1 panel B."
