## Evidence

### Izaguirre 2023

Izaguirre, L. A., Rodríguez‐Fernández, A., & Fernández‐Zabala, A. (2023). Perceived academic performance explained by school climate, positive psychological variables and life satisfaction. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 93, 318–332. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12557

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Standardized path coefficients from the final structural model (Table 3, 'Direct, indirect and total effects'). Emotional clarity (βd = .37) and emotion regulation (βd = .44) had moderate direct effects on resilience; the PEI dimensions plus school climate explained 45% of resilience variance.

> "Curiously, emotional attention was found to have only a small negative direct effect on this same variable (β d = − .15, p < .01), whereas its indirect influence through emotional clarity was positive and stronger than its direct influence (β d = .20, p < .01)."

### Izaguirre 2023 (2)

Izaguirre, L. A., Rodríguez‐Fernández, A., & Fernández‐Zabala, A. (2023). Perceived academic performance explained by school climate, positive psychological variables and life satisfaction. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 93, 318–332. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12557

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Indirect-effect results from the same structural model in the Results section. Emotional clarity's indirect effect on life satisfaction was βi = .31; its indirect effect on PAP was βi = .119, and emotion regulation's was βi = .090, both p < .01. No direct PEI-to-PAP paths were retained.

> "The results also reveal the indirect effect of emotional clarity and emotion regulation on life satisfaction and PAP, with the influence of emotional clarity being particularly noteworthy due to the degree to which it was found to contribute to life satisfaction (β i = .31, p < .01)."
