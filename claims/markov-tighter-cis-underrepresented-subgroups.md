## Evidence

### Tedeschi MN 2023

Tedeschi MN, Hose TM, Mehlman EK, Franklin S, Wong TE. (2023). Improving Models for Student Retention and Graduation using Markov Chains. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2302.00464](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2302.00464)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Markov versus traditional SYGR for AALANA and first-generation College of Science majors (Table 4, Figure 2a and c). Traditional CIs for AALANA science majors are "consistently more than 20 percentage points wide", e.g. Fall 2013 spans 40 to 68%.

> "The Markov approach improves the confidence interval widths by between 25 and 42% (tightening) relative to the traditional model"

### Tedeschi MN 2023 (2)

Tedeschi MN, Hose TM, Mehlman EK, Franklin S, Wong TE. (2023). Improving Models for Student Retention and Graduation using Markov Chains. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2302.00464](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2302.00464)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Markov estimates incorporating data as it becomes available reduce the interannual variability that the article calls problematic for AALANA science majors, whose traditional medians varied widely year to year (55% in Fall 2013, 59% in Fall 2014, 70% in Fall 2015).

> "Thus, the estimates of SYGR are better centered on the long-term median: 62% in Fall 2013, 64% in Fall 2014, 69% in Fall 2015, and 65% overall, for AALANA students. Further, the CI widths are consistently below 20 percentage points"
