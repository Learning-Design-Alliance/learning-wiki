## Evidence

### Yanghee Kim · Michael Tscholl 2021

Yanghee Kim · Michael Tscholl. (2021). Young children's embodied interactions with a social robot. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-021-09978-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative observation in Study One: sessions were designed to last fifteen to twenty minutes with about thirty minutes scheduled, yet ran over an hour, and children asked whether the robot would come back. The authors interpret this as extended attention when activity infuses a sense of play.

> "However, each session ran more than one hour because the team found it difficult to interrupt the children's flow in their engagement with the robot"

### Yanghee Kim · Michael Tscholl 2021 (2)

Yanghee Kim · Michael Tscholl. (2021). Young children's embodied interactions with a social robot. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-021-09978-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

An unexpected pattern in Study One: although activities were designed for one child and the robot, children invited peers, and the pairs voluntarily discussed problems, took turns, and helped each other. This motivated the design of Study Two's collaborative triad.

> "Also noticeable was that the children voluntarily invited their friends nearby to their play with the robot although the play was designed for one-on-one interaction"
