# A validity problem in Kapur's standard deviation transfer item brackets its transfer findings as working hypotheses

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q1` argument or single case

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The commentary argues that Kapur's transfer item on the standard deviation is not answerable by students who have just learned about SD and depicts a distribution the author found hard to imagine, so the transfer findings must be bracketed as working hypotheses. [→ Arthur Bakker 2018](#arthur-bakker-2018)

## Evidence

### Arthur Bakker 2018

Arthur Bakker. (2018). Discovery learning: zombie, phoenix, or elephant? Instructional Science, 46, 169–183. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-018-9450-8

`q1 · i?`

The author's expert critique of the sprint-versus-swim item Kapur (2018) used with 14 to 15-year-olds who had just learned the standard deviation. He argues students cannot yet answer it, citing statistics education literature showing even university students and statistics teachers struggle with SD in relation to distribution and mean. This is an interpretive argument, not a tested result.

> "For me the validity problem of using such a task implies that the findings on transfer in this paper have to be bracketed, and cannot be more than working hypotheses."
