# Students' main motives to prepare seminar assignments were studying material before the MCQ exam, exam training and comparing answers with peers

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` 78% of students rated studying the material before the upcoming multiple-choice exam 4 or 5 as a motive to prepare, 73% cited useful training for the end-of-course exam, 57% wanted to compare answers with peers, and 53% wanted to know whether they could complete assignments on their own. ## Evidence

### Bouwmeester Rianne A. M. 2016

Bouwmeester Rianne A. M., de Kleijn Renske A. M., van Rijen Harold V. M. (2016). Peer-instructed seminar attendance is associated with improved preparation, deeper learning and higher exam scores: a survey study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0715-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive analysis of final-questionnaire five-point-scale motive items. The article reports 78% for studying before the MCQ exam, 73% for exam training, 53% for self-checking and 57% for comparing answers with peers; the authors interpret these motives as relating to autonomy.

> "with 78 % of the students scoring a 4 or 5, a main reason for preparing the seminar assignments was to study the material before the upcoming multiple-choice exam"
