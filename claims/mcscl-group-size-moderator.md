# mCSCL effects do not differ significantly by group size, though tetrads show the largest subgroup effect and dyads alone are not significant

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Tetrad groups produced the largest ES (g = 0.819), followed by groups of more than four people (g = 0.635); dyad effects were not significant (g = 0.216, k = 2). The between-group test was not significant (QB = 3.885, df = 5), so these subgroup differences are descriptive and do not show that group size moderates the effect. ## Evidence

### Sung Yao-Ting 2017

Sung Yao-Ting, Yang Je-Ming, Lee Han-Yueh. (2017). The Effects of Mobile-Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning: Meta-Analysis and Critical Synthesis. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654317704307

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random-effects moderator analysis of group size. Table 4 reports tetrad g = 0.819 (k = 6), more than four people g = 0.635 (k = 7), mixed g = 0.494 (k = 14), triad g = 0.492 (k = 10), dyad g = 0.216 (k = 2, not significant); 9 studies did not report group size (g = 0.368). The between-group test QB = 3.885 (df = 5) was not significant, so the differences between sizes are descriptive estimates, not a demonstrated moderation; with only two dyad studies the dyad estimate is especially uncertain. Combined dyad/triad showed a medium ES. The authors' discussion says group size "deserves additional scrutiny" and that more studies are needed to establish an optimal group size.

> "the mCSCL studies in which group size was a tetrad produced the largest ES, followed by groups with more than four people, learners with mixed group sizes, and triad. In addition, the ES for studies with the dyad group size was not significant."
