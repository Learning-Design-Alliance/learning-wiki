Evidence¶

Casey Inez Canfield & Baruch Fischhoff & Alex Davis 2019¶

Casey Inez Canfield & Baruch Fischhoff & Alex Davis. (2019). Better beware: comparing metacognition for phishing and legitimate emails. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-019-09197-5

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Linear regressions (Table 2) combining both experiments (N = 231) predicting calibration and resolution from attention, training time, time per email, perceived consequences, age, gender, and education. The article calls this inconsistent with earlier research finding better calibration with more time per email.

"None of the predictors were significantly related to resolution ( p >. 0 1 )."

Casey Inez Canfield & Baruch Fischhoff & Alex Davis 2019 (2)¶

Casey Inez Canfield & Baruch Fischhoff & Alex Davis. (2019). Better beware: comparing metacognition for phishing and legitimate emails. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-019-09197-5

q3 · i? · associational · r2

The strongest effect in the Table 2 regressions, for perceived consequences on calibration for both email types. The article attributes it to a bias toward perceiving emails as phishing, improving phishing accuracy while reducing legitimate accuracy.

"For legitimate emails, participants who thought that falling for phishing attacks had greater (negative) consequences were less well calibrated (i.e. calibration was further from 0). In contrast, for phishing attacks, they were better calibrated."
