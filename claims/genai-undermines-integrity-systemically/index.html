Evidence¶

Tristan B. Taylor & Travis LaCroix 2026¶

Tristan B. Taylor & Travis LaCroix. (2026). Purpose before policy: academic integrity, generative AI, and rhetorical stance. Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-026-01706-1

q1 · i? · theoretical · r2

Conceptual argument (type e) in the academic-integrity section. It rests on the authors' account that LLM objective functions optimise for plausibility rather than truth, making LLM outputs truth-indifferent in Frankfurt's sense, and on the claim that integrity is a collective epistemic practice institutions must sustain.

"AI use in the university undermines academic integrity; however, not because it constitutes academic dishonesty or misconduct. Instead, the use of GenAI in scholarly contexts, regardless of whether it is sanctioned by the institution, generates institutional hypocrisy and policy incoherence."

q2 · i? · theoretical · r2

Narrative review attribution: the authors cite Kosmyna et al. (2025) on reduced neural connectivity and poor recall of students' own writing, and Gerlich (2025) on declines in metacognitive skills and critical thinking. These are second-hand findings; the designs were not described in this article.

"Evidence also suggests that GenAI impairs learning itself, with automated tools diminishing foundational cognitive capacities, weakening the very competencies that academic integrity presupposes"
