# Children in the reliable condition were significantly more likely to wait the full 15 min than children in the unreliable condition (64.3% vs 7.1%)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` 9 of 14 children in the reliable condition waited the full 15 min versus 1 of 14 in the unreliable condition, a significant difference in proportions. [→ Kidd 2013](#kidd-2013)

## Evidence

### Kidd 2013

Kidd, C., Palmeri, H., & Aslin, R. N. (2013). Rational snacking: Young children's decision-making on the marshmallow task is moderated by beliefs about environmental reliability. Cognition, 126(1), 109-114. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2012.08.004

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Binary analysis of the same 28-child randomized experiment (Fig. 2): whether children waited the full 15 min without tasting the marshmallow. The proportion difference was tested with Newcombe's two-sample test with continuity correction, X2 = 7.62, df = 1, p < 0.006; no effect size is printed.

> "In the unreliable condition, only 1 out of the 14 children (7.1%) waited the full 15 min; in the reliable condition, however, 9 out of the 14 children (64.3%) waited. A two-sample test for equality of proportions with continuity correction at α2-tail = 0.05 (Newcombe, 1998) was highly significant (X2 = 7.62, df = 1, p < 0.006)."
