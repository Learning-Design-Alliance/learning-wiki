# In a large introductory mechanics course, the majority of students (60.4%) successfully completed a proctored computational modeling evaluation on a novel central force problem after a semester of computational homework.

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Across three semesters, 820 of 1357 students (60.4%) correctly modeled the grading case of a novel central force problem in a 30-minute proctored assignment. [→ Caballero 2012](#caballero-2012)
`q2 i?` Success varied by assignment version: 64.3% (Version 1), 51.0% (Version 2), and 64.2% (Version 3) modeled the grading case correctly. [→ Caballero 2012 (2)](#caballero-2012-2)

`q3 i?` On average over three semesters, 60% of students completed a proctored computational modeling assignment with complete success. [→ Marcos D. Caballero 2011](#marcos-d-caballero-2011)

## Evidence

### Caballero 2012

Caballero, M. D., Schatz, M. F., and Kohlmyer, M. A. (2012). Implementing and assessing computational modeling in introductory mechanics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.8.020106](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.8.020106)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Proctored final lab assignment delivered over three semesters to 1357 students in a large lecture mechanics course; students completed a partially written VPython program for a randomized central force problem. The article reports roughly 40% were unable to model the grading case, i.e., the majority succeeded.

> "Overall, roughly 40% of the students were unable to model the grading case. Students with some previous programming experience were no more successful than those without any experience as indicated by a contingency table analysis."

### Caballero 2012 (2)

Caballero, M. D., Schatz, M. F., and Kohlmyer, M. A. (2012). Implementing and assessing computational modeling in introductory mechanics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.8.020106](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.8.020106)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Cross-semester comparison of three slightly modified versions of the proctored assignment (Table I). Version 2 dropped to 51.0% correct after Version 1's 64.3%, with Version 1 performance likely inflated because some students attempted it twice; Version 3 reached 64.2%.

> "Students were more successful on Version 3 of the assignment; 64.2% modeled the grading case correctly."

### Marcos D. Caballero 2011

Marcos D. Caballero, Matthew A. Kohlmyer and Michael F. Schatz. (2011). Fostering Computational Thinking In Introductory Mechanics. [doi:10.1063/1.3679982](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3679982)

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Proctored final-lab evaluation in a large introductory mechanics course, delivered via WebAssign in timed 30-minute mode with TAs barred from helping. The article reports "60% of students completed the proctored assignments with complete success"; no effect size is printed.

> "Overall, we found that, on average over three semesters, 60% of students completed the proctored assignments with complete success."
