# Factor analysis of student and faculty perceptions yields a three-factor student model and a two-factor faculty model, not the four conceptions of the Scardamalia-Bereiter framework

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2006 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Exploratory and confirmatory factor analyses produced a three-factor student model (Fostering, Collaborative, Transmitting) and a two-factor faculty model, with three Scardamalia-Bereiter conceptions coalescing into fostering student development. [→ Dickie 2006](#dickie-2006)

## Evidence

### Dickie 2006

Dickie, L. O., Dedic, H., Rosenfield, S., Rosenfield, E., & Simon, R. A. (2006). Success and Persistence in Science: The Influence of Classroom Climate. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0610243](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0610243)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Factor analysis of the 37-item student perception survey (EFA on one CEGEP, CFA confirming the three-factor model on the other three) and the 30-item faculty survey. The data "did not support the four-factor model implied in the Scardamalia and Bereiter (1989) model".

> "Exploratory factor analysis and confirmatory factor analysis of the faculty data revealed a two-factor model: teaching as transmission and teaching as fostering student development."
