# Neither teaching motivation nor teaching self-efficacy is directly associated with students' ratings of teaching quality in bedside teaching

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a mixed-effects regression of 500 student ratings across 94 bedside teaching lessons, no type of teaching motivation and teaching self-efficacy were significantly associated with students' ratings of teaching quality. [→ Dybowski Christoph 2017](#dybowski-christoph-2017)

## Evidence

### Dybowski Christoph 2017

Dybowski Christoph, Sehner Susanne, Harendza Sigrid. (2017). Influence of motivation, self-efficacy and situational factors on the teaching quality of clinical educators. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0923-2

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Prospective observational study with a random intercept mixed-effects linear regression of student ratings of teaching quality as outcome, simultaneously modeling five teaching motivation scores, teaching self-efficacy, and situational variables. The study found "no type of teaching motivation nor teaching self-efficacy were significantly associated" with the ratings; no effect size was printed.

> "As for physician's personal dispositions, no type of teaching motivation nor teaching self-efficacy were significantly associated with students' ratings of teaching quality."
