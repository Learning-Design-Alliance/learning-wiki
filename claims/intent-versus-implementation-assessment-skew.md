## Evidence

### Ghimire 2025

Ghimire, A. and Singh, C. (2025). Perspectives of Women and Men Students and Faculty on Conceptual and Quantitative Problem-Solving in Physics from Introductory to Graduate Levels. [doi:10.3390/educsci15121602](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci15121602)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Qualitative analysis of open-ended survey explanations; students reported instructors emphasized the why behind things, yet homework and exams were heavily quantitative, e.g., "the homework and recitations are heavily based on math problems".

> "The difference lies in intent versus implementation: the teaching emphasized concep ts, but assessment and practice prioritized equations and calculations."

### Ghimire 2025 (2)

Ghimire, A. and Singh, C. (2025). Perspectives of Women and Men Students and Faculty on Conceptual and Quantitative Problem-Solving in Physics from Introductory to Graduate Levels. [doi:10.3390/educsci15121602](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci15121602)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Semi-structured interviews with seven physics faculty; instructors explained that courses still lean on quantitative assessments despite introductory-level intent to prioritize conceptual understanding, so students focus on what earns points.

> "Students are driven by what is emphasized in exams…if a course emphasizes quantitative aspects, students will lean toward that, and vice versa ."
