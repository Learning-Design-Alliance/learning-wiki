# PICO versus truncated PIC in PubMed: PICO showed higher median sensitivity and precision on the first 40 records, but PIC showed higher sensitivity on the full output

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · quant-synthesis `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` An observational study of 15 PubMed search strategies reported that PICO queries gave increased median sensitivity and precision compared with truncated PIC queries when the first 40 records were assessed, but on the full output PIC detected about 85% of relevant records versus about 69% for PICO. [→ Mette Brandt Eriksen 2018](#mette-brandt-eriksen-2018)
`q2 i?` PubMed links to related articles achieved higher sensitivity and precision than both the PICO and PIC models in the same study. [→ Mette Brandt Eriksen 2018 (2)](#mette-brandt-eriksen-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Mette Brandt Eriksen 2018

Mette Brandt Eriksen, PhD; Tove Faber Frandsen, PhD. (2018). The impact of patient, intervention, comparison, outcome (PICO) as a search strategy tool on literature search quality: a systematic review. Journal of the Medical Library Association 106 (4). https://doi.org/10.5195/jmla.2018.345

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Observational study (Agoritsas et al., 2012) evaluating 15 search strategies varying in query structure, Clinical Queries filters, and limits, with 450 searches; relevance was assessed on the first 40 records and the complete output against a gold standard of Cochrane reviews. The review reports the PICO advantage was based on the first forty records only.

> "One study compared the PICO model to the truncated PIC model in PubMed and reported that the PICO model resulted in increased median sensitivity and precision of the searches [21]."

### Mette Brandt Eriksen 2018 (2)

Mette Brandt Eriksen, PhD; Tove Faber Frandsen, PhD. (2018). The impact of patient, intervention, comparison, outcome (PICO) as a search strategy tool on literature search quality: a systematic review. Journal of the Medical Library Association 106 (4). https://doi.org/10.5195/jmla.2018.345

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r1`

Same observational study, full-output analysis: the review reports "about 85% of records were detected by the PIC queries and about 69% by the PICO queries", reversing the first-40-records advantage. The review also notes PubMed links to related articles resulted in higher sensitivity and precision than both models.

> "When the full output was screened for relevant studies, about 85% of records were detected by the PIC queries and about 69% by the PICO queries [21]."
