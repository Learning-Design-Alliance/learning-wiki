## Evidence

### Mikko Haavisto 2026

Mikko Haavisto, Tomi Jaakkola. (2026). Fifth graders study videos faster than illustrated texts: Studying rate, review strategies, and learning implications. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09817-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Stepwise regression on slide-level studying durations (N of slides = 31 per Table 1). In video models text characters explained R2 = 0.898; slide number had a large effect in illustrated texts (change in R2 = 0.284) and a small effect in videos (0.039), with earlier slides receiving more time.

> "In the illustrated text models, the most significant predictor was the number of text characters, which explained half of the variance ( R2 = 0.500) in the initial pass slide durations: each character increased the slide’s studying duration by 0.07 s."

### Mikko Haavisto 2026 (2)

Mikko Haavisto, Tomi Jaakkola. (2026). Fifth graders study videos faster than illustrated texts: Studying rate, review strategies, and learning implications. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09817-z

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Stepwise regression found picture type (diagrams vs photographs) nonsignificant in both modalities; the article reports extremely small estimated effects (change in R2 < 0.001 in videos and < 0.02 in illustrated texts) and notes no a priori power analysis was conducted.

> "Picture type did not significantly affect the initial pass slide duration in either condition."
