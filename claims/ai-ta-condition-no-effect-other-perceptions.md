# AI TA condition shows no statistically significant differences in perceived task-comprehension support, correctness, or interaction stress

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Differences across the four AI TA conditions were not statistically significant for perceived support for task comprehension, provision of correct information, or stressful interaction, though Socratic conditions descriptively rated lower on comprehension support and Socratic + Full Context rated highest on stress. [→ Madeleine Eastwood 2026](#madeleine-eastwood-2026)

## Evidence

### Madeleine Eastwood 2026

Madeleine Eastwood, Harshith Narne, Joseph Hilby, Paul Denny, Ashish Aggarwal, and Amanpreet Kapoor. (2026). Guardrails or Roadblocks? Effects of Pedagogical Style and Context Awareness in AI Teaching Assistants for Programming. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29995 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.29995](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2609.29995)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-survey Likert perception measures from the same randomized experiment (N=132); Kruskal-Wallis H tests found no significant condition differences on these three measures. Descriptively, "students in the Direct conditions rated the AI TA higher on task comprehension than those in the Socratic conditions".

> "Differences across the four conditions were not statistically sig- nificant for perceived support fortask comprehension( 𝜒 2(3)= 4.79, 𝑝=. 188), provision ofcorrect information( 𝜒 2(3)= 1.71, 𝑝=. 635), orstressful interaction( 𝜒 2(3)= 4.50, 𝑝=. 213)."
