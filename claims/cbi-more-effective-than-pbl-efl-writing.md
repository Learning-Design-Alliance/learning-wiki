# Content-Based Instruction was more effective than Problem-Based Learning for teaching EFL writing in a quasi-experimental comparison

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` EFL learners taught writing with Content-Based Instruction scored higher on average than those taught with Problem-Based Learning, and the ANOVA/Tukey test found a significant difference between the two groups. [→ Jaelani 2017](#jaelani-2017)

## Evidence

### Jaelani 2017

Selamet Riadi Jaelani. (2017). Treating of Content-Based Instruction to Teach Writing Viewed from EFL Learners' Creativity. English Language Teaching, 10(11). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v10n11p156

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experiment with 80 EFL learners in two classes assigned by cluster random lottery to CBI (experimental) or PBL (control); writing tested after treatment and analyzed with 2x2 ANOVA and Tukey test. The article reports "a significant difference between the two groups (Group A and group B)"; no effect size is printed.

> "based on the result analysis by using 2 x 2 ANOV A and Tukey test, it was found that H o was rejected because F o> Ft. (Fo is higher than Ft) and there was a significant difference between the two groups (Group A and group B)."
