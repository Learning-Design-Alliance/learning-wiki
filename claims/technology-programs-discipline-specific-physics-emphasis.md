# Technology programs examine discipline-related physics topics at higher cognitive levels within a disciplinary context

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In Engineering Technology programs, the cognitive process required by physics questions depends heavily on content: topics directly related to the program's focus area are examined at higher cognitive levels within a disciplinary context, while other topics require only remembering or understanding. [→ Zakani 2019](#zakani-2019)

## Evidence

### Zakani 2019

Zakani, S., Kaupp, J., Turner, R. D., & Frank, B. (2019). Analyzing implicit science and math outcomes in engineering and technology programs. The Canadian Journal for the Scholarship of Teaching and Learning, 10(1). https://doi.org/10.5206/cjsotl-rcacea.2019.1.7994

`q2 · i?`

Content-specialist scoring of physics exam questions plotted in the rigor-relevance grid (Figures 3a and 3b) shows depth of analysis and cognitive process per question. The example from Engineering Technology program 3 illustrates that "the cognitive processes required to answer questions in mechanics are remember and understand" while electricity and magnetism topics reach the application level.

> "For example, in Engineering Technology program 3 with a focus on Electronics Engineering, the cognitive processes required to answer questions in mechanics are remember and understand. The same program examines topics in electricity and magnetism at the application level."
