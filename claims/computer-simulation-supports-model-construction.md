## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The article reports that computers can aid visualization of models, that dynamic components such as wave propagation, superposition and light velocity in different media are presented well by simulation programs, and that exercises in which students apply the model themselves support learning. [→ Martin Erik Horn 2004](#martin-erik-horn-2004)

## Evidence

### Martin Erik Horn 2004

Martin Erik Horn, Antje Leisner, Helmut F. Mikelskis. (2004). Difficulties of Model Construction in Optics. German Physical Society Spring Conference Leipzig 2002. Originally published 2002. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0402080](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0402080)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Answer to case-study research question 5 on the computer's role in model construction; the authors add that "Exercises in which the students use the particular model itself, support the learning process". In the study students worked with the programs phenOpt and Albert.

> "Computers can aid in the visualization of models. The dynamic components of models in particular (the propagation of waves, superposition and interference of waves, velocity of light in different media) allow themselves to be presented very well through computer simulation programs."
