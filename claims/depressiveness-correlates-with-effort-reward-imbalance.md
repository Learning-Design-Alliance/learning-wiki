## Subclaims
`q2 i3` The strongest correlation in the study was between depressiveness and the perceived imbalance between efforts spent and rewards received (ρ = 0.6073). [→ Hahn Henry 2017](#hahn-henry-2017)
`q2 i3` Acute test anxiety before the oral exam correlated with effort-reward imbalance (ρ = 0.5637), more strongly than before the written exam (ρ = 0.4967). [→ Hahn Henry 2017](#hahn-henry-2017)

## Evidence

### Hahn Henry 2017

Hahn Henry, Kropp Peter, Kirschstein Timo, Rücker Gernot, Müller-Hilke Brigitte. (2017). Test anxiety in medical school is unrelated to academic performance but correlates with an effort/reward imbalance. PLoS ONE 12(2): e0171220. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0171220

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Spearman rank correlation analysis of the cohort's questionnaire data; acute test anxiety correlated with effort-reward imbalance with coefficients of 0.4967 (written) and 0.5637 (oral), and the table shows depressiveness versus ERI at ρ = 0.6073, the strongest correlation reported.

> "Acute test anxiety at T1 or T2 correlated the strongest with an effort-reward-imbalance and the respective correlation coefficients of 0.4967 and 0.5637 yielded a power of 0.96 and 0.99, respectively"
