# Trim and Fill publication bias analysis suggests the pooled short-term adventure therapy effect (.47) slightly underestimates the true effect (adjusted .52)

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Daniel J. Bowen and James T. Neill 2013

Daniel J. Bowen and James T. Neill. (2013). A Meta-Analysis of Adventure Therapy Outcomes and Moderators. The Open Psychology Journal, 2013, 6, 28-53. http://www.daniel-bowen.com.au/meta-analysis

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Publication bias assessment via funnel plot and Trim and Fill in CMA suggested "18 Pre-Post adventure therapy studies with higher than average effect sizes were missing"; without imputation g = .47 (95% CI: .41-.53). No effect size for the bias adjustment itself is printed, so impact is null. For Base-Pre and Post-Follow-Up comparisons no studies were missing and estimates were unchanged.

> "Using Trim and Fill, Hedges’ g was estimated to be slightly higher (.52; 95% CI: .46-.58)."
