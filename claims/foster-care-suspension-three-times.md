# Students in foster care were consistently about three times more likely to be suspended than all other student groups, with rates reverting to pre-pandemic levels by 2022–23

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Roughly 14 percent of students in foster care were suspended annually versus about 4 percent of all students, with pandemic-era decreases reversing once schools reopened. [→ Ximenes Barrat 2025](#ximenes-barrat-2025)

## Evidence

### Ximenes Barrat 2025

Ximenes Barrat, V., Diercks, C., Berliner, B., Derby, K., Chen Gaddini, M., & Pivnick, L. (2025). Revisiting California's invisible achievement gap: Trends in education outcomes of students in foster care in the context of the local control funding formula. WestEd. https://www.wested.org/resource/revisiting-californias-invisible-achievement-gap/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of CDE CALPADS suspension data, 2014–15 through 2022–23, across four student groups. Suspension rates dropped during pandemic remote learning but had reverted to pre-pandemic levels by 2022–23.

> "students in foster care were consistently three times more likely to be suspended than all other student groups, meaning that an average of about one in seven students in foster care received exclusionary discipline that limited their learning time"
