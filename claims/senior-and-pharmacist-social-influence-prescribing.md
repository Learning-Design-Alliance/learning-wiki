# Senior doctors, pharmacists, and nurses exerted informational and normative social influence on trainee doctors' prescribing

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants described two kinds of social influence on their prescribing, informational and injunctive normative, with senior doctors seen as highly influential through both pathways and pharmacists and nurses also influential. [→ Duncan Eilidh M 2012](#duncan-eilidh-m-2012)

## Evidence

### Duncan Eilidh M 2012

Duncan Eilidh M, Francis Jill J, Johnston Marie, Davey Peter, Maxwell Simon, McKay Gerard A, McLay James, Ross Sarah, Ryan Cristín, Webb David J, Bond Christine. (2012). Learning curves, taking instructions, and patient safety: using a theoretical domains framework in an interview study to investigate prescribing errors among trainee doctors. Implementation Science. https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-7-86

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview data on social influences, distinguishing informational influence from injunctive normative influence. Seniors were “highly influential in influencing prescribing behavior”; pharmacists were an informational resource, and nurses gave advice that was at times treated with caution.

> "Senior doctors were seen to be highly influential in influencing prescribing behavior through both the informational and normative pathways."
