## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Medical books were the most frequently used source of health information (83.6%), while specialized databases were rarely used (Scopus 8.2%, Embase 6.1%, Cochrane Library 5.9%, Ovid 1.0%). [→ Hamza K 2026](#hamza-k-2026)

Hamza K, Qawasmeh H, Ahmad Rasha K, Alkhateeb S, Awawdeh F, Douden B, Zrineh A, Akwan R. (2026). Knowledge, attitudes, and skills towards evidence-based medicine among Palestinian undergraduate medical students: a cross-sectional study. BMC Med Educ. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08750-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Multiple-choice information-source items in the 709-student cross-sectional survey; descriptive percentages, no effect size printed.

### Hamza K 2026 (2)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Chi-square comparisons of search-engine and source use by EBM training status (Table 3) in the same survey; printed percentages and p values, no effect size.

> "Notably, Scopus usage was slightly higher by students trained in EBM compared to non-trainees (11.8% vs. 6.0%, p value = 0.007)."
