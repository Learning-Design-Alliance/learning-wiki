# Pairing coaching with group trainings and instructional materials is associated with larger effects, while video libraries are associated with smaller effects

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Pairing coaching with group trainings is associated with a 0.31 SD larger effect on instruction and 0.12 SD larger on achievement. [→ Kraft 2018](#kraft-2018)
`q2 i?` Pairing coaching with instructional resources and materials is associated with 0.21 SD larger instructional gains, while providing a video library is associated with a 0.27 SD smaller effect. [→ Kraft 2018 (2)](#kraft-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Kraft 2018

Kraft, M.A., Blazar, D., Hogan, D. (2018). The effect of teaching coaching on instruction and achievement: A meta-analysis of the causal evidence. Review of Educational Research, 88(4), 547-588. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654318759268

`q2 · i?`

Exploratory meta-analytic regression (Table 4) across the 60 causal studies; the authors stress these regressions are descriptive and do not capture the causal effect of a program feature.

> "we find that pairing coaching with group trainings is associated with 0.31 SD larger effect size on instruction and 0.12 SD larger effect size on achievement."

### Kraft 2018 (2)

Kraft, M.A., Blazar, D., Hogan, D. (2018). The effect of teaching coaching on instruction and achievement: A meta-analysis of the causal evidence. Review of Educational Research, 88(4), 547-588. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654318759268

`q2 · i?`

Same exploratory meta-analytic regression of design features on pooled effect sizes; coefficients are associations from between-study comparisons, not experimental contrasts.

> "pairing coaching with instructional resources and materials (e.g., curriculum) also is associated with greater gains (0.21 SD larger), while providing teachers with a video library is associated with more limited benefits (-0.27 SD smaller)."
