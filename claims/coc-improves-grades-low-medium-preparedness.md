## Evidence

### Hawkins Whitney 2021

Hawkins Whitney, W.; Goddard, K.; Favero, C. (2021). A Cocurricular Program That Encourages Specific Study Skills and Habits Improves Academic Performance and Retention of First-Year Undergraduates in Introductory Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.20-06-0117

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of Bio101 grades between full CoC participants and nonparticipants, grouped by predicted preparedness; t-tests compared exam and final grades. Low-preparedness participants scored "significantly better on exam 2" (64.1 vs 56.9); their final grades, with participation extra credit removed, did not differ significantly (1.73 vs 1.26, t(40) = 1.807, p = 0.078). Participation was chosen by students, so the comparison is not randomised.

> "Low-preparedness students scored significantly better on exam 2 (64.1 ± 13.03) than nonparticipants (56.9 ± 10.66), t(53) = 2.074, p = 0.043."

### Hawkins Whitney 2021 (2)

Hawkins Whitney, W.; Goddard, K.; Favero, C. (2021). A Cocurricular Program That Encourages Specific Study Skills and Habits Improves Academic Performance and Retention of First-Year Undergraduates in Introductory Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.20-06-0117

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same quasi-experimental comparison for the medium-preparedness group: 23 full participants vs 101 nonparticipants, t-tests on exam 2 and final GPA after removing extra credit. Participants scored "significantly higher on exam 2" and on final course grade.

> "For the medium-preparedness group, the 23 full CoC participants scored significantly higher on exam 2 (82.2 ± 11.42) than the 101 nonparticipants (73.3 ± 14.99), t(122) = 3.116, p = 0.003, and on the final course grade (2.88 ± 0.94 full participants vs. 2.24 ± 0.94 nonparticipants), t(111) = 2.911, p = 0.004."
