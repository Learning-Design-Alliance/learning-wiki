# Human-AI collaboration requires refined group-awareness concepts for asymmetrical relationships between users and AI partners

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In human-AI interactions, the concept of group awareness needs to be refined to allow for more asymmetrical relationships, since humans may know about the AI's competencies and shortcomings while the reverse is uncertain. ## Evidence

### Ulrike Cress · Joachim Kimmerle 2023

Ulrike Cress · Joachim Kimmerle. (2023). Co-constructing knowledge with generative AI tools: Reflections from a CSCL perspective. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-023-09409-w

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument extending group awareness research to human-AI collaboration. The authors note awareness requires users to know the training basis and output generation of the tool, and it remains open whether the AI partner can create awareness of its partner.

> "Research has described the relevance of group awareness (the perception of certain characteristics of a group and its members) for interaction among humans (e.g., Engelmann et al., 2009). In this new format of human-AI interactions, the awareness concept needs to be refined to allow for more asymmetrical relationships."
