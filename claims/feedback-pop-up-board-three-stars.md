## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In early versions the planned feedback was not implemented and several players requested it spontaneously; the solution was a feedback pop-up board covering order, completeness, and correctness of values, summarised with three stars. [→ Berg H 2023](#berg-h-2023)

## Evidence

### Berg H 2023

Berg H, Prasolova-Førland E, Steinsbekk A. (2023). Developing a virtual reality (VR) application for practicing the ABCDE approach for systematic clinical observation. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04625-2

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

During iterative testing, different feedback types were tested to balance focus on correct order, time to understand feedback, and pointing to what to improve. The board marks each area green if correct, partly filled otherwise, and offers replay with the encouragement "practice makes perfect".

> "In the first versions tested, the planned feedback was not implemented, and several players requested this spontaneously. They wanted to know if they had got the observations right."
