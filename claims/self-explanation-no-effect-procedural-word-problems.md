## Evidence

### Mwangi 1998

Wangari Mwangi and John Sweller. (1998). Learning to Solve Compare Word Problems: The Effect of Example Format and Generating Self-Explanations. Cognition and Instruction, 16(2). https://www.jstor.org/stable/3233720

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 3 randomly assigned 48 Year 4 children to integrated or split-source formats, each with or without self-explanation prompts; on total incorrect attempts across test phases, "The effect of self-explanation was not significant, F(1, 44) = 0.78".

> "The effect of self-explanation was not significant, F(1, 44) = 0.78."

### Mwangi 1998 (2)

Wangari Mwangi and John Sweller. (1998). Learning to Solve Compare Word Problems: The Effect of Example Format and Generating Self-Explanations. Cognition and Instruction, 16(2). https://www.jstor.org/stable/3233720

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The 2 (format) x 2 (self-explanation) analyses in Experiment 3 showed the format-by-explanation interaction was not significant, "F(1, 44) = 0.01", so the integrated-format advantage held regardless of whether children self-explained.

> "The interaction between format and self-explanation was not significant, F(1, 44) = 0.01, indicating that the integrated group made fewer incorrect attempts than the split-source group in both the self-explanation and no-explanation conditions."
