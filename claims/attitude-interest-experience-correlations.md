## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Attitudes toward depression correlated with interest in providing care for older patients with depression (r = 0.226), interest in nursing (r = 0.213), interest in late-life depression issues (r = 0.219), and knowledge (r = 0.272), all p < .001. [→ Ni Cheng-Hua 2020](#ni-cheng-hua-2020)

## Evidence

### Ni Cheng-Hua 2020

Ni Cheng-Hua, Guo Shu-Liu, Chao Chia-Yu, Wang Chia-Hui, Susanty Sri, & Chuang Yeu-Hui. (2020). Nurses’ Late-Life Depression Knowledge and Attitudes Toward Depression: A Cross-Sectional Study. Inquiry: A Journal of Medical Care Organization, Provision and Financing. https://doi.org/10.1177/0046958020945179

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Pearson correlations in the cross-sectional survey (N = 307). All four printed correlations fall between r = 0.213 and r = 0.272, small-to-moderate positive associations; attitudes also differed by school-lecture attendance (t = −2.474, p = .014) and pamphlet reading (t = −2.628, p = .009).

> "there were also positive relationships between nurses’ attitudes toward depression and a couple of variables including their interest level in nursing (r = 0.213, p < .001), their interest level in providing care for older patients with depression (r = 0.226, p < .001), their interest level in issues regarding late-life depression (r = 0.219, p < .001), and their knowledge level of late-life depression (r = 0.272, p < .001)."
