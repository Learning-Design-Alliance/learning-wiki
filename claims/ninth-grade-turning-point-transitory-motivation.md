# Ninth grade marked a turning point where transitory motivation trajectories reversed direction, and these inconsistent trajectories were associated with lower AP enrollment and science performance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Transitory Decreasing and Transitory Increasing groups showed self-concept and task value trajectories that reversed direction around ninth grade. [→ Wang 2016](#wang-2016)
`q3 i?` The transitory groups had lower average AP science course enrollment and science academic performance than most other groups, per the authors' interpretation. [→ Wang 2016 (2)](#wang-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Wang 2016

Wang, Ming-Te; Chow, Angela; Degol, Jessica Lauren; Eccles, Jacquelynne Sue. (2016). Does Everyone's Motivational Beliefs about Physical Science Decline in Secondary School?: Heterogeneity of Adolescents' Achievement Motivation Trajectories in Physics and Chemistry. Journal of Youth and Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-016-0620-1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the growth mixture model results, Transitory Decreasing declined through ninth grade then increased, while Transitory Increasing rose then declined; both reversals coincided with the middle-to-high-school transition.

> "These groups were characterized by self-concept and task value trajectories that reversed or changed direction around ninth grade, when students transitioned into high school."

### Wang 2016 (2)

Wang, Ming-Te; Chow, Angela; Degol, Jessica Lauren; Eccles, Jacquelynne Sue. (2016). Does Everyone's Motivational Beliefs about Physical Science Decline in Secondary School?: Heterogeneity of Adolescents' Achievement Motivation Trajectories in Physics and Chemistry. Journal of Youth and Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-016-0620-1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Authors' interpretation in the discussion linking motivational instability to lower AP enrollment and, consequently, lower science grades; the article frames this as a possible explanation rather than a tested mediation.

> "the fluctuations in motivation detected for these two transitory groups may have reduced students' likelihood of enrolling in advanced courses in science."
