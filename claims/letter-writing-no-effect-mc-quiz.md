# Participating in an informal letter-writing assignment did not significantly improve scores on a multiple-choice-only quiz

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2013 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students in the writing condition scored similarly to controls on the multiple-choice post-test quiz, with no significant ANCOVA difference. [→ Bersamin 2013](#bersamin-2013)

## Evidence

### Bersamin 2013

Bersamin, M., Zamboanga, B.L. & Orsak-Neff, N. (2013). Understanding course content through letter writing: Do informal writing assignments improve grades? Psychology Teaching Review, 19(1). https://www.bps.org.uk/journal/psychology-teaching-review

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Counterbalanced pre-test/post-test experiment in a lower-division child development course; ANCOVA on the multiple-choice quiz (post-test 1) with pre-test as covariate. The writing group's adjusted mean (M=73.96) was "similar" to controls (M=71.61) with no significant difference; equivalence was not tested.

> "individuals in the writing treatment condition (adjusted means: M=73.96, SE=2.56) scored similarly to the control group (M=71.61, SD=2.49). Results from the ANCOVA revealed no significant differences between the two groups"
