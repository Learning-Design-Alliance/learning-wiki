# Higher daily tea and instant coffee consumption during the exam period is negatively associated with medical students' grades

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2024 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Daily tea and instant coffee consumption during exams are negatively associated with grades in both Spearman correlation and regression analyses. [→ Bitar Anas 2024](#bitar-anas-2024)
`q2 i?` In multiple linear regression, daily tea (B = -0.334) and instant coffee (B = -0.682) consumption were negatively associated with grades. [→ Bitar Anas 2024 (2)](#bitar-anas-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Bitar Anas 2024

Bitar Anas, Barakat Fady, Hawat Angie, Alsaid Bayan. (2024). Dietary and smoking habits during the exam period and their effect on the academic achievement among Syrian medical students. BMC Medical Education 24:60. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04950-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Spearman's rank correlation analysis of self-reported habits among 608 medical students found "a significant, but weak, negative correlation" between grades and daily tea (rs = -0.115) and instant coffee (rs = -0.168) consumption during exams. The article characterizes the correlation as weak; no standardised effect size beyond the correlation coefficients is printed.

> "a significant, but weak, negative correlation between students' grades and their daily consumption of traditional coffee (rs = -0.122, p = 0.003), tea (rs = -0.115, p = 0.005), and instant coffee (rs = -0.168, p < 0.001)"

### Bitar Anas 2024 (2)

Bitar Anas, Barakat Fady, Hawat Angie, Alsaid Bayan. (2024). Dietary and smoking habits during the exam period and their effect on the academic achievement among Syrian medical students. BMC Medical Education 24:60. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04950-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple linear regression (Enter Method) adjusted for age, sex, residency type, and residency partners showed that "daily consumption of tea (B = -0.334, 95% CI -0.618 – -0.049, p = 0.022)" and instant coffee remained negatively associated with grades. Coefficients are unstandardized; no standardised effect size is printed.

> "daily consumption of tea (B = -0.334, 95% CI -0.618 – -0.049, p = 0.022) and instant coffee (B = -0.682, 95% CI -1.099 – -0.265, p = 0.001)"
