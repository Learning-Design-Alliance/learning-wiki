# ICT-supported writing interventions targeting higher-level writing processes show a large overall positive effect on students' writing quality (g = 1.264)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Across 42 studies, ICT-supported writing interventions produced a large pooled improvement in writing quality relative to non-ICT control conditions. [→ López 2026](#lopez-2026)

## Evidence

### López 2026

López, P., Fidalgo, R., Álvarez-Moreno, C., Díaz-Tejedor, R., van den Bergh, H., & Rijlaarsdam, G. (2026). Compositional Analysis of Writing Instructional Practices Performed with Digital Tools: A Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10153-4

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Three-level random-effects meta-analysis with cluster-robust inference of 53 effect sizes from 42 papers, comparing ICT-supported writing interventions to non-ICT controls on writing quality. The pooled effect was "g = 1.264 (95% CI [0.957, 1.570])", significant at p < .001.

> "The pooled effect was g = 1.264 (95% CI [0.957, 1.570]), t(39.93) = 8.33, p < .001, which is similar to the mean effect reported for SRSD strategy instruction in Graham and Perin’s (2007) meta-analysis (mean weighted effect size = 1.14)."
