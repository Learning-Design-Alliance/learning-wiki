## Evidence

### Dalrymple 2013

Dalrymple, O., Sears, D. A., & Evangelou, D. (2013). The Relative Pedagogical Value of Disassemble/Analyze/Assemble (DAA) Activities. Advances in Engineering Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1076105

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary analyses on seven-point scales: men's prior disassembly experience 4.49 ± 1.68 versus women's 2.78 ± 1.63; perceived competence on the DAA activity also differed significantly, t(92) = 2.93, p = 0.004. Prior experience and perceived competence were correlated, r(325) = 0.362, r2 = .131, p < .001.

> "the average prior experience reported by men was 4.49 ± 1.68 (M ± SD), and for women it was 2.78 ± 1.63. The results of the t-test indicate that this difference is significant, t (323) = 7.46, p < .001"

### Dalrymple 2013 (2)

Dalrymple, O., Sears, D. A., & Evangelou, D. (2013). The Relative Pedagogical Value of Disassemble/Analyze/Assemble (DAA) Activities. Advances in Engineering Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1076105

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary analyses testing whether gender, prior disassembling experience, and motivation mediated transfer from the DAA task found no significant relationships with posttest 1 responses; no effect sizes printed for these null findings.

> "Beyond the aforementioned, no other relationships were found among the variables (gender, prior disassembling experience and motivation) examined, and none of them accounted for any significant variability in posttest 1 responses for students in the DAA First sequence"
