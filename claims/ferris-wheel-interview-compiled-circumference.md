### Charlotte Zimmerman 2020

Charlotte Zimmerman, Alexis Olsho, Suzanne White Brahmia, Andrew Boudreaux, Trevor Smith, and Philip Eaton. (2020). Exploring student facility with “goes like” reasoning in introductory physics. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.zimmerman](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.zimmerman)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Validation interviews at another public university in the Pacific Northwest found that "nearly all students interviewed were highly invested in answer choice (d), citing it as familiar" because it contains the circumference expression. Interview findings are from a small sample and are qualitative.

### Charlotte Zimmerman 2020 (2)

Charlotte Zimmerman, Alexis Olsho, Suzanne White Brahmia, Andrew Boudreaux, Trevor Smith, and Philip Eaton. (2020). Exploring student facility with “goes like” reasoning in introductory physics. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.zimmerman](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.zimmerman)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The authors interpret interview behavior as students connecting total distance traveled (changing in time) with the circumference of one revolution (fixed in this problem); lacking spontaneous consideration of total distance as changing, "they didn’t demonstrate facility with expert-like “goes like” reasoning."

> "Because the students interviewed didn’t spontaneously consider the total distance as it is changing , they didn’t demonstrate facility with expert-like “goes like” reasoning."
