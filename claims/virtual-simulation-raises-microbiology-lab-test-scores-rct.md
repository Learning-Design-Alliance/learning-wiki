# Pre-laboratory virtual simulation training raises medical students' pathogenic cocci test scores overall in a randomized controlled trial

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who received virtual simulation training before traditional laboratory practice scored significantly higher (mean 80.82, SD 17.10) than control students (mean 67.45, SD 16.81; P<.001). [→ Lei Xu 2025](#lei-xu-2025)

## Evidence

### Lei Xu 2025

Lei Xu, Xichuan Deng, Tingting Chen, Nan Lu, Yuran Wang, Jia Liu, Yanan Guo, Zeng Tu, Yuxin Nie, Yeganeh Hosseini, Yonglin He. (2025). A Large-Scale Multispecialty Evaluation of Web-Based Simulation in Medical Microbiology Laboratory Education: Randomized Controlled Trial. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/72495

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial of 1282 medical students from 9 specialties randomly assigned to virtual simulation plus traditional laboratory practice (n=653) or traditional teaching alone (n=629) over 6 weeks. The reported overall difference was significant at P<.001; no effect size was printed.

> "The test scores were significantly higher in the experimental group, with a mean of 80.82 (SD 17.10), compared to the control group, with a mean of 67.45 (SD 16.81)."
