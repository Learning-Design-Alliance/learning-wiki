Evidence¶

Melissa Roderick 2009¶

Melissa Roderick, Jenny Nagaoka, and Vanessa Coca. (2009). College Readiness for All: The Challenge for Urban High Schools. The Future of Children. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/college-readiness-all-challenge-urban-high-schools doi:10.1353/foc.0.0024

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing economists Christopher Avery and Thomas Kane, that Boston Public Schools seniors with similar aspirations lagged suburban peers on concrete application steps: obtaining applications (the quoted contrast) and applying to a four-year college by fall (18 percent versus 41 percent). Descriptive percentages only.

"only slightly more than half of the Boston sample, compared with 91 percent of the suburban sample, had obtained an application from the college they were interested in attending by the fall of their senior year"

Melissa Roderick 2009 (2)¶

Melissa Roderick, Jenny Nagaoka, and Vanessa Coca. (2009). College Readiness for All: The Challenge for Urban High Schools. The Future of Children. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/college-readiness-all-challenge-urban-high-schools

q2 · i? · review · r2

The authors' own study, From High School to the Future: Potholes on the Road to College, identified three obstacle points: downshifting plans to two-year or delayed enrollment, not applying by spring of senior year (only 60 percent of aspirants reported applying), and failure to enroll after acceptance. Students with higher qualifications were also at risk.

"We found that only 41 percent of Chicago seniors who stated that they aspired to complete a four-year degree actually applied to and enrolled in a four-year college."
