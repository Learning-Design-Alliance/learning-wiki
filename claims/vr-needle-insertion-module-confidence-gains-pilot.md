# In a pilot at a cancer centre, the needle insertion VR module was associated with statistically significant increases in self-reported confidence across all four procedural domains (N=27)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After the needle insertion VR module, paired Wilcoxon tests showed increased confidence for recalling steps (W = 292, p < 0.001) and explaining steps (W = 347, p < 0.001). [→ Varlukhin 2024](#varlukhin-2024)
`q2 i?` Confidence for identifying equipment (W = 355, p < 0.001) and explaining equipment function (W = 354, p < 0.001) also increased after the needle insertion module. [→ Varlukhin 2024 (2)](#varlukhin-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Varlukhin 2024

Varlukhin, A., Smith, M., Alam, F., Tagger, A., Morton, G., Paudel, M., Loblaw, A., Mendez, L., Hoover, D., Karshafian, R., Nusrat, H. (2024). Development and pilot evaluation of a virtual reality simulator for HDR prostate brachytherapy. [doi:10.1016/j.jmir.2024.03.029](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmir.2024.03.029)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Single-group pre/post pilot of the needle insertion module at Sunnybrook Odette Cancer Centre; twenty-seven participants completed paired surveys. The article reports "(W = 292, p < 0.001)" and "(W = 347, p < 0.001)"; no effect sizes are printed.

> "Recalling steps had increased after the training session (W = 292, p < 0.001). The explanation of steps increased after the training session (W = 347, p < 0.001)."

### Varlukhin 2024 (2)

Varlukhin, A., Smith, M., Alam, F., Tagger, A., Morton, G., Paudel, M., Loblaw, A., Mendez, L., Hoover, D., Karshafian, R., Nusrat, H. (2024). Development and pilot evaluation of a virtual reality simulator for HDR prostate brachytherapy. [doi:10.1016/j.jmir.2024.03.029](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmir.2024.03.029)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same paired-survey pilot cohort (N=27) at the cancer centre; the article reports "(W = 355, p < 0.001)" for identifying equipment and "(W = 354, p < 0.001)" for explaining equipment function, with no effect sizes printed.

> "Identification of equipment increased after the training session (W = 355, p < 0.001). Explanation of equipment function increased after the training session (W = 354, p < 0.001)."
