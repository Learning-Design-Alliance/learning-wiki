## Evidence

### Semb 1994

Semb, G. B., & Ellis, J. A. (1994). Knowledge Taught in School: What Is Remembered? Review of Educational Research, 64(2), 253-286. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543064002253

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Reviewers computed mean relative loss scores for recognition, recall, and cognitive skills across six retention-interval blocks (1-5 to more than 104 weeks) derived from Table 1 and displayed in Figure 3; cognitive skills showed no consistent decline over intervals.

> "The rate of decline for recognition increases sharply from the first to the second interval and then levels off. For recall, the largest decline is from the second to the third interval (the decline from the fifth to the sixth interval is based on one study)."

### Semb 1994 (2)

Semb, G. B., & Ellis, J. A. (1994). Knowledge Taught in School: What Is Remembered? Review of Educational Research, 64(2), 253-286. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543064002253

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Narrative review of interval-event studies: Landauer and Ainslie's practice-exam group showed no loss at one year, while the reviewers report the control group that did not take the practice exam lost 0.73 standard deviation units. Advanced coursework and job-related use also aided retention.

> "Landauer and Ainslie (1975) mailed students a practice exam after 6 months of a 1-year retention interval and found that students performed at the same level as they had on the end-of-term exam."
