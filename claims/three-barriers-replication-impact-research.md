### J. Michael Spector 2015

J. Michael Spector, Tristan E. Johnson, Patricia A. Young. (2015). An editorial on replication studies and scaling up efforts. Education Tech Research Dev 63:1–4. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-014-9364-3

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Editorial argument (authors' interpretation, no data): European Commission networks of excellence last only 4 years and none have yet produced systemic change; the editors urge funders to "promote more types of long-term efforts".

> "The first pertains to funding. National funding agencies seldom support studies that extend beyond a few years."

### J. Michael Spector 2015 (2)

J. Michael Spector, Tristan E. Johnson, Patricia A. Young. (2015). An editorial on replication studies and scaling up efforts. Education Tech Research Dev 63:1–4. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-014-9364-3

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Editorial argument: one-off studies may be published and bring notoriety but "often fail to result in systemic and systematic improvement of educational practice"; promotion and tenure requirements reinforce this disconnect.

> "Researchers have a tendency to want to create their own instruments and engage in an effort that is closely tailored to their personal interests."

### J. Michael Spector 2015 (3)

J. Michael Spector, Tristan E. Johnson, Patricia A. Young. (2015). An editorial on replication studies and scaling up efforts. Education Tech Research Dev 63:1–4. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-014-9364-3

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Editorial argument on the third barrier: reluctance to share instruments and procedures openly, with fees that block graduate students and junior faculty; NSF's ITEST program is cited as a notable exception.

> "too many researchers want to retain full rights and ownership of their instruments and all too often charge those who wish to use those instruments a fee that is prohibitive for graduate students and junior faculty."
