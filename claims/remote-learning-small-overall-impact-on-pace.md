# Pandemic remote learning had a relatively small overall impact on students' ability to keep up with the pace of instruction, with on-time and early submission identical across settings and slightly higher engagement during remote learning.

## Evidence

### Zhongzhou Chen and Tom Zhang 2021

Zhongzhou Chen and Tom Zhang. (2021). Analyzing the Heterogeneous Impact of Remote Learning on Students' Ability to Stay on Track During the Pandemic. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2108.00601](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2108.00601)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Authors' overall interpretation of the LMS log-data analysis comparing three stay-on-track indicators between SP20 face-to-face and FA20 remote semesters; they state "on-time and early submission frequencies are identical in F2F and remote learning settings". No effect sizes are printed.

> "Both on-time and early submission frequencies are identical in F2F and remote learning settings and the fraction of engaged modules increased slightly during remote learning."

### Zhongzhou Chen and Tom Zhang 2021 (2)

Zhongzhou Chen and Tom Zhang. (2021). Analyzing the Heterogeneous Impact of Remote Learning on Students' Ability to Stay on Track During the Pandemic. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2108.00601](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2108.00601)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Observational comparison of engagement fractions for the same 41 modules between the FA20 remote and SP20 face-to-face semesters; the difference is "only a few percent across the board but still statistically significant" (U = 34806, p = 0.038). It remained significant after excluding students passing within 2 attempts before accessing materials. No effect size printed.

> "For the ﬁrst 41 modules, FA20 students somewhat unexpectedly had a higher overall fraction of engaged modules compared to SP20. As shown in Figure 7(b), the diﬀerence is only a few percent across the board but still statistically signiﬁcant (U = 34806, p= 0.038)."
