# Associations between programme engagement and self-efficacy/mindset do not permit causal inference, and effects on academic outcomes were not measured

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors state that engagement with the programme was associated with self-efficacy and mindset, but causation cannot be inferred and the project did not measure how changed academic behaviour translates into academic and welfare outcomes. [→ Harvey Christopher-James 2025](#harvey-christopher-james-2025)

## Evidence

### Harvey Christopher-James 2025

Harvey Christopher-James, Leedham-Green Kathleen E., Koppel Cristina, Maini Arti, Smith Susan F., Morrell Mary J., Emerson Michael. (2025). Improving medical students' learning strategies, management of workload and wellbeing: a mixed methods case study in undergraduate medical education. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07118-6

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

The authors' own interpretive statement in the Conclusions of the mixed methods evaluation. They explicitly note that "it is not possible to infer causation" and that the constructs are also shaped by social history; no effect size is attached to this statement.

> "Engagement with the programme and experience of the programme were indeed associated with self-efficacy and mindset, but it is not possible to infer causation, and we highlight that these constructs are also impacted by an individual’s social history."
