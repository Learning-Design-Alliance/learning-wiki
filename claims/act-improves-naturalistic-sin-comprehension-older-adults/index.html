Evidence¶

Ockelmann 2025¶

Ockelmann, J., Scherpiet, S., Stropahl, M. & Giroud, N. (2025). Personalized and gamified auditory-cognitive training improves naturalistic speech-in-noise comprehension in older adults with hearing loss. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00369-4

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Item-level GLMM analysis of the RCT's naturalistic SIN comprehension task (conversational sentences in cafeteria noise, 54 analyzed participants). The session-by-group interaction was significant ("OR = 1.47, 95% CI [1.25, 1.72],p < 0.001"), with the experimental group improving more than controls; the main effect of session alone was not significant (OR = 1.02).

"we identified a significant interaction effect between training session and group (OR = 1.47, 95% CI [1.25, 1.72],p < 0.001), demonstrating differential improvements in speech-in-noise (SIN) comprehension from pre- to post-training across groups."

Related Claims¶

  • Noise level moderates training-related SIN comprehension gains, with attenuated improvements under high- and low-noise conditions relative to no-noise trials — related
  • Off-training cognitive transfer shows gains in working memory and focused attention for the ACT group only, with no significant group-by-session interactions — related
  • ACT does not change speech-in-noise intelligibility, subjective hearing, or listening effort, dissociating low-level fidelity from higher-order comprehension — related
  • Baseline cognitive abilities do not significantly predict the size of ACT-related speech-in-noise improvement — related
  • On-training cognitive performance improves across all four trained domains after ACT, with large effect sizes — related
