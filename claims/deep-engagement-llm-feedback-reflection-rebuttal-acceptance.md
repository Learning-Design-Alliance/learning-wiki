# Students engaged deeply with LLM feedback, with coded evidence of reflection, rebuttal, and acceptance in the large majority of responses

## Evidence

### Daniel M. Oppenheimer 2025

Daniel M. Oppenheimer, Trent N. Cash, Allison E. Connell Pensky. (2025). You've Got AI Friend in Me: LLMs as Collaborative Learning Partners. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-025-00521-6

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Two blind undergraduate research assistants coded each student's response on three dimensions, including whether the student reflected by "evaluating the quality of the output, comparing the LLM's output to their own thoughts, or explicitly discussing the LLM's output." Inter-rater reliability was high (ks = 0.81 − 0.89).

> "On average, the coders found evidence of reflection in 92.7% of responses."

### Daniel M. Oppenheimer 2025 (2)

Daniel M. Oppenheimer, Trent N. Cash, Allison E. Connell Pensky. (2025). You've Got AI Friend in Me: LLMs as Collaborative Learning Partners. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-025-00521-6

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

In the same blind coding of student responses, rebuttal was coded as "arguing against any of the feedback" or "explaining why the LLM's response may not be valid/appropriate"; acceptance appeared in 93.6% of responses. The authors conclude students were "making active decisions about what feedback to accept and what feedback to rebut."

> "On average, the coders found evidence of rebuttal in 87.8% of responses."
