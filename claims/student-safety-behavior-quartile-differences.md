# Student-reported safety and classroom behavior are more positive in top-quartile schools, and students feel less safe outside school than inside

## Evidence

### Luppescu 2005

Luppescu, S., & Hart, H. (2005). Improving Chicago's Schools 2005 Survey Report: Details of Student and Teacher Responses. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/improving-chicagos-schools-sample-high-school-details-2005

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Summary comparison of the Safety scale across school quartiles from student survey responses. The report continues that about one-third of top-school students feel somewhat safe or not safe, while in the lowest-rated schools the most common category is somewhat safe, with 40 percent of students.

> "In the schools rated highest on student safety, 28 percent of students feel very safe and 39 percent feel mostly safe."

### Luppescu 2005 (2)

Luppescu, S., & Hart, H. (2005). Improving Chicago's Schools 2005 Survey Report: Details of Student and Teacher Responses. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/improving-chicagos-schools-sample-high-school-details-2005

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The report's general statement about the Safety scale, which measures students' sense of personal safety in classes, hallways and bathrooms, traveling between home and school, and outside around the school.

> "In general, students are much less apt to feel safe outside the school than inside."

### Luppescu 2005 (3)

Luppescu, S., & Hart, H. (2005). Improving Chicago's Schools 2005 Survey Report: Details of Student and Teacher Responses. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/improving-chicagos-schools-sample-high-school-details-2005

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Summary comparison of the Student Classroom Behavior scale, which asked students whether classmates treat each other with respect, work together, and help each other learn. In bottom-quartile schools 41 percent rate behavior negative and 14 percent very negative.

> "Sixty-three percent of students in the top quartile schools on this scale report very positive or moderately positive classroom behavior."
