## Evidence

### Chi et al. 1981

Michelene Chi, Paul Feltovich, Robert Glaser. (1981). Categorization and Representation of Physics Problems by Experts and Novices. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog0502_2

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Study Two sorting of 20 problems constructed by crossing surface features (e.g., pulleys, springs, inclined planes) with three physics laws; a novice collapsed problems across physics laws based on surface structure, while a physics professor's sorting validated the a priori principle analysis.

> "we were able to replicate the initial find- ing- that experts categorize physics problems by the underlying physics princi- ples, a kind of "deep structure," whereas novices categorize problems by the surface structure of the problem."

### Chi et al. 1981 (2)

Michelene Chi, Paul Feltovich, Robert Glaser. (1981). Categorization and Representation of Physics Problems by Experts and Novices. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog0502_2

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

A fourth-year undergraduate physics major (advanced novice) sorted the 20 Study Two problems partly by principle but separated groups by surface entities such as springs, with several groupings discrepant from the prior principles analysis.

> "with learning, advanced novices begin to categorize problems by the principles with gradual release from depen- dence on the physical characteristics of the problems, although their group- ings are still constrained by surface features."
