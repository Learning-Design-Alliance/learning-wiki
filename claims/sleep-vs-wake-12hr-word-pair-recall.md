# At 12 hr retest, word pair recall is superior after a night of sleep than after a day of wakefulness, driven by a sleep-wake difference for unrelated but not related pairs

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Overall recall change from training was superior in the 12 hr Sleep group (+6.6%) than the 12 hr Wake group (+0.8%). [→ Payne JD 2012](#payne-jd-2012)
`q3 i?` The sleep advantage was qualified by a Condition x Word-pair type interaction: wake and sleep produced equivalent recall of related pairs, while recall of unrelated pairs was disrupted in the wake relative to the sleep condition. [→ Payne JD 2012 (2)](#payne-jd-2012-2)

## Evidence

### Payne JD 2012

Payne JD, Tucker MA, Ellenbogen JM, Wamsley EJ, Walker MP, Schacter DL, Stickgold R. (2012). Memory for Semantically Related and Unrelated Declarative Information: The Benefit of Sleep, the Cost of Wake. PLoS ONE 7(3): e33079. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0033079

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

12 hr retest ANOVA (Condition: Sleep vs Wake) in the 12 hr condition of this experiment, N = 80. The main effect showed "word pair recall was superior 12 hr later when the retention interval spanned a night of sleep"; no effect size printed.

> "a significant main effect of Condition (change from training: 12 hr Sleep: +6.6%, 12 hr Wake: +0.8%, F1,76 = 7.23, p = .009; Figure 2b), indicating that word pair recall was superior 12 hr later when the retention interval spanned a night of sleep, rather than a day of wakefulness"

### Payne JD 2012 (2)

Payne JD, Tucker MA, Ellenbogen JM, Wamsley EJ, Walker MP, Schacter DL, Stickgold R. (2012). Memory for Semantically Related and Unrelated Declarative Information: The Benefit of Sleep, the Cost of Wake. PLoS ONE 7(3): e33079. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0033079

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Follow-up t-tests decomposing the significant Condition x Word-pair type interaction (F1,76 = 7.94, p = .006) at the 12 hr retest. Related pairs showed "equivalent recall" across sleep and wake; unrelated pairs were significantly worse after wake (p = .001).

> "while wake and sleep led to equivalent recall of related word pairs (t 40 = 0. 01, p = .92), recall of unrelated pairs was disrupted in the wake relative to the sleep condition (t 36 = 3.62, p = .001, white bars)"
