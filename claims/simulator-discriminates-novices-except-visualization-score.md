# Initial simulator evaluation discriminates novices from experienced bronchoscopists on identification score, procedure time and overall performance, but not visualization score

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` At initial evaluation, novices scored significantly lower than experienced groups on identification score (p = 0.001), total procedure time and overall performance score (p = 0.0001), while visualization score, scope-wall contact time and scope at mid-lumen time did not differ significantly. [→ Veaudor Martin 2018](#veaudor-martin-2018)

## Evidence

### Veaudor Martin 2018

Veaudor Martin, Gérinière Laurence, Souquet Pierre-Jean, Druette Loïc, Martin Xavier, Vergnon Jean-Michel, Couraud Sébastien. (2018). High-fidelity simulation self-training enables novice bronchoscopists to acquire basic bronchoscopy skills comparable to their moderately and highly experienced counterparts. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1304-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Initial cross-group evaluation in the prospective cohort (n = 8, 17, 9 across groups A, B, C) showing the simulator discriminates experience levels on anatomical knowledge and speed but not on visualization score or dexterity measures. No effect size printed.

> "The identification score was significantly lower in ev-A compared to ev-B and ev-C (p = 0.001), as was the median overall performance score: 5.9% (IQR = 5.1) in ev-A compared to 25.3% (IQR = 13.8) in ev-B and 22.2%, IQR = 5.6) in ev-C (p = 0.0001)."
