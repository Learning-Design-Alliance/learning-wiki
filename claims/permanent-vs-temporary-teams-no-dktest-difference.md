# Permanent versus temporary TBL teams did not differ in domain knowledge test scores

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r3` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2024 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the randomized controlled trial, students working in permanent teams had nearly identical domain knowledge test scores to students working in temporary teams reassigned for every problem. [→ Heber Stefan 2024](#heber-stefan-2024)
`q3 i?` In negative-control analyses of dkTest items on the TBL topic not attended before the test, team composition did not affect scores. [→ Heber Stefan 2024 (2)](#heber-stefan-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Heber Stefan 2024

Heber Stefan, Wagner-Menghin Michaela, Volf Ivo, Slak Rupnik Marjan, Schmid Diethart, Marz Richard, Fischer Michael J.M. (2024). Short-term cognitive learning outcomes in team-based learning: is the permanent team important? Medical Education Online, 29(1). https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2024.2397864

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Primary-endpoint analysis of the two-arm randomized trial in an online TBL module, using a full-factorial linear mixed model with 464 students; the team composition × TBL-topic interaction tested the endpoint and permanent and temporary teams showed "nearly identical dkTest scores".

> "Students working in permanent teams had nearly identical dkTest scores as those working in temporary teams (estimated dkTest score difference = 0.034 points, 95% confidence interval −0.29–0.36, Figure 2A)."

### Heber Stefan 2024 (2)

Heber Stefan, Wagner-Menghin Michaela, Volf Ivo, Slak Rupnik Marjan, Schmid Diethart, Marz Richard, Fischer Michael J.M. (2024). Short-term cognitive learning outcomes in team-based learning: is the permanent team important? Medical Education Online, 29(1). https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2024.2397864

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Negative-control analysis of dkTest questions referring to the TBL topic students did not attend prior to the test; team composition affected neither set of scores (p > 0.45 each), consistent with the primary null result.

> "As expected, whether the students were part of permanent or temporary teams did not affect the test scores (p > 0.45 each)."
