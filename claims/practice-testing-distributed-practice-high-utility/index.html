Evidence¶

Dunlosky 2013¶

Dunlosky, J., Rawson, K. A., Marsh, E. J., Nathan, M. J., & Willingham, D. T. (2013). Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques: Promising Directions From Cognitive and Educational Psychology. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4–58. https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266

q2 · i? · review · r3

The monograph's own overall assessment, a narrative synthesis of the literatures on the two techniques. The authors state the techniques "benefit learners of different ages and abilities" across many criterion tasks and educational contexts; no pooled effect size is printed for this summary judgment.

"Practice testing and distributed practice received high utility assessments because they benefit learners of different ages and abilities and have been shown to boost students' performance across many criterion tasks and even in educational contexts."
