# Switching from FES to PES is associated with increased dropout probability and decreased GPA

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Around 16% of FTIC students in the college of science who switched from FES to PES after three semesters dropped out, compared to less than 1% of students who maintained FES. [→ Shahab Boumi 2020](#shahab-boumi-2020)
`q2 i?` For FTIC students who switch from FES to PES, the average GPA difference (fourth semester minus first three semesters) is -0.64, compared to +0.44 for students who stay FES. [→ Shahab Boumi 2020 (2)](#shahab-boumi-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Shahab Boumi 2020

Shahab Boumi, Adan Vela. (2020). Quantifying the relationship between student enrollment patterns and student performance. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2003.10874](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2003.10874)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparative analysis of enrollment persistence between students who switch from FES to PES and those who maintain FES, matched by college and GPA. Results across colleges are summarized in Table 18.

> "Results indicated that around %16 of students in the college of science, who switched after three FES semesters to PES, have dropped out from their program. This percentage is signiﬁcantly lower for students who maintain FES throughout the same period (less than 1% dropout)."

### Shahab Boumi 2020 (2)

Shahab Boumi, Adan Vela. (2020). Quantifying the relationship between student enrollment patterns and student performance. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2003.10874](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2003.10874)

`q2 · i?`

GPA trend comparison between switchers and non-switchers matched by college and GPA. Two-way ANOVA shows the switching variable (p-value close to 0) is the only factor impacting students' GPA, regardless of enrollment college.

> "For FTIC students, the average diﬀerence for students who switch from FES to PES is -0.64, while for students who stay FES is +0.44."
