# The CCT intervention produced statistically significant main effects favoring CCT on reading comprehension (ES = 0.14), TOSREC (ES = 0.28), and taught vocabulary (ES = 0.43), but not on SWE or ORF

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Nathan H. Clemens 2019

Nathan H. Clemens, Eric Oslund, Oi-man Kwok, Melissa Fogarty, Deborah Simmons, and John L. Davis. (2019). Skill Moderators of the Effects of a Reading Comprehension Intervention. Exceptional Children, 85(2), 197–211. https://doi.org/10.1177/0014402918787339

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Main-effects analysis from the parent RCT (Fogarty et al., 2017) using latent variable modeling with 226 students nested within three teachers. Significant effects were also found on TOSREC (β = 0.13; ES = 0.28) and taught vocabulary (β = 0.23; ES = 0.43); "Statistically significant main effects were not observed for SWE or ORF".

> "the standardized effect of intervention condition on the posttest reading comprehension latent variable was positive and statistically significant (β = 0.08; p = .031; effect size [ES] = 0.14), indicating statistically significant effects favoring the CCT group compared to the typical-practice condition"
