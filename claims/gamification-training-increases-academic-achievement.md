# Gamification-based addiction-prevention training raised male adolescents' scores on a self-report academic achievement questionnaire, compared with an untrained control group

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Scores on a self-report academic achievement questionnaire rose only in the intervention group, immediately and one month after training, compared with controls who received no training (P < 0.001); grades and test performance were not measured, and the training was bundled with WhatsApp support and feedback. [→ Taghipour Esmaeel 2023](#taghipour-esmaeel-2023)

## Evidence

### Taghipour Esmaeel 2023

Taghipour Esmaeel, Vizeshfar Fatemeh, Zarifsanaiey Nahid. (2023). The effect of gamification-based training on the knowledge, attitudes, and academic achievement of male adolescents in preventing substance and internet addiction. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04858-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experiment using Herman's Academic Advancement Questionnaire, 29 incomplete sentences the students complete themselves (a self-report, not a measure of grades or test performance); controls received no training. Intervention-group academic achievement rose from 70.05 ± 17.43 pre-test to 96.49 ± 8.61 post-test, versus 68.07 ± 16.37 in controls (P = 0.000); the gain persisted at one month (91.19 ± 5.48).

> "Academic achievement also saw an increase only in the intervention group immediately and one month after training, highlighting the positive impact of the training on individuals' Internet attitudes and academic performance among adolescents in the intervention group"
