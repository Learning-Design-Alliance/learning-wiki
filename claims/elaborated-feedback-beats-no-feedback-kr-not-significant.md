## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Conceptual and procedural elaborated feedback each produced significantly higher posttest achievement than no feedback after controlling for prior knowledge, but KR feedback did not differ significantly from no feedback. [→ Fynn Töllner 2026](#fynn-tollner-2026)

## Evidence

### Fynn Töllner 2026

Fynn Töllner, Poldi Kuhl, Michael Besser. (2026). Feedback complexities interact with prior knowledge. Learning and Instruction 105 (2026) 102413. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2026.102413

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled pretest/posttest study of N = 190 seventh-graders solving fraction-addition tasks on iPads, analyzed with multilevel models controlling pretest scores. Linear contrasts showed elaborated feedback beat no feedback; the KR contrast was non-significant, t(5.22) = 2.77, p = .226.

> "Both conceptual t(4.13) = 13.12, p < .001, 95% CI [6.05, 12.91] and procedural feedback t(4.60) = 6.38, p = .011, 95% CI [1.80, 9.94], as types of elaborated feedback, were significantly more conducive to learning than providing no feedback group"
