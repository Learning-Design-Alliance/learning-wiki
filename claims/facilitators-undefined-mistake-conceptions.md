# Experienced simulation facilitators do not explicitly define what constitutes a mistake, alternating between 'severe' and 'minor' mistakes

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Lervik W 2025

Lervik W, Solberg M, Wiig AC, Berg H. (2025). Turning nursing students' mistakes into resources for learning in simulation-based training: facilitators' assumptions about providing feedback in debriefing. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06628-z

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of semi-structured interviews with nine experienced facilitators from three Norwegian universities found facilitators "did not specify or define what they considered a mistake", alternating between severe and minor mistakes; incorrect medication handling exemplified severe mistakes.

> "When the facilitators discussed mistakes as phenomena, they did not specify or define what they considered a mistake. Instead, they alternated between expressions like making "severe mistakes" and "minor mistakes" when discussing mistakes in general."
