# Published ILSs differ from unpublished ILSs in design time, reuse, apps, and resources

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Published ILSs had significantly higher design time, more copies, more Golabz apps, and more resources than unpublished ILSs, and the odds of publishing increased with reuse, design time, number of Golabz apps, and number of Golabz labs. [→ de Jong 2021](#de-jong-2021)
`q2 i?` Teachers working with project members were more likely to publish their ILS (25.7%) compared to groups of teachers (11.0%) or single teachers (11.1%). [→ de Jong 2021 (2)](#de-jong-2021-2)

## Evidence

### de Jong 2021

de Jong, T., Gillet, D., Rodríguez-Triana, M. J., Hovardas, T., Dikke, D., Doran, R., Dziabenko, O., Koslowsky, J., Korventausta, M., Law, E., Pedaste, M., Tasiopoulou, E., Vidal, G., & Zacharia, Z. C. (2021). Understanding teacher design practices for digital inquiry–based science learning: the case of Go-Lab. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-020-09904-z

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression on 2414 implemented ILSs (349 published, 2065 unpublished) with 89.1% correctly predicted cases. "A logistic regression (forward stepwise method; percentage of correctly predicted cases = 89.1%) showed that the odds of publishing an ILS increased with the number of times an ILS was reused" as well as with design time, number of Golabz apps, and number of Golabz labs. Published ILSs averaged 463.97 minutes design time versus 294.30 for unpublished.

> "A logistic regression (forward stepwise method; percentage of correctly predicted cases = 89.1%) showed that the odds of publishing an ILS increased with the number of times an ILS was reused"
