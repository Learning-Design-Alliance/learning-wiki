## Evidence

### Marshall A. Baker & J. Shane Robinson 2017

Marshall A. Baker & J. Shane Robinson. (2017). The Effects of an Experiential Approach to Learning on Student Motivation. Journal of Agricultural Education, 58(3), 150-167. https://doi.org/10.5032/jae.2017.03150

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

MCA of KLSI 3.1 learning-style scores against total motivation in the same 87-student experiment: "R2 = .14; p = .02" per Table 5. The authors interpret that students' inherent learning preferences related to motivation though not to performance.

> "The MCA analysis for the third research question yielded a significant R2 value with 14% of the variance in motivation attributed to a linear combination of learning style measures"

### Marshall A. Baker & J. Shane Robinson 2017 (2)

Marshall A. Baker & J. Shane Robinson. (2017). The Effects of an Experiential Approach to Learning on Student Motivation. Journal of Agricultural Education, 58(3), 150-167. https://doi.org/10.5032/jae.2017.03150

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Follow-up correlation within the same MCA: the AE subscale was the highest-performing subscale with "r = .30, p = .00"; semipartial contribution was .20 (p = .06), indicating the collective system of variables explained the motivational construct.

> "Further correlation analysis elucidat ed the relationship as the Active Experimentation (AE) learning preference was highly correlated to motivational measures ( r = .30, p = .00)."
