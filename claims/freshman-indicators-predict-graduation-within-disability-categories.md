# Within every student category, freshman GPA, course failures, and absences are strongly related to five-year graduation rates, with large drops per half grade point, per additional course failure, and per absence band

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2`–`r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students with a 2.5 GPA or higher graduate at high rates in all categories (89 percent without disabilities, 78 percent below grade level, 86 percent learning disabilities, 83 percent mild cognitive disabilities), while only one-quarter to one-third of students with a 1.0 average graduate in five years. [→ Julia Gwynne 2009](#julia-gwynne-2009)
`q2 i?` Students with zero to four absences per semester have graduation rates of 90 percent or greater among non-disabled students and students with learning or mild cognitive disabilities. [→ Julia Gwynne 2009 (2)](#julia-gwynne-2009-2)

### Julia Gwynne 2009

Julia Gwynne, Joy Lesnick, Holly M. Hart, Elaine M. Allensworth. (2009). What Matters for Staying On-Track and Graduating in Chicago Public Schools: A Focus on Students with Disabilities. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago Urban Education Institute. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/what-matters-staying-track-and-graduating-chicago-public-schools-focus-students

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Chapter 3 analysis (Figure 8) of the 2001 cohort relating freshman GPA to five-year graduation within each of five categories. The report notes a large decrease in graduation rates when moving down each half-grade point.

> "Students with a 2.5 (C+) average or higher are very likely to graduate from high school within five years: 89 percent of students without identified disabilities, 78 percent of students who are two or more years below grade level, 86 percent of students with a learning disability, and 83 percent of students with a mild cognitive disability graduated within five years."

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Chapter 3 analysis (Figure 10) relating semester absences to five-year graduation for students who do not receive special education services and students with learning or mild cognitive disabilities. The zero-to-four-absence bar is missing for students with emotional disturbances due to insufficient sample.

> "these students have graduation rates of 90 percent or greater for zero to four absences per semester."
