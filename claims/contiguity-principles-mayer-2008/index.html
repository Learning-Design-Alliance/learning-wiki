Evidence¶

Richard E. Mayer 2008¶

Richard E. Mayer. (2008). Applying the Science of Learning: Evidence-Based Principles for the Design of Multimedia Instruction. American Psychologist. https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066x.63.8.760

q3 · i3 · review · r2

Controlled experiments comparing integrated versus separated page layouts for paper-based brakes and lightning lessons and computer-based lightning lessons; the article reports the quoted 5-of-5 result and that "The effect size was 1.12, which is a large effect."

"In 5 of 5 tests, involving paper-based lessons on brakes and lightning and computer-based lessons on lightning, learners who received integrated presentations performed better on transfer tests than did students who received separated presentations"

Richard E. Mayer 2008 (2)¶

Richard E. Mayer. (2008). Applying the Science of Learning: Evidence-Based Principles for the Design of Multimedia Instruction. American Psychologist. https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066x.63.8.760

q3 · i3 · theoretical · r3

Controlled experiments comparing simultaneous versus successive presentation of narration and animation on pumps, brakes, lungs, and lightning; the article reports the quoted 8-of-8 result and that "The median effect size was 1.31, which is considered a large effect."

"in 8 out of 8 experiments involving computer-based lessons on pumps, brakes, lungs, and lightning, learners performed better on answering transfer questions when they received simultaneous presentation of animation and narration rather than successive presentation"
