# Timing of divorce (years since dissolution) cannot explain the maternal-education heterogeneity in the divorce–GPA association

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Although highly educated parents divorced somewhat later, the negative association between years since divorce and GPA was strongest among lowly educated mothers, so timing cannot account for the heterogeneity. [→ Nilsen 2020](#nilsen-2020)

## Evidence

### Nilsen 2020

Nilsen, S. A., Breivik, K., Wold, B., Askeland, K. G., Sivertsen, B., Hysing, M., & Bøe, T. (2020). Divorce and adolescent academic achievement: Heterogeneity in the associations by parental education. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229183

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Robustness analysis plotting GPA as a function of years since divorce across parental education levels (Fig 4), using linear fits and GAMs. The authors state the plotted GAM curves "do not give any strong indications that GPA is highly influenced by the timing of divorce".

> "The negative association between time since divorce and GPA was strongest among lowly educated mothers. As lowly educated mothers on average had most years since divorce, this finding highlights that time since divorce could not explain the heterogeneity"
