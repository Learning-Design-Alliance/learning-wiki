# Vocabulary and listening comprehension are distinct rather than unitary constructs for struggling ELs and non-ELs, with a four-factor model fitting better than a three-factor model

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A four-factor model separating vocabulary, listening comprehension, word reading, and reading comprehension fit better than a three-factor general linguistic comprehension model across groups. [→ Cho 2019](#cho-2019)

## Evidence

### Cho 2019

Cho, E., Capin, P., Roberts, G., Roberts, G. J., & Vaughn, S. (2019). Examining Sources and Mechanisms of Reading Comprehension Difficulties: Comparing English Learners and Non-English Learners within the Simple View of Reading. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000332

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Confirmatory factor analyses comparing nested factor structures in a sample of 446 struggling fourth-grade readers; the four-factor model met fit guidelines (CFI/TLI > .90, RMSEA < .08, SRMR < .06) for all samples, and the fit deterioration was more apparent for ELs (vocabulary–listening comprehension r = .68).

> "the model fit for the four-factor model (Figure 1b) was better than the fit for the three-factor model (Figure 1a) across the total sample, the ELs, and the non-ELs."
