# Student interpreters who practiced with an automated scoring system outperformed the control group on total interpreting scores at post-test

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` After 14 weeks, the experimental group scored significantly higher than the control group at post-test (d = 1.03). [→ Chen C and Liu T 2026](#chen-c-and-liu-t-2026)

## Evidence

### Chen C and Liu T 2026

Chen C and Liu T (2026) Impact of automated scoring on interpreting performance and self-regulated learning: evidence from a pedagogical experiment. Front. Psychol. 17:1867463. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1867463

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

In the 14-week quasi-experiment with two intact classes (n1 = 24, n2 = 22), the post-test between-group comparison showed the experimental group ahead with a large printed effect: "d = 1.03, 95% CI [0.41, 1.64]". Groups were well-matched at pre-test (d = 0.10).

> "At post-test the experimental group ( M = 85.58, SD = 5.07) scored higher than the control group ( M = 80.84, SD = 4.01), t(44) = 3.50, p = 0.001, d = 1.03, 95% CI [0.41, 1.64]"
