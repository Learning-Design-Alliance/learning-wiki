## Evidence

### Lovin 2014

Lovin, E., & Lambeth, D. T. (2014). Effects of information and communication technology on engagement and art production for eighth-grade students. li-manager's Journal of School Educational Technology, 10(3). https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Effects+of+Information+and+Communication+Technology+on+Engagement+and+Art+Production+for+Eighth-Grade+Students

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of rubric-scored art projects (printmaking, ceramics, paper cut) between two 15-student eighth-grade classes over an 8-week intervention; the article reports "no significant difference in the OLC scores (M=93.58)" versus face-to-face (M=90.91).

> "While there was no significant difference in the OLC scores (M=93.58) when compared to the face-to-face class scores (M=90.91), students who used the online-learning component did increase their project scores throughout the study."

### Lovin 2014 (2)

Lovin, E., & Lambeth, D. T. (2014). Effects of information and communication technology on engagement and art production for eighth-grade students. li-manager's Journal of School Educational Technology, 10(3). https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Effects+of+Information+and+Communication+Technology+on+Engagement+and+Art+Production+for+Eighth-Grade+Students

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r1`

The same two-class comparison of art project scores; the article reports "The treatment had a small effect (d = 0.31)" on project scores between OLC and face-to-face classes.

> "Cohen's “d” was calculated to determine the practical significance of student art project scores in the OLC class and face-to-face class. The treatment had a small effect (d = 0.31)."
