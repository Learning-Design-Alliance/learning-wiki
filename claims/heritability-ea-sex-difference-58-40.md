## Evidence

### Arpawong 2023

Arpawong, Thalida Em; Gatz, Margaret; Zavala, Catalina; Gruenewald, Tara L.; Walters, Ellen E.; Prescott, Carol A. (2023). Nature, Nurture, and the Meaning of Educational Attainment: Differences by Sex and Socioeconomic Status. Twin Research and Human Genetics. https://doi.org/10.1017/thg.2023.6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Five-group structural equation modeling of twin and sibling data (3546 individuals, 1778 families) decomposing EA into additive genetic, shared, and unique environmental components with sex-specific estimates. Heritability "was calculated as 40% and 58% respectively" for women and men.

> "Men had a greater amount of variance overall, and more genetic variance than women. In relative amounts, genetic variance accounted for 51% of the total variance, but within women and men, heritability was calculated as 40% and 58% respectively. Women had more shared environmental variance contributing to EA than men."

### Arpawong 2023 (2)

Arpawong, Thalida Em; Gatz, Margaret; Zavala, Catalina; Gruenewald, Tara L.; Walters, Ellen E.; Prescott, Carol A. (2023). Nature, Nurture, and the Meaning of Educational Attainment: Differences by Sex and Socioeconomic Status. Twin Research and Human Genetics. https://doi.org/10.1017/thg.2023.6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Chi-square model-difference tests in the five-group SEMs: equality across sex was rejected (χ2 = 33.59, df = 3) while the HES effect was equated across sex (χ2 = 1.35, df = 1, no difference in fit).

> "Model-fitting results reject that there is equality across sex (χ2= 33.59, df = 3, model 1 in Table S5 in the supplementary material).
