# Some QM difficulties reflect failure to shift frames when the problem requires a transition

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · qualitative `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2019 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When a problem requires shifting between frames, students who fail to shift, or shift into an unproductive frame, exhibit transition errors; two published difficulties showed only transition errors. [→ Bahar Modir 2019](#bahar-modir-2019)
`q2 i?` In one superposition-time-dependence task, roughly 10–20% of students (N=416) argued the time drops out without doing the math, which the authors reinterpret as a transition error rather than a single-phase misconception. [→ Bahar Modir 2019 (2)](#bahar-modir-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Bahar Modir 2019

Bahar Modir, John D. Thompson, and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2019). Framing difficulties in quantum mechanics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020146](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020146)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Definitional statement of the transition error category from the results tabulation; the authors tabulated published difficulties (Table I) by whether their problems required frame shifting, identifying difficulties exhibiting only transition errors.

> "Transition errors occur when a problem statement ex- pects students to shift between two frames, and the stu- dents either do not shift, or shift into an unproductive frame."

### Bahar Modir 2019 (2)

Bahar Modir, John D. Thompson, and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2019). Framing difficulties in quantum mechanics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020146](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020146)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Written-test data from Emigh et al. as reported in the transition error example section; students reasoned conceptually that pure terms lose time dependence without checking cross terms. The authors reinterpret what Emigh et al. called a difficulty as a frame transition error. No effect size is printed.

> "About 10− 20% of the students (N=416) from Emigh et al., applied the same kind of reasoning to argue that “time drops out” or “the probability is squared and the time won’t matter”"
