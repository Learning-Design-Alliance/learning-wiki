# Specialists reflecting on their own consultations focused on generic communication skills rather than specific intercultural communication skills

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In stimulated-recall reflection on their own videotaped consultations, medical specialists mainly discussed generic communication skills (listening, checking understanding) rather than specific intercultural communication skills such as asking about language proficiency. [→ Paternotte E. 2016](#paternotte-e-2016)

## Evidence

### Paternotte E. 2016

Paternotte E., Scheele F., van Rossum T. R., Seeleman M. C. M., Scherpbier A. J. J. A., van Dulmen A. M. (2016). How do medical specialists value their own intercultural communication behaviour? A reflective practice study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0727-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview study with 17 specialists reflecting on videotaped consultations; participants had not received formal intercultural communication training. The article reports they focused on "generic communication skills rather than on specific intercultural communication skills" and found it difficult to apply specific ICC skills such as asking for language proficiency.

> "The participants focused on their generic communication skills rather than on specific intercultural communication skills, which could either indicate their lack of awareness, or demonstrate that practicing generic communication is more important than applying specific intercultural communication."
