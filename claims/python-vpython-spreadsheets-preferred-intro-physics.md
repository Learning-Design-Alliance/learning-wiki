## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Justin Gambrell and Eric Brewe 2024

Justin Gambrell and Eric Brewe. (2024). Towards a Generalized Assessment of Computational Thinking for Introductory Physics Students. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010128](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010128)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview question on class programming environment, 109 responses from all 26 participants. Participants cited accessibility: browser-based platforms like Glowscript or Trinket avoid installing software, so "the hurdle is less or minimal."

> "Python (20 participants 77%) and Vpython (14 partici- pants 54%) had the highest response rate in this category."

### Justin Gambrell and Eric Brewe 2024 (2)

Justin Gambrell and Eric Brewe. (2024). Towards a Generalized Assessment of Computational Thinking for Introductory Physics Students. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010128](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010128)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Same environment question; participants described spreadsheets as showing intermediate numbers step by step (e.g. Euler-method velocity changes per delta-t) and as familiar software reducing student apprehension.

> "Spreadsheets were the next highest response rate (11 par- ticipants 42%)."
