# Moral identity moderates the U-shaped relationship between academic performance and antisocial behavior: the curve is steeper and reverses positively only for students with low moral identity

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The interaction between the academic performance square and moral identity negatively predicts antisocial behavior, indicating that moral identity flattens the U-shaped curve. [→ Zhu Xiaobo 2024](#zhu-xiaobo-2024)
`q2 i?` At high moral identity the slope remains negative at every level of performance, while at low moral identity it becomes significantly positive at 2SD. [→ Zhu Xiaobo 2024 (2)](#zhu-xiaobo-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Zhu Xiaobo 2024

Zhu Xiaobo, Jiang Wenyi, Shi Weijin, Shi Junfeng. (2024). Good learners or trouble makers? Study on the relationship between academic performance and antisocial behavior of junior high school students. PLOS ONE 19(1): e0295705. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0295705

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Moderated curvilinear regression (Model 5, Aiken and West approach) on the CEPS sample. Fig 4 shows antisocial behavior first falls then rises with performance for low moral identity but remains stably negative for high moral identity.

> "Model 5 in Table 3 shows that the interactive term between academic performance square and moral identity is negatively related to antisocial behavior (β = -. 004, p < 0.01), which suggests that moral identity moderates the U-shaped relationship between academic performance and antisocial behavior."

### Zhu Xiaobo 2024 (2)

Zhu Xiaobo, Jiang Wenyi, Shi Weijin, Shi Junfeng. (2024). Good learners or trouble makers? Study on the relationship between academic performance and antisocial behavior of junior high school students. PLOS ONE 19(1): e0295705. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0295705

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Simple slope tests by moral identity level (Table 7). For low moral identity the 2SD slope is positive and significant, which the authors note is "stronger than the main effect"; for high moral identity all slopes, including -0.028 at 2SD, are negative.

> "when the academic performance raise to the value of 2SD, there is a positive significant effect of academic performance on antisocial behavior (β = .092, P < 0.001)."
