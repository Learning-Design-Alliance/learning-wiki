# Raising the Year-1 credit standard from 67% to 100% significantly increased Year-1 pass rates in medical students

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Year-1 pass rates were significantly higher in the 100%-credit cohort than in the 67%-credit cohort (OR = 4.65). [→ Stegers-Jager KM 2020](#stegers-jager-km-2020)

## Evidence

### Stegers-Jager KM 2020

Stegers-Jager KM, Savas M, van der Waal J, van Rossum EFC, Woltman AM. (2020). Gender-specific effects of raising Year-1 standards on medical students' academic performance and stress levels. Med Educ. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.14068

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Prospective comparative cohort study of two consecutive cohorts (n = 410 and n = 413) at one Dutch medical school; logistic regression on Year-1 pass rate showed "significantly higher in the 100%-credit cohort" with printed effect size OR = 4.65 (95% CI 2.79-7.75), a large effect by the article's own OR thresholds (3.00 = large).

> "The logistic regression analysis revealed that Year-1 pass rates were significantly higher in the 100%-credit cohort (Wald χ2 (1) = 34.77, P < .001; OR 4.65, 95% CI 2.79-7.75)"
