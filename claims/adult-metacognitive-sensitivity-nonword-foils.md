## Evidence

### Liang 2026

Liang, F., Zhu, Y., Li, R. & Fu, Q. (2026). Foil Type Modulates Developmental Changes in Statistical Learning Across Childhood to Adulthood. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00434-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Group × Confidence GLMM (M3a) fitted to nonword foil trials for Grade 5 versus adults; the interaction was significant, supporting the article's conclusion of an adult advantage in metacognitive sensitivity for nonword foils.

> "the Group × Confidence interaction was significant, 𝜒2(2) = 6.440, 𝑝 = 0.040; 𝐵𝐹10 = 1.18"

### Liang 2026 (2)

Liang, F., Zhu, Y., Li, R. & Fu, Q. (2026). Foil Type Modulates Developmental Changes in Statistical Learning Across Childhood to Adulthood. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00434-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Simple-effect analysis for adults on nonword foils: Remember exceeded Guess (B = 1.523, pHolm < 0.001, BF10 = 7039.1) and Familiar (EMM = 0.755) also exceeded Guess (B = 1.158, pHolm < 0.001); Remember vs Familiar did not reach significance.

> "accuracy was significantly higher for Remember responses (𝐸𝑀𝑀 = 0.816, 95% CI [0.750, 0.867]) than Guess responses (𝐸𝑀𝑀 = 0.491, 95% CI [0.387, 0.596])"

### Liang 2026 (3)

Liang, F., Zhu, Y., Li, R. & Fu, Q. (2026). Foil Type Modulates Developmental Changes in Statistical Learning Across Childhood to Adulthood. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00434-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Simple-effect analysis for Grade 5 children on nonword foils in the same M3a model: none of the confidence-level contrasts reached significance, so the adult sensitivity was not matched in children despite comparable overall nonword accuracy.

> "for Grade 5 children, no significant differences in accuracy were observed among Remember, Familiar, and Guess responses (all |𝐵| ≤ 0.604, 𝑎𝑙𝑙 𝑝𝐻𝑜𝑙𝑚 ≥ 0.113"
