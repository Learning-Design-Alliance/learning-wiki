# A personalized ability signal increased the probability of participating in AP classes by 49 percentage points among surveyed students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among surveyed students, receiving a personalized message signaling potential to succeed in AP coursework increased the probability of participating in AP classes by 49 percentage points relative to students who did not receive the signal. [→ Naihobe Gonzalez 2017](#naihobe-gonzalez-2017)

## Evidence

### Naihobe Gonzalez 2017

Naihobe Gonzalez. (2017). How Learning About One's Ability Affects Educational Investments: Evidence from the Advanced Placement Program. Working Paper 52, Mathematica Policy Research. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/how-learning-about-ones-ability-affects-educational-investments-evidence-from-the-advanced-placement

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Regression discontinuity analysis of the 2013 PSAT ability-signal intervention in Oakland Unified School District. The signal "increased the probability of participating in AP classes by 49 percentage points among surveyed students" compared to students who did not receive the signal. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "Using a regression discontinuity design, I found that the signal increased the probability of participating in AP classes by 49 percentage points among surveyed students, leading them to enroll in and pass about one more AP course the following year compared to students who did not receive the signal."
