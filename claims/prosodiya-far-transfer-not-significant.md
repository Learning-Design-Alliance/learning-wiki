# Far transfer to inflected training words shows only a marginal, non-significant and non-robust effect

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The training effect on spelling of inflected training words (far transfer) was marginal and not significant, and not robust across estimation panels. [→ Holz 2023](#holz-2023)

## Evidence

### Holz 2023

Holz, H., Ninaus, M., Schwerter, J., Parrisius, C., Beuttler, B., Brandelik, K., & Meurers, D. (2023). A Digital Game-Based Training Improves Spelling in German Primary School Children – A Randomized Controlled Field Trial. Learning and Instruction, 87, 101771. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2023.101771

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Random-effects regression with controls (Table 3 Panel B) on the SDST far-transfer category (inflected training words, e.g. rennt from trained base form rennen); the effect was "marginal though not significant" (p = .072) and non-significant in Panels A and C.

> "Finally, we found a marginal though not significant training effect on children’s spelling of inflected training words (far transfer learning),𝛽 = 0.86,𝑆𝐸 = 0.48,𝑡(187) = 1 .81,𝑝 = .072, whereas this effect is neither significant in Panel A nor in Panel C, which indicates no robustness and caution in interpretation."
