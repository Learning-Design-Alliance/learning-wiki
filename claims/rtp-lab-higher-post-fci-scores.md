# Students in RealTime Physics inquiry-based labs were predicted to have higher post FCI scores than students in traditional labs

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Controlling for course, semester, preparation, and gender, the RealTime Physics curriculum (with or without reflections) predicted a higher post FCI score than the traditional curriculum. [→ Danny Doucette 2022](#danny-doucette-2022)
`q2 i?` Mean FCI scores rose from pre to post for RealTime Physics students but fell for traditional-lab students. [→ Danny Doucette 2022 (2)](#danny-doucette-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Danny Doucette 2022

Danny Doucette, Russell Clark, and Chandralekha Singh. (2022). Students' attitudes toward experimental physics in a conceptual inquiry-based introductory physics lab. [doi:10.1139/cjp-2021-0121](https://doi.org/10.1139/cjp-2021-0121)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Linear model of matched pre/post FCI responses from the stand-alone lab course, in which no students were concurrently taking a mechanics lecture; the Inquiry effect was that post FCI scores were "2 .60 ± 2.58 points higher".

> "we ﬁnd that students in labs that used the RealTime Physics inquiry-based curriculum (with or without reﬂection questio ns) were predicted to have a post FCI score that was 2 .60 ± 2.58 points higher."

### Danny Doucette 2022 (2)

Danny Doucette, Russell Clark, and Chandralekha Singh. (2022). Students' attitudes toward experimental physics in a conceptual inquiry-based introductory physics lab. [doi:10.1139/cjp-2021-0121](https://doi.org/10.1139/cjp-2021-0121)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mean FCI comparison reported alongside the model, out of a maximum score of 30: "an increase from 16 .7 ± 0.3 to 17 .8 ± 0.3" for RealTime Physics students, "compared with a decrease from 15 .5 ± 0.7 to 14 .8 ± 0.7" for traditional-lab students.

> "an increase from 16 .7 ± 0.3 to 17 .8 ± 0.3, on average, for students enrolled in labs that used the RealTime Physics conceptual inquiry-based curr iculum, compared with a decrease from 15 .5 ± 0.7 to 14 .8 ± 0.7"
