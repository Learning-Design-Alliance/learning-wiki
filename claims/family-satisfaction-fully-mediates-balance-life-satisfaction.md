# Family satisfaction fully mediates the relationship between school-family balance and life satisfaction among student parents

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The direct path from school-family balance to life satisfaction was non-significant (b = .16, SE = .09), while the indirect path through family satisfaction was significant (b = .61, SE = .11). [→ Tricia M. van Rhijn & Donna S. Lero 2014](#tricia-m-van-rhijn-donna-s-lero-2014)

## Evidence

### Tricia M. van Rhijn & Donna S. Lero 2014

Tricia M. van Rhijn & Donna S. Lero. (2014). The influence of self-efficacy beliefs for student parents attending university. International Journal of Lifelong Education. http://dx.doi.org/10.1080/02601370.2014.884178

`q2 · i?`

Bootstrapped mediation analysis in the same SEM of 398 student parents. The school-family balance total effect on life satisfaction (b = .81) split into a non-significant direct effect (b = .16, SE = .09) and a significant indirect effect via family satisfaction (b = .61, SE = .11); the school satisfaction indirect path was non-significant (b = .04, SE = .02).

> "The total effect of school-family balance (F3) on life satisfaction (F6) (b = .81) separated into a non-significant direct effect (b = .16, SE = .09) and the two hypothesized mediation pathways: (1) a significant indirect effect through the mediator, family satisfaction (F5) (b = .61, SE = .11); and (2) a non-significant indirect effect through the other mediator, school satisfaction (F4) (b = .04, SE = .02)."
