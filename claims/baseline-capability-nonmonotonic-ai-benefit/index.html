Evidence¶

Won Ik Cho 2026¶

Won Ik Cho, Seong-hun Kim, Geunhye Kim. (2026). Position: Adopting AI in Practice Does Not Guarantee the Productivity Boost. Proceedings of the 43rd International Conference on Machine Learning, PMLR 306. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24688

q1 · i? · theoretical · r3

The paper cites two field experiments as its empirical grounding: a customer-support field experiment reporting gains up to 36% for the lowest-skilled workers, and a randomized experiment with management consultants showing workers using AI on out-of-frontier tasks were "19 percentage pointsless likely to be accuratethan those without AI access." The paper itself reports no new data.

"Brynjolfsson et al. (2025) found productivity gains of up to 36% for the lowest-skilled workers, with minimal impact on the most experienced staff, while Dell'Acqua et al. (2023) demonstrated that for tasks outside AI's capability frontier, workers using AI performed 19 percentage pointsless likely to be accuratethan those without AI access."
