Evidence¶

Gwynne 2017¶

Gwynne, J.A., & Moore, P.T. (2017). Chicago's charter high schools: Organizational features, enrollment, school transfers, and student performance. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. http://cps.edu/NewSchools/Pages/RFP2017.aspx

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Chapter 4 transfer analysis of the primary sample of 103,506 first-time ninth-graders (2010-13) in 147 CPS high schools, 46 of them charters, using statistical models controlling for eighth-grade characteristics; charter transfers were 24.2 percent versus 17.2 percent.

"Among CPS students who entered high school in 2010-13 with typical eighth-grade skills, school experiences, and background charac- teristics, 24.2 percent of those who enrolled in a charter school in ninth grade changed schools at some point during the next three years, compared to only 17.2 per- cent of students who first enrolled in a non-charter high school."

Gwynne 2017 (2)¶

Gwynne, J.A., & Moore, P.T. (2017). Chicago's charter high schools: Organizational features, enrollment, school transfers, and student performance. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. http://cps.edu/NewSchools/Pages/RFP2017.aspx

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Chapter 4 finding that transfer likelihood held across achievement levels, addressing the counseling-out concern, with the highest transfer rates concentrated in low-performing or newly established charter schools.

"We found that both low- and high-achieving charter school students were more likely to change schools than non-charter students with comparable test scores. Transfer rates were highest for charter school students when they began high school in a low-performing or newly established charter school."
