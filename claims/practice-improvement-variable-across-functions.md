# Improvement during practice is highly variable across functions and stages, with final abilities in different functions correlating only about 0.4

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Final ability in one practiced function correlates about 0.4 with final ability in other functions, and improvement in one function does not generally indicate similar improvement in others. [→ Gates 1922](#gates-1922)

## Evidence

### Gates 1922

Gates, Arthur I. (1922). Educational psychology at the Princeton meeting of the American Psychological Association. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Educational_Psychology

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r?`

Conference report of G. S. Gates (Barnard) practice study in which subjects practiced a variety of functions; the report states improvement was "extremely variable" and uncorrelated across halves of the practice period. The printed correlation of about 0.4 (r = 0.4) between final abilities in different functions is reported as printed.

> "Final ability in one function gave correlations of about 0.4, with final ability in others, these correlations being considerably higher than correlations between initial scores in the several functions."
