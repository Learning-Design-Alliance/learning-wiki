# Treatment teachers significantly improved on all three video-analysis embedded assessment questions about interpreting a student's dilation approach

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2` · `i1` small
> **Currency** · newest study 2013 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Treatment teachers improved significantly on all three video analysis questions (effect size = 0.32, 0.25, 0.21), while the comparison group did not. [→ Seago 2013](#seago-2013)

## Evidence

### Seago 2013

Seago, N. (2013). Supporting Teachers' and Students' Knowledge of Geometric Similarity. Proceedings of the 35th annual meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED584497

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Wilcoxon Signed Ranks Tests on a video analysis task administered to 66 treatment teachers (data from one site removed because participants discussed the task beforehand) and 39 comparison teachers; effect size = 0.32, 0.25, and 0.21. "For the comparison group, the Wilcoxon Signed Ranks Test did not reveal significant changes for any of the questions."

> "for the treatment group, on average, there was a significant improvement in the overall scores for all three questions, suggesting improved understanding of mathematical similarity in relation to dilation (p < .05 for all questions). The effect sizes for these questions were 0.32, 0.25, and 0.21 respectively."
