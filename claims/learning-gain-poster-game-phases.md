## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Pretest to first posttest improvement is significant and large (t (69.96) = -7.91, p < .0001, d = 1.9). [→ Sharma Kshitij 2020](#sharma-kshitij-2020)
`q2 i?` First to second posttest improvement is not significant (t (69.88) = 0.39, p > .05, d = 0.09); average improvement was 2%. [→ Sharma Kshitij 2020 (2)](#sharma-kshitij-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Sharma Kshitij 2020

Sharma Kshitij, Leftheriotis Ioannis, Giannakos Michail. (2020). Utilizing Interactive Surfaces to Enhance Learning, Collaboration and Engagement: Insights from Learners’ Gaze and Speech. Sensors (Basel). https://doi.org/10.3390/s20071964

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Paired t-tests across the single-group time series design with 36 participants; the printed effect size for the improvement is "d = 1.9". A floor effect on the pretest (Mean = 0.2) meant learning gain was not used as a variable.

> "We observe a significant average improvement from the pretest score to the first posttest score (t (69.96) = −7.91, p < .0001, d = 1.9, Figure 7a)"

### Sharma Kshitij 2020 (2)

Sharma Kshitij, Leftheriotis Ioannis, Giannakos Michail. (2020). Utilizing Interactive Surfaces to Enhance Learning, Collaboration and Engagement: Insights from Learners’ Gaze and Speech. Sensors (Basel). https://doi.org/10.3390/s20071964

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Second paired t-test; the printed effect size is "d = 0.09", negligible. The authors note the first posttest mean of 0.70 left little room for improvement, and 18 participants did not improve.

> "Even though there is improvement from the first to the second posttest, the improvement is not significant (t (69.88) = 0.39, p > .05, d = 0.09 Figure 7)"
