Evidence¶

Sifatul Anindho 2026¶

Sifatul Anindho, Videep Venkatesha, Jaclyn Ocumpaugh, and Nathaniel Blanchard. (2026). Ordered Network Analysis of Epistemic Emotions during Collaborative Problem Solving. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23317 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.23317

q2 · i? · associational · r1

ONA of retrospective cued-recall affect reports from 9 groups completing a situated CPS task shows a "stable epistemic core which links curiosity, optimism and confusion" with the strongest ordered connections and self-transitions, indicating short-range persistence rather than frequent switching.

"Across ONA models, we observe a stable epistemic core which links curiosity, optimism and confusion. These states were consistently involved in the strongest ordered connections, including self-transitions, indicating that aﬀective experience during collaboration is often characterized by short-range persistence rather than frequent switching."

Related Claims¶

  • Curious, optimistic, and confused are the most frequently reported affective states, and self-caught reports are more evenly distributed than probe-caught reports — related
  • Faster CPS groups show stronger transitions among curiosity and optimism, while slower groups show stronger transitions among confusion, conflict, and frustration — related
  • Probe-caught affect reports show stronger self-loops among core epistemic states, while self-caught reports show more distributed transitions involving higher-arousal states — related
  • Emotion dynamics during problem-solving predict learning outcomes in a manner that depends on scaffolding design — related
  • In over 60% of inquiry threads of low-achieving students, curiosity was followed by confusion, and joy or confusion by curiosity (sequential patterns, not shown causes) — related
  • Most variance in students' epistemic emotions was situational (within-person) fluctuation, with 18–37% due to between-person variability. — related
  • Situational motivation is highly context-dependent and fluctuates within and between learners, but most studies rely on self-reports — related
  • Curiosity gains depend on operator sequencing: how operators are ordered across turns, not only which operator is applied, shapes curiosity induction — related
  • Low-achieving students' emotions in Knowledge Building threads tended to transition to the same or similar emotions — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Curiosity is a special case of interest, with the two constructs overlapping in a part-whole rather than mutually exclusive relationship — related
  • A UK-wide expert panel (n=30) conceptualised transitions-context emotional wellbeing as affective state, tangible signs of adjustment, and mechanisms to maintain a stable affective state — related
  • Curiosity and situational interest differ on five dimensions: theoretical account, biology, triggers, affect, and information search — related
  • Confusion+frustration and curiosity+engagement were the only affective state pairs co-occurring above chance, and within each pair one state was more likely to imply the other — related
  • Five of six predicted affect transitions were significant and aligned with the affect dynamics model, but boredom transitioned to engagement rather than to frustration — related
  • Low-learning groups tended to make stronger connections from features associated with confusion and uncertainty to individual exploration and inactivity, while high-learning groups exhibited stronger connections between features related to knowledge construction. — related
  • Educators prioritize a creative mindset including creative problem-solving, diversity of perspectives, and playful inquiry — related
  • Physiological research on academic emotion constructs is sparse: no studies of normal confusion, one of frustration, and one of curiosity — related
