## Evidence

### Koch 2008

Koch, S. C., Müller, S. M., & Sieverding, M. (2008). Women and computers. Effects of stereotype threat on attribution of failure. Computers & Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2008.05.007

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive comparisons among the sampled students showed men reported "a signiﬁcantly higher computer self-efﬁcacy", "identiﬁed themselves more with the computer do- main", and "scored higher in the computer knowledge test (PRACOWI) than women did" (all p < 0.01).

> "Men reported a signiﬁcantly higher computer self-efﬁcacy (t(95) = /C04.94, p < 0.01; MM = 3.8, SDM = 0.50; MW = 3.60, SDW = 0.40), identiﬁed themselves more with the computer do- main ( t(95) = /C04.41, p < 0.01; MM = 3.40, SDM = 0.50; MW = 2.70, SDW = 0.70), and scored higher in the computer knowledge test (PRACOWI) than women did ( t(95) = /C05.00, p < 0.01; MM = 4.3, SDM = 1.6; MW = 2.8, SDW = 1.4)."
