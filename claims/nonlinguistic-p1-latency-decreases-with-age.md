# P1 latency for nonlinguistic auditory probes decreases with age from childhood through adulthood

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Nonlinguistic-probe P1 latency was shorter in older age groups, with adults significantly shorter than 13- and 10-year-olds but not 16-year-olds. [→ Karns Christina M. 2015](#karns-christina-m-2015)

## Evidence

### Karns Christina M. 2015

Karns Christina M., Isbell Elif, Giuliano Ryan J., Neville Helen J. (2015). Auditory attention in childhood and adolescence: An event-related potential study of spatial selective attention to one of two simultaneous stories. Developmental cognitive neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2015.03.001

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Cross-sectional ERP study of five age groups (twenty 3–5-year-olds, nineteen 10-year-olds, eighteen 13-year-olds, eighteen 16-year-olds, twenty adults) performing a dichotic story-listening task. The Age-group ANOVA on nonlinguistic P1 latency was significant, "F(3,71) = 52.4, p < .001", with latencies shortening from 114 ms at age 10 to 83 ms in adults.

> "P1 latency was shorter in older age groups (Figure 3: Main effect of Age group: F(3,71) = 52.4, p < .001) with average latencies at 114 ms in 10-year-olds, 91 ms in 13-year-olds, 85 ms in 16-year-olds, and 83 ms in adults."
