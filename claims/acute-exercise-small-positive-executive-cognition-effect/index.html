Basso Julia C. 2017¶

Basso Julia C., Suzuki Wendy A. (2017). The Effects of Acute Exercise on Mood, Cognition, Neurophysiology, and Neurochemical Pathways: A Review. Brain Plasticity. https://doi.org/10.3233/BPL-160040

q2 · i? · review · r3

Narrative review synthesis of narrative and meta-analytic reviews of acute exercise effects on human cognition. The review reports the executive-function benefit and two-hour duration, and notes individual studies range from strongly positive to detrimental. Quotes the fragment "receive the most benefit from acute exercise".

"executive functions including attention, working memory, problem solving, cognitive flexibility, verbal fluency, decision making, and inhibitory control receive the most benefit from acute exercise [9], with effects lasting for up to two hours post-exercise cessation"

Related Claims¶

  • Aerobic exercise improves executive functioning, most notably inhibitory control, in typically developing children, though evidence is tentative and one week-long study found no main effect — related
  • Exercise intensity differentially moderates acute exercise cognitive benefits, with moderate intensity favoring executive functions and high intensity favoring information processing — related
  • Acute cardio exercise improves executive functions, response time, and academic performance in children with ADHD — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • The timing of acute exercise relative to learning modulates memory consolidation — related
  • A 10-minute bout of moderate-intensity exercise improves Stroop inhibitory control with increased left dorsolateral prefrontal activation in young adults — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Adults with ADHD who frequently engage in physical exercise report healthier psychological well-being than those who do not — related
  • The effects of physical activity on cognition and academic outcomes depend on the subdomain examined — related
  • Acute exercise significantly improves cognitive flexibility in children and adolescents with a small effect size (SMD = −0.34) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Acute exercise significantly improves inhibitory control in children and adolescents with a small effect size (SMD = −0.25) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • In adults with ADHD, acute cardio exercise does not improve sustained attention but enhances mood and motivation — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Acute exercise significantly improves working memory in children and adolescents with a moderate effect size (SMD = −0.72) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Acute exercise alleviates negative affect, enhances positive affect, and reduces stress responses, with mood effects lasting up to 24 hours — related
  • Acute moderate aerobic exercise speeds children's reaction times without improving accuracy, with greater speed gains on choice than simple reaction time tasks — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Non-cardio exercise (yoga, tai chi, walking) shows predominantly positive but questionable acute and chronic effects in children with ADHD — related
  • Physical exercise has a significant modest positive effect on functional outcomes (executive functions and motor skills) in children with ADHD — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Reported benefits of physical activity programs on executive functions depend on program duration and the executive domain analyzed — related
  • Metacognition interventions positively impact children's executive functions, a novel outcome in this literature — related
  • Chronic cardio exercise programs improve executive functions, attention, and behavior in children with ADHD — related
  • Chronic exercise significantly improves working memory, inhibitory control, and cognitive flexibility in children and adolescents — related
  • Null Flanker effects under ecologically realistic exercise constrain claims of broad, generalized enhancement of executive control — reports the opposite
  • Physical activity interventions in childhood show no overall benefit for executive functions or academic achievement — related
  • Including behavior change techniques in acute physical activity interventions is the only moderator associated with larger academic effects (g = 0.54 vs 0.23) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
