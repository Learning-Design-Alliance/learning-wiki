## Evidence

### Griffin Pitts 2026

Griffin Pitts, Neha Rani, and Weedguet Mildort. (2026). Trust and Reliance on AI in Education: AI Literacy and Need for Cognition as Moderators. Preprint, accepted to AIED 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01114 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2604.01114](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2604.01114)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Moderation regression in the Results section (Table 2) with standardized trust and AI literacy among 432 participants; the interaction coefficient was b = -1.03, p = .014. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "The trust×AI literacy interaction was negative (b=−1.03, p=.014), meaning the estimated trust slope became more negative as AI literacy increased."

### Griffin Pitts 2026 (2)

Griffin Pitts, Neha Rani, and Weedguet Mildort. (2026). Trust and Reliance on AI in Education: AI Literacy and Need for Cognition as Moderators. Preprint, accepted to AIED 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01114

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The Results section describes the interaction pattern plotted in Figure 4a with 95% confidence bands; conditional slopes were approximately -3.59, -4.62, and -5.65 at -1 SD, the mean, and +1 SD AI literacy. No effect size is printed.

> "At lower trust levels, higher AI literacy corresponded to higher predicted appropriate reliance, while this difference narrowed and slightly reversed at higher trust levels due to the steeper negative trust slope at higher AI literacy."
