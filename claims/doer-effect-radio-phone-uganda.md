# A Doer Effect is observed in the non-WEIRD sample: practice remains significantly related to exam scores while accounting for prior radio listening, whereas prior listening alone is not

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Doing (active learning) is significantly related to higher exam scores when accounting for prior listening (standardized coefficient = 0.42), while prior listening before practice alone does not significantly relate to exam scores. [→ Darren Butler 2025](#darren-butler-2025)

## Evidence

### Darren Butler 2025

Darren Butler, Conrad Borchers, Michael W. Asher, Yongmin Lee, Sonya Karnataki, Sameeksha Dangi, Samyukta Athreya, John Stamper, Amy Ogan, and Paulo F. Carvalho. 2025. Does the Doer Effect Exist Beyond WEIRD Populations? Toward Analytics in Radio and Phone-Based Learning. In LAK25: The 15th International Learning Analytics and Knowledge Conference (LAK 2025). ACM. https://doi.org/10.1145/3706468.3706505

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r1`

Linear regression of exam score on doing and prior listening (Table 3) in the N = 234 Ugandan learner dataset. The printed standardized effect size for doing is 0.42 (CI95 [0.26 – 0.50]); prior listening had an estimate of −0.02 [−0.14 – 0.10], p = .686; the model's R2 was 0.142 (adjusted 0.130). This is a conceptual replication of the Doer Effect with basic phone and radio technologies.

> "doing remained significantly related to higher exam scores while accounting for listening,𝐶𝐼 95% = 0.42,𝐶𝐼 95% [0.26 – 0.50], p <0.001. However, prior listening before practice alone did not relate significantly to exam score (𝑝′𝑠 > .05). Thus, we observe a Doer Effect."
