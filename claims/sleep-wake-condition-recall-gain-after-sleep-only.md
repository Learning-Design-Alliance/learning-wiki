## Evidence

### Backhaus et al. 2008

Jutta Backhaus, Ralf Hoeckesfeld, Jan Born, Fritz Hohagen, Klaus Junghanns. (2008). Immediate as well as delayed post learning sleep but not wakefulness enhances declarative memory consolidation in children. Neurobiology of Learning and Memory 89, 76-80. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2007.08.010

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within the Sleep-Wake condition of the 27-child experiment, paired t-tests showed a significant learning-to-first-retrieval gain after sleep (t = 6.0, p < 0.001) and no further gain after the wake interval (t = 0.27, p = 0.78). Means were 28.7 at criterion, 32.2 after sleep, 32.3 after wake.

> "in the Sleep–Wake condition, children showed a signiﬁcant increase in the number of recalled word pairs from learning to the ﬁrst retrieval after the sleep retention interval, whereas no further increase in recall performance was revealed at the second testing after the wake interval (F2,25 = 17.8, p < 0.001, t-test pairs: learning—ﬁrst retrieval: t = 6.0, p < 0.001"
