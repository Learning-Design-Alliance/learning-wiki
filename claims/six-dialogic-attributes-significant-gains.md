# The intervention significantly increased teachers' noticing of six dialogic attributes — persuasive interaction, one leads and another is led, agreement and collective attention, students have equal status, dialogic gap, and monologue — but not of dialogue-expansion attributes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Significant changes after the intervention occurred in persuasive interaction, one leads and another is led, agreement and collective attention, dialogic gap, monologue, and students with equal status (all p < 0.05 in Table 2). [→ Benzi Slakmon 2024](#benzi-slakmon-2024)
`q2 i?` No significant changes were found for dialogue-expansion attributes (deepening and widening the dialogue). [→ Benzi Slakmon 2024](#benzi-slakmon-2024)

## Evidence

### Benzi Slakmon 2024

Benzi Slakmon, Rotem Abdu. (2024). Learning to notice collaboration: examining the impact of professional development on mathematics teachers’ enhanced awareness in CSCL settings. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn 19:137–155. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-024-09423-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Series of two-tailed paired-samples t-tests per all 11 dialogic attributes across 14 teachers, with per-attribute pre/post utterance counts in Table 2 (e.g., dialogic gap 12 to 44, t = −4.02). The dialogue-expansion attributes showed no significant change, per the article's own statement about few related utterances.

> "Beyond these significant changes in listening to dialogue, responding to dialogue, and total utterances after the intervention, we observed significant changes after the intervention in persuasive interaction, one leads and another is led, agreement and joint action, dialogic gap, monologue, and students with equal status (Table 2)."
