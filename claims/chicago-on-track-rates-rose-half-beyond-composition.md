# On-track rates after ninth grade rose from 42.2 to 50.6 percent, with slightly less than half the adjusted improvement attributable to better-prepared entering students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The percent of high school eligible students on track after their first year rose from 42.2 percent in 1993–94 to 50.6 percent in 1999–00; after adjusting for student composition the increase was 5.4 percentage points, suggesting slightly less than half the improvement resulted from better-prepared students. ## Evidence

### Shazia Rafiullah Miller 2002

Shazia Rafiullah Miller, Elaine M. Allensworth, Julie Reed Kochanek. (2002). Student Performance: Course Taking, Test Scores, and Outcomes — The State of Chicago Public High Schools: 1993 to 2000. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/student-performance-course-taking-test-scores-and-outcomes

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Cohort trend analysis of Chicago Public Schools administrative data tracking all high school eligible students one year after elementary school, adjusted for race/ethnicity, gender, SES, special education, age, and eighth-grade ITBS. The report finds "5.4 percent more students were on track" after adjustment, with more than half the gain due to other factors.

> "After adjusting for changes in student composition, 5.4 percent more students were on track (from 42.2 percent in 1993–94 to 47.6 percent in 1999–00). This suggests that slightly less than half of the improvement in on-track rates is the result of better-prepared students entering high schools; more than half is due to other factors ."
