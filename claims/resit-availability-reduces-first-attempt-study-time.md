## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants invested less fictional study time preparing for a first exam (R1) when a resit was available compared to a no-resit condition, with decisive Bayesian evidence. [→ Nijenkamp 2016](#nijenkamp-2016)

## Evidence

### Nijenkamp 2016

Nijenkamp, R., Nieuwenstein, M. R., de Jong, R., & Lorist, M. M. (2016). Do Resit Exams Promote Lower Investments of Study Time? Theory and Data from a Laboratory Study. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0161708

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1, a within-subjects laboratory investment task with 50 first-year psychology students investing fictional study time for a fictional 60-item multiple-choice exam. Participants invested less time for R1 (M = 5.2, SD = 0.7) than for NR (M = 6.2, SD = 0.5), with "decisive evidence in favor of our main prediction" (BF = 3.7 × 10^13 for NR > R1).

> "the results produced decisive evidence in favor of our main prediction that participants would invest less study time in preparing for an exam when a resit was available"
