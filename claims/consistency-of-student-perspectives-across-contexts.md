## Evidence

### Charles Baily and Noah D. Finkelstein 2009

Charles Baily and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2009). Teaching and understanding of quantum interpretations in modern physics courses. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.6.010101

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Cross-tabulation of responses from both courses to the double-slit essay question and the atomic-electron statement (Fig. 8). The article reports "about 70%" of realist essay responders also responded consistently with a realist perspective on atomic electrons.

> "about 70% of the students who preferred a realist interpretation in the essay question also chose a response to the statement on atomic electrons that would be consistent with a realist perspective."

### Charles Baily and Noah D. Finkelstein 2009 (2)

Charles Baily and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2009). Teaching and understanding of quantum interpretations in modern physics courses. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.6.010101

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same cross-tabulation: among students who preferred the quantum interpretation in the essay question, the article reports 46% still agreed an electron in an atom has a definite position. Only agnostic-preferring students showed majority disagreement.

> "46% of these students still agreed that an electron in an atom has a definite position at all times."
