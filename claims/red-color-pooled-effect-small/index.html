Evidence¶

Gnambs Timo 2020¶

Gnambs Timo. (2020). Limited evidence for the effect of red color on cognitive performance: A meta-analysis. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-020-01772-1

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Random-effects meta-analysis (metafor, restricted maximum likelihood) of 67 effect sizes from 38 independent samples contrasting red with a control color; the pooled effect was "only a small, albeit significant (p = .011), difference between colors."

"Even after acknowledging sampling error the pooled effect of Δ = -0.13, 95% CI [-0.23, -0.03] revealed only a small, albeit significant (p = .011), difference between colors."

Related Claims¶

  • Control color choice, color matching, presentation medium, and presentation timing showed no moderating effects on red color effects
  • Red color effects declined over time: initial studies found substantially larger effects than subsequent research — related
  • No significant difference between red and control color conditions for anagram tests or knowledge tests — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • After correcting for publication bias, no evidential value remains for an effect of red color on intellectual performance — reports the opposite
  • Reasoning test performance was significantly lower in the red condition (Δ = -0.34), but the omnibus test across cognitive measures was not significant — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Red color effects, if they exist, may be limited to low-stakes laboratory research and may not generalize to applied testing settings — related
