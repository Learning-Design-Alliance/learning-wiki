# Many students do not realize degenerate perturbation theory should be used, and memorization of which basis is good masks lack of deep understanding of DPT

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When asked to determine a good basis for fine-structure corrections, many students did not realize DPT should be used, and others incorrectly claimed DPT was not needed because no terms in the first-order energy correction blow up. [→ Emily Marshman 2020](#emily-marshman-2020)
`q2 i?` Students who realized DPT was needed often admitted they had memorized which representation is a good basis, masking their lack of deep understanding. [→ Emily Marshman 2020](#emily-marshman-2020)

## Evidence

### Emily Marshman 2020

Emily Marshman, Christof Keebaugh, and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). Student difﬁculties with ﬁnding the corrections to the energy spectrum of the hydrogen atom for the strong and weak ﬁeld Zeeman effects using degenerate perturbation theory. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2006.07144](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2006.07144)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Written-question and interview data from advanced QM courses: two years of responses from 52 upper-level undergraduates and 42 first-year graduate students, plus 13 individual think-aloud interviews (45 hours). "many students did not even realize that DPT should be used".

> "many students did not even realize that DPT should be used. Other students knew that they had to use DPT to ﬁnd corrections to the wavefunction, but they did not use DPT to ﬁnd the ﬁrst-order corrections to the energies"
