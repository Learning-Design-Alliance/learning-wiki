# AI-assisted performance during skill-building does not transfer to subsequent unassisted performance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Experimental evidence shows participants performed significantly better with AI assistance during the access period, but this advantage did not carry over to later unassisted assessment; the article treats this dissociation as empirically established. [→ Lovett 2026](#lovett-2026)

## Evidence

### Lovett 2026

Lovett, N. (2026). The tragedy of the cognitive commons: How AI could disrupt the regeneration of professional expertise. Human Resource Development Review. Advance online publication. https://doi.org/10.1177/15344843261470602

`q3 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article cites Wiles et al.'s (2024) experimental evidence on AI assistance during a skill-building task. Assisted performance gains did not transfer to unassisted contexts; a companion citation reports no significant advantage on unassisted assessment after AI-assisted training. No effect size is printed.

> "when AI assistance was provided during a skill-building task, participants performed significantly better during the access period, but this performance advantage did not transfer to subsequent unassisted performance"
