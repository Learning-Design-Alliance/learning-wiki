Evidence¶

Femke van der Wilt 2019¶

Femke van der Wilt, Chiel van der Veen, Claudia van Kruistum, Bert van Oers (2019). Why Do Children Become Rejected by Their Peers? A Review of Studies into the Relationship Between Oral Communicative Competence and Sociometric Status in Childhood. Educational Psychology Review (2019) 31:699–724. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09479-z

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, from van der Wilt et al. (2018b) (N = 570 children aged 3.99–7.08, Nijmegen Test for Pragmatics, comparative analyses, cross-sectional design, five sociometric groups via peer nomination), the group differences quoted here. No effect size is printed.

"Children who were rejected or neglected by their peers exhibited lower levels of oral communicative competence than average children; popular and controversial children did not differ from average children in their level of oral communicative competence."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Oral communicative competence relates to peer acceptance only for boys and not to peer rejection, indicating gender as a moderator
  • Children's oral communicative competence is significantly related to their sociometric status in childhood — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Children with poorer oral communicative competence experience higher levels of peer rejection after controlling for gender, age, and SES — related
  • The relation between oral communicative competence and sociometric status is expected to be interactional and transactional rather than unidirectional — related
