# Collaborative dyadic learning in the self-study phase increases students' motivation and interest in the topic

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In self-study phase 2 the collaborative group outscored the basic group on every motivation/interest measure and was more motivated to learn than the individual group (p < 0.001). [→ Kühl Susanne J. 2019](#kuhl-susanne-j-2019)

## Evidence

### Kühl Susanne J. 2019

Kühl Susanne J., Schneider Achim, Kestler Hans A., Toberer Matthias, Kühl Michael, Fischer Martin R. (2019). Investigating the self-study phase of an inverted biochemistry classroom – collaborative dyadic learning makes the difference. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1497-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Likert-scale questionnaires (1–6) on motivation, relevance recognition and interest administered at the start of on-site phases, compared across the three groups with ANOVAs and Tukey-HSD; only p-values are printed, no effect sizes.

> "In self-study phase 2, the collaborative group scored higher than the basic group in every respect (all p < 0.05), were more motivated to learn than the individual group (p < 0.001) and received a higher score in terms of an increased interest through the self-study phase 2 (p = 0.016)."
