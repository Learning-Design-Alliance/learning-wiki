# Arrows did not guide initial reading behavior in matrix diagrams with two nominal headers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In diagrams with arrows pointing left to right, 51.2% of participants' initial reading sequence was left to right, and in diagrams with top-down arrows, 61.5% was top-down, a non-significant difference. [→ Jan van der Meij 2017](#jan-van-der-meij-2017)

## Evidence

### Jan van der Meij 2017

Jan van der Meij, Marije van Amelsvoort, Anjo Anjewierden. (2017). How design guides learning from matrix diagrams. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-017-9425-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Eye-tracking analysis of initial body-cell sequences among 81 university students randomly assigned to four diagram conditions. The article reports the arrows "did not inﬂuence initial reading behavior" with χ²(1) = 1.31, p = 0.252, φ = 0.067.

> "in the diagrams in which arrows pointed from left to right, 51.2% of all participants' initial reading sequence was also from left to right, and in the diagrams in which arrows pointed top–down, 61.5% of all participants' initial reading sequence was also top–down,v2(1) = 1.31, p = 0.252, k = 0.067."
