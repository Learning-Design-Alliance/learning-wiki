# Schooling has a compensatory effect on inequality in reading, language, and science skills

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Seasonal comparisons of national assessment data indicate that skill inequality in reading, language use, and science grows more slowly during school years than summers, consistent with a compensatory effect of schooling. [→ Dennis Condron 2021](#dennis-condron-2021)

## Evidence

### Dennis Condron 2021

Dennis Condron, Douglas Downery, Megan Kuhfeld. (2021). Schools as refractors: Change in variance in children's cognitive skills change while in school versus out. Sociology of Education, 94(4), 316-340. https://journals.sagepub.com/home/soe

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

The study analyzes recent national data on U.S. public school students from the NWEA MAP Growth assessment using seasonal comparisons across numerous grade levels; the abstract reports that "schooling has a compensatory effect on inequality in reading, language, and science skills". No effect size is printed in the available text.

> "Our results suggest that schooling has a compensatory effect on inequality in reading, language, and science skills but not math skills."
