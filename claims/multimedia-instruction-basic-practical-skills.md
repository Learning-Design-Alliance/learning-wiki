# Multimedia instruction improves basic practical skills learning when used in the right setting, with simpler interactivity outperforming complex interaction

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

> **Currency** · newest study 2016 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Learning cardiac auscultation with a CD-ROM in addition to usual clinic rotation significantly improved auscultation skills, with improvement lasting up to one year after the intervention. [→ Vogel Daniela 2016](#vogel-daniela-2016)
`q2 i?` For an interactive abdominal examination program, students using the simpler click-version performed better than students using the drag-version of the same program. [→ Vogel Daniela 2016 (2)](#vogel-daniela-2016-2)
`q2 i?` For suturing, working individually with an interactive video was associated with better performance than working in student tandems with the same video, though this pattern cannot be generalized to more complex tasks. [→ Vogel Daniela 2016 (3)](#vogel-daniela-2016-3)

## Evidence

### Vogel Daniela 2016

Vogel Daniela, Harendza Sigrid. (2016). Basic practical skills teaching and learning in undergraduate medical education – a review on methodological evidence. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001063](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001063)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Review-reported controlled study (ref 52) on multimedia-supported auscultation learning during clinical rotation, with durable one-year improvement.

> "When cardiac auscultation was learned with a CD-ROM in addition to the usual clinic rotation, auscultation skills significantly improved and this improvement lasted even until one year after the intervention"

### Vogel Daniela 2016 (2)

Vogel Daniela, Harendza Sigrid. (2016). Basic practical skills teaching and learning in undergraduate medical education – a review on methodological evidence. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001063](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001063)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Review-reported controlled comparison (ref 21) of two interaction modes of the same program; the discussion attributes the difference to possible cognitive overload generated by dragging items.

> "Students who learned with the “click-version” of an interactive program of abdominal exam performed better than students who worked with the “drag-version” of the same program."

### Vogel Daniela 2016 (3)

Vogel Daniela, Harendza Sigrid. (2016). Basic practical skills teaching and learning in undergraduate medical education – a review on methodological evidence. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001063](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001063)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Review-reported controlled study (ref 45) comparing individual versus tandem work with the same interactive suturing video.

> "Working alone with an interactive video on suturing skills was associated with better performance than working in student tandems with the same video"
