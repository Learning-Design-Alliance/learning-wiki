## Evidence

### Eric M. Hickok and Cassandra A. Paul 2025

Eric M. Hickok and Cassandra A. Paul. (2025). Examining teaching assistant pedagogies in traditional laboratories and recitations. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2509.09136](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2509.09136)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Figure 4 analysis plotting interaction time against percent of interaction time spent in dialogue with, listening to, or observing students, comparing SJSU lab and recitation observations with West et al.'s CLASP data. Lab and recitation TAs also spent on average much more of their interaction time explaining content.

> "all the CLASP TAs interact with their students more than 70% of class time, while the TAs teaching the labs and recitations interact with their students anywhere from 10% to 95% of class time."

### Eric M. Hickok and Cassandra A. Paul 2025 (2)

Eric M. Hickok and Cassandra A. Paul. (2025). Examining teaching assistant pedagogies in traditional laboratories and recitations. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2509.09136](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2509.09136)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Figure 5 sensitivity analysis re-plotting interactivity without counting RIOT observing codes as interaction. Removing observing produced "much more overlap between the CLASP curriculum and general Recitations", leading the authors to conclude observation is a major distinguishing factor between the settings.

> "Indeed, it becomes much more difficult to differentiate CLASP from the majority of our recitation observations. We conclude then thatObservationremains an important interaction to monitor between instructors and students, as it seems to be a major distinguishing factor between Recitation sections and CLASP sections."
