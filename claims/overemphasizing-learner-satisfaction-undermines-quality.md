## Evidence

### Patel Hitesh H.; Lin Erica 2025

Patel Hitesh H.; Lin Erica. (2025). Leaning into Necessary Discomforts: Striving for Clinical Excellence While Maintaining Wellness. ATS Scholar. https://doi.org/10.34197/ats-scholar.2025-0033PS

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The perspective cites evidence from other fields that "prioritizing patient satisfaction over clinical outcomes has been linked to increased adverse events" and that excessive focus on student satisfaction is linked to reduced academic rigor; no effect sizes are printed.

> "Evidence from other fields supports this concern: in healthcare, prioritizing patient satisfaction over clinical outcomes has been linked to increased adverse events (47), while in higher education, excessive focus on student satisfaction has been linked to reduced academic rigor (48)."

### Patel Hitesh H.; Lin Erica 2025 (2)

Patel Hitesh H.; Lin Erica. (2025). Leaning into Necessary Discomforts: Striving for Clinical Excellence While Maintaining Wellness. ATS Scholar. https://doi.org/10.34197/ats-scholar.2025-0033PS

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The perspective reports, citing prior studies, that faculty "often fear that providing honest, constructive evaluations may lead to retaliatory negative evaluations from learners," compounded because faculty evaluations affect promotion and job security.

> "Faculty members often fear that providing honest, constructive evaluations may lead to retaliatory negative evaluations from learners (39–41)."
