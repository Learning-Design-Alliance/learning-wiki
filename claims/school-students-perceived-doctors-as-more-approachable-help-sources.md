## Evidence

### Cahill Helen 2015

Cahill Helen, Coffey Julia, Sanci Lena. (2015). ‘I wouldn’t get that feedback from anywhere else’: learning partnerships and the use of high school students as simulated patients to enhance medical students’ communication skills. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0315-4

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Post-workshop survey completed by 66 school students, rated out of 10 with means and standard deviations computed in SPSS. The article reports the top overall outcome item as "Increase your confidence that doctors may be useful" at mean 8.13. Descriptive only.

> "The highest rated ‘overall outcome’ was the item ‘Increase your confidence that doctors may be useful when young people have personal health problems’ (mean 8.13)."

### Cahill Helen 2015 (2)

Cahill Helen, Coffey Julia, Sanci Lena. (2015). ‘I wouldn’t get that feedback from anywhere else’: learning partnerships and the use of high school students as simulated patients to enhance medical students’ communication skills. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0315-4

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of 12 post-workshop focus groups with 69 school students aged 15–16; one student said "Now we know they are just there to help us; it’s not like they’re going to judge you". Qualitative, no effect sizes.

> "In focus groups following the workshops, school students said the experience made doctors seem more ‘human’ and more approachable, and thus became more likely sources of help."
