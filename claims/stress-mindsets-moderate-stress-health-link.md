# Stress mindsets moderate the link between perceived distress and health, with enhancing mindsets buffering but not eliminating the negative effect

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The effect of perceived distress on mental health varied as a function of general and acute uncontrollable stress mindsets, and was stronger for participants with more debilitating mindsets. [→ Jenkins Anna 2021](#jenkins-anna-2021)

## Evidence

### Jenkins Anna 2021

Jenkins Anna, Weeks Molly S., Hard Bridgette Martin. (2021). General and specific stress mindsets: Links with college student health and academic performance. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0256351

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Hierarchical regressions with mindset × stress interactions (Table 4, Table 5, Fig 1) in the 498-participant study; simple slopes probed at the 20th, 50th, and 80th percentiles. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "The effect of perceived distress on mental health varied as a function of general and acute uncontrollable stress mindsets (see Fig 1). In each case of moderation, the effect of perceived distress on health was significant at each level of mindset, but the effect was stronger for participants at the 20th percentile (i.e., for participants with more debilitating mindsets)."

### Jenkins Anna 2021 (2)

Jenkins Anna, Weeks Molly S., Hard Bridgette Martin. (2021). General and specific stress mindsets: Links with college student health and academic performance. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0256351

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression results for general self-reported poor health (Fig 2, Table 5); the article notes this direction contrasts with the other health measures. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "the effect of perceived distress did vary as a function of mindset, for general stress mindsets only (Fig 2). The effect of perceived distress on self-reported health was significant at each level of mindset, but, in contrast to other measures of health, the effect was strongest at the 80th percentile (i.e., for participants with more enhancing mindsets)."

### Jenkins Anna 2021 (3)

Jenkins Anna, Weeks Molly S., Hard Bridgette Martin. (2021). General and specific stress mindsets: Links with college student health and academic performance. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0256351

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Table 5 reports significant life-events × mindset interactions for general, acute uncontrollable, and chronic controllable mindsets predicting days health interfered with normal activity; at the 80th percentile the general and chronic controllable simple slopes were not significantly different from zero.

> "Predicting Days Health Interfered with Normal Activity from History of Stressful Life Events"
