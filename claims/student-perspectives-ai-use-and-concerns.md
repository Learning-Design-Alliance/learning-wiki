### Favero 2026

Favero, L., Pérez-Ortiz, J.A., Käser, T., Oliver, N. (2026). AI in Education Beyond Learning Outcomes: Cognition, Agency, Emotion, and Ethics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2602.04598](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.04598)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The review reports an OpenAI survey of 1,200 US students aged 18–24, in which AI tools were most often used for initiating papers and projects (49%), summarizing texts (48%), brainstorming (45%), topic exploration (44%), and revising writing (44%).

> "In a survey of 1,200 students in this age group, AI tools were most of-ten used for initiating papers and projects (49%), summarizing texts (48%), brainstorming (45%), topic exploration (44%), and revising writing (44%)."

### Favero 2026 (2)

Favero, L., Pérez-Ortiz, J.A., Käser, T., Oliver, N. (2026). AI in Education Beyond Learning Outcomes: Cognition, Agency, Emotion, and Ethics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2602.04598](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.04598)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r1`

The review reports, citing a 2024 international survey of 4,000 university students in 16 countries [7], that 61% worry about data privacy and many question AI reliability and the academic risks of overreliance. It adds that learners with stronger prior academic preparation or digital literacy are generally better positioned to question and contextualize AI outputs.

> "61% worry about data privacy, while many question the reliability of AI-generated content and the academic risks of overreliance."
