# E-M scoring partially accounts for observed RIT score differences between disengaged first tests and engaged retests

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among students retested within one day who showed disengagement (10%+ rapid guesses) on the first test and zero rapid guesses on the retest, E-M scores partially accounted for the observed RIT score differences. [→ study1](#study1)

## Evidence

### study1

Wise, S., & Kuhfeld, M. (2020, September). Using retest data to evaluate and improve effort-moderated scoring. National Council on Measurement in Education virtual conference. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/using-retest-data-to-evaluate-and-improve-effort-moderated-scoring-presentation/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Study 1, an archival analysis of MAP Growth RIT-score data from students retested within a short period, identified "over 5,000 cases" of disengagement on first testing with zero rapid guesses on retest; Table 1 showed E-M scores "partially accounted for the observed RIT score differences."

> "Within this group, over 5,000 cases were found in which students exhibited disengagement (10%+ rapid guesses) on the first testing and zero rapid guesses on the retest. Table 1 shows that E-M scores partially accounted for the observed RIT score differences."
