## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among all participants, perceived CS instrument knowledge and suturing skills improved significantly after simulation, while knowledge of anatomical layers and comfort performing a CS did not reach significance. [→ Acosta Tatiana 2020](#acosta-tatiana-2020)

## Evidence

### Acosta Tatiana 2020

Acosta Tatiana, Sutton Jill Marie, Dotters-Katz Sarah. (2020). Improving Learners' Comfort With Cesarean Sections Through the Use of High-Fidelity, Low-Cost Simulation. MedEdPORTAL. https://doi.org/10.15766/mep_2374-8265.10878

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre/post survey study of a single simulation event at East Carolina University; survey responses from 21 learners analyzed with a paired t test (5-point scale). Instrument knowledge mean difference 0.7 (0.2 to 1.3), p = .01; suturing skills 0.9 (0.1 to 1.7), p = .02. "We found a statistically significant improvement in perceived CS instrument knowledge, suturing skills, and satisfaction with the model among all participants."
