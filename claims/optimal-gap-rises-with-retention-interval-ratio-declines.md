# The optimal gap increases as the retention interval increases, but the ratio of optimal gap to retention interval declines

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For RIs of 7, 35, 70, and 350 days, the optimal gaps for recall were 1, 11, 21, and 21 days respectively. [→ Cepeda et al. 2008](#cepeda-et-al-2008)
`q3 i?` The ratio of optimal gap to RI declines as RI increases; for RI = 350 the optimal gap of 23 days is about 7% of the RI, versus roughly 20% for delays of a few weeks. [→ Cepeda et al. 2008](#cepeda-et-al-2008)

## Evidence

### Cepeda et al. 2008

Nicholas J. Cepeda, Edward Vul, Doug Rohrer, John T. Wixted, and Harold Pashler. (2008). Spacing effects in learning: A temporal ridgeline of optimal retention. Psychological Science, 19(11). https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Results of the 26-condition internet spacing experiment: estimated optimal gap values rise as retention interval is increased, and the article states these "depart noticeably from the fixed ratio of retention interval suggested by some earlier researchers". The fitted surface gives an optimal gap of 23 days for RI = 350, "just 7% of the RI".

> "For the RIs of 7, 35, 70, and 350 days, the optimal gaps (of those included in the study) were 1, 11, 21, and 21 days, respectively, for recall data, and 1, 7, 7, and 21 days, respectively, for recognition data."
