# An instructional spark-chamber experiment yields a muon mass of (100±5) MeV, consistent with the accepted value of 106 MeV

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Data from cosmic-ray muon decays in a spark chamber, fitted to a Monte Carlo simulation via least squares, give a muon rest energy of (100±5) MeV, a range consistent with the accepted muon mass value of 106 MeV. [→ Benjamin Brau 2009](#benjamin-brau-2009)

## Evidence

### Benjamin Brau 2009

Benjamin Brau, Christopher May, Robert Ormond, and John Essick. (2009). Determining the Muon Mass in an Instructional Laboratory. https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.5461 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0907.5461](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0907.5461)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Analysis of the experiment's data run (Ntotexp = 43 decay images) compared with simulation predictions. The authors report the fitted value and its uncertainty as printed: "(100±5) MeV", consistent with the accepted 106 MeV.

> "Through such investigations as well as from noting that the minimum region in Fig. 5 is relatively ﬂat over the region from 95 to 105 MeV, we estimate our experimental value for mµc2 to be (100±5) MeV, a range consistent with the accepted muon mass value of 106 MeV."
