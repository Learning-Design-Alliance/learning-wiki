## Subclaims
`q1 i?` It is unresolved whether anonymous unpredictable student IDs suffice, since failed and multiple assessments are publicly documented and records cannot be deleted. [→ Sommer 2018](#sommer-2018)

## Evidence

### Sommer 2018

Sommer, T., Deppe, G., Stehling, V., Haberstroh, M., & Hees, F. (2018). Request for Comments: Proposal of a Blockchain for the Automatic Management and Acceptance of Student Achievements. E-Prüfungs-Symposium, RWTH Aachen. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1806.09335](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1806.09335)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The Further Research section raises data protection as the primary open question, noting that anonymity does not prevent public documentation of failed or multiple assessments and that deletion is technically impossible.

> "First and foremost, the issue of data protection must be discussed in the community. Is it sufficient if every student receives an unpredictable ID? Failed assessments and multiple assessments by students are publicly documented, even if this information is anonymous. Deleting a record in the blockchain is technically impossible."
