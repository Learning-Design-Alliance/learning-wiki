## Evidence

### Jeffrey M. Rabin 2024

Jeffrey M. Rabin, Andrew Meyertholen, Brian Shotwell. (2024). Students' Experience of Cultural Differences Between Mathematics and Physics. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Summary of survey question 6 responses on specific mathematical topics: the delta function and Fourier analysis were absent from students' math coursework, and several students also said infinitesimals were not treated rigorously in math courses.

> "Students cited the Dirac delta function and Fourier analysis as topic s they never saw presented in math classes; they had some diﬃculties with the topics a s they were presented in physics."

### Jeffrey M. Rabin 2024 (2)

Jeffrey M. Rabin, Andrew Meyertholen, Brian Shotwell. (2024). Students' Experience of Cultural Differences Between Mathematics and Physics. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Survey question 6 summary: dual exposure to 3D vector calculus, differential equations, and derivative/integral calculation improved understanding, while responses about linear algebra were mixed and little was said about infinite series or dimensions/units.

> "For the most part, students said their understanding was improved by having seen 3D vector calculus and diﬀerential equations in both subjects, and nearly everyone agreed that the ir ability to calculate derivatives/integrals was improved by exposure in both subjects."
