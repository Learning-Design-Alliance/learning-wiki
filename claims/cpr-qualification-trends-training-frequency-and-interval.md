# CPR qualification rate rises with training frequency and falls with longer training intervals among senior high school students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Qualification rate increased significantly with greater training frequency (5.5% vs. 9.3% vs. 20%, P for trend = 0.013) and decreased significantly with longer training intervals (12.8% vs. 5.3% vs. 1.8%, P for trend <0.001). [→ Tang Hong-mei 2020](#tang-hong-mei-2020)

## Evidence

### Tang Hong-mei 2020

Tang Hong-mei, Wu Xiao, Jin Yin, Jin Yi-qing, Wang Zi-jun, Luo Jin-yan, Hu Yan-qi, Jin Ting, Shang Moses, Chang Qing, Wang Fei. (2020). Shorter training intervals increase high school students’ awareness of cardiopulmonary resuscitation: a questionnaire study. Journal of International Medical Research. https://doi.org/10.1177/0300060519897692

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Univariate χ2 trend analysis in the questionnaire survey of 430 students (Figure 1 shows the qualification rate trend under different conditions). Qualification rose from 5.5% to 20% across training-frequency groups and fell from 12.8% to 1.8% across lengthening training intervals.

> "The qualification rate increased with greater training frequency (5.5% vs. 9.3% vs. 20%, P for trend = 0.013), decreased significantly with longer training intervals (12.8% vs. 5.3% vs. 1.8%, P for trend <0.001)"
