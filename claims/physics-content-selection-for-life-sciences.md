### Edward F. Redish and Todd J. Cooke 2012

Edward F. Redish and Todd J. Cooke. (2012). Learning Each Other's Ropes: Negotiating interdisciplinary authenticity. To be published in CBE-LSE. [doi:10.1187/cbe.12-09-0147](https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.12-09-0147)

In the NEXUS course-design section, the authors report that interdisciplinary discussions "led us to some dramatic changes in our thinking about what a physics course for biologists should look like", including dropping circular motion and adding diffusion.

> "We dropped or si gnificantly reduced canonical top- ics that could be treated mathematically in full, such as circular motion, projectiles, and collisions, while adding topics that depended on a more phe nomenological introduction"

### Edward F. Redish and Todd J. Cooke 2012 (2)

Edward F. Redish and Todd J. Cooke. (2012). Learning Each Other's Ropes: Negotiating interdisciplinary authenticity. To be published in CBE-LSE. [doi:10.1187/cbe.12-09-0147](https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.12-09-0147)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

The authors' stated rationale: circular motion has "extremely limited and only highly technical applications in biology and medicine", while the article reports that "an understanding of diffusion ... plays an essential role in students' development of an accurate picture of the functioning of a cell".

> "On the other hand, an understanding of diffusion (and random motion in general) plays an essential role in students' development of an accurate picture of the functioning of a cell."
