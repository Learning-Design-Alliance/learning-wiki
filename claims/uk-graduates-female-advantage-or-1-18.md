# UK female medical graduates had 1.18 times the odds of passing PACES at first attempt compared with male UK graduates

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Among UK medical graduates, adjusted for ethnicity, female candidates had significantly higher odds of passing at first attempt (OR = 1.18). [→ Unwin Emily 2018](#unwin-emily-2018)

## Evidence

### Unwin Emily 2018

Unwin Emily, Potts Henry W. W., Dacre Jane, Elder Andrew, Woolf Katherine. (2018). Passing MRCP (UK) PACES: a cross-sectional study examining the performance of doctors by sex and country. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1178-2

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r3`

Subgroup regression of 3574 UK medical graduates, predominantly women (58%) and of white ethnicity (67%); GMC registration and centre location were removed as not significantly associated. The printed "OR = 1.18; 95% CI = 1.03-1.35" is smaller than the pre-2009 format estimate.

> "Female UK graduates had 1.18 times the odds of passing PACES at first attempt compared with male UK graduates, adjusting for ethnicity (OR = 1.18; 95% CI = 1.03-1.35; P = 0.02); see Table 2."
