## Evidence

### Gregor Benz 2026

Gregor Benz, Sarah Taha, Andreas Vorholzer. (2026). Data evaluation processes of different-sized datasets: an eye-tracking with concurrent think-aloud study. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2605.31045](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.31045)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Eye-tracking fixation analysis across conditions in the N = 20 study shows "the proportion of trend-based fixations increases from the small to the large dataset" for force data, with global-feature fixations falling from 17.8% to 3.8%. Only descriptive percentages are printed; no effect size.

> "For force-related fixations, the proportion of trend-based fixations increases from the small to the large dataset (e.g., F-T-: 14.3 % to 26.8 % for Conclusion 2), while fixations on global features decreases (e.g., F-G-: 17.8 % to 3.8 %)."
