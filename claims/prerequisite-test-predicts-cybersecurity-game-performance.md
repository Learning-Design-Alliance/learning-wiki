# A well-designed prerequisite test statistically significantly predicts game score and levels completed in cybersecurity game G1, and adding confidence assessment roughly doubles the model's R²

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In game G1, dichotomously scored pretest results significantly predicted game score and finished levels (R² = 0.17 and R² = 0.14, p ≤ 0.01). [→ Valdemar Švábenský and Jan Vykopal 2018](#valdemar-svabensky-and-jan-vykopal-2018)
`q3 i?` Incorporating confidence testing improved the models, with R² rising to 0.31 for score and 0.24 for levels (p ≤ 0.004), almost double the dichotomous pretest. [→ Valdemar Švábenský and Jan Vykopal 2018 (2)](#valdemar-svabensky-and-jan-vykopal-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Valdemar Švábenský and Jan Vykopal 2018

Valdemar Švábenský and Jan Vykopal. (2018). Challenges Arising from Prerequisite Testing in Cybersecurity Games. Proceedings of SIGCSE '18: The 49th ACM Technical Symposium on Computing Science Education. https://doi.org/10.1145/3159450.3159454

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Linear regression analyses of 47 participants playing game G1, using the 5-item pretest without the two extra session-4 questions. The article reports "Statistically significant fits were computed for the score or level prediction based on the dichotomously scored pretest"; only R² and p are printed, no effect size beyond variance explained.

> "Statistically significant fits were computed for the score or level prediction based on the dichotomously scored pretest (R2 = 0.17 or R2 = 0.14, p≤ 0.01)."

### Valdemar Švábenský and Jan Vykopal 2018 (2)

Valdemar Švábenský and Jan Vykopal. (2018). Challenges Arising from Prerequisite Testing in Cybersecurity Games. Proceedings of SIGCSE '18: The 49th ACM Technical Symposium on Computing Science Education. https://doi.org/10.1145/3159450.3159454

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same G1 regression analyses with confidence-scored pretest. The discussion states "the con/f_idence testing improved the models, as the value of R2 almost doubled compared to the dichotomous pretest." Regression diagnostics confirmed homoscedasticity and normality; an outlier's removal changed R² by ±0.01.

> "An even more promising relationship emerged when incorporating con/f_idence testing ( R2 = 0.31 or R2 = 0.24, p≤ 0.004)."
