# Online survey respondents significantly preferred the question and answer label over the fun fact label as more likely to stick in memory

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The question and answer label format was chosen as more likely to stick with respondents significantly more often than the fun fact label (62.1% vs 37.9%). [→ Lisa F. Smith 2013](#lisa-f-smith-2013)

## Evidence

### Lisa F. Smith 2013

Lisa F. Smith, Kimberly K. Arcand, Jeffrey K. Smith, Randall K. Smith, Jay Bookbinder, Megan Watzke. (2013). Examining Perceptions Of Astronomy Images Across Mobile Platforms. [doi:10.22323/2.13020201](https://doi.org/10.22323/2.13020201)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

In the same online survey (2183 respondents), for the item on which label is more likely to stick so respondents remember the information, the question and answer format was preferred 62.1% to 37.9%, "at p <.001". The fun fact format was chosen for Saturn and the Bullet Cluster. No effect size is reported.

> "the question and answer label format (62.1%) was preferred significantly more than the fun fact label (37.9%), at p <.001."
