# Post-hoc finding: P1 latency was increased for attended versus unattended unilateral stimuli, selectively over the ipsilateral ROI

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` P1 latency was significantly increased for attended versus unattended conditions ipsilateral to the stimulus location, with no contralateral difference. [→ Banerjee Snigdha 2015](#banerjee-snigdha-2015)

## Evidence

### Banerjee Snigdha 2015

Banerjee Snigdha, Frey Hans-Peter, Molholm Sophie, Foxe John J. (2015). Interests shape how adolescents pay attention: The interaction of motivation and top-down attentional processes in biasing sensory activations to anticipated events. The European Journal of Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1111/ejn.12810

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc analysis (no a-priori prediction) of P1 latency (100–180 ms) for unilateral stimuli in 24 adolescents; ANOVA showed main effect of attention F(1,23) = 11.46, p =.003 and attention × ROI interaction F(1,23) = 17.21, p <.001. No bilateral-condition latency difference.

> "paired samples t-tests were performed, which revealed significant increases in P1 latency for attended versus unattended conditions, but ipsilateral to the stimulus location (t(23) = 4.37, p <.001)."
