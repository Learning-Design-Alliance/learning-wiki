## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the multifactorial model, highly complex tasks and assessments taking place in large clinics significantly increased 'overall' mini-CEX scores (small vs large clinics −0.26, p < .05; low vs high complexity −0.23, p < .05). [→ Rogausch A 2015](#rogausch-a-2015)

## Evidence

### Rogausch A 2015

Rogausch A, Beyeler C, Montagne S, Jucker-Kupper P, Berendonk C, Huwendiek S, Gemperli A, Himmel W. (2015). The influence of students' prior clinical skills and context characteristics on mini-CEX scores in clerkships – a multilevel analysis. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0490-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel regression of clerkship mini-CEX ratings in 45 clinics of three size categories. Table 2 prints small vs large clinics −0.26 (95% CI −0.48 to −0.039) and low vs high complexity −0.23 (95% CI −0.44 to −0.019), both p < .05; no standardised effect size is reported.

> "Additionally, highly complex tasks and assessments taking place in large clinics also increased 'overall' scores."
