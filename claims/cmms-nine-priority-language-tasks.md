## Evidence

### Javid 2014

Javid, C. Z. (2014). EMP Needs of Medical Undergraduates in a Saudi Context. Kashmir Journal of Language Research, 14(1), 89-110. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1410.6469](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1410.6469)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Structured 27-item questionnaire based on Basturkmen (1998), 4-point Likert scale for items 6-27, returned by 236 students with 228 responding to items 6-27; ranked task priorities are reported with means in Table 3 (e.g. listening to lectures mean 1.22, reading textbooks mean 1.25).

> "The following are the nine most important language tasks according to the findings of the student questionnaire. These are recorded here in order of priority and include: listening to lectures; reading textbooks; taking notes during lectures; interacting with doctors in field; participating in discussions; asking questions; understanding power point presentations; understanding instructions and answering the questions."

### Javid 2014 (2)

Javid, C. Z. (2014). EMP Needs of Medical Undergraduates in a Saudi Context. Kashmir Journal of Language Research, 14(1), 89-110. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1410.6469](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1410.6469)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Questionnaire item 9 in Table 3: writing skill received mean 3.73 (SD 0.56) on the 4-point importance scale, the highest mean of items 6-10, and the author concludes writing was "considered the least important".

> "How important is writing skill for their studies at CMMS? 228 3.73 0.56"
