Nilsen P 2015¶

Nilsen P. (2015). Making sense of implementation theories, models and frameworks. Implementation Science. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-015-0242-0

q1 · i? · review · r2

Theoretical argument in the Summary section, not an empirical test. The article presents this as reasoned argument about advantages of formal theory over informal theory, citing prior conceptual work. No effect size is printed.

"Theories are explicit and open to question and examination; common sense usually consists of implicit assumptions, beliefs and ways of thinking and is therefore more difficult to challenge."

Nilsen P 2015 (2)¶

Nilsen P. (2015). Making sense of implementation theories, models and frameworks. Implementation Science. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-015-0242-0

q1 · i? · review · r2

The review's own statement of an open empirical question, acknowledging critics who argue theory is not necessarily better than common sense for guiding implementation. No data are offered either way.

"empirical research is needed to study how and the extent to which the use of implementation theories, models and frameworks contributes to more effective implementation and under what contextual conditions or circumstances they apply"
