Evidence¶

Martin 2019¶

Martin, Nicole D., Tissenbaum, Catherine Dornfeld, Gnesdilow, Dana, Puntambekar, Sadhana. (2019). Fading distributed scaffolds: the importance of complementarity between teacher and material scaffolds. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-018-9474-0

q2 · i? · design · r2

Mixed-model ANOVA of pre/post physics conceptual test scores (29 items, Cronbach's alpha = .76) for two teachers' sixth-grade classes (n = 28 and n = 44). The article reports a significant main effect of time (F(1,70) = 61.49, p < .001) and a significant teacher × time interaction (F(1,70) = 11.01, p = .001), with Mrs. Lewis's students "overcame this disadvantage".

"There was also a significant interaction between teacher and time (F(1,70) = 11.01, p = .001). While Mrs. Lewis’s students had lower scores on the pre-test than Mr. Green’s students, Mrs. Lewis’s students overcame this disadvantage and these differences were no longer present on the post-test."

Related Claims¶

  • As material scaffolds faded, Mrs. Lewis's students' performance returned to initial levels between High and Low support, while Mr. Green's students declined significantly across the unit
