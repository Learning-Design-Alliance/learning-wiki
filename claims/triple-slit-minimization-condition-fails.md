# Triple-slit simulation tasks made students reconsider the interference conditions because the two-slit minimization condition fails for three slits

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

> **Currency** · newest study 2012 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The constructive interference condition holds for three slits but the minimization condition is more complicated, since the two-slit condition would leave the third wave undiminished and it is mathematically impossible for all distance differences to be a half integer number of wavelengths. [→ Leon N. Maurer 2012](#leon-n-maurer-2012)

## Evidence

### Leon N. Maurer 2012

Leon N. Maurer. (2012). Simulating Interference and Diffraction in Instructional Laboratories. [doi:10.1088/0031-9120/48/2/227](https://doi.org/10.1088/0031-9120/48/2/227)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Authors' explanation in the simulations section of why the triple-slit minimum condition differs from the two-slit case: "the minimization condition is more complicated" and cancellation leaves the third wave undiminished.

> "However, the minimization condition is more
complicated.17 The two slit condition caused the
two waves to roughly cancel. That would leave the third
wave undiminished. Additionally, it is mathematically
impossible for all diﬀerences in the distances to be a half
integer number of wavelengths."
