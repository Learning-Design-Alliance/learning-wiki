# Children as young as 3½ years perform significantly above chance identifying and comparing multidigit numerals

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` All three age groups (3½, 4½, and 5 years) performed significantly above chance on numerals tasks, with a significant age main effect (ηp2=.23). [→ Mix 2014](#mix-2014)

## Evidence

### Mix 2014

Mix, Kelly S., Prather, Richard W., Smith, Linda B., & Stockton, Jerri DaSha. (2014). Young Children's Interpretation of Multidigit Number Names: From Emerging Competence to Mastery. Child Development. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.12197

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Experiment 2 tested 92 children aged 3½ to 5 years on 16 numerals items. A repeated measures ANOVA showed a significant main effect of age, F(1, 89) = 13.59, p < .001, ηp2=.23, and "they nonetheless performed significantly above chance on both tasks by 3½ years of age."

> "they nonetheless performed significantly above chance on both tasks by 3½ years of age and on most items within each task by 4½ years of age"
