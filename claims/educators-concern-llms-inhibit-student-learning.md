## Evidence

### Emma Harvey 2025

Emma Harvey, Allison Koenecke, and René F. Kizilcec. (2025). "Don't Forget the Teachers": Towards an Educator-Centered Understanding of Harms from Large Language Models in Education. arXiv. [doi:10.1145/3706598.3713210](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713210)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Educator interview findings (§5.3). This harm was not prompted by interviewers; it was proactively raised in open-ended responses. A few educators mitigated it by having students critique LLM outputs or consider alternate solutions. No effect size is reported.

> "The concern that LLMs could inhibit students' learning, esp ecially their critical thinking skills and the development of their own unique 'voice,' was proactively raised by educators (E2, E3, E5-E8, E10, E11, E15-E21)."

### Emma Harvey 2025 (2)

Emma Harvey, Allison Koenecke, and René F. Kizilcec. (2025). "Don't Forget the Teachers": Towards an Educator-Centered Understanding of Harms from Large Language Models in Education. arXiv. [doi:10.1145/3706598.3713210](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713210)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Educator interview findings (§5.3) on mitigation responses to learning-inhibition concerns. Providers, meanwhile, tried to measure tool 'helpfulness' but there is no consensus on what helpfulness means or how it can be quantified. No effect size is reported.

> "most educators either limited the use of LLMs in their classroom as a result of these concerns (E3, E7, E8, E 10, E16, E17, E18, E19, E20, E21) or expressed that they did not know how to best address them (E2, E6)."
