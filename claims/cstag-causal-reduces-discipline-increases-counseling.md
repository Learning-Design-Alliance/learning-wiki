# Causal studies find CSTAG reduces exclusionary discipline and bullying and increases counseling, with fidelity moderating effects

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In randomized studies, CSTAG students making threats were significantly more likely to receive counseling services and a parent conference, while control students were significantly more likely to receive long-term suspension or transfer. [→ DePaoli 2025](#depaoli-2025)
`q3 i?` Among CSTAG schools, higher fidelity to the model was associated with the greatest reductions in long-term suspensions and increases in counseling. [→ DePaoli 2025 (2)](#depaoli-2025-2)

## Evidence

### DePaoli 2025

DePaoli, J. L., & Loewe, S. B. (2025). Keeping schools safe? The research on behavioral threat assessments. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/808.437

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The report describes causal studies in which some schools were randomly assigned to receive CSTAG training while others conducted business as usual until the following year. "Students who made threats of violence in schools that used the CSTAG model were significantly more likely to receive counseling services." No effect sizes are printed.

> "Students who made threats of violence in schools that used the CSTAG model were significantly more likely to receive counseling services and a parent conference than students in control schools, while students in the control group were significantly more likely to receive a long-term suspension or be transferred to a different school."

### DePaoli 2025 (2)

DePaoli, J. L., & Loewe, S. B. (2025). Keeping schools safe? The research on behavioral threat assessments. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/808.437

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The report states that in the causal CSTAG studies, "those with higher fidelity to the model showed the greatest reductions in long-term suspensions and increases in counseling provided," identifying implementation fidelity as a moderator.

> "Among CSTAG schools, those with higher fidelity to the model showed the greatest reductions in long-term suspensions and increases in counseling provided."
