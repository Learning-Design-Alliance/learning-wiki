The introduction's summary of the Zimmerman, Land, Faimon, and Chiu study of family sense-making with a mobile AR app featuring a time-travel narrative across eight micro-sites in a children's garden.

"Findings from this research illustrate how families’ understandings of geologic time and geoscience processes were impacted by the immersive qualities of Time Explorers."

Related Claims¶

  • Sensory immersion prompted by the MAR app supported families' geoscientific talk and observation of rock features during place-based LOTM. — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Families engaged in multiple immersive processes at the same microsites, combining sense-making conversations, observational inquiry, and deep geoscience engagement. — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • All families took photographs at each microsite as evidence of their observation, with only one family personalizing photos by including children. — related
  • No substantial example of emancipatory immersion was found in the family data, despite the framework including it. — related
  • All families in the dataset successfully used the GPS wayfinding map for actional immersion, including a 5-year-old navigating alone. — related
  • Mobile apps increased timely, direct communication between parents and educators during remote schooling — related
