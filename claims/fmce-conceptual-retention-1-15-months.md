## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox 2020

Bethany R. Wilcox, Steven J. Pollock, and Daniel R. Bolton. (2020). Retention of conceptual learning after an interactive introductory physics course. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010140](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010140)

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of matched FMCE post-test (PHYS I) and pre-test (PHYS II) scores for N = 1068 students; the difference between the two score distributions was statistically significant (Mann-Whitney U test, p < 0.001) but an effect size of only d = 0.1, described as having negligible practical significance.

> "The average shift was 0.87 points (with a maximum possible shift magnitude of 47 points), meaning the students got roughly one more question right on the FMCE in PHYS II than they did in PHYS I."

### Bethany R. Wilcox 2020 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox, Steven J. Pollock, and Daniel R. Bolton. (2020). Retention of conceptual learning after an interactive introductory physics course. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010140](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010140)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The histogram of score shifts shown in Fig. 1 deviates from normality (Skewness-Kurtosis test, p < 0.001) because of "the large spike in the number of students with an overall shift of zero," i.e., most students scored identically both times.

> "This histogram is not normally distributed (Skewness-Kurtosis test, p < 0.001) primarily because of the large spike in the number of students with an overall shift of zero."
