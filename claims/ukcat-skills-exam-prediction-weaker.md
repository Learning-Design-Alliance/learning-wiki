## Evidence

### Tiffin Paul A. 2016

Tiffin Paul A., Mwandigha Lazaro M., Paton Lewis W., Hesselgreaves H., McLachlan John C., Finn Gabrielle M., Kasim Adetayo S. (2016). Predictive validity of the UKCAT for medical school undergraduate performance: a national prospective cohort study. BMC Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-016-0682-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Univariable multilevel regression of skills-based exam scores on UKCAT scales in the same national cohort. The article reports coefficients "small, at the 0.05 to 0.1 level" and notes a trend for verbal reasoning and total score to more strongly predict skills performance as medical school progressed.

> "Likewise, the UKCAT scores generally significantly predicted performance at skills-based exams throughout medical school, though ‘quantitative reasoning’ scores were of borderline or non-significance for years 1 and 2 (Fig. 3). Again, absolute values for the coefficients were small, at the 0.05 to 0.1 level."
