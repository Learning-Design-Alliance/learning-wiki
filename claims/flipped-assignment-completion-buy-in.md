## Evidence

### Webb 2016

Webb, M., & Doman, E. (2016). Does the Flipped Classroom Lead to Increased Gains on Learning Outcomes in ESL/EFL Contexts? The CATESOL Journal, 28(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1111606

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive analysis of the 10 online quizzes and discussion posts across both flipped classes; Table 6 prints 82% quiz completion, 73.5% discussion completion, and 77.75% overall. The article relates this to the 73-80% range in prior flipped studies.

> "On average between both contexts, 77.75% of all the flipped grammar assignments were completed."

### Webb 2016 (2)

Webb, M., & Doman, E. (2016). Does the Flipped Classroom Lead to Increased Gains on Learning Outcomes in ESL/EFL Contexts? The CATESOL Journal, 28(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1111606

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Appendix D trend and Table 6 show completion declining during weeks 6, 7, and 8 and rising again at semester's end; the article states "the Macau students completed more of the assignments than the US students did" (83% vs. 72.5%). Teachers responded with one-on-one consultations and reflective essays.

> "the average num-ber of completed assignments rose again"
