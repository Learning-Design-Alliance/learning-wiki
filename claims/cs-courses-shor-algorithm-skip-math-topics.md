# Computer science-listed introductory QISE courses disproportionately teach Shor's factoring algorithm (100% vs 68% of non-CS courses) and disproportionately omit commutators, outer products, density matrices/mixed states, and quantum sensing/metrology

## Evidence

### Josephine C. Meyer 2024

Josephine C. Meyer, Gina Passante, Steven J. Pollock, Bethany R. Wilcox. (2024). Introductory quantum information science coursework at US institutions: Content coverage. arXiv. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1140/epjqt/s40507-024-00226-0](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjqt/s40507-024-00226-0)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Fisher's exact tests with Hochberg-Bonferroni adjustment across N = 63 courses (NCS = 22 CS-listed) found discipline differences; Table 3 prints commutators 30% CS vs 84% non-CS (p adj< 0.001) and Shor's algorithm 100% vs 68% (p adj = 0.012).

> "Computer science courses disproportionately taught Shor's factoring algorithm (100% of CS-listed courses, 68% of non-CS courses)."

### Josephine C. Meyer 2024 (2)

Josephine C. Meyer, Gina Passante, Steven J. Pollock, Bethany R. Wilcox. (2024). Introductory quantum information science coursework at US institutions: Content coverage. arXiv. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1140/epjqt/s40507-024-00226-0](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjqt/s40507-024-00226-0)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same discipline comparison found CS courses "disproportionately likely not to teach commutators, outer products, density matrices/mixed states, and quantum sensing/metrology", topics the authors link to quantum information theory and hardware rather than algorithms.

> "Additionally, computer science courses were disproportionately likely not to teach commutators, outer products, density matrices/mixed states, and quantum sensing/metrology – all topics of great interest to quantum information theory and the physical construction and characterization of quantum technologies but with fewer direct connections to quantum algorithms."
