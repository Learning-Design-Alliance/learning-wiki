# Supplemental blended learning with the Memrise app produced significantly higher post-test vocabulary scores than face-to-face instruction in Iranian EFL learners

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After a 4-week supplemental blended-learning period using Memrise asynchronously outside class, the experimental group significantly outperformed the control group on the vocabulary post-test (p = 0.031 < 0.05). [→ Ebadi and Ghuchi 2018](#ebadi-and-ghuchi-2018)

## Evidence

### Ebadi and Ghuchi 2018

Saman Ebadi, Khadijeh Davood Ghuchi. (2018). Investigating the Effects of Blended Learning Approach on Vocabulary Enhancement from EFL Learners' Perspectives. i-manager's Journal of English Language Teaching, Vol. 8 No. 2, April - June 2018. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1185411

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized two-group experiment with 40 intermediate Iranian EFL learners (20 per group); control received face-to-face teaching, experimental added asynchronous Memrise use for 4 weeks. Post-test means were control M = 25.85 and experimental M = 28.85, with an independent-samples t-test showing "statistically significant differences between the research sample groups in the post-test". No effect size was printed.

> "The results (Table 5) measured by the independent samples t-test showed that the value of (F) would be 0.069, and the level of significance would be 0.795, (p = 0.031< 0.05), which had statistically significant differences between the research sample groups in the post-test."
