# In one uncontrolled pre-post study, the proportion of third-year medical students selecting the correct therapeutic concept for esophageal cancer cases rose from pre-test to post-test after using the ALICE immersive patient simulator

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After working with the ALICE esophageal cancer module, the proportion of students prescribing the suitable therapy rose from 32% (20/62) to 76% (47/62) in one uncontrolled pre-post study; the article reports this rise as significant. [→ Kleinert R 2015](#kleinert-r-2015)

## Evidence

### Kleinert R 2015

Kleinert R, Heiermann N, Plum PS, Wahba R, Chang D, Maus M, Chon S, Hoelscher AH, Stippel DL. (2015). Web-Based Immersive Virtual Patient Simulators: Positive Effect on Clinical Reasoning in Medical Education. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.5035

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre- and postsimulator comparison of written clinical reasoning files in a single-group evaluation of 62 third-year medical students using the ALICE esophageal cancer module; the tutor rated answers right or wrong. Correct therapy rose from "32% (20/62)" to "76% (47/62)".

> "only 32% (20/62) of students chose the right therapeutic concept, and in the postquestionnaire, 76% (47/62) of students prescribed the suitable therapy. As this improvement was significant, simulator use had a positive impact on clinical decision making."
