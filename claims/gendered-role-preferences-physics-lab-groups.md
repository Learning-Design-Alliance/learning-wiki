## Evidence

### Eugenio Tufino and Giovanni Organtini 2023

Eugenio Tufino and Giovanni Organtini. (2023). Evaluation of the effectiveness of an introductory mechanics Lab with Arduino and smartphone. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2311.00606](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2311.00606)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Gender-analysed responses from the anonymous end-of-course questionnaire (46 respondents: 28 male, 17 female, one no response) in a course where groups were deliberately mixed and roles were recommended to rotate. The article reports gender was "not a significant factor" for building and data-collection roles.

> "Contrary to popular belief, we found that gender is not a significant factor in the selection of roles for those responsible for building the equipment and collecting data."

### Eugenio Tufino and Giovanni Organtini 2023 (2)

Eugenio Tufino and Giovanni Organtini. (2023). Evaluation of the effectiveness of an introductory mechanics Lab with Arduino and smartphone. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2311.00606](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2311.00606)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive gender breakdown of role-distribution preference items in the same 46-respondent questionnaire, shown in figure 6. The article states the findings "align with previous research" and calls the topic a valuable foundation for further studies.

> "In general, about the role distribution scheme, females prefer a collaborative approach where everyone works together, while males tend to prefer a rotating system where roles are assigned among group members, as shown in figure 6."
