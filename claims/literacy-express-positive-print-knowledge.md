# Literacy Express has positive effects on print knowledge for preschool children, with significant effects in two of three studies

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i1`–`i2`

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Farver, Lonigan, and Eppe (2009) found a statistically significant positive effect size of 0.64 on print knowledge. [→ Literacy Express 2010](#literacy-express-2010)
`q3 i1` Lonigan et al. (2005) found a statistically significant and substantively important positive effect size of 0.32 on print knowledge. [→ Literacy Express 2010 (2)](#literacy-express-2010-2)
`q3 i?` PCER Consortium (2008) found no statistically significant differences on print knowledge measures, though a substantively important effect of 0.30 on WJ-III Letter-Word Identification. [→ Literacy Express 2010 (3)](#literacy-express-2010-3)

## Evidence

### Literacy Express 2010

Literacy Express. (2010). WWC Intervention Report: Literacy Express. U.S. Department of Education, What Works Clearinghouse. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/288

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

In the Los Angeles Head Start randomized trial (94 children analyzed), the Pre-CTOPPP Print Knowledge subtest showed a printed effect size of 0.64 favoring the combined Literacy Express groups over High/Scope control.

> "Their analyses showed, and the WWC confirmed, a statistically significant positive effect of 0.64 when comparing the Literacy Express group and the control group."

### Literacy Express 2010 (2)

Literacy Express. (2010). WWC Intervention Report: Literacy Express. U.S. Department of Education, What Works Clearinghouse. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/288

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

In the 48-preschool Florida/California randomized trial (722 children), the Pre-CTOPPP Print Knowledge subtest showed a printed effect size of 0.32 favoring Literacy Express over standard curricula.

> "Their analyses showed, and the WWC confirmed, a statistically significant and substantively important positive effect of 0.32 when Literacy Express was compared to the control group curriculum."

### Literacy Express 2010 (3)

Literacy Express. (2010). WWC Intervention Report: Literacy Express. U.S. Department of Education, What Works Clearinghouse. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/288

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the PCER Florida randomized trial (188 children analyzed), TERA-3, WJ-III Letter-Word Identification, and WJ-III Spelling showed no statistically significant differences; the 0.30 Letter-Word effect was not significant.

> "The authors report, and the WWC confirms, that differences between Literacy Express and the control group curriculum are not statistically significant on any of these measures, although there is a substantively important effect of 0.30 on the WJ-III Letter-Word Identification subtest."
