# NITARP teams function most efficiently with approximately five people: a mentor astronomer, a mentor teacher, and three new educators

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teams of about five people (one mentor astronomer, one mentor educator, three new educators) work most efficiently; smaller teams create too much work per person and larger teams are unwieldy. [→ Rebull 2018](#rebull-2018)

## Evidence

### Rebull 2018

Rebull, L. M., Fitzgerald, M., Roberts, T., French, D. A., Laurence, W., Gorjian, V., & Squires, G. K. (2018). The NASA/IPAC Teacher Archive Research Program (NITARP). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1804.08743](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1804.08743)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

Program-design observation from the authors' 13 years running NITARP, stated in the abstract and elaborated in the team-size section: "More than about five or at most six people per team has proven to be unwieldy." No effect size is printed.

> "NITARP teams appear to function most efficiently with approximately 5 people: a mentor astronomer, a mentor teacher (who has been through the program before), and 3 new educators."
