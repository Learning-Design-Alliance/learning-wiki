## Evidence

### Wickens 2013

Wickens, C. D., Hutchins, S., Carolan, T., & Cumming, J. (2013). Effectiveness of Part-Task Training and Increasing-Difficulty Training Strategies: A Meta-Analysis Approach. Human Factors, 55(2), 461-470. https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720812451994

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Prior-experience moderator analysis on studies clearly identifying naive learners. Low-experienced learners showed "a benefit for ID (TR = 1.1, k† = 6; g = +0.75*, k† = 6)" absent in the full dataset; the printed effect size g = +0.75 is medium.

> "For the low-experienced learners, the results revealed a benefit for ID (TR = 1.1, k† = 6; g = +0.75*, k† = 6), a benefit that of course was not present when the entire data set was examined"

### Wickens 2013 (2)

Wickens, C. D., Hutchins, S., Carolan, T., & Cumming, J. (2013). Effectiveness of Part-Task Training and Increasing-Difficulty Training Strategies: A Meta-Analysis Approach. Human Factors, 55(2), 461-470. https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720812451994

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Experience moderator within the PTT meta-analysis. For low-experience learners the PTT cost "remained significant in the g measure but was reduced in its magnitude (g = –0.25*, k† = 11)"; the printed effect size g = –0.25 is small.

> "However, when experience was low, this cost remained significant in the g measure but was reduced in its magnitude (g = –0.25*, k† = 11)."
