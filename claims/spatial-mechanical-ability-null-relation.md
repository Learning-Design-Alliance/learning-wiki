# Learners' mechanical ability and spatial ability were not significantly related to identification performance in the main study

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Correlations of mechanical ability and spatial ability with motion and arrangement identification were nonsignificant, so neither was used as a covariate. [→ Rolf Ploetzner 2021](#rolf-ploetzner-2021)

## Evidence

### Rolf Ploetzner 2021

Rolf Ploetzner, Sandra Berney, Mireille Bétrancourt. (2021). When learning from animations is more successful than learning from static pictures: learning the specifics of change. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-021-09541-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the main study (N = 88), mechanical ability (MeTeV, 12 motion verification tasks) and spatial ability (KFT Subtest N3 paper folding) were assessed as potential covariates; groups did not differ on either (e.g. spatial ability F(2, 85) = 1.16, p = 0.319) and all ability–performance correlations were nonsignificant, so no effect sizes are printed.

> "The correlations between the learners' mechanical ability and their identifications of motions as well as between the learners' mechanical ability and their identifications of arrangements were nonsignificant (cf. Table 2). Likewise, the correlations between the learners' spatial ability and their identifications of motions as well as between the learners' spatial ability and their identifications of arrangements were nonsignificant"
