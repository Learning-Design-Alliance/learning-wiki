# Retention over 24 h is significantly better when sleep follows learning directly (sleep–wake) than when sleep is delayed by a day of waking (wake–sleep)

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` Forgetting was less for the sleep–wake group than for the wake–sleep group over 24-h intervals containing similar amounts of sleep and waking. [→ Talamini and Takashima 2008](#talamini-and-takashima-2008)

`q3 i?` The 24 hr Immediate Sleep condition outperformed the Delayed Sleep condition (change from training: +0.7±2.1% vs −6.5±2.6%; F1,63 = 5.44, p = .02). [→ Payne JD 2012](#payne-jd-2012)

`q3 i?` A significant Word-pair type effect emerged at 24 hr with no Condition x Word-pair type interaction, so the related-unrelated difference was similar in both conditions. [→ Payne JD 2012 (2)](#payne-jd-2012)

## Evidence

### Talamini and Takashima 2008

Lucia M. Talamini, Ingrid L.C. Nieuwenhuis, Atsuko Takashima, and Ole Jensen. (2008). Sleep directly following learning benefits consolidation of spatial associative memory. Learning & Memory 15:233–237. https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.771608

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Between-group comparison of the two 24-h conditions in the same four-group experiment; because both intervals contain similar sleep and waking, the authors argue the difference implies "enduring effects of either diurnal or nocturnal memory processing that 'carry over' into the subsequent phase".

> "Moreover, comparison of the 24-h groups showed that forgetting was less for the sleep–wake group than for the wake–sleep group (t = ⳮ2.46, df = 36, P = 0.019)."

### Payne JD 2012

Payne JD, Tucker MA, Ellenbogen JM, Wamsley EJ, Walker MP, Schacter DL, Stickgold R. (2012). Memory for Semantically Related and Unrelated Declarative Information: The Benefit of Sleep, the Cost of Wake. PLoS ONE 7(3): e33079. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0033079

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

24 hr retest ANOVA comparing Immediate Sleep (trained 9pm, slept first) vs Delayed Sleep (trained 9am, slept second), N = 67. Immediate Sleep participants "performed much better than the Delayed Sleep Condition"; no effect size printed.

> "there was a main effect of Condition (F 1,63 = 5.44, p = .02; Figure 3b) demonstrating that participants in the Immediate Sleep Condition performed much better than the Delayed Sleep Condition at retest (change from training: 0.762.1% vs. 26.562.6%)"
