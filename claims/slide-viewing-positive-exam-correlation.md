# Viewing lecture slides during class is positively correlated with exam scores, predicting 6.0% of variance beyond standardized test scores

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Duration of slide viewing during class is positively correlated with exam score (r(101) = 0.25, p = 0.01). [→ Day Alison J. 2021](#day-alison-j-2021)
`q3 i?` Slide viewing predicts exam score over and above ACT/SAT proxy intelligence scores, adding 6.0% of variance. [→ Day Alison J. 2021 (2)](#day-alison-j-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Day Alison J. 2021

Day Alison J., Fenn Kimberly M., Ravizza Susan M. (2021). Is it worth it? The costs and benefits of bringing a laptop to a university class. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0251792

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of tracked class-related laptop activities; among four class-related categories, only slide viewing reached significance with Bonferroni correction. The article reports "a positive relationship between the duration of viewing slides and exam score, r(101) = 0.25, p = 0.01".

> "we sought to investigate the extent to which each individual activity predicted exam score and found a positive relationship between the duration of viewing slides and exam score, r(101) = 0.25, p = 0.01"

### Day Alison J. 2021 (2)

Day Alison J., Fenn Kimberly M., Ravizza Susan M. (2021). Is it worth it? The costs and benefits of bringing a laptop to a university class. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0251792

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical regression with standardized test score in step one and slide-viewing duration in step two. The article reports that "slide viewing contributed significantly to the model, F(2, 86) = 15.87, p < 0.001, and accounted for an additional 6.0% of the variance in exam score".

> "slide viewing contributed significantly to the model, F(2, 86) = 15.87, p < 0.001, and accounted for an additional 6.0% of the variance in exam score"
