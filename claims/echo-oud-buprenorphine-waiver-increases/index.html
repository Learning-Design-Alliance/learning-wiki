Evidence¶

Puckett 2021¶

Puckett, H. M., Bossaller, J. S., & Sheets, L. R. (2021). The impact of project ECHO on physician preparedness to treat opioid use disorder: a systematic review. Addiction Science & Clinical Practice, 16, 6. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-021-00215-z

q2 · i? · review · r2

Observational report on the University of New Mexico ECHO clinic (654 unique participants since 2010). The review notes "it is impossible to tell how many became waiver certified as a direct result of ECHO".

"Since 2006, only 36 NM physicians were listed as buprenorphine waivered, but post ECHO training, 375 physicians in NM are now waivered."

Puckett 2021 (2)¶

Puckett, H. M., Bossaller, J. S., & Sheets, L. R. (2021). The impact of project ECHO on physician preparedness to treat opioid use disorder: a systematic review. Addiction Science & Clinical Practice, 16, 6. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-021-00215-z

q2 · i? · review · r2

Exploratory evaluation of the NYC health+hospitals buprenorphine ECHO program (16 weekly one-hour sessions). The printed prescriber count rose from 7 to 10; no test statistic reported.

"The number of participants prescribing buprenorphine rose from 7 to 10."

Related Claims¶

  • ECHO session attendance is variable, higher among prescribers than non-prescribers, with time demands and clinic leadership support as reported barriers — related
  • A hub-and-spoke ECHO-supported OUD program reported majority-physician gains in knowledge, reduced isolation, improved MOUD ability, and reduced patient wait times — related
  • Project ECHO participation is associated with increased self-reported knowledge and self-efficacy for treating opioid use disorder — related
  • The evidence that ECHO improves physician preparedness for OUD rests almost entirely on self-report, so actual preparedness gains remain untested — related
