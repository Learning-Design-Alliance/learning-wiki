`q2 i?` Clustering logged path data from 145 students with Self-Organizing Maps yielded three major groups: A (48 students who skipped early review loops), B (39 students with many attempts through all loops), and C (23 students who gave up). [→ Matti Harjula 2024](#matti-harjula-2024)

## Evidence

### Matti Harjula 2024

Matti Harjula, Ville Havu, Inkeri Kontro, Kimmo Kulmala, Jarmo Malinen, and Petri Salo. (2024). Student behaviour and engagement with adaptive exercises on a thermodynamics course. A PREPRINT. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2403.04424](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2403.04424)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The clustering analysis (Section 5.1) encoded student paths through the Stateful Diesel exercise as vectors of attempts per node and clustered them with SOM on a 3x2 grid; group A is defined as "Students who skipped the first and/or the second review loops" and contained 48 students.

> "Group A: Students who skipped the first and/or the second review loops, concerning maximum pressure and temperature in Diesel cycle, respectively. 48 students."

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same SOM clustering of 145 students' path vectors identified group C, defined in the article as "Students who gave up without reaching the end scene", comprising 23 students.

> "Group C: Students who gave up without reaching the end scene. 23 students."

### Matti Harjula 2024 (3)

Matti Harjula, Ville Havu, Inkeri Kontro, Kimmo Kulmala, Jarmo Malinen, and Petri Salo. (2024). Student behaviour and engagement with adaptive exercises on a thermodynamics course. A PREPRINT. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2403.04424](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2403.04424)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

> "These three student groups A, B, and C amount to 76% of the test subject population, 110 students in total."
