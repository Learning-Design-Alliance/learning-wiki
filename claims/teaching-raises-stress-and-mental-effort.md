## Evidence

### Sarah Shi Hui Wong 2026

Sarah Shi Hui Wong, Stephen Lim. (2026). The Powerful Teacher: A Power Hypothesis for the Benefits of Learning-by-Teaching. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10097-1

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory analysis of post-studying phenomenological ratings in the same experiment: stress differed across groups, F(2, 239) = 4.79, p=.01, with "d = 0.43 and 0.40" for teaching versus notetaking and explaining; the notetaking and explaining groups did not differ from each other.

> "the teaching group reported higher stress when learning about the subject matter than the notetaking and explaining groups, both ps = .01, d = 0.43 and 0.40, respectively"

### Sarah Shi Hui Wong 2026 (2)

Sarah Shi Hui Wong, Stephen Lim. (2026). The Powerful Teacher: A Power Hypothesis for the Benefits of Learning-by-Teaching. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10097-1

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Mental effort ratings also differed across groups, F(2, 239) = 3.85, p=.02, with "d = 0.43" for teaching versus notetaking; the teaching-versus-explaining contrast did not reach significance. No other phenomenological items showed significant differences, all ps>.05.

> "the teaching group reported investing more mental effort than the notetaking group, p=.01, d = 0.43, but not the explaining group, p=.13, d = 0.25"
