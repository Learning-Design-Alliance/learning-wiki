## Evidence

### Nasirzade Amirreza 2024

Nasirzade Amirreza, Deldar Kolsoum, Froutan Razieh, Shakeri Mohammad Taghi. (2024). Comparison of the effects of burn assessment mission game with feedback lecture on nursing students’ knowledge and skills in the burn patients’ assessment: a randomized clinical trial. BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-024-02558-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Station-level ordinal logistic regression analysis of OSCE scores in the RCT found the BAM game group "had significantly higher skills rank compared to the feedback lecture group (all P < .05)" for stations 1 to 8, with score advantages of 1.6 to 3.9.

> "The results univariable ordinal logistic regressions showed that for station 1 to station 8, the BAM game group had significantly higher skills rank compared to the feedback lecture group (all P < .05)."

### Nasirzade Amirreza 2024 (2)

Nasirzade Amirreza, Deldar Kolsoum, Froutan Razieh, Shakeri Mohammad Taghi. (2024). Comparison of the effects of burn assessment mission game with feedback lecture on nursing students’ knowledge and skills in the burn patients’ assessment: a randomized clinical trial. BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-024-02558-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

At stations 9 and 10 the univariable analyses were non-significant (P = 0.135 and P = 0.184); the authors attribute the comparable performance to repeated emphasis of these topics in the control group's lectures.

> "Yet, when it came to evaluating the burn extent (station 9) and burn depth (station 10), the practical skills of both groups showed no significant differences."
