# Medical students scored significantly higher on visual memory than dental students, while spatial intelligence did not differ between the disciplines

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Mean visual memory was significantly higher in medical students (17.1±5.3) than dental students (14.3±4.6), P<0.001; mean spatial intelligence did not differ significantly (31.5±5.9 vs 31.9±4.9, P=0.56). [→ Amin Aspanani 2023](#amin-aspanani-2023)

## Evidence

### Amin Aspanani 2023

Amin Aspanani, Hosein Sadeqhi, Omid Athar. (2023). The relationship between visual memory and spatial intelligence with students’ academic achievement in anatomy. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04327-9

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Independent t-tests comparing 148 medical and 85 dental students (Table 2): visual memory means 17.1±5.3 vs 14.3±4.6, significant at P<0.001; spatial intelligence means 31.5±5.9 vs 31.9±4.9, P=0.56, not significant. No effect size printed; equivalence not tested.

> "Independent t-test showed that the mean score of visual memory in medical students was significantly higher than dental students (P-value<0/001). The mean score and standard deviation of spatial intelligence in medical students was 31.5±5.9 and in dental students was 31.9±4.9."
