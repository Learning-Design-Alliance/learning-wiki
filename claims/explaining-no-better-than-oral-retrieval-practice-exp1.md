# Explaining was not more effective than oral retrieval practice overall in Experiment 1

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Contrasting the two explaining conditions against the oral retrieval practice condition yielded no significant difference on conceptual knowledge. [→ Lachner 2020](#lachner-2020)

## Evidence

### Lachner 2020

Lachner, A., Backfisch, I., Hoogerheide, V., van Gog, T., & Renkl, A. (2020). Timing matters! Explaining between study phases enhances students' learning. Journal of Educational Psychology, 112(4), 841–853. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000396

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In Experiment 1's three-group contrast analysis (in-between explaining and after-study explaining each weighted 1, oral retrieval practice weighted -2), controlling for prior knowledge, the explaining contrast was not significant; the authors note "explaining was as effective as retrieval practice".

> "the explaining-contrast was not significant, F(1, 87) = 0.47, p = .496, ηp2 = .005, suggesting that explaining was as effective as retrieval practice"
