# Metacognitive ability increases with age during adolescence and plateaus in adulthood when task performance is held constant

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a cross-sectional sample aged 11–41, metacognitive ability (Aroc) showed a significant age × age group interaction, driven by a positive correlation between age and Aroc in adolescents (r = .38, p = .048) and a non-significant negative correlation in adults. [→ Weil L.G. 2013](#weil-lg-2013)

## Evidence

### Weil L.G. 2013

Weil L.G., Fleming S.M., Dumontheil I., Kilford E.J., Weil R.S., Rees G., Dolan R.J., Blakemore S.-J. (2013). The development of metacognitive ability in adolescence. Consciousness and Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2013.01.004

`q3 · i?`

Cross-sectional behavioural study of 28 adolescents (11–17 years) and 28 adults (19–41 years) on a visual pop-out task with confidence ratings. The age × age group interaction on Aroc was significant (F(1,52) = 6.11, p = .017), with "a significant positive correlation between age and Aroc in the adolescent group".

> "The interaction was driven by a significant positive correlation between age and Aroc in the adolescent group (r = .38, p = .048) (Fig. 2A) and a non-significant but negative correlation in the adult group (r = −.22, p = .25)."
