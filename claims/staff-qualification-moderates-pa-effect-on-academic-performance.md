# Physical activity interventions delivered by staff with higher professional qualifications show larger academic performance effects than those delivered by less qualified staff

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The weighted mean population effect was rw = 0.22 for interventions performed by staff with higher professional qualifications versus rw = 0.14 for staff with lower qualifications, a statistically significant difference (χ2 = 4.464; p = 0.035). [→ Sember Vedrana 2020](#sember-vedrana-2020)

## Evidence

### Sember Vedrana 2020

Sember Vedrana, Jurak Gregor, Kovač Marjeta, Morrison Shawnda A., Starc Gregor. (2020). Children's Physical Activity, Academic Performance, and Cognitive Functioning: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Frontiers in Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2020.00307

`q3 · i?`

Chi-square moderator analysis across the 44 included interventions comparing effects by staff qualification level. The article reports the higher-qualification group effect as 0.22 and the lower-qualification group effect as 0.14, with "a significant difference (χ2 = 4.464; p = 0.035)"; Cramer's V was 0.319.

> "Chi-square statistics were calculated and showed a significant difference (χ2 = 4.464; p = 0.035) on AP between studies in which the intervention and measurement were performed by practitioners with higher professional qualifications compared to those conducted by staff with lower professional qualifications"
