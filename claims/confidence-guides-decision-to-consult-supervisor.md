# Trainees use their sense of confidence (or near confidence) to decide whether to manage an encounter alone or consult their supervisor

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` First-year GP trainees used feelings of confidence or near confidence to decide whether to manage encounters independently or consult their supervisor, taking patient safety into account. [→ Sagasser MH 2016](#sagasser-mh-2016)

## Evidence

### Sagasser MH 2016

Sagasser MH, Kramer AWM, Fluit CRMG, van Weel C, van der Vleuten CPM. (2016). Self-entrustment: how trainees' self-regulated learning supports participation in the workplace. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-016-9723-4

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

From the observational study of seven supervisor-trainee pairs; interview and observation data showed trainees reporting either "a sense of confidence or a sense of near confidence" when managing encounters independently, sometimes temporising treatment provided the patient would not be put at risk.

> "Trainees used their sense of confidence to decide if they could manage the encounter alone or if they should consult their supervisor."
