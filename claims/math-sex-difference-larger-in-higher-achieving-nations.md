## Evidence

### Stoet G 2013

Stoet G, Geary DC (2013) Sex Differences in Mathematics and Reading Achievement Are Inversely Related: Within- and Across-Nation Assessment of 10 Years of PISA Data. PLoS ONE 8(3): e57988. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0057988

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of averaged mathematics sex differences between OECD and non-OECD countries (Figure 4, Table 4). The printed contrast was 5.4 points for non-OECD versus 10.5 points for OECD countries, t(73) = −2.5, p = .02; only the t statistic is printed, so no effect size is coded.

> "If anything, economically developed countries with strong gender-equality and human development scores tended to have a larger sex difference in mathematics than less economically developed countries (e.g., for non-OECD countries the sex difference in mathematics was 5.4 vs 10.5 points for OECD countries, t(73) = 22.5, p = .02, Figure 4)."

### Stoet G 2013 (2)

Stoet G, Geary DC (2013) Sex Differences in Mathematics and Reading Achievement Are Inversely Related: Within- and Across-Nation Assessment of 10 Years of PISA Data. PLoS ONE 8(3): e57988. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0057988

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Figure 4 and Table 4 analysis of non-OECD countries, where mathematics sex differences ranged between −15.0 and 30.0 points, with boys higher in some countries (e.g., Costa Rica) and girls higher in others (e.g., Albania).

> "Further, we found considerable variation among lower scoring countries, with some showing a large sex difference in mathematics achievement favoring boys and others favoring girls (Figure 4, Table 4)."
