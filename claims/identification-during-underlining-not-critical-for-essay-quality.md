# Appropriately identifying the author's statement and grounds while underlining was not associated with essay coherence or constructiveness scores

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2019 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` No significant differences in logical coherence or constructiveness between students who did and did not appropriately identify the argument while underlining. [→ Mochizuki 2019](#mochizuki-2019)

## Evidence

### Mochizuki 2019

Mochizuki, T., Nishimori, T., Tsubakimoto, M., Oura, H., Sato, T., Johansson, H., Nakahara, J., & Yamauchi, Y. (2019). Development of software to support argumentative reading and writing by means of creating a graphic organizer from an electronic text. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-019-09676-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

T tests across the 63 students compared those who identified the statement and grounds appropriately while underlining (coherence M = 4.93, constructiveness M = 4.73) with those who did not (M = 4.93, M = 4.71); no effect size was printed.

> "We found no significant differences (logical coherence: t (61) = 0.000, p = 1.00; constructiveness: t (61) = − 0.017, p = .986) between the students who identified the author's main statement and grounds appropriately while underlining and/or highlighting the text"
