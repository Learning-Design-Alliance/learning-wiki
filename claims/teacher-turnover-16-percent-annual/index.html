Evidence¶

Carver-Thomas 2017¶

Carver-Thomas, D. & Darling-Hammond, L. (2017). Teacher turnover: Why it matters and what we can do about it. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/teacher-turnover doi:10.54300/454.278

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive analysis of the nationally representative Schools and Staffing Survey (2011–12) and Teacher Follow-Up Survey (2012–13), reporting that "about 8% shift schools" and attrition is about 8%, yielding roughly 16% total turnover.

"In addition to the 8% of teachers who leave the profession each year, about 8% shift schools. Thus, the overall turnover rate is currently about 16%."

Related Claims¶

  • Between 2020–21 and 2021–22, 15.1% of U.S. public school teachers moved schools or left the profession, and turnover is now about 20% higher than in 1992 — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Teachers of color leave schools or the profession at higher annual rates than White teachers, driven largely by school moving — related
  • Teachers of color leave the teaching profession at higher rates than white teachers (about 19 percent vs. 15 percent turnover). — related
  • Teacher turnover is 35%–37% higher in schools with the largest concentrations of students of color and students from low-income backgrounds than in schools with the smallest — related
  • Dissatisfaction is the most-cited reason for leaving, cited by 55% of profession-leavers and 66% of school-movers — related
  • Alternatively certified teachers are 25% more likely to leave their schools and the profession, controlling for other factors — related
  • Turnover is higher in charter schools (17.8%) than traditional public schools (14.9%) and higher in city schools (17.7%) than other locales — related
  • Teachers with little or no pedagogical preparation are 2 to 3 times more likely to leave the profession than those with the most comprehensive preparation — related
  • Teaching has a relatively high turnover rate compared to nursing and national employee turnover benchmarks — related
  • Teacher attrition rose from 5.1% in 1992 to 8.4% in 2005 and has hovered around 8% since — related
  • Teachers of color are more likely than White teachers to intend to leave teaching and to actually leave the profession — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Occupational commitment is the strongest positive consequence and turnover intentions the strongest negative consequence of teacher wellbeing — related
