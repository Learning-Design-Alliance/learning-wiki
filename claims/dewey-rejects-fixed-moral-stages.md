# Dewey rejects Kohlberg's fixed stage hierarchy: moral inquiry happens only in problematic situations where no single action seems morally justified, and must be repeated as concrete situations arise

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q1` argument or single case

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Dewey disagreed with Kohlberg's sequenced six-stage model, holding that moral inquiry occurs only in problematic situations and that values cannot be obtained at once but require repeated inquiry in concrete situations. [→ Xiangdong Liu 2014](#xiangdong-liu-2014)

## Evidence

### Xiangdong Liu 2014

Xiangdong Liu. (2014). The Problem of Character Education and Kohlberg's Moral Education: Critique from Dewey's Moral Deliberation. Philosophical Studies in Education 45. https://www.ovpes.org/

`q1 · i?`

Philosophical critique in the section on what Dewey would say about Kohlberg's moral education. The article quotes Dewey that "moral theory cannot emerge when there is positive belief" and adds that moral inquiry needs to be done over and over in terms of concrete situations.

> "Dewey noted that “moral theory cannot emerge when there is positive belief as to what is right and what is wrong, for then there is no occasion for reflection.”"
