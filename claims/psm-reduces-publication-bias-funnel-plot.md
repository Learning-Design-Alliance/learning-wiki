# Funnel plot asymmetry indicates high publication bias before propensity score matching and low publication bias after matching

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Before matching, funnel plot data were asymmetrically distributed from the total mean effect size, indicating high publication bias; after matching, data were symmetrically distributed near the center line, indicating low publication bias. [→ Itsarangkul Na Ayutthaya 2022](#itsarangkul-na-ayutthaya-2022)

## Evidence

### Itsarangkul Na Ayutthaya 2022

Itsarangkul Na Ayutthaya, T., & Damrongpanit, S. (2022). A Meta-Analysis of Instructional Management Model for Students’ Creative Thinking Development: An Application of Propensity Score Matching. European Journal of Educational Research, 11(4), 2429-2444. https://doi.org/10.12973/eu-jer.11.4.2429

`q2 · i?`

Funnel plot analysis (Figures 4 and 5) of effect size dispersion before and after propensity score matching. The article states that before matching the data were "asymmetrically distributed from the total mean effect size", indicating high publication bias.

> "After the propensity score matching (Figure 5), it was found that most of the  effect sizes were positively distributed within the tr iangle and symmetrically distributed close to the center line, that was the total mean effect size. This indicates that the instructional models have a low effect on publication bias."
