# ChatGPT feedback significantly improved university students' answer scores in an AR quantum cryptography lab experiment, with a large effect

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2` · `i3` large
> **Currency** · newest study 2025 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` In a crossover design with 38 students, questions answered with ChatGPT feedback received significantly higher scores than questions answered without feedback (t=2.721, p=0.014, d=1.189). [→ Atakan Coban 2025](#atakan-coban-2025)

## Evidence

### Atakan Coban 2025

Atakan Coban, David Dzsotjan, Stefan Küchemann, Jürgen Durst, Jochen Kuhn & Christoph Hoyer. (2025). AI Support Meets AR Visualization for Alice and Bob: Personalized Learning Based on Individual ChatGPT Feedback in an AR Quantum Cryptography Experiment for Physics Lab Courses. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1140/epjqt/s40507-025-00310-z](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjqt/s40507-025-00310-z)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Crossover experiment with 38 physics lab students: Group A received ChatGPT feedback on Q1/Q3, Group B on Q2/Q4; D-scores compared via independent sample t-test, showing "a large effect (t= 2.721, p = 0.014, d = 1.189)" favouring feedback questions.

> "As Table 5 shows, the difference between the D-scores for the two groups is statistically significant with a large effect (t= 2.721, p = 0.014, d = 1.189). The D-scores of Group A are higher than those of Group B; therefore, the significant difference is in favour of the questions with feedback."
