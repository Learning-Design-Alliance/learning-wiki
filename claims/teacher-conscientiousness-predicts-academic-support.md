# Student-reported teacher conscientiousness is the strongest Big Five predictor of perceived teacher academic support in secondary mathematics and English classes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Mean student-reports of teacher conscientiousness were the strongest level-2 predictor of academic support for both mathematics (β = .74) and English (β = .67) teachers. [→ Kim 2018](#kim-2018)

## Evidence

### Kim 2018

Kim, Lisa, Dar-Nimrod, Ilan and MacCann, Carolyn (2018) Teacher Personality and Teacher Effectiveness in Secondary School: Personality Predicts Teacher Support and Student Self-Efficacy but not Academic Achievement. Journal of Educational Psychology. pp. 309-323. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000217

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel regressions on ratings from 2,082 students of their mathematics and English teachers, controlling for student gender, student personality and previous achievement at level 1 and teacher gender at level 2. Teacher-level variables predicted 90% and 79% of level-2 variance in mathematics and English academic support, with conscientiousness the strongest predictor ("β = .74 and .67, respectively").

> "At level 2, mean student-reports of teacher conscientiousness was the strongest predictor of academic support (β = .74 and .67, respectively) and agreeableness was the strongest predictor of personal support (β = .84 and .68, respectively), again in line with H4a and H4b."
