## Evidence

### Nkenke Emeka 2012

Nkenke Emeka, Vairaktaris Elefterios, Bauersachs Anne, Eitner Stephan, Budach Alexander, Knipfer Christoph, Stelzle Florian. (2012). Acceptance of technology-enhanced learning for a theoretical radiological science course: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-18

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre-course 30-item attitude questionnaire (1–6 scale) completed anonymously by both randomized groups (n = 21 each); group means did not differ significantly on any question. Both groups valued e-learning's time and place flexibility.

> "The answers to the questionnaire concerning attitudes and opinions towards face-to-face lectures and e-learning filled in during the introductory session did not show statistically significant differences between the traditional group and the technology-enhanced learning group (Table 1)."

### Nkenke Emeka 2012 (2)

Nkenke Emeka, Vairaktaris Elefterios, Bauersachs Anne, Eitner Stephan, Budach Alexander, Knipfer Christoph, Stelzle Florian. (2012). Acceptance of technology-enhanced learning for a theoretical radiological science course: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-18

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Attitude questionnaire items 5 and 8, answered by both groups before and after the course; both groups rated face-to-face lectures as the basis for learning (post-course means 4.1 and 5.0–5.2 on the 1–6 scale). Descriptive means only; no inferential test of change printed.

> "The answers of both groups indicated that face-to-face lectures were still considered the basis for learning at university and that the lecturer had a strong influence on the students' interest in a specific subject (Question 5 and Question 8, Table 1)."
