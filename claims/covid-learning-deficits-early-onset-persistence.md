## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The magnitude of learning deficits shows no significant change over months since the pandemic onset (βmonths = −0.00, P = 0.097), indicating deficits opened early and persisted. [→ Bastian A. Betthäuser 2023](#bastian-a-betthauser-2023)

## Evidence

### Bastian A. Betthäuser 2023

Bastian A. Betthäuser, Anders M. Bach-Mortensen & Per Engzell. (2023). A systematic review and meta-analysis of the evidence on learning during the COVID-19 pandemic. Nature Human Behaviour. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-022-01506-4

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Bivariate ordinary least squares regression of deficit magnitude on measurement date across 291 estimates, with standard errors clustered at the study level (42 clusters), shown in Fig. 4. The slope is not significantly different from zero (P = 0.097).

> "We find no evidence that the slope coefficient is different from zero (βmonths = −0.00, t(41) = −7.30, two-tailed P = 0.097, 95% CI −0.01 to 0.00)."

### Bastian A. Betthäuser 2023 (2)

Bastian A. Betthäuser, Anders M. Bach-Mortensen & Per Engzell. (2023). A systematic review and meta-analysis of the evidence on learning during the COVID-19 pandemic. Nature Human Behaviour. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-022-01506-4

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Supplementary within-country analyses for the United States, the UK and the Netherlands replicate the persistent-deficit pattern. The authors caution that estimates are based on distinct samples of students, not followed cohorts.

> "As shown in Supplementary Fig. 8, the pattern of persistent learning deficits also emerges within each of the three countries for which we have a relatively large number of estimates at different timepoints: the United States, the UK and the Netherlands."
