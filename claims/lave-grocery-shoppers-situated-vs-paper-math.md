## Evidence

### Peter Smagorinsky 1995

Peter Smagorinsky. (1995). The Social Construction of Data: Methodological Problems of Investigating Learning in the Zone of Proximal Development. Review of Educational Research, 65(3), 191-212. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543065003191

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Narrative review attributing this to Lave, Murtaugh, and de la Rocha (1984), who accompanied shoppers and tape recorded their calculations as they sought the best prices. The article reports the shoppers answered "fewer than half of the problems correctly" on the paper test; no effect size is printed.

> "the shoppers were virtually infallible in their mathematics in this situated activity. Yet these same people, when tested on identical mathematical problems presented in abstract form through the means of a paper-and-pencil test, performed quite poorly"

### Peter Smagorinsky 1995 (2)

Peter Smagorinsky. (1995). The Social Construction of Data: Methodological Problems of Investigating Learning in the Zone of Proximal Development. Review of Educational Research, 65(3), 191-212. https://doi.org/10.2307/1170682

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article reports Lave, Murtaugh, and de la Rocha's (1984) study, in which researchers accompanied shoppers and tape recorded their price calculations; shoppers were 'virtually infallible' in situated activity but answered 'fewer than half of the problems correctly' on the abstract test. Attributed as the article attributes it.

> "The researchers found that the shoppers were virtually infallible in their mathematics in this situated activity. Yet these same people, when tested on identical mathematical problems presented in abstract form through the means of a paper-and-pencil test, performed quite poorly, answering fewer than half of the problems correctly."
