## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After normalizing grades for time of day, owls show a significant academic performance deficit compared to larks and finches at all class times. [→ Smarr 2018](#smarr-2018)

## Evidence

### Smarr 2018

Smarr, B. L., & Schirmer, A. E. (2018). 3.4 million real-world learning management system logins reveal the majority of students experience social jet lag correlated with decreased performance. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-23044-8

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

N-way ANOVA and Kruskal-Wallis post-hoc comparisons of class grades by chronotype category and class start time, with grades normalized within class times to control for time-of-day effects. "owls showed a significant academic performance deficit at all class times (χ2 = 114, p = 2 × 10−25)". No standardized effect size is printed.

> "Effect of chronotype remained significant, with similar overall differences as described above: owls showed a significant academic performance deficit at all class times (χ2 = 114, p = 2 × 10−25), and larks significantly underperformed finches only in the morning (p < 0.05)."
