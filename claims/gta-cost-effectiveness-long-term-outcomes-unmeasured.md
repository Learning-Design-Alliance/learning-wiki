## Evidence

### Janjua Aisha 2018

Janjua Aisha, Roberts Tracy, Okeahialam Nicola, Clark T Justin. (2018). Cost-effective analysis of teaching pelvic examination skills using Gynaecology Teaching Associates (GTAs) compared with manikin models (The CEAT Study). BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2017-015823

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Stated limitation of the economic evaluation: outcomes were limited to "competence at the end of the placement", and long-term outcomes including patient safety and satisfaction "were not feasible within this study". Student and patient perspectives were also not evaluated.

> "The economic analysis reflects short-term outcomes, that is, competence at the end of the placement. Long-term outcomes such as proficiency as a qualified junior doctor, patient safety, costs of unnecessary investigations ordered and patient satisfaction were not feasible within this study."

### Janjua Aisha 2018 (2)

Janjua Aisha, Roberts Tracy, Okeahialam Nicola, Clark T Justin. (2018). Cost-effective analysis of teaching pelvic examination skills using Gynaecology Teaching Associates (GTAs) compared with manikin models (The CEAT Study). BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2017-015823

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The authors note that unlike clinical interventions with accepted cost-effectiveness thresholds, "there is no predefined threshold with which to compare the results of this study", so policy makers must judge the value of GTA teaching themselves.

> "since the results of cost-effectiveness analysis presented here are in natural units and not QALYS there is no predefined threshold with which to compare the results of this study which makes interpretation difficult."
