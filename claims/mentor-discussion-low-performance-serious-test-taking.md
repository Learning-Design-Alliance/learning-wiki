## Subclaims
`q2 i3` When low test performance is discussed with the mentor, the odds of taking the BPT seriously increase by 1423% (OR = 15.23) compared with no such discussion, controlling for other strategies and design factors. [→ Schüttpelz-Brauns 2020](#schuttpelz-brauns-2020)

## Evidence

### Schüttpelz-Brauns 2020

Schüttpelz-Brauns, K., Hecht, M., Hardt, K., Karay, Y., Zupanic, M., & Kämmer, J. E. (2020). Institutional strategies related to test-taking behavior in low stakes assessment. Advances in Health Sciences Education, 25(2), 321–335. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-019-09928-y

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of 108,140 BPT participations at nine medical schools using a mixed effects logistic regression with Medical School, Wave, Student and Medical School × Wave controls; the printed effect size for discussing results with the mentor is OR = 15.23 (95% CI 7.53–30.80, p < 0.001), a "1423%" increase in odds.

> "When the results of low test performance are discussed with the mentor, the odds of taking the BPT seriously are increased by 1423% as compared to a situation where the results of low test performance are not discussed with the mentor."
