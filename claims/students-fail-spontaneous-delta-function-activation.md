# Many junior-level electrostatics students do not spontaneously activate the delta function as the appropriate tool when the problem does not explicitly prompt it

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2014

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2014). Student Difficulties with the Dirac Delta Function. [doi:10.1119/perc.2014.pr.064](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.064)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Think-aloud interview data targeting the Activation element A2, which the exams did not assess. Participants were asked for a volume charge density of a line charge without delta functions being mentioned: "only 2 of 5 interview participants suggested the use of delta functions"; the others expressed confusion.

> "When presented with the question shown in Fig. 1(b), only 2 of 5 interview participants suggested the use of delta functions. The remaining three participants all expressed confusion at being asked to provide a volume charge density of a 1-dimensional charge distribution."

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2015

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2015). Upper-division student difficulties with the Dirac delta function. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.010108](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.010108)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Think-aloud interviews with students who had completed E&M 1 one to two semesters prior used an A2-type prompt asking for the volume charge density of a line charge. Only "2 of 5 interviewees spontaneously suggested using δ-functions"; the other three expressed confusion.

> "When presented with the A2-type prompt shown in Sec. II C, 2 of 5 interviewees spontaneously suggested using δ-functions."
