# The eikonal equation of geometrical optics has the same form as the conservative Hamilton-Jacobi equation, with the mechanical refractive index equal to √(2m(E−V))

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the geometrical-optics limit (λ0 → 0) the eikonal equation |∇L|² = n² takes the same form as the Hamilton-Jacobi equation |∇W|² = 2m[E − V(r)], so Hamilton's characteristic function W plays the role of the eikonal L and √(2m(E−V)) plays the role of the refractive index. `q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Analytical comparison drawn in Section V between the eikonal equation (28), obtained by taking λ0 → 0 in the wave equation for an inhomogeneous medium, and the conservative Hamilton-Jacobi equation (12). The identification completes the analogy between Hamilton's and Fermat's principles since p = √(2m(E−V)). No empirical content; no n.

> "Looking at both equations we see that Hamilton's characteristic function W plays the same role than the eikonal L, and that the expression √ 2m(E −V ) plays the role of the index of refraction."
