# Below-norm students' pass rates improved post-policy because their skills relative to classroom peers improved under sorting

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Below-norm students were less likely to pass if their skill levels were well below the classroom average (coefficient of –.25); their relative skill levels improved post-policy, so their pass rates improved from .71 to .88 logits. ## Evidence

### Takako Nomi and Elaine M. Allensworth 2013

Takako Nomi and Elaine M. Allensworth. (2013). Sorting and Supporting: Why Double-Dose Algebra Led to Better Test Scores but More Course Failures. American Educational Research Journal. https://doi.org/10.3102/0002831212469997

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Logistic models predicting pass rates for below-norm students. "below-norm students were less likely to pass if their skill levels were well below the classroom average" — pass rates increased from .71 logits pre-policy (69 percent) to .88 logits (71 percent) post-policy.

> "below-norm students were less likely to pass if their skill levels were well below the classroom average (coefficient of –.25). As discussed earlier, relative skill levels of below-norm students improved post-policy (i.e., they were less like to be well below their classmates than pre-policy below-norm students), so their pass rates improved."
