Robin Schmucker, Meng Xia, Amos Azaria, and Tom Mitchell. (2024). Ruffle&Riley: Insights from Designing and Evaluating a Large Language Model-Based Conversational Tutoring System. [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-64302-6_6](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-64302-6_6)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of temporal usage of conversation, scrolling, and help-request features among 31 R&R participants who completed the activity (Figure 4, Table 5) identified four usage patterns. The conversation-focused group (n = 4) showed relative gain 0.65 ± 0.09 versus help-focused (n = 3) 0.28 ± 0.09; the article states sample sizes are too small to draw conclusions.

> "While sample sizes are too small to draw conclusions, we observe that the conversation-focused users (group (III)) achieve the highest learning gains. Further, the non-help seeking users (group (II) and (III)) achieve higher performance measures than the help-seeking users (group (I) and (IV))."
