# GTAs adopted pedagogical jargon from the course but provided minimal explanations of how active-engagement strategies would impact student learning

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Postcourse maps included terms such as metacognition, active learning, and formative assessment in 7 or 8 of 8 maps, but GTAs gave superficial descriptions without details of how or why these would improve learning, so terms were coded instructive rather than reform based. ## Evidence

### Star W. Lee 2019

Star W. Lee. (2019). The Impact of a Pedagogy Course on the Teaching Beliefs of Inexperienced Graduate Teaching Assistants. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.18-07-0137

`q3 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative analysis of postcourse concept map presentations in Biology 301. The article reports GTAs "provided superficial descriptions of the terms without any details of how or why those items would improve student learning", so active learning and formative assessment were coded as instructive.

> "GTAs provided a definition for metacognition, but they did not explain how or why metacognition would occur with active students."
