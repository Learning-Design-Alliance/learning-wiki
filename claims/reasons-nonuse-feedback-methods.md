## Evidence

### Kunz Kevin 2019

Kunz Kevin, Burkert Mirka, Heindl Felix, Schüttpelz-Brauns Katrin, Giesler Marianne. (2019). The frequency of using certain feedback methods in the teaching of medicine: a survey of teachers at the medical faculties in Baden-Wurttemberg. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001253](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001253)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Survey item asking respondents who never used a method to give one reason per method (Table 1, N=464). "A total of 76% of the instructors reported that they did not use checklists", most often because the method is unfamiliar (56%); 65% did not use written feedback (31% unnecessary, 23% time constraints), and 81% did not use technology-based feedback (24% unfamiliar, 35% technical reasons).

> "A total of 76% of the instructors reported that they did not use checklists. The reason most often cited for this is that the method is unfamiliar (56%)."
