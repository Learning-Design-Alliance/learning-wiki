## Evidence

### Burgess Annette 2014

Burgess Annette, Roberts Chris, Clark Tyler, Mossman Karyn. (2014). The social validity of a national assessment centre for selection into general practice training. BMC Medical Education 14:261. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0261-6

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Survey results section reports thematic prevalence from the candidate questionnaire. Respondents valued the MMI as it "allowed them to 'explain and discuss answers'" and to highlight past skills and experience.

> "It appeared that candidates placed greater value on the MMI questions and the face to face opportunity for interactions with interviewers, with 384/886 (43%) of survey respondents commenting that the interviews allowed them to "explain and discuss answers""

### Burgess Annette 2014 (2)

Burgess Annette, Roberts Chris, Clark Tyler, Mossman Karyn. (2014). The social validity of a national assessment centre for selection into general practice training. BMC Medical Education 14:261. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0261-6

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Second thematic result from the same survey: 62/886 (7%) valued being assessed on "different skills both personal and professional" beyond knowledge and clinical skills.

> "Candidates felt that they were able to display relevant attributes other than knowledge and clinical skills, with 62/886 (7%) of survey respondents commenting that they were assessed "on different skills both personal and professional"."
