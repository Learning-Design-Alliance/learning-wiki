## Evidence

### Foppoli 2018

Foppoli, A., Choudhary, R., Blair, D., Kaur, T., Moschilla, J. and Zadnik, M. (2018). Public and Teacher Response to Einsteinian Physics in Schools. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/aae4a4](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/aae4a4)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Post-program student attitudinal questionnaire (seven statements, N=20) at the Public Outreach Workshop; Table 2 shows 100% strongly agreeing they wish they could do experiments like this at school and 70% agreement on wanting to learn the modern ideas at school.

> "The next three statements (2(c), 2(d) and 2(e)) relate to the curriculum and show that students strongly agree that the material should be in the curriculum."

### Foppoli 2018 (2)

Foppoli, A., Choudhary, R., Blair, D., Kaur, T., Moschilla, J. and Zadnik, M. (2018). Public and Teacher Response to Einsteinian Physics in Schools. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/aae4a4](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/aae4a4)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same student questionnaire (N=20), statement 2(g) posed negatively; 35% strongly disagreed, 20% somewhat disagreed, while 40% agreed they were too young, giving mixed results after the brief informal introduction.

> "Statement 2(g) tested young people's views on ability to understand modern physics at their age. It was deliberately asked in a negative context. Overall, 55% of students disagreed that they were too young."
