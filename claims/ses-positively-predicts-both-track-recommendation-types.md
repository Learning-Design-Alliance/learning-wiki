# Students' SES is significantly positively related to both test‐based and judgement‐based track recommendations even after controlling for prior performance, with a small impact that does not differ between the two types

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` SES is significantly positively related to both test‐based and judgement‐based track recommendations after controlling for students' prior performance, and the impact of SES is small. [→ van Leest 2021](#van-leest-2021)
`q2 i?` The impact of SES on track recommendations does not differ significantly between test‐based and judgement‐based recommendations. [→ van Leest 2021 (2)](#van-leest-2021-2)

## Evidence

### van Leest 2021

van Leest, A., Hornstra, L., van Tartwijk, J., & van de Pol, J. (2021). Test‐ or judgement‐based school track recommendations: Equal opportunities for students with different socio‐economic backgrounds? The British Journal of Educational Psychology, 91(1), 193–216. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12356

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multilevel regression models of 8,639 Dutch grade 6 students from 105 primary schools showed SES significantly positively related to both recommendation types 'even after controlling for students' prior performance', with 'the impact of SES was small' for both types.

> "However, the impact of SES was small (test‐based recommendations: b  = .04, p  = .016; judgement‐based recommendations: b  = .05, p < .001)."

### van Leest 2021 (2)

van Leest, A., Hornstra, L., van Tartwijk, J., & van de Pol, J. (2021). Test‐ or judgement‐based school track recommendations: Equal opportunities for students with different socio‐economic backgrounds? The British Journal of Educational Psychology, 91(1), 193–216. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12356

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

A Wald test comparing the SES coefficients across the two multilevel models found 'no significant difference between the two types of recommendations regarding to the impact of SES' (z = −.50, p = .309).

> "The difference between test‐based and judgement‐based recommendations was not significant (z = −.50, p = .309), indicating that there was no significant difference between the two types of recommendations regarding to the impact of SES."
