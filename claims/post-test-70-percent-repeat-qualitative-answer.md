## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On a pre/post test question about the relevance of area in Kepler's 2nd law, all 10 students had the qualitative understanding before instruction, and post-instruction 70% repeated the pre-test answer while 30% gave answers tying area to finding the planet, time, or the absence of equal angles. ## Evidence

### Lyra 2020

Lyra, W. (2020). A Historical Method Approach to Teaching Kepler's 2nd law. New Mexico State University. [doi:10.35542/osf.io/a5bqn](https://doi.org/10.35542/osf.io/a5bqn)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre-and-post test in the 2019 graduate class of 10 students (Table 1), administered three weeks after instruction and after the Celestial Mechanics module. The text reports 70% repeated the pre-test answer and 30% changed. No effect size or significance test is printed.

> "Post-instruction, the answers varied little from the pre-test, with 70% of the students repeating the "equal areas equal times" response of the pre-test. 30% showed a different answer: that area "allows position of the planet to be determined", "is time", and "shows there is no reference point from which an object appears to orbit equal angles in equal times"."
