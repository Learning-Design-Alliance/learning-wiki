## Evidence

### Robin Schmucker 2024

Robin Schmucker, Meng Xia, Amos Azaria, and Tom Mitchell. (2024). Ruffle&Riley: Insights from Designing and Evaluating a Large Language Model-Based Conversational Tutoring System. [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-64302-6_6](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-64302-6_6)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Evaluation 2 compared R&R (34 filtered participants) and reading (38 filtered participants) on a six-question post-test with pre-test, absolute and normalized learning gains. One-sided t-tests found "do not detect significant differences (p <0.05) for any of the four measures"; no effect sizes were printed, and equivalence was not tested.

> "Comparing R&R and reading condition via one-sided t-tests, we do not detect significant differences (p <0.05) for any of the four measures. Thus, we do not find support for H1."

### Robin Schmucker 2024 (2)

Robin Schmucker, Meng Xia, Amos Azaria, and Tom Mitchell. (2024). Ruffle&Riley: Insights from Designing and Evaluating a Large Language Model-Based Conversational Tutoring System. [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-64302-6_6](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-64302-6_6)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Evaluation 2 reports mean learning times of 20.8 minutes for R&R versus 5.5 minutes for reading; the authors note engagement scores "might not be directly comparable due to large differences in learning times."

> "Engagement scores might not be directly comparable due to large differences in learning times (R&R (20.8min), reading (5.5min))."
