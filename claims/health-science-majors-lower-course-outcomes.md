## Evidence

### Mason 2018

Mason, A. J., & Bertram, C. A. (2018). Consideration of Learning Orientations as an Application of Achievement Goals in Evaluating Life Science Majors in Introductory Physics. University of Central Arkansas, Department of Physics and Astronomy. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010125](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010125)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

FCI analysis (Table III) shows health science majors lowest on pretest and posttest among the three major groups, with gains (+.11) below biology (+.17) and other natural science (+.20). Differences on pretest are "p < .05, each comparison"; gains comparison is p < .02. No effect size printed.

> "the normalized gain for health science majors is statistically lower than respective gains for both groups of natural science students (p < .02, each comparison)."

### Mason 2018 (2)

Mason, A. J., & Bertram, C. A. (2018). Consideration of Learning Orientations as an Application of Achievement Goals in Evaluating Life Science Majors in Introductory Physics. University of Central Arkansas, Department of Physics and Astronomy. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010125](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010125)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Overall course grade comparison (Table V): Biology (n = 91) averages 3.18 (SE 0.08) versus Health Science (n = 85) at 2.58 (SE 0.10), p < 0.00001; biology also outperforms other natural science majors (p < 0.02).

> "When considering students by choice of major, Health Science majors average almost two thirds of a letter grade below Biology majors."
