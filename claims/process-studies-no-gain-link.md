# Process-oriented SLA studies do not demonstrate a link between modified interaction and language gains

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Studies showing that tasks or question types elicit more modified interaction do not measure whether that interaction produces language gains. [→ Sadtono 1991](#sadtono-1991)

## Evidence

### Sadtono 1991

Sadtono, Eugenius, Ed. (1991). Language Acquisition and the Second/Foreign Language Classroom. Anthology Series 28. SEAMEO Regional Language Centre. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED367161

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Nunan's critical review of process-oriented studies such as Doughty and Pica (1986), which established that two-way information gap tasks prompted more modified interaction than one-way tasks, but did not test acquisition outcomes. The review states the research "does not demonstrate (nor was it intended to demonstrate) a relationship between modified interaction and language gains".

> "This type of research does not demonstrate (nor was it intended to demonstrate) a relationship between modified interaction and language gains."
