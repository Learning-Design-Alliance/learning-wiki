# An eHealth care experiential learning program significantly improves eHealth literacy among patients with type 2 diabetes, with effects persisting 3 months after the intervention

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The intervention group showed a significantly greater increase in eHealth literacy than the control group at both the immediate posttest (β=19.94, SE 3.52; P<.001) and the 3-month follow-up (β=18.19, SE 3.82; P<.001). [→ Yu-Shan Cheng 2024](#yu-shan-cheng-2024)

## Evidence

### Yu-Shan Cheng 2024

Yu-Shan Cheng, Cheng-Pei Lin, Lu-Yen Anny Chen, Wei-Ren Hwang, Yi-Chun Lin, Yu-Chi Chen. (2024). Short-Term Effects of an eHealth Care Experiential Learning Program Among Patients With Type 2 Diabetes: Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/53509

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

GEE analysis by intention-to-treat in a single-blind RCT of 92 patients with type 2 diabetes (46 per group), with sex and economic status as covariates. The group×time contrast favored the intervention at both time points: "β=19.94, SE 3.52; P<.001" at T1 and "β=18.19, SE 3.82; P<.001" at T2.

> "Specifically, the improvement in the intervention group was greater than that in the control group (β=19.94, SE 3.52; P<.001 and β=18.19, SE 3.82; P<.001) at T1 and T2. These findings indicate that the intervention positively improved eHL, which persisted for up to 3 months afterward."
