## Evidence

### L. Gajewski 2016

L. Gajewski, J. Chołoniewski, J. A. Hołyst. (2016). Key courses of academic curriculum uncovered by data mining of students' grades. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.12693/aphyspola.129.1071](https://doi.org/10.12693/aphyspola.129.1071)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Maximal spanning trees built from course-grade correlations for merged classes J, K, L (Fig. 2, FP1 degree 9) and for class L alone (Fig. 3, degree 6); FP1 remains central "in all ca ses", with FP2 and AG as local hubs of degrees 4 and 5 in Fig. 2.

> "In all ca ses however the course FP1 remains a central node of MST and the courses FP 2 and AG are close to it. It suggests that this triple of courses lay s a foundation of knowledge for the students"

### L. Gajewski 2016 (2)

L. Gajewski, J. Chołoniewski, J. A. Hołyst. (2016). Key courses of academic curriculum uncovered by data mining of students' grades. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.12693/aphyspola.129.1071](https://doi.org/10.12693/aphyspola.129.1071)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pearson correlation analysis of course grades (Fig. 1a heat map); the same three courses also have the largest sums of correlation coefficients with other courses (C_FP1 = 12.29, C_FP2 = 11.52, C_AG = 10.93).

> "There is a triple of courses FP1, FP2 and AG that are strongly correlated one to another, i.e. CF P 1,F P 2 = 0.73, CF P 1,AG = 0.7 and CF P 2,AG = 0.62."
