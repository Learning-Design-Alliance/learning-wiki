## Evidence

### Robinson SJA 2024

Robinson SJA, Ritchie AMA, Pacilli M, Nestel D, McLeod E, Nataraja RM. (2024). Simulation-Based Education of Health Workers in Low- and Middle-Income Countries: A Systematic Review. Glob Health Sci Pract. https://doi.org/10.9745/GHSP-D-24-00187

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Dean et al., a study across 5 sub-Saharan African countries in which 50 trainee ophthalmologists received a 5-day simulation program (synthetic eyes, feedback, deliberate practice, reflective learning) or standard training exclusively. The review prints "7.8% vs. 26.6%, P<.001"; no effect size is given.

> "Following completion, clinical outcomes and a validated competency assessment were compared between groups, and posterior capsule rupture rates were significantly lower among the simulation group (7.8% vs. 26.6%, P<.001)."

### Robinson SJA 2024 (2)

Robinson SJA, Ritchie AMA, Pacilli M, Nestel D, McLeod E, Nataraja RM. (2024). Simulation-Based Education of Health Workers in Low- and Middle-Income Countries: A Systematic Review. Glob Health Sci Pract. https://doi.org/10.9745/GHSP-D-24-00187

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Same review-narrated trial: competency scores significantly higher at 3 and 12 months (P<.001), with the difference normalizing only at the 15-month assessment, indicating decay of the competency advantage over time.

> "Competency assessment scores were significantly higher after 3 months and 12 months (P<.001). It was not until the 15-month assessment that the difference in scores normalized."
