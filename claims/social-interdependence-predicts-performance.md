# Social interdependence, not role interdependence or specialization, correlates with performance and well-being

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · `q2` quasi-experiment · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Performance indicators are unrelated to role interdependence and specialization but correlate rather strongly with social interdependence. [→ Pennings 1974](#pennings-1974)
`q2 i1` Greater team belongingness and harmony accompany less psychological strain (correlation -.35 with an anxiety scale). [→ Pennings 1974 (2)](#pennings-1974-2)

## Evidence

### Pennings 1974

Pennings, Johannes M. (1974). Differentiation, Interdependence, and Performance in Formal Organizations. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED099988

`q2 · i1`

Analysis of company-record performance criteria (market share, productivity, efficiency, turnover) and questionnaire psychological outcomes across the 40 offices. The article reports the indicators "correlateratherstrongly withsocial interdependence", including new customers acquired and money lost to transactional errors, standardized for office size.

> "Althoughtheseperformanceindicators are notrelated torole inter- dependence andspecializationthey correlateratherstrongly withsocial interdependence."

### Pennings 1974 (2)

Pennings, Johannes M. (1974). Differentiation, Interdependence, and Performance in Formal Organizations. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED099988

`q2 · i1`

Correlation between the social interdependence measure and an anxiety scale (feelings of having a jumpy job, sleeplessness from job-related strain) was -.35, indicating social interdependence is conducive to anxiety reduction.

> "the greater the feelingof teambelonginess andharmony, theless prevalent arefeelings ofpsychologicalstrain."
