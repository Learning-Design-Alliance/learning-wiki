## Evidence

### Subih Maha 2025

Subih Maha, Rababa Majdi, Aryan Fatima Sabri, Alnaeem Mohammad, AlRahahleh Mohammad H, Al niarat Tahany Fareed, Saleh Zyad T., Alsulami Ghaida Shujayyi, Almagharbeh Wesam T., Elshatarat Rami A. (2025). Factors influencing nurses’ knowledge and competence in warfarin-drug and nutrient interactions and patient counseling practices. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07074-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cross-sectional survey of 176 Jordanian nurses using an 18-item warfarin–food interaction subscale (mean 12.27, SD 3.84 out of 18). The article reports these item-level percentages as printed, contrasting them with over 90% correct for rice and potato.

> "only 60.8% correctly identified spinach as a food that may interfere with warfarin, with 39.2% of participants incorrectly responding. Similarly, just under half of the participants recognized parsley (46.0% correct) and lettuce (46.6% correct) as interacting foods, and only 19.9% correctly identified cabbage"

### Subih Maha 2025 (2)

Subih Maha, Rababa Majdi, Aryan Fatima Sabri, Alnaeem Mohammad, AlRahahleh Mohammad H, Al niarat Tahany Fareed, Saleh Zyad T., Alsulami Ghaida Shujayyi, Almagharbeh Wesam T., Elshatarat Rami A. (2025). Factors influencing nurses’ knowledge and competence in warfarin-drug and nutrient interactions and patient counseling practices. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07074-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same cross-sectional survey, beverage and non-interfering items. The article also reports "only 14.8% correctly identifying that these items do not interfere with warfarin" for carrots and fats, interpreting this as overestimation of interacting foods.

> "While 73.9% correctly recognized grapefruit juice as an interacting beverage, only 40.9% knew that green tea could interfere with warfarin, with the remaining 59.1% responding incorrectly"
