Quasi-experimental study (Omlor et al., 2022) summarized in the review's VR table: students watched 3D clinical procedure videos via VR headsets or 2D on standard devices, then took knowledge and self-assessment tests. "No significant difference in learning success" was found; equivalence was not tested.

"No significant difference in learning success between the immersive and non-immersive groups was found. However, students in the immersive virtual reality group rated the course more positively in terms of clarity, learning atmosphere and overall satisfaction."

Related Claims¶

  • Immersive procedural training in VR shows a significant medium positive effect on learning outcomes compared to less immersive environments (Hedges' g = 0.58) — reports the opposite
  • Immersion principle: people do not necessarily learn better in 3D immersive virtual reality than with a corresponding 2D desktop presentation (d = -0.10 across 9 tests) — related
  • Review syntheses: VR/AR most often improved academic achievement and motivation, but sometimes performed no better than conventional technologies — related
  • Immersion in virtual reality adds extraneous load yet can boost transfer, enjoyment, and motivation while leaving retention unaffected — related
  • 3D virtual-reality lecture was statistically non-inferior to 2D lecture on knowledge and rated better on spatial understanding and enjoyment (Kockro et al.) — related
