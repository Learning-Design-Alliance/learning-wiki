## Evidence

### Nicholas Munyan-Penney and Sarah Mehrotra 2023

Nicholas Munyan-Penney and Sarah Mehrotra. (2023). Future of Assessments: Centering Equity and the Lived Experiences of Students, Families, and Educators. Ed Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED628034

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Pre-focus group caregiver survey reported with Figure 2, which shows 47% not helpful at all, 13% mostly not helpful, 26% somewhat helpful, and 13% very helpful. The printed figure is that "40% of caregivers said they were very helpful or somewhat helpful"; no inferential statistic is reported.

> "In our pre-focus group survey, when we asked how helpful statewide assessments were for keeping a child on track for college after high school, 40% of caregivers said they were very helpful or somewhat helpful (see Figure 2)."

### Nicholas Munyan-Penney and Sarah Mehrotra 2023 (2)

Nicholas Munyan-Penney and Sarah Mehrotra. (2023). Future of Assessments: Centering Equity and the Lived Experiences of Students, Families, and Educators. Ed Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED628034

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative finding from the caregiver focus groups, part of the same 50-group study. The report states that "Black parents said more often that assessments were generally helpful to their child"; this is a comparative qualitative observation with no effect size.

> "In comparison to Latino and White parents, Black parents said more often that assessments were generally helpful to their child and a useful tool to understand their child’s academic progress and college readiness."
