## Evidence

### El Ashry M 2026

El Ashry M, El Ashry A, Khalique H, Abdalle Y, Yeung T. (2026). Augmented Reality in Surgical Training: Systematic Review of Its Impact on Technical Performance in Surgical Trainees. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/71572

`q3 · i3` · `review · r2`

Crossover RCT (n=40 MIS-naive medical students) of iSurgeon AR telestration versus verbal guidance on laparoscopic tasks; the review reports "a mean of 0.18 errors per task compared with 1.94" with P<.01 and ηp²=0.92.

> "Felinska et al [4] demonstrated the most dramatic reduction, with AR-instructed trainees making a mean of 0.18 errors per task compared with 1.94 for the verbal instruction group (P<.01; ηp²=0.92), representing a tenfold reduction in error rate."

### El Ashry M 2026 (2)

El Ashry M, El Ashry A, Khalique H, Abdalle Y, Yeung T. (2026). Augmented Reality in Surgical Training: Systematic Review of Its Impact on Technical Performance in Surgical Trainees. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/71572

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Crossover RCT (n=21 medical students) comparing HoloLens 2 step-by-step AR guidance with conventional paper/video SOP for ECMO cannulation; the review reports the 66% knowledge-error reduction with handling errors unchanged.

> "Wolf et al [1] found that AR-based extracorporeal membrane oxygenation cannulation instructions resulted in a 66% reduction in knowledge-related errors for the more complex second procedure (18 vs 53 errors; P<.05), while handling errors were unchanged"

### El Ashry M 2026 (3)

El Ashry M, El Ashry A, Khalique H, Abdalle Y, Yeung T. (2026). Augmented Reality in Surgical Training: Systematic Review of Its Impact on Technical Performance in Surgical Trainees. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/71572

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Parallel RCT (n=40 medical students) across 10 ex vivo laparoscopic cholecystectomies comparing iSurgeon AR telestration with verbal guidance; the review reports the CVS contrast of 79.5% versus 41.4% (P≤.001).

> "Cizmic et al [3] reported that the iSurgeon group incurred significantly fewer total complications and achieved the critical view of safety in 79.5% of procedures compared with only 41.4% in the verbal guidance group (P≤.001)."
