# In a laboratory word-learning task, introducing feedback in the second half of learning improved immediate meaning-extraction performance, while withdrawing feedback produced no significant drop on day 1

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` When participants who began without feedback started receiving feedback in the second half, immediate meaning-extraction performance improved substantially (67.7% to 75.1%), while withdrawal produced only a small, non-significant drop (68.4% to 66.7%). [→ Vavra 2023](#vavra-2023)

## Evidence

### Vavra 2023

Vavra, P., Sokolovič, L., Porcu, E., Ripollés, P., Rodriguez-Fornells, A., & Noesselt, T. (2023). Entering into a self-regulated learning mode prevents detrimental effects of feedback removal on memory. npj Science of Learning, 8(1). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00150-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

GLMM on day-1 learning performance in the laboratory experiment (N = 60) with feedback, congruence (within) and order (between). The feedback-by-order interaction was significant (χ2(1) = 4.42, p = 0.04), with improvement only when feedback was "added in the second half of the experiment".

> "When participants who began without feedback started receiving feedback in the second half of the experiment, their performance improved substantially (67.7% to 75.1% when collapsing across incongruent and congruent trials, odds-ratio = 0.696 ± 0.069, p = 0.0003)"
