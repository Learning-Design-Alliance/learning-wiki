# Full alignment of recording, expected, and task angles yields significantly higher task performance than any non-full-alignment configuration

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` When all three angles were aligned (all first-person or all third-person), task performance was significantly higher than without full alignment. [→ de Jonge 2026](#de-jonge-2026)

## Evidence

### de Jonge 2026

de Jonge, T. H. S., de Koning, B. B., & van Wermeskerken, M. (2026). It's all a matter of expectancy: How expectancies about task execution perspective influence learning through video modeling examples. Learning and Instruction, 105, 102422. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2026.102422

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc linear mixed-effects contrast including a three-way match dummy variable, which added significant explained variance (ΔR2 = 0.04, p < .001). Full alignment of all three angles outperformed non-full alignment, "t (126) = 4.33, p < .001"; no standardized effect size was printed for this contrast.

> "it was observed that when all angles were aligned – either all first-person or all third-person – task performance was significantly higher compared to when there was no full alignment, t (126) = 4.33, p < .001"
