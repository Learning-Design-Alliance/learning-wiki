## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Changes exceeding these between-day MDC thresholds indicate genuine skill change rather than natural variation, providing reference values for interpreting skill decay studies. [→ Almeida Debora 2020](#almeida-debora-2020)

## Evidence

### Almeida Debora 2020

Almeida Debora, Clark Carol, Jones Michael, McConnell Phillip, Williams Jonathan. (2020). Consistency and variability in human performance during simulate infant CPR: a reliability study. Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine. [doi:10.1186/s13049-020-00785-y](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13049-020-00785-y)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Between-day MDC analysis across the 1-week interval in the same 27-participant reliability study; Table 2 reports the corresponding MDC values (1.23 mm depth, 7.28 cpm rate, 9.36% duty cycle, 0.65 kg leaning), with residual leaning and duty cycle showing about 3 times the normalized MDC of depth and rate.

> "changes in performance between days which are greater than 3% of the initial value for compression depth, 7% for compression rate, 21% for residual leaning and 22% for duty cycle, represent true change in performance, indicating a decay or improvement of the iCPR skill."
