# Students in the technology-enhanced learning group are significantly less confident in passing the exam than traditional-group students, despite equal results

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After the course, the TEL group rated their confidence in exam success lower than the traditional group (3.3 ± 1.7 vs 4.5 ± 1.4, p = .020), although actual exam results did not differ. [→ Nkenke Emeka 2012](#nkenke-emeka-2012)

## Evidence

### Nkenke Emeka 2012

Nkenke Emeka, Vairaktaris Elefterios, Bauersachs Anne, Eitner Stephan, Budach Alexander, Knipfer Christoph, Stelzle Florian. (2012). Acceptance of technology-enhanced learning for a theoretical radiological science course: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-18

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-course attitude questionnaire (1–6 scale) in the randomized trial. Question 21 asked whether e-learning alone could yield exam results as good as face-to-face attendance; the TEL group mean was "3.3 ± 1.7" versus "4.5 ± 1.4", p = .020. No effect size printed.

> "there was a statistically significant difference between the traditional group and the technology-enhanced learning group for Question 21 (4.5 ± 1.4 for the traditional group and 3.3 ± 1.7 for the technology-enhanced learning group, p = .020, Table 1)."
