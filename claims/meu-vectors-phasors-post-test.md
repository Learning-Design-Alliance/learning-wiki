Anastasia Popkova, David Blair and David Treagust. (2023). Maths for Einstein's Universe - Tools for Understanding Modern Reality. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2307.06969](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2307.06969)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Post-test results of the Maths of Arrows module (program trials in Table 3) showing vector understanding and application to force addition problems. Descriptive percentages only; no inferential statistics reported.

> "The results of theMaths of Arrowsmodule from the post-test demonstrated that nearly 100% of students were able to understand the idea of vector and vectors addition as well as use it for solving problems such as the addition of forces when many people push a car."

### Anastasia Popkova 2023 (2)

Anastasia Popkova, David Blair and David Treagust. (2023). Maths for Einstein's Universe - Tools for Understanding Modern Reality. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2307.06969](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2307.06969)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Post-testing of phasor and quantum spin vector understanding within the vectors module trials, covering right-hand rule use, phasor addition with the phasor wheel toy, and correlation of resultant vectors with light interference intensity. Descriptive percentages with no test statistics.

> "In post-testing nearly 90% of students were able to correctly use the right-hand rule for defining spin vectors of spinning objects. More than 83% of students could add phasors shown by a phasor wheel and proximately 90 % could correctly correlate the resultant with light intensity in an interference pattern."
