## Evidence

### Linstead E 2017

Linstead E, Dixon DR, Hong E, Burns CO, French R, Novack MN, Granpeesheh D. (2017). An evaluation of the effects of intensity and duration on outcomes across treatment domains for children with autism spectrum disorder. Translational Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1038/tp.2017.207

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Authors' interpretation of the regression results from the 1468-participant retrospective analysis: duration coefficients exceeded intensity coefficients in every domain model. The article reports "treatment duration had a stronger impact than intensity on treatment outcomes across all domains."

> "In addition, treatment duration had a stronger impact than intensity on treatment outcomes across all domains. This may suggest that some skills cannot be acquired in a shorter period of time, regardless of the intensity"

### Linstead E 2017 (2)

Linstead E, Dixon DR, Hong E, Burns CO, French R, Novack MN, Granpeesheh D. (2017). An evaluation of the effects of intensity and duration on outcomes across treatment domains for children with autism spectrum disorder. Translational Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1038/tp.2017.207

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

From the Results section reporting the domain-specific regression models (Table 2): the language domain model showed the largest coefficients, with the duration coefficient "nearly double the effect size of any other domain." These are unstandardized regression parameters as printed; no standardized effect size is reported.

> "The language domain had the highest effect sizes for both treatment intensity and duration at 1.85 and 9.02, respectively. The strong effect of duration was nearly double the effect size of any other domain."
