## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Despite identical structure and resources, graduates experienced the telehealth simulation either face-to-face pretending, as a telephone call in separate rooms, or in one room with a divider; the two face-to-face participants described limited learning and transferability. ## Evidence

### Jones 2025

Jones, C. and Cominos, N. (2025). The Future of Healthcare—Simulation‐Based Learning Curricula and Preparing Future Midwives for Effective Rapport‐Building in Telehealth Settings. Journal of Advanced Nursing. https://doi.org/10.1111/jan.16927

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview findings from the graduate cohort on variation in simulation delivery. One graduate who did the face-to-face version said it "was quite limited" and "not particularly relevant... so it was very different from then going into placement", while telephone-modality participants judged the session beneficial for learning.

> "Whilst the structure and resources were the same in each session, it transpired that there were three different modalities for teaching it. Graduates were either face‐to‐face ‘pretending’ to simulate a telehealth, undertaking a telephone call and separated in different rooms, or in the same room with a divider which removed the visual feedback."
