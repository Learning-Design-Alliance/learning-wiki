# The attendance impact of Future Forward is larger for Black students, male students, and students with low baseline attendance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (4 entries) · causal `r2`–`r3` · `q3` · `i1`–`i2`

## Subclaims

`q3 i1` Differential-impact analyses found larger attendance effects for Black students (β = 0.18, p = 0.047) and male students (β = 0.27, p = 0.009). [→ Jones 2021](#jones-2021)
`q3 i2` Students with low school attendance prior to the start of the program benefited more from FF participation (β = 0.25, p = 0.025). [→ Jones 2021 (2)](#jones-2021-2)

`q3 i?` FF's impact on attendance was larger for Black students (β = 0.18) and male students (β = 0.27) than for White students (β = 0.01) and female students (β = 0.06). [→ Curtis J. Jones and DongMei Li 2021](#curtis-j-jones-and-dongmei-li-2021)

`q3 i?` Students with low school attendance prior to the program benefited more (β = 0.25, p = 0.025) than students with high baseline attendance (β = 0.04, p = 0.470). [→ Curtis J. Jones and DongMei Li 2021 (2)](#curtis-j-jones-and-dongmei-li-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Jones 2021

Jones, C. J., & Li, D. (2021). Testing the Impact and Scalability of the Future Forward Literacy Program. University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee. https://uwm.edu/sreed

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r?`

Exploratory subgroup analyses in the same RCT using variations of the GLM equation. Model-adjusted attendance rates were 1.9 percentage points higher for Black FF participants and 2.8 percentage points higher for male FF participants than BAU peers.

> "The impact of FF was larger with Black (β = 0.18, SE = 0.09, p = 0.047) and male (β = 0.27, SE = 0.10, p = 0.009) students (Table 8)."

### Jones 2021 (2)

Jones, C. J., & Li, D. (2021). Testing the Impact and Scalability of the Future Forward Literacy Program. University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee. https://uwm.edu/sreed

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r?`

Subgroup analysis by baseline attendance in the same RCT: students with low school attendance prior to the program benefited more, with a standardized impact of "β = 0.25, SE = 0.11, p = 0.025" (2.5 percentage points).

> "Across gender and race, students with low school attendance prior to the start of the program also benefited more from their participation (β = 0.25, SE = 0.11, p = 0.025)."

### Curtis J. Jones and DongMei Li 2021

Curtis J. Jones and DongMei Li, University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee. (2021). Testing the Impact and Scalability of the Future Forward Literacy Program. https://uwm.edu/sreed

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Differential-effects GLM analyses of the 2019-20 RCT found larger attendance impacts for Black students (1.9 percentage points) and male students (2.8 percentage points); Table 8 shows White students (β = 0.01) and females (β = 0.06) had non-significant impacts.

> "The impact of FF was larger with Black (β = 0.18, SE = 0.09, p = 0.047) and male (β = 0.27, SE = 0.10, p = 0.009) students (Table 8). The model-adjusted Black FF participants’ attendance rate was 1.9 percentage points higher than BAU students."

### Curtis J. Jones and DongMei Li 2021 (2)

Curtis J. Jones and DongMei Li, University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee. (2021). Testing the Impact and Scalability of the Future Forward Literacy Program. https://uwm.edu/sreed

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

In the same RCT's differential-effects models, students with low baseline attendance showed a statistically significant larger impact (2.5 percentage points), while students with high baseline attendance showed a non-significant impact (β = 0.04, p = 0.470).

> "Across gender and race, students with low school attendance prior to the start of the program also benefited more from their participation (β = 0.25, SE = 0.11, p = 0.025)."
