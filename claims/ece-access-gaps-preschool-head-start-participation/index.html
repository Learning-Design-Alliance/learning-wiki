Evidence¶

Building a National Early Childhood Education System That Works 2021¶

Building a National Early Childhood Education System That Works. (2021). Learning Policy Institute. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED614493

q2 · i? · review · r1

The whitepaper's Background section reports participation rates for U.S. early childhood programs, citing Child Trends indicator data. It states that "just 54% of 3- and 4-year-olds" attend any preschool and "just 35% of eligible children participate in Head Start." No effect sizes are reported; these are descriptive participation statistics.

"Yet just 54% of 3- and 4-year-olds in the United States participate in any preschool; just 35% of eligible children participate in Head Start; and only a small fraction of eligible infants and toddlers receive subsidized, licensed child care."

Related Claims¶

  • Only about half of eligible children attend NC Pre-K, leaving almost 33,000 eligible children per year unserved — related
  • Evidence on preschool attendance by young children in foster care is largely unexamined, with one cited source reporting six percent of foster children under age six attend Head Start — related
  • Publicly funded ECE programs in California served only 33% of eligible children under age 5 in 2015–16, leaving nearly 650,000 eligible children without access — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Access to subsidized ECE is extremely limited for California infants and toddlers, with approximately 14% of eligible infants and toddlers enrolled — related
  • Including subsidized child care, California enrolled about 37% of all 4-year-olds in publicly funded early childhood programs in 2019–20 and 55% in 2023–24 — related
  • California's subsidized ECE system serves only a minority of eligible children, with the lowest access among infants and toddlers — related
  • Combined enrollment in TK, CSPP, and Head Start raised California's coverage of all 4-year-olds from about 34% in 2019–20 to 50% in 2023–24 — related
