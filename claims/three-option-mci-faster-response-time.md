# Physicians spent less time per item on 3-option than 4-option items on both subspecialty exams, but response speed per word showed exam-dependent patterns

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` For the ITE-CCM, 3-option items took less time than 4-option items (55.5 vs 58.9 seconds per item), with a medium-to-large effect size, while response speed per word did not differ significantly. [→ Chen Dandan 2023](#chen-dandan-2023)

## Evidence

### Chen Dandan 2023

Chen Dandan, Harman Ann E., Sun Huaping, Ye Tianpeng, Gaiser Robert R. (2023). A comparison of 3- and 4-option multiple-choice items for medical subspecialty in-training examinations. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04277-2

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Paired t-tests on response time and response speed across the 150 matched items of the 4- and 3-option ITE-CCM. Mean response time was 55.5 ± 20.8 versus 58.9 ± 22.8 seconds per item, so physicians spent "3.4" fewer seconds on 3-option items.

> "For the ITE-CCM, the response speed was not statistically significant between 4-option and 3-option formats, t(149) = 1.060, p = 0.330, and the 3-option items took less time than the 4-option items, t(149) = 7.720, p < 0.001, with a medium-to-large effect size (Cohen's d = 0.63)"
