# People significantly underestimate AI-assisted completion times on simple cognitive tasks, even though AI assistance saves little or no actual time (the speedup illusion)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 causal `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` Actual AI-assisted completion time was almost one minute greater than predicted time, while actual and predicted independent times did not differ significantly. [→ Sunny Yu 2026](#sunny-yu-2026)

`q3 i?` Predicted time savings from AI (55.7 seconds) far exceed actual savings (7.5 seconds), and actual AI-assisted completion time (86.2 s) is nearly double the predicted time (43.3 s). [→ Sunny Yu 2026](#sunny-yu-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Sunny Yu 2026

Sunny Yu, Myra Cheng, Ahmad Jabbar, Ilia Sucholutsky, Katherine M. Collins, Dan Jurafsky, Robert D. Hawkins. (2026). Cognitive offloading and the speedup illusion in human-AI interaction. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23177 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.23177](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2605.23177)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Preregistered between-subjects behavioral study (prediction and completion samples, N=1237) fitting a linear mixed-effects model of predicted vs. actual times. The article reports "the actual completion time was almost one minute greater than the predicted time" for AI assistance, holding across all four task categories and both difficulty levels. Overall, actual AI-assisted and independent completion times did not differ, but AI assistance did make difficult tasks faster, by 26.14 seconds (SE = 11.9, p < .05); no difference was found for easy tasks.

> "the actual completion time was almost one minute greater than the predicted time (β=57.8,SE=8.79,z=6.57,p<0.001). The speedup illusion holds across all four task categories and both difficulty levels."

### Sunny Yu 2026 (2)

Sunny Yu, Myra Cheng, Ahmad Jabbar, Ilia Sucholutsky, Katherine M. Collins, Dan Jurafsky, Robert D. Hawkins. (2026). The efficiency-gain illusion: People underestimate the rate of AI use and overestimate its benefits on simple tasks. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22687 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.22687](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2605.22687)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Pre-registered Study 2 (prediction sample N=600, completion sample N=1001 on Prolific) compared predicted versus actual times for independent and AI-assisted completion. The speedup illusion held "across all four task categories and both difficulty levels" per the article.

> "Specifically, participants expected AI to save more time (55.7 seconds) than it actually did (7.5 seconds) (Equation (1);β=−48.33,p<0.001). This difference primarily arises from miscalibration about AI-assisted performance, i.e., ˆtA(τ) versust A(τ): the actual completion time with AI (86.2 seconds) is nearly double the predicted time with AI (43.3 seconds) (β= 42.9,p<0.001)."
