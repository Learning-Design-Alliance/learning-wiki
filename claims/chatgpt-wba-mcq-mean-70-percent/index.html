Evidence¶

Ivory 2026¶

Ivory, M., Finnerty, S., Dunn, K., Philpot, R., & Warmelink, L. (2026). The Integrity of Psychology Assessments in the AI Age: A Critical Examination. Journal of Academic Ethics. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10805-026-09729-0

q2 · i? · design · r2

Automated scoring of ChatGPT answers on unsupervised weekly web-based assessments; individual WBA mean was 70% (SD = 17.30), and when grouped by module average accuracy increased to 74% (min = 58, max = 93, SD = 14.90).

"Across all individual WBAs, the mean average score was 70% (min = 35, max = 100, SD = 17.30), highlighting their susceptibility to being answered by Gen-AI."

Ivory 2026 (2)¶

Ivory, M., Finnerty, S., Dunn, K., Philpot, R., & Warmelink, L. (2026). The Integrity of Psychology Assessments in the AI Age: A Critical Examination. Journal of Academic Ethics. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10805-026-09729-0

q2 · i? · design · r2

In class tests using a 'higher MCQ' format with described scenarios (e.g. patients presenting symptoms in PSYC317), ChatGPT scored as highly as 87% when given the same information as students.

"The Gen-AI was adept at breaking down the scenarios and typically choosing the correct answers, scoring as highly as 87%, highlighting that the application of declarative knowledge to novel scenarios is a task very easily managed by Gen-AI when given the same information as is given to students."
