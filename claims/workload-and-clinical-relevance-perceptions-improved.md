## Evidence

### Halliday N 2015

Halliday N, O'Donoghue D, Klump KE, Thompson B. (2015). Human Structure in Six and One-Half Weeks: One Approach to Providing Foundational Anatomical Competency in an Era of Compressed Medical School Anatomy curricula. Anatomical Sciences Education 8(2):149-157. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.1476

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Top-box end-of-course evaluation ratings across the first and second Human Structure iterations. The article reports 46.8% agreement in the first iteration and, after streamlining resources, "78% agreeing that the workload was reasonable during the second iteration."

> "During the first iteration (2010–2011) of Human Structure, less than half (46.8%) of the students felt the workload was reasonable"

### Halliday N 2015 (2)

Halliday N, O'Donoghue D, Klump KE, Thompson B. (2015). Human Structure in Six and One-Half Weeks: One Approach to Providing Foundational Anatomical Competency in an Era of Compressed Medical School Anatomy curricula. Anatomical Sciences Education 8(2):149-157. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.1476

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

An additional end-of-course evaluation question added in 2011-2012, after specific guidelines and criteria for clinical cases (differential diagnoses, relevant anatomy, final diagnosis, treatment, prognosis, literature evidence) were provided. The article reports "almost all students (86.3%)" agreement.

> "During the second iteration of the course, almost all students (86.3%) agreed or strongly agreed that through the weekly case presentations, the clinical relevance of the course content was made explicit."
