# Across a geometry curriculum, overall percent correct stays fairly constant over time while percent correct on actions involving a single skill increases, as ACT-R's knowledge-component claim predicts

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For 88 geometry students, percent correct remained fairly constant over time because rising task difficulty canceled out learning. [→ Ritter 2007](#ritter-2007)
`q2 i?` When tracking only actions involving a single skill (area of a regular polygon), percent correct increased over actions, supporting learning at the knowledge-component level. [→ Ritter 2007 (2)](#ritter-2007-2)

## Evidence

### Ritter 2007

Ritter, S., Anderson, J. R., Koedinger, K. R., & Corbett, A. (2007). Cognitive Tutor: Applied research in mathematics education. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 14(2), 249-255. https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03194060

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

A log-data analysis of 88 students using the Cognitive Tutor Geometry curriculum showed percent correct plotted over sequentially numbered actions remained fairly constant, which the authors attribute to increasing task difficulty canceling out learning.

> "Figure 2 shows the percent correct, over time, for 88 students using the Cognitive Tutor Geometry curriculum in a school. The percentage correct remains fairly constant over time."

### Ritter 2007 (2)

Ritter, S., Anderson, J. R., Koedinger, K. R., & Corbett, A. (2007). Cognitive Tutor: Applied research in mathematics education. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 14(2), 249-255. https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03194060

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The same 88 students' actions restricted to a single skill (area of a regular polygon, one side horizontal) showed the increasing learning curve that ACT-R predicts for individual knowledge components; the article argues failure to see such a curve means the cognitive model misrepresents student knowledge.

> "Figure 3 shows percent correct for the same group of students as in Figure 2, this time tracking only those student actions that the cognitive model considers to be relevant to a single skill (calculating the area of a regular polygon, in an orientation in which one side is horizontal)."
