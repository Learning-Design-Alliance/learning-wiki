Li 2023¶

Li, J., Peng, P., Ma, X., Ding, N., & Zhao, J. (2023). How does family socioeconomic status influence children's reading ability? Evidence from meta-analytic structural equation modeling. Educational Psychology Review, 35(4), 119. doi:10.1007/s10648-023-09834-1 (read from the PsyArXiv preprint, doi:10.31234/osf.io/7yv6r)

q3 · i2 · quant-synthesis · r2

ROBUMETA synthesis of 27 longitudinal articles (29 independent samples, 114 effect sizes), children aged 3 to 9 at Time 1 with most predictions within a 2-year interval. The pooled correlation r = 0.24 is the printed effect size.

"Results indicated that SES significantly predicted later reading performance and the overall correlation was r = 0.24, 95% CI [0.20, 0.28]"

Li 2023 (2)¶

Li, J., Peng, P., Ma, X., Ding, N., & Zhao, J. (2023). How does family socioeconomic status influence children's reading ability? Evidence from meta-analytic structural equation modeling. Educational Psychology Review, 35(4), 119. doi:10.1007/s10648-023-09834-1 (read from the PsyArXiv preprint, doi:10.31234/osf.io/7yv6r)

q3 · i1 · quant-synthesis · r?

ROBUMETA synthesis of 13 longitudinal articles (87 effect sizes) partialing out both linguistic skills. The partialled correlation "r = 0.15" is the printed effect size, with the two skills jointly explaining about 45.11% of the SES-reading variance.

"After partialing out phonological awareness, the correlation between SES and later reading was r = 0.15, 95% CI [0.08, 0.22]. Taken together, phonological awareness and vocabulary knowledge explained about 45.11% of the variance in the relationship between SES and later reading"

Li 2023 (3)¶

Li, J., Peng, P., Ma, X., Ding, N., & Zhao, J. (2023). How does family socioeconomic status influence children's reading ability? Evidence from meta-analytic structural equation modeling. q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Metaregression (ROBUMETA) of the partialled longitudinal SES-reading relation with age at Time 1, time interval, writing system, country SES and SES measurement entered simultaneously; no moderator was significant (Table S16).

"we did not find any moderator to affect this relation. This result is also consistent with the results of our model-based moderation from concurrent datasets, revealing that the direct impact from SES to reading is stable both currently and longitudinally"
