# Spelling Mastery has potentially positive effects on writing for students with learning disabilities, based on two RCTs with a small extent of evidence

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Across two RCTs meeting WWC standards without reservations, Spelling Mastery showed a statistically significant positive effect in one study and substantively important positive effects in the other, yielding a domain-average effect size of 0.85 and an improvement index of +30 percentile points. ## Evidence

### wwc-summary-1

U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, What Works Clearinghouse. (2014, January). Students with Learning Disabilities intervention report: Spelling Mastery. Retrieved from https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/579

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

WWC synthesis of two randomized controlled trials (Darch & Simpson, 1990; Darch et al., 2006) totaling 70 students with learning disabilities in grades 2–4. The report states "one study found a statistically significant positive effect and one study found substantively important positive effects," producing the potentially positive effects rating. Appendix C prints a domain average effect size of 0.85 and improvement index of +30.

> "Thus, for the writing domain, one study found a statistically significant positive effect and one study found substantively important positive effects. This results in a rating of potentially positive effects, with a small extent of evidence."
