# Higher parental expectations predict higher anxiety, anger, and shame one year later, opposite in sign to contemporaneous effects

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In lagged models, higher parental expectations predicted higher levels of anxiety, anger, and shame in the following year, with signs opposite to the contemporaneous effects. [→ Sakaki 2025](#sakaki-2025)
`q2 i?` The lagged anxiety result was replicated when the same-year school grade was controlled and when contemporaneous and lagged expectation paths were modeled together. [→ Sakaki 2025 (2)](#sakaki-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Sakaki 2025

Sakaki, M., Murayama, K., Frenzel, A. C., Goetz, T., Marsh, H. W., Lichtenfeld, S., Wünsch, M., & Pekrun, R. (2025). Parents' academic expectations and aspirations predict students' achievement emotions. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000959

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Lagged dynamic panel models predicted each emotion from parental expectations at the previous wave, controlling the grade from two years earlier and covariates; expectation paths flipped sign, 'predictive of higher levels of anxiety, anger and shame'.

> "Interestingly, the sign of the path coefficients was opposite from those from the contemporaneous effects analysis, such that higher parental expectations were predictive of higher levels of anxiety, anger and shame."

### Sakaki 2025 (2)

Sakaki, M., Murayama, K., Frenzel, A. C., Goetz, T., Marsh, H. W., Lichtenfeld, S., Wünsch, M., & Pekrun, R. (2025). Parents' academic expectations and aspirations predict students' achievement emotions. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000959

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Robustness check for the lagged models controlling the same-year grade; the anxiety finding held, and a joint model with contemporaneous and lagged expectation paths showed the same pattern.

> "The results for anxiety were replicated when the school grade from the same year (as expectation and aspiration) was controlled (Table S15)."
