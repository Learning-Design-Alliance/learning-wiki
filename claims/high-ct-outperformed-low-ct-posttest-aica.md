# High CT students significantly outperformed low CT students on the post-test after AICA-assisted coding instruction

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After the AICA intervention, the high CT group scored significantly higher than the low CT group on the coding post-test, with lower score variability. [→ Shu Zhao 2026](#shu-zhao-2026)

## Evidence

### Shu Zhao 2026

Shu Zhao, Ziqi Wang, Tingting Chen, Chunchen Kang, Yu-Ju Lan. (2026). Exploring the effect of computational thinking levels on students' learning performance, cognition, and behavior when using AI coding assistants. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10663-6

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Independent samples t-test comparing post-test scores of 26 high CT and 26 low CT eighth-graders after a four-week AICA-assisted Python course. The article reports "a significant difference (t(50) = 2.23, p =.031)"; the high CT group scored 10.81 points higher on average with lower variability. No effect size is printed.

> "Results indicated that the independent samples t-test revealed a significant difference (t(50) = 2.23, p =.031). This demonstrates that after the AICA's intervention, there was a significant difference in students' performance between the high and low CT groups."
