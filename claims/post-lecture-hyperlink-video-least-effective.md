# The author's teaching experience suggests that providing students hyperlinks to watch online video after class is the easiest but least effective route, with many students not watching the video

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Hyperlinks for after-class viewing rely on students watching on their own time, and in the author's experience a large portion do not, leaving a disconnect between viewing and discussion. [→ Micolich 2008](#micolich-2008)

## Evidence

### Micolich 2008

Micolich, A. (2008). The latent potential of YouTube – Will it become the 21st Century Lecturer's Film Archive? CAL-laborate, 2008. https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.3441 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0808.3441](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0808.3441)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The author's experiential report on the hyperlink-after-lecture route in the section 'Providing hyperlinks for use after the lecture', stating "in my experience this can be 95% or more" of students may not watch. No systematic data are reported; this is expert opinion, not a measured result.

> "Firstly you need to rely on the students bothering to watch the video after the class, they will not always do this, so the video is wasted for some portion of the students (in my experience this can be 95% or more)."
