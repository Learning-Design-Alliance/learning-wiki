## Evidence

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2017

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2017). Effect of Scaffolding on Helping Introductory Physics Students Solve Quantitative Problems Involving Strong Alternative Conceptions. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05697](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05697)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Calculus-based course recitation-quiz results (Table 3): intervention 2 (n=72) averaged 7.2, intervention 3 (n=39) 7.1, intervention 1 (n=34) 5.9; differences among interventions were "not necessarily statistically different".

> "Students in the intervention groups 2 and 3 on average achieved a score of 7.2 or 7.1 out of 10, while students in the intervention group 1 had an average score of 5.9."

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2017 (2)

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2017). Effect of Scaffolding on Helping Introductory Physics Students Solve Quantitative Problems Involving Strong Alternative Conceptions. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05697](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05697)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Algebra-based course recitation-quiz results (Tables 3 and 4): intervention 2 (n=51) significantly outperformed interventions 1 (n=63) and 3 (n=66); there was no significant difference between the latter two groups.

> "In the algebra-based course, the p-values show that intervention group 2 students, who achieved an average score of 6.8, performed significantly better than students in intervention groups 1 and 3, whose average scores were 5.1 and 5.0, respectively."
