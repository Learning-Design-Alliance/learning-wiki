# Students' transition into a sensemaking frame is accompanied by a verbalized question or statement of uncertainty that names a perceived gap or inconsistency in understanding

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the analyzed clinical-interview corpus, students' shift from brainstorming, oral examination, or expert interview frames into sensemaking was marked by a verbalized question or statement of uncertainty, and students often returned to the same question multiple times during sensemaking episodes. ## Evidence

### Tor Ole B. Odden and Rosemary S. Russ 2019

Tor Ole B. Odden and Rosemary S. Russ. (2019). Recurring questions that sustain the sensemaking frame. arXiv:1807.03137v2 [physics.ed-ph]. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1080/09500693.2019.1589655](https://doi.org/10.1080/09500693.2019.1589655)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative analysis by the first author (with input from the second) of video- and audio-recorded, transcribed semi-structured paired clinical interviews in an introductory algebra-based E&M course, coded for framing. The authors report that the transition into sensemaking was "accompanied by a verbalized question" and that students "returned to this same question" repeatedly. No effect size is printed.

> "This transition seemed to be accompanied by a verbalized question or statement of uncer- tainty, and students often returned to this same question mul- tiple times throughout their episodes of sensemaking."
