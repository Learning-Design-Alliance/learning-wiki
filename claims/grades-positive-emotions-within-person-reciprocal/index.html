Evidence¶

Reinhard Pekrun 2022¶

Reinhard Pekrun, Herbert W. Marsh, Felix Suessenbach, Anne C. Frenzel, Thomas Goetz. (2022). School grades and students' emotions: Longitudinal models of within-person reciprocal effects. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2022.101626

q3 · i? · associational · r3

RI-CLPM analysis of five annual waves (grades 5–9) from the PALMA study of 3,425 German students, with latent emotion constructs and covariates. The article reports that "grades had positive predictive effects on subse- quent enjoyment and pride" and the reverse, within persons.

"More specifically, grades had positive predictive effects on subse- quent enjoyment and pride, and enjoyment and pride had positive ef- fects on subsequent grades."

Related Claims¶

  • Grades and positive affect show significant positive reciprocal within-person effects, and grades and negative affect show significant negative reciprocal effects — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Within-person deviations of enjoyment and pride correlate positively with grade deviations (r = .12 to .55), and negative-emotion deviations correlate negatively (r = −.17 to −.44) — related
  • Within persons, grades negatively predict subsequent anger and hopelessness, and anger, anxiety, boredom, and hopelessness negatively predict subsequent grades — related
  • Students use positive and negative achievement emotions as cues when making predictions about their comprehension — related
  • Reciprocal relations: initial task engagement predicted later enjoyment in the Reverse model — related
  • Specific emotion patterns: relationship quality predicts later enjoyment, pride, anger, and shame but not anxiety or boredom; earlier enjoyment, anger, boredom, shame, and anxiety predict later relationship quality — related
