# In a one-group physiotherapy pretest–posttest study, students' self-reported amotivation decreased and external regulation increased from pretest to posttest after team-based learning

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2` · `i2` medium
> **Currency** · newest study 2017 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Students' amotivation significantly decreased from pretest to posttest (d = 0.64). [→ Jeno Lucas M. 2017](#jeno-lucas-m-2017)
`q2 i2` Students reported higher external regulation at posttest than at pretest after team-based learning, in one group (d = −0.42). [→ Jeno Lucas M. 2017](#jeno-lucas-m-2017)

## Evidence

### Jeno Lucas M. 2017

Jeno Lucas M., Raaheim Arild, Kristensen Sara Madeleine, Kristensen Kjell Daniel, Hole Torstein Nielsen, Haugland Mildrid J., Mæland Silje. (2017). The Relative Effect of Team-Based Learning on Motivation and Learning: A Self-Determination Theory Perspective. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-03-0055

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Repeated-samples t tests in the same one-group pretest–posttest physiotherapy study. Figure 3 prints "external regulation, d = −0.42; amotivation, d = 0.64"; descriptive means are external regulation 4.96 (pretest) to 5.49 (posttest) and amotivation 2.72 to 2.01. The external-regulation increase is the article's mixed result.

> "Students reported higher mean levels of external regulation in the TBL-condition compared with the lecture-condition. This may be due to few choices in the learning process (i.e., learning activity, choosing teams, few choices in working with significant cases), whereby thwarting the basic need for autonomy (Ryan and Deci, 2002)."
