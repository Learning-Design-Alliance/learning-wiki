## Subclaims
`q2 i?` There was no difference in quiz scores between the force-feedback group with a hardware tutorial (Group 4) and the force-feedback group without one (Group 3). [→ Felix G. Hamza-Lup 2019](#felix-g-hamza-lup-2019)

## Evidence

### Felix G. Hamza-Lup 2019

Felix G. Hamza-Lup, Benjamin Page. (2019). Haptics-Augmented Physics Simulation: Coriolis Effect. The 7th International Conference on Virtual Learning ICVL 2012. Originally published 2012. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1903.11567](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1903.11567)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Null comparison in the four-group quasi-experiment: Group 4 received ten practice trials and a tutorial on the haptic devices before the simulation, Group 3 did not. The article reports "no difference in quiz scores" between the two force-feedback groups.

> "There was no difference in quiz scores in the G3-G4 pairing, showing that a tutorial on the haptic hardware prior to the Coriolis effect simulations either didn't sufficiently increase students' familiarity, or they familiarized quickly to the simulations and practice was not necessary."
