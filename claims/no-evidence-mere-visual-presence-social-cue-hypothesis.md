## Evidence

### Nicole C. Krämer & Gary Bente 2010

Nicole C. Krämer & Gary Bente. (2010). Personalizing e-Learning. The Social Effects of Pedagogical Agents. Educ Psychol Rev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-010-9123-x

`q2 · i?`

The review reports Moreno's (2003) postulate that "there is no evidence for the social cue hypothesis" because mere presence of a human-like body has not been shown to produce distinct effects.

> "Moreno ( 2003) therefore postulated that there is no evidence for the social cue hypothesis as it has not been shown that the mere presence of social aspects such as a human-like body leads to distinct effects."

### Nicole C. Krämer & Gary Bente 2010 (2)

Nicole C. Krämer & Gary Bente. (2010). Personalizing e-Learning. The Social Effects of Pedagogical Agents. Educ Psychol Rev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-010-9123-x

`q2 · i?`

The review reports Domagk's (2008) finding that "likeable agents lead to a higher motivation and increased transfer performance," though no general agent effect on motivation or learning was found.

> "However, when taking the specific appearance and the resulting “likeability” into account, it was shown that likeable agents lead to a higher motivation and increased transfer performance."
