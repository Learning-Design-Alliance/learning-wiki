# First key press latencies confirm that retrieval is more difficult after longer ISIs and becomes less difficult as the number of correct retrievals increases

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` First key press latencies were longer for correct retrievals after a longer versus shorter ISI. [→ Mary A. Pyc 2009](#mary-a-pyc-2009)

## Evidence

### Mary A. Pyc 2009

Mary A. Pyc, Katherine A. Rawson. (2009). Testing the retrieval effort hypothesis: Does greater difficulty correctly recalling information lead to higher levels of memory? Journal of Memory and Language 60 (2009) 437–447. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jml.2009.01.004

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 latency analysis (2 x 2 x 10 mixed ANOVA on first key press latency): significant main effect of ISI, means 1.75 s versus 1.44 s, supporting the ISI difficulty assumption.

> "First key press latencies were longer for correct retrievals after a longer versus a shorter ISI (long ISI, M = 1.75, SE = .04; short ISI M = 1.44, SE = .03), indicating that more effort was expended correctly retrieving items with a longer versus a shorter ISI."

### Mary A. Pyc 2009 (2)

Mary A. Pyc, Katherine A. Rawson. (2009). Testing the retrieval effort hypothesis: Does greater difficulty correctly recalling information lead to higher levels of memory? Journal of Memory and Language 60 (2009) 437–447. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jml.2009.01.004

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 paired comparisons of first versus tenth correct retrieval latencies in each of the four groups; all differences favored shorter latency on the tenth retrieval, supporting the criterion difficulty assumption.

> "First key press latencies were shorter on the tenth correct retrieval compared to the first correct retrieval for all groups (short contracting ISI: Md = .98, CI = .83, 1.14; short fixed ISI: Md = .80, CI = .66, .93; long contracting ISI: Md = 1.39, CI = 1.24, 1.53; long fixed ISI: Md = 1.18, CI = 1.03, 1.32)."
