# Random-intercept and lag2 panel models rest on different assumptions and offer alternative perspectives on controlling covariates

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` RI models can control fixed unmeasured covariates stable over time but rely on strong, not easily tested assumptions, while lag2 models adjust for unmeasured confounders without those assumptions; the two address different questions. [→ Marsh 2024](#marsh-2024)

## Evidence

### Marsh 2024

Marsh, H. W., Guo, J., Pekrun, R., Lüdtke, O., & Núñez-Regueiro, F. (2024). Cracking Chicken-Egg Conundrums: Juxtaposing Contemporaneous and Lagged Reciprocal Effects Models of Academic Self-Concept and Achievement's Directional Ordering. Educational Psychology Review, 36. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09887-w

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Theoretical-methodological argument in the research-hypotheses section, not an empirical test. The article argues RI models depend on strong modeling assumptions (e.g., no nonlinear effects) that are not easily tested and might overcorrect cross-lagged effects if misspecified, whereas lag2 paths help adjust for unmeasured confounders without those assumptions.

> "RI and lag2 models are based on different assumptions, address different questions, and offer alternative perspectives on the control of covariates."
