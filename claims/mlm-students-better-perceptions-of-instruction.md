# MLM students report more positive perceptions of instructor clarity, class atmosphere, and effective use of class time than non-MLM students despite identical instructors and in-class activities

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` MLM students rated effective use of class time 20% higher and instructor clarity 11% higher than non-MLM students, with significant differences at p < 1% in all areas except instructor effectiveness (p = 0.012). [→ Moore 2018](#moore-2018)

## Evidence

### Moore 2018

James Christopher Moore. (2018). Efficacy of Multimedia Learning Modules as Preparation for Lecture-Based Tutorials in Electromagnetism. Educ. Sci. 2018, 8, 23. https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci8010023

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

End-of-semester standardized faculty evaluations on a 7-point Likert scale (non-MLM n = 42, MLM n = 37). Class time ratings were 97% vs 77%, clarity 91% vs 80%, atmosphere 99% vs 88%, effectiveness 93% vs 84% — "a significant difference at the p <1% level" in all but effectiveness.

> "A one-tail unpaired t-test indicates a signiﬁcant difference at the p <1% level between the MLM and non-MLM groups with respect to students’ attitudes in all areas except instructor effectiveness (p = 0.012)."
