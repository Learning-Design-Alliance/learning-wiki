# Feedback source does not significantly affect revised essay quality once initial draft quality is controlled

## Evidence

### Farrokhnia 2026

Farrokhnia, M., Latifi, S., Papadopoulos, P. M., Hogenkamp, L., Gijlers, H., Khosravi, H., & Noroozi, O. (2026). Generative AI offers more, but students revise less: comparing the effects of teacher and AI feedback on student essay revisions. International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00579-9

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANCOVA comparing revised essay quality across teacher, Zero-shot, and CoT conditions controlling for initial drafts found no significant condition effect (F(2, 64) = 1.19, p = .30, ηp2 = .03); equivalence was not tested.

> "After adjusting for initial draft quality, there was no significant effect of feedback condition on revised essay quality, F(2, 64) = 1.19, p = .30, ηp2 = .03, suggesting that the type of feedback did not significantly influence revision outcomes."

### Farrokhnia 2026 (2)

Farrokhnia, M., Latifi, S., Papadopoulos, P. M., Hogenkamp, L., Gijlers, H., Khosravi, H., & Noroozi, O. (2026). Generative AI offers more, but students revise less: comparing the effects of teacher and AI feedback on student essay revisions. International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00579-9

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

In the same ANCOVA, initial essay quality was a significant covariate, F(1, 64) = 34.25, p < .001, ηp2 = .35, showing a "strong and significant relationship between students’ baseline writing ability and their revised performance".

> "However, initial essay quality was a significant covariate, F(1, 64) = 34.25, p < .001, ηp2 = .35, indicating a strong and significant relationship between students’ baseline writing ability and their revised performance."
