Jessica Watkins, Janet E. Coffey, Edward F. Redish, & Todd J. Cooke. (2011). Disciplinary authenticity: Enriching the reforms of introductory physics courses for life-science students. Originally published 2010. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1109.4983](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1109.4983)

From the qualitative coding of the 75 course questions, the making-sense-of-biological-phenomena category was the most prevalent. Examples include the kangaroo hop question and the diffusion-constraints-on-worms question, where physics is used "in service of understanding biological phenomena, not just the physical situation."

### Jessica Watkins 2011 (2)

Jessica Watkins, Janet E. Coffey, Edward F. Redish, & Todd J. Cooke. (2011). Disciplinary authenticity: Enriching the reforms of introductory physics courses for life-science students. Originally published 2010. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1109.4983](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1109.4983)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Analysis of a Fick-equation exam question about ventilatory and circulatory pumps shows selective attention to tool characteristics as a distinctive biologically authentic use. The authors conclude these problems "reflect the authentic activities of biology by using mathematical and physical tools for uniquely biological ends."

> "Instead of thinking of the equation holistical- ly, as would be needed to describe the physical phenomena of diffusion, this question asked stu- dents to focus on the relevant variables to under- stand the biological phenomena."
