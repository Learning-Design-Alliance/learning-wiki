Evidence¶

Mahsa Bakhshaei 2020¶

Mahsa Bakhshaei, Angela Hardy, Jason Ravitz, John Seylar. (2020). Instructional Coaching Holds Promise as a Method to Improve Instruction with Technology. SITE 2020 - Online, April 7-10, 2020. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/5465e54a-b290-4210-97fb-514a3aa13e21

q2 · i? · associational · r1

DLP year 2 survey contrast on sustained duration of PD among core-subject teachers: 47% of coached versus 30% of non-coached peers reported PD "sustained over time with coherent follow-up" ("Chi-Sq p < .001" printed, no effect size). A separate correlation linked sustained PD with ITU confidence (corr = .3, p < .001).

"While 47% of coached teachers in core subject areas reported that to a great extent their PD was sustained over time with coherent follow-up, only 30% of their non-coached peers reported the same (Chi-Sq p < .001)."

Mahsa Bakhshaei 2020 (2)¶

Mahsa Bakhshaei, Angela Hardy, Jason Ravitz, John Seylar. (2020). Instructional Coaching Holds Promise as a Method to Improve Instruction with Technology. SITE 2020 - Online, April 7-10, 2020. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/5465e54a-b290-4210-97fb-514a3aa13e21

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive survey data from DLP coached teachers on coaching dosage: "more than 81%" received at least 30 minutes weekly, 43% more than an hour, and the average exceeded 16 hours over the school year; no inferential test or effect size is printed for these figures.

"While working with their coach, more than 81% of coached teachers received at least 30 minutes of one-on-one coaching per week, with 43% of them receiving more than an hour of coaching each week. Coached teachers received, on average, more than 16 hours of coaching support over the school year."
