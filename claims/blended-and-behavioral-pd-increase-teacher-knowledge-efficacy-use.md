# Both Blended and Behavioral professional development training significantly increased teachers' knowledge, self-efficacy, and self-reported use of instructional methods compared with a no-training control

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Both experimental groups' mean scores increased significantly after training on teacher knowledge, teacher self-efficacy, and teacher self-report of use, while the Control group had low or negative mean increases on all four post-test measures. [→ Dille 2013](#dille-2013)
`q2 i?` There were no significant differences between the Blended and Behavioral groups' mean increases on post knowledge, post self-efficacy, or post self-report of use, though Blended means were generally higher. [→ Dille 2013](#dille-2013)

## Evidence

### Dille 2013

Dille, L. (2013). A Comparison of Two Curricular Models of Professional Development to Increase Teacher Repertoires for Instructing Students with Autism. Journal of the American Academy of Special Education Professionals (JAASEP), Fall 2013. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1135486

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized three-group pretest-posttest study with 48 teachers (16 per group) assigned via a JavaScript random number generator; one-way ANOVAs on post-test measures. The Blended group's knowledge rose from M = 13.187 to M = 22.812 and self-efficacy from M = 37.562 to M = 66.312, described as "the highest mean increase of the three groups" on each measure.

> "the mean scores of both the Blended group and the Behavioral group increased significantly after the two experimental groups received the Blended Model training and the Behavioral Model of instructional training, were as the Control group had a low or negative mean increase on all four post-test measures"
