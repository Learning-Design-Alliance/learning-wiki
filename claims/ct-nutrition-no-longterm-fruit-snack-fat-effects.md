## Evidence

### Springvloet 2015

Springvloet, L., Lechner, L., de Vries, H., & Oenema, A. (2015). Long-term efficacy of a Web-based computer-tailored nutrition education intervention for adults including cognitive and environmental feedback: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Public Health, 15, 372. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-015-1707-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Linear mixed model analysis of the three-group RCT (n = 1349) from baseline to nine-month follow-up. Time effects were significant for fruit intake but the "group X time" interaction was not (p = .50); equivalence was not tested.

> "Fruit intake increased over time (p < .001), but no differences were found between the groups (p = .50) (Tables 2 and 3). Similar results were found in the risk group"

### Springvloet 2015 (2)

Springvloet, L., Lechner, L., de Vries, H., & Oenema, A. (2015). Long-term efficacy of a Web-based computer-tailored nutrition education intervention for adults including cognitive and environmental feedback: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Public Health, 15, 372. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-015-1707-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Linear mixed model analysis (n = 1349; analyses on the natural logarithm of high-energy snacks). All three groups decreased snack intake over time without significant between-group differences; the same pattern held in the risk group.

> "Snack intake decreased in the total sample (p < .001), but the change over time did not differ between the groups (p = .14)"

### Springvloet 2015 (3)

Springvloet, L., Lechner, L., de Vries, H., & Oenema, A. (2015). Long-term efficacy of a Web-based computer-tailored nutrition education intervention for adults including cognitive and environmental feedback: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Public Health, 15, 372. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-015-1707-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Linear mixed model analysis of saturated fat intake ('fat-points') in the three-group RCT (n = 1349), baseline to nine months. Fat intake declined over time in all groups with no group-by-time differences; similar results in the risk group (n = 627).

> "The intake of saturated fat was lower at follow-up than at baseline (p < .001), but this change over time was not different between the groups (p = .13)"
