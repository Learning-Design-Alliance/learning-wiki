## Evidence

### de Zeeuw Eveline L. 2017

de Zeeuw Eveline L., van Beijsterveldt Catharina E. M., Ehli Erik A., de Geus Eco J. C., Boomsma Dorret I. (2017). Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder Symptoms and Low Educational Achievement: Evidence Supporting A Causal Hypothesis. Behavior Genetics. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10519-017-9836-4

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r3`

Cross-sectional phenotypic correlations between CRS-R symptom subtypes and Cito achievement; the article reports "larger correlations for inattention than hyperactivity". The cross-sectional hyperactivity direct path was not significant (β = 0.06, p = 0.833), a negligible standardized path coefficient.

> "The magnitude of the phenotypic correlations depended on the subtype of symptoms, with larger correlations for inattention than hyperactivity."

### de Zeeuw Eveline L. 2017 (2)

de Zeeuw Eveline L., van Beijsterveldt Catharina E. M., Ehli Erik A., de Geus Eco J. C., Boomsma Dorret I. (2017). Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder Symptoms and Low Educational Achievement: Evidence Supporting A Causal Hypothesis. Behavior Genetics. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10519-017-9836-4

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Longitudinal bivariate twin model, ADHD symptoms at age 7 and achievement at age 12. The article reports the inattention path "was significant (β = −0.51, χ2 (1) = 13.62, p < 0.001)" while the hyperactivity path was not (p = 0.775); no effect size is printed for the null hyperactivity path.

> "The direct path between the inattentive symptoms and educational achievement was significant (β = −0.51, χ2 (1) = 13.62, p < 0.001), but this was not true for the hyperactive symptoms (β = −0.21, χ2 (1) = 0.08, p = 0.775)."
