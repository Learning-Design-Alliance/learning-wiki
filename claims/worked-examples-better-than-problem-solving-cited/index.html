Evidence¶

Chen 2016¶

Chen, Ouhao. (2016). The Worked Example Effect, the Generation Effect, and Element Interactivity. PhD thesis, School of Education, UNSW. https://hdl.handle.net/1959.4/55865

q1 · i? · causal · r?

The thesis introduction reports this as background literature on the worked example effect, described as based on cognitive load theory, with worked examples providing full guidance containing the key steps needed to solve a problem.

"Sweller and Cooper (1985) indicated that worked examples could result in better performance than conventional problem solving which has no guidance."

Related Claims¶

  • Under many circumstances, studying worked examples produced superior learning compared to active problem solving, as suggested by previous research — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Studying worked examples is more effective and more efficient than problem solving for novice learners (the worked example effect) — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Studying worked examples improves novices' algebra performance on problems with the same structure as the examples — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices. — related
  • The worked example effect is explained by reduced element interactivity relative to problem solving, and reverses with expertise — related
  • The worked example effect failed to replicate for geometry and kinematics problems, and was reinstated when worked examples were restructured to reduce split attention — related
  • Split-attention worked examples lose their advantage over conventional practice, while integrated formats restore it — related
