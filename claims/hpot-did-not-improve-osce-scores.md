# HPOT relaxation training did not improve medical students' final OSCE scores

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The HPOT procedure did not significantly improve the final score (median 11/20 in both groups, p = 0.7). [→ Massalou Damien 2025](#massalou-damien-2025)

## Evidence

### Massalou Damien 2025

Massalou Damien, Doyen Jérôme, Almairac Fabien, Rohrlich Pierre-Simon, Fournier Jean-Paul, Vandersteen Clair, Sadaghianloo Nirvana. (2025). Can relaxation exercises improve students’ OSCE grades: a prospective study. Canadian Medical Education Journal. https://doi.org/10.36834/cmej.77739

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized comparison of 103 control and 96 HPOT students' OSCE grades in the same prospective study. The article reports no significant improvement, with "median score of 11/20 in the control group, versus 11/20 in the HPOT group"; equivalence was not tested and no effect size is printed.

> "Nor did the HPOT procedure significantly improve the final score (Spearman’s rank coefficient of -0.05 between VAS after relaxation and final score, p = 0.4; median score of 11/20 in the control group, versus 11/20 in the HPOT group, p = 0.7, Mann-Whitney U test)."
