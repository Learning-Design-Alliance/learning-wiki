## Evidence

### Bergman EM 2013

Bergman EM, de Bruin ABH, Herrler A, Verheijen IWH, Scherpbier AJJA, van der Vleuten CPM. (2013). Students' perceptions of anatomy across the undergraduate problem-based learning medical curriculum: a phenomenographical study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-152

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Perceptions reported by senior students in the qualitative focus-group study of 78 students; this is a student-perceived, not measured, effect on retention. The article notes "retention was thought to benefit more from revisiting topics on more than two occasions".

> "although a more stringent assessment procedure might make them study harder, it would not necessarily promote retention of anatomical knowledge. Retention was thought to benefit more from revisiting topics on more than two occasions in the course of the curriculum."

### Bergman EM 2013 (2)

Bergman EM, de Bruin ABH, Herrler A, Verheijen IWH, Scherpbier AJJA, van der Vleuten CPM. (2013). Students' perceptions of anatomy across the undergraduate problem-based learning medical curriculum: a phenomenographical study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-152

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Senior students critiqued the 'one shot approach' of presenting all major and minor structures in one long session; they argued they would learn less per session but remember more eventually, and would be more motivated on revisiting a known topic.

> "It was suggested that multiple shorter sessions, preferably spread over (more than) the first three years of the curriculum, would be more effective. The first session might cover the major structures ('the basics') with more detail being added in subsequent sessions."
