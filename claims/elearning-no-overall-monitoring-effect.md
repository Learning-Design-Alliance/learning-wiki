## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Implementation of an e-learning module in six departments did not detectably change the overall rate of acceleromyography use, partly because a pre-existing positive trend and high baseline rates left little room for improvement. [→ Thomsen JLD 2022](#thomsen-jld-2022)

## Evidence

### Thomsen JLD 2022

Thomsen JLD, Mathiesen O, Hägi‐Pedersen D, Skovgaard LT, Østergaard D, Gätke MR; the INVERT collaborator group. (2022). Improving neuromuscular monitoring and reducing residual neuromuscular blockade via e‐learning: A multicentre interrupted time‐series study (INVERT study). Acta Anaesthesiol Scand. https://doi.org/10.1111/aas.14038

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multicentre interrupted time-series study using segmented regression on routinely collected data from 6525 post-intervention cases against a 30,430-case baseline; the authors report they "could not detect an effect of the e‐learning module on the monitoring rate in general." No control group was included.

> "There was a significant positive pre‐intervention trend in use of acceleromyography both in cases involving succinylcholine only and cases involving non‐depolarising NMBAs, but we could not detect an effect of the e‐learning module on the monitoring rate in general."
