## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In a split-attention detector activity, learners given problems and questions on the same page reported fewer cognitive-related challenges than learners whose questions appeared on the flip side of the page. [→ Dhlamini 2016](#dhlamini-2016)

## Evidence

### Dhlamini 2016

Dhlamini, J. J. (2016). Enhancing learners’ problem solving performance in mathematics: A cognitive load perspective. European Journal of STEM Education, 1(1), 27-36. https://doi.org/10.20897/lectito.201604

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Split-attention detector activity within the pilot study: one group received an integrated A4 task (Example 2), the other a split-format task (Example 3). The article reports the same-page group "experienced minimal cognitive-related challenges"; no statistics are printed.

> "The results of the activity in Example 2 and Example 3 showed that learners who were given context-based problem-questions on the same page experienced minimal cognitive-related challenges compared to learners subjected to a relatively more inducing split-attention conditions"
