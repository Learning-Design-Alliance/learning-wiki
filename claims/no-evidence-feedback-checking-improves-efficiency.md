# Checking auto-grader feedback was not shown to be associated with increased time efficiency

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The study could not show that checking feedback is associated with increased efficiency as measured by estimated time spent. [→ Adam Zhang 2025](#adam-zhang-2025)

## Evidence

### Adam Zhang 2025

Adam Zhang, Heather Burte, Jaromir Savelka, Christopher Bogart, and Majd Sakr. (2025). Auto-grader Feedback Utilization and Its Impacts: An Observational Study Across Five Community Colleges. [doi:10.5220/0013276800003932](https://doi.org/10.5220/0013276800003932)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Observational analysis using ProjectHours, estimated by counting distinct clock hours of navigation or submissions per project. The article reports a positive correlation between feedback checks and ProjectHours (cor = 0.497, p < 0.001) and could not determine an efficiency threshold.

> "We could not determine if past a certain threshold students who check feedback more often per submission end up spending less time, which would support the hypothesis that checking feedback allows students to learn more efficiently."
