# The review identifies pervasive methodological problems in DLL research, including inconsistent DLL definitions and SES-bilingualism confounding

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Studies used inconsistent terminology and rarely specified children's dual language exposure; only 51% specified simultaneous versus sequential learner status and 29% reported exposure amounts. [→ Hammer 2014](#hammer-2014)
`q2 i?` SES and bilingualism can be confounded in DLL studies because many DLL children grow up in low-SES homes. [→ Hammer 2014 (2)](#hammer-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Hammer 2014

Hammer, C. S., Hoff, E., Uchikoshi, Y., Gillanders, C., Castro, D., & Sandilos, L. E. (2014). The Language and Literacy Development of Young Dual Language Learners: A Critical Review. Early childhood research quarterly. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecresq.2014.05.008

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's sample description of the 182 coded articles reports inconsistent child labeling without defined criteria, with only 51% of studies specifying simultaneous versus sequential status and 29% providing exposure information.

> "Ninety-three (51%) of the studies specified whether children were simultaneous learners (i.e., children who learn two languages from birth) or sequential language learners (i.e., children who learn their home language from birth and their second language at age three or later)."

### Hammer 2014 (2)

Hammer, C. S., Hoff, E., Uchikoshi, Y., Gillanders, C., Castro, D., & Sandilos, L. E. (2014). The Language and Literacy Development of Young Dual Language Learners: A Critical Review. Early childhood research quarterly. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecresq.2014.05.008

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review notes only 46% of studies reported SES information, that US studies specifying SES mostly involved low-SES children, and that SES and bilingualism can be confounded given monolingual research linking low SES to lower vocabulary abilities.

> "Because many DLL children grow up in low-SES homes, SES and bilingualism can be confounded in studies of children’s development."
