## Evidence

### Shih-Yin Lin 2016

Shih-Yin Lin, John M. Aiken, Daniel T. Seaton, Scott S. Douglas, Edwin F. Greco, Brian D. Thoms, Michael F. Schatz. (2016). Exploring University Students’ Engagement with Online Video Lectures in a Blended Introductory Mechanics Course. Originally published 2013. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1603.03348](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1603.03348)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Log analysis of Coursera clickstream data from the 161-student Fall 2013 blended course (Figure 5). The authors report that "the fraction of students accessing lecture-oriented videos dropped to lower than 40%" for the last assigned batch, while lab-video access was unaffected by placement.

> "the fraction of students accessing lecture-oriented videos dropped to lower than 40% for the last batch of lecture-oriented videos assigned in the course. However, the fraction of students accessing laboratory videos did not seem to be affected by the videos’ placement in the course"

### Shih-Yin Lin 2016 (2)

Shih-Yin Lin, John M. Aiken, Daniel T. Seaton, Scott S. Douglas, Edwin F. Greco, Brian D. Thoms, Michael F. Schatz. (2016). Exploring University Students’ Engagement with Online Video Lectures in a Blended Introductory Mechanics Course. Originally published 2013. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1603.03348](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1603.03348)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Complementary cumulative distribution analysis of unique videos accessed (Figure 4). The article reports "more than half of the students access ed all the lab videos" versus fewer than 20% accessing all lecture-oriented videos, indicating selective rather than exhaustive viewing.

> "While more than half of the students access ed all the lab videos, less than 20% of the students accessed all the lecture-oriented videos"
