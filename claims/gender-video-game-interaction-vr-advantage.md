# Men outperformed in the VR condition, with gender and video game experience highly correlated, leaving the responsible variable unclear

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

> **Currency** · newest study 2020 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In regression models, men outperformed in the VR condition (interaction B = 2.56, p = 0.011), and video game experience showed a main effect (B = 1.42, p = 0.016) with a potential negative interaction with the desktop condition (B = -1.82, p = 0.03). [→ Madden J. 2020](#madden-j-2020)
`q2 i?` Gender and video game experience were significantly correlated (r = 0.47), so the analysis could not determine which variable drives the VR advantage. [→ Madden J. 2020 (2)](#madden-j-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Madden J. 2020

Madden J., Pandita S., Schuldt J. P., Kim B., S. Won A., Holmes N. G. (2020). Ready student one: Exploring the predictors of student learning in virtual reality. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229788

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory linear regressions of post-test score controlling for pre-score, with condition-by-demographic interactions. The gender × VR interaction was significant (B = 2.56, p = 0.011); the authors interpret that men and participants with video game experience learn more from VR, then desktop, then hands-on.

> "As seen in Table 2, there is an interaction between gender and condition in Model 3, with men outperforming in the VR condition."

### Madden J. 2020 (2)

Madden J., Pandita S., Schuldt J. P., Kim B., S. Won A., Holmes N. G. (2020). Ready student one: Exploring the predictors of student learning in virtual reality. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229788

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Variable correlation checks before regression found gender and video game experience correlated at 0.47, so the two were analyzed in separate models. The article states it is unclear which variable is responsible for the VR-condition differences.

> "The only significant correlation was between gender and video game experience, with a correlation coefficient of 0.47."
