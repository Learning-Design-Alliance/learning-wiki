## Evidence

### Honegger M. 2026

Honegger M., Lindner-Rabl S., Ogliari G., Soraci L., Pertinatsch Ch., Polidori M. C., Roller-Wirnsberger R. (2026). Interprofessional geriatric competencies in the DACH region (Germany, Austria and Switzerland): Insights from a multinational multilingual open online survey. Zeitschrift Fur Gerontologie Und Geriatrie. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00391-026-02646-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Secondary analysis of the PROGRAMMING COST Action 21122 online survey comparing n = 291 DACH HCPs with n = 5134 other European HCPs using a Wilcoxon rank-sum test. The article reports "significantly higher levels of self-assessed knowledge" with p < 0.001; no effect size is printed.

> "Compared with participants from other European region countries, DACH respondents reported significantly higher levels of self-assessed knowledge (p < 0.001)."

### Honegger M. 2026 (2)

Honegger M., Lindner-Rabl S., Ogliari G., Soraci L., Pertinatsch Ch., Polidori M. C., Roller-Wirnsberger R. (2026). Interprofessional geriatric competencies in the DACH region (Germany, Austria and Switzerland): Insights from a multinational multilingual open online survey. Zeitschrift Fur Gerontologie Und Geriatrie. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00391-026-02646-2

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Ordinal logistic regression of overall perceived knowledge adjusted for age and gender in the same survey sample. The printed odds ratio is OR 2.92 (95% CI 2.35–3.65), favouring DACH respondents over other European region respondents.

> "HCPs from the DACH region had significantly higher odds of reporting greater overall perceived knowledge and competence in geriatric care compared with HCPs from other European region countries (OR 2.92, 95% CI 2.35–3.65), independently of age and gender."
