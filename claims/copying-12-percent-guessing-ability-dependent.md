# Copying occurs on average about 12% of initial submissions nearly independent of ability, while low-ability students guess more than high-ability students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the copying-only model, copying does not appear to depend on ability: all learners copy on average about twelve percent of the time, with outliers copying up to half of first attempts. [→ Emre Gönülateş and Gerd Kortemeyer 2015](#emre-gonulates-and-gerd-kortemeyer-2015)
`q2 i?` In the guessing-only model, low-ability learners guess more than high-ability learners; some students guess wrong up to eighty percent of the time, but zero to twenty percent is more typical. [→ Emre Gönülateş and Gerd Kortemeyer 2015 (2)](#emre-gonulates-and-gerd-kortemeyer-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Emre Gönülateş and Gerd Kortemeyer 2015

Emre Gönülateş and Gerd Kortemeyer. (2015). A New Item Response Theory Model for Open-Ended Online Homework with Multiple Allowed Attempts. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.10.010118](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.10.010118)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Copying trait estimates from the reduced 2P3TL model (γj ≡ 0) of first-attempt homework data in the 256-student course; the copying-versus-ability fit shows R² = 0.00109, and "copying does not appear to depend on ability."

> "in this model, copying does not appear to depend on ability: all learners copy, on the average about twelve percent of the time. There are some notable outliers, though, who according to this model copy half of their first homework attempts from others."

### Emre Gönülateş and Gerd Kortemeyer 2015 (2)

Emre Gönülateş and Gerd Kortemeyer. (2015). A New Item Response Theory Model for Open-Ended Online Homework with Multiple Allowed Attempts. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.10.010118](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.10.010118)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Guessing trait estimates from the reduced 2P3TL model (χj ≡ 0) of first-attempt homework data; the guessing-versus-ability fit shows R² = 0.28661 with a negative slope, and "low-ability learners guess more than high-ability learners."

> "It is apparent that low-ability learners guess more than high-ability learners (Fig. 4, left panel), which is not surprising: high ability on exams may well be correlated with more diligence while doing homework."
