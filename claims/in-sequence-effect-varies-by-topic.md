# The in-sequence advantage reached statistical significance for the ORS topic (d = 0.85) but not for the FWS topic (d = 0.38)

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` For the ORS topic, in-sequence students scored 19% higher on the post-test, a large effect (Cohen's d = 0.85). [→ Pusic Martin V 2012](#pusic-martin-v-2012)
`q3 i1` For the FWS topic, in-sequence students scored 6% higher, not statistically significant (Cohen's d = 0.38). [→ Pusic Martin V 2012 (2)](#pusic-martin-v-2012-2)

## Evidence

### Pusic Martin V 2012

Pusic Martin V, MacDonald Wendy A, Eisman Harley O, Black John B. (2012). Reinforcing outpatient medical student learning using brief computer tutorials: the Patient-Teacher-Tutorial sequence. BMC Medical Education, 12:70. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-70

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Topic-level breakout of the randomized ANCOVA: the ORS in-sequence advantage was "19% higher" with "a large Cohen's-d effect size of 0.85 (95% CI 0.25, 1.5)"; the ORS interaction F-test was F = 4.0 (1,87); p ≪ 0.05.

> "This effect reached statistical significance for the ORS topic where students who did the tutorial in the in sequence condition scored 19% higher (95% confidence interval for the difference +7%, +31%) corresponding to a large Cohen's-d effect size of 0.85 (95% CI 0.25, 1.5)."

### Pusic Martin V 2012 (2)

Pusic Martin V, MacDonald Wendy A, Eisman Harley O, Black John B. (2012). Reinforcing outpatient medical student learning using brief computer tutorials: the Patient-Teacher-Tutorial sequence. BMC Medical Education, 12:70. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-70

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

FWS subanalysis of the same randomized trial: in-sequence students scored "an average of 6% HIGHER", d = 0.38 (95% CI -0.20, +0.97), not statistically significant; the FWS interaction F-test was F = 1.7 (1,87); p = NS.

> "Students who did the FWS tutorial in the in sequence condition scored an average of 6% HIGHER on the post-test than the comparison group (95% confidence interval for the difference (-3%, +16%) corresponding to a Cohen's-d effect size of 0.38 (95% CI -0.20, +0.97) which is not statistically significant."
