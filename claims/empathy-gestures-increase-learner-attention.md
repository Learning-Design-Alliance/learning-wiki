The review reports, citing Sidner et al. (2005), that gesture-based empathetic agents drew more learner attention and were perceived as more natural and meaningful than agents lacking social cues. The reviewed study's design is not described in this chapter; no effect sizes are printed.

> "Sidner et al. (2005) have shown that learners direct more attention to the educational agents that employ gestures to indicate empathy, with feedback suggesting that these interactions are perceived more natural and meaningful compared to those lacking social cues."
