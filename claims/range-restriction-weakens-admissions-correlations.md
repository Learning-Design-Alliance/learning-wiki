# Range restriction via admissions selection weakens observed correlations between admissions variables and subsequent student performance, even under an accurate admissions model

## Subclaims
`q1 i3` In simulations, the correlation between an admissions variable and performance drops from r = 0.502 in the full pool to r = 0.219 among admitted students selected by an accurate performance model. [→ Alexander Small 2017](#alexander-small-2017)

## Evidence

### Alexander Small 2017

Alexander Small. (2017). Range restriction, admissions criteria, and correlation studies of standardized tests. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1709.02895](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1709.02895)

`q1 · i3` · `theoretical · r3`

Computer simulation of admissions using an accurate performance model (Equation 2), with correlations computed from 10^5 simulated students (Table 1). The printed effect size r = 0.502 in the full pool fell to r = 0.219 among all admitted students and r = 0.017 in the lower admitted tier, showing range restriction on independent variables.

> "the correlation of each independent variable with observed performance is strongest in the overall pool of domestic students (where the range is widest because it includes low-performing students) and weakest in the lower half of admitted students (where the range is narrowest)."
