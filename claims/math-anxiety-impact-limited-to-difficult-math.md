## Evidence

### Mark H. Ashcraft and Jeremy A. Krause 2007

Mark H. Ashcraft and Jeremy A. Krause. (2007). Working memory, math performance, and math anxiety. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 14(2), 243-248. https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03194059

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Rescoring the WRAT line-by-line for the 80-undergraduate sample showed "Simple accuracy is at ceiling for all groups on the initial lines of the test"; group performance diverged around Line 4 or 5, with the high-anxious group averaging fewer than one in five correct on the most difficult line.

> "Simple accuracy is at ceiling for all groups on the initial lines of the test, suggesting no evidence of lower achievement per se for math-anxious individuals on the whole-number arithmetic taught in elementary school"

### Mark H. Ashcraft and Jeremy A. Krause 2007 (2)

Mark H. Ashcraft and Jeremy A. Krause. (2007). Working memory, math performance, and math anxiety. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 14(2), 243-248. https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03194059

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Citing Faust, Ashcraft, & Fleck (1996), the review reports that "when we gave untimed paper-and-pencil tests of our whole-number arithmetic stimuli, we found no math-anxiety differences on accuracy", though the same stimuli produced anxiety effects in an RT task.

> "when we gave untimed paper-and-pencil tests of our whole-number arithmetic stimuli, we found no math-anxiety differences on accuracy, even though these same stimuli generated online anxiety effects in an RT task"
