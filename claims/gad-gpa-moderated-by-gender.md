# The relationship between possible GAD and GPA is moderated by gender: anxious females have lower GPAs while males show no significant difference

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` A significant group-by-gender interaction: females in the Possible GAD group had lower GPAs (d = 0.48) than females in the Non-GAD group, while males in the Possible GAD group had non-significantly higher GPAs. [→ Awadalla Suheir 2020](#awadalla-suheir-2020)

## Evidence

### Awadalla Suheir 2020

Awadalla Suheir, Davies E. Bethan, Glazebrook Cris. (2020). A longitudinal cohort study to explore the relationship between depression, anxiety and academic performance among Emirati university students. BMC Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-020-02854-z

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Two-way ANOVA with GAD group and gender as factors and age as covariate on baseline GPA, reporting "eta =0.15" for the interaction and the female-contrast effect size "d = 0.48". Males in the Possible GAD group had non-significantly higher GPA.

> "a significant group by gender interaction (F = 5.75, df = 1387, eta =0.15). The effect of GAD group on GPA was moderated by gender: females in the Possible GAD group had lower GPA (mean 2.91 SD, 0.62) than those in Non-GAD group (mean 2.57, SD 0.73) (t = 3.8, df = 280, p < 0.001 d = 0.48)."
