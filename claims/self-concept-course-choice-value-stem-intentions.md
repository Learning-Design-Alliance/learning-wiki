# Domain-specific academic self-concept predicts advanced course choice, whereas value predicts STEM study intentions

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Jansen 2020

Jansen, M., Becker, M., & Neumann, M. (2020). Dimensional Comparison Effects on (Gendered) Educational Choices. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 113(2), 330-350. https://nbn-resolving.org/urn:nbn:de:0111-pedocs-220459

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

SEM path model (probit link) predicting dichotomous advanced mathematics and German course choices in the longitudinal model. The article reports that "self-concept was a significant predictor of course choice" in both domains, while value and the latent self-concept × value interaction added no incremental effects.

> "In both domains, self-concept was a significant predictor of course choice, but there were no incremental effects of value and the interaction between self-concept and value (H2.a and H2.b partially confirmed)."

### Jansen 2020 (2)

Jansen, M., Becker, M., & Neumann, M. (2020). Dimensional Comparison Effects on (Gendered) Educational Choices. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 113(2), 330-350. https://nbn-resolving.org/urn:nbn:de:0111-pedocs-220459

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

SEM path model (probit link) predicting dichotomous STEM study intentions reported in Grade 12. The article states that "STEM study intentions were best predicted by value", with no incremental direct effects of academic self-concept or the self-concept × value interaction.

> "Contrary to course choices, STEM study intentions were best predicted by value with no incremental direct effects of academic self-concept or their interaction (H2.c partially confirmed)."
