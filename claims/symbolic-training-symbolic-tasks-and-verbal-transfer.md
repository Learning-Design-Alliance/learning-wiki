## Evidence

### Honoré Nastasya 2016

Honoré Nastasya, Noël Marie-Pascale. (2016). Improving Preschoolers' Arithmetic through Number Magnitude Training: The Impact of Non-Symbolic and Symbolic Training. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0166685

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVA on the symbolic number line task: the time × group interaction was significant, F(2,48) = 3.31, p = .045, η2 = 0.121, with corrected contrasts showing a significant decrease of imprecision in the symbolic group only.

> "the interaction effect between time and group was significant, F(2,48) = 3.31, p = .045, η2 = 0.121 and corrected contrasts revealed a significant decrease of imprecision in the symbolic group, t(48) = 3.45; p = .003, η2 = 0.198, only"

### Honoré Nastasya 2016 (2)

Honoré Nastasya, Noël Marie-Pascale. (2016). Improving Preschoolers' Arithmetic through Number Magnitude Training: The Impact of Non-Symbolic and Symbolic Training. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0166685

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

On the verbal number comparison task the time × group interaction was marginal, F(2,51) = 2.94; p = .062, η2 = 0.103; the authors nonetheless computed contrasts, which showed "a highly significant enhancement of performance for the symbolic group" and no significant change in the other groups.

> "They indicated a highly significant enhancement of performance for the symbolic group, t(51) = 3.33; p = .006, η2 = 0.178, but not for the non-symbolic, t(51) = 0.45; ns, η2 = 0.005, or control, t(51) = 0.23; ns, η2 = 0.001, groups."
