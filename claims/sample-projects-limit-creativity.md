# Sharing multiple sample e-textile projects and sample code appeared to limit children's creativity, with final artifacts imitating the samples

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Children's final artifacts imitated or closely resembled the sample projects shown at the outset (e.g., a FIFA logo, cats, a heart), and their programming stayed close to the sample code, suggesting the samples limited creativity. [→ Ioannou 2025](#ioannou-2025)

## Evidence

### Ioannou 2025

Ioannou, A., Miliou, O., Georgiou, Y., Timotheou, S., Barkhuus, L., & Rode, J. (2025). Learning design for short-duration e-textile workshops: outcomes on knowledge and skills. Education Tech Research Dev, 73, 443–463. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-024-10411-8

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Qualitative analysis of fieldnotes, artifacts, and video coded with the computational making framework; the authors observed one FIFA logo, three cats, and a heart among projects, with some pairs appearing entirely original (Pineapple, Yellow man, Black TV with camera).

> "While these ideas seemed to have inspired the children, at the same time, they may have limited their creativity. In particular, children's final artifacts imitated or were very similar to the sample e-textile projects, as often occurs with young learners during modelling and observational learning."
