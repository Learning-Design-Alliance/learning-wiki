## Evidence

### Joey Ka-Yee Essoe 2022

Joey Ka-Yee Essoe, Nicco Reggente, Ai Aileen Ohno, Younji Hera Baek, John Dell'Italia and Jesse Rissman. (2022). Enhancing learning and retention with distinctive virtual reality environments and mental context reinstatement. npj Science of Learning 7:31. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00147-6

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

In the behavioural experiment's surprise telephone test (T5) among high-presence participants (mean split), "the dual-context group exhibited a striking 92% (±7%) one-week retention rate" versus 76% (±12%), with a printed interaction effect size of partial η² = 0.11 and simple main effect p = 0.002.

> "the dual-context group exhibited a striking 92% (±7%) one-week retention rate, which was significantly higher than 76% (±12%) retention rate exhibited by the single-context group (Fig. 3d; RM-ANOVA interaction, p = 0.03, ηp 2 = 0.11"

### Joey Ka-Yee Essoe 2022 (2)

Joey Ka-Yee Essoe, Nicco Reggente, Ai Aileen Ohno, Younji Hera Baek, John Dell'Italia and Jesse Rissman. (2022). Enhancing learning and retention with distinctive virtual reality environments and mental context reinstatement. npj Science of Learning 7:31. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00147-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same behavioural experiment found that among low-presence participants "did not perform differently on one-week retention" (p = 0.47); no effect size was printed, so the null is not evidence of equivalence.

> "Single- and dual-context participants who reported low presence did not perform differently on one-week retention (simple main effect for low-presence participants, p = 0.47)"
