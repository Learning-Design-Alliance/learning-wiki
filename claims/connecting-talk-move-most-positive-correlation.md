# Connecting, the least frequent talk move, correlates most positively with attendance, section ratings, and assignment completion

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In linear regressions with instructor and student demographic controls, the hourly rate of Connecting moves correlates positively with subsequent section attendance (0.067), section helpfulness rating (0.038), and completed assignments (0.034), the largest positive coefficients of the five moves. [→ Ashlee Kupor 2023](#ashlee-kupor-2023)

## Evidence

### Ashlee Kupor 2023

Ashlee Kupor, Candice Morgan, Dorottya Demszky. (2023). Measuring Five Accountable Talk Moves to Improve Instruction at Scale. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2311.10749](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2311.10749)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis of ~4k Code in Place transcripts, talk moves predicted by RoBERTa models; linear regressions clustered at instructor level (attendance N=2895, ratings N=774, assignments N=909). Coefficients are unstandardized betas; the article reports no standardized effect size.

> "For example, using 10 more connecting moves in an hour-long session results increases section attendance by .67 (30% more than the average), course ratings by .38 (17%) and number of completed assignments by .34 (34%)."
