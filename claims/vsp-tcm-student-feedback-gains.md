# Students trained with VSP-TCM report greater gains in TCM thinking, history collection, communication, and autonomous learning than traditionally trained students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On 6 of 11 postcourse questionnaire items, significantly more VSP-TCM students endorsed improvements in TCM thinking, medical history collection, syndrome differentiation and critical thinking, comprehensive clinical application, interpersonal communication, and autonomous learning. [→ Yang H 2023](#yang-h-2023)
`q2 i?` Overall course satisfaction did not differ between groups, and students did not report improved medical writing ability. [→ Yang H 2023 (2)](#yang-h-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Yang H 2023

Yang H, Xiao X, Wu X, Fu X, Du Q, Luo Y, Li B, Zeng J, Zhang Y. (2023). Virtual Standardized Patients Versus Traditional Academic Training for Improving Clinical Competence Among Traditional Chinese Medicine Students: Prospective Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/43763

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Self-report postcourse questionnaire administered to both randomized groups (N=84); the printed counts and chi-squared P values for the six significant items are quoted above.

> "The postcourse feedback questionnaire indicated that VSP-TCM can better enhance students’ TCM thinking ability (n=39, 93%, vs n=37, 88%; P=.002), medical history collection (n=38, 90%, vs n=30, 72; P=.001), syndrome differentiation and treatment and critical thinking (n=38, 90%, vs n=37, 88%; P=.046), comprehensive clinical application ability (n=40, 95%, vs n=36, 86%; P=.009), interpersonal communication skills (n=36, 86%, vs n=28, 67%; P=.01), and autonomous learning ability (n=37, 88%, vs n=28, 67%; P=.01) than traditional academic training."

### Yang H 2023 (2)

Yang H, Xiao X, Wu X, Fu X, Du Q, Luo Y, Li B, Zeng J, Zhang Y. (2023). Virtual Standardized Patients Versus Traditional Academic Training for Improving Clinical Competence Among Traditional Chinese Medicine Students: Prospective Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/43763

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Null questionnaire results: overall satisfaction and perceived medical writing improvement did not differ significantly between the two groups.

> "Overall, students in both groups were satisfied with the course (P=.23); however, VSP-TCM did not improve their medical writing ability compared to traditional academic training (P=.13)."
