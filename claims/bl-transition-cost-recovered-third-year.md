## Evidence

### Maloney S 2015

Maloney S, Nicklen P, Rivers G, Foo J, Ooi YY, Reeves S, Walsh K, Ilic D. (2015). A Cost-Effectiveness Analysis of Blended Versus Face-to-Face Delivery of Evidence-Based Medicine to Medical Students. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.4346

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Present-value break-even analysis over 10 years of program iterations, using a real discount rate of 4% and transition costs reported separately from the ICER. The plotted accumulated cost difference shows transition costs "recovered by the lower running cost of the BL method in the third year".

> "The graphed line demonstrates the sunk cost of the transition to the blended learning approach, the cost of which is recovered by the lower running cost of the BL method in the third year."

### Maloney S 2015 (2)

Maloney S, Nicklen P, Rivers G, Foo J, Ooi YY, Reeves S, Walsh K, Ilic D. (2015). A Cost-Effectiveness Analysis of Blended Versus Face-to-Face Delivery of Evidence-Based Medicine to Medical Students. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.4346

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same present-value break-even analysis, plotted across 10 years in Figure 4. The article reports that subsequent years show "a relative saving using the BL method", with the printed present-value savings of approximately AUD $17,000 at 5 years and AUD $63,000 at 10 years.

> "Subsequent years show a relative saving using the BL method, with PV savings of approximately AUD $17,000 after 5 years and AUD $63,000 after 10 years."
