## Evidence

### Awsaf 2026

Awsaf, A.A., Giansanti, N., Sunbury, S., & Dou, R. (2025, March). Concurrent phenomenological analysis of STEM career aspirations in underrepresented youth: Role of experiences and identity [Presentation]. 2025 NARST Annual International Conference, National Harbor, MD. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2607.06035](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.06035)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Cross-case observation from the interviews: non-STEM participants held awards, certificates, and competition invitations, yet participation was driven by external motivations such as high school requirements. Jessica had strong support, high math competency, and several STEM accomplishments and "never experienced any misrecognition, yet she never considered herself a STEM person."

> "their narratives suggested that public recognition of STEM accomplishments, without a personal interest in STEM fields, may not meaningfully nurture an increase in STEM identity."
