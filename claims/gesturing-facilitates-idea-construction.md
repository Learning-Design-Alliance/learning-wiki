## Evidence

### Rachel E. Scherr 2007

Rachel E. Scherr. (2007). Gesture analysis for physics education researchers. Accepted to Physical Review Special Topics – Physics Education Research, May 2007. https://journals.aps.org/prper/ [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0712.0581](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0712.0581)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes this to Goldin-Meadow and colleagues' experiment on explaining math and word/letter recall, as evidence that gesture lightens cognitive load. The review narrates the finding without effect sizes.

> "Gesturing in order to explain a math problem, for example, improves participants’ ability to simultaneously remember a list of words or letters."

### Rachel E. Scherr 2007 (2)

Rachel E. Scherr. (2007). Gesture analysis for physics education researchers. Accepted to Physical Review Special Topics – Physics Education Research, May 2007. https://journals.aps.org/prper/ [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0712.0581](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0712.0581)

`q1 · i?` · `review · r3`

An anecdote the author uses to illustrate a kinesthetic mechanism: sensorimotor information from holding the keys in the moving chair changed the student's prediction without her needing to do the experiment.

> "She initially thought the keys would fall in front of her foot (due their initial forward velocity), but when she sat in the rolling chair and held her hand out over her foot, she immediately said the keys would hit her toe."
