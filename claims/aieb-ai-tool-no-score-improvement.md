# In the AIEB module, AI tool access showed no statistically significant difference in exam marks versus control, alongside reduced score variability.

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Amir Yunus 2025

Amir Yunus, Gay Peng Rend, Lee Oon Teng (2025). From Co-Design to Metacognitive Laziness: Evaluating Generative AI in Vocational Education. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2512.12306](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2512.12306)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of AIEB examination marks between a control group without AI tool access and an AI tool group in a module known for difficulty and high failure rates. Means of 17.43 vs 17.04 marks showed "no statistically significant difference"; equivalence was not tested and no effect size is printed.

> "The control group achieved a mean of 17.43 marks (38.72%), compared to the AI tool group's 17.04 marks (37.87%), showing no statistically significant difference."

### Amir Yunus 2025 (2)

Amir Yunus, Gay Peng Rend, Lee Oon Teng (2025). From Co-Design to Metacognitive Laziness: Evaluating Generative AI in Vocational Education. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2512.12306](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2512.12306)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

In the same AIEB comparison, score variability fell "from 5.15 to 4.70 in the AI group", an 8.7% reduction the authors link to more consistent performance across students.

> "The standard deviation was reduced from 5.15 to 4.70 in the AI group, representing an 8.7% reduction in score variability."
