# As English proficiency decreases, children with EAL become more likely than monolingual peers with equivalent scores to exceed Year 2 curriculum targets

## Evidence

### Whiteside 2017

Whiteside, Katie E.; Gooch, Debbie; Norbury, Courtenay F. (2017). English Language Proficiency and Early School Attainment Among Children Learning English as an Additional Language. Child Development, 88(3), 812-827. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.12615

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r3`

Hierarchical logistic regression predicting above-target Year 2 performance; the unadjusted interaction odds ratio was 1.05 (95% CI 1.03–1.08, p < .001), while EAL status alone was not significant (OR = 0.87, p = .404).

> "However, there was a significant CCC–S × EAL Status interaction; as CCC–S scores increased, reflecting lower English language proficiency in reception year, children with EAL were more likely to perform above target in Year 2 assessments relative to monolingual peers with equivalent CCC–S scores"
