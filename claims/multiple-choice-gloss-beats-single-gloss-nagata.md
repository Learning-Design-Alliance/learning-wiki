# A multiple-choice gloss format (L1 + L2) produced significantly higher vocabulary posttest scores than a single-gloss format in Nagata's Japanese study

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` University students of Japanese using a multiple-choice gloss with both English and Japanese outperformed students using a single English translation gloss on a vocabulary posttest. [→ Yun 2008](#yun-2008)

## Evidence

### Yun 2008

Yun, J. H. (2008). The Effects of Hypertext Glosses on L2 Vocabulary Acquisition: A Meta-Analysis. Doctoral dissertation, University of Kansas. https://kuscholarworks.ku.edu

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

The dissertation reports, citing Nagata (1999), a study of 26 university students of Japanese measured by vocabulary pretest and posttest, in which the "multiple-choice-gloss format group ( M= 13.5, SD = 5.5)" beat the single-gloss group. Only means and SDs are printed; no effect size.

> "The multiple-choice-gloss format group ( M= 13.5, SD = 5.5) outperformed significantly better on a vocabulary posttest than the single-gloss format group ( M= 10.8, SD = 4.8)."
