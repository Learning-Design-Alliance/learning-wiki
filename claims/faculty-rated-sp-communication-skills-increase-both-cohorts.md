# Faculty-rated basic communication skills with standardized patients increase significantly during the program in both the 2008 and 2009 cohorts

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Faculty SEGUE ratings of interviews with standardized patients rose significantly from T0 in both samples (2008: F(3, 72) = 40.31, p < 0.01; 2009: F(3, 57) = 48.69, p < 0.01), with significant gains at T1, T2 and T3 in each cohort. [→ Carvalho IP 2014](#carvalho-ip-2014)

## Evidence

### Carvalho IP 2014

Carvalho IP, Pais VG, Silva FR, Martins R, Figueiredo-Braga M, Pedrosa R, Almeida SS, Correia L, Ribeiro-Silva R, Castro-Vale I, Teles A, Mota-Cardoso R. (2014). Teaching communication skills in clinical settings: comparing two applications of a comprehensive program with standardized and real patients. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-14-92

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

GLM repeated-measures analysis of two consecutive cohorts (25 professionals in 2008, 20 in 2009) evaluated at four time points on SEGUE-rated interviews with standardized patients. The article reports "F(3, 57) = 48.69, p < 0.01" for the 2009 sample and a significant effect for the 2008 group as well.

> "Effects of the program on communication skills assessed by faculty are statistically significant for the 2009 sample (as shown by within-subjects tests, including corrected Greenhouse-Geisser, Huynh-Feldt and Lower-bound procedures, F(3, 57) = 48.69, p < 0.01), as well as the 2008 group"
