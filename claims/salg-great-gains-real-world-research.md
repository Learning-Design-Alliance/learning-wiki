## Evidence

### Peter Hugo Nelson 2012

Peter Hugo Nelson. (2012). Teaching introductory STEM with the Marble Game. [doi:10.1016/j.bpj.2012.11.2943](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bpj.2012.11.2943)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Student Assessment of their Learning Gains survey administered after the final exam but before final grades were posted in Biophysics (Spring 2012), with open-text comments extracted verbatim. Students "perceived that their learning gains were greater for real scientific research and real world issues than for other classes", and their written comments supported the assertion that the materials relate to real world issues and scientific research.

> "Students assessed that they had made great gains in their ability to perform real (quantitative modeling) scientific research (Table 1). Students perceived that their learning gains were greater for real scientific research and real world issues than for other classes."
