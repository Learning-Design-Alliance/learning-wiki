Evidence¶

Fanguy 2017¶

Fanguy, M., Costley, J., & Baldwin, M. (2017). Pinch Hitter: The Effectiveness of Content Summaries Delivered by a Guest Lecturer in Online Course Videos. International Review of Research in Open and Distributed Learning, 18(7). http://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl/article/view/3210

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Authors' interpretation (type e) of the quasi-experimental results, offered as a plausible explanation rather than a tested mechanism; the guest-lecturer condition uniquely contained scene changes while summaries-only kept the same slide background throughout.

"The sudden change in the setting of the video from a familiar-looking PowerPoint slide background to an informal coffee shop scene may have seemed jarring to the students, causing an interruption to their concentration on the content of the video."
