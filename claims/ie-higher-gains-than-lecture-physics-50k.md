## Evidence

### Von Korff 2016

Von Korff, J., Archibeque, B., Gomez, K.A., Heckendorf, T., McKagan, S.B., Sayre, E.C., Schenk, E.W., Shepherd, C., Sorell, L. (2016). Secondary Analysis of Teaching Methods in Introductory Physics: a 50k-Student Study. Originally published 2014. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1603.00516](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1603.00516)

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Secondary analysis of published FCI and FMCE gains from about 45,000 students in introductory mechanics courses in the United States and Canada, with the individual class as the unit of analysis. The article reports a "significantly higher" gain for interactive engagement than for traditional lecture on both tests.

> "Our analysis confirms previous studies8,9 showing that classes that use interactive engagement have significantly higher learning gains than those that use traditional lecture (Fig. 1)."
