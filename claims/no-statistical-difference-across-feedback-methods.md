## Evidence

### S. Bansal 2025

S. Bansal, M. Yadav, J. Zhou, R. A. Ebert‐Allen, R. M. Klute, W. F. Bond, S. Bhat. (2025). Medical Students' Perception of Automated Note Feedback After Simulated Encounters. The Clinical Teacher 22(6): e70273. https://doi.org/10.1111/tct.70273

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Descriptive survey results from 37 students on a 6-point Likert scale across clarity, perceived utility, and skill-building domains. Results were "marginally positive but no statistical differences were found across feedback methods"; the study was not powered for superiority-inferiority.

> "Survey results for automated feedback in areas including clarity, perceived utility, and impact on skill building in history taking and physical examination, were marginally positive but no statistical differences were found across feedback methods."
