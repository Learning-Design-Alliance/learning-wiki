Evidence¶

Utkarsh Upadhyay 2021¶

Utkarsh Upadhyay, Graham Lancashire, Christoph Moser and Manuel Gomez-Rodriguez. (2021). Large-scale randomized experiments reveals that machine learning-based instruction helps people memorize more effectively. npj Science of Learning 6:26. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00105-8

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Engagement analysis from the randomized controlled trial, reported in the main text with details deferred to Supplementary Information. The article reports median return-likelihood percentages and notes the early-quit counter-effect; no standardized effect size is printed.

"learners of the ‘select’ (‘difﬁculty’) group were 50.6% (47.6%) more likely, in median, to return to the app within 4 –7 days than learners of the ‘random’ group. However, learners of the ‘select’ group were also more likely to stop using the app in the initial 2 days than those of the other groups."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Algorithm-selected learners remember content over ~69% longer, with median half-life increases of ~92% vs random and ~40% vs difficulty groups — related
  • ML-based question selection (SELECT) lowers learners' normalized empirical forgetting rate relative to difficulty-ordered and random selection after controlling for review time and number of reviews — related
  • Regression analysis shows the spacing effect holds in aggregate and the select group's advantage over other groups increases with number of reviews — related
