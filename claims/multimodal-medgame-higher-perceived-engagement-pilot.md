# In a pilot with eight medical students, multimodal MedGame was perceived as more engaging and useful than text-only MedGame and original case material

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Multimodal MedGame achieved the highest overall perception score, with a significant one-sided paired advantage over text-only MedGame (p = 0.0039). [→ Qian Wu 2026](#qian-wu-2026)

## Evidence

### Qian Wu 2026

Qian Wu, Xinrong Zhou, Zizhan Ma, Kai Chen, Zheyao Gao, Xun Lin, Hongqiu Wu, Longfei Gou, Yixiao Liu, Ann Sin Nga Lau, Qi Dou. (2026). MedGame: Storytelling Gamification Empowered by Large Language Models for Medical Education. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21570 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.21570](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2607.21570)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Paired pilot perception study with eight senior medical students, each rating the same five clinical cases under original case, text-only MedGame, and multimodal MedGame conditions on 1–5 Likert scales; student-level means were averaged over cases before testing. No effect size printed.

> "multimodal MedGame achieves the highest overall perception score (4.19), followed by text-only MedGame (3.79) and original case material (3.19); the multimodal advantage over text-only is significant under a one-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test over student-level means (p= 0.0039 )."
