Evidence¶

Davis 2010¶

Davis, E. L., Levine, L. J., Lench, H. C., & Quas, J. A. (2010). Metacognitive Emotion Regulation: Children’s Awareness that Changing Thoughts and Goals Can Alleviate Negative Emotions. Emotion, 10(4), 498–510. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018428

q3 · i? · qualitative · r3

Interview study of 80 kindergarten children (ages 5;1–6;5) who heard four familiar narratives and answered multiple probe questions about how protagonists could feel better. The article reports "More than half of the children in this study (52%) reported at least one metacognitive regulation strategy."

Related Claims¶

  • In hypothetical scenarios, 5- and 6-year-olds most often suggest goal reinstatement and goal substitution, and their strategies do not differ for sadness versus anger — related
  • When recalling their own experiences, 5- and 6-year-olds use metacognitive strategies more to alleviate sadness and fear than anger — related
  • Children's emotion-specific strategy use emerges for autobiographical events but not hypothetical stories, suggesting personal experience supports flexible regulation — related
  • Five- and six-year-olds describe changing thoughts more often than changing goals as a metacognitive strategy — related
  • Communicating feelings of difficulty is significantly associated with six-year-olds' deployment of metacognition and self-regulation, with the exception of planning — related
  • Younger children (3–5-year-olds) answered story comprehension questions less accurately than all older age groups — related
