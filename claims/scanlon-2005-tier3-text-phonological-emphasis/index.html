Evidence¶

Austin 2017¶

Austin, C. R., Vaughn, S., & McClelland, A. M. (2017). Intensive Reading Interventions for Inadequate Responders in Grades K–3: A Synthesis. Learning Disability Quarterly, 40(4), 191–210. https://doi.org/10.1177/0731948717714446

q3 · i2 · quant-synthesis · r2

Study in Table 6: grade 1 inadequate responders were randomly assigned to one of two one-on-one Tier 3 conditions targeting fluency via re-reading, phonological skills, sight words, and writing; the "Text-Emphasis condition spent more time reading and re-reading text". Effects versus typical school remediation were positive.

"Treatment (Text Emphasis) > Control: WRMT-R Basic Skills: d = 0.58; WRMT-R Word ID: d = 0.59; WRMT-R Word Attack: d = 0.40; WIAT Reading Comprehension: d = 0.42"

Related Claims¶

  • In Denton et al. (2006), a Tier 3 Phono-Graphix intervention for grades 1–3 inadequate responders produced significantly better performance than inadequate-responder controls on all reading measures — related
  • In Denton et al. (2013), an adapted Tier 3 intervention with data-based decision making produced positive effects for grade 2 inadequate responders versus inadequate-responder controls — related
  • In Wanzek and Vaughn (2008), doubling Tier 3 dosage to two daily 30-min sessions did not produce positive effects, while one daily session showed mixed effects for grade 1 inadequate responders versus at-risk controls — related

  • When Tier 3 inadequate responders are compared with higher-responding peers, treatment groups show large negative effects, indicating they often fail to catch up — related
