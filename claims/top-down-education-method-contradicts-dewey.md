# The Republic's top-down education method contradicts Dewey's pedagogical principles and his understanding of democracy

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · `q1` argument or single case

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The method adopted in the Republic's education project, from the top to the bottom and from the outside towards the inside, contradicts Dewey's pedagogical principles and his understanding of democracy. [→ Özsoy 2009](#ozsoy-2009)
`q1 i?` Where education acquires meaning through its instrumental aspect in the Turkish modernization project, Dewey attributes education an intrinsic value instead of an instrumental one. [→ Özsoy 2009 (2)](#ozsoy-2009-2)

## Evidence

### Özsoy 2009

Özsoy, S. (2009). "Turkish Modernization," Democracy, and Education: An Analysis from Dewey's Perspective. Educational Sciences: Theory & Practice, 9(4). https://eric.ed.gov/?q=%22Turkish+Modernization%22+Democracy+and+Education

`q1 · i?`

Theoretical argument in the balance-sheet section, where the author distinguishes Dewey's democracy design from an authoritarian modernization implemented as a kind of human and social engineering. The quoted method is said to contradict Dewey's pedagogy and democracy.

> "The method adopted in the Republic's education project is "from the top to the bottom, from the outside towards the inside." This method contradicts with Dewey's pedagogical principles as much as his understanding of democracy"

### Özsoy 2009 (2)

Özsoy, S. (2009). "Turkish Modernization," Democracy, and Education: An Analysis from Dewey's Perspective. Educational Sciences: Theory & Practice, 9(4). https://eric.ed.gov/?q=%22Turkish+Modernization%22+Democracy+and+Education

`q1 · i?`

The article contrasts the instrumental framing of education in the Turkish modernization project with Dewey's attribution to education of an "intrinsic" value rather than an "instrumental" one.

> "Whilst education always acquires meaning and value with its instrumental aspect in the Turkish modernization project; Dewey (1996) attributes education an "intrinsic" value instead of an "instrumental" one."
