# Students rate their test-taking experience more favorably on paper-based exams than on computer-based exams, with medium-to-large effects

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Students report being better able to work in a structured manner, monitor progress, and concentrate during paper-based exams than during the computer-based exam (Cohen's d 0.33 to 0.64). [→ Boevé A.J. 2015](#boeve-aj-2015)

## Evidence

### Boevé A.J. 2015

Boevé A.J., Meijer R.R., Albers C.J., Beetsma Y., Bosker R.J. (2015). Introducing Computer-Based Testing in High-Stakes Exams in Higher Education: Results of a Field Experiment. PLoS ONE 10(12): e0143616. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0143616

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Bonferroni corrected (α = .017) dependent-sample t-tests comparing ratings of the computer-based exam with ratings of paper-based exams in general. Structured approach d = 0.64, monitor progress d = 0.33, concentration d = 0.46; all p < .001.

> "Table 4 shows that students are more positive in terms of their ability to work in a structured manner, monitor their progress, and concentrate during paper-based exams compared to the computer-based exam, with medium (0.33) to large (0.64) effect sizes."
