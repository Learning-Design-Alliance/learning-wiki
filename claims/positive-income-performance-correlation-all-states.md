## Evidence

### Wodarz M.N. 2025

Wodarz M.N., Komarova N.L., Ma T. (2025). The functional form of the association between K-12 student performance and household income in U.S. school districts. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0329296

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of district-level data from 42 U.S. states (2018-2019), reported in Results. The article reports that the Pearson correlation between performance score and income "is positive for all the states and all the subject-grade combinations," with varying strength; no correlation coefficients are printed for individual states.

> "Fig 3 shows histograms of the Pearson correlation coefficients between the performance score and the income for all the states. It is positive for all the states and all the subject-grade combinations, although the correlation strength varies."

### Wodarz M.N. 2025 (2)

Wodarz M.N., Komarova N.L., Ma T. (2025). The functional form of the association between K-12 student performance and household income in U.S. school districts. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0329296

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Based on the fitted linear slopes across states (Results). The article reports the typical slope b≈3.6×10−3, implying "a $10,000 difference in the median household income will increase a district's performance score by about 3.6%." This is an unstandardised slope, not a standardised effect size.

> "For the typical value of the slope b≈3.6×10−3 for function (2), we can see that a $10,000 difference in the median household income will increase a district's performance score by about 3.6%."
