# The self-diagnosis treatment produced no significant difference in presentation scores on the subsequent midterm problem, despite the rubric group's better performance on the self-diagnosis exercise itself

## Evidence

### E. Yerushalmi 2008

E. Yerushalmi, A. Mason, E. Cohen, C. Singh. (2008). Effect of Self Diagnosis on Subsequent Problem Solving Performance. AIP Conf. Proc. 1064. [doi:10.1063/1.3021272](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3021272)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Classroom quasi-experiment; Table 6 gives midterm presentation means across groups A-D (0.410-0.462) and Table 7 reports between-group p-values of 0.580 (B vs A) and 0.154 (D vs C); Table 8 reports no significant intra-group correlations.

> "There is no significant difference or correlation between any of the groups. In addition, there was no effect of the treatment on presentation performance, even though group B did better on the self-diagnosis exercise."
