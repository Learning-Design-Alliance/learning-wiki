q1 · i? · theoretical · r2

Theoretical argument in an editorial: some teachers realize the chief difficulty is that the pupil has no thoughts to express, insisting that "the real task of composition work is thought building" and calling for more rather than polishing. No data are reported.

"These teachers insist that the real task of composition work is thought building. Instead of refining and polishing and deleting and rearranging, the composition teacher should take everything that is presented and call for more, — always more."

Related Claims¶

  • The editorial proposes building thought-content scales from roughly ten topics of child interest, administered to many children, to yield a profile of thinking power — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • The editorial argues existing composition scales measure form on topics of artificial interest and cannot measure improvement in thought content — related
  • The editorial frames remediation of weak thinking fields versus intensive cultivation of strong fields, and transfer across fields, as open questions — related
  • The editorial predicts thought-content scales would amply repay their construction cost through diagnostic value and studies of higher thought processes — related
