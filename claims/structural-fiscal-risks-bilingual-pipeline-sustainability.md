# Structural and fiscal conditions—including grade-3 class-size incentive cliffs and declining enrollment—jeopardize the bilingual teacher pipeline and program sustainability

## Evidence

### Grayson 2026

Grayson, D., & Jacobson, A. (2026). Advancing achievement through biliteracy: Brief #2: State policy levers to support a strong and sustainable bilingual teacher workforce in California. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED681323

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Regional pilot partners identified this structural challenge to bilingual program continuity: the LCFF class-size incentive ends after grade 3, and some families stay at their current school rather than transfer, causing "unintended program attrition," most acute in smaller or partial-site programs.

> "Under the Local Control Funding Formula, class-size incentives end after grade 3. As student cohorts advance to grade 4, class sizes increase and smaller residual groups make it difficult to maintain funding for full bilingual classes."

### Grayson 2026 (2)

Grayson, D., & Jacobson, A. (2026). Advancing achievement through biliteracy: Brief #2: State policy levers to support a strong and sustainable bilingual teacher workforce in California. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED681323

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Statewide analysis finding: although Multilingual Learner enrollment has declined over the past decade, demand for bilingual programs persists, while declining K–12 enrollment and budget pressures create sustainability risks for preparation and program expansion.

> "Despite recent growth in bilingual authorizations, the number of BLA teachers remains a small share of the total workforce, and declining K–12 enrollment and budget pressures risk undermining efforts to expand access to bilingual programs and increase BLA workforce capacity."
