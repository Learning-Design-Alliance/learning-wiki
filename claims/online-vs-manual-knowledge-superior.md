## Evidence

### Richmond Helen 2017

Richmond Helen, Copsey Bethan, Hall Amanda M., Davies David, Lamb Sarah E. (2017). A systematic review and meta-analysis of online versus alternative methods for training licensed health care professionals to deliver clinical interventions. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1047-4

`q2 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random-effects meta-analysis of three RCTs comparing online training with written manuals in licensed health professionals. Knowledge favoured online with printed effect size SMD 0.99 (95% CI 0.02, 1.96), a large effect by the article's own thresholds; clinical behaviour showed no difference (SMD 0.09). All assessments were study-derived self-assessments; estimates judged very low quality.

> "For knowledge (3 RCTs, n = 180), we found very low quality evidence that online training was more effective than a manual (SMD 0.99; 95% CI: 0.02, 1.96). For clinical behaviour, we found very low quality evidence that online training was no different to using a written manual (SMD 0.09; 95% CI: -0.27, 0.46)."
