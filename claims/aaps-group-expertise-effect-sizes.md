## Evidence

### Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Surveying Graduate Students' Attitudes and Approaches to Problem Solving. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/37/5/055704](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/37/5/055704)

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Cross-group survey comparison of introductory students, astronomy students, graduate students (introductory and graduate problem framing), and faculty. Cohen's means difference effect sizes were calculated over all 33 questions; the article reports "large to very large effect size (1 < d < 2.5), in favor of the more assumed expert-like group." All pairwise t-tests were significant except between the two faculty groups.

> "Table 2 shows that the ef- fect sizes between groups of different levels of exper- tise have a large to very large effect size (1 < d < 2.5), in favor of the more assumed expert-like group."
