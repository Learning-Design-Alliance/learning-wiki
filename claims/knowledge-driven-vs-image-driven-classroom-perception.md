# Expert teachers' classroom perception appears knowledge-driven while novices' appears image-driven, guided by salient visual features

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 1 associational `r1`, 1 theoretical `r3` · `q1`–`q2`

## Subclaims

`q1 i?` The authors assert expert perception is knowledge-driven and novice search image-driven. [→ Charlotte E. Wolff 2016](#charlotte-e-wolff-2016)

`q2 i?` Experts' eye movements disperse in a rapid, focused search for relevant events driven by knowledge, while novices' eye movements are strewn across many potentially relevant events, driven by visually salient features. [→ Charlotte E. Wolff 2020](#charlotte-e-wolff-2020)

## Evidence

### Charlotte E. Wolff 2016

Charlotte E. Wolff, Halszka Jarodzka, Niek van den Bogert, Henny P. A. Boshuizen. (2016). Teacher vision: expert and novice teachers' perception of problematic classroom management scenes. Instr Sci 44:243-265. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-016-9367-z

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Authors' interpretive claim (type e) framing their exploratory findings: experts' event-structured knowledge drives monitoring of interactions, while novices' monitoring is guided by location of disruptions and salient features such as bright colours. The eye-tracking results are consistent with this but the interpretation itself is not directly tested.

> "In brief, we assert that the classroom perception of expert teachers’ is more likely to be knowledge-driven, whereas novices’ search is likely to be more image-driven."

### Charlotte E. Wolff 2020

Charlotte E. Wolff, Halszka Jarodzka, Henny P.A. Boshuizen. (2020). Classroom Management Scripts: a Theoretical Model Contrasting Expert and Novice Teachers' Knowledge and Awareness of Classroom Events. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09542-0

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article reports eye-tracking research (Wolff et al. 2016) showing novices' scattered, image-driven (bottom-up) search versus experts' rapid, focused, knowledge-driven (top-down) search, with experts repeatedly monitoring areas displaying student interactions. The article concludes internal factors, i.e. knowledge, take priority over external factors in perception.

> "Novices'eye movements were shown to be strewn across many potentially relevant events in the classroom, resulting in a scattered search for information that was driven by visually salient features of the classroom"
