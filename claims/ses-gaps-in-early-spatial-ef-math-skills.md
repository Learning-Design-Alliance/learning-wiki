# Higher-SES preschoolers outperform lower-SES peers on math, spatial, EF, and vocabulary measures, except the Tap Test where the difference is a non-significant trend

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Independent-samples t-tests showed higher-SES children scored significantly better on all measures except the Tap Test (p = .056); the SES gap on WIAT math had Hedges g = 1.79 and on TOSA g = 1.63. [→ Verdine 2014](#verdine-2014)

## Evidence

### Verdine 2014

Verdine, B. N., Irwin, C. M., Golinkoff, R. M., & Hirsh-Pasek, K. (2014). Contributions of Executive Function and Spatial Skills to Preschool Mathematics Achievement. Journal of Experimental Child Psychology, 126, 37-51. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2014.02.012

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Independent-samples t-tests comparing lower-SES (n = 20) and higher-SES (n = 24) groups, reported in Results and Table 3 with Hedges g effect sizes: WIAT g = 1.79, TOSA g = 1.63, VMI g = 1.12, FIST g = 0.84, PPVT percentile g = 1.81; the Tap Test showed only a trend, p = .056 (g = 0.58).

> "Children in the higher-SES group demonstrated significantly better performance on all the measures except the Tap Test (see Table 3), which had a similar trend (p = .056)."
