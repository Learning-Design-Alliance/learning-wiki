## Evidence

### Shaban Eman E. 2025

Shaban Eman E., Elmelliti Hussam, Shaban Amira, Shaban Ahmed, Zaki Hany A. (2025). Impact of early clinical exposure on medical students’ competency development: a systematic review of evidence from Asian and African countries. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08103-9

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Systematic review with narrative synthesis of 15 studies of early clinical exposure (ECE) in Asian and African medical schools. Each perception finding here comes from one included study: in one cross-sectional study most students perceived that ECE improved their problem-solving skills; in another, "12.1% strongly agreed and 50.5% agreed" that ECE increased their confidence to deal with patients; and a qualitative study reported that 59% of students redefined their understanding of medical professionalism after ECE, which is a change in understanding rather than a perceived improvement in professionalism. All are self-reports, and no effect size is printed.

> "the majority of medical students receiving the ECE intervention reported that this method increased their confidence to deal with patients (12.1% strongly agreed and 50.5% agreed)"
