# Brain-based learning delivered with technology outperformed conventional teaching on the immediate post-test in application level and overall achievement

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a quasi-experimental study of grade eight science students, the technological experimental group scored significantly higher than the control group on the application level and overall performance of the immediate post-test. [→ Al-Balushi 2018](#al-balushi-2018)

## Evidence

### Al-Balushi 2018

Al-Balushi, K. A., & Al-Balushi, S. M. (2018). Effectiveness of Brain-Based Learning for Grade Eight Students’ Direct and Postponed Retention in Science. International Journal of Instruction, 11(3), 525-538. https://doi.org/10.12973/iji.2018.11336a

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental study of 197 grade-eight students in six randomly assigned classes; MANCOVA on post-test scores showed significant differences in the application level (f=3.86) and overall performance (f=4.25), with LSD comparisons favouring the technological group, described as "in favour of the technological group".

> "The ad -hoc LSD analysis reveals that the only significant differences were between the technolog ical and control groups in both the application level and the overall performance in the post -test (in favour of the technological group)."
