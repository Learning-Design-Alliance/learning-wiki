Spencer Mercedes 2022¶

Spencer Mercedes, Fuchs Lynn S., Geary David C., Fuchs Douglas. (2022). Connections between Mathematics and Reading Development: Numerical Cognition Mediates Relations between Foundational Competencies and Later Academic Outcomes. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000670

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Model-fit results from the path analysis on 370 children: "The final pruned model had excellent fit, χ2 = 14.526 (15), p = .486; CFI = 1.000; TLI = 1.001; RMSEA < .001" with the printed variance-accounted-for values for all three outcomes.

"The final pruned model had excellent fit, χ2 = 14.526 (15), p = .486; CFI = 1.000; TLI = 1.001; RMSEA < .001; 90% CI [0.000, 0.048], p-close = .962; SRMR = .013, and accounted for 58.0%, 62.2%, and 53.2% of the variance in later calculations, WPS and reading, respectively."

Related Claims¶

  • Crossover effects: early reading fluency predicts later mathematics outcomes more strongly than early mathematics skills predict later reading — related
  • Numerical cognition assessed at end of first grade statistically mediates associations between early processing speed, working memory, calculation skill, word-problem solving, and attentive behavior and later calculations, word-problem solving, and reading — related
  • End-of-first-grade numerical cognition directly predicts later calculations, word-problem solving, and word reading, with the strongest relation to calculations — related
  • Early listening comprehension directly predicts later word-problem solving and word reading but not calculations, and numerical cognition does not mediate this relation — related
  • Early working memory, processing speed, and attentive behavior predict later calculations, word-problem solving, and reading, but nonverbal reasoning is not significantly associated with any outcome — related
