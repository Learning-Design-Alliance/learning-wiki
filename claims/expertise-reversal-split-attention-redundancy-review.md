## Evidence

### Kalyuga 2003

Kalyuga, S., Ayres, P., Chandler, P., & Sweller, J. (2003). The Expertise Reversal Effect. Educational Psychologist, 38(1), 23-31. https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes this to Kalyuga et al. (1998): in experiments with electrical trainees, novices needed integrated text but more experienced trainees did significantly better with diagrams alone and reported lower mental effort, so "The split-attention effect for novices was replaced by the redundancy effect for experts."

> "Kalyuga, Chandler, and Sweller (1998) found that inexperienced electrical trainees benefitted from textual explanations integrated into the diagrams of electrical circuits (to reduce split attention). They were not able to comprehend a diagram-only format. However, more experienced trainees performed significantly better with the electrical circuit diagram-only format."

### Kalyuga 2003 (2)

Kalyuga, S., Ayres, P., Chandler, P., & Sweller, J. (2003). The Expertise Reversal Effect. Educational Psychologist, 38(1), 23-31. https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Yeung, Jin, and Sweller (1998), a textual study showing that integrated explanatory notes helped low language-competence learners but "retarded learning for more knowledgeable learners."

> "Integrating explanatory notes into the primary text assisted learners with low levels of language competence. The same format retarded learning for more knowledgeable learners because the integrated notes, although redundant, were difficult to ignore when integrated into the primary text."

### Ginns 2006

Ginns, P. (2006). Integrating information: A meta-analysis of the spatial contiguity and temporal contiguity effects. Learning and Instruction, 16(6), 511-525. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2006.10.001

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

The discussion cites Kalyuga et al. (1998), reported in the article as finding that after domain training, the diagram-only version outperformed integrated and split-source versions — a redundancy effect limiting the generalisability of integration benefits to novices. This is a cited study narrated in the discussion, not re-analysed in the meta-analysis.

> "Following training to increase expertise in the specific domain, those who studied a diagram-only version of the materials outperformed those in the other conditions, indicating that a redundancy effect –  that is, more experienced students could rely on their schemas to comprehend and learn from the diagrammatic materials"
