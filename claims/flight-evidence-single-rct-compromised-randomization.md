# The supporting evidence for FLIGHT comes from a single randomized controlled trial with compromised random assignment

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The one study meeting WWC group design standards (with reservations) is an RCT whose random assignment was compromised because one student was not randomly assigned and unequal assignment probabilities were not accounted for in the analysis. [→ What Works Clearinghouse 2019](#what-works-clearinghouse-2019)

## Evidence

### What Works Clearinghouse 2019

What Works Clearinghouse, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. (2019). Transition to College intervention report: Facilitating Long-Term Improvements in Graduation and Higher Education for Tomorrow (FLIGHT)/Take Stock in Children (TSIC)®. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/694

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

WWC assessment of Philp (2015): 315 eligible students in grades 7, 9, and 11 in four Florida districts were randomly assigned to FLIGHT or comparison, stratified by county and grade, but "compromised random assignment" lowered the rating to meets-standards-with-reservations.

> "This is a randomized controlled trial (RCT) with compromised random assignment, and the analysis did not account for unequal probabilities of assignment to the treatment and comparison groups in one study site. Baseline equivalence of the treatment and comparison group is established."
