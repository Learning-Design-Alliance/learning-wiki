# Heterogeneity within demographic categories means group variables as predictors can disadvantage atypical group members and underrepresented groups

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` If there is significant heterogeneity within a group, using the group variable will disadvantage atypical members, biasing towards a group mean. [→ Baker 2023](#baker-2023)
`q1 i?` Including one group's demographic variable can eliminate a shared mediating variable, worsening prediction for other underrepresented groups not represented in the data. [→ Baker 2023 (2)](#baker-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Baker 2023

Baker, R. S., Esbenshade, L., Vitale, J., & Karumbaiah, S. (2023). Using Demographic Data as Predictor Variables: a Questionable Choice. Journal of Educational Data Mining, 15(2). https://jedm.educationaldatamining.org/index.php/JEDM/article/view/619

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument (type e) in section 4.3 on categorization limits: "If there is significant heterogeneity within a group, then the use of the group variable wi ll disadvantage atypical members"; the article adds that heavily demographic models may predict typical members better but atypical members worse, biasing towards a group mean.

### Baker 2023 (2)

Baker, R. S., Esbenshade, L., Vitale, J., & Karumbaiah, S. (2023). Using Demographic Data as Predictor Variables: a Questionable Choice. Journal of Educational Data Mining, 15(2). https://jedm.educationaldatamining.org/index.php/JEDM/article/view/619

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article's worked example: if only African American can be included as a variable, "including that variable may eliminate disciplinary in fractions from the model", worsening prediction for Native (North) Americans, Native Canadians/First Nations, and Native South Americans; Baker & Hawn (2021) are cited that Native Americans are often insufficiently represented in US education training sets.

> "then including that variable may eliminate disciplinary in fractions from the model, reducing model effectiveness for Native (North) Americans, Native Canadians/First Nations individuals, and Native South Americans while only mildly improving model performance for African American students."
