## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Student understanding of symmetry and Gauss’s law of electricity. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07376](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07376)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Test administered over two consecutive years to 33 physics graduate students in a TA seminar course, not counting for their course grade; the article reports "approximately 75%" and a statistically significant difference from undergraduates.

> "The average test score for the graduate students was approximately 75% with the reliability coeﬃcient α≈ 0.8.16 The better performance of graduate students compared to the undergraduates is statistically signiﬁcant."

### Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Student understanding of symmetry and Gauss’s law of electricity. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07376](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07376)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of graduate student response patterns in Table V shows the same misconceptions as undergraduates on Problems 13 and 17, where graduate students performed less than 50%; the PBD for these questions was 0.5 and 0.6.

> "Problems 13 and 17 were the only ones on which graduate students performed less than 50% with misconceptions similar to those of the undergraduates."
