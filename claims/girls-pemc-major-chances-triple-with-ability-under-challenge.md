# Girls' likelihood of majoring in PEMC is 3.1 times greater at the highest versus lowest value of 12th grade perceived mathematics ability under challenge (5.6% vs. 1.8%)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among 12th grade girls, predicted probabilities of declaring a PEMC major rose from 1.8% at the most negative to 5.6% at the most positive perceptions of mathematics ability under challenge, all else equal — a 3.1-fold difference. [→ Perez-Felkner L 2017](#perez-felkner-l-2017)

## Evidence

### Perez-Felkner L 2017

Perez-Felkner L, Nix S and Thomas K (2017) Gendered Pathways: How Mathematics Ability Beliefs Shape Secondary and Postsecondary Course and Degree Field Choices. Front. Psychol. 8:386. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00386

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multinomial logistic regression models of specific major choice used predicted probabilities (Stata 14 margins commands) for girls at the 75th percentile of observed 10th grade mathematics ability, with other predictors at sample means. Similar smaller effects appeared for 10th grade perceived ability (1.8 to 5.5%) and growth mindset (1.9 to 4.5%).

> "12th grade girls with the most negative perceptions had a 1.8% chance of choosing PEMC majors; those girls with the most positive perceptions of their mathematics ability under challenge had a 5.6% chance, all e lse being equal."
