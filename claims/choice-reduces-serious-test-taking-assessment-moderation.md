## Evidence

### Schüttpelz-Brauns 2020

Schüttpelz-Brauns, K., Hecht, M., Hardt, K., Karay, Y., Zupanic, M., & Kämmer, J. E. (2020). Institutional strategies related to test-taking behavior in low stakes assessment. Advances in Health Sciences Education, 25(2), 321–335. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-019-09928-y

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Mixed effects logistic regression of 108,140 participations; the printed effect size for Give choices is OR = 0.01 (estimate −4.25, SE 0.31, p < 0.001), a "99%" decline in odds of serious test-taking.

> "if students are given some choice about modalities of their participation (“Give choice”), their odds of taking the BPT seriously declines by about 1 − exp(− 4.25) = 99% as compared to not providing them with this choice."

### Schüttpelz-Brauns 2020 (2)

Schüttpelz-Brauns, K., Hecht, M., Hardt, K., Karay, Y., Zupanic, M., & Kämmer, J. E. (2020). Institutional strategies related to test-taking behavior in low stakes assessment. Advances in Health Sciences Education, 25(2), 321–335. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-019-09928-y

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

The Presentation type × Give choice interaction in the same regression model: OR = 11.21 (estimate 2.42, SE 0.94, p = 0.010, 95% CI 1.79–70.04). Considering only students given choice, odds increase by "1512%" when the BPT is presented as assessment versus evaluation.

> "provided that students are given choice, the odds of taking the BPT seriously when the test was presented as an assessment as compared to being presented as an evaluation, is 11.21 higher than the same odds for students who are not given choice."
