# Perceived preparation of peers is positively associated with perceived seminar quality (deepening of knowledge and confidence in answers)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students' perception of how well their peers prepared was positively associated with perceived deepening of knowledge (B = 0.18; p = 0.02, R2 = 0.09) and with confidence in having correct answers (B = 0.40; p < 0.01, R2 = 0.21). [→ Bouwmeester Rianne A. M. 2016](#bouwmeester-rianne-a-m-2016)

## Evidence

### Bouwmeester Rianne A. M. 2016

Bouwmeester Rianne A. M., de Kleijn Renske A. M., van Rijen Harold V. M. (2016). Peer-instructed seminar attendance is associated with improved preparation, deeper learning and higher exam scores: a survey study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0715-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression analysis relating self-reported preparation time and perceived peer preparation to two five-point-scale quality measures averaged over seven questionnaires. Perceived peer preparation was significant for both outcomes; preparation time was not (p = 0.06 and p = 0.21).

> "perceived preparation of peers (B = 0.18; p = 0.02) could explain why students experienced that attending seminars deepened their knowledge (R2 = 0.09, ΔR2 = 0.08) and could also explain why students have confidence in having correct answers (B = 0.40; p < 0.01, R2 = 0.21, ΔR2 = 0.20)"
