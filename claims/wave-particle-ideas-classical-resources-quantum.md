## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Wave ideas are used to explain how quantum systems propagate, while particle ideas are used to explain how quantum systems interact with measurement devices. [→ Keith Oliver and Lei Bao 2002](#keith-oliver-and-lei-bao-2002)

## Evidence

### Keith Oliver and Lei Bao 2002

Keith Oliver and Lei Bao. (2002). Student Resources in quantum mechanics, or why students need meta-resources. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0207090](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0207090)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The authors' theoretical argument about which classical resources quantum mechanics learning requires, not a measured comparison. The article assigns functional roles: wave ideas for propagation, and 'particle ideas are resources brought in to explain how quantum systems interact with measurement devices'.

> "The wave ideas are resources brought in to explain the way quantum systems propagate. The particle ideas are resources brought in to explain how quantum systems interact with measurement devices."

### Keith Oliver and Lei Bao 2002 (2)

Keith Oliver and Lei Bao. (2002). Student Resources in quantum mechanics, or why students need meta-resources. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0207090](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0207090)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

The authors' assertion in the resources section, offered without reported data, that merging the two structures is an unmet demand of the quantum curriculum.

> "Most students have not been asked to merge the two cognitive structures (developed in different contexts) into one new structure."
