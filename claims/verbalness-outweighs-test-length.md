## Evidence

### Arthur I. Gates 1923

Arthur I. Gates. (1923). The Correlations of Achievement in School Subjects with Intelligence Tests and Other Variables (Concluded). The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0066477

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Regression analysis of combined data for grades IV to VIII. The printed effect size is multiple R = 0.725: "a multiple correlation with achievement of 0.725", against a verbalness weight of 1.00 and a time weight of 0.224.

> "Combining time and verbalness perfectly by the weights given above, a multiple correlation with achievement of 0.725 is obtained as compared to a partial correlation of 0.69 which verbalness alone yields, or 0.21 which time alone yields."

### Arthur I. Gates 1923 (2)

Arthur I. Gates. (1923). The correlations of achievement in school subjects with intelligence tests and other variables (Continued). The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1923-02675-001

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Regression and partial-correlation analysis of test length (minutes) and rated verbalness against mean achievement correlations for 14 tests (Table V). Partial r for verbalness (time constant) is 0.69; for time (verbalness constant) 0.21; multiple r for verbalness plus time is 0.73. The author concludes "verbalness alone yields a higher correlation with achievement than time alone."

> "Both time and the degree of verbalness are determining factors, but verbalness alone yields a higher correlation with achievement than time alone."
