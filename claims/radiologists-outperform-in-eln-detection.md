# Radiologists outperform radiographers and psychology students in detecting enlarged lymph nodes in dynamic CT videos

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Experts detected ELNs more accurately than both semi-experts and naïve participants, who performed at chance level. [→ Bertram Raymond 2013](#bertram-raymond-2013)

## Evidence

### Bertram Raymond 2013

Bertram Raymond, Helle Laura, Kaakinen Johanna K., Svedström Erkki. (2013). The Effect of Expertise on Eye Movement Behaviour in Medical Image Perception. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0066169

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Experiment comparing 7 radiologists, 9 CT radiographers and 22 psychology students on ELN detection in dynamic CT videos. The article reports "a significant group effect in the ANOVA analysis for overall accuracy" with experts at 84% accuracy versus chance-level performance for the other groups; no effect size is printed.

> "This led to a significant group effect in the ANOVA analysis for overall accuracy, F(2, 35) = 7.64, p<.01, with post-hoc comparisons (using Tukey's honestly significant difference (HSD) test) showing that experts performed better than both semi-experts and naïve participants (both ps<.02)."
