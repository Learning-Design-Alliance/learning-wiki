## Evidence

### Lee Hye-Yoon 2025

Lee Hye-Yoon, Kim Jundong, Choi Hyojae, Bae Hyojin, Jeong Aram, Choi Sungyoul, Kim Ji-Hwan, Kim Chang-Eop. (2025). Comparing AI chatbot simulation and peer role-play for OSCE preparation: a pilot randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08308-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-training OSCE Learning-Process Survey completed by both groups; between-group Likert comparisons with bootstrap CIs, Hedges' g, and Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment. No item reached adjusted significance (adjusted p ≥ 0.05).

> "The AI chatbot group tended to rate higher on ‘Q5. The practice method felt free from time and environmental restrictions (Mean Diff = 0.73, Hedges’ g = 0.64) and ‘Q6. It was easy to self-regulate the learning pace’ (Mean Diff = 0.80, Hedges’ g = 0.60)."

### Lee Hye-Yoon 2025 (2)

Lee Hye-Yoon, Kim Jundong, Choi Hyojae, Bae Hyojin, Jeong Aram, Choi Sungyoul, Kim Ji-Hwan, Kim Chang-Eop. (2025). Comparing AI chatbot simulation and peer role-play for OSCE preparation: a pilot randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08308-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same survey analysis: PRP group scored higher than the AI chatbot group on communication, unexpected situations, and exam-like tension items, with effect sizes at or beyond the |g| = 0.5 threshold but adjusted p ≥ 0.05 for all items.

> "Conversely, items where the PRP group scored higher than the AI chatbot group were ‘Q3. It helped improve communication skills with patients’ (Mean Diff=−1.13, Hedges’ g=−1.06), ‘Q17. My ability to handle various unexpected situations improved’ (Mean Diff=−1.18, Hedges’ g=−0.80), ‘Q19. I felt appropriate tension and responsibility during the medical interview practice’ (Mean Diff=−0.79, Hedges’ g=−0.78)"
