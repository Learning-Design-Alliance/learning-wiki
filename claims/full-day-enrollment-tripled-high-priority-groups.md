# Full-day pre-k enrollment grew substantially after the policy changes, tripling for Black, lowest-income, and mostly-Black-neighborhood students

## Evidence

### Ehrlich 2020

Ehrlich, S.B., Connors, M.C., Stein, A.G., Francis, J., Easton, J.Q., Kabourek, S.E., & Farrar, I.C. (2020). Closer to home: More equitable pre-k access and enrollment in Chicago. Chicago, IL: UChicago Consortium on School Research, NORC at the University of Chicago, and Start Early. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/closer-to-home

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive comparison of full-day enrollment rates across six CPS cohorts before and after the policy shift. Latinx students and mostly-Latinx neighborhoods also showed meaningful increases, particularly in 2015-16, but remained far below the city average at about 5.2 percent; Black students in racially-diverse, half-White neighborhoods did not see the same gains.

> "In 2010–11, only 3.2 percent of all age-eligible students enrolled in full-day pre-k, but in 2015–16 nearly three times as many (11.6 percent) enrolled. As Figure 5 illustrates, Black students, lowest-income students, and students living in mostly-Black neighborhoods, enrolled in full-day pre-k at a rate that was three times greater after the policy changes than before."
