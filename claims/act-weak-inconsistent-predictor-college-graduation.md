# ACT scores show a weak relationship with college graduation, with slope variation across high schools larger than the average signal

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The standardized linear ACT coefficient (0.129) is much smaller than HSGPA's (0.703), and the standard deviation of ACT slopes (0.192) exceeds the average slope. [→ Allensworth 2019](#allensworth-2019)

## Evidence

### Allensworth 2019

Allensworth, E. M., & Clark, K. (2019). High School GPAs and ACT Scores as Predictors of College Completion: Examining Assumptions about Consistency across High Schools. University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/high-school-gpas-and-act-scores-as-predictors-of-college-completion

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Continuous-variable HLM models (Model 2, Table 4) predicting six-year graduation for the same CPS sample. The article reports the odds increase of 14 percent per SD of ACT and that "the variance in the slopes (s.d. of slopes = 0.192) is larger than the average slope (0.129)."

> "The standardized linear term is much smaller than that of standardized HSGPA scores (0.129 vs. 0.703), with the odds of graduating increasing by 14 percent (odds coefficient of 1.14) for every standard deviation increase in ACT scores"
