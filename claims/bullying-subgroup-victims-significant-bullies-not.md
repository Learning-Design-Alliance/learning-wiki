## Evidence

### Muthanna 2021

Samara, M., Da Silva Nascimento, B., El-Asam, A., Hammuda, S., & Khattab, N. (2021). How Can Bullying Victimisation Lead to Lower Academic Achievement? A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of the Mediating Role of Cognitive-Motivational Factors. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 18(5), 2209. [doi:10.3390/ijerph18052209](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18052209)

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Subgroup meta-analysis restricted to studies of victims. The article reports victims were significantly less likely to be motivated (OR = 0.74, p = 0.002) and, separately, more likely to have low academic achievement (k = 10, OR = 0.62, p = 0.001).

> "As for victims only, they were also significantly less likely to be motivated (k = 4) (OR = 0.74, 95% CI (0.61, 0.89), p = 0.002)."

### Muthanna 2021 (2)

Samara, M., Da Silva Nascimento, B., El-Asam, A., Hammuda, S., & Khattab, N. (2021). How Can Bullying Victimisation Lead to Lower Academic Achievement? A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of the Mediating Role of Cognitive-Motivational Factors. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 18(5), 2209. [doi:10.3390/ijerph18052209](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18052209)

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Subgroup meta-analyses for bully/victims (k = 2, OR = 0.58, p = 0.367) and bullies (k = 2, OR = 0.55, p = 0.128) on academic achievement were not significant; equivalence was not tested.

> "The results for bully/victims only and bullies only were not significant (bully/victims: k = 2, OR = 0.58, 95% CI (0.18, 1.89), p = 0.367); bullies: k = 2, OR = 0.55, 95% CI (0.26, 1.19), p = 0.128)"
