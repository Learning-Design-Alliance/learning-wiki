## Evidence

### Ryan 2017

Ryan, Q. X., Wilcox, B. R., & Pollock, S. J. (2017). Student Difficulties with Boundary Conditions in Electrodynamics. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020126](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020126)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Exam-solution coding (N=48 solutions with this construction component) of reflected-wave field direction. The authors hypothesize students conflate propagation direction with field direction.

> "When asked explicitly to draw arrows representing the direction of the ﬁelds, a third of the stu- dents (31%, 15 out of 48) incorrectly ﬂipped the direction of ⃗Eref l in the case of n1 >n 2."

### Ryan 2017 (2)

Ryan, Q. X., Wilcox, B. R., & Pollock, S. J. (2017). Student Difficulties with Boundary Conditions in Electrodynamics. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020126](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020126)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Oblique-incidence exam solutions (N=36) coded for dot-product handling. Students treated k·r as a scalar product; of those who attempted it, only 4 worked it out correctly.

> "Half of the solutions (18 out of 36) did not write down this expression for the dot product ⃗k·⃗ r first."
