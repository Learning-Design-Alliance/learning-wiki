# Students allocated roughly equal attention to the graph and the molecular model, both above chance, while attending little to the equations

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Viewing time on the graph and model each exceeded the chance baseline defined by screen area; the general equation received less time than chance. [→ Mike Stieff 2011](#mike-stieff-2011)

## Evidence

### Mike Stieff 2011

Mike Stieff, Mary Hegarty, Ghislain Deslongchamps. (2011). Identifying Representational Competence with Multi-Representational Displays. Cognition and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1080/07370008.2010.507318

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

One-sample t tests against chance in the eye-tracking study of ten organic chemistry undergraduates. Graph (34%) and model (32%) viewing times exceeded the 18% chance baseline: "(one-sample t(9) = 6.03, p < .001 for the graph; t(9) = 4.49, p=.003 for the model)".

> "The percentages of time spent viewing the graph and the model were both greater than chance, which was 18% for both of these representations, (one-sample t(9) = 6.03, p < .001 for the graph; t(9) = 4.49, p=.003 for the model)."
