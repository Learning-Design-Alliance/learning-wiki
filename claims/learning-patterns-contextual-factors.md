## Evidence

### Jan D. Vermunt & Vincent Donche 2017

Jan D. Vermunt & Vincent Donche. (2017). A Learning Patterns Perspective on Student Learning in Higher Education: State of the Art and Moving Forward. Educ Psychol Rev 29:269–299. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-017-9414-6

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Large-scale study by Donche et al. (2013) of 90 lecturers and 1126 undergraduates in a university college, controlling for personality traits and academic motivation; in disciplines with more direct instruction, students scored lower on the use of external regulation.

> "Students within disciplines in which teachers were more learning focused in their teaching scored higher on all processing strategies and were more regulated (self or external) in their learning"

### Jan D. Vermunt & Vincent Donche 2017 (2)

Jan D. Vermunt & Vincent Donche. (2017). A Learning Patterns Perspective on Student Learning in Higher Education: State of the Art and Moving Forward. Educ Psychol Rev 29:269–299. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-017-9414-6

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Law and Meyer (2011a) studied a large group of Hong Kong students; higher perceived workload related to more undirected learning, inappropriate assessment to more reproductive patterns, and emphasis on independence showed the strongest positive relations with meaning-directed learning.

> "'Good teaching'showed positive correlations with 18 out of 20 ILS scales (the exceptions being an ambivalent learning orientation and lack of regulation, two elements of the undirected learning pattern)"

### Jan D. Vermunt & Vincent Donche 2017 (3)

Jan D. Vermunt & Vincent Donche. (2017). A Learning Patterns Perspective on Student Learning in Higher Education: State of the Art and Moving Forward. Educ Psychol Rev 29:269–299. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-017-9414-6

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Vermunt (2005) compared learning patterns of students of seven disciplines at a Dutch university; disciplinary differences were explained by differences in the nature of the discipline, dominant pedagogical practices, and student selection into disciplines.
