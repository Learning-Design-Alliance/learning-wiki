# Solution rates in the initial problem-solving phase moderate whether studies find beneficial PS-I effects

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across studies cited by the article, low solution rates in the initial problem-solving phase co-occur with null PS-I effects and high solution rates with beneficial effects. Katharina Loibl · Timo Leuders · Inga Glogger-Frey · Nikol Rummel. (2025). CID: a framework for the cognitive analysis of composite instructional designs. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09665-9

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article's cross-study comparison of printed solution rates as an indicator of well-structured intermediate knowledge. It reports low rates (28%, 14%) in studies not finding PS-I benefits and high rates (80%, 90%) in studies finding benefits; the solution rate in Glogger-Frey et al. (2017) rose from 34 to 56% across problems.

> "Studies with low solution rates (28% in Glogger-Frey et al., 2015, 14% in Roelle & Berthold, 2016) in the initial problem-solving phase do not find beneficial effects of PS-I; studies with high solution rates (80% in Schwartz et al., 2011, 90% in Schalk et al., 2018) in the initial problem-solving phase do find beneficial effects of PS-I."
