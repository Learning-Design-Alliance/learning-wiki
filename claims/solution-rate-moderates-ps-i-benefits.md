# Across studies, high solution rates in the initial problem-solving phase co-occur with beneficial PS-I effects and low rates with no beneficial effects, a cross-study pattern not yet tested in a shared sample

## Evidence

### Katharina Loibl · Timo Leuders · Inga Glogger-Frey · Nikol Rummel 2025

Katharina Loibl · Timo Leuders · Inga Glogger-Frey · Nikol Rummel. (2025). CID: a framework for the cognitive analysis of composite instructional designs. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09665-9

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

A conceptual framework article; this is its cross-study comparison of the solution rates reported in earlier studies, read as an indicator of well-structured intermediate knowledge. The authors say future studies with a shared sample need to check whether the comparison is valid, so it shows co-occurrence, not moderation or cause. It reports low rates (28%, 14%) in studies not finding PS-I benefits and high rates (80%, 90%) in studies finding benefits; the solution rate in Glogger-Frey et al. (2017) rose from 34 to 56% across problems.

> "Studies with low solution rates (28% in Glogger-Frey et al., 2015, 14% in Roelle & Berthold, 2016) in the initial problem-solving phase do not find beneficial effects of PS-I; studies with high solution rates (80% in Schwartz et al., 2011, 90% in Schalk et al., 2018) in the initial problem-solving phase do find beneficial effects of PS-I."
