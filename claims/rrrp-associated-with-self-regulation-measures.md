## Evidence

### Day 2017

Day, Stephanie L.; Connor, Carol M. (2017). Examining the relations between self-regulation and achievement in third grade students. Assessment for Effective Intervention, 42(2), 97-109. https://doi.org/10.1177/1534508416670367

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical linear modeling of children nested within teachers (282 third graders, 34 classrooms) tested fall RRRP Part 1 against self-regulation measures. The authors report that "both Memory for Digits and the SWAN Attention subscale were significantly related" to Part 1 performance; no effect sizes are printed.

> "On Part 1 of the RRRP, both Memory for Digits and the SWAN Attention subscale were significantly related to performance on the fall RRRP (Table 3). Ratings of inhibitory control on the SWAN indicated a trend (p =.071)."

### Day 2017 (2)

Day, Stephanie L.; Connor, Carol M. (2017). Examining the relations between self-regulation and achievement in third grade students. Assessment for Effective Intervention, 42(2), 97-109. https://doi.org/10.1177/1534508416670367

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Two further HLM models in the same sample tested fall RRRP Part 2 and the 'wait for go' score. The authors report that "only Memory for Digits was significantly and positively associated" with Part 2, and only SWAN inhibitory-control ratings predicted waiting for 'go'; no effect sizes are printed.

> "For Part 2 (color switch) of the fall RRRP, only Memory for Digits was significantly and positively associated with performance on Part 2 of the RRRP (Table 3). Finally, a third model was run for ‘wait for go,’ which was designed to capture inhibitory control. In this model, only ratings of inhibitory control were significantly related to children’s ability to wait for “go” (Table 4)."
