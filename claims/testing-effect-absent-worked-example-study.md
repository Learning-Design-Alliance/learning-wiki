# No testing effect was found for acquiring problem-solving skills through worked example study across eight experiments

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across eight experiments with varied problem-solving tasks, student populations and practice test types, practice testing after worked example study showed no retention benefit over restudy on delayed final tests. [→ van Gog 2015](#van-gog-2015)

## Evidence

### van Gog 2015

van Gog, T., & Sweller, J. (2015). Not New, but Nearly Forgotten: the Testing Effect Decreases or even Disappears as the Complexity of Learning Materials Increases. Educational Psychology Review, 27, 247–264. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-015-9310-x

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review's synthesis of the special-issue worked example experiments (van Gog et al., four experiments; Leahy et al., three experiments) plus van Gog and Kester (2012), all comparing practice testing after example study with further example study, concludes there was "no evidence was found that the testing effect would also apply when acquiring problem-solving skills through worked example study".
