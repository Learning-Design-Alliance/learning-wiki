## Evidence

### Charles Baily and Noah D. Finkelstein 2009

Charles Baily and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2009). Teaching and understanding of quantum interpretations in modern physics courses. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.6.010101

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

End-of-semester online survey (response rate approximately 90%, N ~ 60 per course) in which students responded to an essay question presenting three fictional perspectives on the double-slit experiment. The article reports PHYS3B students were "nearly four times more likely to prefer the Realist statement" than PHYS3A students.

> "Students from PHYS3B were nearly four times more likely to prefer the Realist statement than students from PHYS3A; similarly, PHYS3B students were half as likely to favor the Quantum description."

### Charles Baily and Noah D. Finkelstein 2009 (2)

Charles Baily and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2009). Teaching and understanding of quantum interpretations in modern physics courses. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.6.010101

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same end-of-semester essay survey comparing the two courses. The article prints the proportions: "29% of PHYS3B students chose to agree with the Realist statement" and 27% with the Agnostic stance, against a combined 11% from PHYS3A.

> "29% of PHYS3B students chose to agree with the Realist statement of Student One, and 27% of PHYS3B students agreed with the Agnostic stance of Student Three, while only a combined 11% of students from PHYS3A chose either of these responses."
