# 9th-grade students taught electrical circuits with a 4C/ID-based digital learning environment performed better on a learning transfer test than students taught by conventional teacher-centred instruction

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The experimental group scored higher than the control group on the transfer test, with percentage differences of 21.4% at first application and 22.6% at follow-up. [→ Melo 2018](#melo-2018)

## Evidence

### Melo 2018

Melo, M. (2018). The 4C/ID-Model in Physics Education: Instructional Design of a Digital Learning Environment to Teach Electrical Circuits. International Journal of Instruction, 11(1), 103-122. https://doi.org/10.12973/iji.2018.1118a

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experiment with 125 9th-graders in five natural classes randomly assigned to experimental (nE = 76) and control (nC = 49) groups; percentage differences were computed from mean scores, with "21.4% for the transfer test" at first application and 22.6% at follow-up.

> "for the 1 st application 6.6% for the reproduction test and 21.4% for the transfer test; for the follow-up application 11 .4% for the reproduction test and 22 .6% for the transfer test. Then, it turns out that students from the experimental group performed better in the application of knowledge about electrical circuits in new problem situations."
