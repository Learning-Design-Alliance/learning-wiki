# Dental undergraduates' ergonomic knowledge scores rose from pre-test to post-test after a microscope-based experiential ergonomics course, in one single-group pre-post study (p < 0.001 for all three knowledge items)

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Post-course subjective knowledge of ergonomic postures rose from 2.72 to 4.28 (p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 1.87), and understanding of the posture-MSD link rose from 3.26 to 4.58 (d = 1.61). [→ Li Zimeng 2026](#li-zimeng-2026)

## Evidence

### Li Zimeng 2026

Li Zimeng, Xia Yanlin, Chen Yujie, Huang Cui, Liang Shanshan. (2026). Can experiential learning boost dental ergonomics? evaluating a microscope-based curriculum through Kirkpatrick's model. BMC Oral Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-026-08173-1

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Single-group pretest-posttest study of 89 matched second-year dental undergraduates at Wuhan University; paired t-tests on researcher-developed questionnaires. The article reports "Cohen's d = 1.87" for posture knowledge and d = 1.61 for the MSD-link item.

> "The post-course score for subjective knowledge of ergonomic postures was 4.28 ± 0.64, which was higher than the pre-course score of 2.72 ± 0.97 (p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 1.87)."

### Li Zimeng 2026 (2)

Li Zimeng, Xia Yanlin, Chen Yujie, Huang Cui, Liang Shanshan. (2026). Can experiential learning boost dental ergonomics? evaluating a microscope-based curriculum through Kirkpatrick's model. BMC Oral Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-026-08173-1

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Same paired pretest-posttest study; the multiple-choice posture-identification item (scored 0-8) improved significantly, with the article printing "Cohen's d = 0.81".

> "For the identification of inappropriate postures, a post-course score of 6.34 ± 2.12 was recorded, showing a significant increase from the pre-course average of 4.56 ± 2.29 (p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.81)."
