## Evidence

### Kramer A 2025

Kramer A, Foley J, Hansen C, Teramoto M. (2025). Parent‐reported academic outcomes after a mild traumatic brain injury in the pediatric population. J Sch Health. https://doi.org/10.1111/josh.13502

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Contingency table analysis in the 285-parent survey: 75.0% of children with recovery scores 0-6 had negative grade change versus 9.9% of those completely recovered, "more likely to sustain negative grade changes (p < .001)". The associated regression effect size for degree of recovery 7-9 was OR = 3.92 (95% CI = 1.55, 9.90).

> "Children reporting lower degrees of recovery (0‐6 and 7‐9) and those reporting not yet completely recovered at the time of survey were more likely to sustain negative grade changes (p < .001)."

### Kramer A 2025 (2)

Kramer A, Foley J, Hansen C, Teramoto M. (2025). Parent‐reported academic outcomes after a mild traumatic brain injury in the pediatric population. J Sch Health. https://doi.org/10.1111/josh.13502

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression in the same survey; printed effect sizes were OR = 3.92 (95% CI = 1.55, 9.90) for degree of recovery 7-9 and OR = 6.61 (95% CI = 1.53, 28.65) for not yet completely recovered; predicted probability of negative grade change was 0.50 for children not yet completely recovered versus 0.18-0.22 for those achieving recovery.

> "degree of recovery of 7‐9 (vs complete recovery) and not yet completely recovered (vs length of recovery of 0‐1 month) were significantly associated with higher odds of negative grade changes (OR = 3.92; 95% CI = 1.55, 9.90 and 6.61; 95% CI = 1.53, 28.65, respectively; p < .05)"
