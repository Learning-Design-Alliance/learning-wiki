Evidence¶

Poquet 2026¶

Poquet, O., Nanduri, M. S., Salinas Loyer, M. X., Stadler, M., Sailer, M., Jovanovic, J. (2026). Profiling Cognitive Offloading in LLM-mediated Synthesis Writing: Volume vs. Content. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10434

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Content-based k-means clustering (five features: proportions of active-local, active-global, passive-local, passive-global prompts, plus prior knowledge) yielded Full Offloading (n=41), Strategic Offloading (n=12), Comprehension Offloading (n=15), Clarifying (n=17), and Non-users (n=12). The profiles "represented qualitatively distinct patterns" with significant strategy differences.

"The five profiles represented qualitatively distinct patterns of how cognitive activity was distributed, with significant differences in learning strategies."

Poquet 2026 (2)¶

Poquet, O., Nanduri, M. S., Salinas Loyer, M. X., Stadler, M., Sailer, M., Jovanovic, J. (2026). Profiling Cognitive Offloading in LLM-mediated Synthesis Writing: Volume vs. Content. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10434

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Within the content-based solution, Strategic Offloaders (36% active prompts) directed structure and generation at high volume and fast pace, while Full Offloaders were passive at 96% of prompts. The article notes "Strategic Offloaders offloaded at the highest volume (M=12.9)" yet maintained learner engagement in directing comprehension.

"Strategic Offloaders offloaded at the highest volume (M=12.9) and the fastest pace (M=1.05 min), followed by the Full Offloaders who prompted moderately (M=5.5)."
