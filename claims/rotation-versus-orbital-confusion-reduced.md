# About a quarter of Essay 1 responses could not distinguish rotational from orbital motion, a confusion that dropped to 2% by Essay 3 after in-class demonstrations

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` 27% of Essay 1 conceptual responses (6 of 22) showed students unable to distinguish rotational from orbital motion; after feedback and demonstrations, this confusion was reduced to 2% in Essay 3. [→ Dcruz 2014](#dcruz-2014)

## Evidence

### Dcruz 2014

Dcruz, N. L. (2014). Essays on Eclipses, Transits and Occultations as Teaching Tools in the Introductory Astronomy College Course. Revista Latino-Americana de Educação em Astronomia - RELEA, n. 17, p. 39-66. [doi:10.37156/relea/2014.17.039](https://doi.org/10.37156/relea/2014.17.039)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Analysis of Essay 1 conceptual responses on rotation period and angular size effects (goals IIc, IId); the author reports "27% of responses (6 of 22)" could not distinguish rotational from orbital motion, an error the author had not anticipated.

> "27% of responses (6 of 22) showed that students were unable to distinguish between rotational and orbital motion, and therefore were not able to respond correctly."

### Dcruz 2014 (2)

Dcruz, N. L. (2014). Essays on Eclipses, Transits and Occultations as Teaching Tools in the Introductory Astronomy College Course. Revista Latino-Americana de Educação em Astronomia - RELEA, n. 17, p. 39-66. [doi:10.37156/relea/2014.17.039](https://doi.org/10.37156/relea/2014.17.039)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Follow-up analysis across Essay 3 submissions after the instructor explained rotation versus orbital motion with demonstrations, including having students stand and spin in place; the author reports the confusion "was reduced to 2% in Essay 3", noting this is a lower limit.

> "It is encouraging to note that this confusion was reduced to 2% in Essay 3, though again this is a lower limit since not all students chose to address the effects of rotation period on transit duration and decrease in stellar brightness during the transit."
