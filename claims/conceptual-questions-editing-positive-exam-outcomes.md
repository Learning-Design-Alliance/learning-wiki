# Students' conceptual-question and editing-request LLM usage is positively correlated with exam outcomes in the spring semester

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In spring-semester regressions, the broad dialogue act features Conceptual Questions and Editing Request were statistically significant positive correlates of exam 1 scores. [→ reg-broad-s25](#reg-broad-s25)

## Evidence

### reg-broad-s25

Hunter McNichols, Fareya Ikram, and Andrew Lan. 2026. The StudyChat Dataset: Analyzing Student Dialogues With ChatGPT in an Artificial Intelligence Course. In LAK26: 16th International Learning Analytics and Knowledge Conference (LAK 2026). https://doi.org/10.1145/3785022.3785029

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational regression analysis of dialogue act counts from the StudyChat logs. In spring-semester broad-DA regressions, Conceptual Questions (coef 0.0133, p=0.024) and Editing Request (coef 0.0296, p=0.017) significantly predicted e1 scores, which the authors read as "a learning-focused usage of LLMs being positively correlated with assessment outcomes."

> "We observe the broad feature for Conceptual Questions and Editing Request as positively correlated with e1 outcomes, indicating a learning-focused usage of LLMs being positively correlated with assessment outcomes."
