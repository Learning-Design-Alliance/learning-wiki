# Effect size indices and power analyses are rarely reported in educational research across all four design paradigms

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Keselman 1998

Keselman, H. J., Huberty, C. J., Lix, L. M., Olejnik, S., Cribbie, R. A., Donahue, B., Kowalchuk, R. K., Lowman, L. L., Petoskey, M. D., Keselman, J. C., & Levin, J. R. (1998). Statistical Practises of Educational Researchers: An Analysis of Their ANOVA, MANOVA and ANCOVA Analyses. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Statistical+Practises+of+Educational+Researchers

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Content analysis of between-subjects univariate articles. The review reports that "The issue of power and/or effect size calculations arose in only 10 articles (16.1%)," with effect sizes calculated in six, and post hoc power analyses in two articles concerned about interaction power.

> "The issue of power and/or effect size calculations arose in only 10 articles (16.1%). Effect sizes were calculated in six of these, but the statistic used was not routinely reported and main effects were more often of interest than interactions"

### Keselman 1998 (2)

Keselman, H. J., Huberty, C. J., Lix, L. M., Olejnik, S., Cribbie, R. A., Donahue, B., Kowalchuk, R. K., Lowman, L. L., Petoskey, M. D., Keselman, J. C., & Levin, J. R. (1998). Statistical Practises of Educational Researchers: An Analysis of Their ANOVA, MANOVA and ANCOVA Analyses. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Statistical+Practises+of+Educational+Researchers

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Content analysis of 79 MANOVA articles. "Effect size index values were reported in onl y eight of the 79 articles"; the actual test criterion (e.g., Wilks) was reported in only a handful of studies, hindering readers' recovery of effect sizes.

> "Power/Effect Size Analysis Effect size index values were reported in onl y eight of the 79 articles. Seven studies used univariate indexes and one study reported multivaria te eta-squared values"
