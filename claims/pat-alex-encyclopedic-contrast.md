# Two students who can both solve problems correctly may attach qualitatively different encyclopedic meaning to the same equation

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In clinical interviews about v = v0 + at, Alex treated the equation mainly as a calculation tool, while Pat connected it to units, dimensional consistency, and acceleration as a process changing initial velocity, and later used the equation expert-like without plugging in numbers. [→ Edward F. Redish and Ayush Gupta 2010](#edward-f-redish-and-ayush-gupta-2010)

## Evidence

### Edward F. Redish and Ayush Gupta 2010

Edward F. Redish and Ayush Gupta. (2010). Making Meaning with Math in Physics: A Semantic Analysis. Department of Physics, University of Maryland, College Park. Originally published 2009. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1002.0472](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1002.0472)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Qualitative analysis of clinical interview excerpts (AG as interviewer) with two introductory physics students, Pat and Alex, on the constant-acceleration velocity equation. The authors report that "Though both students can use the equation satisfactorily for solving problems", Pat's explanation engages knowledge of units, change processes, and equation structure, while Alex's stays at symbol meaning and calculation.

> "Though both students can use the equation satisfactorily for solving problems, but the 'encyclopedic meaning' ascribed to the equation by Pat is different than that for Alex."
