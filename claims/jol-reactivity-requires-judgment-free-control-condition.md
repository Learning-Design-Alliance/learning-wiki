# Making JOLs is not a neutral act: metacognitive ratings reactively affect subsequent learning, so experiments need judgment-free conditions

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The authors conclude that because JOLs reactively potentiate subsequent learning, researchers should include a judgment-free condition to obtain unbiased memory assessments. [→ Veit Kubik 2022](#veit-kubik-2022)

## Evidence

### Veit Kubik 2022

Veit Kubik, Kenneth Koslowski, Torsten Schubert, Alp Aslan. (2022). Metacognitive judgments can potentiate new learning: The role of covert retrieval. Metacognition and Learning (2022) 17:1057–1077. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09307-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' methodological conclusion drawn from the N = 161 experiment: making JOLs "is not a neutral act of assessing one’s learning" but reactively changes subsequent learning, hence the recommendation that "researchers typically need to include a judgment-free condition". No effect size is attached to this methodological statement.

> "As shown in the present experiment, making JOLs is not a neutral act of assessing one’s learning; in fact, JOLs do not only affect previous learning but may also potenti- ate subsequent learning of new information."
