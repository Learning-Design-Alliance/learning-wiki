## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Inter-rater reliability between peer and instructor assessment of simulation-based clinical performance was high, with ICC2 and ICC3 values of 0.685 (p < .001) and agreement of 68.5% between the two rater groups. [→ Yu Ji Hye 2021](#yu-ji-hye-2021)

## Evidence

### Yu Ji Hye 2021

Yu Ji Hye, Lee Mi Jin, Kim Soon Sun, Yang Min Jae, Cho Hyo Jung, Noh Choong Kyun, Lee Gil Ho, Lee Su Kyung, Song Mi Ryoung, Lee Jang Hoon, Kim Miran, Jung Yun Jung. (2021). Assessment of medical students’ clinical performance using high-fidelity simulation: comparison of peer and instructor assessment. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-021-02952-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Reliability analysis of assessment scores from 34 student groups rated by peers and two professors using a modified QSAT. The study reports "ICC value was found to be 0.6 or higher" (ICC2 0.685, CI 0.439 ~ 0.823; ICC3 0.685, CI 0.436 ~ 0.824, p < .001) and 68.5% agreement.

> "the ICC value was found to be 0.6 or higher. ICC values of 0.6 or higher can be interpreted as a significantly high consistency level among assessors [16]. The agreement between the two groups of raters was 68.5 %, indicating that reliability between raters was high."
