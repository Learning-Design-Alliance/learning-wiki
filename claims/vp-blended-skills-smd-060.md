# Virtual patients blended with traditional education show moderate positive skills effects versus traditional education alone, with wide uncertainty (pooled SMD=0.60)

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Pooled skills effect for blended virtual patients versus traditional education alone is SMD=0.60 (95% CI −0.07 to 1.27), a moderate positive effect with a CI crossing zero. [→ Kononowicz AA 2019](#kononowicz-aa-2019)

## Evidence

### Kononowicz AA 2019

Kononowicz AA, Woodham LA, Edelbring S, Stathakarou N, Davies D, Saxena N, Tudor Car L, Carlstedt-Duke J, Car J, Zary N. (2019). Virtual Patient Simulations in Health Professions Education: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis by the Digital Health Education Collaboration. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/14676

`q3 · i2`

Random-effects meta-analysis of blended-learning skills outcomes; individual studies showed significantly improved procedural skills (P<.001), clinical reasoning (P<.001), and communication skills (P<.01), but two nursing studies showed no significant difference. Very small pooled sample (n=247).

> "The pooled effect for skills outcomes (SMD=0.60, 95% CI −0.07 to 1.27, I2=83%, n=247; Figure 5) suggests that virtual patients blended with traditional education have moderate positive effects in comparison with traditional education alone."
