# Final-year students lack pharmacovigilance competencies, particularly knowledge of adverse drug reaction reporting

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Final-year students showed low awareness and knowledge of pharmacovigilance and many had received no training in ADR reporting. [→ Brinkman 2018](#brinkman-2018)

## Evidence

### Brinkman 2018

Brinkman, D. J., Tichelaar, J., Graaf, S., Otten, R. H. J., Richir, M. C., and van Agtmael, M. A. (2018). Do final‐year medical students have sufficient prescribing competencies? A systematic literature review. British Journal of Clinical Pharmacology. https://doi.org/10.1111/bcp.13491

`q2 · i?`

Cross-sectional online questionnaire of 298 final-year (6th-year) Dutch medical students across eight medical schools (Schutte et al. 2017), reported in the review's Table 2. Only 48% felt sufficiently prepared to report ADRs; other pharmacovigilance studies similarly reported low knowledge and training.

> "Only 48% of the final‐year students felt sufficiently prepared to report ADRs in future practice, and a substantial proportion did not know the authority to which they should report ADRs"
