# Extraversion correlates positively with achievement in language domains but not in STEM, a domain pattern driven by grades

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · quant-synthesis `r?` · `q4` · `i0` negligible

## Subclaims
`q4 i0` Extraversion correlations are larger in language (ρ = 0.06) than STEM (ρ = –0.02) domains, though both are very small. [→ Meyer 2023](#meyer-2023)
`q4 i0` The extraversion domain pattern appears for grades (ρ = 0.08 vs. –0.01) but not for test scores (ρ = 0.02 vs. –0.02). [→ Meyer 2023 (2)](#meyer-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Meyer 2023

Meyer, J., Jansen, T., Hübner, N., & Lüdtke, O. (2023). Disentangling the Association Between the Big Five Personality Traits and Student Achievement: Meta-Analytic Evidence on the Role of Domain Specificity and Achievement Measures. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09736-2

`q4 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Meta-analysis of 250 extraversion effect sizes from 61 studies (N = 258,461) found "larger correlations in language compared to STEM subject domains"; both printed correlations are below r = .10.

> "we found a domain-specific pattern, with larger correlations in language compared to STEM subject domains (ρ = 0.06 vs. –0.02; z = 4.94; p < 0.001)"

### Meyer 2023 (2)

Meyer, J., Jansen, T., Hübner, N., & Lüdtke, O. (2023). Disentangling the Association Between the Big Five Personality Traits and Student Achievement: Meta-Analytic Evidence on the Role of Domain Specificity and Achievement Measures. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09736-2

`q4 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

The domain-specific pattern for extraversion was not consistent across measures: significant for grades only; the overall domain × measure interaction was not significant (t[44] = –1.71; p = 0.095).

> "we found larger associations for grades in language compared to STEM subject domains (ρ = 0.08 vs. –0.01; z = 6.33; p < 0.001) but not for test scores (ρ = 0.02 vs. –0.02; z = 1.45; p = 0.149)"
