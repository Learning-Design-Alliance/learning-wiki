# Software cartoons depicted significantly more character poses and far more physical character-to-character interaction

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Six significant differences in universal poses favoured the experimental group for touching, flying/jumping, sitting, lying down and raising arms, while control participants knelt more often. [→ M. Madden 2007](#m-madden-2007)
`q3 i?` Physical interaction was much greater in software cartoons: touching was more than twenty times more frequent, and only experimental participants depicted picking up/throwing or punching/kicking. [→ M. Madden 2007 (2)](#m-madden-2007-2)

## Evidence

### M. Madden 2007

M. Madden, P.W.H. Chung, C.W. Dawson. (2007). The Effect of a Computer-Based Cartooning Tool On Children's Cartoons and Written Stories. Loughborough University Institutional Repository. http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-nd/2.5/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Fine-grained coding of poses in the Ramayana cartoon comparison (control n=17, experimental n=13), with unpaired t-tests. The quote covers two of the six significant differences; the others (flying/jumping p=0.01, sitting p=0.00, lying down p=0.02, raising arms p=0.02) also favoured the experimental group.

> "the average control participant depicted characters kneeling more often (control average: 0.7 depictions, experimental average: 0.1 depictions, p=0.00), whereas the experimental group's averages are greater for touching (control average: 0.1 depictions, experimental average: 1.3 depictions, p=0.04)"

### M. Madden 2007 (2)

M. Madden, P.W.H. Chung, C.W. Dawson. (2007). The Effect of a Computer-Based Cartooning Tool On Children's Cartoons and Written Stories. Loughborough University Institutional Repository. http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-nd/2.5/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Analysis of poses unique to one group or the other in the Ramayana cartoon comparison. No control participant showed picking up/throwing or punching/kicking; these depictions were split roughly evenly between boys and girls with no significant gender difference.

> "the average number of instances of a character touching another is more than twenty times greater for the experimental group than for the control. Secondly, nine of the thirteen experimental participants showed a character picking up or throwing another character, and, thirdly, three showed a character punching or kicking another"
