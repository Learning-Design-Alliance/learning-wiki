Evidence¶

Russell Scheinberg 2026¶

Russell Scheinberg, Ameeta Agrawal, Tetyana Sydorenko, Kalab Kahsay, Nina Vyatkina, Griet Boone. (2026). What Changes When the Interlocutor Is an AI? Interactional Fluency and Linguistic Uptake in L2 Spoken Dialogue. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22225

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Paired t-test on pre/post surveys from 55 matched respondents (52 with complete composite data) in the multi-site study. Post-study, 67.3% liked the AI interaction and 81.8% agreed AI can be useful for L2 speaking practice.

"a pairedt-test on directional sentiment scores showed a mean increase of +0.48 (t(51) =−4.58,p<.0001, d= 0.64), with 75% of participants becoming more positive."

Russell Scheinberg 2026 (2)¶

Russell Scheinberg, Ameeta Agrawal, Tetyana Sydorenko, Kalab Kahsay, Nina Vyatkina, Griet Boone. (2026). What Changes When the Interlocutor Is an AI? Interactional Fluency and Linguistic Uptake in L2 Spoken Dialogue. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22225

q3 · i? · causal · r2

OLS regressions predicting post-satisfaction and attitude change from AI-dialogue behavioral features (n=55, site dummy-coded). No standardised effect size is printed for these regression coefficients. Floor ratio was not significant, suggesting how fluently learners spoke mattered more than how much they spoke.

"the strongest behavioral predictor was within-turn pause ratio (β=−4.78,p=.008): learners who paused more during AI turns, reflecting greater online planning difficulty, reported lower satisfaction and smaller attitude gains (β=−5.91,p=.006)."
