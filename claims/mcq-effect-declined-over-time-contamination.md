## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors found that MCQs were answered in learning groups and shared on closed Facebook and WhatsApp groups, so the randomized groups could not be clearly divided over time. [→ Jud Sebastian M. 2020](#jud-sebastian-m-2020)

## Evidence

### Jud Sebastian M. 2020

Jud Sebastian M., Cupisti Susanne, Frobenius Wolfgang, Winkler Andrea, Schultheis Franziska, Antoniadis Sophia, Beckmann Matthias W., Heindl Felix. (2020). Introducing multiple-choice questions to promote learning for medical students: effect on exam performance in obstetrics and gynecology. Archives of Gynecology and Obstetrics. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00404-020-05758-1

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion of the observed decline in the effect over time; they note questions were published on social media platforms and that after the randomization ratio changed, only the most motivated control students sought access. No effect size is printed.

> "It was found that the multiple-choice questions were answered together in learning groups, which led to even students in the group without multiple-choice questions having access to the additional questions."
