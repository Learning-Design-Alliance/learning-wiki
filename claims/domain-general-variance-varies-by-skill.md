# Domain-general factors account for more variance in written arithmetic and word problems than in basic calculation fluency

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Domain-general factors accounted for 43% of variance in written arithmetic and even more in word problems, compared to 36% in basic calculation fluency, when entered first. [→ Cowan 2014](#cowan-2014)

## Evidence

### Cowan 2014

Cowan, R., & Powell, D. (2014). The Contributions of Domain-General and Numerical Factors to Third-Grade Arithmetic Skills and Mathematical Learning Disability. Journal of Educational Psychology, 106(1), 214-229. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0034097

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Fixed-order regression analyses across three arithmetic outcomes in 258 third graders. The proportion of variance accounted for by domain-general factors when entered first increased from basic calculation fluency to written arithmetic to word problems.

> "Domain-general factors account for more variation in written arithmetic (43%) than in basic calculation fluency (36%)."
