## Evidence

### Sommer GM 2021

Sommer GM, Broschewitz J, Huppert S, Sommer CG, Jahn N, Jansen-Winkeln B, Gockel I, Hau HM. (2021). The role of virtual reality simulation in surgical training in the light of COVID-19 pandemic: Visual spatial ability as a predictor for improved surgical performance: a randomized trial. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000027844

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the randomized trial, VSA was measured with a validated tube figure test before and after the nine-day period. The training group's day-9 VSA score (13.1 ± 2.4) exceeded the control group's (9.8 ± 3.3) with P ≤ .001; only the test statistic is printed, so no effect size is reported.

> "significant differences were observed in the day-9 TG VSA test scores after curriculum training compared with that of the CG (day 9, TG: VSA score 13.1 ± 2.4 versus CG: VSA score 9.8 ± 3.3; P ≤ .001)"
