## Evidence

### Amogh Sirnoorkar 2024

Amogh Sirnoorkar, Dean Zollman, James T Laverty, Alejandra J Magana, Sanjay Rebello, Lynn A Bryan. (2024). Student and AI responses to physics problems examined through the lenses of sensemaking and mechanistic reasoning. [doi:10.1016/j.caeai.2024.100318](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeai.2024.100318)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Quantitative distribution of the second sensemaking element (Table 3): six of eight student solutions to the open-ended task and all eight to the scaffolded version evidenced conceptual reasoning. The authors offer the tentative explanation that "the presence of sub-prompts" prompted explicit force identification.

> "However, a students’ better evidencing of this sensemaking feature to the scaffolded version may be due to the presence of sub-prompts, where the participants were first asked to identify the forces (and assumptions) acting on the rider before solving the problem."
