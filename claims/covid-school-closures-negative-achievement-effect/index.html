Evidence¶

Hammerstein Svenja 2021¶

Hammerstein Svenja, König Christoph, Dreisörner Thomas, Frey Andreas. (2021). Effects of COVID-19-Related School Closures on Student Achievement-A Systematic Review. Frontiers in Psychology. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.746289

q3 · i0 · quant-synthesis · r2

Systematic review synthesis of eleven studies meeting inclusion criteria (data collected before and during/after closures, statistical analyses with effect sizes). The review reports "effects ranging from−0.37 SD to +0.25 SD (Mdn = −0.08 SD)"; the median effect size Mdn = −0.08 SD is negligible by the d scale. No meta-analytic pooling was feasible given the small number of studies.

"The studies on the effect of COVID-19-related school closures on student achievement selected for our review reported mixed findings, with effects ranging from−0.37 SD to +0.25 SD (Mdn = −0.08 SD)."

  • Younger children were more negatively affected by COVID-19 school closures than older children (−0.37 SD vs. −0.10 SD) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Remote learning implemented during the spring 2020 closures achieved effects similar to no teaching at all during summer vacation — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • The 2020 COVID-19 school closures caused a decrease in Flemish grade 6 school-average mathematics scores of roughly 0.19 standard deviations relative to previous cohorts — related
  • Education leaders had little data on the impacts of school closures on learning at the time of the COVID-19 spring 2020 closures — related
  • Students in The Netherlands lost on average 3.16 percentile points (0.08 SD) in achievement during the 8-week COVID-19 lockdown — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Science and French scores also decreased significantly after the 2020 school closures, by roughly 0.22-0.33 and 0.19-0.30 standard deviations respectively — related
  • Schools reported that about 40 percent of their students experienced considerable or major academic losses since March 2020 — related
  • Low-performing students were more affected by closures in mathematics while high-performing students were more affected in reading, and low-performing students benefited more from systematic online learning — related
  • Achievement declines from 2019 to 2022 were larger for students in high-poverty schools than in low-poverty schools in grades 1–2 — related
