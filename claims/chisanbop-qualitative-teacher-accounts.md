# Qualitative teacher data showed enthusiasm and covert finger use among younger and lower-functioning students, but resistance among grade 5 students who calculated as quickly in their heads

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The grade 2 experimental teacher reported strong interest, pride, and enjoyment, and saw large attitude changes in the lowest-functioning students. [→ Stegemann 2014](#stegemann-2014)
`q1 i?` The grade 5 experimental teacher reported resistance, with many students calculating in their heads just as quickly and lower-functioning students using finger tapping covertly, not the Chisanbop method. [→ Stegemann 2014 (2)](#stegemann-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Stegemann 2014

Stegemann, K. C., & Grünke, M. (2014). Revisiting an Old Methodology for Teaching Counting, Computation, and Place Value: The Effectiveness of the Finger Calculation Method for At-Risk Children. Learning Disabilities: A Contemporary Journal, 12(2), 191-213. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1047461

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Qualitative analysis of teacher journals, meeting transcripts, and emails following Miles, Huberman, and Saldaña's procedure; the grade 2 teacher's observations corroborated the quantitative attitude findings.

> "the children “showed a lot of interest and enthusiasm while practicing their skills”, “felt proud of being the only students (at their school) learning this method”, and “enjoyed using their fingers.” In addition, Fiona saw “huge” changes in the attitudes of the lowest functioning students."

### Stegemann 2014 (2)

Stegemann, K. C., & Grünke, M. (2014). Revisiting an Old Methodology for Teaching Counting, Computation, and Place Value: The Effectiveness of the Finger Calculation Method for At-Risk Children. Learning Disabilities: A Contemporary Journal, 12(2), 191-213. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1047461

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Grade 5 teacher journal and meeting data: older students were reluctant, entrenched in earlier-taught strategies, and lower-functioning students hid covert finger tapping that was not the Chisanbop method; class-wide attitudes did not change significantly.

> "noting that there was “some resistance with the older kids” and many students were capable of successfully doing the calculations “in their head just as quickly” and, therefore, did not use Chisanbop."
