# Old high performers reach a Stroop performance level comparable to middle-aged or even young adults

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Block 3 accuracy, old high performers did not differ significantly from young adults, while all groups outperformed old low performers. [→ Patrick D. Gajewski 2020](#patrick-d-gajewski-2020)
`q3 i?` In the paper Stroop interference list, middle-aged adults did not differ from old high performers. [→ Patrick D. Gajewski 2020 (2)](#patrick-d-gajewski-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Patrick D. Gajewski 2020

Patrick D. Gajewski, Michael Falkenstein, Sven Th€ones, Edmund Wascher. (2020). Stroop task performance across the lifespan: High cognitive reserve in older age is associated with enhanced proactive and reactive interference control. NeuroImage 207 (2020) 116430. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2019.116430

`q3 · i?`

Bonferroni-corrected post-hoc tests on incongruent-minus-congruent error differences in Block 3 of the PC-based Stroop task. "no difference between YA vs. OH was found" while "all groups outperformed OL", indicating old high performers matched young adults in accuracy under high interference.

> "accuracy in the mixed block (Block 3) was higher in YA vs. MA ( p < .01), YA vs. OM ( p < .0001), YA vs. OL ( p < .0001) but no difference between YA vs. OH was found ( p > .05). OH accuracy was higher compared to OM ( p < .05) and all groups outperformed OL (all p's < 0.001)."

### Patrick D. Gajewski 2020 (2)

Patrick D. Gajewski, Michael Falkenstein, Sven Th€ones, Edmund Wascher. (2020). Stroop task performance across the lifespan: High cognitive reserve in older age is associated with enhanced proactive and reactive interference control. NeuroImage 207 (2020) 116430. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2019.116430

`q3 · i?`

Post-hoc comparisons in the paper-and-pencil Stroop interference list (part 3) confirmed the computer-based group pattern: "MA did not differ from OH", while old low performers were slower than all other groups except old middle performers.

> "MA did not differ from OH ( p > .05), but performed faster than OM and OL ( p < .001). OL performance was lower than all other groups' (all p's < 0.001) except OM ( p > .05)."
