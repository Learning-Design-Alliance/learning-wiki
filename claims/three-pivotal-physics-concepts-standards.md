## Evidence

### Singh 2016

Singh, C. and Schunn, C. D. (2016). Connecting Three Pivotal Concepts in K-12 Science State Standards and Maps of Conceptual Growth to Research in Physics Education. Originally published 2008. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1603.06024](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1603.06024)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The authors' three-part analysis examined Project 2061 and self-developed concept maps, standards from California, Colorado, Rhode Island, and Wisconsin, and the physics-education research literature. They report that "Three concepts emerged: Newton's laws" (qualitatively at middle school, quantitatively at high school), conservation of energy, and geometrical optics, and that no other concepts came close to meeting all three criteria.

> "Finally, we sought those physics concepts that were salient in all three steps: conceptually pivotal, found in all four state standards, and particularly difficult to learn. Three concepts emerged: Newton's laws"
