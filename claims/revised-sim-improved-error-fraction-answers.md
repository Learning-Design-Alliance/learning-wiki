# Revised simulation and activity significantly improved introductory students' answers on how the 25% error fraction comes about (78.0% vs 41.1% correct)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the 2016 introductory trial, 78.0% of students correctly explained the error fraction compared with 41.1% in 2014, a statistically significant improvement (U=1887, p<0.005). [→ Antje Kohnle and Aluna Rizzoli 2016](#antje-kohnle-and-aluna-rizzoli-2016)

## Evidence

### Antje Kohnle and Aluna Rizzoli 2016

Antje Kohnle and Aluna Rizzoli. (2016). Interactive simulations for quantum key distribution. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/aa62c8](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/aa62c8)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison across two years of the introductory course (N2=73 in 2014, N1=82 in 2016) using identical activity questions; the article reports "U=1887, N1=82, N2=73, p<0.005 for the error fraction question" and the 78.0% vs 41.1% correct rates. No effect size is printed.

> "Both of these questions were answered signiﬁcantly better in 2016 compared with 2014 (U=1887, N1=82, N2=73, p<0.005 for the error fraction question, U=2420, N1=82, N2=73, p=0.01 for the actions to detect the presence of Eve question). In 2016, 78.0% of students answered both questions correctly, compared with 41.1% and 58.9% respectively in 2014."
