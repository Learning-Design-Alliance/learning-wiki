## Evidence

### Bailey E. G. 2020

Bailey E. G., Greenall R. F., Baek D. M., Morris C., Nelson N., Quirante T. M., Rice N. S., Rose S., Williams K. R. (2020). Female In-Class Participation and Performance Increase with More Female Peers and/or a Female Instructor in Life Sciences Courses. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-12-0266

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Stepwise multiple linear regression predicting classroom performance gender gaps (female minus male ACT-adjusted grade) for 30 classrooms (instructor F8 excluded as an outlier 2.5 SD from the mean). Model 2: female instructor β = 0.538, percent female in attendance β = 0.478. No instructor-controlled teaching variables significantly predicted gaps.

> "When the percentage of females in attendance was added as a predictor, the model could now explain ∼57% of the variation in performance gaps (see Table 3, model 2; n = 30), and more females in attendance predicted better female performance"
