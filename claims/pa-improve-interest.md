# Pedagogical agents significantly improve learners' interest (g = 0.30), with the effect depending on the type of control condition

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3` · `i1`–`i2`

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Across 50 comparisons from 12 studies, PAs had a small-to-moderate significant effect on interest (g = 0.30, p = 0.01). [→ Gladstone 2025](#gladstone-2025)
`q3 i2` PAs were significantly more effective than video-based interventions (g = 0.54) and no-intervention comparisons (g = 0.33) for interest, but not more effective than software without PAs (g = 0.26, p = 0.16), and significantly less effective than traditional training (g = -0.31, p = 0.02). [→ Gladstone 2025 (2)](#gladstone-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Gladstone 2025

Gladstone, J. R., Schroeder, N. L., Heidig, S., Zhang, S., Palaguachi, C., & Pitera, M. (2025). Do Pedagogical Agents Enhance Student Motivation? Unraveling the Evidence Through Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10050-2

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Three-level meta-analysis with CHERVE of PA-versus-non-PA comparisons measuring interest. The pooled effect was significant with heterogeneity (Q(49) = 130.76, p < 0.001).

> "A random effects 3LMA with CHERVE exploring the impact of PAs on learners' interest found that PAs have a small to moderate, statistically significant effect (g = 0.30, p = 0.01, kcomparisons = 50, kstudies = 12)."

### Gladstone 2025 (2)

Gladstone, J. R., Schroeder, N. L., Heidig, S., Zhang, S., Palaguachi, C., & Pitera, M. (2025). Do Pedagogical Agents Enhance Student Motivation? Unraveling the Evidence Through Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10050-2

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Control-condition type significantly moderated interest effects (F(3.00, 3.06) = 400.67, p < 0.001). The traditional-training contrast rests on only one study with two comparisons.

> "PAs were significantly more effective than video-based interventions (g = 0.54, p < 0.01) and no intervention comparisons (g = 0.33, p = 0.02). However, they were not significantly more effective than software programs without PAs (g = 0.26, p = 0.16), and they were significantly less effective than traditional training (g = − 0.31, p = 0.02)"
