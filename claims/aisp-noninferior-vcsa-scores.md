## Evidence

### Baraza W. 2026

Baraza W., Staples G., Wells C., Barbier L., Tonga I., Singh P., Puttick M., Webster C. (2026). Artificially Intelligent Simulated Patients in Undergraduate Surgical Teaching: A Single Blinded Randomized Controlled Study. The Clinical Teacher. https://doi.org/10.1111/tct.70486

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Single-blinded randomized controlled study of 27 fourth-year medical students (13 control, 14 intervention) at a teaching hospital; virtual clinical skills assessment scores compared between groups. The article reports "statistical noninferiority of the AISP in comparison to conventional teaching methods" with a difference favouring the intervention of 1.0 points (95% CI −2.3 to 4.2).

> "The mean (SD) total assessment score for the control group was 12.1 (5.1) versus 13.1 (2.4) for the intervention group (p = 0.11, ns). However, the mean observed difference in scores between groups indicated statistical noninferiority of the AISP in comparison to conventional teaching methods"
