## Evidence

### Sebastian Kilde-Westberg 2025

Sebastian Kilde-Westberg, Andreas Johansson, and Jonas Enger. (2025). Generative AI as a lab partner: a case study. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/ggy1-3kjk](https://doi.org/10.1103/ggy1-3kjk)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Group 5's prompt "How do you adjust sound frequency on a Levilab" received a "seemingly irrelevant" reply describing a different product; the group then avoided ChatGPT until the lab's end.

> "The response was seemingly irrelevant and mentioned a product called Levilab. However, it differed from the equipment they had access to, and the students re-read the instructions without interacting with ChatGPT until the end of the lab session."

### Sebastian Kilde-Westberg 2025 (2)

Sebastian Kilde-Westberg, Andreas Johansson, and Jonas Enger. (2025). Generative AI as a lab partner: a case study. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/ggy1-3kjk](https://doi.org/10.1103/ggy1-3kjk)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Group 6 probed ChatGPT's claim that levitation occurs in "both nodes and anti-nodes"; follow-up answers increased their confidence in their own node-only hypothesis but left them confused about ChatGPT's reasoning.

> "the claim that objects can be levitated in both nodes and anti-nodes did not agree with the group’s hypothesized explanation of why the particle did not levitate at all speaker separation distances."
