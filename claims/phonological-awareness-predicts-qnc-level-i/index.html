Evidence¶

Krajewski 2009¶

Krajewski, K., & Schneider, W. (2009). Exploring the impact of phonological awareness, visual-spatial working memory, and preschool quantity-number competencies on mathematics achievement in elementary school: Findings from a 3-year longitudinal study. Journal of Experimental Child Psychology, 103(4), 516-531. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2009.03.009

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Structural equation model on the longitudinal sample (108 children at T1, 91 at T4) showed phonological awareness at T1 predicted QNC Level I at T2; "About 34% of the differences in QNC Level I were explained by differences in phonological awareness". The path coefficient was printed without its beta symbol.

Related Claims¶

  • Phonological awareness has no direct effect on higher-order QNC (Level II and above) or Grade 3 math achievement once QNC skills are modeled, confirming the isolated number-words hypothesis — related
  • Phonological awareness at age 4–5 does not predict later arithmetic skills despite being predicted by early oral language — reports the opposite
  • QNC Level I predicts QNC Level II/III, which in turn predicts Grade 3 mathematics achievement; QNC Level I also retains a direct path to Grade 3 math — related
  • Preschool counting skills and number knowledge directly predict formal arithmetic skills in primary school, together accounting for 40–46% of variance — related
  • Visual-spatial working memory predicts higher-order QNC (Level II/III) but not basic numerical skills, and is linked to Grade 3 math only indirectly — related
