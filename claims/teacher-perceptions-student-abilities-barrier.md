### Talafian 2024

Talafian, H., Stelzer, T., Lundsgaard, M., Mahmood, M., & Kuo, E. (2024). Teachers' Experiences with Implementing Open-ended Labs in High School Physics Classes. University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, Department of Physics. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2411.04275](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2411.04275)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Coding of open-response survey and online PD data in the IPaSS program found "Fourteen teachers (66.6%) cited students' abilities in dealing with investigative-style challenges as a barrier to implementing open-ended labs," leading teachers to advocate more guided, structured labs with prescriptive procedures.

> "Fourteen teachers (66.6%) cited students' abilities in dealing with investigative-style challenges as a barrier to implementing open-ended labs."

### Talafian 2024 (2)

Talafian, H., Stelzer, T., Lundsgaard, M., Mahmood, M., & Kuo, E. (2024). Teachers' Experiences with Implementing Open-ended Labs in High School Physics Classes. University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, Department of Physics. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2411.04275](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2411.04275)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Survey data tied low perceptions of student abilities to three sub-themes: unfamiliarity with investigative approaches (6 teachers), "unproductive struggle" with fewer scaffolds (5 teachers), and lack of interest, focus, or confidence leading to anxiety and giving up (4 teachers).

> "Survey data also revealed that teachers' low perceptions of their students' abilities were related to (1) students' unfamiliarity with investigative science learning approaches (6 teachers), (2) students' "unproductive struggle" when completing labs with fewer scaffolds (5 teachers)"
