## Evidence

### David P. Waters 2019

David P. Waters, Dragos Amarie, Rebecca A. Booth, Christopher Conover and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2019). Investigating students seriousness during selected conceptual inventory surveys. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020118](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020118)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

In the PhysPort actual populations, 'the UAT identified slightly more than 2% of students as non-serious for each RBAI,' based on choosing at least 4 uncommon answers (FCI, CSEM) or at least 3 (BEMA) among nine identified questions per instrument.

> "In the actual populations, on the other hand, the UAT identified slightly more than 2% of students as non-serious for each RBAI, and the EQT identified between 3% and 4.6% of students as non-serious for each RBAI."

### David P. Waters 2019 (2)

David P. Waters, Dragos Amarie, Rebecca A. Booth, Christopher Conover and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2019). Investigating students seriousness during selected conceptual inventory surveys. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020118](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020118)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Among 20,000 simulated all-random-guesser results per instrument, '68% and 52% of the respective simulated popula- tions for the FCI and CSEM had four or more Uncommon Answers' and 21% of the BEMA simulation did, supporting the UAT's ability to differentiate random guessers.

> "68% and 52% of the respective simulated popula- tions for the FCI and CSEM had four or more Uncommon Answers in their responses, and 21% of the BEMA ran- dom population had three or more Uncommon Answers"
