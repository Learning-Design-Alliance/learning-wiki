Evidence¶

Willower 1992¶

Willower, Donald J. (1992). Dewey's Theory of Inquiry and Reflective Administration. Paper presented at the annual convention of the University Council for Educational Administration, Minneapolis. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED355669

q1 · i? · theoretical · r3

Theoretical exposition of Dewey's naturalistic, empirical ethics as presented in the paper. The paper states that the location of morality in everyday life means that what students of educational administration call practice is chiefly an ethical undertaking, a matter of reflective appraisal of values served by decision options. The article offers a philosophical argument, not empirical data.

"Indeed, the separation of morals from the common problems of living in favor of an abstract creed or ritual creates social ills."

Related Claims¶

  • The limited empirical literature on administrative decisionmaking supports the utility of reflective procedures — related
  • Reflective methods must be internalized as habit to operate in everyday moral choice, and internalization depends on supportive social arrangements — related
  • For Dewey, moral knowledge is a product of moral inquiry, so character education should add moral problem solving rather than teach fixed principles through example, reward, or punishment — related
  • Dewey rejects Kohlberg's fixed stage hierarchy: moral inquiry happens only in problematic situations where no single action seems morally justified, and must be repeated as concrete situations arise — related
  • Character education is criticized for didacticism and de-emphasizing reflection and moral judgment, and Dewey's deliberation is proposed as a bridge between it and Kohlberg's approach — related
