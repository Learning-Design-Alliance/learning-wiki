# Task-level account of upskilling and the Triad of Skill Failure (deskilling, never-skilling, mis-skilling) with differing empirical support

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Upskilling occurs when a learner engages tasks at the appropriate subzone, task-specific knowledge accumulates, and tasks migrate toward subzones of greater independence. [→ Tsim 2026](#tsim-2026)
`q1 i?` The triad comprises deskilling (regression through disuse), never-skilling (failure to acquire a competency that should have formed), and mis-skilling (acquisition of a flawed competency presenting as genuine learning), best understood at the task level rather than cohort-wide. [→ Tsim 2026 (2)](#tsim-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Tsim 2026

Tsim, F., Gutoreva, A., Weiss, A., & Dubosh, N. (2026). AI as Teammate: Rethinking Task Distribution in Medical Training. Correspondence: fenditsim@gmail.com. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28373

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument in Section 3.3 framing routine clinical tasks as subzone-migration opportunities governed by three metacognitive components: monitoring determines subzone position, control operates retrospectively, and learning drives migration over time.

> "Upskilling, according to SCAN, occurs when (1) a learner identifies, and thus engages, a task at the appropriate subzone; (2) learner's task-specific knowledge accumulates; and (3) the task migrates toward subzones with greater independence over time."

### Tsim 2026 (2)

Tsim, F., Gutoreva, A., Weiss, A., & Dubosh, N. (2026). AI as Teammate: Rethinking Task Distribution in Medical Training. Correspondence: fenditsim@gmail.com. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28373

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument defining the three failure modes and mapping each onto distinct SCAN pathways (Figure 2, Table 2). Table 2 notes deskilling has direct clinical evidence, never-skilling is a conceptual risk model only, and mis-skilling has direct RCT evidence per the cited works.

> "Deskilling is a regression through disuse (Budzyń et al., 2025). Never-skilling is a failure to acquire a competency that should have formed in the first place (Ke et al., 2026). Mis-skilling is the acquisition of a flawed competency that presents as genuine learning (Teng et al., 2026)."
