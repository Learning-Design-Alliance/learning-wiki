# Typical textbook and course problems fail to capture authentic problem solving because they involve no decisions based on limited information

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Argenta M. Price 2021

Argenta M. Price, Candice J. Kim, Eric W. Burkholder, Amy V. Fritz, and Carl E. Wieman. (2021). A Detailed Characterization of the Expert Problem-Solving Process in Science and Engineering; Guidance for Teaching and Assessment. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1187/cbe.20-12-0276](https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.20-12-0276)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Authors' analysis of three example course problems (Fig. 1) from mechanical engineering, physics, and biology: all have well-defined answers reached by straightforward paths, with relevant concepts and assumptions stated or obvious. The authors tie this to the failure of efforts to measure and teach problem solving.

> "they do not involve needing to use judgement to make any decisions based on limited information, e.g., insufficient to specify correct decision with certainty."
