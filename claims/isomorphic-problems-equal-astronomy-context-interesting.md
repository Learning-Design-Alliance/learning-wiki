# Physics and astronomy students solve isomorphic astronomy- and physics-context problems about equally well, but the majority find the astronomy context more interesting

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` 13 of the 16 interviewed students solved both isomorphic centripetal-force problems entirely correctly (6 of 8 physics, 7 of 8 astronomy students). [→ Melanie Good 2018](#melanie-good-2018)
`q2 i?` The majority of both physics and astronomy students interviewed reported the astronomy-context problem was more interesting than the physics-context problem. [→ Melanie Good 2018 (2)](#melanie-good-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Melanie Good 2018

Melanie Good, Andrew Mason, and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Comparing introductory physics and astronomy students’ attitudes and approaches to problem solving. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/aae35b](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/aae35b)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview study in which 8 physics and 8 astronomy students were given an isomorphic problem pair (Earth–Sun orbit versus a spinning yo-yo) requiring centripetal force and uniform circular motion. The authors note this subset is too few students to make statistical inferences.

> "Altogether 13 out of 16 of all students (both introductory physics and introductory astronomy) who were presented with these problems were able to solve both problems entirely correctly."

### Melanie Good 2018 (2)

Melanie Good, Andrew Mason, and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Comparing introductory physics and astronomy students’ attitudes and approaches to problem solving. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/aae35b](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/aae35b)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Follow-up interviews with the same 16 students asked which isomorphic problem they enjoyed more; the article reports "the majority of both introductory physics and introductory astronomy students reported that they found the astronomy context more interesting," even when they found it more difficult.

> "In particular, the majority of both introductory physics and introductory astronomy students reported that they found the astronomy context more interesting."
