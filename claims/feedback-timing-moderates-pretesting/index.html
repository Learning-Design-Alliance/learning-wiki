Evidence¶

Steven C. Pan 2023¶

Steven C. Pan, Shana K. Carpenter. (2023). Prequestioning and Pretesting Effects: a Review of Empirical Research, Theoretical Perspectives, and Implications for Educational Practice. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09814-5

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review's synthesis of Grimaldi and Karpicke (2012), Vaughn and Rawson (2012), and Hays et al. (2013) reports that "delaying feedback nullified that advantage" for paired associates on cued recall posttests.

"In all three studies, only the provision of immediate correct answer feedback yielded a pretesting effect relative to a non-testing control condition on a subsequent cued recall posttest and delaying feedback nullified that advantage"

Steven C. Pan 2023 (2)¶

Steven C. Pan, Shana K. Carpenter. (2023). Prequestioning and Pretesting Effects: a Review of Empirical Research, Theoretical Perspectives, and Implications for Educational Practice. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09814-5

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports that across three Kornell (2014) experiments with trivia facts, "pre-instruction testing enhanced learning regardless of whether feedback was withheld", including a 24-hour delay.

"pre-instruction testing enhanced learning regardless of whether feedback was withheld until other intervening items had been encountered or even 24 h after participants had finished practice testing on all to-be-learned items"
