# Think-aloud interviews suggest students who worked only on the DSE QuILT spontaneously transferred WPI reasoning to the polarizer questions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q1`
> **Currency** · newest study 2017 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Interviews with students who had completed Modern Physics 1 and worked only on the DSE QuILT showed they applied WPI reasoning learned in the lamp context to the polarizer questions, mostly correctly. [→ Alexandru Maries 2017](#alexandru-maries-2017)

## Evidence

### Alexandru Maries 2017

Alexandru Maries, Ryan Sayer, and Chandralekha Singh. (2017). Effectiveness of interactive tutorials in promoting “which-path” information reasoning in advanced quantum mechanics. Physical Review Physics Education Research 13, 020115. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.13.020115

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Qualitative think-aloud interviews with students who had not worked on the MZI QuILT; the article states the data "was not analyzed in great detail" but the interviewer took careful notes. Examples include students Andrew and John reasoning correctly about WPI on polarizer questions after the tutorial.

> "The interviews suggested that the DSE QuILT helped students reason using WPI to determine the pattern observed on the screen for a given DSE setup. In many cases, they were able to transfer this reasoning correctly to the DSE polarizer questions."
