## Evidence

### Gaudet Andrew D. 2010

Gaudet Andrew D., Ramer Leanne M., Nakonechny Joanne, Cragg Jacquelyn J., Ramer Matt S. (2010). Small-Group Learning in an Upper-Level University Biology Class Enhances Academic Performance and Student Attitudes Toward Group Work. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0015821

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre-post survey study of 36 consenting students in the 2008 section using the 54-item SAGE questionnaire; paired t tests/Wilcoxon tests found significant positive shifts on three subscores (p<0.05, no effect size). The Student Interdependence subscore did not change.

> "On three of the four subscores – Quality of Product, Peer Support, and Frustrations – the students' attitudes exhibited a significant positive shift between the start (entrance survey) and end (exit survey) of the course"

### Gaudet Andrew D. 2010 (2)

Gaudet Andrew D., Ramer Leanne M., Nakonechny Joanne, Cragg Jacquelyn J., Ramer Matt S. (2010). Small-Group Learning in an Upper-Level University Biology Class Enhances Academic Performance and Student Attitudes Toward Group Work. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0015821

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive SAGE Frustrations subscore means from the same entrance-exit survey study; the article calls the shift particularly dramatic but prints no effect size.

> "a lower score on this scale reflects less frustration, and most students were much less frustrated with group work on the exit survey (Entrance: 2.1±0.9; Exit: 1.3±0.8)"
