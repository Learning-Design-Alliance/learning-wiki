## Evidence

### Jones Rebecca L. 2024

Jones Rebecca L., Prusmetikul Suwimol, Whitehorn Sarah. (2024). Differential attainment in assessment of postgraduate surgical trainees: a scoping review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05580-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing White & Welch, that initial gender differences in laparoscopic tasks disappeared after training; microsurgical skill differences were not significant and all trainees improved significantly after training. Second-hand findings via the review.

> "In laparoscopic tasks, men were initially more skilful in peg transfer and intracorporeal knot tying than women. Following training, the performance was not different between genders [47]."

### Jones Rebecca L. 2024 (2)

Jones Rebecca L., Prusmetikul Suwimol, Whitehorn Sarah. (2024). Differential attainment in assessment of postgraduate surgical trainees: a scoping review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05580-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Milam et al. and Myers et al., that studies hypothesising stereotype threat would impair female trainees' performance found no supporting evidence. Men performed better in mental rotation and women in working memory, but the threat hypothesis was disproved.

> "They found no evidence of stereotype threat influencing female performance, disproving their hypothesis, a finding supported by Myers et al. [50]."
