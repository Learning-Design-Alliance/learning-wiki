## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Using the term micro-gravity without specifying its exact meaning can strengthen misconceptions associating weight with gravitational force, because 'micro' suggests gravity is very small far from Earth. [→ José M. L. Figueiredo 2005](#jose-m-l-figueiredo-2005)
`q1 i?` The acceleration due to Earth's gravity at 400 km altitude is around 8.4 m/s², so orbiting weightlessness is not caused by a small gravitational acceleration. [→ José M. L. Figueiredo 2005 (2)](#jose-m-l-figueiredo-2005-2)

José M. L. Figueiredo. (2005). Advantages of adopting the operational definition of weight. arXiv physics.ed-ph. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0505193](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0505193)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article's argument in Section V that 'the term “micro” could lead to the idea that acceleration due to gravity is very small because the distance from Earth'; this is an interpretive claim, not a measured result.

> "The use of the term micro-gravity without specifying its exact meaning can strengthen the misconceptions associated to weight and gravitational force because the term “micro” could lead to the idea that acceleration due to gravity is very small because the distance from Earth."
