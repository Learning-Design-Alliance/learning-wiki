# In one school, well-defined instructional priorities plus deliberate leadership facilitated sustained developmental practices

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Efforts to collectively improve instructional practices were unlikely to happen spontaneously but were facilitated by well-defined instructional priorities and explicit efforts to lead and coordinate them. [→ Stevens 2006](#stevens-2006)

## Evidence

### Stevens 2006

Stevens, W. D., with Kahne, J. (2006). Professional Communities and Instructional Improvement Practices: A study of small high schools in Chicago. Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/professional-communities-and-instructional-improvement-practices-study-small-high

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In the school C case study, sustained collective improvement activities, such as aligning curriculum to standards, happened through professional development organized by the principal; without deliberate coordination, teachers did not engage in regular systematic improvement activities in group meetings.

> "Well-defined instructional priorities alone, however, will not lead to developmental practices. We found that developmental practices also require intentional efforts to initiate and organize them."
