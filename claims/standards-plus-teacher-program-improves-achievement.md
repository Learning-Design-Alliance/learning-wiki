# Combining elaborated education standards with a teacher improvement program significantly improved student reading achievement compared with a control group, while standards alone did not reach significance at p < .01

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a multilevel covariance analysis, the second intervention group had better student achievement rates, significant at p < .01 (1-tailed), while the first intervention was significant only at p < .05 (1-tailed). [→ Azkiyah et al. 2014](#azkiyah-et-al-2014)

## Evidence

### Azkiyah et al. 2014

S.N. Azkiyah, Simone Doolaard, Bert P. M. Creemers, M.P.C. (Greetje) Van Der Werf. (2014). The Effects of Two Intervention Programs on Teaching Quality and Student Achievement. Journal of Classroom Interaction, 49(1), 4-11. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1100411

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multilevel covariance analysis (model 4) of post-test reading scores controlling for pre-test achievement, with 1660 students in 50 schools randomly assigned to two interventions or control. The intervention two coefficient was 1.679 (SE .537) and intervention one .904 (SE .545); the models explained 16% of variance before interventions and 20% after.

> "The results showed that the second intervention group had better student achievement rates, which were significant at p < .01 (1-tailed) 1. Although the students in the first intervention had also improved their achievements, this result was only significant at p < .05 (1-tailed)."
