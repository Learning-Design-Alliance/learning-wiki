Miranda 2026¶

Miranda, J. P. P., Parreño, E. B., & Rivera, J. G. (2026). Defining AI Fatigue in Academic Contexts: Dimensions, Indicators, and a Stage-Based Model Using Grounded Theory. International Journal of Learning, Teaching and Educational Research, 25(5), 91–107. https://doi.org/10.26803/ijlter.25.5.5

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Grounded theory analysis (open, axial, selective coding by two researchers with a third arbitrating disagreements) of open-ended interview and survey responses from 1,054 students across three Philippine universities. The study reports that analysis "produced five dimensions of AI fatigue" with two indicators each; no effect sizes are printed.

"Analysis produced five dimensions of AI fatigue, namely Cognitive Overload, Motivational Disengagement, Moral Unease, Physical Strain, and Attentional Drift, each consisting of two indicators grounded in participant accounts."

  • Motivational Disengagement, reported by 270 participants, captures reduced initiative and eroding confidence linked to AI reliance — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Moral Unease, reported by 190 participants, reflects authorship guilt and a gap between AI output quality and actual understanding — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Physical Strain, reported by 120 participants, comprises visual fatigue and session exhaustion from reading and verifying AI outputs — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
