# Secondary students taught chemistry with computer simulations achieved higher mean scores than students taught with lecture method

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students taught with computer simulations showed significantly higher posttest chemistry achievement than students taught with lecture method (F = 100.756, p < .05). [→ Okwuduba Emmanuel Nkemakolam; Offiah Francisca Chinelo; Madichie Chinyere Jane 2018](#okwuduba-emmanuel-nkemakolam-offiah-francisca-chinelo-madichie-chinyere-jane-2018)

## Evidence

### Okwuduba Emmanuel Nkemakolam; Offiah Francisca Chinelo; Madichie Chinyere Jane 2018

Okwuduba Emmanuel Nkemakolam; Offiah Francisca Chinelo; Madichie Chinyere Jane (2018). Effect of Computer Simulations on Secondary School Students’ Academic Achievement in Chemistry in Anambra State. Asian Journal of Education and Training, 4(4): 284-289. https://doi.org/10.20448/journal.522.2018.44.284.289

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental non-equivalent control group study of 78 SS2 students in two coeducational schools; ANCOVA on posttest scores controlling for pretest. Mean gain was "42.26" for the simulation group versus 26.35 for the lecture group.

> "The result in Table 3 indicates that the probability of obtaining the F -value (100.756) is zero (0.00), which is less than the 0.05 level of significance. The null hypothesis therefore is rejected, which means that there is a significant difference between the mean achievement scores of students in chemistry taught with computer simulation and those taught with lecture method in favor of experimental group with high mean score."
