# MMI performance is significantly related to gender and age: female and older candidates scored higher

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Female candidates scored significantly higher on the MMI than male candidates (Md = 3.74 vs 3.55; W = 5781, p < 0.01), and age correlated positively with MMI results (ρ = .16, p < .05). [→ Knorr M 2018](#knorr-m-2018)

## Evidence

### Knorr M 2018

Knorr M, Schwibbe A, Ehrhardt M, Lackamp J, Zimmermann S, Hampe W. (2018). Validity evidence for the Hamburg multiple mini-interview. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1208-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of the 2014 cohort control variables: age correlated with MMI "ρ = .16, p < .05"; a Wilcoxon rank-sum test showed female candidates (Md = 3.74) scored higher than male candidates (Md = 3.55; W = 5781, p < 0.01). No standardised effect size printed.

> "There was a small but significant correlation between age and MMI result (ρ = .16, p < .05)."

### Knorr M 2018 (2)

Knorr M, Schwibbe A, Ehrhardt M, Lackamp J, Zimmermann S, Hampe W. (2018). Validity evidence for the Hamburg multiple mini-interview. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1208-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In robust regression models for GP psychosocial skills, GP suitability and OSCE overall score, gender and age were non-significant predictors (Table 5); gender differences were not significant for any outcome variable. No effect sizes printed.

> "Both gender and age were non-significant predictors in each of the models (Table 5)."
