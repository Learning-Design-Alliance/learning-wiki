## Evidence

### Garet 2017

Garet, M.S., Wayne, A.J., Brown, S., Rickles, J., Song, M., and Manzeske, D. (2017). The Impact of Providing Performance Feedback to Teachers and Principals, Executive Summary (NCEE 2018-4000). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Implementation documentation from vendor online systems and attendance records in the eight study districts. Teachers present in spring of Year 2 averaged 6.8 sessions across two years instead of the intended eight, primarily due to teacher mobility.

> "The average number of feedback sessions per teacher was 3.7 in Year 1 and 3.9 in Year 2."

### Garet 2017 (2)

Garet, M.S., Wayne, A.J., Brown, S., Rickles, J., Song, M., and Manzeske, D. (2017). The Impact of Providing Performance Feedback to Teachers and Principals, Executive Summary (NCEE 2018-4000). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of online portal access logs in Year 1. In response, Year 2 reports were printed and distributed, and were viewed by all principals and received by 98 percent of teachers.

> "access rates were low—39 percent of the teachers with value-added scores and 40 percent of the principals"
