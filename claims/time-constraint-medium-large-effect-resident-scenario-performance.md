# Imposing a time constraint on simulated pediatric decompensation scenarios has a medium-to-large effect (Cohen's d = 0.60) on resident performance, raising interventions performed by 6.2%, but the difference is not statistically significant

## Evidence

### Collins Candace 2025

Collins Candace, Parr Madeline F. E., Lozy Tara, Chirico Amy. (2025). The effect of time constraints on resident performance in pediatric mock scenarios. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07865-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Single-center prospective cohort study of 14 matched low-fidelity simulation scenarios performed pre- and post-time constraint by pediatric residents, analyzed with two-sided t-tests. The post-time constraint group showed "a 6.2% increase in raw points" with "Cohen’s d = 0.60", not statistically significant (p = 0.126).

> "When comparing the percentage of total possible points completed, there was a 6.2% increase in raw points in the post-time constraint group. This difference reflected a medium to large effect size (Cohen’s d = 0.60); however, the difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.126, Fig. 1)."
