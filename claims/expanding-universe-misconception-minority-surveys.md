## Evidence

### Kragh 2013

Kragh, Helge. (2013). Cosmology and Science Education: Problems and Promises. Manuscript version of chapter to appear in Michael R. Matthews, ed., Handbook of Historical and Philosophical Research in Science Education, Springer Handbook series. [doi:10.1007/978-94-007-7654-8_20](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-007-7654-8_20)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The chapter reports the Lightman and Miller (1989) survey of 1111 interviewed American adults, in which "nearly 60%" said the universe remains about the same size and "only 24%" said it is expanding. The figures are reported as printed; no effect size is given.

> "Asked whether the universe is systematically changing in size or remaining about the same size, nearly 60% of 1111 interviewed American adults offered the last response. According to the survey conducted by Lightman and Miller (1989) only 24% of the respondents said that the universe is expanding."

### Kragh 2013 (2)

Kragh, Helge. (2013). Cosmology and Science Education: Problems and Promises. Manuscript version of chapter to appear in Michael R. Matthews, ed., Handbook of Historical and Philosophical Research in Science Education, Springer Handbook series. [doi:10.1007/978-94-007-7654-8_20](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-007-7654-8_20)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The chapter reports later large-scale surveys of students in introductory astronomy courses (Wallace, Prather & Duncan 2012), where "only a minority of the students revealed a reasonably correct understanding" of expansion; some treated the phrase as a metaphor for growing knowledge.

> "Only a minority of the students revealed a reasonably correct understanding of the meaning of the 'expansion of the universe', and a sizeable minority denied that the universe is increasing in size."
