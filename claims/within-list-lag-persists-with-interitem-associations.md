# A within-list lag effect persisted at all retention intervals (up to 7 days) when the orienting task allowed interitem associations, contrary to what a context-only retrieval account predicts for that condition

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Experiment 2 (superordinate generation task), a significant within-list lag effect appeared in almost every list regardless of retention interval, and the predicted four-way interaction was far from significant (F < 1). [→ Glenberg 1980](#glenberg-1980)
`q3 i?` In Experiment 1 (pair-relation task), the predicted four-way interaction was significant, with the lag effect present at short retention intervals and absent where recall was near floor. [→ Glenberg 1980 (2)](#glenberg-1980-2)

## Evidence

### Glenberg 1980

Glenberg, A. M., & Lehmann, T. S. (1980). Spacing repetitions over 1 week. Memory & Cognition, 8(6), 528-538. https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03213772

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 within-list analysis on 96 subjects. The result ran contrary to the component-levels prediction; the authors attribute the persistence of the lag effect to incidental storage of interitem associations acting as extra retrieval cues, noting subjects reused superordinates and sometimes generated previous list words as associates, and say the finding "can be accommodated within component-levels theory" if such associations are stored. It challenges a context-only account under these conditions, not context retrieval generally.

> "Contrary to the predic­tion from components-levels theory, there was a within­list lag effect in almost every list, regardless of the retention interval. The effect was significant [F(1 ,48) = 64.35, MSe =.90], and it did not interact with any variable in a way that changes these conclusions. The predicted four-way interaction was far from significant (F < 1)."

### Glenberg 1980 (2)

Glenberg, A. M., & Lehmann, T. S. (1980). Spacing repetitions over 1 week. Memory & Cognition, 8(6), 528-538. https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03213772

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1 analysis of 48 subjects revealed a significant four-way interaction [F(1,44) = 9.45, MSe = 2.26], "exactly as predicted by component-levels theory", but the absence of the effect elsewhere may reflect floor effects, motivating Experiment 2.

> "There was a substan­tial within-list lag effect in both lists in Con­dition 0-0 and in the second list in Condition 7-0, whereas the effect was absent in all other lists. Unfor­tunately, recall in these latter lists was so low that the absence of a lag effect may simply reflect a floor effect."
