# Laptop versus longhand note-taking effects are mixed: typing benefited memory in some studies but longhand benefited conceptual questions in others

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Schoen (2012) and Bui et al. (2013, study 1) found computer note-taking led to better memory performance than longhand, while Mueller and Oppenheimer (2014) found the opposite pattern for conceptual questions. [→ Jansen 2017](#jansen-2017)

## Evidence

### Jansen 2017

Jansen, R. S., Lakens, D., & IJsselsteijn, W. (2017). An integrative review of the cognitive costs and benefits of note-taking. Educational Research Review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2017.10.001

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Narrative review of Mueller and Oppenheimer (2014), two experiments (n = 65 and n = 149) with TEDx talks, contrasting with Schoen (2012) and Bui et al. (2013) where computer note-taking outperformed longhand. The review reports the divergent patterns without effect sizes.

> "In both experiments, participants’ performance on the conceptual questions was better in the longhand notes condition compared to the computer notes condition. Performance on the factual questions showed a trend towards better performance with longhand note-taking."
