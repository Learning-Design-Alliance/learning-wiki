# Both inadequate responder groups show more teacher-rated attention problems and lower self-reported self-efficacy than typical readers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Inadequate responders showed significantly greater teacher-rated attention problems and lower self-reported academic self-efficacy than typical readers. [→ Cho Eunsoo 2015](#cho-eunsoo-2015)
`q3 i?` Adequate responders were also rated as more inattentive than typical readers, but inadequate and adequate responders did not differ on attention or self-efficacy. [→ Cho Eunsoo 2015](#cho-eunsoo-2015)

## Evidence

### Cho Eunsoo 2015

Cho Eunsoo, Roberts Garrett J., Capin Philip, Roberts Greg, Miciak Jeremy, Vaughn Sharon. (2015). Cognitive Attributes, Attention, and Self-Efficacy of Adequate and Inadequate Responders in a Fourth Grade Reading Intervention. Learning Disabilities Research & Practice. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4793275/

`q3 · i3`

Series of ANOVAs comparing the four reader groups on teacher-rated attention (SWAN scale) and student-reported academic self-efficacy. The contrasts showed inadequate responders had "greater attention problems, t (130) = 6.33, corrected p < .001, and lower self-efficacy, t (136) = 4.23, corrected p < .001, compared to typical readers". Table 3 reports Hedges' g = 1.14 for attention and g = .80 for self-efficacy on the TR vs. IR contrast.

> "Pairwise contrasts indicated that inadequate responders showed greater attention problems, t (130) = 6.33, corrected p < .001, and lower self-efficacy, t (136) = 4.23, corrected p < .001, compared to typical readers. Adequate responders were more inattentive than typical readers, t(61) = 3.56, corrected p = .004."
