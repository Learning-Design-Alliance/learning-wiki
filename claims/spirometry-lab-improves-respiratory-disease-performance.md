# The spirometry-based active learning lab produced the largest performance gain on questions about the hallmarks of restrictive and obstructive respiratory diseases

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Test sections significantly outperformed control sections on hallmarks of restrictive and obstructive diseases questions (77.5% vs 57.2%, p<0.005), a roughly 20-point difference. [→ Wolf et al. 2015](#wolf-et-al-2015)

## Evidence

### Wolf et al. 2015

Alex M. Wolf, Carlos Liachovitzky & Abass S. Abdullahi. (2015). Active Learning Improves Student Performance in a Respiratory Physiology Lab. Journal of Curriculum and Teaching, 4(1). http://dx.doi.org/10.5430/jct.v4n1p19

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Midterm exam comparison between test (n=73) and control (n=76) sections on disease-concept questions. Test sections scored "77.5±12.7%" versus "57.2±12.2%", p<0.005, the largest difference among the three measured concepts; no effect size was printed.

> "on the hallmarks of restrictive and obstructive diseases (Test: 77.5±12.7%, Control: 57.2±12.2%, p<0.005), the test sections performed significantly better than the control sections."
