## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Five days after a simulated ER night shift, students' general feeling of preparedness increased significantly compared to baseline, with a large effect size (d = 1.86). [→ Stroben Fabian 2016](#stroben-fabian-2016)

## Evidence

### Stroben Fabian 2016

Stroben Fabian, Schröder Therese, Dannenberg Katja A., Thomas Anke, Exadaktylos Aristomenis, Hautz Wolf E. (2016). A simulated night shift in the emergency room increases students' self-efficacy independent of role taking over during simulation. BMC Medical Education 16:177. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0699-9

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Pilot pre-post study at Charité Berlin: 30 final-year students in a six-hour simulated ER night shift; preparedness measured at baseline and five days later via online survey. The 18 (60%) who completed follow-up showed a significant increase from a baseline mean of −0.34 to 0.66, "the effect is large (d = 1.86)".

> "18 of the 30 participants (60 %) completed the online survey five days after the simulation and showed a significant increase in their general feeling of preparedness compared to before the simulation (p = 0.001). Participants now report to generally feel prepared (Mean 0.66); the effect is large (d = 1.86)."
