## Evidence

### Corey McInerney 2026

Corey McInerney. (2026). A Thematic Analysis of A-level Physics Examiner Reports on Gravity. arXiv. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/ae806d](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/ae806d)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Thematic coding of examiner report comments on gravitational field questions across six UK exam boards. The results report that "38% of field question comments pointed to conceptual errors as the reason for dropped marks", including confusing force with field and misdirected field-line arrows.

> "50% of questions relating to gravitational fields were coded as 'negative', the highest of any of the topics identified. 38% of field question comments pointed to conceptual errors as the reason for dropped marks."

### Corey McInerney 2026 (2)

Corey McInerney. (2026). A Thematic Analysis of A-level Physics Examiner Reports on Gravity. arXiv. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/ae806d](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/ae806d)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Sentiment coding of examiner comments on energy and potential questions in the same 137-question dataset. The results state that "Topics such as energy and potential have a high proportion of 'negative' comments (41%)", with conceptual errors, such as sign errors in gravitational potential energy, causing the most problems.

> "Topics such as energy and potential have a high proportion of 'negative' comments (41%)."
