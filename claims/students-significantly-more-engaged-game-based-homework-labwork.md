## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students taking the game-based course experienced significantly more Intrinsic Motivation and more Positive Affect, and significantly less Negative Affect. [→ Coller et al. 2011](#coller-et-al-2011)

## Evidence

### Coller et al. 2011

B. D. Coller, D. J. Shernoff, and A. D. Strati. (2011). Measuring Engagement as Students Learn Dynamic Systems and Control with a Video Game. Advances in Engineering Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1076061

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Two-level regression models estimated the average difference in each experiential variable between students in Year 1 (without the game) and those in Year 3 (with the game), controlling for gender, ethnicity, baseline mechanics score, and learning style. Results showed that 19% of the person-level variance in Engagement was accounted for by the treatment condition.

> "Results suggest that students were significantly more engaged in Y ear 3 when they were working on their game-based homework and labwork, compared to students in Y ear 1, whose coursework was not game based."
