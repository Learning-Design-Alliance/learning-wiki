`q3 i2` There were statistically significant differences in impacts by race/ethnicity for receptive vocabulary and early numeracy, with larger impacts among Hispanic students (0.23 and 0.22) than among Black students (0.05 and 0.03). [→ Bloom and Weiland 2014](#bloom-and-weiland-2014)

## Evidence

### Bloom and Weiland 2014

Howard S. Bloom & Christina Weiland. (2014). To What Extent Do Head Start's Effects on Children's Language, Literacy, Mathematics, and Socio-Emotional Skills Vary Across Individuals, Subgroups, and Centers? SREE Spring 2014 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED562943

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Race/ethnicity subgroup contrasts in the Head Start Impact Study first follow-up data; significance of differences between subgroups was determined via an omnibus test. Table 3 prints Hispanic effect sizes of 0.23*** (PPVT) and 0.22*** (W-J AP) versus Black effect sizes of 0.05 and 0.03.

> "Not surprising given the strong correlation between Hispanic and dual language learners, there were statistically significant results by race/ethnicity for these same two outcomes, with larger impacts among Hispanic students."
