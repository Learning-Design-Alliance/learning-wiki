# Prior anthropomorphic-interface studies show inconsistent findings, with some favouring anthropomorphic feedback and others not, motivating the affordance-based explanation

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The literature review reports that findings across anthropomorphic feedback studies do not always agree—some studies found advantages (e.g., pronunciation correction, social presence), others found no significant effects or negative perceptions—so overall results do not follow a clear pattern. [→ Murano 2011](#murano-2011)

## Evidence

### Murano 2011

Murano, P. & Sethi, T. (2011). Anthropomorphic User Interface Feedback in a Sewing Context and Affordances. International Journal of Advanced Computer Science and Applications, Vol. 2, No. 4. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1208.3323](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1208.3323)

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Narrative review by the authors of studies including David et al. on quiz contexts, Prendinger et al. on eye tracking, Qiu and Benbasat on e-commerce agents, McBreen et al. on retail embodied agents, and the main author's own prior PC building and pronunciation studies, which reported mixed and sometimes contradictory outcomes for effectiveness and user satisfaction.

> "the results overall tend to not follow  a clear pattern. In some cases anthropomorphism appears to be better and in some cases the opposite seems true."
