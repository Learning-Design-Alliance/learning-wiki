# Confidence in addressing sexual concerns rose from baseline to immediately after e-learning but did not change significantly by 4 weeks in one uncontrolled pre-post study

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Confidence in ability to address sexual concerns rose significantly from baseline to immediately post e-learning (mean 2.59 vs 3.13, P = 0.001) but did not change significantly from baseline to 4 weeks (mean 2.59 vs 2.66, P = 0.063), in one uncontrolled pre-post study. [→ McCaughan E.M. 2021](#mccaughan-em-2021)

## Evidence

### McCaughan E.M. 2021

McCaughan E.M., Flannagan C., Parahoo K., Bingham S.L., Brady N., Connaghan J., Maguire R., Thompson S., Jain S., Kirby M., O'Connor S.R. (2021). Effects of a Brief E-Learning Resource on Sexual Attitudes and Beliefs of Healthcare Professionals Working in Prostate Cancer Care: A Pilot Study. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph181910045

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pairwise comparisons following repeated measures ANOVA (overall F = 27.351; P = 0.001) in the 44-participant pilot. The confidence gain at T1 decayed by T2; the article reports the T0–T2 comparison as not significant at the Bonferroni-adjusted threshold.

> "For 'confidence in ability to address sexual concerns', significant differences were found between T0 and T1 (mean score/4 = 2.59 vs. 3.13, respectively: P = 0.001) but not between T0 and T2 (mean score/4 = 2.59 vs. 2.66, respectively: P = 0.063)"
