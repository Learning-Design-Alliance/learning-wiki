# Young DLLs have two separate phonological and grammatical language systems early in life

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` DLL infants, toddlers, and preschoolers have two separate phonological systems with different mental representations for each sound in their two languages. [→ Hammer 2014](#hammer-2014)
`q2 i?` Based on nine case studies, DLL infants/toddlers have two separate grammatical systems, learning language-specific rules such as subject realization in each language. [→ Hammer 2014 (2)](#hammer-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Hammer 2014

Hammer, C. S., Hoff, E., Uchikoshi, Y., Gillanders, C., Castro, D., & Sandilos, L. E. (2014). The Language and Literacy Development of Young Dual Language Learners: A Critical Review. Early childhood research quarterly. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecresq.2014.05.008

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's synthesis of multiple phonological studies (e.g., Anderson, 2004; Fabiano-Smith & Barlow, 2010) concludes that "DLL infants/toddlers and preschoolers have two separate phonological systems" with language-specific sound representations, though cross-linguistic effects appear in younger children's speech.

> "The vast preponderance of the evidence suggests that DLL infants/toddlers and preschoolers have two separate phonological systems, meaning that DLLs have different mental representations for each sound in their two languages"

### Hammer 2014 (2)

Hammer, C. S., Hoff, E., Uchikoshi, Y., Gillanders, C., Castro, D., & Sandilos, L. E. (2014). The Language and Literacy Development of Young Dual Language Learners: A Critical Review. Early childhood research quarterly. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecresq.2014.05.008

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports that nine case studies of DLL infants/toddlers, supported by subject-realization and aspectual-marker investigations, indicate DLL children have two separate grammatical systems and learn each language's rules without difficulty.

> "Based on nine case studies involving DLL infants/toddlers living in a variety of countries including the U.S., DLL children have two separate grammatical systems."
