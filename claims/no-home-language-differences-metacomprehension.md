# Intervention effects on comprehension and regulation accuracy do not differ by home language (English vs other)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Adding home language as a factor showed no main effect of home language on monitoring accuracy, comprehension, or either regulation accuracy measure, and the overall pattern of results was similar. [→ Hepner 2024](#hepner-2024)

## Evidence

### Hepner 2024

Hepner, S. L., Oudman, S., Carlson, T. E., van de Pol, J., & van Gog, T. (2024). Improving (meta)comprehension: Feedback and self-assessment. Learning and Instruction, 92, 101922. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.101922

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory re-analysis of the randomized experiment with home language (103 of 258 students spoke a language other than English at home) as an additional fixed effect and interaction term; no significant home-language effects emerged.

> "The models that added home language not English as a factor showed no main effect of home language (monitoring accuracy: F (1,237) = 0.06, p = .8; comprehension: F (1,237) = 2.95, p = .09, partial η2 = .01; monitoring-based regulation accuracy: F (1,237) = 0.02, p = .90; comprehension-based regulation accuracy: F (1,237) = 0.19, p = .66)"
