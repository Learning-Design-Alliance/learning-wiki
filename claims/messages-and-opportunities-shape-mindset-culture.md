# Experimentally manipulated growth mindset-supportive messages and opportunities causally affect students' willingness to act on their growth mindset beliefs, with strongest effects when both are present

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across four laboratory studies, manipulations of teachers' verbal messages and improvement opportunities affected perceived classroom culture and students' willingness to enact growth mindset beliefs; the positive belief-behavior association was nullified in the fixed-mindset classroom. [→ Hecht 2023](#hecht-2023)
`q2 i?` Effects were strongest when teachers provided both growth mindset-supportive messages and opportunities, rather than one or the other. [→ Hecht 2023](#hecht-2023)

## Evidence

### Hecht 2023

Hecht, C. A., Murphy, M. C., Dweck, C. S., Bryan, C. J., Trzesniewski, K. H., Medrano, F. N., Giani, M., Mhatre, P., & Yeager, D. S. (2023). Shifting the mindset culture to address global educational disparities. npj Science of Learning 8:29. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00181-y

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Across four scenario-based laboratory experiments with adolescent participants, Hecht and colleagues manipulated teacher messages and opportunities (e.g., revising assignments for credit). Participants perceived the intended culture differences, and the belief-choice association was nullified in the fixed mindset teacher's class.

> "a positive association between participants' growth mindset beliefs and learning-oriented choices in the growth mindset teacher's class"
