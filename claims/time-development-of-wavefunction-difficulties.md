# Students incorrectly time-evolve superposed wave functions with a single phase factor and judge the resulting probability density time-independent

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The most common mistakes on questions asking for the wave function after time t are responses multiplying the whole superposition by one phase factor, which are not solutions of the TDSE. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016)
`q2 i?` Even students who recognize that each stationary state evolves with a different phase factor often say the probability density of a superposition state is time-independent, adding probabilities instead of amplitudes. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)](#chandralekha-singh-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Transfer of Learning in Quantum Mechanics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05655](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05655)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

On written questions asking for the wave function after time t, "the most common mistakes" were responses like (AΨ1 + BΨ2)exp(−iEt /¯h), which the article notes are not solutions of the TDSE.

> "Our research shows that the most common mistakes in the questions asking for the wave function after time t, given the wave function at t = 0, are responses such as (AΨ1 + BΨ2)exp(−iEt /¯h) or Ax(a − x)exp(−iEt /¯h)."

### Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Transfer of Learning in Quantum Mechanics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05655](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05655)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

For an infinite-square-well state AΨ1 + BΨ2, even students knowing the distinct phase factors "often said that the probability density should be time-independent", adding probabilities rather than amplitudes.

> "even those who recognized that each stationary state evolves according to different time-dependent phase factors, often said that the probability density should be time-independent."
