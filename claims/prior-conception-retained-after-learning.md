# After successful re-categorization, learners' feature ratings show the prior conception is retained in memory alongside the newly learned conception.

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Ramsburg 2015

Ramsburg, J. T., & Ohlsson, S. (2015). Category Change in the Absence of Cognitive Conflict. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22Category+Change+in+the+Absence+of+Cognitive+Conflict%22

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r?`

Post-task two-way ANOVA of 7-point feature-importance ratings in Experiment 1 (21 participants' ratings lost to a programming error). The misconception feature nuclei was rated marginally more important by the Confirmatory-Only condition, indicating the initial conception persisted after target learning.

> "The Confirmation-Only condition rated nuclei as being marginally more important compared to the Complete condition, F(1, 306) = 3.43  p = .065, ηp2 = .011."

### Ramsburg 2015 (2)

Ramsburg, J. T., & Ohlsson, S. (2015). Category Change in the Absence of Cognitive Conflict. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22Category+Change+in+the+Absence+of+Cognitive+Conflict%22

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In Experiment 2's post-task ratings ANOVA there was no condition main effect and no condition × attribute interaction; participants rated both the misconception feature (nuclei) and the target feature (ribosomes) as most important, consistent with both conceptions remaining in memory.

> "Pairwise comparisons for attribute type revealed that regardless of condition participants rated nuclei and ribosomes as more important than any other attribute, p's < .001."
