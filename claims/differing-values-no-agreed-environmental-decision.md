# Students' differing values about a campus predator-prey problem produced no agreed definition of the issue and no agreed action

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the predator-prey problem-posing space, students held systems knowledge and varied, value-based views (danger, balance, ecosystem significance), yet reached no collective definition of the problem and lacked action-related knowledge for what to do. [→ Erin Bridges Bird · Heidi L. Ballard · Margaret Harte 2023](#erin-bridges-bird-heidi-l-ballard-margaret-harte-2023)

## Evidence

### Erin Bridges Bird · Heidi L. Ballard · Margaret Harte 2023

Erin Bridges Bird · Heidi L. Ballard · Margaret Harte. (2023). Data to decision-making: how elementary students use their Community and Citizen Science project to reimagine their school campus. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-022-09612-6

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Ethnographic analysis of the Cooper's Hawk problem-posing space (Findings). Macky saw the hawk as a sign of a thriving ecosystem, Selena valued safety and wanted it removed, Garret wanted raptors to control House Sparrows, and Isaak and McKenna expressed uncertainty about the hawk's death and diet.

> "Because student-ornithologists valued different things and therefore perceived the situation's priorities in different ways, there was no collective and agreed upon definition of what the specific issue was, nor did student-ornithologists seem to have the action-related knowledge to know what to do."
