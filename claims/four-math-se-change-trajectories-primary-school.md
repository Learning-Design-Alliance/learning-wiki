## Evidence

### Peura 2026

Peura, P., Koponen, T., Aro, T., Viholainen, H., & Aro, M. (2026). Dynamics of change in math self-efficacy, sources of self-efficacy, and math skills. Journal of Educational Psychology, 118(2), 209-223. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0001006

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Growth mixture modeling of math self-efficacy across three time points in 1,032 children in grades 2–4 selected the four-trajectory solution based on fit indices, entropy, and interpretability. The printed proportions were High Stable 83.7%, Low Stable 8.5%, Decreasing 4.2%, and Increasing 3.6%.

> "Most children belonged to the profile with initially high and over the year rather stable math SE (High Stable SE, 83.7%). In contrast, the Decreasing SE profile (4.2%) was characterized by initially high math SE but decreasing SE over the year."
