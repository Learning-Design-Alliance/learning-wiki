# Reading an analogous story analog substantially increases production of the analogous solution to a transfer problem

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` High school subjects who read an analogous story produced the pendulum solution to the cord problem far more often than control subjects, both before and after a hint. [→ Gick 1983](#gick-1983)

## Evidence

### Gick 1983

Gick, M. L., & Holyoak, K. J. (1983). Schema Induction and Analogical Transfer. Cognitive Psychology, 15, 1-38. https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(83)90002-6)90002-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Experiment 1, a booklet-based two-part study with 69 high school students in analog-recall, analog-summary, and control-summary conditions. Subjects receiving the analogous story produced more pendulum solutions prior to the hint (71 vs 20%), G(1) = 15.8, p < .001; total frequencies were 98 vs 30%, G2(1) = 30.1, p < .001.

> "Combining the two analog conditions, subjects who received the analogous story produced more pendulum solutions prior to the hint than did those who received the control story (71 vs 20% ), G(1) = 15.8, p < .001."
