# Nursing students' patient and injury assessment skills, including emotional, nutritional and history-taking items, rose from pre-test to post-test after clinical simulation in one uncontrolled pre-post study

Borges Francisca Edinária de Sousa; Pereira Francisco Gilberto Fernandes; de Aguiar Maria Isis Freire; Fernandes Bruna Karen Cavalcante; Machado Ana Larissa Gomes; Beserra Eveline Pinheiro; Caetano Joselany Áfio. (2025). Nursing students' skills in surgical wound care improve after clinical simulation: a quasi-experiment. Revista Brasileira de Enfermagem. https://doi.org/10.1590/0034-7167-2024-0171

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Before-and-after quasi-experiment with 50 students comparing checklist performance pre/post simulated scenario; Table 1 shows emotional status 26(52.0) to 44(88.0), D = 0.72, and use of anamnesis forms 20(40.0) to 37(74.0), D = 0.92, both significant.

> "Significant differences were obtained in all items assessed, from hand hygiene (<0.001), procedure guidance (0.016), all domains of clinical anamnesis, such as patient identification (0.016), assessment of emotional status (<0.001), nutritional assessment (<0.001)"
