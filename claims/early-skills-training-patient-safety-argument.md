## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors argue that ensuring first skill experience in a simulation-based environment can mitigate "trial and error" learning on patients during clinical rotations, potentially contributing to patient safety. [→ Schwill Simon 2020](#schwill-simon-2020)

## Evidence

### Schwill Simon 2020

Schwill Simon, Hundertmark Jan, Fahrbach-Veeser Johanna, Eicher Christiane, Tonchev Pencho, Kurczyk Sonia, Szecsenyi Joachim, Loukanova Svetla. (2020). The AaLplus near-peer teaching program in Family Medicine strengthens basic medical skills—A five-year retrospective study. PLoS ONE 15(5): e0233748. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0233748

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Theoretical argument in the Discussion, not a tested result: the authors reason from the cited problem of trial-and-error learning on patients that AaLplus "may therefore indirectly contribute to patient-safety". No data on patient safety are reported.

> "This can be mitigated by ensuring first skill experience in a simulation-based environment [51]. AaLplus, as a program strengthening students’ basic medical skills, may therefore indirectly contribute to patient-safety and positive physician-patient-relationships."
