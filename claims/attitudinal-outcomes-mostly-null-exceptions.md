## Evidence

### Amechghal A 2026

Amechghal A, Naciri A, Takhdat K, Loubbairi S, Mellouki A, Lahlou L, Nassik H. (2026). The impact of the learner role in healthcare simulation-based education on learning outcomes: a systematic review. J Educ Eval Health Prof. https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2026.23.24

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Review synthesis of 8 attitudinal-outcome studies: confidence in clinical practice (P=0.710), self-regulation (P=0.648), and overall learning effectiveness (P=0.847) also showed no differences; emotional clarity showed no difference (P>0.05).

> "Self-efficacy improved significantly in both roles across 4 studies [18-20,22], with no statistically significant between-role differences in 3 studies (P>0.05 [18]; P=0.102 [20]; P=0.283 [22]); however, one study reported higher self-efficacy among active participants than observers (P<0.05) [19]."

### Amechghal A 2026 (2)

Amechghal A, Naciri A, Takhdat K, Loubbairi S, Mellouki A, Lahlou L, Nassik H. (2026). The impact of the learner role in healthcare simulation-based education on learning outcomes: a systematic review. J Educ Eval Health Prof. https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2026.23.24

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Review reports, citing Blanie et al. [17] (N=104 anesthesia residents RCT); the review reclassified perceived transfer as a Level 2 attitudinal outcome because it reflects self-reported beliefs rather than observed behavioral change.

> "Perceived learning transfer did not differ significantly immediately after simulation (P=0.701) but was significantly higher in the active participant-observer group than in the observer-only group at 3 months (P=0.036) [17]."
