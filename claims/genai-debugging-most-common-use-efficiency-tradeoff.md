## Evidence

### Karl Henrik Fredly 2026

Karl Henrik Fredly, Tor Ole B. Odden and Benjamin M. Zwickl. (2026). How undergraduate physics students use generative AI for computational modeling. [doi:10.1103/vg1v-dkrh](https://doi.org/10.1103/vg1v-dkrh)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of retrospective interviews with 13 groups (19 students) in a third-semester electromagnetism course found debugging was the most common genAI use-case: "around half took the problem straight to genAI", often by pasting code and asking it to fix it.

> "Using genAI to debug code was the most common use-case of genAI among all 12 groups who used it. When the students encountered an error message or incorrect result, around half took the problem straight to genAI."

### Karl Henrik Fredly 2026 (2)

Karl Henrik Fredly, Tor Ole B. Odden and Benjamin M. Zwickl. (2026). How undergraduate physics students use generative AI for computational modeling. [doi:10.1103/vg1v-dkrh](https://doi.org/10.1103/vg1v-dkrh)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview analysis found students appreciated the speed of genAI debugging but "did not take the time to learn how to identify errors in their program", a trade-off several students themselves noted as hard to manage.

> "However, this efficiency also meant that students did not take the time to learn how to identify errors in their program, a trade-off several students noted as hard to manage."
