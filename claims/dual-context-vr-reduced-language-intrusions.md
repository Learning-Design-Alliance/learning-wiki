# Learning each language in its own distinctive VR context reduced cross-language intrusions relative to learning both languages in a single context

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Dual-context participants made 38% fewer opposite-language intrusions than single-context participants during testing, with a printed partial eta-squared of 0.13. [→ Joey Ka-Yee Essoe 2022](#joey-ka-yee-essoe-2022)

## Evidence

### Joey Ka-Yee Essoe 2022

Joey Ka-Yee Essoe, Nicco Reggente, Ai Aileen Ohno, Younji Hera Baek, John Dell'Italia and Jesse Rissman. (2022). Enhancing learning and retention with distinctive virtual reality environments and mental context reinstatement. npj Science of Learning 7:31. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00147-6

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

A randomised behavioural experiment with 24 single-context and 24 dual-context participants found dual-context participants "exhibited 38% fewer intrusions (4.09 ± 4.82)" than single-context participants; the article prints a partial eta-squared effect size of ηp2 = 0.13 (p = 0.014), a small-to-medium effect.

> "dual-context participants exhibited 38% fewer intrusions (4.09 ± 4.82) than the single-context (6.57 ± 4.69) participants (Fig. 3c; RM-ANOVA, p = 0.014, ηp 2 = 0.13"
