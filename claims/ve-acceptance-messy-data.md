# Students accept virtual experiments for hands-on activities when the interfaces approximate real instruments, the data are genuinely messy, differences from realistic portrayal are clarified, and the tool is flagged as training

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Open University distance students accepted a telescope simulator as a virtual experiment under conditions of interface realism, messy data, explicit clarification, and training-tool framing. [→ Kolb U. 2018](#kolb-u-2018)

## Evidence

### Kolb U. 2018

Kolb U., Brodeur M., Braithwaite NStJ., and Minocha S. (2018). A Robotic Telescope For University-Level Distance Teaching. RTSRE Proceedings Vol. 1, No. 1. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1806.02989](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1806.02989)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Mixed-methods evaluation (2013-2015) of OU students using PIRATE and its simulator, drawing on surveys and interviews; the summary reports that students accept virtual experiments when they "deliver genuine, ”messy” data" and approximate real interfaces.

> "Students accept virtual experiments (telescope simulator) for ”hands on” activities when VEs approximate the interfaces used to operate real-world instruments, when VEs deliver genuine, ”messy” data"
