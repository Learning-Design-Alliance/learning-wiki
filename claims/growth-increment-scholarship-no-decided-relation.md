# When individual growth-increment curves are correlated with scholarship without reference to body size or stage of development, no decided relation between scholarship and physical growth appears

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For the twenty-nine boys, curve correlations of weight and height increments with scholarship show no decided relation; height increment shows slightly more negative than positive correlation. [→ Stewart 1917](#stewart-1917)
`q2 i?` Weight increment and scholarship show slightly more positive correlation during high school than during preceding grades, and slightly more positive correlation for boys of thirteen years or over. [→ Stewart 1917 (2)](#stewart-1917-2)

## Evidence

### Stewart 1917

Stewart, S. F. (1917). A study of physical growth and school standing of boys. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0070804

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Individual curve analysis of twenty-nine boys with four or more successive years of records (Chart VIII). Weight increment was entirely positively correlated with scholarship for eight boys and entirely negatively for six; height increment correlations split 3 positive, 3 negative, 11 mixed or zero.

> "The above tabulation suggests that when sets of curves are considered there does not seem to be any decided relation between scholarship and physical growth."

### Stewart 1917 (2)

Stewart, S. F. (1917). A study of physical growth and school standing of boys. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0070804

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Chart IX distributes yearly curve correlations of all twenty-nine boys by school grade: weight-and-scholarship totals 36 positive versus 31 negative, height-and-scholarship 27 positive versus 32 negative. Chart X shows slightly more positive weight correlation for boys thirteen and over.

> "The numbers indicate that there is slightly more positive correlation of scholarship and weight in- crement during high school than during the preceding grades. For scholarship and height increment there is slightly more negative than positive correlation."
