# Functional connectivity between parietal cortex and occipito-temporal cortex during numerical processing is present in adults but absent in children, whose IFG instead correlates with both regions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` BOLD ratio effects in left superior parietal cortex correlated with OTC in adults but not in children, with the group difference marginally significant. [→ Cantlon Jessica F. 2009](#cantlon-jessica-f-2009)

## Evidence

### Cantlon Jessica F. 2009

Cantlon Jessica F., Libertus Melissa E., Pinel Philippe, Dehaene Stanislas, Brannon Elizabeth M., Pelphrey Kevin A. (2009). The Neural Development of an Abstract Concept of Number. Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn.2008.21159

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis of ratio-dependent BOLD variability across children and adults. In children, IFG ratio effects correlated with parietal and OTC effects, but "there was no correlation between the BOLD ratio effect in left superior parietal cortex and OTC in children (r = −.10, p = .74)."

> "Interestingly, however, there was a correlation between BOLD activity in left superior parietal cortex and OTC in adults (right OTC: r = .59, p < .05; left OTC: r = .49, p = .07). In addition, the difference between children and adults in the strength of the correlation between parietal and OTC was marginally significant (χ2 = 3.33, p = .07)."
