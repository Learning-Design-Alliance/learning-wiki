# Simulation effectiveness depends on the teaching method and role: higher fidelity alone does not outperform lower fidelity, but simulator plus standardized patients outperforms standardized patients alone

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · causal `r?` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2016 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Training heart sounds with a high-fidelity simulator (Harvey) did not yield significantly better performance than training with a low-fidelity simulator (CD). [→ Vogel Daniela 2016](#vogel-daniela-2016)
`q2 i?` Students training cardiac examination on standardized patients plus a cardiac simulator (Harvey) performed significantly better in cardiac skills than a control group working only with standardized patients. [→ Vogel Daniela 2016 (2)](#vogel-daniela-2016-2)
`q2 i?` Training chest examination with a manikin (Laerdal SimMan 3G) resulted in better chest examination skills than performing chest examination on a peer. [→ Vogel Daniela 2016 (3)](#vogel-daniela-2016-3)

## Evidence

### Vogel Daniela 2016

Vogel Daniela, Harendza Sigrid. (2016). Basic practical skills teaching and learning in undergraduate medical education – a review on methodological evidence. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001063](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001063)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Review-reported controlled comparison (ref 5) of high- versus low-fidelity simulation for heart sound learning; no significant difference was detected.

> "Students who received training of heart sounds with a high-fidelity simulator (Harvey) did not perform significantly better than students who trained with a low-fidelity simulator (CD)"

### Vogel Daniela 2016 (2)

Vogel Daniela, Harendza Sigrid. (2016). Basic practical skills teaching and learning in undergraduate medical education – a review on methodological evidence. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001063](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001063)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Review-reported controlled study (ref 22) combining standardized patients with the Harvey cardiac simulator versus standardized patients alone, with significantly better cardiac skills in the combined group.

> "Students who were given the opportunity to train cardiac examination skills on standardized patients and a cardiac simulator (Harvey) performed significantly better in cardiac skills than a control group, who only worked with standardized patients"

### Vogel Daniela 2016 (3)

Vogel Daniela, Harendza Sigrid. (2016). Basic practical skills teaching and learning in undergraduate medical education – a review on methodological evidence. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001063](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001063)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Review-reported controlled study (ref 53) comparing manikin-based chest examination training with peer-based practice in preclinical students.

> "Training with a manikin (Laerdal SimMan 3G) resulted in better chest examination skills than performing chest examination on a peer"
