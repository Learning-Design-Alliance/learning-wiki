# Providing lower-intermediate L2 learners with supplementary readings (short stories containing idioms) produces a statistically significant post-test advantage in conceptual fluency over idiom instruction alone

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After thirty sessions of idiom instruction, the experimental group given supplementary short stories scored significantly higher on the post-test than the control group (t = -2.815, p < 0.005). [→ Mokhtari and Talebinezhad 2014](#mokhtari-and-talebinezhad-2014)

## Evidence

### Mokhtari and Talebinezhad 2014

Elahe Mokhtari, Mohammed Reza Talebinezhad. (2014). Using Supplementary Readings (Short Stories) in Increasing the Conceptual Fluency, the Case of Idioms in English. Advances in Language and Literary Studies. https://doi.org/10.7575/aiac.alls.v.5n.1p.134

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of two intact groups of 15 female lower-intermediate learners at SPLC; both received 15 minutes of idiom instruction for thirty sessions, only the experimental group received supplementary readings. Post-test means were 23.6667 (experimental) versus 20.4000 (control), and the article reports "a statistically important difference between experimental and control group".

> "The results, as Table 4 shows, indicate that there is a statistically important difference between experimental and control group (t2, 30 = -2.815, p < 0.005) regarding their conceptual fluency on post-test."
