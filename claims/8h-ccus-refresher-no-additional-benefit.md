# An additional 8-hour intensive CCUS refresher training provides no statistically significant additional improvement over standard fellowship training in critical care medicine fellows

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Over a 6-month intervention, fellows receiving an additional 8-hour training did not improve in total CCUS proficiency test scores significantly more than fellows receiving standard fellowship education (median improvement 18 vs 31, P = .09). [→ Suzuki Reina 2022](#suzuki-reina-2022)
`q3 i?` Neither the knowledge domain (P = 0.26) nor the skill domain (P = 0.20) showed a statistically significant difference in improvement between the standard and intensive groups. [→ Suzuki Reina 2022 (2)](#suzuki-reina-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Suzuki Reina 2022

Suzuki Reina, Kanai Mio, Oya Kazumasa, Harada Yohei, Horie Ryohei, Sekiguchi Hiroshi. (2022). A prospective randomized study to compare standard versus intensive training strategies on long-term improvement in critical care ultrasonography proficiency. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-022-03780-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Prospective randomized comparison of two training strategies at Mayo Clinic; 21 fellows analyzed (ST n = 10, IT n = 11) after stratified allocation by baseline score. Median "improvement in CCUS proficiency tests between the ST group and the IT group" was 18 (3.8–38) versus 31 (21–46), P = .09. Equivalence was not tested.

> "No statistically significant difference was observed in the median (interquartile range [IQR]) improvement in CCUS proficiency tests between the ST group and the IT group: 18 (3.8–38) versus 31 (21–46) (P = .09)."

### Suzuki Reina 2022 (2)

Suzuki Reina, Kanai Mio, Oya Kazumasa, Harada Yohei, Horie Ryohei, Sekiguchi Hiroshi. (2022). A prospective randomized study to compare standard versus intensive training strategies on long-term improvement in critical care ultrasonography proficiency. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-022-03780-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same randomized comparison, domain-level analysis of improvement scores (Fig. 2): knowledge 8.5 (2.5–15) versus 14 (5–21), P = 0.26; skill 11 (0.5–19) versus 16 (2–30), P = 0.20. No effect sizes were printed, so no magnitude was inferred.

> "no statistically significant difference was observed between the 2 groups (Fig. 2): knowledge, median (IQR), 8.5 (2.5–15) versus 14 (5–21), P = 0.26; skill, 11 (0.5–19) versus 16 (2–30), P = 0.20; and total score, 18 (3.8–38) versus 31(21–46), P = 0.09."
