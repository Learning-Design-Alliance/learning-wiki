## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students whose mothers had a college degree had higher STEM GPA than those whose mothers did not, while family income did not significantly predict STEM GPA. [→ Pilato Jazelle 2025](#pilato-jazelle-2025)

## Evidence

### Pilato Jazelle 2025

Pilato Jazelle, Cortes Robert, Peterson Emily Grossnickle, Uttal David, Kolvoord Robert, Green Adam. (2025). Neurocognitive correlates of spatial thinking mediate the relation between socioeconomic status and STEM achievement. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-29345-z

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Two regressions on STEM GPA in the cognitive sample (N=303 for education; N=104 for income). The education model was significant; the article reports "B = 0.12, t(302) = 3.55, p < .001"; the income model was not significant.

> "Students whose mothers had a college degree had a 0.12 increase in their STEM GPA compared to those whose mothers did not have a college degree (B = 0.12, t(302) = 3.55, p < .001). Family income did not significantly predict STEM GPA, nor did the model explain a significant portion of the variance"
