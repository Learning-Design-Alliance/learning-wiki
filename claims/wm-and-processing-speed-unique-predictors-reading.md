# Working memory and processing speed, but not inhibition or non-alphanumeric naming speed, uniquely predict both word reading and reading comprehension in children ages 8 to 16

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Working memory significantly predicted both comprehension and word reading after controlling for the other cognitive factors. [→ Christopher 2012](#christopher-2012)
`q3 i?` Processing speed significantly predicted word reading and marginally predicted comprehension, with a stronger path to word reading. [→ Christopher 2012](#christopher-2012)
`q3 i?` Inhibition and naming speed (non-alphanumeric stimuli) did not independently predict comprehension or word reading. [→ Christopher 2012 (2)](#christopher-2012-2)

## Evidence

### Christopher 2012

Christopher, M. E., Miyake, A., Keenan, J. M., Pennington, B., DeFries, J. C., Wadsworth, S. J., Willcutt, E., & Olson, R. K. (2012). Predicting Word Reading and Comprehension with Executive Function and Speed Measures Across Development: A Latent Variable Analysis. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3360115/

`q3 · i?`

Structural equation model with six latent factors (four cognitive, two reading) fit to 483 participants ages 8 to 16, constrained across age groups. The article reports "comprehension was significantly predicted by working memory (p < .01)" and word reading by both working memory and processing speed (p < .01 for both).

> "comprehension was significantly predicted by working memory (p < .01) and marginally so by processing speed (p = .08). Word reading was significantly predicted by both working memory and processing speed (p < .01 for both)."

### Christopher 2012 (2)

Christopher, M. E., Miyake, A., Keenan, J. M., Pennington, B., DeFries, J. C., Wadsworth, S. J., Willcutt, E., & Olson, R. K. (2012). Predicting Word Reading and Comprehension with Executive Function and Speed Measures Across Development: A Latent Variable Analysis. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3360115/

`q3 · i?`

Same constrained SEM across age groups. The article reports null paths: "Inhibition and naming speed did not independently predict comprehension or word reading" with p values ranging from .12 to .72. No effect sizes were printed, so impact is null.

> "Inhibition and naming speed did not independently predict comprehension or word reading (p = .52 for inhibition to comprehension, p = .72 for naming speed to comprehension, p = .12 for inhibition to word reading, and p = .25 for naming speed and word reading)."
