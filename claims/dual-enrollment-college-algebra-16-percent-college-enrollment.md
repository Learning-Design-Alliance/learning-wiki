# Passing the college algebra placement test near the cut-off and enrolling in dual enrollment college algebra was associated with a 16 percent higher likelihood of going to college

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students near the eligibility cut-off who took a rigorous dual enrollment college algebra class were 16 percent more likely to go to college than similar students who did not take the class. [→ Cecilia Speroni 2011](#cecilia-speroni-2011)

## Evidence

### Cecilia Speroni 2011

Cecilia Speroni. (2011). High School Dual Enrollment Programs: Are We Fast-Tracking Students Too Fast? National Center for Postsecondary Research. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/high-school-dual-enrollment-programs-are-we-fast-tracking-students-too-fast

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

The study tracked Florida 2000-2001 and 2001-2002 high school seniors who took a college algebra placement test, comparing students with scores very near the eligibility cut-off. It found participants were "16 percent more likely to go to college" than similar non-participants; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Students who passed the test (with a score very near the cut-off for eligibility) and enrolled in a rigorous dual enrollment college algebra class were 16 percent more likely to go to college and 23 percent more likely to earn a college degree than similar students who did not take the class."
