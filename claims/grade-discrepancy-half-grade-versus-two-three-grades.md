## Evidence

### Schauber 2024

Schauber, S. K., Olsen, A. O., Werner, E. L., & Magelssen, M. (2024). Inconsistencies in rater-based assessments mainly affect borderline candidates: but using simple heuristics might improve pass-fail decisions. Advances in Health Sciences Education, 29(5), 1749–1767. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-024-10328-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive and bootstrap results for A–F grades from the same ten-examiner video-rating study. The article reports the weaker candidates' discrepancies spanned "a range of discrepancies spanning two to three grades"; no standardised effect size is printed.

> "differences were on average half a grade for the two best performing students ‘Marit’ and ‘Anne’, meaning that these two candidates had a one-grade range in their evaluations. These differences were significantly lower than those for ‘Lars’ and ‘Kari’ who varied by 1.1, and 1.6 grades, respectively."
