## Evidence

### N.G. Holmes 2015

N.G. Holmes, Carl E. Wieman, D.A. Bonn. (2015). Teaching critical thinking. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1505329112

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Cohort comparison on week 17 (LR circuit) written work, where the physical model was inadequate for achievable data quality; neither group received explicit prompts to identify or explain a disagreement. The article prints proportions but no standardized effect size.

> "In week 17, over 3/4 of the students in the experimental group identiﬁed the disagreement, nearly four times more than in the control group, and over half of the experimental group provided the correct physical interpretation."

### N.G. Holmes 2015 (2)

N.G. Holmes, Carl E. Wieman, D.A. Bonn. (2015). Teaching critical thinking. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1505329112

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of student work on the third experiment of a different sophomore lab course with different instructors and structure. The sophomore instructions hinted that a technical model modification might be necessary, which the authors say raised control-group identification. Proportions only; no effect size printed.

> "However, only 10% of the students in the control group provided the physical interpretation, compared to 40% in the experiment group."
