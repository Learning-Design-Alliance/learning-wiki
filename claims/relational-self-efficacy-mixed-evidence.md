# Teachers' relational self-efficacy—beliefs in their capability to form, maintain, and repair relationships—is hypothesized to motivate TSR-building, but existing global self-efficacy measures show mixed links to TSR quality

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The article defines relational self-efficacy as a distinct, task-specific belief construct that existing multi-dimensional teacher self-efficacy scales do not directly measure. [→ Robinson 2022](#robinson-2022)
`q2 i?` Studies of general teacher self-efficacy and TSR quality report conflicting findings: some positive associations, others null or very slight. [→ Robinson 2022 (2)](#robinson-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Robinson 2022

Robinson, C. D. (2022). A Framework for Motivating Teacher-Student Relationships. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-022-09706-0

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Definitional/theoretical statement grounded in social cognitive theory and Bandura's four sources of self-efficacy (mastery experiences, vicarious experiences, verbal persuasion, affective and physiological states). The article argues non-domain-linked scales "usually have poor predictive validity."

> "I define teachers' relational self-efficacy as teachers' beliefs about their capability to successfully form, maintain, and (when necessary) repair relationships with students."

### Robinson 2022 (2)

Robinson, C. D. (2022). A Framework for Motivating Teacher-Student Relationships. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-022-09706-0

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Narrative review of cited studies: some (Hajovsky et al.; Summers et al.) found positive associations between self-efficacy and TSR perceptions, while others (Chung et al.; De Jong et al.; Yoon) found null or very slight associations. The review attributes this discrepancy to measures not targeting relational self-efficacy directly.

> "several studies have found no, or very slight, associations between teachers' self-efficacy and the quality of their relationships with individual students"
