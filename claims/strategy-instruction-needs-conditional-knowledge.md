# Strategy Instruction Needs Conditional Knowledge

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · none recorded yet

Teaching learners *when* and *why* to use a strategy — not just *how* — is required for strategy use to transfer beyond the training context. Conditional knowledge is what allows learners to select and adapt strategies independently rather than executing a memorized procedure on near tasks only.

## Discussion

**Declarative vs. conditional knowledge.** Strategy instruction that stops at procedural steps ("first do X, then do Y") produces execution of the strategy on near tasks but poor maintenance and transfer. Conditional knowledge — knowing under which task conditions, for which goals, and with what expected payoff a strategy applies — is what allows learners to select and adapt strategies independently. This is a core component of [self-regulated learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md), where strategy use is coupled to monitoring and conditional decision-making.

**Design implication.** Effective strategy programs pair modeling of the strategy with explicit discussion of its conditions of applicability, and prompt learners to evaluate strategy–task fit during practice. Without this pairing, learners may acquire the procedure yet fail to deploy it when the task changes — a pattern consistent with the broader finding that [activation](activation-improves-learning.md) of relevant knowledge and explicit [advance organizers](advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) support appropriate strategy selection [+M]. Programs that embed conditional knowledge in modeling — for example, reciprocal teaching's explicit linkage of summarizing, questioning, clarifying, and predicting to the demands of the text segment at hand — illustrate the pairing of procedure with conditions of use [+M].

**Constraints on the claim.** Explicit conditional instruction is not uniformly beneficial: for novices, extended discussion of when-and-why can add extraneous load before the procedure itself is fluent, diluting limited working-memory resources in line with [cognitive load theory](../theories/cognitive-load-theory.md) [-M]. Conversely, for advanced learners, conditional knowledge they have already induced from experience can become redundant, and re-teaching it yields little gain [~M] — an instance of the [expertise reversal effect](../theories/expertise-reversal-effect.md). The claim therefore applies most cleanly to learners who have at least procedural fluency in the strategy but have not yet induced its conditions of applicability.

**Open questions.** The evidence base for this claim still needs to be populated with specific studies (see Evidence section). Open issues include how much conditional knowledge can be inferred from practice versus needing explicit instruction, whether conditional knowledge benefits are moderated by learner expertise, and how durable conditional knowledge is relative to procedural knowledge of the strategy itself.
