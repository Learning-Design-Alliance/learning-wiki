# The retention-interval main effect shows forgetting occurred over one week in both experiments

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In Experiment 1, participants tested after 5 min outperformed those tested after 1 week. [→ de Jonge 2015](#de-jonge-2015)
`q3 i2` In Experiment 2, the retention-interval main effect was also significant, with lower recall after one week. [→ de Jonge 2015 (2)](#de-jonge-2015-2)

## Evidence

### de Jonge 2015

de Jonge, M., Tabbers, H. K., & Rikers, R. M. J. P. (2015). The Effect of Testing on the Retention of Coherent and Incoherent Text Material. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-015-9300-z

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1 main effect of retention interval: 70% versus 50% recall, "F(1, 60)=27.48, p<.001, ηp2=.31".

> "Participants in the 5-min group outperformed the participants in the 1-week group (70 vs. 50 %), F(1, 60)=27.48, p<.001, ηp2=.31, suggesting that forgetting occurred during the 1-week interval."

### de Jonge 2015 (2)

de Jonge, M., Tabbers, H. K., & Rikers, R. M. J. P. (2015). The Effect of Testing on the Retention of Coherent and Incoherent Text Material. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-015-9300-z

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 main effect of retention interval: 54% versus 45% recall, "F(1, 60) = 6.42,p<.05, ηp2=.10".

> "There was a significant main effect of retention interval, F(1, 60) = 6.42,p<.05, ηp2=.10. Participants in the 5-min group recalled more on the final test (54 %) compared to participants in the 1-week group (45 %)."
