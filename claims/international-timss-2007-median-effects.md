# Across countries in TIMSS 2007, the strongest estimated effects on math achievement were a positive effect of ClassMemo followed by a negative effect of ClassDaily, with median effects matching the Swedish pattern

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Separate multi-level analyses of each of the 50 countries in TIMSS 2007 showed the strongest estimated effects were positive for ClassMemo and negative for ClassDaily, and median effects across countries had the same direction as in Sweden. [→ Kimmo Eriksson 2019](#kimmo-eriksson-2019)

## Evidence

### Kimmo Eriksson 2019

Kimmo Eriksson, Ola Helenius, Andreas Ryve. (2019). Using TIMSS items to evaluate the effectiveness of different instructional practices. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-018-9473-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multi-level analyses run separately on data from each of the 45 countries in TIMSS 2003 and 50 countries in TIMSS 2007; median and mean fixed-effect estimates are reported in Table 5. The article reports qualitative consistency across waves and with the Swedish findings; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Note that the strongest estimated effects on achievement were the positive effect of ClassMemo followed by the negative effect of ClassDaily."
