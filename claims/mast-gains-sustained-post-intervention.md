## Evidence

### Phillips 2016

Phillips, J. A., McElwain, M. C., & Clemmer, K. W. (2016). Metacognitive Training in Professional Development Can Improve and Sustain Student Achievement. Loyola Marymount University. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1607.07856](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1607.07856)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Five-year follow-up of CST mathematics scores (Figure 5) for the five schools showing improvement during MAST, spanning one year before, two years during, and two years after participation. The article reports post-intervention normalized changes of "+0.15" and "–0.33", smaller than during the intervention.

> "the average normalized changes in these two student populations from the final year of MAST participation to two years later were +0.15 (Advanced or Proficient) and –0.33 (below Basic)"

### Phillips 2016 (2)

Phillips, J. A., McElwain, M. C., & Clemmer, K. W. (2016). Metacognitive Training in Professional Development Can Improve and Sustain Student Achievement. Loyola Marymount University. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1607.07856](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1607.07856)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The same five-school follow-up analysis of Figure 5 shows retained gains. The article states "all of the schools that demonstrated significant implementation of adaptive expert strategies were able to retain the student achievement gains" and that four continued to improve.

> "It was apparent from Figure 5 that all of the schools that demonstrated significant implementation of adaptive expert strategies were able to retain the student achievement gains that they had made."
