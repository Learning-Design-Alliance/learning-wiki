## Evidence

### Timonen Tomi 2022

Timonen Tomi, Dietz Aarno, Linder Pia, Lehtimäki Antti, Löppönen Heikki, Elomaa Antti-Pekka, Iso-Mustajärvi Matti. (2022). The effect of virtual reality on temporal bone anatomy evaluation and performance. European Archives of Oto-Rhino-Laryngology, 279(9), 4303–4312. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00405-021-07183-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Poisson loglinear comparison of total errors between the five-novice and five-expert groups by viewing method. The article reports "127 errors vs. 76 errors, p < 0.001" in cross-sectional viewing but no significant group difference in VR.

> "the novice group made significantly more errors with cross-sectional viewing than the expert group (127 errors vs. 76 errors, p < 0.001), whereas with VR, the difference between the groups remained statistically insignificant"

### Timonen Tomi 2022 (2)

Timonen Tomi, Dietz Aarno, Linder Pia, Lehtimäki Antti, Löppönen Heikki, Elomaa Antti-Pekka, Iso-Mustajärvi Matti. (2022). The effect of virtual reality on temporal bone anatomy evaluation and performance. European Archives of Oto-Rhino-Laryngology, 279(9), 4303–4312. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00405-021-07183-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Linear mixed model comparison of task completion time between groups; Table 3 shows PACS group difference of 16.36 min at "0.001*" versus VR difference of -7.03 min at "0.665". The authors state this refuted their second hypothesis.

> "The experts were significantly faster when using the cross-sectional viewing in PACS interface than novices, but with VR, the difference between the groups remained statistically insignificant."
