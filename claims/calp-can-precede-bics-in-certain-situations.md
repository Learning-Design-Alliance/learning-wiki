# The BICS-before-CALP sequence is typical for immigrant children but not universal: high L2 CALP can precede fluent L2 BICS in certain situations

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Attainment of high levels of L2 CALP can precede attainment of fluent L2 BICS in certain situations, so the sequential order was suggested as typical, not absolute. [→ Cummins 1999](#cummins-1999)

## Evidence

### Cummins 1999

Cummins, J. (1999). BICS and CALP: Clarifying the Distinction. ERIC Document ED 438 551. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED438551

`q1 · i?`

In this opinion paper Cummins clarifies that the sequential nature of BICS/CALP acquisition "was suggested as typical in the specific situation of immigrant children learning a second language", not as an absolute order. He cites Garcia's example of a scientist reading a text in a language she does not speak.

> "Thus attainment of high levels of L2 CALP can precede attainment of fluent L2 BICS in certain situations."
