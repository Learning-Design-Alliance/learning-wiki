## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Nusinovici Simon 2018

Nusinovici Simon, Olliac Bertrand, Flamant Cyril, Müller Jean-Baptiste, Olivier Marion, Rouger Valérie, Gascoin Géraldine, Basset Hélène, Bouvard Charlotte, Rozé Jean-Christophe, Hanf Matthieu. (2018). Impact of parental separation or divorce on school performance in preterm children: A population-based study. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0202080

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Matched cohort quantile regression results comparing the two separation-timing groups (n = 104 before two years; n = 164 between 3 and 5 years) against children whose parents were still living together (n = 2,439). The article states "only separations that occurred between three and five years were associated with decreased school performance".

> "Moreover, only separations that occurred between three and five years were associated with decreased school performance of children at five years of age. For term infants, the same result was found regarding the age at separation."

### Nusinovici Simon 2018 (2)

Nusinovici Simon, Olliac Bertrand, Flamant Cyril, Müller Jean-Baptiste, Olivier Marion, Rouger Valérie, Gascoin Géraldine, Basset Hélène, Bouvard Charlotte, Rozé Jean-Christophe, Hanf Matthieu. (2018). Impact of parental separation or divorce on school performance in preterm children: A population-based study. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0202080

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion of why separations before age two showed no effect; this is an explanatory claim, not a tested result, and the article offers no direct evidence for the mechanism.

> "An explanation for this could be that parental separation is unlikely to have negative consequences for children who have not yet formed a mental representation of their relationship with their parents. Moreover, these infants were likely to be too young to remember this stressful event."
