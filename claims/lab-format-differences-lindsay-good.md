## Evidence

### Marta R. Stoeckel 2020

Marta R. Stoeckel. (2020). Constructivism in Online Science Courses. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2007.07745](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2007.07745)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports this second-hand from Lindsay and Good (2005), comparing remote and hands-on lab access modes; remote-lab students were "more likely to recognize nonidealities in their data". The review attributes this to reduced attention demands of equipment manipulation.

> "Lindsay and Good (2005) found that students engaged in a remote lab were more likely to recognize nonidealities in their data than peers engaged in a comparable hands-on lab."

### Marta R. Stoeckel 2020 (2)

Marta R. Stoeckel. (2020). Constructivism in Online Science Courses. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2007.07745](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2007.07745)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Lindsay and Good (2005), that simulation-lab students were "more able to focus on the process of learning" than remote or hands-on peers, which the review links to constructivist requirements for reflection on thinking.

> "Lindsay and Good (2005) found that students who conducted a simulation lab were more able to focus on the process of learning, including asking and answering their own questions and reflecting on their thinking, than students who completed an equivalent remote or hands-on lab."
