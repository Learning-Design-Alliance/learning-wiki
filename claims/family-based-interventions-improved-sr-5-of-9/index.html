Narrative review of 9 interventions in which parents and/or siblings were an important part of the intervention to enhance SR. The review reports "Family-based interventions were successful in bringing a consistent change in SR measures in intervention groups in 5 of the 9 studies (56%)."

Related Claims¶

  • Social and personal skills interventions were highly effective, with only 2 of 6 studies (33%) reporting a small and statistically insignificant change. — related
  • Some interventions brought more improvements in self-regulation measures of those with lower baseline scores, offering promise for resource-constrained settings. — related
  • Universal self-regulation interventions improve self-regulation in children and adolescents, with a pooled effect size of d = 0.42 (95% CI, 0.32-0.53) on task performance scores. — related
  • Exercise-based interventions improved self-regulation in 4 of 6 interventions (67%), significant on conventional tests of statistical significance. — related
  • Interventions with high schoolers and young adults show strong consistent improvement in cognitive regulation and small but significant improvements in health, mental health, and delinquency — related
  • Social-emotional learning benefits persist at follow-up — related
  • A practice brief reviewing self-regulation interventions reports substantial benefits of mindfulness programs for cognitive and emotional regulation, stress and mental health in adolescents and young adults — related
  • Mindfulness and yoga interventions improved self-regulation in 4 of 8 interventions (50%), mostly applied within the adolescent age group. — related
