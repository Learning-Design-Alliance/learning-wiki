# Within-domain emotions are differentiated along valence and activation, but anger relates more strongly to boredom than to anxiety, with boredom–anger correlations of .81 to .90

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Latent within-domain emotion factors were clearly differentiated, with negative relations between positive and negative emotions, but anger clustered with boredom (r = .81 to .90) and enjoyment related strongly negatively to boredom (r = −.76 to −.91). [→ Goetz et al. 2007](#goetz-et-al-2007)

## Evidence

### Goetz et al. 2007

Thomas Goetz, Anne C. Frenzel, Reinhard Pekrun, Nathan C. Hall, and Oliver Lüdtke. (2007). Between- and Within-Domain Relations of Students' Academic Emotions. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.99.4.715

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

SEM analysis of within-domain latent emotion factors in four domains for both grade samples (542 students). All positive–negative emotion correlations were negative; positive–positive and negative–negative correlations were positive. Printed effect sizes: boredom–anger r = .81 to .90 and enjoyment–boredom r = −.76 to −.91, large correlations.

> "Conspicuously strong correlations were found between boredom and anger (range /H11005 .81 to .90, across domains and grade levels) and between enjoyment and boredom (range /H11005/H11002 .76 to /H11002.91, across domains and grade levels)."
