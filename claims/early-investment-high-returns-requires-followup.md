## Evidence

### Cunha 2007

Flavio Cunha and James Heckman. (2007). The Technology of Skill Formation. NBER Working Paper No. 12840. http://www.nber.org/papers/w12840

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The paper summarizes the intervention literature (Barnett 2004, CHLM) showing high economic returns for early remedial investment in disadvantaged young children, which it attributes to dynamic complementarity and self-productivity in its technology.

> "Third, despite the low returns to interventions targeted toward disadvantaged adolescents, the empirical literature shows high economic returns for remedial investments in young disadvantaged children. See W. Steven Barnett (2004), the evidence in CHLM and the papers they cite."

### Cunha 2007 (2)

Flavio Cunha and James Heckman. (2007). The Technology of Skill Formation. NBER Working Paper No. 12840. http://www.nber.org/papers/w12840

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The paper cites Currie and Thomas (1995) on Head Start fadeout among minority participants who return to disadvantaged environments. The paper interprets this as evidence that investments are complementary and require follow-up to remain effective.

> "Currie and Duncan Thomas (1995) document a decline in the performance of Head Start minority participants after they leave the program, return to disadvantaged environments, and receive the low levels of investment experienced by many disadvantaged children."
