# Teachers perceive their role shifting from knowledge transmitters to facilitators, co-investigators, and ethical supervisors of AI use

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` With AI handling routine questions and feedback, teachers report becoming facilitators and co-investigators who guide deeper inquiry. [→ Li 2025](#li-2025)
`q2 i?` Teachers also take on a supervisory role fostering ethical, honest, and verifiable AI use. [→ Li 2025 (2)](#li-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Li 2025

Li, S. (2025). Artificial Intelligence in Science Learning within the Framework of Situated Learning Theory: A Qualitative Investigation of Teachers' Perspectives. Creative Education, 16, 1858-1882. https://doi.org/10.4236/ce.2025.1611114

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

RQ2 theme on transforming teacher-student interaction and pedagogical roles. One teacher noted AI teaching assistants answer basic questions, freeing time to help struggling students, and teachers monitored group progress in real time.

> "Rather than serving merely as transmitters of knowledge, teachers became facilitators, questioners, and co -investigators"

### Li 2025 (2)

Li, S. (2025). Artificial Intelligence in Science Learning within the Framework of Situated Learning Theory: A Qualitative Investigation of Teachers' Perspectives. Creative Education, 16, 1858-1882. https://doi.org/10.4236/ce.2025.1611114

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

RQ2 theme establishing the teacher as supervisor and ethicist of AI use. Teachers required students to mark AI-generated report sections, used cross-validation and retained AI dialogue records, and awarded an "AI Integrity Star".

> "Many teachers designed explicit activities to foster ethical awareness— such as conducting "AI ethics lessons," signing usage pledges, or showcasing model cases of responsi ble use"
