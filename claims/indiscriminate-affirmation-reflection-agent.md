# The agent's praise was indiscriminate affirmation: validation followed about half of participant turns regardless of what the participant said

### validation-content

Subigya K. Nepal, Serena Soh, Noah Vinoya, SoHyun Park, Mahnaz Roshanaei, and Gabriella Harari. 2027. Faithful Where It Can Be Checked: Auditing a Reflection Agent Against Its System Prompt in a Randomized Trial. In Proceedings of CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI '27). ACM, New York, NY, USA. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19635 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.19635](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2609.19635)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Study 2 turn-level analysis testing whether praise was automatic, as the prompt's wording invites. The authors call the pattern indiscriminate affirmation and note participants described it as comfort, e.g. feeling "more confident and self affirmed on my desired career path".

> "Whether the participant was exploring options, describing a barrier or a hard day, revising a plan or typing a minimal answer under ten words, praise followed about half the time (between 48% and 57%), and it peaked at 67% after the participant committed to something."
