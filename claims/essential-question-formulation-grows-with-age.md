## Evidence

### Squire 1912

Squire, C. R. (1912). Graded Mental Tests. Part I. Attention, Perception, Comprehension and Memory. The Journal of Educational Psychology, III(7). https://www.jstor.org/journal/jeducpsych

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

In the question-formulation form of the picture test, questions were classified as irrelevant, minor, or essential. The author reports it is "more difficult than to find an appropriate title" with "numerous failures to frame any kind of a question", especially among six-year-olds.

> "To formulate in a pertinent question the main thought of a picture is more difficult than to find an appropriate title for it. There were numerous failures to frame any kind of a question."

### Squire 1912 (2)

Squire, C. R. (1912). Graded Mental Tests. Part I. Attention, Perception, Comprehension and Memory. The Journal of Educational Psychology, III(7). https://www.jstor.org/journal/jeducpsych

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Table IV shows essential questions rising from 0 percent at age six to 58 percent at age thirteen, while failures and irrelevant questions decline; the author describes "a fairly steady growth in the ability to frame essential questions".

> "There appears to be a fairly steady growth in the ability to frame essential questions through the remaining years."
