## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a nationally representative US adolescent sample, a spline-modeled physical activity ANOVA revealed a significant curvilinear effect of physical activity on academic achievement, with optimal grades at 5–7 days/week rather than at guideline adherence. ## Evidence

### Brown Denver M. Y. 2022

Brown Denver M. Y., Porter Carah, Hamilton Faith, Almanza Fernanda, Narvid Christina, Pish Megan, Arizabalo Diego. (2022). Interactive Associations between Physical Activity and Sleep Duration in Relation to Adolescent Academic Achievement. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph192315604

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional analysis of 2019 YRBSS data from 13,677 US adolescents in grades 9–12, using linear regression fit with cubic splines and adjusted for grade, race/ethnicity, sex, weight status, and screen time. The spline ANOVA was significant, "F(3,34) = 6.13, p < 0.001". The discussion reports optimal grades at 5–7 days/week of activity.

> "Results from our physical activity ANOVA model revealed a significant effect of physical activity on academic achievement (F(3,34) = 6.13, p < 0.001)."
