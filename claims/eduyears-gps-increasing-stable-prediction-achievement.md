# EduYears polygenic scores predict achievement increasingly with age (about 4% at KS1 to 10% at GCSE) and show largely stable effects, with less than 1% age-specific prediction

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` EduYears GPS prediction of core achievement rises across school years: about 4% at KS1, 6% at KS2, 8% at KS3, and 10% at GCSE. [→ Rimfeld 2018](#rimfeld-2018)
`q3 i?` GPS prediction is largely stable from age to age, with little (<1%) age-specific prediction once prediction from all previous ages is accounted for. [→ Rimfeld 2018 (2)](#rimfeld-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Rimfeld 2018

Rimfeld, K., Malanchini, M., Krapohl, E., Hannigan, L. J., Dale, P. S. & Plomin, R. (2018). The stability of educational achievement across school years is largely explained by genetic factors. npj Science of Learning 3:16. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0030-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Polygenic score (EduYears GPS, derived from a GWA of years of education) regression analyses in the TEDS genotyped sample; delta R2 variance explained rises from "about 4% for KS1" to "10% for GCSE". Variance explained, not a standardised effect size.

> "Edu- Years GPS increasingly predicted core educational achievement — about 4% for KS1, 6% for KS2, 8% for KS3, and 10% for GCSE."

### Rimfeld 2018 (2)

Rimfeld, K., Malanchini, M., Krapohl, E., Hannigan, L. J., Dale, P. S. & Plomin, R. (2018). The stability of educational achievement across school years is largely explained by genetic factors. npj Science of Learning 3:16. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0030-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Age-specificity regression analyses of EduYears GPS prediction across the four ages; after accounting for prediction from all preceding ages, the article prints "little (<1%)" age-specific genetic prediction, indicating stable GPS effects. No standardised effect size is printed.

> "our regression analyses indicated little (<1%) age-speciﬁc genetic prediction once the stable prediction of EduYears GPS from all previous ages was taken into account."
