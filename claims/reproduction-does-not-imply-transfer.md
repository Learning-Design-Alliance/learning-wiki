## Evidence

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2012

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2012). Using an Isomorphic Problem Pair to Learn Introductory Physics: Transferring from a Two-step Problem to a Three-step Problem. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.9.020114](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.9.020114)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of intervention 1 students' scores on the isomorphic snowboard problem reproduced immediately after browsing its solution; the same students' quiz (transfer) problem scores dropped by 3.2 and 3.9 points out of 10 in the calculus-based and algebra-based courses.

> "the calculus-based and algebra-based students achieved an average score of 9.6 and 8.3 out of 10, respectively, on the snowboard problem. These results suggest that both algebra-based and calculus-based students were generally good at reproducing the solved problem they just learned from"

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2010

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2010). Using Analogy to Solve a Three-Step Physics Problem. PERC Proceedings. [doi:10.1063/1.3515228](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3515228)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Table 5 reports intervention 1 students' average scores on the solved snowboard problem immediately after learning from it versus the putty quiz problem, in both courses. The article calls the reproduction score “somewhat superficial when it comes to transfer.”

> "Although students performed well on the snowboard problem when they were asked to reproduce it, this score turned out to be somewhat superficial when it comes to transfer. An average drop of 3.2 and 3.8 out of 10 on the putty problem were found in the calculus and algebra-based courses, respectively."
