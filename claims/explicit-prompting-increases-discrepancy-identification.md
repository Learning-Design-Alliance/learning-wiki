## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students in the transformed course were more likely than students in the traditional course to identify the input impedance of the meter as the source of the prediction-measurement discrepancy (100% vs 83%, p < 0.01). ## Evidence

### H. J. Lewandowski and Noah Finkelstein 2015

H. J. Lewandowski and Noah Finkelstein. (2015). Redesigning a junior-level electronics course to support engagement in scientific practices. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.043](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.043)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of two cohorts (traditional N = 47, transformed N = 44) taught one year apart by the same instructor, coding student write-ups of the week-2 voltage-divider lab. Table I reports "Identiﬁed discrepancy" 83% traditional vs 100% transformed, "p < 0.01 by measure of binomial sampling."

> "The table shows the percentage of stu- dents who correctly identiﬁed the input impedance of the me- ter as the source of the discrepancy and the percentage that re- fined the model to include this impedance to make a predic- tion."
