# Students instructed to prepare in collaborative dyads during the self-study phase of an inverted classroom achieve higher knowledge-test scores than students instructed to prepare individually

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2019 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Collaborative-group students scored significantly higher than both basic and individual groups on combined conceptual and conditional knowledge tests (both p < 0.00001). [→ Kühl Susanne J. 2019](#kuhl-susanne-j-2019)

## Evidence

### Kühl Susanne J. 2019

Kühl Susanne J., Schneider Achim, Kestler Hans A., Toberer Matthias, Kühl Michael, Fischer Martin R. (2019). Investigating the self-study phase of an inverted biochemistry classroom – collaborative dyadic learning makes the difference. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1497-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental comparison of three assigned study groups (basic n=42, individual n=76, collaborative n=78) in a biochemistry seminar; formative tests at the start of on-site phases. The collaborative group beat both others, with "both p < 0.00001"; no effect size printed.

> "students in the collaborative group experienced a significantly higher acquisition of knowledge, divided equally between both conceptual and conditional knowledge, compared to students in the basic and individual groups (Fig. 2a; both p < 0.00001)"
