Karageorgiou Athina, Papastergiou Eleni, Tsanakas John, Kirvassilis Fotios, Desli Evangelia, Talimtzi Persefoni, Fydantsi Aikaterini, Laguia Eleni, Demiri Maria, Hatziagorou Elpis. (2026). Structured Education Enhances Inhaler Technique and Asthma Management Skills of Health Care Providers in Pediatric Emergency Departments. Pediatric Pulmonology. https://doi.org/10.1002/ppul.71803

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The article reports, citing the review by Plaza et al., that only 15.5% of 6304 health care professionals across 55 studies exhibited correct inhaler technique. This is second-hand evidence attributed to the cited review, not a study conducted in this article.

> "In a systematic review of 55 various clinical studies from low, middle, and high‐income countries, with 6304 different HCP, only 15.5% of them exhibited correct IT, and this percentage decreased over time from 20.5% during 1975–1995 to 10.8% during 1996–2014."

### De Miguel-Díez Javier 2026

De Miguel-Díez Javier, Giner Donaire Jordi, Gómez Sáenz José Tomas, Trigueros Carrero Juan Antonio, Artigas Feliu Remei, Cruz Culebra Núria, Plaza Vicente. (2026). Opinion and Knowledge of Pulmonologists, Primary Care Physicians, and Nurses About Inhaled Therapy in COPD. Inhal-Epoc Survey. International Journal of Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease. https://doi.org/10.2147/COPD.S499279

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The review reports, citing its reference 30 (Plaza et al., systematic review), that healthcare professionals demonstrated inadequate knowledge of proper inhaler use, with technique "considered correct in 15.5% of cases" and declining over the two periods. Second-hand attribution; the underlying review was not read.

> "The inhaler technique was considered correct in 15.5% of cases, decreasing over time from 20.5% from the early period (defined as 1975–1995) to 10.8% (95% CI, 7.3–14.8) during the late period (1996–2014)."
