## Evidence

### Civitillo 2025

Civitillo, S., S. Glock, L. A. Naudszus, L. Venhoff, D. Lieck, and P. Jugert. (2025). Investigating Preservice Teachers' Competence to Notice Ethnic Microaggressions in the Classroom. Journal of Community Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1002/jcop.70056

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r3`

Preregistered between-subject vignette experiment (Study 1, N = 147 German preservice teachers) with a 2x3 mixed ANOVA; the omnibus main effect of ambiguity was F(2, 143) = 338.02, p < 0.001, ηp² = 0.825. Planned comparisons showed "significantly more microaggressions in the low‐ambiguity condition" than high-ambiguity (d = 1.46) and control in both vignette sets.

> "In the school performance vignettes, participants perceived significantly more microaggressions in the low‐ambiguity condition (M = 5.87, SD = 0.86) than in the high‐ambiguity condition (M = 4.57, SD = 1.32), t(72) = −8.137, p < 0.001, d = 1.46."
