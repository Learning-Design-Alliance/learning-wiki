# Only 13 of 40 school weeks are reliably focused on grade-level curriculum, and best-case students receive about 540 annual instruction hours

## Evidence

### BetsAnn Smith 1998

BetsAnn Smith. (1998). It's About Time: Opportunities to Learn in Chicago's Elementary Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/its-about-time-opportunities-learn

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

From teacher interviews and observations, the report identifies 13 reliably focused weeks in a 40-week year, with other weeks disconnected by special and bad days and seasonal cycles such as leveling and year-end slowdowns.

> "there are two good periods for their most important and challenging instructional work: the seven weeks between October 1 and November 20, and the six weeks from approximately January 15 to March 1. In a 40-week school year, this adds up to 13 weeks of schooling that is reliably and continuously focused on teaching the grade-level curriculum"

### BetsAnn Smith 1998 (2)

BetsAnn Smith. (1998). It's About Time: Opportunities to Learn in Chicago's Elementary Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/its-about-time-opportunities-learn

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Figure 5 charts annual instructional hours: the allocated year is 900 hours, the well-managed best case about 540 hours, and poorly managed classrooms about half the recommended hours; the report estimates nearly half of Chicago students fall into the latter category.

> "This best case scenario suggests that students received about 540 hours of instruction (see Figure 5). For comparison purposes, we also calculate the experiences of students who attend classrooms that are not well managed (with a 30 percent level of non-instruction and with 10 percent more lost time for special days and a few bad days across the school year). Such Chicago students receive about half the recommended hours of instruction."
