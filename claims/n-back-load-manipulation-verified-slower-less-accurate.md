## Evidence

### Marta Isabel Garrido 2016

Marta Isabel Garrido, Chee Leong James Teng, Jeremy Alexander Taylor, Elise Genevieve Rowe and Jason Brett Mattingley. (2016). Surprise responses in the human brain demonstrate statistical learning under high concurrent cognitive demand. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/npjscilearn.2016.6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Behavioral check in Experiment 2 with 17 included participants performing a visual N-back task (1-back vs 2-back) while ignoring tone streams. Both slowing, F(1,16) = 14.91, P = 0.001, and accuracy costs, F(1,16) = 29.89, P = 5.17×10−5, "verif[ied] the effectiveness of the cognitive load manipulation".

> "participants were signi ﬁcantly slower (F(1,16) = 14.91, P = 0.001) and less accurate (F(1,16) = 29.89, P = 5.17 × 10− 5) in the high-load than in the low-load condition, regardless of contextual variance, thus verifying the effectiveness of the cognitive load manipulation"
