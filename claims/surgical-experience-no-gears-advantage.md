## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Residents and Fellows did not differ significantly in GEARS overall score, and the authors suggest that increased general surgical experience may not correlate significantly with learning basic robotic surgical skills. [→ Wang Rong 2025](#wang-rong-2025)

## Evidence

### Wang Rong 2025

Wang Rong, Zheng Nan, Liang Yujing, Cui Haoxin, Ren Tong, Xing Wenhui, Li Yanghui. (2025). Multi-perspective analysis of daVinci surgical virtual reality training: a prospective randomized controlled study. Journal of Robotic Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11701-025-02309-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Comparison of GEARS overall scores between Residents (≤ 5 years' experience) and Fellows (> 5 years) in the randomized controlled study; no significant difference was detected, and equivalence was not tested.

> "We found that surgeons with more experience had higher GEARS scores, but the difference was not significant. Therefore, basic robotic surgical skills differ from conventional skills, and increased experience in general surgical operations may not have a significant correlation with the learning basic robotic surgical skills."
