## Evidence

### Gehlbach 2018

Gehlbach, H., & Vriesema, C. C. (2018). Meta‐bias: A practical theory of motivated thinking. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9454-6

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

In its limitations section the article reports, citing Kruger (1999), that students systematically rate themselves below average in hard domains, apparently against the self-enhancement motive; the authors argue this may instead serve cognitive efficiency via an easier proxy question. The article also notes hostile attribution bias and cultural variation as open challenges.

> "However, Kruger (1999) also describes a “below-average” effect in which he asks students about domains that are generally perceived to be hard—e.g., chess, juggling, and computer programming—and finds that they systematically rate themselves below the average of their peers."
