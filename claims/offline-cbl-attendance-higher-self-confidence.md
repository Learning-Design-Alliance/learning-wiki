# Attending offline CBL classes is associated with significantly higher self-confidence, particularly in collaboration, communication, and critical thinking

## Evidence

### Zhi Fei-Hang 2025

Zhi Fei-Hang, Liu Wei, Zhang Xinyao, Zhang Tao, Su Yuhan, Huang Ruoxuan, Feng Yan-Fen, Lei Yi-Yan. (2025). Exploration of CBL combined with “Metronomic” online classroom in thoracic trauma and closed chest drainage teaching. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08427-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Comparison of seven students who missed the offline class (two CBL, five CBL+MOC) with the 36 attendees. Attendance was “associated with significantly higher self-confidence scores” at p = 0.01; attendance was not randomized, so this is associational.

> "In contrast, participation in offline CBL classes was associated with significantly higher self-confidence scores (65.63 ± 16.31) compared with non-participation (50.43 ± 11.50; p = 0.01)."

### Zhi Fei-Hang 2025 (2)

Zhi Fei-Hang, Liu Wei, Zhang Xinyao, Zhang Tao, Su Yuhan, Huang Ruoxuan, Feng Yan-Fen, Lei Yi-Yan. (2025). Exploration of CBL combined with “Metronomic” online classroom in thoracic trauma and closed chest drainage teaching. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08427-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subscale comparison between attendees and non-attendees on the six-domain confidence questionnaire. Three domains favored attendees significantly; creativity and innovation (p = 0.13), self-direction (p = 0.11), and technology use (p = 0.10) showed no significant differences.

> "Subscale analysis further revealed that students attending offline classes scored significantly higher in collaboration (p = 0.02), communication (p = 9.40E − 03), and critical thinking (p = 2.50E − 03)."
