# The ability confound hypothesis for normative goal-achievement links is unsupported

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` In 24 survey studies, baseline ability correlates weakly with normative goals (mean r = .10), and 94% of studies controlling for ability or competence perceptions found the normative goal-achievement link intact. [→ Senko 2011](#senko-2011)

## Evidence

### Senko 2011

Senko, C., Hulleman, C. S., & Harackiewicz, J. M. (2011). Achievement Goal Theory at the Crossroads: Old Controversies, Current Challenges, and New Directions. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2011.538646

`q3 · i1` · `review · r2`

The review's tabulation of 24 peer-reviewed survey studies (Tables 1 and 2) shows "mean r = .10" for ability with normative goals and "mean r = .39" for competence perceptions with mastery goals, belying the confound logic.

> "Baseline ability actually correlates only weakly with normative goals (mean r = .10) and mastery goals (mean r = .08) alike. Competence perceptions do correlate modestly with normative goals (mean r = .27) but they correlate even more strongly with mastery goals (mean r = .39)"

### Senko 2011 (2)

Senko, C., Hulleman, C. S., & Harackiewicz, J. M. (2011). Achievement Goal Theory at the Crossroads: Old Controversies, Current Challenges, and New Directions. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2011.538646

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports that of 18 studies statistically controlling for baseline ability or competence perceptions, "All but one (94%) found that the relationship remained intact".

> "Additionally, 18 of these 24 studies also tested the normative goal link with achievement while statistically controlling for baseline ability or competence perceptions. All but one (94%) found that the relationship remained intact."
