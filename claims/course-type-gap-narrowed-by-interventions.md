## Evidence

### Lin 2016

Lin, S.-Y. and Singh, C. (2016). Using Analogies to Learn Introductory Physics. Department of Physics and Astronomy, University of Pittsburgh. Originally published 2008. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06388](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06388)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Cross-course comparison of average quiz scores (Tables 2 and 3). The article reports calculus-based students "performed better than the algebra-based students in all three groups" and that the gap "decreaseed" after the interventions, attributing it partly to initial knowledge differences.

> "Table 2 and 3 also show that the calculus-based students on average performed better than the algebra-based students in all three groups, regardless of the scaffoldings they received. The difference between the score of the calculus- and algebra-based students decreaseed after the interventions were implemented."
