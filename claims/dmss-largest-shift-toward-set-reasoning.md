## Evidence

### H. J. Lewandowski 2018

H. J. Lewandowski, Robert Hobbs, Jacob T. Stanley, Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer, and Benjamin Pollard (2018). Student reasoning about measurement uncertainty in an introductory lab course. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/perc.2017.pr.056](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.056)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pre-post Mann-Whitney U-test comparisons of S-coded PMQ responses per probe. The article reports "a statistically significant difference for DMSS (p ≪ 0.01)" with the non-significant p values for RD, UR, and SMDS as printed; no effect size is printed.

> "for whether the responses were coded as S, we see a statistically signiﬁcant diﬀerence for DMSS ( p ≪ 0.01), but not for RD (p = 0.37), UR (p = 0.16), and SMDS (p = 0.09)."

### H. J. Lewandowski 2018 (2)

H. J. Lewandowski, Robert Hobbs, Jacob T. Stanley, Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer, and Benjamin Pollard (2018). Student reasoning about measurement uncertainty in an introductory lab course. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/perc.2017.pr.056](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.056)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Results-section description of the DMSS probe (different means, same spread). The article states "The shifts for DMSS are markedly larger than the other three probes" and that DMSS starts with a smaller fraction of S and a larger fraction of P than SMDS; no effect size is printed.

> "The shifts for DMSS are markedly larger than the other three probes, bringing the DMSS post-test fractions of P and S to roughly the same levels as the SMDS probe after instruction."
