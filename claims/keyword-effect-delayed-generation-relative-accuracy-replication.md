# Generating keywords after a delay increases relative judgment accuracy compared with no keyword generation, replicating the keyword effect with a new sample and new materials

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` University students who generated five keywords per text after a delay ranked their comprehension across four texts more accurately than control students who had a break instead. [→ Waldeyer 2021](#waldeyer-2021)

## Evidence

### Waldeyer 2021

Waldeyer, J., & Roelle, J. (2021). The keyword effect: A conceptual replication, effects on bias, and an optimization. Metacognition and Learning, 16, 37–56. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-020-09235-7

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Contrast analysis in the between-subject experiment (N = 108 university students reading four expository texts; keyword conditions contrasted against control, weights 1/1/−2) on relative accuracy from JOLT1 and posttest. The keyword groups "reached higher relative accuracy ... than the control group", t(93) = 2.06, p = .021 (one-tailed), ηp2 = .04.
