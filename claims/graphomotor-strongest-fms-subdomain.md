# Graphomotor skills show the strongest links of the FMS subdomains with academic-cognitive skills, significantly exceeding dexterity

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Among FMS subdomains, graphomotor skills (r = .338) showed the strongest links with academic-cognitive skills, significantly greater than dexterity (p < .001) and FMS miscellaneous (p = .027), but not significantly different from speed, bimanual, general FMS, or finger gnosia. [→ Sebastian P. Suggate 2025](#sebastian-p-suggate-2025)

## Evidence

### Sebastian P. Suggate 2025

Sebastian P. Suggate, Viktoria L. Karle, Tanja Kipfelsberger, Heidrun Stoeger. (2025). Keep the hands in mind: A meta-analysis of correlations between fine motor skills and reading, writing, mathematics, and cognitive development in children and adolescents. Educational Research Review 49 (2025) 100748. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2025.100748

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Post-hoc ANOVA comparisons within the multilevel meta-regression of FMS subdomains (Table 1: graphomotor r = .338, dexterity r = .289). The article reports "the effect sizes for graphomotor skills were greater compared to dexterity (p < .001)".

> "the effect sizes for graphomotor skills were greater compared to dexterity (p < .001), general FMS (p < .001) and FMS miscellaneous (p = .027), but not significantly different to speed (p = .173), bimanual (p = .095), general FMS (p = .099), or finger gnosia (p = .336)."
