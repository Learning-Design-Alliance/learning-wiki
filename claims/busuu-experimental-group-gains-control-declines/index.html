Evidence¶

Kétyi 2015¶

Kétyi, A. (2015). Practical evaluation of a mobile language learning tool in higher education. Critical CALL – Proceedings of the 2015 EUROCALL Conference, Padova, Italy. http://dx.doi.org/10.14705/rpnet.2015.000350

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Eight-week quasi-experiment with 94 volunteer Budapest Business School students in four languages, self-selected into experimental (51) and control (43) groups; language tests at pre- and post-measurement. The article reports the experimental group "increased their performance (+2.2%)" while the control group decreased, with a significant post difference.

"the experimental group increased their performance (+2.2%) while the control group decreased it (-3.1 %), and the difference at the post measurement became statistically significant (p=.013)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Female students in the experimental group performed significantly better than male students at post-measurement (p=.032) — related
  • Actual app use was sporadic and short — about once per week for 10–15 minutes, roughly 120 minutes total — which the authors say cannot explain the significant difference between groups — related
  • Students found busuu easy and useful especially for vocabulary and writing, but 73.3% thought it helps without being the ultimate tool, and language learning apps were largely unknown to them — related
  • Experimental group motivation was significantly higher in 3 of 5 sub-categories, but no correlation was found between test results and motivation — related
