## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Higher financial scarcity increases the probability of dropping out in the first year of tertiary vocational education, while lower perceived family wealth does not directly predict dropout. [→ Finkenauer 2023](#finkenauer-2023)

## Evidence

### Finkenauer 2023

Finkenauer, C., Boer, M., Spitzer, J., Weinberg, D., Visser, K., Jonker, M. & Stevens, G. W. J. M. (2023). Examining the role of civic attitudes in the link between family wealth and school dropout among tertiary vocational students. npj Science of Learning 8:38. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00189-4

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Logistic regression models (M1a-c) in the pre-registered study of 1,231 Dutch vocational students tested three family wealth indicators against officially reported dropout. Financial scarcity showed a significant association (B = 0.358, p = 0.015, OR = 1.430 in Table 3), and "lower levels of perceived family wealth did not" predict dropout.

> "In our univariate models (M1a-c), lower levels of family afﬂuence and higher levels of ﬁnancial scarcity increased adolescents' probability of dropping out of school, but lower levels of perceived family wealth did not (Table 3)."
