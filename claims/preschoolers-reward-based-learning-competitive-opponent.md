# Preschoolers' Chicken game choices are best explained by reward-based learning with a higher learning rate for the competitive opponent

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among hierarchical Bayesian RL models, a simple reward-based model with separate learning rates per opponent best fit preschoolers' trial-by-trial choices. [→ Zhang 2026](#zhang-2026)
`q2 i?` Preschoolers updated more strongly from outcomes when facing the high-competitive opponent. [→ Zhang 2026 (2)](#zhang-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Zhang 2026

Zhang, R., Zhang, L., Hao, X.-M., Dreher, J.-C., & Qu, C. (2026). Learning to adaptively cooperate through social interactions during childhood and adolescence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00423-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Hierarchical Bayesian computational modeling of Study 1 choices; LOOIC comparison favored the reward-based two-learning-rate model with preference parameter, and posterior predictive checks showed high trial-level correlations (rLCO = 0.86, rHCO = 0.85).

> "Further model comparison revealed that M1 -3 was the best-fitting model (LOOIC = 4564.48), which posited that children's cooperative behaviors were based on the current reward, following a simple RL rule, and that children had different learning rates for different opponents."

### Zhang 2026 (2)

Zhang, R., Zhang, L., Hao, X.-M., Dreher, J.-C., & Qu, C. (2026). Learning to adaptively cooperate through social interactions during childhood and adolescence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00423-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Group-level hyperparameters of the winning model: mean learning rate for HCO was 0.32 versus 0.20 for LCO; a Bayesian Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test found a significant difference (BF₁₀ > 100).

> "Preschoolers showed higher learning rates when interacting with HCO (M = 0.32, SD = 0.11, 95% HDI = [0.105, 0.561]) than with LCO (M = 0.20, SD = 0.15, 95% HDI = [0.2×10-4, 0.519])."
