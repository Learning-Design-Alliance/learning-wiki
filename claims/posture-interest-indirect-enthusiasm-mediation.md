# Instructor body posture shows no direct effects on students' situational interest, but indirect-only mediation effects through perceived enthusiasm

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` In the preregistered experiment, neither posture dimension nor their interaction significantly affected situational interest directly. [→ Traulsen 2024](#traulsen-2024)
`q3 i?` The vertical posture dimension affected situational interest indirectly through perceived enthusiasm, with no total effect. [→ Traulsen 2024 (2)](#traulsen-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Traulsen 2024

Traulsen, S. J., & Zander, L. (2024). Straighten your back, open your arms! Effects of instructor's body postures in educational videos on students' interest and motivation. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.101959

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r3`

Preregistered experiment with 434 students; 2×2 ANOVAs on situational interest (α = .87) found no significant main or interaction effects (all partial η2 ≤ .009), and contrast comparisons showed no significant group differences.

> "Our analyses revealed no significant treatment influences on situational interest for the vertical dimension, F (1, 430) = 3.696, p = .055, partial η2 = .009, the horizontal dimension, F (1, 430) = 0.988, p = .321, partial η2 = .002, and the interaction of both dimensions, F (1, 430) = 2.367, p = .125, partial η2 = .005."

### Traulsen 2024 (2)

Traulsen, S. J., & Zander, L. (2024). Straighten your back, open your arms! Effects of instructor's body postures in educational videos on students' interest and motivation. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.101959

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Bootstrapped mediation analysis (PROCESS, 5000 iterations) in the preregistered experiment: no total effect (B = 0.167, p = .053), but a significant indirect effect ab = 0.118, 95%-CI [0.028, 0.213] through perceived enthusiasm; no standardised effect size is printed.

> "Thus, while the predicted total effect was not found, the effect of the experimental variations on the vertical dimension affected students' interests indirectly mediated by perceived enthusiasm."
