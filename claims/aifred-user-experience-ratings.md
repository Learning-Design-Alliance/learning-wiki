# AIfred users report higher perceived learning support, innovation, satisfaction, and cognitive demand, with no difference in perceived productivity

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Perceived learning support was higher for AIfred (4.5/5 vs. 3.4/5, p<.001), as were innovation (4.5 vs. 3.5, p<.001) and user satisfaction (4.2 vs. 3.5, p=.04). [→ Gregorio Orlando 2026](#gregorio-orlando-2026)
`q2 i?` Cognitive demand trended higher with AIfred (3.4/5 vs. 2.9/5, p=.03), while perceived productivity was statistically indistinguishable (4.1/5 vs. 4.0/5, p=.61). [→ Gregorio Orlando 2026 (2)](#gregorio-orlando-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Gregorio Orlando 2026

Gregorio Orlando, Milan Groshev, Eduardo Castelló Ferrer. (2026). AIfred: Augmented Learning through Functional Robotic Embodiment at the Desk. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38737

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-experiment Likert (1-5) questionnaires in the n=36 study showed AIfred "scored higher than ChatGPT in perceived learning support (4.5/5 vs. 3.4/5;p < .001)"; innovation and satisfaction also favored AIfred. Only p-values and means are printed.

> "AIfred scored higher than ChatGPT in perceived learning support (4.5/5 vs. 3.4/5;p < .001)"

### Gregorio Orlando 2026 (2)
