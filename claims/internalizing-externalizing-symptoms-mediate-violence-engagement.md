# Externalizing and internalizing symptoms, but not posttraumatic stress symptoms, mediate the association between community violence exposure and later school engagement

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Externalizing symptoms fully mediated and internalizing symptoms partially mediated the community violence to school engagement relationship. [→ Borofsky Larissa A. 2013](#borofsky-larissa-a-2013)

## Evidence

### Borofsky Larissa A. 2013

Borofsky Larissa A., Kellerman Ilana, Baucom Brian, Oliver Pamella H., Margolin Gayla. (2013). Community Violence Exposure and Adolescents' School Engagement and Academic Achievement Over Time. Psychology of Violence. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0034121

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical multiple regression with 118 adolescents testing symptom mediators. Time 1 community violence related to time 2 school engagement (β = −.27, p = .003), to externalizing (β = .39, p < .001) and internalizing (β = .25, p = .006); indirect-effect CIs were −.04, −.01 (externalizing) and −.02, −.0003 (internalizing).

> "Finally, externalizing symptoms were found to significantly mediate the relationship between time 1 community violence exposure and time 2 school engagement, such that community violence and school engagement were no longer significantly related once externalizing symptoms were included in the model. Internalizing symptoms also reduced this relationship and were found to be a significant partial mediator."

### Borofsky Larissa A. 2013 (2)

Borofsky Larissa A., Kellerman Ilana, Baucom Brian, Oliver Pamella H., Margolin Gayla. (2013). Community Violence Exposure and Adolescents' School Engagement and Academic Achievement Over Time. Psychology of Violence. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0034121

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the same regression series, adolescent-reported posttraumatic stress symptoms (Youth Symptom Survey Checklist, α = .89) related to community violence but "were not significantly correlated with school engagement", so mediation was not tested.

> "Posttraumatic stress symptoms, although associated with community violence, β = .27, t(116) = 3.02, p = .003, were not significantly correlated with school engagement, thus mediation effects were not assessed because criteria for mediation were not met (Baron & Kenny, 1986)."
