## Evidence

### Potasznik 2026

Potasznik, Amanda, and Daniel Haehn. (2026). Measuring Changes in Instructor Class Design and Student Learning After the Release of Large Language Models (LLMs). 19th International Conference on e-Learning & Innovative Pedagogies. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2605.16284](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.16284)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Student survey correlation analysis. The article reports "Spearman r = 0.08, p = 0.76" for study hours, r = 0.03 (p = 0.91, n = 15) for GPA, and r = −0.16 (p = 0.52) for the ConCal composite — none statistically significant.

> "LLM use for graded assignments was not associated with the number of hours students reported studying (Spearman r = 0.08, p = 0.76), indicating that LLM use does not appear to meaningfully alter overall study time."

### Potasznik 2026 (2)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same student survey correlation analysis. The article reports "Spearman r = −0.32, p = 0.19" for reduced practice without AI and notes the ConCal composite showed a moderate positive relationship with GPA (r = 0.44, p = 0.10), not statistically significant.

> "For study behaviors and time use, higher self -reported LLM use was weakly associated with reduced practice without AI (Spearman r = −0.32, p = 0.19),  which suggests that  there is partial substitution between AI -assisted work and independent practice, thoug h the relationship was not statistically significant."
