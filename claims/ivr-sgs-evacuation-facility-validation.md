## Evidence

### Zhenan Feng 2018

Zhenan Feng, Vicente A. González, Robert Amor, Ruggiero Lovreglio, Guillermo Cabrera-Guerrero. (2018). Immersive Virtual Reality Serious Games for Evacuation Training and Research: A Systematic Literature Review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2018.09.002

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Ronchi et al. (2016), who placed participants in IVR SGs to test flashing-light designs at emergency exit portals and collected questionnaire recommendations on the designs. No significant preference difference was found between different numbers and layouts of portal lighting.

> "Results suggested that participants preferred green and white flashing lights rather than blue lights, at a flashing rate of 1 and 4 Hz rather than 0.25 Hz, a light emitting diode light source rather than single and double strobe lights."

### Zhenan Feng 2018 (2)

Zhenan Feng, Vicente A. González, Robert Amor, Ruggiero Lovreglio, Guillermo Cabrera-Guerrero. (2018). Immersive Virtual Reality Serious Games for Evacuation Training and Research: A Systematic Literature Review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2018.09.002

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Cosma et al. (2016), who exposed participants to a rail tunnel evacuation scenario and recorded movement paths and evacuation time to evaluate way-finding lighting installations. The null difference between the two named systems was not accompanied by an effect size.

> "Results showed that both dynamic alternate and continuous way-finding lighting systems had a positive impact on evacuation, and no significant differences were found between these two systems."
