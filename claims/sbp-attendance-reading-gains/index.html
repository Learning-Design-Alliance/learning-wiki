Curnutte Mary 2026¶

Curnutte Mary, Chapman Leah, Kesack Ashely, Afsharian Parisa, Francis Lucine, Genova Gina, Hager Erin, Cohen Juliana. (2026). School Nutrition Policies and Associations With Academic Outcomes: A Comprehensive Summary of Current Evidence. Journal of School Health. https://doi.org/10.1111/josh.70244

q3 · i? · review · r3

Review of a Wisconsin elementary school study (Bartfeld et al.) exploiting within-school variation over time; printed estimates were a 3.5%pt reduction (p = 0.015) and reading scores "0.080 SD higher" among boys (p = 0.018), with improvements greater among lower-income students.

"A linear regression found that SBP availability was associated with a reduction in the percentage of students with low attendance (3.5%pt reduction; p = 0.015) and an increase in reading test scores among boys (0.080 SD higher; p = 0.018) [32]."

Related Claims¶

  • A binding state mandate makes schools 33 percentage points more likely to offer the SBP, and SBP availability increases math, reading, and science achievement in ECLS-K data — related
  • A binding SBP mandate increases milk and fruit consumption and decreases soda consumption, but its effects on attendance are imprecise, suggesting the achievement effect operates through improved nutrition — related
  • Robust RCTs of school breakfast programs failed to find behavioral benefits, with low and variable attendance and a shift from at-home to at-school consumption limiting impact — reports the opposite
  • School breakfast programs show suggestive positive effects on academic performance, with clearest effects on mathematics and arithmetic grades in undernourished children — related
  • Each year of attending a school with a binding SBP mandate increases math achievement by 0.572 points (2.7% of a standard deviation), reading by 0.470 points (2.0%), and science by 0.130 points (0.9%) — related
  • The effect of a binding SBP mandate is larger for low-income students, males, and students from larger families, and does not vary with threshold levels from 10 to 40 percent — related
  • A binding state SBP mandate increases 4th-grade math achievement by 2.2 points (7.7% of a standard deviation) and reading by 2.0 points (5.4% of a standard deviation) in NAEP difference-in-differences estimates — related
  • In NAEP regression discontinuity estimates, exceeding the state threshold increases math achievement by 2.6 points (9.1% of a standard deviation) and reading achievement by 4.4 points (12.2% of a standard deviation) — related
