# The Train-the-Trainer model is grounded in Adult Learning Theory and Diffusion of Innovation Theory, with peer-assisted learning expected to match professional teaching

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors justify the Train-the-Trainer model by Adult Learning Theory (people remember most of what they teach others) and Diffusion of Innovation Theory (people adopt new information better through trusted social networks), and cite peer-assisted learning in medical education as comparable to professional teachers. `q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

This is the article's theoretical rationale in the Background, an author argument rather than a tested result. The article offers these theories and cited literature on peer-assisted learning as the basis for expecting Train-the-Trainer effectiveness; no data in this protocol tests them.

> "The Train-the-Trainer model of small group interactive educational training is based on Adult Learning Theory [17], which states that people who train others remember 90% of what they teach others, and on the Diffusion of Innovation Theory stating that people adopt new information better through their trusted social networks [18]."
