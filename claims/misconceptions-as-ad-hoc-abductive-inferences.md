# Student misconceptions are ad hoc abductive inferences rather than stable entertained theories

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The article argues students likely arrive at Aristotelian notions of motion through abductive inferences when facing puzzling problems, not by entertaining coherent faulty theories. [→ Bruun 2014](#bruun-2014)

## Evidence

### Bruun 2014

Bruun, J. and Christiansen, F. V. (2014). Kinesthetic activities in physics instruction: Image schematic justification and design based on didactic situations. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1410.4602](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1410.4602)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument in the section on student conceptions, drawing on diSessa's critique of stable misconceptions and on reports that nearly all students are inconsistent in applying the same concept across situations. The article offers this as an interpretation, not a new empirical test.

> "It therefore seems unlikely that students actually entertain e.g. 'Aristotelian' theories of motion and apply them to conceptual problems. Rather, they (may) arrive at 'Aristotelian' notions of motion as a result of (abductive) inferences when facing puzzling conceptual problems."
