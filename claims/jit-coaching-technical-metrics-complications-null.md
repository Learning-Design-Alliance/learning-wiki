(2024). Coaching inexperienced clinicians before a high stakes medical procedure: randomized clinical trial. The BMJ. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11648086/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary outcome comparisons in the randomized trial using GEE and median regression. The direct laryngoscopy findings were not significant, and the direct laryngoscopy grade 1 view result favored the control group; no standardized effect sizes were printed for these continuous and count outcomes.

> "The treatment group had more modified Cormack-Lehane grade 1 views (the best possible airway view) for video laryngoscopy than the control group, half the number of endotracheal tube advancement maneuvers, fewer technical difficulties during laryngoscopy, and faster intubation times (table 3, supplementary table S1)."

### Flynn Stephen G 2024 (2)

Flynn Stephen G, Park Raymond S, Jena Anupam B, Staffa Steven J, Kim Samuel Y, Clarke Julia D, Pham Ivy V, Lukovits Karina E, Huang Sheng Xiang, Sideridis Georgios D, Bernier Rachel S, Fiadjoe John E, Weinstock Peter H, Peyton James M, Stein Mary Lyn, Kovatsis Pete G. `q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Complications were categorized as severe or non-severe per Pediatric Difficult Intubation Collaborative definitions and compared between randomized groups; the trial was powered for first attempt success, not complications, so this null does not establish equivalence.

> "The overall complication rate was 2.75% (7/255) in the treatment group and 4.71% (16/340) in the control group (odds ratio 0.57 (95% CI 0.23 to 1.41), P=0.22; table 3, supplementary table S1)."
