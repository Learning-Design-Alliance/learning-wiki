# Some students who cannot immediately propose a cause for an observed discrepancy converge on one post hoc through continuous, iterative measurements and revisions

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When students cannot immediately propose a cause for a discrepancy, they may iteratively and continuously make measurements and revisions, eventually proposing a cause post hoc — a pathway not previously described in the Modeling Framework. [→ Laura Ríos 2019](#laura-rios-2019)

## Evidence

### Laura Ríos 2019

Laura Ríos, Benjamin Pollard, Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer, and H. J. Lewandowski. (2019). Pathways to proposing causes for unexpected experimental results. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1119/perc.2018.pr.rios](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2018.pr.rios)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative analysis of think-aloud problem-solving interviews in which ten upper-division students troubleshot an inverting amplifier circuit; the authors describe "two episodes where students undergo this process to converge on a proposed cause post hoc."

> "We observed that some participants iteratively and continuously made measurements and revisions if they could not immediately propose a cause for an observed discrepancy."
