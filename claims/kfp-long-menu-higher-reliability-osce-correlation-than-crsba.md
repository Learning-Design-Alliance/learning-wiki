# Key-Feature Problems with Long Menu show higher reliability and higher OSCE correlations than context-rich Single Best Answer questions, with no difference in difficulty

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Huwendiek S 2019

Huwendiek S. (2019). Design and implementation of virtual patients for learning of clinical reasoning. GMS Journal for Medical Education 36(4):Doc33. [doi:10.3205/zma001241](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001241)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Psychometric comparison of two exam formats after students (n=377) underwent clerkships and corresponding exams (n=11). The statistical analysis found no difficulty difference and "a higher reliability (G coefficient) was found for Key-Feature Problems with Long Menu".

> "According to the statistical analysis, there was no difference in difficulty; however, a higher reliability (G coefficient) was found for Key-Feature Problems with Long Menu, even when corrected for testing time (reliability per testing time 1 hour: KFP 0,84 vs crSBA 0,79)"

### Huwendiek S 2019 (2)

Huwendiek S. (2019). Design and implementation of virtual patients for learning of clinical reasoning. GMS Journal for Medical Education 36(4):Doc33. [doi:10.3205/zma001241](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001241)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same exam-format comparison study: correlations of the two question formats with OSCE results were printed as OSCE-KFP 0,54 versus OSCE-crSBA 0,41, favouring Key-Feature Problems with Long Menu.

> "The Key-Feature Problems showed higher correlations with the OSCE results (OSCE-KFP: 0,54; OSCE-crSBA: 0,41)"
