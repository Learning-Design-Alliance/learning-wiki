# Prephonological spellers do not show the syllable-length signature predicted by the hypothesized syllabic stage

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Brazilian prephonological spellers (mean age 4;8) did not produce longer spellings for two-syllable than one-syllable words. [→ Treiman Rebecca 2013](#treiman-rebecca-2013)
`q2 i?` U.S. prephonological spellers (mean age 4;7) likewise showed no syllable-length effect, and other Brazilian work failed to find a syllabic stage. [→ Treiman Rebecca 2013 (2)](#treiman-rebecca-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Treiman Rebecca 2013

Treiman Rebecca, Kessler Brett. (2013). Learning to Use an Alphabetic Writing System. Language Learning and Development. https://doi.org/10.1080/15475441.2013.812016

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Quantitative study using a rearrangement procedure to identify Brazilian prephonological spellers. The null finding speaks against Ferreiro and Teberosky's claim that children pass through a stage in which each symbol represents a syllable.

> "These children, who were on average 4 years and 8 months of age, did not produce longer spellings when trying to spell two-syllable words than when trying to spell one-syllable words."

### Treiman Rebecca 2013 (2)

Treiman Rebecca, Kessler Brett. (2013). Learning to Use an Alphabetic Writing System. Language Learning and Development. https://doi.org/10.1080/15475441.2013.812016

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Same study's U.S. sample replicated the null result; the review adds that Cardoso-Martins and colleagues also failed to find evidence for a syllabic stage in Brazilian children.

> "In the same study, we also found negative results in a group of U.S. prephonological spellers with an average age of 4 years and 7 months."
