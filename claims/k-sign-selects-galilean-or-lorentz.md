# In the one-postulate derivation, the sign of the invariant K selects the geometry: K = 0 gives Galilean, K > 0 gives Lorentz transformations, and K < 0 is discarded for lack of causal structure

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · theoretical `r3` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2007 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The derivation leaves a constant K undetermined; K = 0 recovers Galilean transformations and K > 0 yields Lorentz transformations with an invariant velocity c. [→ Silagadze 2007](#silagadze-2007)
`q2 i?` The K < 0 case is discarded because its Euclidean space-time supports no invariant time order, i.e. no causal structure. [→ Silagadze 2007 (2)](#silagadze-2007-2)

## Evidence

### Silagadze 2007

Silagadze, Z. K. (2007). Relativity without tears. https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.0929 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0708.0929](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0708.0929)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical derivation in Section 2: introducing a scale factor k(V²) and using reciprocity, the velocity-addition rule contains a constant K; K = 0 recovers the Galilean case and K = 1/c² > 0 gives the Lorentz transformations with c an invariant velocity.

> "Finally, if K = 1c2 > 0, one gets the Lorentz transformations"

### Silagadze 2007 (2)

Silagadze, Z. K. (2007). Relativity without tears. https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.0929 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0708.0929](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0708.0929)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical derivation in Section 2: for K < 0 the transformations are ordinary rotations whose Euclidean norm does not allow defining an invariant time order between events, so the case is rejected as a timeless nirvana without causal structure.

> "However, the real reason why we should discard the case K <0 is the absence of causal structure."
