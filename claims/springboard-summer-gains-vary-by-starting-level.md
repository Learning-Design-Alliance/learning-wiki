# Reading growth after Springboard Summer varies by starting point relative to grade level, with the largest gains for scholars who started below grade level

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Whether all starting-level groups show significant growth differs by grade: in some grades scholars at or above grade level did not experience statistically significant changes, while scholars below grade level generally did. [→ Piccinino 2020](#piccinino-2020)

## Evidence

### Piccinino 2020

Piccinino, K., Pepper, S. K., Salomon, H., Greenfield, S., McClanahan, W., & Robertson-Kraft, C. (2020). Springboard Summer Reading Program Evaluation Report. https://www.cde.state.co.us/cdelib/summerslide

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Paired t-tests within starting-level categories (at/above, within one grade below, more than one grade below) by grade. For rising second and third graders, scholars "who began the program already reading at or above grade level did not experience statistically significant changes."

> "The next few figures demonstrate the answer is mixed: in some cases all scholars show statistically significant reading growth, and in other cases, only scholars who are behind grade level show it."
