Evidence¶

Austin 2017¶

Austin, C. R., Vaughn, S., & McClelland, A. M. (2017). Intensive Reading Interventions for Inadequate Responders in Grades K–3: A Synthesis. Learning Disability Quarterly, 40(4), 191–210. https://doi.org/10.1177/0731948717714446

q2 · i? · review · r2

Descriptive synthesis results (Results section, Tables 2–3) coding how each of the 12 studies intensified Tier 3 relative to Tier 2. The article reports "Reducing group size was used by seven of 12 studies (58.3%)" and that only three studies (25%) increased implementer expertise or used data-based decision making.

"Thirty-three percent of studies increased the frequency of sessions, 50% of studies increased the session length, and 66.7% of studies increased the duration of intervention from start to finish."

Related Claims¶

  • Tier 2 dosage and scheduling findings are mixed: more frequent kindergarten sessions helped, but first-grade studies found no outcome differences across 16-hour and 32-hour schedules — related
  • In Gilbert et al. (2013), an intensified one-on-one Tier 3 intervention produced negative effects for grade 1 inadequate responders compared with inadequate responders continuing Tier 2 — related
  • In Wanzek and Vaughn (2008), doubling Tier 3 dosage to two daily 30-min sessions did not produce positive effects, while one daily session showed mixed effects for grade 1 inadequate responders versus at-risk controls — related
  • Tier 3 interventions produce significant positive effects for K–3 inadequate responders when compared with control groups of also-inadequate responders, in three of four studies with comparable controls — related
