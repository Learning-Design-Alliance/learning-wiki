## Evidence

### Zhao 2025

Zhao, N., Dou, D., Chen, X., Chen, F., Luo, R., Zhu, X., & Xiang, G.-X. (2025). Exploring the congruence between perceived parent–teacher achievement goals and student academic outcomes: A study using polynomial regression with response surface analysis. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 95, 603–626. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12742

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Response surface analysis of the same 4944-student survey for language achievement. The authors state "The significance of a 1 and a 2 suggests the effect is along the line of congruence", with language performance higher when perceived mastery goals match at higher levels.

> "According to the full model regression results (see Table 6), a 1 = 0.11 (SE = 0.012, p < .001), a 2 = −0.04 (SE = 0.009, p < .001), a 3 = 0.09 (SE = 0.013, p < .001) and a 4 = −0.056 (SE = 0.015, p < .001)."

### Zhao 2025 (2)

Zhao, N., Dou, D., Chen, X., Chen, F., Luo, R., Zhu, X., & Xiang, G.-X. (2025). Exploring the congruence between perceived parent–teacher achievement goals and student academic outcomes: A study using polynomial regression with response surface analysis. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 95, 603–626. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12742

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion of the language response surface: an optimal level of goal congruence may exist beyond which language outcomes are negatively impacted, possibly because surface strategies aid language memorization.

> "However, it is important to note that at exceptionally high levels of congruence, students' language performance showed a tendency to decline."
