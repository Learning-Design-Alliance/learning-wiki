## Evidence

### Melanie Good 2018

Melanie Good, Emily Marshman, Edit Yerushalmi, and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Physics teaching assistants' views of different types of introductory problems: Challenge of perceiving the instructional benefits of context-rich and multiple-choice problems. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020120](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020120)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Worksheet ranking activity in a semester-long physics TA professional development course, in which TAs ranked problem types on instructional benefit; the article reports the multiple-choice and context-rich types were "seen by TAs as the least instructionally beneﬁcial of all example problem types."

> "the average rankings for “instructional beneﬁt,” indicate that the multiple-choice and context-rich problem types are seen by TAs as the least instructionally beneﬁcial of all example problem types"

### Melanie Good 2018 (2)

Physics teaching assistants' views of different types of introductory problems: Challenge of perceiving the instructional benefits of context-rich and multiple-choice problems. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020120](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020120)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same worksheet ranking activity, use category: the article reports the multiple-choice and context-rich problem types "were ranked the lowest of all problem types," and written responses indicated limited ways TAs would use them.

> "the average rankings for “use” indicate that the multiple-choice and context-rich problem types were ranked the lowest of all problem types"
