# Across 78 observed classrooms, 46% of teacher ratings were at the unsatisfactory or basic level, with AMPS teachers earning generally higher ratings than IDS and Renaissance 2010 teachers

## Evidence

### Daniel C. Humphrey 2009

Daniel C. Humphrey, Patrick M. Shields. (2009). High School Reform in Chicago Public Schools: An Overview. SRI International / Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=High+School+Reform+in+Chicago+Public+Schools+An+Overview

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Exploratory classroom observation study of 78 teachers using a modified Danielson Framework, each observation lasting one class period. Ratings were distributed as: Unsatisfactory 10%, Basic 36%, Proficient 49%, Distinguished 5% (Exhibit 5); no effect size was printed.

> "Amalgamated ratings from all of the observations indicate that 46% of the ratings were at the unsatisfactory or basic level."

### Daniel C. Humphrey 2009 (2)

Daniel C. Humphrey, Patrick M. Shields. (2009). High School Reform in Chicago Public Schools: An Overview. SRI International / Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=High+School+Reform+in+Chicago+Public+Schools+An+Overview

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Cross-initiative comparison of observation ratings (Exhibit 6): AMPS 3% unsatisfactory/22% basic/60% proficient/15% distinguished versus IDS 12%/42%/43%/3% and Renaissance 2010 10%/39%/49%/2%. The authors caution ratings should not lead to conclusions about teacher quality across schools; no effect size was printed.

> "However, among the classrooms we observed, we did find more examples of proficient and distinguished instructional practice in AMPS than in IDS and Renaissance 2010 schools (see Exhibit 6)."
