## Evidence

### Gartmeier Martin 2019

Gartmeier Martin, Pfurtscheller Theresa, Hapfelmeier Alexander, Grünewald Marc, Häusler Janina, Seidel Tina, Berberat Pascal O. (2019). Teacher questions and student responses in case-based learning: outcomes of a video study in medical education. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1895-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Evolutionary classification tree analysis (RQ3) of 1688 teacher question–student (non-)response sequences from the videotaped seminars. The tree showed the highest elaborative share (50%) and lowest non-response (30%) at the follow-up reasoning node. Percentages are descriptive; no effect size is printed.

> "The highest degree of elaborative student answers—50% in node 6—is associated with follow-up reasoning questions. Node 6 is also characterised by the lowest rate of student non-response in our analysis (30%)"

### Gartmeier Martin 2019 (2)

Gartmeier Martin, Pfurtscheller Theresa, Hapfelmeier Alexander, Grünewald Marc, Häusler Janina, Seidel Tina, Berberat Pascal O. (2019). Teacher questions and student responses in case-based learning: outcomes of a video study in medical education. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1895-1

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The conclusion section recommends that, when a knowledge-based angle on a new topic is chosen, an easy closed reproduction question may work better than an initial reasoning question, reflecting the tree's non-response findings.

> "Posing an easy to answer closed reproduction question might be a better idea"
