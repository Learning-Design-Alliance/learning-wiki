### Steinbach 2022

Steinbach, S. (2022). Looking beyond vision: Supports for students who are blind or visually impaired in mathematics. NWEA. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/looking-beyond-vision-supports-for-students-who-are-blind-or-visually-impaired-in-mathematics/

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Bell & Silverman (2019), a survey in which "About 85% of middle and high school BVI students surveyed reported" their STEM materials arrived late at least some of the time. Descriptive percentage only.

> "About 85% of middle and high school BVI students surveyed reported that their science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM) materials arrived late at least some of the time (Bell & Silverman, 2019)."

### Steinbach 2022 (2)

Steinbach, S. (2022). Looking beyond vision: Supports for students who are blind or visually impaired in mathematics. NWEA. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/looking-beyond-vision-supports-for-students-who-are-blind-or-visually-impaired-in-mathematics/

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes to Herzberg & Rosenblum (2014) a quality assessment of braille and tactile math/science worksheet production finding "only 5% of worksheets given to students were totally without error" and 20% had five or more errors.

> "a study assessing the quality of the production of braille and tactile math and science worksheets (Herzberg & Rosenblum, 2014) found that only 5% of worksheets given to students were totally without error and 20% had five or more errors."

### Steinbach 2022 (3)

Steinbach, S. (2022). Looking beyond vision: Supports for students who are blind or visually impaired in mathematics. NWEA. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/looking-beyond-vision-supports-for-students-who-are-blind-or-visually-impaired-in-mathematics/

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Rosenblum et al. (2020), that with 10 teachers of the visually impaired describing mathematics graphics per national guidelines, there was "wide variability in how teachers described the graphics" and questioned students about them.

> "when 10 teachers of the visually impaired were asked to describe mathematics graphics to BVIs according to the National Council of Accessible Materials' guidelines, Rosenblum et al. (2020) discovered that there was wide variability in how teachers described the graphics, and in how they questioned students about the graphics features and the information presented."
