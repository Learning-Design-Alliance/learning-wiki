## Evidence

### Laura M. Akesson 2026

Laura M. Akesson, Jessica L. Rosenberg, Nancy Holincheck, and Benjamin W. Dreyfus. (2026). Defining a physics person: Tensions in the identity development of undergraduate women in physics. Physical Review Physics Education Research 22, 020102. https://doi.org/10.1103/j9bh-pz14

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Cross-tabulation of coded open-ended identity explanations by identity level found low-identity students "more than twice as likely to frame their response in terms of performance/competence (5 of 18, 27.8%)" compared with mid-to-high identity students (12.7%).

> "Students with a low physics identity (A –C) were more than twice as likely to frame their response in terms of performance/competence (5 of 18, 27.8%) as compared with students with mid-to-high physics identities (13 of 102, 12.7%)."

### Laura M. Akesson 2026 (2)

Laura M. Akesson, Jessica L. Rosenberg, Nancy Holincheck, and Benjamin W. Dreyfus. (2026). Defining a physics person: Tensions in the identity development of undergraduate women in physics. Physical Review Physics Education Research 22, 020102. https://doi.org/10.1103/j9bh-pz14

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Of the 18 participants writing about performance and/or competence, only two made positive comments; "16 of 18, 88.9%" described their abilities negatively, framing struggles as fixed realities rather than growth.

> "The remainder of responses (16 of 18, 88.9%) reflected a negative perception of their abilities to understand physics content and/or perform physics tasks."
