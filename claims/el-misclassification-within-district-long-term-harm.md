The report attributes this emerging evidence to Okhremtchouk (2014) on within-district misclassification and Umansky (2014) on long-term outcomes near the cutpoint; the underlying studies are cited, not reanalyzed, here.

> "There is also emerging evidence that inconsistent classification practices leading to misclassifications can occur even within a given district or school (Okhremtchouk, 2014) and that misclassifying students, especially those very near the cutpoint for initial fluent English proficiency, could lead to long-term negative academic outcomes (Umansky, 2014)."
