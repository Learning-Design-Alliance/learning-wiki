# In the SEM, GenAI dependence is positively associated with critical thinking (β = 0.492), human–AI collaboration quality (β = 0.538), and research creativity (β = 0.064), with satisfactory model fit

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` GenAI dependence positively predicts critical thinking (β = 0.492, p < 0.001), human–AI collaboration quality (β = 0.538, p < 0.001), and research creativity (β = 0.064, p < 0.05); critical thinking strongly predicts research creativity (β = 0.731, p < 0.001). [→ Yin S 2026](#yin-s-2026)

## Evidence

### Yin S 2026

Yin S, Wu R, Liu X and Li R (2026) GenAI dependence and graduate students' research creativity: the roles of critical thinking and human–AI collaboration quality. Front. Psychol. 17:1896963. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1896963

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Structural equation modeling in AMOS 24 with a second-order GenAI dependence construct and serial mediators, N = 1,157. The article reports model fit of χ2/df = 7.541, CFI = 0.988, GFI = 0.981, SRMR = 0.017, noting the χ2/df ratio was relatively high.

> "GenAI dependence was positively associated with critical thinking (β = 0.492, p < 0.001), human–AI collaboration quality (β = 0.538, p < 0.001), and research creativity (β = 0.064, p < 0.05). Critical thinking was positively associated with human–AI collaboration quality (β = 0.348, p < 0.001) and research creativity (β = 0.731, p < 0.001)."
