## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In 2010 1HSG, males achieved higher language scores (286.1 ± 48.6) than females (274.1 ± 49.70) (F = 8.35; p = .0040) and higher mathematics scores (308.3 ± 50.7 vs 277.1 ± 51.6) (F = 52.75; p < .0001). [→ Ivanovic Daniza M. et al 2019](#ivanovic-daniza-m-et-al-2019)

## Evidence

### Ivanovic Daniza M. et al 2019

Ivanovic Daniza M. et al. (2019). A multifactorial approach of nutritional, intellectual, brain development, cardiovascular risk, socio-economic, demographic and educational variables affecting the scholastic achievement in Chilean students: An eight- year follow-up study. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0212279

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

ANOVA comparison of 2009 SIMCE means by sex in the 2010 1HSG sample; the regression model for 1HSG mathematics likewise entered sex (males) as a predictor.

> "in the 2010 1HSG, males achieved higher LSA scores (286.1 ± 48.6 (n = 330)) than females (274.1 ± 49.70 (n = 341)); (F = 8.35; p = .0040) and the same was observed for MSA scores (308.3 ± 50.7 (n = 330)) and (277.1 ± 51.6 (n = 341)), respectively (F = 52.75; p < .0001)"

### Ivanovic Daniza M. et al 2019 (2)

Ivanovic Daniza M. et al. (2019). PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0212279

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

ANOVA comparison of 2009 SIMCE means by sex in the 2010 5ESG sample found no sex difference in either test; in regression, sex (females) entered the 5ESG language model.

> "In school-age children from the 2010 5ESG, LSA and MSA did not differ between males and females"
