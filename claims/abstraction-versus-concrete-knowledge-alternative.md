## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Because children also acquired concrete knowledge from the video, expert-detection gains might reflect causal abstractions formed during viewing or ad hoc abstraction supported by still-present concrete details. [→ Chuey 2021](#chuey-2021)

## Evidence

### Chuey 2021

Chuey, A., McCarthy, A., Lockhart, K., Trouche, E., Sheskin, M. & Keil, F. (2021). No guts, no glory: underestimating the benefits of providing children with mechanistic details. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00108-5

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' stated limitation in the Discussion: two interpretations of the expert-detection improvement cannot yet be separated. They suggest future work using more complex mechanisms where concrete details are harder to remember, or longer delays between exposure and testing.

> "their subsequent improvement in expert detection might not be the result of causal abstractions formed while watching the video, but rather from their still-present concrete knowledge during the expert-detection task itself."
