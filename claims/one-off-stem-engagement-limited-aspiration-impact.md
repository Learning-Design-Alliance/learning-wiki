# One-off STEM engagement experiences are unlikely to have a lasting measurable effect on young people's aspirations

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` One-off STEM engagement experiences can yield short-term content learning and affective outcomes but are unlikely to significantly impact aspirations and educational trajectories. [→ Martin Archer 2021](#martin-archer-2021)

## Evidence

### Martin Archer 2021

Martin Archer, Jennifer DeWitt, Carol Davenport, Olivia Keenan, Lorraine Coghill, Anna Christodoulou, Samantha Durbin, Heather Campbell, Lewis Hou. (2021). Going beyond the one-off: How can STEM engagement programmes with young people have real lasting impact? Manuscript in preparation for Research for All. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.14324/rfa.05.1.07](https://doi.org/10.14324/rfa.05.1.07)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The article's landscape review of aspiration research (citing L. Archer & DeWitt, 2017) argues that aspirations are complex, multifaceted and evolve over time, so "one-off experiences, while inspirational" are unlikely to significantly impact aspirations. This is the review's synthesis, not a new experiment.

> "Consequently, it should not be surprising that one-off experiences, while inspirational and having potential to support a range of outcomes, are unlikely to significantly impact aspirations and educational trajectories."
