Related Claims¶

  • The educational value of GAI is contingent on pedagogical integration, not on the technology itself — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it
  • Pretraining learners on a system's key parts and terms before the main lesson improved knowledge and real-world transfer in one VR experiment, and is reported second-hand to improve learning in multimedia studies — related
  • Perceived immersive, interactive, and distributed affordances are each positively associated with a distinct lacquer-painting imagery characteristic in cross-sectional survey data — related
  • The review reports that real-world experiential simulations offer affordances beyond virtual-only formats, including heightened presence through embodiment, place, and face-to-face social immersion — related
  • Immersive technologies are rarely adopted in robotics GBL/gamification but studies using them report heightened engagement, improved spatial reasoning, and enhanced perceived realism — related
  • Review syntheses: VR/AR most often improved academic achievement and motivation, but sometimes performed no better than conventional technologies — related
  • Video review raises learning interest under virtual simulation but not under hands-on activity — related
  • Immersive VR simulation increased self-efficacy and intrinsic motivation more than a conventional safety manual in laboratory safety training — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • AI-generated and prebuilt simulations produced higher immediate conceptual-assessment scores than physical laboratory work, but the advantage did not persist in later examinations — related
  • Digital tools complemented hands-on investigations by providing opportunities not possible in the classroom and letting children easily practice what they learned in hands-on activities. — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Conventional training received significantly higher ratings for perceived realism and immersion than VR-based training (r=0.774) — related
  • Qualitative analysis found radiation became perceptible in VR for all participants, while all reported operability differences favoring conventional training — related
  • VR-based training with radiation field visualization received significantly higher ratings for radiation understanding and visualization than conventional training (r=0.878) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • VR-based training received significantly higher ratings for medical safety awareness and radiation protection than conventional training (r=0.856) — related
  • The VR application did not increase students' interest in biodiversity, and only the academic-track control group showed a significant interest increase — related
