## Evidence

### Ferreira Pedro Marques 2022

Ferreira Pedro Marques, Alves Rauni Jandé Roama, Zantut-Wittmann Denise Engelbrecht. (2022). Impact of the use of illicit and licit substances and anxiety disorders on the academic performance of medical students: a pilot study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-022-03752-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Spearman correlations (Table 2) between the STAI trait anxiety score and ASSIST substance-use scores in the pilot cohort were positive for tobacco (ƿ = 0.25821), amphetamines or ecstasy (ƿ = 0.31782) and hypnotics or sedatives (ƿ = 0.32528), graded "poor" to "fair"; no separate standardized effect size is printed, so impact is coded null.

> "Trait anxiety score (obtained by STAI) showed a positive correlation with the use of tobacco and its derivatives (ƿ = 0.25821, poor), amphetamines or ecstasy (ƿ = 0.31782, fair) and hypnotics or sedatives (ƿ = 0.32528, fair) (obtained by ASSIST)."

### Ferreira Pedro Marques 2022 (2)

Ferreira Pedro Marques, Alves Rauni Jandé Roama, Zantut-Wittmann Denise Engelbrecht. (2022). Impact of the use of illicit and licit substances and anxiety disorders on the academic performance of medical students: a pilot study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-022-03752-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Mann–Whitney comparisons (Table 1) of STAI scores by ASSIST intervention category in the pilot cohort; the article reports that "need for intervention for use of hypnotics and sedatives" was related to trait anxiety score, with only p-values printed.

> "Trait anxiety and state score were related to the need for intervention for the use of amphetamines or ecstasy, as well as for trait anxiety score and need for intervention for use of hypnotics and sedatives (Table 1)."
