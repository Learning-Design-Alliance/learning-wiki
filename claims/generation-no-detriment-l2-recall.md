# Generation as the sole desirable difficulty produced no detrimental effect on L2 vocabulary recall, in contrast to prior short-term findings

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The generation treatment did not negatively influence recall for either Confidence or Knowledge scores, unlike Barcroft (2009), who found lower short-term recall when generation was the sole study method. [→ John Duplice 2025](#john-duplice-2025)

## Evidence

### John Duplice 2025

John Duplice. (2025). Generation and L2 Vocabulary Learning: A Classroom Action Study on the Efficacy of the Generation Desirable Difficulty in Learning L2 Vocabulary. Vocabulary Learning and Instruction, 14(1). https://doi.org/10.29140/vli.v14n1.102482

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Authors' interpretation of the full pre/post dataset (Confidence and Knowledge scores at three test points). The article states there was "no adverse effect from generation either."

> "The results indicated that neither perceived nor actual knowledge demonstrated a significant advantage from the generation activity compared to baseline activities; however, there was no adverse effect from generation either, indicating no detriment compared to the baseline."
