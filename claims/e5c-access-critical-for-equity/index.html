Evidence¶

Li 2024¶

Li, Q., Moallem, M., Boettinger, J., Cai, Q., & Levi, M. (2024). An educational model of equity and computing: a study of marginalized student experience during COVID pandemic. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-024-10443-0

q2 · i? · design · r3

Authors' conclusion in the Discussion of the design-based case study of 82 girls, over 56% from underrepresented groups, in free after-school clubs moved fully online. The article offers this as an interpretive finding; no effect size is printed.

"The examination of the girls' experience during the COVID pandemic shows that access to opportunities is critical to promote equity."

Related Claims¶

  • Computing education has historically excluded marginalized student populations, and large inequities in access persist — a broader claim this one bears on
  • AI in adult education risks reinforcing existing inequalities via the digital divide, particularly for rural, low-income, and marginalized learners — related
  • A combination of human facilitators and well-constructed tutorials has the potential to improve girls' self-study skills and prepare them to become more independent learners — related
  • Beyond cognitive gains, the experience increased girls' interest in computing and confidence, and fostered work ethics, perseverance, social development, digital citizenship, and independent learning — related
  • Girls produced 34 comprehensive PSAs and dozens of mini-Scratch projects, providing project-based evidence of computing knowledge gain — related
  • Girls in the online E-5C-guided clubs showed significant pretest-to-posttest gains in creativity, critical thinking, and overall computational thinking, but not in algorithmic thinking, cooperativity, or problem solving — related
  • Digital learning environments during the pandemic raise implications for addressing social inequities and education policy reform — related
