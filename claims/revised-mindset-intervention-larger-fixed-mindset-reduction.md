## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The revised intervention produced a larger pre-to-post reduction in self-reported fixed mindset (Δ=−0.48) than the original intervention (Δ=−0.22), both significant. [→ Yeager 2016](#yeager-2016)

## Evidence

### Yeager 2016

Yeager, D. S., Romero, C., Paunesku, D., Hulleman, C. S., Schneider, B., Hinojosa, C., Lee, H. Y., O'Brien, J., Flint, K., Roberts, A., Trott, J., Greene, D., Walton, G. M., & Dweck, C. S. (2016). Using Design Thinking to Improve Psychological Interventions: The Case of the Growth Mindset During the Transition to High School. *Journal of Educational Psychology, 108*(3), 374–391. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000098

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same 7,501-student randomized comparison, three-item fixed mindset scales were administered at Time 1 and Time 2 (α=.74). The revised group changed "Δ =−0.48 scale point (out of 6)" versus −0.22 for the original group; both p<.001 and significantly different. No effect size printed.

> "The original mindset group showed a change score of Δ =−0.22 scale points (out of 6), as compared to Δ =−0.48 scale point (out of 6) for the revised mindset group. Both change scores were significant at p<.001, and they were significantly different from one another"
