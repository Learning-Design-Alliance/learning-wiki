## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Intervention doctors were more likely to engage in key shared decision-making behaviors in standardized patient visits (e.g., suggesting the choice of testing was up to the patient), even though knowledge of shared decision making did not differ between groups. [→ Wilkes Michael S. 2017](#wilkes-michael-s-2017)

## Evidence

### Wilkes Michael S. 2017

Wilkes Michael S., Day Frank C., Fancher Tonya L., McDermott Haley, Lehman Erik, Bell Robert A., Green Michael J. (2017). Increasing confidence and changing behaviors in primary care providers engaged in genetic counselling. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0982-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Transcript analysis of audio-recorded standardized patient visits (breast cancer scenario, ~5 weeks post-curriculum) plus blinded SP post-encounter ratings on a 10-item Likert questionnaire. The article reports intervention physicians "more likely to engage in shared decision-making"; SP ratings showed a non-significant trend toward higher intervention ratings.

> "While both groups received information around shared decision making in curricular materials, and while there was no difference between the groups with regard to knowledge of shared decision making, intervention doctors were more likely to engage in shared decision-making in standardized patient visits as determined by transcript analysis and SPs rated them higher on a 10 item questionnaire around communication and shared decision making skills."
