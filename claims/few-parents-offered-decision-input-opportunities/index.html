Evidence¶

Morgan 2024¶

Morgan, I., Gillispie, C., & Waller, A. (2024). Supporting Young Learners Through Family Engagement: Engaging First. EdTrust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED658182

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Survey 2. Figure 4 shows 74% of all parents agreed, with higher percentages among Black (86%), Latino (80%), and multilingual (78%) parents. Parents of students with disabilities agreed at 78% vs. 73% overall.

"Three-quarters of parents agree that their child’s school encourages their family to have a role in schoolwide planning, leadership, and policy and program decisions."

Morgan 2024 (2)¶

Morgan, I., Gillispie, C., & Waller, A. (2024). Supporting Young Learners Through Family Engagement: Engaging First. EdTrust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED658182

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Survey 2, shown in Figure 5. Lower-income parents reported fewer input opportunities than other parents (15% vs. 21% classroom; 11% vs. 16% schoolwide), and parents of students with disabilities reported 12% vs. 18% for classroom input.

"Fewer than one-fifth of parents said that their school gave them opportunities to provide input about teachers’ decisions in the classroom (17%) or during administrative and schoolwide decision-making processes (15%)."
