# Mental effort and judgments of learning show a medium-sized negative correlation, moderated by regulation mode and task type

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · theoretical `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A meta-analysis finds a medium-sized negative correlation between mental effort and judgments of learning, which approaches zero/the positive range as learners engage more in goal-driven regulation. [→ de Bruin 2020](#de-bruin-2020)
`q3 i?` The negative correlation between mental effort and judgments of learning is stronger for problem-solving tasks than for reading or word learning tasks. [→ de Bruin 2020 (2)](#de-bruin-2020-2)

## Evidence

### de Bruin 2020

de Bruin, A. B. H., Roelle, J., Carpenter, S. K., & Baars, M. (2020). Synthesizing Cognitive Load and Self-regulation Theory: a Theoretical Framework and Research Agenda. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09576-4

`q3 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The editorial summarizes the meta-analysis by Baars et al. (this issue) on the relation between mental effort and judgments of learning, reporting a "medium-sized negative correlation"; no correlation coefficient value is printed in the editorial.

> "Their findings indicate that overall, there is a medium-sized negative correlation between mental effort and judgments of learning and that several factors moderate this relationship."

### de Bruin 2020 (2)

de Bruin, A. B. H., Roelle, J., Carpenter, S. K., & Baars, M. (2020). Synthesizing Cognitive Load and Self-regulation Theory: a Theoretical Framework and Research Agenda. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09576-4

`q3 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Moderator finding from the same meta-analysis as summarized in the editorial: task type moderates the effort-JOL correlation, with the negative correlation stronger for problem-solving than for reading or word learning tasks. No coefficient is printed.

> "The negative correlation was found to be stronger for problem-solving tasks than for reading or word learning tasks."
