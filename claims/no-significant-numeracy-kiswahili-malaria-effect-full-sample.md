## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the full multivariate model, birth-year PfPR coefficients on numeracy and Kiswahili are reduced in magnitude and become statistically insignificant. [→ Klejnstrup 2018](#klejnstrup-2018)
`q1 i?` The authors interpret the insignificant numeracy and Kiswahili results as reflecting strong ceiling effects rather than absence of any effect. [→ Klejnstrup 2018 (2)](#klejnstrup-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Klejnstrup 2018

Klejnstrup, Buhl-Wiggers, Jones, & Rand. (2018). Early life malaria exposure and academic performance. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0199542

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same OLS fixed-effects analysis of the pooled Uwezo–MAP dataset (246,325 children); the full-sample numeracy and Kiswahili coefficients in Table 2 "become insignificant" once controls are added, with no effect size printed.

> "For numeracy and Kiswahili the coefficient magnitude is reduced even further, becoming insignificant. These results are robust to ignoring sampling weights (results available upon request)."

### Klejnstrup 2018 (2)

Klejnstrup, Buhl-Wiggers, Jones, & Rand. (2018). Early life malaria exposure and academic performance. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0199542

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion, citing a Kenyan validation finding that the numeracy and Kiswahili tests discriminate only to a limited extent between skill levels because items do not span the ability range in early grades.

> "the insignificant effect on numeracy and Kiswahili literacy scores does not necessarily mean that early life malaria exposure has no effect, but rather reflects strong ceiling effects in the numeracy and Kiswahili test scores"
