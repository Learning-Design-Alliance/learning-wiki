## Evidence

### Klimas J. 2017

Klimas J., Small W., Ahamad K., Cullen W., Mead A., Rieb L., Wood E., McNeil R. (2017). Barriers and facilitators to implementing addiction medicine fellowships: a qualitative study with fellows, medical students, residents and preceptors. Addiction Science & Clinical Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-017-0086-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In the community-concerns domain, the community of practice—colleagues, preceptors, and harm reduction organisations—"provided support and mentoring to junior learners"; a participant described easy access to "giant brains" and creating a web of people as resources.

> "This community of practice provided support and mentoring to junior learners, as well as linkages between the senior clinicians and staff."

### Klimas J. 2017 (2)

Klimas J., Small W., Ahamad K., Cullen W., Mead A., Rieb L., Wood E., McNeil R. (2017). Barriers and facilitators to implementing addiction medicine fellowships: a qualitative study with fellows, medical students, residents and preceptors. Addiction Science & Clinical Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-017-0086-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Some learners were challenged to advocate for addiction medicine, seldom considered a "sexy area of medicine" by colleagues; a participant explained entrenched attitudes made "not only... the learning difficult, but it also makes discussion and solidification of ideas much more difficult".

> "Other inter-professional challenges within addiction medicine, such as entrenched attitudes and clinical practices, made implementation of new learning difficult"
