## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Pupils receiving P4C made approximately two additional months' progress in reading (effect size +0.12) and maths (+0.10) compared with control pupils. [→ Gorard et al. 2015](#gorard-et-al-2015)

## Evidence

### Gorard et al. 2015

Stephen Gorard, Nadia Siddiqui and Beng Huat See. (2015). Philosophy for Children: Evaluation report and Executive summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED581147

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

School-level randomised controlled trial with waitlist control across 48 schools; 1,550 treatment and 1,609 control pupils pre-tested, KS2 results from the National Pupil Database for 1,529 original Year 5 pupils, analysed as KS1-to-KS2 gain scores. The report's results table prints effect sizes of +0.12 for reading and +0.10 for maths, described as "+2 months" of progress.

> "The evaluation found evidence that P4C had a positive impact on pupils' Key Stage 2 (KS2) progress in reading and maths."
