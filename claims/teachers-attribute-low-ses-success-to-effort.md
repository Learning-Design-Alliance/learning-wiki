## Evidence

### Schoneveld 2023

Schoneveld, E., & Brummelman, E. (2023). "You did incredibly well!": teachers' inflated praise can make children from low-SES backgrounds seem less smart (but more hardworking). npj Science of Learning, 8:31. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00183-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory two-tailed Wilcoxon signed rank test in the Study 1 online experiment with 106 teachers. "teachers attributed the success of low-SES students more often to effort than the success of high-SES students"; ability attribution difference scores did not significantly deviate from 0 (V = 101, p = 0.878).

> "The location of teachers' effort attribution difference scores was significantly >0, V = 338, p = 0.002, indicating that teachers attributed the success of low-SES students more often to effort than the success of high-SES students."
