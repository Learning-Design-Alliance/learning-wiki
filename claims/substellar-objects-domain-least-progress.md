# Views of (sub-)stellar objects show the least progress: no significant difference between lower and middle cohorts (p = 0.94), with only low pairwise effect sizes for later grades

## Subclaims
`q2 i0` Domain 4 shows the lowest descriptive statistics (means 32.9%, 31.8%, 43.1%); the overall comparison is significant (F(2, 363) = 4.46, p < 0.05), but cohorts 1 and 2 do not differ (p = 0.94) and pairwise effect sizes are low (d = 0.38, 0.42). ## Evidence

### Philipp Bitzenbauer 2023

Philipp Bitzenbauer, Sarah Navarrete, Fabian Hennig, Malte S. Ubben, and Joaquin M. Veith. (2023). A cross-age study on secondary school students' views of stars. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020165](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020165)

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Cross-age ANOVA on domain 4 ((sub-)stellar objects) responses of N = 366 students; this domain "holds the overall lowest descriptive statistics" and only later grades (11 and 12) meaningfully separate from the rest. The lower-middle null is reported, not equivalence.

> "While overall group comparison still results in a statistically significant difference [F (2, 363) = 4 .46, p <0.05; η2p = 0 .02], no statistically significant difference was found between cohorts 1 and 2 (p = 0 .94). The differences between cohort 1 and 3 as well as cohort 2 and 3 were found to be statistically significant by Tukey-Kramer post-hoc tests ( p <0.05 each) but the corresponding effect sizes were low with d = 0.38 and d = 0.42, respectively."
