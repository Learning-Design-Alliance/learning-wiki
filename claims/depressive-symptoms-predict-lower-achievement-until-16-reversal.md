## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Depressive symptoms in early adolescence are associated with worse later achievement between ages 11-14 and 14-16, with the direction reversing between 16 and 18 years. [→ López-López José A. 2021](#lopez-lopez-jose-a-2021)

## Evidence

### López-López José A. 2021

López-López José A., Kwong Alex S.F., Washbrook Liz, Tilling Kate, Fazel Mina S., Pearson Rebecca M. (2021). Depressive symptoms and academic achievement in UK adolescents: a cross-lagged analysis with genetic covariates. Journal of Affective Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2021.01.091

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

RI-CLPM results for boys (N=1,871) from the ALSPAC cohort. The unadjusted model showed depressive symptoms negatively associated with later achievement "between ages 11 and 14 (-0.04, -0.08 to 0) and between ages 14 and 16 (-0.07, -0.12 to -0.02)". In girls the negative association appeared between 14-16 years (-0.05, -0.09 to -0.01), reversing between 16-18 (0.09, 0.02 to 0.16). No standardised effect size is printed for these paths.

> "Cross-lagged paths suggest that depressive symptoms were negatively associated with later academic achievement between ages 11 and 14 (-0.04, -0.08 to 0) and between ages 14 and 16 (-0.07, -0.12 to -0.02), with weak evidence of a positive association found between 16 and 18 years (0.39, -0.01 to 0.79)."
