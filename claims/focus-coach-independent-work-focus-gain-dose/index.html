Evidence¶

Michael D. Mrazek 2026¶

Michael D. Mrazek, Dustyn S. Ransom, Amy R. Texter, Calvin V. Tower, Bryce A. Rutila, Thomas F. Kennedy, Elijah J. Woodson, Jonathan W. Schooler, Alissa J. Mrazek. (2026). Theory-driven design and validation of a tool to enhance academic focus. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10730-y

q2 · i1 · design · r3

Study 2 used a refined two-item percentage-based measure of independent work focus. In the pre-registered room-to-grow subset, focus increased (Table 2: SMD = 0.28), and the increase was larger for students who completed more sessions, b = 0.61, p = .028.

"Among this a priori and pre-registered subset, focus during independent work significantly increased from pre- to post-test (Table 2). This increase was larger for students who completed more sessions, b = 0.61, SE = 0.28, t (378.0) = 2.20, p = .028."

Michael D. Mrazek 2026 (2)¶

Michael D. Mrazek, Dustyn S. Ransom, Amy R. Texter, Calvin V. Tower, Bryce A. Rutila, Thomas F. Kennedy, Elijah J. Woodson, Jonathan W. Schooler, Alissa J. Mrazek. (2026). Theory-driven design and validation of a tool to enhance academic focus. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10730-y

q2 · i? · design · r3

An exploratory whole-sample analysis in Study 2 found the independent-work-focus increase significant (Table 2: SMD = 0.10), with greater improvements associated with more completed sessions, b = 0.57, p = .024. The dose association is correlational.

"An exploratory analysis determined that the increase in independent work focus was also significant across the entire sample (Table 2), with greater improvements in independent work focus associated with a higher number of completed sessions, b = 0.57, SE = 0.25, t (177.9) = 2.28, p = .024."
