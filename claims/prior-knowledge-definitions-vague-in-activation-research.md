The review's own coding of definitions across the 54 included articles, following the Murphy and Alexander (2000) and Dinsmore et al. (2008) coding system distinguishing explicit, implicit, and no definitions. The review reports that "only 10 of the 54 articles explicitly defined this term" and that most articles (n = 43) relied on implicit conceptual definitions.

> "analysis of prior knowledge definitions revealed that only 10 of the 54 articles explicitly defined this term"

### Courtney Hattan 2023 (2)

Courtney Hattan, Patricia A. Alexander, and Sarah M. Lupo. (2023). Leveraging What Students Know to Make Sense of Texts: What the Research Says About Prior Knowledge Activation. Review of Educational Research. https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Leveraging+What+Students+Know+to+Make+Sense+of+Texts+Prior+Knowledge+Activation

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review's coding of activation definitions found nine explicit definitions emphasizing recall from long-term to working memory, and all 54 articles included an implicit definition mentioning the purpose or operationalization of activation. Definitions varied, with some emphasizing integration into long-term memory or the need for external scaffolding.

> "Nine of the 54 articles included explicit definitions of prior knowledge activation, which primarily emphasized the process of recalling previously learned knowledge"
