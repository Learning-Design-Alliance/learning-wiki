## Evidence

### Han 2022

Han, K.-T., Ruan, L.-W., Liao, L.-S., & Tchounwou, P. B. (2022). Effects of Indoor Plants on Human Functions: A Systematic Review with Meta-Analyses. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 19(12), 7454. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph19127454

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r1`

Random-effect meta-analysis of three studies (200 participants) measuring EEG α waves in Hertz; high heterogeneity (I2 = 94.488%). The review reports the difference was "nonsignificant (p = 0.110)".

> "The pooled effect size (MD) was 1.140, and the 95% confidence interval ranged from −0.260 to 2.540. The results indicated that the group with plants had greater EEG α waves than the group without plants, but the difference was nonsignificant (p = 0.110)"

### Han 2022 (2)

Han, K.-T., Ruan, L.-W., Liao, L.-S., & Tchounwou, P. B. (2022). Effects of Indoor Plants on Human Functions: A Systematic Review with Meta-Analyses. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 19(12), 7454. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph19127454

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r1`

Random-effect meta-analysis of two studies (110 participants) measuring EEG β waves in Hertz; high heterogeneity (I2 = 97.133%). The review reports the difference was "not significant (p = 0.381)".

> "The pooled effect size (MD) was 1.455, and the 95% confidence interval ranged from −1.799 to 4.709. Though the results indicated that the group with plants had greater EEG β waves than the group without plants, the difference was not significant (p = 0.381)"
