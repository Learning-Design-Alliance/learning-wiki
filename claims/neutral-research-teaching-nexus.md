# Research performance indicators show little to no relationship with student evaluations of teaching, consistent with a neutral research-teaching nexus

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Discretized research indicators (publications, citations, grants, awards) had trivial associations with overall teaching quality, with confidence intervals crossing zero for nearly all estimates. [→ Murray 2020](#murray-2020)
`q2 i?` Non-parametric Kendall Rank Tau correlations between continuous research indicators and teaching quality were trivial, the strongest being 0.046 for article count. [→ Murray 2020 (2)](#murray-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Murray 2020

Murray, D.; Boothby, C.; Zhao, H.; Minik, V.; Bérubé, N.; Larivière, V.; Sugimoto, C. R. (2020). Exploring the personal and professional factors associated with student evaluations of tenure-track faculty. PLoS ONE 15(6): e0233515. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0233515

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational regression with field-normalized, discretized research indicators on the matched dataset; grants, awards, and citations estimates were similarly trivial. A continuous-indicator model and a principal-component model likewise showed no evidence of a relationship (ANOVA p = 0.47).

> "Compared to faculty with no publications in the past five years, faculty with moderate publication were associated with 0.034 point higher ratings (β = 0.034, 95% CI = [−0.001, 0.069])—this was the only estimate for which confidence intervals only barely crossed zero."

### Murray 2020 (2)

Murray, D.; Boothby, C.; Zhao, H.; Minik, V.; Bérubé, N.; Larivière, V.; Sugimoto, C. R. (2020). Exploring the personal and professional factors associated with student evaluations of tenure-track faculty. PLoS ONE 15(6): e0233515. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0233515

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Non-parametric Kendall Rank Tau analysis (Fig 1B) of continuous research indicators against overall teaching quality; decile-rank trends for citations and publications disappeared when partitioned by discipline (S3 Fig).

> "However, we observed only trivial correlations between research indicators and ratings of overall teaching quality, the strongest having a value of 0.046 for the number of articles, followed by the number of books."
