The article's own position argument in the introduction, offered without new data of its own, resting on cited empirical work. It states that generic and binary declarations "provide limited insight into how tools are actually being used and offer little pedagogical value to students or staff."

> "when a declaration cannot distinguish brainstorming from ghost-writing, it simultaneously fails as an integrity safeguard, as a reflective prompt for students, and as a meaningful source of information for educators adapting their practice."
