# Detailed analogies produce significantly higher post-test success in learning computer hardware than traditional instruction

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students taught computer hardware via detailed analogies scored significantly higher on the post-test than students taught traditionally (84.18% vs 61.97%). [→ Dincer 2011](#dincer-2011)

## Evidence

### Dincer 2011

Dincer, S. (2011). Exploring the Impacts of Analogies on Computer Hardware. The Turkish Online Journal of Educational Technology, 10(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ932231

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

One Factor ANCOVA on pre-/post-test scores of 86 volunteers split into control (traditional) and experimental (detailed analogies) groups of 43 each over two months. The article reports "F(1-83) = 34.14 (sig. = 0.00), p < 0.05" with post-test totals of 61.97% vs 84.18%.

> "Moreover, there was a significant difference between the elaborate analogy used for computer hardware learning and the posttest, F(1-83) = 34.14 (sig. = 0.00), p < 0.05, as shown in Table 4. It was 61.97 % for the control group and 84.18 % for the experimental group."
