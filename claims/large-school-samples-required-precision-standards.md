# Large school samples are required to achieve appropriate precision standards in clustered education experiments

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Because clustering effects are typically large, large school samples are required for education impact evaluations to meet precision standards. [→ Peter Z. Schochet 2005](#peter-z-schochet-2005)

## Evidence

### Peter Z. Schochet 2005

Peter Z. Schochet. (2005). Statistical Power for Random Assignment Evaluations of Education Programs. Princeton, NJ: Mathematica Policy Research. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/statistical-power-for-random-assignment-evaluations-of-education-programs

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r?`

Theoretical and empirical power analysis for experimental education evaluations, focused on standardized test scores of elementary school students, concludes that "large school samples are required" to meet the discussed precision standards.

> "Clustering effects vary by design but are typically large; consequently, large school samples are required, suggesting that most impact studies will only be able to address broad questions rigorously."
