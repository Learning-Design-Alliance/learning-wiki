## Evidence

### Edit Yerushalmi 2016

Edit Yerushalmi, Ryan Sayer, Emily Marshman, Charles Henderson and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Physics graduate teaching assistants' beliefs about a grading rubric: Lessons learned. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.097](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.097)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

End-of-semester homework assignment in which the same 15 TAs re-graded SSD and SSE without a rubric; comparison of pre and post mean scores found no significant change, with the article printing p-values of 0.6 for both solutions in Table II.

> "Table II indicates that TAs’ average scores on the solutions SSD and SSE did not change significantly between the beginning (pre) and the end of the semester (post) after working through the grading activities and having one semester of TA experience."

### Edit Yerushalmi 2016 (2)

Edit Yerushalmi, Ryan Sayer, Emily Marshman, Charles Henderson and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Physics graduate teaching assistants' beliefs about a grading rubric: Lessons learned. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.097](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.097)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

The authors' interpretation of the end-of-semester grading data: despite the PD grading activities and class discussions, "many of the TAs were still willing to infer student understanding in SSE even when there was little explicit evidence of it".

> "It appears that many of the TAs were still willing to infer student understanding in SSE even when there was little explicit evidence of it and did not use a rubric that weighed explanation of reasoning and explication of the PS process suitably."
