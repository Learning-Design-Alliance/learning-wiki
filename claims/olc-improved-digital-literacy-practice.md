## Evidence

### Lovin 2014

Lovin, E., & Lambeth, D. T. (2014). Effects of information and communication technology on engagement and art production for eighth-grade students. li-manager's Journal of School Educational Technology, 10(3). https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Effects+of+Information+and+Communication+Technology+on+Engagement+and+Art+Production+for+Eighth-Grade+Students

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Weekly teacher digital literacy checklist data for the two 15-student classes; the article reports OLC students "located art resources from the Internet more effectively" (M=70) than face-to-face technology users (M=35.56).

> "When working with information about art content, students who participated in the OLC (M=70) located art resources from the Internet more effectively than students using technology in the face-to-face class (M=35.56)."

### Lovin 2014 (2)

Lovin, E., & Lambeth, D. T. (2014). Effects of information and communication technology on engagement and art production for eighth-grade students. li-manager's Journal of School Educational Technology, 10(3). https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Effects+of+Information+and+Communication+Technology+on+Engagement+and+Art+Production+for+Eighth-Grade+Students

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Checklist item analysis showing the groups' most similar scores on the copyright, fair use, and ethics item, which the article attributes to pre-intervention instruction on fair use, copyright, and ethics tied to the Acceptable Use Policy.

> "The scores of the OLC students and students who used technology in the face-to-face class were most similar considering copyright, fair use, and ethics."
