## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who created a social media account in grade 6 scored approximately 0.22 standard deviations lower in Italian and 0.18 lower in mathematics by grade 8, and −0.22 and −0.27 respectively by grade 10, compared with matched late adopters. [→ Gui Marco 2026](#gui-marco-2026)

## Evidence

### Gui Marco 2026

Gui Marco, Respi Chiara, Abbiati Giovanni, Paladini Cristiana, Ercolanoni Sofia, Milzani Francesca, Pirola Tiziana, Vezzoli Giovanni. (2026). Longitudinal effect of early social media use on standardized learning outcomes during school career. Nature Human Behaviour. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-026-02522-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Difference-in-differences analysis with student fixed-effects models and entropy-balancing matching on 5,227 northern Italian students, comparing grade-6 early adopters with grade-9-or-later adopters on INVALSI scores. The study reports students scored "approximately 0.22 standard deviations lower in Italian" and 0.18 in mathematics by grade 8, growing to −0.22 and −0.27 by grade 10. No standardized effect size statistic beyond these regression coefficients is printed.

> "Compared with late adopters, students who created a social media account in grade 6 scored approximately 0.22 standard deviations lower in Italian ((95% CI −0.32, −0.12); df = 7) and 0.18 ((95% CI −0.27, −0.08); df = 7) in mathematics by grade 8."

### Gui Marco 2026 (2)

Gui Marco, Respi Chiara, Abbiati Giovanni, Paladini Cristiana, Ercolanoni Sofia, Milzani Francesca, Pirola Tiziana, Vezzoli Giovanni. (2026). Longitudinal effect of early social media use on standardized learning outcomes during school career. Nature Human Behaviour. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-026-02522-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' contextualization in the Results section: "a 0.2 estimate in our data is equivalent to 6 months of schooling", and the grade-6 loss accounts for about 30% of the grade-5 social-gradient gap in scores. No additional effect size is printed for this framing.

> "In this setting, a 0.2 estimate in our data is equivalent to 6 months of schooling."
