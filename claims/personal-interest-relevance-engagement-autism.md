## Subclaims
`q2 i?` One participant reported not engaging with chemistry because it did not connect to their interests, while a course aligned with their interests enabled hyper-fixation, high engagement, and enjoyment. [→ Westley James 2019](#westley-james-2019)

## Evidence

### Westley James 2019

Westley James, Kamryn Lamons, Roberto Spilka, Caroline Bustamante, Erin Scanlon and Jacquelyn J. Chini. (2019). Hidden walls: STEM course barriers identified by students with disabilities. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1909.02905](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1909.02905)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

IPA interview finding from Participant 8, who has autism: the chemistry course's lack of relevance led to reduced time dedicated to it, while an environmental science class became "a special interest of mine" and was easier to keep up with.

> "I think that the particular disability helped me kinda thrive was in my environmental science class, because it like kind of allowed me to um... kind of create this  hyper-fixation on the subject."
