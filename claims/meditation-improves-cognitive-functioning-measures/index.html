Evidence¶

Lea Waters 2015¶

Lea Waters, Adam Barsky, Amanda Ridd, Kelly Allen. (2015). Contemplative Education: A Systematic, Evidence-Based Review of the effect of Meditation Interventions in Schools. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-014-9258-2

q2 · i2 · review · r2

The review reports Warner's (2005) between-subjects study of TM versus controls. Table 3 prints the effect sizes: working memory d=.45, reflectivity d=1.12, flexibility d=.34, conservation d=.39 (dIG, SDpost).

"Grade K–5 students who had experienced awareness training performed at a higher level than controls on tests of working memory, reflectivity, flexibility, and conservation"

Lea Waters 2015 (2)¶

Lea Waters, Adam Barsky, Amanda Ridd, Kelly Allen. (2015). Contemplative Education: A Systematic, Evidence-Based Review of the effect of Meditation Interventions in Schools. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-014-9258-2

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports Flook et al.'s (2010) RCT of Mindfulness Awareness Practices in 7–9-year-olds (intervention=32, control=32), with null overall executive functioning effects and a compensatory benefit for children with poorer initial EF. No effect size is printed for the subgroup finding.

"No overall differences were evident between intervention and control groups on parent or teacher reported executive functioning (EF). However, children with poorer initial EF showed improved EF subsequent to training compared to controls"
