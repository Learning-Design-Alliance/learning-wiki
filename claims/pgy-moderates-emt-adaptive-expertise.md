## Evidence

### Aliaga L 2024

Aliaga L, Bavolek RA, Cooper B, Mariorenzi A, Ahn J, Kraut A, Duong D, Burger C, Gisondi MA. (2024). Error Management Training and Adaptive Expertise in Learning Computed Tomography Interpretation: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.31600

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Two-way ANOVA (factors: cohort and PGY level) on adaptive expertise posttest scores in the randomized trial. The "significant cohort-PGY interaction, with a medium effect size (η2 = 0.10; P = .006)" held in sensitivity analyses removing PGY 4 participants (η2 = 0.07; P = .008).

> "The 2-way ANOVA on the adaptive expertise cases showed a significant cohort-PGY interaction, with a medium effect size (η2 = 0.10; P = .006), indicating that the learning strategies' effect on improving adaptive expertise depended on the PGY level"

### Aliaga L 2024 (2)

Aliaga L, Bavolek RA, Cooper B, Mariorenzi A, Ahn J, Kraut A, Duong D, Burger C, Gisondi MA. (2024). Error Management Training and Adaptive Expertise in Learning Computed Tomography Interpretation: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.31600

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANOVA within the difficult EMT cohort stratified by PGY; PGY 1 scored highest, followed by PGY 2, then senior residents. The unexpected reversal of the usual seniority advantage ("more effective in improving adaptive expertise for residents earlier in training, with a large effect size (η2 = 0.25; P = .002)") is described with Tukey-corrected pairwise comparisons.

> "The difficult EMT learning strategy was more effective in improving adaptive expertise for residents earlier in training, with a large effect size (η2 = 0.25; P = .002)"
