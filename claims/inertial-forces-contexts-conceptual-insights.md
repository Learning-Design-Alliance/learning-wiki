## Evidence

### Korner 2025

Korner, M., & Likos, C. N. (2025). On the design of a profession-oriented course on Theoretical Mechanics for physics education students. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2511.10102](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2511.10102)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Qualitative interview and open-questionnaire finding on the course's chosen contexts; student feedback on the specific contexts was diverse, with the inertial-forces treatment highly appreciated while Boltzmann's nose was hardly mentioned.

> "The clear differentiation between inertial and rotating frames, explained with reference to contexts of daily life such as an accelerating streetcar or a merry-go-round, led to conceptual insights that were not present in the introductory lecture on experimental physics."

### Korner 2025 (2)

Korner, M., & Likos, C. N. (2025). On the design of a profession-oriented course on Theoretical Mechanics for physics education students. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2511.10102](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2511.10102)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same qualitative corpus: students connected the polar-coordinates emphasis to the Kepler problem treatment and reported gaining quantitative insight into planetary trajectories; this is self-reported perception, not a tested measure.

> "Our choice to introduce and focus on polar coordinates was valued because a deeper, quantitative insight into the planetary trajectories was reported."
