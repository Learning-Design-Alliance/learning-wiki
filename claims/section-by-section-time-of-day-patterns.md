# Section-by-section analyses in the infants and girls' series show the later-working group's advantage across proficiency levels, while the boys' series shows the reverse in every section.

## Evidence

### W. H. Winch 1911

W. H. Winch. (1911). Mental Adaptation During the School Day as Measured by Arithmetical Reasoning.—Part II. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Mental+Adaptation+During+the+School+Day+as+Measured+by+Arithmetical+Reasoning

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of Table VI (third series, infants): marks compared section by section from the highest to the lowest preliminary-mark bands for the 9.40 A.M. and 10.25 A.M. groups; the article states the final-test differences favour the later-working sections. No effect size is printed.

> "The differences between the results of the final tests seem steadily to favor the sections of the later -working group."

### W. H. Winch 1911 (2)

W. H. Winch. (1911). Mental Adaptation During the School Day as Measured by Arithmetical Reasoning.—Part II. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Mental+Adaptation+During+the+School+Day+as+Measured+by+Arithmetical+Reasoning

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Analysis of Table X (fifth series, boys): each preliminary-mark section of the 9.40 A.M. group advanced more than the corresponding section of the 11.30 A.M. group. The article attributes this reversal to the many boys who began paid work at 6.30 A.M. No effect size is printed.

> "In the case of every section we find the children working their final tests earlier in the morning have made a greater advance on their preliminary work than those working later."
