q2 · i? · review · r?

The article summarizes the CCSR literature review (Farrington et al. 2012), which brought together hundreds of studies. It reports that "noncognitive factors have a direct positive relationship to students’ school performance"; no effect sizes are printed.

"A range of studies have found that noncognitive factors have a direct positive relationship to students’ school performance as well as their future outcomes."
