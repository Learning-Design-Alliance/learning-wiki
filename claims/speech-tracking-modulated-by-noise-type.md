# Noise condition modulates neural speech tracking, with better decoding of speech in continuous than quiet or intermittent noise

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` A repeated-measures ANOVA on speech-reconstruction decoding accuracy showed a significant main effect of noise condition, with continuous noise decoded better than quiet (d=0.37) and intermittent (d=0.41) noise. [→ Levy 2025](#levy-2025)

## Evidence

### Levy 2025

Levy, O., Shadi, T., Korisky, A., Bleichner, M. G., & Zion Golumbic, E. (2025). Differential effects of external noise and situational interest on neurophysiological responses during video based learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00392-5

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Encoding/decoding speech-tracking analysis (n=32) across the three noise conditions: decoding ANOVA was significant [F(2,62)=6.846, p=0.002], with "Cohen’s d=0.37" and "Cohen’s d=0.41" for the printed pairwise comparisons.

> "significantly better decoding performance in the continuous noise condition compared to both the quiet condition [t(31) = 3.020, p = 0.007, Cohen’s d=0.37] and the intermittent noise condition [t(31) = 3.362, p = 0.004, Cohen’s d=0.41]."
