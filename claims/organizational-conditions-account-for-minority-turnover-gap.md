# Less positive school organizational conditions account for the higher minority teacher turnover rate; once conditions are controlled, the minority-nonminority turnover difference becomes statistically insignificant

## Evidence

### Ingersoll 2017

Ingersoll, R., May, H., & Collins, G. (2017). Minority teacher recruitment, employment, and retention: 1987 to 2013. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/minority-teacher-recruitment

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Supplementary combined-sample logistic regression with a minority-teacher predictor, run on the SASS/TFS data. The report concludes that "less positive organizational conditions in schools accounted for the higher rates of minority teacher turnover," since the elevated odds (almost 50% higher) fell to below 5% and lost significance.

> "However, once organizational conditions were controlled, the coefficient for minority teachers became statistically insignificant and small (the odds of minority teachers departing were less than 5% higher than the odds for nonminority teachers)."
