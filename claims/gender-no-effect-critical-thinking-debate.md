# Gender is not a significant factor in EFL learners' critical thinking skill gains from debate instruction

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There was no significant main effect for gender on CCTST scores, and no significant interaction between gender and participant group. [→ Tous et al. 2015](#tous-et-al-2015)

## Evidence

### Tous et al. 2015

Danaye Tous, M., Tahriri, A., and Haghighi, S. (2015). The Effect of Instructing Critical Thinking through Debate on Male and Female EFL Learners' Reading Comprehension. Journal of the Scholarship of Teaching and Learning, Vol. 15, No. 4. https://doi.org/10.14434/josotl.v15i4.13191

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The two-way ANOVA on CCTST gain scores showed no significant main effect of gender (F(1, 84) = 1.642, p = 0.204) and no significant gender × participant interaction (F(1, 84) = 0.026, p = 0.873).

> "However, there was no significant main effect for “gender”, F (1, 84) = 1.642, p = 0.204 > 0.05. This result suggested that male and female students were almost at the same level of CT. Further, the interaction between gender and participant was not significant, F (1, 84) = 0.026, p = 0.873 > 0.05."
