## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Compared to white applicants, odds ratios for Asian applicants are significant for higher cut-offs, while those for Black, Latinx, Multi or Native applicants are significant only for a few cut-offs near 710. [→ Nils J. Mikkelsen 2021](#nils-j-mikkelsen-2021)

## Evidence

### Nils J. Mikkelsen 2021

Nils J. Mikkelsen, Nicholas T. Young, and Marcos D. Caballero. (2021). Investigating institutional influence on graduate program admissions by modelling physics GRE cut-off scores. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010109](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010109)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression significance analysis across 19 cut-off models; the article reports racial-group odds ratios "only statis- tically signiﬁcant for some cases". Racial and ethnic minorities were combined into a URM category, which the authors note leads to loss of information.

> "Of the applicant-related features, the odds ratios of U-GPA and gender are always statistically signiﬁcant. However, contrary to expectations, odds ratios between applicants from diﬀerent racial groups were only statis- tically signiﬁcant for some cases."
