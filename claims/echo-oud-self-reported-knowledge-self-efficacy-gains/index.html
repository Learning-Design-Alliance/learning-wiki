Evidence¶

Puckett 2021¶

Puckett, H. M., Bossaller, J. S., & Sheets, L. R. (2021). The impact of project ECHO on physician preparedness to treat opioid use disorder: a systematic review. Addiction Science & Clinical Practice, 16, 6. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-021-00215-z

q2 · i? · review · r2

Pre/post self-report survey in a national ECHO OUD course with 1079 consenting clinicians (of 1315 attendees, 92% from IHS, tribal, or urban AI/AN programs). Means "rose to 10.1 out of 12 for “knowledge”" and 4.4 out of 6 for self-efficacy; no significance test or effect size reported.

"The mean scores before participating in an OUD ECHO were 8.6 out of 12 for “knowledge” and 3.7 out of 6 for “self-efficacy”; after, the mean scores rose to 10.1 out of 12 for “knowledge” and 4.4 out of 6 for “self-efficacy”."

Puckett 2021 (2)¶

Puckett, H. M., Bossaller, J. S., & Sheets, L. R. (2021). The impact of project ECHO on physician preparedness to treat opioid use disorder: a systematic review. Addiction Science & Clinical Practice, 16, 6. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-021-00215-z

q2 · i? · review · r2

Exploratory evaluation of the NYC health+hospitals buprenorphine ECHO program (16 weekly one-hour sessions; 53% of participants attended 10/16). Self-report means "rose to 58.4% for “knowledge”" and 73.2 for self-efficacy; no test statistics printed.

"The mean scores before participating in an OUD ECHO were 51.4% for “knowledge” and 58.1% for “self-efficacy”; after, the mean scores rose to 58.4% for “knowledge” and 73.2 for “self-efficacy”."
