# Giving students both isomorphic problems simultaneously did not improve performance or reveal the underlying similarity

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Response distributions when 49 students worked both isomorphic problems were not statistically distinct from distributions when students worked only one problem, and 28% explicitly judged the problems different. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)](#chandralekha-singh-2016-2)
`q3 i?` No student in the sample solved one problem correctly with attention to both variables while failing on the other; pairing did not help students discern the isomorphism. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)](#chandralekha-singh-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Assessing Student Expertise in Introductory Physics with Isomorphic Problems, Part I: Performance on Non-intuitive Problem Pair from Introductory Physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06499](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06499)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Forty-nine students answered both problems of the IPP in a recitation quiz plus an open-ended question on similarity; response distributions were compared with the single-problem groups. Only "There was only one student in Category (4)" reasoned correctly on both.

> "The pattern of responses when both problems were given to the same student is not statistically distinct from the case when each student answered only one of the problems."

### Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Assessing Student Expertise in Introductory Physics with Isomorphic Problems, Part I: Performance on Non-intuitive Problem Pair from Introductory Physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06499](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06499)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

On the open-ended similarity question given to students answering both problems, "28% explicitly said that the problems are diﬀerent", though the authors judge some stated differences in initial conditions to be careful, reasonable observations.

> "In response to the open-ended question on whether the problems are similar or diﬀerent, 28% explicitly said that the problems are diﬀerent."
