## Evidence

### Tejinder Kaur 2017

Tejinder Kaur, David Blair, Rahul Kumar Choudhary, Yohanes Sudarmo Dua, Alexander Foppoli, and Marjan Zadnik. (2017). Gender response to Einsteinian physics interventions in School. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1712.06323](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1712.06323)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre-programme attitudinal Likert results from the two Australian 10-week interventions on the statement that physics is an interesting subject: the article reports male agreement of "88% in intervention 3 and 82% in intervention 4" versus female agreement of 62% and 51%, showing females entered with lower attitudes.

> "The percentages of female students who agree/strongly agree with this statement were 62% and 51% in interventions 3 and 4 respectively."

### Tejinder Kaur 2017 (2)

Tejinder Kaur, David Blair, Rahul Kumar Choudhary, Yohanes Sudarmo Dua, Alexander Foppoli, and Marjan Zadnik. (2017). Gender response to Einsteinian physics interventions in School. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1712.06323](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1712.06323)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-programme attitudinal results from the three-week Indonesian intervention (intervention 7): "92% of females found physics as an interesting subject" and 89% of males did, up from 22% of males and 8% of females before the programme.

> "After attending a three -week programme on modern physics concepts, 92% of females found physics as an interesting subject. There was also significant improvement observed in male students, whe re 89% of males found physics interesting, while the percentage was only 22% in the beginning."
