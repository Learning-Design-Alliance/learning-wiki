# The modality effect may disappear or reverse for lengthy, complex spoken text

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The advantage of audiovisual presentation over visual-only presentation may vanish or invert when spoken text is lengthy and complex, with written text becoming superior. [→ Fred Paas & John Sweller 2011](#fred-paas-john-sweller-2011)

## Evidence

### Fred Paas & John Sweller 2011

Fred Paas & John Sweller. (2011). An Evolutionary Upgrade of Cognitive Load Theory: Using the Human Motor System and Collaboration to Support the Learning of Complex Cognitive Tasks. Educ Psychol Rev (2012) 24:27–45. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-011-9179-2

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article's own boundary-condition argument, citing Leahy and Sweller (2011): because spoken information is transitory, it must be low in element interactivity to be held in working memory, so the audiovisual advantage is limited to spoken text of low content and complexity.

> "The advantage of using the biologically primary knowledge associated with listening and looking simultaneously may disappear when dealing with lengthy, complex, spoken text. Under those circumstances, the modality effect may disappear or even reverse, with written text being superior to spoken text (Leahy and Sweller 2011)."
