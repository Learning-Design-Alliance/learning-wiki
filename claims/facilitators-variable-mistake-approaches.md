## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Some facilitators openly discussed mistakes in the group, others talked about related procedures or guidelines hoping students would realize mistakes indirectly, and a few did not mention mistakes because they assumed students were aware of them. [→ Lervik W 2025](#lervik-w-2025)

## Evidence

### Lervik W 2025

Lervik W, Solberg M, Wiig AC, Berg H. (2025). Turning nursing students' mistakes into resources for learning in simulation-based training: facilitators' assumptions about providing feedback in debriefing. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06628-z

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview finding: one facilitator (Nancy) stated "they see all the mistakes themselves, they just mention them themselves", representing what the authors call a strong belief in students' abilities to assess their own performances. Other facilitators proactively raised mistakes or used indirect approaches around learning objectives.

> "As a rule, they see all the mistakes themselves, they just mention them themselves. I can never mention anything negative because they see it themselves."
