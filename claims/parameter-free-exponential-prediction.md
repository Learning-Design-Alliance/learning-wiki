## Evidence

### Kail 1991

Robert Kail. (1991). Developmental Change in Speed of Processing During Childhood and Adolescence. Psychological Bulletin, 109(3), 490-501. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.109.3.490

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Secondary analysis of Kail (1986b, Exp. 1), in which 8- to 21-year-olds were tested on mental rotation and name retrieval; Equation 4 accounted for at least 99.65% of variance in youths' RTs and the independently derived exponential captured 98.13% of variance in mi values — a parameter-free prediction.

> "Equation 6 accounts for 98.13% of the variance in the observed slowing coefficients. It is important to emphasize that the function in Figure 3 was not derived empirically from the data in that figure."

### Kail 1991 (2)

Robert Kail. (1991). Developmental Change in Speed of Processing During Childhood and Adolescence. Psychological Bulletin, 109(3), 490-501. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.109.3.490

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Secondary analysis of Kail (1988, Exp. 2) data on four tasks (mental rotation, memory search, mental addition, analogical reasoning), 24 mean RTs per age. Observed slowing coefficients were "systematically smaller than those predicted"; after deleting the analogical reasoning task, Equation 6 accounted for 97.32% of the variance.

> "The values for mh shown in the top right panel of Figure 3, are systematically smaller than those predicted from Equation 4, which accounts for 81.8% of the variance in observed mt values."
