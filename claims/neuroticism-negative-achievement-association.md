## Subclaims
`q4 i0` Neuroticism shows more negative correlations in STEM (ρ = –0.09) than language (ρ = –0.03) domains and with test scores (ρ = –0.10) than grades (ρ = –0.03). [→ Meyer 2023](#meyer-2023)
`q4 i0` The most negative neuroticism correlation is with STEM test scores (ρ = –0.14), and the domain × measure interaction is not significant. [→ Meyer 2023 (2)](#meyer-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Meyer 2023

Meyer, J., Jansen, T., Hübner, N., & Lüdtke, O. (2023). Disentangling the Association Between the Big Five Personality Traits and Student Achievement: Meta-Analytic Evidence on the Role of Domain Specificity and Achievement Measures. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09736-2

`q4 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analysis of 264 neuroticism effect sizes from 61 studies (N = 488,862) found "negative effects on achievement" with both domain and measure moderation; all printed correlations are below r = .10 in magnitude.

> "We found significant differences when comparing language (ρ = –.03) and STEM (ρ = –.09) domains (RQ1; z = 4.47; p < 0.001) and also when comparing grades and standardized test scores (ρ = –.03 vs. –0.10; RQ2; z = 2.86; p = 0.004)"

### Meyer 2023 (2)

Meyer, J., Jansen, T., Hübner, N., & Lüdtke, O. (2023). Disentangling the Association Between the Big Five Personality Traits and Student Achievement: Meta-Analytic Evidence on the Role of Domain Specificity and Achievement Measures. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09736-2

`q4 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

In the specific comparisons, test scores showed more negative neuroticism correlations than grades in both domains, and STEM exceeded language in negativity within both measures; the overall interaction was not significant (t[49] = 0.26; p = 0.793).

> "we found more negative correlations of neuroticism with test scores compared to grades in both STEM subject domains (ρ = –.14 vs. –0.07; z = –2.48; p = 0.013) and language subject domains (ρ = − 0.06 vs. .00; z = –2.81; p = 0.005)"
