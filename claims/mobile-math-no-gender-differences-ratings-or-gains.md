## Evidence

### Fabian 2018

Fabian, K., Topping, K. J., & Barron, I. G. (2018). Using mobile technologies for mathematics: effects on student attitudes and achievement. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-018-9580-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Independent t-tests comparing male and female activity and tablet usability ratings in the quasi-experimental study "showed no significant gender difference in all categories".

> "An independent t-test of the ratings categorised by gender showed no signiﬁcant gender difference in all categories."

### Fabian 2018 (2)

Fabian, K., Topping, K. J., & Barron, I. G. (2018). Using mobile technologies for mathematics: effects on student attitudes and achievement. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-018-9580-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Within the experimental group, gain-score t-tests showed "no significant difference in the gain scores", t(22) = -1.567, d = -0.64, though a medium descriptive effect favoured female students; equivalence was not tested.

> "There was no signiﬁcant difference in the gain scores, t(22) = -1.567, d = -0.64 but there was a medium effect size in favour of female students."
