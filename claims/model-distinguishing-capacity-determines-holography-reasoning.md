# Students' capacity for distinguishing between models of light determines whether they can apply holography knowledge in a problem-oriented manner

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Only students with a developed capacity for distinguishing between the models of light could handle transfer tasks and argue in a conscious, problem-oriented manner, while students without this capacity jumped randomly between model conceptions. ## Evidence

### Martin Erik Horn 2004

Martin Erik Horn, Helmut F. Mikelskis. (2004). Concept Mapping as an Instrument for Evaluating an Instruction Unit on Holography. ESERA Conference, Noordwijkerhout. Originally published 2003. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0411081](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0411081)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Interview and concept-map findings from the 54-student pre-post study. Students lacking "capacity for distinguishing" between models delivered consistent explanations less often; this was also reflected in their number of correctly marked propositions. No effect size is printed.

> "Students without this capacity for distinguishing randomly jumped between different model conceptions and were able to impart consistent patterns of explanation less frequently than students who had a more distinct capacity."
