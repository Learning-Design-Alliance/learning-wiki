# STH infection and stunting are associated with poorer Flanker task performance in sex-specific patterns: less accurate congruent responses in infected girls, slower congruent responses in stunted girls, and slower incongruent responses in stunted boys

## Evidence

### Beckmann 2021

Beckmann, J.; Lang, C.; du Randt, R.; Gresse, A.; Long, K.Z.; Ludyga, S.; Müller, I.; Nqweniso, S.; Pühse, U.; Utzinger, J.; Walter, C.; Gerber, M. (2021). Prevalence of Stunting and Relationship between Stunting and Associated Risk Factors with Academic Achievement and Cognitive Function: A Cross-Sectional Study with South African Primary School Children. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 18(8), 4218. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18084218

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Mixed regression models for girls (n = 613; n = 598 after chance-level exclusion). Table 5 prints STH β = −0.16, p = 0.015 for congruent accuracy and stunting β = 0.09, p = 0.006 for congruent reaction time; the quoted sentence reports the significant associations.

> "Being infected with STH was associated with less accurate responses to congruent Flanker task stimuli (p < 0.05), whereas being stunted was related to higher reaction times in response to congruent Flanker task stimuli."

### Beckmann 2021 (2)

Beckmann, J.; Lang, C.; du Randt, R.; Gresse, A.; Long, K.Z.; Ludyga, S.; Müller, I.; Nqweniso, S.; Pühse, U.; Utzinger, J.; Walter, C.; Gerber, M. (2021). Prevalence of Stunting and Relationship between Stunting and Associated Risk Factors with Academic Achievement and Cognitive Function: A Cross-Sectional Study with South African Primary School Children. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 18(8), 4218. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18084218

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Mixed regression models for boys (n = 664; n = 641 after chance-level exclusion). Table 4 prints stunting β = 0.08, p = 0.021 for incongruent reaction time; the quoted sentence describes the direction of the association.

> "When incongruent stimuli were presented, stunted boys had slightly higher reaction times in the Flanker task than their non-stunted counterparts."
