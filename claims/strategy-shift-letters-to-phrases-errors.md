## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Subjects first read by letters, then better subjects read two or three letters, a word, or a phrase at a time, and some eventually claimed to read by sentences; this transition was frequently accompanied by more errors, which disappeared as the new procedure became fixed. [→ Baldwin 1913](#baldwin-1913)

## Evidence

### Baldwin 1913

Baldwin, B. T. (1913). The learning of delinquent adolescent girls as shown by a substitution test. The Journal of Educational Psychology, IV(6), 317-332. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0069968

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Interpretation of introspective notes collected at the end of practice periods, in which subjects reported reading larger units and remembering whole words. One subject wrote that she remembered the word and kept her eyes on the keyboard.

> "The transition from letters to words or phrases is fre- quently accompanied by more errors, but these tend to disap- pear as rapidly as the new mode of procedure becomes more fixed."

### Baldwin 1913 (2)

Baldwin, B. T. (1913). The learning of delinquent adolescent girls as shown by a substitution test. The Journal of Educational Psychology, IV(6), 317-332. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0069968

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

From the same introspective-notes section: subjects reported knowing where to look for letters without searching the whole paper, and most continued looking up the numerical equivalents because that required less time than thinking them out.

> "Familiarity in the location of the letters resulted in increased speed."
