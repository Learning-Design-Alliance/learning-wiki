# Autograder feedback improves induction proof writing relative to rubric-only self-evaluation

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who interacted with the autograder gained significantly more points from initial to best submission than students who self-evaluated with the rubric alone. [→ Zhao 2025](#zhao-2025)

## Evidence

### Zhao 2025

Zhao, C., Silva, M., & Poulsen, S. (2025). Autograding Mathematical Induction Proofs with Natural Language Processing. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education, 35, 3202–3232. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-025-00498-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Classroom user study in a discrete mathematics course (68 Self-eval, 59 First, 42 Random students), with an OLS regression controlling for initial submission score. First-group students gained "11.6 and 11.3 more points" over Self-eval, with the regression coefficients reported at p < 0.001; the article prints no standardized effect size.

> "Compared withSelf-eval students, students in theFirst and Random groups are able to gain respectively 11.6 and 11.3 more points from the proofs they write after controlling for their initial submission performances."
