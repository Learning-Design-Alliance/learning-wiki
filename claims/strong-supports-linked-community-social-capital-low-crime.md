# Schools with strong essential supports were more likely to sit in communities with strong social capital, low crime, and low density of abused or neglected children

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Schools with strong essential supports were more likely to exist in communities with active religious participation, collective efficacy, extensive outside connections, low crime rates, and low density of abused or neglected children. [→ Sebring 2006](#sebring-2006)
`q2 i?` Communities with weak social capital, high crime, and higher percentages of abused or neglected children were likely to have schools with weak essential supports. [→ Sebring 2006](#sebring-2006)

## Evidence

### Sebring 2006

Sebring, P. B., Allensworth, E., Bryk, A. S., Easton, J. Q., & Luppescu, S. (2006). The Essential Supports for School Improvement. Research Report, Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/essential-supports-school-improvement

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Community-context analysis using social capital measures from PHDCN surveys, Chicago Police Department crime statistics, and Chapin Hall data on substantiated abuse or neglect cases per school. Strong-support schools clustered in low-crime, low-density communities; the reverse held for weak supports.

> "Schools with strong supports also were found more often in communities with a low crime rate, and they were far more likely to exist in school communities with a low density of abused or neglected children."
