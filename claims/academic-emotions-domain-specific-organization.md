## Evidence

### Goetz et al. 2007

Thomas Goetz, Anne C. Frenzel, Reinhard Pekrun, Nathan C. Hall, and Oliver Lüdtke. (2007). Between- and Within-Domain Relations of Students' Academic Emotions. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.99.4.715

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Structural equation modeling (LISREL 8.72) of latent domain-specific emotion factors for five emotions in four domains, with correlated uniquenesses for parallel items, in 542 German high school students. The study reports that interrelations were "relatively weak, indicating generally weak between-domains relations for the emotion constructs."

> "Even disattenuated, the interrelations among the domain- and emotion-specific factors were relatively weak, indicating generally weak between-domains relations for the emotion constructs."

### Goetz et al. 2007 (2)

Thomas Goetz, Anne C. Frenzel, Reinhard Pekrun, Nathan C. Hall, and Oliver Lüdtke. (2007). Between- and Within-Domain Relations of Students' Academic Emotions. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.99.4.715

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same SEM analysis of 542 students. The study reports coefficients below .35 in Grade 8 and below .20 in Grade 11, with exceptions for mathematics–physics emotions (r .38 to .61 in Grade 8; .48 to .64 in Grade 11) and pride between English and German.

> "With the exception of substantial relations between emotions in mathematics and physics and the relatively strong relations between pride in English and German, all coefficients were below .35 in the Grade 8 sample and below .20 in the Grade 11 sample."
