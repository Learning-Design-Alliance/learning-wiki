A cited study on learning journals reviewed in the educational sciences section found reflection type moderates the association with achievement: "the type of re ﬂection is important". Separate studies reported journals yielding stronger meta-control and meta-knowledge, with good instructions crucial and summarising yielding less benefit than reflective writing.

> "Ofﬂine meta-knowledge processes operationalised by learning journals were found to be positively associated with academic achievement when related to re ﬂection on learning activities but negatively associated when related to re ﬂection on learning materials, indicating that the type of re ﬂection is important 89."
