## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Averaged across male-instructed classes, female ACT-adjusted grades were 0.18 points (0.20 SD) lower than male grades (p = 0.001); in female-instructed classes the gap was not significant (p = 0.48). [→ Bailey E. G. 2020](#bailey-e-g-2020)

## Evidence

### Bailey E. G. 2020

Bailey E. G., Greenall R. F., Baek D. M., Morris C., Nelson N., Quirante T. M., Rice N. S., Rose S., Williams K. R. (2020). Female In-Class Participation and Performance Increase with More Female Peers and/or a Female Instructor in Life Sciences Courses. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-12-0266

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Registrar final course grades for 31 classes (1949 students), ACT-adjusted via two-way ANCOVA (classroom × student gender). One-sample t tests of classroom gaps: male-instructed classes p = 0.001 (n = 17 classes); female-instructed classes p = 0.48 (n = 14 classes), where the average female grade was 0.05 higher (0.05 SD, not significant). Only two individual male-instructed classes (M12, M15) were significant alone.

> "there was a small but consistent gender performance gap in favor of male students (the average female grade was 0.18 points lower than the average male grade on the four-point scale, which is 0.20 SD)"
