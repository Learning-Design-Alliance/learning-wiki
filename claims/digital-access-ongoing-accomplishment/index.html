Evidence¶

Smythe 2017¶

Smythe, S., & Breshears, S. (2017). Complicating Access: Digital Inequality And Adult Learning In A Public-Access Computing Space. The Canadian Journal for the Study of Adult Education, 29(1), 67-81. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1139334

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Ethnographic case study of one participant (Malek) at the Digital Café over a year, drawn from field notes and interviews. The authors report that Malek "moved in and out of digital connectivity" as devices, subscriptions, and support came and went, illustrating access as a provisional assemblage rather than a stable state.

"Malek moved in and out of digital connectivity, which suggests that access is not an event but an ongoing accomplishment that requires the provisional assemblage of different kinds of materials: a familiar and working device, an Internet connection, a password, a charger, a patient tutor, timely and appropriate instruction, and more."
