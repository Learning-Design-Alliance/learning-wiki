## Evidence

### Smith et al. 2017

Stephen W. Smith, Ann P. Daunic, James Algina, Donna L. Pitts, Kristen L. Merrill, Michelle M. Cumming, and Courtney Allen. (2017). Self-Regulation for Students With Emotional and Behavioral Disorders: Preliminary Effects of the I Control Curriculum. Journal of Emotional and Behavioral Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1177/1063426616661702

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre–post pilot analysis in Mplus of 152 students in 17 randomly assigned self-contained classrooms, with pretest as covariate. The Covariate × Treatment interaction for the BRIEF BRI was significant, and simple effects favored I Control at moderate and high baseline risk, with the printed ES values of −.38 and −.76.

> "For BRI, regression lines indicated that simple effect tests were significant when the covariate score was moderate (z = −1.68, p = .046, ES = −.38) or high (z = −3.284, p = .000, ES = −.76), indicating relatively moderate or high risk at baseline."

### Smith et al. 2017 (2)

Stephen W. Smith, Ann P. Daunic, James Algina, Donna L. Pitts, Kristen L. Merrill, Michelle M. Cumming, and Courtney Allen. (2017). Self-Regulation for Students With Emotional and Behavioral Disorders: Preliminary Effects of the I Control Curriculum. Journal of Emotional and Behavioral Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1177/1063426616661702

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same Mplus pilot analysis, the BRIEF Metacognitive Index Initiate subscale showed a significant treatment main effect (z = −2.09, p = .037, ES = −.53 in Table 2), with the article stating the advantage held "regardless of pretest score."

> "were better able to initiate (ability to begin a task or activity and indepen- dently generate ideas, responses, or problem-solving strate- gies) than control students, as measured by the MI of the BRIEF."
