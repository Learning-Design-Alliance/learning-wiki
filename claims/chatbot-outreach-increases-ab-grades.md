# Academic chatbot outreach increased the likelihood students earned an A or B by four percentage points across two large online undergraduate courses

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students randomly assigned to receive chatbot messaging were four percentage points more likely to earn an A or B relative to 61% of control-group students. [→ Meyer 2026](#meyer-2026)

## Evidence

### Meyer 2026

Meyer, Katharine, Lindsay C. Page, Catherine Mata, Eric N. Smith, B. Tyler Walsh, C. Lindsey Fifield, Michelle Tyson, Amy Eremionkhale, Michael Evans, Shelby Frost, and Eye Eoun Jung. (2026). Let's Chat: Leveraging Chatbot Outreach for Improved Course Performance. (EdWorkingPaper: 22-564). https://edworkingpapers.com/ai22-564

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Intent-to-treat estimates from randomized controlled trials pooling Government and Microeconomics samples (1,568 and 915 randomized students). Treatment increased A-or-B earning "relative to 61% of control-group students (about a 7% increase)"; Westfall-Young adjusted p-values show significant increase in earning a B or higher but not an A.

> "Treatment students are four percentage points more likely to earn an A relative to 36% of control-group students (about an 11% increase) and arere four percentage points more likely to earn an A or B relative to 61% of control-group students (about a 7% increase)."
