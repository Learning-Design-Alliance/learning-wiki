## Evidence

### Hewitson 2021

Hewitson, C. L., Shukur, S. T., Cartmill, J., Crossley, M. J., & Kaplan, D. M. (2021). Camera realignment imposes a cost on laparoscopic performance. Scientific Reports, 11, 17634. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-96965-6

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Mixed-design ANOVA on movement smoothness (dimensionless total squared jerk) in the peg-transfer experiment; the main effect of realignment was significant ("η2 = 0.4") with a post hoc "d = − 0.9".

> "Realignment decreased movement smoothness for both groups as evidenced by a significant main effect of condition [F(1,18) = 12.12, p < 0.001, η2 = 0.4], and supported by a post hoc t-test [t(19.0) =  − 3.05, p < 0.01, d =  − 0.9]."

### Hewitson 2021 (2)

Hewitson, C. L., Shukur, S. T., Cartmill, J., Crossley, M. J., & Kaplan, D. M. (2021). Camera realignment imposes a cost on laparoscopic performance. Scientific Reports, 11, 17634. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-96965-6

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Group × condition interaction on smoothness in the same experiment ("η2 = 0.27"), with a post hoc group comparison under realignment showing "d = 1.27", indicating controls were more severely degraded.

> "However, movement smoothness was more severely degraded for controls than for experts as evidenced by a significant group × condition interaction [F(1,18) = 6.79, p < 0.05, η2 = 0.27], and supported by a post hoc t-test comparing groups under realignment [rot × group: t(18.0) = 2.84, p < 0.05, d = 1.27]."
