# Negative feedback lowered math self-concept indirectly via enjoyment and hope; positive feedback raised it via hope

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` Negative feedback had small significant negative indirect effects on math self-concept via enjoyment (β = -.05, p = .046) and hope (β = -.15, p = .001), with no direct effects of feedback on self-concept. [→ Van der Beek 2025](#van-der-beek-2025)
`q3 i1` Positive feedback had a small positive indirect effect on math self-concept via hope (β = .07, p = .050). [→ Van der Beek 2025](#van-der-beek-2025)

## Evidence

### Van der Beek 2025

Van der Beek, J.P.J.; Van der Ven, S.H.G.; Van de Weijer-Bergsma, E.; Kroesbergen, E.H. (2025). How feedback affects emotions, performance and self-concept in mathematics. Learning and Instruction 99, 102169. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102169

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Model 2 of the randomized path analysis (n = 219), with math self-concept (PISA-2003-derived scale, α = .91) as outcome: "no direct effects of feedback on math self-concept", but the printed indirect effects via enjoyment (β = .05) and hope (β = .15) as quoted, plus positive feedback via hope (β = .07).

> "There were no direct effects of feedback on math self-concept, but the results did show small significant negative indirect effects of negative feedback via enjoyment (β = .05, SE = .03, p = .046) and hope (β = .15, SE = .05, p = .001) on math self-concept, and a small positive indirect effect of positive feedback via hope on math self-concept (β = .07, SE = .04, p = .050)."
