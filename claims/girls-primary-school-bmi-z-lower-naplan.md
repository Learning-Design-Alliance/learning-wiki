## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among girls, BMI z-score at age 6–8 years was more strongly associated with lower NAPLAN scores in most domains at age 9 years than BMI z-score at age 3–5 years, with a one-unit higher BMI z-score associated with scores up to 21 points lower in numeracy, spelling and language. ## Evidence

### Watson A 2023

Watson A, D'Souza NJ, Timperio A, Cliff DP, Okely AD, Hesketh KD. (2023). Longitudinal associations between weight status and academic achievement in primary school children. Pediatric Obesity. https://doi.org/10.1111/ijpo.12975

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear regression in the HAPPY longitudinal cohort (Table 2), controlling for child age, maternal BMI, SEP, household composition and siblings, adjusted for recruitment centre, stratified by sex. The article reports "scores up to 21 points lower in numeracy, spelling and language" for girls; only unstandardized B coefficients are printed, so no effect size is available.

> "For girls, compared with BMI z‐score at age 3–5 years, BMI z‐score at age 6–8 years was more strongly associated with lower scores in most NAPLAN domains at age 9 years. Specifically, a one‐unit higher BMI z‐score at age 6–8 years was associated with scores up to 21 points lower in numeracy, spelling and language at age 9 years."
