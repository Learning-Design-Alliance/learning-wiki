# In decentralized networks, social influence also improves the accuracy of the group mean, contrary to the prediction that the mean stays fixed

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After two rounds of social influence in decentralized networks, the group mean's average error fell 10% (0.69 to 0.62 SD), which the DeGroot i.i.d. model did not predict. [→ Becker et al. 2017](#becker-et-al-2017)

## Evidence

### Becker et al. 2017

Joshua Becker, Devon Brackbill, and Damon Centola. (2017). Network dynamics of social influence in the wisdom of crowds. PNAS. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1615978114

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Trial-level analysis of 13 decentralized networks found the group mean error dropped from 0.69 to 0.62 SD, "resulting in a 10% reduction in the average error of the group mean"; the article explains this via non-i.i.d. self-weights rather than printing an effect size.

> "the average error of the group mean at round 3 (0.62 SD) was significantly lower than at round 1 (0.69 SD) (n = 13 trials, P <0.01, Wilcoxon signed rank test), resulting in a 10% reduction in the average error of the group mean"
