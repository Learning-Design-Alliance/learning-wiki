# More benchmark videos at varied performance levels may clarify the expected standard, but increasing video number or explanation complexity risks overload that reduces engagement; examiners judged two to three videos an optimal compromise

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants believed multiple videos at different levels with detailed rationales would illustrate scale points and build a clearer sense of expected performance, though these variables were not manipulated. [→ Edwards 2025](#edwards-2025)

## Evidence

### Edwards 2025

Edwards, R. J., Yeates, P., Lefroy, J., & McKinley, R. (2025). Understanding contexts and mechanisms through which video based benchmarking promotes alignment of examiners' scoring in objective structured clinical exams. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-025-10454-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Participant supposition within the realist interviews, not a manipulated comparison; the article explicitly notes these variables were not manipulated and the desire was strongest among novice examiners.

> "participants believed that multiple benchmark videos showing different levels of student performance (i.e. borderline vs. good) or more detailed explanations of the expert panel's rationale would illustrate the different points on the assessment scale through comparison"

### Edwards 2025 (2)

Edwards, R. J., Yeates, P., Lefroy, J., & McKinley, R. (2025). Understanding contexts and mechanisms through which video based benchmarking promotes alignment of examiners' scoring in objective structured clinical exams. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-025-10454-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Examiners' judgements within the interviews: additional videos would bring "a somewhat diminishing return" and time constraints, so two to three videos were seen as balancing comparative information against load.

> "Participants judged that two to three videos, depending on length, seemed an optimal compromise between comparative information and the load placed on participants."
