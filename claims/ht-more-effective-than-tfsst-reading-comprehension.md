`q2 i?` Ninth-grade students taught reading comprehension with the Herringbone technique scored significantly higher on a multiple-choice post-test than students taught with the Tri Focus Steve Snyder technique (post-test means 77.5 vs 68). [→ Rosyida 2018](#rosyida-2018)

## Evidence

### Rosyida 2018

Rosyida, F., & Ghufron, M. A. (2018). Herringbone and Tri Focus Steve Snyder Technique: The Techniques for Teaching Reading Comprehension Viewed from Students' Reading Habit. International Journal of Instruction, 11(3), 603-616. https://doi.org/10.12973/iji.2018.11341a

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experiment in one Indonesian junior high school: class IX-A (n=26) taught with HT, class IX-B (n=25) with TFSST, six meetings, then a 30-item multiple-choice post-test analysed by two-way ANOVA. The article reports "Fobs= 436.6268 is higher than Fα= 4.4071" for the teaching-technique factor.

> "Based on the mean score, students who were taught by using HT obtained 77.5; whereas the students who were taught by using TFSST obtained 68, so it can conclude d that students who were taught by using HT is better than the students who were taught by using TFSST."
