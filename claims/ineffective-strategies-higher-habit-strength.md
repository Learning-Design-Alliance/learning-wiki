# Ineffective learning strategies show substantially higher habit strength than effective strategies in a proof-of-concept survey

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a proof-of-concept study, strategy effectiveness significantly predicted habit strength, with ineffective strategies (rereading, highlighting) associated with higher habit strength than effective strategies (self-testing, concept mapping). [→ Ann-Kathrin Krause 2025](#ann-kathrin-krause-2025)

## Evidence

### Ann-Kathrin Krause 2025

Ann-Kathrin Krause, Jasmin Breitwieser, Garvin Brod. (2025). Understanding Learning Strategy Use Through the Lens of Habit. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10087-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Proof-of-concept survey of 64 Bachelor's and Master's students rating habit strength of four strategies via SRHI items on a 7-point Likert scale. A linear mixed-effects regression with planned contrasts found "a statistically significant effect of strategy effectiveness on habit strength"; the model explained 41% of variance (R²marginal = 0.41). No standardized effect size was printed.

> "The analysis revealed a statistically significant effect of strategy effectiveness on habit strength, B = 2.43, SE = 0.18, p <.001, indicating that the ineffective strategies were associated with substantially higher habit strength than the effective strategies."
