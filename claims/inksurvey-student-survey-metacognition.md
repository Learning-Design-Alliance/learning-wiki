## Evidence

### F.V. Kowalski 2013

F.V. Kowalski, Gus Greivel, Todd Ruskell, Tracy Q. Gardner, S.E. Kowalski, Thomas J. Colling, J.V. Gutierrez Cuba, and Enrique Palou. (2013). Using InkSurvey with Pen-Enabled Mobile Devices for Real-Time Formative Assessment II. Indications of Effectiveness in Diverse Educational Environments. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1308.3729](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1308.3729)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Anonymous survey of 63 junior-level undergraduate physics students in a Fall 2012 Advanced Laboratory class; 54 (86%) agreed their responses gave the instructor better understanding of their knowledge. Student comments emphasized instant feedback letting the instructor re-explain or move on.

> "In an anonymous survey of 63 students, 54 students (86%) agreed that their responses with InkSurvey gave the instructor a better understanding of what they do and do not understand."

### F.V. Kowalski 2013 (2)

F.V. Kowalski, Gus Greivel, Todd Ruskell, Tracy Q. Gardner, S.E. Kowalski, Thomas J. Colling, J.V. Gutierrez Cuba, and Enrique Palou. (2013). Using InkSurvey with Pen-Enabled Mobile Devices for Real-Time Formative Assessment II. Indications of Effectiveness in Diverse Educational Environments. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1308.3729](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1308.3729)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same survey probed metacognition: 48 of 63 students (76%) reported that constructing InkSurvey responses helped them understand class content or recognize gaps. Comments noted that drawing solutions "shows me my shortcomings with the material very clearly".

> "Forty-eight students (76%) found that creating their responses in InkSurvey actually helped them better understand what was being covered in class, or helped them realize what they did and did not understand."
