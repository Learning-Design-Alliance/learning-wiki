# Preliminary evidence suggests perceived problem behavior effects on teaching stress are mediated by teachers' perceptions of relationship conflict

## Evidence

### Jantine L. Spilt 2011

Jantine L. Spilt, Helma M. Y. Koomen, & Jochem T. Thijs. (2011). Teacher Wellbeing: The Importance of Teacher–Student Relationships. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-011-9170-y

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports preliminary evidence that "the effects of perceived problem behavior on teaching stress are (fully) mediated by teachers ’perceptions of conflict" within the dyadic relationship, supporting a mediational model.

> "Indeed, preliminary evidence suggests that the effects of perceived problem behavior on teaching stress are (fully) mediated by teachers ’perceptions of conflict within the relationship with that student (Koomen and Spilt 2011)."

### Jantine L. Spilt 2011 (2)

Jantine L. Spilt, Helma M. Y. Koomen, & Jochem T. Thijs. (2011). Teacher Wellbeing: The Importance of Teacher–Student Relationships. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-011-9170-y

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports longitudinal school-year research showing reciprocal relations between perceptions of externalizing behavior and conflict representations; the cycle "was driven by perceptions of heightened levels of child problem behavior in the beginning."

> "Longitudinal research over the course of a school year indicates that teachers ’perceptions of externalizing student behavior are reciprocally related to representations of conflictual relationships, suggesting that perceptions of problem behavior exacerbate representations of conflict and vice versa (Doumen et al . 2008)."
