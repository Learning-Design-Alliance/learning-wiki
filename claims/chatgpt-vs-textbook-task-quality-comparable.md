## Evidence

### Stefan Küchemann 2023

Stefan Küchemann, Steffen Steinert, Natalia Revenga, Matthias Schweinberger, Yavuz Dinc, Karina E. Avila, and Jochen Kuhn. (2023). Physics task development of prospective physics teachers using ChatGPT. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020128](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020128)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Group comparison in the randomized controlled trial, in which 26 prospective physics teachers created kinematics tasks with ChatGPT 3.5 or a textbook; t-tests across the rated categories found "no signiﬁcant dif- ference in task speciﬁcity, correctness, whether phrasing was misleading, or whether tasks had adequate diﬃculty".

> "A t-test revealed no signiﬁcant dif- ference in task speciﬁcity, correctness, whether phrasing was misleading, or whether tasks had adequate diﬃculty (eachp> 0.05)."

### Stefan Küchemann 2023 (2)

Stefan Küchemann, Steffen Steinert, Natalia Revenga, Matthias Schweinberger, Yavuz Dinc, Karina E. Avila, and Jochen Kuhn. (2023). Physics task development of prospective physics teachers using ChatGPT. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020128](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020128)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Ratings of the developed tasks on the binary specificity category showed "both groups achieved only low values in task speciﬁcity of around 0.4", meaning relevant information to solve the tasks was missing in both conditions.

> "In contrast, both groups achieved only low values in task speciﬁcity of around 0.4, indicating that students in both groups had diﬃculty providing suﬃcient information for tasks to be solvable."
