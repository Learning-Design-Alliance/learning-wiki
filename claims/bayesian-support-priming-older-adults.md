## Evidence

### Thevenot 2018

Thevenot, C., Dewi, J., Bagnoud, J., Wolfer, P., Fayol, M., & Castel, C. (2018). The use of automated procedures by older adults with high arithmetic skills during addition problem solving. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Bayesian paired t-tests with Cauchy prior 0.707 on contrasts with significant SOA effects in older adults. "very strong for small addition problems (BF10 = 86.12)" but BF01 = 1.81 for +1 problems, weaker than in young adults.

> "In older adults, the support for alternative hypothesis was substantial for large addition problems (BF10 = 3.41) and very strong for small addition problems (BF10 = 86.12). However, contrary to the frequentist approach repported above, the estimated Bayes factor (null/hypothesis) of BF01 = 1.81 for 1-digit addition problems suggested an anecdotal support for null hypothesis of a model without an effect of SOA."
