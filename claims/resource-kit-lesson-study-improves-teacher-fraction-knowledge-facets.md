## Evidence

### Catherine Lewis & Rebecca Perry 2014

Catherine Lewis & Rebecca Perry. (2014). Lesson Study with Mathematical Resources: A Sustainable Model for Locally-led Teacher Professional Learning. Mathematics Teacher Education and Development. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1046670

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomised controlled trial: 39 educator groups randomly assigned to three conditions; this report focuses on 13 experimental lesson study groups (N = 73 teachers) versus control groups (N = 140). The article reports "significant increases in three of the four facets" (fraction as number, understanding of the whole, unit fractions; not equal parts) with paired-difference t tests within the experimental group (e.g., fraction as number t = 4.802, 72 df, p < 0.001; linear representation t = 7.25, 72 df, p < 0.001). Control teachers showed no significant pre-to-post change on those facets, but both groups significantly reduced mathematics errors (control t = 2.568, 139 df, p < 0.05), with a greater reduction in the experimental group. Each group was tested separately against its own pre-test; the article reports no test of the difference in change between groups and no effect size, and its authors note the design cannot separate the resource kit from the lesson study process.

> "Teachers who participated in lesson study with mathematical resources showed significant increases in three of the four facets of fractions knowledge, and in use of linear measurement representations."
