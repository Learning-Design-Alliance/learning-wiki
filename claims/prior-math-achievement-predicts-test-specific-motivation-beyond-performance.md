## Evidence

### Benden 2026

Benden, D. K., & Lauermann, F. (2026). The Interplay of Personal and Situational Factors in Situated Expectancy-Value Theory: Cross-Classified Analyses of Students' Situation-Specific Motivations in the Math Domain. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0001032

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Student-level predictors in Model 3 of the cross-classified analyses showed prior achievement effects on motivation beyond actual test performance; utility value was the exception, which the authors attribute to ceiling effects (M = 4.53, SD = 0.97).

> "Students with comparatively higher high school math grades had significantly higher test-specific self-concepts, found the test content more interesting, and experienced lower costs (0.09 ≤ |b| ≤ 0.14, ps < .001), even after controlling for students' test performance."

### Benden 2026 (2)

Benden, D. K., & Lauermann, F. (2026). The Interplay of Personal and Situational Factors in Situated Expectancy-Value Theory: Cross-Classified Analyses of Students' Situation-Specific Motivations in the Math Domain. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0001032

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

In the same Model 3 student-level analyses, attendance of advanced high-school math courses predicted test-specific self-concept, intrinsic value, and costs; the article reports personal characteristics explained up to 2% additional variance at the student level.

> "students who completed advanced math courses in high school reported higher test-specific self-concepts, intrinsic values, and lower costs (0.15 ≤ |b| ≤ 0.20, ps < .001)"
