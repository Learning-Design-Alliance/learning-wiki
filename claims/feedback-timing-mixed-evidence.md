## Evidence

### Hepburn CY 2026

Hepburn CY. (2026). Evidence to support integrating feedback best practice for computer‐based assessment in pharmacology education. Br J Clin Pharmacol. https://doi.org/10.1002/bcp.70302

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The review reports a small study in second-year graduate entry medical students comparing immediate and delayed elaborate feedback; it "found no effect of feedback timing" on retention.

> "a small study (n = 41) of retention of constructs from immediate (feedback after each question) vs. delayed (feedback after test item group) elaborate feedback on near and far transfer single best answer questions focused on diagnostic reasoning found no effect of feedback timing"

### Hepburn CY 2026 (2)

Hepburn CY. (2026). Evidence to support integrating feedback best practice for computer‐based assessment in pharmacology education. Br J Clin Pharmacol. https://doi.org/10.1002/bcp.70302

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports van der Kleij et al.'s study of first-year commercial economics students (n = 152) in the Netherlands, which found no summative performance difference across feedback timing conditions.

> "there was no difference in summative assessment performance for either students who had immediate corrective and elaborate feedback, delayed corrective and elaborate feedback or only a delayed grade with no corrective feedback; rather, the primary predictor of performance is student characteristics"
