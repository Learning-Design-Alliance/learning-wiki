# Students perform better on conceptual questions of an IPP when the paired quantitative question is also given, for at least three of five pairs

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 causal `r1` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2016 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Students who received both the quantitative and conceptual questions of an IPP performed better on the conceptual question than students given only the conceptual question, for at least three of the five quantitative/conceptual IPPs. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016)

`q2 i?` For three of the five problem pairs, performance on conceptual questions was better when both questions of the pair were given than when only the conceptual question was given. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Assessing Student Expertise in Introductory Physics with Isomorphic Problems, Part II: Effect of Some Potential Factors on Problem Solving and Transfer. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07678](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07678)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Experiment 1 across nine calculus-based introductory physics courses: multiple-choice IPPs given back to back or singly, with Chi-square comparisons in Table 1. The article reports students "performed better on the conceptual questions" when both were given, and written work shows students used the quantitative solution as a hint.

> "students who worked on both questions of the IPPs involving a conceptual and a quantitative problem performed better on the conceptual questions at least for three of the ﬁve IPPs than when they were given only the conceptual questions."
