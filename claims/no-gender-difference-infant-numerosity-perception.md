## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among 80 6-month-old infants, boys and girls showed equivalent preferential looking preference for the numerically alternating image stream, with no significant difference in mean preference or variance. [→ Kersey 2018](#kersey-2018)

## Evidence

### Kersey 2018

Kersey, A. J., Braham, E. J., Csumitta, K. D., Libertus, M. E., & Cantlon, J. F. (2018). No intrinsic gender differences in children's earliest numerical abilities. npj Science of Learning, 3, 12. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0028-7

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Previously published data from 80 6-month-old infants (38 girls, 42 boys) using a preferential looking paradigm with alternating vs. non-changing numerosity image streams. "An independent-samples t test and Schuirmann 's test of equivalence revealed no gender differences" with t(78) = 0.14, p = 0.89, and both equivalence tests significant.

> "An independent-samples t test and Schuirmann 's test of equivalence revealed no gender differences ( t test: t(78) = 0.14, p = 0.89, difference = 0.41%, 95% conﬁdence interval (CI) = −6 to 7; equivalence test: t1(78) = 2.36, p = 0.01; t2(78) = −2.08, p = 0.026)"
