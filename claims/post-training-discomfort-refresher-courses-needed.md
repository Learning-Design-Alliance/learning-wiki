## Evidence

### Michels Nele R. 2019

Michels Nele R., Vanhomwegen Els. (2019). An educational study to investigate the efficacy of three training methods for infiltration techniques on self-efficacy and skills of trainees in general practice. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-019-1023-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive analysis of minimal-maximal self-efficacy questionnaire values (Table 1) in the randomized study of 48 trainees, showing post-training minimum scores of 2 or lower in all groups and regions. This is a descriptive finding from the printed score ranges; no effect size is applicable.

> "The scores (minimal-maximal value) also show that some participants still felt uncomfortable to perform infiltrations after training (scores of 2 (somewhat disagree) or lower), and this for all the training groups and for all kind of infiltrations."
