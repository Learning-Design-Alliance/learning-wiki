## Evidence

### Yanjun Pan 2024

Yanjun Pan, Elizabeth L. Adams, Leanne R. Ketterlin-Geller, Eric C. Larson, Corey Clark. (2024). Enhancing middle school students' computational thinking competency through game-based learning. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-024-10400-x

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Paired-samples t-tests on the self-efficacy survey in the one-group study. Female students' increase was significant (t(11) = −2.53, p = .028, d = .58); male students' was not (t(13) = −2.05, p = .061, d = .78). The authors note this contradicted their hypothesis.

> "We also observed that female students increased in CT self-efficacy, t(11) = −2.53, p = .028, d = .58; whereas male students did not increase significantly, t(13) = −2.05, p = .061, d = .78."

### Yanjun Pan 2024 (2)

Yanjun Pan, Elizabeth L. Adams, Leanne R. Ketterlin-Geller, Eric C. Larson, Corey Clark. (2024). Enhancing middle school students' computational thinking competency through game-based learning. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-024-10400-x

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Wilcoxon Signed-Rank subgroup tests in the same one-group study. Significant increases for limited-Minecraft-experience students (z = −2.09, p = .037, r = .45) and Grade 8 students (z = −2.37, p = .018, r = .63); sufficient-experience students were marginal (p = .053).

> "There were statistically significant increases in self-efficacy for students with limited Minecraft experience, z = −2.09, p = .037, r = .45, and students entering Grade 8, z = −2.37, p = .018, r = .63."
