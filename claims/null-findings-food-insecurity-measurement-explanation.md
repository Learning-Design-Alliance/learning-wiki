# Four studies found no significant association between food insecurity and academic performance, plausibly explained by non-standard measurement tools and restricted sampling

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Four cross-sectional studies (USA, Mexico, Saudi Arabia) found no significant association; the review attributes this plausibly to use of household-level or non-standard instruments, sample homogeneity, and uncontrolled confounders. [→ Galseku Prosper M. 2026](#galseku-prosper-m-2026)

## Evidence

### Galseku Prosper M. 2026

Galseku Prosper M., Keenan Gregory S., Stevenson Leo, Cousins Rosanna, Lingwood Jamie, Dixon Hannah, Bourke Lorna. (2026). The Association between Food Insecurity and Academic Performance among Higher Education Students: A Systematic Review. Current Nutrition Reports. https://doi.org/10.1007/s13668-026-00744-6

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

The review's critical appraisal of the four null studies; it notes three used instruments not consistently used in higher education food insecurity research, and that restricted sampling frames (health-programmes-only recruitment) and uncontrolled confounders may have contributed.

> "That three of the studies that found no effect were also the only studies in the review that used these instruments suggests a plausible explanation for their results."
