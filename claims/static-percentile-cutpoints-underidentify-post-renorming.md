# Static percentile screening cut points carried across norm sets can under-identify students who remain below grade level

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r1` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Applying prior-norm percentile thresholds without adjustment can lead districts to under-identify students needing intervention, because the same performance now ranks higher. [→ Hashim 2026](#hashim-2026)
`q1 i?` Apparent percentile improvements may reflect changes in the national comparison group rather than changes in student achievement. [→ Hashim 2026](#hashim-2026)

## Evidence

### Hashim 2026

Hashim, A. K. (2026). How post-COVID norm shifts change the meaning of MAP Growth metrics for school accountability. NWEA White Paper. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/how-post%e2%80%91covid-norm-shifts-change-the-meaning-of-map-growth-metrics-for-school-accountability/

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r1`

The paper's screening illustration: a third-grade reader at the 40th achievement percentile under 2025 norms would have ranked near the 35th under 2020 norms, so a fixed "below the 40th percentile" threshold no longer flags the student. Illustrative example, no effect size printed.

> "If a district maintains a screening threshold of “below the 40th percentile,” this student may no longer qualify for intervention despite demonstrating essentially the same level of reading and remaining below grade level expectations."
