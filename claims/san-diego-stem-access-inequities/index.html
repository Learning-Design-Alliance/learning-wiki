Evidence¶

Babineau 2025¶

Babineau, K., Shah, Z., Jacobs, B., Miller, A., Alvarado, B., Aguirre, N., Fennelly-Atkinson, R., & Young, V. (2025). Building Bridges: Cultivating Interconnectedness for STEM Pathways in San Diego. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/254

q2 · i? · design · r2

The report's regional context section, citing workforce and education data, states that "sharp inequities remain along the lines of race, gender, and socioeconomic status in access to STEM professions". Figures 1 and 2 show subgroup college-going rates and annual income by gender and field.

"Despite the concentration of STEM opportunities and K-12 / workforce partnerships, sharp inequities remain along the lines of race, gender, and socioeconomic status in access to STEM professions across the San Diego region and the country"

Babineau 2025 (2)¶

Babineau, K., Shah, Z., Jacobs, B., Miller, A., Alvarado, B., Aguirre, N., Fennelly-Atkinson, R., & Young, V. (2025). Building Bridges: Cultivating Interconnectedness for STEM Pathways in San Diego. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/254

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Regional data reported in the context section, attributed to San Diego Regional EDC (2025): Hispanic and Latino students are "49% of San Diego's future workforce but represent only 20% of the local innovation workforce". White and Asian workers make up 73% of the innovation economy but 49% of K-12 students.

"Recent data shows that Hispanic and Latino students make up 49% of San Diego's future workforce but represent only 20% of the local innovation workforce"

Babineau 2025 (3)¶

Babineau, K., Shah, Z., Jacobs, B., Miller, A., Alvarado, B., Aguirre, N., Fennelly-Atkinson, R., & Young, V. (2025). Building Bridges: Cultivating Interconnectedness for STEM Pathways in San Diego. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/254

q2 · i? · qualitative · r?

The report's context section, referencing Figure 2 (annual income by gender and field, comparing STEM and non-STEM jobs), states the STEM "gender pay gap remains stark". Figure 1 similarly shows college-going rates varying by subgroup.

"Among those who do end up working in STEM careers, the gender pay gap remains stark"
