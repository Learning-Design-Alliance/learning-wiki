## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Residents with childhood video-game experience scored significantly higher only on the baseline theoretical test (30.3 ± 4.1 vs 27.1 ± 4.8, P = .014); no significant differences appeared on later theoretical or practical tests. [→ Weber U 2019](#weber-u-2019)

## Evidence

### Weber U 2019

Weber U, Zapletal B, Base E, Hambrusch M, Ristl R, Mora B, Schaller B. (2019). Resident performance in basic perioperative transesophageal echocardiography: Comparing 3 teaching methods in a randomized controlled trial. Medicine 98(36):e17072. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000017072

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary analysis comparing residents with and without childhood video-game experience (>1/wk) using t tests and Wilcoxon rank sum tests. Only the baseline theoretical difference was significant (P = .014); the second theoretical test and all three practical exams showed no significant difference. No effect size is printed.

> "There was a significant difference in the theoretical test scores at baseline (playing video games vs no video games: 30.3 ± 4.1 vs 27.1 ± 4.8, P = .014), but no significant difference in the theoretical test scores at the second theoretical test"
