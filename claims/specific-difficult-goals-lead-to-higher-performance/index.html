Claims

Specific, difficult goals lead to higher performance than easy or vague "do your best" goals.¶

Claim · All claims

Setting clear, measurable, and challenging targets helps focus attention, increase effort and persistence, and encourages the development of effective task strategies.

Locke & Latham 2002¶

Locke, E. A., & Latham, G. P. (2002). Building a practically useful theory of goal setting and task motivation: A 35-year odyssey. American Psychologist, 57(9), 705–717. https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.57.9.705

A narrative summary of 35 years of research on goal-setting theory. The authors argue that specific, difficult goals consistently outperform vague or easy goals by directing attention, mobilizing effort, and increasing persistence.

Nagaoka 2014¶

A Framework for Developing Young Adult Success in the 21st Century: White Paper — Defining Young Adult Success. University of Chicago Consortium on Chicago School Research Working Paper. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/framework-developing-young-adult-success-21st-century

The white paper attributes to Locke and Latham (2006) that "clear, specific, and challenging goals have been linked to higher task performance than simple encouragement," with commitment, competence, and absence of conflicting goals as stated conditions. Second-hand attribution, no effect size printed.

"Clear, specific, and challenging goals have been linked to higher task performance than simple encouragement, as long as the individual is committed to the goals, has the competence to meet the goals, and is not encumbered by conflicting goals (Locke & Latham, 2006)."

Discussion¶

Goal setting is most effective when accompanied by feedback, as feedback allows learners to monitor their progress and adjust their strategies.
