# Students report that MAES places them at the center of CPR learning and increases perceived knowledge retention and integration

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Compared with teacher-designed scenarios and standard simulation-based CPR training, students felt MAES helped them relate knowledge and retain more, feeling more prepared for emergencies. [→ Fenzi Giulio 2024](#fenzi-giulio-2024)

## Evidence

### Fenzi Giulio 2024

Fenzi Giulio, Alemán-Jiménez Carolina, López-Ferrándiz Lucia, Leal-Costa Cesar, Díaz-Agea José Luis. (2024). Enhancing Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation Training: An Interprofessional Approach With Undergraduate Medicine and Nursing Students Using Self-Learning Methodology in Simulated Environments (MAES)—A Qualitative Study. Journal of Nursing Management. https://doi.org/10.1155/jonm/9470402

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Focus-group theme from the qualitative study. One student contrasted MAES with teacher-made scenarios: "We felt that we were at the center of our learning, and it allowed us to retain more knowledge about the topic" (S19); another reported "a broader knowledge and I feel more prepared" (S21).

> "The MAES methodology places students at the center of their CPR training, enhancing knowledge retention and providing a comprehensive understanding that goes beyond technical skills."
