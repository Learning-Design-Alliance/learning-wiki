# Numerical simulation shows that when the machine works, the interesting phase from the rope's first straightening to the lid reaching vertical lasts only some tenths of a second

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In the first worked example, the motion from the rope's first straightening (t=0.51s) until the lid reaches vertical (t=0.82s) lasts 0.31 seconds. [→ U Schröter 2009](#u-schroter-2009)

## Evidence

### U Schröter 2009

U Schröter. (2009). Kermit's What-Happens-Next-Machine Reloaded. https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.0110 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0903.0110](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0903.0110)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Numerical simulation (first example, dt=0.001 s) of the What-Happens-Next-Machine with the printed parameters; the lid received enough angular momentum at one impact and reached vertical at t=0.82s. The article notes "that is 0.31 seconds" for the interesting phase.

> "Remark that the interesting part of the motion from the rope's ﬁrst straightening until the lid's reaching vertical position only lasts from t=0.51s to t=0.82s, that is 0.31 seconds."
