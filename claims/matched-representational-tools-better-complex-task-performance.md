# Teams receiving part-task congruent representational tools (causal then simulation) outperformed teams with partly matched or mismatched tools on a complex business-economics learning task

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Condition significantly affected team learning-task performance, with the matched causal-simulation condition scoring highest. [→ B. Slof 2012](#b-slof-2012)

## Evidence

### B. Slof 2012

B. Slof, G. Erkens, P. A. Kirschner, J. Janssen, J. G. M. Jaspers. (2012). Successfully carrying out complex learning-tasks through guiding teams' qualitative and quantitative reasoning. Instr Sci 40:623–643. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-011-9185-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANOVA (Brown-Forsythe) on total team performance scores across four conditions (34 teams); matched teams scored M = 39.14 (SD = 1.22) versus 28.22–31.56 in other conditions; the article reports the condition effect as significant with no standardized effect size printed.

> "One-way ANOVA revealed a signiﬁcant effect of condition on learning-task performance, F(3, 21.50) = 7.00, P\ 0.01, x2 = 0.33 (Brown-Forsythe because homogeneity of variance assumption was violated)."
