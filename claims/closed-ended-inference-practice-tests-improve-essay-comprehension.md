## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Both multiple-choice (d = 0.53) and true—false (d = 0.59) practice tests produced better final essay comprehension than rereading, with no significant difference between formats (d = 0.05). [→ Hildenbrand Lena; Wiley Jennifer 2025](#hildenbrand-lena-wiley-jennifer-2025)

## Evidence

### Hildenbrand Lena; Wiley Jennifer 2025

Hildenbrand Lena; Wiley Jennifer. (2025). Supporting comprehension: The advantages of multiple–choice over true—false practice tests. Memory & Cognition. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-025-01726-9

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1 randomly assigned 116 undergraduates to multiple-choice, true—false, or rereading conditions and tested comprehension after 48 h with a how-and-why essay. "formats resulted in better comprehension outcomes than did rereading the text." The overall condition effect was significant, F(2, 113) = 4.31, p = 0.016, ηp2 = 0.071.

> "Planned comparisons using Tukey corrections revealed that taking a practice test in both multiple-choice, t(113) = 2.38, p = 0.050, d = 0.53, and true—false, t(113) = 2.60, p = 0.028, d = 0.59, formats resulted in better comprehension outcomes than did rereading the text."
