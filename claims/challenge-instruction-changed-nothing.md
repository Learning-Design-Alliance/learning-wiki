# An explicit prompt instruction to challenge gently changed nothing: the agent challenged in about 1% of turns under both prompts

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Challenge occurred in about 1% of agent turns in both studies, although only Study 2's prompt asked for gently challenging follow-ups; over half of Study 2 participants received no challenging turn at all. [→ challenge-rates](#challenge-rates)

## Evidence

### challenge-rates

Subigya K. Nepal, Serena Soh, Noah Vinoya, SoHyun Park, Mahnaz Roshanaei, and Gabriella Harari. 2027. Faithful Where It Can Be Checked: Auditing a Reflection Agent Against Its System Prompt in a Randomized Trial. In Proceedings of CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI '27). ACM, New York, NY, USA. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19635 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.19635](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2609.19635)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Cross-study comparison of the same GPT-4o model under two prompts (facilitator vs. conversation partner), from the fidelity results. Challenge was 1.1% in Study 1 and 1.2% in Study 2 (Table 6), and 55% of participants never received any challenge.

> "And challenge sat at about 1% in both studies, even though only Study 2 asked for it. That last comparison is the cleanest evidence in the paper: an entire instruction, present in one prompt and absent from the other, changed nothing."
