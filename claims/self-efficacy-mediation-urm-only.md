# For URM students, the self-efficacy increase fully mediates the effect of active learning on grades and KAI gains; no mediation occurs for non-URM students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In full-mediation models, self-efficacy was correlated with performance for URM students (pgrade = 0.054; pKAI = 0.010) but not for non-URM students (pgrade = 0.996; pKAI = 0.685). [→ Ballen C. J. 2017](#ballen-c-j-2017)
`q2 i?` Full mediation fit better than partial mediation: adding direct covariate effects did not improve fit for grades (χ2(4) = 6.17, p = 0.19) and the full model fit significantly better for KAI (χ2(4) = 13.7, p = 0.008). [→ Ballen C. J. 2017 (2)](#ballen-c-j-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Ballen C. J. 2017

Ballen C. J., Wieman C., Salehi S., Searle J. B., Zamudio K. R. (2017). Enhancing Diversity in Undergraduate Science: Self-Efficacy Drives Performance Gains with Active Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-12-0344

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Separate full-mediation structural equation analyses for URM and non-URM students, with semester, gender, and incoming preparation as covariates. The article reports full mediation for URM students and states self-efficacy "was not correlated with performance" for non-URM students.

> "For URM students, the covariates also predicted self-efficacy, but self-efficacy was correlated with performance, and in fact fully mediated the dependence of performance on the covariates (pgrade = 0.054; pKAI = 0.010; Figure 2 and Supplemental Table S5)."

### Ballen C. J. 2017 (2)

Ballen C. J., Wieman C., Salehi S., Searle J. B., Zamudio K. R. (2017). Enhancing Diversity in Undergraduate Science: Self-Efficacy Drives Performance Gains with Active Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-12-0344

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Model comparison of partial versus full mediation using AIC-based assessment and chi-square fit tests. The article reports the printed χ2 values favoring full mediation, supporting self-efficacy as the mediating pathway.

> "For grades, the inclusion of the direct effect did not improve the fit (χ2(4) = 6.17, p = 0.19); for KAI, the full mediation with no direct effect fitted the data significantly better (χ2 (4) = 13.7, p = 0.008)."
