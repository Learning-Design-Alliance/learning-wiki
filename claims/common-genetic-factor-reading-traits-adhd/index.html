Evidence¶

Shiying Wang 2026¶

Shiying Wang, Joan Bosson-Heenan, Andrew Grotzinger, Jan C. Frijters & Jeffrey R. Gruen. (2026). Shared genetic architecture of reading and attention disorders using genomic structural equation modeling. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00433-7

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Genomic SEM analysis of LDSC-derived genetic covariance among five GWAS summary statistics (four reading traits and ADHD). The article reports the model "demonstrated a good fit according to CFI (0.993)" and acceptable SRMR (0.068), with significant loadings positive for reading traits and negative for ADHD; no effect size is printed.

"The significant loadings (𝑝 < 0.05) are positive for the four reading-related traits and negative for ADHD. Model fit was assessed using two indices: Comparative Fit Index (CFI) and Standardized Root Mean Square Residual (SRMR). The model demonstrated a good fit according to CFI (0.993) and an acceptable fit according to SRMR (0.068)."

Related Claims¶

  • Stratified Genomic SEM finds the common factor enriched only in primate-conserved genomic regions — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • The common factor shows significant genetic correlations with six psychiatric disorders and six behavioral traits, strongest with PTSD (rg = -0.47) and educational attainment (rg = 0.74) — related
  • The common factor model is specific to reading and ADHD rather than broader psychiatric liability, and stability analyses support the reading-ADHD structure — related
  • Nonword reading was excluded from the Genomic SEM model because its genetic correlations with word reading and spelling exceeded 1.0 — related
  • Literacy, phonological awareness, oral language and PWM abilities share a large proportion of underlying genetic variation in unrelated youth — related
