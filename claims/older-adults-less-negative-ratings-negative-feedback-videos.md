# Older adults rate negative social feedback videos as less negative than young adults do, consistent with an age-related positivity effect

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Ratings of negative feedback videos differed significantly between young and older adults, with no age differences in the neutral and positive conditions. [→ Alexandra Sobczak and Nico Bunzeck 2023](#alexandra-sobczak-and-nico-bunzeck-2023)

## Evidence

### Alexandra Sobczak and Nico Bunzeck 2023

Alexandra Sobczak and Nico Bunzeck. (2023). Effects of positive and negative social feedback on motivation, evaluative learning, and socio-emotional processing. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00178-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Valence ratings of feedback videos (scale −10 to 10) in the SID-Video study; the 2 × 3 mixed permutation F-test showed significant main effects of age (p = 0.0003) and condition (p = 0.0001) and a significant interaction (p = 0.0003). Mean negative-video ratings: −6.41 (young) vs −3.69 (older).

> "The post-hoc two-sample permutation t-tests for the interaction comparing the average ratings in each condition between the young and older adults showed a signi ﬁcant difference in the ratings of the negative feedback videos (p = 0.0003, Fig. 6c), but no differences between the ratings of young and older adults in the neutral and positive condition."
