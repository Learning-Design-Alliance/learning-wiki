Evidence¶

Yu et al. 2015¶

Ka Chun Yu, Kamran Sahami, Victoria Sahami, Larry C. Sessions. (2015). Using A Digital Planetarium For Teaching Seasons To Undergraduates. Journal of Astronomy & Earth Sciences Education, 2(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1101071

q2 · i2 · causal · r2

Quasi-experimental comparison of 17 AST1040 classes in three groups; Table 2 reports Group III mean gains of 17.4% (Cont-Pre) and 20.9% (Post-Pre) versus smaller gains for Groups I and II, expressed as "ES=0.696 for Cont-Pre; ES=0.741 for Post-Pre gains".

"The value of immersive visualizations is evident since the Group III gains ( ES=0.696 for Cont-Pre; ES=0.741 for Post-Pre gains) are larger than comparable gains in Groups I ( ES=0.174; ES=0.556) and II (ES=0.270; ES=0.125)."

Yu et al. 2015 (2)¶

Ka Chun Yu, Kamran Sahami, Victoria Sahami, Larry C. Sessions. (2015). Using A Digital Planetarium For Teaching Seasons To Undergraduates. Journal of Astronomy & Earth Sciences Education, 2(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1101071

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Figure 3 plots Cont-Pre gains with 95% confidence intervals; the authors report non-overlap of the Group III CI with Groups I and II, giving "p<0.01" under the stated null hypothesis. No effect size beyond the printed ES values is given for this contrast.

"From Figure 3, the Group III CI does not overlap with either the Group I or II CIs, meaning that p<0.01 when considering the null hypothesis that either the Group I or II mean is outside the Group III CI"
