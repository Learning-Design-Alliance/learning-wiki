## Evidence

### Williams 2017

Williams, E. A., Zwolak, J. P., Dou, R., & Brewe, E. (2017). Linking engagement and performance: The social network analysis perspective. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1706.04121](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1706.04121)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Network-level descriptive analysis across the five collections found reported ties decreased (358 to 302, with median indegree and outdegree stabilizing around week 8) while network visualizations showed seatmate clusters giving way to a more unified network. The authors offer the selection-effect explanation as their interpretation, supported by instructor observations and follow-up interviews.

> "We interpret this change as evidence of a selection eﬀect: While the quantity of interactions decreases over time, their quality improves, suggesting that, after the initial exploratory in nature interactions, students begin to make more calculated decisions regarding whom to interact with."
