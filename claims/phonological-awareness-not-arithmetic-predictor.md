## Evidence

### Moll K. 2015

Moll K., Snowling M. J., Göbel S. M., Hulme C. (2015). Early language and executive skills predict variations in number and arithmetic skills in children at family-risk of dyslexia and typically developing controls. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2015.03.004

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the longitudinal path model, phonological awareness at t2 did not predict arithmetic at t3 in either group. Additional analyses examining addition or subtraction efficiency as separate outcomes "revealed no differences in the predictive patterns".

> "our findings fail to support the idea that phonological awareness exerts a causal influence on the development of arithmetic skills, at least when measured in 4–5 year-olds, despite the fact that oral language skills at t1 predicted phonological awareness at t2."
