# Self-explaining led to significantly higher written procedural knowledge than re-experiencing, opposite to the hypothesized direction

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` The self-explaining group scored significantly higher than the re-experiencing group on the written procedural knowledge post-test (d = −0.34). [→ Valdemar Stenberdt 2025](#valdemar-stenberdt-2025)
`q3 i?` No significant differences between groups on written factual or conceptual knowledge. [→ Valdemar Stenberdt 2025](#valdemar-stenberdt-2025)

## Evidence

### Valdemar Stenberdt 2025

Valdemar Stenberdt, Ban Mouid Shiwalia, Guido Makransky. (2025). Re-Experiencing vs. Self-Explaining: Comparing Generative Learning Activities in VR. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10096-2

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Independent-samples t-tests on the full sample of 161 students comparing written post-test scores. Procedural knowledge favored self-explaining (M = 5.12 vs 4.23), "in the opposite direction of the hypothesized relationship". Factual knowledge showed no difference (p = 0.845); conceptual knowledge was marginal (p = 0.058).

> "a significant difference between the self-explaining ( M = 5.12, SD = 2.59) and re-experiencing (M = 4.23, SD = 2.60) groups, t(df = 159) = 2.17, p = 0.031, d = −0.34, 95% CI = [−0.65, −0.03]."
