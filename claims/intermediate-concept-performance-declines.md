# Newtonian concept performance declines significantly at intermediate points during an iteratively structured unit, with declines timed differently across concepts

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2010 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Performance declines significantly along all conceptual dimensions at intermediate points; first law, acceleration and net force decline at BC while second and third law decline at RB. [→ Camp 2010](#camp-2010)
`q2 i?` Repeated measures ANOVAs show significant dependence of performance on test time for each concept cluster, but not all adjacent quiz differences are significant (e.g., no significant difference from CC to BC for second law). [→ Camp 2010 (2)](#camp-2010-2)

## Evidence

### Camp 2010

Camp, P. J. (2010). U-shaped Development of Newtonian Concepts: Implications for Pedagogical Design and Research Practice. Spelman College. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1007.1647](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1007.1647)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of quiz data from one LBD classroom across pre, CC, BC, RB and post tests, grouped into five concept clusters. The author describes "significant performance declines along all conceptual dimensions at intermediate points" occurring at different quiz points for different concepts.

> "The most notable pattern by far that emerges from this data are the significant performance declines along all conceptual dimensions at intermediate points. First Law, acceleration and net force decline at BC (quiz 3) whereas second and third law performance declines later, at RB (quiz 4)."

### Camp 2010 (2)

Camp, P. J. (2010). U-shaped Development of Newtonian Concepts: Implications for Pedagogical Design and Research Practice. Spelman College. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1007.1647](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1007.1647)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated measures ANOVA on first law cluster scores across test times in the LBD classroom, reported with p < .001 and F(2.852,199.665) = 111.166. Parallel ANOVAs are reported for the other clusters; for second law, the article notes "There was no significant difference from CC to BC."

> "A repeated measures ANOVA was performed on this data revealing a significant dependence of performance on test time with p < .001, F(2.852,199.665) = 111.166."
