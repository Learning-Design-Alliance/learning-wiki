# Error timing and stakes shape trust in AI systems: later errors may be more damaging, early errors may matter more, and trust recovers quickly after good post-error performance, with high-stakes errors causing abrupt collapse

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Two reviewed studies on error timing reached complementary conclusions—one found later errors more damaging, the other early errors—while both agreed trust can recover relatively quickly if the system performs well after an error. [→ Abramson SL 2026](#abramson-sl-2026)
`q2 i?` Error timing effects were highly context-dependent: errors in high-stakes systems caused an abrupt collapse of trust, whereas users in low-stakes scenarios were more forgiving. [→ Abramson SL 2026 (2)](#abramson-sl-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Abramson SL 2026

Abramson SL, Sora-Cardenas J, Nandakumar P, Bruce JE, Gopinath N, Zangari LG and Feijóo-García PG (2026). Trust and interaction design in AI-enabled systems: a systematic literature review. Front. Psychol. 17:1932655. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1932655

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports two studies (average sample size n=286) on error placement with complementary rather than conflicting perspectives; both agreed trust "can recover relatively quickly if the system performs well following an error".

> "One study found that errors occurring later in an interaction were more damaging to trust, while the other suggested that early errors had the greater impact (Freeletal., 2025 ; Kahretal., 2024)."

### Abramson SL 2026 (2)

Abramson SL, Sora-Cardenas J, Nandakumar P, Bruce JE, Gopinath N, Zangari LG and Feijóo-García PG (2026). Trust and interaction design in AI-enabled systems: a systematic literature review. Front. Psychol. 17:1932655. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1932655

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes this stakes-moderation finding to Freele et al. (2025): high-stakes errors caused "an abrupt collapse of trust" while low-stakes users maintained trust despite identical errors. No effect sizes are printed.

> "Freeletal.(2025) additionallyfoundthattheeﬀectsoferrortiming were highly context-dependent: errors in high-stakes systems caused an abrupt collapse of trust, whereas users in low-stakes scenarios were more forgiving, often maintaining trust despite identicalerrors."
