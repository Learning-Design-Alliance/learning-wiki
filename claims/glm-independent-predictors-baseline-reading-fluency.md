# In a general linear model, attention, auditory working memory, and school independently predict baseline reading fluency, while speech discrimination in noise and IQ do not

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Speech discrimination in noise (p = 0.107) and IQ (p = 0.988) were not independent predictors of baseline reading fluency, while auditory working memory, attention, and school were significant in the general linear model. [→ Shiels 2025](#shiels-2025)

## Evidence

### Shiels 2025

Shiels, L., Carew, P., Tomlin, D. & Rance, G. (2025). The impact of classroom soundfield systems on reading fluency in normal-hearing students. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00350-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

General linear model of baseline reading fluency with the significant baseline correlates plus test site as independent variables (Table 2). The model prints attention (p = 0.002), auditory working memory (p = 0.016) and school (p = 0.006) as significant, with η² of 0.064, 0.037 and 0.107; no standardized effect sizes for individual predictors beyond these η² values.

> "Speech discrimination in noise (p = 0.107) and IQ ( p = 0.988) were not independent predictors of baseline readingﬂuency."

### Shiels 2025 (2)

Shiels, L., Carew, P., Tomlin, D. & Rance, G. (2025). The impact of classroom soundfield systems on reading fluency in normal-hearing students. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00350-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Tukey post hoc comparisons following the general linear model of baseline reading fluency showed the rural school was "signiﬁcantly poorer than for the metropolitan schools"; no effect size is printed.

> "Tukey post hoc comparisons showed that readingﬂuency ﬁndings for the rural school were signiﬁcantly poorer than for the metropolitan schools."
