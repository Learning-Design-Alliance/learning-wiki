## Evidence

### UMANODAN Rino 2014

UMANODAN Rino, SHIMAZU Akihito, MINAMI Masahide, KAWAKAMI Norito. (2014). Effects of Computer-based Stress Management Training on Psychological Well-being and Work Performance in Japanese Employees: A Cluster Randomized Controlled Trial. Industrial Health 52(6). https://doi.org/10.2486/indhealth.2013-0209

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Sub-group analysis splitting participants by median baseline psychological distress. The article reports the quoted interaction for the low-distress subgroup and no significant interactions on other variables (p>0.05).

> "Among participants with low initial psychological distress levels, a repeated-measures mixed-model ANOVA revealed a significant group × time interaction on “knowledge about stress management” (F=5.186, p=0.006)."

### UMANODAN Rino 2014 (2)

UMANODAN Rino, SHIMAZU Akihito, MINAMI Masahide, KAWAKAMI Norito. (2014). Effects of Computer-based Stress Management Training on Psychological Well-being and Work Performance in Japanese Employees: A Cluster Randomized Controlled Trial. Industrial Health 52(6). https://doi.org/10.2486/indhealth.2013-0209

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same median-split sub-group analysis for the high-distress subgroup. The article reports only the marginally significant seeking social support interaction (F=2.863, p=0.059), with simple main effect of time significant for the intervention group (F=5.459, p=0.005).

> "In contrast, among participants with high initial psychological distress levels, a repeated-measures mixed-model ANOVA revealed a marginally significant group × time interaction on “seeking social support” (F=2.863, p=0.059)."
