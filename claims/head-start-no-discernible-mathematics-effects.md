# Head Start shows no discernible effects on mathematics achievement for 4-year-old children

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` No statistically significant or substantively important difference was found between the Head Start and comparison groups on the Counting Bears Test for the 4-year-old cohort. [→ What Works Clearinghouse 2015](#what-works-clearinghouse-2015)

## Evidence

### What Works Clearinghouse 2015

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2015). Head Start: WWC intervention report. http://www.acf.hhs.gov/programs/ohs

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

In the DHHS ACF (2010) randomized controlled trial, the Counting Bears Test, measuring counting ability and one-to-one correspondence, was administered to the 4-year-old cohort only. The average improvement index was +3 percentile points; the WWC characterized the effect as indeterminate because it was "neither statistically significant nor substantively important."

> "The authors reported, and the WWC confirmed, no statistically significant or substantively important difference between the Head Start and comparison groups on this measure."
