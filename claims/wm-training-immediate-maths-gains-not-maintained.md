# Working memory training improves children's mathematical reasoning immediately after training relative to an adaptive control, but the effect is not maintained at 3 months

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` At immediate outcome, mathematical reasoning was higher than control for both training groups, significant for Cogmed and borderline for MetaCogmed. [→ Jones JS 2020](#jones-js-2020)
`q3 i?` At the 3-month outcome, neither training group differed significantly from control in mathematical reasoning. [→ Jones JS 2020 (2)](#jones-js-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Jones JS 2020

Jones JS, Milton F, Mostazir M, Adlam ALR. (2020). The academic outcomes of working memory and metacognitive strategy training in children: A double‐blind randomized controlled trial. Developmental Science. https://doi.org/10.1111/desc.12870

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVAs on the WIAT-II Mathematical Reasoning subtest in the RCT (N = 77) showed immediate far-transfer to maths for Cogmed versus adaptive control; the difference was "statistically significant for the Cogmed group (p = 0.019) and borderline significant for the MetaCogmed group (p = 0.059)".

> "This difference was statistically significant for the Cogmed group (p = 0.019) and borderline significant for the MetaCogmed group (p = 0.059)."

### Jones JS 2020 (2)

Jones JS, Milton F, Mostazir M, Adlam ALR. (2020). The academic outcomes of working memory and metacognitive strategy training in children: A double‐blind randomized controlled trial. Developmental Science. https://doi.org/10.1111/desc.12870

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mixed models at 3-month follow-up found the immediate maths gain had dissipated: "no significant improvement in Maths scores for the MetaCogmed (p = 0.285) or Cogmed groups (p = 0.340)"; equivalence was not tested.

> "Relative to the Control group, there was no significant improvement in Maths scores for the MetaCogmed (p = 0.285) or Cogmed groups (p = 0.340) at the 3‐month outcome."
