# Observations across MEU trials show all ages engaged enthusiastically with toys and games, while younger students forgot more quickly after a few weeks

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Anastasia Popkova 2023

Anastasia Popkova, David Blair and David Treagust. (2023). Maths for Einstein's Universe - Tools for Understanding Modern Reality. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2307.06969](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2307.06969)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Qualitative observations drawn from post-lesson notes, video and photographic data across trials with students aged 7-14, categorised by age group. The observations are descriptive; no effect sizes are reported.

> "From youngest to oldest, independent on gender, all students enthusiastically participated in mathematical games. It was observed that students in years 3-4 showed a higher level of engagement and preference for games that involved physical and tactile experiences, compared to older year groups."

### Anastasia Popkova 2023 (2)

Anastasia Popkova, David Blair and David Treagust. (2023). Maths for Einstein's Universe - Tools for Understanding Modern Reality. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2307.06969](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2307.06969)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Learning retention observations from trials comparing Year 3-4 and Year 7-9 students after a few weeks. The authors attribute the reduced retention to developmental factors and recommend ongoing reinforcement in a spiral curriculum.

> "In many trials younger (Year 3-4) and older children (Year 7-9) demonstrated similar learning performance. However, by testing after a few weeks, we found that younger students forget more quickly."

### Anastasia Popkova 2023 (3)

Anastasia Popkova, David Blair and David Treagust. (2023). Maths for Einstein's Universe - Tools for Understanding Modern Reality. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2307.06969](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2307.06969)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Concept engagement duration observations across age groups: Year 5-6 engaged more with advanced toys such as phasor wheels, Year 3-4 with simpler toys, and Year 7-9 moved quickly to formal explanation. The article also notes about one third of students in a low SES school had difficulty linking activities to concepts.

> "Older students who spent less time engaged with toy magnetic arrows were more likely to confuse the direction of a resultant vector."
