# In HLM fits controlling for FCI pre-scores and calculus grades, the demographic gap is +0.38 in the concepts-first class and -0.39 in the standard classes, differing by 0.77 ± 0.17 (t = 4.43)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 1 causal `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2023 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` After controlling for incoming calculus and Newtonian mechanics understanding, the demographic gaps under the two course organizations are of opposite sign and differ by 0.77 ± 0.17, i.e. 4.43 standard errors. [→ David J. Webb 2021](#david-j-webb-2021)

`q2 i?` The URM*Course interaction coefficient of 0.77 (z = 4.12, p < 10^-3) confirms the demographic gap difference when both course organizations are fit in one model. [→ David J. Webb 2021](#david-j-webb-2021)

`q2 i?` The HLM interaction between URM status and concepts-first structure was significantly different from zero (z = 3.16, P = 0.002), indicating the URM gap was reduced in the concepts-first class. [→ David J. Webb and Cassandra A. Paul 2023](#david-j-webb-and-cassandra-a-paul-2023)

## Evidence

### David J. Webb 2021

David J. Webb. (2021). Addendum to Concepts First Paper: A Student Deﬁcit Model is Untenable in Understanding a Demographic Grade Gap. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2109.09240](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2109.09240)

`q2 · i?`

Hierarchical linear modeling fits of the final exam equation run separately for the concepts-first class and the three standard classes (633 students total). The fitted b_URM values are 0.38 and -0.39; the article reports "the diﬀerence is 0.77 ± 0.17" with t = 4.43. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "Calculating the diﬀerence in the two values of the gap we ﬁnd that the diﬀerence is 0.77 ± 0.17. For this comparison the t-statistic = 4.43 so the two values of demographic gap diﬀer by 4.43 standard errors."

### David J. Webb and Cassandra A. Paul 2023

David J. Webb and Cassandra A. Paul. (2023). Attributing equity gaps to course structure in introductory physics. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020126](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020126)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Hierarchical Linear Modeling of N = 633 students in 4 classes found the interaction coefficient b_URM*CncptFrst = 0.82 with z-statistic 3.16 and P = 0.002, so the concepts-first URM gap was about 3.16 standard errors above the chapter-by-chapter background gap.

> "bU RM∗CncptF rstis significantly differ-ent from zero so the URM students from the concepts-first class did much better on the final exam than their URM peers in the regular classes"
