# Multi-agent AI standardized patient training improved final OSCE-aligned examination scores compared with a structured non-LLM control condition

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Students trained with the multi-agent AI standardized patient system achieved higher final examination scores than the structured control group, with a large standardized effect size. [→ Yang 2026](#yang-2026)
`q3 i?` In a linear model, multi-agent assignment was associated with a 16.4-percentage-point higher final examination score, and a higher weighted checklist score. [→ Yang 2026 (2)](#yang-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Yang 2026

Yang, L., Liu, H., Li, S., Jia, R., Xiao, Y., Chen, G., & Lu, L. (2026). Evaluating Scaffolding-Oriented Multi-Agent Large Language Model System for Clinical Interview Training. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10939

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Two-arm randomized controlled study of medical students (MA n = 47, CT n = 48 analyzed). The Mann-Whitney U test on final exam scores showed "a statistically significant between-group difference(P = 5.51 × 10−5)", with means of 71.8% (MA) vs 55.6% (CT); the article reports a large Hedges' g = −0.81 favoring MA, sign reflecting coding direction.

> "The mean final examination score was71.8%in the multi-agent group and55.6%in the control group, with corresponding medians of78.8%and60 .6%, respectively. A Mann-Whitney U test showed a statistically significant between-group difference(P = 5.51 × 10−5)"

### Yang 2026 (2)

Yang, L., Liu, H., Li, S., Jia, R., Xiao, Y., Chen, G., & Lu, L. (2026). Evaluating Scaffolding-Oriented Multi-Agent Large Language Model System for Clinical Interview Training. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10939

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Linear-model analysis of the same randomized comparison confirmed the exam-score advantage: "a 16.4-percentage-point higher final examination score" (β = 16.4; 95% CI, 7.7 to 25.1; P = 3.30 × 10−4). Multi-agent learning was also associated with a higher weighted checklist score (β = 11.7; 95% CI, 0.2 to 23.1; P = 0.046).

> "Assignment to the multi-agent group was associated with a 16.4-percentage-point higher final examination score compared with the control group(β = 16.4; 95% CI, 7.7 to 25.1; P = 3.30 × 10−4)"
