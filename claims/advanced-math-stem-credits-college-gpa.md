# High-achieving underserved students who took advanced math earned more STEM credits and higher first-year college GPAs than peers who did not

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Advanced-math takers earned credits for almost five more STEM courses (14, 13, and 14 credits for Black, Latino, and low-SES students respectively). [→ Melodie Baker 2023](#melodie-baker-2023)
`q2 i?` First-year college GPAs were about 0.5 points higher for Black and Latino advanced-math takers and about 0.6 points higher for low-SES students. [→ Melodie Baker 2023](#melodie-baker-2023)

## Evidence

### Melodie Baker 2023

Melodie Baker, Ivy Morgan, and Gizella Wade. (2023). Opportunities Denied: High-Achieving Black and Latino Students Lack Access to Advanced Math. EdTrust and Just Equations. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED641831

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive HSLS:09 comparison of college STEM credit-earning by high school advanced course-taking status. Endnotes mark all STEM-credit differences as statistically significant and college-GPA differences as significant for Latino and low-SES students; no effect sizes are printed.

> "High-achieving Black and Latino students and students from low-SES backgrounds who took advanced math courses in high school earned credits for almost five more STEM courses (14, 13, and 14 credits, respectively) than their high-achieving peers who did not take advanced math course."
