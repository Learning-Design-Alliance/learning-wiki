# Student-AI exchanges in an undergraduate research methods course are empirically classifiable into three interaction paths, with Passive Review most frequent (46%) and Strategic Dialogue 36%

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Qualitative content analysis of 50 double-coded messages classified exchanges into the three typological paths, with R1 final classifications of 46% Path 1, 18% Path 2, and 36% Path 3. [→ Zahra 2025](#zahra-2025)
`q2 i?` Path-level inter-rater agreement between two human coders was 68% (κ = .48), rated moderate, with 80% agreement on Strategic Dialogue classification. [→ Zahra 2025 (2)](#zahra-2025-2)
`q2 i?` A three-coder majority-vote distribution (P1 = 48%, P2 = 16%, P3 = 36%) closely mirrored the primary researcher's classifications, corroborating the proportions. [→ Zahra 2025 (3)](#zahra-2025-3)

## Evidence

### Zahra 2025

Zahra, F. T. (2025). Three Pathways of Student-AI Interaction: Constraint-First Design for Higher-Order Thinking. Manuscript under blind review; framework first presented at EAAI-25, Philadelphia, PA. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00338 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2610.00338](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2610.00338)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative content analysis of 50 randomly sampled student-AI messages from a corpus of 580 produced by 14 undergraduates across seven assignments. The reported distribution is "46% of coded exchanges" for Path 1, 18% Path 2, 36% Path 3; no effect size is printed.

> "Using R1’s final classifications, Path 1 (Passive Review) accounted for 46% of coded exchanges, Path 2 (Direct Question) for 18%, and Path 3 (Strategic Dialogue) for 36%."

### Zahra 2025 (2)

Zahra, F. T. (2025). Three Pathways of Student-AI Interaction: Constraint-First Design for Higher-Order Thinking. Manuscript under blind review; framework first presented at EAAI-25, Philadelphia, PA. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00338. [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2610.00338](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2610.00338)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Two human coders independently coded all 50 user messages against a ten-label codebook; the article reports "68% (κ = .48)" path-level agreement, moderate by Landis and Koch. Coders agreed on Path 3 in 80% of cases (40/50). No effect size is printed.

> "Path-level inter-rater agreement between R1 and R2 was 68% (κ = .48), which Landis and Koch (1977) classify as moderate."

### Zahra 2025 (3)

Zahra, F. T. (2025). Three Pathways of Student-AI Interaction: Constraint-First Design for Higher-Order Thinking. Manuscript under blind review; framework first presented at EAAI-25, Philadelphia, PA. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00338. [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2610.00338](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2610.00338)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Supplementary triangulation computed a majority-vote distribution across R1, R2, and GPT-5, which the article says "closely mirrors R1's classifications" (46/18/36), offered as corroboration that proportions are not an artifact of a single coder.

> "the majority-vote distribution across all three coders (R1, R2, and GPT-5) was computed separately: P1 = 48%, P2 = 16%, P3 = 36%."
