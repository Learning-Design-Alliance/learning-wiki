Evidence¶

Isabelle Regner 2002¶

Isabelle Regner, Pascal Huguet, Jean-Marc Monteil. (2002). Effects of socioEconomic status (SES) information on cognitive ability inferences: When low-SES students make use of a self-threatening stereotype. Social Psychology of Education, 5(3), 253-269. https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1016313908667

q2 · i? · causal · r?

The article reports two studies testing moderation of SES-stereotype use by "students' Socioeconomic Status (SES) and academic achievement level." The reported outcome was that only low-SES students generated stereotype-consistent inferences in both studies.

"Two studies tested whether students' Socioeconomic Status (SES) and academic achievement level moderate their use of the SES stereotype (i.e., the belief that the low-SES individuals are intellectually inferior to their high-SES counterparts)."

Discussion¶

Learner Variables¶

  • Inferencing — outcome: instruction changes it
  • Socioeconomic Status — moderator: an instructional effect differs by group

Related Claims¶

  • Low-SES students, but not high-SES students, generate stereotype-consistent inferences linking targets' social class and memory ability — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Low-SES students also generate stereotype-consistent inferences in the reverse direction, inferring SES indicators from memory performance information — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • The authors interpret stereotype threat as a partial explanation for the standardized-test achievement gap between low- and high-SES students — related
