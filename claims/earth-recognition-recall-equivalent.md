# Children scored equally high and consistent on a recall interview as on a recognition test of earth knowledge, and the two measures correlated only moderately

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There was no significant difference between the interview (recall) and the EARTH-2 (recognition) in mean score or consistency score. [→ Straatemeier 2008](#straatemeier-2008)
`q3 i?` EARTH-2 and interview scores correlated at rs = .55, and only 30.8% of interviewed children were classified with a mental model by both methods. [→ Straatemeier 2008 (2)](#straatemeier-2008-2)

## Evidence

### Straatemeier 2008

Straatemeier, M., van der Maas, H. L. J., & Jansen, B. R. J. (2008). Children's knowledge of the earth: A new methodological and statistical approach. Journal of Experimental Child Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2008.03.004

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Paired comparison of the EARTH-2 and interview among the 65-68 interviewed children, using a common 3-point score. The article concludes "the children scored just as high and consistently on a recall task (the interview) as on a recognition task (the EARTH-2)"; equivalence was not tested.

> "There was no signiﬁcant difference in mean score between the research methods, t(64) = 1.63, p = .11, Wilcoxon Z = 1.42, p = .16. Similarly, there was no signiﬁcant difference in the consistency score of the two test methods, t(64) = 0.43, p = .67, Wilcoxon Z = 0.03, p = .98."

### Straatemeier 2008 (2)

Straatemeier, M., van der Maas, H. L. J., & Jansen, B. R. J. (2008). Children's knowledge of the earth: A new methodological and statistical approach. Journal of Experimental Child Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2008.03.004

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Validity analysis of the main experiment reporting a Spearman correlation of .55 (p < .001); all but 2 of the doubly classified children held the scientific model by both methods, and 69.2% were nonclassifiable by at least one method.

> "The correlation between the scores on the EARTH-2 and the interview, which can be seen as a measure of validity, was .55 (Spearman's rho, p < .001). Only 20 children (30.8%) were classiﬁed as having a mental model based on both methods."
