Evidence¶

D. Giofrè · K. Allen · E. Toffalini · S. Caviola 2022¶

D. Giofrè · K. Allen · E. Toffalini · S. Caviola. (2022). The Impasse on Gender Differences in Intelligence: a Meta-Analysis on WISC Batteries. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-022-09705-1

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Meta-analysis of 21 studies (32 samples) for the gF factor. The pooled effect was "significant, but negligible"; the estimate from new WISC versions alone was d = 0.05, described as virtually zero.

"The estimated mean effect size was significant, but negligible, with slightly higher scores in males than in females, d = 0.09 [0.04, 0.15], p < 0.001."

D. Giofrè · K. Allen · E. Toffalini · S. Caviola 2022 (2)¶

D. Giofrè · K. Allen · E. Toffalini · S. Caviola. (2022). The Impasse on Gender Differences in Intelligence: a Meta-Analysis on WISC Batteries. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-022-09705-1

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Subtest-level meta-analysis (Table 2) of the Matrix reasoning subtest across 5 studies; the article notes newer fluid-intelligence tasks showed either no differences (Picture concepts) or differences favouring females.

"As far as Matrix reasoning is concerned, a subtest tapping heavily on core aspects of fluid reasoning, and very similar to Raven's matrices, the effect, favouring females, is virtually zero: d = -0.04 [-0.10, 0.02]."
