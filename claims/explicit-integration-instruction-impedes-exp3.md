# Apprentices left to study redundant text-diagram material implicitly spent less time processing it and outperformed apprentices explicitly instructed to integrate text with diagram

## Evidence

### Chandler 1991

Chandler, P. & Sweller, J. (1991). Cognitive Load Theory and the Format of Instruction. Cognition and Instruction, 8(4). http://ro.uow.edu.au/edupapers/128

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Laboratory-type experiment with 20 first-year apprentices studying a self-explanatory DOL starter control circuit diagram with redundant text at their own pace. The explicit group spent almost twice as much time processing instructions, consistent with the cognitive load hypothesis that unnecessary mental integration consumed resources.

> "A t test indicated that the implicit group, which was asked simply to study the material, spent significantly less time processing the instructions than the explicit group, which, in addition to being asked to study the material, was instructed to assimilate the text with the diagram, t(18) = 3.28."

### Chandler 1991 (2)

Chandler, P. & Sweller, J. (1991). Cognitive Load Theory and the Format of Instruction. Cognition and Instruction, 8(4). http://ro.uow.edu.au/edupapers/128

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

On the 30-mark construction and labeling problem, the implicit group significantly outperformed the explicit group despite substantially less study time. The direction of results on the other two problems also favored the implicit group, but differences were not significant, possibly due to the reduced marking scales (out of 12 and 5).

> "The implicit group scored significantly higher (using a one-tailed test) than the second group on the first test problem, t(18) = 1.81."
