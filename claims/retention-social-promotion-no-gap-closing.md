# Neither promotion nor retention significantly closed the achievement gap of low-achieving students close to the cutoff, and the lowest achievers deteriorated further

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Low-achieving students started school behind and fell further behind before the gate grade; the gap did not significantly close afterward for students near the cutoff. [→ Nagaoka 2004](#nagaoka-2004)
`q3 i?` Very low-achieving retained sixth graders deteriorated in relative performance, averaging nearly one and one-quarter standard deviations below cohort reading achievement two years after retention, though no effect size for retention itself is printed. [→ Nagaoka 2004 (2)](#nagaoka-2004-2)

## Evidence

### Nagaoka 2004

Nagaoka, J., & Roderick, M. (2004). Ending Social Promotion: The Effects of Retention. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/ending-social-promotion-effects-retention

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Cohort gap analysis from first grade to two years after the retention or promotion decision for three score groups shows low achievers started substantially behind and the gap widened most between first and third grade, before the policy took effect.

> "Neither promotion nor retention led to a significant closing of this achievement gap after the gate grade for those low-achieving students who had test scores close to the cutoff."

### Nagaoka 2004 (2)

Nagaoka, J., & Roderick, M. (2004). Ending Social Promotion: The Effects of Retention. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/ending-social-promotion-effects-retention

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive tracking of the very low-achieving group (most of whom were retained) shows deterioration in relative performance after retention, averaging "nearly one and one-quarter standard deviations below the average reading achievement of their cohort"; the report notes that without a comparison group the negative effect of retention cannot be isolated.

> "Two years after retention, the average achievement of sixth graders in our very low-achieving group was nearly one and one-quarter standard deviations below the average reading achievement of their cohort."
