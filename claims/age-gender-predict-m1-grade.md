## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Female students' mean M1 grade (2.90) was significantly higher (worse) than male students' (2.50), Cohen's d = 0.5. [→ Kötter T 2017](#kotter-t-2017)

## Evidence

### Kötter T 2017

Kötter T, Wagner J, Brüheim L, Voltmer E. (2017). Perceived Medical School stress of undergraduate medical students predicts academic performance: an observational study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1091-0

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of M1 grades by gender in the matched LUST sample (higher grade value = worse performance). The printed effect size Cohen's d = 0.5 indicates a medium effect favouring male students; the authors call the finding surprising given better female high-school grades in Germany.

> "Finally, M1 grades differed between male and female medical students (2.50 vs 2.90; t 4.56, df 454, p < .01; 95% CI of the difference 0.23–0.58; see Table 1). Cohen's d for this difference is 0.5 (medium effect size)."

### Kötter T 2017 (2)

Kötter T, Wagner J, Brüheim L, Voltmer E. (2017). Perceived Medical School stress of undergraduate medical students predicts academic performance: an observational study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1091-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive group comparison of students aged 25 or older versus younger students; only the t statistic was printed, with no standardized effect size for this contrast, so impact is not quantified.

> "The mean M1 grade of the “older 20%” of the medical students in our sample was statistically significantly higher when compared to the rest of the sample (3.07 ± 0.84 vs 2.69 ± 0.91; t − 3.72, df 454, p < .01; 95% CI of the difference 0.58–0.18)."
