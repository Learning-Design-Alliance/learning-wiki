## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2014

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2014). Student Difficulties with the Dirac Delta Function. [doi:10.1119/perc.2014.pr.064](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.064)

`q3 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Coding of traditional midterm exam solutions (N=303) against the operationalized ACER element C1 found "25%, N=77 of 303" of solutions had an incorrect shape; percentages are descriptive, no effect size printed.

> "Roughly one quarter of students’ solutions (25%, N=77 of 303) had an incorrect shape on the exams. On the CUE diagnostic administered at the end of the semester, the fraction of students who selected an incorrect shape increased to slightly less than half the students (42%, N=35 of 84)."

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2014 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2014). Student Difficulties with the Dirac Delta Function. [doi:10.1119/perc.2014.pr.064](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.064)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The multiple-choice CUE diagnostic (N=84) provided quantitative prevalence data; among students with incorrect shapes, "53%, N=41 of 77" misidentified volume densities with 1 or 2 delta functions as point charges. No effect size printed.

> "The most common difficulty was misidentifying volume charge densities with 1 or 2 delta functions as point charges (53%, N=41 of 77). The drop-off in student success on the CUE indicates that students are not forming and/or maintaining a robust understanding of how delta functions relate to the shape of a charge distribution."
