# Higher-performing applicants differ from lower performers mainly on diagnosis, transfer management and communication, and are more often male-free and public-university graduates

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Applicants scoring above 7 outperformed those at or below 7 on all 12 checklist items, with the largest gaps in transfer recognition (100% vs 31%), communication (55% vs 15%), STEMI identification (89% vs 51%) and transfer persistence (45% vs 8%), all p < 0.0001. [→ Stocco AU 2020](#stocco-au-2020)
`q2 i?` The lower-performance group had a higher prevalence of males (63% vs 52%; p = 0.003), while the higher-performance group had more public-university graduates (71% vs 61%; p = 0.0016). [→ Stocco AU 2020 (2)](#stocco-au-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Stocco AU 2020

Stocco AU, Miranda CH. (2020). Clinical Competence in ST-segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction Management by Recently Graduated Physicians Applying for a Medical Residency Program. Arquivos Brasileiros de Cardiologia. https://doi.org/10.36660/abc.20180309

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of the 363 applicants with general score > 7 against the 408 with score ≤ 7 in the simulated station; the results table reports "100% vs. 31%; p < 0.0001" for transfer need and similar contrasts. Test statistics printed; no effect sizes.

> "The prevalence of identification of immediate transfer need was 100% vs. 31%; p < 0.0001, the percentage of adequate communication skills was 55% vs. 15%; p < 0.0001, the identification of the diagnosis of STEMI was 89% vs. 51%; p < 0.0001 and the persistence to organize the transfer even in case of non-availability of beds was 45% vs. 8%; p < 0.0001"

### Stocco AU 2020 (2)

Stocco AU, Miranda CH. (2020). Clinical Competence in ST-segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction Management by Recently Graduated Physicians Applying for a Medical Residency Program. Arquivos Brasileiros de Cardiologia. https://doi.org/10.36660/abc.20180309

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same high vs low performer comparison; the article reports "a higher prevalence of males was observed in the group with lower performance (63% vs. 52%; p = 0.003)". No age, graduation-time or region differences were observed.

> "a higher prevalence of males was observed in the group with lower performance (63% vs. 52%; p = 0.003) and a higher prevalence of graduates from public universities in the group with greater performance (71% vs. 61%; p = 0.0016)"
