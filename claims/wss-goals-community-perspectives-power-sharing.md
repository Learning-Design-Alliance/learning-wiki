# CPS officials and CBO leaders intended the WSS Framework to ground safety plans in community perspectives, facilitate power-sharing, and redefine school safety

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` District and CBO leaders held three interrelated goals for the WSS Framework: mitigating one-size-fits-all policymaking by centering individual school communities, facilitating power-sharing between district, schools, and community, and redefining safety beyond metal detectors, security officers, and cameras to include school climate and social-emotional wellbeing. ### Johnson 2026

Johnson, D.W. (2026). Co-designing new approaches to school safety: District and community perspectives on Chicago's Whole School Safety Framework. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/co-designing-new-approaches-to-school-safety

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Findings statement from the Question 2 section of the interview-based field scan. District officials described how a safe and welcoming environment "is not always the same thing for everyone," and a CBO leader explained that true partnership "means [that] you share power." District officials sought to "build culture and climate into the definition of [school] safety."

> "CPS officials and CBO leaders intended the WSS Framework to help ground safety plans in individual communities' perspectives, facilitate power-sharing, and support the reimagining of safety."
