## Evidence

### Lotzin Annett 2019

Lotzin Annett, Buth Sven, Sehner Susanne, Hiller Philipp, Pawils Silke, Metzner Franka, Read John, Härter Martin, Schäfer Ingo. (2019). Reducing barriers to trauma inquiry in substance use disorder treatment – a cluster-randomized controlled trial. Substance Abuse Treatment, Prevention, and Policy. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13011-019-0211-8

`q3 · i?`

Linear mixed model from the cluster-randomized trial. The article prints "b = − 0.28" with CI and p = .009; no standardized effect size is reported. The authors suggest trained professionals may have experienced that clients continued treatment despite being asked.

> "Compared with the control group, the intervention group showed a significantly greater decrease in the ‘Fear that client may terminate treatment’ when asking about traumatic events (b = − 0.28, 95% CI [− 0.49, 0.07], p = .009)."

### Lotzin Annett 2019 (2)

Lotzin Annett, Buth Sven, Sehner Susanne, Hiller Philipp, Pawils Silke, Metzner Franka, Read John, Härter Martin, Schäfer Ingo. (2019). Reducing barriers to trauma inquiry in substance use disorder treatment – a cluster-randomized controlled trial. Substance Abuse Treatment, Prevention, and Policy. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13011-019-0211-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Linear mixed model on the sixth barrier in the same trial. The article reports the difference was "not significant" with p = .059; equivalence was not tested. Younger age predicted greater reduction in this belief (b = −0.25, p = .001).

> "The training group showed a slightly greater decrease in the belief that ‘No trauma-specific treatment’ is ‘available’ than the control group, but the difference was not significant (b = − 0.25, 95% CI [− 0.51, − 0.01], p = .059)."
