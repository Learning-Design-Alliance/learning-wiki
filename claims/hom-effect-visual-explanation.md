# Visual puzzle-based representation can convey the Hong-Ou-Mandel effect without mathematical formalism

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The Hong-Ou-Mandel optical effect, which requires quantum interference with no classical analogue, can be conveyed through the puzzle visualization tool with a lower barrier of entry than Dirac-notation mathematics. [→ Nita 2021](#nita-2021)

## Evidence

### Nita 2021

Nita, L., Mazzoli Smith, L., Chancellor, N. and Cramman, H. (2021). The challenge and opportunities of quantum literacy for future education and transdisciplinary problem-solving. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1080/02635143.2021.1920905](https://doi.org/10.1080/02635143.2021.1920905)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

This is a demonstration within the article, not an empirical study: the authors first derive the Hong-Ou-Mandel outcome mathematically using Bra-Ket notation and beam-splitter matrices, then encode the same experiment in the puzzle tool, where 'Quantum interference can be seen in the cancellation of blue and red balls in Figure (2).'

> "We suggest this as a more accessible method to create intuition and understanding of the effect of the beam splitter proposed in the Hong-Ou-Mandel experiment."
