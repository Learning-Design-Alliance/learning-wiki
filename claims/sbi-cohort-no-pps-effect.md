# Cohort (teacher experience with SBI) was not a significant predictor of proportional problem-solving posttest or delayed posttest scores

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Multilevel models showed Cohort was not a significant predictor of PPS posttest or delayed posttest scores; the only significant predictor was PPS pretest. [→ Jitendra 2016](#jitendra-2016)

## Evidence

### Jitendra 2016

Jitendra, A. K., Harwell, M. R., Karl, S. R., Dupuis, D. N., Simonson, G., Slater, S. C., & Lein, A. E. (2016). Schema-based instruction: The effects of experienced and novice teacher implementers on seventh-grade students' proportional problem solving. Learning and Instruction, 44, 53-64. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2016.03.001

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multilevel (two-level) models with covariates at both levels were fitted using HLM 6 software for 1,845 seventh-grade students nested within 73 teachers. The article reports "Cohort was not a significant predictor of PPS posttest scores" and the same pattern emerged for the PPS delayed posttest, with PPS pretest as the only significant predictor.

> "Results indicated that Cohort was not a significant predictor of PPS posttest scores, suggesting that students on average performed the same on the outcome regardless of whether their teacher was part of Cohort 1 or Cohort 2. The only statistically significant predictor among the student covariates was PPS pretest"
