Evidence¶

Bosch 2017¶

Bosch, N., & D'Mello, S. (2017). The Affective Experience of Novice Computer Programmers. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0069-5

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Fine-grained affect incidence analysis of 9696 retrospective affect judgments from 99 novice students learning Python in a lab study; the article reports the five frequent states "collectively accounted for approximately 73 % of all affect judgments" and infrequent states only 10%, with neutral at 17%.

"The results indicated that engagement, confusion, frustration, boredom, curiosity (henceforth referred to as frequent affective states) occurred at least 5 % of the time and collectively accounted for approximately 73 % of all affect judgments."

Related Claims¶

  • Confusion+frustration and curiosity+engagement were the only affective state pairs co-occurring above chance, and within each pair one state was more likely to imply the other — related
  • Confusion and frustration followed incorrect solution submissions and preceded hint requests, while coding was the central activity surrounding all affective states — related
  • After partialling out control variables, boredom and frustration during scaffolded learning negatively correlated with fadeout performance, while confusion↔frustration transitions and boredom→engagement transition positively correlated with it — related
  • Five of six predicted affect transitions were significant and aligned with the affect dynamics model, but boredom transitioned to engagement rather than to frustration — related
  • Interaction-based affect detectors outperformed posture-based detectors, with boredom detected best and posture detectors near chance — related
  • Frustration during the design process was related to unexpected events, with more unexpected events yielding greater frustration and worse perceived performance — related
