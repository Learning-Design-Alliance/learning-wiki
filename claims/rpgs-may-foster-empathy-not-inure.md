# The authors hypothesize that violent role-playing games appear to engage individuals in ethical reasoning that may foster empathy, tolerance, and understanding rather than inuring them to violence

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Simkins 2008

Simkins, D. W., & Steinkuehler, C. (2008). Critical ethical reasoning and role-play. Games and Culture. https://doi.org/10.1177/1555412008317313

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

This is the authors' interpretive alternative hypothesis offered in the introduction, based on interview data from a larger ethnography of role-playing communities; the article offers no direct test of empathy outcomes. The authors state such games "appear to engage individuals in forms of ethical reasoning that may very well foster empathy."

> "Rather, such games appear to engage individuals in forms of ethical reasoning that may very well foster empathy, tolerance, and understanding for others—aptitudes crucial for humane behavior in a complex and culturally diverse world."
