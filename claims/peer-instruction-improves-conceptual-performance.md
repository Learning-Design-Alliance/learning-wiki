## Evidence

### Singh 2023

Singh, C., Maries, A., Heller, K., & Heller, P. (2023). Instructional Strategies that Foster Effective Problem-Solving. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1063/9780735425477_017](https://doi.org/10.1063/9780735425477_017)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports that in Peer Instruction (Mazur, 1997), lectures are built around electronic-voting conceptual questions followed by peer discussion, resubmission, and lecturer explanation; "performance on conceptual problems improved significantly." No effect size is printed.

> "Student performance on conceptual problems improved significantly. Additionally, if quantitative problems contributed to the course grade, then emphasizing conceptual understanding in lectures also slightly improved the performance on quantitative problems."

### Singh 2023 (2)

Singh, C., Maries, A., Heller, K., & Heller, P. (2023). Instructional Strategies that Foster Effective Problem-Solving. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1063/9780735425477_017](https://doi.org/10.1063/9780735425477_017)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review qualifies the quantitative effect: emphasizing conceptual understanding in lectures "slightly improved the performance on quantitative problems" only when quantitative problems contributed to the course grade — a graded-condition moderator stated in the review.

> "if quantitative problems contributed to the course grade, then emphasizing conceptual understanding in lectures also slightly improved the performance on quantitative problems."
