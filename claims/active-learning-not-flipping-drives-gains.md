## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When active learning is held constant, flipping the classroom adds no measurable benefit to learning or attitudes; prior flipped-vs-lecture successes may reflect the shift to active learning. [→ Jensen Jamie L. 2015](#jensen-jamie-l-2015)

## Evidence

### Jensen Jamie L. 2015

Jensen Jamie L., Kummer Tyler A., Godoy Patricia D. d. M. (2015). Improvements from a Flipped Classroom May Simply Be the Fruits of Active Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-08-0129

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' interpretation of their quasi-experimental results: with the 5-E learning cycle in both conditions and only the instructor's role varied, no learning or attitudinal advantages of flipping emerged, supporting active learning as the operative causal factor.

> "This study shows that the flipped classroom does not result in higher learning gains or better attitudes over the nonflipped classroom when both utilize an active-learning, constructivist approach."
