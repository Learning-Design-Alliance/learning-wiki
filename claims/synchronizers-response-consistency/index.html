Evidence¶

Silvia Bonacina 2021¶

Silvia Bonacina, Stephanie Huang, Travis White-Schwoch, Jennifer Krizman, Trent Nicol and Nina Kraus. (2021). Rhythm, reading, and sound processing in the brain in preschool children. npj Science of Learning 6:20. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00097-5

q3 · i2 · associational · r2

Repeated-measures ANOVA on response consistency comparing the two synchronization groups; the article reports "higher response consistency (i.e., more consistent neural responses between trials) than “Non-synchronizers” (F(1,79) = 4.593, p = 0.035, ηp2 = 0.055)".

"Regardless of stimulus, “Synchronizers” had higher response consistency (i.e., more consistent neural responses between trials) than “Non-synchronizers” (F(1,79) = 4.593, p = 0.035, ηp2 = 0.055)"

Silvia Bonacina 2021 (2)¶

Silvia Bonacina, Stephanie Huang, Travis White-Schwoch, Jennifer Krizman, Trent Nicol and Nina Kraus. (2021). Rhythm, reading, and sound processing in the brain in preschool children. npj Science of Learning 6:20. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00097-5

q3 · i3 · associational · r2

Within the same response-consistency ANOVA, the article reports "RC was higher in the added polarity than subtracted ( F(1,79) = 23.197, p < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.227)" and a four-way synchronization group by mode of presentation by time region by polarity interaction (F(1,79) = 11.068, p = .001, ηp2 = .123).

"For response consistency, we also introduced stimulus polarity as one of the within-group variables into the repeated- measures ANOVA. We found that RC was higher in the added polarity than subtracted ( F(1,79) = 23.197, p < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.227)"
