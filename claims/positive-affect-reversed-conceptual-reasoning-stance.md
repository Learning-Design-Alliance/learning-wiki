# An affectively positive experience of informal conceptual reasoning temporarily reversed Judy's stance that conceptual reasoning is useless

## Subclaims

`q1 i?` After successfully reasoning informally about waveforms and feeling satisfied, Judy called conceptual reasoning 'very useful', reversing her earlier stance that it was unproductive. [→ Ayush Gupta 2010](#ayush-gupta-2010)

`q1 i?` When Judy felt good about her informal squeezing-area solution to a waveform question, she called that kind of conceptual reasoning very useful, a reversal the affect-free model does not predict. [→ Ayush Gupta 2013](#ayush-gupta-2013)

## Evidence

### Ayush Gupta 2010

Ayush Gupta, Brian A. Danielak, Andrew Elby. (2010). Understanding students' difficulties in terms of coupled epistemological and affective dynamics. 40th ASEE/IEEE Frontiers in Education Conference. [doi:10.1109/fie.2010.5673256](https://doi.org/10.1109/fie.2010.5673256)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Disconfirmatory Episode A from the clinical interview: pressed to reason through a wave-comparison question without her book, Judy produced informal area-under-the-curve reasoning and expressed satisfaction. The authors report this reversal as evidence an affect-free toy model cannot predict.

> "Though Judy says of her solution, "it's not very formal"—it's the kind of qualitative reasoning she earlier labeled unproductive—in this moment, feeling good about her solution, she calls this kind of conceptual reasoning "very useful.""

### Ayush Gupta 2013

Ayush Gupta, Brian A. Danielak, Andrew Elby. (2013). Toward affect-inclusive models of cognitive dynamics: Coupling epistemological resources and emotions. Pre-print for arxiv.org, under review at Physical Review Special Topics – Physics Education Research. https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.1333

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Episode A from the interview: Judy solved a conceptual waveform question via informal reasoning about area under the curve, felt satisfaction evident in the video, and called the reasoning "very useful." The authors state the affect-free toy model does not predict such a reversal. No effect sizes are reported.

> "Though Judy says of her solution, “it’s not very formal”—it’s the kind of qualitative reasoning she earlier labeled unproductive—in this moment, feeling good about her solution, she calls this kind of conceptual reasoning “very useful.”"
