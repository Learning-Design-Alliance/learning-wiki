# Playing a linear board game including negative integers significantly improved first graders' placement of negative integers on empty number lines

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` First graders who played the negative-integer board game showed a significantly greater mean change in level of understanding for negative integers than control first graders. [→ Bofferding 2015](#bofferding-2015)

## Evidence

### Bofferding 2015

Bofferding, L., Hoffman, A., Suazo, E., & Lisy, N. (2015). Number Line Estimation with Negatives. Proceedings of the 37th annual meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED584204

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment with 47 first graders (22 game, 25 control) randomly assigned after pretest; three 15-minute sessions. The game group's mean change in level of understanding for negatives was significantly higher, t(34) = -2.70, p = .011, with means of "M = 0.55, SD = 1.18" versus control M = -0.24.

> "However, there was a significant difference seen the mean change with respect to the negatives in first grade between the control ( M = -0.24, SD = 0.72) and the game group ( M = 0.55, SD = 1.18), t(34) = -2.70, p = .011."
