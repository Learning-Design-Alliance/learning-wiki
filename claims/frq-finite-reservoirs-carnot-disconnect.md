## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith 2015

Trevor I. Smith, Warren M. Christensen, Donald B. Mountcastle, and John R. Thompson. (2015). Identifying Student Diﬃculties with Entropy, Heat Engines, and the Carnot Cycle. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020116](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020116)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Graded homework data from four years of the Thermo course at School 1 (N = 38), including a preliminary least-efficient-engine version (N = 13) and the full FRQ (N = 25). The article reports 20 out of 38 correct overall and, for the full FRQ, "only 12 students correctly answered all parts of the question."

> "In total, about half of the students for which data ex- ist (20 out of 38) answered the FRQ or a related ques- tion correctly after lecture instruction alone."

### Trevor I. Smith 2015 (2)

Trevor I. Smith, Warren M. Christensen, Donald B. Mountcastle, and John R. Thompson. (2015). Identifying Student Diﬃculties with Entropy, Heat Engines, and the Carnot Cycle. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020116](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020116)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Error analysis of full-FRQ homework responses (N = 25) at School 1: four students identified the most efficient engine as the Carnot cycle but erred on ΔSuni = 0, and four stated ΔSuni = 0 without using it productively for the final reservoir temperature. No effect size is printed.

> "even students who realized that Ralph represented the Carnot cycle did not necessarily recognize that the change in entropy of the universe would have to be zero: four students made this error."
