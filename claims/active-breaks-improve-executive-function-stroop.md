## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Students in the experimental condition scored higher on the Stroop Test than controls across time points (group main effect F(1,58) = 4.26, p = 0.041, η² = 0.0350), but the Group × Time interaction was not significant (F = 0.42, p = 0.516), so the difference cannot be attributed to the active breaks. Latino, F., Tafuri, F., Maisuradze, M., & Tafuri, M. G. (2025). Enhancing Academic Performance, Cognitive Functions, and Mental Well-Being Through Active Breaks: Evidence from a Pilot Study with University Student Sample. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. [doi:10.3390/ijerph22111605](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph22111605)

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r1`

Two-way mixed-design ANOVA across the 12-week trial found the group effect significant only for the Stroop Test, "indicating a difference between groups regardless of time"; Table 3 prints η² = 0.0350 for the group effect. The Stroop time effect (F = 1.01, p = 0.316) and the Group × Time interaction (F = 0.42, p = 0.516) were both non-significant: the groups differed overall, regardless of time, and the difference did not change over the 12 weeks.
