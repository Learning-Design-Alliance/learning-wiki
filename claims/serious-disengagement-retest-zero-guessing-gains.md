# Seriously disengaged initial testers who stopped rapid-guessing on retest showed large RIT gains, averaging about 6 points in Reading and 12 in Math

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For students serious rapid-guessing on the initial test, zero rapid guesses on the retest was associated with mean RIT differences around 6 points for the Reading tests and about 12 points for the Math tests. [→ Wise 2019](#wise-2019)

## Evidence

### Wise 2019

Wise, S. (2019). The Relationship Between Test-Taking Disengagement and Performance on MAP Growth Retests. (The Collaborative for Student Growth at NWEA Working Paper). https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/the-relationship-between-test-taking-disengagement-and-performance-on-map-growth-retests/

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of same-day retests shown in Figures 1-4: students serious (30% or more) rapid-guessing initially but showing "zero rapid guesses on the retest" averaged large RIT gains; gains exceeded typical standard errors of roughly 3.5 points in Reading and 3.0 in Math. No inferential statistics or effect sizes are printed.

> "For each of the figures, less rapid guessing on the retest was associated with a higher positive RIT score difference. For example, when there were zero rapid guesses on the retest, the RIT differences tended to be large, averaging around 6 points for the two Reading tests and about 12 points for the two Math tests."
