## Subclaims
`q3 i1` In the FSM-only subsample analysis, intervention pupils eligible for free school meals scored higher on the Woodcock at end of Reception (Hedges' g=0.22, p=0.03) and showed a marginal advantage on the end-of-Year-1 phonics check (g=0.16, p=0.06). [→ Miller et al. 2017](#miller-et-al-2017)

## Evidence

### Miller et al. 2017

Sarah Miller, Andy Biggart, Seaneen Sloan, Liam O'Hare. (2017). Success for All: Evaluation report and executive summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED581417

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Pre-specified subgroup analysis repeating the main multilevel model on the ever-FSM subsample (n=386 pupils before missing data). The report describes "some evidence that the SfA programme benefits children eligible for FSM", with the mid-point effect statistically significant and the phonics effect marginal.

> "It appears that there is some evidence that the SfA programme benefits children eligible for FSM, particularly with respect to mid -test literacy ability as measured by Woodcock at the end of Reception class (Hedges’ g=0.22, p=0.03) and the post-test phonics check at the end of Year 1 (Hedges’ g=0.16, p=0.06)."
