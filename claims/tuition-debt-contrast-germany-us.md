Narrative review section on US cost of attendance, citing survey figures. The review reports "76% of medical students graduate with educational debt" with the printed averages and medians for public and private schools, and notes tuition at public schools averages $34,592 per year for residents.

> "A total of 76% of medical students graduate with educational debt [60]. Of these students with debt, the average for students graduating from public medical schools is $180,610 (median $180,000) and the average for students graduating from private schools is $203,201 (median $200,000) [60]."

### Zavlin D 2017 (2)

Zavlin D, Jubbal KT, Noé JG, Gansbacher B. (2017). A comparison of medical education in Germany and the United States: from applying to medical school to the beginnings of residency. GMS German Medical Science, Vol. 15, Doc15. [doi:10.3205/000256](https://doi.org/10.3205/000256)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review section on German financial aspects. After a period of 500-Euro-per-semester charges starting around 2005, "these fees were all abolished again by the winter semester of 2014/15", leaving only administrative fees of about 50–90 Euros per semester; cost of living, particularly housing, is the major financial burden.

> "these fees were all abolished again by the winter semester of 2014/15 [29] due to massive protests from students and the general public and shifts in the political atmosphere. Today, merely administrative fees exist ranging around 50–90 Euros per semester"
