## Evidence

### Rickard 1997

Rickard, T. C. (1997). Bending the Power Law: A CMPL Theory of Strategy Shifts and the Automatization of Cognitive Skills. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 126(3), 288-311. https://psycnet.apa.org/record/xge126030288

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Instance-theory power-function fits to Experiment 1 log RT and log SD averaged across participants and problems (Figure 8); the combined fit gave "r 2 for the combined RT and SD fits was .94" with systematic deviations of observed from predicted values.

> "Overall, r 2 for the combined RT and SD fits was .94. Note the systematic deviations of the observed from the predicted values for both the RT and SD"

### Rickard 1997 (2)

Rickard, T. C. (1997). Bending the Power Law: A CMPL Theory of Strategy Shifts and the Automatization of Cognitive Skills. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 126(3), 288-311. https://psycnet.apa.org/record/xge126030288

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The article's abstract states the headline result: "The power law of practice did not hold in the overall data" for mean or SD of latency, while holding within each strategy. This is the article's own summary of the reported fits.

> "The power law of practice did not hold in the overall data for either the mean or the standard deviation of response latency, but it did hold within each strategy (algorithm or retrieval)."
