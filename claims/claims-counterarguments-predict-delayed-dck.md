## Evidence

### Larrain 2018

Larrain, A., Freire, P., Grau, V., López, P., Salvat, I., Silva, M., & Gastellu, V. (2018). The effect of peer-group argumentative dialogue on delayed gains in scientific content knowledge. New Directions for Child and Adolescent Development, 162, 67–87. https://doi.org/10.1002/cad.20263

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Regressions testing which argumentative utterance types predicted delayed DCK scores with time of group work and pre-test controlled. "Claims were significant predictors (β = .29, t = 2.42, p = .020)" in a model explaining 62.3% of variance.

> "Claims were significant predictors (β = .29, t = 2.42, p = .020) in a model that explained 62.3% of variance (F(3/39) = 24.12, p < .01), as well as counter-arguments (β = .26, t = 2.44, p = .019), in a model that explained 62.4% of the variance (F(3/39) = 22.22, p < .01)."
