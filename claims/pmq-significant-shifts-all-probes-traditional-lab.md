# A traditional introductory lab course produced statistically significant pre-post shifts in PMQ reasoning distributions across all four probes, with all shifts toward set-like reasoning nonzero

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across all four PMQ probes, pre- and post-test distributions of all paradigms taken together differed significantly in a traditional introductory lab course, with significant p values for each probe. [→ H. J. Lewandowski 2018](#h-j-lewandowski-2018)

## Evidence

### H. J. Lewandowski 2018

H. J. Lewandowski, Robert Hobbs, Jacob T. Stanley, Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer, and Benjamin Pollard (2018). Student reasoning about measurement uncertainty in an introductory lab course. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/perc.2017.pr.056](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.056)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre-post analysis of PMQ responses from a traditional introductory lab course, comparing score distributions per probe with the Mann-Whitney U-test at the 5% level. The article reports "a statistically significant difference across all probes" with p = 0.03 for SMDS and p ≪ 0.01 for RD and DMSS; no effect size is printed.

> "We see a statistically signiﬁcant diﬀerence across all probes between pre-test and post-test distributions of all paradigms taken together ( p ≪ 0.01 for RD and DMSS, p = 0.01 for UR, and p = 0.03 for SMDS)."
