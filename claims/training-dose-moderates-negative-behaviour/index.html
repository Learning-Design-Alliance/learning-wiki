Evidence¶

Dunning 2019¶

Dunning, D. L., Griffiths, K., Kuyken, W., Crane, C., Foulkes, L., Parker, J., & Dalgleish, T. (2019). Research Review: The effects of mindfulness-based interventions on cognition and mental health in children and adolescents – a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12980

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Random effects meta-regression across all 33 RCTs testing dose (total training hours) as a moderator. The review reports dose "was a significant moderator of a reduction in Negative Behaviour"; no effect size is printed for the moderation.

"total training hours was a significant moderator of a reduction in Negative Behaviour ( Q = 7.30, p = .007), with larger effect sizes related to more hours of training."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Across all RCTs, mindfulness-based interventions significantly improve mindfulness, executive functioning, attention, depression, anxiety/stress and negative behaviours in youth, with small effect sizes — related
  • When only RCTs with active control groups are analysed, MBI benefits are restricted to Mindfulness (d = .42), Depression (d = .47) and Anxiety/Stress (d = .18) — related
  • Effect sizes vary by control group type, with wait-list controls yielding the largest mean effect and no-contact controls the smallest — related
  • Publication bias is evident for Negative Behaviour and Anxiety/Stress across all RCTs and for Mindfulness in active-control RCTs, suggesting overestimation of MBI effects — related
