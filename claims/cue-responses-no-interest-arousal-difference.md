# Sensory evoked responses to the cue stimuli did not differ between high- and low-interest blocks, arguing against block-level arousal differences

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Paired t-tests found no significant differences in C1, P1, N1, or P3 mean amplitude between high and low interest conditions for the cue stimuli. [→ Banerjee Snigdha 2015](#banerjee-snigdha-2015)

## Evidence

### Banerjee Snigdha 2015

Banerjee Snigdha, Frey Hans-Peter, Molholm Sophie, Foxe John J. (2015). Interests shape how adolescents pay attention: The interaction of motivation and top-down attentional processes in biasing sensory activations to anticipated events. The European Journal of Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1111/ejn.12810

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-hoc control analysis prompted by reviewer concern about the blocked design: sensory responses to cue stimuli were compared across high and low interest blocks using the same time windows and ROIs as the S2 analyses. Equivalence was not formally tested.

> "Paired t-tests revealed no significant differences in mean amplitude during the C1 (t(23) = -.691, p = .496), P1 (t(23) = -.887, p = .384), N1 (t(23) = -.345, p = .733), or P3 (t(23) = -1.206, p = .240) timeframes over parieto-occipital scalp regions between high and low interest conditions"
