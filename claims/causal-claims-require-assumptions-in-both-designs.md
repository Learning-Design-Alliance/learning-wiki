## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The article argues that the blurry dichotomy of scientific experimental versus unscientific non-experimental work falls short, since causal interpretation in either design rests on assumptions about data, design, and methods. [→ Zitzmann 2023](#zitzmann-2023)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument in the commentary, citing Wadhwa et al. (2019) and Hübner et al. (2023): causal interpretation requires assumptions in both experimental and non-experimental work, and challenges to replicate randomized experiments further complicate the dichotomy. The authors do not dispute that causality should be at the core of research informing practice.

> "whether or not results represent causal effects can only be answered with great knowledge and assumptions about the data, design, and the methods used, and this holds for non-experimental as well as for experimental work (Wadhwa et al., 2019)."
