Marina Lepp 2026¶

Marina Lepp. (2026). Patterns of Learner–AI Interaction and Academic Performance in an Object-Oriented Programming Course. IEEE Frontiers in Education (FIE) 2026 Conference. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24755

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Kruskal–Wallis tests across clusters on difficulty and understanding ratings (Table II: homework difficulty H = 11.640, p = 0.020; understanding H = 10.646, p = 0.031), with Mann–Whitney U pairwise tests at p < 0.05.

"Specifically, the perceived difficulty of homework in the Very high usage cluster differed significantly from that of the Zero usage cluster, and the perceived difficulty of the first test in the Very high usage cluster differed significantly from the Zero and Low usage clusters (Mann–Whitney U tests, p < 0.05 in all cases)."

Marina Lepp 2026 (2)¶

Marina Lepp. (2026). Patterns of Learner–AI Interaction and Academic Performance in an Object-Oriented Programming Course. IEEE Frontiers in Education (FIE) 2026 Conference. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24755

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Cluster comparison on the eight Likert perception statements (Table III); statement S4 showed the strongest differentiation (H = 20.513, p = 0.000), and norm-related statement S8 also differed (H = 9.764, p = 0.045).

"The Zero usage cluster reported significantly lower trust than the Moderate, “Smart” high, and High usage clusters (Mann–Whitney U tests, p < 0.01 in all cases). Interestingly, the “Smart” high usage cluster reported the greatest trust in AI-generated code."

Related Claims¶

  • Cluster analysis of self-reported GenAI use identifies five profiles, including a strategic high-usage profile low on code generation but high on debugging and explanations — related
  • Profiles differ in self-perceived academic skills: Engaged and Literate users report the highest problem-solving and cognitive endurance gains, while Uncritical Reliant users report the highest independent thinking — related
  • Structural paths did not differ significantly across AI usage-frequency groups, though exploratory coefficients varied numerically — reports the opposite
  • Chatbot users held more positive attitudes and reported greater perceived knowledge gains than non-users — related
  • No significant differences in AI-free assessment performance across GenAI usage clusters in an OOP course — related
  • High-frequency LLM users show higher minimum course outcomes and reduced score variability, though interaction level is not a dominant outcome predictor — reports the opposite
  • Students who implemented code without AI (usage level 0) maintained consistently high code comprehension, while level-3 AI users showed very little comprehension in the Mid period — related
  • Across the student body as a whole, no clear statistical trend was confirmed between AI usage levels and code comprehension — related
  • Prior genAI access and incentives interact on perceived difficulty of the memory test — related
  • Student LLM usage patterns varied across data science courses: Course-BD showed the highest frequency and over-reliance with near-zero perceived learning effect
