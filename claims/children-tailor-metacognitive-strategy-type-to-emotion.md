# Children tailor metacognitive strategy type to emotion: changing goals most for sadness, changing thoughts most for fear

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In Study 2, the frequency of changing goals versus thoughts differed across emotions: changing goals was reported most for sadness and changing thoughts most for fear. [→ Davis 2010](#davis-2010)

## Evidence

### Davis 2010

Davis, E. L., Levine, L. J., Lench, H. C., & Quas, J. A. (2010). Metacognitive Emotion Regulation: Children’s Awareness that Changing Thoughts and Goals Can Alleviate Negative Emotions. Emotion, 10(4), 498–510. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018428

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Mixed ANOVA on specific metacognitive strategy frequencies in Study 2 (92 children, autobiographical events). The emotion × strategy-type interaction was significant, η2p = .08. Post hoc tests: changing goals more for sadness than anger, t(91) = 2.70, p < .01, or fear, t(91) = 2.58, p < .015; changing thoughts more for fear than anger, t(91) = 3.83, p < .001, or sadness, t(91) = 2.40, p = .018.

> "Also, as predicted, the frequency with which children reported changing goals versus thoughts differed across emotions, F(2, 180) = 7.62, p < .01, η2p = .08."
