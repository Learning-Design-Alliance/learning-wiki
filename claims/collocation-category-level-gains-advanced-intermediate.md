# Error correction feedback produces significant gains across all six collocation categories at advanced and intermediate levels but none at the elementary level

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` All six collocation categories (Adj.+Prep., N+Prep., V+Prep., V+N, Adj.+N, N+N) showed significant improvement at advanced and intermediate levels (p<0.05) but not at the elementary level (p>0.05). [→ Jafarpour 2012](#jafarpour-2012)

`q2 i?` Error correction feedback had a significant positive effect on collocation competence at advanced and intermediate levels (p<0.05) but not at the elementary level (p>0.05). [→ Jafarpour 2012](#jafarpour-2012)

## Evidence

### Jafarpour 2012

Jafarpour, A. A., & Sharifi, A. (2012). The effect of error correction feedback on the collocation competence of Iranian EFL learners. Teaching English with Technology, 12(3), 3-17. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1144965

`q2 · i?`

Category-level analysis of pretest-posttest score differences in the experimental group across six collocation categories at three proficiency levels. At the advanced level, significance values ranged from 0.005 to 0.032; at the intermediate level all categories showed p=0.000; at the elementary level p values ranged from 0.41 to 0.48, none significant.

> "A significant relation was observed after intervention in all categories at advanced and intermediate levels (p<0.05). Table 4 shows these results."

### Jafarpour 2012

Jafarpour, A. A., & Sharifi, A. (2012). The effect of error correction feedback on the collocation competence of Iranian EFL learners. Teaching English with Technology, 12(3), 3-17. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1144965

`q2 · i?`

Experimental study of 181 Iranian EFL university students randomly assigned to experimental (n=91) and control (n=90) groups, with a 90-item multiple-choice collocation pretest and a post-test one month later. The experimental group received error correction feedback on detected collocation errors. "significant effect in the advanced and intermediate levels (p<0.05)" was found, with no significant difference at the elementary level.

> "The comparison between the score differences in pretest and post-test of the experimental group showed the significant effect in the advanced and intermediate levels (p<0.05). This difference was not significant at the elementary level (p>0.05)."
