# Taking Algebra I instead of remedial math increased algebra credit earning but raised failure rates and lowered grades for low- and average-ability students, with no test-score benefit

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A 20-percentage-point increase in Algebra I enrollment produced a 10% increase in algebra credit but raised math failure rates by 3.0 to 8.9 percentage points among low- and average-ability students and lowered math grades. [→ Allensworth 2009](#allensworth-2009)
`q2 i?` Math test scores were unaffected by taking Algebra I instead of remedial math for any ability group. [→ Allensworth 2009 (2)](#allensworth-2009-2)

## Evidence

### Allensworth 2009

Allensworth, E., Nomi, T., Montgomery, N., & Lee, V. E. (2009). College Preparatory Curriculum for All: Academic Consequences of Requiring Algebra and English I for Ninth Graders in Chicago. Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis, 31(4), 367-391. https://doi.org/10.3102/0162373709343471

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Results from hierarchical interrupted time-series models with difference-in-difference comparisons, presented as simulations of a 20-percentage-point enrollment increase. The article reports that "Math failure rates increased among low-ability students (3.0 percentage points; p < .01)" and grades declined "the most among average-ability students (0.18 grade points; p < .05)."

> "Math failure rates increased among low-ability students (3.0 percentage points; p < .01) and among average-ability students (8.9 percentage points; p < .05). Students' math grades also decreased across ability groups by moderate amounts, declining the most among average-ability students (0.18 grade points; p < .05)."

### Allensworth 2009 (2)

Allensworth, E., Nomi, T., Montgomery, N., & Lee, V. E. (2009). College Preparatory Curriculum for All: Academic Consequences of Requiring Algebra and English I for Ninth Graders in Chicago. Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis, 31(4), 367-391. https://doi.org/10.3102/0162373709343471

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Null finding from the same enrollment-effect models: ninth-grade math test scores showed no significant change. The authors note that "Only 7 of 48 questions on the Tests of Academic Proficiency test Algebra knowledge," a possible measurement limitation.

> "Math test scores were unaffected by taking algebra instead of remedial math, although it is possible that the test was not sensitive to the change in curriculum. Only 7 of 48 questions on the Tests of Academic Proficiency test Algebra knowledge."
