# Praise has a U-shaped relationship with antisocial behavior, turning significantly positive when praise exceeds roughly 1 SD

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The praise square positively predicts antisocial behavior, indicating a curvilinear relationship between perceived teacher praise and antisocial behavior. [→ Zhu Xiaobo 2024](#zhu-xiaobo-2024)
`q2 i?` Simple slopes of praise on antisocial behavior are significantly negative up to 0 and significantly positive at 2SD. [→ Zhu Xiaobo 2024 (2)](#zhu-xiaobo-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Zhu Xiaobo 2024

Zhu Xiaobo, Jiang Wenyi, Shi Weijin, Shi Junfeng. (2024). Good learners or trouble makers? Study on the relationship between academic performance and antisocial behavior of junior high school students. PLOS ONE 19(1): e0295705. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0295705

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression analysis (Model 7) on the CEPS sample using students' perceived praise from mathematics, Chinese, and English teachers (Cronbach .88). The praise square term carries "β = .040, p < .001"; Fig 3 plots the curve.

> "The regression results of Model 7 in Table 3 show that there is a positive correlation between the praise square and antisocial behavior (β = .040, p < .001), which indicates that there is a curvilinear relationship between praise and antisocial behavior."

### Zhu Xiaobo 2024 (2)

Zhu Xiaobo, Jiang Wenyi, Shi Weijin, Shi Junfeng. (2024). Good learners or trouble makers? Study on the relationship between academic performance and antisocial behavior of junior high school students. PLOS ONE 19(1): e0295705. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0295705

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Simple slope tests (Table 5) of praise on antisocial behavior. Slopes were significantly negative at -2SD, -SD and 0 (e.g., "β = -.161, p < .001"), nonsignificant at 1SD (β = .006), and significantly positive at 2SD.

> "When the praise rises to 2 SD, antisocial behavior has turned to be significantly positively related to the praise (β = .062, p < .010)."
