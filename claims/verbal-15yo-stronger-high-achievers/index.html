Evidence¶

Keller 2020¶

Keller, L., Preckel, F., & Brunner, M. (2020). Nonlinear Relations Between Achievement and Academic Self-Concepts in Elementary and Secondary School: An Integrative Data Analysis Across 13 Countries. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000533

q3 · i0 · associational · r2

Quadratic regressions on representative PISA 2000 verbal-domain data (the only cycle measuring verbal self-concept); 6 of 13 country-level quadratic relations were significant. The printed coefficient "mean β = 0.05" is small in magnitude.

"the average quadratic relation between reading achievement and verbal self-concept was significantly positive in the group of 15-year-olds (mean β = 0.05, 95% CI [0.03, 0.07], Figure 4), it was significantly negative in Grade 4 (mean β = -0.02, 95% CI [-0.03, -0.01])."

Keller 2020 (2)¶

Keller, L., Preckel, F., & Brunner, M. (2020). Nonlinear Relations Between Achievement and Academic Self-Concepts in Elementary and Secondary School: An Integrative Data Analysis Across 13 Countries. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000533

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Interrupted regressions with the two-lines test on the same PISA 2000 verbal data; the printed coefficients "mean β1 = 0.20" and "mean β2 = 0.32" showed significant heterogeneity across countries.

"The achievement-related increase in verbal self-concept in 15-year-olds was somewhat stronger for higher than for lower achieving students (mean β1 = 0.20, 95% CI [0.14, 0.26]; mean β2 = 0.32, 95% CI [0.25, 0.39]; Figure 1b)."

Related Claims¶

  • Nonlinear relations in elementary school are heterogeneous: positive on average in mathematics but significant in only about half the countries, and reversed (negative quadratic) in the verbal domain — related
  • For 15-year-olds in mathematics, the achievement–self-concept relation is not significantly positive for lower achieving students but is significantly positive for higher achieving students — related
  • For Grade 8 students, the mathematics achievement–self-concept relation is stronger for higher achieving students than for lower achieving students — related
  • A linear regression model fails to capture the achievement–self-concept relation for a substantial proportion (12%–22%) of students — related
  • In secondary school, positive quadratic relations between mathematics achievement and mathematics self-concept indicate weaker relations for lower achieving students than for higher achieving students — related
  • Mathematics self-concept correlates strongly with mathematics achievement (r = .43), while verbal self-concept barely does (r = .12) — related
