Yen-Chen Chen. (2020). Boost the Public Demand for Soft Matter Education and Career Opportunities with a Homemade Video. Worcester Polytechnic Institute Interactive Qualifying Project. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2006.09852](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2006.09852)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Survey analysis by major category (Figure 4.2.1), with majors sorted into four categories including Chemical and Bio-engineering and Social Study. Descriptive comparison; no test statistic or effect size is printed.

### Yen-Chen Chen 2020 (2)

Yen-Chen Chen. (2020). Boost the Public Demand for Soft Matter Education and Career Opportunities with a Homemade Video. Worcester Polytechnic Institute Interactive Qualifying Project. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2006.09852](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2006.09852)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same Figure 4.2.1 analysis: the article attributes physical engineering students' largest improvement to insufficient prior knowledge, noting biophysics and soft matter courses are seldom required in engineering curricula. Descriptive only.

> "It is also noted that students who pursue physical engineering related majors have the largest improvement on the levels of interest and understanding to soft matter after watching the video because their knowledge in soft matter is comparatively insufficient before watching the video."
