## Evidence

### Schripsema NR 2016

Schripsema NR, van Trigt AM, van der Wal MA, Cohen-Schotanus J. (2016). How Different Medical School Selection Processes Call upon Different Personality Characteristics. PLoS ONE 11(3): e0150645. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0150645

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Independent samples t-tests in the 1019-student cohort showed female students scored higher on the three named factors; because of these differences, gender was added as a covariate in all subsequent ANCOVA models.

> "mean personality factor scores among female students were significantly higher than among male students for neuroticism (Mean difference MD = 2.98; SE = 0.46; t1017 = 6.46; p<0.001), agreeableness (MD = 3.75; SE = 0.30; t1017 = 12.32; p<0.001), and conscientiousness (MD = 2.98; SE = 0.40; t1017 = 7.49; p<0.001)."
