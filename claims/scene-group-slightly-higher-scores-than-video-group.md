## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The scene group obtained higher scores than the video group on theoretical knowledge (6.41 ± 0.73 vs 5.93 ± 1.31, p = 0.05), subjective learning perception (16.28 ± 3.51 vs 15.92 ± 2.47, p = 0.072), and the overall questionnaire (22.45 ± 4.15 vs 21.48 ± 2.94, p = 0.027); only the overall difference was statistically significant. ## Evidence

### Robles M. J. 2019

Robles M. J., Miralles Ramón, Esperanza Ascension, Riera Mercedes. (2019). Different ways to present clinical cases in a classroom: video projection versus live representation of a simulated clinical scene with actors. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1494-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Between-group comparison in the same quasi-experimental seminar study (scene n = 68, video n = 63). The article states "The difference was only statistically significant in the latter" (the overall questionnaire); theoretical knowledge reached p = 0.05 and subjective perception p = 0.072. No standardised effect size is printed for the between-group contrasts.

> "Students of the scene group obtained higher scores on theoretical delirium knowledge [6.41 ± 0.73 vs 5.93 ± 1.31 (p = 0.05)], subjective learning perception questions (what they thought they knew about delirium) (16.28 ± 3.51 versus 15.92 ± 2.47 (p = 0.072)], and the overall questionnaire (22.45 ± 4.15 versus 21.48 ± 2.94 (p = 0.027)] than the video group."
