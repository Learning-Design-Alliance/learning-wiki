# ERPs reveal SES disparities in children's auditory attention filtering even when behavioral task performance shows no significant SES difference

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In dichotic listening studies, ERPs revealed SES disparities in filtering irrelevant sounds although none of the studies found a significant SES-related difference in behavioral performance. [→ Martha J. Farah 2017](#martha-j-farah-2017)

## Evidence

### Martha J. Farah 2017

Martha J. Farah. (2017). Socioeconomic status and the brain: prospects for neuroscience-informed policy. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41583-018-0023-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The Perspective reports, citing several dichotic listening ERP studies of children, that "None of these studies found a significant SES-related difference in performance" while ERPs did show SES disparities in attention filtering. The null behavioral result is not an equivalence test.

> "None of these studies found a significant SES-related difference in performance, even when performance was below ceiling, indicating that ERPs show greater sensitivity in these studies to disparities in attention compared to the concurrently collected behavior."
