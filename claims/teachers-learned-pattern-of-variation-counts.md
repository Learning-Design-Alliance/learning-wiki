# Teachers in the chemistry learning study concluded that what counts is not active participation but experiencing a pattern of variation and invariance that allows discernment

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` All three chemistry teachers reported learning that student activity alone does not produce learning; the opportunity to experience the intended pattern of variation and invariance is what matters. [→ Ming Fai Pang 2011](#ming-fai-pang-2011)

## Evidence

### Ming Fai Pang 2011

Ming Fai Pang, Lo Mun Ling. (2011). Learning study: helping teachers to use theory, develop professionally, and produce new knowledge to be shared. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-011-9191-4

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Post-lesson interviews with the three chemistry teachers after the two research lesson cycles. The class 4A teacher also reported that the test showed students had not mastered what he had expected, and that the whole team's research was 'a win–win situation'.

> "what really counts is not whether students are actively involved and are fully participating in activities, but whether they engage in activities that give them an opportunity to experience a pattern of variation and invariance that allows them to discern the intended object of learning."
