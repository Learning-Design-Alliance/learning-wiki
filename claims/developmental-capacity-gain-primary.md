# Working memory capacity increases with age independently of knowledge, encoding speed, and articulation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Age differences in working memory capacity persist when knowledge, filtering ability, encoding speed, and articulation are controlled, suggesting a primary maturational change. [→ Cowan Nelson 2014](#cowan-nelson-2014)

## Evidence

### Cowan Nelson 2014

Cowan Nelson. (2014). Working Memory Underpins Cognitive Development, Learning, and Education. Educational Psychology Review. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4207727/

`q2 · i?`

The review reports a series of experiments using a change-detection procedure with colored items. Slow one-item-per-second presentation and an articulatory suppression manipulation left the developmental effect unchanged, and filtering of irrelevant shapes matched across age groups, so "neither encoding speed nor articulation could account for the age differences."

> "It appears that neither encoding speed nor articulation could account for the age differences. So we believe that age differences in capacity may be primary rather than derived from another process."
