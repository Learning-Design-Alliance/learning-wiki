# Student-prepared aids did not yield significantly better exam performance than standard no-aids testing conditions

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Although students in the student-prepared aids condition scored higher on average than students in the no-aids condition, the difference was not statistically significant (M = 79.38 vs M = 77.78). [→ Larwin et al. 2008](#larwin-et-al-2008)

## Evidence

### Larwin et al. 2008

Karen H. Larwin, David A. Larwin, Jennifer Gorman. (2008). Mnemonic aids during tests: Worthless frivolity or effective tool in statistics education? Journal of Instructional Pedagogies. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Mnemonic+aids+during+tests+statistics+education

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post hoc comparison from the same quasi-experimental factorial ANOVA of course exam scores; the article reports the descriptive means for the two conditions and states the difference "did not reach statistical significance". No effect size is printed for this contrast, so none is reported.

> "In contrast, the mean difference in exam performance between the Student-prepared aids condition (M = 79.38) and the No aids condition (M = 77.78) did not reach statistical significance."
