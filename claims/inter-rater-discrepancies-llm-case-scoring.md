## Evidence

### Zhang Qi 2025

Zhang Qi, Huang Zijing, Huang Yuqiang, Wang Geng, Zhang Riping, Yang Jianling, Cheng Yinglin, Chen Binyao, Wang Hongxi, Qiu Kunliang, Chen Haoyu. (2025). Generative AI in medical education: feasibility and educational value of LLM-generated clinical cases with MCQs. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08085-8

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVA on quality scores from eight teachers across twelve cases, reported in Results. Teachers formed high-, intermediate- and low-scoring tiers (e.g., teacher6 57.17 ± 1.12 vs teacher3 45.58 ± 3.60).

> "Significant inter-rater discrepancies were observed in scoring consistency for identical clinical cases (F = 16.050, P < 0.001)."

### Zhang Qi 2025 (2)

Zhang Qi, Huang Zijing, Huang Yuqiang, Wang Geng, Zhang Riping, Yang Jianling, Cheng Yinglin, Chen Binyao, Wang Hongxi, Qiu Kunliang, Chen Haoyu. (2025). Generative AI in medical education: feasibility and educational value of LLM-generated clinical cases with MCQs. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08085-8

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion of the observed tiered scoring: the two lowest-scoring evaluators had extensive experience in undergraduate medical education and exam development and applied more stringent quality standards.

> "Analysis revealed distinct scoring patterns linked to teaching experience: less experienced teachers tended to assign higher scores, while more senior educators gave lower ratings."
