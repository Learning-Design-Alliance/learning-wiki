## Evidence

### S. Vonschallen 2026

S. Vonschallen, D. Oberle, T. Schmiedel, and F. Eyssel. (2026). Knowledge-Based Design Requirements for Generative Social Robots in Higher Education. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12873 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2602.12873](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2602.12873)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative interview findings from the Results section: while all participants wanted assertiveness for motivation, some preferred a robot that pushes students to learn while others perceived this as too intrusive, so the desired degree "highly varied from person to person".

> "Participants unanimously agreed that the robot should have some level of assertiveness to motivate students to learn. However, the desired degree of assertiveness highly varied from person to person"

### S. Vonschallen 2026 (2)

S. Vonschallen, D. Oberle, T. Schmiedel, and F. Eyssel. (2026). Knowledge-Based Design Requirements for Generative Social Robots in Higher Education. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12873

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In the Results section, most participants preferred a study-buddy role on equal footing with students, but specific preferences ranged across coach, mentor, tutor, formal lecturer, and technical learning-tool roles, supporting the need for customization.

> "Overall, participants repeatedly expressed the need to customize the robot's role (S2, S4, L3, L4, L5)."
