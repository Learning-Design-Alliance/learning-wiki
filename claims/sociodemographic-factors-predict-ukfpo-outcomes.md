## Evidence

### MacKenzie R K 2016

MacKenzie R K, Cleland J A, Ayansina D, Nicholson S. (2016). Does the UKCAT predict performance on exit from medical school? A national cohort study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2016-011313

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel linear analysis (Table 5) of the linked national cohort with confounders adjusted. The article reports these factors were "significantly and positively associated with all four outcome measures"; no standardised effect sizes are printed.

> "Table 5 shows that UKCAT cognitive total score, all cognitive domain scores, except AR for the SJT (particularly VR), more affluent IMD quintile, female gender, increasing age and the year in which the UKCAT was attempted were significantly and positively associated with all four outcome measures"

### MacKenzie R K 2016 (2)

MacKenzie R K, Cleland J A, Ayansina D, Nicholson S. (2016). Does the UKCAT predict performance on exit from medical school? A national cohort study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2016-011313

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multilevel model coefficient for IMD quintile in the Results section. The article also reports per-quintile reductions of 0.18, 0.23 and 0.13 points for EPM decile, EPM total and SJT respectively; these are unstandardised point differences, not effect sizes.

> "Each increase in IMD quintile (where higher quintiles indicate lower socioeconomic class) gives 0.357 fewer UKFPO points, an average difference of 1.43 UKFPO points between the highest and lowest IMD quintiles after correcting for other variables."
