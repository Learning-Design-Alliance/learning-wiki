# PD support type and salience depends on a teacher team's current phase in the adaptive learning cycle

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment · `i1` small · n=2 teacher teams, one over 1 year (3 video-feedback sessions), one over 2+ years (6 sessions plus a Year 3 follow-up)

Reflective, video-based examination of one's own practice appears most generative during a teacher team's problematization and conservation phases, while invoking and experimenting with external practices, frameworks, or curricular resources appears more generative during reorganization and growth phases — meaning the same PD design can produce very different trajectories for two teams depending on what phase (per the [adaptive cycles framework](../theories/adaptive-cycles-framework.md)) they enter it in and what resources their broader context provides.

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` One teacher team, entering the PD partnership already past problematization, engaged mainly in reorganization and growth (invoking external groupwork practices) but did not reach a stable conservation phase within the study period; a second team progressed through all four phases across two years, with video-based reflection driving problematization and external resources (a teacher's prior curriculum experience, a colleague's new departmental authority) driving reorganization. [→ Ehrenfeld & Stengel 2025](#ehrenfeld-stengel-2025)

`q2 i1` The team that completed a full cycle had access to institutional and personal resources (departmental flexibility, relevant prior teaching experience) that the other team's context did not provide, suggesting a team's broader "learning ecology," not just the PD design itself, shapes whether and how far a cycle progresses. [→ Ehrenfeld & Stengel 2025](#ehrenfeld-stengel-2025)

## Evidence

### Ehrenfeld & Stengel 2025

Ehrenfeld, N., & Stengel, B. (2025). The temporal dimension of teacher learning in a video-based professional development program: An ecological perspective. *Journal of the Learning Sciences, 34*(3), 285-328. [https://doi.org/10.1080/10508406.2025.2468205](https://doi.org/10.1080/10508406.2025.2468205)

`q2 · comparative case study of two school-based teacher teams using interaction analysis of video-based feedback sessions, no control condition` · `i1-i2 · clear qualitative contrast in trajectory completeness and resource use between the two teams; no quantified outcome measure` · `n=2 teacher teams, one over 1 year (3 video-feedback sessions), one over 2+ years (6 sessions plus a Year 3 follow-up)`

Two school-based teacher teams participated in [video-based feedback cycles](../strategies/video-based-feedback-cycle-for-teacher-teams.md) aimed at supporting collaborative groupwork facilitation. One team entered the partnership already having identified their problem of practice, and their work together mostly involved reorganization and growth (experimenting with groupwork structures) without reaching a stable, consolidated ("conservation") phase within the study window. The other team's problematization was substantially driven by watching their own video and recognizing overreliance on teacher-led answers; their subsequent reorganization drew on a team member's prior experience with problem-based curricula and a colleague's new departmental leadership role, and by a Year 3 follow-up, groupwork had become a routine, expected classroom structure.

## Discussion
This is a two-case comparative study with no control condition and no standardized outcome measure — the "phases" are the researchers' own interpretive coding of interaction data, not an independently validated instrument, and the two teams differed in more ways (school context, starting point, personnel) than just which phase they began in, so the phase-based explanation cannot be cleanly isolated from these other differences. The claim should be read as a plausible interpretive framework for why the same broad PD design produced different trajectories in these two specific teams, not as a validated principle for timing PD support in general.
