## Evidence

### Tiffin Paul A. 2016

Tiffin Paul A., Mwandigha Lazaro M., Paton Lewis W., Hesselgreaves H., McLachlan John C., Finn Gabrielle M., Kasim Adetayo S. (2016). Predictive validity of the UKCAT for medical school undergraduate performance: a national prospective cohort study. BMC Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-016-0682-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariable multilevel models adjusting for advanced qualification attainment (best of three A-levels or equivalent) predicting theory exam performance from UKCAT scales, shown in Fig. 6; 50 models in total were run per exam type. The article reports coefficients generally reduced but remained significant.

> "In all but two cases (‘quantitative reasoning’ for year 4 and ‘decision analysis’ for year 5), their magnitudes reduce upon adjusting for the effect of advanced qualification. In addition, it may be noted that adjusting for advanced qualification renders the coefficient of ‘abstract reasoning’ for year 3 and ‘quantitative reasoning’ for year 1 non-significant."

### Tiffin Paul A. 2016 (2)

Tiffin Paul A., Mwandigha Lazaro M., Paton Lewis W., Hesselgreaves H., McLachlan John C., Finn Gabrielle M., Kasim Adetayo S. (2016). Predictive validity of the UKCAT for medical school undergraduate performance: a national prospective cohort study. BMC Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-016-0682-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

GEE logistic regression (Table 4) predicting odds of passing each year at first attempt, adjusted for advanced qualifications and time. The article reports the adjusted quantitative reasoning OR as 1.03 (0.95–1.10), P = 0.50, while other scales remained significant.

> "However, the effect of the ‘quantitative reasoning’ score is less than that of the other UKCAT components and is not statistically significant once the influence of advanced qualifications is accounted for."
