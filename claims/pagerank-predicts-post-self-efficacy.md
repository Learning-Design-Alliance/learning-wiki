## Evidence

### Remy Dou 2016

Remy Dou, Eric Brewe, Justyna P. Zwolak, Geoff Potvin, Eric A. Williams, and Laird H. Kramer. (2016). Beyond performance metrics: Examining a decrease in students' physics self-efficacy through a social networks lens. Physical Review Physics Education Research 12, 020124. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.12.020124

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bootstrapped linear regressions (1000 iterations) predicting post SOSESC-P total scores while controlling for pre scores tested inDegree, outDegree, and PageRank in separate models; "PageRank explained an additional 3.7% of the variance in students' post self-efficacy scores (see Table II)."

> "the models revealed that PageRank was the only statistically significant predictor besides the control variable. Regression coefficients for inDegree and outDegree had confidence intervals that included zero. PageRank explained an additional 3.7% of the variance in students' post self-efficacy scores (see Table II)."

### Dou 2018

Dou, R., Brewe, E., Potvin, G., Zwolak, J. P., & Hazari, Z. (2018). Understanding the development of interest and self-efficacy in active-learning undergraduate physics courses. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1080/09500693.2018.1488088](https://doi.org/10.1080/09500693.2018.1488088)

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Structural equation modeling (model A) on data from three MI courses (N = 221), controlling for pre-course self-efficacy and interests. The article reports the PageRank-to-self-efficacy path was "significant (β = 0.17, p < .01)"; model fit was RMSEA = 0.08, SRMR = 0.08.

> "The path leading from PageRank directly to post physics self-efficacy was significant (β = 0.17, p < .01), as well as the path from post physics self-efficacy to post physics interest (β = 0.20, p < .001) and general science interest (β = 0.19, p < .001)."
