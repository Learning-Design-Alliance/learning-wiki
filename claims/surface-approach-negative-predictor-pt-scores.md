## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In step-wise regressions predicting PT scores, surface approach uniquely predicted scores above year in programme, age, and gender, with higher surface scores predicting lower PT scores at both time points. [→ Chen Yan 2015](#chen-yan-2015)

## Evidence

### Chen Yan 2015

Chen Yan, Henning Marcus, Yielder Jill, Jones Rhys, Wearn Andy, Weller Jennifer. (2015). Progress testing in the medical curriculum: students' approaches to learning and perceived stress. BMC Medical Education 15:147. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0426-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Step-wise hierarchical regressions at each time point entered year, gender and age at step 1 and approaches and stress at step 2, with PT score as dependent variable; the article reports that "higher surface approach scores predicted lower PT scores at both time points".

> "In contrast, students' surface approach to learning score was a unique negative predictor of PT scores, above and beyond the influence of year in programme, age, and gender, such that higher surface approach scores predicted lower PT scores at both time points."
