# Selective attention moderated the effect of stimulus condition on children’s originality: better selective attention favoured the low stimulus environment, weaker selective attention the high stimulus environment

## Evidence

### van Dijk 2020

van Dijk, M., Blom, E., Kroesbergen, E. H., & Leseman, P. P. M. (2020). The Influence of Situational Cues on Children’s Creativity in an Alternative Uses Task and the Moderating Effect of Selective Attention. Journal of Intelligence, 8(4), 37. https://doi.org/10.3390/jintelligence8040037

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA on decorrelated originality in the same between-subjects experiment (N = 70 analysed minus outliers). The "interaction effect of condition and selective attention on originality was significant" with η2 = 0.11.

> "The interaction effect of condition and selective attention on originality was significant with a medium to strong effect size, F(1, 62) = 7.26, p = 0.01, η2 = 0.11."

### van Dijk 2020 (2)

van Dijk, M., Blom, E., Kroesbergen, E. H., & Leseman, P. P. M. (2020). The Influence of Situational Cues on Children’s Creativity in an Alternative Uses Task and the Moderating Effect of Selective Attention. Journal of Intelligence, 8(4), 37. https://doi.org/10.3390/jintelligence8040037

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Regions-of-significance analysis (Bauer and Curran) of the interaction, plotted in Figure 2. The low-vs-high originality advantage was "only significant at a relatively high level of selective attention"; the high-vs-low advantage was significant at moderately below-average selective attention.

> "The higher bound of significance of the interaction effect (SA ≥ −1.92) is located beyond one standard deviation above the mean (SA −2.49), indicating that the positive effect of the low vs. high stimulus condition is only significant at a relatively high level of selective attention."
