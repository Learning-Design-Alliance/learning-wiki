# Final grade adds predictive power beyond centrality for outdegree and closeness, but indegree no longer predicts persistence once grade is accounted for

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Grade alone was a significantly better predictor than the full model only for indegree, while two-predictor models (grade plus centrality) fit best for outdegree and closeness. [→ Zwolak 2016](#zwolak-2016)

## Evidence

### Zwolak 2016

Zwolak, J. P., Dou, R., Williams, E. A., & Brewe, E. (2016). Students' network integration as a predictor of persistence in introductory physics courses. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.010113](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.010113)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Likelihood ratio test comparing full logistic models (persistence ∼ centrality + grade) against grade-only models found "grade alone gave a signiﬁcantly better ﬁt only for indegree", with VIF checks confirming no collinearity between grade and outdegree (VIF = 1.00) or closeness (VIF = 1.01).

> "we found that grade alone gave a signiﬁcantly better ﬁt only for indegree [χ2(1) = 0.31, adjusted p = 0.62]"

### Zwolak 2016 (2)

Zwolak, J. P., Dou, R., Williams, E. A., & Brewe, E. (2016). Students' network integration as a predictor of persistence in introductory physics courses. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.010113](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.010113)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same likelihood ratio comparison showed "Full models remained better ﬁts for outdegree" and closeness, per the printed χ2 statistics and adjusted p values, so the combined centrality-plus-grade models retained their advantage.

> "Full models remained better ﬁts for outdegree [χ2(1) = 4.56, adjustedp = 0.048] and closeness [χ2(1) = 8.25, adjusted p = 0.007]"
