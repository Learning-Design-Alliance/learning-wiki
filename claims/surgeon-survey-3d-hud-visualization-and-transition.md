## Evidence

### Aadithreya Varman 2024

Aadithreya Varman, Varman N V Arulmozhi, Balakumar Dinesh. (2024). Assessing the surgical competency of novice surgeons by using a three-dimensional heads-up display microscope. Indian Journal of Ophthalmology. https://doi.org/10.4103/IJO.IJO_2677_23

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Questionnaire of the same 20 surgeons (30 variables) found 20 (100%) preferred the 3D display's peripheral field, image quality and depth of field. Every participant also "reported no significant difficulty in transitioning between the 3D and TM approach."

> "When using the 3D heads-up display, all study participants, 20 (100%), felt that the peripheral field, the image quality, and the depth of the field were better than that of conventional microscopy."

### Aadithreya Varman 2024 (2)

Aadithreya Varman, Varman N V Arulmozhi, Balakumar Dinesh. (2024). Assessing the surgical competency of novice surgeons by using a three-dimensional heads-up display microscope. Indian Journal of Ophthalmology. https://doi.org/10.4103/IJO.IJO_2677_23

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Survey part of the study: all 20 doctors endorsed the 3D heads-up display "as both a teaching and training tool and for general use" and reported easy transition between systems.

> "All study participants, 20 (100%), reported no significant difficulty in transitioning between the 3D and TM approach. Every study participant endorsed the use of a 3D heads-up display as both a teaching and training tool and for general use."
