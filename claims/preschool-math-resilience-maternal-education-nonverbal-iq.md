## Evidence

### van Viersen 2026

van Viersen, S., Psyridou, M., & Torppa, M. (2026). General introduction to the special issue on resilience in learning. Learning and Instruction, 101, Article 102254. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102254

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The editors report, citing Muñez et al. (2025), a latent growth mixture modelling analysis of preschool mathematics resilience in which "6% of the at-risk sample could be identified as resilient" with higher initial levels and stronger growth.

> "Using latent growth mixture modelling, they found that 6% of the at-risk sample could be identified as resilient; despite their high risk for learning difficulties, this group broke the trend compared to their similar risk peers, showing higher initial levels of math achievement as well as stronger growth."

### van Viersen 2026 (2)

van Viersen, S., Psyridou, M., & Torppa, M. (2026). General introduction to the special issue on resilience in learning. Learning and Instruction, 101, Article 102254. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102254

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The editors report, citing Muñez et al. (2025), that nonverbal intelligence predicted resilience status while executive function did not, and that maternal education, "as a proxy for the home learning environment", was the key protective factor.

> "While nonverbal intelligence, but not executive function, predicted resilience status, maternal education (as a proxy for the home learning environment) emerged as a key protective factor for the resilient developmental trajectory of preschoolers."
