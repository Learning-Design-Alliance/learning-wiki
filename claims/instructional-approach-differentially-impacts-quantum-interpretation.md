## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Instructors who taught their own interpretations produced student post-instruction responses aligned with that instruction, while the agnostic instructor's students showed no bias. [→ Charles Baily 2015](#charles-baily-2015)

## Evidence

### Charles Baily 2015

Charles Baily, Noah D. Finkelstein. (2015). Teaching Quantum Interpretations: Revisiting the goals and practices of introductory quantum physics courses. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020124](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020124)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Discussion of the comparative survey study of three engineering modern physics courses (Instructors A, B, C) using an online Likert-scale interpretation survey with reasoning textboxes. The article reports that post-instruction responses aligned with each instructor's explicit approach, and Instructor C's students were evenly split. The article says the results demonstrate that "different instructional approaches with respect to interpretation can have different, measurable impacts on student thinking."

> "Instructors A and B both taught their own physical interpretations of the double-slit experiment, and the most common responses from their respective students are aligned with that instruction."
