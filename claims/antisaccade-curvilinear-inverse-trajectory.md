# Antisaccade performance follows a curvilinear developmental trajectory best fit by an inverse function, with intra-subject latency variability decreasing with age

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Antisaccade error rates and latencies fall steeply through childhood with a lower but significant slope through adolescence, stabilizing in adulthood, and intra-subject variability decreases with age. [→ Constantinidis Christos 2019](#constantinidis-christos-2019)

## Evidence

### Constantinidis Christos 2019

Constantinidis Christos, Luna Beatriz. (2019). Neural Substrates of Inhibitory Control Maturation in Adolescence. Trends in Neurosciences. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tins.2019.07.004

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing multiple studies, that antisaccade developmental trajectories are curvilinear with inverse-function best fits, depicting "steep decreases in error rates" through childhood and a lower but significant adolescent slope, confirmed in a longitudinal study of ages 8 to 30.

> "The developmental trajectory is curvilinear [14,25–27], and an inverse function provides best fits to it [6,14,26]."
