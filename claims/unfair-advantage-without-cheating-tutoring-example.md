## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Help such as parental or tutor proofreading, and teachers knowingly giving inadequate grades, produce unfair advantages without cheating, so discrepancies between grades and worth are not treated as problematic only when cheating causes them. [→ Mathieu Bouville 2009](#mathieu-bouville-2009)

## Evidence

### Mathieu Bouville 2009

Mathieu Bouville. (2009). Why is cheating wrong?. [doi:10.1007/s11217-009-9148-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11217-009-9148-0)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument (section 2.3) using hypothetical cases: an essay proofread by parents or a tutor gives a better grade to a student of equal talent who received help, and teachers knowingly give good students bad grades. The article concludes "Cheating and tutoring both create an unfair advantage". This is a philosophical argument, not an empirical study.

> "a smart and talented student, a student with a tutor, and a cheater will look the same even though the first is superior to the other two. Cheating and tutoring both create an unfair advantage."
