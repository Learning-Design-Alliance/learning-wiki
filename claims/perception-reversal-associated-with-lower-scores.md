# Teams whose perception of who uses more AI reversed between t1 and t3 achieved lower scores than teams with stable perceptions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Reversal teams scored lower (medians 94.5 vs. 98 percentage points; Mann–Whitney U = 192, p = .011, r_rb = .427), and the association held in robust regression controlling for initial misalignment and prior performance (b = −1.90, t = −2.32, p = .021). [→ Laura Graf 2026](#laura-graf-2026)

## Evidence

### Laura Graf 2026

Preprint, Technical University of Munich. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23237 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2606.23237](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2606.23237)

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Mann–Whitney U comparison of teams with versus without a t1→t3 perception reversal in the 103-pair study; the article prints the rank-biserial effect size "r_rb = .427" (with U = 192, p = .011). Only 25 teams experienced a reversal, so the authors call the result indicative rather than conclusive.

> "Teams experiencing a direction reversal achieved lower scores than teams with stable perceptions (medians: 94.5 vs. 98 percentage points; Mann–Whitney U = 192, p = .011, r_rb = .427)."
