## Evidence

### Nathan Hagen 2024

Nathan Hagen. (2024). Graduate education in optics in Japan and the United States: impact of funding levels on educational structure. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2401.08933](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2401.08933)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

The article's comparative analysis reports that because US students expect support, enrollment is capped by professor funding; a professor hiring a PhD student as an RA would pay roughly $50,000 per year including tuition waiver and stipend.

> "the number of PhD students that an academic department allows to en- roll each year is determined almost entirely by the amount of funding that the department's professors can provide for their support."

### Nathan Hagen 2024 (2)

Nathan Hagen. (2024). Graduate education in optics in Japan and the United States: impact of funding levels on educational structure. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2401.08933](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2401.08933)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

The comparative survey attributes Japan's significantly fewer PhD students to lack of basic financial support and to companies' historic preference for educating employees in-house; a stigma against PhD-holders entering industry research careers is also cited.

> "The lack of basic financial support is one of the reasons why there are significantly fewer PhD students in Japan than there are in the United States."
