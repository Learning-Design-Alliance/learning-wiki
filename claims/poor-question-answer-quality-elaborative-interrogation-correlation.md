# Answers to embedded questions and elaborative interrogations were of poor quality, and elaborative-interrogation answer quality correlated with posttest scores only for pretest-incorrect students

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` For pretest-incorrect participants, better elaborative-interrogation answers were associated with higher posttest scores, r = .39, p = .002; there was no association for embedded-question accuracy in either subgroup. [→ Clinton 2013](#clinton-2013)

## Evidence

### Clinton 2013

Clinton, V., Alibali, M. W., & Nathan, M. J. (2013). The effects of diagrams and questioning-while-reading on learning from a statistics lesson. In Martinez, M. & Castro Superfine, A. (Eds.), Proceedings of the 35th annual meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education. Chicago, IL: University of Illinois at Chicago. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED584483

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Correlational analyses of answer quality within the questioning conditions. Embedded questions were answered correctly on average 3.08 of 6 (SD = 1.70); elaborative interrogation averaged 4.80 of 18 (SD = 2.27), with about 37% of answers missing, circular, or wrong.

> "However, for participants who answered the pretest incorrectly, there was a positive correlation between the quality of the answers to the elaborative interrogations and posttest scores, r = .39, p = .002."
