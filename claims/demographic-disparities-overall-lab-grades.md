## Evidence

### Navlekar Anisha S. 2025

Navlekar Anisha S., Theobald Elli J., Griffith Ken, Limeri Lisa B. (2025). Strategically creating maximally heterogeneous lab groups did not improve group performance in an introductory biology lab class. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0323799

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the best-fitting overall-lab-grade models, all three demographics remained as fixed effects; the authors state these "repeated trends known from prior literature that students with minoritized social identities experience worse academic outcomes". Descriptive means only.

> "Final lab grades were lower for students identifying as women than men in both biology 1 (mwomen = 83.05; mmen = 85.22) and in biology 2 (mwomen = 80.87; mmen = 82.96)."

### Navlekar Anisha S. 2025 (2)

Navlekar Anisha S., Theobald Elli J., Griffith Ken, Limeri Lisa B. (2025). Strategically creating maximally heterogeneous lab groups did not improve group performance in an introductory biology lab class. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0323799

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same overall-lab-grade models showed URM students scoring "3.17 points less" in biology 1 and "2.09 points less" in biology 2 than non-URM counterparts; these are unstandardized point differences, not effect sizes.

> "Similarly, URM students scored 3.17 points less than non-URM counterparts in biology 1 and 2.09 points less in biology 2."
