Evidence¶

Paul 2025¶

Paul, U., Mantravadi, A., Shah, J., Shah, S., Mylavarapu, S. V., Rashid, M. P., & Gehringer, E. (2025). Scaling Success: A Systematic Review of Peer Grading Strategies for Accuracy, Efficiency, and Learning in Contemporary Education. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11677

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports, citing James et al., that consensus weighting with an Individual Weight Factor "reduced high-grade errors by over 30% compared to the naive mean" and beat least trimmed squares and Huber estimators. No effect size is printed.

"James et al. [77] demonstrated that consensus-based weighting with an Individual Weight Factor (IWF) reduced high-grade errors by over 30% compared to the naive mean and outperformed both least trimmed squares and Huber estimators."

Paul 2025 (2)¶

Paul, U., Mantravadi, A., Shah, J., Shah, S., Mylavarapu, S. V., Rashid, M. P., & Gehringer, E. (2025). Scaling Success: A Systematic Review of Peer Grading Strategies for Accuracy, Efficiency, and Learning in Contemporary Education. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11677

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review attributes to James et al. a higher exact match rate for consensus-weighted grades versus arithmetic average, "60.7% vs. 56.4%". These are descriptive rates; no effect size is printed.

"James et al. [69] reported a higher exact match rate of consensus-based weighted grades to true grades than arithmetic average (60.7% vs. 56.4%)."
