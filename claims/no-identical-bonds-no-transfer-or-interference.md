# Where there are no identical bonds between stimulus and response in two processes, training in one produces neither transfer nor interference in the other

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When two processes share no identical stimulus-response bonds, training in one yields neither positive nor negative effect on the other, as shown in addition-division and cancellation comparisons. [→ Poffenberger 1915](#poffenberger-1915)

## Evidence

### Poffenberger 1915

Poffenberger, A. T., Jr. (1915). The influence of improvement in one simple mental process upon other related processes. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 6(8), 459-474. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0075269

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Controlled training experiment: trained group (4 subjects) practiced addition 100 times; both trained and control groups were retested on division. Gains of 21.3 vs 19.5 seconds differed by 1.8 seconds, judged of no significance. "A difference of 1.8 seconds between 21.3 and 19.5 would be of no significance."

> "If one takes into account the reliability figure attached to the gain in the case of the trained and control groups in the test series, one finds no difference in the gain made by the trained and control groups."
