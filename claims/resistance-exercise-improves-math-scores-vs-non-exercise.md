# Acute resistance exercise improves middle school students' standardized math test performance compared to non-exercise

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Following 20 min of resistance exercise, eighth-grade students scored significantly higher on standardized mathematics tests than after a non-exercise control (η2 = 0.068). [→ Harveson Andrew T. 2019](#harveson-andrew-t-2019)

## Evidence

### Harveson Andrew T. 2019

Harveson Andrew T., Hannon James C., Brusseau Timothy A., Podlog Leslie, Papadopoulos Charilaos, Hall Morgan S., Celeste EvaRose. (2019). Acute Exercise and Academic Achievement in Middle School Students. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph16193527

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Randomized crossover experiment with 63 eighth-graders (mean age 13.7 ± 0.5 years) completing 20 min resistance exercise or a seated DVD control, tested 5–20 min after each bout with 10-question standardized math tests. Repeated measures ANOVA showed "significant differences in mean math test performance between resistance and non-exercise" with η2 = 0.068.

> "Analysis via repeated measures ANOVA showed significant differences in mean math test performance between resistance and non-exercise (F1,62 = 4.50, p = 0.038, η2 = 0.068)."
