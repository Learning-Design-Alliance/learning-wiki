# Elementary/middle-grade math scores improved incrementally across all three eras while reading scores remained fairly flat for two decades

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Math scores in grades three through eight improved in all three eras, moving the typical student from just meeting state standards to the low end of the meets-standards range. [→ Luppescu 2011](#luppescu-2011)
`q2 i?` Reading scores in the elementary/middle grades showed little improvement over the three eras, with gains limited to lower grades in Era 2 and to white and Asian students. [→ Luppescu 2011 (2)](#luppescu-2011-2)

## Evidence

### Luppescu 2011

Luppescu, S., Allensworth, E. M., Moore, P., de la Torre, M., Murphy, J., with Jagesic, S. (2011). Trends in Chicago's Schools Across Three Eras of Reform. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/trends-chicagos-schools-across-three-eras-reform-full-report

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Trend analysis of rescaled CPS elementary/middle-grade test scores across the three reform eras. The report describes math gains as incremental: students now score like students "one year older in the early 1990s," yet remain at the low end of the meets-standards range. No effect size is printed.

> "Math scores have risen in the elementary/middle grades; students are now scoring at a level similar to students who were one year older in the early 1990s, at least in some grade levels."

### Luppescu 2011 (2)

Luppescu, S., Allensworth, E. M., Moore, P., de la Torre, M., Murphy, J., with Jagesic, S. (2011). Trends in Chicago's Schools Across Three Eras of Reform. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/trends-chicagos-schools-across-three-eras-reform-full-report

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Trend analysis of CPS reading scores by age cohort across three reform eras. The report states reading scores "have not shown much improvement," with some Era 2 lower-grade gains and modest gains among white and Asian students only. No effect size is printed.

> "Reading scores in the elementary/middle grades have not shown much improvement over the three eras of school reform."
