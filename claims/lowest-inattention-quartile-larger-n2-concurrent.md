# Adolescents in the lowest quartile of concurrent inattention symptoms show larger negative N2 amplitudes than the other three quartiles, with no linear concurrent correlation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` A quartile-based ANCOVA on concurrent inattention symptoms showed a main effect for group on N2 amplitude (η2p = 0.14), with the lowest quartile showing larger negative amplitudes than the other three quartiles combined (η2p = 0.13). [→ Einziger Tzlil 2021](#einziger-tzlil-2021)

## Evidence

### Einziger Tzlil 2021

Einziger Tzlil, Ben-Shachar Mattan S., Devor Tali, Shmueli Michael, Auerbach Judith G., Berger Andrea. (2021). "My Brain Can Stop": An ERP Study of Longitudinal Prediction of Inhibitory Control in Adolescence. Brain Sciences, 11(1), 100. https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci11010100

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

ANCOVA (controlling mothers' education) on concurrent symptoms at 17 years in the ERP sample of 57. The article reports the lower quartile "showed larger negative amplitudes than those in the other three quartiles together, F(1, 52) = 7.94, η2p = 0.13"; the omnibus main effect gave η2p = 0.14.

> "Participants in the lower quartile of inattention symptoms showed larger negative amplitudes than those in the other three quartiles together, F(1, 52) = 7.94, MSE = 3.65, p < 0.01, η2p = 0.13; no significant differences were found between the top three quartiles."
