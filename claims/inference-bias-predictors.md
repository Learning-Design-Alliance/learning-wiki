# For inference questions, lower reading skill, higher self-efficacy, and higher self-reported monitoring strategies contributed to stronger overestimation, with openness contributing only for predictions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For inference questions, a lower reading skill was associated with stronger overestimations than higher reading skill, and results were similar for prediction and postdiction bias. [→ Stefanie Golke 2022](#stefanie-golke-2022)
`q3 i?` A higher self-efficacy contributed to stronger overestimations for inference questions, and a higher openness contributed to stronger overestimations but only for prediction bias. [→ Stefanie Golke 2022 (2)](#stefanie-golke-2022-2)
`q3 i?` A higher amount of self-reported general monitoring strategies was related to stronger overestimations for inference questions, contrary to the expectation that lower levels would overestimate more. [→ Stefanie Golke 2022 (3)](#stefanie-golke-2022-3)

## Evidence

### Stefanie Golke 2022

Stefanie Golke, Tim Steininger, & Jörg Wittwer. (2022). What Makes Learners Overestimate Their Text Comprehension? The Impact of Learner Characteristics on Judgment Bias. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-022-09687-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression results for inference-question bias in the N = 255 sample: "the results for the prediction bias and postdiction bias were similar," and as expected "a lower reading skill was associated with stronger overestimations than higher reading skill."

> "Regarding the inference questions, the results for the prediction bias and postdiction bias were similar. As expected, a lower reading skill was associated with stronger overestimations than higher reading skill"

### Stefanie Golke 2022 (2)

Stefanie Golke, Tim Steininger, & Jörg Wittwer. (2022). What Makes Learners Overestimate Their Text Comprehension? The Impact of Learner Characteristics on Judgment Bias. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-022-09687-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the inference-question regressions, "a higher compared with a lower self-efficacy" contributed to stronger overestimations, and openness did so "although only for the prediction bias."

> "a higher compared with a lower self-efficacy and, although only for the prediction bias, a higher compared to a lower openness contributed to stronger overestimations"

### Stefanie Golke 2022 (3)

Stefanie Golke, Tim Steininger, & Jörg Wittwer. (2022). What Makes Learners Overestimate Their Text Comprehension? The Impact of Learner Characteristics on Judgment Bias. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-022-09687-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

For inference questions, higher self-reported general monitoring strategies (General Monitoring Strategies Checklist) predicted stronger overestimation, against the expectation that lower levels would overestimate more; even lower levels "still showed some overestimation."

> "self-reported general monitoring strategies was related to stronger overestimations compared to lower levels of this learner characteristic, which, against our expecta- tion, still showed some overestimation"
