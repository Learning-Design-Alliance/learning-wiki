Tamara van Gog & Liesbeth Kester & Kim Dirkx & Vincent Hoogerheide & Joris Boerboom & Peter P. J. L. Verkoeijen. (2015). Testing After Worked Example Study Does Not Enhance Delayed Problem-Solving Performance Compared to Restudy. Educ Psychol Rev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-015-9297-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Small-scale random-effects meta-analysis of the study's eight delayed final test comparisons between restudy and testing, based on 180 restudy and 173 testing participants, conducted in Cumming's ESCI software. "seven comparisons yielded a numerical testing effect, whereas one comparison showed a numerical reversed testing effect"; the combined effect indicated mean performance was somewhat higher under testing.

> "seven comparisons yielded a numerical testing effect, whereas one comparison showed a numerical reversed testing effect. However, the CIs are very wide, which implies that each of the comparisons provides a rather imprecise estimate of the testing effect parameter"
