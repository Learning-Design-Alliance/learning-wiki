# Sandwiching computational tasks between analytical work in homework problems raised computational task completion from 25-40% to 80-90% of students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Embedding computational tasks within analytical homework problems, rather than appending them at the end, was associated with a large increase in the proportion of students completing the computational tasks (from 25-40% in prior semesters to 80-90%). [→ Marcos D. Caballero and Steven J. Pollock 2013](#marcos-d-caballero-and-steven-j-pollock-2013)

## Evidence

### Marcos D. Caballero and Steven J. Pollock 2013

Marcos D. Caballero and Steven J. Pollock. (2013). A Model for Incorporating Computation Without Changing the Course: An example from middle-division classical mechanics. arXiv. [doi:10.1119/1.4837437](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4837437)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Course-implementation comparison across semesters of CM 1 at CU Boulder, reported in the section on computational instruction. In prior semesters computational tasks were tacked on to the end of problems and were skipped; the redesign sandwiched tasks with analytical work. The authors report "25-40% of the time in previous semesters" versus 80-90% now; no statistical test or effect size is printed.

> "Students completed computational tasks 25-40% of the time in previous semesters, while 80-90% of students now complete these tasks."
