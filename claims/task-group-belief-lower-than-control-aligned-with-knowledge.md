# After the task, the high-level task group's belief of understanding was significantly lower than the control group's and more closely aligned with actual knowledge

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` After completing the task, the task group's belief of understanding was significantly lower than the control group's (d = 0.65), and the belief–knowledge correlation was stronger in the task group (r = 0.36 vs r = 0.21). ## Evidence

### Madeleine Hörnlein and Christoph Kulgemeyer 2025

Madeleine Hörnlein and Christoph Kulgemeyer. (2025). Learning Science and the Illusion of Understanding: Exploring the Effects of Integrating Learning Tasks after Explainer Videos. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2512.02824](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2512.02824)

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Study 1 randomized comparison of the high-level task group against the video-only control group after the task. The article reports the task group significantly lower "with a medium effect size (𝑑=0.65)"; before the task the groups did not differ (t(182)=−0.17, p=0.863).

> "However, after completing the task, the task group’s belief of understanding was significantly lower than that of the control group, with a medium effect size (𝑡(182)=4.39, 𝑝<0.001, 𝑑=0.65)."

### Madeleine Hörnlein and Christoph Kulgemeyer 2025 (2)

Madeleine Hörnlein and Christoph Kulgemeyer. (2025). Learning Science and the Illusion of Understanding: Exploring the Effects of Integrating Learning Tasks after Explainer Videos. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2512.02824](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2512.02824)

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r?`

Correlational analysis in Study 1 between belief of understanding and conceptual knowledge: r = 0.21 (p = 0.045) in the control group after the video versus r = 0.36 (p < 0.001) in the task group after the task, which the article reads as a more accurate alignment.

> "After working on the tasks, a stronger and significant correlation was observed in the task group (𝑟=0.36, 𝑝<0.001), suggesting a more accurate alignment between perceived and actual understanding."
