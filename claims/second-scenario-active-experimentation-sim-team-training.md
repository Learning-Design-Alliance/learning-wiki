## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Omitting the active experimentation phase risks learners not applying new principles in a safe environment, receiving no feedback, and losing untested conceptualised changes over time. [→ Stocker Martin 2014](#stocker-martin-2014)

## Evidence

### Stocker Martin 2014

Stocker Martin, Burmester Margarita, Allen Meredith. (2014). Optimisation of simulated team training through the application of learning theories: a debate for a conceptual framework. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-14-69

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument in Debate 1, applying Kolb's four-stage experiential learning cycle to session structure. The article argues that since "it is important for learners to go through all four steps for the learning to be effective", a second scenario after debriefing is preferable. No empirical data are reported.

> "There are three main outcomes due to omission of this active experimentation phase: First, on an individual basis it may not be possible for the learner to apply the concluded principles in a safe environment, without facing the risk of real adverse outcomes and reactions."
