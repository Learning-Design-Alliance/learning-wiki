## Evidence

### Eleni Fleva 2014

Eleni Fleva. (2014). Imagined Contact Improves Intentions towards a Hypothetical Peer with Asperger Syndrome but not Attitudes towards Peers with Asperger Syndrome in General. World Journal of Education, 5(1). https://doi.org/10.5430/wje.v5n1p1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA on SAQ recreational scores in the 416-participant experiment found no significant imagined-activity effect; equivalence was not tested. The authors attribute this to the match between the imagined scenario and the school environment.

> "For the SAQ recreational activities the main effect of imagined activity was found not to be statistically significant (p= .12) nor were there any other significant main effects or interactions"
