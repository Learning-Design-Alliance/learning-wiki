## Evidence

### Stewart 2026

Stewart, T. M., McCluskey, G., Johnson, H., Fry, D., McAra, L., & Booth, J. N. (2026). Co-developing the Child and Adolescent Mentally Healthy Classroom (CAMH Class) intervention for use in routine teaching in secondary schools. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0359563

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Qualitative finding from co-development workshops with teachers (n = 10) and young people (n = 7). Participants converged on using realistic scenarios, case studies and characterisation to embed flexible thinking; young people cautioned that personal or high-stakes activities would be better as individual activities.

> "Overall, embedding flexible thinking and problem solving activities were perceived as feasible, acceptable and of recognised benefit through co-regulation, playfulness, characterisation and low-stakes whole-class design."
