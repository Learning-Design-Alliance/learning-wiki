# Sequence mining detects distinct SRL process sequences by prior knowledge group: high prior knowledge students generated more frequent sequences and predominantly accurate metacognitive judgment sequences

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` SPAM sequence mining on self-initiated SRL process codes extracted 20 sequences with support values of at least 50% for HPK students versus 10 for LPK students. [→ Taub 2019](#taub-2019)
`q2 i?` The only sequence with support value over 80% in both groups was metacognitive: HPK students' was two accurate metacognitive judgments (SV = 84%) and LPK students' was inaccurate metacognitive judgments (SV = 82%). [→ Taub 2019 (2)](#taub-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Taub 2019

Taub, M., & Azevedo, R. (2019). How Does Prior Knowledge Influence Eye Fixations and Sequences of Cognitive and Metacognitive SRL Processes during Learning with an Intelligent Tutoring System? Int J Artif Intell Educ (2019) 29:1-28. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-018-0165-4

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

SPAM sequence mining of log-file data on user-initiated SRL processes, run separately for each prior knowledge group (HPK n = 19, LPK n = 11) with 2-3 length codes. Sequences and support values were extracted descriptively; this research question was not statistically examined.

> "there were 20 sequences with a support value of at least 50% (the sequence appeared at least once for half of the participants) for high prior knowledge participants, and there were 10 sequences with a support value of 50% for low prior knowledge participants."

### Taub 2019 (2)

Taub, M., & Azevedo, R. (2019). How Does Prior Knowledge Influence Eye Fixations and Sequences of Cognitive and Metacognitive SRL Processes during Learning with an Intelligent Tutoring System? Int J Artif Intell Educ (2019) 29:1-28. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-018-0165-4

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive sequence-mining result: only one sequence exceeded 80% support in each group, both metacognitive codes only (code 5, SV = 84% for HPK; code 6, SV = 82% for LPK). LPK students did not engage in any sequences containing cognitive processes.

> "a majority of participants with high prior knowledge were engaging in sequences with two accurate metacognitive judgments, where a majority of participants with low prior knowledge were engaging in sequences with inaccurate metacognitive judgments."
