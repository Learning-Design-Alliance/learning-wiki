## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Comparing activation signatures between novice and expert on a task may reveal differences in regions, order and timing of activation, which can be used to refocus an intervention on the factors of expert performance. [→ Robert Colvin 2016](#robert-colvin-2016)

## Evidence

### Robert Colvin 2016

Robert Colvin. (2016). Optimising, generalising and integrating educational practice using neuroscience. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/npjscilearn.2016.12

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument, not a tested result: the article proposes that novice-expert signature comparison can inform optimisation, illustrated hypothetically with novices experiencing bottlenecks in a visual processing region during a geometry task. No data are reported.

> "A comparison of activation signatures between novice and expert on a task may show that the same regions are activated in the same order, but with shorter (and/or longer) times spent in each. Alternatively, experts may show activation in other regions"
