## Evidence

### Tom Zhang 2021

Tom Zhang, Michelle Taub, and Zhongzhou Chen. (2021). Measuring the Impact of COVID-19 Induced Campus Closure on Student Self-Regulated Learning in Physics Online Learning Modules. Learning Analytics and Knowledge '21. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1145/3448139.3448150](https://doi.org/10.1145/3448139.3448150)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Across the module-level ANCOVA and sequence-level Friedman/post hoc analyses of Spring 2020 and Fall 2019 log data, the article reports no significant differences for early completion, passing rates, or revisiting. One exception noted: revisiting of sequence S8 was higher than average in Spring 2020, opposite to the predicted impact.

> "We found no significant differences comparing a number of data indicators before and after campus closure: the number of modules completed earlier than the due date, module passing rate before and after study, the number of module revisiting events."

### Tom Zhang 2021 (2)

Tom Zhang, Michelle Taub, and Zhongzhou Chen. (2021). Measuring the Impact of COVID-19 Induced Campus Closure on Student Self-Regulated Learning in Physics Online Learning Modules. Learning Analytics and Knowledge '21. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1145/3448139.3448150](https://doi.org/10.1145/3448139.3448150)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Wilcoxon test comparing the distribution of modules each student revisited in the three days before the second midterm exam between the two semesters. The article reports "Z=8115.5, p=0.696", i.e. no statistically significant difference; equivalence was not tested.

> "There does not appear to be a statistically significant difference in the distribution of the number of modules revisited by students within 3 days of the exam according to the Wilcoxon Test (Z=8115.5, p=0.696)."
