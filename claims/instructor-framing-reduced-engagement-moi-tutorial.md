## Evidence

### Kashyap 2026

Kashyap, J. S., Devaty, R. P. and Singh, C. (2026). Instructor Framing and Incentives Shape Physics Students' Engagement and Learning Gains from an Inquiry-Based Electrostatics Tutorial on the Method of Images. [doi:10.3390/educsci16040594](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci16040594)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Instructor 3's administration in the fourth year: students were told the activities were part of an educational research study, not tied to the electrodynamics course content, and would earn a small amount of extra credit irrespective of correctness.

> "the instructor of one of the classes offered students a small amount of extra credit for engaging with the inquiry -based tutorial and tests, explicitly noting that these activities were not part of the current course syllabus and were primarily conducted to support physics education research."

### Kashyap 2026 (2)

Kashyap, J. S., Devaty, R. P. and Singh, C. (2026). Instructor Framing and Incentives Shape Physics Students' Engagement and Learning Gains from an Inquiry-Based Electrostatics Tutorial on the Method of Images. [doi:10.3390/educsci16040594](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci16040594)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

The authors' interpretation of the unanticipated finding: framing the activities as not relevant to the course may have reduced student motivation to engage with the tutorial and assessments. This is an author interpretation, not a tested contrast; no effect size printed.

> "This kind of framing likely influenced students' motivation and engagement, which underscores how the way the instructor frames the inquiry -based instructional tasks to their students can have a significant impact on student performance."
