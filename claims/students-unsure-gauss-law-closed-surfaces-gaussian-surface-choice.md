# Questions about net flux through closed surfaces and choice of Gaussian surfaces were extremely difficult, with students unsure which surfaces Gauss' law applies to

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On a question asking which surfaces around an infinite line of charge have net flux equal to λL/ε0, many students claimed only symmetric closed surfaces could be used to determine net flux, and some included the open square sheet. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016)
`q2 i?` On the Gaussian-surface-choice question, all answer alternatives were selected with almost even frequency, and students choosing the cubic-box option were confident the field magnitude was the same at every point on the cube. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)](#chandralekha-singh-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Student understanding of Symmetry and Gauss' law. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07725](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07725)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Written and interview analysis of paired items about four surfaces coaxial with an infinite line of charge; item 5 had 38% and item 6 had 42% correct of 168 students per Table 1. The article reports students "were not comfortable with the statement of Gauss’ law" relating net flux to enclosed charge, and some claimed the net flux should be the same through all surfaces including the square sheet.

> "Students were not comfortable with the statement of Gauss’ law that relates the net ﬂux through a closed surface to the net charge enclosed."

### Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Student understanding of Symmetry and Gauss' law. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07725](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07725)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Written-test analysis of the Gaussian-surface-choice item for an infinite line of charge; 42% of 168 students answered correctly per Table 1. In Question (6), "students had to choose the Gaussian surfaces that would help them determine the electric ﬁeld at point P easily", and all alternative choices were selected with an almost even frequency.

> "students had to choose the Gaussian surfaces that would help them determine the electric ﬁeld at point P easily"
