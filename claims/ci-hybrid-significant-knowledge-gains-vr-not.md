# In a pilot randomized study, CI and CI + VR produced statistically significant immediate Knowledge gains while VR's Knowledge gain did not reach significance

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` CI (g = 0.95) and CI + VR (g = 1.12) showed significant Knowledge gains from Pre-test to Post-test; VR's smaller gain (+23.12%) was not significant (p = 0.078) despite g = 0.71. [→ Nalampang Sakarat 2026](#nalampang-sakarat-2026)

## Evidence

### Nalampang Sakarat 2026

Nalampang Sakarat, Yanchinda Jirawit, Prapayasatok Sangsom, Charuakkra Arnon, Kaewkhong Kreetha, Järnstedt Jorma. (2026). Digital and immersive approaches to anatomy education: a pilot comparative study of CI, VR, and hybrid learning in implant planning. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09010-3

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Within-group paired t tests in a pilot pre–post study of 20 undergraduate dental students randomized to CI, VR, or CI + VR after a baseline lecture. The Results report "p = 0.008; Hedges’ g = 0.95, large" for CI and "p = 0.025; Hedges’ g = 1.12, large" for CI + VR Knowledge gains.

> "Statistically significant Knowledge gains were observed in the CI group (2.00 → 2.71; +35.50%; p = 0.008; Hedges’ g = 0.95, large) and the CI + VR group (1.83 → 2.50; +36.61%; p = 0.025; Hedges’ g = 1.12, large)."
