## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Paper participants depicted more than twice as many happy characters on average, and the control group averaged about three times as much facial expressiveness per shot as the experimental group. [→ M. Madden 2007](#m-madden-2007)

## Evidence

### M. Madden 2007

M. Madden, P.W.H. Chung, C.W. Dawson. (2007). The Effect of a Computer-Based Cartooning Tool On Children's Cartoons and Written Stories. Loughborough University Institutional Repository. http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-nd/2.5/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Fine-grained facial-expression coding in the Ramayana cartoon comparison across four expression categories. The happiness difference carried to gender: experimental girls depicted almost six times as many happy faces as boys, while boys depicted more than four times as many angry faces as girls.

> "Table 5 shows more than twice as many happy characters depicted, on average, using paper. No signiﬁcant diﬀerences between the average number of depictions of unhappiness/discomfort, surprise or anger are found."

### M. Madden 2007 (2)

M. Madden, P.W.H. Chung, C.W. Dawson. (2007). The Effect of a Computer-Based Cartooning Tool On Children's Cartoons and Written Stories. Loughborough University Institutional Repository. http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-nd/2.5/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Coarse-grained expressiveness-per-shot analysis in the Ramayana cartoon comparison, covering shot distance, facial expression and pose categories. Pose expressiveness per shot was not significantly different; no gender differences in overall expressiveness per shot were found.

> "the control group averaged about three times as much facial expressiveness per shot as the experimental group (control average: 1.4, experimental average: 0.4, p=0.00), and that the experimental group may have averaged more pose expressiveness per shot (control average: 1.1, experimental average: 1.5, p=0.55)"
