## Evidence

### Zhong Siyi 2026

Zhong Siyi, Tang Xin, Cheng Xiaojun, Pan Yafeng. (2026). Bodily maps of subject-specific feelings and academic emotions among high school students. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-04283-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pixelwise generalized linear models (FDR-corrected, q < 0.05) linking BSMs to grades found "modest positive associations between body sensations in the head and torso regions" and subject-specific performance; beta-values are depicted in Fig. 6 without printed coefficients.

> "GLM analyses further revealed modest positive associations between body sensations in the head and torso regions and academic performance in the corresponding subject domains (in particular, English, Physics, Chemistry, Biology, and History)."

### Zhong Siyi 2026 (2)

Zhong Siyi, Tang Xin, Cheng Xiaojun, Pan Yafeng. (2026). Bodily maps of subject-specific feelings and academic emotions among high school students. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-04283-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The same GLM analysis of academic-emotion maps against overall performance found "only head activity during boredom showed a weak negative correlation with overall academic achievement"; no numeric coefficient is printed.

> "In contrast, for academic emotions, only head activity during boredom showed a weak negative correlation with overall academic achievement (Fig. 6)."
