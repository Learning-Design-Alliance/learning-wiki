## Evidence

### Sela Y 2025

Sela Y, Cassuto M, Capua T, Dadbaeiv L, Molad J, Hallevi H, Gadoth A, Sadan O, Seyman E. (2025). Acute neurology simulation-based training boosts confidence and reduces anxiety in novice neurology residents and nursing staff. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07948-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre-post comparison within the national SBME cohort of 79 participants using Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on VAS self-readiness items; readiness to identify rose from a mean of "2.84 (SD0.73) pre vs. 3.39 (SD0.63) post simulation, p < 0.001".

> "Wilcoxon signed-rank test showed participants’ self-assessed readiness levels to identify and manage neurological emergencies increased (z=-5.24, z=-5.63, p < 0.001, respectively)."

### Sela Y 2025 (2)

Sela Y, Cassuto M, Capua T, Dadbaeiv L, Molad J, Hallevi H, Gadoth A, Sadan O, Seyman E. (2025). Acute neurology simulation-based training boosts confidence and reduces anxiety in novice neurology residents and nursing staff. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07948-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same single-arm pre-post cohort study; readiness to manage rose from "2.61 (SD0.72) pre vs. 3.27 (SD 0.64 post-simulation, p < 0.001". Only test statistics and descriptive means are printed; no effect size is reported.

> "Following the course, participants reported improved self-readiness in identifying neurological emergencies [mean Self-readiness of 2.84 (SD0.73) pre vs. 3.39 (SD0.63) post simulation, p < 0.001] and managing neurological emergencies [mean Self-readiness of 2.61 (SD0.72) pre vs. 3.27 (SD 0.64 post-simulation, p < 0.001]"
