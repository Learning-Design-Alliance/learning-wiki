## Evidence

### Callaghan 2025

Callaghan, K., Kestin, G., Klales, A., McCarty, L., & Deslauriers, L. (2025). Active Learning Through Flexible Collaborative Exams: Improving STEM Assessments. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2502.01994](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2502.01994)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Independent-samples t-test on post-Midterm-2 survey anxiety ratings in the quantum mechanics course (Results, Fig. 3 context). In-person students (M = 4.6, SE = 0.16) reported slightly lower anxiety than online students (M = 4.1, SE = 0.18); no effect size was printed.

> "An independent-samples t-test comparing the anxiety levels of students in the asynchronous online and in-person formats revealed a statistically significant difference, t(55) = 2.08, p < 0.04."

### Callaghan 2025 (2)

Callaghan, K., Kestin, G., Klales, A., McCarty, L., & Deslauriers, L. (2025). Active Learning Through Flexible Collaborative Exams: Improving STEM Assessments. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2502.01994](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2502.01994)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Survey agreement ratings on whether removing the time constraint increased collaboration, reported in Results for both asynchronous cohorts. The comparison did not reach the stated significance threshold (p < 0.10), so this finding is marginal.

> "Both cohorts also demonstrated agreement that removing the time constraint on the asynchronous exam led to increased collaborations and discussion (online: 4.1 + 0.2 versus in-person: 4.48 + 0.16, p < 0.10)."
