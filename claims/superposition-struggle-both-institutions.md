## Evidence

### Justyna P. Zwolak 2013

Justyna P. Zwolak, Mary Bridget Kustusch and Corinne A. Manogue (2013). Re–thinking the Rubric for Grading the CUE: The Superposition Principle. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1307.0902](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1307.0902)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cross-institutional categorization of the superposition question responses (90 OSU, 64 CU tests) grouping correct (A/B) and incorrect or ambiguous (C/D) categories. The authors report that "in both schools only ∼ 15% of all students took a clearly correct" approach. No effect size is printed.

> "It was surprising to us that in both schools only ∼ 15% of all students took a clearly correct (electric or potential ﬁeld) approach to this problem (∼ 30% of relevant responses)."

### Justyna P. Zwolak 2013 (2)

Justyna P. Zwolak, Mary Bridget Kustusch and Corinne A. Manogue (2013). Re–thinking the Rubric for Grading the CUE: The Superposition Principle. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1307.0902](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1307.0902)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The authors' concluding summary of the same comparative response analysis, stating that "more than 40% at each school do not recognize the need for superposition" despite both schools' reforms, and that "almost 70% do not clearly identify what they are superposing". No effect size is printed.

> "Yet, despite these reforms, more than 40% at each school do not recognize the need for superposition on this problem and of those that do, almost 70% do not clearly identify what they are superposing."
