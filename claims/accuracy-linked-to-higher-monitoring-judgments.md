# Adults who answered health math problems correctly reported higher monitoring judgments than those who answered incorrectly, with larger differences at posttest than pretest

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` Accurate responders reported higher monitoring judgments than inaccurate responders on all problems, with the smallest difference at pretest (d = 0.16) and posttest d's between 0.30 and 0.42. [→ Scheibe 2022](#scheibe-2022)

## Evidence

### Scheibe 2022

Scheibe, D. A., Fitzsimmons, C. J., Mielicki, M. K., Taber, J. M., Sidney, P. G., Coifman, K., & Thompson, C. A. (2022). Confidence in COVID problem solving: What factors predict adults’ item-level metacognitive judgments on health-related math problems before and after an educational intervention? Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09300-3

`q3 · i0`

Independent-samples t-tests comparing monitoring judgments of accurate vs. inaccurate responders in the 1,177-participant sample. The pretest difference was "t(1175) = 2.81, p < 0.01, d = 0.16"; posttest d's ranged 0.30–0.42, suggesting group-level metacognitive awareness.

> "the difference between accurate and inaccurate responders on monitoring judgments was smallest at pretest, t(1175) = 2.81, p < 0.01, d = 0.16, compared to posttest d’s between 0.30 and 0.42"
