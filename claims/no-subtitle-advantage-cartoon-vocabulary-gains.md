# Watching cartoons with English subtitles produces no statistically significant vocabulary-gain advantage over watching without subtitles among upper-intermediate ELT students

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Independent-samples t-test showed no statistically significant difference between the subtitle group's and the no-subtitle group's vocabulary gains (p=.094), despite a descriptively higher mean gain in the subtitle group. [→ Ali Karakaş and Arif Sariçoban 2011](#ali-karakas-and-arif-saricoban-2011)

## Evidence

### Ali Karakaş and Arif Sariçoban 2011

Ali Karakaş and Arif Sariçoban. (2011). The Impact of Watching Subtitled Animated Cartoons on Incidental Vocabulary Learning of ELT Students. Teaching English with Technology, 12(4), 3-15. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1144953

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre-test/post-test experiment with 42 first-grade ELT students at Mehmet Akif Ersoy University, randomly assigned to subtitle (n=21) and no-subtitle (n=21) groups; independent-samples t-test on VKS gains, "no statistically significant difference between the two groups’ gains (p=.094)". Equivalence was not tested.

> "Though Group A slightly outperformed Group B on average scores, the t-test results demonstrated that there was no statistically significant difference between the two groups’ gains (p=.094)."
