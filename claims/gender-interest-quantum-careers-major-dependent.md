# Men reported greater interest in quantum careers than women across all majors, but this gender difference disappeared among physical science and computer science majors

## Evidence

### Jessica L. Rosenberg 2024

Jessica L. Rosenberg, Nancy Holincheck, Michele Colandene. (2024). Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics Undergraduates’ Knowledge and Interest in Quantum Careers: Barriers and Opportunities to Building a Diverse Quantum Workforce. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010138](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010138)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Independent samples t-test on survey slider responses (1–5) found men (M = 3.256) reported greater interest than women (M = 2.608), t(115) = 3.201, p=0.002; no effect size was printed.

> "Across all majors, students who identified as men (n = 68, M = 3.256, SD = 1.01) reported a greater interest in pursuing a career in quantum than students who identified as women (n = 49, M = 2.608, SD = 1.17), t(115) = 3.201, p=0.002."

### Jessica L. Rosenberg 2024 (2)

Jessica L. Rosenberg, Nancy Holincheck, Michele Colandene. (2024). Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics Undergraduates’ Knowledge and Interest in Quantum Careers: Barriers and Opportunities to Building a Diverse Quantum Workforce. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010138](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010138)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The same t-test restricted to computer science and physical science majors showed no statistically significant difference between women (M = 3.000) and men (M = 3.30), t(92) = 1.289, p = 0.100; equivalence was not tested.

> "When the independent samples t-test was repeated with only computer science and physical science majors, we found no statistically significant difference between women (n = 32, M = 3.000, SD = 0.20) and men (n = 62, M = 3.30, SD = 0.13) in their interest in a quantum career, t(92) = 1.289, p = 0.100."
