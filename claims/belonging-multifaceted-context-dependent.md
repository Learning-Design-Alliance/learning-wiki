## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Belonging to a group, feeling like one belongs at a moment, and carrying a durable sense of belonging are distinct expressions that research often conflates, producing a jingle-jangle fallacy problem. [→ Kelly-Ann Allen 2025](#kelly-ann-allen-2025)

## Evidence

### Kelly-Ann Allen 2025

Kelly-Ann Allen. (2025). School Belonging: Evidence, Experts, and Everyday Gaps. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10055-x

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Expert interview with Richard Lee in this qualitative narrative-analysis study. Lee distinguishes momentary from durable belonging and notes belonging with peers differs from belonging to an institution, warning of "a jingle-jangle fallacy problem". No quantitative data reported.

> "Belongingness is multi-faceted and context-dependent. To belong to a group is not the same as feeling like you belong."
