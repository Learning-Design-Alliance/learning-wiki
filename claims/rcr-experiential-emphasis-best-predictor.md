# A practical, experiential course emphasis is the best predictor of effective research integrity training across learning outcomes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In meta-regression, a practical, experiential engagement with concrete ethical problems significantly predicted judgment-related learning (coefficient 1.10, p < .001). [→ Johannes Katsarov 2021](#johannes-katsarov-2021)
`q3 i?` A practical course emphasis also significantly predicted knowledge acquisition (coefficient 0.33, p < .05) and combined learning outcomes (coefficient 0.74, p < .001). [→ Johannes Katsarov 2021 (2)](#johannes-katsarov-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Johannes Katsarov 2021

Johannes Katsarov, Roberto Andorno, André Krom, Mariëtte van den Hoven. (2021). Effective Strategies for Research Integrity Training—a Meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review (2022) 34:935–955. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09630-9

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Meta-regression of judgment-related effect sizes from the 30 included RCR studies; the best model explained 98% of between-groups variance (F = 20.70, p = 0.000). The article reports "a practical, experiential engagement with concrete ethical problems" as a significant positive predictor; Table 4 prints its coefficient as 1.10*** [0.79, 1.41], a meta-regression coefficient rather than a standardized effect size.

> "A significant positive effect was found for a practical, experiential engagement with concrete ethical problems related to RCR. A significant negative effect was found for the number of teaching methods that were employed."

### Johannes Katsarov 2021 (2)

Johannes Katsarov, Roberto Andorno, André Krom, Mariëtte van den Hoven. (2021). Effective Strategies for Research Integrity Training—a Meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review (2022) 34:935–955. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09630-9

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-regression pooling all 75 effect sizes (best model F = 8.62, p = 0.000, 95% of between-groups variance explained). Table 4 reports a practical-emphasis coefficient of 0.74*** [0.54, 0.95] for the combined outcomes; the coefficients are regression coefficients, not standardized effect sizes.

> "Significant positive effects were found for a practical engagement with conc rete cases, for courses that did not teach ethical guidelines, and for courses of more than 5 h (in comparison to shorter courses)."
