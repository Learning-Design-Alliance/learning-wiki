### AAS Task Force on Diversity and Inclusion in Astronomy Graduate Education 2019

AAS Task Force on Diversity and Inclusion in Astronomy Graduate Education. (2019). Promoting Diversity and Inclusion in Astronomy Graduate Education: an Astro2020 Decadal Survey White Paper. Endorsed by the AAS Board of Trustees. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1907.06769](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1907.06769)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Background-literature claim: the paper attributes this finding to prior research (Miller and Stassun 2014; Posselt 2016; Steele and Aronson 1995), describing graduate admissions criteria as "one of the steepest barriers to increasing diversity in astronomy." The reviewed studies' designs were not read for this extraction.

> "Education and other social science research have shown that common uses of traditional measures of ability used in graduate admissions, particularly the general Graduate Record Exam (GRE) and Physics subject GRE (PGRE), disproportionately exclude groups who are already underrepresented"

### AAS Task Force on Diversity and Inclusion in Astronomy Graduate Education 2019 (2)

AAS Task Force on Diversity and Inclusion in Astronomy Graduate Education. (2019). Promoting Diversity and Inclusion in Astronomy Graduate Education: an Astro2020 Decadal Survey White Paper. Endorsed by the AAS Board of Trustees. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1907.06769](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1907.06769)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Second-hand attribution: the paper cites Miller et al. 2019, Petersen et al. 2018, and others for the predictive-validity finding that the GRE and PGRE are "poor predictors of PhD completion and long-term success in research." No effect statistics are printed in this paper.

> "Further, these same measures are poor predictors of PhD completion and long-term success in research, the two main goals of most PhD programs"
