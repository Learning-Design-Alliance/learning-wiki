# An exclusive substance metaphor led a student to incorrectly claim energy is released when bonds are broken; switching to a mixed ontology helped her analyze the situation correctly

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Anita initially imagined energy stored in bonds being released on breaking (a piñata-like substance metaphor), but after drawing potential energy diagrams she used a hill-and-bicycle image in which energy must be put in to break a bond and is released when bonds form. [→ Benjamin W. Dreyfus 2013](#benjamin-w-dreyfus-2013)

## Evidence

### Benjamin W. Dreyfus 2013

Benjamin W. Dreyfus, Benjamin D. Geller, Julia Gouvea, Vashti Sawtelle, Chandra Turpen, and Edward F. Redish. (2013). Negative Energy: Why Interdisciplinary Physics Requires Multiple Ontologies. [doi:10.1119/perc.2013.pr.020](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2013.pr.020)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Classroom and interview data from the NEXUS/Physics course. Anita said she used to imagine "these little molecules that float out" when bonds broke, "until I drew … the potential energy diagram, and that's when I realized, to break it you have to put in energy." The authors connect this documented biology/chemistry difficulty partly to an inappropriate substance ontology.

> "Anita’s exclusive use of the substance metaphor led her to claim incorrectly that energy is release d when bonds are broken. Switching to a mixed sub- stance/location ontology helped her analyze this si tu- ation correctly."
