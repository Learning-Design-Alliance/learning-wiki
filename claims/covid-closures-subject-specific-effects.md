# Median achievement losses from spring 2020 school closures were similar for mathematics (−0.10 SD) and reading (−0.09 SD), though individual studies found subject-specific differences

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` When averaging across all studies, the median effect was d = −0.10 SD for mathematics and −0.09 SD for reading, but some included studies found larger losses in reading than mathematics and others the reverse. [→ Hammerstein Svenja 2021](#hammerstein-svenja-2021)

## Evidence

### Hammerstein Svenja 2021

Hammerstein Svenja, König Christoph, Dreisörner Thomas, Frey Andreas. (2021). Effects of COVID-19-Related School Closures on Student Achievement-A Systematic Review. Frontiers in Psychology. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.746289

`q3 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Review synthesis across the eleven included studies. The review reports medians of "d = −0.10 SD and−0.09 SD for mathematics and reading, respectively"; both printed effect sizes fall below d = 0.2. The review offers candidate explanations for subject differences, such as mathematics being easier to teach at distance and parents finding reading support easier to provide.

> "Even though the median for the effect on mathematics and reading is comparable when averaging above all studies (d = −0.10 SD and−0.09 SD for mathematics and reading, respectively), some included studies found different effects for different subjects."
