Framework for the design and delivery of organized physical activity sessions for children and adolescents: rationale and description of the 'SAAFE' teaching principles. `q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Studies cited in the Enjoyable section comparing greater versus less equipment variety within a single exercise bout. Children with "a greater variety of exercise equipment (compared to less variety)" reported greater enjoyment and did more exercise. No effect sizes are printed.

> "when children are provided with a greater variety of exercise equipment (compared to less variety) in a single bout of exercise, they report greater enjoyment of that exercise, and participate in more exercise behavior [131, 132]"

### Lubans David R. 2017 (2)

Lubans David R., Lonsdale Chris, Cohen Kristen, Eather Narelle, Beauchamp Mark R., Morgan Philip J., Sylvester Benjamin D., Smith Jordan J. (2017). Framework for the design and delivery of organized physical activity sessions for children and adolescents: rationale and description of the 'SAAFE' teaching principles. International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity 14:24. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-017-0479-x

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Randomized 6-week exercise program study by Sylvester and colleagues, as reported in this review. Participants randomized to "greater variety (otherwise known as variety support)" showed greater adherence and improved well-being than those in a program "devoid of such variety". No effect sizes are printed.

> "using an experimental design, Sylvester and colleagues found that when a 6-week exercise program was structured to involve greater variety (otherwise known as variety support), participants subsequently experienced greater adherence [133], as well as improved psychological well-being (greater positive affect and subjective vitality and lower negative affect) [134] than those participants randomized to a program that was devoid of such variety"
