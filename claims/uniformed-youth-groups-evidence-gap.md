## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Existing studies of uniformed youth groups and youth social action mostly lacked comparison groups or randomisation and relied on participants' self-reports, leaving the field without robust evaluation evidence. [→ Gorard et al. 2016](#gorard-et-al-2016)

## Evidence

### Gorard et al. 2016

Stephen Gorard, Beng Huat See, Nadia Siddiqui, Emma Smith and Patrick White. (2016). Youth Social Action Trials: Youth United — Evaluation report and executive summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED581107

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Narrative review of background evidence: the report describes prior studies (e.g. Ockenden and Stuart 2014; Moon et al. 2010) as based on volunteer self-reports with no comparison groups, calling causal claims from them strong but unjustified. This trial is presented as the first large-scale UK RCT of uniformed group participation covering academic attainment.

> "There is currently a lack of robust evaluation of the impact of organised uniformed youth groups. Also, none of the studies so far has as sessed the impact of youth participation in social action activities on academic outcomes."
