# FireFlies offered a calmer way for students to request attention and lowered the threshold for students who never raise their hands to show needs and capabilities

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2`–`r3` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2020 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Request help made attention-seeking calmer than hand-raising and shouting, and students were less distracted while waiting. [→ Nine Sellier and Pengcheng An 2020](#nine-sellier-and-pengcheng-an-2020)
`q2 i?` Otherwise silent students gained turns to answer and teachers received needs information from students who never ask questions on their own initiative. [→ Nine Sellier and Pengcheng An 2020 (2)](#nine-sellier-and-pengcheng-an-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Nine Sellier and Pengcheng An 2020

Nine Sellier and Pengcheng An. (2020). How Peripheral Interactive Systems Can Support Teachers with Differentiated Instruction: Using FireFlies as a Probe. To appear in DIS '20, July 6–10, 2020, Eindhoven, Netherlands. https://doi.org/10.1145/3357236.3395497

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Observation with teachers of students aged 12-15, whose attention requests could become noisy and disordered: P1 reported "The classroom is calmer [with the FireFlies]", and P6 noted students were less distracted while waiting for the teacher's attention.

> "The classroom is calmer [with the FireFlies] because when the students try to get my attention they would [normally] put their hands up and start shouting through the classroom."

### Nine Sellier and Pengcheng An 2020 (2)

Nine Sellier and Pengcheng An. (2020). How Peripheral Interactive Systems Can Support Teachers with Differentiated Instruction: Using FireFlies as a Probe. To appear in DIS '20, July 6–10, 2020, Eindhoven, Netherlands. https://doi.org/10.1145/3357236.3395497

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Field-test report after students used answering teachers' questions: a student was surprised that a classmate who "never raises his hand in class" showed he knew the answer and got a turn; teachers similarly received more information from students who never ask questions.

> "That student is really smart. But he never raises his hand in class. Yet now he did show that he knew the answer and got a turn to answer a question."
