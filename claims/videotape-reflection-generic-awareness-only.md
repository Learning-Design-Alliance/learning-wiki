# Watching one's own videotaped consultations made doctors aware of generic communication behaviour but did not prompt deeper reflection on intercultural communication

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants valued reflective practice with their own videotaped consultations and formulated improvement points, but these concerned generic communication (computer use, interrupting, giving the patient space) rather than intercultural communication behaviour. [→ Paternotte E. 2016](#paternotte-e-2016)

## Evidence

### Paternotte E. 2016

Paternotte E., Scheele F., van Rossum T. R., Seeleman M. C. M., Scherpbier A. J. J. A., van Dulmen A. M. (2016). How do medical specialists value their own intercultural communication behaviour? A reflective practice study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0727-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview-study finding: participants recognized the videotaped behaviour as representative of daily practice and found the method valuable, yet confrontation with their own behaviour "did not result in a deeper reflection on their communication behaviour" regarding ICC.

> "They mentioned that watching the videotapes made them aware of their behaviour and some of them formulated points of improvement for themselves. These points of improvement mainly concerned aspects of generic communication, such as not paying so much attention to the computer, not interrupting the patient or giving the patient more space to tell their story before asking questions."
