# Virtual patient simulation sessions significantly increase medical students' clinical reasoning scores without lecturer supervision

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After a two-hour VPS session, students' clinical reasoning item scores increased significantly (pre-test mean 5.3 to post-test mean 7.81, p < 0.001). [→ Watari Takashi 2020](#watari-takashi-2020)

## Evidence

### Watari Takashi 2020

Watari Takashi, Tokuda Yasuharu, Owada Meiko, Onigata Kazumichi. (2020). The Utility of Virtual Patient Simulations for Clinical Reasoning Education. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17155325

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Single-center pre-post study of 169 fourth-year medical students completing a two-hour Body Interact VPS class in a lecture hall, assessed with a 20-item MCQ quiz. The results section reports "CR scores increased significantly" with the printed means and p-value; no effect size was printed.

> "CR scores increased significantly (pre-test: median = 5, mean = 5.3 95%, CI (4.98–5.58); post-test: median = 8, mean = 7.81, 95% CI (7.57–8.05); p-value < 0.001)."
