## Evidence

### Sampasa-Kanyinga 2022

Sampasa-Kanyinga, H., Hamilton, H. A., Goldfield, G. S., & Chaput, J.-P. (2022). Problem Technology Use, Academic Performance, and School Connectedness among Adolescents. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 19(4), 2337. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph19042337

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Symptom-level ordered logistic regressions (Table 3, Model 2) examining each of the six SPIUT items separately for females and males in the cross-sectional sample. The article reports the null association for staying longer than intended and the sex-specific association for being criticized about device time; no standardised effect size is printed.

> "Staying on devices longer than intended was not associated with academic performance in both sexes, whereas getting criticized by parents or friends about time spent on electronic devices was associated with lower academic performance in females (AOR = 0.86, 95% CI = 0.78–0.94), but not males (AOR = 0.98, 95% CI = 0.91–1.05)."
