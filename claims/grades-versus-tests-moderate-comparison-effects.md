## Evidence

### Möller 2020

Möller, J., Zitzmann, S., Helm, F., Machts, N., & Wolff, F. (2020). A Meta-Analysis of Relations Between Achievement and Self-Concept. Review of Educational Research, 90(3), 376–419. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654320919354

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Moderator analysis of achievement measure type among dissimilar-subject data sets (65 test-score and 70 grade data sets), controlling for subject similarity. Supports Hypothesis 7's prediction of stronger social comparison paths for grades, with β = .62 and β = .51 versus β = .48 and β = .40 for tests.

> "The path analyses revealed weaker positive paths from achievement to self-concept within subjects for test results (β = .48, β = .40) than for grades (β = .62, β = .51)."

### Möller 2020 (2)

Möller, J., Zitzmann, S., Helm, F., Machts, N., & Wolff, F. (2020). A Meta-Analysis of Relations Between Achievement and Self-Concept. Review of Educational Research, 90(3), 376–419. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654320919354

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Same moderator analysis: achievement correlations were substantially higher for standardized tests (r = .64 vs. r = .47), while self-concept correlations were similarly low for both measure types, matching the pattern predicted for Hypothesis 7.

> "The intercept showed higher positive achievement correlations for test results (r = .64) than for grades (r = .47), and similarly low positive self-concept correlations for studies with test results (r = .10) and with grades (r = .08)."
