## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The demonstration of step-function RT decreases at the item level challenges the validity of smooth speed-up functions (including power functions) as global empirical laws of learning, since averaging across items masks item-level discontinuities. [→ Rickard 2004](#rickard-2004)

## Evidence

### Rickard 2004

Rickard, T. C. (2004). Strategy execution in cognitive skill learning: An item-level test of candidate models. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition, 30(1), 65-82. https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.30.1.65

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The item-level step-function findings from the alphabet arithmetic experiment "call into question the entire class of smooth speed-up functions as global empirical learning laws," because averaging across items with different shift points produces smooth curves that misrepresent item-level discontinuities.
