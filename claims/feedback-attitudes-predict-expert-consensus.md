# Experiential attitudes toward feedback predict agreement with expert criteria on weak-essay grades

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Higher experiential attitudes toward feedback increased the likelihood of expert-consistent holistic (OR = 1.658, p = .009) and analytic (OR = 1.554, p = .021) grades for the weak essay. [→ Lopera-Oquendo 2024](#lopera-oquendo-2024)

## Evidence

### Lopera-Oquendo 2024

Lopera-Oquendo, C., Lipnevich, A. A., & Mañez, I. (2024). Rating writing: Comparison of holistic and analytic grading approaches in pre-service teachers. Learning and Instruction, 94, 101992. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.101992

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regressions (Hosmer–Lemeshow non-significant, good fit) run separately per essay condition and approach; the quoted weak-essay effects had OR = 1.658 (holistic) and OR = 1.554 (analytic).

> "Increases in experiential attitudes toward feedback were associated with an increase in the likelihood of providing holistic (b = 0.505, OR = 1.658, p = .009) and analytic grades (b = 0.441, OR = 1.554, p = .021) for the weak essay similar to those provided by experts"
