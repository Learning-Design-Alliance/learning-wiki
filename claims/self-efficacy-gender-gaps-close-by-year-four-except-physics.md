## Evidence

### Whitcomb 2020

Whitcomb, K. M., Kalender, Z. Y., Nokes-Malach, T. J., Schunn, C. D., and Singh, C. (2020). A mismatch between self-efficacy and performance: Undergraduate women in engineering tend to have lower self-efficacy despite earning higher grades than men. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2003.06006](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2003.06006)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal survey analysis of engineering students at one US research university (3,928 students with grade data; surveys at end of first, second, and fourth years, average response rate 79%). The article reports a "statistically significant gender gap favoring men" in mathematics, engineering, and physics self-efficacy and "no difference in chemistry" in the first year.

> "there is a statistically significant gender gap favoring men in self -efficacy scores for applying mathematics, engineering, and physics to their work in engineering , and no difference in chemistry ."
