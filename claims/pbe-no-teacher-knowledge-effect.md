## Evidence

### Finkelstein 2011

Finkelstein, N., Hanson, T., Huang, C.-W., Hirschman, B., and Huang, M. (2011). Effects of Problem Based Economics on high school economics instruction (NCEE 2010-4002rev). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED533126

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary teacher-level outcome in the randomized controlled trial: the same 40-item TEL administered to teachers showed model-adjusted means of 37.15 (intervention) versus 36.86 (control), with no statistically significant difference. The report states "a ceiling effect on the Test of Economic Literacy instrument may have masked any true content gains".

> "No statistically significant difference between the intervention and control groups on teachers' knowledge of economics (model-adjusted means were 37.15 and 36.86 for the intervention and control group teachers, respectively). As discussed in the conclusions of the report, a ceiling effect on the Test of Economic Literacy instrument may have masked any true content gains for teachers."
