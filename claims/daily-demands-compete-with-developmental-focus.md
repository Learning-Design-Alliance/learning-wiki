# Daily teaching demands competed with and distracted from a sustained developmental focus on instructional improvement

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teachers reported that day-to-day responsibilities of teaching took attention and time away from developmental activities, so they infrequently addressed instructional issues in a systematic or sustained way within teacher teams. [→ Stevens 2006](#stevens-2006)

## Evidence

### Stevens 2006

Stevens, W. D., with Kahne, J. (2006). Professional Communities and Instructional Improvement Practices: A study of small high schools in Chicago. Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/professional-communities-and-instructional-improvement-practices-study-small-high

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Across interviews in seven CHSRI schools, teachers attributed the rarity of developmental practices to daily work demands. One teacher explained, "I think that we have so many things to cover in our meetings and we have meetings every other day," with staff burnt out and focused on immediate issues like test preparation and attendance.

> "Though teachers recognized the value of, and desired regular collective discussion and activities related to instructional development, they rarely initiated or organized such activities."
