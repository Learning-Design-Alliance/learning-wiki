## Evidence

### Murray R 2026

Murray R, Engel‐Haber E, Snider B, Kirshblum S. (2026). Assessing knowledge about the Americans with Disabilities Act and comfort level in treating persons with disabilities among fourth‐year medical students following a clerkship in physical medicine and rehabilitation. PM&R. https://doi.org/10.1002/pmrj.13434

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre/post Likert-style survey responses from the 99-student cohort, analyzed with paired-samples sign tests at a Bonferroni-adjusted threshold. Unanimous post-clerkship agreement on increased comfort.

> "After the clerkship, all participants somewhat or strongly agreed that the clerkship increased their comfort in interacting with patients with physical disabilities (p < .001)."

### Murray R 2026 (2)

Murray R, Engel‐Haber E, Snider B, Kirshblum S. (2026). Assessing knowledge about the Americans with Disabilities Act and comfort level in treating persons with disabilities among fourth‐year medical students following a clerkship in physical medicine and rehabilitation. PM&R. https://doi.org/10.1002/pmrj.13434

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Multiple paired comfort measures from the same pre/post survey study, each showing statistically significant improvement at p < .001. Confidence in providing the same quality of care to patients with and without disabilities also improved (Figure 2, p < .001).

> "Participants felt more confident regarding their body position during a medical interview with a patient with physical disabilities (p < .001), felt more comfortable speaking to patients with a physical disability about their disability (p < .001), and felt more aware of the appropriate terminology to use when speaking about disability (p < .001)."
