# Kindergartners practicing addition with linear-spatial materials show greater reduction in absolute error on unpracticed problems than those practicing with irregular arrays

## Evidence

### Schiffman J 2018

Schiffman J, Laski EV. (2018). Linear-spatial materials improve young children's addition strategies and accuracy, irregular arrays don't. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0208832

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Randomized experiment with 29 low-income kindergartners assigned to linear-spatial or irregular materials conditions. The ANCOVA "found a condition by time interaction, F(1,26) = 6.63, p = .01, ηp2 = .20"; absolute error fell from 2.53 to 1.51 in the linear-spatial condition (Hedges' g = -.50) but rose slightly in the irregular condition (g = .14).

> "A repeated measures ACNOVA on children's average absolute error, controlling for their numeracy screener score, found a condition by time interaction, F(1,26) = 6.63, p = .01, ηp2 = .20."

### Schiffman J 2018 (2)

Schiffman J, Laski EV. (2018). Linear-spatial materials improve young children's addition strategies and accuracy, irregular arrays don't. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0208832

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Secondary analysis of absolute error using only incorrect items in the same randomized experiment. The "condition by time interaction effect remained, F(1,26) = 10.67, p = .003, ηp2 = .29", driven by a decrease in implausible responses in the linear-spatial condition (Hedges' g = -.87) not seen in the irregular condition.

> "the condition by time interaction effect remained, F(1,26) = 10.67, p = .003, ηp2 = .29, and was explained by a decrease in implausible responses in the linear-spatial condition"
