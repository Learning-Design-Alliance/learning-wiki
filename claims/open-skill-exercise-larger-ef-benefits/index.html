Liu S 2020¶

Liu S, Yu Q, Li Z, Cunha PM, Zhang Y, Kong Z, Lin W, Chen S and Cai Y (2020) Effects of Acute and Chronic Exercises on Executive Function in Children and Adolescents: A Systemic Review and Meta-Analysis. Front. Psychol. 11:554915. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.554915

q3 · i2 · quant-synthesis · r3

Subgroup analysis of chronic exercise effects on inhibitory control by skill type (Q = 14.49, p = 0.001), showing open motor skills (e.g., physical education, basketball, ping-pong) outperformed closed-skill exercises (e.g., yoga, running).

"The results showed a signiﬁcant eﬀect of open motor skills (SMD = − 0.56, 95% CI − 0.73 to − 0.40) on inhibitory control."

Related Claims¶

  • Aerobic exercise improves executive functioning, most notably inhibitory control, in typically developing children, though evidence is tentative and one week-long study found no main effect — related
  • Reported benefits of physical activity programs on executive functions depend on program duration and the executive domain analyzed — related
  • Sustained aerobic exercise improves children's EFs, with dose-response benefits for the most EF-demanding task and math in the high-dose group only — related
  • Physical activities combining exercise with character development or mindfulness improved children's EFs more than exercise alone: tae kwon do outperformed standard PE, and a yoga pilot improved planning — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
