Evidence¶

Trotti 2017¶

Trotti, J., Hendricks, R., & Bledsoe, C. (2017). Emergent Literacy Development and Computer Assisted Instruction. SRATE Journal, 26(1), 30-39. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1134394

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

ANOVA on literacy composite gain scores in the three-group pre-K experiment. The article reports the control group "mean of 19.3 was significantly higher than both" CAI groups, with eta squared = 0.63 described as moderate.

"The ANOVA reveal ed that instructional approach had a significant effect on literacy composite gains, F (2, 159) = 5.350, p =.006 (Tables 5 and 6). Tukey's post hoc test determined that the Control group mean of 19.3 was significantly higher than both the Imagine group (M = 11.8) and the Waterford group (M = 13.1)."

Trotti 2017 (2)¶

Trotti, J., Hendricks, R., & Bledsoe, C. (2017). Emergent Literacy Development and Computer Assisted Instruction. SRATE Journal, 26(1), 30-39. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1134394

q3 · i? · causal · r2

ANCOVA of literacy composite post-test with pre-test as covariate. The article reports "F(2,158) = 2.816, p = 0.063" and concludes instructional approach "had no discernable effect" on post-test composite scores.

"However, the differences between the means were found to be not significant, F(2,158) = 2.816, p = 0.063 (Tables 7 and 8). These results indicate that instructional approach had no discernable effect on literacy composite post-test scores."
