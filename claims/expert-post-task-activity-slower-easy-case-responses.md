## Evidence

### Cortese 2026

Cortese, F., Hruska, P., McLaughlin, K. J., Coderre, S. P., Protzner, A. B., Krigolson, O. E., Hecker, K. G. (2026). Expertise Related Changes in Resting‐State Functional Connectivity Patterns Following a Clinical Reasoning and Decision‐Making Task. Brain and Behavior 16, no. 1: e71153. https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.71153

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Post-task behavior-PLS follow-up: "Only the analysis of response times for “easy” case scenarios yielded a significant LV (p < 0.04; 30.49% crossblock covariance explained)", strongest at bilateral PaCG, SFG, ACC and LOC. No effect size is printed for this latent variable.

> "Only the analysis of response times for “easy” case scenarios yielded a significant LV (p < 0.04; 30.49% crossblock covariance explained)"

### Cortese 2026 (2)

Cortese, F., Hruska, P., McLaughlin, K. J., Coderre, S. P., Protzner, A. B., Krigolson, O. E., Hecker, K. G. (2026). Expertise Related Changes in Resting‐State Functional Connectivity Patterns Following a Clinical Reasoning and Decision‐Making Task. Brain and Behavior 16, no. 1: e71153. https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.71153

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Follow-up Pearson correlation within the expert group: "more negative brain scores during post‐task resting‐state were associated with slower response times (r = −0.88)", each point being an expert's average easy-case response time. The article frames this as a form of neural efficiency.

> "A follow‐up Pearson correlation confirmed that more negative brain scores during post‐task resting‐state were associated with slower response times (r = −0.88)"
