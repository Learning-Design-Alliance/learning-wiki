# Institutionalization devastates affective development in infants and retarded children

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Studies reviewed in the report uniformly demonstrate devastating effects of institutionalization on affective development, including retarded development of a sense of self and greater negative attitudes towards the self. [→ Casto 1976](#casto-1976)

## Evidence

### Casto 1976

Casto, Glendon; And Others. (1976). Affective Development in the Normal and Handicapped Preschool Child. Summary Report. Review and Recommendations. Exceptional Child Center, Utah State University. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED135163

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The report's review chapter on mentally retarded children summarizes multiple cited studies, attributing this finding to Spitz, Pasamanick, Dennis, and Sternlicht and Siegel. It states that "depriving the infant of a caretaker at an early age exacts a severe penalty in terms of later development." The report also cites Goldfarb's studies on self-concept.

> "Indeed, studies by Spitz (1945), Pasamanick (1946), Dennis (1960), and Sternlicht and Siegel (1968),uniformly demonstrate the devasting effects of institutionalization on affective development."
