## Evidence

### Najjuma Josephine N. 2024

Najjuma Josephine N., Muhumuza Albert, Santorino Data, Sekyere Samuel Owusu, Ocheke Amaka N., Yiltok Simon J., Bajunirwe Francis. (2024). Barriers and facilitators to interprofessional simulation-based learning in a Ugandan medical school: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06521-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Key informant interviews at MUST found individual faculty receptiveness: one faculty member stated "we are very open and we would love to have the nurses trained with the medical students." Departmental heads also reported training postgraduates to facilitate interprofessional learning.

> "I think for us we are very open and we would love to have the nurses trained with the medical students, we would love them to be part of our wards because we really believe in…we need them, they can help us in management of the patients."

### Najjuma Josephine N. 2024 (2)

Najjuma Josephine N., Muhumuza Albert, Santorino Data, Sekyere Samuel Owusu, Ocheke Amaka N., Yiltok Simon J., Bajunirwe Francis. (2024). Barriers and facilitators to interprofessional simulation-based learning in a Ugandan medical school: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06521-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis identified absence of formal administrative support as a theme: "There is lack of clear policies outlining how interprofessional education should be conducted," which a faculty member linked to information and communication gaps between departments, students and facilitators.

> "There is lack of clear policies outlining how interprofessional education should be conducted. A faculty member experienced in medical education noted, this in part could also be the cause of the information and communication gaps between the departments, students and facilitators."
