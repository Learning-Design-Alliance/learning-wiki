# A contingent tutor surrendered the full answer in one session in sixteen, against one in six for the question-only tutor

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The welfare floor fired far less often under the contingent tutor than under the question-only tutor, which released only on surrender. [→ Hou 2026](#hou-2026)

## Evidence

### Hou 2026

Hou, X., Weng, Y., Yeh, C. H., Lee, D. L., Zheng, L., Li, F., Wang, W., & Liu, Y. (2026). Adaptive Scaffolding Needs Contingency: An AI Tutor That Escalates and Fades on What the Learner Does. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22993

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Session end-state measure from the log (whether the whole answer was given away), stricter than artifact delivery. Tutor A produced the complete program in three sessions in five; the floor fired under Tutor B more than twice as often as under Tutor C, and distress followed the same order.

> "Tutor B refused, and released only when the learner gave up, which one session in six reached. Tutor C put the request on the ladder, asking about the decisions the build turned on, and the asks ran out in one session in sixteen"
