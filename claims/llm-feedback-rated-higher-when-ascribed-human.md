# LLM-generated feedback was rated as fairer, more useful and more acceptable when perceived as coming from a human source rather than from an LLM

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In regression models, the LLM × incorrect-guess interaction was significant for fairness (b = 1.29, p = .029), usefulness (b = 1.82, p = .007) and acceptance (b = 1.26, p = .041), indicating higher ratings when LLM feedback was misattributed to a human. [→ Jacobsen 2025](#jacobsen-2025)
`q3 i?` Within the LLM condition, ratings were higher when feedback was perceived to come from a peer than when identified as LLM-generated (usefulness: b = 1.22, p = .015; willingness to improve: b = 1.03, p = .033). [→ Jacobsen 2025 (2)](#jacobsen-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Jacobsen 2025

Jacobsen, L. J., Mertens, U., Jansen, T., & Weber, K. E. (2025). AI, Expert or Peer? Provider Biases and Feedback Uptake among Pre-Service Teachers. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2507.16013](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2507.16013)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multiple linear regression on the full sample (N = 222 to 227 depending on outcome), controlling for actual source, recognition accuracy, coded feedback quality, age and gender. The interaction coefficients were adjusted for quality (all ps ≤ .012 for quality in these models).

> "For fairness, a significant interaction between LLM feedback and recognition accuracy (b = 1.29, SE = 0.59, t = 2.20, p = .029) indicated that fairness ratings were higher when LLM-generated feedback was perceived as being provided by a human rather than by an LLM."

### Jacobsen 2025 (2)

Jacobsen, L. J., Mertens, U., Jansen, T., & Weber, K. E. (2025). AI, Expert or Peer? Provider Biases and Feedback Uptake among Pre-Service Teachers. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2507.16013](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2507.16013)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Second set of models restricted to the 85 participants who received LLM-generated feedback, using a three-level perceived-source predictor with correct identification as reference; 83 to 84 cases per model after item-level missing values.

> "Ratings were higher when the feedback was perceived to come from a peer than when it was identified as LLM-generated (usefulness: b = 1.22, SE = 0.49, p = .015; willingness to improve: b = 1.03, SE = 0.47, p = .033)."
