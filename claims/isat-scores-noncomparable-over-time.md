# ISAT scores are not comparable over time: students with identical earlier and later test scores earned different seventh-grade ISAT scores depending on the year tested

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2`–`r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among students scoring 15 on the EXPLORE exam in ninth grade, average seventh-grade ISAT scores rose from 247.5 in 2006 to 251 in 2007 and 254 in 2008 despite nearly identical fourth-grade ITBS scores. [→ Luppescu 2011](#luppescu-2011)
`q2 i?` The number of correct answers required to meet ISAT math standards declined across years, and similar cohort analyses for other cohorts and for reading found similar results. [→ Luppescu 2011 (2)](#luppescu-2011-2)

## Evidence

### Luppescu 2011

Luppescu, S., Allensworth, E. M., Moore, P., de la Torre, M., Murphy, J., with Jagesic, S. (2011). Trends in Chicago's Schools Across Three Eras of Reform. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/trends-chicagos-schools-across-three-eras-reform-full-report

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cohort-comparison analysis matching students on ninth-grade EXPLORE scores (15) and comparing their seventh-grade ISAT and fourth-grade ITBS scores, shown in Figure 2. Average seventh-grade ISAT scores differed by year (247.5, 251, 254) despite nearly identical ITBS scores, suggesting ISAT scores are not comparable over time. No effect size is printed.

> "The average for these students in 2006 was 247.5; in 2007 it was 251; and in 2008 it was 254."

### Luppescu 2011 (2)

Luppescu, S., Allensworth, E. M., Moore, P., de la Torre, M., Murphy, J., with Jagesic, S. (2011). Trends in Chicago's Schools Across Three Eras of Reform. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/trends-chicagos-schools-across-three-eras-reform-full-report

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Documented analysis of ISAT cut-score changes in Chapter 2: the correct-answer threshold for meeting fifth-grade math standards fell from 36 (2006) to 35 (2008) and further to 33 and 32 in 2009 and 2010. The report also replicated the cohort analysis for reading and other cohorts. No effect size is printed.

> "in 2006 a fifth grade student who answered 36 questions correctly on the math exam would be judged as meeting standards. In 2008, a student only needed to answer 35 answers correctly to meet standards."
