# Skills-focused courses differed observationally from concepts-focused courses: fewer verification labs, more student agency, more uncertainty quantification and lab notebooks

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Instructors in concepts-focused courses reported students verifying known principles more often, while skills-focused instructors more often reported students developing their own research questions, choosing analysis methods, and troubleshooting apparatus. [→ Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2016](#bethany-r-wilcox-and-h-j-lewandowski-2016)
`q2 i?` Skills-focused instructors reported students quantifying uncertainty in a measurement and maintaining a lab notebook more often than concepts-focused instructors, in both FY and BFY courses. [→ Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2016 (2)](#bethany-r-wilcox-and-h-j-lewandowski-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2016

Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski (2016). Developing skills vs reinforcing concepts in physics labs: Insight from E-CLASS. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.010108](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.010108)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational comparison of instructor Course Information Survey frequency reports for skills- vs concepts-focused courses (69 course instances), Mann-Whitney U with Holm-Bonferroni corrected p < 0.05; skills-focused courses included fewer "verification labs" and more student-agency items.

> "instructors in concepts-focused courses reported asking their students to “verify known physical principles through experimental tests” more often than instructors in skills-focused courses."

### Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2016 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski (2016). Developing skills vs reinforcing concepts in physics labs: Insight from E-CLASS. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.010108](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.010108)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

FY/BFY-separated comparison of CIS communication-activity reports; apparent differences in oral presentations and reading journal articles were artifacts of differential BFY representation, but the lab-notebook difference held in both levels. No effect size printed.

> "However, in both FY and BFY courses, skills-focused instructors reported asking their students to “maintain a lab notebook” more often than instructors in concepts-focused courses."
