## Evidence

### Widyahening Indah S. 2019

Widyahening Indah S., Findyartini Ardi, Ranakusuma Respati W., Dewiasty Esthika, Harimurti Kuntjoro. (2019). Evaluation of the role of near-peer teaching in critical appraisal skills learning: a randomized crossover trial. International Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.5c39.b55b

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the crossover trial, EPIC self-efficacy (11-point 0-100% scale, Cronbach's α 0.95 at baseline) was measured at module start and end; Table 2 reports the overall end-of-module ANCOVA comparison at "0.179" with p = 0.673, adjusted for baseline score.

> "No statistically significant difference in mean EPIC scale score was observed either for individual items or overall scores at the end of the module."

### Widyahening Indah S. 2019 (2)

Widyahening Indah S., Findyartini Ardi, Ranakusuma Respati W., Dewiasty Esthika, Harimurti Kuntjoro. (2019). Evaluation of the role of near-peer teaching in critical appraisal skills learning: a randomized crossover trial. International Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.5c39.b55b

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Baseline EPIC comparisons in the same trial: item 4 (p = 0.016, t = -2.451) and item 8 (p = 0.010, t = -2.617) favoured the near-peer-tutored group before the module; these were baseline imbalances, not outcomes of tutoring.

> "However, a statistically significant lower score was observed for medical staff on two items (4 - Critically appraise the strengths and weaknesses of study methods, and 8 - Determine if evidence from the research literature applies to your patient's or client's situation)."
