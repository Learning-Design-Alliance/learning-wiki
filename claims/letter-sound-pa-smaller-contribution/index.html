Evidence¶

Puranik Cynthia S. 2014¶

Puranik Cynthia S., Petscher Yaacov, Lonigan Christopher J. (2014). Learning to Write Letters: Examination of Student and Letter Factors. Journal of experimental child psychology. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2014.07.009

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Model 5 fixed effects for 5-year-olds in a cross-sectional item-response analysis. Letter-sound knowledge was significantly associated with correct writing, but the predicted-probability difference (50% to 60%) was smaller than for letter-name knowledge (50% to 82%); no standardized effect size is printed. The authors note the data do not show the direction of the effect or a causal relation.

"Similarly, letter-sound knowledge was related to correct writing, t(2645) = 2.67, p < .01, resulting in an increase in the probability of correct writing from 50% to 60%."

Puranik Cynthia S. 2014 (2)¶

Puranik Cynthia S., Petscher Yaacov, Lonigan Christopher J. (2014). Learning to Write Letters: Examination of Student and Letter Factors. Journal of experimental child psychology. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2014.07.009

q1 · i? · associational · r2

Authors' interpretation in the discussion of why phonological awareness played a smaller role than in letter-sound learning; this is an explanatory claim, not a tested result.

"Writing letters, at least at the decontextualized level (i.e., not in the context of words), does not appear to require sensitivity to sounds in words."
