Evidence¶

Toma 2000¶

Toma, Tony. (2000). Real-Time Courseware Design: The LAVAC Video Sequencer. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED444508

q2 · i? · design · r2

Classroom tests with the LAVAC video sequencer's sequential listening mode, reported in the Results section. The author reversed the classical language-lab order, starting the listening phase with the student's recording, and found students "much more attentive to the teacher's production" afterwards. No effect size is printed.

"Tests proved that students were much more attentive to the teacher's production (the master track in that instance) after they have heard their production than in the opposite way."
