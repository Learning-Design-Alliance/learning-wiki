Evidence¶

Nizamani 2026¶

Nizamani, S. B., Lee, Y., Donekal Chandrashekar, N., Ellis, M., & Ramakrishnan, N. (2026). Understanding Student Use of Large Language Models Across Computer Science Subfields. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01158 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2610.01158

q3 · i? · associational · r1

Descriptive analysis of role conceptualization (assistant, peer, expert, none) reported by 211 students per assignment. The article reports Assistant as most common, with majorities in "Algorithms (61.6%) and Web Development (63.0%)"; Expert use ranged from 0.0% (Software Engineering) to 9.0%.

"Across all assignments, Assistant usage was the most common LLM role among students who used LLMs. This pattern is most visible in Algorithms (61.6%) and Web Development (63.0%), where a majority of students reported using LLMs in an assistive role."

Nizamani 2026 (2)¶

Nizamani, S. B., Lee, Y., Donekal Chandrashekar, N., Ellis, M., & Ramakrishnan, N. (2026). Understanding Student Use of Large Language Models Across Computer Science Subfields. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01158. doi:10.48550/arxiv.2610.01158

q3 · i? · associational · r1

Chi-square test of independence on the role distribution across the seven assignments (N=211 per assignment). The article reports "χ 2 = 358.73, df = 18, p < 0.001"; no effect size is printed.

"A chi-square test of independence confirmed that this distribution differed significantly across assignments (χ 2 = 358.73, df = 18, p < 0.001)."
