## Evidence

### Banerjee 2025

Banerjee, A. V., Bhattacharjee, S., Chattopadhyay, R., Duflo, E., Ganimian, A. J., Rajah, K. & Spelke, E. S. (2025). Children's arithmetic skills do not transfer between applied and academic mathematics. Nature. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08502-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Written ASER assessment administered to the same 201 Kolkata working children after the market transactions. The article reports "only 32% of children could solve a division" and "54% ... two subtractions", performance similar to a representative rural West Bengal grade-5 sample (29% division).

> "only 32% of children could solve a division of a three-digit number by a single-digit number, and 54% of children could solve two subtractions of a single two-digit number from another"

### Banerjee 2025 (2)

Banerjee, A. V., Bhattacharjee, S., Chattopadhyay, R., Duflo, E., Ganimian, A. J., Rajah, K. & Spelke, E. S. (2025). Children's arithmetic skills do not transfer between applied and academic mathematics. Nature. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08502-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Oral presentation of abstract problems to the Kolkata working children. The article reports "Only 3% of children could solve a new division problem" orally and 43% solved oral subtraction after two attempts, showing the abstract framing itself led them astray.

> "Only 3% of children could solve a new division problem like those on the ASER test when orally presented, and 43% could solve orally presented subtraction problems after two attempts"
