# In the PISA 2018 German illustration, students were assigned to 40 intersectional strata with sample sizes ranging from 3 to 512

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Keller 2023

Keller, L., Lüdtke, O., Preckel, F., & Brunner, M. (2023). Educational Inequalities at the Intersection of Multiple Social Categories: An Introduction and Systematic Review of the Multilevel Analysis of Individual Heterogeneity and Discriminatory Accuracy (MAIHDA) Approach. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09733-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Strata formation step in the empirical illustration using the German PISA 2018 sample (N = 5451). The article prints the strata count as "J = 40 strata (i.e., 2 × 2 × 2 × 5 = 40)".

> "This implies that each student is assigned to one of J = 40 strata (i.e., 2 × 2 × 2 × 5 = 40), each of which is characterized by a unique combination of students' gender, immigrant background, parental education, and parental occupational status."

### Keller 2023 (2)

Keller, L., Lüdtke, O., Preckel, F., & Brunner, M. (2023). Educational Inequalities at the Intersection of Multiple Social Categories: An Introduction and Systematic Review of the Multilevel Analysis of Individual Heterogeneity and Discriminatory Accuracy (MAIHDA) Approach. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09733-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Sample-size check reported in the Measures section of the empirical illustration. The article states "The number of students per strata ranged from 3 to 512" and judges the distribution sufficient to preclude substantial underestimation of between-strata variance.

> "The number of students per strata ranged from 3 to 512 (Table S3), with 85% of the strata having 20 or more students and 90% having ten or more students."
