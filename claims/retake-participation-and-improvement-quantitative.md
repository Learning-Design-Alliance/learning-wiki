# Most students participated in in-class quiz retakes and most retakers improved their quiz scores across five course offerings

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across 213 students in five offerings, 76–95% retook at least one quiz and 86–100% of retakers improved at least one quiz score, with median overall quiz-average improvement of 5.2–7.4%. [→ Tripp Brie 2025](#tripp-brie-2025)

## Evidence

### Tripp Brie 2025

Tripp Brie, Ravi Akshaya, Pang Ethan, Furrow Robert E. (2025). Undergraduate STEM Students’ Perceptions of Grading Practices Reveal that Quiz Retakes Positively Impact Drivers of Self-determination. CBE—Life Sciences Education 24(1). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.24-06-0167

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Quantitative analysis of quiz records from five offerings (135 statistics, 78 neurobiology students). "76–95% of students within course" retook at least one quiz; median improvement to retakers' overall quiz average ranged 5.2–7.4% and did not vary greatly by offering.

> "Across all offerings of both courses, a large majority of the 213 total students participated in at least one in-class quiz retake (range among courses: 76–95% of students within course; Table 5)"

### Tripp Brie 2025 (2)

Tripp Brie, Ravi Akshaya, Pang Ethan, Furrow Robert E. (2025). Undergraduate STEM Students’ Perceptions of Grading Practices Reveal that Quiz Retakes Positively Impact Drivers of Self-determination. CBE—Life Sciences Education 24(1). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.24-06-0167

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Wilcoxon signed rank tests compared retake and initial quiz scores across offerings. The article reports "p<0.05 for all five course offerings"; no effect size is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "Scores on the retakes were consistently higher than the initial quiz scores (p<0.05 for all five course offerings)"
