## Evidence

### Daif-Allah 2016

Daif-Allah, A. S., & Khan, M. I. (2016). The Impact of Open Discussion Sessions on Enhancing the Oral Communicative Abilities of Saudi English Language Majors at Buraydah Community College. English Language Teaching, 9(6). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v9n6p108

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive survey questionnaire administered to 35 EFL learners; Table 2 shows 21 of 35 students chose the 'rarely' option for in-class oral discussion participation. The article attributes this to "lack of risk taking, inhibition" and large classes.

> "60 % of the students expressed that they rarely had the chance to participate in oral discussion fluently and correctly due to a number of demotivating factors such as lack of risk taking, inhibition, the large number of students in class and the limited fifty-minute class time."

### Daif-Allah 2016 (2)

Daif-Allah, A. S., & Khan, M. I. (2016). The Impact of Open Discussion Sessions on Enhancing the Oral Communicative Abilities of Saudi English Language Majors at Buraydah Community College. English Language Teaching, 9(6). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v9n6p108

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive survey result from Table 3 of the needs-assessment questionnaire with the same 35 students: 30 of 35 reported rarely using English in oral discussion outside the classroom, preferring Arabic for peer interaction.

> "The above table shows that 30 students making up 86% of the sample rarely use English outside the classroom."
