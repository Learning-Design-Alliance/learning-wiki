# Participants detected emotional valence but did not distinguish between discrete emotions of the same valence

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Ratings of the happy instructor did not differ between the happy and content scales, suggesting participants did not distinguish between positive emotions. [→ Lawson 2022](#lawson-2022)
`q3 i?` Ratings of the frustrated instructor did not differ between the frustrated and bored scales. [→ Lawson 2022 (2)](#lawson-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Lawson 2022

Lawson, A. P., & Mayer, R. E. (2022). The Power of Voice to Convey Emotion in Multimedia Instructional Messages. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-021-00282-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Bonferroni-corrected pairwise t-tests on happy-instructor ratings in the Mturk experiment found the happy instructor rated high on both positive scales; the article reports no significant happy–content difference. Equivalence was not tested.

> "There was no significant difference between the happy and content ratings ( p = 0.136), suggesting that participants did not distinguish between positive emotions."

### Lawson 2022 (2)

Lawson, A. P., & Mayer, R. E. (2022). The Power of Voice to Convey Emotion in Multimedia Instructional Messages. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-021-00282-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Bonferroni-corrected pairwise t-tests on frustrated-instructor ratings in the same experiment found no significant frustrated–bored difference; the article reports no effect size for this contrast. Equivalence was not tested.

> "but there was no significant difference between the frustrated and bored ratings (p = 0.430)."
