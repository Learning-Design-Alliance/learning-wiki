## Evidence

### Gerd Kortemeyer and Wolfgang Bauer 2024

Gerd Kortemeyer and Wolfgang Bauer. (2024). Cheat sites and artificial intelligence usage in online introductory physics courses: what is the extent and what effect does it have on assessments? arXiv preprint. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010145](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010145)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

End-of-semester survey with 0–100% slider items from 221 consenting students in the two courses. Self-reported means (Table I): HwkInt 50%, HwkAI 17.08%, OnlAI 8.07%, HwkFac 16.32%. The article reports "students use other internet resources for half of the homework problems"; no effect size is printed for the survey contrasts.

> "The only one-sigma significant differences are between usage of AI and talking to faculty on the one hand, and using other internet resources while working on homework; students have not yet adopted AI and stick with "traditional" problem-solving sites."
