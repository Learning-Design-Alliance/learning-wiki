# As family circumstances that strain attendance pile up, preschool attendance gets worse, with children facing no obstacles missing 5.6 percent of school and those facing three or more missing 12.9 percent

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Children in families with more attendance-related obstacles (single-parent family, poorer parent health, emergency room use, unemployed or non-college-degree parent) miss progressively more school: 5.6 percent with none, 7.5 percent with one, 9.0 percent with two, and 12.9 percent with three or more. [→ Ehrlich 2014](#ehrlich-2014)

## Evidence

### Ehrlich 2014

Ehrlich, S. B., Gwynne, J. A., Pareja, A. S., & Allensworth, E. M. (2014). Preschool Attendance in Chicago Public Schools: Relationships with Learning Outcomes and Reasons for Absences. UChicago CCSR Research Report. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED555650

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Analysis relating family circumstances (single-parent family, parent health, ER use for primary care, parent employment/education) to preschool absence rates. The report shows a cumulative pattern: "those with one obstacle missed 7.5 percent; those with two missed 9.0 percent; and those with three or more missed 12.9 percent."

> "Children in families without any of the aforementioned circumstances missed roughly 5.6 percent of school; those with one obstacle missed 7.5 percent; those with two missed 9.0 percent; and those with three or more missed 12.9 percent."
