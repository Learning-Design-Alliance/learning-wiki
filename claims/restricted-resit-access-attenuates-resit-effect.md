# Restricting access to a resit exam attenuates the reduction in first-attempt study-time investment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Making resit access probabilistic (50% chance) or conditional on a minimum non-passing grade of 4 increased study-time investment for the first attempt relative to unconditional resit access, with the resit effect weakest in the probabilistic condition and strongest in the unconditional condition. [→ Nijenkamp 2016](#nijenkamp-2016)

## Evidence

### Nijenkamp 2016

Nijenkamp, R., Nieuwenstein, M. R., de Jong, R., & Lorist, M. M. (2016). Do Resit Exams Promote Lower Investments of Study Time? Theory and Data from a Laboratory Study. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0161708

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2, a within-subjects design with 46 participants (after exclusions) across four blocked conditions (NR, UR, GR, PR). Bayesian analyses showed the resit effect was "strongest in the UR condition, weaker in the GR condition and weakest in the PR condition" (e.g., RE_UR > RE_GR BF = 16.6; RE_GR > RE_PR BF = 3.3).

> "the results show that participants invested more fictional study time in preparing for a fictional exam when access to the resit was probabilistic or conditional on obtaining a minimum grade of 4, with the former being most effective in countering the effect of resit exams on study-time investment"
