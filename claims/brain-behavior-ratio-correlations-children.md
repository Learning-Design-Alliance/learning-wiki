# In children, the BOLD numerical ratio effect correlates with individual differences in the behavioral ratio effect in right OTC, left superior parietal cortex, and bilateral IFG; no such correlation reaches significance in adults

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Children showed significant brain-behavior correlations in right OTC (r = .53), left superior parietal cortex (r = .49), and IFG bilaterally (right r = .73; left r = .47). [→ Cantlon Jessica F. 2009](#cantlon-jessica-f-2009)

## Evidence

### Cantlon Jessica F. 2009

Cantlon Jessica F., Libertus Melissa E., Pinel Philippe, Dehaene Stanislas, Brannon Elizabeth M., Pelphrey Kevin A. (2009). The Neural Development of an Abstract Concept of Number. Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn.2008.21159

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis relating each child's behavioral ratio effect (accuracy on 0.5 minus 0.8 ratio trials) to their BOLD ratio effect. For adults, "the ratio effect in BOLD signal did not correlate with individual differences in performance in any region (all ps > .05)."

> "Children, in contrast, showed a significant correlation between the behavioral and BOLD ratio effects in three regions: right OTC (r = .53, p < .05), left superior parietal cortex (r = .49, p < .05), and the IFG bilaterally (right: r = .73, p < .01; left = .47, p < .05)."
