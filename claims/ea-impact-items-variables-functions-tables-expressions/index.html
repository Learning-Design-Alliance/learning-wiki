Related Claims¶

  • On items requiring algebraic generalization, experimental students tended to express rules algebraically and reason with variable quantities, while comparison students gave single numeric answers, left items blank, or treated the number line as static — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Grades 3-4 students in an EALP-based early algebra intervention made significant improvements in constructing function tables and outperformed control students at posttest — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Experimental students significantly outperformed control students at posttest in expressing a functional relationship in words (grades 3-4) and in symbols (all grades), with no students writing correct symbolic rules at pretest — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Third graders found assessment items involving inequalities and graphs most challenging, with items T13A and T13B the only items on which 3rd graders scored zero points — related
  • After the intervention, posttest differences in identifying patterns in a function table were only marginally significant at grade 3, but experimental fifth graders more often described covariational relationships than recursive patterns — related
  • On a 'far' prediction to 10 tables, both conditions improved with no significant posttest differences in correctness, but the experimental group increasingly used a function rule as its strategy — related
