Li Kaiyue, Li Qun, Wang Linding, Wang Xiaonan, Ding Xiaojuan, Liu Boyu. (2025). Virtual simulation experiments in medical education: technology acceptance, learning outcomes, and motivational impacts. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08025-6

Secondary analysis using the five-item Study Habit Questionnaire (Cronbach's α = 0.81) compared high versus low scorers on skill dimensions (Table 7). The advantage was "significantly superior performance in both accuracy and fluency", with a less pronounced difference in flexibility.

> "Students with higher study habit scores ([4, 5]) outperformed their peers in all measured dimensions: accuracy (89.1 ± 4.9 vs. 82.3 ± 7.1), fluency (88.0 ± 5.7 vs. 79.1 ± 9.0), flexibility (87.6 ± 5.2 vs. 80.9 ± 6.6), and independence in decision-making (84.4 ± 6.2 vs. 78.4 ± 7.4)."
