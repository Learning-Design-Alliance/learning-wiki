## Evidence

### Luschin-Ebengreuth M 2015

Luschin-Ebengreuth M, Dimai HP, Ithaler D, Neges HM, Reibnegger G. (2015). Situational judgment test as an additional tool in a medical admission test: an observational investigation. BMC Research Notes. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-015-1033-z

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Pairwise linear correlation analysis of relative scores per admission year (N = 1353, 1702, 1686), reported in Table 4. Printed biology–chemistry r values were 0.732, 0.780 and 0.788; SJT–science r values ranged from 0.048 to 0.193, all significantly different from zero (P < 0.0001).

> "the highest correlation coefficients are invariably seen between biology and chemistry results. In general, the four natural science scores show relatively strong mutual correlations."

### Luschin-Ebengreuth M 2015 (2)

Luschin-Ebengreuth M, Dimai HP, Ithaler D, Neges HM, Reibnegger G. (2015). Situational judgment test as an additional tool in a medical admission test: an observational investigation. BMC Research Notes. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-015-1033-z

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The authors' interpretation of the correlation pattern: SJT correlates only with text comprehension (r = .330–.352) and very weakly with the science parts, which they say is not a big surprise given that Situational Judgment Inventories measure constructs not exclusively identical with cognitive ability.

> "Text comprehension is moderately strongly correlated with all other variables, including SJT, but the latter with all other variables except text comprehension shows very weak correlations."
