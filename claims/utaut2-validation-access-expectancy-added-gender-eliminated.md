## Evidence

### Jordan Talan 2025

Jordan Talan, MHPE, MD; Forster Molly, MD; Joseph Leian, MD; Pradhan Deepak, MHPE, MD. (2025). Exploring the Role of Immersive Virtual Reality Simulation in Health Professions Education: Thematic Analysis. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/62803

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Directed content analysis of 16 interviews through the UTAUT2 lens; the framework explained patterns in use behavior and intention, with age modifying effort expectancy and hedonic motivation.

> "Codes were confirmed for each previously described construct within the UTAUT2 framework [16]. Performance expectancy was the most frequently coded driver for use intention with VR technology."

### Jordan Talan 2025 (2)

Jordan Talan, MHPE, MD; Forster Molly, MD; Joseph Leian, MD; Pradhan Deepak, MHPE, MD. (2025). Exploring the Role of Immersive Virtual Reality Simulation in Health Professions Education: Thematic Analysis. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/62803

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

The study's UTAUT2 modification: access expectancy was added because coding instances for it exceeded hedonic motivation, habit, or social influence; gender was eliminated from the thematic map as antiquated in this context.

> "There were no instances of coding applicable to gender by either independent reviewer, and themes identified did not differ by participant gender. We found no signal for gender as a modifier of any UTAUT2 construct."
