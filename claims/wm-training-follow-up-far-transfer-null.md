# Far-transfer effects at follow-up are absent, and the significant follow-up arithmetic effect appears artefactual

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` At follow-up (on average 5 months after training) there are no significant treated-control effects on nonverbal ability, verbal ability, word decoding, or reading comprehension; the significant arithmetic effect (g = 0.22) is driven by control-group declines and drops to g = 0.14 when excluded. [→ Melby-Lervåg 2016](#melby-lervag-2016)

## Evidence

### Melby-Lervåg 2016

Melby-Lervåg, M., Redick, T. S., & Hulme, C. (2016). Working Memory Training Does Not Improve Performance on Measures of Intelligence or Other Measures of “Far Transfer”: Evidence From a Meta-Analytic Review. Perspectives on Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616635612

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analysis of delayed posttest outcomes (Table 2). The arithmetic effect for treated controls (g = 0.22 [0.04, 0.40], 10 comparisons) was driven by three comparisons across two studies where the control group declined from pretest to posttest; excluding them left a negligible g = 0.14.

> "There is, however, a significant effect at follow-up for treated controls on arithmetic."
