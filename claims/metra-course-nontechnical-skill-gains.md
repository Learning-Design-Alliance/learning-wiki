# Non-technical skills scores rose significantly from pre-test to post-test after a blended simulation-based HEMS basic course, in one uncontrolled pre-post study, with the largest gain in patient management during aircraft malfunction (+1.49 points)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` All four non-technical skill items increased significantly (p < 0.05) from pre- to post-course in one group with no comparison group, with the largest mean increase in patient management during aircraft malfunction (+1.49 points). [→ Omori Kazuhiko 2025](#omori-kazuhiko-2025)

## Evidence

### Omori Kazuhiko 2025

Omori Kazuhiko, Takahashi Jiro, Watanabe Noriko, Iwasaki Hiroko, Mineyama Sachiko, Sakata Kumiko, Yamada Kentaro, Ichikawa Susumu, Takamatsu Manabu, Ogino Ryukoh, Hayakawa Tatsuya. (2025). Effectiveness of a new basic course incorporating medical trainer simulator for HEMS education in Japan: a pre-post intervention study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07047-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Single-group pre-post questionnaire study of 208 HEMS professionals; Wilcoxon signed-rank tests compared pre/post self-ratings. Post-course means show "patient management during aircraft malfunction 4.53 ± 0.63 (95.67% ≥ 4)"; the table reports mean difference +1.49, p < 0.05.

> "After the course, these scores improved significantly: CRM 4.64 ± 0.55 (97.6% ≥ 4), emergency landing response 4.70 ± 0.50 (97.6% ≥ 4), in-flight fire response 4.71 ± 0.51 (98.56% ≥ 4), and patient management during aircraft malfunction 4.53 ± 0.63 (95.67% ≥ 4)."
