## Evidence

### Shiels 2026

Shiels, L., Tomlin, D., Payne, J.M. et al. (2026). Benefits of classroom remote microphone technology for inattentive children. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00427-5

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

30-week open-label extension; mixed effects models with time, group and time-by-group interaction as fixed variables. Table 4 reports a PedsQL student-rated coefficient of 3.01 (95% CI 0.26, 5.77), p = 0.004.

> "Child-administered questionnaires showed a larger improvement in quality-of-life over time for the experimental group compared to controls (P = .004), but there was no difference between groups in classroom listening (P = .405)."

### Shiels 2026 (2)

Shiels, L., Tomlin, D., Payne, J.M. et al. (2026). Benefits of classroom remote microphone technology for inattentive children. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00427-5

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same extension-trial questionnaire analysis; teacher ratings likewise showed no significant group differences for classroom listening (P = .069), inattention (P = .118), hyperactivity (P = .175), or anxiety (P = .989). Equivalence was not tested; these are null findings with no printed effect sizes.

> "Parent-reported outcomes showed no significant experimental group differences over time across listening difficulties (P = .478), quality of life (P = .961), inattention (P = .335), hyperactivity (P = .693), and anxiety (P = .187)."
