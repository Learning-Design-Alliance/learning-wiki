## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Third-year medical students trained with an educational adventure game achieved significantly better scores on a 34-item single-choice test than students trained with a written script (Cohen's d = 0.71, ITT analysis). [→ Boeker Martin 2013](#boeker-martin-2013)

## Evidence

### Boeker Martin 2013

Boeker Martin, Andel Peter, Vach Werner, Frankenschmidt Alexander. (2013). Game-Based E-Learning Is More Effective than a Conventional Instructional Method: A Randomized Controlled Trial with Third-Year Medical Students. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0082328

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial (ITT analysis, 144 analyzed) in routine urology training at Freiburg; intervention was the Uro-Island adventure game versus an 8-page script, with identical learning objectives. The printed effect size was Cohen's d = 0.71, a medium effect, and the authors state the difference "corresponds to a 7.8 % change on the 34-point scale which is a relevant improvement."

> "The means and standard deviations in the ITT analysis were 26.0 (3.99) of a total of 34 points for the script group vs. 28.6 (3.53) in the GbEl group (t-test: p < 0.001) with a Cohen's d effect size of 0.71"
