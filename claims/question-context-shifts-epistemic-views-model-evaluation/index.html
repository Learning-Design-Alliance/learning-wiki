Evidence¶

Lee 2021¶

Lee, S. W.-Y., Wu, H., & Chang, H. (2021). Examining secondary school students' views of model evaluation through an integrated framework of personal epistemology. Instructional Science, 49, 223–248. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-021-09534-9

q2 · i? · associational · r2

McNemar-Bowker tests of consistency across the two question contexts in the survey of 188 students confirmed "the context of the item influenced students' epistemic views in model evaluation," with χ 2 = 23.75, p < 0.001 for eighth graders and χ 2 = 13.43, p = 0.004 for 11th graders.

"Further analysis with McNemar-Bowker tests also confirmed that the context of the item influenced students' epistemic views in model evaluation (χ 2 = 23.75, p < 0.001 for eighth-grade students; χ 2 = 13.43, p = 0.004 for 11th-grade students)."

Lee 2021 (2)¶

Lee, S. W.-Y., Wu, H., & Chang, H. (2021). Examining secondary school students' views of model evaluation through an integrated framework of personal epistemology. Instructional Science, 49, 223–248. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-021-09534-9

q2 · i? · associational · r1

In the same cross-context consistency analysis, "Only 50.4% of the eighth-grade students chose the same answers for the two questions" and 40.2% of 11th-grade students were consistent; many students shifted from "both models can be valuable" (SARS) to "depends on the evidence" (dinosaur).

"Only 50.4% of the eighth-grade students chose the same answers for the two questions; an even lower percentage (40.2%) of the 11th-grade students had consistent answers for the two contexts."
