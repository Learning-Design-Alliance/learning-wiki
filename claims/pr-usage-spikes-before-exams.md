## Evidence

### Evrard 2013

Evrard, A. E., Mills, M., Winn, D., Jones, K., Tritz, J., and McKay, T. A. (2013). Problem Roulette: Studying Introductory Physics in the Cloud. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1309.7678](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1309.7678)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Daily traffic analysis of the Fall 2012 term (Fig. 4), where activity in the 24 hours preceding the exams nearly equals that of the three prior days; the summary section reports peaks at 800 problems served per hour on exam day.

> "Figure 4 shows that PR usage by students varied considerably over the course of the term, with spikes in activity occurring immediately prior to the three midterm examinations and the ﬁnal exam."

### Evrard 2013 (2)

Evrard, A. E., Mills, M., Winn, D., Jones, K., Tritz, J., and McKay, T. A. (2013). Problem Roulette: Studying Introductory Physics in the Cloud. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1309.7678](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1309.7678)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Enrollment and usage counts from Table I: of 571 Phys 140 and 531 Phys 240 enrollees, 367 and 456 answered at least one problem, and 97 and 229 reached fifty or more. Engagement is larger in the Science/Engineering courses, mainly because of larger problem libraries.

> "the majority of students in the Science and Engineering course sequence (Phys 140/240) used the service at least once, and that more than 25% used it on a regular basis ( ≥ 50 problems over the term)"
