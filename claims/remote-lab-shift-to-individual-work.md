## Evidence

### Michael F. J. Fox 2020

Michael F. J. Fox, Alexandra Werth, Jessica R. Hoehn, and H. J. Lewandowski. (2020). Teaching labs during a pandemic: Lessons from Spring 2020 and an outlook for the future. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2007.01271](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2007.01271)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

A Sankey plot of interaction mode before and after the transition (Fig. 6) showed that after the switch "most classes moved to individual work and incorporated less group work."

> "After the switch to remote instruction, most classes moved to individual work and incorporated less group work (Figure 6)"

### Michael F. J. Fox 2020 (2)

Michael F. J. Fox, Alexandra Werth, Jessica R. Hoehn, and H. J. Lewandowski. (2020). Teaching labs during a pandemic: Lessons from Spring 2020 and an outlook for the future. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2007.01271](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2007.01271)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Across Figures 3 and 5, "many students felt that they did not have as productive nor enjoyable collaborations after they switched to remote labs," naming the lack of a partner/group as a greatest challenge.

> "many students felt that they did not have as productive nor enjoyable collaborations after they switched to remote labs and expressed that having a partner/group to help conduct experiments was one of the greatest challenges"

### Alexandra Werth 2022

Alexandra Werth, Jessica R. Hoehn, Kristin Oliver, Michael F. J. Fox, and H. J. Lewandowski. (2022). Rapid Transition to Remote Instruction of Physics Labs During Spring 2020: Instructor Perspectives. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020129](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020129)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Closed-response survey question on whether students "worked in groups with other students" or "worked individually" before and after transition, summarized in Figure 7; the article reports "82% of the courses reported that students only worked in groups" before.

> "82% of the courses reported that students only worked in groups. After the transition to remote instruction, only 24.8% of students exclusively worked in groups, while the percentage of courses where students worked only individually on labs increased to 55.6%."
