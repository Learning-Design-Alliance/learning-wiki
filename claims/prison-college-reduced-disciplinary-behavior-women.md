# Formerly incarcerated women students reported improved disciplinary behavior while participating in a prison college program

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Women who participated in higher education in prison reported fewer disciplinary issues and improved behavior compared with their behavior before enrollment. [→ Jillian Baranger 2018](#jillian-baranger-2018)
`q2 i?` A faculty member with long teaching experience in the program observed that women taking college classes had a stake in avoiding disciplinary programs and were less likely to get into trouble. [→ Jillian Baranger 2018 (2)](#jillian-baranger-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Jillian Baranger 2018

Jillian Baranger, Danielle Rousseau, Mary Ellen Mastrorilli, James Matesanz. (2018). Doing Time Wisely: The Social and Personal Benefits of Higher Education in Prison. The Prison Journal. https://doi.org/10.1177/0032885518776380

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Semi-structured interview with a former student in the pilot qualitative study of the BU Prison Education Program at a women's prison. She reported still having "a few smaller disciplinary issues" but that her "behavior improved dramatically" after enrolling.

> "She continued to say that while she did still have a few smaller disciplinary issues while taking college courses, she said it was “nothing like before” and that her “behavior improved dramatically.”"

### Jillian Baranger 2018 (2)

Jillian Baranger, Danielle Rousseau, Mary Ellen Mastrorilli, James Matesanz. (2018). Doing Time Wisely: The Social and Personal Benefits of Higher Education in Prison. The Prison Journal. https://doi.org/10.1177/0032885518776380

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Faculty interview in the same pilot qualitative study. Michael, who had taught about 15 semesters in the program, described "a change in general comportment within the prison" among women taking college classes.

> "Michael reiterated these ideas from his perspective as a teacher when he said, “there’s a change in general comportment within the prison…that women who take college classes have a stake in avoiding disciplinary programs."
