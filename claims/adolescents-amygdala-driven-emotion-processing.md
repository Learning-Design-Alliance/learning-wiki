# Adolescents rely more on the amygdala and less on frontal regions than adults when processing emotional stimuli, and younger teens often misread facial expressions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In an fMRI study of teens ages 11 to 17 shown fearful facial expressions, teens' frontal lobes were less active and amygdalas more active than adults', and teens under 14 more often misread fear as sadness or anger. [→ Roaten 2011](#roaten-2011)

## Evidence

### Roaten 2011

Roaten, G. K., & Roaten, D. J. (2011). Adolescent Brain Development: Current Research and the Impact on Secondary School Counseling Programs. https://schoolcounselor.org

`q2 · i1`

The review reports, citing Yurgelun-Todd et al. (2002), an fMRI study scanning teens ages 11 to 17 viewing fearful faces; "the teens’ frontal lobes were less active and their amygdalas were much more active" than adults', with under-14s misreading expressions.

> "The researchers found that compared to adults, the teens’ frontal lobes were less active and their amygdalas were much more active."
