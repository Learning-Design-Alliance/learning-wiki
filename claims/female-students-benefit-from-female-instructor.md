## Evidence

### Bailey E. G. 2020

Bailey E. G., Greenall R. F., Baek D. M., Morris C., Nelson N., Quirante T. M., Rice N. S., Rose S., Williams K. R. (2020). Female In-Class Participation and Performance Increase with More Female Peers and/or a Female Instructor in Life Sciences Courses. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-12-0266

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear mixed model predicting standardized course grades for 1949 students in 31 classes, with ACT score and an instructor gender*student gender interaction as fixed effects and classroom as random intercept (Table 4, Figure 7). The male instructor*female student interaction coefficient was −0.253 ± 0.110 relative to the female instructor*female student reference. A separate regression found female instructor (p = 0.045) and percent female attendance (p = 0.034) predicted increased female grades (adjusted R² = 0.242) but did not significantly predict male performance.

> "Based on this analysis, female students benefit by having a female instructor, but male students are not affected."
