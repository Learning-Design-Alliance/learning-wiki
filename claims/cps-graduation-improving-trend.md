### Allensworth 2005

Allensworth, E. (2005). Graduation and Dropout Trends in Chicago: A look at cohorts of students from 1991 through 2004, Report Highlights. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/graduation-and-dropout-trends-chicago-look-cohorts-students-1991-2004

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Trend analysis of age-13 cohorts followed to age 19, a method the report states is not affected by changes in grade progression. The figure shows graduation by age 19 rising from 37.6 percent (1991 cohort) to 54.0 percent (2001 cohort).

> "Graduation rates have been improving steadily since the early 1990s, with the exception of the ﬁrst two cohorts of students subject to the eighth-grade promo- tion standards."

### Allensworth 2005 (2)

Allensworth, E. (2005). Graduation and Dropout Trends in Chicago: A look at cohorts of students from 1991 through 2004, Report Highlights. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/graduation-and-dropout-trends-chicago-look-cohorts-students-1991-2004

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cohort trend analysis printing age-16 dropout rates falling from 16.4 percent to 11.1 percent across the most recent cohorts. The authors' forward-looking interpretation that graduation rates will continue to improve is stated as a suggestion, not a tested result.

> "For the last ﬁve years, dropout rates at age 16 have been declining, which suggests that graduation rates will continue to improve for the next several years."
