## Evidence

### Likun Cao 2026

Likun Cao, Jie Hua and James Evans. (2026). Firstborn Advantage in the Ivory Tower: Mass Science, Expanding Scholarly Families, and the Reshaping of Academic Stratification. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2604.20864](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2604.20864)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Within-family fixed-effects regression (stratified Cox and reghdfe OLS) on 1.5 million U.S. doctoral graduates, comparing later students with the first student in each intellectual family. The article reports "a 12.4% lower hazard ratio to become Ph.D. advisors" and 0.04 fewer students for ranks 6-10.

> "students ranked 6th to 10th have a 12.4% lower hazard ratio to become Ph.D. advisors themselves ( -1 = -12.4%), and, on average, supervise 0.04 fewer students over the course of their careers"

### Likun Cao 2026 (2)

Likun Cao, Jie Hua and James Evans. (2026). Firstborn Advantage in the Ivory Tower: Mass Science, Expanding Scholarly Families, and the Reshaping of Academic Stratification. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2604.20864](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2604.20864)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same fixed-effects models, comparing the most late-ranked students with firstborns. The article prints "2.37% fewer papers in the short run" and a "17.96% lower hazard ratio to become advisors themselves" for students ranked 31 or higher.

> "they produce 2.37% fewer papers in the short run, 2.27% fewer papers in the long run, have a 17.96% lower hazard ratio to become advisors themselves, and have 0.165 fewer students during their lifetime."
