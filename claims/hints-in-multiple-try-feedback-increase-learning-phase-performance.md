## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Receiving additional hints increased the proportion of correct answers in the learning phase by 3.7%, and by 4.7% when only finished exercises were counted. [→ Jakob Schwerter 2022](#jakob-schwerter-2022)

## Evidence

### Jakob Schwerter 2022

Jakob Schwerter, Franz Wortha, Peter Gerjets. (2022). E-learning with multiple-try-feedback: Can hints foster students' achievement during the semester? Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-022-10105-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Random-effects panel regression of 87 students' weekly session outcomes across twelve e-learning sessions with within-person alternating treatment. The article reports the hint condition raised learning-phase performance, with the treatment variable having "significant additional explanatory value".

> "Column (1) shows that treatment, i.e., receiving additional hints, increases performance by 3.7%. When we consider in column (2) only the tasks students actually reached (they might have been pressed for time and unable to solve everything), the treatment coefficient increases to 4.7%."
