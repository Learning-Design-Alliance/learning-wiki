# Regression analysis attributes the largest puzzle effect to knowledge (β = 0.896), with smaller positive effects on motivation and self-efficacy

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In seven significant regression models, the puzzle application predicted the Knowledge Test most strongly (β = 0.896, R² = 0.803) and the motivation sub-dimensions, total motivation, and self-efficacy more modestly (β = 0.245–0.332). ## Evidence

### Barış Eren Nadiye; Çiftçi Bahar 2025

Barış Eren Nadiye; Çiftçi Bahar. (2025). The Use of Puzzles as a Teaching Technique in Nursing Education: A Randomized Controlled Study. Journal of Evaluation in Clinical Practice. https://doi.org/10.1111/jep.70118

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multiple linear regression on posttest scores from the RCT identified 'seven statistically significant models (p < 0.05)'; the knowledge model explained R² = 0.803 (F = 252.018). Standardized betas are reported as printed; no effect sizes were given for these models.

> "The analysis revealed that the puzzle application had a significant impact on the “Attention Subdimension” (β = 0.269, p = 0.030), “Appropriateness Subdimension” (β = 0.314, p = 0.011), “Confidence Subdimension” (β = 0.245, p = 0.049), “Satisfaction Subdimension” (β = 0.332, p = 0.007), “Motivation Scale for Instructional Material” (β = 0.291, p = 0.018), “Academic Self‐Efficacy Scale” (β = 0.331, p = 0.007), and “Knowledge Test” (β = 0.896, p = 0.001)."
