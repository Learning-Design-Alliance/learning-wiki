# In the experimental group, no significant pretest-posttest difference appeared on items 4, 7, 8 and 15, including the item about teachers' use of instruments

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Items 4, 7, 8 and 15 showed no significant pretest-posttest difference in the experimental group; item 15 concerns the music teacher's use of instruments, which the authors expected because karaoke recordings were used. ## Evidence

### Gökhan Özdemir and M. Can Çiftçibaşı 2017

Gökhan Özdemir and M. Can Çiftçibaşı. (2017). Effect of pop music on students’ attitudes to music lessons. Educational Research and Reviews. https://doi.org/10.5897/ERR2017.3321

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Null findings from the same pretest-posttest experimental study: the experimental group's t tests on items 4, 7, 8 and 15 were non-significant (all p>.05). The article explains the item 15 result: "as karaoke recordings were used for the experimental group, this result is expected."

> "After application, in the experimental group taught using pop music samples with karaoke recordings, there was no significant difference observed in pretest and posttest points for items 4, 7, 8 and 15 (t (168) = 1.30, p>.05,  t (168) = .48, p>.05, t (168) = .81, p>.05, t (168) = .68, p>.05)."
