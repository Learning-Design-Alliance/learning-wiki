## Evidence

### Goetz 2021

Goetz, T., Bieleke, M., Gogol, K., van Tartwijk, J., Mainhard, T., Lipnevich, A. A., & Pekrun, R. (2021). Getting along and feeling good: Reciprocal associations between student-teacher relationship quality and students' emotions. Learning and Instruction, 71, 101349. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2020.101349

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-lagged panel models (Model 1) on two-wave data from 535 students assessed in grades 10 and 11 across four school domains, controlling for gender, age, and achievement. The article reports "a weaker but still significant association" from relationship quality to emotions. No standardized effect-size category is printed beyond the β coefficients.

> "T1 positive emotional experience was associated with T2 relationship quality, β = 0.088, SE = 0.017, p < .001, and a weaker but still significant association between T1 relationship quality and T2 positive emotional experience was shown as well, β = 0.027, SE = 0.011, p = .011."

### Goetz 2021 (2)

Goetz, T., Bieleke, M., Gogol, K., van Tartwijk, J., Mainhard, T., Lipnevich, A. A., & Pekrun, R. (2021). Getting along and feeling good: Reciprocal associations between student-teacher relationship quality and students' emotions. Learning and Instruction, 71, 101349. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2020.101349

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

χ² difference tests compared the baseline reciprocal model against models with each cross-lagged path fixed to zero. The article states these constraints "significantly impaired model fit", supporting reciprocal rather than unidirectional associations.

> "We found that constraining either one or both reciprocal effects in such a way significantly impaired model fit, ps ≤ .012."
