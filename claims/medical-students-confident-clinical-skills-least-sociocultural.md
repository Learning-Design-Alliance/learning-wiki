## Evidence

### Doyle Patricia 2015

Doyle Patricia, VanDenKerkhof Elizabeth G, Edge Dana S, Ginsburg Liane, Goldstein David H. (2015). Self-reported patient safety competence among Canadian medical students and postgraduate trainees: a cross-sectional survey. BMJ Quality & Safety. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjqs-2014-003142

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional survey of 255 medical students using the reduced H-PEPSS; dimension scores (1–5) show clinical safety skills M=3.9 and communicating effectively M=3.8, versus managing safety risks M=3.2 and human and environmental factors M=3.1. About half or fewer agreed they were confident on the other five sociocultural dimensions.

> "Medical students were most confident in what they were learning about clinical safety skills and communicating effectively (table 2). They were least confident in what they were learning about managing safety risks, understanding human and environmental factors that contribute to safety, responding to remove immediate risks of harm and culture of safety."
