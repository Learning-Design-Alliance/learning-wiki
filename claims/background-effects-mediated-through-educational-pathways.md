## Evidence

### Tsikas Stefanos A.; Kieca Martin 2026

Tsikas Stefanos A.; Kieca Martin. (2026). Student background, admission routes, and academic success: a structural mediation analysis. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09068-z

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Proportion-mediated analysis (delta-method SEs) of the SEM: Abitur-grade PM on WQ was 0.360 (SE 0.036, p < 0.001); the reported PM value is "approximately 40%" of the total effect.

> "Approximately 40% of the total effect of a higher Abitur grade on admission through WQ (relative to AQ) operates indirectly via vocational training and age at enrolment."

### Tsikas Stefanos A.; Kieca Martin 2026 (2)

Tsikas Stefanos A.; Kieca Martin. (2026). Student background, admission routes, and academic success: a structural mediation analysis. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09068-z

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

PM estimates in Table 5: mother academic 1.366 (p = 0.009), father academic 0.981 (p = 0.003) for WQ admission; both "statistically significant" per the article.

> "For parental education, the proportion mediated is close to or slightly above one and statistically significant, suggesting that the association with WQ admission is almost entirely explained by indirect pathways through earlier educational achievements and career decisions."

### Tsikas Stefanos A.; Kieca Martin 2026 (3)

Tsikas Stefanos A.; Kieca Martin. (2026). Student background, admission routes, and academic success: a structural mediation analysis. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09068-z

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Aggregated indirect effect from the SEM (Table 4): Abitur-grade indirect effect on age was 1.755 (SE 0.146, p < 0.001); PM for age was 0.367 (p < 0.001), i.e. roughly one third mediated.

> "A one-point deterioration in the Abitur grade increases the likelihood of vocational training and precludes direct access to medical school, resulting in an indirect increase in age at enrolment of approximately 1.5 years."
