## Evidence

### Guan Hongyu 2025

Guan Hongyu, Xue Jing, Zhang Yunyun, Chang Fang, Liu Wenting. (2025). Examining the relationship between commuting time, academic achievement, and mental health in rural China: a cross-sectional analysis. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-025-22861-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Adjusted OLS regressions with individual and family covariates (Table 2) show each additional hour of commuting time "correlates with a 0.143 standard deviation decrease in academic performance" and a 2.50-point increase in mental health test scores; the mental-health coefficient is in raw scale points.

> "each additional hour of commuting time correlates with a 0.143 standard deviation decrease in academic performance and a 2.50 standard deviation increase in mental health test scores, signifying a heightened risk of mental health issues."

### Guan Hongyu 2025 (2)

Guan Hongyu, Xue Jing, Zhang Yunyun, Chang Fang, Liu Wenting. (2025). Examining the relationship between commuting time, academic achievement, and mental health in rural China: a cross-sectional analysis. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-025-22861-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Instrumental variable estimation using county settlement-pattern instruments (Table 3) yields estimates that "surpass those obtained from multiple linear regression (-1.433 vs. -0.143; 15.838 vs. 2.500)", which the authors read as a more pronounced effect of commuting time.

> "Notably, the estimates in column (2) and column (4) surpass those obtained from multiple linear regression (-1.433 vs. -0.143; 15.838 vs. 2.500), signifying a more pronounced effect of commuting time on academic success and mental health."

### Guan Hongyu 2025 (3)

Guan Hongyu, Xue Jing, Zhang Yunyun, Chang Fang, Liu Wenting. (2025). Examining the relationship between commuting time, academic achievement, and mental health in rural China: a cross-sectional analysis. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-025-22861-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

A robustness check restricted to students who walk to school (Table 4) finds every hour increase in commuting corresponds to "a decrease of 1.483 standard deviations in math scores, aligning with findings from the overall sample analysis".

> "Table 4 demonstrates that among students who walk to school, every hour increase in commuting time corresponds to a decrease of 1.483 standard deviations in math scores, aligning with findings from the overall sample analysis."
