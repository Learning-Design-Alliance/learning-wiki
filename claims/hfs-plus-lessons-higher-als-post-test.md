# Adding a high-fidelity simulation session to frontal lessons yields higher overall ALS post-test knowledge scores than frontal lessons alone among medical students

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Medical students who received frontal lessons plus an HFS session scored significantly higher on the overall post-test objective section than students receiving frontal lessons only (mean 74.1 vs 65.5, p = 0.0017). [→ Cortegiani A 2015](#cortegiani-a-2015)

## Evidence

### Cortegiani A 2015

Cortegiani A, Russotto V, Montalto F, Iozzo P, Palmeri C, Raineri SM, Giarratano A. (2015). Effect of High-Fidelity Simulation on Medical Students' Knowledge about Advanced Life Support: A Randomized Study. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0125685

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Parallel-group randomized study of 94 medical students randomized 1:1 after identical frontal lessons to an HFS session (group S, n = 46) or no further intervention (group C, n = 48); the quote reports the primary outcome with "mean 74.1, SD 11.2" versus 65.5 (SD 14.3).

> "The overall number of correct answers of the post-test questionnaire was significantly higher in group S (mean 74.1, SD 11.2) than in group C (mean 65.5, SD 14.3), p = 0.0017, 95% C.I. 3.34–13.9."
