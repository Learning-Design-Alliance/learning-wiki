# Structure can be characterized by seven practice components: expectation/goal-setting, rules and routines, organization of lessons and materials, monitoring and signals, feedback, rewards and punishment, and eliciting student involvement

## Evidence

### Patall 2023

Patall, E. A., Yates, N., Lee, J., Chen, S., Bhat, P., Lee, K., Beretvas, S. N., Guan, S., Cohen, M., Zhai, F., Brecht-Heitz, S., DeLorenzo, D., & Gonzales, K. (2023). A meta-analysis of teachers’ provision of structure in the classroom and students’ academic competence beliefs, engagement, and achievement. Educational Psychologist. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED631356

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

In the Data Extraction section, the research team coded the nature of the structure variable using these seven component categories, noting "the last two categories, (6) and (7), are typically only included in research from classroom management perspectives". This is a descriptive coding taxonomy, not a tested result.

> "We categorized the components of structure into the following groups: (1) expectation/goal-setting, (2) establishing rules, routines, and procedures, (3) organization of lessons and materials, (4) monitoring and signals, (5) feedback, (6) use of rewards and punishment, and (7) eliciting student involvement."
