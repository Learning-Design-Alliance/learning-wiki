# General AI literacy is not significantly related to in-service science educators' AI use or to their age, gender, or years of service

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` AI literacy was not significantly related to age, gender, or years of service. [→ Nikolai Maurer 2026 (2)](#nikolai-maurer-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Nikolai Maurer 2026

Nikolai Maurer, Mathea Brückner, Christoph Thyssen, Sebastian Becker-Genschow, Johannes Huwer. (2026). Science educators' AI literacy and AI usage in teaching: Implications for post-qualification programs. Computers and Education Open 10. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeo.2026.100376

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis (SPSS) of survey data from ncorr = 49 teachers who completed both the AI literacy test and usage questionnaire; median-split literacy groups showed no significant differences in usage history. Only p-values are printed, so no effect size is reported; equivalence was not tested.

> "No significant correlations (all p-values between 0.109 and 1.00) were found between AI-literate and AI-illiterate teachers regarding their AI usage history."

### Nikolai Maurer 2026 (2)

Nikolai Maurer, Mathea Brückner, Christoph Thyssen, Sebastian Becker-Genschow, Johannes Huwer. (2026). Science educators' AI literacy and AI usage in teaching: Implications for post-qualification programs. Computers and Education Open 10. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeo.2026.100376

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same survey subsample; correlations of AI literacy with age, gender, and years of service were non-significant, with only the printed p-value range available. The authors note the small sample size could have masked possible correlations.

> "None of these factors were found to be significantly related to AI literacy (all p between 0.333 and 0.462)."
