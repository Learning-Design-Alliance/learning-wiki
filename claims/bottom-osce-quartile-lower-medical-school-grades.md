# Students in the lowest OSCE quartile had significantly lower medical school grades, but the highest OSCE quartile was not distinguished by grades or interview scores

> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2022 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Undergraduates in the lowest national OSCE quartile had lower medical school grades than the top three quartiles (43.9% ± 18.4% vs 29.1% ± 23.9%, p = 0.029), with no difference in structured PGY interview scores (p = 0.752). [→ Wu Jr-Wei 2022](#wu-jr-wei-2022)
`q3 i?` Undergraduates in the highest OSCE quartile did not have significantly higher medical school grades (p = 0.120) or structured PGY interview scores (p = 0.321) than the remaining quartiles. [→ Wu Jr-Wei 2022 (2)](#wu-jr-wei-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Wu Jr-Wei 2022

Wu Jr-Wei, Cheng Hao-Min, Huang Shiau-Shian, Liang Jen-Feng, Huang Chia-Chang, Shulruf Boaz, Yang Ying-Ying, Chen Chen-Huan, Hou Ming-Chih, Huey-Herng Sheu Wayne. (2022). Medical school grades may predict future clinical competence. Journal of the Chinese Medical Association. https://doi.org/10.1097/JCMA.0000000000000782

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Quartile comparison (t tests) in the derivation cohort (N = 50) contrasted the lowest OSCE quartile with the upper three quartiles on medical school grades and interview scores; the printed means and p values are reported with no standardized effect size.

> "Undergraduates within the lowest quartiles in national OSCEs have lower grades in medical school (Q1-Q3 vs Q4 = 43.9% ± 18.4% vs 29.1% ± 23.9%, p = 0.029), but there were no differences in the structured PGY interview (Q1-Q3 vs Q4 = 71.2% ± 29.7% vs 74.4% ± 31.0%, p = 0.752)"

### Wu Jr-Wei 2022 (2)

Wu Jr-Wei, Cheng Hao-Min, Huang Shiau-Shian, Liang Jen-Feng, Huang Chia-Chang, Shulruf Boaz, Yang Ying-Ying, Chen Chen-Huan, Hou Ming-Chih, Huey-Herng Sheu Wayne. (2022). Medical school grades may predict future clinical competence. Journal of the Chinese Medical Association. https://doi.org/10.1097/JCMA.0000000000000782

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Quartile comparison (t tests) in the derivation cohort (N = 50) found no significant difference in medical school grades or structured PGY interview scores between the highest OSCE quartile and the remaining students; only means and p values are printed.

> "undergraduates with the highest quartiles in national OSCEs do not seem to have higher grades in medical school (Q1 vs Q2-Q4 = 48.1% ± 21.0% vs 37.7% ± 20.1%, p = 0.120) or structured PGY interview (Q1 vs Q2-Q4 = 79.1% ± 33.6% vs 69.5% ± 28.4%, p = 0.321)"
