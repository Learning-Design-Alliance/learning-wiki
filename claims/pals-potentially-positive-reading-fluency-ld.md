# PALS shows potentially positive effects on reading fluency for students with learning disabilities, with a substantively important but not statistically significant WWC-calculated effect size of 0.41 in Saenz et al. (2005)

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` WWC calculations found no statistically significant effects on reading fluency in either qualifying study, but the WWC-calculated average effect size across the two fluency measures in Saenz et al. (2005) was 0.41, large enough to be substantively important, yielding a potentially positive effects rating; the entry also reports a domain average across both studies of 0.36 (improvement index +14), a figure not in the quoted passage. ## Evidence

### What Works Clearinghouse 2012

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2012). Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies. WWC Intervention Report, Students with Learning Disabilities. http://kc.vanderbilt.edu/pals/

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r1`

WWC synthesis of two studies (Fuchs et al., 1997, 40 students; Saenz et al., 2005, 20 ELL students with learning disabilities), both against business-as-usual: Saenz et al. (2005) is a randomized controlled trial; Fuchs et al. (1997) randomized schools, but the WWC reviewed it as a quasi-experimental design (meeting standards with reservations) because teachers knew their condition when they selected students. The Saenz et al. (2005) fluency effect size was 0.41; the "domain average for reading fluency across all studies" was 0.36, improvement index +14, not statistically significant.

> "However, the WWC-calculated average effect size across these two measures was 0.41—large enough to be considered substantively important by the WWC. The WWC characterizes these study findings as a substantively important positive effect."
