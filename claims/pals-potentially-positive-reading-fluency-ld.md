# PALS shows potentially positive effects on reading fluency for students with learning disabilities (average effect size 0.36, not statistically significant)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r1` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` WWC calculations found no statistically significant effects on reading fluency in either qualifying study, but the WWC-calculated average effect size of 0.36 (improvement index +14) was substantively important, yielding a potentially positive effects rating. [→ What Works Clearinghouse 2012](#what-works-clearinghouse-2012)

## Evidence

### What Works Clearinghouse 2012

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2012). Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies. WWC Intervention Report, Students with Learning Disabilities. http://kc.vanderbilt.edu/pals/

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r1`

WWC synthesis of two studies (Fuchs et al., 1997, 40 students; Saenz et al., 2005, 20 ELL students with learning disabilities), both RCTs against business-as-usual. The Saenz et al. (2005) fluency effect size was 0.41; the "domain average for reading fluency across all studies" was 0.36, improvement index +14, not statistically significant.

> "However, the WWC-calculated average effect size across these two measures was 0.41—large enough to be considered substantively important by the WWC. The WWC characterizes these study findings as a substantively important positive effect."
