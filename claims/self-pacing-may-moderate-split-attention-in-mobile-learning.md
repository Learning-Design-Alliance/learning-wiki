# Learner pacing is a proposed moderator that may explain the absence of a split-attention effect in visually-based mobile learning

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The authors propose that the self-paced nature of the mobile materials, which weakens cognitive load effects relative to system-paced materials, could explain why no split-attention effect was found. [→ Liu 2011](#liu-2011)

## Evidence

### Liu 2011

Liu, T.-C., Lin, Y.-C., Tsai, M.-J., & Paas, F. (2011). Split-attention and redundancy effects on mobile learning in physical environments. Computers & Education. https://ro.uow.edu.au/edupapers/784

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' interpretation (discussion), citing prior work showing cognitive load effects such as the modality effect are stronger for system-paced than self-paced materials. This is a stated explanation, not a tested result in this study.

> "Although the mobile learning environment in this study only consisted of visual sources of information, the type of pacing used, could explain why we did not find a split-attention effect."
