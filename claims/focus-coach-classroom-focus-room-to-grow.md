## Evidence

### Michael D. Mrazek 2026

Michael D. Mrazek, Dustyn S. Ransom, Amy R. Texter, Calvin V. Tower, Bryce A. Rutila, Thomas F. Kennedy, Elijah J. Woodson, Jonathan W. Schooler, Alissa J. Mrazek. (2026). Theory-driven design and validation of a tool to enhance academic focus. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10730-y

`q2 · i1` · `design · r3`

Study 1 pre-post survey analysis of students who reported focusing less than they ideally should (58.7% of the sample) found classroom focus increased with SMD = 0.14. The subset was identified a priori based on prior studies.

> "Among this a priori subset identified in prior studies (Mrazek et al., 2019b, 2020a, 2022), classroom focus increased from pre-test to post-test, SMD = 0.14 (Table 1)."

### Michael D. Mrazek 2026 (2)

Michael D. Mrazek, Dustyn S. Ransom, Amy R. Texter, Calvin V. Tower, Bryce A. Rutila, Thomas F. Kennedy, Elijah J. Woodson, Jonathan W. Schooler, Alissa J. Mrazek. (2026). Theory-driven design and validation of a tool to enhance academic focus. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10730-y

`q2 · i1` · `design · r3`

Study 2 replicated the classroom-focus increase in the room-to-grow subset (67.9% of students); Table 2 reports b = 3.40, p < .001, SMD = 0.16 on the 0–100% scale. The article reports no effect size in the quoted sentence, so impact reflects the printed SMD of 0.16.

> "Among this a priori subset, classroom focus significantly increased from pre-test to post-test (Table 2)."
