## Evidence

### Poorthuis 2015

Poorthuis, A. M. G., Juvonen, J., Thomaes, S., Denissen, J. J. A., de Castro, B. O., & van Aken, M. A. G. (2015). Do grades shape students' school engagement? The psychological consequences of report card grades at the beginning of secondary school. Journal of Educational Psychology, 107(3), 842-854. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000002

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Structural equation modeling in a three-wave longitudinal study of Dutch seventh graders (N printed as 375 in the abstract; measurement models fit N = 436-438), with grades predicting Time 3 engagement controlling for Time 1 engagement; the article reports the grade effects as significant, with standardized coefficients of .19 for emotional and .13 for behavioral engagement per the model text. "higher grades predicted increased emotional and behavioral engagement"

> "Thus, consistent with Hypothesis 1, higher grades predicted increased emotional and behavioral engagement over the course of the first school year."
