# In a CS1 randomised control trial, learners who taught an AVT session before the exam outperformed the class average on several exam questions, with improvements of 25 to 30 percentage points on the midterm

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Learners who did an AVT session scored significantly higher than the class average on 3 of 5 midterm questions (25–30 p.p.) and showed a similar trend on the final (10–15 p.p.), with significant increases on questions directly related to the AVT topics. [→ Ali Malik 2024](#ali-malik-2024)

## Evidence

### Ali Malik 2024

Ali Malik, Juliette Woodrow, and Chris Piech. 2024. Learners Teaching Novices: An Uplifting Alternative Assessment. In Proceedings of the 55th ACM Technical Symposium on Computer Science Education V. 1 (SIGCSE 2024). https://doi.org/10.1145/3626252.3630887

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomised control trial in a CS1 course at a large R1 university (enrolment about 300); one-sided t-tests compared AVT learners' scores to the class average (ControlC, n=244) on midterm and final questions, with p-values printed per question in Table 2. The article reports "a strong effect of AVT teaching on learner understanding".

> "In 3 out of 5 questions on the midterm, the AVT group outperformed the class average ( 𝑝 < 0.05), with improvements of 25 to 30 percentage points (p.p.). A similar trend is seen in the final, although the improvements are in the 10-15 p.p. range"
