Evidence¶

Nückles 2021¶

Nückles, M. (2021). Investigating Visual Perception in Teaching and Learning with Advanced Eye-Tracking Methodologies: Rewards and Challenges of an Innovative Research Paradigm. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09567-5

q2 · i? · review · r2

Longitudinal study by Rosengrant et al. in which university students wore mobile eye-tracking glasses repeatedly across classes; off-task and on-task attention was coded second-by-second from eye-tracking data.

"They found that students spent on average almost 89% of class sessions lasting 70 min on-task, which is very high."

Nückles 2021 (2)¶

Nückles, M. (2021). Investigating Visual Perception in Teaching and Learning with Advanced Eye-Tracking Methodologies: Rewards and Challenges of an Innovative Research Paradigm. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09567-5

q2 · i? · review · r2

Minute-by-minute plotting of on-task behavior over whole sessions in the same Rosengrant et al. study; the article states these results clearly refute the belief that attentional resources deplete after 15 or 20 min.

"students'vigilance level reached 90% and higher a few minutes after the beginning and stayed on that high level for the rest of the session."
