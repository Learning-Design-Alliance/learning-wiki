Kristina Schaaff 2026¶

Kristina Schaaff, Quintus Stierstorfer and Valerie Hekkel. (2026). Using AI-based Learning Assistants in Higher Education: A Large-Scale Descriptive Analysis. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08748 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.08748

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive comparison of usage counts by gender from the same February 2025 log dataset (49,500 female, 26,876 male, 109 diverse students). The article reports the printed percentages; no statistical test or effect size is reported.

Related Claims¶

  • AI dependency differs by AI usage frequency and academic year but not gender — related
  • A substantial proportion of distance students (44,035 of 76,485) used the AI-based learning assistant Syntea during one observation month — related
  • Syntea usage is slightly higher among Bachelor's (58.17%) than Master's students (54.25%), possibly reflecting program structure rather than acceptance — related
  • No significant differential treatment effects by prior (Grade 4) achievement level or gender; the Grade 4 Level 3 subgroup difference was marginal (p = .053) — related
  • Full-time students use Syntea slightly more than part-time students (59.49% vs 55.71%) — related
  • Female students are significantly more likely than male students to use AI for schoolwork and to seek advice from AI tools — related
  • Gender is associated with small differences in GenAI use, literacy, and monitoring, while educational level shows no significant differences — related
  • Gender differences in GenAI usage clusters are minimal, confined to a male-dominated Zero usage cluster — related
  • Syntea adoption is highest among Gen Z students (63.66%) and declines across older generational cohorts — related
