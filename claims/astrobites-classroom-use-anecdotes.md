## Evidence

### Sanders 2012

Sanders, N. E., Kohler, S., Newton, E., & the Astrobites collaboration. (2012). Preparing Undergraduates for Research Careers: Using Astrobites in the Classroom. [doi:10.3847/aer2012030](https://doi.org/10.3847/aer2012030)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

An informal educator and student survey conducted by the Astrobites team asked readers who had used the site in class to describe their experiences; it received responses from "4 students and 6 educators indicating that they had used Astrobites in a class." Two course anecdotes were then presented.

> "We received responses from 4 students and 6 educators indicating that they had used Astrobites in a class."

### Sanders 2012 (2)

Sanders, N. E., Kohler, S., Newton, E., & the Astrobites collaboration. (2012). Preparing Undergraduates for Research Careers: Using Astrobites in the Classroom. [doi:10.3847/aer2012030](https://doi.org/10.3847/aer2012030)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

The survey responses yielded two anecdotes: a Caltech inquiry-based introductory course where students blogged with Astrobites as a model, and a Swarthmore journal-club-style discussion course where Astrobites served as a resource for selecting papers and background context.

> "In this journal-club-style discussion course for advanced undergraduates, students were responsible for selecting and summarizing research papers for the week's seminar on a rotating basis."
