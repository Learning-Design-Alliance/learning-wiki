## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Level of education and professional rank were each significantly associated with BLS competency (p < 0.001), with BSN nurses outperforming DN and HCN nurses and PNs outperforming ENs and ENAs. [→ Mahembe GR 2025](#mahembe-gr-2025)

## Evidence

### Mahembe GR 2025

Mahembe GR, Mash R. (2025). The competency of nurses in basic life support at district hospitals in Cape Town, South Africa. S Afr Fam Pract. https://doi.org/10.4102/safp.v67i1.6056

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

One-way ANOVA with post hoc comparisons on a composite competency score (knowledge plus skills, out of 20) in the cross-sectional survey of 243 nurses. The quote reports the omnibus association (p < 0.001) and the BSN versus DN/HCN pairwise contrast; no effect size is printed.

> "There was a statistically significant association between the level of education and BLS competency (p < 0.001). A post hoc analysis found that nurses with a Bachelor’s Degree in Nursing (BSN) had significantly higher BLS scores than those with a Diploma in Nursing (DN) or Higher Certificate in Nursing (HCN)."

### Mahembe GR 2025 (2)

Mahembe GR, Mash R. (2025). The competency of nurses in basic life support at district hospitals in Cape Town, South Africa. S Afr Fam Pract. https://doi.org/10.4102/safp.v67i1.6056

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same ANOVA analysis of the competency score by professional rank. The quote reports the rank association (p < 0.001) and the pairwise result that professional nurses "significantly outperformed ENs and ENAs"; no effect size is printed.

> "Moreover, the study also found a significant association between BLS competency scores and professional rank (p < 0.001). A post-hoc analysis revealed that professional nurses (PN) significantly outperformed ENs and ENAs."
