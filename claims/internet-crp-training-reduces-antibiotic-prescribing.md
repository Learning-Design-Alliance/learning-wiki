## Subclaims
`q4 i?` In a cluster-randomised factorial trial, physicians trained via internet in point-of-care CRP testing prescribed antibiotics at a lower rate than those without CRP training (33% vs 48%, adjusted risk ratio 0.54). [→ Little Paul 2013](#little-paul-2013)

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Multinational cluster-randomised factorial controlled trial in 246 primary-care practices in six European countries; 4264 patients recruited after randomisation. The trial reports "The antibiotic prescribing rate was lower with CRP training than without", with adjusted risk ratio 0·54 (95% CI 0·42–0·69).
