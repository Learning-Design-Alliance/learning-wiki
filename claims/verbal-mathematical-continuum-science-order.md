## Evidence

### Jansen 2015

Jansen, M., Schroeders, U., Lüdtke, O., & Marsh, H. W. (2015). Contrast and Assimilation Effects of Dimensional Comparisons in Five Subjects: An Extension of the I/E Model. Journal of Educational Psychology, 107(4), 1086–1101. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000021

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

In the latent correlation analysis of the five self-concept factors, 'Self-concepts in physics and chemistry showed stronger relations to self-concept in mathematics, whereas self-concept in biology showed a stronger relation to self-concept in German', consistent with the Marsh/Shavelson continuum.

> "Self-concepts in physics and chemistry showed stronger relations to self-concept in mathematics, whereas self-concept in biology showed a stronger relation to self-concept in German."

### Jansen 2015 (2)

Jansen, M., Schroeders, U., Lüdtke, O., & Marsh, H. W. (2015). Contrast and Assimilation Effects of Dimensional Comparisons in Five Subjects: An Extension of the I/E Model. Journal of Educational Psychology, 107(4), 1086–1101. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000021

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Applying the Cartesian-plane classification of contrast and assimilation effects to the estimated path coefficients (Figure 4), the authors report 'The resulting order of the three science domains that were examined from mathematical to verbal was physics, chemistry, biology'.

> "The resulting order of the three science domains that were examined from mathematical to verbal was physics, chemistry, biology."
