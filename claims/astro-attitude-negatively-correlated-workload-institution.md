## Evidence

### Adam S. Trotter 2019

Adam S. Trotter, Daniel E. Reichart, Aaron P. LaCluyzé & Rachel Freed. (2019). Factors Contributing to Attitudinal Gains in Introductory Astronomy Courses. Robotic Telescopes, Student Research and Education (RTSRE) Proceedings, Vol. 1, No. 1. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.32374/rtsre.2019.014](https://doi.org/10.32374/rtsre.2019.014)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Simultaneous multiple linear regression on the full survey data set (N = 749). Work Index was significantly negatively correlated; Table 4 prints coefficient -0.10 with p-value 7.8E-10 for Work Index and -0.048 with p-value 6.1E-06 for Institution Index.

> "Students who perceive the course to require more work than average emerge with more negative attitudes towards science. This would suggest that one straightforward way to boost science attitudes would be to reduce the workload in introductory science courses."

### Adam S. Trotter 2019 (2)

Adam S. Trotter, Daniel E. Reichart, Aaron P. LaCluyzé & Rachel Freed. (2019). Factors Contributing to Attitudinal Gains in Introductory Astronomy Courses. Robotic Telescopes, Student Research and Education (RTSRE) Proceedings, Vol. 1, No. 1. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.32374/rtsre.2019.014](https://doi.org/10.32374/rtsre.2019.014)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Institution Index measures institution type from -1 = 2yr college to +1 = Research I university. The regression on 749 responses yields the significant negative correlation; the authors speculate the trend may reflect class size and instructor availability, with smaller, more personal environments serving better.

> "There is a weak but statistically signiﬁcant negative correlation of Attitude Index with the type of institution the course is offered at, with 2-year commu- nity colleges doing better, in general, than 4-year colleges, and both doing better than Research I universities."
