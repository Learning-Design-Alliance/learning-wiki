Evidence¶

Aakriti Kumar 2022¶

Aakriti Kumar, Aaron S. Benjamin, Andrew Heathcote and Mark Steyvers. (2022). Comparing models of learning and relearning in large-scale cognitive training data sets. npj Science of Learning 7:24. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00142-x

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Out-of-sample predictive evaluation (RMSE on 20% held-out data, 80/20 split) of four models fit to individual users' gameplay; Table 1 reports e.g. RMSE 3.55 for M4 vs 3.56 for M3 on all Lost in Migration data, with 19,463 and 19,694 participants per game.

"For lost in migration, M 4 provides the most accurate predictions overall. For ebb and flow, models M 3 and M4 provide equivalently accurate predictions. Taken together, M 4, which uniquely allows for a degree of interactivity between the two timescales of learning, provides the most general account of performance."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Losses after long breaks in naturalistic practice are rapidly offset by very little practice: a single gameplay can restore prior performance levels even after delays of hundreds of days — related
  • Warm-up decrements are ubiquitous and substantial in naturalistic learning: the two-timescale model M2 improves out-of-sample prediction across the board over the baseline model M1 — related
  • Models able to weight performance by recency fit better on the Assistments and KDD datasets, without explicit memory-decay terms being necessary — related
  • Performance loss after breaks in practice grows with delay: a 100-day delay yields a 20% reduction and a 400-day delay a 50% reduction in first-gameplay performance — related
  • Repeated retrieval practice alters the rate at which learned items are forgotten, according to learn-to-criterion studies and forgetting-curve analyses the chapter reviews — related
  • No consistent learning effect of repeated TrackFX gameplay was found across children, though 37% showed positive performance slopes and improvement reached an asymptote after six sessions — related
