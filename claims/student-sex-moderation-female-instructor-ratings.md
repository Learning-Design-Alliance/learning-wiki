## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For evaluations of female instructors, the treatment effect was statistically significant for male students on instructor and course ratings but not for female students, and post-hoc tests of coefficient equality were not significant. [→ Peterson David A. M. 2019](#peterson-david-a-m-2019)

## Evidence

### Peterson David A. M. 2019

Peterson David A. M., Biederman Lori A., Andersen David, Ditonto Tessa M., Roe Kevin. (2019). Mitigating gender bias in student evaluations of teaching. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0216241

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Ordered logit models splitting treatment effects by self-reported student sex (Table 3, N = 158). Treatment for male students: 1.48 (SE 0.50) instructor and 1.63 (SE 0.49) course, significant at p<0.05; treatment for female students non-significant on all three measures.

> "However, there is some evidence that the intervention had an effect on male students; this effect was statistically significant for both the overall rating of the instructor and the course, but not the effectiveness measure. Additionally, none of the post hoc tests of equality in the coefficients, essentially the difference-in-difference estimators, are statistically significant."
