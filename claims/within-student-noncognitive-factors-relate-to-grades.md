# Within-student differences in noncognitive factors are associated with differences in course grades across the same student's classes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across two of their core classes, students earned higher grades in the class where they reported better belonging, motivation, self-regulation, participation, delay of gratification, learning strategies, and less performance avoidance (β=0.067 to 0.111). ## Evidence

### Camille A. Farrington 2019

Camille A. Farrington, Shanette Porter, Joshua Klugman. (2019). Do Classroom Environments Matter for Noncognitive Aspects of Student Performance and Students' Course Grades? University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/do-classroom-environments-matter-for-noncognitive-aspects-of-student-performance-and-students-course-grades

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of BEL-S survey and administrative grade data from 8,318 students across 25 schools, using within-student (student-fixed effects) comparisons across two target classes. The article reports "significant, within-student relationships between student noncognitive factors and student grades," with coefficients from β=0.067 to 0.111, equivalent to a 0.08 to 0.12 grade point difference.

> "From Table 3, we see that there are significant, within-student relationships between student noncognitive factors and student grades. These relationships are small, but reliable (significant from β=0.067 to 0.111)."
