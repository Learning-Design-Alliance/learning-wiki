# No moderator variable (intervention type, group size, grade level, implementer, hours of intervention) significantly explained variance in intervention effects, though moderator analyses were underpowered.

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Intervention effectiveness did not significantly differ by intervention type, size of instructional group, grade level, implementer, or number of hours of intervention. [→ Wanzek 2016](#wanzek-2016)

## Evidence

### Wanzek 2016

Wanzek, J., Vaughn, S., Scammacca, N., Gatlin, B., Walker, M. A., & Capin, P. (2016). Meta-analyses of the effects of tier 2 type reading interventions in grades K-3. Educational Psychology Review, 28, 551-576. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED579894

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Moderator analyses (mixed-effects models) across the meta-analyses comparing intervention type, group size (1:1, 2-3, 4-5), grade level, implementer, and hours categories. The article reports "no statistically significant differences were found between groups based on any moderator variable."

> "No statistically significant differences were found between groups based on any moderator variable, meaning there was no evidence that intervention effectiveness differed by intervention type, size of instructional group, grade level, implementer, or the number of hours of intervention."

### Wanzek 2016 (2)

Wanzek, J., Vaughn, S., Scammacca, N., Gatlin, B., Walker, M. A., & Capin, P. (2016). Meta-analyses of the effects of tier 2 type reading interventions in grades K-3. Educational Psychology Review, 28, 551-576. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED579894

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Authors' own limitation on the null moderator findings in the Discussion section: "Statistical power was below .90 for many of the moderator analyses," so null results may reflect insufficient power rather than true equivalence.

> "Statistical power was below .90 for many of the moderator analyses. Therefore, our discussion of the implications of the moderator findings should be considered in light of the related limitations within the available research, including insufficient power."
