## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Costly high fidelity prototypes of near finished systems or products could hinder design fluency, as participants get distracted with operational details. [→ Lataifeh 2018](#lataifeh-2018)

## Evidence

### Lataifeh 2018

Lataifeh, M. (2018). Attitude, Aptitude, and Amplitude (AAA): A framework for design driven innovation. Originally published 2015. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1808.00544](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1808.00544)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article's prototype-stage discussion (citing Liedtka & Ogilvie, 2011; Atladottir, Hvannberg, & Gunnarsdottir, 2011) reports that rapidly constructed prototypes must be good enough to learn, and that high fidelity prototypes 'could hinder this fluency' as participants get distracted with operational details.

> "In contrast, costly (time and efforts) high fidelity prototypes—of near finished systems or products—could hinder this fluency, as participants get distracted with the operational details and less with contemplating improvements (Atladottir, Hvannberg, & Gunnarsdottir, 2011)."
