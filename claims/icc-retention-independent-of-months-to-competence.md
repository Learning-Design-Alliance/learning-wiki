## Evidence

### Gugelmin-Almeida Debora 2022

Gugelmin-Almeida Debora, Jones Michael, Clark Carol, Rolfe Ursula, Williams Jonathan. (2022). A novel retraining strategy of chest compression skills for infant CPR results in high skill retention for longer. European Journal of Pediatrics 181(12):4101-4109. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00431-022-04625-2

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Observational 12-month simulation study of 118 healthcare students; groups were formed by the month (1-4) in which two consecutive monthly 'PASSES' were achieved. The Fisher exact test found no significant difference between groups for skill retention at follow-up (p = 0.13); equivalence was not tested.

> "Application of the Fisher exact test demonstrated no significant difference between groups for skill retention at follow-up (p = 0.13), indicating that once competence was achieved, the likelihood of retaining the skills at follow-up was no different across the groups."
