# Noncognitive skills, test-taking strategies, and test effort do not explain the female advantage in sustaining performance

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` None of the validated PISA noncognitive-skill measures mediated the gender gap in sustaining performance. [→ Balart 2019](#balart-2019)

## Evidence

### Balart 2019

Balart, P. & Oosterveen, M. (2019). Females show more sustained performance during test-taking than males. Nature Communications, 10, 3798. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-11691-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Mediation analyses across PISA 2006, 2009, and 2012 waves using validated noncognitive measures (interest, motivation, attitudes, self-efficacy, conscientiousness, etc.); the article reports "none of the validated measures were able to mediate the gender gap".

> "Our results indicate that none of the validated measures were able to mediate the gender gap in sustaining performance."

### Balart 2019 (2)

Balart, P. & Oosterveen, M. (2019). Females show more sustained performance during test-taking than males. Nature Communications, 10, 3798. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-11691-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Replication of the baseline analysis on computer-based PISA 2015 data in 58 countries where navigation between question units was restricted, ensuring question position matched answering position; the article reports the gender differences were "very similar".

> "Our findings reveal that the gender differences for this analysis are very similar; therefore, we can disregard the possibility that test-taking strategies are an important determinant for the gender difference."

### Balart 2019 (3)

Balart, P. & Oosterveen, M. (2019). Females show more sustained performance during test-taking than males. Nature Communications, 10, 3798. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-11691-y

`q2 · i?`

Analysis of PISA 2015 computer-log proxies for effort (time spent per question and number of actions per question); the article reports time per question showed no obvious pattern between sexes while "the number of actions per question during the test decreased more quickly for females than for males".

> "Panel (b) of Fig. 4 reveals that for most of the countries, the number of actions per question during the test decreased more quickly for females than for males."
