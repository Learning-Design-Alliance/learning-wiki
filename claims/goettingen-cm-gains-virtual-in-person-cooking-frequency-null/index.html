Evidence¶

Stock-Schröer B 2026¶

Stock-Schröer B, Edelhäuser F, Scheffer C, Schweigmann L, Homberg A. (2026). How does culinary medicine training impact the diet-related knowledge, skills and attitudes of undergraduate medical students in Germany?—A systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09580-2

q2 · i? · review · r2

Longitudinal study (Böttcher, 2023) across Göttingen, Brandenburg and Gießen with 240 medical students, comparing virtual (70) and in-person (80) formats via inferential pre-post and format comparison. Significance is reported without a printed effect size.

"demonstrated that the CM elective significantly improved the counselling skills, nutritional knowledge, attitudes towards nutritional counselling, subjective well-being and healthy eating habits of medical students in both virtual and in-person settings."

Same longitudinal Böttcher study: the table marks cooking frequency as -/+ (no difference measured), while eating habits differed in some points between formats and pre-post. The null on cooking frequency is descriptive, not an equivalence test.

"The only parameter that did not change in the before-and-after comparison of the two groups was how often they cooked"
