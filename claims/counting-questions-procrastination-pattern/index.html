Evidence¶

Iman YeckehZaare & Paul Resnick 2025¶

Iman YeckehZaare & Paul Resnick. (2025). Counting days is a spacing incentive that unlocks the potential of low GPA students. npj Science of Learning 10:35. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00322-5

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Daily activity analysis in the within-class experiment, shown in Fig. 2 with the midterm and final exam dates marked: counting days students "answered more questions" on most days, and the pattern reversed at semester's end, indicating catch-up cramming by the control group. No effect size is printed.

Related Claims¶

  • Counting days incentive strongly increases the number of days of practice in both a within-class and a between-instructor experiment — related
  • Low-performing students tended to procrastinate more on graded problem-solving exercises and on video watching with formative MCQs — related
  • Low performers completed activities on time for fewer topics and showed less catching-up only in early topics, while high performers showed no catching-up on later topics — related
  • On the final exam, high performers had more dominant strategy–time-management links than low performers, who showed dominant catching-up, ahead-with-errors, and video-revision links — related
  • In examination weeks, students revisited prior topics and used exercises, whose deadlines had passed, as formative practice for the exams — related
  • The spacing incentive benefits lower-GPA students more, nearly equalizing exam performance across prior GPA levels — related
  • A counting days grading incentive raises final exam scores more than a per-question points incentive in an introductory programming course — related
  • The counting days treatment's benefit for exam scores is mediated almost entirely by the number of days practiced, not the number of questions — related
  • An intervention study of Learning How to Learn reported improved study behaviors and higher second-semester proficiency scores — related
  • Students used the rDSA system extensively during formative assessment, with usage peaking the day before the exam — related
  • Greater procrastination (more days to hand in tasks) was associated with lower final marks. — related
