## Evidence

### Fabian 2018

Fabian, K., Topping, K. J., & Barron, I. G. (2018). Using mobile technologies for mathematics: effects on student attitudes and achievement. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-018-9580-3

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Cross-group gender comparison in the quasi-experimental study found a significant gain-score difference for girls, t(25) = 3.098, p = 0.005, d = 1.19, a large effect favouring the tablet group.

> "but found a signiﬁcant difference between female students of the experimental group and the girls in the control group, t(25) = 3.098, p = 0.005, d = 1.19."

### Fabian 2018 (2)

Fabian, K., Topping, K. J., & Barron, I. G. (2018). Using mobile technologies for mathematics: effects on student attitudes and achievement. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-018-9580-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The same cross-group comparison found no significant difference for boys, t(23) = 1.441, p = 0.163, d = 0.58; equivalence was not tested.

> "An independent t-test of the gains in maths test score between male students in the tablet and male students in the control group found no signiﬁcant difference, t(23) = 1.441, p = 0.163, d = 0.58"
