## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the free fall Doppler experiment, students read peak frequencies at fixed time intervals, plot them against time, and obtain g from the slope, with an example fit giving g = 9.4 ± 0.7 m/s2. [→ Kenichiro Aoki 2009](#kenichiro-aoki-2009)

## Evidence

### Kenichiro Aoki 2009

Kenichiro Aoki, Takahisa Mitsui and Yuki Yamamoto. (2009). Direct quantitative measurements of Doppler effects for sound sources with gravitational acceleration. https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.3819 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0911.3819](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0911.3819)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Analysis of a student free fall Doppler experiment (Fig. 2 right, Δt = 0.05 s), where a linear fit to Doppler-shifted peak frequencies yields "g = 9.4 ± 0.7 m/s2" at ambient temperature 17 °C. The article states g is measured within 10% accuracy, sufficient to convince students of the mechanism.

> "A linear ﬁt to the Doppler shifted frequencies is shown a nd the slope corresponds to g = 9.4 ± 0.7 m/s2 (ambient temperature 17 ◦C)."
