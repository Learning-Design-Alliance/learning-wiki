## Evidence

### Trigg 2026

Trigg, A. B., Keyes, A. W., Gray, S., Hatch, V., Brunet, K. T., & Heller, S. S. (2026). The Effects of Early Childhood Mental Health Consultation on Early Childhood Teachers' Perceptions of Children's Challenging Behaviors, Expulsion Risk, and the Moderating Role of Teaching Stress. Infant Mental Health Journal, 47, e70079. https://doi.org/10.1002/imhj.70079

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Interaction models added pre-consultation teaching stress (PERM Teacher Stress, grand-mean centered) and a stress × time term; Table 5 shows, for example, a Child SDQ T1 Stress × Time estimate of .38 (SE .10, p<.001). Interactions were decomposed by comparing non-overlapping pre-post confidence intervals at ±1 SD of stress. The article reports "Significant teaching stress by time interactions were observed" across these outcomes.

> "Significant teaching stress by time interactions were observed for child behavior problems on the SDQ and DECA behavior problems; resilience and protective factors of the DECA self‐regulation subscale and the overall DECA protective factors scale; and risk for expulsion as measured by teacher reported fear of accountability, hopelessness, and classroom disruption on the PERM (see Table 5, Figure 1)."

### Trigg 2026 (2)

Trigg, A. B., Keyes, A. W., Gray, S., Hatch, V., Brunet, K. T., & Heller, S. S. (2026). The Effects of Early Childhood Mental Health Consultation on Early Childhood Teachers' Perceptions of Children's Challenging Behaviors, Expulsion Risk, and the Moderating Role of Teaching Stress. Infant Mental Health Journal, 47, e70079. https://doi.org/10.1002/imhj.70079

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Decomposition of the significant DECA behavioral-concerns interaction using estimated means at the mean and ±1 SD of pre-consultation teaching stress, plotted in Figure 1. For the high-stress group the article reports scores "moved from above the clinical cut‐off pre‐consultation (T score > 60) to below this cut‐off" at post.

> "As with protective factors, for children in classrooms with teachers with high levels of teaching stress, scores moved from above the clinical cut‐off pre‐consultation (T score > 60) to below this cut‐off at the post‐consultation time point, suggesting clinically meaningful change."
