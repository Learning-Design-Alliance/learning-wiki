Evidence¶

Steven Glazerman 2008¶

Steven Glazerman, Sarah Dolfin, Martha Bleeker, Amy Johnson, Eric Isenberg, Julieta Lugo-Gil, Mary Grider, Edward Britton. (2008). Impacts of Comprehensive Teacher Induction: Results from the First Year of a Randomized Controlled Study. Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/impacts-of-comprehensive-teacher-induction-results-from-the-first-year-of-a-randomized-controlled-study

q3 · i? · causal · r?

First-year results from a large-scale randomized controlled study of two comprehensive teacher induction programs; the report found "any significant improvements in classroom practices" were absent in the first year. No effect size is printed on this page.

"Intensive mentoring and professional development for teachers just entering the profession did not increase teacher retention or generate any significant improvements in classroom practices or student achievement during the first year of teaching."

Steven Glazerman 2009¶

Steven Glazerman, Sarah Dolfin, Martha Bleeker, Amy Johnson, Eric Isenberg, Julieta Lugo-Gil, Mary Grider, Edward Britton. (2009). Comprehensive Teacher Induction: Year 1 Impact Findings from an RCT. Washington, DC: Annual Institute of Education Sciences Research Conference. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/comprehensive-teacher-induction-year-1-impact-findings-from-an-rct

q3 · i? · causal · r?

Year 1 impacts from the randomized controlled study of two comprehensive teacher induction programs report no significant improvements in classroom practices. No effect size or test statistic is printed on the page.

"Intensive mentoring and professional development for teachers just entering the profession did not increase teacher retention or generate any significant improvements in classroom practices or student achievement during the first year of teaching."
