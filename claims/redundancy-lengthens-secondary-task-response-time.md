# Redundant onscreen text (high extraneous load) lengthens secondary-task response time during multimedia learning

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Students in the redundant (high ECL) condition took longer to respond to a secondary task than students in the non-redundant (low ECL) condition, with no significant main effect of sentence complexity. [→ Tianlong Zu 2017](#tianlong-zu-2017)

## Evidence

### Tianlong Zu 2017

Tianlong Zu, John Hutson, Lester C. Loschky, and N. Sanjay Rebello. (2017). Use of Eye-Tracking Technology to Investigate Cognitive Load Theory. 2017 PERC Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.113

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

2x2 mixed ANOVA in the study of 51 students learning an electric-motor multimedia lesson, testing redundancy (between) and sentence complexity (within) on secondary-task response time. The redundancy main effect was significant, "F(1, 44) = 4.985,  p = .031, hp2 = .102" (partial η² = .102, a medium effect); no significant complexity effect was found.

> "We found that there is a significant main effect of redundancy on res ponse time to a secondary task, F(1, 44) = 4.985,  p = .031, hp2 = .102. Students from redundant condition (high  ECL) took longer time to press spacebar than those from non-redundant (low ECL) condition."
