## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A Strongly Increasing Unauthorised Absence trajectory lowers the probability of obtaining five or more GCSEs by 33.1 percentage points, with the other three non-low trajectories lowering it by 8.7 to 28.6 percentage points, all statistically significant at the 5% level. ## Evidence

### Dräger 2024

Dräger, J., Klein, M., & Sosu, E. M. (2024). Trajectories of school absences across compulsory schooling and their impact on children's academic achievement: An analysis based on linked longitudinal survey and school administrative data. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0306716

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Linear probability model on the binary five-or-more-GCSE outcome (59% of the weighted sample passed) using RWR-adjusted estimates. The article reports "33.1 percentage points (95%-Confidence interval [CI]: 24.9–41.3)" for SIUA and 28.6 for SIAA; MIUA is 27.1 and CMAA 8.7 percentage points. No standardised effect size is printed for this outcome, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "A Strongly Increasing Unauthorised Absence (SIUA) trajectory decreases students’ probability of obtaining 5 GCSEs by 33.1 percentage points (95%-Confidence interval [CI]: 24.9–41.3). For students with a Strongly Increasing Authorised Absence (SIAA) trajectory, the likelihood is lowered to 28.6 percentage points (95%-CI: 20.8–36.4)."
