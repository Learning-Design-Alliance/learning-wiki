## Evidence

### Drew B. Headley and Denis Paré 2017

Drew B. Headley and Denis Paré. (2017). Common oscillatory mechanisms across multiple memory systems. npj Science of Learning 2:1. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-016-0001-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports human sleep studies in which "the duration of SWS" and "the coherence of its delta oscillations" positively correlate with retention, and cites transcranial stimulation boosting delta as boosting retention. No effect sizes are printed.

> "Both the duration of SWS 107 and the coherence of its delta oscillations 108 positively correlate with memory retention."

### Drew B. Headley and Denis Paré 2017 (2)

Drew B. Headley and Denis Paré. (2017). Common oscillatory mechanisms across multiple memory systems. npj Science of Learning 2:1. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-016-0001-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review synthesizes rodent reactivation findings: cortical and striatal task-related units are preferentially reactivated during SWS upstates, and hippocampal SWRs occur at upstate onset, reactivating waking activity patterns. The mechanism is stated as a possibility, not a tested causal result.

> "Thus, delta oscillations during SWS may enhance memory consolidation by structuring the reactivation of experience-related neuronal ensembles in the cortex, striatum, and hippocampus."
