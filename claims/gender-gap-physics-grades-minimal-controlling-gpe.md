# Gender differences in undergraduate physics course grades were minimal and statistically insignificant after controlling for prior academic performance

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a multilevel model controlling for course, year of study and GPE, gender differences in physics grades were minimal and insignificant. [→ Steven Turnbull 2017](#steven-turnbull-2017)
`q2 i?` GPE significantly and positively predicted physics course grades in the same multilevel model. [→ Steven Turnbull 2017 (2)](#steven-turnbull-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Steven Turnbull 2017

Steven Turnbull, Dion R. J. O'Neale, Frederique Vanholsbeeck, S. Earl Irving, Thomas Lumley. (2017). A Leaky Pipe Dream? A Study of Gender Differences in Undergraduate Physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1702.06249](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1702.06249)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multilevel model predicting physics course grades from GPE and gender, controlling for course and year of study (Table IV): gender was "minimal and insigniﬁcant in our ﬁnal model (β = 0.075, SD = 0.122, p = 0.542)"; only a test statistic is printed, so no effect size is reported.

> "Gender diﬀer- ences were minimal and insigniﬁcant in our ﬁnal model (β = 0.075, SD = 0.122, p = 0.542), although gender diﬀerences in grades varied considerably between courses (random eﬀects SD = 0.461)."

### Steven Turnbull 2017 (2)

Steven Turnbull, Dion R. J. O'Neale, Frederique Vanholsbeeck, S. Earl Irving, Thomas Lumley. (2017). A Leaky Pipe Dream? A Study of Gender Differences in Undergraduate Physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1702.06249](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1702.06249)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same multilevel model (Table IV) on student course grades nested within physics courses: GPE "significantly and positively predicted physics course grades (β = 0.502, SD = 0.017, p<0.001)"; the unstandardised coefficient is not a standardised effect size.

> "The results show that, overall, GPE significantly and positively predicted physics course grades (β = 0.502, SD = 0.017, p<0.001)."
