# Pre-monitoring judgments before recognition increase rather than decrease overconfidence in kindergartners and second graders

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Children in the experimental pre-monitoring condition showed significantly more overconfidence (bias index) than children in the control condition, independent of age. [→ Sophie Wacker 2024](#sophie-wacker-2024)

## Evidence

### Sophie Wacker 2024

Sophie Wacker, Claudia M. Roebers. (2024). Motivating children to (pre)monitor: positive effects on monitoring accuracy? Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-023-09351-0

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Between-subject ANOVA on the bias index in a randomized two-condition experiment with N = 305 children. The condition main effect was significant, ηp² = .05, with "less overconfidence in the control condition" than in the experimental condition; the condition × age interaction was not significant.

> "results revealed a significant main effect of condition (F (1, 301) = 14.74, p < .001, ηp 2 = .05), with less overconfidence in the control condition (M = .30, SD = .21) than in the experimental condition (M = .38, SD = .19)."
