Evidence¶

Coppens 2019¶

Coppens, L. C., Hoogerheide, V., Snippe, E. M., Flunger, B., & van Gog, T. (2019). Effects of problem–example and example–problem pairs on gifted and nongifted primary school students' learning. Instructional Science, 47, 279–297. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-019-09484-3

q2 · i2 · causal · r2

2 × 2 ANOVA (giftedness × task sequence) on posttest perceived competence, with 126 primary students quasi-randomly assigned to sequence (matched on gender and a standardized math score): "Students in the EPEP condition" reported greater perceived competence than PEPE students, F(1, 122) = 7.71, p = .006, η²p = .06; no interaction with giftedness.

"There was also a main effect of task sequence, F(1, 122) = 7.71, p = .006, /u1D7022 p = .06. Students in the EPEP condition (M = 2.97, SD = 0.59) reported greater levels of perceived competence than students in the PEPE condition (M = 2.70, SD = 0.49)"
