## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Medical students and junior doctors trained by video scored similarly on donning (mean 88.0/100) to those trained by an instructor (mean 84.8/100), with no statistically significant difference. [→ Christensen L 2020](#christensen-l-2020)

## Evidence

### Christensen L 2020

Christensen L, Rasmussen CS, Benfield T, Franc JM. (2020). A Randomized Trial of Instructor-Led Training Versus Video Lesson in Training Health Care Providers in Proper Donning and Doffing of Personal Protective Equipment. Disaster Medicine and Public Health Preparedness. https://doi.org/10.1017/dmp.2020.56

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Randomized controlled pilot trial: 9 instructor-trained and 10 video-trained participants assessed after 1 month with a validated donning checklist by a blinded evaluator. Mean donning score was 84.8 for the instructor group and 88.0 for the video group; the article reports "no statistically significant difference in the donning score."

> "There was no statistically significant difference in the donning score between the instructor and video groups (95% confidence interval for the effect: -7.7 to 9.5; P-value: 0.54)"
