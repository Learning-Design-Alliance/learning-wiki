## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For Chinese students, being in a mixed-ethnicity group significantly decreased exploratory-question and cumulative-reasoning interactions; for foreign students, ethnic composition had no significant impact. [→ Wang Rong 2023](#wang-rong-2023)

## Evidence

### Wang Rong 2023

Wang Rong, Liu Chuanyong, Zhang Shu-Yong. (2023). The quality of group interactions in medical problem–based learning in China: the roles of intercultural sensitivity and group ethnic composition. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04616-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

One-way ANOVAs run separately for each ethnicity on the 139-student sample. Chinese students in unmixed groups (n = 51) scored higher (means 4.08 and 4.12) than in mixed groups (n = 35; means 3.62 and 3.69), while "group ethnic composition was a significant factor that decreased interactions related to exploratory questions". For foreign students, ethnic composition had no significant impact on the interaction types.

> "For the Chinese students, group ethnic composition was a significant factor that decreased interactions related to exploratory questions (F [1,84] = 16.66, p < 0.001) and cumulative reasoning (F [1, 84] = 11.39, p < 0.001)."
