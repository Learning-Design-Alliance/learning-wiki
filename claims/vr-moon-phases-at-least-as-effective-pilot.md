# In a pilot ASTRO 101 study, a VR activity on Moon phases and eclipses was at least as effective as supplemental lecture or hands-on activities on multiple-choice quiz gains

## Evidence

### Philip Blanco 2019

Philip Blanco, Gur Windmiller, William Welsh, and Sean Hauze. (2019). Lessons Learned from Teaching Astronomy with Virtual Reality. ASP Conference Series, Vol. 524, Astronomical Society of the Pacific. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1912.12393](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1912.12393)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental pilot: an ASTRO 101 class at San Diego State University was divided into three cohorts (one VR and two control) after a common lecture, with pre/post quizzes out of 10. The authors state VR "seemed to be at least as effective as the other additional activities"; no statistical test is printed.

> "With the caveats above, we conclude that VR seemed to be at least as effective as the other additional activities. In other words, VR did not "hurt" the students' understanding of Moon phases and eclipses, as tested in a multiple-choice exam."
