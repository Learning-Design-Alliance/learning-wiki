## Evidence

### Clinton 2019

Virginia Clinton. (2019). Reading from paper compared to screens: A systematic review and metaanalysis. Journal of Research in Reading. https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9817.12269

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random effects meta-analysis of literal (factual recall) performance measures across k = 19 studies found a significant screen disadvantage. The author notes this was contrary to expectations that screens would be more detrimental for challenging tasks, since literal tasks are typically easier.

> "literal reading performance from screens was worse than from paper, g = .33, k = 19, SE = .08, 95% CI = [ .48, .18], p < .001"

### Clinton 2019 (2)

Virginia Clinton. (2019). Reading from paper compared to screens: A systematic review and metaanalysis. Journal of Research in Reading. https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9817.12269

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random effects meta-analysis of inferential performance measures across k = 13 studies found a significant screen disadvantage with low heterogeneity (I2 = 0.00). One-study-removed analyses found no outliers in either analysis.

> "Similar to literal reading performance, inferential reading performance from screens was worse than from paper, g = .26, k = 13, SE = .05, 95% CI = [ .36, .17], p < .001"
