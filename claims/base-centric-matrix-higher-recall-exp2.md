# Experiment 2 shows base-centric matrix study supports much higher recall of morphologically complex words than affix-centric and control conditions

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Experiment 2, both morphological conditions outperformed the control condition, with 71.3% recall in the base-centric, 58.7% in the affix-centric and 28.0% in the control condition. [→ Ng Melvin M. R. 2022](#ng-melvin-m-r-2022)

## Evidence

### Ng Melvin M. R. 2022

Ng Melvin M. R., Bowers Peter N., Bowers Jeffrey S. (2022). A promising new tool for literacy instruction: The morphological matrix. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0262260

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A 3 x 2 between-within mixed ANOVA on Experiment 2 (N = 64 undergraduates studying 64 factorially combined words) printed "a main effect of study condition"; correct recall required correct spelling.

> "and a main effect of study condition, F1(2, 61) = 41.609, MSE = 2.064, p < .001; F2 (2, 126) = 506.88, MSE = 6.659, p < .001, with both the affix-centric (58.7% recall), and base-centric (71.3% recall) condition supporting better recall compared to the control condition (28.0% recall)."
