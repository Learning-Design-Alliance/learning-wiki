# In the study data, mean spring grade-2 math RIT was 190.6 with linear spring-to-spring growth of 14.5 RIT, and mean fall-to-spring gain in grade-2 math was 13.4 RIT

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` CP fixed effects for spring-to-spring math (centered at grade two) print a spring grade-2 conditional RIT of 190.6, linear growth of 14.5 RIT with a decelerating quadratic trend, and a grade-2 fall-to-spring gain of 13.4 RIT. [→ Soland 2019](#soland-2019)

## Evidence

### Soland 2019

Soland, J. & Thum, Y.M. (2019). School effectiveness, summer loss, and federal accountability: Applying the compound polynomial model in a program evaluation context. The Collaborative for Student Growth at NWEA Working Paper. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/school-effectiveness-summer-loss-and-federal-accountability-applying-the-compound-polynomialmodel-in-a-program-evaluation-context/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Fixed-effects estimates from the CP model for math centered at grade two (Table 2, column 1), fit to vertically scaled MAP Growth RIT scores from one East Coast state cohort in grades 2-6. Values are descriptive model parameters as printed.

> "the intercept suggests that the mean conditional RIT score in spring of second grade was 190.6, the linear growth rate was 14.5 RIT, and that trend decelerated over time per the quadratic term.  Meanwhile, the intercept for fall-to-spring growth suggests the mean gain in math during second grade between fall and spring was 13.4 RIT."
