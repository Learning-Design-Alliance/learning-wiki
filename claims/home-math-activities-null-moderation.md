# Parent-reported home math activities showed no significant association with children's math achievement, and inhibitory control did not moderate this null relation

## Evidence

### Silver 2022

Silver, A. M., Elliott, L., & Libertus, M. E. (2022). Parental math input is not uniformly beneficial for young children: The moderating role of inhibitory control. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000679

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression with 123 children testing home math activities × inhibitory control showed the "interaction term was not statistically significant"; planned robustness checks were skipped because significance was not reached. The non-significant interaction is plotted in Figure 3.

> "This interaction term was not statistically significant, however, and resulted in only an additional 0.4% of variance explained in children's math achievement, which was not a significant increase from the base model, ΔF(1,113) = 0.63, p = .430."

### Silver 2022 (2)

Silver, A. M., Elliott, L., & Libertus, M. E. (2022). Parental math input is not uniformly beneficial for young children: The moderating role of inhibitory control. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000679

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the covariate-adjusted base model, the main effect of parent-reported home math activity frequency (LeFevre questionnaire) was null, though the bivariate correlation was marginally significant (r = .17); the authors attribute this partly to self-report measurement limits.

> "However, home math activities were not significantly related to children's math achievement."
