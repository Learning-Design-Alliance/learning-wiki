# A double lesson of Particle Builder produced an average gain of 0.16, comparable to 1.5 weeks of traditional instruction (gain 0.17) and below a quark-puzzle control group (0.23)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Playing Particle Builder for a double lesson (1-2 hours) yielded an average gain of 0.16 across 225 students, comparable to the 0.17 gain of a traditional-instruction control group and less than the 0.23 gain of a quark-puzzle control group. ## Evidence

### Lachlan McGinness 2025

Lachlan McGinness, Yutong Ma, Mohammad Attar, Andrew Carse, Yeming Chen, Thomas Green, Jeong-Yeon Ha, Yanbai Jin, Amy McWilliams, Theirry Panggabean, Zhengyu Peng, Jing Ru, Jiacheng She, Lujin Sun, Jialin Wang, Zilun Wei, and Jiayuan Zhu. `q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre/post survey study in which 225 Australian secondary students completed both a nine-question pre-test and post-test with no other particle physics instruction between surveys; the average gain was 0.16, "comparable to the gain from the traditional instruction group (0.17)", and below the quark-puzzle control group's 0.23.

> "The average gain across the entire 225 students was 0.16. This is comparable to the gain from the traditional instruction group (0.17), but less than the group which completed the quark puzzle in their 1.5 weeks of instruction (0.23)."
