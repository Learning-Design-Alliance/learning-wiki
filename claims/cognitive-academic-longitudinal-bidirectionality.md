## Evidence

### Peng 2020

Peng Peng, Kievit Rogier A. (2020). The Development of Academic Achievement and Cognitive Abilities: A Bidirectional Perspective. Child Development Perspectives. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdep.12352

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

A meta-analysis of longitudinal relations (Peng et al., 2019) found nonverbal reasoning and reading/mathematics mutually predicted each other after controlling for initial performance; the article notes the synthesized relations came almost entirely from studies of children ages 6–10.

> "The findings suggest that nonverbal reasoning and reading/mathematics predicted each other in development even after controlling for initial performance, although almost all these synthesized longitudinal relations were from studies of children (ages 6–10)"

### Peng 2020 (2)

Peng Peng, Kievit Rogier A. (2020). The Development of Academic Achievement and Cognitive Abilities: A Bidirectional Perspective. Child Development Perspectives. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdep.12352

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

A cross-lagged modeling study using the Early Childhood Longitudinal Study, Kindergarten sample found working memory and reading/mathematics significantly predicted each other from the beginning of kindergarten through second grade. No effect size is printed.

> "one study showed that working memory and reading/mathematics significantly predicted each other from the beginning of kindergarten through second grade (Miller-Cotto & Byrnes, 2019)"

### Peng 2020 (3)

Peng Peng, Kievit Rogier A. (2020). The Development of Academic Achievement and Cognitive Abilities: A Bidirectional Perspective. Child Development Perspectives. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdep.12352

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

A preschool study (Schmitt et al., 2017) found bidirectional relations between executive function and mathematics and significant growth relations, but per the article it "did not show such a bidirectional relation between executive function and reading" — a scope limit.

> "Another study showed a bidirectional relation between executive function (including flexibility, working memory, and inhibition) and mathematics, and a significant relation between the growth of executive function and the growth of mathematics, over the pre-school period"
