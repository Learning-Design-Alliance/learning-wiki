## Evidence

### Hung Yin-Ling 2026

Hung Yin-Ling, Zhou Yi-Chao, Feng Hsin-Pei, Lin Chia-Huei, Chien Wu-Chien, Tzeng Wen-Chii. (2026). Spherical Video‐Based Virtual Reality Training for Nurses’ Delirium Care Competence: A Randomized Controlled Trial With Qualitative Evaluation. Journal of Nursing Management. https://doi.org/10.1155/jonm/8355655

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

GEE analysis of the same two-group RCT (n = 108) measuring confidence with a single-item 0–10 numeric rating scale. The article reports the significant immediate interaction and the nonsignificant follow-up interaction; no effect size is printed for this contrast.

> "For confidence, the SVVR × T1 interaction was significant (B = 0.85, 95% CI [0.36, 1.35], p = 0.001), indicating greater immediate confidence gains in the experimental group. However, the SVVR × T2 interaction was not statistically significant (B = 0.09, 95% CI [−0.58, 0.77], p = 0.787)."
