# When BC equations are not provided, some students substitute conservation-style reasoning instead of activating boundary conditions

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In think-aloud interviews on an E&M wave problem without provided BC equations, two of three students did not write down the boundary conditions but instead jumped to an amplitude-sum result justified by conservation-like reasoning. [→ Qing X. Ryan 2015](#qing-x-ryan-2015)

## Evidence

### Qing X. Ryan 2015

Qing X. Ryan, Steven J. Pollock, and Bethany R. Wilcox. (2015). Student Difficulties with Boundary Conditions in Electrodynamics. arXiv. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.066](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.066)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Think-aloud interviews with three CU students on an E&M wave problem where BCs were requested but equations not provided. Two students "jumped straight to this result", reasoning that waves "have to add up, kind of like the conservation"; no effect size printed.

> "One student was able to derive the BCs from Maxwells equations, but two students did not write down the BCs but rather, jumped straight to this result (with a sign error on ˜E0R): ˜E0I = ˜E0R + ˜E0T"
