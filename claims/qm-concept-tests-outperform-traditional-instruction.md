## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh and Guangtian Zhu 2016

Chandralekha Singh and Guangtian Zhu. (2016). Improving Students’ Understanding of Quantum Mechanics by Using Peer Instruction Tools. 2010 PERC Proceedings. Originally published 2010. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05461](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05461)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of three experimental classes (n=25, 13, 20) taught with concept tests against a comparison group of 18 traditionally taught students on a 10-point quiz of basic QM concepts. The article reports a significant difference (p<0.0001) between the "experimental groups (with lectures and concept tests)" and the comparison group; no effect size is printed.

> "There is a significant difference ( p<0.0001) in students’ performances between the experimental groups (with lectures and concept tests) and the comparison group with only traditional lectures."

### Chandralekha Singh and Guangtian Zhu 2016 (2)

Chandralekha Singh and Guangtian Zhu. (2016). Improving Students’ Understanding of Quantum Mechanics by Using Peer Instruction Tools. 2010 PERC Proceedings. Originally published 2010. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05461](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05461)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive quiz means for the same comparison: the three concept-test classes averaged "5.5, 7.0 and 7.6", while "The average score for the comparison group students is only 1.8" out of 10. These are printed means; no standardised effect size is reported.

> "The total score for this quiz is 10 and the average scores for the three experimental groups in 2008, 2009 and 2010 are 5.5, 7.0 and 7.6, respectively. The average score for the comparison group students is only 1.8."
