## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Popular vote for choosing peer-customer projects led to a lack of diversity in chosen ideas and exclusion of unique personal ideas, so the second iteration used votes only as a rough guide with instructor discretion dominant. [→ Edward Jay Wang 2024](#edward-jay-wang-2024)

## Evidence

### Edward Jay Wang 2024

Edward Jay Wang. (2024). Using Peer-Customers to Scalably Pair Student Teams with Customers for Hands-on Curriculum Final Projects. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2409.08299](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2409.08299)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Instructor observation comparing two course iterations. The authors list three issues with popular vote: most top-voted ideas were remote-convenience or alarm ideas, unique personal ideas such as motorcycling and off-road 4x4 driving were left out of the top rankings, and some class-chosen ideas were pure software problems outside class scope.

> "In the first iteration of the course, instead of the instructor choosing the peer-customers, the peer-customers were chosen based on popular vote. However, this was removed in the second iteration."
