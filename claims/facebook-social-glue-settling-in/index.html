Evidence¶

Clare Madge 2008¶

Clare Madge, Julia Meek, Jane Wellens, Tristram Hooley. (2008). Facebook, social integration and informal learning at University: 'It is more for socialising and talking to friends about work than for actually doing work'. https://doi.org/10.1080/17439880902923606

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Survey data on social integration show "21 per cent of respondents felt Facebook had been very important in helping them to form friendships at University" and a further 52 per cent important or quite important; 43 per cent agreed and 13 per cent strongly agreed it helped them settle in.

"21 per cent of respondents felt Facebook had been very important in helping them to form friendships at University and a further 52 percent said it had been important or quite important."

Clare Madge 2008 (2)¶

Clare Madge, Julia Meek, Jane Wellens, Tristram Hooley. (2008). Facebook, social integration and informal learning at University: 'It is more for socialising and talking to friends about work than for actually doing work'. https://doi.org/10.1080/17439880902923606

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Responses to the statement "Without Facebook I would be lonely": "only 3 per cent of respondents strongly agreed" and 16 per cent agreed, while 35 per cent disagreed and 29 per cent strongly disagreed, suggesting Facebook was not crucial against loneliness.

"only 3 per cent of respondents strongly agreed, 16 per cent agreed, 17 per cent neither agreed nor disagreed, 35 per cent disagreed and 29 per cent strongly disagreed."
