## Evidence

### Gradl-Dietsch G 2016

Gradl-Dietsch G, Lübke C, Horst K, Simon M, Modabber A, Sönmez TT, Münker R, Nebelung S, Knobe M. (2016). Peyton's four-step approach for teaching complex spinal manipulation techniques – a prospective randomized trial. BMC Medical Education 16:284. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0804-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the randomized trial, both groups received identical 30-min theoretical introductions each session; the end-of-course 10-item MC exam showed no between-group difference. The authors attribute this to identical theoretical teaching and possibly too few MC questions.

> "Results of the multiple choice exam did not differ between groups which might be attributable to the fact that theoretical teaching was identical for both groups and the fact that the number of MC questions might have been too low."
