# Visuospatial ability moderated the effect of interaction format on difficult recognition in Desktop-VR, with camera movement benefiting higher-ability learners

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r?` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Study 1 showed a significant interaction format × visuospatial ability interaction on difficult recognition. [→ Birgit Brucker 2024](#birgit-brucker-2024)
`q3 i?` LSD-adjusted comparisons indicated higher-visuospatial-ability learners achieved higher difficult-recognition values with camera movement than with no interaction (p = .034), while lower-ability learners showed no differences. [→ Birgit Brucker 2024 (2)](#birgit-brucker-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Birgit Brucker 2024

Birgit Brucker, Georg Pardi, Fabienne Uehlin, Laura Moosmann, Martin Lachmair, Marc Halfmann, Peter Gerjets. (2024). How Learners' Visuospatial Ability and Different Ways of Changing the Perspective Influence Learning About Movements in Desktop and Immersive Virtual Reality Environments. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09895-w

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r?`

Study 1 ANCOVA interaction term testing the moderating role of visuospatial ability on difficult recognition reached significance, "F(2, 153) = 3.328" with "η2p = .042".

> "Moreover, there was a significant interaction between interaction format and learners' visuospatial ability for difficult recognition performance (F(2, 153) = 3.328, MSE = 243.692, p = .038, η2p = .042)."

### Birgit Brucker 2024 (2)

Birgit Brucker, Georg Pardi, Fabienne Uehlin, Laura Moosmann, Martin Lachmair, Marc Halfmann, Peter Gerjets. (2024). How Learners' Visuospatial Ability and Different Ways of Changing the Perspective Influence Learning About Movements in Desktop and Immersive Virtual Reality Environments. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09895-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

LSD-adjusted pairwise comparisons in Study 1 following the significant interaction; the Bonferroni-adjusted post-hoc comparisons were not statistically significant (all ps > .102), so this pairwise pattern is a tendency under a less conservative adjustment.

> "participants with higher visuospatial ability achieved higher values for difficult recognition when using the camera movement condition compared to the no interaction condition (p = .034), but not compared to the object movement condition (p = .120)"
