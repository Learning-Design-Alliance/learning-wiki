## Evidence

### Van Dusen 2012

Van Dusen, B., & Otero, V. (2012). Influencing Students' Relationships With Physics Through Culturally Relevant Tools. Physics Education Research Conference proceedings. [doi:10.1063/1.4789739](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4789739)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

AP student survey/interview data reported in FINDINGS (Fig. 3, who determines the steps to be shown). Students vocalized differences in personal agency between screencasts and traditional labs.

> "When the AP students were asked whether the tea cher, themselves, or the class determined what steps should be shown in their work, students were more likely to reference the teacher for traditional lab work and themselves for screencasts (Fig. 3), none said the class."

### Van Dusen 2012 (2)

Van Dusen, B., & Otero, V. (2012). Influencing Students' Relationships With Physics Through Culturally Relevant Tools. Physics Education Research Conference proceedings. [doi:10.1063/1.4789739](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4789739)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The authors' stated inference from the screencast data: the iPad "has the potential to increase student agency more broadly" via self-guided screencasts; students described explaining to others as like being a teacher.

> "We infer from this set of data that the iPad has the potential to increase student agency more broadly through activities such as self-guided screencasts."
