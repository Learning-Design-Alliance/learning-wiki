Evidence¶

Jacob Levine 2026¶

Jacob Levine, Miguel Aenlle, Craig Zilles, Matthew West, and Mariana Silva. (2026). Automated Grading of Handwritten Mathematics Using Vision-Capable LLMs. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19043 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.19043

q2 · i? · design · r2

Complete categorization of each rubric item with AI–human disagreement across five questions in two courses, classifying errors as transcription errors or rubric application errors. The article reports disagreements "were dominated by transcription failures rather than rubric misapplication"; the abstract states 87% in the best model. No effect size is printed.

"As shown in Table 1, disagreements were dominated by transcription failures rather than rubric misapplication, suggesting image capture and preprocessing improvements may yield larger gains than prompt-level refinements."

Related Claims¶

  • Gemini frequently corrects board-written math errors with correction logs, but still shows case-consistency and notation-substitution failures — related
  • Focused rubric refinement that made required physics and scoring conditions explicit reduced AI–official disagreements on targeted questions (e.g., Grover item MAD 1.1 to 0.4 marks, r 0.70 to 0.82) — related
  • In the authors' practical test, vision-capable LLMs recognized handwritten examination letters with high accuracy, with Gemini 3 Flash best at about 98.84% (35 errors in 3009 letters)
  • AI self-reported confidence flags identify parts with smaller grading differences, but some high-confidence parts still differ from official scores by more than one point — related
  • Vision-assisted extraction identified chart type in all sampled images but showed ±5–10% numeric estimation error and two failure modes correctable via mandatory review — related
