Systematic literature search across 43 named journals and the ERIC database (generally spanning 1980–2007) produced the survey corpus: "we collected a total of 306 articles that addressed the effectiveness of feedback for writing development." Figure 1 shows a dramatic increase in studies across 1976–2007.

Related Claims¶

  • Feedback research spans the full range of methodologies, with a qualitative surge in 2000–2004 — related
  • Publication output on WCF in L2 writing grew over 2014–2024, with a small early phase (2014–2018) and a surge after 2019 peaking in 2023 — related
  • Feedback research shows little agreement on outcome measures, with 102 of 306 studies providing no specific writing-development measure — related
  • The meta-analysis corpus comprised 40 studies yielding 45 data comparisons from 2013-2022 — related
  • Only 112 of 306 studies were potentially suitable for meta-analysis, with most excluded for design and reporting inadequacies — related
  • Feedback research shifted from balanced L1/L2 coverage to predominantly English-learner populations by the mid-1990s — related
  • Only 17 of 306 feedback studies report the tone of feedback, almost always mixed positive and negative — related
