# Children's confidence in experimental conclusions increased after training and was higher for unconfounded than confounded experiments, with no training-condition differences

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Confidence rose from pretest to posttest and transfer in both conditions, with no main effect of condition. [→ Triona 2003](#triona-2003)
`q3 i?` Children were more confident in conclusions from unconfounded than confounded experiments, regardless of condition. [→ Triona 2003 (2)](#triona-2003-2)

## Evidence

### Triona 2003

Triona, L. M., & Klahr, D. (2003). Point and Click or Grab and Heft: Comparing the Influence of Physical and Virtual Instructional Materials on Elementary School Students' Ability to Design Experiments. Cognition and Instruction, 21(2), 149–173. https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci2102_02

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A 2 (training condition) × 3 (phase) MANOVA on children's mean confidence on a 4-point scale, with phase within-participant, "did not ﬁnd a main effect of training condition, F(1, 90) = 1.21". Confidence rose from pretest M = 2.8 to posttest M = 3.2 and transfer M = 3.1.

> "did not ﬁnd a main effect of training condition, F(1, 90) = 1.21, p =. 2 7 ."
