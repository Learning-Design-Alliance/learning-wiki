# Processing speed at 19 months correlates with expressive vocabulary between 18 and 31 months, but processing speed at 25 and 31 months does not

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Mean RT at 19 months correlated significantly with expressive vocabulary measures from 18 to 31 months and at 36 months, while RT at 25 and 31 months showed no such associations. [→ Michelle S. Peter 2019](#michelle-s-peter-2019)

## Evidence

### Michelle S. Peter 2019

Michelle S. Peter, Samantha Durrant, Andrew Jessop, Amy Bidgood, Julian M. Pine, Caroline F. Rowland. (2019). Does speed of processing or vocabulary size predict later language growth in toddlers? Cognitive Psychology 115 (2019) 101238. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2019.101238

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis relating each child's mean RT at 19, 25 and 31 months to expressive vocabulary scores from 8 to 37 months, with Holm-corrected p-values. Correlations at 19 months ranged from −0.34 to −0.45; the authors note "no evidence of such an association between speed of processing at either 25 or 31months and vocabulary".

> "There were significant correlations between Mean RT at 19months and all expressive vocabulary measures between 18 and 31months, and at 36months, indicating that individual differences in speed of processing at 19months were associated with earlier, concurrent, and later vocabulary."
