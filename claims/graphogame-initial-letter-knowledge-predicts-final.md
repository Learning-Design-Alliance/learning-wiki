## Evidence

### Jere-Folotiya 2014

Jere-Folotiya, J., Chansa-Kabali, T., Munachaka, J. C., Sampa, F., Yalukanda, C., Westerholm, J., Richardson, U., Serpell, R., & Lyytinen, H. (2014). The effect of using a mobile literacy game to improve literacy levels of grade one students in Zambian schools. Education Tech Research Dev, 62, 417-436. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-014-9342-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Regression analysis of GraphoGame log data from students who played the game (N = 96, reduced from 148 due to game log transmission errors). Initial letter knowledge was the sole significant predictor of final letter knowledge; R2 is variance explained, not a standardized effect size.

> "Model1, F (1,94) = 24.22, p\ .001 shows that initial letter knowledge, as the only predictor of ﬁnal letter knowledge. It explains (adjR 2) 20 % of the ﬁnal letter knowledge b1 = .91, p\ .001."

### Jere-Folotiya 2014 (2)

Jere-Folotiya, J., Chansa-Kabali, T., Munachaka, J. C., Sampa, F., Yalukanda, C., Westerholm, J., Richardson, U., Serpell, R., & Lyytinen, H. (2014). The effect of using a mobile literacy game to improve literacy levels of grade one students in Zambian schools. Education Tech Research Dev, 62, 417-436. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-014-9342-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Second regression model on the same log-data sample added exposure time (total time exposed to learning content, excluding pause times) as a predictor. The new predictor was significant (b2 = .05, p < .01) but added only 6% of variance.

> "Model 2, F(2,93) = 16.96, p\ .001, in which the amount of exposure time was added as a new predictor to model 1, only explained 6 % more of the variance of the ﬁnal letter knowledge scores."
