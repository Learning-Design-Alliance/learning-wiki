# Students reported lower peer and teacher interaction in online classes, with lower ratings accumulating over the semester, yet preferred online learning

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students rated online classes as producing lower or similar interaction with peers and similar interaction with teachers, and distributions toward lower interaction grew stronger by week 23 (P < 0.01). [→ Varachotisate P et al 2022](#varachotisate-p-et-al-2022)
`q2 i?` Despite reporting lower interaction, students preferred online learning to on-site learning at both survey points. [→ Varachotisate P et al 2022](#varachotisate-p-et-al-2022)

## Evidence

### Varachotisate P et al 2022

Varachotisate P et al. (2022). Student academic performance in non-lecture physiology topics following the abrupt change from traditional on-site teaching to online teaching during COVID-19 pandemic. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2022.2149292

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Anonymous voluntary opinion surveys at weeks 7 and 23 of the COVID year (n = 174–266 respondents) with content validity index of item-objective congruence 0.93. The article reports the week-23 potentiation "P < 0.01" and the online preference; no effect size is printed.

> "However, the distributions of answers towards lower levels of student-student and student-teacher interactions were potentiated in the 23rd week (P < 0.01, Table 5). Interestingly, students reported that they preferred online learning to on-site learning, although they reported lower levels of interactions."
