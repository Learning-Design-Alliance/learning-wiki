## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For students matched from freshman to junior year, BEMA scores dropped from 61±3% post-Physics II to 56±2% pre-Phys 301, a small fade that is statistically significantly different from zero (paired t-test p=.01). [→ S. J. Pollock 2009](#s-j-pollock-2009)

## Evidence

### S. J. Pollock 2009

S. J. Pollock. (2009). A Longitudinal Study of Student Conceptual Understanding in Electricity and Magnetism. Physics Education Research Conference proceedings. https://www.compadre.org/PER/ [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0907.5597](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0907.5597)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pretest study in two recent semesters of Phys 301 (before its transformation): matched students' BEMA scores fell about 5 points over an average of 3.6 semesters; a matched, paired 2-sample t-test yielded p=.01. The article notes this fade is much smaller than typical education-psychology loss rates.

> "For those students who we could match back to the introductory course (N=38), the BEMA post-physics II score was 61±3%, the pre-Phys 301 score was 56±2%.  Thus, for this (new) group of students, there is only a very small, roughly 5% fade over the average 3.6 semesters between introductory and junior level physics."
