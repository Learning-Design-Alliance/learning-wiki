## Evidence

### Kim Young Mi 2013

Kim Young Mi, Ansari Nasratullah, Kols Adrienne, Tappis Hannah, Currie Sheena, Zainullah Partamin, Bailey Patricia, Semba Richard, Sun Kai, van Roosmalen Jos, Stekelenburg Jelle. (2013). Assessing the capacity for newborn resuscitation and factors associated with providers' knowledge and skills: a cross-sectional study in Afghanistan. BMC Pediatrics. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2431-13-140

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Provider survey in the cross-sectional assessment of 82 doctors and 142 midwives; the printed contrast shows midwives at 59.2% and doctors at 35.4% for pre-service training, "p < 0.001". Overall any-training rates did not differ significantly (80.8% vs 82.7%).

> "However, doctors were significantly less likely than midwives to have received pre-service training in newborn resuscitation (35.4% and 59.2%, p < 0.001)."
