Evidence¶

Kolloffel 2011¶

Kolloffel, B., Eysink, T. H. S., & de Jong, T. (2011). Comparing the effects of representational tools in collaborative and individual inquiry learning. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning, 6, 223–251. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-011-9110-3

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Across ANOVAs on conceptual, procedural, and situational knowledge in the dyad/individual experiment, no main effect of format emerged on conceptual or procedural knowledge; the only format effect (situational) ran in the unexpected direction.

"The results show that there is no e vidence for this hypothesized mapping between representational format and the enhancement of a specific kind of understanding. For example, constructing a concept map does not enhance conceptual understanding."

Kolloffel 2011 (2)¶

Kolloffel, B., Eysink, T. H. S., & de Jong, T. (2011). Comparing the effects of representational tools in collaborative and individual inquiry learning. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning, 6, 223–251. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-011-9110-3

q3 · i? · causal · r2

ANCOVA on procedural knowledge (max 10, covariates math grade and time-on-task) found no main effects; only a three-way setting × format × tool-use interaction, F(2,201)=4.22, p<.05, possibly driven by arithmetical-condition tool users.

"The ANCOV A indicated no significant differences between setting, format, and tool-use. Only one, rather complicated interaction was observed between setting, format, and tool- use ( F (2,201)=4.22, p<.05)."
