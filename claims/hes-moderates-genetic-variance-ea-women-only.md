# Household economic status moderated genetic influence on educational attainment only for women, nonlinearly: genetic variance was highest in the high HES stratum, about double the low stratum, and lowest in the middle stratum

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` HES moderation of genetic variance in EA was present only for women; equality of genetic variance across HES was rejected for women (χ2 = 14.14, df = 2) but not for men (χ2 = .98, df = 2). [→ Arpawong 2023](#arpawong-2023)
`q2 i?` For women, genetic variance was greatest in the high HES stratum and least in the middle stratum, with both low and middle strata below the high stratum and below men in all strata. [→ Arpawong 2023 (2)](#arpawong-2023-2)
`q2 i?` A moving-window sensitivity analysis across seven overlapping 25% subsamples supported distinct genetic-variance differences for women at the 25% and 75% HES percentiles. [→ Arpawong 2023 (3)](#arpawong-2023-3)

## Evidence

### Arpawong 2023

Arpawong, Thalida Em; Gatz, Margaret; Zavala, Catalina; Gruenewald, Tara L.; Walters, Ellen E.; Prescott, Carol A. (2023). Nature, Nurture, and the Meaning of Educational Attainment: Differences by Sex and Socioeconomic Status. Twin Research and Human Genetics. https://doi.org/10.1017/thg.2023.6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Fifteen-group biometric SEM comparisons testing HES-strata invariance by sex; a model allowing variance sources to differ by HES strata and sex fit substantially better (χ2 = 70.11, df = 12). "The test for equality of genetic variance across HES was rejected (χ2= 14.14, df = 2)" for women only.

> "A model equating genetic variance across HES for males did not show worse fit than allowing HES-specific genetic estimates (χ2 = .98, df = 2). However, in women, the test for equality of genetic variance across HES was rejected (χ2= 14.14, df = 2)."

### Arpawong 2023 (2)

Arpawong, Thalida Em; Gatz, Margaret; Zavala, Catalina; Gruenewald, Tara L.; Walters, Ellen E.; Prescott, Carol A. (2023). Nature, Nurture, and the Meaning of Educational Attainment: Differences by Sex and Socioeconomic Status. Twin Research and Human Genetics. https://doi.org/10.1017/thg.2023.6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Variance estimates from the 15-group model (Table 2): women's additive genetic variance was 4.51 in the high HES stratum, 2.13 in the low, and 0.78 in the middle, with shared environment 35.5% of total variance in the middle stratum. "Genetic factors contribute the most in variance for the high HES stratum and least in variance for the middle HES stratum."

> "For women, genetic factors contribute the most in variance for the high HES stratum and least in variance for the middle HES stratum, with both low and middle HES strata having less variance than the high stratum and less variance compared to men in all strata."

### Arpawong 2023 (3)

Arpawong, Thalida Em; Gatz, Margaret; Zavala, Catalina; Gruenewald, Tara L.; Walters, Ellen E.; Prescott, Carol A. (2023). Nature, Nurture, and the Meaning of Educational Attainment: Differences by Sex and Socioeconomic Status. Twin Research and Human Genetics. https://doi.org/10.1017/thg.2023.6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Sensitivity analysis estimating the sex-differences model in seven overlapping subsamples, each 25% of the full sample, defined by a moving window of HES percentile. It showed "more distinct differences in genetic variance for EA at HES percentile points of 25% and 75% for women."

> "These estimates, shown in Figure S3 in the supplementary material, illustrate that there were more distinct differences in genetic variance for EA at HES percentile points of 25% and 75% for women, whereas for men differences were more moderate across HES percentiles."
