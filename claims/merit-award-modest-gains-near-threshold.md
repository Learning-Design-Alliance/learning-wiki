# The merit award program produced modest achievement improvements concentrated in schools close to the award threshold

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In schools near the award threshold, math and reading scores increased more strongly than in other schools after the announcement; the closer to the cut-off, the larger the improvement. [→ Rivkin 2023](#rivkin-2023)

## Evidence

### Rivkin 2023

Rivkin, S.G., Sartain, L., & Schiman, J.C. (2023). Training and retaining effective school leaders: Results from efforts to improve principal effectiveness in Chicago Public Schools. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/training-retaining-effective-school-leaders

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Comparison of achievement growth (ISAT scores, grades 3–8) between schools whose principals received bonuses and schools that just missed the cut-off. Gains were concentrated near the threshold: "the closer the school was to the cut-off... the larger the improvement in student achievement." No effect size printed.

> "The closer the school was to the cut-off for the principal to receive a merit award—meaning it was easier to reach the threshold for an award—the larger the improvement in student achievement, which suggests that the merit award was a motivator when the goal felt within reach."
