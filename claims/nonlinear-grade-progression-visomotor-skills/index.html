Capellini 2017¶

Capellini, S. A., Giaconi, C., & Germano, G. D. (2017). Relation between Visual Motor Integration and Handwriting in Students of Elementary School. Psychology, 8, 258-270. https://doi.org/10.4236/psych.2017.82015

Cross-grade comparison of the five groups' Beery VMI-6 subtest means (Table 1). The article states "there is not a progression of acquisition and development of the subtests", printing the group orderings for each test.

"We can observe that there is not a progression of acquisition and development of the subtests, as in visoperception (GI < GII < GIII < GV < GIV), motor coordination (GI < GII < GV < GIII < GIV) and visomotor integration (GI < GII < GV < GIII < GIV)"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • First-grade students show inferior visual-motor integration, visual perception and motor coordination performance compared with the other grade groups — related
  • Dysgraphia classification differs significantly across grade groups, with 80% of 1st graders, 10% of 3rd graders and 60% of 5th graders classified with dysgraphia — related
  • Visual-motor integration was the lowest-scoring ability and motor coordination the best-performing ability in every grade group — related
  • No significant difference was found between grade groups in the classification distribution of VMI, VP and MC performance levels — related
  • Only in the 5th-grade group were moderate positive correlations found, between visual-motor integration and motor coordination (r = 0.748) and between visual perception and dysgraphia classification (r = 0.643) — related
