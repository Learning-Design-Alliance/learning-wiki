# No overall multimedia effect on problem-solving performance in vector calculus: graphs did not significantly improve correctness

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students solving problems with graphs did not answer significantly more correctly than students without graphs (56% vs 52%). [→ Ögren 2017](#ogren-2017)

## Evidence

### Ögren 2017

Ögren, M., Nyström, M., & Jarodzka, H. (2017). There's more to the multimedia effect than meets the eye: is seeing pictures believing? Instructional Science, 45, 263–287. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-016-9397-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Between-subject experiment (N = 16 without graphs, N = 20 with graphs), 36 engineering-physics students, multi-level logistic regression predicting correctness. The article states "there is no statistically significant effect of presentation condition on students' abilities to answer the problems correctly."

> "Participants solving problems with graphs answered correctly to 56 % of the problems compared to 52 % for participants without graphs."
