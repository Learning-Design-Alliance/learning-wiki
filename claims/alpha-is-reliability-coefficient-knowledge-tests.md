# Cronbach's alpha equals the reliability of the scale score under classical test theory, domain sampling, and generalizability theory, and should be interpreted as a reliability coefficient

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` A derivation under the assumption of standardized parallel items shows alpha equals the ratio of true score variance to scale score variance, i.e., the reliability of the scale score. [→ Steffen Zitzmann 2025](#steffen-zitzmann-2025)

## Evidence

### Steffen Zitzmann 2025

Steffen Zitzmann, Gabe A. Orona. (2025). Why We Might Still be Concerned About Low Cronbach's Alphas in Domain-specific Knowledge Tests. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10015-5

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical derivation in the commentary: assuming standardized parallel items, alpha is algebraically reduced to the true-score-variance-to-scale-score-variance ratio, which "is exactly what a reliability coefficient is expected to do (Cho, 2016)" under classical test theory, domain sampling, and generalizability theory.

> "This ratio compares the amount of true score variance to the scale score variance, which is exactly what a reliability coefficient is expected to do (Cho, 2016)."
