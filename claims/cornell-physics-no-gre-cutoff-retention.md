## Evidence

### Ruxandra Bondarescu 2018

Ruxandra Bondarescu, Jayashree Balakrishna, Christine Corbett Moran, and Anuja DeSilva. (2018). Women in Science: Surpassing Subtle and Overt Biases through Intervention Programs. MNRAS. [doi:10.31235/osf.io/uz2h6](https://doi.org/10.31235/osf.io/uz2h6)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r1`

The article reports Cornell physics graduated 54 of 74 women PhDs (73%) between 1993 and 2011, versus an NSF-reported average graduation rate of 59% in 2008. The article attributes this to a supportive environment and role models, including 8 women faculty (15% of the faculty body).

> "We looked at data starting 1993, and found that between 1993 and 2011, the Cornell physics program graduated 54 women PhDs out of 74. This means that 73% of women graduated with a PhD from this ivy league school, when the average graduation rate for graduate students was reported by the National Sci-ence Foundation (NSF) to be 59% in 2008."
