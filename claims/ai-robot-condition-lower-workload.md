## Evidence

### Leyi Li 2026

Leyi Li, Chenyu Du, Jiafei Sun, Erick Purwanto, and Qing Zhang. (2026). RoboBlockly Studio: Conversational Block Programming with Embodied Robot Feedback for Computational Thinking. Designing Interactive Systems Conference (DIS '26). https://doi.org/10.1145/3800645.3813071

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

NASA-TLX Effort subscale in the within-subject study (N=32), paired t-test on approximately normal differences: AI-Only M = 7.50 versus AI+Robot M = 4.92, t(31)=4.10, p<.001, with printed effect size dz = 0.72, a medium effect favoring the AI+Robot condition.

> "Effort scores were lower in AI+Robot (𝑀= 4.92) compared to the AI–Only ( 𝑀= 7.50), indicating that AI support reduced the exertion required to make progress on the tasks."

### Leyi Li 2026 (2)

Leyi Li, Chenyu Du, Jiafei Sun, Erick Purwanto, and Qing Zhang. (2026). RoboBlockly Studio: Conversational Block Programming with Embodied Robot Feedback for Computational Thinking. Designing Interactive Systems Conference (DIS '26). https://doi.org/10.1145/3800645.3813071

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Frustration violated normality, so medians and a Wilcoxon signed-rank test were reported: "Frustration was markedly lower in the AI+Robot condition" (Mdn = 2 vs 12), V ≈ 528, Z ≈ 4.94, p < .001, r ≈ .87, a large effect.

> "Frustration was markedly lower in the AI+Robot condition (𝑀𝑑𝑛= 2,𝐼𝑄𝑅= 1–3) than in the AI–Only condition (𝑀𝑑𝑛= 12,𝐼𝑄𝑅= 10–14). A Wilcoxon signed-rank test confirmed that this difference was significant (𝑉≈ 528,𝑍≈ 4.94,𝑝<. 001,𝑟≈. 87)"
