# Peyton's four-step approach yields significantly higher practical skill scores than conventional instruction for complex spinal manipulation

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Medical students taught with Peyton's four-step approach scored significantly higher on the OSPE practical exam than students receiving conventional teaching (d = 0.66). [→ Gradl-Dietsch G 2016](#gradl-dietsch-g-2016)

## Evidence

### Gradl-Dietsch G 2016

Gradl-Dietsch G, Lübke C, Horst K, Simon M, Modabber A, Sönmez TT, Münker R, Nebelung S, Knobe M. (2016). Peyton's four-step approach for teaching complex spinal manipulation techniques – a prospective randomized trial. BMC Medical Education 16:284. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0804-0

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Prospective randomized trial: 87 completing students randomly assigned to Peyton group or conventional teaching across four 120-min sessions; multivariate analysis of videotaped OSPE scores showed a significant intervention effect, with "d = 0.66". Peyton group OSPE total was 57–61.9 vs 54.7–45.2 in controls.

> "Using Pillai's trace, there was a significant effect of type of intervention on the results of the practical exam (V = 0.35, F(10,74) = 4.1; p < 0.001; d = 0.66)."
