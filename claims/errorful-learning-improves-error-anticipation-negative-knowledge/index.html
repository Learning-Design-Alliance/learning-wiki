Evidence¶

Sarah Shi Hui Wong 2026¶

Sarah Shi Hui Wong. (2026). Deliberate erring improves scientific reasoning: Learning experimentation skills by intentionally designing invalid experiments. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09819-x

q3 · i2 · causal · r3

In the randomized experiment (N = 276), the error anticipation test asked students to describe potential design errors a researcher might make in a spacing-versus-cramming study; one point was awarded per problem-specific error. The omnibus test was significant, F(2, 273) = 13.62, p < .001, ŋ² = .09; the errorful groups "did not significantly differ, p =.88".

"The spot-explain-fix ( M = 1.48, SD = 1.11) and derr-explain-fix (M = 1.46, SD = 0.94) groups anticipated more problem-specific errors than the errorless practice control ( M = 0.82, SD = 0.86), both ps <.001, d = 0.66 and 0.71, respectively."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Both errorful learning methods (self- and other-generated invalid designs) improve near transfer of experimental design principles more than errorless practice, and self-generated deliberate errors yield the best near transfer — related
  • Deliberately designing and correcting one's own invalid experiments improves far transfer to a structurally more complex factorial design, whereas spotting and correcting others' errors does not outperform errorless practice on far transfer — related
  • Both errorful learning methods improve design critique (understanding the indeterminacy of invalid experiments) equally, outperforming errorless practice — related
  • Knowing what not to do does not suffice for knowing what to do: spot-explain-fix improved design critique and error anticipation but produced weaker near and far transfer than deliberate erring and no far-transfer advantage over errorless practice — related
