## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors argue booklets are less effective for practice change because students are not active and there is no human interaction or guarantee of in-depth reading. [→ Adib-Hajbaghery Mohsen 2016](#adib-hajbaghery-mohsen-2016)

## Evidence

### Adib-Hajbaghery Mohsen 2016

Adib-Hajbaghery Mohsen, Faraji Mona. (2016). Comparison of effect between group discussion and educational booklet on Iranian nursing students’ attitude and practice toward patient privacy. Journal of Educational Evaluation for Health Professions. https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2016.13.29

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' interpretive argument in the Discussion, citing prior inconsistent booklet findings (effective on patients' decisions and healthcare practices in some studies, ineffective on nurses' knowledge in another). This is an interpretation, not a tested result of the present study; the trial itself showed the booklet raised attitude but not practice (within-group P=0.203).

> "However, this method may be less effective on the students or people’s practice because in this method the students are not much active and there is no real human interaction."
