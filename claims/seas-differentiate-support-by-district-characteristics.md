# SEAs differentiate support to districts based on the number of CSI/TSI-identified schools, district capacity, and district willingness to engage.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` SEA personnel tailor support intensity and autonomy to district characteristics, giving more attention and oversight to districts with more low-performing schools and less direct support to high-capacity districts. [→ Dunn 2019](#dunn-2019)

## Evidence

### Dunn 2019

Dunn, L., & Ambroso, E. (2019). Balancing act: State and district roles in school improvement under ESSA. San Francisco, CA: WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED602966

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview finding from the 10-SEA study. The article reports that districts with more low-performing schools "are typically provided more attention" and that in Alaska the SEA provides less direct support to high-capacity districts, intervening as needed.

> "SEA personnel described differentiating support based on factors such as the number of CSI/TSI-identified schools, district capacity, and district willingness to engage with the SEA."
