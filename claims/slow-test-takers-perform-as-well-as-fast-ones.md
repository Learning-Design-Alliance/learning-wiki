## Evidence

### Weiner Saul J. 2026

Weiner Saul J., Park Yoon Soo, Gernsbacher Morton Ann, Petersen Kristina H. (2026). The myth that slow test-takers are worse students: Implications for time-limited testing. Medical education. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.70215

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The commentary cites a body of studies going back to at least 1921 showing that "student's pace on a timed exam does not reflect performance," and that slow students do on average just as well as fast ones when all are given the time they need.

> "numerous studies demonstrating that student's pace on a timed exam does not reflect performance"

### Weiner Saul J. 2026 (2)

Weiner Saul J., Park Yoon Soo, Gernsbacher Morton Ann, Petersen Kristina H. (2026). The myth that slow test-takers are worse students: Implications for time-limited testing. Medical education. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.70215

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The commentary reports, citing a 2023 Nature Communications study, that brain imaging "demonstrated that slower but more accurate problems solvers exhibit higher functional connectivity," suggesting knowing when to slow down predicts better performance.

> "Imaging demonstrated that slower but more accurate problems solvers exhibit higher functional connectivity"
