## Evidence

### Freytag Julia 2019

Freytag Julia, Stroben Fabian, Hautz Wolf E., Schauber Stefan K., Kämmer Juliane E. (2019). Rating the quality of teamwork—a comparison of novice and expert ratings using the Team Emergency Assessment Measure (TEAM) in simulated emergencies. Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13049-019-0591-9

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Reliability analysis of the German TEAM in the simulation study; item–total correlations averaged 0.71 (experts) and 0.69 (novices), and the sum score correlated with the GRS at r = 0.90 (experts) and r = 0.85 (novices), both p < .001.

> "reliability of TEAM instrument—calculated separately for each case and independently for expert and novice raters—had a mean Cronbach's alpha of 0.89 (SD = 0.06) for experts and a mean Cronbach's alpha of 0.85 (SD = 0.19) for novices."

### Freytag Julia 2019 (2)

Freytag Julia, Stroben Fabian, Hautz Wolf E., Schauber Stefan K., Kämmer Juliane E. (2019). Rating the quality of teamwork—a comparison of novice and expert ratings using the Team Emergency Assessment Measure (TEAM) in simulated emergencies. Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13049-019-0591-9

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Principal component analysis conducted independently for novice and expert ratings after adequate KMO (0.87 for both) and significant Bartlett tests (p < 0.001), confirming one underlying component as in the original version.

> "Results were largely comparable since for both, experts and novices, a dominant first component was found which explained 59 and 65% of the observed variance, respectively."
