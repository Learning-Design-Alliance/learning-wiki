Evidence¶

window-argument¶

Jacobs, B. (2026, September). Task automation, worker augmentation: Opportunities for career and technical education in the heartland. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/317

q1 · i? · theoretical · r2

This is the brief's central interpretive argument, not a tested result: low exposure and low adoption in Heartland occupations give CTE programs, which sit closest to hands-on occupations, direct visibility into how AI is introduced on the shop floor before it appears in national statistics. The brief stresses the window will not stay open indefinitely.

"That gap between technical exposure and actual adoption is not evidence that Heartland jobs are safe from AI. It is a limited window in which CTE programs can help shape how the technology is adopted, rather than simply react once adoption arrives."
