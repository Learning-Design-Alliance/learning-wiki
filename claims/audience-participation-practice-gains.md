# Audience participation during a factual film improves learning of the practiced verbal material compared with simple viewing, with gains attributable to practice rather than motivation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Audience participation, overt or covert and with or without knowledge of results, improved learning of verbal material compared with simple viewing of the film. [→ Michael 1953](#michael-1953)
`q2 i?` The improvement appears due to practice effects rather than motivation, since gains were found only on points covered in the participation sessions. [→ Michael 1953](#michael-1953)

## Evidence

### Michael 1953

Michael, D. N. (1953). Some Factors Influencing the Effects of Audience Participation on Learning from a Factual Film. Memo Report No. 13A, Human Resources Research Laboratories, Bolling Air Force Base. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Michael+audience+participation+factual+film

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experimental study in which eight groups of about 640 high school subjects viewed a civil defense film interrupted by questions; half answered overtly, half covertly, half received feedback. The review reports "considerable improvement in learning verbal material as compared to simple viewing of the film." Gains were limited to items used in participation periods.

> "Audience participation, whether overt or coven, and with or with- out knowledge of results, resulted in considerable improvement in learning verbal material as compared to simple viewing of the film."
