# Preservice teachers became more likely to notice the absence of students building on each other's ideas and marginally more likely to notice open-ended questions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants were significantly more likely to notice the absence of students building on each other's ideas (McNemar p = .03; 6% pre to 41% post). [→ Blair 2024](#blair-2024)
`q2 i?` Mentions of open-ended/authentic teacher questions increased from 65% to 95% of PSTs (marginal, p = .06); student-centeredness mentions showed a nominal trend (p = .12). [→ Blair 2024 (2)](#blair-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Blair 2024

Blair, K. P., Banes, L. C., & Martin, L. (2024). Fostering noticing of classroom discussion features through analysis of contrasting cases. Instructional Science, 52:417–452. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09661-z

`q2 · i?`

Study 1 exploratory McNemar tests on specific codes; only the test statistic and percentages are printed, no effect size. Supports the hypothesized increase in noticing what is missing in discourse.

> "Participants were more likely to notice the absence of students building on each other’s ideas (exact McNemar’s test, p = .03), mentioned by 6% (1) of PSTs at pre and 41% (7) of PSTs at post."

### Blair 2024 (2)

Blair, K. P., Banes, L. C., & Martin, L. (2024). Fostering noticing of classroom discussion features through analysis of contrasting cases. Instructional Science, 52:417–452. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09661-z

`q2 · i?`

Study 1 exploratory analyses of open-ended question codes (marginal, p = .06) and student-centeredness (p = .12, nominal trend); the article notes these analyses do not control for multiple comparisons.

> "participants were marginally more likely to talk about the presence and/or absence of teacher’s open-ended questions at post (exact McNemar’s test, p = .06)"
