## Evidence

### Melody M. Chao 2025

Melody M. Chao, Allen H. Huang, Anirban Mukhopadhyay & Janghoon Shon. (2025). Divergent effects of mindsets on performance trajectories. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00355-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Term-level longitudinal regressions on students' self-reported mindsets (6,918 student-terms; observational, so the association cannot show that mindset causes the difference) found a significant Mindset × Term Squared interaction (B = −0.003, SE = 0.001, p = 0.029), with "greater" concavity for stronger growth mindsets. Figure 1 plots the predicted divergent-then-converging trajectories.

> "Assuming the same starting point, this suggests that students with growth mindsets outperform those with fixed mindsets in earlier terms, but the difference decreases over time in the later terms."

### Melody M. Chao 2025 (2)

Melody M. Chao, Allen H. Huang, Anirban Mukhopadhyay & Janghoon Shon. (2025). Divergent effects of mindsets on performance trajectories. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00355-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Follow-up comparisons of the estimated trajectories in Fig. 1 showed the growth-fixed gap emerging in Term 2, peaking around Term 4, and converging by Term 7; differences were "statistically indistinguishable" at the end of the program.

> "The difference became statistically signiﬁcant from Term 3, was greatest in Term 4, and became weaker from Terms 5 and 6. Eventually, the performance of those with growth and ﬁxed mindsets became statistically indistinguishable from Term 7"
