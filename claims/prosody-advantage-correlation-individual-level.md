## Evidence

### Matilda Knierim 2024

Matilda Knierim, Sahil Jain, Murat Han Aydoğan, Kenneth Mitra, Kush Desai, Akanksha Saran, and Kim Baraka. (2024). Prosody as a Teaching Signal for Agent Learning: Exploratory Studies and Algorithmic Implications. INTERNATIONAL CONFERENCE ON MULTIMODAL INTERACTION (ICMI '24). https://doi.org/10.1145/3678957.3685735

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r1`

Spearman rank correlations (Bonferroni corrected) between prosodic features and advantage values per participant in the Wizard-of-Oz interactive RL study. Three of 14 correlations were significant, with printed coefficients r = 0.15 and r = 0.25 ("with a correlation coefficient of 0.15 and 0.25"); one participant showed a negative coefficient of −0.25.

> "For loudness and energy, three out of14 correlations were signif- icant, with a correlation coefficient of 0.15 and 0.25."

### Matilda Knierim 2024 (2)

Matilda Knierim, Sahil Jain, Murat Han Aydoğan, Kenneth Mitra, Kush Desai, Akanksha Saran, and Kim Baraka. (2024). Prosody as a Teaching Signal for Agent Learning: Exploratory Studies and Algorithmic Implications. INTERNATIONAL CONFERENCE ON MULTIMODAL INTERACTION (ICMI '24). https://doi.org/10.1145/3678957.3685735

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Repeated-measure correlation across participants in the same interactive RL study. The loudness/energy advantage relationship "did not hold at the population level"; the pitch–advantage correlation likewise did not hold (p = 0.51), and repetition and duration correlations were not significant.

> "The result showed that the correlation did not hold at the population level with a p-value = 0.23."
