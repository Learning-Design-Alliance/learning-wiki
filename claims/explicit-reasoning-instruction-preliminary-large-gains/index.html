Evidence¶

Moore 2011¶

Moore, J. C., & Rubbo, L. J. (2011). Scientific reasoning abilities of non-science majors in physics-based courses. https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.2764 doi:10.48550/arxiv.1110.2764

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Preliminary test class (15 students from both physics-major and general-education populations) embedded Lawson-style IAT activities and activities targeting proportional, control-of-variables, probability, and correlation reasoning within Physics by Inquiry content. Table III: DIRECT g=0.60 (N=15), TUG-K g=0.55 (N=14), LCTSR g=0.68 (N=14).

"As shown in tab. III, an average normalized gain of 68% (n=14) was achieved on the LCTSR in comparison to 11% (n=42) for previous courses taught similarly with respect to content, though lacking explicit reasoning intervention."

Related Claims¶

  • In physics-based courses for non-science majors taught with active-engagement pedagogy, normalized gains were 0.38–0.42 on content tests but 0.06 on a scientific-reasoning test — related
  • Pre-instruction LCTSR scores correlate with normalized learning gains, most strongly for theoretical content (TUG-K r=0.59) and more weakly for descriptive content (DIRECT r=0.50) — related
  • Argumentation Improves Reasoning — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Productive discourse practices correlate with improved performance on standardized tests of reasoning — related
  • Explicit teaching of the control-of-variables strategy shows no advantage over not explicitly teaching it, per the Schwichow et al. meta-analysis cited in the review — related
