## Evidence

### Sela Y 2025

Sela Y, Cassuto M, Capua T, Dadbaeiv L, Molad J, Hallevi H, Gadoth A, Sadan O, Seyman E. (2025). Acute neurology simulation-based training boosts confidence and reduces anxiety in novice neurology residents and nursing staff. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07948-4

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Subgroup analysis (Fig. 3) comparing STAI-6 scores pre and post simulation by sector in the same cohort. The nursing-sector null is reported as no significant change; equivalence was not tested.

> "A significant decrease in anxiety levels as assessed by STAI-6 was observed only in the medical sector (z=-4.98, p < 0.001), while there was no significant change in the nursing sector (z=-1.4, p = 0.16)."

### Sela Y 2025 (2)

Sela Y, Cassuto M, Capua T, Dadbaeiv L, Molad J, Hallevi H, Gadoth A, Sadan O, Seyman E. (2025). Acute neurology simulation-based training boosts confidence and reduces anxiety in novice neurology residents and nursing staff. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07948-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same subgroup analysis: readiness improved in both sectors, with smaller statistics in the nursing group (z=-2.27 identify; z=-2.75 manage) than the medical group.

> "However, self-readiness levels to identify (z= -4.7, p < 0.001, z=-2.27, p = 0.02) and manage (z=-4.94, p < 0.001, z=-2.75, p = 0.006) neurological emergencies improved in both the medical and nursing participants respectively."
