## Evidence

### James Overduin 2021

James Overduin, Jacob Buchman, Jonathan Perry and Thomas Krause. (2021). The Scourge of Online Solutions and an Academic Hertzsprung-Russell Diagram. The Physics Educator. [doi:10.1142/s2661339521500074](https://doi.org/10.1142/s2661339521500074)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Regression of test grade on homework grade for the twelve-semester dataset (Table 1), fit separately before and after 2016. The relative standard error is taken as a measure of badness of fit; the quoted percentages are fit-quality values, not effect sizes, so no impact magnitude is printed.

> "Over the period 2016-2020, the “badness of ﬁt” of the academic main sequence jumped from 17% to 40% for Physics 1, and from 30% to 74% for Physics 2, relative to its value from 2012-2016."

### James Overduin 2021 (2)

James Overduin, Jacob Buchman, Jonathan Perry and Thomas Krause. (2021). The Scourge of Online Solutions and an Academic Hertzsprung-Russell Diagram. The Physics Educator. [doi:10.1142/s2661339521500074](https://doi.org/10.1142/s2661339521500074)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The authors report, from the per-semester regression plots (Fig. 6), individual semesters in which nearly all students scored homework averages near 90% while test grades varied between 40% and 100%, so homework no longer predicts tests in those semesters.

> "there are some individual courses and semesters where the “main sequence” resembles a vertical line, so that there is no functional relationship between homework and test grades at all"
