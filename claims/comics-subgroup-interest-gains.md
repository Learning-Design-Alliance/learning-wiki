# Comics use showed significant interaction effects with class and gender on interest in reading, with gains among fourth-grade boys and fifth-grade pupils of both genders

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The effect of the programme on interest in reading interacted significantly with class and with gender and class. [→ Kerneža and Košir 2016](#kerneza-and-kosir-2016)
`q2 i?` Fourth- and fifth-grade experimental pupils reported significantly improved interest in reading literary texts relative to control peers, while second- and third-graders improved more in the control group. [→ Kerneža and Košir 2016 (2)](#kerneza-and-kosir-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Kerneža and Košir 2016

Maja Kerneža and Katja Košir. (2016). Comics as a Literary-Didactic Method and Their Use for Reducing Gender Differences in Reading Literacy at the Primary Level of Education. c e p s Journal, Vol. 6, No. 2. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1129008

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated measures ANOVA with Bonferroni post hoc test on interest in reading literary texts. The article reports a significant measurement × inclusion × class interaction and a significant measurement × gender × class interaction; post hoc differences between second- and third-grade (p = 0.02) and second- and fifth-grade (p = 0.01) pupils.

> "Table 9 shows a statistically significant interaction effect between inclusion in the programme, measurement period and class, as well as between measurement period, gender and class."

### Kerneža and Košir 2016 (2)

Maja Kerneža and Katja Košir. (2016). Comics as a Literary-Didactic Method and Their Use for Reducing Gender Differences in Reading Literacy at the Primary Level of Education. c e p s Journal, Vol. 6, No. 2. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1129008

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Subgroup analysis of the same quasi-experimental sample by class and gender. The article reports "fourth- and fifth-grade pupils in the experimental group reported a significantly improved level of interest in reading literary texts than their peers in the control group"; no effect size is printed.

> "The repeated measures ANOV A and the results presented in Table 8 show that fourth- and fifth-grade pupils in the experimental group reported a significantly improved level of interest in reading literary texts than their peers in the control group"
