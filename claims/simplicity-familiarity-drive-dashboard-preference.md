# Perceived ease-of-use based on simplicity and familiarity with common chart types heavily influenced teachers' visualization preferences

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teachers cited simplicity of representation and prior familiarity as reasons for preferring whole class bar and line charts. [→ Ali Raza 2024](#ali-raza-2024)
`q2 i?` Teacher responses to the Choropleth Heatmap were polarized, with most finding it difficult and a few finding it easy due to prior familiarity. [→ Ali Raza 2024 (2)](#ali-raza-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Ali Raza 2024

Ali Raza, Tamara Sumner and William R. Penuel. (2024). The Science Student Electronic Exit Ticket (SEET) System: Visualizations to Help Teachers Notice and Reflect on Classroom Inequalities. Journal of Learning Analytics, 11(1), 87-100. https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2024.8199

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Inductive grounded-theory coding of interview rationales from 42 teachers, with two coders reaching consensus. Teachers cited reasons such as simple/easier (24), helpful/quickest (9), and faster to get information (6) for the bar charts.

> "Teachers reported that ease -of-use, simplicity, and familiarity with common chart types — such as bar and line ch arts — heavily influenced what visualizations they chose to use."

### Ali Raza 2024 (2)

Ali Raza, Tamara Sumner and William R. Penuel. (2024). The Science Student Electronic Exit Ticket (SEET) System: Visualizations to Help Teachers Notice and Reflect on Classroom Inequalities. Journal of Learning Analytics, 11(1), 87-100. https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2024.8199

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Interview coding of heatmap rationales: most teachers reported finding the heatmap very difficult, citing colours making it hard to read (17), too many colours (13), no previous experience/familiarity (17), and a high learning curve (8); 12 teachers found it easy, citing prior familiarity.

> "Teachers' perspectives on the ease -of-use of the Choropleth Heatmap were polarized. A few teachers (12) found it easy-to-use, often citing their prior familiarity with these types of representations."
