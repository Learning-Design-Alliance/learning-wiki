## Evidence

### Rahul K. Choudhary 2018

Rahul K. Choudhary, Alexander Foppoli, Tejinder Kaur, David G. Blair, Marjan Zadnik, Richard Meagher. (2018). Can a short intervention focused on gravitational waves and quantum physics improve students' understanding and attitude?. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/aae26a](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/aae26a)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Likert-scale attitudinal questionnaire (seven items, 1-5 scale) analyzed by gender and year level with t-tests, alongside conceptual gains, in the 103-student one-day intervention. The article reports the printed improvement factors; no standardised effect size is given.

> "When averaged over all the students the conceptual mean scores improve by a factor of 1.7 and 2.2 for boys and girls respectively. In spite of strong ceiling effect , the attitudinal mean scores improve by a factor of 1.17 and 1.14 for boys and girls respectively."

### Rahul K. Choudhary 2018 (2)

Rahul K. Choudhary, Alexander Foppoli, Tejinder Kaur, David G. Blair, Marjan Zadnik, Richard Meagher. (2018). Can a short intervention focused on gravitational waves and quantum physics improve students' understanding and attitude?. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/aae26a](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/aae26a)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Attitudinal item 4 ("I would like to have a career in science") showed little change across year levels in the one-day intervention. The article reports "minimal effect on career choices", noting a small effect for Year 8 and Year 9 boys; no effect size is printed.

> "This question indicates the program had  minimal effect on career choices , contrary to the results reported by S. Laursen et al . [15]"
