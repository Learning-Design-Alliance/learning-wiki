## Evidence

### Karadeniz 2020

Karadeniz, E. & Degirmencay, Ş. A. (2020). The Effect of the Science-Fiction Books on Arousing Curiosity about Science in Secondary School Students. Journal of Turkish Science Education, 17(2), 225-241. https://doi.org/10.36681/tused.2020.23

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Content analysis of the semi-structured diaries the 30 experimental-group students (grades 5 and 6, one Turkish boarding middle school) wrote after each of the 12 books they read: 9 science-fiction books and 3 non-science-fiction books from the Catalhoyuk Stories series (The First Dawn of Our World, Children of Fire, Abundance and War), chosen to alternate the genre. Per book, 24 to 29 students said the book increased their science curiosity; these are students' own reports, not a measured change. For From the Earth to the Moon the article reports "29 students stated that the book increased their science curiosity" and that all students said the book was entirely about science; no effect size is printed.

> "And 29 students stated that the book increased their science curiosity. All students stated that the book was entirely about science and most of them said that it contained useful information."
