### Alona Strugatski and Giora Alexandron 2025

Alona Strugatski and Giora Alexandron. (2025). Applying Item Response Theory to Distinguish Between Human and Generative AI Responses to Multiple-Choice Assessments. LAK'25 pre-print. https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02713

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Kruskal-Wallis comparison of the three chatbots' PFS on the chemistry dataset (976 responses: 931 human, 45 GenAI at 15 per agent). Post-hoc Dunn tests with Bonferroni correction found ChatGPT distinct from Gemini and Claude on G and ZU3.

> "The Kruskal-Wallis test indicated significant differences in the PFS values among the three GenAI agents as follows: for𝐺 (H = 14.00, p < 0.001), for𝐺∗ (H = 6.60, p < 0.05) and𝑍𝑈 3 (H = 9.11, p<0.05) . However, for𝑈 3 (H = 5.84, p = 0.054), the differences were not statistically significant."

### Alona Strugatski and Giora Alexandron 2025 (2)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Kruskal-Wallis comparison on the psychometric dataset (1040 responses: 980 human, 60 GenAI at 20 per agent). Post-hoc analysis showed distinct Claude/Gemini groupings on three measures, while ChatGPT was distinct from neither.

> "The Kruskal-Wallis test similarly revealed significant differences among the three GenAI agents for𝐺∗ (H = 7.95, p < 0.05),𝑈 3 (H = 10.53, p < 0.01) and𝑍𝑈 3 (H = 13.52, p < 0.01). However, the differences for𝐺 (H = 4.43, p = 0.11) were not statistically significant."
