## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In formal adult classrooms forming quasi-communities, hierarchy is dynamic and task-oriented, with any member exhibiting mastery by contributing expertise to the task at hand. [→ Emad 2016](#emad-2016)

## Evidence

### Emad 2016

Emad, G. R., & Roth, W.-M. (2016). Quasi-Communities: Rethinking Learning in Formal Adult and Vocational Education. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-016-9386-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Ethnographic analysis of video recordings, interviews, and field notes in a Canadian maritime vocational institute course shows the authors observed that "The hierarchy in the quasi-community is not structured but is dynamic and distributed across the community members." For example, James, a tugboat worker, became the center of attention and took the role of instructor during an anchoring discussion.
