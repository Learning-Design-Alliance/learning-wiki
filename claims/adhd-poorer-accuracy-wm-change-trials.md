# Adolescents with ADHD show poorer accuracy than non-ADHD peers on all conditions of a visual short-term memory task, with the largest drop on trials requiring WM updating

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q3` · `i2`–`i3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` ADHD adolescents showed poorer accuracy than non-ADHD adolescents across all task conditions (main effect of group). [→ Ortega 2020](#ortega-2020)
`q3 i2` The Group x Trial Type interaction shows the ADHD accuracy deficit was significantly larger on Different trials (S2 different from S1), i.e., when WM updating was needed. [→ Ortega 2020 (2)](#ortega-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Ortega 2020

Ortega, R., López, V., Carrasco, X., Escobar, M. J., García, A. M., Parra, M. A. & Aboitiz, F. (2020). Neurocognitive mechanisms underlying working memory encoding and retrieval in Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder. Scientific Reports 10:7771. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-64678-x

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Mixed-model repeated-measures ANOVA of accuracy in 18 ADHD and 18 matched non-ADHD adolescents on the visual short-term memory binding task. The article reports "a poorer performance on all conditions" in ADHD, F(1, 34) = 10.047, p = 0.00322, η2 = 0.23.

> "Regarding accuracy, the ADHD group showed a poorer performance on all conditions (F(1, 34) = 10.047, p = 0.00322, η2 = 0.23) compared to the non-ADHD group."

### Ortega 2020 (2)

Ortega, R., López, V., Carrasco, X., Escobar, M. J., García, A. M., Parra, M. A. & Aboitiz, F. (2020). Neurocognitive mechanisms underlying working memory encoding and retrieval in Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder. Scientific Reports 10:7771. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-64678-x

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Same ANOVA on accuracy: the Group x Trial Type interaction was significant, F(1,34) = 5.2559, p = 0.02818, η2 = 0.13, with post-hoc tests showing "a significant drop of performance in the ADHD group during different trials".

> "A significant interaction was found between Group and Trail Type (F(1,34) = 5.2559, p = 0.02818, η2 = 0.13). Post-hoc analyses showed a significant drop of performance in the ADHD group during different trials (i.e., S2 different from S1)"
