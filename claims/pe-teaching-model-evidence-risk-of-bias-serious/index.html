Evidence¶

Tian Qi 2026¶

Tian Qi, Liu Yaowen, Wang Ziyu, Qin Gang. (2026). Comparative effects of physical education teaching models on motor skill development in school-aged children and adolescents: a systematic review and network meta-analysis. Frontiers in Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1905122

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Retrospective study-level risk-of-bias appraisal of all 21 included studies (RoB 2 for randomized, ROBINS-I for quasi-experimental). "None of the four randomized comparisons was at low risk: all were rated as having some concerns under RoB 2," mainly for concealment, masking, or outcome handling.

"All 17 non-randomized comparisons were rated as having serious risk under ROBINS-I, primarily because of confounding, non-random allocation, or incomplete reporting of baseline equivalence and outcome handling."

Related Claims¶

  • Included studies show risk-of-bias limitations concentrated in allocation concealment, participant/teacher blinding, and design-sensitive bias — related
  • Most included studies carried a high risk of bias or some concerns, particularly for confounding and outcome measurement, limiting certainty — a broader claim this one bears on
  • The evidence base on inclusion effects is methodologically weak: no randomised studies and nearly all synthesised studies at serious risk of bias — related
  • The overall certainty of evidence for educational programme effects on nurses' knowledge and practice is very low (GRADE), driven by bias, inconsistency and imprecision — related
  • In randomized trials, long-term outcomes rated high risk of bias had larger significant effects (g = .29) than low/some-concerns outcomes (g = .07, nonsignificant) — related
  • Methodological quality of included studies was limited: RCTs often lacked randomisation description and blinding, and cohort/case-control studies did not account for confounding — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
