# Technologists' oversegmentation error share fell from 37% to 16% of corrected cases while undersegmentation error share rose from 63% to 84% from before to after supplementary training, in one uncontrolled pre-post study

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The share of oversegmentation errors among corrected cases decreased significantly (37% to 16%, p = 0.003) while the share of undersegmentation errors increased significantly (63% to 84%, p = 0.003) after supplementary training. [→ Suman Garima 2020](#suman-garima-2020)

## Evidence

### Suman Garima 2020

Suman Garima, Panda Ananya, Korfiatis Panagiotis, Edwards Marie E., Garg Sushil, Blezek Daniel J., Chari Suresh T., Goenka Ajit H. (2020). Development of a volumetric pancreas segmentation CT dataset for AI applications through trained technologists: a study during the COVID 19 containment phase. Abdominal Radiology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00261-020-02741-x

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Chi-square comparison of error-type proportions between the two batches of segmentations needing correction. The paper also reports "a decrease in the share of oversegmentation errors (37% in the first batch and 16% in the second batch, p = 0.003)". Differences were significant at p = 0.003 in both directions.

> "The trend of decline in the proportion of accurate segmentations in the second batch was primarily due to a relative increase in the share of undersegmentation errors (63% in the first batch and 84% in the second batch, p = 0.003)."
