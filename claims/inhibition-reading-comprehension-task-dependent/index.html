Evidence¶

Ting Zhou 2026¶

Ting Zhou, Thuy Anh Nguyen, Aprezo Pardodi Maba, Damon Thomas, Young-Suk Grace Kim, Elizabeth J. Edwards. (2026). Executive Functions and Reading in Children: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10160-5

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r?

Qualitative synthesis of eight inhibition-reading comprehension studies: "Five out of eight studies reported moderate to large associations", strongest when response inhibition tasks were paired with cloze-style comprehension assessments; no effect sizes are printed for this summary.

"Five out of eight studies reported moderate to large associations, with stronger associations typically observed when response inhibition tasks (e.g., NEPSY-II) were paired with cloze-style assessments like WJ Passage Comprehension (Barber et al., 2020)."

Ting Zhou 2026 (2)¶

Ting Zhou, Thuy Anh Nguyen, Aprezo Pardodi Maba, Damon Thomas, Young-Suk Grace Kim, Elizabeth J. Edwards. (2026). Executive Functions and Reading in Children: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10160-5

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r?

Within-study contrast (Hernández et al., 2018): with the same WJ Passage Comprehension subtest, the Continuous Performance Task gave a negligible association while the Head-Toes, Knees-Shoulders task gave a moderate one, implicating task differences.

"when response inhibition was indexed by the Continuous Performance Task and reading comprehension was assessed using the WJ Passage Comprehension subtest, the association was negligible."
