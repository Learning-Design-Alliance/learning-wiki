## Evidence

### Drost RMWA 2016

Drost RMWA, Paulus ATG, Jander AF, Mercken L, de Vries H, Ruwaard D, Evers SMAA. (2016). A Web-Based Computer-Tailored Alcohol Prevention Program for Adolescents: Cost-Effectiveness and Intersectoral Costs and Benefits. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.5223

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subgroup cost-effectiveness bootstrap analyses of the Alcoholic Alert cluster RCT. For female adolescents (weekly alcohol use), the ICER was labeled "Inferior" from both perspectives; for non-Dutch ethnicity, also "Inferior." The article attributes this partly to lower baseline consumption in these subgroups (e.g., female baseline mean 1.49 glasses vs male 4.18 glasses).

> "From a health care perspective, it was found to be cost-effective for the male and nonreligious adolescent subgroups as well. The intervention was not cost-effective for those with a non-Dutch ethnicity or for female adolescents for the weekly alcohol use outcome measure."
