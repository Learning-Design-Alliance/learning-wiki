# Teachers and students diverge in perceived YPT contributions for high-school mathematics, cookbook experiments, teamwork, information location and creativity

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The contribution of YPT to high-school mathematics was assessed positively by teachers but negatively by students, possibly because calculus use is not considered high-school mathematics by students or students underestimate the mathematics involved. [→ Sergej Faletič 2025](#sergej-faletic-2025)
`q1 i?` Students assessed YPT's contribution to teamwork, ability to locate and use information, and creativity as neutral to negative compared to their other activities, contrary to teachers' positive assessment, likely because students considered non-physics activities. ## Evidence

### Sergej Faletič 2025

Sergej Faletič, Assen Kyuldjiev, Thomas Lindner, Dorottya Schnider, Éva Izsa, Mihály Hömöstrei, Péter Jenei, Boyka Aneva, Daniela D. Ivanova, Hynek Němec, František Kundracik, and Martin Plesch. (2025). Development of soft and hard skills of high-school students via Young Physicists' Tournament. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2508.15324](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2508.15324)

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Authors' interpretation of the survey results in the Discussion section, offered as explanation rather than tested finding. They attribute the divergence to unspecified other activities such as team-based sports, musical ensembles and debate clubs, which teachers may not have considered.

> "We expected that the contribution of YPT to Teamwork, Ability to locate and use information, and Creativity would be positive compared to regular classes and other activities, as was also the assessment of teachers. However, students assessed it neutral to negative when compared to their other activities."
