Evidence¶

Alba Rubio 2022¶

Alba Rubio, Eduardo Vidal-Abarca, Marian Serrano-Mendizábal. (2022). How to assist the students while learning from text? Effects of inserting adjunct questions on text processing. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-022-09592-7

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Randomized between-subjects experiment with 76 freshmen (39 inserted, 37 post-reading) studying two science texts with ten adjunct questions, tested five days later on a 10-item near-transfer test. The inserted condition outperformed the post-reading condition with a medium effect size, d = − 0.59 ("t(74) = − 2.59, p = .011, d = − 0.59").

"Results showed that learning was higher for inserted condition (M = 6.22, SD = 1.81) than for post-reading condition (M = 5.08, SD = 2.01), t(74) = − 2.59, p = .011, d = − 0.59"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Students in the inserted-question condition spend more time reading the text initially than students in the post-reading condition
  • Inserted questions elicit more correct and fewer incorrect elaborations than post-reading questions, with no condition differences in paraphrases
  • Post-reading-question students spend more time reading the questions for the first time than inserted-question students
  • Post-reading-question students search significantly more text segments than inserted-question students, indicating less efficient search
  • Question timing interacts with relevance of rereading: inserted-question students reread more relevant and less non-relevant information than post-reading students
  • Question prompts improve learning — related
  • Adjunct aids such as learning objectives and embedded questions show limited effects and may interfere with competent readers' natural strategies — reports the opposite
