### Steven J. Pollock and Noah D. Finkelstein 2008

Steven J. Pollock and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2008). Sustaining Educational Reforms in Introductory Physics. Physical Review: Special Topics in Physics Education Research. https://journals.aps.org/prper/ [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0805.0277](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0805.0277)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Aggregate observational analysis across Tables 1 and 2, covering roughly 4000 students over twelve semesters, comparing normalized gains by faculty coding (P, I, T). The article reports PER-involved semesters post the highest gains and informed faculty outperform traditional-only semesters; no effect size is printed.

> "instances when PER faculty are involved in instruction, in either the lead or secondary role, students post the highest learning gains. When PER-informed faculty, “I”s, are involved in instruction, students post higher learning gains than when only traditional faculty are involved."
