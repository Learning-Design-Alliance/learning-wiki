# More controversial, unresolved AI-coder debates produce higher discussion-prompted coding accuracy, while compromising and collaborating modes reduce it

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies (4 entries) · 2 design `r2` · `q2` · `i0`–`i1`

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` More discussion turns predict higher discussion-prompted coding accuracy (effect size 0.168, p<0.0001). [→ Jeongyeon Kim and John Mitchell 2026](#jeongyeon-kim-and-john-mitchell-2026)
`q2 i1` Lower ratios of resolved conflicts (effect size -0.139) and correctly resolved conflicts (effect size -0.169) each predict higher discussion-prompted accuracy. [→ Jeongyeon Kim and John Mitchell 2026 (2)](#jeongyeon-kim-and-john-mitchell-2026-2)
`q2 i0` Lower ratios of compromising (effect size -0.066) and competing (effect size 0.054) modes show respectively a positive accuracy association and no relationship. [→ Jeongyeon Kim and John Mitchell 2026 (3)](#jeongyeon-kim-and-john-mitchell-2026-3)
`q2 i1` Less collaborating discussion predicts higher discussion-prompted accuracy (effect size -0.255, p<0.0001). [→ Jeongyeon Kim and John Mitchell 2026 (4)](#jeongyeon-kim-and-john-mitchell-2026-4)

### Jeongyeon Kim and John Mitchell 2026

`q2 · i1` · `design · r2`

Mixed-effects analysis of discussion controversy (Table 5) across the four datasets. The turns row reports "(7.997, 0.168)", p<0.0001 with effect size 0.168; contentious disagreements extended debates and increased turns.

> "Number of Turns in Discussions < 0.0001 **** (7.997, 0.168) The more controversial the discussions, the higher the accuracy of discussion-prompted coding."

### Jeongyeon Kim and John Mitchell 2026 (2)

> "Ratio of Resolved Conflicts < 0.0001 **** (-6.300, -0.139) The lower the agreement ratio in the discussions, the higher the accuracy of discussion-prompted coding."

### Jeongyeon Kim and John Mitchell 2026 (3)

> "Ratio of Competing Modes in Discussions < 0.01 ** (2.789, 0.054) No relationship between the ratio of competing modes of AI agents in discussions and the accuracy of discussion-prompted coding."
