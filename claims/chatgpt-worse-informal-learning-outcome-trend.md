# ChatGPT users showed a non-significant trend toward worse overall informal learning outcomes than Google Search users over 8 days

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants using ChatGPT scored lower on average on the post-learning knowledge test than Google Search users, but the difference did not reach statistical significance. [→ Shravika Mittal 2026](#shravika-mittal-2026)

## Evidence

### Shravika Mittal 2026

Shravika Mittal, Su Lin Blodgett, Q. Vera Liao. (2026). Learning by Chatting? Investigating the Impact of Generative AI on Information Seeking and Learning. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11669

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Welch's t-test comparing post-learning knowledge test scores in an 8-day between-subjects field experiment (14 ChatGPT vs 21 Google completers). ChatGPT mean 11.62 (SD 2.32) vs Google 12.86 (SD 2.21); "did not reach statistical significance"; regression controlling for baseline showed a similar trend.

> "The test did not reach statistical significance but indicates a trend that participants in the ChatGPT condition had worse learning outcomes than those in the Google condition (t-statistic:−1.523,p:0.069)."
