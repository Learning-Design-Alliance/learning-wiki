## Evidence

### Qoura & Zahran 2018

Qoura, A. A., & Zahran, F. A. (2018). The Effect of the 6+1 Trait Writing Model on ESP University Students Critical Thinking and Writing Achievement. English Language Teaching, 11(9). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v11n9p68

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental comparison of first-year pharmacy students randomly assigned to an experimental group (N = 35) taught via the 6+1 trait model and a control group receiving regular teaching. Table 1 reports experimental post-test mean 30.10 (SD 2.82) versus control 24.07 (SD 4.09). The t values in the table sit on each group's pre/post rows (experimental t = 9.100, Sig. 0.001, the same figures Table 2 gives for the experimental pre/post comparison; control t = 2.907, Sig. 0.489), so no between-group test of the post-test difference is printed, although the article's discussion states the groups differed significantly in favour of the experimental group. The control group's size is not given, and the experimental group was taught by one of the researchers.

> "Results in Table 1 shows that the higher mean score is for the post administration of the experimental group post-test. The increase in students' level in the experimental group could be interpreted that students were trained to use the 6+1 trait writing model that helped students to think critically."
