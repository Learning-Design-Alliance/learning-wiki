## Evidence

### Kelley Durkin 2026

Kelley Durkin, Manshuo Lin, Michael H. Kolodrubetz, Ryan P. McMahan. (2026). QISCIT: A validated concept inventory assessment for quantum information science. arXiv. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1119/5.0287731](https://doi.org/10.1119/5.0287731)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

The article's comparison of prior quantum assessments (Table I) characterizes their prerequisite knowledge. As printed, "Most prior assessments involve quantum aspects that require advanced mathematics, Dirac notation, or both," which the authors argue makes them largely inaccessible to students without linear algebra.

> "Most prior assessments involve quantum aspects that require advanced mathematics, Dirac notation, or both."

### Kelley Durkin 2026 (2)

Kelley Durkin, Manshuo Lin, Michael H. Kolodrubetz, Ryan P. McMahan. (2026). QISCIT: A validated concept inventory assessment for quantum information science. arXiv. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1119/5.0287731](https://doi.org/10.1119/5.0287731)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

The article's review of prior instruments states that "Only a few of the prior quantum assessments address the key concepts for future QIS learners," naming gates, entanglement, teleportation, and decoherence; it identifies Salehi et al.'s assessment as the only prior one addressing teleportation and the QMCA as the only one addressing decoherence.

> "Only a few of the prior quantum assessments address the key concepts for future QIS learners, such as gates, entanglement, teleportation, and decoherence."
