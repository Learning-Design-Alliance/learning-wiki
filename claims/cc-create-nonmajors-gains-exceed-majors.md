# Nonmajors in community college CREATE courses made larger self-rated gains than majors on SAAB and SALG measures

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2` · `q2` · `i2` medium
> **Currency** · newest study 2016 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` On SAAB attitude/ability categories, nonmajors (n = 39) made more pre/post changes than majors (n = 25), with significant nonmajor gains in visualization (ES = 0.65) and interpreting data (ES = 0.23). [→ Kenyon Kristy L. 2016](#kenyon-kristy-l-2016)
`q2 i2` On SALG categories, both groups changed significantly in all categories with larger ESs for nonmajors (e.g., understanding: majors 1.34 vs. nonmajors 1.68). [→ Kenyon Kristy L. 2016 (2)](#kenyon-kristy-l-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Kenyon Kristy L. 2016

Kenyon Kristy L., Onorato Morgan E., Gottesman Alan J., Hoque Jamila, Hoskins Sally G. (2016). Testing CREATE at Community Colleges: An Examination of Faculty Perspectives and Diverse Student Gains. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-07-0146

`q2 · i2` · `design · r2`

SAAB majors/nonmajors subset analysis (majors n = 25, nonmajors n = 39) by Wilcoxon signed-rank test; nonmajors showed significant gains in more categories, e.g. "visualization (ES = 0.65)", and the authors note gains for nonmajors exceed those of majors.

> "more pre/postcourse changes were made by the nonmajors cohort (n = 39) than by majors (n = 25; Table 8). Both groups made significant gains in the decoding primary literature and research in context categories, with moderate ES = (0.5–0.7). Nonmajors also changed significantly on visualization (ES = 0.65)"

### Kenyon Kristy L. 2016 (2)

Kenyon Kristy L., Onorato Morgan E., Gottesman Alan J., Hoque Jamila, Hoskins Sally G. (2016). Testing CREATE at Community Colleges: An Examination of Faculty Perspectives and Diverse Student Gains. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-07-0146

`q2 · i2` · `design · r2`

SALG majors/nonmajors comparison (majors n = 35, nonmajors n = 49) pooled from five campuses; all category changes significant at p ≤ 0.05, with nonmajor ESs larger throughout, e.g. understanding "ES = 0.85 and 1.68, respectively."

> "both nonmajors and majors made significant changes in all categories, with ESs larger for nonmajors in each category. For majors, ESs ranged from a low of 0.46 (integration of learning) to a high of 1.34 (understanding); nonmajors in the same categories had ES = 0.85 and 1.68, respectively."
