# Classified examples are more often correct than generated examples during learning, but feedback does not improve classification

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Student teachers classified 69% of examples correctly versus 59% for generated examples, a significant difference. [→ Tim M. Steininger 2022](#tim-m-steininger-2022)
`q3 i?` Feedback did not improve classification correctness from the first to the second trial. [→ Tim M. Steininger 2022 (2)](#tim-m-steininger-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Tim M. Steininger 2022

Tim M. Steininger, Jörg Wittwer, Thamar Voss. (2022). Classifying Examples is More Effective for Learning Relational Categories Than Reading or Generating Examples. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-022-09584-7

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Correctness of learning-phase examples was scored by two raters (κ = 0.87 for generation). Classified examples were correct more often than generated ones: "F(1, 56) = 4.08, p = .048, η2 = 0.07".

> "Student teachers generated 59% of all examples correctly (SD = 23.72). In contrast, student teachers classified 69% of all examples correctly ( SD = 15.76). This difference was significant, F(1, 56) = 4.08, p = .048, η2 = 0.07."

### Tim M. Steininger 2022 (2)

Tim M. Steininger, Jörg Wittwer, Thamar Voss. (2022). Classifying Examples is More Effective for Learning Relational Categories Than Reading or Generating Examples. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-022-09584-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A repeated-measures analysis with example format as between-subject factor and trial as within-subject factor found "no significant effect of trial, F(1, 56) = 2.46, p = .12" and no trial × format interaction; equivalence was not tested.

> "There was no significant effect of trial, F(1, 56) = 2.46, p = .12, ηp2 = 0.04, and no significant interaction effect between trial and example format, F(1, 56) = 1.84, p = .18, ηp2 = 0.03."
