# Regular rhythmic primes do not improve performance on a non-grammatical visual cancellation control task

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2023 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Neither prime, group, nor their interaction improved the model for the visual cancellation task; only chronological age improved fit. No effect size printed. [→ Fiveash 2023](#fiveash-2023)

## Evidence

### Fiveash 2023

Fiveash, A., Ladányi, E., Camici, J., Chidiac, K., Bush, C. T., Canette, L.-H., Bedoin, N., Gordon, R. L. & Tillmann, B. (2023). Regular rhythmic primes improve sentence repetition in children with developmental language disorder. npj Science of Learning 8:23. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00170-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Generalized linear mixed model (poisson) on count data from the visual cancellation priming task: "none of the additions" of prime, group, or interaction improved fit; only chronological age improved the model (r = 0.61). The task ruled out general arousal or motivational effects.

> "Compared to the intercept-only model (AIC = 1155.1), none of the additions: prime, χ2 (1) = 0.01, p = 0.91, AIC = 1157.1, group, χ2 (1) = 0.09, p = 0.76, AIC = 1157.0, or prime x group interaction improved the model"
