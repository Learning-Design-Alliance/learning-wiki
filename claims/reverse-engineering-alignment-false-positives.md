`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

The authors' conceptual analysis of the common "simple" approach, in which a research principle is matched to a product feature to suggest alignment. They state it "can lead to false positives, such as identifying a connection where none exists" and may adopt a narrow perspective on learning because principles derive from specific theories rather than a holistic approach. No empirical test is reported for this assertion.

> "However, this reverse-engineering approach, which attempts to match a specific tool to established research principles, has two key limitations: (1) it can lead to false positives, such as identifying a connection where none exists, or overlooking the interrelat ionships between features"
