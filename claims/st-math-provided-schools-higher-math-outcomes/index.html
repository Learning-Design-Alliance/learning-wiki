Evidence¶

Wendt 2019¶

Wendt, S., Rice, J., & Nakamoto, J. (2019). A cross-state evaluation of MIND Research Institute's ST Math program and math performance. San Francisco, CA: WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED601907

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

Hierarchical linear modeling of 1,132 treatment and 1,132 matched comparison grade-level clusters across 16 states, adjusting for baseline achievement, demographics, and nesting of grades within schools. The analysis found "0.17 of a standard deviation for proficiency rates and 0.13 for scale scores," both significant at p < .01 with BH correction.

"The magnitude (or effect size) of the difference was 0.17 of a standard deviation for proficiency rates and 0.13 for scale scores."

Wendt 2019 (2)¶

Wendt, S., Rice, J., & Nakamoto, J. (2019). A cross-state evaluation of MIND Research Institute's ST Math program and math performance. San Francisco, CA: WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED601907

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Separate multiple regressions by grade level (N = 392, 366, 374 matched pairs) adjusting for baseline achievement and school demographics. The report states grade 3 proficiency rates were "0.13 of a standard deviation higher" than comparison clusters; no standardized effect size is printed for the other grade-level contrasts.

"students in clusters of grade level 3 provided with the ST Math program had z-score proficiency rates in math that were, on average, 0.13 of a standard deviation higher compared to students in clusters of grade level 3 not provided with the ST Math program"
