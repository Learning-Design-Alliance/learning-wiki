## Evidence

### Kohlmyer 2009

Kohlmyer, M. A., Caballero, M. D., Catrambone, R., Chabay, R. W., Ding, L., Haugan, M. P., Marr, M. J., Sherwood, B. A., & Schatz, M. F. (2009). A Tale of Two Curricula: The performance of two thousand students in introductory electromagnetism. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.0022 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0906.0022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0906.0022)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

GT contingency-table analysis of category scores (item counts per category ranging from 2 to 12), chosen for the discrete, non-normal, unequal-variance data; the article interprets this as better performance across topics for M&I students.

> "On the pre-test, we found no signiﬁcant association between course treatment (M&I versus traditional) and overall BEMA score on any category. In contrast, the results of the contingency table analysis (see Appendix A 7) for the post-test scores show signiﬁcant association of BEMA score with treatment in each category."
