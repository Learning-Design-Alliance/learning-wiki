# Graduate students show less expert-like attitudes and approaches for graduate-level problem solving than for introductory-level problem solving

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Graduate students' responses differ between graduate-level and introductory-level problem framing with a small to moderate effect size (0.4 < d < 0.6), generally less favorable for graduate-level problems. [→ Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#andrew-mason-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)
`q2 i?` For example, about 40% felt stuck without help on graduate-level problems versus 20% on introductory problems, and about 15% would give up after 10 minutes on a graduate-level problem while none would on an introductory problem. [→ Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)](#andrew-mason-and-chandralekha-singh-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Surveying Graduate Students' Attitudes and Approaches to Problem Solving. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/37/5/055704](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/37/5/055704)

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Same-cohort survey comparison of graduate students answering the AAPS about their own graduate courses versus introductory physics problems. The article reports "small to moderate effect size (0 .4 < d < 0.6)" and interprets it as a real difference in attitudes across problem levels.

> "Comparison of the graduate students' problem solving attitudes and approaches for introductory and graduate level problems in Table 2 shows that there is small to moderate effect size (0 .4 < d < 0.6)."

### Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Surveying Graduate Students' Attitudes and Approaches to Problem Solving. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/37/5/055704](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/37/5/055704)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Survey item analysis (Figure 1) comparing graduate students' responses under the two problem-level framings; also none would give up after 10 minutes on introductory problems versus approximately 15% on graduate-level problems, and fewer enjoyed challenging graduate-level problems.

> "approximately 40% of the graduate students felt that if they were not sure about the right way to start a problem, they would be stuck unless they got help but only 20% felt this way when solving introductory physics problems."
