## Evidence

### Jayson M. Nissen 2018

Jayson M. Nissen, Manher Jariwala, Xochith Herrera, Eleanor W. Close, and Ben Van Dusen. (2018). Performance differences for in-class and online administration of low-stakes research-based assessments. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/perc.2017.pr.044](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.044)

`q2 · i?`

Posttest HLMs from the same experiment. The overall CBT intercept was -0.02 and the largest predicted effect, for E&M students (2.2 points higher for CBT against an overall predicted gain of 11.6 points), bordered on meaningful but was not reliable, with large 95% CI overlap.

> "For algebra-based mechanics students there was effectively no difference, -0.21. For calc-based mechanics students there was a small negative difference, -1.19. For the E&M students there was a small positive difference, 2.10. None of these values were statistically reliable (p»0.05)."

`q2 · i?`

A between-groups experiment at a medium-sized US regional university randomly assigned 1,310 students in 25 sections of three introductory physics courses to CBT or PPT conditions. The article concludes there was "no mean- ingful difference in test scores on low-stakes RBAs" between the two modes. Null equivalence was not directly tested; inference rests on non-significant HLM contrasts.

> "This evidence led us to conclude that there was no mean- ingful difference in test scores on low-stakes RBAs between students who completed the exam in class using paper and pencil and those who completed the exam outside of class using the online LASSO platform."

### Nissen 2018

Nissen, J. M., Jariwala, M., Close, E. W., & Van Dusen, B. (2018). Participation and Performance on Paper- and Computer-Based Low-Stakes Assessments. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1186/s40594-018-0117-4](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40594-018-0117-4)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

HLM analysis of scores from the study's between-groups experimental design with 1,310 students in 25 sections of three introductory physics courses over two semesters. Adding assessment condition to the models did not reduce total variance, and "diﬀerences were not statistically signiﬁcant".

> "Model 2 for both CI and AS indicated that the diﬀerences in scores between PPTs and CBTs were very small and that these diﬀerences were not statistically signiﬁcant."

### Nissen 2018 (2)

Nissen, J. M., Jariwala, M., Close, E. W., & Van Dusen, B. (2018). Participation and Performance on Paper- and Computer-Based Low-Stakes Assessments. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1186/s40594-018-0117-4](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40594-018-0117-4)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Model 3 disaggregated by the three courses and plotted via the HLM hypothesis-testing function (Fig. 5): seven cases had PPT higher, five had CBT higher, differences ranged from -2.1% to 2.2%, and "neither assessment condition was consistently higher than the other".

> "All of the diﬀerences between CBTs and PPTs were small, none of the diﬀerences were statistically signiﬁcant, and neither assessment condition was consistently higher than the other."
