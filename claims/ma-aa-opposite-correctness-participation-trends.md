## Subclaims
`q2 i1` In MA interactions, participation of correct students grows relative to incorrect students as the percentage correct drops; in AA interactions the opposite occurs, with correct students' participation growing as percentage correct rises. [→ Joss Ives 2016](#joss-ives-2016)

## Evidence

### Joss Ives 2016

Joss Ives, Matias de Jong Van Lier, Nutifafa Kwaku Sumah, and Jared B. Stang. (2016). Examining Student Participation in Two-Phase Collaborative Exams through Video Analysis. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.038](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.038)

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r1`

Figure 3 analysis of groups of 4 comparing correct vs incorrect students' participation by percentage correct. For AA at 50% correct, means were 2.11 (correct) vs 1.76 (incorrect), p = .015, "an effect size of d = .28".

> "When looking at cases where both correct and incorrect people are present in the group (percentage correct from 25% to 75% in Figure 3), we see that the trends are the opposite for MA and AA interactions."
