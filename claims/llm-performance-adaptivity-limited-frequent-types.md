# LLM feedback adaptivity to student performance occurs only in frequently produced focus types and only in large or closed models

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The teacher's feedback differs significantly between high- and low-performing students in Task Constraints and Praise, while models that show performance-based adaptivity do so only in the focus type they produce most frequently, and only large or closed models show any such adaptivity. [→ Norah Almousa 2026](#norah-almousa-2026)

## Evidence

### Norah Almousa 2026

Norah Almousa, Shayan Peyghambari Oskoui, Raquel Coelho, Gayle Rogers, Xiang Lorraine Li, Diane Litman. (2026). Evaluating Feedback Focus and Pedagogical Adaptivity in LLM-Generated Feedback on Student Writing. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28026 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.28026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2609.28026)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Fisher's Exact Test comparison of feedback focus type distributions between high- and low-performing students (median grade split) in the composition course under zero-shot prompting. The study reports that "all models that show adaptivity based on student performance are large or closed models."

> "the teacher's feedback shows significant differences between high- and low-performing students in Task Constraints and Praise. Gemini and Mistral-24B show significant differences in Clarification, LearnLM in Mistakes, and Qwen-70B in Elaboration, while Llama-7B and Mistral-7B show no significant differences across any feedback focus type."
