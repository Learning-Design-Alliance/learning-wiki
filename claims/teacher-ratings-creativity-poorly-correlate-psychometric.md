# Teachers' ratings of children's creativity correlate poorly with psychometric performance, and teachers tend to prefer appropriate answers over novel ones

## Evidence

### Bruce S. Rawlings & Sarah J. Cutting 2024

Bruce S. Rawlings & Sarah J. Cutting. (2024). Linking Disparate Strands: A Critical Review of the Relationship Between Creativity and Education. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09973-z

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing multiple studies, that "teachers' ratings are typically weakly correlated with children's performance on psychometric assays of creativity", self-assessments, and parent and peer ratings.

> "teachers' ratings are typically weakly correlated with children's performance on psychometric assays of creativity (Gralewski & Karwowski, 2019; Hoff, 2011), self-assessments (Beghetto et al., 2011), parent and peer ratings (Chan, 2000; Lau & Li, 1996)."

### Bruce S. Rawlings & Sarah J. Cutting 2024 (2)

Bruce S. Rawlings & Sarah J. Cutting. (2024). Linking Disparate Strands: A Critical Review of the Relationship Between Creativity and Education. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09973-z

`q1 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing prior studies, that "teachers ignore creative questions by students" and "prefer relevant answers over novel ones", and that fictitious creative children were rated as disruptive.

> "teachers ignore creative questions by students (Beghetto, 2007; Scott, 1999), prefer relevant answers over novel ones as well as obedience to authority, and report the latter as impulsive and disruptive (Beghetto, 2007; Cropley, 1996)."
