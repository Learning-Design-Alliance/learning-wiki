# Higher overall undergraduate GPA is associated with lower hazards of first academic probation in PA students (aHR = 0.16, 95% CI 0.08–0.33).

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a multivariable Cox proportional hazards model of 605 PA students, higher overall undergraduate GPA was associated with significantly lower hazards of first academic probation (aHR = 0.16, 95% CI 0.08–0.33). [→ Waynick 2026](#waynick-2026)

## Evidence

### Waynick 2026

Waynick, J., Hussaini, S. S., Stonerook, E., Hiatt, T., Sisson, C., Peacock, B., O'Neal, S., McKinnond, A., Steele, L., & Gillette, C. (2026). Using academic admissions data to predict PA training attrition and academic difficulties: a survival analysis approach. Medical Education Online, 31(1). https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2026.2745370

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Retrospective cohort analysis of 605 PA students (cohorts 2021–2027) at one academic medical center using semi-parametric multivariable Cox proportional hazards models; the abstract reports the adjusted hazard ratio for overall GPA of "aHR = 0.16, 95% CI = 0.08–0.33" for time to first probation.

> "Overall GPA (aHR = 0.16, 95% CI = 0.08–0.33), verbal GRE scores (aHR = 0.91, 95% CI = 0.87–0.94), having a science major in undergraduate studies (aHR = 0.43, 95% CI = 0.26–0.69) and number of undergraduate course withdrawals (aHR = 1.14, 95% CI = 1.05–1.24) were associated with time to first probation."
