# Pre-post gains show a significant gender difference favoring men in the VR treatment only, not in the video or image treatments

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Men's pre-post gains were significantly larger than women's in the VR treatment group, while no significant gender difference in gains appeared for the video or image treatments. [→ J. R. Smith 2018](#j-r-smith-2018)

## Evidence

### J. R. Smith 2018

J. R. Smith, A. Byrum, T. M. McCormick, N. Young, C. Orban, and C. D. Porter. (2018). A controlled study of stereoscopic virtual reality in freshman electrostatics. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/perc.2017.pr.089](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.089)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Gender analysis of pre-post gains in the randomized three-treatment electric field study (Table II reports a gains-vs-gender correlation of 0.25, p = 0.01 for VR). The authors connect this to prior findings on gender differences in spatial abilities across cultures.

> "there were signiﬁcant differences between pre-post gains for men compared to women in the case of the VR treatment, but not for the video or image treatment, with men's gains being larger than women's gains in the VR treatment group."
