# Computer-based simulation tasks assess problem-solving, reasoning, and evaluation skills at scale

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Computer-based simulation tasks, used prominently in medical, architecture, and accountancy licensure examinations and in NAEP science items, assess problem-solving, reasoning, and evaluation skills and can be scored by computer as well as by humans. [→ Darling-Hammond 2017](#darling-hammond-2017)

## Evidence

### Darling-Hammond 2017

Darling-Hammond, L. (2017). Developing and Measuring Higher Order Skills: Models for State Performance Assessment Systems. Council of Chief State School Officers. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED606777

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The paper describes computer-based case simulations measuring physicians' patient-management skills, in which the patient's condition changes in real time and performance is scored for the appropriateness of the sequence of actions, plus NAEP 8th-grade science simulation tasks requiring internet research and experiment design. No effect sizes are printed.

> "These simulation tasks assess problem-solving, reasoning, and evaluation skills valued within the scientific discipline, providing new possibilities for evaluating student cognition and learning. They, too, can use computer-based scoring as well as human scoring."
