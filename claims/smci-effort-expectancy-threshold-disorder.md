# Effort Expectancy scale response thresholds failed to increase monotonically until the neutral category was collapsed with an adjacent category

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the Effort Expectancy scale, the threshold for the neutral response category (Neither Agree or Disagree) exceeded that of Somewhat Agree, indicating higher response categories do not necessarily correspond to higher ability level; rescoring by collapsing the neutral category restored monotonically increasing thresholds. [→ Alber JM 2015](#alber-jm-2015)

## Evidence

### Alber JM 2015

Alber JM, Bernhardt JM, Stellefson M, Weiler RM, Anderson-Lewis C, Miller MD, MacInnes J. (2015). Designing and Testing an Inventory for Measuring Social Media Competency of Certified Health Education Specialists. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.4943

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Rasch rating scale model analysis of field test data (n=353) found the Effort Expectancy thresholds 'did not increase in order,' with printed values ranging from -2.76 to 2.33. Follow-up rescoring (01123 and 01223 sequences) both yielded monotonically increasing thresholds.

> "While the threshold values for the Effort Expectancy scale ranged from -2.76 to 2.33, the values did not increase in order. The original categories were 0 (Strongly Disagree), 1 (Somewhat Disagree), 2 (Neither Agree or Disagree), 3 (Somewhat Agree), and 4 (Strongly Agree)."
