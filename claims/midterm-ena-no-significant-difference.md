# High- and low-performing students did not differ significantly on the midterm-exam ENA network dimensions despite detected network differences

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Two-sample t-tests on the midterm ENA network dimensions found no statistically significant difference between high- and low-performing students (X: d = −0.388; Y: d = 0.412). [→ Raković 2023](#rakovic-2023)

## Evidence

### Raković 2023

Raković, M., Ahmad Uzir, N., Matcha, W., Eagan, B., Jovanović, J., Shaffer, D. W., Pardo, A., & Gašević, D. (2023). Network Analytics to Unveil Links of Learning Strategies, Time Management, and Academic Performance in a Flipped Classroom. Journal of Learning Analytics, 10(3), 64–86. https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2023.7843

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Two-sample t-tests with Bonferroni correction comparing ENA networks of students scoring above the 90th percentile (Nabove90th = 23) and below the 25th percentile (Nbelow25th = 63) on the midterm exam. The article reports "no significant difference was observed between these two mean networks" on either dimension, though the printed Cohen's d values are moderate in size.

> "However, no significant difference was observed between these two mean networks on the network dimensions ( X and Y ) shown in Figure 8 (X dimension, t =−1.766, p = 0.083, Cohen's d =−0.388; Y dimension t = 1.577, p = 0.123, Cohen's d = 0.412)."
