In its synthesis of 12 peer-reviewed studies on teachers aged 50 and above, the review reports that tailored interventions addressing late-career teachers' specific needs are notably absent from the literature.

Related Claims¶

  • The literature defines late-career teachers inconsistently, by age, experience or perceived expertise, with no coherent conceptualisation — related
  • Professional learning for late-career teachers is operationalised in varied ways, from formal structured programmes to informal workplace-embedded practices — related
  • Late-career teachers prefer socially embedded and collaborative learning opportunities such as mentoring, dialogue and reflective practice — related
  • Mentoring of late-career teachers is typically treated as a one-way relationship in which only the mentee learns, not a shared learning experience — related
