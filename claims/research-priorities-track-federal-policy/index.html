Quesen 2026¶

Quesen, S. (2026). What educational research looked like when scores were rising: A comparative analysis of AERA conference programs, 2005–2014 and 2022–2025. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED681368

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Cross-era frequency analysis shows the term was "virtually absent during the earlier era, with counts of zero in five of those 10 years"; the author argues policy infrastructure creates the conditions for research by producing the data researchers study.

"Chronic absenteeism became a required federal reporting metric under ESSA in 2015, meaning the construct was not systematically measured during most of the earlier period."

Quesen 2026 (2)¶

Quesen, S. (2026). What educational research looked like when scores were rising: A comparative analysis of AERA conference programs, 2005–2014 and 2022–2025. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED681368

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Timing analysis in the discussion argues Marshall's implied causal story fails chronologically; the author offers more plausible explanations including the shift from federal to state accountability, Common Core implementation disruption, post-2008 budget constraints, and pandemic learning loss.

"NAEP scores began declining around 2015, the same year ESSA passed and federal accountability mandates ended. The large equity research increase occurred after 2020, 5 years after score declines began."
