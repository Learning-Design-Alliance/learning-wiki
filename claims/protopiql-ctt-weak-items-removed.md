## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith 2020

Trevor I. Smith, Philip Eaton, Suzanne White Brahmia, Alexis Olsho, Charlotte Zimmerman, Andrew Boudreaux. (2020). Toward a valid instrument for measuring physics quantitative literacy. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.smith_t](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.smith_t)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

CTT difficulty and discrimination distributions for each PIQL version (Fig. 1) informed item decisions. The protoPIQL's weak parameters led the authors to keep only nine of its items and add 11 items in v1.0 based on prior research on the three PQL facets.

> "Five of the items in the protoPIQL were considered too easy (difﬁculty above 0.8), and three items had discrimination values below 0.3; moreover, there was a gap in the middle of the difﬁculty distribution with only one item having a difﬁculty in the range between 0.3 and 0.55."
