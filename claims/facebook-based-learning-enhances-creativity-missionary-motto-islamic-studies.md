## Evidence

### Alias et al. 2013

Norlidah Alias, Saedah Siraj, Mohd Khairul Azman Md Daud & Zaharah Hussin. (2013). Effectiveness of Facebook Based Learning to Enhance Creativity Among Islamic Studies Students by Employing Isman Instructional Design Model. TOJET: The Turkish Online Journal of Educational Technology, 12(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1008868

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

A 14-week comparison in Malaysian secondary schools that the authors call experimental, but whose groups were not randomly assigned: the treatment group (n = 40) was "randomly selected among students, while the control group of 40 participants was drawn from existing classes", so it is a nonrandomised, quasi-experimental comparison. The treatment group learned through Facebook-based learning and the control group through traditional classroom texts; pretest creativity did not differ significantly (19.84 vs 21.16). On the post-test for creativity in producing a missionary motto, the treatment group scored higher (25.98 vs 21.05, t(78) = 2.19, p = .032), a mean difference of 4.93. No effect size is reported.

> "Table 4 shows that post test achievement mean score for creativity in producing missionary motto for the control group (n = 40) is 21.05 (SD = 11.52), while that for the treatment group (n = 40) indicates higher achievement i.e 25.98 (SD = 8.39)."
