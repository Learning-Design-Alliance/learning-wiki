## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The CBL group scored significantly higher on the final exam (p=0.03) and had significantly more A grades (p<0.001). [→ Kulak 2015](#kulak-2015)

## Evidence

### Kulak 2015

Kulak, V., & Newton, G. (2015). An Investigation of the Pedagogical Impact of Using Case-based Learning in a Undergraduate Biochemistry Course. International Journal of Higher Education, 4(4), 13. https://doi.org/10.5430/ijhe.v4n4p13

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Final exam comparison (worth 40% of the final grade, ~95% same questions) between the non-CBL (n=86) and CBL (n=103) groups. The article reports significant differences in overall grade and grade distribution with no printed effect size.

> "The CBL group performed significantly better on the final exam than the non-CBL group (p=0.03) (Figure 4). There was also a significant difference (p<0.001) between the two groups in the distribution of grades, with more A’s in the CBL group"
