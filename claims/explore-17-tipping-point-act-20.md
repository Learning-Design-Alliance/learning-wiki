# A ninth-grade EXPLORE composite score of 17 is the tipping point for reaching an ACT score of 20, and can serve as an interim benchmark

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students scoring 17 on ninth-grade EXPLORE have a greater than 50 percent chance of reaching 20 on the ACT; virtually none below 15 do so. [→ John Q. Easton 2008](#john-q-easton-2008)

## Evidence

### John Q. Easton 2008

John Q. Easton, Stephen Ponisciak, Stuart Luppescu. (2008). From High School to the Future: The Pathway to 20. Research Report, Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/high-school-future-pathway-20

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of 42,456 matched CPS students' EXPLORE and ACT scores across 2005-2007 junior cohorts shows the score-to-probability curve is "very steep in the middle," rising from under 1 percent at low scores to 55 percent at 17 and higher thereafter.

> "An EXPLORE score of 17 is the "tipping point," where students have greater than a 50 percent chance of reaching 20 on the ACT. Virtually all students with high EXPLORE scores (18 and above) make it to 20 on the ACT."
