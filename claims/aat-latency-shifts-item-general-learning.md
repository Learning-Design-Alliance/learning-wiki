# In the alphabet arithmetic task, participants with detected latency shifts show item-general learning, contrary to the instance-model prediction

> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants with detected latency shifts showed more item-general and less item-specific learning than participants without shifts in both complexity conditions, and rereached their last-practice latency level by the second transfer block. [→ Haider and Frensch 2002](#haider-and-frensch-2002)

## Evidence

### Haider and Frensch 2002

Hilde Haider & Peter A. Frensch. (2002). Why Aggregated Learning Follows the Power Law of Practice When Individual Learning Does Not: Comment on Rickard (1997, 1999), Delaney et al. (1998), and Palmeri (1999). Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.28.2.392

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment with 39 participants trained on an alphabet arithmetic task under high- or low-complexity conditions, with shift detection based on fast-trial criteria (latencies faster than 1,100 ms); 9 of 19 high-complexity and 10 of 20 low-complexity participants produced shifts. Figure 4 difference scores show "participants with detected latency shifts show more item– general learning ... than do participants with no detected shift in both conditions".

> "participants with detected latency shifts show more item– general learning (i.e., difference between the first practice block and the first transfer block) and less item–specific learning (i.e., difference between the last practice block and the first transfer block) than do participants with no detected shift in both conditions."
