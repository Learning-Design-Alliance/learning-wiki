## Evidence

### Knorr Mirjana 2024

Knorr Mirjana, Mielke Ina, Amelung Dorothee, Safari Mahla, Gröne Oana R., Breil Simon M., MacIntosh Alexander. (2024). Measuring personal characteristics in applicants to German medical schools: Piloting an online Situational Judgement Test with an open-ended response format. GMS Journal for Medical Education. https://www.egms.de/en/journals/zma/2024-41/zma001685.shtml

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Welch t-tests on the overall pilot sample comparing mean Casper performance by gender and migration background; effect sizes reported as Cohen's d. The female advantage is d=0.37, described as "in line with a North-American study on Casper".

> "Single group comparisons revealed that female participants (t(107.16)=2.73, p=0.01, d=0.37) and participants without a migration background (t(263.09)=3.65, p<.001, d=0.40) showed a better mean Casper performance compared to male participants and participants with a migration background, respectively."

### Knorr Mirjana 2024 (2)

Knorr Mirjana, Mielke Ina, Amelung Dorothee, Safari Mahla, Gröne Oana R., Breil Simon M., MacIntosh Alexander. (2024). Measuring personal characteristics in applicants to German medical schools: Piloting an online Situational Judgement Test with an open-ended response format. GMS Journal for Medical Education. https://www.egms.de/en/journals/zma/2024-41/zma001685.shtml

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Follow-up regression analyses (Table 4) with Casper performance as outcome showed adding native language as predictor explained the migration-background effect; gender and language remained significant when controlling for cognitive criteria. The migration-background difference itself was d=0.40.

> "Our follow-up analyses suggest that native language rather than migration background was related to performance differences which diverges from findings in a U.S. study where differences in ethnicity remained when controlled for language use"

### Knorr Mirjana 2024 (3)

Knorr Mirjana, Mielke Ina, Amelung Dorothee, Safari Mahla, Gröne Oana R., Breil Simon M., MacIntosh Alexander. (2024). Measuring personal characteristics in applicants to German medical schools: Piloting an online Situational Judgement Test with an open-ended response format. GMS Journal for Medical Education. https://www.egms.de/en/journals/zma/2024-41/zma001685.shtml

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Welch t-test comparing Casper performance by parental academic degree as the socio-economic status indicator in the pilot sample. No significant difference was detected; equivalence was not tested.

> "Casper performance did not significantly differ depending on parents' level of education (t(203.67)=1.30, p=0.19, d=0.15)."
