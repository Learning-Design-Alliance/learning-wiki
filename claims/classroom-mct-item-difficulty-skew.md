## Evidence

### Slepkov 2021

Slepkov, A. D., Van Bussel, M. L., Fitze, K. M., & Burr, W. S. (2021). A baseline for multiple-choice testing in the university classroom. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1177/21582440211016838](https://doi.org/10.1177/21582440211016838)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Distribution of item difficulty across 11,246 items from 182 classroom tests (Figure 2), showing that despite the mean falling in the optimal range, the distribution is heavily skewed towards easy items.

> "Across all items, our surveyed mean item difficulty is 𝑝̅ = 0.64 ± 0.22, which is indeed in the optimal range. However, as shown in Figure 2, the distribution of item difficulty in classroom tests is such that it spans the entire range from 0 to 1 and is skewed towards more easy items of p > 0.6."

### Slepkov 2021 (2)

Slepkov, A. D., Van Bussel, M. L., Fitze, K. M., & Burr, W. S. (2021). A baseline for multiple-choice testing in the university classroom. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1177/21582440211016838](https://doi.org/10.1177/21582440211016838)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Prevalence of extremely easy items in the corpus, contrasting with validated standardized tests that avoid such items; the authors find "this practice does not prove particularly detrimental" given that easy questions maintain adequate discrimination.

> "items for which over 90% of students chose the correct response comprise nearly 15% of all test items."
