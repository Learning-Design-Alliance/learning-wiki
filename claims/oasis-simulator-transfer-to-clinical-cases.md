## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Between 3 and 6 months after training, 7 participants witnessed severe lacerations and reported the simulation was useful for surgical correction, which the authors interpret as Kirkpatrick level 3 behavioral change. [→ Knobel Roxana 2018](#knobel-roxana-2018)

## Evidence

### Knobel Roxana 2018

Knobel Roxana, Volpato Lia Karina, Gervasi Liliam Cristini, Viergutz Raquel de Almeida, Trapani Alberto Júnior. (2018). A Simple, Reproducible and Low-cost Simulator for Teaching Surgical Techniques to Repair Obstetric Anal Sphincter Injuries. RBGO Gynecology & Obstetrics. https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0038-1668527

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Follow-up questionnaire 3–6 months after the simulation: four residents and three of four experts attended severe lacerations and reported feeling calmer and more confident. The authors call this "level 3 on the Kirkpartick scale". Self-reported, no effect size.

> "Four resident doctors attended cases of severe perineal rupture after participating in the simulation and considered that the training helped them remain calm and know how to proceed, in addition to having improved their surgical skills. Of the four experts, three attended serious lacerations after participating in the simulation and also considered that they were more confident and calmer when performing the procedure."
