## Evidence

### Khorsand Derek D 2018

Khorsand Derek D, Khwaja A, Schmale GA. (2018). Early musculoskeletal classroom education confers little advantage to medical student knowledge and competency in the absence of clinical experiences: a retrospective comparison study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1157-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

A short 2-3 day required second-year rheumatology course including classroom and clinical experiences preceded the improvement on the arthritis question ("p < 0.001"); the comparison is across survey periods.

> "The addition of a two-day rheumatology-teaching event during the second year of classroom instruction was followed by a significant improvement in knowledge of arthritis. Performance on the survey’s arthritis-based question improved from 72% of students in all grades answering correctly to 91% answering correctly (p < 0.001)"
