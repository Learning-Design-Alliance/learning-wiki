## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Explanatory feedback, which explains the nature of correct and incorrect answers, benefits students more than corrective feedback that only indicates correctness. [→ Rodríguez Rincón 2025](#rodriguez-rincon-2025)

## Evidence

### Rodríguez Rincón 2025

Rodríguez Rincón, Y., Munárriz, A., Campión Arrastia, M. J., & Goicoechea López-Vailo, M. I. (2025). Instructional design for tutoring on interactive platforms: creating educational interventions overcoming the digital gap. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10516-8

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

The article reports, citing Hattie and Timperley (2007), that explanatory feedback has greater benefit than corrective feedback, and adds that Belland et al. (2017) and Van der Kleij et al. (2015) ratify that explaining both correct and incorrect answers has a superior impact on learning outcomes.

> "Supporting this approach, Hattie and Timperley (2007) conclude that the explanatory strategy has a greater benefit for students than the corrective one."
