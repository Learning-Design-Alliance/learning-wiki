## Evidence

### Schneider 2013

Schneider, M., & Hardy, I. (2013). Profiles of Inconsistent Knowledge in Children's Pathways of Conceptual Change. Developmental Psychology, 49(9). https://doi.org/10.1037/a0030976

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of pathway frequencies across the three experimental conditions (high support, low support, baseline) in the 161-child sample; the overall relation was significant, χ²(14) = 39.744, p < .001. The reversed pattern held for the increasing-fragmentation path (24% low vs. 14% high support).

> "On P2, the decreasing-fragmentation path, 30% of the children were from the group with high instructional support, but only 2% of the children were from the group with low instructional support."

### Schneider 2013 (2)

Schneider, M., & Hardy, I. (2013). Profiles of Inconsistent Knowledge in Children's Pathways of Conceptual Change. Developmental Psychology, 49(9). https://doi.org/10.1037/a0030976

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pathway-by-condition analysis showed reaching the scientific profile occurred only via two paths, both exclusive to instructed children. The authors read this as demonstrating "the general importance of extensive instruction for reaching a scientific understanding in a domain."

> "Only two paths (i.e., the ideal-learning path and the dynamic-learning path) led to the profile of scientific understanding, and only children from the two instructional groups, but no child from the baseline group, were found on these learning paths."
