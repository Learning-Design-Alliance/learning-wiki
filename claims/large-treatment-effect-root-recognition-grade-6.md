## Evidence

### Amy C. Crosson & Margaret G. McKeown 2016

Amy C. Crosson & Margaret G. McKeown. (2016). Middle School Learners' Use of Latin Roots to Infer the Meaning of Unfamiliar Words. Cognition and Instruction, 34(2), 148-171. https://doi.org/10.1080/07370008.2016.1145121

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of 17 intervention and 12 control sixth graders on the dynamic assessment recognition dimension; the article reports F(1,27) = 43.29, p < .000, "alargetreatmenteffect(partial η2 =.616)" — a large effect. Assumptions of heterogeneity of variance and normality were met.

> "For sixth grade, a one-way ANOVA on therecognition dimension revealed a significant difference betweengroups,[ F(1,27) =43.29,p <.000]demonstratingalargetreatmenteffect(partial η2 =.616)."

### McKeown 2018

McKeown, M. G., Crosson, A. C., Moore, D. W., & Beck, I. L. (2018). Word Knowledge and Comprehension Effects of an Academic Vocabulary Intervention for Middle School Students. American Educational Research Journal. https://doi.org/10.3102/0002831217744181

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Dynamic assessment of morphological analysis administered individually to 29 students (17 RAVE, 12 control) sampled across GMRT quartiles. The article reports partial η² = 0.61 for recognition, characterizing effects as small for meaning and comprehension and large for recognition in this small sample.

> "RAVE students scored significantly higher than the control group on the recognition, F(1, 113.43) = 41.41, p \ .001, partial h2 = 0.61; meaning, F(1, 28.61) = 6.02, p = .02, partial h2 = 0.19; and comprehension dimensions, F(1, 39.82) = 7.80, p = .01, partial h2 = 0.23"
