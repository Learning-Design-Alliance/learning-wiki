Means 2009¶

Means, Toyama, Murphy, Bakia, & Jones. (2009). Evaluation of Evidence-Based Practices in Online Learning: A Meta-Analysis and Review of Online Learning Studies. U.S. Department of Education. https://www.ed.gov/about/offices/list/opepd/ppss/reports.html

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Learner-type moderator analysis across the 51 contrasts; the K–12 subset comprised seven contrasts from five studies. Of the K–12 contrasts, three favored blended conditions significantly, one favored face-to-face significantly, and three did not attain significance.

"Online learning appeared to be an effective option for both undergraduates (mean effect of +0.35, p < .001) and for graduate students and professionals (+0.17, p < .05) in a wide range of academic and professional studies. Though positive, the mean effect size is not significant for the seven contrasts involving K–12 students"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Of 13 online learning practice variables examined, only use of a blended approach and expanded time on task significantly influenced effectiveness; the other 11 did not. — related
  • A systematic search found almost no rigorous studies contrasting online and face-to-face instruction for K–12 students: none with computable effect sizes through 2006, and only five meeting meta-analysis criteria through July 2008. — related
  • In the narrative synthesis, blended and purely online conditions within a single study generally produced similar learning outcomes, and media elements such as video or online quizzes did not appear to influence learning. — related
