# Post-hoc subgroup analysis suggests task-relevant active math improves math scores while extra physical education alone does not, and combining both is most beneficial

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Compared with controls, extra PE alone showed no significant difference in change in math score, active math improved scores significantly, and active math plus extra PE showed the largest improvement. [→ Have Mona 2018](#have-mona-2018)

## Evidence

### Have Mona 2018

Have Mona, Nielsen Jacob Have, Ernst Martin Thomsen, Gejl Anne Kaer, Fredens Kjeld, Grøntved Anders, Kristensen Peter Lund. (2018). Classroom-based physical activity improves children's math achievement – A randomized controlled trial. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0208787

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc four-subgroup analysis (control n = 100, extra PE n = 111, active math n = 132, active math + extra PE n = 162) comparing general versus task-relevant PA stimulus (Fig 3). "no significant difference in change in math score between the control group and group with extra physical education classes"; active math significant vs control.

> "Post-hoc analysis revealed no significant difference in change in math score between the control group and group with extra physical education classes (Fig 3). The active math intervention improved math score significantly more compared to the controls, and the addition of extra physical education classes to active math increased the positive effect even more."
