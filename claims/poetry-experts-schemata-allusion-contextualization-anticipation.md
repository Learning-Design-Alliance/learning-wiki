# In reading textually constrained period poetry, experts' organized domain knowledge (schemata) is the major factor differentiating them from novices, enabling allusion, contextualization and anticipation.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Experts rapidly alluded to other literary works, categorized both poems by genre and period, and explicitly anticipated content, while novices lacked the relevant schemata and mostly failed to grasp the extended metaphor. [→ Peskin 1998](#peskin-1998)

## Evidence

### Peskin 1998

Peskin, J. (1998). Constructing meaning when reading poetry: An expert-novice study. Cognition and Instruction, 16(3), 235-263. https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci1603_1

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Think-aloud expert-novice comparison (eight experts, eight novices) reading two period poems; protocol analysis coded for organized knowledge. The Results state that "the experts had a rich deposit of schemata" enabling allusion, contextualization and anticipation, while not one novice categorized the metaphysical poem.

> "the experts had a rich deposit of schemata which enabled them fairly early to allude to other literary texts, to place the poem in a general context or category constrained by certain expectations pertinent to the genre of poem, and then to anticipate the content."
