# Prompting students to draw a diagram makes them statistically more likely to draw expert-like diagrams than giving no instruction, while providing a pre-drawn diagram leaves many students using an unmodified basic diagram

> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students prompted to draw a diagram were statistically more likely to draw expert-like diagrams than students in the no-support group. [→ Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#alexandru-maries-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)
`q2 i?` Students given a pre-drawn diagram were statistically more likely than prompted students to use a diagram of only the two charges, i.e., they did not modify the provided diagram to make it expert-like. [→ Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)](#alexandru-maries-and-chandralekha-singh-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). To Use or Not to Use Diagrams: The Effect of Drawing a Diagram in Solving Introductory Physics Problems. Originally published 2010. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1601.05467](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1601.05467)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of intervention groups on recitation quiz problems in an algebra-based introductory physics course, contrasting the percentage of students who drew expert-like diagrams in the prompt-only group versus the no-support group. The article reports this difference as statistically significant.

> "Table 3 shows that students in PO are statistically more likely to draw expert-like diagrams than students in NS."

### Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). To Use or Not to Use Diagrams: The Effect of Drawing a Diagram in Solving Introductory Physics Problems. Originally published 2010. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1601.05467](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1601.05467)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same recitation-quiz intervention study, contrasting the diagram-only group with the prompt-only group on use of a two-charge-only diagram. The article reports the diagram-only group was statistically more likely to keep the unmodified basic diagram they had been given.

> "It also shows that students in DO are statistically more likely than students in PO to use a diagram of only the two charges."
