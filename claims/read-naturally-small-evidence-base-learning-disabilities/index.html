Of 43 studies reviewed for students with learning disabilities, only one randomized controlled trial (Chenault et al., 2006; 20 students in grades 4–6 at one parochial school) met WWC evidence standards; the remaining 42 did not meet standards or eligibility screens, so the extent of evidence is small for reading fluency and writing and no studies cover seven other domains. → Read Naturally® 2010

Read Naturally® 2010¶

Read Naturally®. (2010). WWC Intervention Report: Read Naturally®. What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. https://www.readnaturally.com

q3 · i? · review · r2

WWC synthesis of the literature search (documents publicly available by December 2009): 43 studies reviewed, one RCT met standards, 42 excluded for reasons such as no comparison group or samples with less than 50% students with learning disabilities. Appendix A5 lists one study, one school, and 20 students for each covered domain.

"The WWC considers the extent of evidence for Read Naturally ® to be small for students with learning disabilities in the reading fluency and writing domains. No studies that meet WWC evidence standards with or without reservations examined the effectiveness of Read Naturally ® on students with learning disabilities in the alphabetics, reading comprehension, general reading achievement, math, science, social studies, or progressing in school domains."
