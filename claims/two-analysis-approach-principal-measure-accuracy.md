# The study used two sets of analyses—rating stability and the stable component's link to future contributions—to simulate each measure's predictive accuracy

## Evidence

### Hanley Chiang 2016

Hanley Chiang, Moira McCullough, Stephen Lipscomb, Brian Gill. (2016). Performance Measures Based on Test Scores – How Well Do They Predict Principals' Impacts? (In Focus Brief). Cambridge, MA: Mathematica Policy Research. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/performance-measures-based-on-test-scores-issue-brief

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The brief describes its two sets of analyses: rating stability across years, and the relationship between "the stable part of each principal's rating" and future contributions. Results from both fed a simulation of each measure's accuracy for predicting the following year's contributions.

> "First, the study assessed the extent to which ratings from each measure are stable by examining the association between principals' ratings from earlier and later years. Second, the study examined the relationship between the stable part of each principal's rating and his or her contributions to student achievement in future years."
