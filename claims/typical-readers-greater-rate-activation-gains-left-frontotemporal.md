# Typical readers show significantly greater rate-related activation gains than dyslexic readers in left prefrontal and left superior temporal regions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` On the parametric Fast > Medium > Slow contrast, typical readers showed greater activation than dyslexic readers in left middle frontal gyrus, left superior temporal gyrus, and brainstem/cerebellar clusters, while dyslexic readers showed no greater activation than typical readers. ## Evidence

### Christodoulou JA 2014

Christodoulou JA, Del Tufo SN, Lymberis J, Saxler PK, Ghosh SS, et al. (2014) Brain Bases of Reading Fluency in Typical Reading and Impaired Fluency in Dyslexia. PLoS ONE 9(7): e100552. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0100552

`q3 · i?`

Whole-brain parametric modulation analysis (cluster-level FDR corrected, p<.01) compared groups on the Fast > Medium > Slow contrast. Typical readers showed greater activation in left middle frontal, left superior temporal, and brainstem/cerebellar clusters; "Readers with dyslexia did not show any activation greater than typical readers".

> "Compared to readers with dyslexia on the parametric analysis (Fast . Medium . Slow), typical readers showed greater activation in a cluster with a peak in left middle frontal gyrus that extended into the inferior frontal gyrus, insula, and precentral gyrus; a cluster with a peak in left superior temporal gyrus"
