## Subclaims
`q4 i?` Retained treatment and control teachers showed no statistically significant positive differences in college entrance exam scores, college selectivity, advanced degrees, first-year observation scores, or third-year student test scores. [→ Glazerman 2010](#glazerman-2010)

## Evidence

### Glazerman 2010

Glazerman, S., E. Isenberg, S. Dolfin, M. Bleeker, A. Johnson, M. Grider, and M. Jacobus. (2010). Impacts of Comprehensive Teacher Induction: Final Results From a Randomized Controlled Study (NCEE 2010-4027). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Analysis of district stayers after three years comparing treatment and control teachers on characteristics including College Board and ACT entrance exam scores, college selectivity, advanced degrees, first-year classroom observation scores, and third-year student test scores; no significant positive differences.

> "There were no statistically significant positive differences between treatment teachers retained in the district and control teachers retained in the district in teacher characteristics such as college entrance exam scores, college selectivity, and advanced degrees"
