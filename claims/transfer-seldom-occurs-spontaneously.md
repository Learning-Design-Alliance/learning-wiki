## Evidence

### PERKINS 1989

D. N. Perkins and Gavriel Salomon. (1989). Are Cognitive Skills Context-Bound? Educational Researcher, 16-23. https://www.jstor.org/stable/1176259

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports the early, large-scale Thorndike transfer experiments as the origin of the argument from transfer; the review attributes this to the cited studies without reprinting effect sizes.

> "E. L. Thorndike (e.g., 1923) and Thorndike and Woodworth (1901) reported experiments, some on a large scale, show- ing that training in such fields as Latin and math has no measurable influence on other cognitive functions"

### PERKINS 1989 (2)

D. N. Perkins and Gavriel Salomon. (1989). Are Cognitive Skills Context-Bound? Educational Researcher, 16-23. https://www.jstor.org/stable/1176259

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's own synthesis of the negative transfer literature, including logical-problem isomorphs, literacy studies, strategy training, and programming-effects research; the review concludes the generalist case was more wishful thinking than empirical evidence.

> "To the extent that transfer does take place, it is highly specific and must be cued, primed, and guided; it seldom occurs spontaneously."
