# Achievement in mathematics and German shows positive within-domain effects and smaller negative marginal cross-domain effects on academic self-concept

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a nationally representative German ninth-grade sample, achievement in each domain positively predicted same-domain self-concept and negatively predicted self-concept in the other domain, for grades and for test scores. [→ Jansen 2015](#jansen-2015)
`q2 i?` Using test scores as the achievement indicator, the same pattern of positive within-subject and negative between-subject effects emerged. [→ Jansen 2015 (2)](#jansen-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Jansen 2015

Jansen, M., Schroeders, U., Lüdtke, O., & Marsh, H. W. (2015). Contrast and Assimilation Effects of Dimensional Comparisons in Five Subjects: An Extension of the I/E Model. Journal of Educational Psychology, 107(4), 1086–1101. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000021

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Structural equation models regressing self-concepts on grades in the 20,050-student representative sample replicated the I/E pattern: 'achievement in German showed a positive effect on self-concept in German' (printed path coefficient .63) and a smaller negative marginal effect on mathematics self-concept (printed as −.25).

> "achievement in German showed a positive effect on self-concept in German (␤ ⫽ .63; see also Table 3, Model 1) and a smaller negative marginal effect on self-concept in mathematics (␤⫽⫺.25)"

### Jansen 2015 (2)

Jansen, M., Schroeders, U., Lüdtke, O., & Marsh, H. W. (2015). Contrast and Assimilation Effects of Dimensional Comparisons in Five Subjects: An Extension of the I/E Model. Journal of Educational Psychology, 107(4), 1086–1101. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000021

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

In the same replication step with Rasch-scaled test scores as achievement indicators, 'a similar pattern of positive effects within subject and negative contrast effects between subjects emerged' in the structural equation models.

> "Using test scores as an achievement indicator, a similar pattern of positive effects within subject and negative contrast effects between subjects emerged."
