# Difficulty-index differences between active learning technique groups appeared only in units 2 and 5, with TLM showing a higher unit-2 difficulty index and jigsaw a higher unit-5 index than QC.

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` For unit 2, TLM students had a higher examination difficulty index than control, jigsaw, and QC (effect size 0.66); for unit 5, control had a higher difficulty index than CM and QC, and jigsaw higher than QC (effect size 0.64). [→ Bradley LJ 2025](#bradley-lj-2025)

## Evidence

### Bradley LJ 2025

Bradley LJ, Meyer KE, Kerr MS, Maddux SD, Heck AJ, Reeves RE, Handler EK. (2025). Do active learning techniques promote higher academic performance in an online graduate anatomy course? Anatomical Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.70066

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Difficulty-index analysis (percentage of learners answering correctly) of examination questions by group in the online anatomy course. Unit 2 showed significant differences (H = 9.6, p = 0.05, effect size = 0.66), with TLM higher than control, jigsaw, and QC; unit 5 showed differences (H = 10.0, p = 0.04, effect size = 0.25) with control higher than CM and QC and jigsaw higher than QC (effect size = 0.64).

> "However, for unit 2, a Kruskal–Wallis test indicated significant differences (H  =  9.6, p  =  0.05, effect size  =  0.66). Dunn's post hoc tests indicated that students in TLM had a higher difficulty index than the control, jigsaw, and QC."
