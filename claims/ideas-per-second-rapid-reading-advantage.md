## Evidence

### Gilliland 1922

Gilliland, A. R. (1922). The effect of rate of silent reading on ability to recall. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1922-02093-001

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Efficiency analysis of the same rate-of-reading experiment: comprehension score divided by reading time in seconds per hundred words gave ideas gained per second (Table III). The author reports the printed percentages favoring rapid reading for all four groups.

> "There were 20 per cent fewer ideas gained per second in slow reading than in reading at the normal rate and 26 per cent more ideas gained per second in rapid reading than in reading at the normal rate. This superiority of rapid reading is shown by each of the four groups of subjects tested."
