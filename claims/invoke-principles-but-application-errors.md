# With the solved analog provided, students typically invoke the correct principles but often fail to apply them correctly, e.g., using the same centripetal-acceleration sign as in the analog

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students given the solved problem usually identified the correct principles for the quiz problem but some applied them incorrectly, e.g., writing T=mg-mv2/r with an incorrect sign for the centripetal acceleration. [→ Lin 2016](#lin-2016)

## Evidence

### Lin 2016

Lin, S.-Y. and Singh, C. (2016). Using Analogies to Learn Introductory Physics. Department of Physics and Astronomy, University of Pittsburgh. Originally published 2008. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06388](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06388)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Error analysis of students' quiz work across both courses. The article reports the sign error "T=mg-mv2/r" arose from copying the analog's equation; it was "rarely observed if students were not provided any scaffolding" and more common in the algebra-based course.

> "Some students failed to differentiate between these two cases; they used the same equation that was used for the normal force in the solved problem to solve for the maximum tension in the tire swing problem and arrived at an expression that has an incorrect sign for the centripetal acceleration: T=mg-mv2/r."
