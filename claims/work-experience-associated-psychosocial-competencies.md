## Evidence

### Senf B  2019

Senf B , Fettel J , Demmerle C , Maiwurm P . (2019). Physicians' attitudes towards psycho‐oncology, perceived barriers, and psychosocial competencies: Indicators of successful implementation of adjunctive psycho‐oncological care? Psycho‐Oncology. https://doi.org/10.1002/pon.4962

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis of demographic variables in the survey of 102 physicians, using Spearman's ρ. The article prints the effect size ρ = 0.430 for general work experience with psychosocial education and knowledge, and ρ = 0.373 for cancer-related experience, both P < 0.001.

> "We found associations of physicians' general work experience and their specific cancer‐related work experience with psychosocial education and knowledge (ρ (96) = 0.430, P < 0.001 and ρ (95) = 0.373, P < 0.001, respectively)"

### Senf B  2019 (2)

Senf B , Fettel J , Demmerle C , Maiwurm P . (2019). Physicians' attitudes towards psycho‐oncology, perceived barriers, and psychosocial competencies: Indicators of successful implementation of adjunctive psycho‐oncological care? Psycho‐Oncology. https://doi.org/10.1002/pon.4962

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Kruskal-Wallis group comparisons across general work-experience groups, followed by Bonferroni-corrected Mann-Whitney U tests (Table 4). Only the lowest and highest experience groups differed significantly from one another. No effect sizes are printed.

> "The results of group differences in physicians' general work experience indicate that there are significant differences in terms of psychosocial education and knowledge (H (2) = 13.21, P = 0.001) as well as skills and abilities (H (2) = 9.99, P = 0.007)."
