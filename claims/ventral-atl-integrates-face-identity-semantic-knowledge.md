# The ventral anterior temporal lobe integrates facial identity with social and conceptual knowledge

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The ventral ATL face patch is up-regulated when faces carry conceptually unique, socially unique, or famous status, and novel objects trained with social versus non-social concepts activate distinct social-brain regions. [→ Collins 2014](#collins-2014)

## Evidence

### Collins 2014

Collins, Jessica A., & Olson, Ingrid R. (2014). Knowledge is Power: How Conceptual Knowledge Transforms Visual Cognition. Psychonomic bulletin & review. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-013-0564-3

`q2 · i?`

The review reports meta-analytic and empirical evidence (Von Der Heide, Skipper, & Olson) that the ventral ATL integrates complex perceptual face representations with socially important semantic knowledge, plus a training study showing social-concept associations with novel objects activate amygdala, temporal pole, and medial prefrontal cortex. No effect sizes printed.

> "This region is up-regulated when a face is accompanied by certain types of conceptual knowledge, such as knowledge that makes the face conceptually unique (e.g. “This person invented television”"
