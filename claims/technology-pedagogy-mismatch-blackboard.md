## Evidence

### David Dockterman 2018

David Dockterman. (2018). Insights from 200 + years of personalized learning. npj Science of Learning 3:15. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0033-x

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Historical analysis of nineteenth-century reports on chalkboard use. Reports from 1839 and 1842 complained that "Blackboards are not uncommon, but are but little resorted to by the teacher" and that teachers knew little how to use them; the author attributes this to mismatch with competency-based small-group instruction.

> "The blackboard didn ’t ﬁt the system, and the system, and the teachers in it, rejected it."

### David Dockterman 2018 (2)

David Dockterman. (2018). Insights from 200 + years of personalized learning. npj Science of Learning 3:15. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0033-x

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Historical analysis of the shift to age-graded schooling in the late nineteenth century. As age-grading made whole-group instruction the norm, the article reports the chalkboard became indispensable, paralleling its warning that digital technology for individualized learning may mismatch today's age-graded system.

> "A change in pedagogy followed the change in school organization. Within this context, the chalkboard did become an “indispensable” tool of instruc-tion."
