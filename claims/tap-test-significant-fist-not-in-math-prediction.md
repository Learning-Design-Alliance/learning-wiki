# EF measures jointly account for 43% of math variance, with inhibition (Tap Test) a significant unique predictor but cognitive flexibility (FIST) not significant in any model

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the final model, the FIST and Tap Test together accounted for 43% of variance in WIAT scores (adj. R2 = .433), with the Tap Test a significant predictor (std. = .270, p = .010) and the FIST not (std. = .189, p = .093). [→ Verdine 2014](#verdine-2014)
`q3 i?` The FIST was not a significant predictor in any of the three regression models (p = .912, p = .372, p = .093), contrary to the authors' expectations. [→ Verdine 2014 (2)](#verdine-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Verdine 2014

Verdine, B. N., Irwin, C. M., Golinkoff, R. M., & Hirsh-Pasek, K. (2014). Contributions of Executive Function and Spatial Skills to Preschool Mathematics Achievement. Journal of Experimental Child Psychology, 126, 37-51. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2014.02.012

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Step 1 of the final hierarchical regression (Model 3) in the 44-child longitudinal sample. The two EF measures jointly accounted for adj. R2 = .433, F(2, 41) = 17.41, p < .001; the Tap Test was significant and the FIST was not.

> "These variables significantly accounted for 43% of the variance in total WIAT raw scores (adj. R2 = .433, F(2, 41) =17.41, p = <.001). Again, the Tap Test (std. = .270, t = 2.70, p = .010) was a significant predictor in the overall model, but the FIST was still not (std. = .189, t = 1.72, p = .093)"

### Verdine 2014 (2)

Verdine, B. N., Irwin, C. M., Golinkoff, R. M., & Hirsh-Pasek, K. (2014). Contributions of Executive Function and Spatial Skills to Preschool Mathematics Achievement. Journal of Experimental Child Psychology, 126, 37-51. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2014.02.012

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Model 2 of the hierarchical regressions, in which the PPVT alone was entered at step 1. The FIST again failed to reach significance (std. = 0.105, t = 0.90, p = .372) while the Tap Test remained significant; the FIST was also non-significant in Model 1 (p = .912).

> "The Tap Test was a significant predictor in the overall model (std. = 0.235, t = 2.24, p = .031), but again the FIST was not (std. = 0.105, t = 0.90, p = .372)."
