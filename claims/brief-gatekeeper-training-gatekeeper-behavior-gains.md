## Evidence

### Teo Alan R. 2016

Teo Alan R., Andrea Sarah B., Sakakibara Rae, Motohara Satoko, Matthieu Monica M., Fetters Michael D. (2016). Brief gatekeeper training for suicide prevention in an ethnic minority population: a controlled intervention. BMC Psychiatry 16, 211. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-016-0924-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Controlled intervention with 106 community members (intervention n=85, control n=48); regression models compared pre-to-post scale-score change between groups. The adjusted mean difference in gatekeeper behavior change was "0.52 points; 95%CI = 0.28–0.75 points", favoring the intervention group.

> "In adjusted models, the mean improvement in gatekeeper behavior (0.52 points; 95%CI = 0.28–0.75 points) and self-efficacy (0.55 points; 95%CI = 0.32–0.79 points) scores was significantly greater in the intervention group than control group."

### Teo Alan R. 2016 (2)

Teo Alan R., Andrea Sarah B., Sakakibara Rae, Motohara Satoko, Matthieu Monica M., Fetters Michael D. (2016). Brief gatekeeper training for suicide prevention in an ethnic minority population: a controlled intervention. BMC Psychiatry 16, 211. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-016-0924-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Within-group pre-post comparisons on the five-point gatekeeper behavior scale (α = 0.63) showed intervention gains on all items, e.g. referring moved from 4.01 ± 1.12 to 4.76 ± 0.61 (p < 0.001), while "control participants showed no changes".

> "Intervention participants showed significant improvement in their likelihood of using overall gatekeeper behavior and all three specific behaviors (questioning, persuading, and referring), while control participants showed no changes."
