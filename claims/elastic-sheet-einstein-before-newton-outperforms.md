# Elastic-sheet-based Einstein-before-Newton teaching outperformed a Newtonian force-law variant with French 9th-graders

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` An elastic sheet approach realised as a pillow with compact spheres, teaching Einstein's concepts before Newton, led to better outcomes than a particular variant of teaching the Newtonian gravitational force law with French 9th-graders, as measured by diagnostic questions on adherence to models of attraction. [→ Markus Pössel 2018](#markus-possel-2018)
`q1 i?` A similar elastic-sheet approach was successfully evaluated for teaching general undergraduate students, but without a control group. [→ Markus Pössel 2018 (2)](#markus-possel-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Markus Pössel 2018

Markus Pössel. (2018). Relatively complicated? Using models to teach general relativity at different levels. Invited talk, DPG Spring Meeting 2017. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1812.11589](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1812.11589)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Baldy, a study with French 9th-graders in which diagnostic questions measured students' adherence to models of attraction of different degrees of realism; the "Einstein before Newton" approach led to better outcomes. No effect size is printed.

> "an elastic sheet approach, in this particular case realised by a pillow with compact spheres placed upon it, proved superior to a particular variant of teaching the Newtonian gravitational force law in French 9th-graders."

### Markus Pössel 2018 (2)

Markus Pössel. (2018). Relatively complicated? Using models to teach general relativity at different levels. Invited talk, DPG Spring Meeting 2017. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1812.11589](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1812.11589)

`q1 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Watkins, a successful evaluation of a similar approach with general undergraduate students that lacked a control group. In both cases the teaching did not address issues beyond Newtonian gravity.

> "A similar approach was suc- cessfully evaluated for teaching general undergraduate students, albeit without a control group (Watkins 2014)."
