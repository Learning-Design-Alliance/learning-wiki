## Subclaims

`q3 i?` Pooled analysis of trials comparing virtual patients with traditional education shows no clear knowledge advantage (SMD=0.11, 95% CI −0.17 to 0.39). [→ Kononowicz AA 2019](#kononowicz-aa-2019)

`q3 i?` Pooled analysis of randomized trials comparing virtual patients with traditional education shows no significant difference in knowledge outcomes (SMD=0.11, 95% CI −0.17 to 0.39). [→ Kononowicz AA 2019](#kononowicz-aa-2019)

## Evidence

### Kononowicz AA 2019

Kononowicz AA, Woodham LA, Edelbring S, Stathakarou N, Davies D, Saxena N, Tudor Car L, Carlstedt-Duke J, Car J, Zary N. (2019). Virtual Patient Simulations in Health Professions Education: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis by the Digital Health Education Collaboration. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/14676

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analysis (random-effects) of RCTs comparing virtual patients with traditional education on knowledge outcomes, pooled in Figure 2. The review reports "SMD=0.11, 95% CI −0.17 to 0.39, I2=74%, n=927", a small, non-significant difference; heterogeneity was high.

> "The pooled effect for knowledge outcomes (SMD=0.11, 95% CI −0.17 to 0.39, I2=74%, n=927; Figure 2) suggests that virtual patient interventions are as efficient as traditional education."
