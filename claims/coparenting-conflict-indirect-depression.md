# Co-parenting conflict is indirectly associated with lower adolescent academic performance through higher depression

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Co-parenting conflict positively predicts adolescent depression, which negatively predicts academic performance, yielding a significant indirect effect. [→ Yang Hexin 2022](#yang-hexin-2022)

## Evidence

### Yang Hexin 2022

Yang Hexin, Wu Chaoyue, Chen Ji-Kang. (2022). Interparental and Intergenerational Co-parenting Conflict and Adolescent Academic Performance: The Mediating Roles of Adolescent Academic Engagement and Depression. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph192315952

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bootstrapped mediation analysis (5000 samples) in the same SEM. The depression pathway indirect effect was −0.028 (SE = 0.008, CI = [−0.046, −0.015]); the CI excluded zero. The authors link this to emotional security theory, in which family conflict provokes depressive symptoms.

> "the indirect effects of co-parenting conflicts on academic performance through academic engagement and depression were, respectively, −0.053 (SE = 0.012, CI = [−0.079, −0.031]) and −0.028 (SE = 0.008, CI = [−0.046, −0.015])."
