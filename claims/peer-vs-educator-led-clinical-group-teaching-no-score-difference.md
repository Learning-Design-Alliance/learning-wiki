## Evidence

### Scott 2014

Scott, D. and Jelsma, J. (2014). The effectiveness of peer taught group sessions of physiotherapy students within the clinical setting: A quasi-experimental study. Journal of Peer Learning, 7, 105-117. http://ro.uow.edu.au/ajpl/vol7/iss1/9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Dependent t-test comparing mean scores on test sections taught by peers versus the clinical educator among 36 third-year physiotherapy students. The article reports "no significant difference between the scores of the sections taught by the clinical educator and those taught by peers"; no effect size is printed.

> "There was no significant difference between the scores of the sections taught by the clinical educator and those taught by peers ( p = .29)."

### Scott 2014 (2)

Scott, D. and Jelsma, J. (2014). The effectiveness of peer taught group sessions of physiotherapy students within the clinical setting: A quasi-experimental study. Journal of Peer Learning, 7, 105-117. http://ro.uow.edu.au/ajpl/vol7/iss1/9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Repeated measures ANOVA across self-taught, peer-taught, and educator-taught test sections in the same quasi-experimental study of 36 students. The article prints F(2,52) = 1.99, p = .15 and no effect size; the self-taught mean (7.3) was descriptively higher than peer-taught (6.4) and educator-taught (6.9).

> "There was also no significant difference between the mean scores of the test sections taught by the student, by their peers or by the clinical educator, F(2,52) = 1.99, p = .15."
