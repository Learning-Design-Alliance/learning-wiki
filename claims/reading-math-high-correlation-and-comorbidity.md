## Evidence

### Bailey et al. 2020

Drew H. Bailey, Yoonkyung Oh, George Farkas, Paul Morgan, Marianne Hillemeier. (2020). Reciprocal Effects of Reading and Mathematics? Beyond the Cross-Lagged Panel Model. Developmental Psychology. https://dx.doi.org/10.1037/dev0000902

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Descriptive correlations from the ECLS-K:2011 analytic sample (N=9,612). Concurrent cross-domain correlations are .73, .73, .74, and .72 across the four waves; the article calls these among the highest for conceptually distinct variables in the social and behavioral sciences.

> "In the nationally representative ECLS-K: 2011 data analyzed in the present study, children's reading and math achievement correlated at .73, .73, 74, and .72 in grades K, 1, 2, and 3 are, respectively."

### Bailey et al. 2020 (2)

Drew H. Bailey, Yoonkyung Oh, George Farkas, Paul Morgan, Marianne Hillemeier. (2020). Reciprocal Effects of Reading and Mathematics? Beyond the Cross-Lagged Panel Model. Developmental Psychology. https://dx.doi.org/10.1037/dev0000902

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

The article's introductory review, citing Wilson et al. (2015), reports dyslexia-dyscalculia comorbidity of about 4%, "more than ten times larger" than the 0.36% expected under independence; it is background evidence, not a new analysis of this dataset.

> "Instead, their comorbidity is actually about 4%, more than ten times larger (Wilson et al., 2015)."
