Evidence¶

Mary H. Sawyer 1991¶

Mary H. Sawyer. (1991). A Review of Research in Revising Instructional Text. Journal of Reading Behavior. https://doi.org/10.1080/10862969109547744

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports, citing multiple studies (Kintsch & Yarbrough, 1982; Meyer & Freedle, 1984; Richgels et al., 1987; Taylor & Samuels, 1983), that organized texts were better remembered when readers were aware of the structure.

"as long as readers are aware of the text's structure, a well organized text (one that follows rhetorical schemata such as cause/ effect or comparison/contrast) is better remembered than a disorganized or randomly organized text"

Mary H. Sawyer 1991 (2)¶

Mary H. Sawyer. (1991). A Review of Research in Revising Instructional Text. Journal of Reading Behavior. https://doi.org/10.1080/10862969109547744

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports, citing numerous recall-protocol studies, that high-level information was more likely to be recalled and retained over time, with Meyer finding high-structure information better recalled even at a one-week delay.

"most studies have found that information high in the text's structure is more likely to be recalled than information low in the text's structure"
