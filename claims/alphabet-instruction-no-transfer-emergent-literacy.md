## Evidence

### Piasta 2010

Piasta, S. B., Purpura, D. J., & Wagner, R. K. (2010). Fostering Alphabet Knowledge Development: A Comparison of Two Instructional Approaches. Reading and Writing, 23(6), 607-626. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11145-009-9174-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated measures ANOVAs in the randomized study found phonological awareness gains (and letter-word identification gains) rose overall but were "not differential across conditions"; all pairwise contrasts had small ds (0.02-0.10).

> "Children's phonological abilities increased from pretest to posttest, F(1, 52) = 5.746, p = .010, but, again, these gains were not differential across conditions"

### Piasta 2010 (2)

Piasta, S. B., Purpura, D. J., & Wagner, R. K. (2010). Fostering Alphabet Knowledge Development: A Comparison of Two Instructional Approaches. Reading and Writing, 23(6), 607-626. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11145-009-9174-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANOVAs on posttest emergent reading and developmental spelling found "no significant differences among conditions"; both measures had low reliability in this sample (emergent reading α = 0; DST α = .35), warranting caution.

> "Contrasts on the emergent reading and developmental spelling tasks showed no significant differences among conditions"
