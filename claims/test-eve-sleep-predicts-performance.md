## Evidence

### Estevan I 2021

Estevan I, Sardi R, Tejera AC, Silva A, Tassino B. (2021). Should I study or should I go (to sleep)? The influence of test schedule on the sleep behavior of undergraduates and its association with performance. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0247104

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Survey #1 logit regression of correct answer rate on sleep duration (n = 97): "an hour increase in sleep duration was associated with a 15.0% increase in the odds ratio of correct answers". Predicted rate 52.3% for an all-nighter vs 77.1% for 8 h sleep.

> "an hour increase in sleep duration was associated with a 15.0% increase in the odds ratio of correct answers (z = 6.8, p < 0.001; Cragg-Uhler pseudo-R2 = 0.38)"

### Estevan I 2021 (2)

Estevan I, Sardi R, Tejera AC, Silva A, Tassino B. (2021). Should I study or should I go (to sleep)? The influence of test schedule on the sleep behavior of undergraduates and its association with performance. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0247104

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey #2 logit regression (n = 252): "an hour increase in sleep duration before the test was associated with a 3.8% increase in the odds ratio of correct answers"; model fit rose to pseudo-R2 = 0.25 when shift was added.

> "an hour increase in sleep duration before the test was associated with a 3.8% increase in the odds ratio of correct answers (z = 5.7, p < 0.001; Cragg-Uhler pseudo-R2 = 0.12)"
