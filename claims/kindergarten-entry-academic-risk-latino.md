# Children who entered school in kindergarten rather than pre-kindergarten were twice as likely to be at academic risk at the end of first grade

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Kim Yeonwoo 2018

Kim Yeonwoo, Calzada Esther J., Barajas-Gonzalez R. Gabriela, Huang Keng-Yen, Brotman Laurie M., Castro Ashley, Pichardo Catherine. (2018). The Role of Authoritative and Authoritarian Parenting in the Early Academic Achievement of Latino Students. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000192

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive comparisons in a longitudinal sample of 750 Latino families; Table 3 reports the at-risk χ2 = 10.01** for the grade contrast. No mean-level differences in achievement by ethnicity or gender were significant.

> "children from the kindergarten cohort were more likely than those from the pre-k cohort to have lower mean-level scores of first grade achievement (96.28 vs. 98.50) and to have scores at least one standard deviation below the mean, or at academic risk (22.9% vs. 11.8%)."

### Kim Yeonwoo 2018 (2)

Kim Yeonwoo, Calzada Esther J., Barajas-Gonzalez R. Gabriela, Huang Keng-Yen, Brotman Laurie M., Castro Ashley, Pichardo Catherine. (2018). The Role of Authoritative and Authoritarian Parenting in the Early Academic Achievement of Latino Students. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000192

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Post-hoc descriptive analyses on ethnic and gender subgroups reported in the Discussion; the article calls for future research on risk factors disproportionately affecting DA boys.

> "28% of DA boys were at risk for academic underachievement at the end of first grade, compared with approximately 15% of MA boys, MA girls and DA girls."
