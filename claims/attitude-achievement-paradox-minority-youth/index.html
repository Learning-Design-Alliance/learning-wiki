Evidence¶

Phalet 2004¶

Phalet, K., Andriessen, I., & Lens, W. (2004). How Future Goals Enhance Motivation and Learning in Multicultural Classrooms. Educational Psychology Review, Vol. 16, No. 1. https://hdl.handle.net/1874/23520

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports, citing Ogbu and Simons (1998), a repeated finding among African American youth in the United States labelled the attitude-achievement paradox, commonly explained as ambivalent attitudes toward achievement in the presence of racism and discrimination (citing Mickelson, 1990).

"have repeatedly found conﬂicting evidence of low levels of school achieve- ment, in spite of high levels of motivation to achieve upward mobility through schooling (Ogbu and Simons, 1998)."

Phalet 2004 (2)¶

Phalet, K., Andriessen, I., & Lens, W. (2004). How Future Goals Enhance Motivation and Learning in Multicultural Classrooms. Educational Psychology Review, Vol. 16, No. 1. https://hdl.handle.net/1874/23520

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports, citing Mickelson (1990), the discrepancy between abstract and concrete mobility beliefs, which it reads as suggesting limits to perceived instrumentality of doing well in school in the absence of sufficient opportunities.

"most African American and Latino youth are found to endorse "abstract" mobility beliefs about the value of education for success later in life. At the same time, however, they tend to reject "concrete" beliefs about the benefits of education for their personal future (Mickelson, 1990)."
