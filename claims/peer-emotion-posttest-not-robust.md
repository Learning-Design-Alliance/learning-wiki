## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Analysis A found lower posttest performance in the enjoyment group than the neutral and frustration groups, while Analysis B found no group differences. [→ Hu 2024](#hu-2024)

## Evidence

### Hu 2024

Hu, Y., Elliot, A. J., Wouters, P., van der Schaaf, M., Kester, L., & Pekrun, R. (2024). Effects of peers' emotions on students' emotions, achievement goals, mental effort, and performance. Journal of Educational Psychology, 116(7), 1283–1299. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000895

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Posttest knowledge scores (maximum 20 points) from the randomized experiment were analyzed twice: by intended condition (Analysis A, excluding mismatching perceptions) and by perceived emotion (Analysis B). The two analyses disagreed; no effect sizes were printed.

> "students exposed to peers’ enjoyment reported lower posttest performance than those exposed to peers’ neutral state and those exposed to peers’ frustration in Analysis A (ae < 0 = af) and there were no differences among groups in Analysis B (ae = 0 = af)"
