# Interactive web-based genetics education produced significantly greater overall knowledge gains in primary care physicians than traditional CME review articles

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` PCPs randomized to the web-based intervention increased their cumulative knowledge score significantly more than PCPs given review articles (Δ = 13.8 vs Δ = 6.3, p < 0.01). [→ Wilkes Michael S. 2017](#wilkes-michael-s-2017)

## Evidence

### Wilkes Michael S. 2017

Wilkes Michael S., Day Frank C., Fancher Tonya L., McDermott Haley, Lehman Erik, Bell Robert A., Green Michael J. (2017). Increasing confidence and changing behaviors in primary care providers engaged in genetic counselling. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0982-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized trial of 121 California and Pennsylvania PCPs assigned to a 6 h interactive web-based curriculum or traditional CME review articles; cumulative knowledge (38 items, percent correct) measured pre and post. The article reports the intervention gain "significantly greater (p < 0.01)" at Δ = 13.8 versus Δ = 6.3; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "In both groups, knowledge increased significantly (p < 0.01) after education (Intervention: 62.1➔75.9; Control: 59.1➔65.5). However, the increase in knowledge was significantly greater (p < 0.01) in the Intervention Group (Δ = 13.8) than in the Control Group (Δ = 6.3)."
