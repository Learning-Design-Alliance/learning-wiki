# Higher confidence is positively associated with better ability to modulate SMT force–time characteristics, especially preload, time to peak force, and 800N impulse force

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` As students' confidence increased, their ability to modulate SMT force–time characteristics improved overall, especially keeping the pre-established preload, keeping time to peak force under 150ms, and delivering the 800N impulse force. [→ Nim Casper et al 2024](#nim-casper-et-al-2024)
`q2 i?` When the target impulse force was 800N, students who performed forces closer to the target force were generally more confident. [→ Nim Casper et al 2024 (2)](#nim-casper-et-al-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Nim Casper et al 2024

Nim Casper et al. (2024). The association between students’ confidence and ability to modulate spinal manipulation force–time characteristics of specific target forces: a cross-sectional study. Chiropractic & Manual Therapies. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12998-024-00557-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear mixed regression models on data from 149 students, with SMT force–time outcomes as the outcome and confidence interacting with targets as fixed effects. The article reports "a positive association was found between confidence and the ability to modulate their force–time characteristics".

> "A positive association was found between confidence and the ability to modulate their force–time characteristics, especially keeping the same pre-load force, keeping the time to peak force < 150ms, and providing the 800N impulse force."

### Nim Casper et al 2024 (2)

Nim Casper et al. (2024). The association between students’ confidence and ability to modulate spinal manipulation force–time characteristics of specific target forces: a cross-sectional study. Chiropractic & Manual Therapies. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12998-024-00557-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear mixed-model results reported in the Results section (Fig. 5 forest plots) for the 800N target impulse force. The authors describe the confidence–DIP and target impulse force association as "only a weak association".

> "Although there was only a weak association between confidence and the DIP and target impulse forces, we noticed that when the target impulse force was 800N, students who performed forces that were closer to the target force were generally more confident"
