## Evidence

### Sakles John C MD 2014

Sakles John C MD, Mosier Jarrod MD, Patanwala Asad E. PharmD, Dicken John BS. (2014). Learning Curves for Direct Laryngoscopy and GlideScope® Video Laryngoscopy in an Emergency Medicine Residency. Western Journal of Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2014.9.23691

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Retrospective analysis of 1,035 DL intubations by EM residents at an academic ED used a logistic mixed-effects model adjusting for reason for intubation, difficult airway characteristics, and calendar year. The model "did not improve" for PGY-2 (aOR 1.3) or PGY-3 (aOR 1.5, p-value 0.067).

> "In the mixed-effects model for DL, first pass success for PGY-2 and PGY-3 residents did not improve compared to PGY-1 residents (PGY-2 aOR 1.3, 95% CI 0.9–1.9; p-value 0.236) (PGY-3 aOR 1.5, 95% CI 1.0–2.2, p-value 0.067)"

### Sakles John C MD 2014 (2)

Sakles John C MD, Mosier Jarrod MD, Patanwala Asad E. PharmD, Dicken John BS. (2014). Learning Curves for Direct Laryngoscopy and GlideScope® Video Laryngoscopy in an Emergency Medicine Residency. Western Journal of Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2014.9.23691

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive proportions from the seven-year CQI-form dataset report DL first pass success of "69.9% (160/229)" for PGY-1 rising only to "72.9% (309/424)" for PGY-3, plotted in Figure 2.

> "For DL, the first past success of PGY-1 residents was 69.9% (160/229; 95% CI 63.5%–75.7%), for PGY-2 residents 71.7% (274/382; 95% CI 66.9%–76.2%), and for PGY-3 residents 72.9% (309/424; 95% CI 68.4%–77.1%)."
