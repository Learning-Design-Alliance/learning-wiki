## Evidence

### Kates Meghan 2026

Kates Meghan, Fitzpatrick Alexa, Zhang Yupeng, Gibbs Alison, Liu Qin, da Silveira Fleck Alan, Freedman Leora, Andreopoulos Stavroula. (2026). Effectiveness of Scientific Literacy and Communication E‐Modules in a Molecular Biology Course: A Qualitative and Quantitative Analysis. Biochemistry and Molecular Biology Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/bmb.70043

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

End-of-course anonymous Likert survey during the final week of the Winter 2022–2024 BCH311 cohorts (n = 71 students for this item, 106 responses total). Students rated whether e-module activities made assignment completion easier, with "52.1%" agreeing and "14.1%" strongly agreeing.

> "The majority of students agreed (52.1%) or strongly agreed (14.1%) with this statement (Figure 2A)."

### Kates Meghan 2026 (2)

Kates Meghan, Fitzpatrick Alexa, Zhang Yupeng, Gibbs Alison, Liu Qin, da Silveira Fleck Alan, Freedman Leora, Andreopoulos Stavroula. (2026). Effectiveness of Scientific Literacy and Communication E‐Modules in a Molecular Biology Course: A Qualitative and Quantitative Analysis. Biochemistry and Molecular Biology Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/bmb.70043

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same end-of-course anonymous Likert survey (n = 74 students for this item, 3 cohorts). Students rated whether the four e-modules were useful for developing scientific literacy, communication, and writing skills, with "51.4% of students agreed" and "9.5% of students strongly agreed".

> "Specifically, 51.4% of students agreed and 9.5% of students strongly agreed with the question."
