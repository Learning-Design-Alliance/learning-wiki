## Evidence

### Zhengtao Xu 2026

Zhengtao Xu, Junti Zhang, Anthony Tang, and Yi-Chieh Lee. (2026). Who You Explain To Matters: Learning by Explaining to Conversational Agents with Different Pedagogical Roles. Proceedings of the 2026 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI '26). https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3790298

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Qualitative coding of all user chat messages across five rounds, using a scheme with high inter-rater reliability (Krippendorff's alpha = 0.831). Tutee messages shifted from Stating Fact/Definition (24 in turn one) toward Elaborating Causality/Principle (65).

> "interactions in the Tutee group centered on Cognitive acts (141). The interaction trajectory reveals that this high output was not uniform but followed a structured, scaffolded pattern"

### Zhengtao Xu 2026 (2)

Zhengtao Xu, Junti Zhang, Anthony Tang, and Yi-Chieh Lee. (2026). Who You Explain To Matters: Learning by Explaining to Conversational Agents with Different Pedagogical Roles. Proceedings of the 2026 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI '26). https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3790298

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Coded chat messages in the Control condition showed disengagement beginning by the second turn, with turn-closing becoming the dominant and nearly sole activity by the final round. Peer and Challenger groups instead sustained Elaborating Causality (94 and 96), with Challenger showing elevated Self-Monitoring (18) and Planning/Strategizing (28).

> "In the Control group, we observed a marked absence of sustained dialogue. The most frequent behavior wasTurn Closing/Refusal to Elaborate(49)"
