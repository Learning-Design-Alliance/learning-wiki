The article reports, citing Gurin Schleifer et al. (2025), this asymmetry in ML coding of student ideas within its Phase 4 discussion of comparability across diverse populations. It is a second-hand attribution, not a study conducted in this article.

"ML methodologies are generally better at coding productive and canonically correct ideas than coding the diverse and often unpredictable ways in which students articulate unproductive ideas because the range of expression for unproductive ideas tends to be broader than that for productive ideas"
