# The competence-belief component measures' interactions with the effort self-talk contrasts remained significant when analyzed separately

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r3` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2020 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` When self-concept in mathematics and self-perceived scholastic competence were analyzed separately rather than aggregated, their interactions with the effort versus no self-talk contrast (ts ≤ −2.475, ps ≤ .014) and the effort versus ability contrast (ts ≤ −2.019, ps ≤ .045) remained significant. [→ Thomaes 2020](#thomaes-2020)

## Evidence

### Thomaes 2020

Thomaes, Tjaarda, Brummelman, and Sedikides. (2020). Effort Self‐Talk Benefits the Mathematics Performance of Children With Negative Competence Beliefs. Child Development, 91(6), 2211–2220. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.13347

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Robustness analysis repeating the primary regressions with self-concept in mathematics and self-perceived scholastic competence analyzed separately instead of aggregated (N = 212): the component measures' interactions with both effort contrasts remained significant ("ts ≤ −2.475, ps ≤ .014").

> "the interactions of these measures with the effort versus no self‐talk contrast, ts ≤ −2.475, ps ≤ .014, bs ≤ −.134, and the effort versus ability self‐talk contrast, ts ≤ −2.019, ps ≤ .045, bs ≤ −.118"
