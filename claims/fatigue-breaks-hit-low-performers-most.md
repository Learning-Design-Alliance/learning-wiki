# Fatigue and break effects are concentrated at the low end of the test-score distribution

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2016 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` At the 10th percentile, a break improves scores by 2.7% of an SD (95% CI, 2.0–3.5%) and each later hour worsens them by 1.3% of an SD (95% CI, 1.0–1.5%); at the upper end there is no break effect and the hourly decline is only 0.4% of an SD (95% CI, 0.2–0.6%). [→ Sievertsen HH 2016](#sievertsen-hh-2016)

## Evidence

### Sievertsen HH 2016

Sievertsen HH, Gino F, Piovesan M. (2016). Cognitive fatigue influences students' performance on standardized tests. PNAS 113(10): 2621–2624. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1516947113

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quantile regression (Canay's plugin fixed effect estimator) at the 10th through 90th percentiles of the test score distribution shows "both breaks and time of day affect the lower end of the distribution... significantly more than the upper end". No standardized effect size is printed.

> "For the 10th percentile, a break causes 2.7% (95% CI, 2.0–3.5%) of an SD improvement in test score, and for every hour later in the day, the test performance worsens by 1.3% (95% CI, 1.0–1.5%) of an SD."
