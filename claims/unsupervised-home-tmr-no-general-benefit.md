# Unsupervised TMR of Dutch vocabulary during three nights of sleep at home does not generally improve memory for cued over uncued words

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across three nights of unsupervised TMR at home, memory improvement did not differ between cued and uncued Dutch words. [→ Göldi 2019](#goldi-2019)

## Evidence

### Göldi 2019

Göldi, M. & Rasch, B. (2019). Effects of targeted memory reactivation during sleep at home depend on sleep disturbances and habituation. npj Science of Learning 4:5. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0044-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Home experiment with 66 participants learning 120 Dutch-German word pairs over four days, with 60 words cued nightly via mp3 during sleep. Cued words improved "163.42 ± 4.66%" vs 167.29 ± 5.61% for uncued, no significant difference (P > 0.25); equivalence was not tested.

> "For cued words, participants increased their memory performance from day 1 to day 4 by 163.42 ± 4.66%, while uncued words improved by 167.29 ± 5.61%. We observed no signiﬁcant difference in memory improvement between cued and uncued words ( P > 0.25)."
