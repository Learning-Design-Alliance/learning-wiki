## Evidence

### Ballen C. J. 2017

Ballen C. J., Wieman C., Salehi S., Searle J. B., Zamudio K. R. (2017). Enhancing Diversity in Undergraduate Science: Self-Efficacy Drives Performance Gains with Active Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-12-0344

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Survey-based analysis of science self-efficacy (six Likert items, Cronbach's alpha > 0.7) modeled with general linear models including presemester self-efficacy as a covariate. The article reports the increase as significant with F(1, 1.73) = 6.55, p = 0.011, and no effect size is printed.

> "Reported self-efficacy increased from 2014 to 2015 equally for all demographic groups (F(1, 1.73) = 6.55, p = 0.011; Figure 2 and Supplemental Tables S2, S4, and S5)."
