## Evidence

### McBain Lynn 2016

McBain Lynn, Pullon Susan, Garrett Sue, Hoare Kath. (2016). Genital examination training: assessing the effectiveness of an integrated female and male teaching programme. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0822-y

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Gender-subgroup analysis of the matched pre/post questionnaire data (28 male, 34 female respondents) using Wilcoxon tests. The article also reports no significant gender differences in pre-to-post score changes, and that "none of the differences in student gender were significant" for mean changes.

> "For the questions assessing skill, comfort and confidence with male and female examinations, both genders rated their pre-programme skill level for female examination much higher than for male examination, however only the results for female students were significant (Male students p = 0.14; Female students p = <0.0001)."
