## Evidence

### Wu Ju-Hui 2024

Wu Ju-Hui, Lin Pei Chen, Lee Kun-Tsung, Liu Hsin-Liang, Lu Peih-Ying, Lee Chen-Yi. (2024). Situational simulation teaching effectively improves dental students’ non-operational clinical competency and objective structured clinical examination performance. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05546-4

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Path analysis model (AMOS 26) tested in this observational study of 87 fifth-year dental students; the model fit the data satisfactorily (χ2 = 2.493, df = 2, P = 0.287; RMSEA = 0.054; GFI = 0.986; TLI = 0.965; CFI = 0.988), and "participation in situational simulation teaching course directly affects OSCE performance (β = 0.356, P < 0.001)".

> "The model revealed that academic performance directly affects OSCE performance (β = 0.281, P = 0.003) and clerkship performance (β = 0.441, P < 0.001); participation in situational simulation teaching course directly affects OSCE performance (β = 0.356, P < 0.001)."

### Wu Ju-Hui 2024 (2)

Wu Ju-Hui, Lin Pei Chen, Lee Kun-Tsung, Liu Hsin-Liang, Lu Peih-Ying, Lee Chen-Yi. (2024). Situational simulation teaching effectively improves dental students’ non-operational clinical competency and objective structured clinical examination performance. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05546-4

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Univariate chi-square analysis in the same cohort found SSC participation significantly associated with OSCE outcome (P < 0.001), with 60 of 70 participants (85.7%) passing versus 7 of 17 non-participants (41.2%); age, SSC outcome, and clerkship performance showed no significant association.

> "The chi-square test revealed that age, the SSC outcome, and the score of clerkship performance had no significant association with OSCE outcome. However, sex, academic performance, and participation in the SSC showed a significant association (Table 5)."
