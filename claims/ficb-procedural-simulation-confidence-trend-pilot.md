# FICB procedural simulation produced positive but non-significant increases in residents' perceived comfort and knowledge with performing the procedure

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After the FICB procedural simulation, residents' perceived comfort performing the procedure rose from a median of 4.0 to 9.0 on a 1–10 scale, a positive trend that was not statistically significant (P = 0.08). [→ Griesmer Katherine B. MD 2025](#griesmer-katherine-b-md-2025)

## Evidence

### Griesmer Katherine B. MD 2025

Griesmer Katherine B. MD, MPH, Thompson Maxwell MD, Miller Briana MD, Zhai Guihua PhD, Raper Jaron MD, Bloom Andrew MD. (2025). Combining Immersive Simulation with a Collaborative Procedural Training on Local Anesthetic Systemic Toxicity and Fascia Iliaca Compartment Block: A Pilot Study. Western Journal of Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.25020

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Prospective pilot study of 16 EM residents (PGY 1–3) at a Level I trauma center completing a hands-on FICB procedural simulation; pre/post surveys analyzed with a generalized Stuart-Maxwell test. The "pre-simulation improving from 4.0 (1.0–5.0) to 9.0 (7.0–10.0)" medians were reported, with P = 0.08 in Table 2; no effect size was printed.

> "Following simulation, learners reported an improvement in confidence and knowledge with performing a FICB, with the pre-simulation improving from 4.0 (1.0–5.0) to 9.0 (7.0–10.0) post-simulation"
