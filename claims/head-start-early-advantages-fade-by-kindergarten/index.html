Evidence¶

Mashburn 2013¶

Mashburn, A. J., & Downer, J. T. (2013). Methods that Examine the Extent to Which the Quality of Children's Experiences in Elementary School Moderate the Long-Term Impacts of Head Start. SREE Spring 2013 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED563295

q3 · i? · causal · r?

The abstract reports results of prior intent-to-treat analyses of the HSIS randomized controlled trial, in which newly entering 3- and 4-year-olds were randomly assigned to Head Start access or a control condition. The authors state the benefits were "concentrated on language and literacy skills for the 4-year-old group" and that most early advantages were "non-significant by the end of kindergarten and first grade."

"Results of prior analyses that address the first goal of the HSIS found benefits for children at the end of Head Start that were concentrated on language and literacy skills for the 4-year-old group and benefits at the end of Head Start on cognitive, social-emotional, health, and parenting outcomes for the 3-year old group. However, the early advantages found at the end of Head Start on most of these outcomes were non-significant by the end of kindergarten and first grade."
