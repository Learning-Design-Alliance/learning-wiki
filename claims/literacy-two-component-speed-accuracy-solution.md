# The component analysis of the five literacy measures yielded a speed factor (word and nonword reading speed) and an accuracy factor (word and nonword reading accuracy), with spelling analysed separately due to less clear-cut loadings

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The first principal component analysis of the five literacy measures indicated a two-component solution with reading speed measures on the first and reading accuracy measures on the second component. [→ Moll 2014](#moll-2014)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

A principal component analysis (Varimax rotation) of the five literacy attainment measures in the pooled sample showed "a two component solution with clear loadings of word and nonword reading speed on the first component" and accuracy on the second.

> "The results indicated a two component solution with clear loadings of word and nonword reading speed on the first component (speed factor: eigenvalue = 2. 38 ; both factor loadings = .90), and word and nonword reading accuracy on the second component (accuracy factor: eigenvalue = 1.1 0 ; factor loadings = .76 and .80, respectively)."

In the same component analysis, "The spelling measure loaded higher on the accuracy component than on the speed component (.64 versus . 40 )", but loadings were not clear-cut, so the authors analysed spelling as a separate outcome measure.

> "The spelling measure loaded higher on the accuracy component than on the speed component (.64 versus . 40 ), but the loadings were not as clear-cut as for the reading measures. Therefore, we decided to analyse spelling separately,"
