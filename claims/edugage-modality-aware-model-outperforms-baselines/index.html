q2 · i? · design · r2

Benchmark evaluation of the EduGage dataset under a participant-grouped four-fold cross-validation protocol (Table 4). The modality-aware model achieved the lowest MAE of 0.80, versus an MAE of 1.05 for the task-informed Peer Window Mean baseline. The authors note the remaining error demonstrates the difficulty of the task.

"Across participant-based cross-validation, the modality-aware reference model achieves an MAE of 0.80, 83.18% within-1 accuracy, 70.96% binary accuracy, and 62.90% binary Macro-F1, outperforming sensor-free, statistical, deep temporal, foundation-model, and LLM-based baselines."

Related Claims¶

  • Videos experienced as higher attention difficulty were associated with lower normalized quiz learning gains, providing convergent evidence that the probe captures an educationally meaningful aspect of momentary engagement — related
  • One-minute-ahead direct forecasting performs similarly to contemporaneous estimation, but performance degrades at longer horizons and a carry-forward baseline outperforms the sensor model at every future horizon — related
  • Chest-worn ECG provides the strongest cardiac-sensing performance for attention-difficulty prediction, and fusing all cardiac streams does not improve upon chest ECG alone — related
  • Predictive performance of sensor configurations does not change monotonically with the number of sensing streams; preferred configuration depends on target metric and sensing cost — related
  • A head-confined multimodal configuration (EEG, eye tracking, forehead EDA) predicts held-out learners' five-level engagement ratings at 75.0% balanced 1-off accuracy, 12.0 points above the mode baseline — related
  • A DDQN-based attention quantification model trained on multimodal EEG-fNIRS features produces predicted attention levels at one-second temporal resolution — related
  • The Residual Fusion Transformer shows the lowest worst-group MAE and smallest gender gap among baselines, while the Sensor GRU shows the smallest age gap alongside the weakest overall performance — related
  • The end-to-end attention-estimation pipeline meets the one-second prediction stride on an NVIDIA A100 GPU, with warm latency of 50.65 ms per window and 1.02 ms for the temporal model
  • Facial visual dynamics provide the most informative cues for attention estimation: the Visual GRU outperforms both the train-mean baseline and the Sensor GRU — related
