## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Pooled analysis of 11 RCTs found no statistically significant difference in knowledge acquisition scores between GAI-based and traditional teaching (SMD 0.27, 95% CI -0.31 to 0.85; p = 0.36). [→ Li Juan 2025](#li-juan-2025)

## Evidence

### Li Juan 2025

Li Juan, Yin Kaiyu, Wang Yida, Jiang Xiaoqin, Chen Dongxu. (2025). Effectiveness of generative artificial intelligence-based teaching versus traditional teaching methods in medical education: a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07750-2

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random-effects meta-analysis of knowledge scores from all eleven included RCTs (395 GAI-based students and 391 traditional teaching students). The pooled SMD was "0.27, 95% CI −0.31 to 0.85" with p = 0.36 and I² = 93%; GRADE rated the evidence low quality.

> "Meta-analysis with a random-effects model showed that there was no significant difference in knowledge scores between the groups (SMD 0.27, 95% CI −0.31 to 0.85; p = 0.36; I² = 93%; Fig. 3)."
