# Effect sizes were not moderated by publication year, age, or percentage of females, but were related to study quality; publication-bias tests gave mixed signals

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Publication year, sample age and percentage of females were not significantly associated with effect sizes, whereas study quality was significantly related to effect sizes. [→ Karle 2026](#karle-2026)
`q2 i?` Publication-bias indicators were mixed: Egger's mixed-effects test found no bias (z = -1.267, p = .205), Kendall's rank test found significant bias (τ = 0.080, p < .001), and PET-PEESE corrected Fisher's Z to 0.226. [→ Karle 2026 (2)](#karle-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Karle 2026

Karle, V. L., Suggate, S. P., Winter, R., & Stoeger, H. (2026). When Fine is Large and Gross is Small: A Meta-Analysis of Links Between Fine and Gross Motor Skills with Academic-Cognitive Skills. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10161-4

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Separate mixed-effects meta-regressions for each predictor (Table 1). Post-hoc ANOVAs showed non-peer-reviewed papers did not differ from peer-reviewed ones (p = .274), nor at-risk from normal samples (p = .685). Heterogeneity was high.

> "Year of publication, age, and percentage of females in the samples were not significantly associated with effect sizes. Study quality was significantly related to effect sizes."

### Karle 2026 (2)

Karle, V. L., Suggate, S. P., Winter, R., & Stoeger, H. (2026). When Fine is Large and Gross is Small: A Meta-Analysis of Links Between Fine and Gross Motor Skills with Academic-Cognitive Skills. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10161-4

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Triangulated publication-bias assessment: funnel plot, Egger's mixed-effects test, Kendall's rank test, and PET-PEESE. The corrected Fisher's Z was 0.226, slightly below the uncorrected null-model estimate of 0.239.

> "a mixed-effects Egger’s test for funnel plot asymmetry was run, which indicated no publication bias (z = -1.267, p = .205). However, a second analysis using Kendall’s rank test revealed significant publication bias (τ = 0.080, p < .001)."
