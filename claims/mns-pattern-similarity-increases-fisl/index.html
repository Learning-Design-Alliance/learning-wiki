Evidence¶

Qi Liu 2025¶

Qi Liu, Siyu Zhu, Can Liu, Yanmiao Yang, Chunmei Lan, Xinwei Song, Benjamin Becker, Keith M. Kendrick, Xinqi Zhou & Weihua Zhao. (2025). Neural signals gradually shift towards inferior frontal gyrus and long-term effects following facial imitation-based social learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00372-9

q3 · i? · causal · r1

fNIRS neural pattern similarity (adjusted cosine similarity) measured in 80 participants during the 2nd, 9th and 16th FISL sessions; repeated measures ANOVAs showed main effects of valence in the MNS and all subregions, "with greater AUC values for NPS within positive than negative emotions".

"with greater AUC values for NPS within positive than negative emotions (Fig. 2a), revealing that the MNS and its subregions have more similar neural representation durin g the imitative learning of positive emotion"

Qi Liu 2025 (2)¶

Qi Liu, Siyu Zhu, Can Liu, Yanmiao Yang, Chunmei Lan, Xinwei Song, Benjamin Becker, Keith M. Kendrick, Xinqi Zhou & Weihua Zhao. (2025). Neural signals gradually shift towards inferior frontal gyrus and long-term effects following facial imitation-based social learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00372-9

q3 · i2 · causal · r1

Correlation analysis linking fNIRS NPS change to behavioral improvement across the FISL course: the article prints an effect size of r = 0.278 in the overall MNS (pFDR = 0.025) and r = 0.337 in IFG (pFDR = 0.009), reporting "a signiﬁcant positive correlation between behavioral performance changes and NPS changes"; not significant in STS or IPL, or for negative emotions.

"a signiﬁcant positive correlation between behavioral performance changes and NPS changes when imitative learning of positive emotion in the overall MNS ( r(79) = 0.278, pFDR = 0.025"
