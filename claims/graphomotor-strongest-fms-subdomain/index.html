Keep the hands in mind: A meta-analysis of correlations between fine motor skills and reading, writing, mathematics, and cognitive development in children and adolescents. q3 · i2 · quant-synthesis · r2

Post-hoc ANOVA comparisons within the multilevel meta-regression of FMS subdomains (Table 1: graphomotor r = .338, dexterity r = .289). The article reports "the effect sizes for graphomotor skills were greater compared to dexterity (p < .001)".

"the effect sizes for graphomotor skills were greater compared to dexterity (p < .001), general FMS (p < .001) and FMS miscellaneous (p = .027), but not significantly different to speed (p = .173), bimanual (p = .095), general FMS (p = .099), or finger gnosia (p = .336)."
