# Digital policies imagine a "model user" whose experience contrasts sharply with the actualities of adults on the margins of access

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The City of Vancouver Digital Strategy envisions an idealized digitally proficient citizen (Stephanie) whose experience contrasts with actualities observed among Digital Café participants. [→ Smythe 2017](#smythe-2017)

## Evidence

### Smythe 2017

Smythe, S., & Breshears, S. (2017). Complicating Access: Digital Inequality And Adult Learning In A Public-Access Computing Space. The Canadian Journal for the Study of Adult Education, 29(1), 67-81. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1139334

`q2 · i?`

Policy analysis of the City of Vancouver Digital Strategy's "A day in the life..." model user approach. The authors contrast this imagined "Stephanie" with Digital Café participants who lack devices, struggle with email, or get locked out of accounts, arguing the model user masks digital inequality.

> "Stephanie is regarded as digitally proficient and seems to have continuous access to multiple devices, data, and a high-speed connection at work, at home, and on transit. She values ease and efficiency of services, meaning that she can achieve her personal life goals almost instantaneously"
