## Evidence

### Nolan K. Weinlader 2019

Nolan K. Weinlader, Eric Kuo, Benjamin M. Rottman, and Timothy J. Nokes-Malach. (2019). A new approach for uncovering student resources with multiple-choice questions. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1909.01455](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1909.01455)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Coding of students' generated explanations in the interview study identified distance-only explanations (e.g., the closer target is hit first). The article states these incorrect explanations "reflect correct dependences of distance on time if the speeds were equal throughout," though speeds are not equal.

> "Although both of these explanations are incorrect, they reflect correct dependences of distance on time if the speeds were equal throughout."

### Nolan K. Weinlader 2019 (2)

Nolan K. Weinlader, Eric Kuo, Benjamin M. Rottman, and Timothy J. Nokes-Malach. (2019). A new approach for uncovering student resources with multiple-choice questions. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1909.01455](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1909.01455)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Coded explanations from the same interview study included speed-only accounts. The article reports these "rely on a common faster means less time  resource, which is consistent with kinematics," and are incomplete because they omit distance's effect on time.

> "These speed  only explanations rely on a common faster means less time  resource, which is consistent with kinematics. What makes these explanations incomplete is that they do not consider the effect of distance on time."
