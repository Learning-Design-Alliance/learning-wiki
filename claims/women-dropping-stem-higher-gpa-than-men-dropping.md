# Women who dropped STEM majors earned significantly higher STEM GPAs than men who dropped the same majors, with small-to-medium effect sizes.

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Among students who dropped biological science, computer science/engineering, and math/physical science majors, women's mean STEM GPA exceeded men's, with Cohen's d of 0.30, 0.56, and 0.36 respectively. [→ Alexandru Maries 2022](#alexandru-maries-2022)

## Evidence

### Alexandru Maries 2022

Alexandru Maries, Kyle Whitcomb, and Chandralekha Singh. (2022). Gender Inequities Throughout STEM: Compared to Men, Women with Significantly Higher Grades Drop STEM Majors. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2202.02438](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2202.02438)

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of cumulative STEM GPA from institutional records for students who dropped the same major sets (Table 2a): women who dropped earned higher grades than men who dropped, e.g., d = 0.56 for computer science and engineering (M 2.68 vs 2.23), d = 0.30 for bio, d = 0.36 for math/physical science, all p < .01. The article reports "the women were earning higher grades than the men."

> "In each set of majors, we find that the mean STEM GPA difference between women and men who dropped each set of STEM majors is larger than the STEM GPA difference between women and men who persisted in the majors"
