# Accessing online CME courses and independent study behaviors show positive but statistically non-significant associations with endline test scores

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Whether participants ever accessed the online CME courses had a positive but not statistically significant association with endline test scores (ß=0.16; P=.19). [→ Nofal MR 2018](#nofal-mr-2018)
`q3 i?` None of the six independent study behaviors reached statistical significance in any model, for intervention or control participants. [→ Nofal MR 2018 (2)](#nofal-mr-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Nofal MR 2018

Nofal MR, Halim N, Le BN, et al. (2018). Unpacking the “black box”: how an SMS-based continuing medical education intervention improved medical knowledge among HIV clinicians in Vietnam. Glob Health Sci Pract. 2018;6(4):668-679. https://doi.org/10.9745/GHSP-D-18-00298

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Adjusted linear regression Model 4 (n=48) testing ever-access of online CME courses; the association was positive but not statistically significant. Across both arms, 43% of participants ever accessed the courses.

> "Similarly, whether or not the participants ever accessed the online CME courses had a positive association with endline test scores in Model 4 (ß=0.16), but the association was not statistically significant (P=.19)."

### Nofal MR 2018 (2)

Nofal MR, Halim N, Le BN, et al. (2018). Unpacking the “black box”: how an SMS-based continuing medical education intervention improved medical knowledge among HIV clinicians in Vietnam. Glob Health Sci Pract. 2018;6(4):668-679. https://doi.org/10.9745/GHSP-D-18-00298

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Adjusted regression Models 3–4 (n=47 intervention, n=44 control) of six self-reported independent study behaviors on endline scores. Among intervention participants, researching online (ß=0.26; P=.11) and reviewing scientific papers (ß=0.21; P=.15) were positive but non-significant; consulted colleagues was negative (ß=−0.23; P=.11).

> "None of these associations were statistically significant."
