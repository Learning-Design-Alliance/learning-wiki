## Subclaims
`q1 i?` A high-positive correlation between a selection tool and course outcomes is not necessarily expected or desirable, because tools are designed to discriminate around selection thresholds rather than to predict ranked performance. [→ Edwards Daniel 2013](#edwards-daniel-2013)

## Evidence

### Edwards Daniel 2013

Edwards Daniel, Friedman Tim, Pearce Jacob. (2013). Same admissions tools, different outcomes: a critical perspective on predictive validity in three undergraduate medical schools. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-173

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Theoretical argument in the discussion: UMAT is "designed to be highly discriminating" around selection thresholds, while course assessments rarely discriminate at the high end, so the article argues the assumption "that high-positive correlations are good or even expected in these studies requires critical reflection". No empirical test of this argument is offered.

> "A high-positive correlation is thus not expected, as the instruments are designed for differing purposes."
