# Lessons in Character has potentially positive effects on academic achievement, with statistically significant effects on mathematics grades and attendance in one randomized controlled trial

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In the Dietsch, Bayha, & Zheng randomized controlled trial, mathematics grades showed a statistically significant difference favoring the Lessons in Character group, with an effect size of 0.46. [→ Lessons in Character 2006](#lessons-in-character-2006)
`q3 i2` In the same trial, attendance (absences) showed a statistically significant difference favoring the Lessons in Character group, with an effect size of 0.48. [→ Lessons in Character 2006](#lessons-in-character-2006)
`q3 i?` Reading grades favored the Lessons in Character group (effect size 0.31) but were not statistically significant as calculated by the WWC. [→ Lessons in Character 2006](#lessons-in-character-2006)

### Lessons in Character 2006

Lessons in Character. (2006). WWC Intervention Report, What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/InterventionReport/270

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

WWC recalculation of the Dietsch, Bayha, & Zheng (2005) randomized controlled trial of 372 fourth-grade students in 21 classrooms. The WWC found "0.46 Statistically significant" for mathematics grades and an attendance effect size of 0.48, also statistically significant; reading (ES 0.31) was not significant.
