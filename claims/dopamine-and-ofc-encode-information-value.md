# Dopamine neurons treat informational reward like primary reward, and OFC encodes information value separately from reward size

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Dopamine neuron activity is enhanced by the prospect of both a possible reward and early information, suggesting subcortical reward structures treat informational value like other valued goods. [→ Kidd 2015](#kidd-2015)
`q2 i?` In the curiosity tradeoff task, OFC neurons encode both gamble stakes and information value but do not integrate them into a single value signal. [→ Kidd 2015 (2)](#kidd-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Kidd 2015

Kidd, Celeste and Hayden, Benjamin Y. (2015). The psychology and neuroscience of curiosity. Neuron. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.010

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports single-unit recordings from the temporal resolution of uncertainty task (Bromberg-Martin & Hikosaka), showing "dopamine neuron activity (DA) is enhanced by the prospect of both a possible reward and early information". Lateral habenula neurons likewise encode unexpected occurrence and denial of information.

> "In this task, dopamine neuron activity (DA) is enhanced by the prospect of both a possible reward and early information."

### Kidd 2015 (2)

Kidd, Celeste and Hayden, Benjamin Y. (2015). The psychology and neuroscience of curiosity. Neuron. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.010

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports that in the curiosity tradeoff task, "OFC neurons encode both the stakes of the gamble, and also the information value of the options", but do not integrate them into a single value signal; the authors propose curiosity is computed separately and combined downstream between OFC and the dopamine nuclei.

> "In the curiosity tradeoff task (see above and Figure 3), OFC neurons encode both the stakes of the gamble, and also the information value of the options (Blanchard, Hayden, & Bromberg-Martin, 2015)."
