# Metacognitive scaffolding produced a small significant positive effect on the individual metacognitive knowledge students acquired

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Students in scaffolding conditions acquired significantly more metacognitive knowledge than control students, t(108) = 1.63, p < 0.05 (one tailed), r = 0.16. [→ Molenaar 2010](#molenaar-2010)

## Evidence

### Molenaar 2010

Molenaar, I., van Boxtel, C. A. M., & Sleegers, P. J. C. (2010). Metacognitive scaffolding in an innovative learning arrangement. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-010-9154-1

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Planned comparison of the control group with the two scaffolding conditions on a procedural knowledge test (writing steps for redoing the assignment, 18-point maximum). The article reports a significant small effect, r = 0.16; 27 students unable to answer were excluded, equally distributed over conditions.

> "scaffolding did signiﬁcantly affect metacognitive knowledge, t (108) = 1.63; p\0.05 (one tailed). The effect size r = 0. 16 indicated a small positive effect of scaffolding on the amount metacognitive knowledge acquired."
