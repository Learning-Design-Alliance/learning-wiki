# Students graded on a percent scale in introductory physics were over 5 times more likely to receive course grades below C- than students graded on a 4-point scale

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Webb 2020

Webb, D. J., Paul, C. A., & Chessey, M. K. (2020). Relative impacts of diﬀerent grade-scales on student success in introductory physics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020114](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020114)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of 10 years of course-grading archives from the CLASP series (95 classes, ~250 students each), comparing fail fractions by grade scale. The ratio was 4.4 ± 0.5 in CLASP A and 7.0 ± 1.0 in CLASP B; chi-square tests were significant for both courses.

> "From the averages shown on the ﬁgure we see that instructors using the percent grad- ing scale gave 5.3 ± 0.4 times as many grades lower than C− than instructors using a 4-point scale."

### Webb 2020 (2)

Webb, D. J., Paul, C. A., & Chessey, M. K. (2020). Relative impacts of diﬀerent grade-scales on student success in introductory physics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020114](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020114)

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression with entering GPA and grade scale as predictors of receiving less than C-, with printed odds ratios of 5.5 ± 0.7 (CLASP A) and 8.1 ± 1.2 (CLASP B), leading the authors to conclude that "student academic performance does not explain the large fraction of students with these low grades under percent grading."

> "we find that a student graded under a percent scale had 5 .5 ± 0.7 times higher odds of receiving less than C − than the same student under 4-point grading"
