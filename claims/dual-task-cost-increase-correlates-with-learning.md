## Evidence

### Dahm 2026

Dahm, S. F., Kraft, V., Martini, M., Krause, D. & Hermsdoerfer, J. (2026). Motor sequences resist automatization as attentional demands increase with sequence learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00412-y

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory Pearson correlation analysis (N = 87; critical r = 0.21) linking dual-task cost increases in the practiced sequence to learning and explicit-knowledge measures, including r = 0.21 for visual recognition, suggesting establishing explicit sequence knowledge requires greater attentional control.

> "Further, the increase in dual-task costs from Pretest to Posttest in the Practice Sequence correlated with the index of sequence learning ( r = 0.76), Free Generation Performance (response: r = 0.35; visual: r = 0.32) and Recognition Performance (response: r = 0.22; visual: r = 0.21)."
