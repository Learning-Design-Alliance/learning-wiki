# Self-explanation benefits learning across ages, but evidence is mixed for elementary-school children and effects shrink when time on task is equated

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` A meta-analysis of self-explanation found a mean effect of 0.55 SDs, reduced to 0.41 SDs when time on task was equated, with slightly smaller effects for elementary (0.48) and high-school (0.43) than university (0.61) students. [→ Garvin Brod 2021](#garvin-brod-2021)
`q2 i?` In elementary mathematics, self-explanation gains over a time-equated active control (additional practice problems) were not significant, though it beat a non-time-equated control. [→ Garvin Brod 2021 (2)](#garvin-brod-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Garvin Brod 2021

Garvin Brod. (2021). Generative Learning: Which Strategies for What Age? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09571-9

`q3 · i2` · `review · r3`

The review reports Bisra et al.'s meta-analysis (mean 0.55 SDs; 0.41 SDs with time on task equated) showing an effect size of 0.48 SDs for elementary-school (10 studies) and 0.43 SDs for high-school (13 studies) students versus 0.61 SDs for university students.

> "Bisra et al. (2018) reported a slightly reduced effect sizeof this strategy for elementary-school students (0.48 SDs, based on 10 studies) and high-school students (0.43 SDs, based on 13 studies) compared with university students (0.61 SDs)."

### Garvin Brod 2021 (2)

Garvin Brod. (2021). Generative Learning: Which Strategies for What Age? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09571-9

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

A study of second- through fourth-graders compared self-explanation with solving additional practice problems; gains over the non-time-equated control were larger, but there was no difference against the time-equated control, which the review calls a rigorous generative control.

> "there was no difference in gains between the self-explanation condition and the time-equated control condition."
