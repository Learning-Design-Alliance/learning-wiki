## Evidence

### Joni Lämsä 2026

Joni Lämsä, Susanne de Mooij, Martine Baars, Inge Molenaar, Sanna Järvelä. (2026). Self-Regulated Learning, Multimodal Data, and Analysis Grid: Where Are We Now and Where Are We Going? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10113-4

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The commentary summarizes Kubsch et al.'s (2025) empirical study of an AI-based early warning system in inquiry-based science classrooms, combining workbook log data (cognitive processes) with self-reports (metacognitive and affective processes) across an instructional unit spanning 2–3 weeks. The authors found that "the importance of different CAM dimensions" varied over time.

> "While self-reports on affect and metacognition were more accurate in predicting learning productivity at the beginning of the instructional unit (based on students' scores across all unit tasks), the predictive accuracy of students' cognitive artifacts increased toward the end of the unit."

### Joni Lämsä 2026 (2)

Joni Lämsä, Susanne de Mooij, Martine Baars, Inge Molenaar, Sanna Järvelä. (2026). Self-Regulated Learning, Multimodal Data, and Analysis Grid: Where Are We Now and Where Are We Going? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10113-4

`q2 · i?` · `review · r1`

In the same study, the authors applied a range of machine learning methods; "boosting yielded the highest overall predictive accuracy" and showed the least gender bias in predictive accuracy. However, when predictive accuracy was examined by parental education background, the decision tree model outperformed boosting.

> "By applying various machine learn - ing methods, the authors found that boosting yielded the highest overall predictive accuracy. Boosting also showed the least bias regarding predictive accuracy by gen- der."

### Kubsch 2025

Kubsch, M., Strauß, S., Grimm, A., Gombert, S., Drachsler, H., Neumann, K., & Rummel, N. (2025). Self-regulated Learning in the Digitally Enhanced Science Classroom: Toward an Early Warning System. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10011-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-validation results across five timepoints (Fig. 3) comparing unimodal data-source combinations. Affective/metacognitive-only models showed "rather flat time-trends" while models including cognitive data showed increasing accuracy over time.

> "Interestingly, the models that used only cognitive data had a lower accuracy at timepoints P1 and P2 compared to the models that use only affective and/or metacognitive data. At time point P3, models that use only cognitive and models that used only affective, and/or metacognitive data, were similar in accuracy."
