# More teaching experience is associated with lower teacher self-efficacy and somewhat lower student-rated classroom management and student self-efficacy

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Years of teaching experience negatively predicted TSE at the class level and was introduced as a control variable. [→ Burić 2020](#buric-2020)

## Evidence

### Burić 2020

Burić, Irena and Kim, Lisa (2020) Teacher self-efficacy, instructional quality, and student motivational beliefs: An analysis using multilevel structural equation modeling. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2019.101302

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional survey of 94 Croatian high school teachers: experience showed significant negative correlations with TSE, cognitive activation, classroom management, and SSE at the teacher level, and negatively predicted TSE (β ≈ -.24) in all three structural models.

> "The results also revealed that more experienced teachers reported lower levels of TSE (β = -.239, p = .029; β = -.244, p = .028; and β = -.242, p = .028, for each of the models respectively)."
