# First-year teachers without mentors declined in self and impact concerns but task concerns stayed at 50%

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among 8 first-year teachers without mentors, self concerns fell from 50% to 25% and impact concerns rose from 0% to 25%, but task concerns remained at 50% from the first to third interviews, so the Fuller theory fit them less well. [→ Olsen 1990](#olsen-1990)

## Evidence

### Olsen 1990

Olsen, Dwayne G.; Heyse, Kathy L. (1990). Development and Concerns of First-year and Reentry Teachers with and without Mentors. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED323188

`q2 · i?`

Panel data from the same longitudinal study, for the 8 first-year teachers without assigned mentors across the three interviews. The authors conclude the Fuller theory "does explain the development of first-year teachers without mentors but not as well as the development of first-year teachers with mentors."

> "First-year teachers without mentors also decreased in concern for "self" (from 50% in intervlw one to 25% in interview three) but did not decline in concern for "task" issues from the first to the third interviews (50% in both interviews)"
