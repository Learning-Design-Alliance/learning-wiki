# Explicit CVS training combined with probe questions increased children's use of unconfounded experimental designs across hands-on phases, with transfer gains sustained in both transfer tasks.

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Only children in the Training-Probe condition improved their CVS use across the four hands-on phases. [→ Chen 1999](#chen-1999)
`q3 i?` In the Training-Probe condition, Assessment, Transfer-1, and Transfer-2 performance exceeded Exploration performance, with no differences among the three later phases. [→ Chen 1999 (2)](#chen-1999-2)

## Evidence

### Chen 1999

Zhe Chen and David Klahr. (1999). All Other Things Being Equal: Acquisition and Transfer of the Control of Variables Strategy. Child Development, 70(5), 1098-1120. https://www.jstor.org/stable/1132052

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Condition-level ANOVAs in the hands-on study (N = 87 second through fourth graders, 3 condition x 3 grade x 4 phase design) showed that "only children in the Training-Probe condition increased their performance over phases, F(3, 87) = 12.8, p \< .001"; the two No Training conditions did not improve.

> "Separate one-way analyses of variance (ANOVAs) for each condition revealed that only children in the Training-Probe condition increased their performance over phases, F(3, 87) = 12.8, p \< .001."

### Chen 1999 (2)

Zhe Chen and David Klahr. (1999). All Other Things Being Equal: Acquisition and Transfer of the Control of Variables Strategy. Child Development, 70(5), 1098-1120. https://www.jstor.org/stable/1132052

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within the Training-Probe condition, paired comparisons showed children "did better in the Assessment, Transfer-i, and Transfer-2 phases than in the Exploration phase", and mean CVS scores did not differ among the three later phases, indicating stable post-training use including transfer tasks.

> "Paired comparisons showed that children did better in the Assessment, Transfer-i, and Transfer-2 phases than in the Exploration phase, but there were no differences in mean CVS scores among the three later phases."
