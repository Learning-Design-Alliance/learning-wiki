# Obligation to attend at a fixed time and place produced higher commitment to face-to-face courses

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students experienced the fixed time and location of face-to-face courses as producing higher commitment and willingness to complete the course. [→ Keis Oliver 2017](#keis-oliver-2017)

## Evidence

### Keis Oliver 2017

Keis Oliver, Grab Claudia, Schneider Achim, Öchsner Wolfgang. (2017). Online or face-to-face instruction? A qualitative study on the electrocardiogram course at the University of Ulm to examine why students choose a particular format. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1053-6

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview analysis (qualitative content analysis, Mayring) of ten students; three respondents referred to the supportive character of fixed location and time, which "made students commit to participating". No effect size reported.

> "It is the obligation to be present on a particular day at a fixed time at a fixed location that results in face-to-face courses requiring a higher degree of commitment both concerning adequate preparation of the learning content and willingness to complete the course."
