# Motivation did not interact with visuospatial attention effects on post-stimulus visual evoked potentials to imperative stimuli

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For bilateral non-target stimuli, attention modulated C1, P1, N1 and P3, but no significant interactions with the factor of motivation were found. [→ Banerjee Snigdha 2015](#banerjee-snigdha-2015)

## Evidence

### Banerjee Snigdha 2015

Banerjee Snigdha, Frey Hans-Peter, Molholm Sophie, Foxe John J. (2015). Interests shape how adolescents pay attention: The interaction of motivation and top-down attentional processes in biasing sensory activations to anticipated events. The European Journal of Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1111/ejn.12810

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-stimulus VEP analysis of bilateral non-targets (average 85 trials per condition) in the cued attention task; attention effects were significant (e.g., P3 attended location × ROI F(1,23) = 68.37, p <.001) but motivation effects were not tested via main effects. Equivalence was not tested.

> "There were no significant interactions with the factor of motivation, and main effects of motivation were not considered as differences in features across high and low interest stimuli may have influenced the amplitudes of these components"
