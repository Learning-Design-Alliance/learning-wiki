# Students primarily used the Socratic-tutor chatbot as a study aid aligned with existing study habits rather than for the intended Socratic reflection

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Despite its Socratic design, students in the third cycle mainly used the chatbot to answer questions in line with their individual study habits, including collaborative study sessions and targeted queries about text details. [→ Hilliger 2026](#hilliger-2026)

## Evidence

### Hilliger 2026

Hilliger, I., Pérez-Sanagustín, M., González, C., Villalobos, E. and Celis, S. (2026). A GAI-Based Chatbot for Scaffolding Self-Regulated Learning: Insights from a Design-Based Research Approach. Journal of Learning Analytics, 13(1), 74–88. https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2026.9143

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Individual cognitive walkthroughs with five of eight active users, analyzed thematically. Students valued the chatbot's course-content specialization, calling it a kind of "ChatGPT of the course," and used it for clarifying concepts, study methods, and group study dialogue.

> "its use did not necessarily align with the intended Socratic model. Instead, students primarily used it to answer questions in line with their individual study habits."
