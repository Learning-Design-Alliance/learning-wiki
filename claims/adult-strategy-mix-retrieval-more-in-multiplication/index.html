Evidence¶

Bert De Smedt 2010¶

Bert De Smedt, Bart Boets. (2010). Phonological processing and arithmetic fact retrieval: Evidence from developmental dyslexia. Neuropsychologia, 48, 3973–3981. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2010.10.018

q3 · i2 · associational · r2

Strategy-distribution ANOVAs on trial-by-trial verbal reports in the 25 dyslexia vs 25 control study. Retrieval use showed an operation main effect (ηp² = .12) and a group main effect (F(1, 47) = 11.16, p < .01, ηp² = .19); procedure use showed the reverse pattern (operation F(1, 47) = 28.05, p < .01, ηp² = .37).

"There was a main effect of operation ( F(1, 47) = 6.13, p = .02, /DC1p2 = .12), indicating more frequent retrieval use in multiplication than in subtraction."

Related Claims¶

  • Adults with dyslexia execute both retrieval and procedural arithmetic strategies more slowly than controls
  • The association between phonological awareness and fact retrieval is prominent in multiplication but largely absent in subtraction at the subgroup level
  • Adults with developmental dyslexia use arithmetic fact retrieval less frequently than matched controls during single-digit multiplication and subtraction — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Adults with dyslexia are slower than controls on single-digit arithmetic, with a larger multiplication-minus-subtraction reaction-time difference, even after controlling digit identification speed — related
  • Phonological awareness is uniquely associated with multiplication fact retrieval frequency, in both dyslexia and control groups — related
