## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants whose lesson materials included labeling scored higher on the posttest than participants whose materials did not include labeling. [→ Clinton 2016](#clinton-2016)

## Evidence

### Clinton 2016

Clinton, V., Morsanyi, K., Alibali, M. W., & Nathan, M. J. (2016). Learning about Probability from Text and Tables: Do Color Coding and Labeling through an Interactive-user Interface Help? Applied Cognitive Psychology, 30(3), 440-453. https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.3223

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized 2 (color coding) × 2 (labeling) between-subjects ANCOVA of posttest scores with pretest and need for cognition as covariates in a study of 98 undergraduates. Labeling produced a significant advantage, reported by the article as "Cohen’s d^ = .50"; no standardized effect size in conventional notation was printed, so impact is not coded.

> "Participants whose materials included labeling scored higher on posttest than did participants whose materials did not include labeling, F(1, 95) = 5.64, p = .02, Cohen’s d^ = .50."
