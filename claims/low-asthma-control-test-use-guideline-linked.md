## Evidence

### Çifci İ. 2025

Çifci İ., Gök U., Yorgancıoğlu A. (2025). A current assessment of family physicians’ knowledge and attitudes toward asthma management in primary care in Turkey according to the GINA strategy report. NPJ Primary Care Respiratory Medicine 36, 5. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41533-025-00468-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Control-assessment item in the cross-sectional survey of 596 physicians: "61.2%" assessed control by the number of exacerbations and "11.9%" used an asthma control test; sputum eosinophilia testing is not available in Turkish family health centers.

> "In response to the question of which methods physicians use to assess asthma control, 61.2% of physicians answered by looking at the number of exacerbations. The proportion of physicians using an asthma control test was found to be 11.9%."

### Çifci İ. 2025 (2)

Çifci İ., Gök U., Yorgancıoğlu A. (2025). A current assessment of family physicians’ knowledge and attitudes toward asthma management in primary care in Turkey according to the GINA strategy report. NPJ Primary Care Respiratory Medicine 36, 5. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41533-025-00468-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Chi-square comparison in the same survey: physicians who read the asthma guideline in the past year applied the asthma control test at a significantly higher proportion than those who did not (p < 0.001); no effect size is reported.

> "When physicians were evaluated based on their reading of the asthma guideline over the past year, the proportion of those who applied the asthma control test was significantly higher among those who read the guideline compared to those who did not (p < 0.001)."
