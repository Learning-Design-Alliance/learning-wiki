# Students grading practice exams award significantly more points than a professional grader, about 25% more overall

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Freeman S. 2010

Freeman S., Parks J.W. (2010). How Accurate Is Peer Grading? CBE Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.10-03-0017

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized, double-blind comparison in Biology 180 (≈340 students): a professional grader blind to student identity re-graded randomly chosen peer-graded practice exams across five exercises. Paired t tests showed significant leniency each week (e.g., week 10 autumn: 6.45 vs 4.84, t = −11.45, p ≪ 0.0001).

> "In all five practice exams examined, students were significantly easier graders than the professional. Figure 1 shows the results from exercises graded by individual students versus the professional; Table 1 summarizes the results of paired t tests."

### Freeman S. 2010 (2)

Freeman S., Parks J.W. (2010). How Accurate Is Peer Grading? CBE Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.10-03-0017

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Across all scored exercises, the global average difference was computed: professional mean 5.43 versus student mean 6.76, a "24.5% increase in points awarded" — about 1.33 points per 10-point exercise, or 0.27 points per 2-point question.

> "The mean of professionally assigned scores on all of the exercises was 5.43; the mean of student-assigned scores was 6.76. The difference of 1.33 points per 5-question, 10-point exercise represents a 24.5% increase in points awarded to students due to peer versus professional grading."
