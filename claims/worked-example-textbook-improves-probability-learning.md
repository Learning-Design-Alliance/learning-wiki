# Students using a worked-example revised probability textbook improved more in probabilistic reasoning than students using the standard textbook

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Classrooms randomly assigned to the revised textbook with correct and incorrect worked examples and self-explanation prompts showed significantly greater probabilistic reasoning gains at posttest than control classrooms. [→ Begolli 2021](#begolli-2021)

## Evidence

### Begolli 2021

Begolli, K. N., Dai, T., McGinn, K. M., & Booth, J. L. (2021). Could probability be out of proportion? Self-explanation and example-based practice help students with lower proportional reasoning skills learn probability. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-021-09550-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Cluster-randomized classroom experiment with 220 seventh-graders in 10 classrooms (5 treatment, 5 control) using OLS regression with cluster sandwich estimator. The treatment predictor was significant: "bTreatment = 0.85, SE = 0.31, t = 2.78, p = 0.021". The final model explained 29.4% of variance.

> "the students who used the worked example revised textbook improved significantly more in Probabilistic Reasoning scores than students who used the standard CMP textbook, confirmed by a significant treatment predictor (bTreatment = 0.85, SE = 0.31, t = 2.78, p = 0.021)."
