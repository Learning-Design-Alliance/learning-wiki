# Students preferring combination-style chatbots had higher total EBAPS scores than students preferring direct-answer chatbots (nominal significance only)

> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Dunn's post-hoc test with Bonferroni correction revealed statistically different total-score distributions between the Answer and Combination groups (p = 0.029), with the Combination group higher. ## Evidence

### Amogh Sirnoorkar and Omkar Mamidpalliwar 2026

Amogh Sirnoorkar and Omkar Mamidpalliwar. (2026). Students' Epistemological Beliefs and their Chatbot Preferences in AI-mediated Physics Learning. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29385 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.29385](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2607.29385)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Dunn's post-hoc test with Bonferroni correction following the significant omnibus Kruskal-Wallis test on total EBAPS scores; Table II prints means of 69.7% (Combination) versus 67.9% (Answer). No effect size is printed for the pairwise contrast.

> "For the total score, the test revealed that Answer and Combination groups had statistically different distributions ( p = 0.029). Furthermore, students preferring combination-style chatbot had higher EBAPS scores than those who prefer direct answers."
