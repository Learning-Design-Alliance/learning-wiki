CSAC recipient survey (Figure 3) reports self-reported perceptions: "Nearly 3 in 4 (74%) respondents said the grant was influential or very influential in their decision to pursue teaching", with 93% citing importance for completing preparation and 69% for teaching in a priority school.

"Nearly 3 in 4 (74%) respondents said the grant was influential or very influential in their decision to pursue teaching. More than 9 in 10 respondents (93%) said it was important or very important for making it through preparation, and more than two thirds (69%) said the grant was influential or very influential in incentivizing them to teach in a priority school"

Leung-Gagné 2025 (2)¶

Leung-Gagné, M., Maria Castillo, M., Patrick, S. K., Carver-Thomas, D., Kini, T., Bingener, C., & Giani, M. V. (2025). Strengthening the educator pipeline through service scholarships: California's Golden State Teacher Grant Program. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/518.478

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Self-reported 2025 CSAC survey data (Figure 7) show "over 7 in 10 (71%) identified as a person of color", exceeding the approximately 57% of new teacher candidates statewide who were people of color; the report states perceived impacts were strongest among candidates of color.

"Among teacher candidate recipients who responded to the California Student Aid Commission (CSAC) survey in 2025, over 7 in 10 (71%) identified as a person of color."
