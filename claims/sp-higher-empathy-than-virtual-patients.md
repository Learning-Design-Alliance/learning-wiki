# Students reported significantly more empathy toward standardized patients than virtual patients, on both affective and cognitive subscales

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Affective empathy toward the patient differed significantly between units (η2p = 0.45), and a significant speaker-observer difference also emerged on this subscale (η2p = 0.10). [→ Graf Linda 2026](#graf-linda-2026)
`q3 i3` Cognitive empathy also differed significantly between units (η2p = 0.29), with no role difference or interaction. [→ Graf Linda 2026 (2)](#graf-linda-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Graf Linda 2026

Graf Linda, Golz Vera, Prehn Sophia, Masuch Maic, Gradl-Dietsch Gertraud. (2026). Virtual vs. standardized patients - a preliminary comparison of advantages and disadvantages of a VR and a conventional learning unit for medical students in child and adolescent psychiatry. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08760-4

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Mixed ANOVA on the State Empathy Scale affective subscale in the within-subjects study; the unit difference was "independent from which role the students had". No interaction effect was found.

> "The affective subscale measuring empathy towards the respective patients (virtual vs. standardized) differs significantly, F (1, 43) = 34.96, p < .001, η2p = 0.45. There was also a significant difference between speaker and observer (F (1, 43) = 4.80, p = .034, η2p = 0.10)"

### Graf Linda 2026 (2)

Graf Linda, Golz Vera, Prehn Sophia, Masuch Maic, Gradl-Dietsch Gertraud. (2026). Virtual vs. standardized patients - a preliminary comparison of advantages and disadvantages of a VR and a conventional learning unit for medical students in child and adolescent psychiatry. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08760-4

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Mixed ANOVA on the SES cognitive empathy subscale in the same study; virtual patients were still rated positively on cognitive empathy, though significantly less than standardized patients. No role difference or interaction.

> "Regarding the cognitive subscale, we also found a significant difference between the learning units (F (1, 43) = 17.9, p < .001, η2p = 0.29), but no difference between speaker and observer"
