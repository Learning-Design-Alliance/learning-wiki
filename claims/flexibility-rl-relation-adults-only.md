## Evidence

### Bamberg 2025

Bamberg, C., Weigelt, S. & Hagelweide, K. (2025). Reversal learning is influenced by cognitive flexibility and develops throughout early adolescence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00308-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Separate linear regressions per age group (Table 2) showed the adult flexible-reactions coefficient was 0.46 (p < 0.001), while early-adolescent (0.23, p = 0.201) and mid-adolescent (0.22, p = 0.263) coefficients were not significant.

> "Indeed, results show no signiﬁcant relation between ﬂexible reactions and RL performance in early-adolescents or mid-adolescents while a signiﬁcant association was observed in adults (Table 2;F i g .4)."

### Bamberg 2025 (2)

Bamberg, C., Weigelt, S. & Hagelweide, K. (2025). Reversal learning is influenced by cognitive flexibility and develops throughout early adolescence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00308-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In a regression predicting RL from age, flexible responses and their interaction, "coeff = 0.03, SE = 0.02, 95% CI = [−0.01, 0.07],p = 0.174" for the interaction; the article concludes flexible reactions related to better RL irrespective of age, but most pronounced for adults.

> "we found no signiﬁcant interaction between age andﬂexible reactions (coeff = 0.03, SE = 0.02, 95% CI = [−0.01, 0.07],p = 0.174) while age andﬂexible responses still signiﬁcantly predicted RL performance."
