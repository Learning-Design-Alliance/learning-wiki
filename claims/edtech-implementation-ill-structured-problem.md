## Evidence

### Scott Allen 2024

Scott Allen, Lisa Bardach, Jamie Jirout, Allyson Mackey, Dana McCoy, Luca Maria Pesando, and René Kizilcec. (2024). Implementing New Technology in Educational Systems. In Human-Technology Partnerships at Work, Cambridge University Press. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2409.12039](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2409.12039)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The chapter's authors argue, citing prior literature, that the key point of failure is not the technology's capabilities but the incorporation process. This is an authors' argument, not a tested result, and the chapter offers no new empirical data for it.

> "Even when the technology works as promised and the problem it is intended to solve is a well-structured one, incorporating that solution into an educational organization is a remarkably ill-structured problem"

### Scott Allen 2024 (2)

Scott Allen, Lisa Bardach, Jamie Jirout, Allyson Mackey, Dana McCoy, Luca Maria Pesando, and René Kizilcec. (2024). Implementing New Technology in Educational Systems. In Human-Technology Partnerships at Work, Cambridge University Press. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2409.12039](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2409.12039)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The authors narrate the 2013 Los Angeles Unified School District case, citing Lin (2015) and Lapowski (2015), as an example of implementing technology without assessing needs; the case is reported second-hand, not studied here.

> "Without a discernible need for the new devices, many teachers coped with the disruption by simply not using them (Lin, 2015; Lapowski, 2015)"
