# High CT students significantly exceeded low CT students in task strategies, elaboration, and self-assessment during AICA interactions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Chi-square tests found significant differences favoring high CT students in task strategies (TS), elaboration (E), and self-assessment (SA), with no significant differences in IV, SE, CrT, SH, IE, or SS. [→ Shu Zhao 2026](#shu-zhao-2026)

## Evidence

### Shu Zhao 2026

Shu Zhao, Ziqi Wang, Tingting Chen, Chunchen Kang, Yu-Ju Lan. (2026). Exploring the effect of computational thinking levels on students' learning performance, cognition, and behavior when using AI coding assistants. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10663-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Chi-square tests on frequencies of cognitive behaviors coded from reflection journals using an SRL scheme (Table 5 prints TS χ2 = 11.226, p =.001; E χ2 = 18.281, p =.000; SA χ2 = 7.239, p =.007). The article reports "Significant differences were observed in TS, E, and SA"; no effect sizes are printed.

> "Significant differences were observed in TS, E, and SA indicating that high CT students outperformed low CT students. No significant differences were observed in IV, SE, CrT, SH, IE, and SS."
