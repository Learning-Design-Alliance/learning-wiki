## Evidence

### Sarah Lindley and Chandralekha Singh 2025

Sarah Lindley and Chandralekha Singh. (2025). Becoming a physicist: Major educational transition points impact women's physics self-efficacy and sense of belonging. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2510.12024](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2510.12024)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative thematic analysis of six semi-structured interviews with female physics majors; the discussion section reports that four interviewees' statements suggest self-efficacy declines "as a result of their introductory classes." No effect size is reported.

> "Moving into the first year of college, statements from Kayla, Molly, Mary, and Susie suggest declines in physics self-efficacy as a result of their introductory classes."

### Sarah Lindley and Chandralekha Singh 2025 (2)

Sarah Lindley and Chandralekha Singh. (2025). Becoming a physicist: Major educational transition points impact women's physics self-efficacy and sense of belonging. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2510.12024](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2510.12024)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In the same interview study, the discussion of the second transition reports that as women moved into major courses they "continued to experience declines in their self-efficacy and sense of belonging in physics." Qualitative finding; no effect size printed.

> "As the women progressed into the rest of the physics major, they continued to experience declines in their self-efficacy and sense of belonging in physics."
