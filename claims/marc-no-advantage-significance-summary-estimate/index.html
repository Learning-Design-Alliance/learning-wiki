Kaitlyn G. Fitzgerald and Elizabeth Tipton. (2022). The Meta-Analytic Rain Cloud Plot: A New Approach to Visualizing Clearinghouse Data. Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness. https://doi.org/10.1080/19345747.2022.2031366

RQ1 results (Table 5): Q7 proportions were 0.476 (FP), 0.458 (BP), 0.420 (RFP), 0.500 (MARC); Q6 proportions 0.598-0.667. The article reports "the MARC plot did not offer any advantages over the other visualizations in making this determination."

"Overall, practitioners struggled to determine whether or not there was sufficient evidence to conclude the new curriculum improves scores (as determined by statistical significance), with only roughly half answering correctly (Q7). Despite the new design features, the MARC plot did not offer any advantages over the other visualizations in making this determination."

Related Claims¶

  • In a pre-registered experiment, visualization type significantly affected education practitioners' accuracy in interpreting meta-analytic evidence, with the MARC plot outperforming bar plots, forest plots, and rainforest plots — reports the opposite
  • The MARC plot enabled 89% of practitioners to identify the least certain study, versus less than 70% for the other three visualizations — related
  • The MARC plot's direct y-axis encoding of study weight enabled 95% of practitioners to identify the most-weighted study, versus fewer than 6 in 10 for the other three visualizations — related
  • Education researchers outperformed education practitioners on all four visualizations, with the largest gaps on forest and rainforest plots; differences were significant for FP and RFP but not BP — related
  • Visualization type did not significantly affect practitioners' subjective ratings of evidence strength and curriculum effectiveness, though the magnitude of the summary effect and its interaction with statistical significance did — related
