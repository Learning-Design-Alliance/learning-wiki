Evidence¶

Turgut 2015¶

Turgut, F., & Kayaoğlu, M. N. (2015). Using rubrics as an instructional tool in EFL writing courses. Journal of Language and Linguistic Studies, 11(1), 47-58. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1105202

q1 · i? · causal · r1

Interview content analysis by rubric category. A student reported becoming "more aware of in particular, audience and purpose"; others reported attending to introductory, supporting and concluding paragraphs, transitional markers, and frequent strategic dictionary use, including a shift toward monolingual English dictionaries.

"Formerly, I did not have much anxiet y for the content. After the rubrics, I became more aware of in particular, audience and purpose. There should be a thesis statement with supporting ideas."

Turgut 2015 (2)¶

Turgut, F., & Kayaoğlu, M. N. (2015). Using rubrics as an instructional tool in EFL writing courses. Journal of Language and Linguistic Studies, 11(1), 47-58. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1105202

q1 · i? · causal · r1

Interview analysis of the mechanics category: students gave it the least attention for the two stated reasons and because of its low point weight. For language use, students reported attempting more complicated sentences and varied grammatical structures.

"The category of mechanics received the least attention from the students for two reasons: first, students use spelling software programs on computers when they hand in their papers; secondly, they consider this a ma tter of personal attention rather than lack of know ledge. Additionally, this category gets only 5 point out of 100."
