## Evidence

### Najjuma Josephine N. 2024

Najjuma Josephine N., Muhumuza Albert, Santorino Data, Sekyere Samuel Owusu, Ocheke Amaka N., Yiltok Simon J., Bajunirwe Francis. (2024). Barriers and facilitators to interprofessional simulation-based learning in a Ugandan medical school: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06521-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

A student in focus group described facilitator bias: "If it's a doctor who is taking us through the debriefing... They will concentrate more on the medicine side because that's what they are comfortable with," suggesting paired doctor-nurse debrief facilitation would project both groups' roles.

> "If it's a doctor who is taking us through the debriefing, remember, we are with medicine students. They will concentrate more on the medicine side because that's what they are comfortable with."

### Najjuma Josephine N. 2024 (2)

Najjuma Josephine N., Muhumuza Albert, Santorino Data, Sekyere Samuel Owusu, Ocheke Amaka N., Yiltok Simon J., Bajunirwe Francis. (2024). Barriers and facilitators to interprofessional simulation-based learning in a Ugandan medical school: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06521-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

An administrator-faculty reported resistance to change among faculty: "some people are so static, they don't want to change." Fifth-year students also felt mannequin practice was unnecessary after managing real patients on wards, suggesting simulation should end at third-year level.

> "Many people don't think they need it… they're thinking of, this is how we have always done it, some people are so static, they don't want to change."
