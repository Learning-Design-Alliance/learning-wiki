# Students in a large biochemistry course using tutor-less PBL showed a statistically significant ~13% improvement in generic problem-solving test scores over one term, while control courses without PBL showed no significant change

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2013 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` BIOC students exposed to tutor-less PBL improved their generic problem-solving test scores significantly over the term (blinded analysis P = 0.04). [→ Klegeris Andis 2013](#klegeris-andis-2013)
`q2 i?` No trend toward significant change in scores was observed in any of the three control courses not using PBL. [→ Klegeris Andis 2013 (2)](#klegeris-andis-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Klegeris Andis 2013

Klegeris Andis, Bahniwal Manpreet, Hurren Heather. (2013). Improvement in Generic Problem-Solving Abilities of Students by Use of Tutor-less Problem-Based Learning in a Large Classroom Setting. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.12-06-0081

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Blinded regrading of BIOC course tests by five independent markers, analyzed with a generalized linear model randomized block design with term (September vs December) as fixed factor, confirmed a statistically significant (P = 0.04) beginning-to-end-of-term score difference; blinded markers recorded ~13% improvement versus ~15% by the original unblinded marker.

> "This analysis confirmed a statistically significant (P = 0.04) difference between the average scores obtained at the beginning and end of the term."

### Klegeris Andis 2013 (2)

Klegeris Andis, Bahniwal Manpreet, Hurren Heather. (2013). Improvement in Generic Problem-Solving Abilities of Students by Use of Tutor-less Problem-Based Learning in a Large Classroom Setting. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.12-06-0081

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Blinded marking of the three control courses' tests by a single independent marker showed no trends toward significant differences between beginning and end of term, with the lowest P value being 0.69; equivalence was not tested.

> "The data presented in Table 4 show that, even after blinded marking, no trends toward significant differences were observed, with the lowest P value being 0.69"
