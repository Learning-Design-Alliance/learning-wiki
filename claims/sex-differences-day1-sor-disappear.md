## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Sommer GM 2021

Sommer GM, Broschewitz J, Huppert S, Sommer CG, Jahn N, Jansen-Winkeln B, Gockel I, Hau HM. (2021). The role of virtual reality simulation in surgical training in the light of COVID-19 pandemic: Visual spatial ability as a predictor for improved surgical performance: a randomized trial. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000027844

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subgroup analysis of the randomized trial's simulator task data (Table 3) compared men and women in the training group on day 1. Men showed "significantly better results in the performance of hand movement and reduced completion time" on the SOR task only; no effect size is printed.

> "Male participants depicted significantly better results in the performance of hand movement and reduced completion time on day 1 than women in the basic simulator task “SOR”, but not for the complex CD task"

### Sommer GM 2021 (2)

Sommer GM, Broschewitz J, Huppert S, Sommer CG, Jahn N, Jansen-Winkeln B, Gockel I, Hau HM. (2021). The role of virtual reality simulation in surgical training in the light of COVID-19 pandemic: Visual spatial ability as a predictor for improved surgical performance: a randomized trial. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000027844

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same subgroup analysis found that during the training process there were "no significant differences regarding surgical performance and times required to complete the exercises between men and women in the TG"; the null test is reported without an effect size, so equivalence was not tested.

> "During the training process no significant differences regarding surgical performance and times required to complete the exercises between men and women in the TG were observed"
