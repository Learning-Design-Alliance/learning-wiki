Evidence¶

Allensworth 2019¶

Allensworth, Elaine M., & Clark, Kallie. (2019). Are GPAs an Inconsistent Measure of College Readiness across High Schools? Examining Assumptions about Grades versus Standardized Test Scores. University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/are-gpas-inconsistent-measure-achievement-across-high-schools-examining-assumptions

q2 · i? · associational · r3

Non-parametric binned HSGPA models with school-level random effects on graduation, controlling for student background and college institutional variables. The article reports "significant high school variance in college graduation rates for students in each HSGPA bin".

"There is significant high school variance in college graduation rates for students in each HSGPA bin. For example, among students with HSGPAs between 3.25-3.5, a two-standard deviation range of high school effects is 0.144 ± 0.575 in log-odds in the conditional model."

Allensworth 2019 (2)¶

Allensworth, Elaine M., & Clark, Kallie. (2019). Are GPAs an Inconsistent Measure of College Readiness across High Schools? Examining Assumptions about Grades versus Standardized Test Scores. University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/are-gpas-inconsistent-measure-achievement-across-high-schools-examining-assumptions

q2 · i? · associational · r3

Comparison of the continuous-HSGPA model's intercept variance (0.603) against a controls-only model (0.447). The authors interpret the 26% extra variance as consistent with "frogpond" effects wherein HSGPAs are suppressed at schools with more positive school effects.

"About one-fourth of the school-level variation in Model 2 ((0.603-0.447)/0.603 = 26%) is “extra” variation that is induced by comparing students with similar HSGPAs."
