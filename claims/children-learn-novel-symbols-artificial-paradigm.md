## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Children aged 6-10 learned novel numerical symbols from passive training with dot arrays and performed significantly above chance on symbolic comparison. [→ Amy Bennett 2018](#amy-bennett-2018)

## Evidence

### Amy Bennett 2018

Amy Bennett, Matthew Inglis and Camilla Gilmore. (2018). The Cost of Multiple Representations: Learning Number Symbols with Abstract and Concrete Representations. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000318

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1, a within-subjects study of 74 children (6-10 years), trained participants on five novel symbols per condition and tested symbolic comparison. Accuracy was above chance in both conditions: abstract "M= .72, SD = .166" and multiple-concrete M = .66. No effect size printed.

> "Children were significantly more accurate than chance (0.5) in both the abstract, M= .72, SD = .166, t(73) = 11.41, p < .001, and multiple-concrete conditions, M = .66, SD = .195, t(73) = 7.25, p < .001"
