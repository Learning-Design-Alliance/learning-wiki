## Evidence

### Walsh Jason L 2018

Walsh Jason L, Harris Benjamin H L, Denny Paul, Smith Phil. (2018). Formative student-authored question bank: perceptions, question quality and association with summative performance. Postgraduate Medical Journal. https://doi.org/10.1136/postgradmedj-2017-135018

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Students were divided into usage categories (table 1) and their summative scores compared by one-way ANOVA with subsequent t-tests. The omnibus test was significant at "p<0.0001"; no effect size is printed.

> "One-way ANOVA showed that there were significant differences in mean summative examination performance between the writing, answering and commenting frequency groups (p<0.0001)."

### Walsh Jason L 2018 (2)

Walsh Jason L, Harris Benjamin H L, Denny Paul, Smith Phil. (2018). Formative student-authored question bank: perceptions, question quality and association with summative performance. Postgraduate Medical Journal. https://doi.org/10.1136/postgradmedj-2017-135018

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Post-hoc t-tests on answering frequency categories found only the prolific group (≥1000 answers) differed: "Prolific answerers scored significantly higher than all other groups (p<0.0001)", with no stepwise differences among the other three groups.

> "Prolific answerers scored significantly higher than all other groups (p<0.0001). There were no significant differences between the mean summative scores of the rare, occasional and frequent question answering frequency groups (figure 2C)."
