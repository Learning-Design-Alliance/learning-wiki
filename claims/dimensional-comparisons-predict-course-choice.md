# Dimensional comparisons predict cross-domain academic course and major choice, with negative effects of verbal-domain beliefs on math-related choices

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Several studies found negative cross-domain effects of self-concepts, task values, and expectancy beliefs on course and major choice, indicating dimensional comparisons in academic choices. [→ Jens Möller 2024](#jens-moller-2024)
`q2 i?` Findings are not fully consistent: some studies did not find cross-domain effects of grades on career plans. [→ Jens Möller 2024 (2)](#jens-moller-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Jens Möller 2024

Jens Möller. (2024). Ten Years of Dimensional Comparison Theory: On the Development of a Theory from Educational Psychology. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09918-6

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports multiple studies on choice: Nagy et al. (2008) found English interest decreased likelihood of taking advanced math courses in German students; Umarji et al. (2018) found English self-concept negatively associated with math-related majors; Wan et al. (2023) found English and biology task values negatively related to math intensiveness of majors.

> "Von Keyserlingk et al. (2021) also showed negative cross-domain effects of self-concepts on university major choice (i.e., German self-concepts negatively predicted math course choice)."

### Jens Möller 2024 (2)

Jens Möller. (2024). Ten Years of Dimensional Comparison Theory: On the Development of a Theory from Educational Psychology. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09918-6

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review notes null findings alongside positive ones: Nagy et al. (2008) did not replicate the English-interest finding in American students, and Lazarides and Lauerman (2019) found no cross-domain grade effects on career plans.

> "However, Lazarides and Lauerman (2019) did not find cross-domain effects of grades on career plans in math and language-related fields."
