## Subclaims
`q3 i?` VRS scenarios achieved above-average item discrimination (r'=0.40 septic, r'=0.33 anaphylactic) versus below-average PHS values (0.12 and 0.25), and the highest discrimination index values (D=0.25 and 0.26) versus PHS values of 0.10 and 0.12. [→ Mühling T 2025](#muhling-t-2025)

## Evidence

### Mühling T 2025

Mühling T, Schreiner V, Appel M, Leutritz T, König S. (2025). Comparing Virtual Reality–Based and Traditional Physical Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE) Stations for Clinical Competency Assessments: Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/55066

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Item-characteristic analysis of the randomized OSCE comparison (Results, Table 1): VRS discrimination values of 0.40 and 0.33 exceeded the all-station average of r'=0.30, while the PHS scenarios showed "below-average values of 0.12 and 0.25".

> "The VRS scenarios proved to be above average with values of 0.40 (septic shock) and 0.33 (anaphylactic shock), in contrast to the PHS scenarios, for which below-average values of 0.12 and 0.25, respectively, were determined"

### Mühling T 2025 (2)

Mühling T, Schreiner V, Appel M, Leutritz T, König S. (2025). Comparing Virtual Reality–Based and Traditional Physical Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE) Stations for Clinical Competency Assessments: Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/55066

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Discrimination index analysis across all OSCE stations in the same randomized comparison: against an all-station average D of 0.16, "The VRS scenarios outperformed all other stations, achieving the highest values of 0.25 and 0.26", while PHS values fell below the average.

> "When calculating the discrimination index D, an average of 0.16 was observed across all stations. The VRS scenarios outperformed all other stations, achieving the highest values of 0.25 and 0.26"
