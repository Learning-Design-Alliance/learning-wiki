## Evidence

### Acaray Ali 2026

Acaray Ali. (2026). How digital stress affects academic procrastination and subjective well-being: mediating effect of ego depletion. BMC Psychology, 14, 449. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-04164-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Independent-samples t-tests in the 768-student survey. Printed group means show males higher on availability stress and procrastination, females higher on approval anxiety and online vigilance, all "p < 0.05"; no effect sizes are printed.

> "Gender was significantly associated with availability stress (x̄female=2.31, x̄male=2.49, p < 0.05), approval anxiety (x̄female=2.00, x̄male = 1.86, p < 0.05), online vigilance (x̄female=3.08, x̄male = 2.85, p < 0.05), and academic procrastination (x̄female = 2.42, x̄male = 2.62, p < 0.05) variables."

### Acaray Ali 2026 (2)

Acaray Ali. (2026). How digital stress affects academic procrastination and subjective well-being: mediating effect of ego depletion. BMC Psychology, 14, 449. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-04164-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

One-way ANOVA across daily social-media-usage groups in the same survey; printed means rise from 2.44 in the 0–2 h group to 3.49 in the 6–8 h group, all reported at "p < 0.05", with no effect sizes printed.

> "Online vigilance increased with social media usage time, showing significant differences between nearly all usage groups, particularly between the lowest (0–2 h) and highest (> 6 h) usage groups ((x̄0−2 h=2.44, x̄2−4 h=2.85, p < 0.05), (x̄0−2 h=2.44, x̄4−6 h=3.06, p < 0.05), (x̄0−2 h = 2.44, x̄6−8 h = 3.49, p < 0.05), (x̄0−2 h = 2.44, x̄>8 h = 3.37, p < 0.05)"
