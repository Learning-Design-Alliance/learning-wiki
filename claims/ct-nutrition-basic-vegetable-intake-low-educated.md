## Evidence

### Springvloet 2015

Springvloet, L., Lechner, L., de Vries, H., & Oenema, A. (2015). Long-term efficacy of a Web-based computer-tailored nutrition education intervention for adults including cognitive and environmental feedback: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Public Health, 15, 372. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-015-1707-4

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r3`

Linear mixed model analysis of a three-group RCT (n = 1349 analyzed; low/moderate-educated subgroup n = 733), baseline to nine months. The basic version beat "the control program (ES = 0.32, p = .001)" and the plus version (ES = 0.22, p = .03) on vegetable intake change.

> "Among low/moderate-educated participants in the total sample the basic version resulted in a larger increase in vegetable intake compared to the control program (ES = 0.32, p = .001) and the plus version (ES = 0.22, p = .03)"

### Springvloet 2015 (2)

Springvloet, L., Lechner, L., de Vries, H., & Oenema, A. (2015). Long-term efficacy of a Web-based computer-tailored nutrition education intervention for adults including cognitive and environmental feedback: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Public Health, 15, 372. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-015-1707-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Type III tests in the linear mixed models for the three-group RCT (total sample n = 1349). The three-way education moderation was significant, while among "high-educated participants in the total sample" no group differences were found (p = .25), and no differences emerged in the risk group (p = .23; p = .07).

> "For vegetable intake, the interaction of 'group X time X education' was significant in both the total sample (p = .02) and risk group (p = .03)"
