# Number of learning modalities used in Learn Your Way was not significantly related to recall performance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2026 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Immediate recall did not differ between Immersive Text-only users (n = 12) and users of additional modalities (n = 18) (Z = 1.48, p = .144, r = .27), and modality usage did not correlate with immediate recall (Pearson's r = .24, p = .206). [→ Courtney Heldreth 2026](#courtney-heldreth-2026)
`q2 i?` Long-term recall also did not differ between modality-usage groups (Z = 0.87, p = .396, r = .16) and did not correlate with usage (Pearson's r = .17, p = .380). [→ Courtney Heldreth 2026 (2)](#courtney-heldreth-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Courtney Heldreth 2026

Courtney Heldreth, Diana Akrong, Laura M. Vardoulakis, Nicole E. Miller, Yael Haramaty, Lidan Hackmon, Lior Belinsky, Abraham Ortiz Tapia, Lucy Tootill, and Scott Siebert. (2026). An Experimental Evaluation of an AI-Powered Interactive Learning Platform. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.3389/frai.2026.1783117](https://doi.org/10.3389/frai.2026.1783117)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory analysis splitting the 30 Learn Your Way participants by modality usage found "no significant difference between groups" for immediate recall and no significant correlation, though scores were directionally higher with more modalities.

> "A two-sided Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney test revealed no significant difference between groups (Z = 1.48, p = .144, r = .27). Furthermore, while we see, directionally, that scores are higher for students that used more modalities in Learn Your Way, we did not find a significant correlation between learning modality usage and performance on the immediate recall assessment (Pearson'sr = .24, p = .206)"

### Courtney Heldreth 2026 (2)

Courtney Heldreth, Diana Akrong, Laura M. Vardoulakis, Nicole E. Miller, Yael Haramaty, Lidan Hackmon, Lior Belinsky, Abraham Ortiz Tapia, Lucy Tootill, and Scott Siebert. (2026). An Experimental Evaluation of an AI-Powered Interactive Learning Platform. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.3389/frai.2026.1783117](https://doi.org/10.3389/frai.2026.1783117)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same exploratory comparison for the long-term assessment (n = 29) found no significant group difference and no significant correlation with modality usage.

> "we did not find a significant difference between Immersive Text + QuizMe only (n = 12; M dn = 4 .5; M = 4 .4, SD = 1 .3) vs. Immersive Text + any additional modality(ies) (n = 17; M dn = 5; M = 4.8, SD = 1.1) for long-term recall scores (Z = 0.87, p = .396, r = .16) nor did we find a significant correlation (Pearson'sr = .17, p = .380)"
