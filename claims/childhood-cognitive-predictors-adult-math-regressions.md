## Evidence

### Henry 2025

Henry, L. N., Gross, R. A., & Hinshaw, S. P. (2025). Mathematics Achievement in Women with and without ADHD: Childhood Predictors and Developmental Trajectories into Adulthood. Journal of Learning Disabilities, 58(6). https://doi.org/10.1177/00222194241301044

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Preliminary regressions from Wave 1 cognitive measures to Wave 4 math scores, covarying ADHD status and SES, applying a false discovery rate of 0.05. All three predictors were then carried into LGCMs.

> "regression results between Wave 1 predictors and Wave 4 math, covarying ADHD status and SES, and with Benjamini-Hochberg alpha corrections, revealed that global EF errors (with an additional covariate of PIQ, β = −0.14 t =−2.19, p = 0.03, ΔR2 Adj. = 0.02), PS (β = 0.34, t = 5.49, p < 0.001, ΔR2 Adj. = 0.10), and WM (β = 0.32, t = 4.78, p <.001, ΔR2 Adj. = 0.06) significantly predicted Wave 4 math."

### Henry 2025 (2)

Henry, L. N., Gross, R. A., & Hinshaw, S. P. (2025). Mathematics Achievement in Women with and without ADHD: Childhood Predictors and Developmental Trajectories into Adulthood. Journal of Learning Disabilities, 58(6). https://doi.org/10.1177/00222194241301044

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Exploratory secondary regressions reconducted with childhood diagnosis as a potential moderator found the predictor–math relations did not differ by group; the authors describe childhood EF as possibly a transdiagnostic indicator of later math growth.

> "These predictive relations were not different between the ADHD and comparison groups."
