## Evidence

### Gerd Kortemeyer and Wolfgang Bauer 2024

Gerd Kortemeyer and Wolfgang Bauer. (2024). Cheat sites and artificial intelligence usage in online introductory physics courses: what is the extent and what effect does it have on assessments? arXiv preprint. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010145](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010145)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Classical item analysis (Table IV) of homework and online-exam items across the four usage clusters: mean percent correct and point-biserial discrimination "only insignificantly differ between different classes of online resource usage patterns". No effect size is printed.

> "The values only insignificantly differ between different classes of online resource usage patterns. Overall, they are consistent with the low predictive power of these un- supervised assessments, but they still indicate that items can provide feedback to students and instructors."

### Gerd Kortemeyer and Wolfgang Bauer 2024 (2)

Gerd Kortemeyer and Wolfgang Bauer. (2024). Cheat sites and artificial intelligence usage in online introductory physics courses: what is the extent and what effect does it have on assessments? arXiv preprint. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010145](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010145)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Secondary quartile analysis using the final exam as proxy for learning success: no difference between higher and lower quartiles in benefiting or being harmed by online resources; "we found no difference in that regard between these populations". Equivalence was not formally tested.

> "higher or lower performing quartiles of the students may have benefitted or been harmed by the use of online resources, but we found no difference in that regard between these populations."
