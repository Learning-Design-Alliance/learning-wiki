# Non-shared environmental variance in educational performance decreases with increasing school SES, but this moderation appears attributable to parental SES

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Non-shared environmental variance statistically significantly decreases with increasing school SES in the model without parental SES. [→ Kim Stienstra 2024](#kim-stienstra-2024)
`q2 i?` Once parental SES is controlled, the non-shared environmental moderation by school SES is no longer present. [→ Kim Stienstra 2024 (2)](#kim-stienstra-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Kim Stienstra 2024

Kim Stienstra, Antonie Knigge & Ineke Maas. (2024). Gene-environment interaction analysis of school quality and educational inequality. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00225-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

ACE-moderation Model 2 (school SES only) on the twin sample. The article reports the non-shared environmental moderation as unexpected but statistically significant: "it statistically signiﬁcantly decreases with increasing school SES".

> "Although we did not have expectations on the moderation of non-shared environmental variance, we ﬁnd that it statistically signiﬁcantly decreases with increasing school SES (Model 2, Table 2)."

### Kim Stienstra 2024 (2)

Kim Stienstra, Antonie Knigge & Ineke Maas. (2024). Gene-environment interaction analysis of school quality and educational inequality. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00225-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Model comparison in the same analysis: adding parental SES as moderator (Model 4) removes the school-SES moderation of non-shared environmental variance found in Model 3, attributing it to family SES.

> "the decreasing non-shared environmental variance by school SES that we found in Model 3 (Table 2), appears to be attributable to parental SES (see Model 4, Table 2)."
