## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Synchronous students valued immediate lecturer feedback, while asynchronous students reported that delaying questions allowed deeper reflection (theme 'Different Forms of Learning Interaction'). [→ Battista Simone 2023](#battista-simone-2023)

## Evidence

### Battista Simone 2023

Battista Simone, Furri Laura, de Biagi Giorgia, Depedri Lucia, Broggi Valeria, Testa Marco. (2023). Clinical records after asynchronous and synchronous e-learning courses: a multi-method randomised controlled trial on students’ performance and experience. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04528-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Reflexive thematic analysis of semi-structured interviews with 30 of the 50 students (15 per group); theme 1 contrasted synchronous "immediate feedback" with asynchronous delayed questioning. The authors interpret this as "reflection-in-action" versus "reflection-on-action".

> "the possibility of pushing back the question time seemed to allow the students to reflect deeper on what they had learned, making the questions more targeted and well-reasoned."
