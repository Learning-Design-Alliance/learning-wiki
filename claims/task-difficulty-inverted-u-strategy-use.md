# Task difficulty shows an inverted U-shaped relation with cognitive strategy use and a positive linear relation with metacognitive strategy use, mediated by cognitive load (Seufert et al., 2024)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Cognitive strategy use follows an inverted U-shaped relation with task difficulty, validating the Seufert (2018) model. [→ de Bruin 2025](#de-bruin-2025)
`q2 i?` Metacognitive strategy use increases linearly with task difficulty; cognitive load mediates both relationships while learner resources show competitive mediation. [→ de Bruin 2025 (2)](#de-bruin-2025-2)

## Evidence

### de Bruin 2025

de Bruin, A. B. H., Janssen, E. M., Waldeyer, J., & Stebner, F. (2025). Cognitive Load and Challenges in Self-regulation: An Introduction and Reflection on the Topical Collection. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10042-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Within-subjects study with 67 participants by Seufert et al. (2024) examining cognitive and metacognitive strategy use across tasks of varying difficulty. The editorial reports the inverted U-shaped pattern with no printed effect size.

> "Results showed an inverted U-shaped relationship between task difficulty and learners’ use of cognitive strategies, validating the Seufert (2018) model."

### de Bruin 2025 (2)

de Bruin, A. B. H., Janssen, E. M., Waldeyer, J., & Stebner, F. (2025). Cognitive Load and Challenges in Self-regulation: An Introduction and Reflection on the Topical Collection. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10042-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Same within-subjects study (67 participants) reporting the linear difficulty–metacognitive-strategy relation and mediation by cognitive load. The editorial notes competitive mediation by learner resources, suggesting further mediators.

> "A positive linear relationship was observed between task difficulty and learners’ use of metacognitive strategies. Cognitive load mediated both relationships, while learner resources showed competitive mediation, suggesting the relevance of further mediators."
