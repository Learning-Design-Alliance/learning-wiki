## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Pearson correlations between first-semester Ph.D. GPA and GRE verbal (r = 0.20, p = 0.24), writing (r = 0.21, p = 0.21), quantitative (r = 0.13, p = 0.44), and undergraduate GPA (r = 0.21, p = 0.22) were all weak and non-significant. [→ Williams Tremaine B. 2021](#williams-tremaine-b-2021)

## Evidence

### Williams Tremaine B. 2021

Williams Tremaine B., Prince Latrina Y., Allen Antiño R., Sterba Kristen M., Thomas Billy R., McGehee Robert E. (2021). Performance measures of racially underrepresented Ph.D. students in biomedical sciences: The UAMS IMSD Program Outcomes. PLoS ONE 16(2): e0246683. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0246683

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pearson correlation analysis of 37 IMSD trainees relating first-semester Ph.D. GPA to GRE verbal, writing, and quantitative scores and undergraduate GPA (Figs 3–6). All coefficients were weak (0.13–0.21) with p-values above .05; the article concludes it is improbable that GRE scores could serve as predictors of UR trainees' first-semester Ph.D. GPA.

> "Pearson’s correlation coefficient of 0.20 reflected a weak relationship between first semester Ph.D. GPA and GRE verbal score. Given the p-value of 0.24 was above the .05 α-level, no significant relationship existed between a UR trainee’s first-semester Ph.D. GPA and their GRE verbal score."
