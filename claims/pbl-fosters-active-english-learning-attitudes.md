## Evidence

### Lin 2017

Lin, L.-F. (2017). Impacts of the Problem-based Learning Pedagogy on English Learners’ Reading Comprehension, Strategy Use, and Active Learning Attitudes. Journal of Education and Training Studies, 5(6). https://doi.org/10.11114/jets.v5i6.2320

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Independent samples t test on the 20-item AELAs questionnaire (7-point scales; PBL n = 26, non-PBL n = 30) after the 10-week treatment. The PBL group's total mean was significantly higher. No effect size is printed.

> "the total mean score of the PBL group ( M = 4.71) was higher than that of the non-PBL group ( M = 4.39). Furthermore, the Independent Samples t tests revealed a significant difference between the two groups ( t = 2.36, p < .05)"

### Lin 2017 (2)

Lin, L.-F. (2017). Impacts of the Problem-based Learning Pedagogy on English Learners’ Reading Comprehension, Strategy Use, and Active Learning Attitudes. Journal of Education and Training Studies, 5(6). https://doi.org/10.11114/jets.v5i6.2320

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subscale t tests from Table 5: MI (t = 2.299, p = .025), DLE (t = 2.335, p = .023), and CIOC (t = 2.065, p = .044) favored the PBL group; IIFA did not differ (t = .143, p = .887). No effect sizes are printed.

> "Independent samples t tests showed that there were significant differences between the two groups in the subscales of MAIN (t = 2.27, p < .05), DLE (t = 2.34, p < .05) and CIOC ( t = 2.07, p < .05)."
