## Evidence

### Karimian Z 2025

Karimian Z, Momeni M, Zarifsanaiey N. (2025). Enhancing radiographic interpretation: effects of gamification on medical students' knowledge, skills, and satisfaction — a quasi-experimental study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07523-x

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Authors' interpretive claim in the Discussion, offered without new data, that gamification's impact is "a multivariable and context-dependent phenomenon" modulated by delivery mode, platform, elements, and learner traits.

> "Factors such as the mode of delivery (in-person versus electronic), the choice of gaming platform, the specific game elements employed, and even individual learner characteristics-including personality traits and learning preferences-can significantly modulate its effectiveness"

### Karimian Z 2025 (2)

Karimian Z, Momeni M, Zarifsanaiey N. (2025). Enhancing radiographic interpretation: effects of gamification on medical students' knowledge, skills, and satisfaction — a quasi-experimental study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07523-x

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The discussion attributes this null result to Kirsch & Spreckelsen (2023), citing it as evidence that competitive designs may fail where individualized or collaborative designs succeed; the review reports this second-hand.

> "Kirsch & Spreckelsen (2023) conducted a randomized crossover study revealing that a competitive learning program did not significantly improve students' electronic test scores or intrinsic motivation, even though students reported enjoyment and motivation to study."
