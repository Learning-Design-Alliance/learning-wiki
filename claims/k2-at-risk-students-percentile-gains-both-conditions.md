# Students in both interventions gained on average from at or below the 10th percentile to at least the 20th percentile on FRA reading tasks

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across grades K–2 in both conditions, students started at or below the 10th percentile on FRA reading tasks and ended the year at least at the 20th percentile, with baseline-to-outcome differences of 13–25 percentile points. [→ Foorman 2018](#foorman-2018)
`q3 i?` The largest average percentile gain was grade 1 sentence comprehension (35–39 points), on a task normed on kindergarten students. [→ Foorman 2018 (2)](#foorman-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Foorman 2018

Foorman, B. R., Herrera, S., & Dombek, J. (2018). The relative impact of aligning Tier 2 intervention materials with classroom core reading materials in grades K–2. The Elementary School Journal, 118(3). https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/journals/esj

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Descriptive analysis of baseline and outcome FRA percentile ranks by intervention and grade (Table 4); the article reports students "had, on average, reached at least the twentieth percentile" by year end, with reading-task gains of 13 to 25 percentile points.

> "On average, students in grades K–2 in both interventions started at or below the tenth percentile on the FRA reading tasks"

### Foorman 2018 (2)

Foorman, B. R., Herrera, S., & Dombek, J. (2018). The relative impact of aligning Tier 2 intervention materials with classroom core reading materials in grades K–2. The Elementary School Journal, 118(3). https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/journals/esj

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Descriptive analysis reporting grade 1 students rose from just below the 30th to above the 60th percentile, "an average difference between baseline and outcome from 35 to 39 percentile points across interventions"; norms are kindergarten-based, so percentiles reflect a kindergarten scale.

> "The largest average percentile difference between baseline and outcome for any FRA task was in grade 1 for sentence comprehension."
