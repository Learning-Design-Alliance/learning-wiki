## Evidence

### Cao Xi 2026

Cao Xi, Wang Min, Huang Ye, Hu Qiu-Min, Tang Rong. (2026). Research on the application of debriefing in simulation-based education of surgical nursing. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09133-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Questionnaire survey using the Questionnaire of Effectiveness Evaluation of Debriefing (15 items) administered to the experimental phase. "more than 90% of students perceived the debriefing as useful for understanding, mastering, and applying knowledge".

> "The results showed that in terms of knowledge acquisition, more than 90% of students perceived the debriefing as useful for understanding, mastering, and applying knowledge. In terms of emotional attitude, over 86% of students reported that the debriefing was perceived as supportive of their confidence in solving clinical problems, humanistic literacy, and interpersonal communication skills. In terms of nursing skills, more than 87% of students believed that the debriefing contributed to the development of their nursing skills."

### Cao Xi 2026 (2)

Cao Xi, Wang Min, Huang Ye, Hu Qiu-Min, Tang Rong. (2026). Research on the application of debriefing in simulation-based education of surgical nursing. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09133-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same questionnaire survey. "88.23% of students liked the relaxed and lively classroom atmosphere" and "78.53% thought that the debriefing provided a platform for self-expression and catharsis".

> "In the evaluation feedback, 88.23% of students liked the relaxed and lively classroom atmosphere and felt the professional value of nursing. Most students (78.53%) thought that the debriefing provided a platform for self-expression and catharsis, which was perceived as helpful for alleviating tension, anxiety, and stress in clinical scenarios. The majority (87.65%) of students hoped to continue applying debriefing in situational simulation classes in the future."
