# Immediately after the discussion, participants who explained did not recall more than participants who only listened

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Floris M. van Blankenstein 2011

Floris M. van Blankenstein, Diana H. J. M. Dolmans, Cees P. M. van der Vleuten, Henk G. Schmidt. (2011). Which cognitive processes support learning during small-group discussion? The role of providing explanations and listening to others. Instructional Science 39, 189–204. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-009-9124-7

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Planned contrast on immediate recall in the same randomized experiment (explanation N = 24, listening N = 24): the printed effect size was "r = 0.08", with t(67) = .53 and p = .60, so no immediate advantage of explaining was detected; equivalence was not tested.

> "However, participants who had given explanations did not recall more than those who had listened, t(67) = .53, p = .60, r = 0.08."
