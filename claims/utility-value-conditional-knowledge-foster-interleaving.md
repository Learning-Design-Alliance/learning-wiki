# Combining utility value of distinguishing with conditional knowledge fosters interleaving of confusable categories (Abel et al., 2024)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across two experiments, increasing the utility value of distinguishing as a task goal and providing conditional knowledge that distinguishing and detection differences matter fostered learners' use of interleaving. [→ de Bruin 2025](#de-bruin-2025)
`q3 i?` Providing the conditional knowledge that interleaving highlights differences alone did not affect interleaving use, suggesting learners were already aware of this. [→ de Bruin 2025 (2)](#de-bruin-2025-2)

## Evidence

### de Bruin 2025

de Bruin, A. B. H., Janssen, E. M., Waldeyer, J., & Stebner, F. (2025). Cognitive Load and Challenges in Self-regulation: An Introduction and Reflection on the Topical Collection. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10042-2

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Two experiments by Abel et al. (2024) investigating why learners scarcely apply interleaving with easily confusable categories, as summarized in this editorial. "Combining two factors" — utility value and conditional knowledge — "fostered learners’ use of interleaving." No effect size is printed.

> "Together, their two experiments showed that combining two factors—increasing the utility value of distinguishing as a task goal and providing learners with the conditional knowledge that distinguishing and detection differences matter—fostered learners’ use of interleaving."

### de Bruin 2025 (2)

de Bruin, A. B. H., Janssen, E. M., Waldeyer, J., & Stebner, F. (2025). Cognitive Load and Challenges in Self-regulation: An Introduction and Reflection on the Topical Collection. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10042-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Same two-experiment study by Abel et al. (2024), reporting the null effect of the isolated conditional-knowledge factor on interleaving use; the editorial attributes this to learners' existing awareness. No effect size is printed.

> "Providing the conditional knowledge that interleaving strategies highlight differences did not affect the use of interleaving, suggesting that learners were already aware of this."
