## Evidence

### Kara and Eveyik-Aydin 2019

Ketevan Kara, Evrim Eveyik-Aydın. (2019). Effects of TPRS on Very Young Learners' Vocabulary Acquisition. Advances in Language and Literary Studies, 10(1). https://doi.org/10.7575/aiac.alls.v.10n.1p.135

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Item-level analysis of Table 2 data from the 19 participants. The authors attribute pan's difficulty to its lower frequency in children's language and its weaker connection to children's immediate environment compared with spoon and bowl.

> "pan (target word in Week 4) turned out to be the most challenging item of the cycle as four students failed to produce it on the immediate posttest, and the delayed posttest showed even lower results."

### Kara and Eveyik-Aydin 2019 (2)

Ketevan Kara, Evrim Eveyik-Aydın. (2019). Effects of TPRS on Very Young Learners' Vocabulary Acquisition. Advances in Language and Literary Studies, 10(1). https://doi.org/10.7575/aiac.alls.v.10n.1p.135

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Item-level analysis of Week 3 data: seven students failed to produce beach on the posttest while moon and shop were produced by 18. The authors label this result unexpected since all items received the same TPRS techniques and repetition.

> "This finding cannot be explained by its frequency, as the word beach is used as frequently as shop, and even more frequently than moon (Cobb, 2017)."
