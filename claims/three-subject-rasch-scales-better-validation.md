## Evidence

### Hissbach Johanna C 2011

Hissbach Johanna C, Klusmann Dietrich, Hampe Wolfgang. (2011). Dimensionality and predictive validity of the HAM-Nat, a test of natural sciences for medical school admission. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-11-83

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Confirmatory WLS factor analysis of the subject-specific Rasch scales in validation sample B. The article states these "are high correlations which indicate that the item set is not really three-dimensional", yet the item sets transferred well to Rasch models in sample B.

> "The 3-factor model showed good fit to the data (CFI = .97; TLI = .97; RMSEA = .023) when factors were allowed to correlate (r(Physics, Chemistry) = .76; r(Physics, Biology) = .57; r(Chemistry, Biology) = .51)."

### Hissbach Johanna C 2011 (2)

Hissbach Johanna C, Klusmann Dietrich, Hampe Wolfgang. (2011). Dimensionality and predictive validity of the HAM-Nat, a test of natural sciences for medical school admission. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-11-83

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-validation of the HAM-Nat-uni scale: nearly half the single-factor loadings dropped below 0.4 in sample B and residual correlations rres>|.20| rose from 5.1% to 12.0%, so the scale "did not conform to one single dimension" in validation.

> "The one factor solution found in sample A was not sufficient to explain the correlation matrix in sample B (CFI = .89; TLI = .90; RMSEA = .046). Of the 33 items, 8 showed poor fit to the Rasch model (ZSTD > |1.96|)."
