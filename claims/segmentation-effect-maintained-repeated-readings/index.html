Evidence¶

David S. Weiss 1983¶

David S. Weiss. (1983). The effects of text segmentation on children's reading comprehension. Discourse Processes, 6:1, 77-89. https://doi.org/10.1080/01638538309544555

q3 · i? · causal · r2

The repeated measures analysis (incomplete latin square of passage by format combinations, means of the nine combinations analyzed) reproduced the first passage findings. Only the qualitative duplication is reported here; no effect size was printed.

"The finding that the repeated measures analysis duplicated the first passage analysis results indicated that the positive effects of Text Segmentation are maintained even when the participants are involved in repeated readings."

David S. Weiss 1983 (2)¶

David S. Weiss. (1983). The effects of text segmentation on children's reading comprehension. Discourse Processes, 6:1, 77-89. https://doi.org/10.1080/01638538309544555

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Descriptive analysis of adjusted mean scores (each passage's overall mean subtracted from individual scores) in the repeated measures design with all 648 participants. The proportions favoring each phrased format over prose are descriptive percentages, not effect sizes.

"The results indicated that 74.2% of all (N = 648) participants had higher scores on the syntactic phrase format when compared to their scores on the prose format. In addition, 74.7% of all the participants had higher scores on the pausal phrase format when compared to their scores on the prose format."
