## Evidence

### Garvin Brod 2021

Garvin Brod. (2021). Generative Learning: Which Strategies for What Age? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09571-9

`q3 · i2` · `review · r2`

The review reports Fiorella and Zhang's (2018) meta-analysis of published studies, with effect sizes of 0.46 SDs for text comprehension and 0.75 SDs for transfer against reading-only conditions; drawing beat provided illustrations only under very high instructional support.

> "drawing was clearly more effective than just reading a text for both text comprehension (0.46 SDs) and transfer performance (0.75 SDs)."

### Garvin Brod 2021 (2)

Garvin Brod. (2021). Generative Learning: Which Strategies for What Age? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09571-9

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Van Meter et al. (2006) compared fourth- and sixth-graders: only sixth-graders benefited, enhanced under high support, while fourth-graders did not benefit even with high support despite comparable prior knowledge. Van Essen and Hamaker (1990) found first- and second-graders' problem solving did not profit from drawing at all whereas fifth-graders' did.

> "Only the sixth-graders benefited from drawing, and this benefit was enhanced in the high-support condition, in which students were prompted to compare their drawing with a provided illustration."
