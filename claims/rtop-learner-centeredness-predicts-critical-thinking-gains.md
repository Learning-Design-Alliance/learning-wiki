# Learner-centeredness of in-class instruction (RTOP) explains variation in students' higher-order cognitive skill gains, with RTOP level III outperforming level I

## Evidence

### Holt Emily A. 2015

Holt Emily A., Young Craig, Keetch Jared, Larsen Skylar, Mollner Brayden. (2015). The Greatest Learning Return on Your Pedagogical Investment: Alignment, Assessment or In-Class Instruction? PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0137446

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Ordinary least squares regression on the 15 sections, with section-mean pre-quiz scores as covariate and RTOP score as predictor of post-quiz scores. RTOP was significant (F1,12 = 4.85, p = 0.04) with R2 = 0.38; no effect size beyond R2 was printed.

> "In our final regression model, RTOP (F1,12 = 4.85, p = 0.04) and the pre-quiz scores (F1,12 = 5.34, p = 0.05) both explained a significant amount of variation in student post-quiz scores (R2 = 0.38)."

### Holt Emily A. 2015 (2)

Holt Emily A., Young Craig, Keetch Jared, Larsen Skylar, Mollner Brayden. (2015). The Greatest Learning Return on Your Pedagogical Investment: Alignment, Assessment or In-Class Instruction? PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0137446

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

ANCOVA with RTOP level as categorical predictor (F2,11 = 4.53, p = 0.04) and Tukey-Kramer post hoc comparisons among the three RTOP levels observed. RTOP III beat RTOP I (p = 0.04); RTOP III vs II was marginal (p = 0.08); I and II did not differ (p = 0.22).

> "From a post hoc Tukey-Kramer adjustment for multiple comparisons test, we concluded that RTOP level III was better than both the RTOP I (t11 = -2.79, p = 0.04) and RTOP II categories (t11 = -2.42, p = 0.08) in explaining post-quiz scores."
