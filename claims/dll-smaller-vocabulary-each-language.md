## Evidence

### Hammer 2014

Hammer, C. S., Hoff, E., Uchikoshi, Y., Gillanders, C., Castro, D., & Sandilos, L. E. (2014). The Language and Literacy Development of Young Dual Language Learners: A Critical Review. Early childhood research quarterly. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecresq.2014.05.008

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's synthesis of vocabulary studies (Marchman et al., 2010; Place & Hoff, 2011; Vagh et al., 2009) finds that "DLLs have smaller vocabularies and slower rates of growth over time in each language when compared to monolinguals," though many remain within the normal monolingual range in their dominant language.

> "With regard to the size of DLL infants/toddlers’ vocabularies in their individual languages, studies consistently found that DLLs have smaller vocabularies and slower rates of growth over time in each language when compared to monolinguals"

### Hammer 2014 (2)

Hammer, C. S., Hoff, E., Uchikoshi, Y., Gillanders, C., Castro, D., & Sandilos, L. E. (2014). The Language and Literacy Development of Young Dual Language Learners: A Critical Review. Early childhood research quarterly. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecresq.2014.05.008

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Junker & Stockman (2002), Lundén & Silvén (2011), and Pearson et al. (1993), that DLLs' combined conceptual vocabularies match monolinguals' in size, with some overlap of words across languages.

> "DLL infants/toddlers’ conceptual vocabularies (i.e., the number of vocabulary concepts known in their two languages combined) are the same size as monolinguals’ vocabularies"
