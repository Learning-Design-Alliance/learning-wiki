Evidence¶

Koponen 2017¶

Koponen, T., Georgiou, G., Salmi, P., Leskinen, M., & Aro, M. (2017). A Meta-Analysis of the Relation Between RAN and Mathematics. Journal of Educational Psychology, 109(7), 977-992. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000182

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Meta-regression with dummy-coded moderators and small-sample adjustments. The β = .04 is a moderator coefficient, no effect size printed; the article cautions the significant p may be an estimation artifact, so this finding is effectively null.

"Numeric RAN tasks also produced significantly larger correlations with math outcomes than non-numeric RAN (β = .04, p < .01); however, the statistically significant p (despite the low beta) might be an artifact (type I error) associated with the estimation methods"

Koponen 2017 (2)¶

Koponen, T., Georgiou, G., Salmi, P., Leskinen, M., & Aro, M. (2017). A Meta-Analysis of the Relation Between RAN and Mathematics. Journal of Educational Psychology, 109(7), 977-992. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000182

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

The results section lists these four factors and reports each "were not significant moderators." No effect size is printed for these contrasts. This contrasts with reading, where alphanumeric RAN predicts more strongly.

"The type of RAN stimulus (alphanumeric vs. non-alphanumeric), the design of the study (cross-sectional vs. longitudinal), age when math was assessed (at or below 9 years vs. above 9 years), and type of sample"
