## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In post-OSCE interviews, experimental-arm residents reported the eye-tracking video enabled more individualized moment-by-moment feedback and generated new insights about how they learn, including recognizing cognitive load effects on their performance. [→ Braund H 2024](#braund-h-2024)

## Evidence

### Braund H 2024

Braund H, Hall AK, Caners K, Walker M, Dagnone D, Sherbino J, Sibbald M, Wang B, Howes D, Day AG, Wu W, Szulewski A. (2024). Evaluating the Value of Eye-Tracking Augmented Debriefing in Medical Simulation—A Pilot Randomized Controlled Trial. Simulation in Healthcare. https://doi.org/10.1097/SIH.0000000000000825

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Thematic analysis of one-on-one 15-minute resident interviews after the OSCE, coded inductively in NVivo with intercoder reliability greater than 90%. Residents described learning "something new about themselves as learners" from reviewing their gaze-overlaid first-person video.

> "Many residents in the experimental group indicated that they learned something new about themselves as learners. This included the need to orient oneself before the start of a simulation, being overly critical, and that it was useful to review the performance using the video generated by the eye-tracking glasses."
