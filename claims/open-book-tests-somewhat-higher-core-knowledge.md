## Evidence

### Heijne-Penninga M. 2012

Heijne-Penninga M., Kuks J. B. M., Hofman W. H. A., Muijtjens A. M. M., Cohen-Schotanus J. (2012). Influence of PBL with open-book tests on knowledge retention measured with progress tests. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-012-9386-8

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of core-knowledge progress test subtest scores between the two PBL schools (PBLob n = 600, PBLcb n = 540); Table 2 reports weighted average effect sizes of 0.30 (year 5) and 0.23 (year 6). The authors state "PBLob students scored somewhat higher on core knowledge than PBLcb".

> "The results of our study show, that PBLob students scored somewhat higher on core knowledge than PBLcb and TC students did. Possibly, as a result of the distinction between core and backup knowledge in their Bachelor’s programme, PBLob students had paid more attention to core knowledge."
