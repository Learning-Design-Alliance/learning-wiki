## Evidence

### Carolyn Sealfon 2025

Carolyn Sealfon, Minerva University. (2025). Embodying Newtonian Mechanics. OAPT Newsletter. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2501.03344](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2501.03344)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

In the article's 'In practice' account, students first extend the arm with fingers angled upward, then press against a wall; the observed further wrist bend is used to 'invite students to propose possible explanations for this observation'. The description quotes 'the wrist clearly bends further, almost at a right angle'.

> "Then, invite students to press their hands against a wall, and ask them what they observe about how far their wrist can bend with the help of the wall. As in image (b) below, the wrist clearly bends further, almost at a right angle."

### Carolyn Sealfon 2025 (2)

Carolyn Sealfon, Minerva University. (2025). Embodying Newtonian Mechanics. OAPT Newsletter. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2501.03344](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2501.03344)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

The article reports the common initial one-way interpretation of the wall activity and contrasts it with the mutual-interaction reading, stating the wall pushes back 'as much as' the hand pushes on it, with both forces arising simultaneously.

> "Most students initially tend to think of this action as "the person is pushing the wall," when really the wall and the person are both pushing together. In fact, the wall pushes back on the person's hand as much as the person's hand pushes on the wall."
