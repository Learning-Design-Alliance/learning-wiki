Evidence¶

Keselman 1998¶

Keselman, H. J., Huberty, C. J., Lix, L. M., Olejnik, S., Cribbie, R. A., Donahue, B., Kowalchuk, R. K., Lowman, L. L., Petoskey, M. D., Keselman, J. C., & Levin, J. R. (1998). Statistical Practises of Educational Researchers: An Analysis of Their ANOVA, MANOVA and ANCOVA Analyses. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Statistical+Practises+of+Educational+Researchers

q2 · i? · review · r2

Content analysis of 61 between-subjects univariate articles (Table 2). The review found that "almost three-quarters of those identified (72.4%) were comprised of cells containing unequal numbers of units of analysis," and overall unbalanced designs were more common than balanced designs.

"This is particularly evident in the case of studies invol ving factorial designs, where almost three-quarters of those identified (72.4%) were comprised of cells containing unequal numbers of units of analysis"

Keselman 1998 (2)¶

Keselman, H. J., Huberty, C. J., Lix, L. M., Olejnik, S., Cribbie, R. A., Donahue, B., Kowalchuk, R. K., Lowman, L. L., Petoskey, M. D., Keselman, J. C., & Levin, J. R. (1998). Statistical Practises of Educational Researchers: An Analysis of Their ANOVA, MANOVA and ANCOVA Analyses. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Statistical+Practises+of+Educational+Researchers

q2 · i? · review · r2

Assessment of variance heterogeneity across 86 dependent variables in one-way designs: "The average value of the ratio of the largest to smallest standard deviation was 2.0 ( = 2.6), with a median of 1.5" and a maximum ratio of 23.8; factorial studies showed a mean ratio of 2.8 and a maximum of 29.4.
