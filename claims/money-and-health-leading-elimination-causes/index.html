Evidence¶

E. D. Jennings 1911¶

E. D. Jennings. (1911). The Elimination of Freshmen from the University of Texas. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=elimination+of+freshmen+from+the+university+of+texas

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Questionnaire given to six hundred freshmen with 308 replies, ranking five candidate causes; students had two days to reply. The authors judged second- and third-rank figures not valuable but first-rank figures significant.

"those in the first rank do seem significant. "Lack of money" is by far the most prominent, followed in order by "Ill-health," "Disappointed" and "Tired of school.""

E. D. Jennings 1911 (2)¶

E. D. Jennings. (1911). The Elimination of Freshmen from the University of Texas. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=elimination+of+freshmen+from+the+university+of+texas

q2 · i? · associational · r1

From the conclusions: lack of means accounts for about 34 per cent of elimination; a separate conclusion states "Ill-health causes 28 per cent, of all elmination." The authors recommend state scholarships and sanitary dormitories.

"2. Lack of means causes about 34 per cent, of this elimination."

Related Claims¶

  • University scholarship ratings ran about 16 per cent below high-school ratings for the same students, ranking them only two-thirds as high per hour of study, a gap the authors link to discouragement and withdrawal — related
  • A postal follow-up of 850 non-returning freshmen corroborated the earlier findings, with lack of money again the most common first-ranked cause (54 of 108 replies) — related
  • Students with the least means studied the most and made the best grades, while students tired of school studied least and made relatively low grades — related
  • About 60 per cent of University of Texas freshmen withdrew permanently each year, based on student-history tracing of 1901–1908 freshman cohorts — related
  • The questionnaire method failed to reach the views of the laggard students themselves, with only about one in six replying — related
  • The large majority of freshmen scoring below 77.5 Alpha drop out before senior year — related
