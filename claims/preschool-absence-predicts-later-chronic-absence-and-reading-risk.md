# Chronically absent preschoolers are much more likely to stay chronically absent, and each successive year of chronic absenteeism compounds risk of low second-grade reading

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Roughly one-third of chronically absent four-year-old preschoolers continue to be chronically absent kindergarteners, compared with 7 percent of non-chronically absent four-year-olds. [→ Ehrlich 2014](#ehrlich-2014)
`q3 i?` Chronically absent preschool children are five times more likely to be chronically absent in second grade than their non-chronically absent preschool peers. [→ Ehrlich 2014 (2)](#ehrlich-2014-2)
`q3 i?` Second-graders chronically absent every year since preschool are, on average, close to the threshold for needing intensive reading intervention to read at grade level by third grade. [→ Ehrlich 2014 (3)](#ehrlich-2014-3)

## Evidence

### Ehrlich 2014

Ehrlich, S. B., Gwynne, J. A., Pareja, A. S., Allensworth, E. M., Moore, P., Jagesic, S., & Sorice, E. (2014). Preschool Attendance in Chicago Public Schools: Relationships with Learning Outcomes and Reasons for Absences. UChicago CCSR Research Report. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/preschool-attendance-chicago-public-schools-relationships-learning-outcomes-and-reasons

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal analysis following CPS preschool students into kindergarten, comparing subsequent chronic absenteeism of chronically versus non-chronically absent four-year-olds. The report describes this as "a detrimental pattern that often continues into elementary school."

> "While just 7 percent of non-chronically absent four-year-old students go on to be chronically absent in kindergarten, roughly one-third of chronically absent four-year-old preschoolers continue to be chronically absent kindergarteners."

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal analysis of attendance records from preschool through second grade showing persistence of chronic absenteeism into the elementary grades.

> "chronically absent preschool children are five times more likely to be chronically absent in second grade than their non-chronically absent preschool peers."

### Ehrlich 2014 (3)

Ehrlich, S. B., Gwynne, J. A., Pareja, A. S., Allensworth, E. M., Moore, P., Jagesic, S., & Sorice, E. (2014). Preschool Attendance in Chicago Public Schools: Relationships with Learning Outcomes and Reasons for Absences. UChicago CCSR Research Report. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/preschool-attendance-chicago-public-schools-relationships-learning-outcomes-and-reasons

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of second-grade reading outcomes by cumulative years of chronic absenteeism between preschool and second grade; students with multiple years of chronic absenteeism had significantly lower end-of-second-grade learning outcomes than peers without early chronic absence.

> "second-graders who have been chronically absent every year since preschool are, on average, close to the threshold for needing intensive reading intervention in order to be reading at grade level by third grade."
