## Evidence

### Secchi Agostina 2025

Secchi Agostina, Whiskey Eromona, Nair Sajitha, Shergill Sukhi, Thomas Trudy. (2025). Competence and confidence of health care professionals in using clozapine: a qualitative systematic review and thematic synthesis. Acta Neuropsychiatrica. https://doi.org/10.1017/neu.2025.10024

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's synthesis of training studies: it reports that training delivered online and/or face-to-face "can improve competence in clozapine management", citing Freudenreich et al. (2013), Cotes et al. (2022) and Oloyede et al. (2023).

> "Previous studies have shown how training (online and/or face-to-face) can improve competence in clozapine management (Freudenreich et al., 2013; Cotes et al., 2022; Oloyede et al., 2023), reducing the time of initiation and early identification of patients with TRS."

### Secchi Agostina 2025 (2)

Secchi Agostina, Whiskey Eromona, Nair Sajitha, Shergill Sukhi, Thomas Trudy. (2025). Competence and confidence of health care professionals in using clozapine: a qualitative systematic review and thematic synthesis. Acta Neuropsychiatrica. https://doi.org/10.1017/neu.2025.10024

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review describes the Freudenreich Clozapine Training Programme, combining face-to-face workshops with weekly participation in a community clozapine clinic, tested on "a small cohort of trainees (n = 15)" with significant knowledge improvement.

> "The programme was tested on a small cohort of trainees (n = 15) who showed a significant improvement in general knowledge about clozapine (p < 0.001) after 6 weeks."
