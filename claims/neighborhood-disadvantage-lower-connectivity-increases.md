## Evidence

### Rakesh 2025

Rakesh, D., Sadikova, E., & McLaughlin, K. A. (2025). Associations among socioeconomic disadvantage, longitudinal changes in within-network connectivity, and academic outcomes in the ABCD study. Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2025.101587

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Linear mixed effects models on residualized connectivity change in the ABCD sample (N = 4745), with income-to-needs, parental education, and neighborhood disadvantage entered simultaneously, covarying motion, age, and sex; FDR-corrected p values for the ADI term were 0.005 (cingulo-opercular), 0.001 (default), 0.005 (RTN), and 0.005 (visual).

> "Specifically, greater neighborhood disadvantage was associated with lower increases in connectivity within the cingulo-opercular, default mode, and visual networks, and greater decreases in connectivity within the RTN (Fig. 1A-D)."
