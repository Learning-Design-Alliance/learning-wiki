# Students' perception of learning and academic self-perception are associated with deeper approaches and reduced surface approaches, while perception of teachers shows little or no influence

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students' use of deep approaches increased with their perception of good learning (0.32 SD per 1 SD increase) and academic self-perception (0.19), and surface approaches decreased with perception of good learning (−0.22). [→ Gustin Marie-Paule 2018](#gustin-marie-paule-2018)
`q2 i?` Perception of teachers had little or no influence on learning approaches (deep: −0.06; surface: −0.03, both non-significant). [→ Gustin Marie-Paule 2018 (2)](#gustin-marie-paule-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Gustin Marie-Paule 2018

Gustin Marie-Paule, Abbiati Milena, Bonvin Raphael, Gerbase Margaret W., Baroffio Anne. (2018). Integrated problem-based learning versus lectures: a path analysis modelling of the relationships between educational context and learning approaches. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2018.1489690

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Path analysis Model 1_All (RMSEA = 0; CFI = 1) relating the five DREEM subscales to deep and surface approaches in all participants; the model explained 22% of the variance of deep and 12% of surface approaches. Perception of learning carried the largest standardized coefficient (0.32); no standardized effect size beyond these path coefficients is printed.

> "Students’ use of deep learning approaches was increased by their perception of good learning and academic self-perception (e.g., students increased their use of deep approaches of 0.32 SD for an increase of 1 SD in their perception of learning)."

### Gustin Marie-Paule 2018 (2)

Gustin Marie-Paule, Abbiati Milena, Bonvin Raphael, Gerbase Margaret W., Baroffio Anne. (2018). Integrated problem-based learning versus lectures: a path analysis modelling of the relationships between educational context and learning approaches. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2018.1489690

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion of the Model 1 results, contrasting the influential perception-of-teaching and academic self-perception dimensions with perception of teachers, which the models showed had "little or no influence on their learning approaches".

> "By contrast, their perception of the type of teaching (e.g., student-centred, stimulating, focused, emphasizing long-term learning) as well as of their own academic development (e.g., prepared for the profession, developing problem-solving skills, learning relevant for the future career) did impact the way they learned, stimulating the use of deep and decreasing the use of surface approaches."
