## Evidence

### obj1-mixed-model

Bentley DC, Sotto A, Lombardi P. Comparison of examination performance and test anxiety during bell‐paced and self‐paced gross anatomy laboratory examinations. Anat Sci Educ. 2026;19:732–742. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.70215

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The first mixed-effects model from the randomized crossover study assessed exam performance with participants as a random effect. Findings showed no significant main effects of assigned pacing condition or exam type, with one verbatim fragment of source_quote: β = 2.07, 95% CI [−2.45, 6.58].

> "There were no significant main effects of assigned pacing condition (β = 2.07, 95% CI [−2.45, 6.58]) or exam type (β = 0.28, 95% CI [−4.24, 4.80]) on exam performance."

### obj1-interaction

Bentley DC, Sotto A, Lombardi P. Comparison of examination performance and test anxiety during bell‐paced and self‐paced gross anatomy laboratory examinations. Anat Sci Educ. 2026;19:732–742. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.70215

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same first mixed-effects model from the randomized crossover study assessed the interaction between assigned pacing condition and exam type. Findings showed a statistically non-significant interaction, with one verbatim fragment of source_quote: β = −2.05, 95% CI [−10.60, 6.51].

> "The interaction between assigned pacing condition and exam type was also statistically non-significant (β = −2.05, 95% CI [−10.60, 6.51]), suggesting that academic rigor was consistent between pacing conditions and between exams (Figure 1)."
