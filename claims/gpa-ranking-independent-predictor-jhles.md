# GPA ranking is an independent predictor of JHLES scores in multivariate logistic regression, with a monotonic odds-ratio gradient

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2025 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Compared with the top 20–50% GPA reference group, the top 5% group had higher odds of high JHLES scores (OR 0.52, 95% CI 0.44–0.62) and the top 80–100% group had lower odds (OR 1.87, 95% CI 1.62–2.12), all p < 0.001. [→ Liu Yifan 2025](#liu-yifan-2025)

## Evidence

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariate logistic regression on seven variables (age, gender, ethnicity, major, grade, native place, GPA) in the 10,901-student cross-sectional survey, with the top 20–50% GPA group as reference; GPA impact was significant (p < 0.001). The article also prints OR 0.76 (0.69–0.85) for top 5–20%, OR 1.48 (1.34–1.64) for 50–80%, and OR 1.87 (1.62–2.12) for 80–100%. No standardised effect size was printed.

> "the odds ratio for the group with GPA ranked in the top 5% was 0.52 (95% CI: 0.44–0.62). It indicated that students in the top 20–50% of GPA were less likely to give high JHLES scores than students in the top 5% of GPA."
