# Adolescents' physics and chemistry ability self-concept and task values from seventh to twelfth grade fall into seven distinct motivational trajectories rather than a single declining pattern

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2`–`r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Growth mixture modeling of longitudinal data identified seven trajectory groups labeled Stable High, Stable Moderate, Steady Decreasing, Sharp Decreasing, Increasing, Transitory Decreasing, and Transitory Increasing. [→ Wang 2016](#wang-2016)
`q3 i?` Significant intercept and slope variances for both constructs indicated heterogeneity of motivation development among individuals. [→ Wang 2016 (2)](#wang-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Wang 2016

Wang, Ming-Te; Chow, Angela; Degol, Jessica Lauren; Eccles, Jacquelynne Sue. (2016). Does Everyone's Motivational Beliefs about Physical Science Decline in Secondary School?: Heterogeneity of Adolescents' Achievement Motivation Trajectories in Physics and Chemistry. Journal of Youth and Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-016-0620-1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Growth mixture modeling of the cross-sequential sample (N = 699) estimated intercepts, linear slopes, and quadratic terms for both constructs; a seven-group solution was selected based on BIC, LMR tests, and an entropy value of .75, with assignment probabilities ranging from .78 to .90.

> "According to the pattern of changes in science ability self-concept and task values, the seven groups were labeled as Stable High, Stable Moderate, Steady Decreasing, Sharp Decreasing, Increasing, Transitory Decreasing, and Transitory Increasing."

### Wang 2016 (2)

Wang, Ming-Te; Chow, Angela; Degol, Jessica Lauren; Eccles, Jacquelynne Sue. (2016). Does Everyone's Motivational Beliefs about Physical Science Decline in Secondary School?: Heterogeneity of Adolescents' Achievement Motivation Trajectories in Physics and Chemistry. Journal of Youth and Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-016-0620-1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Latent growth curve analysis preceded the mixture models; curvilinear models fit better than linear ones, and the printed significant variance components supported modeling distinct subgroups rather than one average trend.

> "the variance of intercept (0.97) and slope (0.70) for self-concept as well as intercept (0.85) and slope (0.75) for task values were statistically significant (p < .05), indicating the heterogeneity of motivation development among individuals."
