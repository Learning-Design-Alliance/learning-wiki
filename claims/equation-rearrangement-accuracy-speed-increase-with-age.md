# Accuracy and speed of simple equation rearrangement increase with students' age across grades 7–12

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Accuracy increased with age (main effect of Age on accuracy, effect size η2 p = 0.081). [→ Susac A 2014](#susac-a-2014)
`q3 i2` Reaction times decreased with age (main effect of Age on RTs, effect size η2 p = 0.107). [→ Susac A 2014 (2)](#susac-a-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Susac A 2014

Susac A, Bubic A, Vrbanc A and Planinic M (2014) Development of abstract mathematical reasoning: the case of algebra. Front. Hum. Neurosci. 8:679. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00679

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Two-way ANOVA with factors Age (7th vs. 8th vs. 1st vs. 2nd grade) and Gender on accuracy in the computerized equation test with 331 students; printed effect size η2 p = 0.081 for the Age main effect, and "accuracy increased with the age of participants."

> "The obtained results for accuracy indicated a statistically signiﬁcant main effect of Age [F(3,323) = 9.43, p < 0.001, η2 p = 0.081]"

### Susac A 2014 (2)

Susac A, Bubic A, Vrbanc A and Planinic M (2014) Development of abstract mathematical reasoning: the case of algebra. Front. Hum. Neurosci. 8:679. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00679

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Same two-way ANOVA design for reaction times; printed effect size η2 p = 0.107 for the Age main effect, and "Average RT s decreased with the age of participants."

> "A corresponding comparison for RT s revealed a statistically signiﬁcant main effect of Age [ F(3,322) = 12.91, p < 0.001, η2 p = 0.107]"
