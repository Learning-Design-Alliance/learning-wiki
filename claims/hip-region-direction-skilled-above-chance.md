## Evidence

### Causer Joe 2017

Causer Joe, Smeeton Nicholas J., Williams A. Mark. (2017). Expertise differences in anticipatory judgements during a temporally and spatially occluded task. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0171330

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Bonferroni-corrected t-tests against the 25% chance level in the hip-only condition; the abstract-level summary is supported in Results by all skilled-group t-tests against chance being significant (all p < .05).

> "Performance in the hip only condition was significantly better than chance for the skilled group across all occlusion conditions."

### Causer Joe 2017 (2)

Causer Joe, Smeeton Nicholas J., Williams A. Mark. (2017). Expertise differences in anticipatory judgements during a temporally and spatially occluded task. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0171330

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the correct-direction analysis, the less-skilled group's -160 ms hip-region scores were "not significantly different to chance (t22 = 1.514, p = 0.14)", indicating they could not extract direction cues from the isolated hip region early in the kick.

> "For the less-skilled group in the -160 ms hip region conditions performance scores were not significantly different to chance (t22 = 1.514, p = 0.14)."
