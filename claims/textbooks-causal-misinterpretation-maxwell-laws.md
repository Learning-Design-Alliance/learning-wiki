# Ten of 12 analysed textbooks interpret Faraday's and Ampère-Maxwell's laws as describing cause-and-effect relationships in which time-varying fields generate each other

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Only two textbooks (Chabay and Sherwood; Moore) comply with criterion 1.2, that the laws do not imply causal relationships between field operators and field generation; the other ten do not comply. [→ Álvaro Suárez 2023](#alvaro-suarez-2023)

## Evidence

### Álvaro Suárez 2023

Álvaro Suárez, Arturo C. Martí, Kristina Zuza and Jenaro Guisasola. (2023). Presentation of the electromagnetic field in introductory physics textbooks and consequences for its teaching. arXiv:2305.05431. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020113](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020113)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative textbook analysis (Criterion 1.2) of the same 12 textbooks. Ten were rated Does not comply, presenting the time-varying magnetic field as the source of the electric field and the time-varying electric field as the source of the magnetic field; only two texts comply.

> "10 of the 12 textbooks analysed interpret Faraday and Ampère-Maxwell’s laws as describing cause-and-eﬀect relationships between diﬀerent terms, such that the time-varying magnetic ﬁeld is the source of the electric ﬁeld (Faraday’s law) and vice versa"
