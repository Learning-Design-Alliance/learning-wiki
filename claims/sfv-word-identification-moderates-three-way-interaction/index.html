Edwards 2022¶

Edwards, A. A., Steacy, L. M., Seigelman, N., Rigobon, V. M., Kearns, D. M., Rueckl, J. G., & Compton, D. L. (2022). Unpacking the Unique Relationship Between Set for Variability and Word Reading Development: Examining Word- and Child-Level Predictors of Performance. Journal of Educational Psychology, 114(6). https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000696

q2 · i? · associational · r3

Model 4 (full prediction model) of the cross-classified analysis. The article reports the word identification main effect with probabilities of ".35 at 1SD above the mean on WID" versus .19 below.

"The main effect of word identification suggests that at the mean of all other predictors, the probability of a correct response is .35 at 1SD above the mean on WID compared to a probability of .19 at 1SD below the mean on WID."

Edwards 2022 (2)¶

Edwards, A. A., Steacy, L. M., Seigelman, N., Rigobon, V. M., Kearns, D. M., Rueckl, J. G., & Compton, D. L. (2022). Unpacking the Unique Relationship Between Set for Variability and Word Reading Development: Examining Word- and Child-Level Predictors of Performance. Journal of Educational Psychology, 114(6). https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000696

q2 · i? · associational · r3

Results text on the significant three-way interaction between word attack, word identification, and max P→O surprisal (Figure 3). The article interprets this as knowledge of the phonology-orthography connection being required for orthographic facilitation.

"Interestingly, this interaction also shows that those with low word attack skills are essentially unaffected by this consistency regardless of word identification skills."

Edwards 2022 (3)¶

Edwards, A. A., Steacy, L. M., Seigelman, N., Rigobon, V. M., Kearns, D. M., Rueckl, J. G., & Compton, D. L. (2022). Unpacking the Unique Relationship Between Set for Variability and Word Reading Development: Examining Word- and Child-Level Predictors of Performance. Journal of Educational Psychology, 114(6). https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000696

q2 · i? · associational · r3

Variance increments for the full prediction model. The study reports the model "explained 76.01% of the child-level variance" and 48.82% of word-level variance with the printed extra amounts.

"This model explained 76.01% of the child-level variance, 48.82% of the word-level variance, and 99.17% of the school-level variance, an extra 6.57%, 0.13%, and 6.99% respectively above the phonology-orthography connection predictor model."
