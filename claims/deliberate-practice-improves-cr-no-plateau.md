## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Over 20 cases, DxJ improved from 27% to 46.5% (+72%), test ordering from 46% to 64.5% (+40%), cognitive errors halved from 0.87 to 0.43 per student per case, and misdiagnoses fell threefold from 27% to 9.0%, with no plateau at 20 cases. [→ Waechter Jason 2026](#waechter-jason-2026)

## Evidence

### Waechter Jason 2026

Waechter Jason, Kusnoor Anita, Eickman Kara, Smith David, Wong Matt, Rogers Suzanne, Lee Chel Hee. (2026). Reducing misdiagnoses and cognitive errors using virtual patients and automated feedback in a clinical reasoning curriculum. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08647-4

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Learning-curve analysis using a generalized mixed-effects model with fixed-effect trends plotted across case sequence for 1,066 students completing up to 20 cases; the article reports p < 0.0001. Percent improvements are descriptive gains, not standardized effect sizes, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "Cognitive errors dropped by half from 0.87 to 0.43 per student per case, and misdiagnoses fell threefold from 27% to 9.0% (Fig. 3, p < 0.0001). Learning curves did not plateau for any of the psychometric variables at 20 cases."
