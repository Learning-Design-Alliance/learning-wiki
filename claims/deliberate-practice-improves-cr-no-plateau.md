## Evidence

### Waechter Jason 2026

Waechter Jason, Kusnoor Anita, Eickman Kara, Smith David, Wong Matt, Rogers Suzanne, Lee Chel Hee. (2026). Reducing misdiagnoses and cognitive errors using virtual patients and automated feedback in a clinical reasoning curriculum. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08647-4

`q2 · prospective observational study, not randomised` · `i?` · `associational · r2`

Learning-curve analysis using a generalized mixed-effects model with fixed-effect trends plotted across case sequence for 1,066 students completing up to 20 cases; the article reports p < 0.0001. The study is a prospective multi-site observational study with no randomisation, so these are trends across case sequence and do not show that the practice itself caused them. Its comparison between learners is a non-equivalent cohort one: seven M1 cohorts (20 cases) against three M2 cohorts who had not used the curriculum in their first year (10 cases), a separate finding (it also compares correctly with incorrectly diagnosed cases). Percent improvements are descriptive gains, not standardized effect sizes, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "Cognitive errors dropped by half from 0.87 to 0.43 per student per case, and misdiagnoses fell threefold from 27% to 9.0% (Fig. 3, p < 0.0001). Learning curves did not plateau for any of the psychometric variables at 20 cases."
