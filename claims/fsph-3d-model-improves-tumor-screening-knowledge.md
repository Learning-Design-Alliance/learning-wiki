## Evidence

### Zhou Fen 2026

Zhou Fen, Yu Ping, Yu Xiaofen, Han Xuyan, Wang Zheng. (2026). Family-school-hospital collaboration model for urinary system tumor early screening education. Frontiers in Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1837667

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Non-randomized controlled pre-post study of 120 clinical medical interns (60 per group) in a provincial hospital in China. The intervention group received a 6-month family-school-hospital collaborative program via family WeChat groups with 3D printing-assisted teaching; the control group received traditional clinical teaching only. The knowledge outcome was a National Medical Licensing Examination simulation score. The abstract and the results section print different figures for it: the abstract gives mean changes of 12.48 ± 9.98 vs. 6.28 ± 11.55 (p = 0.0046, Cohen's d = 0.56), while the results section gives 14.84 ± 9.76 vs. 5.35 ± 11.29 (p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.88) and, from the baseline-adjusted ANCOVA the authors name as primary, adjusted post-test means of 69.87 vs. 61.34 (difference 8.53, 95% CI 6.78–10.28, F = 92.48, p < 0.001, partial η² = 0.439); the paper does not explain the difference, and the `i2` here follows the smaller printed figure. Groups were formed by non-randomised convenience grouping on dormitory (boarding) status, and the authors note that the only inherent difference they identified between groups was the boarding interns' unified daily centralised learning time, a potential confounder alongside unmeasured ones such as self-learning initiative. The authors state that "all results of this study only demonstrate statistical associations between the combined teaching model and score improvement" and that they cannot conclude the intervention independently improves trainees' competencies.

> "After adjusting for baseline, the intervention group showed significantly greater improvements in knowledge score (mean change: 12.48 ± 9.98 vs. 6.28 ± 11.55; p = 0.0046, Cohen's d = 0.56, 95% CI: 1.97 to 10.43)" (abstract)

> "The mean score change was 14.84 ± 9.76 in the observation group versus 5.35 ± 11.29 in the control group (t = 4.82, df = 118, p<0.001, Cohen's d = 0.88)" (results)
