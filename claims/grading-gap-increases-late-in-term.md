## Evidence

### Freeman S. 2010

Freeman S., Parks J.W. (2010). How Accurate Is Peer Grading? CBE Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.10-03-0017

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Differences between each student score and the professional score on the same question were compared across weeks: spring 2005 week 5 (0.89 ± 0.18) versus week 10 (1.81 ± 0.17), unpaired t test; autumn 2005 weeks 2, 5, 10 (0.46, 1.61, 1.9), ANOVA F = 21.93, p ≪ 0.0001.

> "Statistical tests reported in Table 3 confirm that there was significant heterogeneity in the average student-professional difference in each quarter of the study, based on time in the term."

### Freeman S. 2010 (2)

Freeman S., Parks J.W. (2010). How Accurate Is Peer Grading? CBE Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.10-03-0017

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Bloom's level of questions was compared across weeks: autumn 2005 showed a significant increase (F = 6.6, p = 0.006; week 2 mean 2.8 versus week 10 mean 4.0), while spring 2005 did not reach significance (t = −0.77, p = 0.46).

> "Finally, in autumn 2005 there was a significant difference in the average Bloom's level based on time in the term (Table 5)—in both quarters, practice exam exercises late in the quarter tended to include more high-level questions."
