# Students who played the tutor role in fixed-role peer tutoring show statistically significant Reading Self-Concept gains, whereas tutees in fixed tutoring and students in reciprocal tutoring show no significant gains

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Tutors in fixed tutoring improved significantly (t=-3.42, p=.00) while tutees did not (t=-1.59, p=.11), and reciprocal tutoring showed no significant change (t=-1.73, p=.09). [→ Flores 2013](#flores-2013)

## Evidence

### Flores 2013

Flores, M., & Duran, D. (2013). Effects of Peer Tutoring on Reading Self-Concept. International Journal of Educational Psychology, 2(3), 297-324. https://doi.org/10.4471/ijep.2013.29

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Related-samples t-tests within the intervention group's fixed-role tutoring subsample (172 tutors, 173 tutees; Table 5). Findings include one verbatim fragment: tutors "show statistically -significant differences between their pre -test and post -test".

> "The students who acted as tutors show statistically -significant differences between their pre -test and post -test, whereas the tutees do not show any statistically-significant differences"

### Flores 2013 (2)

Flores, M., & Duran, D. (2013). Effects of Peer Tutoring on Reading Self-Concept. International Journal of Educational Psychology, 2(3), 297-324. https://doi.org/10.4471/ijep.2013.29

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Related-samples t-tests comparing tutoring types within the intervention group (96 reciprocal, 345 fixed; Table 4). Findings include one verbatim fragment: fixed tutoring shows "significant differences" but "not in the group performing reciprocal tutoring".

> "significant differences are observed between the pre-test and the post -test in the group performing fixed tutoring, but not in the group performing reciprocal tutoring"
