Evidence¶

McCormick 2016¶

McCormick, M. P., Cappella, E., O'Connor, E. E., Hill, J., & McClowry, S. G. (2016). Do Effects of Social-Emotional Learning Programs Vary by Level of Parent Participation? Evidence from a Randomized Trial. SREE Spring 2016 Conference Abstract. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED567204

q3 · i? · causal · r2

IPTW-weighted growth models from the randomized trial show faster math and reading growth for both high participation (B = 2.23 and B = 4.14) and low participation students, with "similar findings" across groups.

"Growth in math (B = 2.23, p < .01) and reading (B = 4.14, p < .05) achievement was faster for students in INSIGHTS with high levels of parent participation, relative to their counterfactual condition. There were similar findings, however, for the low participation group."

McCormick 2016 (2)¶

McCormick, M. P., Cappella, E., O'Connor, E. E., Hill, J., & McClowry, S. G. (2016). Do Effects of Social-Emotional Learning Programs Vary by Level of Parent Participation? Evidence from a Randomized Trial. SREE Spring 2016 Conference Abstract. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED567204

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Coefficient-difference tests in the results section show the low parent dosage group's achievement growth effects were statistically larger than the high dosage group's for math (χ2 = 7.06, p < .01) and reading (χ2 = 9.18, p < .01).

"Differences for the low dosage group were larger for both math (χ2 = 7.06, p < .01) and reading (χ2 = 9.18, p < .01) achievement."
