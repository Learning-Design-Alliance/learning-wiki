Evidence¶

Cho 2019¶

Cho, E., Capin, P., Roberts, G., Roberts, G. J., & Vaughn, S. (2019). Examining Sources and Mechanisms of Reading Comprehension Difficulties: Comparing English Learners and Non-English Learners within the Simple View of Reading. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000332

q3 · i? · associational · r2

From the MG-SEM results: the EL model explained 98% and the non-EL model 94% of reading comprehension variance, consistent with other latent-variable SVR studies the article cites (e.g., Foorman et al., 2018, reporting 68%–99%).

"linguistic comprehension (vocabulary and listening comprehension) and word reading account for 94% to 98% of the variance in reading comprehension for ELs and non-ELs, respectively."

Related Claims¶

  • Vocabulary and listening comprehension are distinct rather than unitary constructs for struggling ELs and non-ELs, with a four-factor model fitting better than a three-factor model — related
  • Struggling ELs scored lower than non-ELs in reading comprehension and linguistic comprehension but outperformed non-ELs in word reading — related
  • For non-ELs the effect of word reading on reading comprehension is larger than the combined effects of linguistic comprehension, whereas for ELs linguistic comprehension effects are greater — related
  • Word reading is a significant direct predictor of reading comprehension for both ELs and non-ELs with reading difficulties in grade 4 — related
  • A general oral language factor comprising syntax and vocabulary indicators significantly predicts reading comprehension in all grades 4–10, in models whose latent factors together account for 72%–99% of its variance — related
