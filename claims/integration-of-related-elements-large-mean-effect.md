## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Across 50 independent effects from between-group randomized studies, redesigns that increased spatial or temporal contiguity of related information yielded a weighted mean effect of d = 0.85 on learning performance. [→ Ginns 2006](#ginns-2006)

## Evidence

### Ginns 2006

Ginns, P. (2006). Integrating information: A meta-analysis of the spatial contiguity and temporal contiguity effects. Learning and Instruction, 16(6), 511-525. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2006.10.001

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 50 effects representing 2375 students from 29 journal articles, 1 conference paper, and 1 PhD thesis, all between-group studies with random assignment. The article reports "d = 0.85 (95% confidence interval 0.68 – 1.02)" with significant heterogeneity (Q = 117.77), indicating moderators condition the mean effect.

> "the weighted mean effect size of the 50 effects was large, d = 0.85 (95% confidence interval 0.68 – 1.02), and the homogeneity statistic was highly significant, Q = 117.77, df = 49, p < 0.001"
