# Students who engaged in no repetition activity scored significantly lower on long-term anatomical retention than all other groups

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In an ANCOVA controlling for pre-course test scores, the no-repetition group scored significantly lower on the long-term retention test than the four groups with repetition activities (partial eta-squared = 0.144). [→ Kooloos Jan G.M. 2020](#kooloos-jan-gm-2020)

## Evidence

### Kooloos Jan G.M. 2020

Kooloos Jan G.M., Bergman Esther M., Scheffers Marieke A.G.P., Schepens-Franke Annelieke N., Vorstenbosch Marc A.T.M. (2020). The Effect of Passive and Active Education Methods Applied in Repetition Activities on the Retention of Anatomical Knowledge. Anatomical Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.1924

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental curriculum study of 180 students in five groups; ANCOVA with long-term retention test as dependent variable, group as fixed variable, pre-course test as covariate. Pair-wise comparisons showed Group 1 lower than all others, with "P = 0.008, P = 0.0001, P = 0.001, and P = 0.0001, respectively" for Groups 2–5; "Partial eta‐squared indicated a moderate effect size of 0.144."

> "The ANCOVA showed a significant difference in long‐term retention among the five groups (df = 180; F = 7.325; P = 0.0001). The pair‐wise comparisons based on estimated means showed that scores on the long‐term retention of Group 1 were lower than the scores of all the other groups"
