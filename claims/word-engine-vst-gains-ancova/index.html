Evidence¶

McLean 2013¶

McLean, S., Hogg, N., & Rush, T. W. (2013). Vocabulary learning through an online computerized flashcard site. The JALT CALL Journal, 9(1), 79-98. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1108008

q2 · i3 · causal · r1

Quasi-experimental ANCOVA across 12 intact, non-randomly assigned classes (control n = 57, vocabulary+ER n = 61, vocabulary n = 64), posttest VST as dependent variable and pretest as covariate. Pretest scores differed significantly between groups. The control classes were assigned one hour a week of extensive reading, while both Word Engine groups were assigned two hours of out-of-class work (one hour of Word Engine plus one graded reader, or two hours of Word Engine), and extensive reading was run differently in the control and vocabulary+ER groups. The article reports an effect size of η² = .55 (printed as ŋ²= .55) and states the relationship was "very strong". The authors acknowledge selection threats and uncontrolled time on task, and conclude only that "it is plausible that a significant portion of the increased gains in VST scores were due to the use of Word Engine", so the study does not establish that Word Engine use caused the difference.

"The ANCOVA was significant F (2, 178) = 108.77, MSE = 252270.28, p = < .001, ŋ²= .55. The strength of the relationship between the word-card variable and the dependent variable was very strong, accounting for 55% of the variance of the dependent variable, holding constant the vocabulary size before the word card treatment."
