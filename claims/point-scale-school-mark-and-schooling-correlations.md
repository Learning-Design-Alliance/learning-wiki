### Arthur S. Otis 1917

Arthur S. Otis. (1917). An Absolute Point Scale for the Group Measurement of Intelligence.—Part II. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0074753

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Separate per-grade correlations between teachers' final school marks and total point scores, because marks from different teachers were not comparable. The article reports "The coefficients were found for the fourth, sixth, and eighth grades to be respectively .80, .41, and .50," while noting teachers' marks have no reliability measure.

> "The coefficients were found for the fourth, sixth, and eighth grades to be respectively .80, .41, and .50."

### Arthur S. Otis 1917 (2)

Arthur S. Otis. (1917). An Absolute Point Scale for the Group Measurement of Intelligence.—Part II. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0074753

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Analysis of self-reported schooling time for 121 pupils grouped by retardation or advance (Table VII), with data from 10 pupils missing. Average brightness coefficients rose from 77 for pupils retarded 3 years to 114 for those advanced 2 years; the author reports "a very definite tendency for advanced pupils to obtain high coefficients of brightness."

> "There is, however, a very definite tendency for advanced pupils to obtain high coefficients of brightness and for retarded pupils to obtain low coefficients of brightness."
