# Many students initially failed to identify that photon wavelength shorter than the slit separation destroys interference (Q1), and the QuILT largely remedied this for undergraduates

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On Q1, only 9% of undergraduates and 14% of graduate students correctly identified the photon wavelength condition on the pre-test; after the QuILT, 91% of undergraduates and 64% of graduates received full credit. [→ Sayer 2015](#sayer-2015)

## Evidence

### Sayer 2015

Sayer, R., Maries, A., & Singh, C. (2015). Developing and evaluating a tutorial on the double-slit experiment. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.070](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.070)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Pre/post categorization of student responses to Q1 (single-electron DSE with a lamp introduced near the slits), summarized in Table V. A correct response mentions that the lamp's photon wavelength should be shorter than the slit separation so that which-path information is obtained.

> "On the pre -test 9% of undergraduates  and 14% of graduate students were able to correctly identify the photon wavelength condition for whether an interference pattern will form  on the screen . On the post -test 91% of undergraduates and 64% of graduate students received full credit for their responses."
