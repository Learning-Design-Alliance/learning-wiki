# Providing a non-technical observer tool during observation of high-fidelity crisis simulation does not significantly improve anaesthesia residents' overall non-technical skills performance (ANTS score)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Anaesthesia residents who used a non-technical OT while observing simulation showed no significant difference in ANTS score compared with those who did not (p = 0.13). [→ Havard C 2025](#havard-c-2025)

## Evidence

### Havard C 2025

Havard C, Jouffroy V, Le Gouez A, Bruyère M, Figueiredo S, Roulleau P, Benhamou D, Margat A, Blanié A. (2025). Use of an observer tool to enhance learning of anaesthesia resident's non-technical skills during high-fidelity simulation: a randomised controlled trial. BMC Medical Education 25:97. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06608-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomised controlled trial at the Paris-Saclay simulation centre: 63 anaesthesia residents analysed (OT+ n = 33, OT- n = 30), ANTS scored from video by an external expert. Medians were "12 [9.5–12.5] out of 16 in the OT + group" versus 10.5 [8.75–12], Mann-Whitney p = 0.13; equivalence was not tested.

> "The performance of NTS assessed by the ANTS score was 12 [9.5–12.5] out of 16 in the OT + group and 10.5 [8.75–12] out of 16 in the OT- group, with no significant difference between the groups (p = 0.13)."
