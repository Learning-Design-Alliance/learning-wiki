## Evidence

### Burmeister Jay 2014

Burmeister Jay, McSpadden Erin, Rakowski Joseph, Nalichowski Adrian, Yudelev Mark, Snyder Michael. (2014). Correlation of admissions statistics to graduate student success in medical physics. Journal of Applied Clinical Medical Physics 15(1):375-385. https://doi.org/10.1120/jacmp.v15i1.4451

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Retrospective cohort of 108 students with undergraduate majors categorized as physics, engineering, or other; faculty rating dichotomized at 2.5. Engineering majors had "a statistically significantly higher faculty rating (p=0.020)"; physics versus other degrees showed no significant difference (p=0.787).

> "Students with undergraduate degrees in engineering had a statistically significantly higher faculty rating (p=0.020), while there was no statistically significant difference between those with a physics degree and those with an undergraduate degree classified as "other" (p=0.787)."
