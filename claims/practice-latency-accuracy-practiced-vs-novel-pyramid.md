## Evidence

### Tenison 2015

Tenison, C., & Anderson, J. R. (2015). Modeling the distinct phases of skill acquisition. https://dx.doi.org/10.1037/xlm0000204

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVA on latency across six blocks in the fMRI arithmetic training task, comparing practiced vs. novel Pyramid problems. The article reports "a significant problem by block interaction, F(5,95)=14.24"; practiced-problem latency fell while novel-problem latency stayed fairly constant.

> "A repeated measures ANOVA run on the latency data revealed a significant main effect of problem group (practiced vs. novel), F(1,19)=69.26, MSE= 296.8, p\<.001, block, F(5,95)=23.59, MSE=9.4, p\<.001, and a significant problem by block interaction, F(5,95)=14.24, MSE=4.5, p\<.001."

### Tenison 2015 (2)

Tenison, C., & Anderson, J. R. (2015). Modeling the distinct phases of skill acquisition. https://dx.doi.org/10.1037/xlm0000204

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Repeated-measures ANOVA on accuracy data from the same training task showed "a significant problem by block interaction" pattern: accuracy rose for practiced problems while novel-problem accuracy stayed relatively constant; the interaction reached p<.05.

> "Participants’ problem solving accuracy increased over time, but their accuracy solving novel problems remained relatively constant (Effect of practice: F(1,19)=18.2, MSE=1.0, p\<.001, effect of block: F(5,95)=8.7, MSE=0.1, p\<.001 , interaction: F(5,95)=2.8, MSE=0.3, p\<.05)"
