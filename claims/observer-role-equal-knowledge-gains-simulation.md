# Observing a stroke-care simulation produces knowledge gains equal to acting in it, with no statistical difference between roles

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### da Silva Gisele Cabral 2025

da Silva Gisele Cabral, Campanharo Cássia Regina Vancini, Lopes Maria Carolina Barbosa Teixeira, Okuno Meiry Fernanda Pinto, Miura Carla Roberta Monteiro, Batista Ruth Ester Assayag. (2025). Clinical simulation and deliberate practice in rapid-cycle deliberate practice: a randomized clinical trial. Revista Brasileira de Enfermagem. https://doi.org/10.1590/0034-7167-2024-0632

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-trial subgroup comparison of the 66 SWSP students, redistributed into 33 acting and 33 observing; both subgroups improved pre-to-post (p<0.001), with the acting group's post-test mean of 8.64 versus 8.24 for observers, but "no statistical difference between them (p=0.76)".

> "However, when comparing the two groups (acting and observing), there was no statistical difference between them (p=0.76)."
