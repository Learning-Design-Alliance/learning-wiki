# Question order (grouped vs. interspersed MCMR items) has no systematic effect on the number of responses chosen

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In an online experiment varying item order, the number of answer choices differed significantly for all items except item 1, but with no systematic direction; a cross-institution paper comparison also showed no systematic significant difference. [→ Alexis Olsho 2021](#alexis-olsho-2021)

## Evidence

### Alexis Olsho 2021

Alexis Olsho, Trevor I. Smith, Philip Eaton, Charlotte Zimmerman, Andrew Boudreaux, and Suzanne White Brahmia. (2021). Online test administration results in students selecting more responses to multiple-choice-multiple-response items. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2106.02137](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2106.02137)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Online administration of the PIQL to one Institution 1 class (N = 83), with 43 students seeing MCMR items grouped at the end and 40 seeing them interspersed with SR items. The article reports significant differences for all items except item 1 but "no systematic effect".

> "While the number of answer choices seems to be statistically signiﬁcantly different for all items except item 1, we note that there is no systematic effect."
