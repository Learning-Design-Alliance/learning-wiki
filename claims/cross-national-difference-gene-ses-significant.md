# The difference in G × SES effect magnitude between US and non-US studies is statistically significant

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` A nation dummy (US vs non-US) significantly predicts the G × SES effect size (b = .101, SE = .032, p = .001), and this difference survives robustness checks excluding TEDS, MTFS, the authors' own studies, and Turkheimer-authored studies. [→ Tucker-Drob 2016](#tucker-drob-2016)

## Evidence

### Tucker-Drob 2016

Tucker-Drob, E. M., & Bates, T. C. (2016). Large Cross-National Differences in Gene × Socioeconomic Status Interaction on Intelligence. Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797615612727

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random-effects meta-regression with a nation dummy across all 43 effect sizes; the printed difference was "b = .101, SE = .032, p = .001". Robustness models excluding key studies yielded differences of .128, .135, .107, and .085, all significant.

> "A model that added a dummy coded nation variable (0=non-US, 1=US) to the meta-regression model indicated that there was a significant difference between G × SES effect sizes from the US compared to those from Western Europe and Australia (b = .101, SE = .032, p = .001)."
