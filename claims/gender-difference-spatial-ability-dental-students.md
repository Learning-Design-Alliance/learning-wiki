## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the overall sample of 326 dental students, males scored statistically significantly higher than females on both spatial ability tests. [→ Erli Sarilita 2022](#erli-sarilita-2022)

## Evidence

### Erli Sarilita 2022

Erli Sarilita, Lita Yurika Ambar, Firman Dani Rizali, Tracey Wilkinson, Sri Susilawati, Risti Saptarini, Dudi Aripin, Endang Sjamsudin. (2022). Spatial ability and anatomy learning performance among dental students. Korean Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.3946/kjme.2022.239

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional study of 326 dental students (49 male, 277 female) taking PSVT-R and MRT online during 2020; independent t-tests detected the gender differences, with males at 70.5%±19.4% versus females at 57.3%±16.1% on PSVT-R. No effect size was printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "For the overall sample, males had statistically significantly higher PSVT-R scores (70.5%±19.4%) compared to females (57.3%±16.1%, t [324]=5.139, p<0.001) (Table 2). Males also had statistically significantly higher MRT scores (69.1%±23.1%) compared to females (59.1±18.9%, t [324]=3.326, p<0.001)."
