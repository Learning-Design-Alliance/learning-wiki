# Math course passing rates improved significantly more in elementary schools with extensive professional development, while course grades improved at similar rates across PD levels

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Elementary improvements in passing rates were significantly higher in extensive-PD schools than limited-PD schools, despite higher academic demand in those schools. [→ Julia A. Gwynne and Sarah Cashdollar 2018](#julia-a-gwynne-and-sarah-cashdollar-2018)
`q2 i?` Math grades improved over time at similar rates for students in grades 6-12 across schools with differing PD levels. [→ Julia A. Gwynne and Sarah Cashdollar 2018 (2)](#julia-a-gwynne-and-sarah-cashdollar-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Julia A. Gwynne and Sarah Cashdollar 2018

Julia A. Gwynne and Sarah Cashdollar. (2018). Changes in Math Instruction and Student Outcomes since the Implementation of Common Core State Standards in Chicago. UChicago Consortium working paper. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/changes-math-instruction-and-student-outcomes-implementation-common-core-state

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Difference-in-difference analysis of math course passing rates (grades 6-12, spring 2011 through spring 2017); at the high-school level, pass-rate improvements were comparable across PD levels.

> "Among elementary schools, improvements in passing rates were signiﬁcantly higher in schools with extensive standards-related professional development compared to schools with limited professional development, which is noteworthy given the higher levels of academic demand that was also evident in these schools."

### Julia A. Gwynne and Sarah Cashdollar 2018 (2)

UChicago Consortium working paper. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/changes-math-instruction-and-student-outcomes-implementation-common-core-state

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of math course grades from hierarchical linear models across the three time periods, shown in Figure 7 for elementary and high school panels.

> "Overall, math grades improved over time and at similar rates for students in sixth through twelfth grade in schools with diﬀering levels of standards-related professional development (Panels A and B in Figure 7)."
