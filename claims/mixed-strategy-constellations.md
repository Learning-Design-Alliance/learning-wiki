# Students' strategy use formed mixed constellations: organisation and elaboration correlated strongly (r = 0.64), and effective strategies were also positively related to less effective ones such as rehearsal

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Organisation correlated strongly with elaboration (r = 0.64) and with rehearsal (r = 0.55), suggesting students use mixed constellations of effective and ineffective strategies rather than dropping ineffective ones. ## Evidence

### Rebecca Hey · Mark McDaniel · Flaviu A. Hodis 2024

Rebecca Hey · Mark McDaniel · Flaviu A. Hodis. (2024). How undergraduate students learn: uncovering interrelationships between factors that support self-regulated learning and strategy use. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-024-09389-8

`q2 · i3`

Correlational analysis of strategy self-reports (Table 2); the article reports organisation–elaboration r = 0.64 and organisation–rehearsal r = 0.55, and concludes that use of more effective strategies "was also positively related to employment of less effective strategies, such as rehearsal and rereading".

> "The largest correlation reported in Table 2 was between two relatively effective learning strategies, namely organisation and elaboration (r = 0.64)."
