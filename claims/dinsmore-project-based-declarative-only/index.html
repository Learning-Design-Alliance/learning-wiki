Evidence¶

Loyens 2008¶

Loyens, S. M. M., & Gijbels, D. (2008). Understanding the effects of constructivist learning environments: introducing a multi-directional approach. Instructional Science, 36, 351–357. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-008-9059-4

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

The special-issue introduction's summary of the Dinsmore, Alexander, and Loughlin study of students' participation in a collaborative, project-based engineering design course and their domain knowledge, interests, and strategic processing. Outcomes diverged by knowledge type; the summary does not state the direction of the declarative-knowledge effect or how it was established, and prints no effect sizes.

"Signiﬁcant effects were found for students’ declarative knowledge, but not for students’ procedural or principled knowledge, nor for students’ personal interest in the domain of engineering"

Remy M. J. P. Rikers 2008¶

Remy M. J. P. Rikers, Tamara van Gog, Fred Paas. (2008). The effects of constructivist learning environments: a commentary. Instr Sci (2008) 36:463–467. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-008-9067-4

q2 · i? · review · r2

The commentary reports Dinsmore and colleagues' study using the Model of Domain Learning to examine effects of a collaborative, project-based engineering design course on interests and declarative, procedural, and principled knowledge. Only declarative knowledge improved.

"Contrary to their expectations, the authors show that although there is some gain in declarative knowledge, other types of knowledge and personal interest do not change during the course."
