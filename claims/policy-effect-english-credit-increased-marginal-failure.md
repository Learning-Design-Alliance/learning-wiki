## Evidence

### Allensworth 2009

Allensworth, E., Nomi, T., Montgomery, N., & Lee, V. E. (2009). College Preparatory Curriculum for All: Academic Consequences of Requiring Algebra and English I for Ninth Graders in Chicago. Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis, 31(4), 367-391. https://doi.org/10.3102/0162373709343471

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

From the overall policy-effect analysis: English I credit earning increased substantially for low-ability students, reflecting extensive pre-policy remedial English use. The article reports "increases of 12% to 36%; p < .01" for the lowest-ability students; failure increases (4.0 percentage points) did not reach significance (p > .05).

> "students in all but the high-ability group were considerably more likely to earn English I credit by the end of ninth grade—particularly, the lowest-ability students (increases of 12% to 36%; p < .01)."
