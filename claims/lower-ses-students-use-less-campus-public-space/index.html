Evidence¶

Bart-Plange 2025¶

Bart-Plange, D.-J., Henderson, K., Hoffman, K., & Trawalter, S. (2025). Critiquing and Reimagining Belonging in Public Spaces in Higher Education. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10061-z

q2 · i? · associational · r?

In a map-sketching study (Trawalter et al., 2021, Study 1), students drew their day's trajectory on a campus map and circled places where they felt they belonged; maps were digitized into ArcGIS. The study found 'lower-SES students used less space on campus' and used fewer belonging spaces, related to self-reported belonging; students of color also had shorter trajectories.

"Analyses revealed that, compared with higher-SES students, lower-SES students used less space on campus—their trajectories across the day were shorter; see Fig. 3. In addition, they used fewer spaces on campus where they felt they belonged."

Bart-Plange 2025 (2)¶

Bart-Plange, D.-J., Henderson, K., Hoffman, K., & Trawalter, S. (2025). Critiquing and Reimagining Belonging in Public Spaces in Higher Education. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10061-z

q2 · i? · review · r2

In a space-listing study (Trawalter et al., 2021, Study 2), students listed favorite, most-used, and study spaces, which assistants coded as private or public. Lower-SES students 'were less likely to list public spaces on campus,' and public space use was associated with lower belonging; students of color showed a similar pattern.

"Analyses revealed that, compared with higher-SES students, lower-SES students were less likely to list public spaces on campus. In turn, their use of public (vs. private) space was associated with a lower sense of belonging at the University."
