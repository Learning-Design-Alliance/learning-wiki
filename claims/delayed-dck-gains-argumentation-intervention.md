## Evidence

### Larrain 2018

Larrain, A., Freire, P., Grau, V., López, P., Salvat, I., Silva, M., & Gastellu, V. (2018). The effect of peer-group argumentative dialogue on delayed gains in scientific content knowledge. New Directions for Child and Adolescent Development, 162, 67–87. https://doi.org/10.1002/cad.20263

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

ANCOVA on immediate and delayed Forces content knowledge post-tests, with pre-test as covariate and condition as independent variable, in a two-classroom experiment. The intervention group achieved significantly higher delayed scores with "η2 = .309" (F(1/46) = 20.1, p < .01), and "significant gains even between post-immediate test and post-delayed test (t = −4.25, p < .01)".

> "There were no significant differences on immediate post-test, but the intervention group achieved significantly higher scores in the delayed content knowledge test (F(1/46) = 20.1, p < .01, η2 = .309), showing significant gains even between post-immediate test and post-delayed test (t = −4.25, p < .01)."

### Larrain 2018 (2)

Larrain, A., Freire, P., Grau, V., López, P., Salvat, I., Silva, M., & Gastellu, V. (2018). The effect of peer-group argumentative dialogue on delayed gains in scientific content knowledge. New Directions for Child and Adolescent Development, 162, 67–87. https://doi.org/10.1002/cad.20263

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The authors' discussion of the two learning trajectories observed in the classroom experiment: "while the intervention group showed significant gains between post-immediate to post-delayed DCK tests, the control group decayed", a pattern they note resembles Howe et al. (2005) and Tolmie et al. (1993). No effect size is printed for this contrast.
