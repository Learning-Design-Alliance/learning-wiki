## Evidence

### Babayiğit 2026

Babayiğit, S., & Trenkic, D. (2026). Vocabulary knowledge is key to understanding and addressing disparities in higher education. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 96, 127–151. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.70000

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

ANOVA of writing speed, essay length, spelling error rate and essay quality across the three groups; the main effect of language group was significant on all writing measures, and "L1E students wrote faster on average, produced longer essays, and had higher quality scores".

> "Pairwise comparisons showed that L1E students wrote faster on average, produced longer essays, and had higher quality scores than their EAL and EFL peers."

### Babayiğit 2026 (2)

Babayiğit, S., & Trenkic, D. (2026). Vocabulary knowledge is key to understanding and addressing disparities in higher education. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 96, 127–151. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.70000

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Follow-up model on essay quality (Table S13, Model 2): with covariates included the group effect was eliminated, but "removing vocabulary as a covariate restored the main effect of group", t = 3.25, p = .004, d = .6.

> "Notably, removing vocabulary as a covariate restored the main effect of group, making the L1E advantage over the EFL group significant again with a medium effect size, t = 3.25, p = .004, d = .6"
