# Women's CLASS scores correlated meaningfully with normalized FCI gains, but men's did not

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` For women, CLASS postscores correlated with FCI gains in almost every category, including the overall score (r = 0.449, p = 0.0047) and sense making or effort (r = 0.457, p = 0.0039), while no meaningful correlations appeared for men. [→ Alma Robinson 2021](#alma-robinson-2021)
`q3 i3` Women's CLASS prescores also correlated with FCI gains in three categories plus overall (r = 0.386, p = 0.0168), and the postscore correlation exceeded the prescore correlation. [→ Alma Robinson 2021](#alma-robinson-2021)

## Evidence

### Alma Robinson 2021

Alma Robinson, John H. Simonetti, Kasey Richardson, and Megan Wawro. (2021). Positive attitudinal shifts and a narrowing gender gap: Do expertlike attitudes correlate to higher learning gains for women in the physics classroom? Physical Review Physics Education Research 17, 010101. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.17.010101

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Linear regression F tests on 148 students (110 men, 38 women) who completed both instruments pre and post; meaningful correlations required r> 0.3 and p< 0.05. Women's post overall: "the overall score (r ¼ 0.449, p ¼ 0.0047)"; sense making or effort r = 0.457, p = 0.0039, both above the r ≥ 0.37 threshold.

> "Interestingly, we found a large gender difference in how CLASS scores correlated with FCI gains: there were meaningful correlations for women, but we did not see any correlations for men."
