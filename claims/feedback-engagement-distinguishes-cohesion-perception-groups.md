# Feedback engagement is the main activity distinguishing perception groups, with divergent-perception teams interacting less with feedback content

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The only engagement activity showing significant differences between transition groups was feedback: teams with different cohesive perceptions had lower feedback interactions than teams with similar perceptions. [→ Zamecnik 2024](#zamecnik-2024)

## Evidence

### Zamecnik 2024

Zamecnik, A., Kovanović, V., Joksimović, S., Grossmann, G., Ladjal, D., Pardo, A. (2024). The perceptions of task cohesion in collaborative learning teams. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-024-09424-5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Log-based engagement analysis of 194 teams across 10 activity types (Table 1). Kruskal–Wallis for feedback: Week 1 "χ 2 (3, N = 194) = 14.737, p < 0.002)" and Week 3 "χ 2 (3, N = 194) = 13.671, p < 0.003)"; other activity types showed no significant group differences.

> "Teams with different cohesive perceptions have comparatively lower feedback interactions than teams with similar cohesive perceptions. Teams who are not cohesive throughout the 3-week program have significantly lower interactions with feedback content than other transition groups."
