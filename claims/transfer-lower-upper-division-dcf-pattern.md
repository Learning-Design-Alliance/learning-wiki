## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Significant transfer-related DCF effects follow a division level: lower-division courses (course No. ≤ 100) are easier for transfers, upper-division courses (course No. > 100) easier for natives. [→ Frederik Baucks 2024](#frederik-baucks-2024)

## Evidence

### Frederik Baucks 2024

Frederik Baucks, Robin Schmucker, Conrad Borchers, Zachary A. Pardos, and Laurenz Wiskott. 2024. Gaining Insights into Group-Level Course Difficulty via Differential Course Functioning. In Proceedings of the Eleventh ACM Conference on Learning @ Scale (L@S '24). https://doi.org/10.1145/3657604.3662028

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

In the discussion of the transfer-status DCF analysis, the authors report the directional pattern: effects "tend to be easier for transfers if the course is in the lower-division", likely because lower-division content overlaps courses taken at prior institutions, while upper-division difficulty may reflect lower preparation or curricular misalignment.

> "Courses that indicate effects tend to be easier for transfers if the course is in the lower-division (course No.≤ 100) and easier for natives if it is in the upper-division (course No. > 100)."
