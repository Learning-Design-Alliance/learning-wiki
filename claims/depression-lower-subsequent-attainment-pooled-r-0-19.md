## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Across 22 studies pooled in random-effects meta-analysis, depression symptoms showed a small, statistically significant negative association with later educational attainment measured from school records (pooled Fisher z = −0.19, equivalent to r = −0.19). [→ Wickersham A. 2021](#wickersham-a-2021)

## Evidence

### Wickersham A. 2021

Wickersham A., Sugg H.V.R., Epstein S., Stewart R., Ford T., Downs J. (2021). Systematic Review and Meta-analysis: The Association Between Child and Adolescent Depression and Later Educational Attainment. Journal of the American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2020.10.008

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 22 longitudinal studies pooling correlation coefficients between depression symptoms and later attainment from school records. The article reports "a pooled correlation coefficient of r = −0.19 (see Supplement 3, available online), a small effect indicating that depression symptoms accounted for 3.6% of the variance in attainment."

> "We found a statistically significant negative association between depression and attainment (pooled Fisher z = −0.19, 95% CI = −0.22 to −0.16, z = 13.32, p < .001)"
