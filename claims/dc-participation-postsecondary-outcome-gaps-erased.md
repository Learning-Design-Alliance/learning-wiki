# Dual credit participation in Texas is associated with stronger postsecondary outcomes, with low-income DC students twice as likely and Black and Latino DC students three times as likely to earn an associate degree in high school

## Evidence

### Kristen Hengtgen 2024

Kristen Hengtgen, Hector Biaggi. (2024). Increasing Access to Advanced Coursework in Texas. EdTrust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED641978

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The brief cites the Community College Research Center and University of Texas at Austin 2023 DC Data Dashboard, an observational dashboard, reporting that "Taking any DC course all but erases college enrollment and first-year persistence gaps by race."

> "The tool highlights the importance of closing DC participation gaps by showing the remarkable effects of DC course participation on postsecondary outcomes: Taking any DC course all but erases college enrollment and first-year persistence gaps by race."

### Kristen Hengtgen 2024 (2)

Kristen Hengtgen, Hector Biaggi. (2024). Increasing Access to Advanced Coursework in Texas. EdTrust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED641978

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same dashboard comparison shows low-income DC students at 10% versus 5% associate-degree attainment and Black and Latino DC students at 10% versus 3% for White peers; the brief reports these descriptive percentages with no standardized effect size.

> "low-income DC students are two times more likely to earn an associate degree in high school than their wealthier peers (10% vs. 5%), and Black and Latino students are three times more likely than their White peers to do so (10% vs. 3%)."
