## Evidence

### Gazit N 2025

Gazit N, Ben-Gal G, Eliashar R. (2025). Game-Based Assessment of Cognitive Abilities and Personality Characteristics for Surgical Resident Selection: A Preliminary Validation Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/72264

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis of intern test data compared male and female total scores. The article reports "mean difference 10.9, 95% CI 3.1-17.6, t150=2.8, P=.002, Cohen d=0.52", a printed effect size of d = 0.52, which the authors characterize as a small-to-medium effect size.

> "males (mean 104.6, SD 16.8) scored significantly higher than females (mean 94.3, SD 21.9) on the gamified test (mean difference 10.9, 95% CI 3.1-17.6, t150=2.8, P=.002, Cohen d=0.52). This represents a small-to-medium effect size."

### Gazit N 2025 (2)

Gazit N, Ben-Gal G, Eliashar R. (2025). Game-Based Assessment of Cognitive Abilities and Personality Characteristics for Surgical Resident Selection: A Preliminary Validation Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/72264

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Follow-up analysis of the intern data controlling for self-reported video game experience. The article states "when we controlled for video game experience, the difference between the genders was no longer significant", with no effect size printed for the controlled comparison.

> "Interestingly, when we controlled for video game experience, the difference between the genders was no longer significant, suggesting that this difference is mainly due to different levels of video game experience."
