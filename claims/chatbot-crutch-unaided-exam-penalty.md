## Evidence

### Daiki Nakamura 2026

Daiki Nakamura. (2026). Augmentation or Erosion? Regulating Cognitive Offloading in Generative AI-Supported STEM Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10224-6

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review reports, citing Bastani et al., a field experiment with nearly a thousand secondary mathematics students. Students treated the tool as a "crutch," reproducing its answers and errors, and believed they had learned as much as peers while performing worse on the unaided exam.

> "access to a standard chatbot interface during practice dramatically improved performance on the practice problems themselves, yet lowered performance on a subsequent exam taken without AI relative to a control group that had never had access"

### Daiki Nakamura 2026 (2)

Daiki Nakamura. (2026). Augmentation or Erosion? Regulating Cognitive Offloading in Generative AI-Supported STEM Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10224-6

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review reports, citing Bastani et al., that the hint-based GPT Tutor withheld answers and offered teacher-crafted hints, keeping the target process with the student and eliminating the learning penalty the answer-giving interface produced.

> "Within a single experiment, the same model produced harm under an answer-giving interface and no harm under a hint-giving one (Bastani et al., 2025)"
