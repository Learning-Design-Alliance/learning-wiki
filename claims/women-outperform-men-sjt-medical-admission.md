## Subclaims
`q2 i0` Across three admission years, negative Cohen's d values indicate women performed consistently better than men on the SJT, with weak to moderate effect sizes (d = -.14 to -.22). [→ Luschin-Ebengreuth M 2015](#luschin-ebengreuth-m-2015)

## Evidence

### Luschin-Ebengreuth M 2015

Luschin-Ebengreuth M, Dimai HP, Ithaler D, Neges HM, Reibnegger G. (2015). Situational judgment test as an additional tool in a medical admission test: an observational investigation. BMC Research Notes. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-015-1033-z

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of relative scores of 4741 applicants over three admission years (2010/11–2012/13), comparing women and men per test part using Cohen's d. Printed SJT d values were -.14, -.19 and -.22, described as "consistent better performances of women with weak to moderate effect size".

> "While in text comprehension men still perform slightly better than women, the reverse is true in SJT; here the negative values of Cohen’s d indicate consistent better performances of women with weak to moderate effect size."
