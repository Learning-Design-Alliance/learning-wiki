## Evidence

### Rhodes 2018

Rhodes, K. T., Lukowski, S., Branum-Martin, L., Opfer, J., Geary, D. C., & Petrill, S. A. (2018). Individual Differences in Addition Strategy Choice: A Psychometric Evaluation. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11360214/

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Frequencies and descriptive statistics of strategies across the 30 addition items in the 376-adolescent sample. The observed ordering of solution times (retrieval fastest, then decomposition, min-counting, algorithm) was described as consistent with prior studies and supportive of self-report validity.

> "Even among these adolescent problem-solvers, overt counting, especially min-counting, was still common; 20% of simple (e.g., 4 + 6), 30% of mixed (e.g., 17 + 5), and 44% of double (e.g., 26 + 15) problems were solved at least once with min-counting."
