## Evidence

### Huang 2026

Huang, Y., Guo, J.-H., Zheng, S., Yin, R., Wang, J. & Jiang, C.-L. (2026). Enhancing attention network and neural efficiency through brief mindfulness meditation. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00455-1

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Pretest-posttest ANCOVA in a randomized study of 72 healthy adults (BMM n=30, control n=42), controlling pretest scores. The BMM group outperformed controls on executive control efficiency, "Executive Control Network ( F = 17.51, p < 0.001, η2 = 0.202)".

> "ANCOVA showed a signi ﬁcant difference between the BMM and the Control groups on the Average Response Time ( F = 21.31, p < 0.001, η2 = 0.142), and Executive Control Network ( F = 17.51, p < 0.001, η2 = 0.202)."

### Huang 2026 (2)

Huang, Y., Guo, J.-H., Zheng, S., Yin, R., Wang, J. & Jiang, C.-L. (2026). Enhancing attention network and neural efficiency through brief mindfulness meditation. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00455-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Bonferroni-adjusted post hoc comparisons from the same ANT ANCOVA; no effect size is printed for these contrasts. Higher scores indicate worse efficiency (costs/RTs); the control group was "48.43 points higher than the BMM group after the intervention on the Average Response Time".

> "Post hoc analyses with Bonferroni adjustment indicated that the control group scored 48.43 points higher than the BMM group after the intervention on the Average Response Time (t = 4.62, p < 0.001), and 21.79 points higher on the Executive Control Network (t = 4.13, p < 0.001)."
