## Evidence

### Kim et al. 2019

James S. Kim, Catherine A. Asher, Mary Burkhauser, Laura Mesite, & Diana Leyva. (2019). Using a Sequential Multiple Assignment Randomized Trial (SMART) to Develop an Adaptive K–2 Literacy Intervention With Personalized Print Texts and App-Based Digital Activities. AERA Open. https://doi.org/10.1177/2332858419872701

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Stage 2 of a SMART: before implementation, students were randomly assigned to the follow-up support they would receive if they did not respond to the summer app (gamification alone, or gamification plus text messages to parents). The analysis is intent-to-treat over all 273 K–2 students, so it includes responders, who were never eligible for the supports, and families with no valid phone number; the authors call it a conservative estimate of the supports' effect. Gamification + Texting students scored marginally higher on the fall MAP (ES = 0.10, p = 0.09, n = 217) and significantly higher on the informational-text craft-and-structure subscore (ES = 0.27, p = 0.02, n = 137, Grades 1–2 only); the key-ideas-and-details subscore (ES = 0.20, p = 0.12), DIBELS (ES = 0.00) and TRC (ES = −0.03) did not differ significantly. App use was not significantly higher either (probability of accessing the app 0.28 vs 0.22, p = 0.33; activities completed 13.6 vs 9.4, p = 0.26).

> "In the fall, Gamification + Texting students scored marginally significantly higher on the MAP (ES = 0.10, p = 0.09) and significantly higher on the MAP subscore related to the craft and structure of informational text (ES = 0.27, p = 0.02) compared with students in the Gamification Only condition."

> "Students assigned to the Gamification + Texting support condition were not significantly more likely to use the MORE@Home app by any of the outcomes presented."
