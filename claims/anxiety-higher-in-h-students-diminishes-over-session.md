## Evidence

### Katai 2020

Katai, Z. (2020). Promoting computational thinking of both sciences- and humanities-oriented students: an instructional and motivational design perspective. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-020-09766-5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

MANOVA on anxiety statements (q2/10, q5/15, q6/15, q7/12; q3/4, q4/3) shown in Fig. 9. Both groups reported relatively low-level feelings of anxiety with strong correlation ("r = 0.86 > 0.70").

> "Although H-students' scores were significantly superior to those of their S-colleagues' (MANOVA, p = .0056), the size of the differences diminished as the two groups advanced during the learning process."

### Katai 2020 (2)

Katai, Z. (2020). Promoting computational thinking of both sciences- and humanities-oriented students: an instructional and motivational design perspective. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-020-09766-5

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis linking anxiety statements to software-registered errors during the doing phases; the article prints correlations of "r = 0.99" (group-Ha) and "r = 0.98" (group-Sa).

> "We also observed strong correlations between students responses to statements (q5/15, q6/15, q7/12) and the number of errors they made during the "doing phases" (group-Ha, r = 0.99 > 0.87; group-Sa, r = 0.98 > 0.87)"
