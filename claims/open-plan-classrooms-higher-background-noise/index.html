Evidence¶

Gary Rance 2023¶

Gary Rance, Richard C. Dowell and Dani Tomlin. (2023). The effect of classroom environment on literacy development. npj Science of Learning 8:9. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00157-y

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Acoustic sampling at each site in both configurations with occupied classrooms engaged in group work; ten-minute dBA samples from the room centre. The authors suggest the difference may reflect the Lombard Effect, increased student vocal effort in noise.

"Background noise levels were also relatively low in the enclosed classroom condition, but showed a signiﬁcant increase (5.4 dB) in the open-plan conﬁguration (Enclosed: mean = 56.7, SD = 5.2 dB L Aeq,; Open: mean = 62.1, SD = 4.2 dB L Aeq,, Paired-T: p = 0.021, 95%CI: 1.20, 9.57)."

Gary Rance 2023 (2)¶

Gary Rance, Richard C. Dowell and Dani Tomlin. (2023). The effect of classroom environment on literacy development. npj Science of Learning 8:9. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00157-y

q3 · i0 · causal · r2

Reverberation time measured via the integrated impulse response technique (ISO 3382), RT(60) averaged across octave frequencies 125 Hz to 8 kHz. Rooms were well acoustically treated, so no reverberation difference was detected between conditions.

"reverberation times were relatively low (i.e. within the range recommended for typically developing children) 48 and showed no difference between open and enclosed classroom conditions (Enclosed: mean =0.42, SD = 0.09 s; Open: mean =0.45, SD = 0.09 s, Paired-T: p = 0.289, 95%CI: −0.03, 0.09)."
