# Qualitative interviews with data collectors attributed outcome differences between placements to curriculum content and dosage, paraeducator support styles, and teacher expectations

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · qualitative `r1` · `q1`
> **Currency** · newest study 2017 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In inclusive settings children participated fully in the regular education curriculum, while segregated settings used remedial or autism-specific curricula not focused on age or grade-level content, with no homework. [→ Strain 2017](#strain-2017)
`q1 i?` Paraeducators in segregated settings either provided extremely high support discouraging independent participation or did paperwork, whereas in inclusive settings they provided cues supporting accurate, independent task completion. [→ Strain 2017 (2)](#strain-2017-2)
`q1 i?` Teachers in segregated settings rarely praised correct academic responding or gave corrective feedback, while inclusive teaching staff regularly gave feedback and held children to a standard of accuracy. [→ Strain 2017 (3)](#strain-2017-3)

## Evidence

### Strain 2017

Phillip S. Strain. (2017). Four-Year Follow-Up of Children in the LEAP Randomized Trial: Some Planned and Accidental Findings. Topics in Early Childhood Special Education, 37(2), 121–126. https://doi.org/10.1177/0271121417711531

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Open-ended follow-up interviews with study data collectors, analyzed with standard qualitative theme-derivation methods; curriculum was a variable the study did not measure directly.

> "These curricula, for the most part, were not focused on age or grade-level academic content and in many ways actually mimicked content that children were exposed to at preschool (e.g., shapes, object names, colors, etc.)."

### Strain 2017 (2)

Phillip S. Strain. (2017). Four-Year Follow-Up of Children in the LEAP Randomized Trial: Some Planned and Accidental Findings. Topics in Early Childhood Special Education, 37(2), 121–126. https://doi.org/10.1177/0271121417711531

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Second qualitative theme from the data-collector interviews, contrasting paraeducator support styles across setting types; the author suggests this reflects a different dosage and quality of instruction.

> "in inclusive settings, paraedu- cators were regularly seen assisting children with academic assignments and particularly providing additional cues (e.g., models, partial physical prompts, prompts to peers) such that children completed tasks accurately and as independently as possible."

### Strain 2017 (3)

Phillip S. Strain. (2017). Four-Year Follow-Up of Children in the LEAP Randomized Trial: Some Planned and Accidental Findings. Topics in Early Childhood Special Education, 37(2), 121–126. https://doi.org/10.1177/0271121417711531

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Third qualitative theme, manifest as high expectations: segregated-setting feedback centered on behavior management and compliance, with little praise or corrective feedback on academic tasks.

> "In inclusive settings, teaching staff regularly gave children feedback on their class work, grading activities, suggesting correct answers, and gener- ally holding all children to a standard of accuracy."
