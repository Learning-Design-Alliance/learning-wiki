# Filtering rankings to responses chosen by at least 1% of the population reduces ambiguous rankings, suggesting ambiguities come from rarely chosen responses

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Restricting Table III rankings to responses given by at least 1% of the population reduces the fraction of ambiguous (≥) rankings from 35% to 29% and increases the fraction of definitive (>) rankings from 36% to 56%. [→ Trevor I. Smith 2020](#trevor-i-smith-2020)

## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith 2020

Trevor I. Smith, Kyle J. Louis, Bartholomew J. Ricci, IV, and Nasrine Bendjilali. (2020). Quantitatively ranking incorrect responses to multiple-choice questions using item response theory. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010107](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010107)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of the full IRT rankings (Tables I and II) with rankings filtered to responses chosen by at least 1% of the population. The authors attribute the ambiguities to broad parameter distributions generated for responses with low probability of being chosen at any level of understanding.

> "a smaller fraction of the rankings in Table III are " ≥" as compared to Tables I and II (29% vs. 35%), and a greater fraction of rankings are " >" (56% vs. 36%)."
