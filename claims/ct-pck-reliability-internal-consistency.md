# The 16-item CT–PCK Survey shows internal consistency reliability around .71–.72 and person separation reliability of .69

## Evidence

### Witherspoon 2026

Witherspoon, E.B.; Dracopoli, M., Hawkins, M., Dahlke, K. (2026). Developing a measure of middle school math teachers' pedagogical content knowledge of computational thinking (REL 2026–007). U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance. https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/resource-library/report/descriptive-study/developing-measure-middle-school-math-teachers-pedagogical-content-knowledge-computational-thinking

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Reliability analysis of the 16-item survey using pooled responses from 102 teachers. KR-20 and Cronbach's alpha were .71 and McDonald's omega .72; Rasch person separation reliability was .69, which the report characterizes as adequate for distinguishing ability levels.

> "Overall internal consistency reliability for the survey showed a Kuder-Richardson Formula 20 (KR-20) coefficient and a Cronbach's alpha of .71, and a McDonald's omega of .72"
