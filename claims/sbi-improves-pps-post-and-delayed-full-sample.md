## Evidence

### Jitendra 2017

Jitendra, A. K., Harwell, M. R., Karl, S. R., Im, S-H., & Slater, S. C. (2017). Investigating a Tier 1 intervention focused on proportional reasoning: A follow-up study. Journal of Learning Disabilities, 83(4), 340-358. https://doi.org/10.1177/0014402917691017

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Multilevel (students within classrooms) analysis of the randomized cluster trial's PPS posttest, with pretest and background covariates. The SBI vs. control difference was ".63 SD or 74% of SBI students scored above the mean of control students"; the article reports this as g = 0.63 SD.

> "Table 3 shows that SBI was a significant predictor of PPS posttest and that SBI students on average scored 2.25 points higher than control students (conditional on the model). Equivalently the SBI vs. control difference was .63 SD or 74% of SBI students scored above the mean of control students."

### Jitendra 2017 (2)

Jitendra, A. K., Harwell, M. R., Karl, S. R., Im, S-H., & Slater, S. C. (2017). Investigating a Tier 1 intervention focused on proportional reasoning: A follow-up study. Journal of Learning Disabilities, 83(4), 340-358. https://doi.org/10.1177/0014402917691017

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Multilevel analysis of the PPS delayed posttest administered 11 weeks after treatment in the same randomized cluster trial. The SBI vs. control difference was ".33 SD or 63% of SBI students scored above the mean of control students"; the article reports this as g = .33 SD.

> "Table 4 shows that SBI was a significant predictor of the delayed posttest and that on average SBI students scored 1.14 points higher on the delayed PPS posttest than control students. Equivalently the SBI vs. control difference was .33 SD or 63% of SBI students scored above the mean of control students."
