# Students in high-poverty and high-minority secondary schools are more than twice as likely to be taught by teachers lacking certification in their fields

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1`–`r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students in high-poverty secondary schools (75% poverty) are more than twice as likely as students in low-poverty schools (<10% poverty) to be taught by teachers not certified in their fields. [→ Barth 2000](#barth-2000)
`q2 i?` Students in secondary schools that are 90% or more African American and Latino are more than twice as likely as students in schools 90% or more White to be taught by teachers without subject certification. [→ Barth 2000 (2)](#barth-2000-2)

## Evidence

### Barth 2000

Barth, Patte, Ed. (2000). Honor in the Boxcar: Equalizing Teacher Quality. Thinking K-16; v4 n1 Spr 2000. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED458350

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of 1993-94 Schools and Staffing Survey data by Richard Ingersoll (University of Georgia), reported in Table 2, compares secondary school classes taught by teachers lacking field certification in high-poverty versus low-poverty schools. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "Students attending high-poverty secondary schools (75% poverty) are more than twice as likely as students in low-poverty schools (<10% poverty) to be taught by teachers not certified in their fields."

### Barth 2000 (2)

Barth, Patte, Ed. (2000). Honor in the Boxcar: Equalizing Teacher Quality. Thinking K-16; v4 n1 Spr 2000. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED458350

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The same Ingersoll analysis of 1993-94 Schools and Staffing Survey data, reported in Table 3, compares certification rates by school racial composition. The article reports the contrast as a likelihood ratio, with no standardized effect size.

> "students attending secondary schools in which African Americans and Latinos comprise 90% or more of the student population are more than twice as likely as students attending schools in which Whites comprise 90% or more of the student population to be taught by teachers without certification to teach their subjects."
