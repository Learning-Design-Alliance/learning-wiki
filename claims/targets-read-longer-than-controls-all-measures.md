# Unknown words are read significantly longer than known control words in all four eye-tracking measures

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For L2 readers, mean reading times for target nonwords were significantly longer than for controls in first fixation duration, gaze duration, number of fixations, and total reading time. [→ Pellicer-Sánchez 2016](#pellicer-sanchez-2016)

## Evidence

### Pellicer-Sánchez 2016

Pellicer-Sánchez, Ana. (2016). Incidental L2 Vocabulary Acquisition From and While Reading: An Eye-Tracking Study. Studies in Second Language Acquisition, 38(1), 97-130. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263115000224

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Nonparametric Kruskal-Wallis tests comparing L2 participants' (n = 23) reading of six target nonwords versus six known control words, each repeated eight times in the story. All four measures showed significantly longer reading times for targets.

> "mean reading times for targets were significantly longer than for controls in all four measures examined (first fixation duration: χ 2 [1] = 21.61, p < .001; gaze duration: χ 2 [1] = 35.59, p < .001; number of fixations: χ 2 [1] = 65.24, p < .001; total reading time: χ 2 [1] = 80.93, p < .001"
