## Evidence

### Dahlkemper 2023

Dahlkemper, M. N., Lahme, S. Z., & Klein, P. (2023). How do physics students evaluate artificial intelligence responses on comprehension questions? A study on the perceived scientific accuracy and linguistic quality. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010142](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010142)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVA results for the first question (rolling motion) in the survey of introductory physics students at Göttingen. The article reports the expert solution "received the highest ratings among all responses" and that linguistic quality was rated higher than scientific accuracy.

> "the expert solution received the highest ratings among all responses ( M = 4.03,SD = 0.81 for scientific accuracy, M = 4.07,SD = 0.76 for linguistic quality); (2) students rated the overall linguistic quality higher (M = 3.69,SD = 0.54) than the scientific accuracy (M = 2.59,SD = 0.48)"

### Dahlkemper 2023 (2)

Dahlkemper, M. N., Lahme, S. Z., & Klein, P. (2023). How do physics students evaluate artificial intelligence responses on comprehension questions? A study on the perceived scientific accuracy and linguistic quality. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010142](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010142)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The article's summary of the rating results across questions: "little diﬀerences in the perceived linguistic quality" between ChatGPT responses and the sample solution, while overall scientific accuracy was rated significantly differently.

> "We found little diﬀerences in the perceived linguistic quality between ChatGPT responses and the sample solution. However, the students rated the overall scientiﬁc accuracy of the responses signiﬁcantly diﬀerently"
