# The high-stakes final exam gender gap is about 50% larger than the medium-stakes in-term exam gap and is not removed by controlling for in-term exam scores

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Controlling for in-term exam scores, the final exam gender gap (b_Female = -0.1155) remained clearly non-zero and was half the size of the in-term gap, implying the full final-exam gap was about 50% larger than the in-term gap. [→ David J. Webb 2025](#david-j-webb-2025)

## Evidence

### David J. Webb 2025

David J. Webb, Cassandra A. Paul. (2025). High stakes exams inflate a gender gap and contribute to systematic grading errors in one introductory physics series. [doi:10.1103/ngrs-dxtf](https://doi.org/10.1103/ngrs-dxtf)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

HLM fit of final exam z-scores (Eq. 3) on 203 classes with 48,428 students, controlling for in-term exam average, GPA structure, and retake status. Table III prints b_Female = -0.1155 (z = -16.2, p<10−3); the Female×Retake interaction was not significant (p= 0.676). The article prints no standardized effect size, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "In fact, the (in-term exam controlled) final exam gender gap is half the size of the gender gap for the in-term exams alone. This tells us that the full gender gap on the high-stakes final exam is about 50% larger than that of the medium-stakes in-term exams."
