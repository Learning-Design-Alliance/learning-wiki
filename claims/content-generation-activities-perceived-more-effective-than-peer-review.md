# Students perceive content-generation activities (web article search, video generation, self-assessment question generation) as more effective for learning SDG content than activities focused on reviewing peer materials.

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Among six SGC activities, searching for complementary web articles was rated highest, followed by generating the video and generating self-assessment questions, with reviewing peer videos rated lowest (partial η² = .384). [→ Blanco 2024](#blanco-2024)

## Evidence

### Blanco 2024

Blanco, J. M., Domínguez, C., Jaime, A., & Usandizaga, I. (2024). Connecting sustainability and computer science curricula through website learning projects embedding different types of student-generated content. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-024-10376-8

`q3 · i3` · `design · r3`

Comparison of six rated SGC activities by 301 students using Friedman test (χ² = 203.864, p < .001, "partial η² = .384"). After Bonferroni correction, "Activity E (search for complementary web articles) remains above all the others", with video and question generation above review activities.

> "Activity E (search for complementary web articles) remains above all the others. Activities A (generate the video) and F (generate self-assessment questions) remain above the rest (B, C and D)."
