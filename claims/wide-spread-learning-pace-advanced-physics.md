# Students in an advanced physics topic show a wide spread of learning paces, with mean 0.25 and standard deviation 0.12 on the iOLM pace measure

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The distribution of learning paces among students in one class ranges from 0.1 to 0.5 (where 1 is the maximum pace allowed by iOLM), with a mean of 0.25 and a standard deviation of 0.12, fitted to a beta probability distribution. [→ T. Sampson 2013](#t-sampson-2013)

## Evidence

### T. Sampson 2013

T. Sampson, and M. Hilke. (2013). Student Variability in Learning Advanced Physics. arXiv:1307.6144v1 [physics.ed-ph]. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1307.6144](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1307.6144)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Log analysis of all student quiz trajectories from one class of a second-year electromagnetic waves course, using the printed definition "pace=6/(# of quizzes)". The pace distribution was fitted to a beta probability distribution with the printed mean and standard deviation; no effect size is reported, so no i is claimed.

> "The differences in learning pace, range from 0.1 let's call him Albert to 0.5 (Marie) with a standard deviation of 0.12. The learning pace is defined as pace=6/(# of quizzes). The average student needs 24 quizzes before reaching level 6."
