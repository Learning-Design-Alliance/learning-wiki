## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Kruskal–Wallis testing found time spent in self-learning differed significantly across time-points (p < 0.001), and Bonferroni comparisons showed it significantly higher (p < 0.001) at post-test 2 and 3 than at pre-test. [→ Murata 2023](#murata-2023)

## Evidence

### Murata 2023

Murata, H., Asakawa, S., Kawamura, T., Yamauchi, H., Takahashi, O., & Henker, R. (2023). Efficacy of modified team‐based learning in a flipped classroom for an acute‐care nursing course: A mixed‐methods study. Nursing Open, 10, 4786–4796. https://doi.org/10.1002/nop2.1730

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Ordinal four-option self-report of self-learning time (including pre-class preparation) compared across time-points with a Kruskal–Wallis test and Bonferroni multiple comparisons; post-test 1 data were irretrievable due to technical issues. The article reports the Kruskal–Wallis result as "statistically significant (p < 0.001)" and the post-test 2 and 3 differences as "significantly higher (p < 0.001)".

> "The Kruskal–Wallis test revealed that the time spent in self‐learning was statistically significant (p < 0.001) at each time‐point. The Bonferroni test revealed that it was significantly higher (p < 0.001) at post‐test 2 and 3 than at pre‐test."
