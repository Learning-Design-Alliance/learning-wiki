# On a short-answer comprehension test, only multiple-choice practice, not true—false practice, significantly beat rereading (Experiment 2)

## Evidence

### Hildenbrand Lena; Wiley Jennifer 2025

Hildenbrand Lena; Wiley Jennifer. (2025). Supporting comprehension: The advantages of multiple–choice over true—false practice tests. Memory & Cognition. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-025-01726-9

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 replicated Experiment 1 with 114 undergraduates and a five-question short-answer final test after 48 h. The condition effect was significant, F(2, 111) = 5.57, p = 0.005, ηp2 = 0.091; "significantly better performance on the SA test than rereading the text" held only for multiple-choice.

> "Planned comparisons using Tukey corrections indicated that multiple-choice practice testing resulted in significantly better performance on the SA test than rereading the text, t(111) = 3.31, p = 0.004, d = 0.77."
