# Research in the teaching-method-centered paradigm does not support its premises that using more named methods, or implementing them faithfully, is more effective

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · qualitative `r?` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2019 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Self-reported use of named methods was often inaccurate: most self-described users of two methods were not following most developer-described components (Henderson & Dancy, as reported). [→ Strubbe 2019](#strubbe-2019)
`q2 i?` No research shows that implementing more teaching methods is better for student learning. [→ Strubbe 2019 (2)](#strubbe-2019-2)
`q2 i?` There is often insufficient research on whether faithful implementation is more effective than making adaptations. [→ Strubbe 2019 (3)](#strubbe-2019-3)

## Evidence

### Strubbe 2019

Strubbe, L. E., Madsen, A. M., McKagan, S. B., & Sayre, E. C. (2019). Beyond teaching methods: highlighting physics faculty's strengths and agency. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1912.09586](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1912.09586)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

The article reports, citing Henderson & Dancy's survey asking faculty to self-report knowledge or use of twenty-four teaching methods, that self-described users "were mostly not following all or even most of the teaching method components described by the developers".

> "self-described users of two methods (Peer Instruction [1] and Cooperative Group Problem Solving [24]) were mostly not following all or even most of the teaching method components described by the developers"

### Strubbe 2019 (2)

Strubbe, L. E., Madsen, A. M., McKagan, S. B., & Sayre, E. C. (2019). Beyond teaching methods: highlighting physics faculty's strengths and agency. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1912.09586](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1912.09586)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

The authors' critique of the familiarity strand: counting implemented methods "may inappropriately suggest to readers that implementing more teaching methods is better for student learning, when again there is not research to support this".

> "research that counts how many teaching methods individual faculty implement (e.g., [4,9,40]) may inappropriately suggest to readers that implementing more teaching methods is better for student learning, when again there is not research to support this"

### Strubbe 2019 (3)

Strubbe, L. E., Madsen, A. M., McKagan, S. B., & Sayre, E. C. (2019). Beyond teaching methods: highlighting physics faculty's strengths and agency. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1912.09586](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1912.09586)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

The authors' critique of the fidelity strand: "there is often insufficient research to support the (sometimes implicit) premise behind the fidelity strand" that faithful use beats adaptation.

> "there is often insufficient research to support the (sometimes implicit) premise behind the fidelity strand, that using teaching methods faithfully relative to the intentions of the developer is more effective than making adaptations to these methods"
