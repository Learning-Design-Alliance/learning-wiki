## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Math placement scores and high school GPAs were important for predicting graduation within 8 semesters, but AP credits were not an important indicator in any semester. [→ Aiken 2020](#aiken-2020)

## Evidence

### Aiken 2020

Aiken, J. M., De Bin, R., Hjorth-Jensen, M., Caballero, M. D. (2020). Predicting time to graduation at a large enrollment American university. arXiv. [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0242334](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0242334)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Per-semester F1-weighted feature importances from the xgboost model (Fig. 8). The article reports math placement scores and high school GPAs were "important in predicting that a student will graduate" for early graduates; no effect size is printed.

> "For early graduating students (≤8 semesters), both math placement scores and high school GPAs were important in predicting that a student will graduate (Fig. 8)."

### Aiken 2020 (2)

Aiken, J. M., De Bin, R., Hjorth-Jensen, M., Caballero, M. D. (2020). Predicting time to graduation at a large enrollment American university. arXiv. [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0242334](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0242334)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The article's discussion of AP credits in the feature-importance results: AP credits indicate graduating in four years (8-9 semesters) but are "not a strong indicator that a student will graduate early" compared with math placement score and high school GPA.

> "In this study, having AP credits is an indicator that a student will graduate in four years (8-9 semesters). However it is not a strong indicator that a student will graduate early in comparison to a student's math placement score and high school GPA."
