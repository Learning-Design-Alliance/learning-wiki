## Evidence

### Gilbert Dizon and Daniel Tang 2017

Gilbert Dizon and Daniel Tang. (2017). Comparing the efficacy of digital flashcards versus paper flashcards to improve receptive and productive L2 vocabulary. The EUROCALL Review, Volume 25, No. 1. https://doi.org/10.4995/eurocall.2017.6964

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-treatment 10-item Likert-scale survey administered after the post-test. The article reports higher agreement in the DF group on all statements except statement two; opinions of both methods were generally positive.

> "Besides statement two, “Using paper/digital flashcards improved my English vocabulary,” there were higher levels of agreement in the DF group."

### Gilbert Dizon and Daniel Tang 2017 (2)

Gilbert Dizon and Daniel Tang. (2017). Comparing the efficacy of digital flashcards versus paper flashcards to improve receptive and productive L2 vocabulary. The EUROCALL Review, Volume 25, No. 1. https://doi.org/10.4995/eurocall.2017.6964

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Authors' interpretation of the Likert survey items on ease of studying, becoming skillful, and learning to study. The article attributes the DF group's higher agreement partly to the ability to study "anytime and anywhere via smartphone".

> "Notably, ease of use seems to be a distinct advantage of DFs over PFs, as shown by the high levels of agreement towards statements five through seven."
