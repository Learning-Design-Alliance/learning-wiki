## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Pietschnig 2023

Pietschnig, J., Oberleiter, S., Toffalini, E., & Giofrè, D. (2023). Reliability of the g factor over time in Italian INVALSI data (2010-2022): What can achievement-g tell us about the Flynn effect? Personality and Individual Differences, 214, 112345. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2023.112345

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bayesian GLM analysis of INVALSI bifactor-model indices; the best-fitting model by WAIC included the year-by-grade interaction (e.g., R2 interaction estimate 0.009, 95% CI [0.002, 0.015]). The article reports "differentiated effects of elapsing time"; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Significant interactions between school grades and years indicated differentiated effects of elapsing time on the positive manifold strength."

### Pietschnig 2023 (2)

Pietschnig, J., Oberleiter, S., Toffalini, E., & Giofrè, D. (2023). Reliability of the g factor over time in Italian INVALSI data (2010-2022): What can achievement-g tell us about the Flynn effect? Personality and Individual Differences, 214, 112345. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2023.112345

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same modeling analysis decomposed by grade: lower grades (2, 5) show decreasing positive-manifold strength whereas higher grades (8, 10) show no decreases or increases, per the article's own description of the interaction.

> "results for younger participants (i.e., those in lower grades) showed evidence for a decreasing strength of the positive manifold. Older students (i.e., those in higher grades) did not show decreases or even showed increases."
