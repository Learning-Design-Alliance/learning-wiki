# Midwives' knowledge gains from e-learning decay: one-month retention knowledge is significantly lower than immediately post-intervention but still significantly above pre-intervention

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Within the intervention group, knowledge one month after the intervention was significantly lower than immediately after (P < .001) yet significantly greater than before the intervention (P < .001). [→ Heidarian H 2025](#heidarian-h-2025)

## Evidence

### Heidarian H 2025

Heidarian H, Mehrabi M, Ghaemmaghami P, Janghorban R. (2025). Effect of E-learning clinical management of substance-dependent pregnant women on the knowledge and clinical skill performance of midwives: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12884-024-07130-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Bonferroni post hoc within-group pairwise comparisons of knowledge scores in the repeated-measures ANOVA of 93 midwives. Table 2 shows intervention means of 10.41 (2.25) before, 28.95 (4.63) immediately after, and 25.76 (4.79) one month after; the 1–3 mean difference was -15.34 (0.55).

> "It should be noted that the level of knowledge in the intervention group decreased significantly one month after the intervention compared to immediately after the intervention (P < .001); however, the mean score of knowledge one month after the intervention was significantly greater than that before the intervention (P < .001)"
