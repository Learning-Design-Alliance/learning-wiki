# Visual imagery strength modulates the circular-motion misconception: a three half-turn tube image induces the impetus belief more strongly than a one half-turn tube

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The more turns seen by students in the tube pictures, the stronger the false belief in ever-lasting circular motion without cause. [→ M. Risch 2010](#m-risch-2010)

## Evidence

### M. Risch 2010

M. Risch. (2010). Investigations about Science Misconceptions. ArXiv.org. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1009.5524](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1009.5524)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Ball-in-circular-tube question (Question 5) with one and three half-turn tubes shown as pictures to 941 students; 75.8% answered straight both times, 10.3% gave the pattern "straight with half turn, bent up with three half turn tube."

> "These answers clearly prove the importance of visual imagery; since the image of a three half-turn tube induces students more strongly towards an impetus misconception of movement than an image of a one half turn tube."
