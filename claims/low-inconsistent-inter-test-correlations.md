# Correlations among the brief tests were low and internally inconsistent: K and M correlated only .32 while M correlated .42 with the information test and K only .18

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The correlation between similar completion tests K and M (.32) was lower than that between M and the information test (.42), while K and the information test correlated only .18. [→ Uhl 1916](#uhl-1916)

## Evidence

### Uhl 1916

Uhl, W. L. (1916). Mentality tests for college freshmen. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/edu

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Spearman footrule correlations (Table IV) computed among the tests for the 100 freshmen, with the article printing "the correlation between tests K and M is only .32" and M-information .42; K and M also differed in their mathematics correlations of .16 and .41.

> "if the correlation between tests K and M is only .32, it would not be expected that test M and the information test should have a correlation of .42, and then that K and the information test should have a correlation of only .18."
