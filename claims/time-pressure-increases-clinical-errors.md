## Evidence

### Weiner Saul J. 2026

Weiner Saul J., Park Yoon Soo, Gernsbacher Morton Ann, Petersen Kristina H. (2026). The myth that slow test-takers are worse students: Implications for time-limited testing. Medical education. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.70215

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The commentary reports an analysis of 63 laparoscopic procedures by proficient novices (n = 43) and experts (n = 20) in which "time pressure led to more errors (reduced suture precision), and increased mean force."

> "time pressure led to more errors (reduced suture precision), and increased mean force, affecting both groups, novices more so than experts"

### Weiner Saul J. 2026 (2)

Weiner Saul J., Park Yoon Soo, Gernsbacher Morton Ann, Petersen Kristina H. (2026). The myth that slow test-takers are worse students: Implications for time-limited testing. Medical education. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.70215

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The commentary reports a randomized study of senior residents diagnosing clinical cases in which those given visual cues implying they were behind schedule "made on average 37% more errors than control participants."

> "Physicians experiencing perceived time pressure made on average 37% more errors than control participants"
