# Financial incentives for effort tasks show no statistically significant impact on overall GCSE attainment in Maths or English

## Subclaims
`q4 i0` In a cluster randomised controlled trial, financial incentives for effort tasks did not produce a statistically significant improvement in GCSE Maths or English points scores (effect sizes 0.04 and 0.02). [→ Sibieta et al. 2014](#sibieta-et-al-2014)

## Evidence

### Sibieta et al. 2014

Luke Sibieta, Ellen Greaves, Barbara Sianesi. (2014). Increasing Pupil Motivation: Evaluation Report and Executive Summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED581249

`q4 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Cluster randomised controlled trial with randomisation at school level; 15 financial-incentive schools compared with 33 control schools, analysed on an intention-to-treat basis using fully-interacted linear matching. The report states the effect sizes "are not statistically significant from zero" and that the evaluation "can rule out medium to large effects on pupil attainment."

> "The small positive effect sizes detected – 0.04 and 0.02 in Maths and English respectively – are not statistically significant from zero. There is also no evidence of a statistically significant effect on GCSE Science."
