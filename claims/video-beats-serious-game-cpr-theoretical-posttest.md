## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Medical students randomised to video-based self-training scored higher on a 10-item theoretical CPR post-test than students randomised to a serious game (adjusted difference 1.05, p = 0.001). [→ de Sena David P. 2019](#de-sena-david-p-2019)

## Evidence

### de Sena David P. 2019

de Sena David P., Fabrício Daniela D., da Silva Vinícius D., Bodanese Luiz Carlos, Franco Alexandre R. (2019). Comparative evaluation of video-based on-line course versus serious game for training medical students in cardiopulmonary resuscitation: A randomised trial. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0214722

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomised trial of 45 first-year medical students assigned to video (n = 22) or game (n = 23) self-training for up to 20 min; ANCOVA adjusted for baseline. The video group scored "7.56±0.21 vs 6.51±0.21 for the game group; p = 0.001" (adjusted difference 1.05, 95%CI 0.45–1.66).

> "The video group had superior performance in both the theoretical test (7.56±0.21 vs 6.51±0.21 for the game group; p = 0.001)"
