# Flipped classroom shifts study time toward pre-class preparation and away from post-class review, with no difference in total studying time

> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` FC residents spent longer pre-class preparation time (2.6 [1.3, 3.6] vs. 1.3 [0.3, 2.3] h, p < 0.0001) and shorter post-class review time (1.0 [0.5, 2.5] vs. 2.1 [1, 3.6] h, p < 0.0001) than CL residents. [→ Wang Yun 2025](#wang-yun-2025)

## Evidence

### Wang Yun 2025

Wang Yun, Tian Hong, Li Jianyun, Zhou Chongzhi, Feng Yiwen, Li Jinbao, Huang Lina. (2025). Effect of flipped classroom to anesthesia crisis management simulation education: a quasi-experimental study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07011-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Self-reported study times recorded through the course's online studying platform, which logged video playing time and gated questions until 95% of the video was played. Times are printed as mean [min, max] in hours; no effect size is printed.

> "The preparation time in the FC group was significantly longer than the CL group (FC vs. CL (mean [min, max]): 2.6 [1.3, 3.6] vs. 1.3 [0.3, 2.3], p < 0.0001), while the post-class review time was significantly shorter in FC group (FC vs. CL (mean [min, max]): 1.0 [0.5, 2.5] vs. 2.1 [1, 3.6], p < 0.0001)."
