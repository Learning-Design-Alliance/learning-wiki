## Evidence

### David Marasco and Bree Barnett Dreyfuss 2020

David Marasco and Bree Barnett Dreyfuss. (2020). SCHOOLS ON DIFFERENT CORNERS: An Investigation into the Effects of Ethnicity and Socioeconomic Status on Physics Offerings in Northern California Public High Schools. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2010.08476](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2010.08476)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive quadrant analysis of the 410-school dataset, summarized in Table 1 (n=183 lower-left schools, n=141 upper-right schools); the article reports 73.8% versus 39% offering at least one AP course. Percentages are descriptive; no inferential test is printed for this contrast.

> "In total, 73.8% of the schools in the lower left (<50% Hispanic and <50% SED) offered at least one AP course for their students, whereas only 39% of schools in the upper right (>50% Hispanic and >50% SED) did the same."

### David Marasco and Bree Barnett Dreyfuss 2020 (2)

David Marasco and Bree Barnett Dreyfuss. (2020). SCHOOLS ON DIFFERENT CORNERS: An Investigation into the Effects of Ethnicity and Socioeconomic Status on Physics Offerings in Northern California Public High Schools. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2010.08476](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2010.08476)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same quadrant analysis reports the full distribution of physics access levels for the 141 schools >50% Hispanic and >50% SED, from "seven (5%) offered no physics whatsoever" to 11 (7.8%) with multiple AP types.

> "In this grouping, seven (5%) offered no physics whatsoever, 80 (56.7%) offered physics with no AP courses, 43 (30.5%) offered a single AP physics choice and 11 (7.8%) offered more than one type of AP class."
