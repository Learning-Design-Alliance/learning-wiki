Evidence¶

Means 2021¶

Means, B., Peters, V., Neisler, J., Wiley, K., & Grifths, R. (2021). Lessons from remote learning during COVID-19. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/116

q2 · i? · associational · r2

National undergraduate survey (n = 1,008) listing six potential non-technology challenges. "Maintaining motivation as a major problem (42%)" topped the list, double the next challenge, finding a quiet place (20%). Case study students described zoning out during lectures and procrastinating on recorded lectures without scheduled class meetings.

"The proportion of students who reported maintaining motivation as a major problem (42%) on the survey was double that for the next most frequently experienced challenge (fnding a quiet place to work on the course, a major problem for 20%)."

  • Motivation, internet connectivity, and hardware/software problems were widespread challenges in remote STEM courses — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • The number of challenges a student experienced was strongly associated with post-COVID course satisfaction: 81 percent satisfaction with no challenges versus 32 percent with four or more — related
  • Hispanic and low-income students reported more challenges and technology problems after the shift online than non-Hispanic White and higher-income peers; rural students did not differ — related
  • Students experiencing more major challenges reported lower satisfaction with their post-COVID STEM course and learning — related
  • Personal instructor messages and reflective assignments showed the largest individual associations with online course satisfaction, and personal messages with fewer motivation problems — related
