# In the SET intervention, zero/light drug users in the treatment group decreased drug use over five years while zero/light control students increased, an effect size gain of Cohen's d = .64

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Zero/light users in the SET treatment group reported a 5-percentage-point decrease in drug use (6% to 1%) from year 1 to 5, while zero/light control students reported a 6-percentage-point increase (2% to 8%), equating to Cohen's d = .64. [→ Alli Klapp 2017](#alli-klapp-2017)

## Evidence

### Alli Klapp 2017

Alli Klapp, Clive Belfield, Brooks Bowden, Henry Levin, Robert Shand, Sabine Zander. (2017). A benefit-cost analysis of a long-term intervention on social and emotional learning in compulsory school. International Journal of Emotional Education, 9(1), 3-19. https://www.um.edu.mt/ijee

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Benefit calculation based on the five-year follow-up evaluation (Kimber & Sandell, 2009) of the SET intervention, in which students were assessed on self-reported drug use at five time points (2001 to 2005). The article reports the treatment-control contrast "an effect size gain of Cohen´s d = .64" for zero/light users.

> "The zero/light treatment group reported a decrease in drug use of 5 percentage points (6% to 1%) from year 1 to 5; zero/light students in the control school reported an increase of 6 percentage points (2% to 8%) over the same period . This difference equates to an effect size gain of Cohen´s d = .64."
