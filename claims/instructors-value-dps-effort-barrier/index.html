Evidence¶

Ahmad D. Suleiman 2026¶

Ahmad D. Suleiman, Daqing Hou, and Maliha Noushin Raida. (2026). LLM-Generated Design Problems for Assessing Higher-Order Thinking in Project-Based Learning. arXiv preprint arXiv:2607.11032. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11032 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.11032

q2 · i? · design · r2

Reflexive thematic analysis of a mostly open-ended survey of 31 PjBL instructors (~10% response rate) found unanimous support for DPs, with "22/31; 71.0%" indicating clear agreement and caveats centering on validity under generative AI use.

"First, all 31 instructors supported the potential of DPs to assess HOT in PjBL. Most indicated a clear agreement that DPs can be effective (22/31; 71.0%), while the remainder agreed but with caveats (9/31; 29.0%)."

Ahmad D. Suleiman 2026 (2)¶

Ahmad D. Suleiman, Daqing Hou, and Maliha Noushin Raida. (2026). LLM-Generated Design Problems for Assessing Higher-Order Thinking in Project-Based Learning. arXiv preprint arXiv:2607.11032. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11032

q2 · i? · design · r2

The same survey found that although 71.0% said they explicitly assess HOT, only "2 instructors (6.5%)" used scenario-based transfer questions, with most relying on project artifacts and oral defenses instead.

"Only 2 instructors (6.5%) explicitly mentioned scenario-based questions that require students to analyze a new situation and propose solutions."
