## Evidence

### Dickie 2006

Dickie, L. O., Dedic, H., Rosenfield, S., Rosenfield, E., & Simon, R. A. (2006). Success and Persistence in Science: The Influence of Classroom Climate. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0610243](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0610243)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of faculty survey data (N = 84 science/math faculty at four CEGEPs, 42% of the population) with student perceptions. The article reports Fostering-perceived teachers "are more likely to have a degree or diploma in Education", agreeing with Darling-Hammond et al. (2005).

> "This study found that teachers who are perceived as more Fostering and less Transmitting by their students (and so enable better performance and persistence), are more likely to have a degree or diploma in Education"
