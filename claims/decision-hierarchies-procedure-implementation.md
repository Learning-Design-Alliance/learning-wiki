## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Interview respondents reported that attending physicians make emergency management decisions, so procedures unfamiliar to attendings are unlikely to be implemented by residents. [→ Parameshwar 2022](#parameshwar-2022)

## Evidence

### Parameshwar 2022

Parameshwar, P. S., Bianco, K., Sherwin, E. B., Meza, P. K., Tolani, A., Bates, P., Sie, L., López Enríquez, A. S., Sanchez, D. E., Herrarte, E. R., & Daniels, K. (2022). Mixed methods evaluation of simulation-based training for postpartum hemorrhage management in Guatemala. *BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth*. [doi:10.1186/s12884-022-04845-2](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12884-022-04845-2)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Theme from grounded-theory analysis of 11 qualitative interviews; residents reported attendings unfamiliar with B-Lynch or UBT did not feel comfortable performing or permitting these procedures during emergencies.

> "Multiple interview respondents noted the importance of attending physicians in decision-making and that decision-making hierarchies impacted procedure implementation."
