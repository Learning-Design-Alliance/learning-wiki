### Dringoli 2024

Dringoli, B. J., Kolosova, K., Rademaker, T. J., Wray, J., Choquette, J., and Hilke, M. (2024). The impact of content sequencing on quantitative problem-solving: A case study in Electromagnetism using an online learning platform. arXiv version of November 2024, first posted 2019 as "Content Sequencing and its Impact on Student Learning in Electromagnetism: Theory and Experiment". [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1910.00145](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1910.00145)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Repeated in-assignment questionnaires tracking content-type preference across test stages (Fig. 7). The article reports preference "evolves towards example for all sub-groups, regardless of initial preference".

> "We ﬁnd that the percentage of students who prefer Example rises from 42% before Test 1 to 66% after the post-test, the preference of Theory falls from 39% before Test 1 to 22% after post-test, and the preference of Concept falls from 19% before Test 1 to 12% after the post-test."
