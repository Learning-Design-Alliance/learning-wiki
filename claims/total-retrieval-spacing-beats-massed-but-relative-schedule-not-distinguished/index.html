Total retrieval spacing improved one-week vocabulary recall while relative schedules were not distinguished¶

Claim · All claims Evidence · 1 study · causal r? · q3 · n=96 undergraduate students

One experiment tested three repeated retrievals after an initial successful recall of Swahili–English pairs. Its lags were intervening trials within a learning session, not days between sessions.

Evidence¶

Karpicke & Bauernschmidt 2011¶

Karpicke, J. D., & Bauernschmidt, A. (2011). Spaced retrieval: Absolute spacing enhances learning regardless of relative spacing. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition, 37(5), 1250–1257. doi:10.1037/a0023436

q3 · randomized experiment with between- and within-subject contrasts · i? · no pooled standard effect used on this page · n=96 undergraduate students · causal · r?

Learners studied and retrieved pairs to one correct recall, then items were assigned to three extra tests under varying total and relative spacing. At the one-week test, total spacing favored longer trial lags; relative schedule had no detected main effect or interaction (reported F<1), so equivalence was not shown. Response-time slopes during initial practice were used as an observed proxy for retrieval difficulty and changed with relative schedule at short and medium total spacing, but that change did not yield a corresponding final-recall advantage. The experiment does not compare day-level course schedules or establish that difficulty itself caused retention.
