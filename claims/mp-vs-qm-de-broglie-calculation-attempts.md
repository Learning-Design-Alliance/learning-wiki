# Modern physics students were more likely than quantum mechanics students to attempt a de Broglie calculation for a sand grain (48% vs. 17%)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On estimating the wavelength of a sand grain, 48% of MP students who knew the de Broglie relation attempted the calculation versus 17% of QM students, while QM students were more likely to use gut feeling (56% vs. 36%). [→ Alexandru Maries 2025](#alexandru-maries-2025)

## Evidence

### Alexandru Maries 2025

Alexandru Maries, Ryan Sayer, and Chandralekha Singh. (2025). Investigation of student and faculty problem solving: An example from quantum mechanics. Physics Education Research Conference. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2504.17487](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2504.17487)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Phase II Q3a responses: 33 of 48 MP students knew the de Broglie relation and 16 attempted the calculation, with 6 of 33 obtaining a reasonable answer (~10^-25 m); 23 of 28 QM students knew the relation but only 4 attempted the calculation.

> "Comparing the two groups, students in MP were more likely to attempt to use the de Broglie relation to estimate the wavelength of a grain of sand compared to QM students (48% vs. 17%), and conversely, MP students were less likely than QM students to ignore their knowledge of the de Broglie relation and use their gut feeling (36% vs. 56%)."
