## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The 2D Kolmogorov-Smirnov test on speed-accuracy distributions was significant for the feedback group from baseline to acquisition, and for training task 3 from trial 3 to trial 16 (p = .003), but not for the no-feedback group (p = .128). [→ Bigras Catherine 2019](#bigras-catherine-2019)

## Evidence

### Bigras Catherine 2019

Bigras Catherine, Kairy Dahlia, Archambault Philippe S. (2019). Augmented feedback for powered wheelchair training in a virtual environment. Journal of NeuroEngineering and Rehabilitation. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12984-019-0482-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc 2D KS analysis of collisions-over-time distributions for VE training task 3, comparing trial 3 to trial 16 within each group of the randomized simulator experiment. The article states this suggests feedback improves speed and accuracy and is most effective during training.

> "looking at each group individually shows a significant difference in the speed-accuracy distributions from trial 3 to trial 16 for the feedback group (p = .003). That difference is not significant for the no feedback group (p = .128)."

### Bigras Catherine 2019 (2)

Bigras Catherine, Kairy Dahlia, Archambault Philippe S. (2019). Augmented feedback for powered wheelchair training in a virtual environment. Journal of NeuroEngineering and Rehabilitation. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12984-019-0482-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

2D KS comparison of baseline and two-day retention speed-accuracy distributions for the VE test task, per group. The article interprets that feedback's effect may not be strong enough to be maintained two days after training.

> "The feedback group’s speed-accuracy distributions from baseline to retention were significantly different. As for the control group, this difference in distributions was similar to the one obtained for the feedback group and almost reached significance (2D KS test; p = .059)."
