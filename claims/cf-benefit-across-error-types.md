## Evidence

### Asassfeh 2013

Sahail M. Asassfeh. (2013). Corrective Feedback (CF) and English-Major EFL Learners' Ability in Grammatical Error Detection and Correction. English Language Teaching, 6(8). http://dx.doi.org/10.5539/elt.v6n8p85

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc comparisons across the eleven error types (article, spelling, preposition substitutions/additions/omissions, subject-verb agreement, logical connectors) in the same quasi-experiment found no significant group differences. The authors state this contradicts Truscott's hypothesis that grammatical errors are less likely to benefit from CF.

> "The results indicated no significant difference at the level of any of the error types covered in this study. This result indicates that students'  benefit from CF was not limited to a specific error type but not another."
