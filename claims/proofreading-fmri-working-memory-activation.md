## Evidence

### Howard 2017

Howard, S. J., Burianová, H., Calleia, A., Fynes-Clinton, S., Kervin, L. and Bokosmaty, S. (2017). The method of educational assessment affects children's neural processing and performance: behavioural and fMRI Evidence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-017-0010-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

PLS fMRI analysis (permutation-tested, p < 0.003) of the mental search phase in the Grade 2 sample; the proofreading-differentiating pattern accounted for 69.94% of covariance. The article interprets this as engagement of "dorsal attentional stream and working memory areas".

> "During the proofreading condition, signiﬁcantly higher activations were found in bilateral dorsal frontoparietal network (comprising dorsolateral prefrontal cortex and inferior parietal lobule), precuneus, and bilateral fusiform gyrus"

### Howard 2017 (2)

Howard, S. J., Burianová, H., Calleia, A., Fynes-Clinton, S., Kervin, L. and Bokosmaty, S. (2017). The method of educational assessment affects children's neural processing and performance: behavioural and fMRI Evidence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-017-0010-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The complementary side of the same PLS pattern in the mental search phase, described in Results as engaging "areas important for semantic processing, memory recognition, conceptual integration, and cue- stimulus binding".

> "In contrast, during the cloze dictation and multiple- choice conditions signi ﬁcantly higher activations were found in the bilateral parahippocampus and hippocampus, temporal poles, insula, inferior frontal gyrus, thalamus, basal ganglia, middle and superior temporal gyrus, and left angular gyrus"
