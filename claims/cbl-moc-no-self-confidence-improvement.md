# The CBL+MOC model does not significantly improve medical students' self-confidence compared with traditional CBL

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Post-course self-confidence showed no significant differences between the CBL+MOC and traditional CBL groups, in total scores or subcategories. [→ Zhi Fei-Hang 2025](#zhi-fei-hang-2025)

## Evidence

### Zhi Fei-Hang 2025

Zhi Fei-Hang, Liu Wei, Zhang Xinyao, Zhang Tao, Su Yuhan, Huang Ruoxuan, Feng Yan-Fen, Lei Yi-Yan. (2025). Exploration of CBL combined with “Metronomic” online classroom in thoracic trauma and closed chest drainage teaching. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08427-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

End-of-course confidence questionnaire (adapted from Bray et al., six domains, Cronbach's alpha 0.971) administered to both randomized groups. No significant between-group differences in total or subcategory scores; no effect size printed.

> "Comparisons of self-confidence scores between the CBL + MOC group and the traditional CBL group revealed no significant differences, either in total scores or across the subcategories of self-confidence."

### Zhi Fei-Hang 2025 (2)

Zhi Fei-Hang, Liu Wei, Zhang Xinyao, Zhang Tao, Su Yuhan, Huang Ruoxuan, Feng Yan-Fen, Lei Yi-Yan. (2025). Exploration of CBL combined with “Metronomic” online classroom in thoracic trauma and closed chest drainage teaching. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08427-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre- and post-course within-group comparisons of self-confidence in both groups showed “modest increases in average self-confidence” that did not reach statistical significance; no effect size printed.

> "However, these improvements did not reach statistical significance (Table S2 and Table S3)."
