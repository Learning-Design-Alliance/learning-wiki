Evidence¶

Janneke van de Pol 2024¶

Janneke van de Pol, Eleanor Rowan, Eva Janssen, Tamara van Gog. (2024). Effects of availability of diagnostic and non-diagnostic cues on the accuracy of teachers' judgments of students' text comprehension. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-024-09383-0

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Within-subjects experiment with 33 Dutch secondary teachers judging anonymous students' reading comprehension in a multilevel regression; the Results section reports "teachers' judgments were significantly more accurate" with diagnostic cues available, with the printed coefficients and R2 values.

"teachers' judgments were significantly more accurate in condition 2 (diagnostic cues only; B = -2.06, p < 0.001, SE = 0.12, R2 = 0.997) and 3 (mixed cues; B = -0.92, SE = 0.07, p < 0.001; R2 = 0.995) than in condition 1 (non-diagnostic cues only)"

Related Claims¶

  • Performance cues were found most diagnostic for students' reading comprehension performance — related
  • Teachers judge more accurately with only diagnostic cues than with a mix of diagnostic and non-diagnostic cues — related
  • A brief cue-diagnosticity intervention did not significantly improve teachers' judgment accuracy, presumably because accuracy was already relatively high — related
  • When making accurate judgments, teachers used diagnostic cues to a greater extent, but also used non-diagnostic cues (IQ and reading self-concept) — related
  • The cue-diagnosticity intervention improved teachers' calibration: confidence and judgment accuracy became more aligned — related
