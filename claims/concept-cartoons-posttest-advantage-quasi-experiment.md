# Concept cartoon© teaching produced a statistically significant posttest score advantage over traditional teaching in fourth-grade science

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2016 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Posttest scores differed significantly between the experimental group taught by concept cartoons© (M = 4,18) and the control group taught traditionally (M = 3,55), t = 3.88, p = 0.0005. [→ Michaela Minárechová 2016](#michaela-minarechova-2016)
`q2 i?` Pretest scores did not differ significantly between groups (p = 0,67), supporting group comparability at baseline. [→ Michaela Minárechová 2016 (2)](#michaela-minarechova-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Michaela Minárechová 2016

Michaela Minárechová. (2016). Using a concept cartoon© method to address elementary school students' ideas about natural phenomena. European Journal of Science and Mathematics Education, Vol. 4, No. 2, 214-228. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1107824

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pretest–posttest quasi-experiment with two intact fourth-grade classes (EG n = 17, CG n = 15 at posttest) taught nine science themes over three months. The t-test showed "P‐value 0,0005 (in posttest) is significant at α 0.05"; no effect size was printed.

> "It showed difference between the mean scores of experimental (M= 4,18) and control (M = 3,55) groups. The significance of the difference was tested using t‐test. T‐test indicates that P‐value 0,0005 (in posttest) is significant at α 0.05 level of significance."

### Michaela Minárechová 2016 (2)

Michaela Minárechová. (2016). Using a concept cartoon© method to address elementary school students' ideas about natural phenomena. European Journal of Science and Mathematics Education, Vol. 4, No. 2, 214-228. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1107824

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Baseline comparison of the same quasi-experiment: the pretest t-test gave "P‐value 0,67 (in pretest) is insignificant at α 0.05", so the null hypothesis of no pretest difference was accepted. No effect size printed.

> "T‐test indicates that P‐value 0,67 (in pretest) is insignificant at α 0.05 level of significance. So the null hypothesis stating that there is no statistically significant difference between the students’ pretest scores in the control group who received traditional teaching method and in the experimental group who was taught by concept cartoons©, is accepted."
