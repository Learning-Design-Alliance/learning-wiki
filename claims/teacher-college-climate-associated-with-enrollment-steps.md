# Teacher assessment of college-going climate is positively associated with students taking each step toward four-year college enrollment

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Using HGLM models controlling for student demographics, qualifications, and neighborhood context, teacher assessment of college-going climate is significantly associated with the conditional likelihood of planning, applying, being accepted, and enrolling in a four-year college. [→ Roderick 2011](#roderick-2011)

## Evidence

### Roderick 2011

Roderick, M., Coca, V., & Nagaoka, J. (2011). Potholes on the Road to College: High School Effects in Shaping Urban Students' Participation in College Application, Four-year College Enrollment, and College Match. Sociology of Education, 84(3), 178-211. https://doi.org/10.1177/0038040711411280

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Two-level HGLM models with 58 high schools at level 2, using teacher assessment of college-going climate (a Rasch-based measure from 5 survey items). Models controlled for demographics, qualifications, neighborhood, school involvement, and student survey measures.

> "Findings suggest that qualifications and college aspirations will not necessarily translate into four-year college enrollment if urban high schools do not develop organizational norms and structures that guide students effectively through the college application process."
