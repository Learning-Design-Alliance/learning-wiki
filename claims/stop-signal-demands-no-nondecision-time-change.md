# Non-decision time (encoding/motor speed) is unchanged when inhibitory demands are introduced

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i0` negligible

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` Non-decision time was equivalent during the stop-signal and no-tone tasks (BF01 = 5.73; d = 0.07), with no group main effect or interaction. [→ Fosco 2019](#fosco-2019)

## Evidence

### Fosco 2019

Fosco, W. D., Kofler, M. J., Alderson, R. M., Tarle, S. J., Raiker, J. S., & Sarver, D. E. (2019). Inhibitory Control and Information Processing in ADHD: Comparing the Dual Task and Performance Adjustment Hypotheses. Journal of Abnormal Child Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10802-018-0504-9

`q3 · i0`

Tier 2 Bayesian mixed ANOVA of drift-diffusion parameters in 144 children. "non-decision time was equivalent during the stop-signal and no-tone tasks" (d = 0.07), inconsistent with the dual-task hypothesis prediction of disrupted stimulus encoding and response execution.

> "There was moderate evidence that non-decision time was equivalent during the stop-signal and no-tone tasks (BF01 = 5.73; d = 0.07). There was also significant evidence against a main effect of group on non-decision time (BF01 = 5.43; d = 0.01), and against the group x task interaction (BF01 = 4.30)."
