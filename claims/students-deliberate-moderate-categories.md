## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students chose moderate categories such as friction even when they realized the problem could be solved with Newton's second law, preferring specific details over general principles. [→ Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#andrew-mason-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)

## Evidence

### Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Assessing Expertise in Introductory Physics Using Categorization Task. Originally published 1974. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.08781](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.08781)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Think-aloud interviews with four introductory students plus a few graduate students and professors. All interviewed students could recall concepts and create principle-based categories but also chose surface-feature categories; one student preferred friction because the term force felt vague. Poor categorizations do not necessarily mean students cannot recognize relevant principles.

> "Students sometimes deliberately chose “friction” as the major category instead of more expert-like categories based upon a fundamental physics principle because they felt the need to address specific details as opposed to the general physical principles."
