Evidence¶

Romain Quentin 2021¶

Romain Quentin, Lison Fanuel, Mariann Kiss, Marine Vernet, Teodóra Vékony, Karolina Janacsek, Leonardo G. Cohen and Dezso Nemeth. (2021). Statistical learning occurs during practice while high-order rule learning during rest period. npj Science of Learning 6:14. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00093-9

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Online/offline decomposition of average RT per block in all three experiments (SRT and two ASRT tasks). The article reports practice decreases and rest increases (e.g., Experiment 1 Moffline = 37.22 ± 24.78 s, d = 1.52) in general skill performance.

"general skill performance decreased during practice (Experiment 1: Monline = −24.58 ± 22.79 s, t(62) = −8.49, p <1 0−11, d = 1.09; Experiment 2: Monline = −24.23 ± 9.37 s, t(179) = −34.61, p <1 0−80, d = 2.49; Experiment 3: Monline = −13.97 ± 6.63 s, t(24) = −10.32, p <1 0−9, d = 2.11)"

Romain Quentin 2021 (2)¶

Romain Quentin, Lison Fanuel, Mariann Kiss, Marine Vernet, Teodóra Vékony, Karolina Janacsek, Leonardo G. Cohen and Dezso Nemeth. (2021). Statistical learning occurs during practice while high-order rule learning during rest period. npj Science of Learning 6:14. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00093-9

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Control analysis restricted to first blocks of sessions, where no within-block fatigue decrement was observed; following rest periods still showed general skill performance increases (e.g., Experiment 1 Moffline = 43.44 ± 46.59 s, d = 0.93).

"No decrease in performance occurred during these ﬁrst session blocks and even a modest performance increase occurred in Experiment 1 (Experiment 1: Monline = 11.13 ± 37.82 s, t(62) = 2.32, p = 0.02, d = 0.30)"

Related Claims¶

  • Halving practice-period duration from 10 s to 5 s leaves micro-offline gains unchanged, arguing against recovery from motor fatigue — reports the opposite
  • Performance improvements during early motor skill learning occur predominantly during short rest periods (micro-offline gains), replicated in a large crowdsourced sample and in a laboratory study — related
  • Efficiency in the work curve is conditioned by factors beyond fatigue proper: practice, adaptation, preparedness, warming up, spurts, voluntary effort, ennui, and diurnal rhythms (Offner) — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Micro-offline gains are inconsistent with a dominant contribution of latent motor slowing, supporting consolidation as their driver — reports the opposite
  • Random termination of practice periods does not reduce micro-offline gains, ruling out preemptive motor slowing — reports the opposite
  • Offline change in general skill between sessions 12 h apart was significant, but between sessions a week apart was not — related
  • Statistical learning in the ASRT task increases during practice periods and decreases during rest periods — related
