# AI assistants can improve short-term task performance while harming durable, independent learning once the AI is removed

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Khosravi 2026

Khosravi, H., Gašević, D., Sadiq, S., Yan, L., Lodge, J. M., Tangen, J. M., Denny, P., DiCerbo, K., Buckingham Shum, S., & Baker, R. S. (2026). Building AI Companions that Prioritise Learning over Performance. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04816

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The paper cites a large randomised experiment in high school mathematics as its strongest evidence for the learning-performance paradox: students "showed improved problem-solving during learning but suffered significant harm to their durable, independent learning once the AI was removed". No effect size is printed.

> "The most compelling empirical evidence for this comes from a large randomised experiment in high school mathematics, in which students given access to an AI assistant showed improved problem-solving during learning but suffered significant harm to their durable, independent learning once the AI was removed"
