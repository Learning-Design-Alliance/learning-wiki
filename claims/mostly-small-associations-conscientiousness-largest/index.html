Evidence¶

Ball 2025¶

Ball, I., Banerjee, M., Holliman, A., & Tyndall, I. (2025). Investigating Success in the Transition to University: A Systematic Review of Personal Risk and Protective Factors Influencing Psychosocial Success. https://osf.io/vxu5q?view_only=ee23e20f57c54891b842c92b7a4858de

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

In the Discussion, the review reports that despite extracting nearly 500 coefficients across the 32 studies, most associations were small; the counts of 219 below .30 and 32 above .50 are as printed.

"219 of the extracted associations failed to reach the threshold for a medium effect size (.30) and only 32 breach the threshold for a large effect size (.50, Cohen, 1988)"

Ball 2025 (2)¶

Ball, I., Banerjee, M., Holliman, A., & Tyndall, I. (2025). Investigating Success in the Transition to University: A Systematic Review of Personal Risk and Protective Factors Influencing Psychosocial Success. https://osf.io/vxu5q?view_only=ee23e20f57c54891b842c92b7a4858de

q2 · i3 · review · r2

The review reports the largest coefficients: conscientiousness–academic adjustment r = .64 (Perera et al., 2015), narrow disengagement coping -.62, and social wellbeing .62 in a male-only sample, replicated twice at .52 (average .55). Kurtz et al. (2012) found .35 with different scales.

"The largest of these coefficients was the association between conscientiousness and academic adjustment at .64 (Perera et al., 2015)"

  • Teacher extraversion, conscientiousness, emotional stability, and openness are each positively associated with teacher effectiveness, while agreeableness is not a significant correlate — related
  • Social support in all forms is a key protective factor for psychosocial success in the transition to university — related
  • 13 of 16 adjusted associations between achievement measures and openness/conscientiousness were statistically significantly positive — related
