## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In Experiment 1, a bilingualism effect on Symbol Math performance appeared only on the hard items (ηp² = 0.08). [→ Mielicki 2017](#mielicki-2017)

## Evidence

### Mielicki 2017

Mielicki, M. K., Kacinik, N. A., & Wiley, J. (2017). Bilingualism and symbolic abstraction: Implications for algebra learning. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2017.03.002

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Experiment 1 with 61 undergraduates (29 bilingual, 32 monolingual) used an ANCOVA with basic math as covariate on Symbol Math scores at three difficulty levels. The follow-up test found the bilingual advantage "onlyonthehard items,F(1,58)=4.87,p =0.03,ηp 2 =0.08", a printed effect size of ηp² = 0.08.

> "Follow-up tests for eachlevelofdifficultyrevealedabilingualismeffectonlyonthehard items,F(1,58)=4.87,p =0.03,ηp 2 =0.08(seeFig.3)."
