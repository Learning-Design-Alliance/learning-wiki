## Evidence

### Lura Forcum and William Harms 2005

Lura Forcum and William Harms. (2005). New report shows that high-quality induction can help keep teachers on the job. University of Chicago press release announcing Keeping New Teachers: A First Look at the Influence of Induction in the Chicago Public Schools. https://consortium.uchicago.edu

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Survey analysis of novice CPS teachers examining classroom and school factors. The report states that a higher percentage of students with behavior problems is linked to lower odds of "a good teaching experience," intending to continue, and planning to remain in the same school; a welcoming staff and strong leadership were associated with more positive outcomes.

> "At the classroom level, teachers working in classrooms with a higher percentage of students with behavior problems are less likely to report a good teaching experience, to intend to continue teaching, and to plan to remain in the same school."
