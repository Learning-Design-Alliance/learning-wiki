# Math self-concept gains from the COMPASS intervention are sustained at three- and six-month follow-ups in Group A, but the three-month gain in waiting-list Group B was not statistically significant

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` In Group A, math self-concept remained significantly increased three months (d = 0.28) and six months (d = 0.38) after the intervention. [→ Hörsch 2026](#horsch-2026)

## Evidence

### Hörsch 2026

Hörsch, H., Schumacher, J., & Wolff, F. (2026). Long-Term Effects of an Intervention Targeting Dimensional and Temporal Comparisons on Students' Math and English Academic Self-Concepts: A Waiting-List Replication and Extension Study. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10137-4

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Baseline change model for Group A (n = 219) with four measurement points over six months showed sustained significant math self-concept gains at follow-ups with printed effect sizes d = 0.28 at three months and d = 0.38 at six months relative to pre-intervention.

> "students' math self-concept was significantly increased three months (MDiff3_1 = 0.17, p =.015, d = 0.28, 95% CI [0.09, 0.46]) and six months (MDiff4_1 = 0.27, p =.017, d = 0.38, 95% CI [0.19, 0.57]) after participation in the COMPASS intervention"
