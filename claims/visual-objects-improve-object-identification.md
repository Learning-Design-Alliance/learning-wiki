# Students taught with two- or three-dimensional visual objects identified visual objects significantly better than conventionally taught students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` One-way ANOVA on visual-object identification scores showed a significant difference among the two-dimensional, three-dimensional, and control groups (F = 4.599, p = .012), with the three-dimensional group highest and the control group lowest. [→ Abass et al. 2014](#abass-et-al-2014)

## Evidence

### Abass et al. 2014

Bada Tayo Abass, Bello Isyakka, Ijisakin Yemi Olaolu & Fajuyigbe Michael Olusegun. (2014). Effects of Two and Three-Dimensional Visual Objects on the Acquisition of Drawing Skills among JSS1 Students in Osun State, Nigeria. World Journal of Education, 4(1). https://doi.org/10.5430/wje.v4n1p62

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA (Table 5) on identification scores of 120 JSS1 students: two-dimensional group mean 10.7750 (SD 1.95445), three-dimensional group mean 11.3500 (SD 1.88856), control mean 9.9500 (SD 2.35285). No effect size is printed.

> "subjected to one way Analysis of Variance as shown in table 5 an F-ratio of 4.599 was obtained. This value is significant at 0.05 level. This implies that there is a significant difference in the ability to identify visual objects in the experimental and control groups."
