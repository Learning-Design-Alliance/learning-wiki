# Correlational conceptions of validity are untenable: perfect correlation does not imply measuring the same attribute, and validity cannot be population-dependent

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The article argues that any correlational view of validity leads to absurdities, illustrated by thunder and lightning correlating perfectly while being different attributes, and by meter sticks remaining valid for length even in populations of rods of equal length where the correlation is zero. [→ Borsboom et al. 2004](#borsboom-et-al-2004)

## Evidence

### Borsboom et al. 2004

Denny Borsboom, Gideon J. Mellenbergh, and Jaap van Heerden. (2004). The Concept of Validity. Psychological Review. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.111.4.1061

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument in the Causality Versus Correlation section using extensive measurement of rod length as the example. The authors also note that "the presence of thunder and the presence of lightning are not the same thing under a different label" despite perfect correlation. Purely conceptual, no data.

> "The correlation between the measurement outcome and the real length will be unity in most populations, as it should be, but there is an important class of populations in which it will be zero. This is the class of populations of rods of equal length."
