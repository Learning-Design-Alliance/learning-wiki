# Dental students' two-point discrimination thresholds fell in both hands after a home training programme, in a single-group pre-post study with no control (per-finger r up to 0.55)

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` All twelve two-point discrimination measures (five fingers, both hands) improved significantly from before to after the programme in a single group with no control, so practice with the test, maturation or regression to the mean could explain the change; with effect sizes ranging from small (r >.29) to large at the middle finger of the dominant hand (r = 0.55). ## Evidence

### Pérez-Chicharro María 2025

Pérez-Chicharro María, González-Olmo María José, Nieto-Moraleda Carolina, Obeso-Benítez Paula, Martínez-Piédrola Rosa M, Sánchez-Herrera-Baeza Patricia, Pérez-de-Heredia-Torres Marta, Romero-Maroto Martín, Huertas-Hoyas Elisabet. (2025). Improving dental students’ dexterity and strength: an intervention study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08015-8

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Pre-post Wilcoxon analysis in the same 37-student single-group study. Two-point discrimination thresholds decreased at every fingertip; Table 2 prints the largest effect at the dominant middle finger (2.92 to 2.24 mm, r = 0.55). The authors state this "provides original evidence for possible sensory plasticity in students without pathology."

> "Statistically significant improvements in tactile sensitivity (r >.29, p < .01) and manual dexterity (r >.26, p < .05) in both hands were observed after the intervention."
