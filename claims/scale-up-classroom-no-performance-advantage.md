# Students taught with identical flipped active-learning instruction show no performance difference between a SCALE-UP–type classroom and a traditional lecture hall

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a quasi-experimental comparison of two sections of the same course, postcourse content knowledge did not differ significantly by classroom type when prior knowledge and effort were considered. [→ Stoltzfus JR 2016](#stoltzfus-jr-2016)

## Evidence

### Stoltzfus JR 2016

Stoltzfus JR, Libarkin J. (2016). Does the Room Matter? Active Learning in Traditional and Enhanced Lecture Spaces. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-03-0126

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Mixed-design ANOVA of pre–post content knowledge scores in a quasi-experimental two-section comparison (SCALE-UP n = 34; Traditional n = 37). The section × time interaction was not significant: "Wilks lambda = 0.996, F(1, 69) = 0.302, p = 0.59, partial eta-squared = 0.004."

> "No significant interaction between section and time was observed, Wilks lambda = 0.996, F(1, 69) = 0.302, p = 0.59, partial eta-squared = 0.004."

### Stoltzfus JR 2016 (2)

Stoltzfus JR, Libarkin J. (2016). Does the Room Matter? Active Learning in Traditional and Enhanced Lecture Spaces. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-03-0126

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Linear regression on postcourse knowledge from the same two-section study found section placement was not a significant predictor; "section placement plays little role in explaining postknowledge scores" once prior knowledge and effort are included.

> "All other variables, including section, do not explain any significant portion of the variance, suggesting that, when pre knowledge and individual effort are considered, section placement plays little role in explaining postknowledge scores."
