## Evidence

### Al-lawama Manar 2023

Al-lawama Manar, Kumwenda Ben. (2023). Decreasing the options’ number in multiple choice questions in the assessment of senior medical students and its effect on exam psychometrics and distractors’ function. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04206-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Distractor analysis of 300 distractors (200 five-option, 100 three-option) from the randomized test comparison; the five-option format showed significantly more NFDs. Percentages are printed; no standardized effect size, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "The frequency of NFDs (those chosen by < 0.05 of the students) was higher in the five-option test: 111 NFDs (56%), versus 39 (39%) in the three-option test, p-value < 0.01."

### Al-lawama Manar 2023 (2)

Al-lawama Manar, Kumwenda Ben. (2023). Decreasing the options’ number in multiple choice questions in the assessment of senior medical students and its effect on exam psychometrics and distractors’ function. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04206-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Distractor-per-item comparison (Table 7) from the same analysis; fully functioning three-option items were far more frequent. No standardized effect size printed.

> "Items with 100% effective distractors (0 NFDs) were significantly higher for the three options: 36%, versus 6% for the five options, p-value = 0.00."
