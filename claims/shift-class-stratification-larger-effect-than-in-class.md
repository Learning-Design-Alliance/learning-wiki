# Shift-class stratification shows a larger effect (SMD = 0.99) than in-class stratification (SMD = 0.51), though the between-group difference is not statistically significant

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 quant-synthesis `r2`–`r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Both modes improved performance (combined Z = 13.770, P = 0.000), but the between-group difference was not significant (P = 0.147); descriptively shift-class (0.99) exceeded in-class (0.51). [→ Shi 2020](#shi-2020)
`q3 i?` Among in-class modes, dominant, recessive, and combined dominant-recessive stratification did not differ significantly (P = 0.431); combined dominant-recessive had the largest single effect size (0.65, 1 dataset). [→ Shi 2020 (2)](#shi-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Shi 2020

Shi, H., Cheung, E.S.T., & Cheung, A.C.K. (2020) The impact of stratified teaching on the academic performance of Chinese middle school students: A meta-analysis. Science Insights Education Frontiers, 7(1):735-760. https://doi.org/10.15354/sief.20.re012

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Stratification-mode moderator analysis (Table 6) of the included studies: 24 in-class and 2 shift-class datasets. The article reports both modes effective, a non-significant between-group difference (P = 0.147), and the descriptive contrast "shift-class stratification (SMD = 0.99) > in-class stratification (SMD = 0.51)".

> "But in the specific effect size, shift-class stratification (SMD = 0.99) > in-class stratification (SMD = 0.51), indicating that shift-class stratification had a greater impact on students' academic performance."

### Shi 2020 (2)

Shi, H., Cheung, E.S.T., & Cheung, A.C.K. (2020) The impact of stratified teaching on the academic performance of Chinese middle school students: A meta-analysis. Science Insights Education Frontiers, 7(1):735-760. https://doi.org/10.15354/sief.20.re012

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Comparison of in-class dominant, in-class recessive, and combined dominant-recessive stratification (Table 7), based on 2, 12, and 1 datasets respectively; combined test Z = 7.666, P = 0.000, between-group P = 0.431 indicating no significant difference among the three in-class modes.

> "The between-group effect size P = 0.431, indicating that the three in-class stratified teaching models had the same effect on students' academic performance."
