# Shopping sheet information about college outcomes (graduation and default rates) did not significantly change enrollment or borrowing share, though borrowing amounts rose overall and were attenuated at higher-default colleges

## Evidence

### Kelly Ochs Rosinger 2017

Kelly Ochs Rosinger. (2017). Federal Policy Efforts to Simplify College-Going: An Intervention in Community College Enrollment and Borrowing. The Annals of the American Academy of Political and Social Science, 671. https://doi.org/10.1177/0002716217698664

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Difference-in-differences interaction models (Table 3) interacting shopping sheet adoption with low graduation rate and continuous default rate, for 625 public two-year colleges. The article reports no statistically significant impact on enrollment or borrowing share.

> "Findings indicate that information about college outcomes did not have a statistically significant impact on the number of students enrolled or the share of students borrowing."

### Kelly Ochs Rosinger 2017 (2)

Kelly Ochs Rosinger. (2017). Federal Policy Efforts to Simplify College-Going: An Intervention in Community College Enrollment and Borrowing. The Annals of the American Academy of Political and Social Science, 671. https://doi.org/10.1177/0002716217698664

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same interaction analysis (Table 3): the shopping sheet × default rate interaction on average amount borrowed is −20.33* in the simplified model but attenuates to −13.74 and −14.65 with covariates, not statistically significant. The article describes the pattern as "attenuated at colleges with higher loan default rates."

> "There is some evidence that the shopping sheet was associated with an overall increase in the amount borrowed. This increase is attenuated at colleges with higher loan default rates, though this relationship was not statistically significant across colleges once additional covariates were included."
