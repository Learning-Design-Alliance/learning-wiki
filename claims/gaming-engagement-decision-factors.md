# Players decided to engage in gaming primarily for escapism and fun, social connectivity, and achievement of task-related goals through control

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Interview analysis revealed three main reasons for deciding to engage in gaming: escapism and fun, social connectivity, and achievement of task-related goals through control. [→ Bobby Hoffman 2010](#bobby-hoffman-2010)
`q2 i?` 84% of participants reported an a priori objective of playing with the intent and expectation of having fun or relaxing. [→ Bobby Hoffman 2010 (2)](#bobby-hoffman-2010-2)

## Evidence

### Bobby Hoffman 2010

Bobby Hoffman, Louis Nadelson. (2010). Motivational engagement and video gaming: a mixed methods study. Education Tech Research Dev 58:245-270. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-009-9134-9

`q2 · i?`

Qualitative content analysis of interviews with 25 highly engaged players (five or more hours per week), coded via a three-phase comparative analysis. The analysis "revealed three main reasons; escapism and fun, social connectivity, and the achievement of task-related goals through control".

> "Analysis revealed three main reasons; escapism and fun, social connectivity, and the achievement of task-related goals through control."

### Bobby Hoffman 2010 (2)

Bobby Hoffman, Louis Nadelson. (2010). Motivational engagement and video gaming: a mixed methods study. Education Tech Research Dev 58:245-270. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-009-9134-9

`q2 · i?`

From the same interview subset: "Almost universally (84%), participants reported an a priori objective of playing video games with the intent and expectation of having fun or relaxing"; the most frequent cited reason was escaping daily routines such as school or work.

> "Almost universally (84%), participants reported an a priori objective of playing video games with the intent and expectation of having fun or relaxing."
