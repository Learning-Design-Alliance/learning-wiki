# Think-aloud interviews suggest the select-all reasoning elements do not significantly inflate MC CUE scores

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Interview observations indicate students who used the reasoning elements to infer the method still demonstrated understanding, and being led astray by inappropriate elements offsets any advantage, so scores are not significantly inflated. [→ Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2013](#bethany-r-wilcox-and-steven-j-pollock-2013)

## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2013

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2013). Multiple-choice Assessment for Upper-division Electricity and Magnetism. [doi:10.1119/perc.2013.pr.079](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2013.pr.079)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Think-aloud validation interviews: three interviews with a 7-question subset and six with the full 16-question instrument, all participants having completed the course one to four weeks prior with final scores ranging from A to C. This is an author interpretation from interview observation, not a tested score comparison.

> "This suggests that students using the reasoning elements to ﬁgure out the answer does not result in a signiﬁcant inﬂation of scores."
