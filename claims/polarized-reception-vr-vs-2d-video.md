# Participant reception of VR-modified video was polarized: 2D viewers reported higher average enjoyment, knowledge gained, and engagement, while exactly half of 3D viewers preferred VR to traditional lecture

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On 5-point Likert opinion items, 2D viewers had higher average reception, but responses were largely polarized and a 50-50 split emerged among 3D viewers on preference for VR over traditional lecture. [→ Killough 2024](#killough-2024)

## Evidence

### Killough 2024

Killough, D. E. (2024). The Ability of Virtual Reality Technologies to Improve Comprehension of Speech Therapy Device Training. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2404.15534](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2404.15534)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Opinion-test portion of the same randomized survey (6 Likert and diagnostic items). Averages for positive prompts were "4.53/5 and 3.82/5, respectively" for 2D and 3D participants, with "five "optimal" responses recorded in the 3D group" versus one in the 2D group; responses are descriptive only.

> "there lies a 50-50 split in the data between those who would prefer virtual reality, 3D immersive lessons over traditional lectures and those who would not prefer such."
