## Evidence

### Yamada Masaaki 2019

Yamada Masaaki, Sekine Michikazu, Tatsuse Takashi, Asaka Yukiko. (2019). Association between lifestyle, parental smoke, socioeconomic status, and academic performance in Japanese elementary school children: the Super Diet Education Project. Environmental Health and Preventive Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12199-019-0776-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariate logistic regression in the cross-sectional survey found both "paternal smoke (OR 1.47; 95% CI, 1.12–1.92), maternal smoke (OR 1.87; 95% CI, 1.29–2.72)" significantly associated with low academic performance.

> "paternal smoke (OR 1.47; 95% CI, 1.12–1.92), maternal smoke (OR 1.87; 95% CI, 1.29–2.72), and low SES (OR 1.48; 95% CI, 1.03–2.14) had significant associations with low AP"

### Yamada Masaaki 2019 (2)

Yamada Masaaki, Sekine Michikazu, Tatsuse Takashi, Asaka Yukiko. (2019). Association between lifestyle, parental smoke, socioeconomic status, and academic performance in Japanese elementary school children: the Super Diet Education Project. Environmental Health and Preventive Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12199-019-0776-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Grade-stratified multivariate analysis showed parental smoke significant only in junior grades (1–3); the authors attribute this to younger children spending more time with their parents, especially mothers.

> "both parental smoke (OR 1.67; 95% CI, 1.13–2.48 for father and OR 2.28; 95% CI, 1.32–3.94 for mother) were significantly associated with low AP"
