# Online students given audio group feedback rated the instructor as more genuinely concerned with whether students learned than students given written group feedback

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students receiving individual written plus audio group feedback reported significantly higher agreement that the instructor seemed genuinely concerned with whether students learned (p = .04, one-tailed) than students receiving individual written plus written group feedback. [→ Portolese Dias 2013](#portolese-dias-2013)

## Evidence

### Portolese Dias 2013

Portolese Dias, L., & Trumpy, R. (2013). Online Instructor's Use of Audio Feedback to Increase Social Presence and Student Satisfaction. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1033260

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experiment across two quarters of ADMG385: fall 2012 control (written group feedback, mean 4.705882353, 34 observations) versus spring 2013 treatment (audio group feedback via Screencast, mean 4.945945946, 37 observations), analyzed with a one-tailed independent-samples t-test. The article reports "a p-value of .04, indicating a statistically significant difference between the two feedback groups." No effect size is printed.

> "Results for question two, “instructor seemed genuinely interested in whether students learned” has a p-value of .04, indicating a statistically significant difference between the two feedback groups."
