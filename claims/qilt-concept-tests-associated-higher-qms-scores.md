# Advanced students' QMS scores are far higher in a class that used concept tests and QuILTs than in classes that did not use these research-based learning tools

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The upper-level undergraduate class using concept tests and QuILTs scored 71.8% and 74.9%, while undergraduate classes without them averaged 31.0% and the graduate course 48.4%. [→ Singh 2010](#singh-2010)

## Evidence

### Singh 2010

Singh, C. and Zhu, G. (2010). Surveying Students' Understanding of Quantum Mechanics. Department of Physics and Astronomy, University of Pittsburgh. [doi:10.1063/1.3515229](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3515229)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison across classes in the survey administration: the class using concept tests and QuILTs averaged 71.8% (first semester) and 74.9% (second semester), while "the average score was 48.4% for the graduate course (15 students) and 31.0% for the undergraduate courses" elsewhere. No effect size is printed.

> "In classes that did not use the research-based learning tools, the average score was 48.4% for the graduate course (15 students) and 31.0% for the undergraduate courses."
