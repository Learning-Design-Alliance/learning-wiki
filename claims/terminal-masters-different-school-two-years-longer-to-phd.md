# Students who complete a master's degree at a different institution before a Ph.D. take about 2 years longer in graduate school than students entering a Ph.D. program directly

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Physical-science students completing a master's and Ph.D. at different schools take over 2 years longer to reach the Ph.D. than students earning both degrees at the same school. [→ Michael B. Lund 2019](#michael-b-lund-2019)
`q2 i?` In University of California physical-science programs, time to graduation averages 5.5 years without a separate master's, 6.5 years with a same-school master's, and 8.4 years with a master's from a different institution. [→ Michael B. Lund 2019 (2)](#michael-b-lund-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Michael B. Lund 2019

Michael B. Lund, Savannah R. Jacklin, and David Ciardi. (2019). Enabling Terminal Master's Degrees as a Step Towards a Ph.D. Astro2020 White Paper. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1909.02103](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1909.02103)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The white paper's synthesis of AIP survey data on median full-time equivalent years of graduate study reports this gap for transferring Ph.D. students, noting "an additional 2 years at their previous institution, for a gap of 1.5 years" at the Ph.D.-granting institution.

> "Students that complete a master's degree and go on to a different Ph.D. program take ∼ 2 years longer in graduate school than students that enter a Ph.D. program directly."

### Michael B. Lund 2019 (2)

Michael B. Lund, Savannah R. Jacklin, and David Ciardi. (2019). Enabling Terminal Master's Degrees as a Step Towards a Ph.D. Astro2020 White Paper. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1909.02103](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1909.02103)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The paper cites Nerad and Cerny (1991), a study of University of California Ph.D. programs in the physical sciences, reporting average times to graduation of 5.5, 6.5, and 8.4 years for the three pathways. The review attributes this to that prior study.

> "In a study of University of California Ph.D. programs in the physical sciences, a student that earns a Ph.D. without earning a separate master's degree in the process graduates in an average of 5.5 years. The time to graduation increases to 6.5 years for students that earn a master's degree at the same school as their Ph.D., and to 8.4 years if they earned a master's degree at a different institution than their Ph.D."
