## Evidence

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2010

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2010). Using Analogy to Solve a Three-Step Physics Problem. PERC Proceedings. [doi:10.1063/1.3515228](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3515228)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Analysis of intervention groups 1–3 versus the comparison group in the calculus-based course (Tables 2 and 3) found no group “statistically different from that of the comparison group,” despite students in intervention 2 improving before-to-after scaffolding.

> "In fact, none of the intervention groups in the calculus-based course show a performance statistically different from that of the comparison group."

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2010 (2)

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2010). Using Analogy to Solve a Three-Step Physics Problem. PERC Proceedings. [doi:10.1063/1.3515228](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3515228)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

In the algebra-based course, all three intervention groups outperformed the comparison group with p value < 0. 05, but the article states “there is still much room for improvement.” No effect size is printed.

> "Although the algebra-based students in all three intervention groups did perform significantly better (with p value < 0. 05) than the comparison group students, there is still much room for improvement."
