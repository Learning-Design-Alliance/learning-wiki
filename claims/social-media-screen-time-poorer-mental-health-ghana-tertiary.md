Mental health and depression as mediators between social media use screen time and academic integrity among tertiary students in Ghana. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-40285-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple regression on survey data from 970 students at public Colleges of Education in Ghana tested the direct effect of digital behaviors on mental health. The model accounted for 31% of variance, with "late-night screen usage" and "emotional investment in social media" as strongest negative predictors.

> "Findings revealed that increased social media use and screen time were significantly associated with poorer mental health outcomes (R2 = 0.31, p < .001), with late-night screen usage (ő≤ = ‚ąí 0.29, p < .001) and emotional investment in social media (ő≤ = ‚ąí 0.30, p < .001) emerging as the strongest negative predictors of mental well-being."
