# Within-year (fall-to-spring) achievement gains decelerate linearly as students move through school, at roughly 1.4 to 4 RIT per year depending on subject and centering grade

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Fixed-effect estimates show fall-to-spring growth in math decelerates linearly at roughly 1.4 RIT (centered at grade two) and 1.99 RIT (centered at grade four), and in reading by almost 4 and 2.5 RIT respectively. [→ Soland 2019](#soland-2019)

## Evidence

### Soland 2019

Soland, J. & Thum, Y.M. (2019). School effectiveness, summer loss, and federal accountability: Applying the compound polynomial model in a program evaluation context. The Collaborative for Student Growth at NWEA Working Paper. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/school-effectiveness-summer-loss-and-federal-accountability-applying-the-compound-polynomialmodel-in-a-program-evaluation-context/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Fixed-effects estimates from CP models fit to MAP Growth RIT scores (grades 2-6, 570 schools), reported in Table 2 for math and reading with time centered at grades two and four. The article reports "within-year growth in math decelerates linearly" at the printed rates.

> "Fixed effect estimates indicate that within-year growth in math decelerates linearly at a rate of roughly 1.4 RIT for models centered at grade two and 1.99 RIT for models centered at grade four.   For reading, that linear deceleration is almost 4 RIT when centered at second grade and 2.5 when centered at fourth grade."
