# Learning-rate asymmetries between opponent types reverse across grade groups, with a non-monotonic age trend

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Grades 1-2 and 10-12 showed higher learning rates for the low-competitive opponent, while grades 4-5 and 7-9 showed higher learning rates for the high-competitive opponent. [→ Zhang 2026](#zhang-2026)
`q2 i?` Learning rates for both opponents followed non-monotonic age trends: LCO rates dipped in grades 7-9 and rose in grades 10-12; HCO rates peaked in grades 7-9. [→ Zhang 2026 (2)](#zhang-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Zhang 2026

Zhang, R., Zhang, L., Hao, X.-M., Dreher, J.-C., & Qu, C. (2026). Learning to adaptively cooperate through social interactions during childhood and adolescence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00423-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Bayesian Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Tests on winning-model hyperparameters found significant αh–αl differences in all grade groups; grades 4-5 and 7-9 had higher rates for HCO than LCO (BF10 > 100 in both).

> "Specifically, children in grades 1-2 had higher learning rates when facing LCO than HCO (BF10 > 100, W = 7.08×106; HCO: M = 0.05, SD = 0.10, 95% HDI = [ 0.4×10-6, 0.234]; LCO: M = 0.09, SD = 0.03, 95% HDI = [0.026, 0.149])"

### Zhang 2026 (2)

Zhang, R., Zhang, L., Hao, X.-M., Dreher, J.-C., & Qu, C. (2026). Learning to adaptively cooperate through social interactions during childhood and adolescence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00423-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Bayesian Mann-Whitney U comparisons (grades 4-5 vs 7-9, and grades 10-12 vs both) showed LCO rates significantly higher in grades 4-5 and 10-12 than grades 7-9 (BF10 > 100); HCO rates peaked in grades 7-9, significantly above grades 4-5 and 10-12 (BF₁₀ > 100).

> "We found that learning rates for LCO decreased initially and then increased with age."
