# In a rolling pool-ball problem, 76% of students claimed higher friction lowers final speed, versus 43% on the isomorphic spinning-wheel problem

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Responses asserting higher friction lowers final speed almost doubled from the wheel problem (43%) to the pool-ball problem (76%), showing context-dependence of invoked knowledge. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016)

## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Assessing Student Expertise in Introductory Physics with Isomorphic Problems, Part I: Performance on Non-intuitive Problem Pair from Introductory Physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06499](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06499)

`q3 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Written recitation-quiz responses from 70 students solving only problem (2), compared with 67 solving only problem (1), categorized into five response types. The article reports "76% of the students" claimed higher friction lowers vf in the pool-ball problem versus 43% for the wheel.

> "in the context of the pool ball which had the initial linear speed v0 ⁄= 0, 76% of the students believed that a higher frictional force will make vf smaller when the rolling begins"
