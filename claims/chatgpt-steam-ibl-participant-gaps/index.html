Evidence¶

Haozhe Jiang 2026¶

Haozhe Jiang, Yixin Li, Ritesh Chugh, Yueyou Xin and Hongyu Cheng. (2026). The AI-powered co-inquirer: a systematic review of ChatGPT for inquiry-based learning in STEAM education. International Journal of STEM Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40594-026-00628-9

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r1

RQ1 participant-group analysis of the 24 reviewed studies, reported alongside the finding that studies were evenly split with 12 in each of K-12 and higher education. The review states "no study focused on elementary school students."

"Undergraduate students constituted the largest single participant group (n = 10), followed by middle school students (n = 8), high school students (n = 4), and postgraduate students (n = 2). Notably, no study focused on elementary school students."

Haozhe Jiang 2026 (2)¶

Haozhe Jiang, Yixin Li, Ritesh Chugh, Yueyou Xin and Hongyu Cheng. (2026). The AI-powered co-inquirer: a systematic review of ChatGPT for inquiry-based learning in STEAM education. International Journal of STEM Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40594-026-00628-9

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

RQ1 sample-size distribution across the 24 studies (Fig. 7), with most studies (n = 13) ranging from 31 to 100 participants. The review concludes the field lacks large-scale studies of ChatGPT in STEAM IBL.

"The largest share (n = 23) involved fewer than 300 participants. Although sample size correlates with methodological design, the finding indicates that ChatGPT has yet to be studied at scale in STEAM-related IBL."
