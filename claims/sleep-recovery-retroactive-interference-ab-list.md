# Sleep after learning provides recovery from retroactive interference, preferentially improving recall of the first (A-B) list compared with wakefulness in an A-B, A-C paradigm

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In Experiment 1, recall of the first A-B list was superior in the sleep group compared with the wake group, while A-C recall did not differ between the two conditions. [→ Drosopoulos 2007](#drosopoulos-2007)

## Evidence

### Drosopoulos 2007

Drosopoulos, S., Schulze, C., Fischer, S., & Born, J. (2007). Sleep's Function in the Spontaneous Recovery and Consolidation of Memories. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General. https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.136.2.169

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Controlled laboratory experiment: 90 participants learned two interfering word-pair lists; sleep (n=10) versus wake (n=10) retention groups tested after the first postlearning night. Sleep group recalled 73.00% of A-B pairs versus 54.50% in the wake group, a "superior recall of A-B associations in the sleep group compared with the wake group", η2 = .23.

> "Post hoc pairwise comparisons performed separately on the retention rates for the A-B and A-C pairs further confirmed the superior recall of A-B associations in the sleep group compared with the wake group, F(1, 18) ⫽ 5.24, p ⫽ .03, ␩2 ⫽ .23, whereas the difference for the A-C associations was not significant"
