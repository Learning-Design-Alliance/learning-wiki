## Evidence

### Madsen 2016

Madsen, McKagan, and Sayre. (2016). Best Practices for Administering Concept Inventories. American Association of Physics Teachers, College Park, MD. [doi:10.1119/1.5011826](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.5011826)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Ding et al. compared completion rates and overall BEMA post-test scores across incentive conditions (Table III); the participation-points condition showed "increased participation rate while scores remained similar to giving no incentive". Adams et al. and Redish report similar success.

> "Giving a small number of participation points increased participation rate while scores remained similar to giving no incentive."

### Madsen 2016 (2)

Madsen, McKagan, and Sayre. (2016). Best Practices for Administering Concept Inventories. American Association of Physics Teachers, College Park, MD. [doi:10.1119/1.5011826](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.5011826)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

In the same Ding et al. comparison, the final-exam condition produced "much higher completion rate and higher scores" than no incentive, but the article recommends against high-stakes use because inventories assess instruction, not individual mastery.

> "When the test became more high-stakes when it was given on the final exam, completion rates and scores increased."
