## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students taught with the PhET-based curriculum scored significantly better on the voltage part of the exam question than students who used Photoelectric Tutor, and comparably or somewhat better on the other parts. [→ S. B. McKagan 2007](#s-b-mckagan-2007)

## Evidence

### S. B. McKagan 2007

S. B. McKagan, W. Handley, K. K. Perkins, and C. E. Wieman. (2007). A Research-Based Curriculum for Teaching the Photoelectric Effect. https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.2165 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0706.2165](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0706.2165)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Cross-semester exam analysis (Table I) comparing CU students (N = 94–189 per semester) with Steinberg et al.'s UW non-PT and PT students on an adapted exam question; the article reports CU students "significantly better on part b" and comparable on part a. No effect size is printed.

> "While the students in the Steinberg et al. study who used PT were able to do significantly better than the students who did not use PT, they still did poorly on part b, the question about voltage. Our students' scores were comparable to the PT students on part a, somewhat better on part c, and significantly better on part b."
