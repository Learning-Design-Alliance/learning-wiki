# Specific processing speed generalises beyond mathematics, significantly predicting Chinese and English achievement in both Grade 7 and Grade 9

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Specific processing speed measures accounted for at least 4.2% of variance in Grade 7 Chinese and English and at least 4.5% in Grade 9 Chinese and English. [→ Cheng Dazhi 2022](#cheng-dazhi-2022)
`q2 i?` All four specific processing speed measures correlated significantly with all academic measures. [→ Cheng Dazhi 2022 (2)](#cheng-dazhi-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Cheng Dazhi 2022

Cheng Dazhi, Shi Kaihui, Wang Naiyi, Miao Xinyang, Zhou Xinlin. (2022). Examining the Differential Role of General and Specific Processing Speed in Predicting Mathematical Achievement in Junior High School. Journal of Intelligence. https://doi.org/10.3390/jintelligence10010001

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical regression analysis of the same 212-student cohort shows specific processing speed predicting Chinese and English in Grade 7 (at least 4.2% of variance) and, per the article, "at least 4.5% of the variance in high school entrance examination achievements" in Grade 9.

> "Specific processing speed also significantly contributed to Chinese and English achievements in the 7th grade, where it accounted for at least 4.2% of the variance in academic achievements."

### Cheng Dazhi 2022 (2)

Cheng Dazhi, Shi Kaihui, Wang Naiyi, Miao Xinyang, Zhou Xinlin. (2022). Examining the Differential Role of General and Specific Processing Speed in Predicting Mathematical Achievement in Junior High School. Journal of Intelligence. https://doi.org/10.3390/jintelligence10010001

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Pearson correlation analysis (Table 1) with Bonferroni correction across 136 correlations found all specific processing speed measures significantly correlated with all academic measures; the article reports intercorrelations among academic measures were also significant (all p < 0.0003).

> "All the specific processing speed measures (word semantic, simple subtraction, complex subtraction, and complex multiplication) were significantly correlated to all academic measures."
