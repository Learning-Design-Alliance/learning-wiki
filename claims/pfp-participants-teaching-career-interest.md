## Evidence

### Edward Price and Noah Finkelstein 2006

Edward Price and Noah Finkelstein. (2006). Preparing graduate students to be educators. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0609003](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0609003)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Informal spring 2006 survey of current UCSD and CU PFP participants compared with AIP Statistical Research Center data on US physics graduate students' top career choice. The survey found PFP respondents chose teaching-faculty careers "at more than twice the rate of US physics graduate students overall" (Table II: 31% vs 13%).

> "But, PFP respondents chose 'Faculty at liberal arts or community college or other teaching' at more than twice the rate of US physics graduate students overall, while choosing 'Research in industry, national lab or university setting' at less than half the rate of US physics graduate students overall."

### Edward Price and Noah Finkelstein 2006 (2)

Edward Price and Noah Finkelstein. (2006). Preparing graduate students to be educators. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0609003](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0609003)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same spring 2006 PFP participant survey, ten-year expectation item. Respondents' expectations shifted toward teaching careers: research tenure-track faculty expectations "dropped to 16%" and teaching-faculty expectations "increased to 44%" (Table II).

> "When asked what they "really think they'll be doing 10 years from now," the percent of PFP respondents choosing 'Research tenure-track faculty' dropped to 16%, while the percent choosing 'Faculty at liberal arts or community college or other teaching' increased to 44%."
