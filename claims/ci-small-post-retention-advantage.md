# High CI practice shows a statistically significant but small advantage in Δ post-retention change favoring high CI

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q4` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q4 i1` An overall significant difference in Δ post-retention favors high CI practice, but the effect size (ES = −0.35) is small. [→ Achraf Ammar 2024](#achraf-ammar-2024)

## Evidence

### Achraf Ammar 2024

Achraf Ammar, Khaled Trabelsi, Mohamed Ali Boujelbane, Atef Salem, Omar Boukhris, Jordan M. Glenn, Piotr Zmijewski, Haitham A. Jahrami, Hamdi Chtourou, Wolfgang I. Schöllhorn. (2024). The Effects of Contextual Interference Learning on the Acquisition and Relatively Permanent Gains in Skilled Performance: A Critical Systematic Review with Multilevel Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09892-z

`q4 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

The same multilevel meta-analysis of 36 studies found "An overall significant difference (p  = 0.001) in favor of high CI practice" for Δ post-retention, with a small printed effect size (ES = −0.35), which the authors judged "not large enough" to yield greater long-term gain.

> "An overall significant difference (p  = 0.001) in favor of high CI practice was detected in “Δ post-retention.” However, this difference was not large enough (ES =  − 0.35) to produce an overall greater long-term gain"
