## Evidence

### Keith C. Herman and Wendy M. Reinke 2016

Keith C. Herman and Wendy M. Reinke. (2016). Improving Teacher Perceptions of Parent Involvement Patterns: Findings From a Group Randomized Trial. School Psychology Quarterly. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/spq0000169

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Equality tests of means across follow-up classes (Table 2) in the group randomized trial. Across most comparisons the Low Contact/High Comfort profile emerged as having the most positive child and family correlates.

> "Additionally, the Low Contact/High Comfort class had the highest academic perfor- mance on all academic measures, even in com- parison to the High Contact/High Comfort group on teacher ratings and WJ Reading scores."

### Keith C. Herman and Wendy M. Reinke 2016 (2)

Keith C. Herman and Wendy M. Reinke. (2016). Improving Teacher Perceptions of Parent Involvement Patterns: Findings From a Group Randomized Trial. School Psychology Quarterly. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/spq0000169

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The authors' interpretation of the person-centered profile results in the Discussion. They state this implies teacher discomfort is based on something other than actual contact, and suggest racial or socioeconomic differences and interpretation of student misbehavior as candidate explanations.

> "Low contact alone was not associated with adverse student performance or out- comes."
