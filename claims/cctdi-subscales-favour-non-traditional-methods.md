## Evidence

### Lee JuHee 2016

Lee JuHee, Lee Yoonju, Gong SaeLom, Bae Juyeon, Choi Moonki. (2016). A meta-analysis of the effects of non-traditional teaching methods on the critical thinking abilities of nursing students. BMC Medical Education. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5025580/

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Subscale meta-analysis of CCTDI datasets using random effects model. Six subscales showed significant increases with printed effect sizes (e.g., truth-seeking SMD = 0.32; open-mindedness SMD = 0.37); maturity did not reach significance (p = 0.06). Only one study scored 50 or higher on open-mindedness and inquisitiveness.

> "Analysis of the CCTDI subscale scores for truth-seeking (SMD: 0.32, 95 % CI: 0.01–0.47, p < .0001), open-mindedness (SMD: 0.37, 95 % CI: 0.22–0.53, p < .00001), analyticity (SMD: 0.28, 95 % CI: 0.09–0.46, p = .004), critical thinking confidence (SMD: 0.34, 95 % CI: 0.18–0.49, p < .0001), inquisitiveness (SMD: 0.36, 95 % CI: 0.21–0.52, p < .00001), and maturity (SMD: 0.16, 95 % CI: −0.01–0.32, p = 0.06) revealed a more effective increase as compared to the control group"
