## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In multilevel regression with individuals nested in families, being male was associated with 0.60 years higher EA and a 1 SD higher HES score with 0.82 years more EA. [→ Arpawong 2023](#arpawong-2023)

## Evidence

### Arpawong 2023

Arpawong, Thalida Em; Gatz, Margaret; Zavala, Catalina; Gruenewald, Tara L.; Walters, Ellen E.; Prescott, Carol A. (2023). Nature, Nurture, and the Meaning of Educational Attainment: Differences by Sex and Socioeconomic Status. Twin Research and Human Genetics. https://doi.org/10.1017/thg.2023.6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multilevel regression (PROC MIXED, individuals nested in families) adjusting for father's and mother's education and father's occupation; the full model accounted for 20.9% of EA variation. It reports "being male was associated with 0.60 years higher EA" and the interaction added no explained variance (R2 < .1%).

> "Results confirmed hypothesis 1: Compared to females, being male was associated with 0.60 years higher EA. Overall, a 1 SD higher HES score predicted 0.82 years more in EA. Adding a sex-by-HES interaction to the model did not increase the explained variance in EA (R2< .1%)"
