Daniel Prol 2026¶

Daniel Prol, Juho Leinonen, Arto Hellas, Saleh Alkhamees, Amin Alipour. (2026). Pulla: A Parsons Problem Tool for Fine-Grained Behavioral Tracing and Instructor-Facing Problem-Solving Analysis. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15944 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.15944

q2 · i? · design · r2

Log analysis of UH deployment attempts across the three Parsons problems. Trace data show ordering errors in "43–52% of students with incorrect attempts" with no dominant pattern, which the authors read as upper-division students cycling through all three difficulty types.

"Control-flow ordering errors appeared consistently across all three problems at the University of Houston (43–52% of students with incorrect attempts), but without a dominant pattern: in P3, wrong ordering (43%) was roughly co-equal with return-for-raise substitution (47%) and exception type confusion (37%)."

q2 · i? · design · r2

Log analysis of Aalto deployment attempts on safe_integer. Ordering errors were "a secondary pattern in safe_integer (P2), present in 31% of students with incorrect attempts", while exception type confusion (62%) and return-for-raise substitution (50%) dominated.

"At Aalto University, control-flow ordering errors were a secondary pattern in safe_integer (P2), present in 31% of students with incorrect attempts; the dominant difficulties were exception type confusion (62%) and return-for-raise substitution (50%)."

Related Claims¶

  • Deploying a trace-capturing Parsons tool in two university courses surfaced recurring difficulty patterns including exception-type misidentification, return-for-throw confusion, and incorrect control-flow ordering — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Exception-type confusion was the dominant difficulty pattern in SafeDivide at the University of Houston (90% of incorrect attempts) and persisted across harder problems in both deployments — related
  • Students across both deployments substituted return statements for exception-raising constructs, treating error conditions as reasons to exit early — related
  • State revisits within solving sessions were rarer in the Pulla deployments (6–12% of UH sessions, 0–3% at Aalto) than the 21–33% previously reported for similar Parsons tasks — related
