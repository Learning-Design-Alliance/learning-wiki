# Parental educational level was strongly associated with children's school test scores in the adjusted models

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

> **Currency** · newest study 2022 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Children whose mothers held a master's degree or higher scored 12.59 points higher, and children whose fathers held a master's degree or higher scored 11.35 points higher, than children of primary-school-educated parents (both p < 0.001); these are unstandardized coefficients, so no effect size applies. [→ Spangmose Anne Lærke 2022](#spangmose-anne-lrke-2022)

## Evidence

### Spangmose Anne Lærke 2022

Spangmose Anne Lærke, Skipper Niels, Knorr Sine, Wullum Gundersen Tina, Beck Jensen Rikke, Damm Peter, Lykke Mortensen Erik, Pinborg Anja, Svensson Jannet, Clausen Tine. (2022). School performance in Danish children exposed to maternal type 1 diabetes in utero: A nationwide retrospective cohort study. PLoS Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003977

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Coefficients from the fully adjusted multiple linear regression (Table 2, Model 3) in the nationwide cohort. Mother's ≥Master's degree carried a coefficient of "12.59 (12.34 to 12.84), p < 0.001" relative to primary school; the father's equivalent coefficient was 11.35 (11.12 to 11.58).
