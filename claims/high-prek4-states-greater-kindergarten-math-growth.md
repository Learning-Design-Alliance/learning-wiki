# Kindergarten math growth from fall to spring is greater in High PreK4 investment states than in Growing or Low states

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students in High PreK4 investment states experienced greater i-Ready math growth from fall to spring than peers in Growing or Low states, with the High-vs-Growing gap doubling from 3 to 6 scale score points. [→ Sarah Collier Villaume 2025](#sarah-collier-villaume-2025)
`q2 i?` 70% of kindergartners in High states improved or maintained grade level in math versus 60% in Growing states. [→ Sarah Collier Villaume 2025 (2)](#sarah-collier-villaume-2025-2)

### Sarah Collier Villaume 2025

Sarah Collier Villaume, Amy Auletto, & Kadir Bahar. (2025). Investing Early: The Impact of State Prekindergarten Funding on Kindergarten Academic Outcomes. E4 Center. https://e4.northwestern.edu/2025/06/30/investing-early-the-impact-of-state-prekindergarten-funding-on-kindergarten-academic-outcomes/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive comparison of fall and spring 2023-24 i-Ready math scale scores across the three state PreK4 investment categories, shown in Figure 1. The report states the "scale score point gap between High and Growing states doubled from 3 points in Fall (342 vs. 339) to 6 points in Spring."

> "in the 2023 -24 school year, the scale score point gap between High and Growing states doubled from 3 points in Fall (342 vs. 339) to 6 points in Spring."

### Sarah Collier Villaume 2025 (2)

Sarah Collier Villaume, Amy Auletto, & Kadir Bahar. (2025). Investing Early: The Impact of State Prekindergarten Funding on Kindergarten Academic Outcomes. E4 Center. https://e4.northwestern.edu/2025/06/30/investing-early-the-impact-of-state-prekindergarten-funding-on-kindergarten-academic-outcomes/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Figure 2 reports the proportion of kindergarten students improving or maintaining grade-level math performance by state category; the report contrasts "70% of kindergarten students in High states" with 60% in Growing states. No significance test is reported for this contrast.

> "only 60% of kindergarten students in Growing states improved a grade level or maintained on-grade-level performance in math from fall to spring , versus 70% of kindergarten students in High states"
