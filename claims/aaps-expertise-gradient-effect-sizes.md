# On the AAPS survey, faculty respond more expert-like than Ph.D. students, who respond more expert-like than introductory students, with large effect sizes

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Pairwise effect sizes between introductory students, Ph.D. students, and faculty over all 33 questions range from 1.19 to 2.18 in favor of the more expert-like group, and all differences are statistically significant. [→ Andrew J. Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#andrew-j-mason-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)

## Evidence

### Andrew J. Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Andrew J. Mason and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Surveying College Introductory Physics Students' Attitudes and Approaches to Problem Solving. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/37/5/055704](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/37/5/055704)

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Cross-group comparison of average AAPS scores (scale -1 to +1) using Cohen's d with pooled standard deviations; printed pairwise values include introductory vs faculty d = 2.18 and introductory vs Ph.D. d = 1.60. The article reports all pairwise differences statistically significant (p<0.05).

> "Table 2 shows that the effect sizes between groups of different levels of expertise have a large to very large effect size (1.19 < d < 2.18), in favor of the more assumed expert-like group."
