# CPS academic productivity gains peaked in 1997, with 1999 gains uniformly lower than 1997 gains at all grades

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` At all grades, 1996 reading and math gains exceeded 1994 gains, while 1999 gains were uniformly lower than 1997 gains, suggesting productivity gains peaked in 1997. [→ John Q. Easton 2001](#john-q-easton-2001)
`q2 i?` Math gains in 2000 rebounded slightly above 1999 levels but remained well below the 1997 peak. [→ John Q. Easton 2001 (2)](#john-q-easton-2001-2)

## Evidence

### John Q. Easton 2001

John Q. Easton, Todd Rosenkranz, Anthony S. Bryk. (2001). Annual CPS Test Trend Review, 2000: Elementary School Test Score Trends in Chicago Public Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/annual-cps-trend-review-2000

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Gain-score analysis (Figure 3, Table E) comparing annual ITBS gains in grade equivalents for grades 3-8, 1994-2000, using constant test-form pairs (K to L for 1994/1996; L to M for 1997/1999) as the most valid comparisons.

> "At all grades, the 1996 gains exceed the 1994 gains. In contrast, the 1999 gains are uniformly lower than the 1997 gains."

### John Q. Easton 2001 (2)

John Q. Easton, Todd Rosenkranz, Anthony S. Bryk. (2001). Annual CPS Test Trend Review, 2000: Elementary School Test Score Trends in Chicago Public Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/annual-cps-trend-review-2000

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The 2000 gains were calculated from a different pair of test forms (M and K); the brief treats them as further evidence that reading gains are leveling off or possibly declining while math rebounded slightly.

> "Math gains in 2000 appear to have rebounded slightly above the 1999 levels, but still well below their 1997 peak."
