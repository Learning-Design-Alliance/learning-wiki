Evidence¶

Ethan Roy 2025¶

Ethan Roy, Mathieu Guillaume, Amandine Van Rinsveld, Project iLead Consortium & Bruce D. McCandliss. (2025). Tablet-based arithmetic fluency assessment reveals developments in math cognition and math achievement from childhood to adolescence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00314-5

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Linear-mixed effects model of RPM with random intercepts per participant, fixed effects of problem size, operation, and grade. The article reports "an average decrease in RPM of 2.03 problems per minute with a one-unit increase in problem size" (partial R2 = 0.020), and grade and operation main effects, all FDR-corrected; partial R2 from regression is not treated as an effect size.

"Students tended to speed up when solving smaller problems, with an average decrease in RPM of 2.03 problems per minute with a one-unit increase in problem size (R2partial = 0.020, t(10,780) =−16.47,padj <0.001"

Ethan Roy 2025 (2)¶

Ethan Roy, Mathieu Guillaume, Amandine Van Rinsveld, Project iLead Consortium & Bruce D. McCandliss. (2025). Tablet-based arithmetic fluency assessment reveals developments in math cognition and math achievement from childhood to adolescence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00314-5

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Same mixed-effects model examining operation effects on RPM. The article reports addition was answered at a "slightly, yet signiﬁcantly, faster rate compared to subtraction problems" (partial R2 = 0.003), noting operation is confounded with problem size because subtraction problems generally have smaller solutions; no standardized effect size is printed.

"This model also revealed that, on average,students correctly answered addition problems at a slightly, yet signiﬁcantly, faster rate compared to subtraction problems (R2partial = 0.003, t(10,800) =−6.62,padj < 0.001, Fig.2a)"
