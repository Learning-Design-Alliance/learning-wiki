# Tracker video analysis lets students infer kinematics quantities from real video, yielding values consistent with gravitational acceleration

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After tracking a projectile's trajectory in Tracker, the data tool determines numerical values of accelerations and initial velocity components in the x and y directions. [→ Loo Kang WEE 2012](#loo-kang-wee-2012)
`q2 i?` The vertical acceleration deduced from the parabolic fit (ay = – 10.08 ms –2) is approximately equal to the gravitational acceleration constant at the surface of Earth. [→ Loo Kang WEE 2012 (2)](#loo-kang-wee-2012-2)

## Evidence

### Loo Kang WEE 2012

Loo Kang WEE, Charles CHEW, Giam Hwee GOH, Samuel TAN, Tat Leong LEE. (2012). Using Tracker as a Pedagogical Tool for Understanding Projectile Motion. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1206.6489](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1206.6489)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Video analysis demonstration in section III using the BallTossOut.mov video: after manually or automatically tracking the projectile's trajectory, Tracker's data tool "allows the determination of the numerical values of quantities such as acceleration, gx and gy and initial velocity ux and uy" by comparing linear and parabolic fit coefficients.

> "the data tool can be used to analyze motion and allows the determination of the numerical values of  quantities such as acceleration, gx and gy and initial velocity ux and uy, in the x and y directions respectively"

### Loo Kang WEE 2012 (2)

Loo Kang WEE, Charles CHEW, Giam Hwee GOH, Samuel TAN, Tat Leong LEE. (2012). Using Tracker as a Pedagogical Tool for Understanding Projectile Motion. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1206.6489](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1206.6489)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

In the same video analysis demonstration, the parabolic fit parameter for the y versus t graph gives ay = – 10.08 ms –2, which the authors interpret as "approximately equal in value to the gravitational acceleration constant at the surface of Earth of – 9.81 ms –2".

> "The value ay = – 10.08 ms –2 can be interpreted to be approximately equal in value to the gravitational acceleration constant at the surface of Earth of – 9.81 ms –2"
