# Social communication skills uniquely predict kindergarten executive functions after accounting for vocabulary, which does not predict EF

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Initial levels (β = .19, p < .05) and rate of growth (β = .29, p < .001) in social communication skills predicted kindergarten EF, while vocabulary level (β = .14, p = .15) and growth (β = .02, p = .84) did not. [→ Ramsook K. Ashana 2020](#ramsook-k-ashana-2020)

## Evidence

### Ramsook K. Ashana 2020

Ramsook K. Ashana, Welsh Janet A., Bierman Karen L. (2020). What you say, and how you say it: Preschoolers' growth in vocabulary and communication skills differentially predict kindergarten academic achievement and self‐regulation. Social Development, 29(3), 783–800. https://doi.org/10.1111/sode.12425

`q3 · i?`

Multilevel models predicting Wave 3 EF composite (Backward Word Span, Peg Tapping, Dimensional Change Card Sort) in 164 Head Start children. In the best-fitting model, "rate of growth in social communication skills significantly predicted EF at Wave 3" while vocabulary did not.

> "Similarly, rate of growth in social communication skills significantly predicted EF at Wave 3 (β = .29, SE = .37, p < .001), but growth in vocabulary was not associated with EF at Wave 3 (β = .02, SE = .04, p = .84)."
