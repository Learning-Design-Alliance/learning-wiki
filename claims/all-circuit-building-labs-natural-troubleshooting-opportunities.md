## Evidence

### Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer and H. J. Lewandowski 2016

Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer and H. J. Lewandowski. (2016). Nothing works the first time: An expert experimental physics epistemology. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.020](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.020)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In the results, instructor Pine asserted that "every electronics lab is speciﬁcally designed to increase a students' ability to troubleshoot" because "Nobody is able to step in and wire them correctly the ﬁrst time."

> "Due to its nature, every electronics lab is speciﬁcally designed to increase a students' ability to troubleshoot. Nobody is able to step in and wire them correctly the ﬁrst time."

### Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer and H. J. Lewandowski 2016 (2)

Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer and H. J. Lewandowski. (2016). Nothing works the first time: An expert experimental physics epistemology. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.020](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.020)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

When asked directly in the interviews whether they had implemented lab activities specifically designed to develop troubleshooting skills, "most instructors said they had not done so," with Dogwood attributing the need to troubleshoot to the natural course of lab work.

> "most instructors said they had not done so: “No. Again because I feel like [the need to troubleshoot] comes up in the natural course of things and we're kind of limited on time.”"
