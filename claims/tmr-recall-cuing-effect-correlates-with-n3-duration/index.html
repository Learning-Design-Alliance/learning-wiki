Evidence¶

Whitmore 2022¶

Whitmore, N. W., Bassard, A. M. & Paller, K. A. (2022). Targeted memory reactivation of face-name learning depends on ample and undisturbed slow-wave sleep. npj Science of Learning, 7:1. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00119-2

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Laboratory nap study (N=24) in which a subset of spoken names plus music from one class was re-presented during N3 sleep; robust linear regression correlational analysis showed the recall cuing effect "was correlated with the duration of stage N3 sleep" (χ²(1,N=24)=5.18, p=0.02).

"the size of the cuing effect on Δrecall was correlated with the duration of stage N3 sleep [ χ² (1,N = 24) = 5.18, p = 0.02]"

Related Claims¶

  • Sleep Disruption Index classifies TMR responders: participants with an index below 0.13 showed a positive recall cuing effect, those above did not show a significant one — related
  • N3 duration, cuing, and sleep-disruption measures are intercorrelated but represent separable facets of sleep quality in predicting the recall cuing effect — related
  • TMR benefits on name recall are negatively correlated with post-cue sleep disruption and post-cue beta-band EEG power, suggesting reactivation depends on undisturbed sleep — related
  • The TMR cuing effect on face recognition accuracy is associated with N3 sleep duration, though cueing did not significantly improve recognition overall — related
  • Sleep-stage durations, sleep fragmentation, and pre- and post-cue EEG band power were not significantly correlated with forgetting of cued items or the TMR-SD effect — related
