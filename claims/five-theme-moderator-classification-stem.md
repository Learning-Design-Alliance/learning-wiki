## Evidence

### Bicakci 2026

Bicakci, M., Heller, F., Stoeger, H., & Ziegler, A. (2026). From RAMSing to Rigor: Improving Moderator Analysis in STEM Education Meta-Research. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10172-1

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

RoBMA-Regression on the 1,786-effect-size corpus with moderator theme as a five-level categorical predictor; residual heterogeneity decreased to "τ  = 0.44, 95% CrI [0.41, 0.47]" but substantial heterogeneity remained.

> "Introducing moderator theme as a five-level categorical predictor markedly improved model fit (posterior inclusion probability = 0.997; BF Inc = 350), providing very strong evidence that the five-theme classification explained systematic varia tion rather than noise."

### Bicakci 2026 (2)

Bicakci, M., Heller, F., Stoeger, H., & Ziegler, A. (2026). From RAMSing to Rigor: Improving Moderator Analysis in STEM Education Meta-Research. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10172-1

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Model-averaged marginal estimates (Table 3): disciplinary focus g = 0.438, sample/context g = 0.364, publication metadata g = 0.275, intervention specifications g = 0.263, design/quality g = 0.197; the disciplinary-focus increment was "about + 0.13 relative to the overall baseline".

> "Overall, the five-theme classification revealed a clear pattern: disciplinary focus yielded the largest expected moderator effects, whereas design/quality yielded the smallest."
