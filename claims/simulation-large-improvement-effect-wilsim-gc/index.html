Evidence¶

Luo 2016¶

Luo, W., Pelletier, J., Duffin, K., Ormand, C., Hung, W., Shernoff, D., Zhai, X., Iverson, E., Whalley, K., Gallaher, C., and Furness, W. (2016). Advantages of Computer Simulation in Enhancing Students' Learning About Landform Evolution: A Case Study Using the Grand Canyon. Journal of Geoscience Education 64, 60-73. https://doi.org/10.5408/15-080.1

q2 · i3 · causal · r2

Quasiexperimental nonrandomized control-group pretest-posttest study in GEOG 102 (treatment n = 20, control n = 23). The article reports Cohen's d of "1.06" for the simulation group versus "0.40" for the paper group, calling the former "a large effect" and the latter small to moderate.

"the effect size captured by Cohen's d for the treatment group was 1.06 ( >0.8), higher than it was for the control group, which was 0.40. This suggests that the WILSIM-GC intervention had high practical signiﬁcance, with improvement above 1 SD, a large effect."

Related Claims¶

  • The control group improved significantly in completion speed from pretest to posttest while the treatment group showed no significant speed improvement — related
  • On higher-level application questions, percentage-correct growth was 15.0% in the simulation group versus 2.6% in the paper group — related
  • The instruction-by-time interaction favoring the simulation group was only marginally significant (one-tailed p = 0.085, g2 = 0.05), and the main effect of instruction was not significant — related
  • Students generally favored the interactive simulation over the paper-based method in attitudinal survey responses — related
  • The cross-section question showed the highest gain for both groups, with the simulation group's gain (60%) double the paper group's (30%) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
