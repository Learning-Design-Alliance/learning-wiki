## Evidence

### Bertini-Pérez D. 2024

Bertini-Pérez D., Martin-Ibañez L., Gómez Chica P., Dobarrio-Sanz I., Rodriguez-Arrastia M., Roman P., Rueda-Ruzafa L. (2024). Effectiveness of a Haemorrhage-Control Task Simulator for Training Nursing Students: A Quasi-Experimental before-after Study. Journal of Nursing Management. https://doi.org/10.1155/2024/9730765

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Practical-skill outcome measured by the simulator's timer in the one-group study (n = 103). Mean time fell from "76.38 (41.28)" seconds at PRE to 41.69 (27.13) at POST and rose to 47.13 (28.88) at 3M; only Z statistics were printed, no effect size.

> "The time required for controlling bleeding differed statistically between the three phases: PRE-POST (Z = −6.65; p < 0.01), PRE-3M (Z = −5; p < 0.01), and POST-3M (Z = −2.48; p < 0.05). Prior to training, the mean time (s) to stop simulated haemorrhaging was 76.38 (41.28), which was reduced to an average of 41.69 (27.13) following the intervention and 47.13 (28.88) after three months"
