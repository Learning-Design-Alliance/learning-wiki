# Teacher-effect variance is larger in low-SES schools than in high-SES schools

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2004 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person · **overdue for a recheck since 2009**: baselines and conditions change

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The between-classroom-within-schools-and-treatment-type variance component is always larger in low-SES schools, with ratios of low- to high-SES teacher-effect variances of 1.4 to 1.7 in mathematics and 1.6 to 3.7 in reading (full models). [→ Nye 2004](#nye-2004)
`q3 i?` Differences in teacher-effect variance between low- and high-SES schools on achievement status are larger and more statistically reliable than those on gains, with all differences significant at the 0.10 level and all but one at the 0.05 level. [→ Nye 2004 (2)](#nye-2004-2)

## Evidence

### Nye 2004

Nye, B., Konstantopoulos, S., & Hedges, L. V. (2004). How Large Are Teacher Effects? Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis, 26(3), 237-257. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=How+Large+Are+Teacher+Effects

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Separate HLM variance-decomposition analyses restricted to schools in the top and bottom quartiles of school SES (proportion eligible for free or reduced price lunch) in STAR. Four of six gain differences were significant at the 0.10 level and one at 0.05; the tests are noted as having low power.

> "The ratios of the teacher effect variances in low-SES schools to those in high-SES schools range from 1.4 to 1.7 in mathematics achievement and 1.6 to 3.7 in reading achievement (full models)."

### Nye 2004 (2)

Nye, B., Konstantopoulos, S., & Hedges, L. V. (2004). How Large Are Teacher Effects? Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis, 26(3), 237-257. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=How+Large+Are+Teacher+Effects

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Variance decomposition of achievement status in low- versus high-SES school quartiles from the STAR data, reported in Table 8. The article notes the pattern of larger teacher than school variance holds in low-SES schools but not in high-SES schools considered separately.

> "All of the differences are statis- tically significant at the 0.10 Ilevel and all but one of these differences is significant at the 0.05 level."
