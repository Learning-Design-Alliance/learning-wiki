`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

A practitioner digest reporting, citing the Johnsons and others, that cooperative learning experiences "tend to promote higher achievement" than competitive and individualistic learning across ages, subjects, and types of learning. No study design, sample, or effect size is printed; the claim is the digest's secondhand research summary.

> "According to the Johnsons, and others, cooperative learning experiences tend to promote higher achievement for students of all ages, across all subject areas, for virtually all types of learning than do competitive and individualistic learning experiences."

### Blosser 1993

Blosser, P. E. (1993). Using Cooperative Learning in Science Education. ERIC Clearinghouse for Science, Mathematics, and Environmental Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED351207

`q2 · narrative review digest reporting a meta-analysis second-hand (was q3) · i?` · `review · r2`

The review, a two-page ERIC clearinghouse digest, reports second-hand Johnson and Johnson's meta-analysis of 122 studies done between 1924 and 1981, whose 286 findings were analyzed using "three different methods: voting, effect size, and z score." No pooled effect size value is printed in the digest.

> "All methods of analysis resulted in the same finding: cooperative learning tends to promote higher achievement than does competition or individual work, with this finding holding for all age levels, all subject areas, and a variety of tasks"

### Kluge 1999

Kluge, David. (1999). A Brief Introduction to Cooperative Learning. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED437840

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The chapter's research section reports, citing Johnson and Johnson (1989), a meta-analysis of "352 of these studies" comparing learning conditions, which "found that achievement was higher for students engaged in cooperative learning." No effect size is printed.

> "Johnson and Johnson (1989) did a meta-analysis of 352 of these studies and found that achievement was higher for students engaged in cooperative learning."

## Discussion

Neither entry is the meta-analysis itself: both are practitioner digests restating Johnson and Johnson's syntheses, and neither prints an effect size, so the page cannot say how large the advantage is or check the conditions under which it held. The primary meta-analyses (and the 352-study synthesis on [the possible duplicate below](cooperative-learning-higher-achievement-than-competitive-individualistic.md)) are what would settle it; they are not recorded here.
