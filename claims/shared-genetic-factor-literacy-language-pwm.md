## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A single shared genetic factor captures the majority (>90%) of the genetic variance of reading fluency, spelling and phonemic awareness, and roughly half for listening comprehension and non-word repetition. [→ Chin Yang Shapland 2021](#chin-yang-shapland-2021)

## Evidence

### Chin Yang Shapland 2021

Chin Yang Shapland, Ellen Verhoef, George Davey Smith, Simon E. Fisher, Brad Verhulst, Philip S. Dale and Beate St Pourcain. (2021). Multivariate genome-wide covariance analyses of literacy, language and working memory skills reveal distinct etiologies. npj Science of Learning 6:23. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00101-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multi-domain GSEM analysis (passage reading subset, N = 6453) of ALSPAC unrelated youth; the best-fitting IPC model identified a shared genetic factor, with factorial co-heritabilities >44%. The article reports that "the majority of genetic variance across all traits (>90%) is captured by a shared genetic factor."

> "Thus, the majority of genetic variance across all traits (>90%) is captured by a shared genetic factor."
