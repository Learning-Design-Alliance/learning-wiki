# ISAT cut scores differ by grade and subject, with eighth-grade mathematics requiring a high score and eighth-grade reading a relatively low one to meet standards

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The Below-to-Meets Standards cut score rises across grades in mathematics (152.5, 157.5, 161.5) and is lower in eighth-grade reading (151.5), so standards stringency varies by grade and subject. [→ Easton 2003](#easton-2003)

## Evidence

### Easton 2003

Easton, J. Q., Correa, M., Luppescu, S., Park, H.-S., Ponisciak, S., Rosenkranz, T., & Sporte, S. (2003). How Do They Compare? ITBS and ISAT Reading and Mathematics in the Chicago Public Schools, 1999 to 2002. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://www.consortium-chicago.org/publications/briefs/onlineresources.html

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive analysis of ISAT cut scores set by a two-stage rating process and shown in Figure 1, on the 120-to-200 scale for grades three, five, and eight in reading and mathematics. In mathematics the cut scores "become progressively higher moving up the grades."

> "In mathematics, the cut scores become progressively higher moving up the grades. In other words, a higher score is required to meet state standards. For third grade it is 152.5, for fifth 157.5, and for eighth 161.5."
