# Screen time of two or more hours per weekday is independently associated with low academic performance

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Children with weekday screen time ≥2 h had significantly higher odds of low academic performance (OR 1.35) after adjusting for other lifestyle and family variables. [→ Yamada Masaaki 2019](#yamada-masaaki-2019)

## Evidence

### Yamada Masaaki 2019

Yamada Masaaki, Sekine Michikazu, Tatsuse Takashi, Asaka Yukiko. (2019). Association between lifestyle, parental smoke, socioeconomic status, and academic performance in Japanese elementary school children: the Super Diet Education Project. Environmental Health and Preventive Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12199-019-0776-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariate logistic regression in the same cross-sectional survey of 1663 children found "screen time ≥ 2 h (OR 1.35; 95% CI, 1.04–1.76)" significantly associated with low academic performance. Screen time included TV and DVD viewing, gaming, and Internet use.

> "screen time ≥ 2 h (OR 1.35; 95% CI, 1.04–1.76), studying at home < 1 h (OR 1.82; 95% CI, 1.34–2.49)"
