## Evidence

### Buehler 2021

Buehler, F. J., van Loon, M. H., Bayard, N. S., Steiner, M., & Roebers, C. M. (2021). Comparing metacognitive monitoring between native and non-native speaking primary school students. Metacognition and Learning, 16, 749–768. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-021-09261-z

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Study 2 matched 36 native and 36 non-native speaking fourth graders and repeated the identical Kanji paired-associates task. The MANOVA replicated Study 1: "no significant group difference between native and non-native speaking students" (F(3, 68) = 0.4; p = 0.75; η2p = 0.02).

> "Using Pillai’s trace, there was no significant group difference between native and non-native speaking students, F(3, 68) = 0.4; p = 0.75; η2p = 0.02. Thus, as hypothesized and replicating findings from Study 1, native and non-native speaking children did not differ significantly in their metacognitive monitoring (resolution) and recognition in the paired-associates task."
