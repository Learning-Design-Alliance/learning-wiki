The review reports, citing Sun et al. (2023), three studies showing labeling effects: visual learners were judged more intelligent than hands-on learners and expected to excel in mathematics, language arts, and social sciences. No effect size is printed.

> "Sun et al. (2023) conducted three studies that showed that "parents, teachers, and children judged children described as visual learners as more intelligent than children described as hands-on learners" (p. 46)."
