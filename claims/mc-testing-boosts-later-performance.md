## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students correctly answered more SAT II-derived questions on a later cued recall test if they had previously taken the multiple-choice test than if they had not. [→ Marsh 2007](#marsh-2007)

## Evidence

### Marsh 2007

Marsh, E. J., Roediger, H. L., III, Bjork, R. A., & Bjork, E. L. (2007). The memorial consequences of multiple-choice testing. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 14(2), 194-199. https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03194051

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Experiment using published SAT II materials (biology, chemistry, U.S. and world history) with Duke undergraduates, who on average answered 55% of multiple-choice questions correctly; a large positive testing effect appeared on the later cued recall test, with tested items at "M 48%" versus 22% for nontested items; the article prints no standardized effect size.

> "Students correctly answered more questions if they had been tested on the prior multiple-choice test (M 48%) than if they had not (M 22%)."
