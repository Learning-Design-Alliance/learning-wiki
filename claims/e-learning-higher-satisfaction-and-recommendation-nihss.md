## Evidence

### Suppan M 2021

Suppan M, Stuby L, Carrera E, Cottet P, Koka A, Assal F, Savoldelli GL, Suppan L. (2021). Asynchronous Distance Learning of the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale During the COVID-19 Pandemic (E-Learning vs Video): Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/23594

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary outcome from 4 five-point Likert-scale questions after quiz completion in the same randomized trial. "Very satisfied" was chosen by 14/35 e-learning versus 4/26 video participants; no effect size printed.

> "The rate of “very satisfied” participants was higher in the e-learning group (14/35, 40%; 95% CI 24%-56%) versus the video group (4/26, 15%; 95% CI 5%-25%, P=.02)"

### Suppan M 2021 (2)

Suppan M, Stuby L, Carrera E, Cottet P, Koka A, Assal F, Savoldelli GL, Suppan L. (2021). Asynchronous Distance Learning of the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale During the COVID-19 Pandemic (E-Learning vs Video): Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/23594

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary Likert outcome on recommendation likelihood in the same trial. 23/35 e-learning versus 8/26 video participants chose the strongest recommendation option; no effect size printed.

> "Participants who followed the e-learning method were more likely to recommend it to a colleague; 23/35 participants (66%) answered “Yes, most certainly” (95% CI 50%-82%), versus 8/26 (31%; 95% CI 13%-49%) in the video group, P=.007"
