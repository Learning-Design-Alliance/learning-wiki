## Evidence

### Chang Yu-Che 2016

Chang Yu-Che, Lee Ching-Hsing, Chen Chien-Kuang, Liao Chien-Hung, Ng Chip-Jin, Chen Jih-Chang, Chaou Chung-Hsien. (2016). Exploring the influence of gender, seniority and specialty on paper and computer-based feedback provision during mini-CEX assessments in a busy emergency department. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-016-9682-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Univariate and multivariate logistic regressions on the 1101-assessment dataset examined factors affecting provision of all three feedback components; after adjustment, gender and specialty were not significant, while the quoted odds ratios were. Only ORs and CIs printed; no standardised effect size.

> "only the seniority of the assessor (OR 0.35; 95 % CI 0.24–0.51) and the mini-CEX format (OR 1.47; 95 % CI 1.01–2.15) were statistically significant."
