# Between-studies variability in cognitive-training far-transfer effects is artifactual, not true heterogeneity

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Second-order sampling error explains all the between-meta-analyses variance with far transfer; no within-studies, between-studies, or between-meta-analyses true variance was found. [→ Gobet Fernand 2022](#gobet-fernand-2022)
`q3 i?` The hypothesis that different intervention types differ in far-transfer effects is empirically incorrect: their effects are null when sampling error, publication bias, and control-group type are accounted for. [→ Gobet Fernand 2022 (2)](#gobet-fernand-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Gobet Fernand 2022

Gobet Fernand, Sala Giovanni. (2022). Cognitive Training: A Field in Search of a Phenomenon. Perspectives on Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916221091830

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Second-order meta-analysis result: residual sampling error fully accounts for between-meta-analyses variance in far transfer, with true variance estimated at zero in both Tables 1 and 2.

> "Fourth, second-order sampling error (i.e., the residual sampling error from first-order meta-analyses) explains all the between-meta-analyses variance with far transfer. That is, we found no evidence of either within-studies, between-studies, or between-meta-analyses true variance."

### Gobet Fernand 2022 (2)

Gobet Fernand, Sala Giovanni. (2022). Cognitive Training: A Field in Search of a Phenomenon. Perspectives on Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916221091830

`q3 · i?`

The authors report testing the hypothesis that intervention types differ in far-transfer effects and finding it incorrect; the null result holds both within and across training domains.

> "Their effect on far-transfer tasks is null when sampling error, publication bias, and type of control group are taken into account. We get the same results when meta-analyses are carried out within one domain (e.g., action video games vs. nonaction video games) or between domains"
