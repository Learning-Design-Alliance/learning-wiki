## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Debriefers valued the scalability and resource efficiency of self-operating IVR scenarios running on computer-generated feedback, yet were frustrated by their inability to adjust and titrate the scenario in real time to learners' needs. [→ Sohlin AM 2025](#sohlin-am-2025)

## Evidence

### Sohlin AM 2025

Sohlin AM, Kjærgaard J, Hoffman IM, et al. (2025). Immersive virtual reality training: Addressing challenges and unlocking potentials. Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.15748

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Finding from the 10-interview qualitative study's appraisal and engagement themes; debriefers wanted to adapt the patient's clinical state in real time to match pressure to learner proficiency. Some noted the self-operating scenario reduced their cognitive load. No effect size reported.

> "the self‐operating nature of the scenario was viewed as an advantage of IVR; however, the rigidity of self‐operating scenarios reduced their engagement in debriefing IVR."
