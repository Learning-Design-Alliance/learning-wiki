# Simulation-based learning is associated with statistically significant improvements in self-efficacy, engagement (vigor, absorption, total), and empathy (compassionate care, total) among undergraduate physiotherapy students, with small effect sizes

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among 409 physiotherapy students, pre-post comparisons showed significant improvements in EAPESA self-efficacy (p < 0.001), UWES-S-17 vigor (p = 0.008), absorption (p = 0.007), total engagement (p = 0.023), JSE compassionate care (p < 0.001), and JSE total (p = 0.007), all with small effect sizes. ## Evidence

### Ochandorena-Acha Mirari 2026

Ochandorena-Acha Mirari, Jolis-Prat Ramon, Escribà-Salvans Anna, Oliveira Vinicius Rosa, Rierola-Fochs Sandra, Parés-Martínez Carles, Terradas-Monllor Marc. (2026). The changes in perceived self-efficacy, academic engagement, and empathy following simulation-based learning among undergraduate physiotherapy students: a quasi-experimental study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09389-z

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental pre-post comparison of 409 physiotherapy students using Mann–Whitney U tests on anonymized pre- and post-SBL questionnaires. The article reports "effect sizes (r) were small" without printing individual r values in the text, so no effect size magnitude is stated.

> "A comparison of pre- and post-simulation results revealed significant differences in the EAPESA Total Score (p < 0.001), UWES-17 Vigor (p = 0.008), UWES-17 Absorption (p = 0.007), UWES-17 Total Score (p = 0.023), JSE Compassionate Care (p < 0.001), and JSE Total Score (p = 0.007). However, effect sizes (r) were small."
