# Memory deficits associated with childhood trauma in healthy adults are not secondary to psychiatric or medical illness

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q1` argument or single case

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The cognitive deficits linked to childhood trauma were found in adults free of current medical or psychiatric illness, suggesting they are not secondary to such conditions. [→ Majer M 2010](#majer-m-2010)

## Evidence

### Majer M 2010

Majer M, Nater UM, Lin J-MS, Capuron L, Reeves WC. (2010). Association of childhood trauma with cognitive function in healthy adults: a pilot study. BMC Neurology. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2377-10-61

`q1 · i?`

The authors' interpretation of their pilot study findings, noting that "cognitive deficits were found in adult survivors of childhood trauma who did not suffer a current medical or psychiatric illness".

> "In our study, however, cognitive deficits were found in adult survivors of childhood trauma who did not suffer a current medical or psychiatric illness (including major depressive disorder) and did not have a history of alcohol or substance abuse."
