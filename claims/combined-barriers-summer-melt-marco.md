# Even students with stellar qualifications and strong plans can fail to enroll after career uncertainty, incomplete financial aid paperwork, and no extended adult conversation about college combine to make choice feel too risky

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Marco, top-five in his class with a 4.05 weighted GPA and a 25 on the ACT, declined a full-tuition scholarship at IIT, enrolled instead at a community college, and then stopped attending because it "didn't feel like college." [→ Melissa Roderick 2008](#melissa-roderick-2008)

## Evidence

### Melissa Roderick 2008

Melissa Roderick, Jenny Nagaoka, Vanessa Coca, Eliza Moeller, with Karen Roddie, Jamiliyah Gilliam, and Desmond Patton. (2008). From High School to the Future: Potholes on the Road to College (Case Studies). Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/high-school-future-potholes-road-college

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative case study of Marco, a first-generation college-goer whose college choice was linked to career interest; when uncertain about engineering, the choice no longer made sense. He had not completed his FAFSA and never had an extended conversation with any adult about his college plans.

> "For Marco, facing these three obstacles in combination left him unable to make any choice at all."
