Evidence¶

Reet Kasepalu 2023¶

Reet Kasepalu, Pankaj Chejara, Luis P. Prieto, Tobias Ley. (2023). Studying teacher withitness in the wild: comparing a mirroring and an alerting & guiding dashboard for collaborative learning. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn (2023) 18:575–606. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-023-09414-z

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Observation-based coding of interventions in the authentic-classroom quasi-experiment: "There was a difference that proved non-significant under a Chi-squared test (5.62, p = 0.06)" between 45.8% intervention in no dashboard and 73.7% in mirroring-only conditions. No effect size printed.

"Overall, the mirroring only condition activated the teachers into intervening more (the participating teachers carried out an intervention in the classroom in 45.8% (11 teachers out of 24) of the cases in the no dashboard condition to 73.7% (14 out of 19) in the mirroring only condition). There was a difference that proved non-significant under a Chi-squared test (5.62, p = 0.06)."

Reet Kasepalu 2023 (2)¶

Reet Kasepalu, Pankaj Chejara, Luis P. Prieto, Tobias Ley. (2023). Studying teacher withitness in the wild: comparing a mirroring and an alerting & guiding dashboard for collaborative learning. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn (2023) 18:575–606. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-023-09414-z

q2 · i? · causal · r1

In the same coded intervention data, "only 33.3% of the participating teachers started an intervention during the CL activity, which means that we did not prove the third hypothesis"; the authors note three of those interventions were coregulation strategies asking about process or prompting group members.

"In the alerting & guiding dashboard condition, however, only 33.3% of the participating teachers started an intervention during the CL activity, which means that we did not prove the third hypothesis."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Guiding support stimulated targeted interactions: teachers posted more messages to groups (14 vs 4) and performed more dashboard interventions than under mirroring — reports the opposite
  • Teacher-facing dashboards that mirror or guide increase teacher awareness and targeted interventions during classroom orchestration — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Guiding dashboard support enabled teachers to perform more orchestration actions, more targeted interactions, and more class announcements than mirroring support — reports the opposite
  • A mirroring dashboard shifts teacher attention toward epistemic over social aspects of learning, while a guiding dashboard divides interventions more equally — related
  • Mirroring dashboards improved teacher situational awareness while alerting-and-guiding dashboards reduced teacher workload and frustration — related
  • Real-time AI alerts can expand teachers' situational awareness but in some cases overload teachers' attention or draw focus away from their own observation flow — related
  • Reported cognitive load was lowest in the mirroring condition (5.4), compared with no dashboard (5.6) and guiding (6.2) — related
  • Explicit group regulation in online PBL was infrequent, emerged in response to challenges, and often involved teacher support — related
  • Teachers reported the highest confidence in their situational awareness in the alerting & guiding dashboard condition — related
  • With the alerting & guiding dashboard, teachers' workload dropped most on the performance and frustration subscales, while cognitive load, temporal demand and effort differences were non-significant — related
  • The alerting & guiding dashboard reduced teachers' overall workload more than the mirroring dashboard in authentic settings (H2 supported) — related
