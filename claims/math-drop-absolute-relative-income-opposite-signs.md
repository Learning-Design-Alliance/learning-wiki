## Evidence

### Wodarz M.N. 2025

Wodarz M.N., Komarova N.L., Ma T. (2025). The functional form of the association between K-12 student performance and household income in U.S. school districts. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0329296

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear regression of the absolute math score drop on district income among districts with ΔiM<0 (Results, Fig 8(d)). The article reports a positive association with p<10−6; only a p-value is printed, no effect size, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect). The authors suggest it may reflect higher starting scores or private-school selection.

> "Performing linear regression, we found that |ΔiM| correlates positively with income (p<10−6), see Fig 8(d). In other words, the drop in Math performance is somewhat deeper for wealthier districts."

### Wodarz M.N. 2025 (2)

Wodarz M.N., Komarova N.L., Ma T. (2025). The functional form of the association between K-12 student performance and household income in U.S. school districts. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0329296

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear regression of the relative math score drop on district income (Results, Fig 8(e)). The article reports the trend reverses: the relative drop is negatively correlated with income (p<10−6), higher in poorer districts; only a p-value is printed, no effect size.

> "If, however, we consider the relative drop in performance score, |ΔiM|/SiM3, the trend reverses. We find that |ΔiM|/SiM3 is negatively correlated with income (p<10−6), see Fig 8(e). This means that the relative drop in mathematics performance is higher in poorer districts and lower in the wealthier districts."
