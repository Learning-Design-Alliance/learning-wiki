# Learning in multiple contexts and generalizing across them makes knowledge both specific and general, addressing why transfer is difficult

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Teaching in multiple contexts and generalizing across those contexts makes knowledge both specific and general, in contrast to abstract school teaching that fosters test-oriented storage strategies. [→ Collins 1988](#collins-1988)

## Evidence

### Collins 1988

Collins, Allan. (1988). Cognitive Apprenticeship and Instructional Technology. Technical Report. BBN Report No. 6899, BBN Labs, Inc. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED331465

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument in the situated learning section of this position paper. The authors argue that learning in multiple contexts lets students "begin to generalize on these ways," unlike abstract school teaching that leads to strategies such as storing information just long enough to retrieve it for a test. No empirical data are offered for this claim.

> "These may be the central issues as to why transfer of knowledge is so difficult: by learning in multiple contexts you learn different ways knowledge can be used, and begin to generalize on these ways."
