## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The article reports that interventions are likely to be effective when teachers are given opportunities to practice their teaching before implementing strategies in actual education, for example via microteaching or peer observations. [→ Douwes‐van Ark 2026](#douwesvan-ark-2026)

## Evidence

### Douwes‐van Ark 2026

Douwes‐van Ark, I. M. E., Fokkens‐Bruinsma, M., Deinum, J. F., & Korpershoek, H. (2026). University teachers' self‐efficacy beliefs. The British Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.70088

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The article's discussion attributes this to prior work (Bray‐Clark & Bates, 2003; Morris & Usher, 2011; Arsal, 2014); it is a narrative recommendation citing earlier studies, not a new experimental result of this study.

> "Interventions are likely to be effective when designed in such a way that teachers are given opportunities to practice their teaching before implementing these strategies in their actual education (Bray‐Clark & Bates, 2003; Morris & Usher, 2011). This could be done using microteaching, where teachers give a lesson to a small group of peers and receive feedback after being observed (Arsal, 2014)."
