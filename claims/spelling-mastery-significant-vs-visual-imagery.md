# Spelling Mastery produced statistically significant positive effects on all three writing measures in a fourth-grade RCT against Visual Imagery instruction

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In Darch and Simpson (1990), 28 fourth-grade students with learning disabilities randomly assigned to Spelling Mastery significantly outperformed the Visual Imagery comparison group on an author-created spelling test (effect size 1.42) and on the TWS Predictable and Unpredictable Words subtests (effect sizes 1.20 and 1.18). ## Evidence

### darch-simpson-1990

U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, What Works Clearinghouse. (2014, January). Students with Learning Disabilities intervention report: Spelling Mastery. Retrieved from https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/579

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial of 28 fourth-grade students with learning disabilities at a university-based summer program; students were randomly assigned to Spelling Mastery or Visual Imagery for 25–30 minutes daily over 25 days. Appendix C prints effect sizes of 1.42 (author-created spelling test), 1.20 (TWS Predictable Words), and 1.18 (TWS Unpredictable Words), each with p < 0.01, and a domain average of 1.27 (+40 improvement index).

> "Darch and Simpson (1990) found statistically significant positive effects of Spelling Mastery on three measures of writing, including the Predictable Words and Unpredictable Words subtests of the Test of Written Spelling and an author-created spelling test. WWC calculations confirm these findings."
