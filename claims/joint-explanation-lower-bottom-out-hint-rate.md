# Dyads instructed to jointly explain requested bottom-out hints at a lower rate than individual self-explainers in the Andes physics tutor

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` In a between-condition experiment, dyads in the joint-explanation condition showed a lower rate of bottom-out hint requests (M = .05, SD = .07) than individuals in the self-explanation condition (M = .16, SD = .17), d = .93. [→ Robert G.M. Hausmann 2008](#robert-gm-hausmann-2008)

## Evidence

### Robert G.M. Hausmann 2008

Robert G.M. Hausmann, Brett van de Sande, Carla van de Sande, and Kurt VanLehn. (2008). Productive Dialog During Collaborative Problem Solving. https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.0599 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0806.0599](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0806.0599)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Controlled lab experiment with 39 undergraduates in second-semester physics randomly assigned to self-explanation (n = 11) or joint-explanation (n = 14) conditions; a one-way ANOVA on bottom-out hint rate found "F(1, 23) = 4.896, p < .037, d = .93".

> "The self-explanation condition (M = .16, SD = .17) demonstrate a higher rate of bottom-out hint requests than the students in the joint-explanation condition (M = .05, SD = .07)"
