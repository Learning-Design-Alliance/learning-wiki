# Freshman OnTrack remains a strong predictor of high school graduation even as on-track rates have risen

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across CPS cohorts from 2007-08 to 2014-15, on-track freshmen graduated at rates around 86-88 percent while off-track freshmen graduated at rates around 30-35 percent, a gap that held as overall on-track rates rose. [→ Seeskin 2020](#seeskin-2020)

## Evidence

### Seeskin 2020

Seeskin, A., Mahaffie, S., & Usher, A. (2020). The forgotten year: Applying lessons from Freshman success to Sophomore year. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/the-forgotten-year

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of CPS freshman cohorts (Figure 1) shows four-year graduation rates of on-track freshmen (85.5-88.0%) versus off-track freshmen (29.7-35.1%) stayed "virtually unchanged" as on-track rates improved, so "on-track status remains a strong indicator" of graduation.

> "although more students are now on-track, a student’s on-track status remains a strong indicator of whether they will graduate from high school."
