## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The authors interpret the constrained genetic variance of middle-HES women as reflecting an outsized role of shared family rearing environment in whether these women pursued more education. [→ Arpawong 2023](#arpawong-2023)

## Evidence

### Arpawong 2023

Arpawong, Thalida Em; Gatz, Margaret; Zavala, Catalina; Gruenewald, Tara L.; Walters, Ellen E.; Prescott, Carol A. (2023). Nature, Nurture, and the Meaning of Educational Attainment: Differences by Sex and Socioeconomic Status. Twin Research and Human Genetics. https://doi.org/10.1017/thg.2023.6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion of the 15-group model estimates, in which women's middle-stratum shared environmental variance was 35.5% of total variance (Table 2) and MZ and DZ women's twin correlations were similar and large. "Family rearing environment ... may take on an outsized role in contributing to EA."

> "Unexpectedly, for women from the middle economic stratum, it appears that family rearing environment, which may in part reflect parents’ own genetic endowments, may take on an outsized role in contributing to EA."
