# Students overgeneralize classical optics of light beams to single photons, treating a single photon incident on a polarizer as partly transmitted and partly absorbed

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Some students incorrectly extrapolate the classical intensity behavior of a light beam through a polarizer to the single-photon case, failing to treat single-photon transmission probabilistically. [→ DeVore 2016](#devore-2016)

## Evidence

### DeVore 2016

DeVore, S. and Singh, C. (2016). An Interactive Learning Tutorial on Quantum Key Distribution. Originally published 2013. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1601.01301](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1601.01301)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Qualitative finding from semi-structured think-aloud interviews conducted during QuILT development with undergraduate and graduate physics students. Interviews suggest students mixed quantum mechanical and classical ideas, interpreting cosine-squared as a fraction of a photon transmitted rather than a probability.

> "They knew from classical optics that the intensity of light generally decreases after passing through a polarizer and is only completely absorbed if the polarization of incident light is orthogonal to the polarization axis of the polarizer and extrapolated this, incorrectly, to the single photon case"
