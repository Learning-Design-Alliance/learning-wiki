# Job relevance of the academic performance measure moderates validity: behavioral coursework (.36) predicts job performance better than general coursework (.13)

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Corrected validities increased from general (.13) to combined (.22) to major-specific (.25) to behavioral coursework (.36) measures, and a WLS regression found a positive job-relevance–validity relationship (β = .56, p < .001). [→ Van Iddekinge 2024](#van-iddekinge-2024)

## Evidence

### Van Iddekinge 2024

Van Iddekinge, C. H., Arnold, J. D., Krivacek, S. J., Frieder, R. E., & Roth, P. L. (2024). Making the Grade? A Meta-Analysis of Academic Performance as a Predictor of Work Performance and Turnover. Journal of Applied Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0001212

`q2 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Moderator analysis within the meta-analysis comparing four categories of academic performance measures coded for job relevance. The article reports the relationship "increases as the job relevance of the AP measures increases," and the WLS regression (k = 92) yielded β = .56, p < .001.

> "Results revealed corrected rs of .13 (CI [.10, .17]), .22 (CI [.19, .25]), .25 (CI [.21, .29]), and .36 (CI [.30, .41]), respectively, for general, combined, major-specific, and behavioral coursework measures."
