# Mandated rigorous-curriculum policies in CPS sometimes had adverse consequences: the IDS initiative saw test scores decline and the college-prep-for-all policy increased course failure without achievement gains

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The CPS Instructional Development System initiative did not produce achievement benefits; test scores declined. [→ Sebring 2021](#sebring-2021)
`q2 i?` A district policy requiring college preparatory courses for all students made low-achieving students more likely to fail courses without achievement improvements, while high-achieving students became less likely to go to college. [→ Sebring 2021 (2)](#sebring-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Sebring 2021

Sebring, Sall, Kheraj, Davis, Allensworth, de la Torre, & Beechum. (2021). Research and Engagement Agenda, 2021–25. UChicago Consortium on School Research. https://www.5-essentials.org/cps/5e/2018/s/610268/essentials/families/#trends?c=district&o=-change

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The agenda reports, citing Sporte, Correa, Hart, & Wechsler (2009), that the 2006 IDS initiative, which included curricular strategies, materials, assessments, professional development, and coaching, "did not produce benefits for achievement; in fact, test scores declined".

> "Consortium research on this initiative indicated that even with all the effort that CPS put into it, the initiative did not produce benefits for achievement; in fact, test scores declined."

### Sebring 2021 (2)

Sebring, Sall, Kheraj, Davis, Allensworth, de la Torre, & Beechum. (2021). Research and Engagement Agenda, 2021–25. UChicago Consortium on School Research. https://www.5-essentials.org/cps/5e/2018/s/610268/essentials/families/#trends?c=district&o=-change

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The agenda reports, citing Montgomery, Allensworth, & Correa (2010), that under the college-prep-for-all policy "low-achieving students became more likely to fail courses" with no achievement improvement, while high achievers became less likely to attend college.

> "Consortium research on a district policy requiring college preparatory courses for all students found that low-achieving students became more likely to fail courses, without improvements in achievement, while high achieving students became less likely to go to college and experienced declining course rigor."
