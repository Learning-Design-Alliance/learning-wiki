## Evidence

### Albert Munoz 2026

Albert Munoz, Mercedez Hinchcliff, Cameron Langfield and Ann Rogerson. (2026). How strong is the evidence in generative AI-related academic misconduct allegations? A mixed-methods analysis. International Journal for Educational Integrity. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40979-026-00235-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Credential ratings across 1,855 coded items in 1,162 cases showed Turnitin/Similarity Reports at 100% Weak inferential force and AI-detection outputs at 92.5% Weak; the strongest types "do not depend on probabilistic classification of text characteristics".

> "Detection tool outputs, comprising text-similarity reports (e.g., Turnitin) and standalone AI-detection tools (e.g., GPTZero), were present but produced uniformly weak inferential force profiles, consistent with their documented limitations (e.g., Weber-Wulff et al. 2023)."

### Albert Munoz 2026 (2)

Albert Munoz, Mercedez Hinchcliff, Cameron Langfield and Ann Rogerson. (2026). How strong is the evidence in generative AI-related academic misconduct allegations? A mixed-methods analysis. International Journal for Educational Integrity. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40979-026-00235-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The credential analysis found interview-inconsistency items uniformly rated because each documents inability to explain one's own work, which is credible but "equally consistent with alternative explanations such as poor comprehension or test anxiety".

> "evidence related to interview inconsistencies exhibits near-uniform probative quality ratings (Moderate relevance, High credibility, Moderate inferential force) across 98.5% of items, reflecting the narrow epistemic range of this evidence type."
