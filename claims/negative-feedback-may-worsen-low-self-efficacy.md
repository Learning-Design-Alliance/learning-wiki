This is a theoretical argument in the Discussion, made citing self-efficacy research by Bandura and colleagues, not an empirical result of this article. The article proposes emphasizing relative improvement or offering training instead of repeated negative feedback. No effect size is printed.

> "In cases where self-efficacy for a given task is low, repeated negative feedback or peer comparison feedback showing diminishing performance relative to peers may worsen the recipient’s self-efficacy"
