# Academic burnout lowers intrinsic and extrinsic motivation and raises amotivation, but has no significant direct effect on academic success

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Direct paths from academic burnout to intrinsic motivation, extrinsic motivation and amotivation were significant (all p ≤ 0.001); a one-unit burnout increase was associated with decreases of 0.07 and 0.061 in intrinsic and extrinsic motivation and a 0.24 increase in amotivation. ## Evidence

### Torkani F 2025

Torkani F, Ahmadi S-H, Binazir A, Fath MM, Shafigh N, Asadiparvar-Masouleh H, Vasli P. (2025). The relationship between health-related quality of life and academic success in nursing students: the mediating role of academic burnout and academic motivation. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06661-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Direct-path coefficients (Table 4, paths b1–b4) from the path analysis of 261 nursing students. The article reports significant burnout-to-motivation paths (p ≤ 0.001) with the printed unstandardized values, and a non-significant burnout-to-GPA path (B = -0.01, p = 0.32).

> "The results showed that a one unit increase in AB was associated with a 0.07 and 0.061 decrease in intrinsic and extrinsic motivation, respectively, and a 0.24 increase in amotivation. In contrast, the direct effect of AB on AS was not statistically significant (b1, B = -0.01, p = 0.32)."
