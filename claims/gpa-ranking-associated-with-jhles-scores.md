Liu Yifan, Lyu Donghao, Xie Sujie, Yao Yuntao, Liu Jun, Lu Bingnan, Zhang Wei, Xian Shuyuan, Yan Jiale, Gong Meiqiong, Wu Xinru, Li Yuanan, Zhang Haoyu, Zhou Jiajie, Zhou Yibin, Lin Min, Yin Huabin, Wang Xiaonan, Wang Yue, Chen Wenfang, Zhang Chongyou, Du Erbin, Lin Qing, Huang Zongqiang, Xu Dayuan, Zhang Jie, Huang Runzhi, Ji Shizhao, Pan Xiuwu. (2025). The impact of grade point average on medical students' perception of the learning environment: a multicenter cross-sectional study across 12 Chinese medical schools. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06977-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Cross-sectional survey of 10,901 students at 12 Chinese medical schools; Pearson's Chi-square test of GPA ranking groups against JHLES low/high categories split at the median (104 points). The bottom GPA group showed "high JHLES scores" in 39% versus 69% in the top 5% group (p < 0.001).

> "individuals with a GPA ranking falling within the top 80–100% of their peers are less likely to exhibit high JHLES scores than those with a higher GPA ranking (39%). Conversely, participants with GPAs ranked in the top 5% group had significantly higher JHLES scores (69%) compared to those with lower GPA ranking (p < 0.001)."

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Welch's ANOVA across the five GPA ranking groups in the same multicenter cross-sectional survey; the article reports a significant trend (p < 0.001) and describes a potential linear relationship, with no effect size printed.

> "Figure 2B displayed the results of Welch's ANOVA, showing that as GPA rankings increased in different groups, the JHLES scores tended to increase. The outcomes were significant (p < 0.001) and the potential linear relationship was shown."
