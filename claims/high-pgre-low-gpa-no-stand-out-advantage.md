## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A high physics GRE score does not raise admission rates for low-GPA applicants above the rate at which high-GPA applicants with low physics GRE scores are admitted. [→ Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero 2020](#nicholas-t-young-and-marcos-d-caballero-2020)

## Evidence

### Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero 2020

Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero. (2020). The Physics GRE does not help applicants “stand out”. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2008.10712](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2008.10712)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Probability-of-admission analysis of 2537 domestic applicants to five physics departments (2017–2018), binning GPA (0.1 width) and physics GRE (50-point width) and computing admission fractions per bin. The study found "applicants with high physics GRE scores and low GPAs are admitted at nearly the same rate" as the reverse combination.

> "Comparing the fraction of admitted applicants in each bin, applicants with high physics GRE scores and low GPAs are admitted at nearly the same rate as applicants with high GPA and low physics GRE scores."
