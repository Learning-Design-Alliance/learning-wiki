# UCSMP Algebra shows mixed effects on the algebra domain: one study finds a statistically significant positive effect and one finds an indeterminate effect

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Thompson et al. (2006) found a positive and statistically significant difference between UCSMP Algebra and comparison groups in the algebra domain, confirmed by the WWC after correction for clustering. [→ University of Chicago School Mathematics Project 2016](#university-of-chicago-school-mathematics-project-2016)
`q2 i?` Peters (1992) found no statistically significant differences between UCSMP Algebra and comparison groups in the algebra domain; the WWC characterizes this as an indeterminate effect. [→ University of Chicago School Mathematics Project 2016 (2)](#university-of-chicago-school-mathematics-project-2016-2)

## Evidence

### University of Chicago School Mathematics Project 2016

University of Chicago School Mathematics Project. (2016). University of Chicago School Mathematics Project (UCSMP) — WWC Intervention Report, Secondary Mathematics. What Works Clearinghouse. https://ucsmp.uchicago.edu

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

In the matched-pairs study of 189 students in three high schools, the WWC confirmed a statistically significant positive effect in the algebra domain, pooling the odd and even forms of the UCSMP Problem-Solving and Understanding Test "after correction for clustering". No effect size is printed for this domain finding.

> "Thompson et al. (2006) found a positive and statistically significant difference between the UCSMP Algebra and comparison groups on the odd and even forms of the UCSMP Problem-Solving and Understanding Test, analyzed separately."

### University of Chicago School Mathematics Project 2016 (2)

University of Chicago School Mathematics Project. (2016). University of Chicago School Mathematics Project (UCSMP) — WWC Intervention Report, Secondary Mathematics. What Works Clearinghouse. https://ucsmp.uchicago.edu

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Randomized trial with compromised random assignment (36 math-talented eighth graders, 17 UCSMP vs 19 comparison using Saxon Algebra I); the WWC confirmed, "using a difference-in-differences approach", no statistically significant difference on the Orleans-Hanna Algebra Prognosis Test. Equivalence was not tested beyond baseline checks.

> "Peters (1992) found, and the WWC confirmed (using a difference-in-differences approach), no statistically significant differences between the UCSMP Algebra and comparison groups in the algebra domain."
