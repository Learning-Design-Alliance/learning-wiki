Baghcheghi Nayereh; Koohestani Hamid Reza. (2026). A scoping review of internet addiction and academic performance among health sciences students. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-04883-x

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Javaeed (2020), a cross-sectional study of 316 Pakistani medical students in which 28.2% had severe IA and "performed significantly worse in their examinations"; Afrin, Elbilgahy, and Poormirzaei similarly reported negative associations.

> "Javaeed reported that 28.2% of Pakistani medical students had severe IA, and these students performed significantly worse in their examinations"

### Baghcheghi Nayereh; Koohestani Hamid Reza 2026 (2)

Baghcheghi Nayereh; Koohestani Hamid Reza. (2026). A scoping review of internet addiction and academic performance among health sciences students. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-04883-x

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports that Díaz Cárdenas (Colombian dental students), Fidanci (Turkish medical students), Sayed (Egyptian pharmacy students, 38.5% potential IA), and Bahrami (Iranian health sciences students) each "failed to find a direct significant association between IA scores and GPA."

> "In contrast, several other studies failed to find a direct significant association between IA scores and GPA"
