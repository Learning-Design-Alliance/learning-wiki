# Both treatment groups significantly improved climate explanatory knowledge, with no reliable condition or teacher-expertise differences

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Both groups improved climate explanatory knowledge from pretest to posttest with no Group effect. [→ Jacobson 2020](#jacobson-2020)
`q3 i?` A significant Group × Teacher Content Expertise interaction on climate explanatory knowledge was judged spurious because its pairwise comparisons contradicted each other and none differed from the overall average. [→ Jacobson 2020 (2)](#jacobson-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Jacobson 2020

Jacobson, Michael, Goldwater, Micah, Markauskaite, Lina, Lai, Polly, Kapur, Manu, Roberts, Gareth, & Hilton, Courtney. (2020). Schema abstraction with productive failure and analogical comparison: Learning designs for far across domain transfer. Learning and Instruction, 65, Article number: 101222. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2019.101222

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Separate linear mixed model for the five explanatory climate items in the same quasi-experimental study. Both conditions improved; the article reports "no effect of Group" on explanatory climate knowledge.

> "there was an effect for Time, F(1,92) = 11.363, p = 0.001, η2 = .11, with both conditions improving in their ability to explain climate knowledge, as evidenced by higher performance on the posttest (M = 1.056, SD = .3371) compared to the pretest (M = 0.896, SD = .3795). There was no effect of Group, F(1,92) = 0.085, p = 0.735, η 2 = .001"

### Jacobson 2020 (2)

Jacobson, Michael, Goldwater, Micah, Markauskaite, Lina, Lai, Polly, Kapur, Manu, Roberts, Gareth, & Hilton, Courtney. (2020). Schema abstraction with productive failure and analogical comparison: Learning designs for far across domain transfer. Learning and Instruction, 65, Article number: 101222. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2019.101222

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The Group × Teacher Content Expertise interaction on explanatory climate knowledge was significant in the mixed model, but Bonferroni-corrected pairwise comparisons were non-significant and contradictory, so the authors concluded the interaction "was spurious".

> "the two-way interaction between Group and Teacher Content Expertise was significant F(1,92) = 4.242, p = 0.042, η2 = .04."
