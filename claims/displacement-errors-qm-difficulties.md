# Some QM difficulties reflect operating in the wrong frame (displacement errors)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When students reason in a frame other than the one the problem promotes, the resulting difficulty is a displacement error; five difficulties in frame-shifting problems and one in a non-shifting problem showed only displacement errors. [→ Bahar Modir 2019](#bahar-modir-2019)
`q2 i?` About 25% of students (N=223) on a final exam treated a superposition state with a single time-dependent phase, coded as a displacement error. [→ Bahar Modir 2019 (2)](#bahar-modir-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Bahar Modir 2019

Bahar Modir, John D. Thompson, and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2019). Framing difficulties in quantum mechanics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020146](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020146)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Definitional statement from the displacement error section, where the authors tabulate difficulties requiring frame shifting (Table II) and simpler problems without shifting (Table III). Students recalling what particle-in-a-box solutions look like illustrate the algorithmic physics frame displacing the intended conceptual physics frame.

> "Displacement errors arise when students are meant to be in one frame, but instead operate in another."

### Bahar Modir 2019 (2)

Bahar Modir, John D. Thompson, and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2019). Framing difficulties in quantum mechanics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020146](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020146)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Final-exam data from Emigh et al. on time dependence of the probability of a position-measurement outcome; students given the time-dependent wave function considered just a single phase wave function for a superposition state, a tendency the original authors described as very common. No effect size is printed.

> "About 25% of their students (N = 223) on a ﬁnal exam showed the same diﬃculty on a version of the same task."
