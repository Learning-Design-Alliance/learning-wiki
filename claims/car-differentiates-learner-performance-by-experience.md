## Evidence

### Cary Tawnya L. 2026

Cary Tawnya L., Hong Seung M., Kowalkowski Anna, Harris Michelle A. (2026). The Conclusion Assessment Rubric (CAR): A Tool to Evaluate Students' Ability to Make Context-Driven Claims with Appropriate Scope from Experimental Biological Evidence. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.25-11-0255

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Cross-sectional field study comparing scientific reports of first-semester ecology students (n = 10) with third-semester physiology students (n = 7) in the same four-semester biology curriculum. Papers were scored by three raters using the CAR. The article reports a statistically significant difference in total CAR scores between the two groups, indicating the CAR "is able to differentiate performance in generating conclusions based on learner experience."

> "Papers written in a capstone process-of-science lab earned significantly higher total CAR scores (12.9 ± 2.7) compared with papers written in the first lab of an integrative four-semester curriculum (9.6 ± 2.5; t15 = 2.53, p = 0.023; Figure 3)."
