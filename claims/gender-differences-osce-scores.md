## Subclaims
`q3 i?` At time point 2, female students achieved significantly higher practical clinical skills scores than male students (p = <.001 and p = .015 for raters 1 and 2), despite no significant difference in exam preparation time. [→ Hoefer 2017](#hoefer-2017)
`q3 i?` Female students also achieved significantly better Global Rating Scale ratings than male counterparts (rater 1 p = .034, rater 2 p = .002), regardless of group assignment. [→ Hoefer 2017 (2)](#hoefer-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Hoefer 2017

Hoefer, S. H., Sterz, J., Bender, B., Stefanescu, M.-C., Theis, M., Walcher, F., Sader, R., Ruesseler, M. (2017). Conveying practical clinical skills with the help of teaching associates—a randomised trial with focus on the long term learning retention. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0892-5

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Secondary analysis of the randomized trial's OSCE checklist scores by gender at the 4-month summative OSCE; the article reports significance values without an effect size for this contrast.

> "However, at time point 2 the female students achieved significant higher results (p = <.001 and p = .015 for raters 1 and 2, respectively)."

### Hoefer 2017 (2)

Hoefer, S. H., Sterz, J., Bender, B., Stefanescu, M.-C., Theis, M., Walcher, F., Sader, R., Ruesseler, M. (2017). Conveying practical clinical skills with the help of teaching associates—a randomised trial with focus on the long term learning retention. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0892-5

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of Global Rating Scale communication and interaction items via multiple linear regression controlling for group; mean sumscores and gender differences are shown in Table 1, with no effect size printed.

> "However, the female students achieved significantly (rater 1 p = .034, rater 2 p = .002) better ratings than their male counterparts (Table 1)."
