# Story length correlates with National Curriculum attainment level in children's Key Stage 2 narrative writing, with roughly 300 words needed for Level 4 and 400 or more for Level 5

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a formative analysis of 12 examiner-marked SATs stories, longer stories achieved higher National Curriculum levels, with approximately 300 words required for Level 4 and approximately 400 words or more likely to achieve Level 5. [→ C.E. Holdich 2002](#ce-holdich-2002)

## Evidence

### C.E. Holdich 2002

C.E. Holdich, R.G. Holdich and P.W.H. Chung. (2002). Assessing aspects of children's written grammar: automating the process. Computers and Education, Vol 39, pp37-50. http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-nd/2.5/

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Computer text analysis of 12 Year 6 SATs stories (levels 2c to 5+) compared word counts to examiner-assigned levels; totals ranged from 228 to 829 words. The authors call this 'the least satisfactory of the ten indicators, but the trend is obvious'.

> "the length of a story correlated successfully with the level of attainment achieved: the longer the story, the higher the level achieved. It would appear that a minimum of approximately 300 words is required to explain a story in sufficient detail in order to gain Level 4, whilst the ability to write approximately 400 words, or more, is likely to achieve Level 5."
