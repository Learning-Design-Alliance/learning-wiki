# A bifactor model of oral language and reading fits better than one-, three-, and four-factor models in every grade from 4th through 10th

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across 4th–10th grades, the unidimensional model fit worst and the bifactor model fit best, with AIC/BIC differences favoring the bifactor model in all grades except 10th. [→ Foorman Barbara R. 2015](#foorman-barbara-r-2015)

## Evidence

### Foorman Barbara R. 2015

Foorman Barbara R., Koon Sharon, Petscher Yaacov, Mitchell Alison, Truckenmiller Adrea. (2015). Examining General and Specific Factors in the Dimensionality of Oral Language and Reading in 4th–10th Grades. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000026

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Confirmatory factor analyses by grade in a cross-sectional sample of 1,792 students in 18 schools compared one-, three-, four-, and bifactor models; the article reports the "unrestricted bifactor model provided the best fit to the data" in every grade, with criterion fit for fourth grade of CFI = .99, TLI = .97, RMSEA = .05.

> "In fourth through 10th grades, model results followed the same pattern in that the unidimensional model consistently provided the worst relative fit to the data, and the unrestricted bifactor model provided the best fit to the data."
