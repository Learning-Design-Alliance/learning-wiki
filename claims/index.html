    In Review¶

    • Autonomy support increases intrinsic motivation, engagement, and persistence in learning.
    • Chunking reduces working memory load by grouping information into fewer, more meaningful units.
    • Contingent scaffolding improves learning more than fixed or absent support.
    • Erroneous examples improve conceptual understanding by forcing comparison with correct models.
    • Example-problem sequences reduce cognitive load and improve learning outcomes.
    • Example–problem sequences reduce cognitive load and improve learning outcomes
    • Fading support promotes the transfer of responsibility from instructor to learner.
    • High-confidence errors lead to better retention after correction than low-confidence errors.
    • Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows.
    • Learners invest more effort in tasks they perceive as interesting, personally important, or useful to their future goals.
    • Learners' task-specific self-efficacy beliefs predict whether they initiate, persist through, and recover from difficulty on academic tasks.
    • Pairing worked examples with practice or fading supports transfer better than examples alone.
    • Part-task practice reduces cognitive load for absolute novices during initial skill acquisition.
    • Process goals lead to better skill acquisition for novices than outcome goals.
    • Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding and problem-solving performance.
    • Self-monitoring improves self-regulation and supports better learning decisions.
    • Specific, difficult goals lead to higher performance than easy or vague \"do your best\" goals.
    • Whole-task performance improves transfer of complex skills to real-world settings.
    • Worked examples can become redundant or counterproductive for advanced learners.
    • Worked examples improve mathematics performance, especially for novices.
    • Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices.
    • Worked-example guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.
