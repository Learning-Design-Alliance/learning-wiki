# Revised visuals reduce cognitive processing of math-relevant visuals for low prior knowledge students but increase it for middle prior knowledge students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Total fixation duration on math-relevant visuals showed a condition by prior knowledge interaction, F(2, 51) = 6.57, p = .002, with revisions reducing processing for low prior knowledge students and increasing it for middle prior knowledge students. [→ Clinton 2017](#clinton-2017)

## Evidence

### Clinton 2017

Clinton, V., Cooper, J.L., Michaelis, J., Alibali, M.W., & Nathan, M.J. (2017). How Revisions to Mathematical Visuals Affect Cognition: Evidence from Eye Tracking. In Eye-tracking technology applications in educational research (pp. 195-218). IGI Global. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED574984

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Eye-tracking experiment measuring total fixation duration on math-relevant visuals. The article states "revisions appeared to reduce cognitive processing for students in the low prior knowledge group, but increased cognitive processing for students in the middle prior knowledge group" (F(1, 15) = 9.74, p = .003).

> "There was an interaction between condition and prior knowledge group for total fixation duration on the math-relevant visuals, F(2, 51) = 6.57, p = .002 (see Figure 7). Participants in the low prior knowledge group had longer total fixation duration on the math-relevant visuals in the original condition than in the revised condition, F(1, 21) = 6.43, p = .01."
