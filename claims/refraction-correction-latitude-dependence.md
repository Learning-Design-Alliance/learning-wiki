## Evidence

### P.K.Aravind 2005

P.K.Aravind. (2005). Sunsets, tall buildings, and the Earth’s radius. http://units.aps.org/units/fed/newsletters/summer2005/wyp.html

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical correction analysis (Appendix 2) assuming constant horizontal refraction α0 (about 39' for Earth) and α0 and h/R small and comparable. At latitude 40° the corrections are under 1%; at latitude 60° they reach "about 13% of the main term."

> "However if the experiment is carried out at 60λ = ∘ and 23δ = ∘  (with the same values of 0α and h as before), the correction terms add up to about 13% of the main term and the neglect of refraction would lead to a serious error;"

### P.K.Aravind 2005 (2)

P.K.Aravind. (2005). Sunsets, tall buildings, and the Earth’s radius. http://units.aps.org/units/fed/newsletters/summer2005/wyp.html

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

From the corrected formula, a plot versus h yields a straight line whose slope gives R and whose vertical intercept determines α0; sea-level sunset time remains unnecessary. The article calls this experiment "more challenging" and notes it requires a location further north or south.

> "An experiment that confirms the straight line plot predicted by Eq.(2) and uses it to infer the values of R  and 0α  would be more challenging than a determination of R based on Eq.(1)."
