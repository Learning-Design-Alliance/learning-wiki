# Tablet-based training reaches peak accuracy faster in training time than real-world practice, with similar repetitions-to-plateau but more total repetitions

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` LOESS analysis of training-period accuracy showed plateaus after ~6 repetitions in both interactive conditions, but peak performance was reached at 19.2 minutes in tablet training versus 26.03 minutes in real-world practice, and tablet training produced more total repetitions in 30 minutes. ## Evidence

### Kryklywy James H. 2021

Kryklywy James H., Roach Victoria A., Todd Rebecca M. (2021). Assessing the efficacy of tablet-based simulations for learning pseudo-surgical instrumentation. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0245330

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Non-parametric LOESS curve fitting with bootstrapped CIs on training-period accuracy from 44 subjects, binned by repetition and 3-minute time bins; plateau-by-repetition was nearly identical (6.06 vs 6.16 repetitions) and more total repetitions occurred in tablet training (t(39) = 6.017).

> "During tablet-based training, peak performance was obtained at 19.2 minutes (Accuracy = 97.2%; CI 95% = 15.03–23.63 minutes; Fig 3C). In contrast, a later performance plateau was observed during real-world practice, with a performance plateau observed after 26.03 minutes (Accuracy = 87.4%; CI 95% = 23.73–28.62 minutes; Fig 3D)."
