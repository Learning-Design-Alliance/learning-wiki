# Second-grade children self-derive new factual knowledge through integration of separate yet related learning episodes in classroom settings

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Second-grade children (7-9 years) successfully self-derived new factual knowledge through integration of separately taught stem facts in classroom sessions, performing significantly above chance in both open-ended and forced-choice formats. [→ Bauer 2020](#bauer-2020)
`q3 i?` Self-derivation through memory integration replicates in a second experiment with science-curriculum stimuli, with forced-choice integration accuracy of .75 and stem-fact accuracy of .69, both significantly above chance. [→ Bauer 2020 (2)](#bauer-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Bauer 2020

Bauer, P. J., Esposito, A. G., & Daly, J. J. (2020). Self-derivation through memory integration: A model for accumulation of semantic knowledge. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2019.101271

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Classroom experiment with 96 second-graders tested for self-derivation after hearing pairs of related story passages. In forced-choice testing, "children selected the correct answers from among distracters on a mean of 3.27 trials (SD = 0.95; max = 4)" and accuracy was significantly above chance (t(95)s > 20.21, ps < .001).

> "In forced-choice testing of integration facts, children selected the correct answers from among distracters on a mean of 3.27 trials (SD = 0.95; max = 4)"

### Bauer 2020 (2)

Bauer, P. J., Esposito, A. G., & Daly, J. J. (2020). Self-derivation through memory integration: A model for accumulation of semantic knowledge. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2019.101271

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Classroom experiment with 103 second-graders using science-curriculum stimuli (matter, sound, space, life cycles). "For both integration facts (.75 correct) and stem facts (.69 correct), forced-choice performance was significantly above chance (.33)" with t(99)s > 15.61, ps < .001.

> "For both integration facts (.75 correct) and stem facts (.69 correct), forced-choice performance was significantly above chance (.33) with a mean difference > .36, 95% CI[.31 to .47], t(99)s > 15.61, ps < .001."
