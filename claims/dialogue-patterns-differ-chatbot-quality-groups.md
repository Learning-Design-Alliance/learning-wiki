Analyzing Middle School Students' Dialogue and Behaviors during Collaborative AI Chatbot Development Using Ordered Network Analysis. arXiv preprint arXiv:2607.21603. `q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Ordered Network Analysis compared dialogue-act networks of 47 middle school pairs split at the median chatbot quality score; the Mann–Whitney U test revealed "significant differences in the dialogue structure between the groups".

> "A Mann–Whitney U test comparing the two ordered network models reveals significant differences in the dialogue structure between the groups (p <0.001)."

### Shan Zhang 2026 (2)

Shan Zhang, Andres Felipe Zambrano, Xiaoyi Tian, Yukyeong Song, Anthony F. Botelho, Kristy Elizabeth Boyer, Maya Israel, and Shiyan Jiang. (2026). Analyzing Middle School Students' Dialogue and Behaviors during Collaborative AI Chatbot Development Using Ordered Network Analysis. arXiv preprint arXiv:2607.21603. [doi:10.1007/978-3-032-29763-1_23](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-29763-1_23)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the same ONA group comparison, high-scoring pairs showed higher self-transition weights, e.g., "explanation(CW = 0.345 vs. 0.282)" and "directive(CW = 0.257 vs. 0.201)", plus stronger ordered transitions among these acts.

> "Groups with high chatbot scores exhibit higher connection weights forexplanation(CW = 0.345 vs. 0.282),directive(CW = 0.257 vs. 0.201),repeat(CW = 0.119 vs. 0.100), andoff-task(CW = 0.249 vs. 0.225), along with stronger ordered transitions among these dialogue acts."
