# Performance-approach goals predict competitive conflict regulation with a more-competent disagreeing partner, but not an equally competent one (Study 1)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` In an online interaction with a fictitious disagreeing partner, performance-approach goals predicted competitive regulation only when the partner was presented as more competent (β = .49, p < .001, partial η² = .09), not when equally competent; the interaction effect was small (partial η² = .03). [→ Sommet 2015](#sommet-2015)

## Evidence

### Sommet 2015

Sommet, N., Darnon, C., & Butera, F. (2015). To Confirm or to Conform? Performance Goals as a Regulator of Conflict With More-Competent Others. Journal of Educational Psychology, 107(2), 580–598. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037240

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Internet study with 139 university students who believed they interacted with a disagreeing partner presented as a bachelor (same-competence) or PhD (superior-competence) student. Regression showed the predicted interaction, β = .19, F(1, 129) = 4.38, p < .04, partial η² = .03 (small effect), with the simple slope in the superior-competence condition at partial η² = .09.
