Evidence¶

Enrico Sella · Monica Bolognesi · Emma Bergamini · Lucia Mason · Francesca Pazzaglia 2023¶

Enrico Sella · Monica Bolognesi · Emma Bergamini · Lucia Mason · Francesca Pazzaglia. (2023). Psychological Benefits of Attending Forest School for Preschool Children: a Systematic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09750-4

q2 · i3 · review · r3

Review's account of Müller et al. (2017), which used the SSRS with parent and teacher forms; teacher ratings favored nature preschool on cooperation (ηp2 = 0.20) and self-control (ηp2 = 0.32), large printed effects.

"teachers’ ratings reported that children in nature preschool showed higher scores in the cooperation ( p = < 0.001, ηp 2 = 0.20) and self-control (p = < 0.001, ηp 2 = 0.32) domains."

q2 · i? · review · r3

Same study's parent-form SSRS results: no significant differences between parental ratings for nature versus indoor preschool children across the printed domains (the quote continues listing self-control, externalizing, and internalizing with null results).

"Although there were no significant differences between parental ratings of children attending nature and indoor preschools for the SSRS domains (assertiveness: p = 0.01, ηp 2 = 0.13; social responsibility: p = 0.03, ηp 2 = 0.11; cooperation: p = 0.06, ηp 2 = 0.08;"

Related Claims¶

  • Forest preschool attendance is associated with higher nature connectedness on some measures, with no significant changes in environmentally responsible behaviors — related
  • Evidence on cognitive function benefits of forest preschool is mixed: teacher-rated global cognition improved, but executive functions and working memory showed no significant differences — related
  • Forest preschool shows mixed subjective-outcome effects: improved sleep, resilience, and curiosity, but no changes in academic readiness or illness and injury incidence — related
  • Forest-school benefits are measured with heterogeneous methods, mostly questionnaires and tasks administered to children, with only two studies using objective measures — a broader claim this one bears on
