## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox 2012

Bethany R. Wilcox, Marcos D. Caballero, Rachel E. Pepper and Steven J. Pollock. (2012). Upper-division Student Understanding of Coulomb's Law: Difficulties with Continuous Charge Distributions. [doi:10.1063/1.4789741](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4789741)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Coding of 172 exam solutions against the ACER framework's Construction component; 154 contained Construction elements. Common dq errors included using total charge, integrating where charge density was zero, and omitting the cylindrical Jacobian.

> "nearly half of the students (41%, N=64) had difficulty expressing the differential charge element, some (13%, N=20) provided limits of integration that were inconsistent with the differential, and about half (47%, N=72) incorrectly expressed the difference vector."

### Bethany R. Wilcox 2012 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox, Marcos D. Caballero, Rachel E. Pepper and Steven J. Pollock. (2012). Upper-division Student Understanding of Coulomb's Law: Difficulties with Continuous Charge Distributions. [doi:10.1063/1.4789741](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4789741)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Five formal think-aloud interviews with students who had completed E&M 1. Four of five indicated the integral is a sum over the charged disk, yet only one produced the correct script-r expression, complicating the initial interpretation.

> "All five interview participants drew vectors that could represent the difference vector on their diagrams, and four of them explicitly identified these vectors as script-r. Yet only one of these students was able to produce the correct expression for script-r"
