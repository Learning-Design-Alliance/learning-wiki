A theoretical argument in this commentary, not an empirical test: the authors reason that sampling family dyads is more feasible than sampling classrooms, so within-family designs "can potentially afford greater statistical power" for testing (mis)match effects. No data are reported.

> "Within-family designs can potentially afford greater statistical power than conventional classroom- and school-based studies for testing the effects of different degrees of (mis)match, because collecting data from a large sample of family dyads with two siblings and a parent is more feasible than assessing the same number of classrooms"
