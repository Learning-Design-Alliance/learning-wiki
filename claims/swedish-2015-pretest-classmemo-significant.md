# In Swedish TIMSS 2015 with grade-6 pretest controls, a unit increase in ClassMemo was associated with 0.27 standard deviations improvement in relative math achievement, while ClassListen and ClassDaily effects were non-significant

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the difference-score analysis of Swedish TIMSS 2015, ClassMemo remained positive and statistically significant (0.27 ± 0.12 SD, p = .022), whereas ClassListen and ClassDaily were non-significant. [→ Kimmo Eriksson 2019](#kimmo-eriksson-2019)

## Evidence

### Kimmo Eriksson 2019

Kimmo Eriksson, Ola Helenius, Andreas Ryve. (2019). Using TIMSS items to evaluate the effectiveness of different instructional practices. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-018-9473-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Alternative analysis of Swedish TIMSS 2015 (n = 4090 students) using z-scored difference scores from grade 6 to grade 8 to address regression-to-the-mean concerns. The article reports the ClassMemo effect of "0.27 ± 0.12 standard deviations, p = .022"; ClassListen and ClassDaily came out non-significant (−0.08 ± 0.14 and −0.08 ± 0.10, p > .400).

> "the estimated effect of ClassMemo remained positive and statistically significant, such that a unit increase in this variable was associated with an improvement in relative math achievement of 0.27 ± 0.12 standard deviations, p = .022."
