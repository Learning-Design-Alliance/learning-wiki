Saqr 2021¶

Saqr, M., & López-Pernas, S. (2021). Modelling diffusion in computer-supported collaborative learning: a large scale learning analytics study. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-021-09356-4

q1 · i? · associational · r2

A single fictional network scenario with printed centrality values (Table 1): node G, whose reply "sparked a meaningful (promising) conversation", had the highest diffusion centrality and shared the highest coreness (k=3) with nodes H, I and J, while node A, whose six connections were mostly isolated (with just one connection each), had the third-highest diffusion centrality despite the highest degree.

"This example demonstrates how diffusion-based centrality measures were capable of accurately capturing the uptake and argumentation process compared to the traditional centrality measures"

Related Claims¶

  • In a correlation meta-analysis across 69 course offerings, diffusion-based centralities correlated with academic achievement with high combined coefficients and predictive intervals
  • Prior SNA studies of centrality-achievement links in CSCL show inconsistent, mostly small-sample results, with betweenness and closeness the most inconsistent measures
  • Diffusion-based centralities show moderate-to-strong statistically significant positive correlations with the frequency of information sharing and argumentation utterances in CSCL — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Diffusion-based centralities elucidate information exchange and uptake in large-scale collaboration, validated against achievement and qualitative analysis, according to the editors' account of Saqr and López-Pernas's study — a broader claim this one bears on
