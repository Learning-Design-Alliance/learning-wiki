# Interns hands-on trained on a scenario outperform other groups in simulated assessment of that scenario

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On simulation assessment of nasal packing for epistaxis, the epistaxis-trained group scored highest (p = 0.001); tracheostomy-trained and NG-tube-trained groups similarly outperformed others on their trained scenarios. [→ MK Goutham 2023](#mk-goutham-2023)

## Evidence

### MK Goutham 2023

MK Goutham, Saldanha Marina, Bhat Vadisha S., A Rajeshwary, Vincent Mark Jittu, Ravikumar Aishwarya. (2023). Simulation-based training in ear, nose and throat skills and emergencies. Brazilian Journal of Otorhinolaryngology. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bjorl.2022.01.001

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Objective simulation assessment on day 15 with checklist scoring by three assessors on mannequins. Group C had the highest nasal packing simulation scores (p = 0.001); Table 2 also shows simulation NG tube insertion significance for Groups B and C versus Group A (p < 0.001).

> "The participants who received hands-on simulation training in nasal packing outperformed others when it came to practical demonstration."

### MK Goutham 2023 (2)

MK Goutham, Saldanha Marina, Bhat Vadisha S., A Rajeshwary, Vincent Mark Jittu, Ravikumar Aishwarya. (2023). Simulation-based training in ear, nose and throat skills and emergencies. Brazilian Journal of Otorhinolaryngology. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bjorl.2022.01.001

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-hoc contrast within the day-15 simulation assessment of tracheostomy tube suctioning. The tracheostomy-trained group's significant advantage (p = 0.019) appeared only against Group B, not Group C, indicating a pairwise-limited effect.

> "Simulation in Tracheostomy tube suctioning showed statistically significant values for Group A (p =  0.019). This significance was present only while comparing with Group B and not with Group C"
