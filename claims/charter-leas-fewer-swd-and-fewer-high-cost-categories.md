# Charter LEAs enroll a lower proportion of students with disabilities than traditional LEAs and serve smaller proportions in higher-cost disability categories

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Charter schools that are LEAs for special education purposes enroll fewer students with disabilities on average than traditional LEAs (9.4 percent versus 10.7 percent of enrollment) and serve smaller proportions of students in autism, intellectual disability, and multiple disabilities. ## Evidence

### Jason Willis 2020

Jason Willis, Sara Menlove Doutre, Kelsey Krausen, Tyson Barrett, Tye Ripma, and Ruthie Caparas. (2020). Study of California's Special Education Funding System: Phase I Report — A Descriptive Analysis of Special Education Funding in California. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED616102

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive analysis of DataQuest 2018/19 data (Figure E-4, Table 2) reports charter LEAs serve similar proportions in specific learning disability, speech and language impairment, emotional disturbance, and other health impairment, but smaller proportions in "other, higher-cost disability categories."

> "charter schools that are LEAs for special education purposes enroll fewer students with disabilities on average than traditional LEAs — 9.4 percent of total enrollment versus 10.7 percent, respectively."
