# For students without relevant prior knowledge, elaborating during the discussion led to lower recall than not elaborating

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` No elaboration without prior knowledge led to higher immediate recall (M = 38.40) than elaboration without prior knowledge (M = 27.39), p = .02, and the pattern repeated on the delayed posttest (p = .04). [→ Floris M. Van Blankenstein 2013](#floris-m-van-blankenstein-2013)

## Evidence

### Floris M. Van Blankenstein 2013

Floris M. Van Blankenstein, Diana H. J. M. Dolmans, Cees P. M. Van der Vleuten, Henk G. Schmidt. (2013). Relevant prior knowledge moderates the effect of elaboration during small group discussion on academic achievement. Instructional Science, 41, 729-744. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-012-9252-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Bonferroni-corrected pairwise comparison on the immediate posttest in the 2×2 experiment. The article also reports the delayed-posttest version of this contrast as significant (M = 22.40 vs. M = 14.61, p = .04). No standardized effect size is printed for the contrast.

> "Pairwise comparisons also revealed that no elaboration without prior knowledge actually led to higher recall ( M = 38.40, SD = 11.48) than elaboration without prior knowledge ( M = 27.39, SD = 15.92), p = .02."
