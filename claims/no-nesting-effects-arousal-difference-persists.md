## Evidence

### Rosegard 2013

Rosegard, E., & Wilson, J. (2013). Capturing students' attention: An empirical study. Journal of the Scholarship of Teaching and Learning, Vol. 13, No. 5, December 2013. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1017063

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Because data were collected over eight consecutive semesters, a mixed effects (hierarchical linear) model tested time and nesting. Adding group membership as a fixed effect significantly improved fit (AIC and BIC both decreased by 23.91), whereas adding class, semester, or year terms did not. The article concludes "There were no nesting effects found in the data, but the difference between conditions (arousal versus no-arousal) continued to be evident."

> "The model with only group membership as a fixed effect (independent variable) significantly increased the fit indices [both Akaike and Bayesian Information Criteria (AIC and BIC) decrease by 23.91] from the base-line model where only the intercept was allowed to vary."
