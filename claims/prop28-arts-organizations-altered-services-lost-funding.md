# Community arts organizations have altered their services and experienced loss of funding and contracts under Prop 28 AMS

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In response to reduced direct funding from schools and districts, some organizations shifted services (e.g., before- or after-school programming, professional development, arts-integration support for general education teachers), and two reported permanently closing. ## Evidence

### Herpin 2025

Herpin, S. A., Wynveen, K., & Berry, W. (2025). California's Proposition 28 Arts and Music Funding Guarantee and Accountability Act: Initial impact on arts organizations. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED677596

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Open-ended survey responses coded by the research team. Respondents described shifting to after-school programming, professional development, and arts-integration coaching; one developed a support initiative helping non-arts teachers "develop arts integrated lessons." Others reported lost contracts as districts hired staff.

> "Other organizations noted that Prop 28 AMS resulted in a loss of funding for their organization. Two respondents shared how this resulted in their organizations permanently closing."
