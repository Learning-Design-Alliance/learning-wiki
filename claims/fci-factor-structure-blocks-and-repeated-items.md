# A substantial part of the exploratory FCI factor structure results from problem blocks and pairs of similar questions rather than the structure of Newtonian mechanics

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Many of the largest factor loadings and strongest item correlations occur for items within problem blocks, suggesting the block structure generates spurious factor structure. [→ John Stewart 2018](#john-stewart-2018)
`q2 i?` Removing blocked items and one of each pair of repeated items yields a reduced 18-item instrument whose 6-factor MIRT solution has factors that cannot be reliably mapped onto subdivisions of Newtonian mechanics. [→ John Stewart 2018 (2)](#john-stewart-2018-2)

## Evidence

### John Stewart 2018

John Stewart, Cabot Zabriskie, Seth DeVore, and Gay Stewart. (2018). Multi-Dimensional Item Response Theory and the Force Concept Inventory. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010137](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010137)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlation analysis of the full FCI item correlation matrix (visualized for |r| > 0.3, all positive) alongside the 9-factor MIRT loadings; the authors conclude it seems likely that at least some of the factor and correlation structure results from the use of problem blocks with a common stem.

> "Examination of Table I shows that many of the largest factor loadings in individual factors occur for problems in the same block; likewise, in Fig. 1 many of the most strongly correlated item pairs are part of prob- lem blocks."

### John Stewart 2018 (2)

John Stewart, Cabot Zabriskie, Seth DeVore, and Gay Stewart. (2018). Multi-Dimensional Item Response Theory and the Force Concept Inventory. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010137](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010137)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Exploratory MIRT on the reduced 18-item FCI (first item of each block retained, second of each repeated pair removed) found 6 factors optimal, but loadings could not be theoretically supported; the article states any general conclusion from factors FC4, FC5, or FC6 about knowledge structure is suspect.

> "Factor FC3 con- tains the Newton's 3rd law items, but it also loads on items 1 and 8 which have nothing to do with Newton's 3rd law. Likewise, item 15, which requires only Newton's 3rd law for its solution, also loads strongly on FC2."
