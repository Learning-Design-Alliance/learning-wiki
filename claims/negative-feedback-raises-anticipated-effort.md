# Predominantly negative feedback leads students to expect a next round of problems will require more effort than predominantly positive feedback (Experiments 1 and 2)

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In two between-subjects experiments, participants who received predominantly negative feedback expected the upcoming round of tasks to require more effort than participants who received predominantly positive feedback, with a large effect in Experiment 1. [→ Tamara van Gog 2024](#tamara-van-gog-2024)

## Evidence

### Tamara van Gog 2024

Tamara van Gog, Eva Janssen, Florence Lucas, Maaike Taheij (2024). A Motivational Perspective on (Anticipated) Mental Effort Investment: The Biopsychosocial Model of Challenge and Threat. Educational Psychology Review (2024) 36:54. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09861-6

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANOVAs comparing randomly assigned predominantly positive vs. negative feedback conditions in two experiments (CRT problems, N = 131; weekday problems, N = 122). The article reports "a large, significant difference between the conditions" with η²p = 0.21 and η²p = 0.13.

> "With regard to how much effort participants anticipated a next round of problems would require, there was a large, significant difference between the conditions in Experiment 1, F(1, 129) = 35.10, p < 0.001, /u1D7022p = 0.21, as well as in Experiment 2, F(1, 120) = 17.59, p < 0.001, /u1D7022p = 0.13."
