Evidence¶

Roser 2017¶

Roser, M. (2017). iPadegogy of reading: Do iPads influence studens' reading? Teachers and Curriculum, 17(2), 25-32. http://dx.doi.org/10.15663/tandc.v17i2.176

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Quasi-experimental five-week study of 45 middle-school students: the treatment group read a novel as an e-book on iPads while the control group read the same novel as a soft-bound copy; reading achievement was measured with the Neale Analysis of Reading Ability pre- and post-test. The article reports "none of the pre- and post-test results for both the groups were considered to be of any statistical significance"; no effect size is printed.

"Although the average success level of the three reading achievement tests was higher for the control group (students who did not use iPads) for both the pre and post-tests, none of the pre- and post-test results for both the groups were considered to be of any statistical significance."
