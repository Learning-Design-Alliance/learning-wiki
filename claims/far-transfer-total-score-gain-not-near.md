# Overall performance improved significantly for far transfer (r = 0.53) but not for near transfer (r = 0.30, p = 0.19)

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Far transfer total scores rose significantly from pre-test to post-test (r = 0.53), while near transfer total scores did not change significantly from pre-test to post-test (r = 0.30, p = 0.19), in one group with no comparison group. [→ Li Zhien 2026](#li-zhien-2026)

## Evidence

### Li Zhien 2026

Li Zhien, Asoodar Maryam, Keulers Tom, Joosten-ten Brinke Desirée, Dolmans Diana. (2026). Ensuring Knowledge Transfer: The Critical Role of “What-If” Discussions in Virtual Patient Simulation. Perspectives on Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5334/pme.2363

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Wilcoxon signed-rank comparison of pre- and post-intervention total scores (n = 21). The far transfer case showed “a significant increase … (Median = 3.50, W = 41.5, p = 0.018, r = 0.53)”, while the near transfer case did not improve significantly. Equivalence was not tested.

> "When examining overall performance, no significant improvement was observed for the near transfer case (Median = 3.33; W = 77.0, p = 0.19, r = 0.30), whereas a significant increase was found for the far transfer case (Median = 3.50, W = 41.5, p = 0.018, r = 0.53)."
