Cancela J 2019¶

Cancela J, Burgo H, Sande E. (2019). Physical fitness and executive functions in adolescents: cross-sectional associations with academic achievement. J. Phys. Ther. Sci. 31: 556–562. https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpts/31/7/31_17-478/_article

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive analysis with Student's t-test comparing males (n=336) and females (n=374) in the cross-sectional sample. The study reports "gender had a significant influence on AA (p=0.002)" and null differences for social skills and inhibition/interference.

"gender had a significant influence on AA (p=0.002), working memory (p=0.003; p=0.013), processing speed (p=0.001) and cognitive flexibility (p=0.042), no significant differences being observed in the social skills (p=0.960) or in inhibition/interference (p=0.925)."

Related Claims¶

  • Inhibition/interference, working memory and processing speed correlate with adolescent academic achievement, while cognitive flexibility and social skills do not — related
  • Specific physical fitness components are differentially associated with specific executive function domains in adolescents — related
  • Bilingual effect evidence appears for inhibition and cognitive flexibility but disappears for working memory in children and adolescents — related
  • Working memory and processing speed, but not inhibition or non-alphanumeric naming speed, uniquely predict both word reading and reading comprehension in children ages 8 to 16 — related
  • In adolescents, physical fitness components except balance are positively associated with academic achievement, with aerobic endurance the strongest (r=0.49) — related
  • Cool EF (Stroop inhibition and CNT cognitive flexibility) predicts academic performance, while no hot EF variable does — related
  • Meta-analytic findings show small-to-medium relations between EF and academic performance, with cognitive flexibility showing the strongest effects — related
