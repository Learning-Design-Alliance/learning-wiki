# Partial credit accounts for only a small proportion of IF-AT exam scores, though weaker students depend on it more

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On the final exam (mean score 67%), removing partial credit lowered the average by only 5 points to 62%, yet as many as 10 of 49 students would have failed without partial credit, each gaining less than 8 percentage points from it. [→ Slepkov 2013](#slepkov-2013)

## Evidence

### Slepkov 2013

Slepkov, A. D. (2013). Integrated Testlets and the Immediate Feedback Assessment Technique. https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.4365

`q2 · i1`

Score decomposition of the IF-AT final exam: the mean was 67% but "without partial credit—i.e. a [1,0,0,0] marking scheme—the average was only 5 points lower at 62%". The author notes an inverse correlation between partial credit granted and exam score, mostly due to opportunity.

> "as many as 10 of 49 students would have failed the ﬁnal exam (earned less than 50%) without partial credit, but partial credit accounted for less than 8 percentage points for each of these students."
