Evidence¶

Strand Cary 2017¶

Strand Cary, M. G., Clarke, B., Doabler, C. T., Smolkowski, K., Fien, H., & Baker, S. K. (2017). A practitioner implementation of a Tier 2 first grade mathematics intervention. Learning Disability Quarterly, 40(4), 211–224. https://doi.org/10.1177/0731948717714715

q2 · i2

Quasi-experimental time-by-condition (gain-score) analysis of 253 first-grade students in ten Hawaii schools, with students most at risk assigned to Fusion (n = 154) and less at-risk peers to comparison (n = 99). The article reports a significant positive effect on the proximal measure, "p = .046; g = .61".

"Results of the time by condition analyses suggest a positive, statistically significant effect for the proximal ProFusion measure (p = .046; g = .61)."

Related Claims¶

  • Fusion showed positive but nonsignificant effects on some distal measures and negative nonsignificant effects on others in the practitioner implementation — related
  • In the concurrent formal pilot study, Fusion students gained significantly more than controls on a proximal conceptual measure (g = 0.82) — related
  • Pretest scores did not differ significantly by condition despite schools assigning the lowest performers to Fusion — related
  • The Year 3 pilot is a randomized control trial assigning at-risk first graders within schools to FUSION or at-risk control, with an additional average control condition — related
  • Fusion produced no statistically significant gains on proximal procedural fluency (EN-CBM) or distal (SAT-10) measures, though effect sizes were small and positive — related
  • Fusion effects on distal measures were non-significant, positive on EN-CBM and SAT-10 procedures and negative on easyCBM and SAT-10 problem solving — related
  • District implementation of Fusion generally matched research recommendations but varied across sites in timing, group size, and lessons covered
