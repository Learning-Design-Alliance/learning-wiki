## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The finding that spatial–sequential working memory remains influential at 9–10 years challenges the expectation that children shift from visuospatial to verbal reliance around age 7. [→ Allen 2020](#allen-2020)

## Evidence

### Allen 2020

Allen, K., Giofrè, D., Higgins, S., & Adams, J. (2020). Using working memory performance to predict mathematics performance 2 years on. Psychological Research. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00426-020-01382-5

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Interpretive discussion of the longitudinal result against prior research suggesting a declarative shift (citing Schneider, 2008). The article offers this as an interpretation of its own data rather than a tested comparison; no effect size is printed.

> "the group of children used in this study are older than the age at which this declarative shift is predicted to take place (around 7 years of age, Schneider, 2008), therefore, suggesting that a shift of this nature may not tell the whole story."
