# Perfect AI execution cannot remove four limits: accuracy does not settle end-setting, safety does not confer legitimacy, detection does not secure integrity, and outputs do not replace participation

### Kai Yao 2026

Kai Yao. (2026). When AI Does the Work, What Is Learning For? Post-Instrumental Learning and the Risk of Capacity Dissolution. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28041 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.28041](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2607.28041)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument within the article's perfect-AI idealization. The article argues accuracy is an epistemic good about representing facts, but "an accurate map does not choose the destination", so end-setting remains a learned human capacity.

> "Even a perfectly accurate model can carry forward a bad problem formulation. It can show which means best serve a target, but it cannot make the target legitimate by serving it well."

### Kai Yao 2026 (2)

Kai Yao. (2026). When AI Does the Work, What Is Learning For? Post-Instrumental Learning and the Risk of Capacity Dissolution. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28041

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument on the safety/legitimacy distinction. The article holds that transparency, explanation, human review, and recourse remain necessary in a world of more reliable AI because "legitimacy is not reducible to error correction".

> "A system can be safe in the narrow sense and still lack legitimacy. It may produce correct benefit classifications while affected people have no meaningful way to challenge the rule."
