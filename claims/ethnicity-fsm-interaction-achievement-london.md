# Black FSM students show more positive stratum interaction effects than Black non-FSM students, and the ethnicity-by-FSM interaction becomes more important by age 16

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the adjusted model, Black FSM-eligible students show on average more positive stratum interaction effects than Black non-FSM students, so Black FSM students do not score as low as the sum of their separate negative main effects implies. [→ Lucy Prior 2022](#lucy-prior-2022)
`q2 i?` Supplementary two-way interaction analyses show the ethnicity by FSM interaction becomes more important for older students, with a PCV of 45% at age 11 rising to 85% at age 16. [→ Lucy Prior 2022 (2)](#lucy-prior-2022-2)

Sociodemographic inequalities in student achievement: An intersectional multilevel analysis of individual heterogeneity and discriminatory accuracy (MAIHDA) with application to students in London, England. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2211.06321](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2211.06321)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Model 2 predicted stratum random effects plotted for ethnicity-by-FSM strata (Figure 3) show "Black FSM students showing, on average, more positive stratum interaction effects than is the case for Black non-FSM students".

> "In contrast, for Black students, the FSM patterning is the other way around with Black FSM students showing, on average, more positive stratum interaction effects than is the case for Black non-FSM students (rows 1 and 2)."

### Lucy Prior 2022 (2)

Lucy Prior, Clare Evans, Juan Merlo and George Leckie. (2022). Sociodemographic inequalities in student achievement: An intersectional multilevel analysis of individual heterogeneity and discriminatory accuracy (MAIHDA) with application to students in London, England. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2211.06321](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2211.06321)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Supplementary models adding each two-way interaction term in isolation show the ethnicity by FSM interaction "does become more important for older students (45% PCV at age 11, 85% PCV at age 16)".

> "Supplementary analyses where we add each possible two-way interaction term between the sociodemographic characteristics in isolation (Supplementary Tables S2 and S3) reveal that the ethnicity by FSM interaction does become more important for older students (45% PCV at age 11, 85% PCV at age 16)."
