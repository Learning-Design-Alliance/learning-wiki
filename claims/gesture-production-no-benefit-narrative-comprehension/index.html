Evidence¶

Dargue 2020¶

Dargue, N; Sweller, N. (2020). Two hands and a tale: When gestures benefit adult narrative comprehension. Learning and Instruction, 68. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2020.101331

q3 · i? · causal · r?

The study reports a null result for the gesture production condition: "no significant benefit of producing gestures was found." The article prints no effect size for this finding, so the null indicates a non-detected effect rather than a measured small one.

"Furthermore, no significant benefit of producing gestures was found, and there was no evidence that gesture production moderated the differential effects of observing typical and atypical gestures on narrative comprehension."

Related Claims¶

  • Gesture production does not moderate the differential effects of observing typical versus atypical gestures on narrative comprehension — related
  • Observing typical gestures, but not atypical gestures, significantly benefits adult narrative comprehension — related
  • Instructing adults to gesture while reading narratives aloud hindered free recall relative to spontaneous gesturing (Study 1) — reports the opposite
  • In Study 2, spontaneous gesturing produced better specific recall than instructed gesturing, and negative narratives were recalled better than positive — reports the opposite
  • Observing typical gestures significantly benefits narrative comprehension compared with atypical or no gestures, which do not differ significantly — related
  • Gesture effects on narrative comprehension are not moderated by total task difficulty or age when measured through free recall and specific questions — related
  • Emotional valence benefited narrative recall: negative narratives were recalled best and neutral narratives worst (Study 1) — related
