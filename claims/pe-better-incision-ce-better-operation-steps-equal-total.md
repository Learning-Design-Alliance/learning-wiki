# Phantom-experienced students showed better incision results while clinical-assistance students performed better on anesthesia, uncovering and tooth removal, with equal total scores

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the osteotomy exercise, the PE group scored significantly better on incision items (marginal course p = 0.006; mesial discharge p = 0.001), while the CE group scored better on performing anesthesia (p = 0.008), uncovering the tooth (p = 0.002) and removal of the tooth (p = 0.007). [→ Schaffrath K 2025](#schaffrath-k-2025)
`q2 i?` The total exercise score did not differ significantly between the PE group (52 (4)) and the CE group (54 (5); p = 0.131); equivalence was not tested. [→ Schaffrath K 2025 (2)](#schaffrath-k-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Schaffrath K 2025

Schaffrath K, Ooms M, Bock A, Katz MS, Hölzle F, Modabber A. (2025). Improvement of surgical skills in students using a newly developed 3D printed osteotomy model of a partially retained wisdom tooth. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08394-y

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Mann-Whitney comparison of rated exercise performance between 26 third-year phantom-experienced (PE) and 29 fifth-year clinical-assistance (CE) students, each criterion scored 0-3. The PE group's incision advantage is reported with the printed medians and p-values.

> "Regarding the incision, the PE group showed significantly better results, especially in drawing the marginal course (PE: 3 (0), CE: 3 (1); p = 0.006) and mesial discharge (PE: 3 (0), CE: 2 (2); p = 0.001) (Table 3)."

### Schaffrath K 2025 (2)

Schaffrath K, Ooms M, Bock A, Katz MS, Hölzle F, Modabber A. (2025). Improvement of surgical skills in students using a newly developed 3D printed osteotomy model of a partially retained wisdom tooth. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08394-y

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The same exercise comparison found no statistically significant difference in total score (maximum 57 points) between the PE and CE groups; the article reports the medians and p = 0.131 with no effect size.

> "However, the total score was equal between the groups (PE: 52 (4), CE: 54 (5); p = 0.131) (Table 3)."
