# Publication bias evidence is mixed: marginal asymmetry for tutees but no missing studies, and no bias for tutors

## Evidence

### Chang 2025

Chang, A., Mauer, E., Wanzek, J., Kim, S., Scammacca, N., & Swanson, E. (2025). Examining the academic effects of cross-age tutoring: A meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review, 37, 19. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-09997-z

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Egger's regression test using a mixed-effects meta-regression model with standard error as predictor for tutee effect sizes. Trim-and-fill analysis found no missing studies on the left side of the funnel plot.

> "The test yielded a value of t = 1.87 (df = 115, p = 0.06), indicating marginal significance for funnel plot asymmetry. This finding suggests that publication bias may be present."

### Chang 2025 (2)

Chang, A., Mauer, E., Wanzek, J., Kim, S., Scammacca, N., & Swanson, E. (2025). Examining the academic effects of cross-age tutoring: A meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review, 37, 19. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-09997-z

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Egger's regression test for tutor effect sizes showed a symmetric funnel plot with no evidence of publication bias.

> "The Egger's test yielded a value of t = − 1.39 (df = 28, p = 0.17), indicating no significant asymmetry in the funnel plot and thus no evidence of publication bias."
