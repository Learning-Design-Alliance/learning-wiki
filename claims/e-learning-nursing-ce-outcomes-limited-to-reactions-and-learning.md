# E-learning interventions for registered nurses in continuing education are evaluated almost exclusively at Kirkpatrick levels 1 and 2, with no reported outcomes on practice change and almost none on patient outcomes

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across 22 included systematic reviews, learning outcomes (level 2) were reported in 18 of 22 SRs (82%) and reaction outcomes (level 1) in 11 of 22 (50%), while no SR reported behavior change (level 3) and only 1 reported patient outcomes (level 4). [→ Rouleau G 2019](#rouleau-g-2019)

## Evidence

### Rouleau G 2019

Rouleau G, Gagnon MP, Côté J, Payne-Gagnon J, Hudson E, Dubois CA, Bouix-Picasso J. (2019). Effects of E-Learning in a Continuing Education Context on Nursing Care: Systematic Review of Systematic Qualitative, Quantitative, and Mixed-Studies Reviews. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/15118

`q3 · i?`

Systematic review of systematic reviews (SRSRs) of e-learning in nursing continuing education; outcomes from 22 included SRs were classified using the Kirkpatrick model. The review reports that "The most reported outcomes were learning (18/22, 82%)" and reactions in 11 of 22 SRs, with no SR reporting level 3 outcomes.

> "The most reported outcomes were learning (18/22, 82%), corresponding to Kirkpatrick's evaluation level 2. Nurse skills were the most frequently reported, followed by knowledge. Outcomes related to evaluation level 1 (ie, nurses' reactions with e-learning) were found in 11 out of the 22 SRs (50%)."
