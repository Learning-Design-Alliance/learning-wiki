## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Female students were less confident in their choice of major prior to participating in informal physics programs (p = 0.01, d = 0.75), and this difference disappeared after participation (p = 0.49). [→ Jessi Randolph 2022](#jessi-randolph-2022)

## Evidence

### Jessi Randolph 2022

Jessi Randolph, Jonathan Perry, Jonan Phillip Donaldson, Callie Rethman, and Tatiana Erukhimova. (2022). Do female physics students benefit from informal physics programs they facilitate?. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020123](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020123)

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Survey analysis of self-reported data from 32 female responses within a database of 117 completed surveys. A Mann-Whitney U test found female students "less conﬁdent at the p = 0.01 level" with "d = 0.75"; after participation the difference was non-significant, p = 0.49.

> "Using a Mann- Whitney U test [75], we found that female students were less conﬁdent at the p = 0.01 level, with a medium ef- fect size, using Cohen’s d with a Hedges’ correction, of d = 0.75. This diﬀerence disappeared after participating in informal physics programs, p = 0.49."
