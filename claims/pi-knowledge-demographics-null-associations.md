## Evidence

### Kocaslan Toran Meryem 2026

Kocaslan Toran Meryem, Özgür Su, Aslan Fatma Eti, Toygar İsmail (2026). Knowledge of Pressure Ulcers Amongst Nurses and Associated Factors: A Cross‐Sectional Study. International Wound Journal. https://doi.org/10.1111/iwj.70888

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Series of non-significant Mann-Whitney U and Kruskal-Wallis tests across demographic and professional variables in the 346-nurse survey (e.g., U = 5706.5 for gender; KW = 1.880 for education). No effect sizes are printed.

> "the median knowledge scores on pressure injuries were found to be not significantly different according to gender (p = 0.803), education level (p = 0.390), current role (p = 0.900), years of nursing experience (p = 0.103), wound care certification status (p = 0.659) or previous reading of articles on pressure injuries (p = 0.851)."

### Kocaslan Toran Meryem 2026 (2)

Kocaslan Toran Meryem, Özgür Su, Aslan Fatma Eti, Toygar İsmail (2026). Knowledge of Pressure Ulcers Amongst Nurses and Associated Factors: A Cross‐Sectional Study. International Wound Journal. https://doi.org/10.1111/iwj.70888

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Spearman correlation in the same survey between age and knowledge scores, printed as r = 0.05, p = 0.361. The article reports "no significant correlation between age and knowledge scores"; the small printed r is a negligible effect size.

> "Finally, the study revealed no significant correlation between age and knowledge scores (r = 0.05, p = 0.361)."
