## Evidence

### Yama Brie A. 2018

Yama Brie A., Hodgins Michael, Boydell Katherine, Schwartz Sarah B. (2018). A qualitative exploration: questioning multisource feedback in residency education. BMC Medical Education 18. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1270-7

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

From the Results' power dynamics theme: residents generally perceived power as resting on their side, with a junior resident reporting being "much, much more receptive" to a charge nurse's feedback than a junior nurse's; AHCPs also acknowledged power they held over residents.

> "This imbalance was particularly striking when residents spoke about receiving feedback from bedside or more junior nurses compared with charge or more senior nurses."

### Yama Brie A. 2018 (2)

Yama Brie A., Hodgins Michael, Boydell Katherine, Schwartz Sarah B. (2018). A qualitative exploration: questioning multisource feedback in residency education. BMC Medical Education 18. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1270-7

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

The Results conclude the power-dynamics theme with this statement; in the Discussion the authors add that examples culminating in punitive consequences amplified negative connotation and could undermine trust in the entire process.

> "The perceived balance of power fluctuates creating barriers to providing and accepting feedback."
