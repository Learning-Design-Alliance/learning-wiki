# Analyzing arts-based data was one of the biggest challenges in community-based arts-based research: what counts as data was unclear, conventional textual analyses dominated, and researcher-led versus participant-led interpretation each risked stereotyping

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Researchers struggled with what counts as data (art products, process, or interview transcripts) and mostly remained close to traditional textual analysis such as thematic and content analysis. [→ Sara Coemans 2017](#sara-coemans-2017)
`q2 i?` Leaving interpretation entirely to participants risks stereotyping and overgeneralizing, while researcher-led formal analysis can also produce a fixed perspective and stereotyping. [→ Sara Coemans 2017 (2)](#sara-coemans-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Sara Coemans 2017

Sara Coemans, Karin Hannes. (2017). Researchers Under the Spell of the Arts: Two Decades of Using Arts-Based Methods in Community-Based Inquiry with Vulnerable Populations. Educational Research Review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2017.08.003

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative synthesis of limitations across the 131 studies. In photography studies thematic analysis (n = 23), constant comparative analysis (n = 9), content analysis (n = 8) and group analysis (n = 8) were mostly used; a visual analysis was performed in only four studies, and many photo and theatre studies did not or vaguely reported analytical procedures.

> "It appears that researchers were often struggling how to analyze the data.  Researchers described how they were overwhelmed with the amount of data generated"

### Sara Coemans 2017 (2)

Sara Coemans, Karin Hannes. (2017). Researchers Under the Spell of the Arts: Two Decades of Using Arts-Based Methods in Community-Based Inquiry with Vulnerable Populations. Educational Research Review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2017.08.003

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review contrasts two analytical practices found in the literature: some researchers left interpretation ultimately to participants, while others enacted a formal analysis to organize the data in a presentable way; both practices carry risks as quoted.

> "On the one hand, there is the danger of stereotyping and overgeneralizing when interpretation is left completely up to participants and/or the audience (Lee & De Finney, 2005).  On the other hand, there are limitations in how the researcher can interpret what is observable."
