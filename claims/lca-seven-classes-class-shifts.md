# Latent class analysis identifies seven qualitatively different answer patterns, and most students change class between pretest and posttest

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A seven-class latent class solution was chosen as most informative and consistent with prior studies. [→ Bekaert 2025](#bekaert-2025)

## Evidence

### Bekaert 2025

Bekaert, H., Van Dooren, W., Van Winckel, H., Poessei, M., Thiering, I., Nicolini, M., Artioli, E., Avgerinou, D., Balla, E., & De Cock, M. (2025). Teaching the apparent motion of Sun and stars across four European countries. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.32374/2025.5.1.084ra](https://doi.org/10.32374/2025.5.1.084ra)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Latent class analysis in Mplus on answers to 10 of 12 AMoSS questions (pretest and posttest, N=262), selecting class number via AIC and BIC. The authors chose the seven-class solution as "substantially more informative".

> "A description with seven classes is substantially more informative as it provides groups that show qualitatively different answering patterns which indicate different mental models, and the seven classes that were obtained moreover are consistent with what was found in previous studies. Therefore, we choose the seven-class solution."

### Bekaert 2025 (2)

Bekaert, H., Van Dooren, W., Van Winckel, H., Poessei, M., Thiering, I., Nicolini, M., Artioli, E., Avgerinou, D., Balla, E., & De Cock, M. (2025). Teaching the apparent motion of Sun and stars across four European countries. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.32374/2025.5.1.084ra](https://doi.org/10.32374/2025.5.1.084ra)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Class-membership evolution between pretest and posttest (Table 7, Figure 8). The article reports growth in the all-correct class from "8 % in the pretest to 20 % in the posttest", significantly higher in group 1; no effect size is printed.

> "The number of students who answered all questions correct grew from 8 % in the pretest to 20 % in the posttest (see Table 7)."
