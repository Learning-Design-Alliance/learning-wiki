# Categorical and binary frameworks inscribed in the mathematics curriculum served as a context for reproducing existing categories through student surveys and graphs

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The curriculum's expectation of comparing variables (e.g., boys and girls, primaries and juniors) invited binary categorizations that were inherited and reproduced in students' surveys and graphs. [→ Takeuchi 2020](#takeuchi-2020)

## Evidence

### Takeuchi 2020

Takeuchi, M.A. (2020). Geopolitical configuration of identities and learning: Othering through the institutionalized categorization of “English language learners. Cognition and Instruction. Advance online publication https://doi.org/10.1080/07370008.2020.1825438

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The author's finding statement from the ethnographic study. The Grade 4 curriculum expectation asked students to “compare similarities and differences between two related sets of data,” and the teacher prompted choices such as “Are you going to ask boys and you're going to ask girls?”

> "Categorical and binary frameworks inscribed in mathematics curriculum served as a context for inheritance and reproduction of existing categories through student surveys and graphs."
