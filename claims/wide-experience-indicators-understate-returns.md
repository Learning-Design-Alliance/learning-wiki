# Common experience-measurement methods that cap or band years of experience substantially understate returns to experience, by as much as 68%

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Using indicator variables for wide ranges of experience understated estimated returns to experience by as much as 68% in one urban district study. [→ Kini 2016](#kini-2016)

## Evidence

### Kini 2016

Kini, T., & Podolsky, A. (2016). Does Teaching Experience Increase Teacher Effectiveness? A Review of the Research. Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/our-work/publications-resources/does-teaching-experience-increase-teacher-effectiveness-review-research

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

A study of elementary and middle schools in a large urban school district in a southern U.S. state from 2000 to 2009 found that broad experience-range indicators "substantially understates the estimated returns to experience … by as much as 68%"; the 68% figure is a percentage of understatement, not an effect size.

> "The authors found that using indicator variables to represent broad ranges of years of experience “substantially understates the estimated returns to experience … by as much as 68%.”"
