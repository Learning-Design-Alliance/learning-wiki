## Subclaims

`q3 i2` Performance-oriented students who perceive irrelevance show a noticeable novice-like shift while relevant-perceivers show little change, with statistical significance and a large effect size (d = 0.68, p = 0.02). [→ Andrew J. Mason 2020](#andrew-j-mason-2020)

## Evidence

### Andrew J. Mason 2020

Andrew J. Mason. (2020). Learning goals and perceived irrelevance to major within life science majors in introductory physics. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/perc.2019.pr.mason](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2019.pr.mason)

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Table 5 comparison of CLASS normalized gains among Performance-oriented students (Irrelevant n = 16, Relevant n = 63): -0.18 vs +0.01, d = 0.68, p = 0.02. Within item clusters, Performance-oriented students had significance (p < 0.05) or borderline significance (0.05 < p < 0.1) across all item clusters, with irrelevance-perceivers shifting more novice-like in each case.

> "students  who perceive relevance experience little change on  average, but students who perceive irrelevance  have a rather noticeable novice -like shift difference (with statistical significance and a large effect size)"

### Andrew J. Mason 2020 (2)

Andrew J. Mason. (2020). Learning goals and perceived irrelevance to major within life science majors in introductory physics. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/perc.2019.pr.mason](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2019.pr.mason)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Table 5 gains: Framework-oriented students +0.05 (Irrelevant, n = 17) vs +0.13 (Relevant, n = 59), d = 0.26, p = 0.35; Vague-oriented students -0.06 (n = 17) vs -0.02 (n = 46), d = 0.18, p = 0.54. The relevance effect on attitudinal shifts is specific to the Performance orientation subgroup.

> "There was no similar effect within framework-oriented students (who had a slight expert -like gain in either case ) or within vague-oriented students (who had a slight novice -like gain in either case )."

### Andrew J. Mason 2020

Andrew J. Mason. (2020). Perceived irrelevance and mastery vs. performance achievement goals: Two mindset variables within attitudinal experiences of life science majors in introductory physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2012.09900](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2012.09900)

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

CLASS analysis within learning orientations (Table 5): Performance-oriented gains d = 0.68 (p = 0.02); Performance-oriented students had significance or borderline significance across all item clusters.

> "perceived relevance appears to only be a factor for Performance-oriented students; those who perceived relevance to their majors did not experience much shifting, while those who did not experienced a novice-like shift. The effect size between relevance and irrelevance for Performance-oriented students is large (d = 0.68)."

### Andrew J. Mason 2020 (2)

Andrew J. Mason. (2020). Perceived irrelevance and mastery vs. performance achievement goals: Two mindset variables within attitudinal experiences of life science majors in introductory physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2012.09900](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2012.09900)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same Table 5 within-orientation analysis: Framework gains d = 0.26 (p = 0.35) and Vague gains d = 0.18 (p = 0.54), both non-significant.

> "Framework -oriented students are only mildly influenced by perceived relevance (d = 0.26 on gains), with a slightly expert-like shift occurring for both relevance and irrelevance groups; Vague -oriented students also had a slight novice-like shift regardless of perceived rel evance."
