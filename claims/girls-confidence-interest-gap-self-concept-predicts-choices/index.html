Halpern 2007¶

Washington, DC: National Center for Education Research, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/5

q2 · i? · review · r2

The guide's overview synthesizes correlational and longitudinal studies (citing Andre et al. 1999; Herbert and Stipek 2005; Jacobs et al. 2002, among others). It notes this difference persists "given that males and females generally enroll in similar courses"; no effect sizes printed.

"researchers have found that girls and women have less confidence in their math abilities than males do and that from early adolescence, girls show less interest in math or science careers."

Halpern 2007 (2)¶

Halpern, D., Aronson, J., Reimer, N., Simpkins, S., Star, J., and Wentzel, K. (2007). Encouraging Girls in Math and Science (NCER 2007-2003). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Research, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/5

q2 · i? · review · r2

Correlational evidence cited in the overview (Simpkins and Davis-Kean 2005; Updegraff and Eccles 1996). The guide uses it to argue that "improving girls' beliefs about their abilities could alter their choices and performance." No effect sizes printed.

"girls, as well as boys, who have a strong self-concept regarding their abilities in math or science are more likely to choose and perform well in elective math and science courses and to select math- and science-related college majors and careers."
