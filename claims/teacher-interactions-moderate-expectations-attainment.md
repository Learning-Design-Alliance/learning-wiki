## Evidence

### Dempsey 2026

Dempsey, C., & McCoy, S. (2026). The role of academic self‐concepts, parent expectations and teacher–student interactions in socio‐economic gaps in educational attainment. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 96, 422–442. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.70025

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Structural equation model (Model 6) adding the teacher-interaction × parent-expectation interaction; multi-group test showed fit reduction, Δχ2(5) = 59.453, p = .0001. Teacher interaction quality alone was also associated with attainment (Std. Est. = .094, p < .0001).

> "the interaction term between parent expectations and teacher–child interaction quality had a differing association with educational attainment for EV children, Std. Est. = .154, z = .019, p < .0001, and non‐EV children, Std. Est. = − .097, z = .014, p < .001."

### Dempsey 2026 (2)

Dempsey, C., & McCoy, S. (2026). The role of academic self‐concepts, parent expectations and teacher–student interactions in socio‐economic gaps in educational attainment. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 96, 422–442. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.70025

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Simple slope plots (Figure 2) probing the significant interaction at ±1 SD of teacher interaction quality for non-EV children (N = 3772) and EV children (N = 957).

> "Positive teacher interactions compensated for lower parental expectations among high SES children (Figure 2a), whereas for children experiencing economic vulnerability (Figure 2b), there was a synergistic role of parents' expectations and high‐quality teacher interactions on educational attainment."
