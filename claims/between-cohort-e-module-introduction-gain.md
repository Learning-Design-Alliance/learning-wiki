## Evidence

### Kwant Kelly J. 2015

Kwant Kelly J., Custers Eugene J. F. M., Jongen-Hermus Femke J., Kluijtmans Manon. (2015). Preparation by mandatory E-modules improves learning of practical skills: a quasi-experimental comparison of skill examination results. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0376-4

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Between-cohort comparison of the same stations before and after mandatory e-module introduction. Scores rose from 3.44 ± 0.63 (n = 308) to 3.68 ± 0.70 (n = 314); the article reports this difference was significant, t = 4.97, p < 0.01, d = 0.36.

> "students obtained a higher score on the stations which had moved from text based preparation in 2012 to e-module preparation in 2013; 3.44 ± 0.63 (n = 308) and 3.68 ± 0.70 (n = 314) (t = 4.97 p < 0.01; Cohen’s d = 0.36) respectively."

### Kwant Kelly J. 2015 (2)

Kwant Kelly J., Custers Eugene J. F. M., Jongen-Hermus Femke J., Kluijtmans Manon. (2015). Preparation by mandatory E-modules improves learning of practical skills: a quasi-experimental comparison of skill examination results. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0376-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Control comparison of stations with text-based preparation in both years across cohorts: 3.57 ± 0.67 (n = 467) versus 3.61 ± 0.67 (n = 517), t = 1.47, p = 0.34. No effect size is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect); equivalence was not tested.

> "The results showed no significant difference between the two cohorts on stations for which students had prepared by text based preparation in both academic years, 3.57 ± 0.67 (cohort 2011–2012, n = 467) and 3.61 ± 0.67 (cohort 2012–2013, n = 517), (t = 1.47 p = 0.34)"
