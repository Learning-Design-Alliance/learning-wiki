## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Children faster at comparing symbolic and nonsymbolic magnitudes are also faster at solving multiplication fact problems, with correlations remaining significant after controlling for general reaction time. [→ Schleepen TMJ 2016](#schleepen-tmj-2016)

## Evidence

### Schleepen TMJ 2016

Schleepen TMJ, Van Mier HI, De Smedt B (2016) The Contribution of Numerical Magnitude Comparison and Phonological Processing to Individual Differences in Fourth Graders' Multiplication Fact Ability. PLoS ONE 11(6): e0158335. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0158335

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis (N = 63 fourth graders) with FDR correction found that children who were faster in processing symbolic and nonsymbolic magnitudes were also faster at multiplication facts; "These correlations remained significant after controlling for general reaction time (rs > .43, p < .01, uncorrected p-value)" and after FDR correction.

> "Significant positive correlations were found between the overall reaction time of both the symbolic and nonsymbolic magnitude comparison task and the speed of solving multiplication fact problems."
