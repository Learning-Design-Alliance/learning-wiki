## Evidence

### Kellman 2009

Kellman, P. J., & Garrigan, P. (2009). Perceptual Learning and Human Expertise. Physics of Life Reviews, 6(2), 53-84. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2008.12.001

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Marcus, Vijayan, Bandi Rau & Vishton (1999), familiarization of 7-month-old infants with syllable sequences such as "li na li" or "ga ti ga" whose first and last elements matched.

> "Afterwards, infants showed a novelty response (longer attention) to a new string such as “wo fe fe” but showed less attention to a new string that fit the abstract pattern of earlier items, such as “wo fe wo”."

### Kellman 2009 (2)

Kellman, P. J., & Garrigan, P. (2009). Perceptual Learning and Human Expertise. Physics of Life Reviews, 6(2), 53-84. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2008.12.001

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Nagarajan et al. (1998), training on interval discrimination of vibratory pulses; transfer across hand parts, to the contralateral hand, and to auditory stimuli with the same intervals indicates the abstract relation was learned.

> "They found that after training, learning transferred to other parts of the trained hand, and to the same position on the contralateral hand. More remarkable, they found that learning also transferred to auditory stimuli with the same intervals."
