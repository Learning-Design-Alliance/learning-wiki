# Faculty categorizations of quantum mechanics problems were rated higher overall than student categorizations by blind faculty evaluators

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a categorization task with 20 quantum mechanics problems, faculty categorizations received higher overall scores than student categorizations when scored by three faculty evaluators without knowing whose categorizations they were. [→ Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#shih-yin-lin-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)

## Evidence

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Assessing Expertise in Quantum Mechanics using Categorization Task. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1603.02948](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1603.02948)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Categorization study in which 22 undergraduates and 6 faculty categorized 20 quantum mechanics problems; three faculty evaluators scored each categorization as good (2), moderate (1), or poor (0), with a maximum of 6 per problem. The article reports that faculty "were rated higher overall than those by students"; no effect size is printed.

> "Figure 1 shows that the categorizations by faculty were rated higher overall than those by students, despite the diversity in faculty responses."
