# The non-ICT paper-and-pencil version of ABRA produced a larger literacy effect (0.231 SD, three months' progress) than the ICT version (0.138 SD)

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` The non-ICT treatment effect (0.231, CI 0.102 to 0.360, three months) exceeded the ICT effect (0.138, CI 0.004 to 0.273, two months). [→ McNally et al. 2016](#mcnally-et-al-2016)

## Evidence

### McNally et al. 2016

Sandra McNally, Jenifer Ruiz-Valenzuela and Heather Rolfe. (2016). ABRA: Online Reading Support — Evaluation report and executive summary. Education Endowment Foundation. http://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN18254678

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Comparison of the two treatment arms in the three-armed RCT; the executive summary table reports the non-ICT effect size as 0.231 (0.102, 0.360), three months' progress, against the ICT effect of 0.138. The report states the non-ICT effect is "considerably higher".

> "Both the ICT and non-ICT treatments were found to have positive results for literacy that were unlikely to have occurred by chance, although the size of the non-ICT effect is considerably higher."
