## Evidence

### MacKenzie R K 2016

MacKenzie R K, Cleland J A, Ayansina D, Nicholson S. (2016). Does the UKCAT predict performance on exit from medical school? A national cohort study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2016-011313

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel linear analysis of linked UKCAT and UKFPO national databases for 6294 students from 30 UK programmes, adjusting for confounders. The article reports "small but significant predictive validity of performance throughout medical school (indicated by the EPM)" and for the SJT; no effect size is printed.

> "We have shown that the UKCAT—as a total score and in terms of the four cognitive subtests—has small but significant predictive validity of performance throughout medical school (indicated by the EPM)."

### MacKenzie R K 2016 (2)

MacKenzie R K, Cleland J A, Ayansina D, Nicholson S. (2016). Does the UKCAT predict performance on exit from medical school? A national cohort study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2016-011313

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r3`

Pearson/Spearman correlation analysis in the Results section of the same cohort. The article reports a "weak correlation with total UKCAT (r=0.208)" and with verbal reasoning (r=0.216); EPM decile and total showed very little correlation with UKCAT tests.

> "SJT showed a weak correlation with total UKCAT (r=0.208), also showing a weak correlation with the VR domain of the UKCAT (r=0.216; table 4)."
