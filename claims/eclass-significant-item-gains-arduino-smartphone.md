# Three E-CLASS item scores on understanding experimental setups, relevant equations, and tool limitations showed statistically significant rises from pre-test to post-test in the Arduino/smartphone lab, in one single-group pre-post study

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2023 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` E-CLASS items Q1 (understanding how the experimental setup works, 0.77 to 0.94), Q8 (understanding relevant equations, about 0.73 to about 0.94), and Q10 (understanding a measurement tool's performance limitations, 0.53 to 0.74) showed statistically significant shifts (Mann-Whitney U-test). [→ Eugenio Tufino and Giovanni Organtini 2023](#eugenio-tufino-and-giovanni-organtini-2023)

## Evidence

### Eugenio Tufino and Giovanni Organtini 2023

Eugenio Tufino and Giovanni Organtini. (2023). Evaluation of the effectiveness of an introductory mechanics Lab with Arduino and smartphone. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2311.00606](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2311.00606)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Item-level analysis of pre/post E-CLASS responses from the 2021/22 edition, with shifts tested using the Mann-Whitney U-test. Q1 rose "from 0.77 to 0.94"; the article also lists Q8 (about 0.73 to about 0.94) and Q10 (0.53 to 0.74) as significant.

> "The questions for which we measured the statistically significant shifts obtained using the Mann-Whitney U-test, are: ● Q1 (When doing an experiment, I try to understand how the experimental setup works), from 0.77 to 0.94."
