# First-year undergraduate students performing the Stirling Engine experiment obtain regression gradients and engine efficiencies of approximately 10% comparable to those obtained by teaching staff

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Student-recorded data yield regression gradients of 0.6–0.7 Hz² K⁻¹ and engine mechanical efficiencies of approximately 10%, similar to teacher-recorded data. [→ Will Yeadon and Mark Quinn 2021](#will-yeadon-and-mark-quinn-2021)

## Evidence

### Will Yeadon and Mark Quinn 2021

Will Yeadon and Mark Quinn. (2021). Thermodynamics education for energy transformation: a Stirling Engine experiment. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/ac142c](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/ac142c)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Regression analysis of data gathered by two students and a teacher, each using individual Stirling Engines, with student repeat data binned and teacher data recorded at discrete ∆T values. Gradients ranged from "0.6− 0.7 Hz2 K−1" with relative errors of 5–14%; goodness of fit r² was 0.9877 (Student 1), 0.8245 (Student 2), and 0.9923 (Teacher).

> "Values of the gradient range from 0.6− 0.7 Hz2 K−1 with relative errors ranging from 5−14%. The y-intercept values can be used to determine the x-intercept (the minimum value of ∆T for rotation) which range from 20-40 K."
