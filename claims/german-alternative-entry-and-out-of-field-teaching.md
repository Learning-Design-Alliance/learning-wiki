## Evidence

### Ben Van Dusen 2021

Ben Van Dusen, Christoph Vogelsang, Joseph Taylor, and Eva Cauet. (2021). How to Teach a Teacher: Challenges and Opportunities in Physics Teacher Education in Germany and the USA. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1007/978-3-030-87391-2_3](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-87391-2_3)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Survey-based figure reported by the chapter from DPG (2010) on alternative entry routes (e.g., candidates with a science master's entering the induction phase or on-the-job training). Evaluations indicate entrants without a Master of Education achieve similar content knowledge but less pedagogical knowledge during traineeship.

> "In the years 2002 to 2008 in Germany on average 45% of new physics teachers have entered the teaching profession following one of these alternative pathways, with an increasing proportion in the later years and differences between the school tracks (DPG, 2010)."

### Ben Van Dusen 2021 (2)

Ben Van Dusen, Christoph Vogelsang, Joseph Taylor, and Eva Cauet. (2021). How to Teach a Teacher: Challenges and Opportunities in Physics Teacher Education in Germany and the USA. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1007/978-3-030-87391-2_3](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-87391-2_3)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Representative survey data cited from Stanat et al. (2019) on out-of-field physics teaching across German federal states. The chapter states this undermines the strategy of quality assurance through high entrance qualification.

> "Representative surveys reveal that roughly 6.5% of teachers are teaching physics out of their field (Stanat et al., 2019), with high variance between the federal states (between 1.7% and 17.9%)."
