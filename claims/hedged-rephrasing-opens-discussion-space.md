# An instructor's hedged, eye-level rephrasing of a student question opened space for a dismissive tutorial group to offer tentative ideas

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` When TA Joey rephrased "what are those two jumps?" as "So, what do you think happened there, do you have any idea?" while kneeling to eye level, students began offering hedged ideas about the graph's jumps. [→ Luke D. Conlin 2015](#luke-d-conlin-2015)

## Evidence

### Luke D. Conlin 2015

Luke D. Conlin. (2015). The use of epistemic distancing to create a safe space to sensemake in introductory physics tutorials. Originally published 2012. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1508.01574](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1508.01574)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Case-study analysis of Episode 2 of the same group's first tutorial session. After the hedged rephrasing, "the students start to offer ideas about what may have happened," with Daria and Alan offering hedged conjectures about the motion detector. No effect size is reported.

> "This version of the question incorporates considerable hedging, both by adding “what do you think” (from “what happened there” to “what do you think happened there”) and asking for “any idea”, which opens up space for someone to offer an idea even if they do not know for sure."
