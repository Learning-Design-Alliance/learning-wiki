## Evidence

### Marshall J 2025

Marshall J, Shiu C, Raatz M, Penman A, Beak K, Clarke S, Ward EC. (2025). Telesimulation for Training in Infant Feeding: A Randomized Controlled Trial. Dysphagia. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00455-024-10746-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experience-level comparison in the randomized controlled trial of 23 novices and 16 experienced clinicians. "novice level participants reported significantly greater changes" in confidence and anxiety than experienced participants (partial ƞ2=0.43 and 0.32), attributed to experienced clinicians' higher baseline confidence.

> "novice level participants reported significantly greater changes across both confidence (Mean difference experienced = 18.94; Mean difference novice = 38.70; F1, 37=27.86; p < 0.001; partial ƞ2=0.43) and anxiety (Mean difference experienced=-9.75; Mean difference novice=-23.65; F1,37=17.30; p < 0.001; partial ƞ2=0.32)"

### Marshall J 2025 (2)

Marshall J, Shiu C, Raatz M, Penman A, Beak K, Clarke S, Ward EC. (2025). Telesimulation for Training in Infant Feeding: A Randomized Controlled Trial. Dysphagia. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00455-024-10746-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experience-level comparison for the clinical vignette outcome in the same trial: "the experienced participants demonstrating greater change" than novices (partial ƞ2=0.11), which the authors did not consider statistically significant. The authors suggest experienced clinicians better incorporated new knowledge to meet the tool's expectations.

> "the experienced participants demonstrating greater change (Mean difference experienced = 2.50; Mean difference novice = 1.65; F1,37=4.51; p = 0.040; partial ƞ2=0.11), although this result was not considered statistically significant."
