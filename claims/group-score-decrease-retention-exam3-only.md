# Students whose group scores decreased showed better cumulative-question retention on exam 3, but this pattern was not significant on exam 4

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` On exam 3, students whose group scores decreased answered more cumulative questions than students whose scores increased or who tested individually (p = 0.005 overall); on exam 4 the same trend was not significant (p = 0.313). [→ Leight H. 2012](#leight-h-2012)

## Evidence

### Leight H. 2012

Leight H., Saunders C., Calkins R., Withers M. (2012). Collaborative Testing Improves Performance but Not Content Retention in a Large-Enrollment Introductory Biology Class. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.12-04-0048

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary ANOVA analysis of cumulative-question scores split by whether group score increased or decreased: "a significant effect ... between the three groups (p = 0.005)" on exam 3, but "the effect was not significant across the three groups (p = 0.313)" on exam 4. No effect size is printed.

> "For exam 3, there was a significant effect on the average number of correct responses to cumulative questions between the three groups (p = 0.005). Specifically, students whose group scores decreased correctly answered more cumulative questions (3 ± 0.3) than did students whose group scores increased (2.09 ± 0.17, p = 0.011) or students who took the previous exam individually (2.09 ± 0.12, p = 0.006)."
