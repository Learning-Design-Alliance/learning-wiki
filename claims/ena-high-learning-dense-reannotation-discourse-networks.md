# Epistemic network analysis shows high-learning groups integrate re-annotations with knowledge-confirming discourse more densely than low-learning groups

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` High-learning groups exhibit denser ENA networks connecting initiating annotations, re-annotations, and confirm-knowledge codes, while low-learning groups show fragmented networks with weaker annotation-discourse connections. ## Evidence

### Yiqiu Zhou and Jina Kang 2026

Yiqiu Zhou and Jina Kang. (2026). From individual annotations to shared attention: How re-annotation supports collaborative knowledge building in CSCL. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-026-09486-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparative epistemic network analysis (RQ3) of the 16 groups, modeling temporal co-occurrences of annotation events and five verbal codes within a temporal window, projected via singular value decomposition. High-learning groups' mean squares sat near re-annotation, modify knowledge, and new knowledge.

> "the ENA network reveals distinct differences in how annotation behaviors and verbal discourse are integrated across performance levels. High-learning groups (represented by red edges) exhibit denser and more interconnected networks, particularly between initiating annotations, re-annotations, and verbal codes, such as confirm knowledge."

### Yiqiu Zhou and Jina Kang 2026 (2)

Yiqiu Zhou and Jina Kang. (2026). From individual annotations to shared attention: How re-annotation supports collaborative knowledge building in CSCL. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-026-09486-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same ENA comparison, low-learning side: the only prominent connection was between confusion and confirm knowledge, and low-learning groups' mean squares sat closer to confusion, reject knowledge, and confirm knowledge, described as "a more reactive pattern of discussion".

> "In contrast, low-learning groups (represented by blue edges) display more fragmented networks. Connections between annotation behaviors and knowledge construction are notably weaker, especially for re-annotations, which appear less integrated into discourse."
