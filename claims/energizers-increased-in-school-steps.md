# A classroom-based physical activity program (Energizers) increases children's daily in-school physical activity compared with no program

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Intervention-group students took significantly more in-school steps than control-group students, with a moderate difference (ES = 0.49). [→ Mahar 2006](#mahar-2006)

## Evidence

### Mahar 2006

Mahar, M. T., Murphy, S. K., Rowe, D. A., Golden, J., Shields, A. T., & Raedeke, T. D. (2006). Effects of a Classroom-Based Program on Physical Activity and On-Task Behavior. Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise, 38(12), 2086-2094. https://doi.org/10.1249/01.mss.0000235359.16685.a3

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental comparison of in-school physical activity among 243 students; intervention students (N = 135) received Energizers while controls (N = 108) did not. An independent groups t-test showed intervention students "took significantly (P < 0.05) more in-school steps" with a moderate ES = 0.49.

> "Students in the intervention group took significantly (P < 0.05) more in-school steps (5587 ± 1633) than control-group students (4805 ± 1543), and the size of this difference was moderate (ES = 0.49)."
