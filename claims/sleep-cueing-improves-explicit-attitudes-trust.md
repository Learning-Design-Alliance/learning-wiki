## Evidence

### Zhao 2025

Zhao, H., Lin, X., Yuan, K., Hu, X., Wang, X., Su, W., Wang, Q., & Lu, L. (2025). Updating social evaluation during sleep. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00356-9

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Explicit preference rating test after sleep on a 1–7 scale. Likeability was higher for the cued name (4.21 ± 1.01 vs 3.61 ± 1.10, d = 0.41); social willingness was also higher (4.25 ± 1.18 vs 3.43 ± 1.19, d = 0.50), both significant paired t-tests.

> "participants showed a signiﬁcantly greater degree of likeability and social willingness to engage with the individuals with cued names than those with uncued names (degree of likeability: mean ± SD; cued name: 4.21 ± 1.01; uncued name: 3.61 ± 1.10;t(75) = 3.55,p < 0.001, Cohen’sd=0 . 4 1"

### Zhao 2025 (2)

Zhao, H., Lin, X., Yuan, K., Hu, X., Wang, X., Su, W., Wang, Q., & Lu, L. (2025). Updating social evaluation during sleep. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00356-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Single-trial ultimatum game with a hypothetical 500 yuan reward, measuring social trust by allocator choice. "The choice of selecting the individuals with the cued name (77.8%) was significantly greater than that with an uncued name (22.2%)" (Χ²(1) = 16.67, p < 0.001). The article prints no standardized effect size for this contrast.
