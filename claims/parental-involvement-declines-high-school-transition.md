# Parental involvement declines as students move from eighth to tenth grade in Chicago and nationally

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Chicago eighth graders report substantially more parental involvement (homework help, checking, school attendance) than tenth graders. [→ Roderick 1998](#roderick-1998)
`q2 i?` Nationally, NELS students report significant declines in parental monitoring and discussion between eighth and tenth grade. [→ Roderick 1998 (2)](#roderick-1998-2)

## Evidence

### Roderick 1998

Roderick, M., Stone, S., with Arney, M., Chiong, J., DaCosta, K., & Waxman, E. (1998). Changing Standards, Changing Relationships: Building Family-School Relationships to Promote Achievement in High Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://ccsr.uchicago.edu/publications/changing-standards-changing-relationships-building-family-school-relationships-promote

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Consortium on Chicago School Research student surveys of approximately 28,000 Chicago eighth and tenth graders in winter 1997. Students reported lower parental homework help, checking, discussion, meeting attendance, and volunteering in tenth grade (e.g., 35% vs. 22% for homework help).

> "eighth graders in Chicago Public Schools are much more likely than tenth graders to say that their parents help with homework or were involved in their school that year"

### Roderick 1998 (2)

Roderick, M., Stone, S., with Arney, M., Chiong, J., DaCosta, K., & Waxman, E. (1998). Changing Standards, Changing Relationships: Building Family-School Relationships to Promote Achievement in High Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://ccsr.uchicago.edu/publications/changing-standards-changing-relationships-building-family-school-relationships-promote

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

National Education Longitudinal Study of 1988 panel of 17,224 students in roughly 1,000 schools surveyed in eighth and tenth grades. The brief reports monitoring "declined significantly between eighth and tenth grade," with discussion of school programs falling from 39% to 19%.

> "Overall, students’ reports of parental monitoring between high school and elementary school declined significantly between eighth and tenth grade. Thirty-nine percent of the eighth graders say that they often discuss programs at school with their parents.  By tenth grade, that level of monitoring drops by more than half, to just 19 per-cent."
