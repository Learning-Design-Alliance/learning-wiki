## Evidence

### Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer 2020

Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer, Laura Ríos, and H. J. Lewandowski (2020). Preliminary model for student ownership of projects. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/perc.2019.pr.dounas-frazer](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2019.pr.dounas-frazer)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview excerpts from students Gandalf and Heather coded in the qualitative study. The synthesis phase was "co-coded with all three interaction types"; Gandalf tied ownership to investing time for good images in the final week, and Heather recognized new understanding during the poster session.

> "SYNTHESIS was also co-coded with all three interaction types, as evidenced by Gandalf and Heather. Gandalf reasoned that he had ownership of his project because, during the ﬁnal phase of the project, he was uniquely committed to investing the time and effort required to get “really good im- ages”"

### Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer 2020 (2)

Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer, Laura Ríos, and H. J. Lewandowski (2020). Preliminary model for student ownership of projects. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/perc.2019.pr.dounas-frazer](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2019.pr.dounas-frazer)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Across the same interview corpus, instructor conceptions of ownership did not include synthesis-phase interactions. The authors report instructors' conceptions "focused only on student-project interactions during CHOICE and EXECUTION ."

> "However, no instructors drew similar connections; instructors’ conceptions of student ownership focused only on student-project interactions during CHOICE and EXECUTION ."
