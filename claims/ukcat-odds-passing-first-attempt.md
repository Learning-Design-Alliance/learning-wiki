# Higher UKCAT scores are associated with higher odds of passing each medical school year at first attempt, with total score showing the largest adjusted effect (OR 1.20)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2016 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On univariable GEE analysis, each SD higher verbal reasoning score was associated with approximately 23% higher odds of passing an end-of-year exam first time; after adjustment for advanced qualifications the verbal reasoning OR was 1.19 (1.11–1.28) and the total score OR 1.20 (1.11–1.30). [→ Tiffin Paul A. 2016](#tiffin-paul-a-2016)

## Evidence

### Tiffin Paul A. 2016

Tiffin Paul A., Mwandigha Lazaro M., Paton Lewis W., Hesselgreaves H., McLachlan John C., Finn Gabrielle M., Kasim Adetayo S. (2016). Predictive validity of the UKCAT for medical school undergraduate performance: a national prospective cohort study. BMC Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-016-0682-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

GEE logistic regression with logit link (clustering at university and student level) predicting odds of passing each year at first attempt in the national cohort. Table 4 prints univariable verbal reasoning OR 1.23 (1.16–1.30), adjusted 1.19 (1.11–1.28); UKCAT total adjusted OR 1.20 (1.11–1.30), P < 0.0001.

> "for every SD above the mean (for that cohort of UKCAT testees) a student had scored on ‘verbal reasoning’ at application, the odds of passing an end of year exam first time increased by approximately 23 %"
