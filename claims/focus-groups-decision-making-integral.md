## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In two focus groups (three and four participants), students indicated decision making is integral to the scientific process, giving contrasting reasons, and none took the positivist position that knowledge is independent of investigator choices. [→ Laszlo Frazer 2019](#laszlo-frazer-2019)

## Evidence

### Laszlo Frazer 2019

Laszlo Frazer, Heather F. Higginbotham, Toby D. M. Bell, and Alison M. Funston. (2019). 'It's fundamental': Quantum dot blinking experiment to teach critical thinking. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1021/acs.jchemed.9b00679](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jchemed.9b00679)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Two focus groups with three and four student participants, conducted after reports, prompted on decisions made during the experiment. One student called decision making "fundamental to the science process"; students did not reach consensus on what laboratory instruction is beyond involving a task.

> "They gave contrasting reasons, including personal autonomy, lack of background information, scientiﬁc integrity, and need for a topic, for why decision making is part of the scientiﬁc process. None of the participants took the positivist position that scientiﬁc knowledge is based purely on evidence, exclusive of the choices of the investigator."
