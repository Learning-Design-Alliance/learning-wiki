# No significant publication bias was detected among the six studies in the PBL–DREEM meta-analysis

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Funnel plot symmetry, Begg's rank correlation test, and Egger's linear regression test showed no significant publication bias. [→ Qin Yongjie 2016](#qin-yongjie-2016)

## Evidence

### Qin Yongjie 2016

Qin Yongjie, Wang Yungui, Floden Robert E. (2016). The Effect of Problem-Based Learning on Improvement of the Medical Educational Environment: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Medical Principles and Practice. https://doi.org/10.1159/000449036

`q3 · i?`

Publication bias assessment conducted in STATA 11.0 alongside a funnel plot, which the authors describe as symmetrical. Both statistical tests were non-significant (p = 0.260 and p = 0.157). The authors note publication bias, the 'file drawer problem', is a major threat to review validity.

> "No significant bias was observed using Begg's test [Z = 1.32, p = 0.260 (>0.05)] or Egger's test [t = 1.74, p = 0.157 (>0.05)]."
