## Evidence

### Sherry Ruan 2024

Sherry Ruan, Allen Nie, William Steenbergen, Jiayu He, JQ Zhang, Meng Guo, Yao Liu, Kyle Dang Nguyen, Catherine Y Wang, Rui Ying, James A Landay, Emma Brunskill. (2024). Reinforcement Learning Tutor Better Supported Lower Performers in a Math Task. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1007/s10994-023-06423-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-023-06423-9)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized comparison by pretest subgroup (low 0-2, medium 3-5, high 6-8) in both studies, shown in Figure 4. The article reports a trend for low pretest subjects to improve much more in the RL narrative condition; these subgroup means are descriptive.

> "The average improvement for these students was 2.02 in study 1 (N=41), and 2.29 in study 2 (N=7), out of a total score range was (0-8)."

### Sherry Ruan 2024 (2)

Sherry Ruan, Allen Nie, William Steenbergen, Jiayu He, JQ Zhang, Meng Guo, Yao Liu, Kyle Dang Nguyen, Catherine Y Wang, Rui Ying, James A Landay, Emma Brunskill. (2024). Reinforcement Learning Tutor Better Supported Lower Performers in a Math Task. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1007/s10994-023-06423-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-023-06423-9)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Wilcoxon rank tests on change scores by pretest subgroup in both studies. Only the study 2 low-pretest contrast reached significance before correction; no effect size is printed, and the article states the finding does not survive correction.

> "There was a signiﬁcant diﬀerence in the change in scores between the RL condition and control condition in study 2 for those with low pretest scores (0-2) (Wilcoxon rank test W = 2,p = 0.013), though this diﬀerence does not persist after correcting for multiple-hypothesis testing, and all other diﬀerences for studies and pretest groups were not statistically signiﬁcant under the same test."
