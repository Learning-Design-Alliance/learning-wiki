Evidence¶

Eitel 2025¶

Eitel, A., Krebs, M.-C., Schöne, C. (2025). Introducing the FoRe-Squares Model: Focus Support and Reward as Key Predictors of Effective Technology-Augmented Instruction. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10045-z

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

The review's empirical support for Hypothesis 1 cites a study in which students learning with videos employing cognitive but not motivational design techniques were "less persistent," illustrating the high-focus-support/low-reward square. The review cites further examples: simulations without guidance were ineffective, and emotional design helped when materials were coherent.

"in an empirical study by Endres et al. (2020) students were less persistent when they learned with instructional videos employing most cognitive design techniques, but not motivational ones; there was thus high focus support but low reward"

Related Claims¶

  • Techniques yield learning gains only when they compensate for otherwise too-low focus support or reward (baseline-levels condition) — related
  • Well-designed games can promote persistence in practice and high-quality engagement, and when basic needs of autonomy, competence, and relatedness are met, learning becomes its own reward, reducing the temptation to outsource cognitive effort to LLMs. — related
  • Techniques raising both focus support and reward simultaneously are predicted to foster learning best; content-irrelevant humor is predicted not to — related
  • Stronger reward manipulations are predicted to yield learning gains with higher likelihood (magnitude-of-change condition) — related
  • Cognitive-motivational techniques that increase both focus support and reward are hypothesized to be most effective for learning — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
