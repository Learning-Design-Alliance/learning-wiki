# Students who met the recommended usage benchmark for ALEKS showed a statistically significant advantage in mathematics proficiency over matched comparison students

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` In the ALEKS fidelity sample, students using the product at the recommended level were significantly more likely to meet proficiency on the state mathematics assessment than matched BAU students (odds ratio 1.354, p = .032, Cox effect size 0.18). [→ Brasiel 2016](#brasiel-2016)

## Evidence

### Brasiel 2016

Brasiel, S., Jeong, S., Ames, C., Lawanto, K., Yuan, M., & Martin, T. (2016). Effects of educational technology on mathematics achievement for K-12 students in Utah. Journal of Online Learning Research, 2(3), 205-226. https://www.learntechlib.org/j/JOLR/

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental propensity-score matched analysis of 633 ALEKS fidelity-sample students against BAU comparison students, using logistic regression on proficiency with clustering-adjusted standard errors. Table 7 reports Exp(B) = 1.354, p = .032, effect size 0.18, which "reach or exceed the 0.16 effect size level" the authors use as the educationally meaningful benchmark.

> "There were only two products where the impact was statistically significant ( p <.05), the fidelity samples for ALEKS and i-Ready, where students were using the products at the recommended level."
