## Evidence

### Denton Carolyn A 2012

Denton Carolyn A. (2012). Response to Intervention for Reading Difficulties in the Primary Grades: Some Answers and Lingering Questions. Journal of Learning Disabilities. https://doi.org/10.1177/0022219412442155

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports this randomized kindergarten dosage study; the more frequent condition "significantly outperformed controls in word reading and comprehension, with large effect sizes." The review prints no numeric effect size values.

> "Al Otaiba, Schatschneider, & Silverman, (2005) randomly assigned kindergarten students to receive the same small-group intervention two or four times per week or to a control condition, finding that those who received intervention more frequently significantly outperformed controls in word reading and comprehension, with large effect sizes, whereas those in the twice per week condition performed significantly better than controls only on one phonemic awareness measure."

### Denton Carolyn A 2012 (2)

Denton Carolyn A. (2012). Response to Intervention for Reading Difficulties in the Primary Grades: Some Answers and Lingering Questions. Journal of Learning Disabilities. https://doi.org/10.1177/0022219412442155

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports this randomized first-grade scheduling study comparing three dosage schedules; "no differences in reading outcomes" were found, and adequate-response rates across all groups were smaller than often reported for more extensive first-grade interventions. Hatcher et al. (2006) similarly found no differential outcomes for 33 hr versus 16.5 hr.

> "In contrast, Denton et al. (2011) found no differences in reading outcomes when first grade students at risk for reading difficulties were randomized to receive small group Tier 2 intervention in 30-min sessions (a) four times per week for 16 weeks (32 hr), (b) four times per week for 8 weeks (16 hr), or (c) two times per week for 16 weeks (16 hr)."
