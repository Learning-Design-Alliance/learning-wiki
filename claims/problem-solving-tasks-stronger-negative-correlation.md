# Task type moderates the correlation: problem-solving tasks show a stronger negative association than word/paired-associate tasks

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The negative effort–monitoring correlation is stronger for problem-solving tasks than for word learning/paired associates and other tasks. [→ Martine Baars & Lisette Wijnia & Anique de Bruin & Fred Paas 2020](#martine-baars-lisette-wijnia-anique-de-bruin-fred-paas-2020)

## Evidence

### Martine Baars & Lisette Wijnia & Anique de Bruin & Fred Paas 2020

Martine Baars & Lisette Wijnia & Anique de Bruin & Fred Paas. (2020). The Relation Between Students' Effort and Monitoring Judgments During Learning: A Meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09569-3

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Task-type moderator analysis (Table 1: problem solving k = 38, r = −.533; word/paired associates k = 71, r = −.250; other k = 14, r = −.267) was significant, Q(2) = 32.20, p < .001, and problem solving remained significant in the meta-regression (b = −.340, p = .001).

> "It seemed that problem-solving tasks had a stronger negative correlation compared with the other types of tasks."
