# Course-based emphasis on reframing and stakeholder inquiry shifts pre-service teachers toward informed-designer stances

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment · `i2` medium · n=43 pre-service teachers (28 in course version 1, 15 in version 2)

Pre-service teachers move toward more "informed designer" stances — treating problems of practice as reframable, systemic, and stakeholder-centered rather than fixed and routine — when a design-focused course explicitly teaches problem reframing and stakeholder inquiry, and this shift is associated with teachers proposing more systemic and equity-oriented classroom interventions.

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Across two iterations of a required pre-service teacher design course, teachers' pre/post reflections and design projects showed movement from "beginning designer" toward "informed designer" anchors across multiple [designerly-stance](../theories/designerly-stances.md) dimensions, with a course revision that added explicit instruction in problem reframing and sociocritical perspectives producing steeper gains than a baseline design-thinking course. [→ Clark et al. 2024](#clark-et-al-2024)

`q2 i2` Teachers who engaged in designerly reframing moved beyond narrow content-delivery or behavior-management framings of a classroom problem to consider systemic, cultural, and socio-emotional factors — for example, reframing a reading-level problem to address anxiety, or reframing "teaching about Indigenous peoples" to "reorienting curriculum through Indigenous pedagogies." [→ Clark et al. 2024](#clark-et-al-2024)

## Evidence

### Clark et al. 2024

Clark, D. B., Scott, D., DiPasquale, J. P., & Becker, S. (2024). Reframing design in education: Proposing a framework to support pre-service teachers in adopting designerly stances. *Journal of the Learning Sciences, 33*(4-5), 613-666. [https://doi.org/10.1080/10508406.2024.2397762](https://doi.org/10.1080/10508406.2024.2397762)

`q2-3 · inductive content analysis of pre/post reflections and design artifacts across two course iterations, not a randomized comparison` · `i2 · substantial qualitative and coded shifts across multiple dimensions, though not a standardized outcome measure` · `n=43 pre-service teachers (28 in course version 1, 15 in version 2)`

Two cohorts of pre-service teachers took a required design course using the Double Diamond design process and a semester-long project identifying and redesigning a response to a classroom problem of practice. Version 1 used general design-thinking and human-centered design readings; version 2 added explicit instruction in problem reframing (Dorst) and sociocritical education perspectives (Gutiérrez). Coding pre/post reflections and design artifacts against the six designerly-stance dimensions showed movement toward "informed designer" anchors in both versions, with version 2 showing steeper gains — for example, roughly half of version-2 teachers reached the "informed" anchor on problem/solution co-evolution post-course. Exemplar projects showed teachers reframing individually-focused problems (a student's reading level) into systemic ones (integrating literacy and socio-emotional support), and instructors judged these reframed designs as feasible within real curricular constraints.

## Discussion
This is a quasi-experimental, within-course comparison (not a randomized trial) with two sequential cohorts rather than a true control condition, so the observed difference between course versions could reflect instructor learning/improvement across iterations as well as the added curricular content. The outcome measure (coded designerly-stance dimensions from reflections and design artifacts) is the researchers' own framework, not an independently validated instrument, and no data track whether these shifts persisted into teachers' actual classroom practice once they left the course. The equity-oriented examples (Indigenous pedagogy integration) are compelling illustrations but come from a small number of exemplar projects, not a systematic count across all 43 teachers.
