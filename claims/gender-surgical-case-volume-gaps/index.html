Evidence¶

Jones Rebecca L. 2024¶

Jones Rebecca L., Prusmetikul Suwimol, Whitehorn Sarah. (2024). Differential attainment in assessment of postgraduate surgical trainees: a scoping review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05580-2

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing Gong et al. and a systematic review, that female trainees performed fewer cases across several specialties, with less robotic console time; Eruchalu et al. found case logs became equivalent once gender parity was reached. No effect sizes are printed.

"Gong et al. [35] found significantly fewer cataract operations were performed by women in ophthalmology residency programmes, which they suggested could be due to trainers being more likely to give cases to male trainees."

Related Claims¶

  • Published research on differential attainment in postgraduate surgical assessment is dominated by quantitative, gender-focused studies from the USA and UK — related
  • Gender differences appear in progression outcomes and workplace-based assessments, including ARCP outcomes, milestone ratings, feedback language and granted autonomy, with direction varying by specialty and rater — related
  • Evidence on gender gaps in surgical examination pass rates is mixed, with lower female MRCS pass rates in some UK studies but no differences in several other examinations — related
  • Technical skill assessments show mixed gender effects, with initial task differences disappearing after training and no evidence that stereotype threat impairs female performance — related
