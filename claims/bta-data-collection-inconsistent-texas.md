# BTA implementation data are often partial, inconsistent, or unreliable, hindering accountability at scale

## Evidence

### DePaoli 2025

DePaoli, J. L., & Loewe, S. B. (2025). Keeping schools safe? The research on behavioral threat assessments. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/808.437

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

A follow-up Texas study requested data directly from the 10 largest districts plus 5 others selected for high BTA or disciplinary counts. Researchers "found that BTA data were often partial or incomplete" and sometimes differed from state agency reports.

> "They found that BTA data were often partial or incomplete and that, in some cases, the data reported by the school district were different than what was reported by the Texas Education Agency."

### DePaoli 2025 (2)

DePaoli, J. L., & Loewe, S. B. (2025). Keeping schools safe? The research on behavioral threat assessments. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/808.437

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The report's policy analysis finds that "only 7 of the 20 states mandating school BTAs require data on BTAs to be collected and reported," with even fewer requiring disaggregated, full-breadth data, and no state mandating public availability.

> "In total, only 7 of the 20 states mandating school BTAs require data on BTAs to be collected and reported, and even fewer require a full breadth of data"
