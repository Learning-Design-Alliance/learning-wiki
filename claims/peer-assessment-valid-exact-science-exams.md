# Peer assessment is equivalent to teacher assessment for exact-science exams with clear marking schemes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teacher and peer marks did not differ significantly (t-tests, H0 accepted at 5%) and correlated highly (Pearson 0.88-0.91) across two exams. [→ Bouzidi 2009](#bouzidi-2009)

## Evidence

### Bouzidi 2009

Bouzidi, L., & Jaillet, A. (2009). Can Online Peer Assessment be Trusted?. Educational Technology & Society, 12(4), 257-268. https://www.ifets.info/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparison of teacher marks (TM) with peer marks (PM) and peer-plus-self marks (SPM) on the CA-2006 exam (68 papers) and CA-2007 exam without Q7 (94 papers); all four t-tests accepted H0 at the 5% level, with Pearson correlations of "0.88 0.91 0.89 0.91" and Cohen's d of 0.11, 0.11, 0.03, 0.06 as printed in Table 5.

> "The correlation coefficients between the teacher's marks and those of the peers are high (0.90±2). The “T-Test”, applied to the marks awarded by the teacher and the marks awarded by peers, is positive."
