Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Struggling participants in the rule-based game initiated emergent facilitation — self-organized peer facilitation — which was fuelled by cognitive load, scope disturbances and high distance and supported first- and second-order learning — related
  • Extraneous cognitive load arising from limited scope and high distance in the rule-based game pushed the facilitator toward first- and second-order interventions instead of the third-order interventions the learning goal required — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • In an open simulation game with wide scope and low distance, second-order interventions effectively elicited second-order responses — related
  • Participants in the rule-based simulation game perceived it as demanding, and learning and applying its procedures and rules hindered investment in their learning goals — related
  • In an open simulation game, third-order interventions effectively evoked third-order responses — related
