# Student-history computation yields appreciably lower elimination percentages than class-population computation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Computing elimination from student histories gives appreciably lower percentages than computing from class populations, because class populations include advanced-standing entrants. [→ E. D. Jennings 1911](#e-d-jennings-1911)

## Evidence

### E. D. Jennings 1911

E. D. Jennings. (1911). The Elimination of Freshmen from the University of Texas. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=elimination+of+freshmen+from+the+university+of+texas

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparison of this study's Texas figures with Strayer's data from twenty-seven colleges of over five hundred students each (Table II). Texas sophomore/junior/senior percentages were 40/23/14 by student history versus 49/33/31 by class population; the text says student histories "include only those pupils who enter as freshmen."

> "It will be seen that the University of Texas computation on the basis of student histories gives appreciably lower percentages than by class population"
