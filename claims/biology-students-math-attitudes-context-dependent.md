# Biology students' resistance to using mathematics in biology is context-dependent, improving when they perceive the activity as doing work for them

## Evidence

### Dawn C. Meredith and Edward F. Redish 2013

Dawn C. Meredith and Edward F. Redish. (2013). De- and Reconstructing Introductory Physics for the Life Sciences. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1308.3667](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1308.3667)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Interview with a student in the biologist-taught Organismal Biology class that used Fick's second law of diffusion; she said "I don't like to think of biology in terms of numbers and variables," planning to memorize equations only for the exam.

> "I don't like to think of biology in terms of numbers and variables. I feel like that's what physics and calculus is for."

### Dawn C. Meredith and Edward F. Redish 2013 (2)

Dawn C. Meredith and Edward F. Redish. (2013). De- and Reconstructing Introductory Physics for the Life Sciences. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1308.3667](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1308.3667)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Later in the same interview, the student described a wooden-horse scaling demonstration in which a model with every dimension doubled broke its dowel legs; she said the explanation "was really helpful to just my general understanding of the world."

> "I never had anyone ex- plain to me that there's a mathematical re- lationship between that, and that was really helpful to just my general understanding of the world."

### Dawn C. Meredith and Edward F. Redish 2013 (3)

Dawn C. Meredith and Edward F. Redish. (2013). De- and Reconstructing Introductory Physics for the Life Sciences. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1308.3667](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1308.3667)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

The authors' interpretation of their student interviews: such disciplinary expectations "can be context dependent," and students respond much better when they perceive the activity as doing work for them.

> "What we learn from this and other interviews is that biology students often bring cultural/disciplinary expectations to their classes that may get in the way of trying to create interdisciplinary instruction – but they can be context dependent."
