## Evidence

### Kabir Humayun 2021

Kabir Humayun, Hasan Md. Kamrul, Mitra Dipak Kumar. (2021). E-learning readiness and perceived stress among the university students of Bangladesh during COVID-19: a countrywide cross-sectional study. Annals of Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1080/07853890.2021.2009908

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive statistics from the cross-sectional survey of 1145 students using a modified 10-item Perceived Stress Scale (Cronbach's alpha 0.81); "76.07% (n = 871), reported moderate level of perceived e-learning stress". Note the text's mild/high labels conflict with Table 1.

> "More than three-quarters of them, 76.07% (n = 871), reported moderate level of perceived e-learning stress, and 9.08% (n = 104) reported high level of perceived stress, whereas 14.85% (n = 170) reported mild level of perceived e-learning stress."

### Kabir Humayun 2021 (2)

Kabir Humayun, Hasan Md. Kamrul, Mitra Dipak Kumar. (2021). E-learning readiness and perceived stress among the university students of Bangladesh during COVID-19: a countrywide cross-sectional study. Annals of Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1080/07853890.2021.2009908

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive statistics from the same survey using the 39-item e-learning readiness questionnaire (alpha 0.95), optimal readiness defined at mean item score 3.40; "58.17% (n = 666), reported sub-optimal level of readiness for e-learning".

> "More than half of them, 58.17% (n = 666), reported sub-optimal level of readiness for e-learning, as opposed to only 41.83% (n = 479) of them reported optimal level of readiness."
