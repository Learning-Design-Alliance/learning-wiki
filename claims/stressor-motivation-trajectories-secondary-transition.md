# Across the secondary school transition, parental academic pressure and perceived negative relationships with teachers increased while peer victimization and autonomous academic motivation decreased, with no average linear change in controlled motivation

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Wang 2025

Wang, J., Kaufman, T., Mastrotheodoros, S., & Branje, S. (2025). Longitudinal associations between academic motivation and school-related stressors in adolescents transitioning to secondary school. Learning and Instruction, 98, 102144. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102144

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Five univariate latent growth curve models on the four-wave sample of 290 adolescents (Mage = 11.58 at T1). The article reports "parental academic pressure significantly increased, while peer victimization, perceived negative relationships with teachers, and autonomous academic motivation significantly decreased across the school transition."

> "Except for controlled academic motivation, all modelled slope means were significant, indicating that, on average, significant linear change was found for parental academic pressure, peer victimization, perceived negative relationships with teachers, and autonomous academic motivation."
