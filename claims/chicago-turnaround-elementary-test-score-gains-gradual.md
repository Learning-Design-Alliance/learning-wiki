# Reformed elementary schools made significant test-score improvements versus matched comparison schools, with gains growing gradually over four years

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Elementary schools that underwent reform improved reading and math scores more than matched comparison schools, but improvements were only marginal in the first year and grew over four years. [→ de la Torre 2013](#de-la-torre-2013)
`q3 i?` Four years after intervention, the gap between reformed elementary schools and the system average decreased by nearly half in reading and almost two-thirds in math, net of student-population changes. [→ de la Torre 2013 (2)](#de-la-torre-2013-2)

## Evidence

### de la Torre 2013

de la Torre, M., Allensworth, E., Jagesic, S., Sebastian, J., & Salmonowicz, M.; with Meyers, C., & Gerdeman, R. D. (2013). Turning Around Low-Performing Schools in Chicago. University of Chicago Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/turning-around-low-performing-schools-chicago-full-report

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Difference-in-difference comparison of reformed elementary schools against matched non-reformed schools with similar prior performance and student types, using standardized ITBS/ISAT scores in grades 3-8. The report finds "significant improvements in test scores compared with similar schools" but notes gains were not immediate.

> "Elementary schools that went through reform made significant improvements in test scores compared with similar schools that did not; however, large improvements in achievement did not occur immediately."

### de la Torre 2013 (2)

de la Torre, M., Allensworth, E., Jagesic, S., Sebastian, J., & Salmonowicz, M.; with Meyers, C., & Gerdeman, R. D. (2013). Turning Around Low-Performing Schools in Chicago. University of Chicago Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/turning-around-low-performing-schools-chicago-full-report

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Trend analysis of standardized reading and math scores four years after intervention, adjusted for changes in entering student characteristics. The gap to the system average "decreased by nearly half in reading and by almost two-thirds in math." No effect size is printed.

> "The gap in test scores between reformed elementary schools and the system average decreased by nearly half in reading and by almost two-thirds in math four years after the intervention took place."
