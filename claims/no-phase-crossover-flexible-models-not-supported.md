# No evidence for phase-crossover effects predicted by flexible models

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Linguistic difficulty and abilities did not affect the second solution phase, and mathematical difficulty and abilities did not affect the first solution phase, contrary to flexible-model predictions. [→ Strohmaier 2025](#strohmaier-2025)

## Evidence

### Strohmaier 2025

Strohmaier, A. R., Schons, C., Knabbe, A., Vogel, M., Saado, K., & Obersteiner, A. (2025). The relation between text comprehension and computation in mathematical word-problem solving. Learning and Instruction, 101, 102235. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102235

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Null finding from the linear mixed models in this eye-tracking experiment (n = 47); Table 2 shows non-significant estimates (e.g., numerical difficulty first-phase text p = .389; lexical difficulty second phase p = .171). No effect sizes printed for these tests.

> "Contrary to the prediction of flexible models, we did not find evidence for phase crossover effects, such that linguistic problem difficulty or abilities would affect the second solution phase, or that mathematical problem difficulty or abilities would affect the first solution phase."
