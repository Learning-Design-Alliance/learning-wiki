Evidence¶

Buckley 2026¶

Buckley, J., Sorby, S., Duffy, G., Maresch, G., & Power, J. (2026). Gender Differences in Mental Rotation and Visualisation Across Adolescence: Large Scale Evidence from the United States, Ireland, and Austria with Single-Paper Meta-Analyses. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10149-0

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r1

Single-paper meta-analysis pooling three large-scale datasets (US multi-cohort panel; Irish and Austrian cross-sectional studies) measuring mental rotation via the PSVT:R and RIF platform. The meta-analysis "reveal[s] medium and small gender differences respectively favouring boys"; no numeric effect size is printed in the available text.

"Single-paper meta-analyses, for both mental-rotation and visualisation tasks, reveal medium and small gender differences respectively favouring boys, with these gender differences already apparent from the beginning of adolescence."
