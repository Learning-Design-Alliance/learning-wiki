# Self-esteem is positively associated with academic performance in Indian adolescents, while life satisfaction is not

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Self-esteem significantly predicted academic performance (β = 0.363, p = .004) in the adjusted multilevel model. [→ Kunnel John Roshin 2020](#kunnel-john-roshin-2020)
`q2 i?` Life satisfaction did not significantly predict academic performance (β = 0.093, p = .244). [→ Kunnel John Roshin 2020 (2)](#kunnel-john-roshin-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Kunnel John Roshin 2020

Kunnel John Roshin, Xavier Boby, Waldmeier Anja, Meyer Andrea Hans, Gaab Jens. (2020). The governmental ranking of class and the academic performance of Indian adolescents. PLoS ONE 15(11): e0241483. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0241483

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Covariate association from the multilevel regression on 858 students, adjusted for governmental classes and the other covariates, with self-esteem measured by the 10-item Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale. The article reports "β = 0.363, SE = 0.124, p = .004"; no effect size is printed.

> "self-esteem, β = 0.363, SE = 0.124, p = .004; life satisfaction β = 0.093, SE = 0.079, p = .244;"

### Kunnel John Roshin 2020 (2)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`
