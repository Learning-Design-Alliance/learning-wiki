# On-grade mathematics item pools yield larger SEMs and lower efficiency than all-grade pools for on-grade students, especially at the low end of the scale

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In CAT simulations, SEMs for on-grade simulees were consistently larger with the on-grade mathematics pool (average SEM 3.16, above the desirable SEM < 3) than with ±1-grade and all-grade pools, with the largest values at the low end of the scale. [→ Li 2019](#li-2019)

## Evidence

### Li 2019

Li, S., & Meyer, J. P. (2019). Simulation study for evaluating MAP® Growth™ grade-level item pools. Portland, OR: NWEA. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/simulation-study-for-evaluating-map-growth-item-pools-with-grade-level-constraints/

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

CAT simulation of mathematics item pools using the SAS sandbox tool, with 500 simulees per true RIT point drawn from empirical 2016–2017 distributions. The on-grade pool's "average SEM for the on-grade pool is 3.16", versus averages below 3 for the ±1-grade and all-grade pools.

> "The average SEM for the on-grade pool is 3.16, which is above the desirable value for precise measurement (i.e., SEM < 3). In the worst case (Grade 8, RIT = 190), the on-grade test would need to be 2.8 times longer than a test from the all-grade pool"
