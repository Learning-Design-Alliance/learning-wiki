## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Among individually randomised eligible pupils, those allocated to additional small group teaching scored significantly higher in extended writing than those receiving whole-class teaching only (0.67 marks, p=0.02, effect size 0.21, 95% CI 0.04 to 0.38). [→ Torgerson 2014](#torgerson-2014)

## Evidence

### Torgerson 2014

Torgerson, D., Torgerson, C., Mitchell, N., Buckley, H., Ainsworth, H., Heaps, C., & Jefferson, L. (2014). Grammar for Writing: Evaluation Report and Executive Summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://www.educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/evaluation

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r3`

Primary individual-level multilevel regression on 631 pupils from 50 schools (210 small group, 421 whole class only), adjusted for predicted KS2 level, gender, FSM, EAL and month of birth. The article reports a "statistically significant increase of 0.67 marks" and an effect size of 0.21 (95% CI: 0.04 to 0.38).

> "There was some evidence of a difference in extende d writing score between the allocated groups, with a statistically significant increase of 0.67 marks for those in the small group arm when compared with those in the whole group arm (p=0.02, 95% CI: 0.12 to 1.23)."
