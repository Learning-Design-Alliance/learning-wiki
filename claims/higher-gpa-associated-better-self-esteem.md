## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Compared to students with GPA 2.99 or less, students with GPA 3–3.99 had significantly lower adjusted odds of low self-esteem (AOR=0.308), as did GPA 4–4.49 (AOR=0.574), but the 4.75–5 band was not significant. [→ Alghamdi Saleh A. 2023](#alghamdi-saleh-a-2023)

### Alghamdi Saleh A. 2023

The impact of low self-esteem on academic achievement and the behaviors related to it among medical students in Saudi Arabia. Saudi Medical Journal. https://doi.org/10.15537/smj.2023.44.6.20230055

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariate regression (Table 5) in the 1,099-student cross-sectional survey, with GPA 2.99 or less as reference. The article prints AOR=0.308 (p=0.001) and AOR=0.574 (p=0.014), with the highest band non-significant (p=0.173). No standardized effect size (d, r) is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "the chance of low esteem among students with GPA ratings between 3 to 3.99 was estimated to decrease by at least 70% (AOR=0.308; 95% CI=0.155 – 0.612; p=0.001), and by almost 40% in GPA ratings of 4 to 4.99 (AOR=0.574; 95% CI=0.365 – 0.894; p=0.014), but it did not reach statistical significance in GPA ratings between 4.75 to 5 (p=0.173)"
