# Normal-hearing children demonstrate novel word learning on the IPLP task at approximately 18 months of age and older

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Normal-hearing 18- and 21-month-olds looked significantly longer to the target than the nontarget, while 12- and 15-month-olds did not. [→ Derek M. Houston 2012](#derek-m-houston-2012)

## Evidence

### Derek M. Houston 2012

Derek M. Houston, Jessica Stewart, Aaron Moberly, George Hollich, and Richard T. Miyamoto. (2012). Word learning in deaf children with cochlear implants: effects of early auditory experience. Dev Sci. 2012 May ; 15(3): 448–461. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-7687.2012.01140.x

`q3 · i?`

Repeated-measures ANOVA on NH groups' longest looks during test blocks. The 21-month-olds showed a significant target preference (F(1, 27) = 7.05, p = .01, ηp2 = .21); 12- and 15-month-olds did not look significantly longer to the target versus the nontarget.

> "Looking time preference for the target approached statistical significance in the 18-month-olds (F(1, 24) = 3.06, p = .08, ηp2 = .11) and was significant in the 21-month-olds (F(1, 27) = 7.05, p = .01, ηp2 = .21)."
