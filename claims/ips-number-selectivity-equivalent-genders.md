# Children engaged number-selective neural responses in the intraparietal sulci during educational videos, and boys and girls did so equally

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across three natural viewing tasks, children showed number- or math-selective activation in the intraparietal sulci, and independent t tests found no differences between girls and boys. [→ Kersey 2019](#kersey-2019)

## Evidence

### Kersey 2019

Kersey, A. J., Csumitta, K. D., & Cantlon, J. F. (2019). Gender similarities in the brain during mathematics development. npj Science of Learning 4:19. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0057-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Three naturalistic viewing fMRI studies (Studies 1–3) showing right IPS number/math selectivity, e.g. Study 2 RIPS: t(34) = 6.86, Cohen's d = 1.16. The article reports independent t tests suggested "no differences between girls and boys (max t value = 0.84, min p value = 0.41)" with Bayes factors favoring similarities.

> "Children showed number-selective neural activation in the intraparietal sulci (IPS) during the mathematics content in the educational videos (Fig. 2) consistent with previous fMRI research on numerical cognition in children and adults."
