## Evidence

### Sincovich A 2025

Sincovich A, Sechague Monroy N, Smithers LG, Brushe M, Boulton Z, Rozario T, Gregory T. (2025). Breakfast skipping and academic achievement at 8–16 years: a population study in South Australia. Public Health Nutr 28(1), e20. https://doi.org/10.1017/S1368980024002258

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Descriptive prevalence analysis (Table 2) of the 28,651-student sample. The article illustrates the gradient: "among students who reported always skipping breakfast, 29·4 % had low achievement on reading, compared with 19·6 % among students who reported sometimes skipping, and 13·8 % among those who reported never skipping breakfast."

> "A gradient in the percentage of children with low v. high academic achievement across breakfast skipping categories was observed for all NAPLAN tests, with a larger percentage of students who sometimes or always skip breakfast having low achievement, compared to those who reported never skipping breakfast (Table 2)."
