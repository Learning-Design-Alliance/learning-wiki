# Individual differences in error-induced quitting are stable across the addition and subtraction domains

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Users' random slopes for sequential errors correlate strongly across the addition and subtraction games (r = .64). [→ Johansson 2024](#johansson-2024)
`q2 i3` Users' baseline quitting rates correlate strongly across the two domains (r = .80). [→ Johansson 2024 (2)](#johansson-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Johansson 2024

Johansson, A. M., Savi, A. O., van der Maas, H. L. J., & Hofman, A. D. (2024). Three Strikes and Who is Out? Individual Differences in Error-Induced Quitting. https://github.com/ann1ejohansson/three-strikes

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r3`

Mixed-effects models refit on subtraction-game data for n = 1765 users who met selection criteria in both domains. The subtraction model showed a significant fixed effect of sequential errors (β = 0.74; SE = 0.007; p < .001) and a random slope variance of 0.12 (σ = 0.35).

> "There was a strong correlation between users’ individual effects of sequential errors on quitting (random slopes) in the addition and the subtraction game (r = .64; p < .001)."

### Johansson 2024 (2)

Johansson, A. M., Savi, A. O., van der Maas, H. L. J., & Hofman, A. D. (2024). Three Strikes and Who is Out? Individual Differences in Error-Induced Quitting. https://github.com/ann1ejohansson/three-strikes

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Correlations between random intercepts and slopes extracted from the same users' addition and subtraction data, visualized in Figure 4 with effect estimates from 300 randomly sampled users.

> "Likewise, there was a strong correlation between users’ baseline quitting rates in both domains (r = .80; p < .001; Figure 4; right)."
