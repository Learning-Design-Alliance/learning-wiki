## Evidence

### Jaeseo Lim and Jooyong Park 2026

Jaeseo Lim and Jooyong Park (2026). Self-study followed by discussion enhances learning for high school students. Instructional Science (2026) 54:56. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09801-7

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Delayed test administered one week after the learning session. The contrast was reported as "statistically significant, t(71) = 4.49, p < .001, d = 1.05, indicating a large effect."

> "For long-term retention, students in the self-study condition (M = 3.97, SD = 1.44) scored higher than those in the lecture condition (M = 2.54, SD = 1.27), as presented in Table 2."

### Jaeseo Lim and Jooyong Park 2026 (2)

Jaeseo Lim and Jooyong Park (2026). Self-study followed by discussion enhances learning for high school students. Instructional Science (2026) 54:56. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09801-7

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Delayed near-transfer contrast reported as "statistically significant, t(71) = 6.51, p < .001, d = 1.52, indicating a very large effect."

> "Students in the self-study condition (M = 8.82, SD = 2.58) outperformed those in the lecture condition (M = 4.74, SD = 2.77)."

### Jaeseo Lim and Jooyong Park 2026 (3)

Jaeseo Lim and Jooyong Park (2026). Self-study followed by discussion enhances learning for high school students. Instructional Science (2026) 54:56. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09801-7

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Within-condition change from immediate to delayed retention. A parallel pattern was reported for transfer: lecture declined, t(68) = 2.38, p = .020, d = 0.57; self-study showed no significant change, t(74) = 0.95, p = .346.

> "Over time, students in the lecture condition showed a significant decline in retention scores, t(68) = 3.41, p < .001, d = 0.82, whereas no significant change was observed in the self-study condition, t(74) = 1.06, p = .294, d = 0.25."
