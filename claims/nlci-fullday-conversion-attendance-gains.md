# Four schools that converted half-day pre-k to full-day showed attendance about 4 percentage points higher than matched comparison schools after expansion

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the North Lawndale Cluster Initiative study, pre-k attendance at the four converting schools was about 4 percentage points higher than at propensity-matched comparison schools in 2015-16 and 2016-17, a statistically significant difference representing about seven additional days of attendance. [→ Ehrlich 2021](#ehrlich-2021)

## Evidence

### Ehrlich 2021

Ehrlich, S.B., Allensworth, E.M., & Tansey, J. (2021). Meeting families' needs: Attendance rates in full-day vs. half-day pre-k. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/meeting-families-needs

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental propensity-score matching study of four NLCI schools versus weighted comparison schools, using hierarchical linear models with students nested within schools and student demographic controls. Baseline equivalence on pre-k attendance was established in 2012-13.

> "Pre-k attendance rates in the NLCI schools improved in the 2015-16 school year and were about 4 percentage points higher than at comparison schools through the 2016-17 school year. The differ -ences were statistically significant despite the small number of schools"
