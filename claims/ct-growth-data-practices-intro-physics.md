# Students in a computationally intensive introductory physics course show large-magnitude growth in Data Practices and moderate growth in Computational Problem-Solving and Systems Thinking on written CT assessments

## Evidence

### Sean Savage 2026

Sean Savage, Anand Shanker, Grace Michlitsch, and N. Sanjay Rebello. (2026). Using LLMs to Detect Growth in Computational Thinking in Introductory Physics. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06200 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2608.06200](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2608.06200)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Paired t-test on the human-graded sample of N=50 students comparing pre- and post-instruction written responses in a calculus-based engineering physics course. The article reports "large-magnitude growth in Data Practices" with d = 1.03, and moderate growth in CPP and STP; all p-values fell below the Bonferroni-corrected threshold of 0.01.

> "Students demonstrated highly significant, large-magnitude growth in Data Practices (p <0.001,d= 1.03), as well as moder- ate growth in Computational Problem-Solving (p <0.001, d= 0.52) and Systems Thinking (p <0.001,d= 0.45)."
