# Students report that tests dominate assessment, carry too much weight, and create stress, while extended performance-based assessments offer better opportunities to learn and showcase learning

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students overwhelmingly feel tests carry too much weight and create stress and anxiety, and perceive tests as unfair when tested content does not align with lessons and homework. [→ Viki M. Young 2026](#viki-m-young-2026)
`q2 i?` Students report they learn better and more consistently through extended performance tasks such as projects, presentations, and essays, which require skills like planning and collaboration. [→ Viki M. Young 2026 (2)](#viki-m-young-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Viki M. Young 2026

Viki M. Young, Ph.D., Mai Chou Vang, Zareen Kasad, Isabelle Acosta, and Alicia Frausto with Cassie Graves. (2026). Reimagining High School: Student Experiences Today, Hopes for Tomorrow. Digital Promise, Center for Learner Pathway Innovations. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/22bd5fad-ec7b-4ec7-a86f-0bed0800ba30

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Focus-group findings on Assessment Practices. One student noted homework and test mismatch: "when it comes to the test, it's something significantly harder and something that we've just never practiced before."

> "Students recognize that tests are an unavoidable aspect of their education, but they overwhelmingly feel that tests carry too much weight and create stress and anxiety."

### Viki M. Young 2026 (2)

Viki M. Young, Ph.D., Mai Chou Vang, Zareen Kasad, Isabelle Acosta, and Alicia Frausto with Cassie Graves. (2026). Reimagining High School: Student Experiences Today, Hopes for Tomorrow. Digital Promise, Center for Learner Pathway Innovations. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/22bd5fad-ec7b-4ec7-a86f-0bed0800ba30

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Same assessment findings section: students advocate diverse assessment options including projects, presentations, and essays; a 12th-grader described a sociology course with two research papers and a group presentation instead of an exam, where "so many more people were interactive and actually focusing on the class."

> "Students report they learn better and more consistently through such extended performance tasks, which give more time to prepare and engage deeply with the content."
