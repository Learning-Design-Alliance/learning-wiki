# Infants and adults automatically take account of others' false beliefs, as shown by looking time and reaction time

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Seven-month-old infants and adults showed shorter surprise responses to an object's appearance when an observer with a false belief that the object was still present had returned. [→ Frith Chris D 2012](#frith-chris-d-2012)

## Evidence

### Frith Chris D 2012

Frith Chris D. (2012). The role of metacognition in human social interactions. Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B: Biological Sciences. https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2012.0123

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

A looking-time/reaction-time study (Kovács et al., as reviewed here) in which infants and adults watched a ball hide behind a screen while an observer (a Smurf) was present or absent. The review reports that a falsely believing observer's presence reduced surprise responses in both age groups.

> "In the presence of a Smurf who falsely believed that the ball was still present, they were not so surprised (in terms of shorter looking time) by the appearance of the ball even though they had seen it leave."
