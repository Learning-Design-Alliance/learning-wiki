## Evidence

### Fallon 2018

Fallon, T. L., Aylett, R., Minnis, H. and Rajendran, G. (2018). Investigating social vulnerability in children using computer mediated role-play. Computers and Education, 125, pp. 458-464. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2018.06.027

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Pearson correlations in the Results section of the same 54-child study: "r = -.439" (p < 0.001) for paper pencil and "r = -.379" (p = 0.003) for computer role-play. The authors treat this as validating the measure.

> "Theory of Mind was negatively correlated to both DSE tasks with a Pearson correlation coefficient of r = -.439 and a significance value of < 0.001 for the paper pencil task and Pearson correlation coefficient of r = -.379 and p = 0.003 for the computer mediated role-play task."
