## Evidence

### Zhao Yao 2026

Zhao Yao, Zhou Haoyu. (2026). Functional data analysis of college students’ sleep patterns and their relationships with academic performance and social networks: A four-year longitudinal study. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0351120

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Functional analysis of the population-average sleep curve across all 76 students (Fig 2) shows an upward trend from roughly 410–430 minutes in freshman year to 420–440 minutes by senior year; the article reports "β=4.13 minutes/year, 95% CI: [2.26, 6.00], p < 0.001". No standardized effect size is printed.

> "This gradual increase was statistically significant (β=4.13 minutes/year, 95% CI: [2.26, 6.00], p < 0.001)."

### Zhao Yao 2026 (2)

Zhao Yao, Zhou Haoyu. (2026). Functional data analysis of college students’ sleep patterns and their relationships with academic performance and social networks: A four-year longitudinal study. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0351120

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The smoothed average curve for the 76-student sample shows cyclical fluctuations matching the academic calendar, with summer peaks of 455 and 450 minutes and troughs at fall semester starts and high-stress periods; these are descriptive curve features, not tested contrasts.

> "Peak sleep durations occur during summer periods (May-August), with the highest peaks observed in Summer 2016 (455 minutes) and Summer 2018 (450 minutes). Troughs consistently appear at the beginning of fall semesters (August-September) and during high-stress academic periods (November and February-March)."
