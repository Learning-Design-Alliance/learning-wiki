## Evidence

### Lahme 2024

Lahme, S. Z., Cirkel, J. O., Hahn, L., Hofmann, J., Neuhaus, J., Schneider, S., & Klein, P. (2024). From enrollment to exams: Perceived stress dynamics among first-year physics students. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020127](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020127)

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of the best (M = 1.0, SD = 0.1) and lowest (M = 2.5, SD = 0.3) high school graduation grade quartiles within the focus sample (Pre1 of groups A1 and B1). The article reports t(76) = 2.72, p = 0.008, with the printed effect size d = 0.62, CI 95% [0.16, 1.07].

> "Between these two quartiles, the PSQ scores are significantly higher for students with the lowest high school graduation grades (t(76) = 2.72, p = 0.008) with an effect size of d = 0.62, CI 95% [0.16, 1.07]."

### Lahme 2024 (2)

Lahme, S. Z., Cirkel, J. O., Hahn, L., Hofmann, J., Neuhaus, J., Schneider, S., & Klein, P. (2024). From enrollment to exams: Perceived stress dynamics among first-year physics students. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020127](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020127)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

T-test on PSQ scores at the first measuring point (Pre1 focus sample) comparing male and female students. No significant difference was found (t(152) = 0.85, p = 0.40); equivalence was not tested, and no effect size is printed.

> "A t-test shows no significant differences ( t(152) = 0.85, p = 0.40) between the PSQ scores of male students ( M = 35.1, SE = 1.4, N = 114) and female students ( M = 37.4, SD = 2.0, N = 40)."
