## Evidence

### Salami Oluwabukola 2024

Salami Oluwabukola, Wong Josephine Pui-Hing, Sawhney Monakshi, Li Jenny S., Patterson Michaela, Gumapac Nathaniel P., Sau Crystal, Akbari Ali. (2024). Addressing Culturally Based Hidden Bias and RacisM (A-CHARM) Using Simulation Experiences, Nik's Story: A Quasi-Experimental Study. The Canadian Journal of Nursing Research. https://doi.org/10.1177/08445621241253124

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-simulation CRISP-VS questionnaire (15 items used, maximum 75) completed by the 47 participants in the quasi-experimental study. All three subscale means were high; no inferential test or effect size is reported for these ratings.

> "The mean CRISP-VS score was 67.6 (SD 7.6) out of 75, with a high mean usability score of 14.7 (SD 1.1) out of 15, a high mean engagement score of 22.1 (SD 3.4) out of 25, and a high mean impact on learning score of 30.8 (SD 4.3)."

### Salami Oluwabukola 2024 (2)

Salami Oluwabukola, Wong Josephine Pui-Hing, Sawhney Monakshi, Li Jenny S., Patterson Michaela, Gumapac Nathaniel P., Sau Crystal, Akbari Ali. (2024). Addressing Culturally Based Hidden Bias and RacisM (A-CHARM) Using Simulation Experiences, Nik's Story: A Quasi-Experimental Study. The Canadian Journal of Nursing Research. https://doi.org/10.1177/08445621241253124

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive post-simulation item from the same 47-participant study: 46 participants strongly or somewhat agreed with continuing to use the simulation experience as a learning activity. Reported descriptively, with no test statistic.

> "The majority of the participants (n = 46) indicated they strongly agreed or somewhat agreed with the statement: 'I would recommend that the faculty/course instructor continue to use the SE as a learning activity.'"
