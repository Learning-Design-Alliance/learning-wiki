# First-grade ELL students taught through traditional small-group instruction significantly outperformed IC-JPA-taught students on math achievement when controlling for initial ability

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` When controlling for initial math ability, the comparison group significantly outperformed the IC-JPA treatment group on the Unit 4 math posttest, p = .047, with a near-large effect size np2 = .118. [→ Hendy 2020](#hendy-2020)

## Evidence

### Hendy 2020

Hendy, Elizabeth and Cuevas, Joshua Dr. (2020). The Effects on Instructional Conversations on English Language Learners. Georgia Educational Researcher: Vol. 17 : Iss. 2 , Article 5. https://digitalcommons.georgiasouthern.edu/gerjournal/vol17/iss2/5

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

ANCOVA on Unit 4 math posttest scores with pretest as covariate, comparing two intact first-grade classrooms (treatment n = 20, comparison n = 19). Posttest means: traditional instruction "M = 79.38, SD = 15.85" versus IC-JPA "M = 66.68, SD = 24.58"; the article prints np2 = .118.

> "when controlling for initial ability in math, there was a statistically significant difference in growth in math skills, with the comparison group significantly outperforming the treatment group, p = .047. The difference approached the threshold for being considered a large effect size, np2 = .118."
