# Skill-based AI literacy (using/understanding and detecting AI) is positively associated with reported AI dependency among higher education students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Using and understanding AI was the strongest positive predictor of AI dependency in the regression model, and detecting AI showed a smaller positive association. [→ Hilit Maizel 2026](#hilit-maizel-2026)

## Evidence

### Hilit Maizel 2026

Hilit Maizel, Maya Kalman Halevi, Miri Sarid, and Rony Tutian. (2026). Understanding Student Dependency on AI: The Role of AI Literacy, Academic Self-Efficacy, and Resource Management Strategies. Educ. Sci. 2026, 16, 1123. https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci16071123

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple linear regression with 478 Israeli higher education students predicting AI dependency from eight simultaneous predictors. The skill-based literacy subscales were positive predictors: "using and understanding AI emerged as the strongest predictor in the model overall (β = 0.404, p < 0.001)".

> "using and understanding AI emerged as the strongest predictor in the model overall (β = 0.404, p < 0.001), and identifying/detecting AI showed a smaller positive association (β = 0.108, p = 0.041)"
