## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors propose, drawing on Feedback Intervention Theory, that staff compare their behaviour to the expected standard and alter behaviour when they identify inconsistency between the two. [→ Collins Jack Charles 2017](#collins-jack-charles-2017)

## Evidence

### Collins Jack Charles 2017

Collins Jack Charles, Schneider Carl Richard, Naughtin Clare Louise, Wilson Frances, de Almeida Neto Abilio Cesar, Moles Rebekah Jane. (2017). Mystery shopping and coaching as a form of audit and feedback to improve community pharmacy management of non-prescription medicine requests: an intervention study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2017-019462

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Theoretical interpretation offered in the Discussion, citing Kluger and DeNisi’s Feedback Intervention Theory and meta-analyses of audit and feedback. "individuals compare their behaviour to the standard that is expected"; the authors note design features used (written feedback, monitoring change, individual feedback) as positive predictors from that literature.

> "This theory postulates that individuals compare their behaviour to the standard that is expected and when they identify that there is inconsistency between the two, they alter their behaviour in order to achieve this benchmark."
