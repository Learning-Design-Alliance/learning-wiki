# Task value predicted cognitive and regulatory strategy use but not classroom grades, whereas self-efficacy predicted grades in all three subjects

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Task value was the greatest individual predictor of both cognitive and regulatory strategy use in all three subject areas, uniquely explaining 13–24% of the variance. [→ Wolters 1998](#wolters-1998)
`q2 i?` Task value was a non-significant predictor of classroom performance in each of the three subject areas, while self-efficacy significantly predicted grades. [→ Wolters 1998 (2)](#wolters-1998-2)

## Evidence

### Wolters 1998

Wolters, C.A. & Pintrich, P.R. (1998). Contextual differences in student motivation and self-regulated learning in mathematics, English, and social studies classrooms. Instructional Science 26: 27–47. https://doi.org/10.1023/a:1003035929216

`q2 · i?`

Multivariate regressions in the 545-student survey used gender, task value, self-efficacy, and test anxiety to predict strategy use; "Task value was the single best predictor of regulatory strategy in all three subject areas", and similarly the greatest predictor of cognitive strategy use.

> "Task value was the single best predictor of regulatory strategy in all three subject areas. This variable alone uniquely explained between 13% (English) and 22% (mathematics) of the variance in students’ reported use of regulatory strategies"

### Wolters 1998 (2)

Wolters, C.A. & Pintrich, P.R. (1998). Contextual differences in student motivation and self-regulated learning in mathematics, English, and social studies classrooms. Instructional Science 26: 27–47. https://doi.org/10.1023/a:1003035929216

`q2 · i?`

In the same regression set predicting teacher-assigned grades, "task value indicated that this variable was a non-signiﬁcant predictor of classroom performance" in mathematics, English, and social studies, unlike the strategy use equations.

> "the standardized regression coefﬁcient for task value indicated that this variable was a non-signiﬁcant predictor of classroom performance in each of the three subject areas studied"
