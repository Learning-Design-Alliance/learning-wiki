## Subclaims
`q1 i?` When instruction is less than optimal, learners need more time, and the amount of additional time needed is an inverse function of the learner's ability to understand instruction. [→ Carroll 1962](#carroll-1962)

## Evidence

### Carroll 1962

John B. Carroll. (1962). A Model Of School Learning. Harvard University. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22A+Model+Of+School+Learning%22+Carroll

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical postulate in the Quality of Instruction section. Learners with high ability to understand instruction can figure out the task and overcome poor instruction by perceiving concepts and relationships that lesser-ability learners will not grasp. The article later asks whether this postulated interaction is correct.

> "The extent of this handicap is conceived to be a function of the learner's ability to understand instruction."
