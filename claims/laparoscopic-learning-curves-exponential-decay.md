## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Learning curves in this study were smoother (exponential decay) and efficiency was learned faster than precision, a shape also seen in several cited training datasets. [→ Kunert W 2020](#kunert-w-2020)

## Evidence

### Kunert W 2020

Kunert W, Storz P, Dietz N, Axt S, Falch C, Kirschniak A, Wilhelm P. (2020). Learning curves, potential and speed in training of laparoscopic skills: a randomised comparative study in a box trainer. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-020-07768-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' characterisation of the fitted learning curves from the randomised box-trainer study, supported by comparison with cited simulator and clinical studies. The article states the curves took "the shape of exponential decay curves" and that many literature datasets fit this type.

> "Analysis of this study revealed learning curves (LCs) in the shape of exponential decay curves. Also many other training data sets found in the literature seem to describe well this type of curve."
