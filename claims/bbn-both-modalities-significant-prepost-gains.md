## Evidence

### Alghzawi A 2026

Alghzawi A, Alhalaseh L, Khalifa S, Qudah R, Saleh A-A, Okour M, Arabas E, Samhouri S. (2026). Enhancing compassionate communication: comparing lecture and simulation training in breaking bad news training. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09034-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental pre-test/post-test study of 267 medical students (129 fifth-year lecture, 138 sixth-year role-play). Table 2 reports perceived knowledge rising from 49.25 to 73.97 (/100), competence from 13.93 to 16.15 (/20), comfort from 2.67 to 3.50 (/5), all "< 0.001". No effect sizes printed in the table.

> "After the training, students across both groups reported increases in self-perceived knowledge, self-rated competence, and comfort related to delivering BBN (Table 2). Paired-sample t-tests indicated statistically significant pre–post changes across all measured domains."
