Evidence¶

Afef Saihi and Vian Ahmed 2026¶

Afef Saihi and Vian Ahmed. (2026). Uncovering adoption personas for generative AI in higher education: a clustering-based segmentation approach. International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00583-z

q2 · i? · associational · r2

K-means clustering (k = 4) on 13 composite perceptual and behavioral variables from 192 survey respondents; personas were labeled from centroid attitudinal traits. The article states the clusters "reflect diverse configurations of trust, usefulness, efficiency, ethical comfort, and satisfaction".

"The identified clusters, Cautious Achievers, Skeptical Utilitarians, Disengaged Doubters, and Engaged Enthusiasts, reflect diverse configurations of trust, usefulness, efficiency, ethical comfort, and satisfaction with AI chatbots."

Afef Saihi and Vian Ahmed 2026 (2)¶

Afef Saihi and Vian Ahmed. (2026). Uncovering adoption personas for generative AI in higher education: a clustering-based segmentation approach. International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00583-z

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Construct profile plot (Fig. 4) of mean scores per cluster on the original unscaled data. The article also reports Cluster 2 "includes the least engaged and satisfied users with overall low scores" and Cluster 3 "exhibits the highest overall scores".

"Cluster 1 shows high efficiency ratings but low trust and personalization, implying utilitarian users who benefit but remain skeptical."

  • Demographic and contextual variables meaningfully differentiate the four GenAI adoption personas — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • A four-cluster solution is optimal for segmenting higher education users' GenAI chatbot adoption perceptions — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Faculty attitudes toward AI fall into four profiles—optimistic, critical, critically reflective, and neutral—and optimistic faculty with high AI self-efficacy are more likely to adopt AI — related
  • University students' engagement with AI tools tends to cluster at two extremes: avoidance and uncritical use — related
  • Exploratory K-means clustering identifies four heterogeneous student AI-user profiles differing in experience, competence, usefulness, trust, and control, but the solution's optimality and stability are not established — related
  • Cluster analysis identifies three stable learner profiles: Engaged and Literate, Uncritical Reliant, and Skeptical Minimal users — related
  • Cluster analysis of instructors identifies three user groups that differ in several AI literacy dimensions, with the ethics scale providing a clear distinction — related
  • Cluster analysis identifies four tentative moral response profiles: Comfortable Adopters, Guilty Non-Users, Cautious Users, and Morally Distressed Avoiders — related
  • Multiple sufficient configurations lead to high AI literacy performance (equifinality) — related
  • Control-group students framed sustainability as an external obligation and held techno-optimistic views of AI centered on efficiency with little ethical reflection — related
