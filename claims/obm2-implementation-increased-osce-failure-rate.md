# Implementing the OBM2 in a high stakes OSCE increased the failure rate from 0.7% to 4.1%

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Replacing the traditional borderline-pass rule with OBM2 reclassification increased the number of students failing the OSCE from 2 (0.7%) to 11 (4.1%). [→ Shulruf B 2018](#shulruf-b-2018)

## Evidence

### Shulruf B 2018

Shulruf B, Adelstein B-A, Damodaran A, Harris P, Kennedy S, O'Sullivan A, Taylor S. (2018). Borderline grades in high stakes clinical examinations: resolving examiner uncertainty. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1382-0

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Analysis of administrative assessment records from the Phase 1 final OSCE (N = 271) comparing pass/fail outcomes computed twice, first with the traditional method and then with OBM2 reclassification of Borderline grades. The article reports the increase from "2 (0.7%)" to "11 (4.1%)"; no effect size is printed.

> "The implementation of the OBM2 increased the number of students who failed the OSCE from 2 (0.7%) using the 'traditional method' to 11 (4.1%)."
