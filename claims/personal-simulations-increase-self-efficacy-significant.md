# In one uncontrolled pre-post study, interns' self-rated self-efficacy changed from pre-test to post-test after personal simulations across professional-knowledge confidence, responding to patients, and coping with patient resistance (all p < 0.01)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Paired-samples t-tests revealed significant pre-post differences in interns' self-rated confidence in professional knowledge, belief they know how to respond to patients, preparation confidence, and belief in coping with each patient and with patient resistance. ## Evidence

### Linder Ifat 2025

Linder Ifat, Lukach Zafit, Pergament Keren, Steckler Rafi, Tepper Sigal. (2025). The effect of simulation training on the counseling skills of nutritional science students in dietetic internships. BMC Medical Education 25:1114. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07674-x

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre-post paired-samples t-test analysis of 20 interns' self-efficacy questionnaires, results plotted in Fig. 3, where the caption reports "all p < 0.01". Only significance is printed; no effect sizes are reported, so impact is coded null.

> "An examination of the self-efficacy parameters before and after the personal simulation revealed significant differences in the interns' sense of confidence in their professional knowledge in their belief that they know how to respond to patients, in their confidence that they felt in preparation for consultations, and in their belief in their ability to cope with each patient and with patient resistance during the consultation (Fig. 3)."
