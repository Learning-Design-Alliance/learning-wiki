## Subclaims
`q3 i?` At the six-month unannounced follow-up, no significant difference in guideline-conform CPR was detected between the intervention group (71.4%) and the control group (54.5%); equivalence was not tested. [→ Berger C 2019](#berger-c-2019)

## Evidence

### Berger C 2019

Berger C, Brinkrolf P, Ertmer C, Becker J, Friederichs H, Wenk M, Van Aken H, Hahnenkamp K. (2019). Combination of problem-based learning with high-fidelity simulation in CPR training improves short and long-term CPR skills: a randomised single blinded trial. BMC Medical Education 19:180. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1626-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Unannounced six-month follow-up scenario with investigators blinded to prior group allocation; 50 of the initially enrolled students participated. Both groups reached similar improved results; only 45% of initial participants took part, which the authors note may create selection bias.

> "During the follow-up session, no differences in guideline conform CPR between groups (71.4% vs. 54.5%, p = 0.55; Fig. 1) were found."
