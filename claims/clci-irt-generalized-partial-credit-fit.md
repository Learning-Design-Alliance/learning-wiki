## Evidence

### Wörner 2022

Wörner, S., Becker, S., Küchemann, S., Scheiter, K., & Kuhn, J. (2022). Development and validation of the Converging Lenses Concept Inventory for middle school physics education. Receipt Date: 19.05.2022. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020131)

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Item response theory analysis with the mirt package on the posttest data of 318 students, using a generalized partial credit model because items were scored 0, 1, or 2 points. The article states this "indicates an overall acceptable fit of the generalized partial credit model to the data"; fit indices are not effect sizes.

> "the SRMSR value = .079 is below the recommended cutoff value of SRMSR <= .08 and the CFI = .763 is below the recommended .95 threshold [29]. This indicates an overall acceptable fit of the generalized partial credit model to the data."

### Wörner 2022 (2)

Wörner, S., Becker, S., Küchemann, S., Scheiter, K., & Kuhn, J. (2022). Development and validation of the Converging Lenses Concept Inventory for middle school physics education. Receipt Date: 19.05.2022. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020131)

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same IRT analysis of item parameters for all 16 original items; slope parameters "ranged from -0.025 (Q2) to 0.800 (Q14)", identifying Q14 as most discriminating and Q2's negative slope as problematic. Slope estimates are item statistics, not effect sizes.

> "The values of the slope parameter (a-parameter) ranged from -0.025 (Q2) to 0.800 (Q14). Larger values (steeper slopes) indicate better differentiation of learners’ abilities."
