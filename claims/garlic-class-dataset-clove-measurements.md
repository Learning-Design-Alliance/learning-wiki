## Evidence

### Nathan Moore and John Deming 2008

Nathan Moore and John Deming. (2008). Using Garlic As A Far-Transfer Problem of Proportional And Probabilistic Reasoning. https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.2133 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0811.2133](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0811.2133)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

Class dataset from 15 students' bulbs written on the whiteboard (Table I); each student massed a bulb and counted plantable cloves. The article reports the average and per-clove mass as descriptive statistics.

> "The bulbs averaged 15 .5 plantable cloves per bulb, with an average mass per plantable clove of about 3.5 grams."

### Nathan Moore and John Deming 2008 (2)

Nathan Moore and John Deming. (2008). Using Garlic As A Far-Transfer Problem of Proportional And Probabilistic Reasoning. https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.2133 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0811.2133](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0811.2133)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Descriptive classification rule applied in class: cloves at or above a dime's diameter counted as plantable, giving the printed 91% usable-mass figure. The article notes fluctuations in reported measurement precision led to a significant-figures discussion.

> "Our cutoﬀ was that only cloves with diameter similar to or larger than a dime would have an acceptable yield. This means that by mass, only 91% of the bulb was usable for seed."
