## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the multifactorial multilevel model, trainers' clinical position was the most influential predictor of 'overall' mini-CEX scores; residents gave higher scores than heads of department (regression coefficient 0.55, 95% CI 0.26–0.84, p < .001). [→ Rogausch A 2015](#rogausch-a-2015)

## Evidence

### Rogausch A 2015

Rogausch A, Beyeler C, Montagne S, Jucker-Kupper P, Berendonk C, Huwendiek S, Gemperli A, Himmel W. (2015). The influence of students' prior clinical skills and context characteristics on mini-CEX scores in clerkships – a multilevel analysis. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0490-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel linear regression (SAS Proc Mixed with random effects for specialties, clinics, trainers and students) of 1773 mini-CEX ratings from 512 trainers for 165 students in 45 clinics. The printed unstandardised coefficient for "residents compared to heads of department" was 0.55 (p < .001); no standardised effect size is reported.

> "Most influential for the prediction of 'overall' scores was the trainers' clinical position, with a regression coefficient of 0.55 (95 %-confidence interval CI: 0.26–0.84; p < .001) for residents compared to heads of department."
