`q3 i?` Medical students randomized to 1–2 day intervals did not reach proficiency faster than those randomized to 6–8 day intervals (mean 291 vs 299 min, p = 0.81). [→ Tang Diana Hai Yen 2024](#tang-diana-hai-yen-2024)

## Evidence

### Tang Diana Hai Yen 2024

Tang Diana Hai Yen, Østdal Theresa Bruun, Vamadevan Anishan, Konge Lars, Houlind Kim, Stadeager Morten, Bjerrum Flemming. (2024). No difference between using short and long intervals for distributed proficiency-based laparoscopy simulator training: a randomized trial. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-023-10522-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Single-center randomized superiority trial; 39 medical students randomized 1:1 to 1–2 day (intervention) or 6–8 day (control) intervals on the LapSim simulator, training four basic modules and a procedural module to proficiency. The intervention group mean was "291 min" versus 299 min for control (p = 0.81).

> "The mean time to proficiency was 291 min (95% CI [113;469], SD 89) and 299 min (95% CI [121;477], SD 89), respectively, for the intervention and control group (p = 0.81)."
