# Perceived usefulness of YPT activities is higher for students in their final or last-to-final year, suggesting YPT acts as a capstone building on existing skills

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Regression analysis found students with more time until their final exam considered YPT-related activities less useful than students in their final or last-to-final year, with no such differences for teamwork and English skills, and the opposite pattern for regular physics classes. ## Evidence

### Aneva 2022

Aneva, B., Faletič, S., Hömöstrei, M., Jenei, P., Kundracik, F., Kyuldjiev, A., Lindner, T., Němec, H., Puhr, H., & Plesch, M. (2022). Development of Soft Skills via IYPT. Wirtschaftsuniversität Wien. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2210.09648](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2210.09648)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Linear regression on student survey data with year of final exam as baseline; printed coefficients for YPT usefulness show negative values for earlier years (e.g., Ability to locate and use information: -0.476, p = 0.057 for one year before), with small R² values (0.015–0.053).

> "students that still had some time until their final exam considered the participation in YPT-related activities as less useful than students who were in their final or last-to-final year."
