# A research-based tree-thinking curriculum produced significantly larger pretest-to-posttest gains than business-as-usual instruction in introductory biology

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students receiving the experimental tree-thinking instruction improved significantly more from pretest to posttest than students receiving business-as-usual instruction (gain 1.16 vs. 0.48 on the composite). [→ Laura R. Novick and Kefyn M. Catley 2016](#laura-r-novick-and-kefyn-m-catley-2016)
`q2 i2` The between-condition advantage corresponds to a medium effect size (Cohen's d = 0.59). [→ Laura R. Novick and Kefyn M. Catley 2016 (2)](#laura-r-novick-and-kefyn-m-catley-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Laura R. Novick and Kefyn M. Catley 2016

Laura R. Novick and Kefyn M. Catley. (2016). Fostering 21st-Century Evolutionary Reasoning: Teaching Tree Thinking to Introductory Biology Students. CBE—Life Sciences Education 15:ar66. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-06-0127

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental pretest/posttest study in a large introductory biology course; 55 business-as-usual and 62 experimental students analyzed with 2 × 2 mixed ANOVAs. The condition-by-time interaction was significant, with gains of "1.16" versus "0.48"; no effect size is attached to the interaction itself.

> "The interaction indicated that the improvement from pretest to posttest was significantly larger for students in the experimental instruction condition (1.16) than for those in the business-as-usual condition (0.48"

### Laura R. Novick and Kefyn M. Catley 2016 (2)

Laura R. Novick and Kefyn M. Catley. (2016). Fostering 21st-Century Evolutionary Reasoning: Teaching Tree Thinking to Introductory Biology Students. CBE—Life Sciences Education 15:ar66. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-06-0127

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

The same ANOVA study's gain scores yield "a Cohen’s d of 0.59", characterized by the authors as a medium size effect for the experimental instruction's increased effectiveness relative to business-as-usual instruction.

> "These gain scores yield a Cohen’s d of 0.59, which represents a medium size effect for the increased effectiveness of our experimental instruction relative to business-as-usual instruction."
