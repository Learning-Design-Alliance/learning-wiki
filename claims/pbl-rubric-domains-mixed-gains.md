# Knowledge application, comprehensive care process, and self-directed learning scores rose from pre-test to post-test after PBL, but motivation to learn did not change, in one uncontrolled pre-post study

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Knowledge application, comprehensive care processes, and self-directed learning scores rose from pre-test to post-test in one uncontrolled pre-post study (r=0.494, 0.532, 0.553 per Table 2). [→ Kiyoshi Shikino 2025](#kiyoshi-shikino-2025)
`q3 i?` Motivation to learn did not change significantly from pre-test to post-test (P=.11), while interpersonal skills scores rose from pre-test to post-test (P=.03, r=0.201) in one uncontrolled pre-post study. [→ Kiyoshi Shikino 2025 (2)](#kiyoshi-shikino-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Kiyoshi Shikino 2025

Kiyoshi Shikino, Kazuyo Yamauchi, Nobuyuki Araki, Ikuo Shimizu, Hajime Kasai, Tomoko Tsukamoto, Hiroshi Tajima, Yu Li, Misaki Onodera, Shoichi Ito. (2025). Understanding Community Health Care Through Problem-Based Learning With Real-Patient Videos: Single-Arm Pre-Post Mixed Methods Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/68743

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Individual-dimension Wilcoxon comparisons from the rubric assessment across the two core time sessions. Table 2 prints effect sizes for the three improving domains: "0.494" for knowledge application, "0.532" for comprehensive care process, and "0.553" for self-directed learning.

> "Regarding individual rubric items, significant improvements were observed in domains such as acquiring knowledge applicable to community health care, developing comprehensive care processes, and fostering self-directed learning."

### Kiyoshi Shikino 2025 (2)

Kiyoshi Shikino, Kazuyo Yamauchi, Nobuyuki Araki, Ikuo Shimizu, Hajime Kasai, Tomoko Tsukamoto, Hiroshi Tajima, Yu Li, Misaki Onodera, Shoichi Ito. (2025). Understanding Community Health Care Through Problem-Based Learning With Real-Patient Videos: Single-Arm Pre-Post Mixed Methods Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/68743

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Rubric item 4 (motivation) showed no significant change (Table 2: P=.11, r=0.151); item 5 (interpersonal skills) improved with P=.03 and r=0.201. No effect size interpretation is offered beyond the article's words "small but statistically significant".

> "In contrast, no significant improvement was observed in the motivation to learn, whereas interpersonal skills showed a small but statistically significant enhancement."
