## Evidence

### Maria D. Fitzpatrick 2008

Maria D. Fitzpatrick (2008). Starting School at Four: The Effect of Universal Pre-Kindergarten on Children's Academic Achievement. The B.E. Journal of Economic Analysis & Policy: Vol. 8: Iss. 1 (Advances), Article 46. http://www.bepress.com/bejeap/vol8/iss1/art46

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Baseline D-D regression with ONGRADE as the dependent variable, using reading and math NAEP samples collapsed into seven years of data. The article reports a statistically significant increase of about one-and-a-half percentage points; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "This model also suggests Universal Pre-K slightly increased (by about one-and-a-half percentage points) the probability of eligible children being on-grade for their age, a result statistically significant at the five percent level."

### Maria D. Fitzpatrick 2008 (2)

Maria D. Fitzpatrick (2008). Starting School at Four: The Effect of Universal Pre-Kindergarten on Children's Academic Achievement. The B.E. Journal of Economic Analysis & Policy: Vol. 8: Iss. 1 (Advances), Article 46. http://www.bepress.com/bejeap/vol8/iss1/art46

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Measurement limitation stated by the article: the ONGRADE variable, defined relative to the median age for state, cohort, grade and birth month, is attenuated by redshirting, so retention effects are biased downward.

> "Because of red-shirting – the name given to the deliberate choice to start a child in school a year after he or she is first eligible made by some parents (rather than by the teacher or school based on a child's performance) – when ONGRADE is used as the dependent variable, the estimates produced are attenuated."
