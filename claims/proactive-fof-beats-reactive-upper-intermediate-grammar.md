# Upper-intermediate EFL learners taught with proactive FOF scored significantly higher on a grammar posttest than learners taught with reactive FOF

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Upper-intermediate learners receiving proactive FOF outperformed those receiving reactive FOF on the grammar achievement posttest (t(44) = 2.612, p = .012). [→ Naeimeh Bakshiri 2011](#naeimeh-bakshiri-2011)

## Evidence

### Naeimeh Bakshiri 2011

Naeimeh Bakshiri, Arshya Keyvanfar. (2011). Time Matters: Proactive vs. Reactive FOF. i-manager's Journal of English Language Teaching, Vol. 1 No. 2, April - June 2011. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1071093

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental comparison of 46 upper-intermediate Iranian EFL learners (22 proactive, 24 reactive) after 22 sessions. The independent-samples t-test on grammar posttest scores showed the proactive group significantly ahead, with a printed mean difference of 2.273 in the results table.

> "the researchers found a significant difference between the scores of the proactive group (M= 22.64, SD = 3.001) and the reactive group (M= 20.00, SD = 2.798) at the upper-intermediate level with t(44) = 2.612. p =.012 (two-tailed)"
