## Evidence

### M. Stella 2021

M. Stella, A. Kapuza, C. Cramer and S. Uzzo. (2021). Mapping computational thinking mindsets between educational levels with cognitive network science. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1093/comnet/cnab020](https://doi.org/10.1093/comnet/cnab020)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Results-section forma mentis reconstruction: students' associations and sentiment labels framed computation as "surrounded by a negative emotional aura of negative associates", which the article links to elicited anxiety and stress.

> "Students perceived "computation" as a negative concept, surrounded by a negative emotional aura of negative associates."

### M. Stella 2021 (2)

M. Stella, A. Kapuza, C. Cramer and S. Uzzo. (2021). Mapping computational thinking mindsets between educational levels with cognitive network science. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1093/comnet/cnab020](https://doi.org/10.1093/comnet/cnab020)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Closer reading of the same network shows the negative aura "emerges mainly from algorithmic concepts", perceived as dry and detached from real-world relevance; the only positive associations were real-world systems such as brain, power, logic and reasoning.

> "Taking a closer look, this negative aura emerges mainly from algorithmic concepts, the perception of which might be dry and detached from their effective relevance in real-world applications of calculations."
