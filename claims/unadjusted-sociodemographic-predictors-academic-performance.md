# In unadjusted analysis, low maternal education, low caregiver income and male child sex are associated with lower overall academic performance, while depression/anxiety and poverty proxies are not

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In unadjusted models, low maternal education (OR 2.40, p = 0.04), no or low caregiver income (OR 1.75, p = 0.02) and male child sex (OR 1.80, p = 0.02) were each significantly associated with lower overall academic performance. [→ Powis Kathleen M. 2023](#powis-kathleen-m-2023)
`q3 i?` Caregiver depression or anxiety, absence of household electricity, household food insecurity, preterm birth and low birth weight were not significantly associated with lower overall academic performance in unadjusted analysis. [→ Powis Kathleen M. 2023](#powis-kathleen-m-2023)

## Evidence

### Powis Kathleen M. 2023

Powis Kathleen M., Lebanna Lesedi, Schenkel Sara, Masasa Gosego, Kgole Samuel W., Ngwaca Martha, Kgathi Coulson, Williams Paige L., Slogrove Amy L., Shapiro Roger L., Lockman Shahin, Mmalane Mompati O., Makhema Joseph M., Jao Jennifer, Cassidy Adam R. (2023). Lower academic performance among children with perinatal HIV exposure in Botswana. Journal of the International AIDS Society. https://doi.org/10.1002/jia2.26165

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Unadjusted logistic regression models in the 398-child FLOURISH cohort, with odds ratios and p-values printed in Table 2: low maternal education OR 2.40 (1.03, 5.60) p = 0.04; no or low income OR 1.75 (1.09, 2.81) p = 0.02; male child OR 1.80 (1.12, 2.89) p = 0.02. The study reports these were "significantly associated with" lower overall academic performance; no standardised effect size is printed.

> "In unadjusted analysis, child HIV exposure status was significantly associated with an increased risk of lower overall academic performance, as was an absence of maternal formal education or highest level as primary school compared to secondary or tertiary education, and male sex of the child"

### Powis Kathleen M. 2023 (2)

Powis Kathleen M., Lebanna Lesedi, Schenkel Sara, Masasa Gosego, Kgole Samuel W., Ngwaca Martha, Kgathi Coulson, Williams Paige L., Slogrove Amy L., Shapiro Roger L., Lockman Shahin, Mmalane Mompati O., Makhema Joseph M., Jao Jennifer, Cassidy Adam R. (2023). Lower academic performance among children with perinatal HIV exposure in Botswana. Journal of the International AIDS Society. https://doi.org/10.1002/jia2.26165

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same unadjusted logistic regression models in the FLOURISH cohort (Table 2 prints caregiver depression/anxiety OR 1.07 p = 0.87; absence of electricity OR 1.48 p = 0.31; food insecurity OR 0.82 p = 0.44). The study reports these "were not significantly associated with a higher risk of lower overall academic performance"; equivalence was not tested.

> "The presence of caregiver depression or anxiety and the three proxy markers of poverty, including household income, absence of household electricity or food insecurity in the last year, were not significantly associated with a higher risk of lower overall academic performance."
