# E-CLASS scores correlate weakly with final course grade overall, with a significant but still weak correlation in beyond-first-year labs

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2015 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across all CU core lab courses, the correlation between post-instruction E-CLASS score and final grade is not statistically significant (r = 0.04). [→ Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2015](#bethany-r-wilcox-and-h-j-lewandowski-2015)
`q2 i?` In beyond-first-year labs the grade correlation is significant (r = 0.17, p = 0.03), while in first-year labs it is negligible (r = 0.004). [→ Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2015 (2)](#bethany-r-wilcox-and-h-j-lewandowski-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2015

Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski. (2015). Students' epistemologies about experimental physics: Validating the Colorado Learning Attitudes about Science Survey for Experimental Physics. arXiv:1512.06869. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010123](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010123)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Convergent validity analysis using post-instruction E-CLASS scores and letter grades from two semesters of CU's four core laboratory courses; the overall correlation r = 0.04 is "neither practically or statistically significant" (p = 0.2).

> "Aggregating across all students in all courses (N=873), we found an overall correlation coeﬃcient of r = 0 .04 between ﬁnal course grade and post-instruction E-CLASS score. This correlation is neither practically or statistically signiﬁcant (p = 0.2)."

### Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2015 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski. (2015). Students' epistemologies about experimental physics: Validating the Colorado Learning Attitudes about Science Survey for Experimental Physics. arXiv:1512.06869. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010123](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010123)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Subgroup analysis splitting the grade sample into FY (N=717) and BFY (N = 156) labs: the FY correlation is small and not significant (r = 0.004, p = 0.9), while the BFY correlation reaches r = 0.17, "statistically significant" at p = 0.03.

> "However, for the BFY labs, this correlation increased to r = 0 .17 and is statistically significant ( p = 0.03)."
