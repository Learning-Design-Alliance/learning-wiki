Evidence¶

Gwynne 2017¶

Gwynne, J.A., & Moore, P.T. (2017). Chicago's charter high schools: Organizational features, enrollment, school transfers, and student performance. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. http://cps.edu/NewSchools/Pages/RFP2017.aspx

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Chapter 5 high school outcome analysis of the primary sample (103,506 students, 2010-13 cohorts) with models controlling for eighth-grade skills, experiences, and background; charter ninth-grade attendance was 93 percent versus 88.5 percent.

"those who enrolled in a charter high school had an attendance rate of 93 percent in ninth grade, compared to 88.5 percent for students enrolled in a non-charter high school, a dif- ference of nearly 5 percentage points, or about eight days of school"

Gwynne 2017 (2)¶

Gwynne, J.A., & Moore, P.T. (2017). Chicago's charter high schools: Organizational features, enrollment, school transfers, and student performance. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. http://cps.edu/NewSchools/Pages/RFP2017.aspx

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Chapter 5 regression-controlled comparison of PLAN and ACT scores in the primary sample; charter students scored "nearly two- tenths of a standard deviation higher" on PLAN and one-quarter of a standard deviation higher on ACT, equivalent to about six-tenths of a point and one full point respectively.

"the typical CPS student in a charter high school scored nearly two- tenths of a standard deviation higher on the tenth-grade PLAN test and one-quarter of a standard deviation high- er on the eleventh-grade ACT than the typical student in a non-charter high school"
