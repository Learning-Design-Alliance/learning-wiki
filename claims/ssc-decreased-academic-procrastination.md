# General academic procrastination declined significantly from pre- to post-course, and of the six procrastination patterns only the oppositional pattern significantly declined

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` APQ general procrastination declined significantly from pre-test (M = 10.9, SD = 3.67) to post-test (M = 8.4, SD = 3.99), t(83) = 5.55, p = .001, not explained by practice effects. [→ Bowering 2017](#bowering-2017)

## Evidence

### Bowering 2017

Bowering, E. R., Mills, J., & Merritt, A. (2017). Learning How to Learn: A Student Success Course for At Risk Students. The Canadian Journal for the Scholarship of Teaching and Learning, 8(3). http://ir.lib.uwo.ca/cjsotl_rcacea/vol8/iss3/12

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre-post APQ analysis in the Results section with N = 84; the article reports the decline "was not explained by practice effects ( p = 0.5)" using the randomly chosen comparison section.

> "The general procrastination score  (N = 84) on the APQ declined significantly from pre -test (M = 10.9, SD = 3.67) to post -test (M = 8.4, SD = 3.99), t(83) = 5.55, p = .001, which was not explained by practice effects ( p = 0.5)"

### Bowering 2017 (2)

Bowering, E. R., Mills, J., & Merritt, A. (2017). Learning How to Learn: A Student Success Course for At Risk Students. The Canadian Journal for the Scholarship of Teaching and Learning, 8(3). http://ir.lib.uwo.ca/cjsotl_rcacea/vol8/iss3/12

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Subscale analysis of the APQ (n = 69) categorizing students into the six patterns of procrastination tendencies; only the oppositional pattern, defined as resistance to external directives, significantly declined.

> "When categorized into the six “patterns of procrastination tendencies” (n = 69), only the oppositional procrastination scores (i.e., resistance to external directives) significantly declined from pre-test to post-test (M = 16.9 to M = 15.9, p < .05)"
