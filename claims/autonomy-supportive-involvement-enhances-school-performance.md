## Evidence

### Pomerantz et al. 2007

Eva M. Pomerantz, Elizabeth A. Moorman and Scott D. Litwack. (2007). The How, Whom, and Why of Parents' Involvement in Children's Academic Lives: More Is Not Always Better. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430305567

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's synthesis of studies of autonomy support versus control (drawing on self-determination theory), spanning laboratory tasks, homework contexts, and longitudinal designs, finds 'parents' autonomy support enhances children' s performance in school.' No pooled effect size is printed.

> "There is now a fairly large body of research using a variety of methods consistent with the idea that parents' autonomy support enhances children' s performance in school, whereas parents' control inhibits it"

### Pomerantz et al. 2007 (2)

Eva M. Pomerantz, Elizabeth A. Moorman and Scott D. Litwack. (2007). The How, Whom, and Why of Parents' Involvement in Children's Academic Lives: More Is Not Always Better. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430305567

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports mediation evidence consistent with motivational development models: children's 'elevated perceptions of competence and intrinsic motivation' partly account for the autonomy support-achievement link. Skill development as a mediator has not received empirical attention.

> "the link between elementary school children's views of their parents as autonomy supportive rather than controlling and children's enhanced achievement is accounted for in part by their elevated perceptions of competence and intrinsic motivation"
