## Evidence

### Dante Angelo 2022

Dante Angelo, La Cerra Carmen, Caponnetto Valeria, Masotta Vittorio, Marcotullio Alessia, Bertocchi Luca, Ferraiuolo Fabio, Petrucci Cristina, Lancia Loreto. (2022). Dose–Response Relationship between High-Fidelity Simulation and Intensive Care Nursing Students’ Learning Outcomes: An Italian Multimethod Study. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph19020617

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Performance measured by two faculty raters using an ad hoc 20-item structured checklist during each simulation session of the single-group multimethod study; within-session "performance average gain" was significant in both modules.

> "A significant performance average gain was detected during both the first (Δ = +4.5/20 ± 1.6, p ≤ 0.001) and the second training sessions (Δ = +3.5/20 ± 1.1, p ≤ 0.001)."

### Dante Angelo 2022 (2)

Dante Angelo, La Cerra Carmen, Caponnetto Valeria, Masotta Vittorio, Marcotullio Alessia, Bertocchi Luca, Ferraiuolo Fabio, Petrucci Cristina, Lancia Loreto. (2022). Dose–Response Relationship between High-Fidelity Simulation and Intensive Care Nursing Students’ Learning Outcomes: An Italian Multimethod Study. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph19020617

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison across the spaced modules in the same study: an "overall average gain" of +7.2/20 points (p ≤ 0.001) and no significant loss across the 15 days (p = 0.422); equivalence was not tested.

> "An overall average gain was found when comparing the nurses’ performances demonstrated during the last simulation session with those showed in the first one (Δ = +7.2/20 ± 3.6, p ≤ 0.001). No significant loss in nurses’ performance was observed after the 15 days that separated the first and second training sessions (Δ = −0.5/20 ± 2.7, p = 0.422)."
