# Self Assessment Improves Self Regulated Learning

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · none recorded yet

When learners appraise the quality of their own work against criteria, they gain the evaluative information needed to plan, monitor, and adjust their studying — the core operations of [self-regulated learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md).

## Discussion

**Mechanism.** Self-assessment is theorized to support self-regulated learning by making performance standards explicit and by generating feedback that learners can act on without waiting for an external evaluator. It connects closely to [assessment for learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md), in which assessment information is used formatively rather than only to certify achievement. In self-regulated learning terms, self-assessment feeds the monitoring and reflection phases: learners who can accurately judge where their work stands relative to a standard are better positioned to set appropriate goals, select strategies, and persist or redirect effort — see [self-regulated learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md).

**Boundary conditions to watch.** Self-assessment quality depends heavily on the availability of clear criteria, exemplars, or rubrics; without external reference points, learners — especially novices — tend to overestimate their competence, and inaccurate self-estimates can lead to premature termination of study or misplaced practice effort. Training in self-assessment and calibration is generally treated in the literature as a prerequisite rather than an optional extra. Rubrics and worked exemplars help here by reducing the extraneous load of judging one's own work — see [cognitive load reduction improves learning](cognitive-load-reduction-improves-learning.md). Self-assessment also appears to benefit self-regulation more directly than it benefits task performance itself; gains in outcomes are typically mediated by improved strategy use rather than by direct effects on scores.

**Calibration.** A recurring theme in the literature is that self-assessment is only as useful as it is accurate. Miscalibrated learners — particularly low performers — may believe they have mastered material they have not, which undermines the monitoring function self-assessment is meant to serve. Interventions that pair self-assessment with external feedback or with comparison against exemplars tend to improve calibration, which in turn is expected to strengthen the downstream self-regulation benefits. This mirrors the broader pattern in which [feedback improves learning](feedback-improves-learning.md) only when it is actionable and tied to criteria learners understand.
