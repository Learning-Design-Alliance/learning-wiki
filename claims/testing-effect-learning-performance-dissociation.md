## Evidence

### Soderstrom 2015

Nicholas C. Soderstrom and Robert A. Bjork. (2015). Learning Versus Performance: An Integrative Review. Perspectives on Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691615569000

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Narrative review of Roediger and Karpicke (2006b), in which participants studied prose passages and then restudied the full passage or took an intervening test without feedback, with final recall after 5 min, 2 days, or 1 week. The review reports the tested group "recalled more of the material after 2 days and 1 week than the restudy group."

> "recalled more of the material after 2 days and 1 week than the restudy group, a finding that was subsequently replicated and extended"

### Soderstrom 2015 (2)

Nicholas C. Soderstrom and Robert A. Bjork. (2015). Learning Versus Performance: An Integrative Review. Perspectives on Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691615569000

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review of Hogan and Kintsch (1971), in which participants studied 40 words either three times before one free-recall test (SSST) or once before three free-recall tests (STTT). The review reports that "repeated studying was better than repeated testing" short term, while delayed recall favored the STTT condition.

> "repeated studying was better than repeated testing. Long- term recall assessed 2 days later, however, favored the STTT condition"
