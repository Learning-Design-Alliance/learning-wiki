## Evidence

### Rohmer 2022

Rohmer, O., Doignon‐Camus, N., Audusseau, J., Trautmann, S., Chaillou, A.‐C., & Popa‐Roch, M. (2022). Removing the academic framing in student evaluations improves achievement in children with dyslexia: The mediating role of self‐judgement of competence. Dyslexia, 28(3), 309–324. https://doi.org/10.1002/dys.1713

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

SEM mediation analysis in a study of 183 French middle-school students (31 with diagnosed dyslexia) tested the effect of dyslexia on three performance variables. The article reports a "significantly negative" dyslexia effect for academic literacy (β = −.46, p < .05) and academic mathematics (β = −.60, p < .01).

> "This effect was significantly negative for academic literacy (β = −.46, p < .05) and academic mathematics (β = −.60, p < .01). This means that students with dyslexia performed significantly less well than students without dyslexia in both academic tasks."

### Rohmer 2022 (2)

Rohmer, O., Doignon‐Camus, N., Audusseau, J., Trautmann, S., Chaillou, A.‐C., & Popa‐Roch, M. (2022). Removing the academic framing in student evaluations improves achievement in children with dyslexia: The mediating role of self‐judgement of competence. Dyslexia, 28(3), 309–324. https://doi.org/10.1002/dys.1713

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

In the same SEM analysis of the 183-student sample, the dyslexia effect on the non-academic illustrated literacy tasks was not statistically significant. The article reports the path coefficient as "not significant for non‐academic literacy (β = .55, p = .19)"; no effect size was printed.

> "However, this effect was not significant for non‐academic literacy (β = .55, p = .19)."
