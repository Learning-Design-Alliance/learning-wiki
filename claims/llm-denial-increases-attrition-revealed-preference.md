# Denial of LLM access increases attrition and LLMs are the most preferred study resource, acting as revealed preference

## Evidence

### Bharat Anand 2026

Bharat Anand, Lihi Idan. (2026). Generative AI and the Productivity Divide: Human–AI Complementarities in Education and Knowledge Work (Working Paper). https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18143 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.18143](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2605.18143)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Attrition analysis in the randomized experiment: the authors interpret dropout as revealed preference, noting participants most commonly cited illness or scheduling conflicts but the imbalance suggests the absence of LLM access undermined motivation to persist.

> "20 participants in the baseline group withdrew before the post-intervention exam, compared to only 9 in the LLM group. A chi-square test showed that assignment to the baseline condition was a strong predictor of attrition (p<0.01)."

### Bharat Anand 2026 (2)

Bharat Anand, Lihi Idan. (2026). Generative AI and the Productivity Divide: Human–AI Complementarities in Education and Knowledge Work (Working Paper). https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18143

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Self-reported resource preferences collected in the same experiment; LLMs were the most-selected resource at "69% of participants selected LLMs". The authors note preferences and attrition reinforce each other.

> "When asked which resources they preferred for learning, 69% of participants selected LLMs, surpassing lectures (64%), YouTube tutorials (67%), and textbooks (40%)."
