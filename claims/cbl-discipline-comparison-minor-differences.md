## Evidence

### Gartmeier Martin 2019

Gartmeier Martin, Pfurtscheller Theresa, Hapfelmeier Alexander, Grünewald Marc, Häusler Janina, Seidel Tina, Berberat Pascal O. (2019). Teacher questions and student responses in case-based learning: outcomes of a video study in medical education. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1895-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Mann-Whitney U tests on descriptive frequencies per discipline (Table 2 section). None of the printed p-values (0.81, 0.94, 0.17, 0.12) reached the exploratory 5% level; equivalence was not tested.

> "Regarding the two medical disciplines, surgical teachers asked more questions than their colleagues from internal medicine (p = 0.81 for absolute numbers and p = 0.94 for questions per minute). In contrast, students made more responses in internal medicine (p = 0.17 for absolute numbers and p = 0.12 for questions per minute)"

### Gartmeier Martin 2019 (2)

Gartmeier Martin, Pfurtscheller Theresa, Hapfelmeier Alexander, Grünewald Marc, Häusler Janina, Seidel Tina, Berberat Pascal O. (2019). Teacher questions and student responses in case-based learning: outcomes of a video study in medical education. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1895-1

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Discussion interpretation: only minor discipline differences were found, so the authors decline to conclude CBL works differently across disciplines; heterogeneity was attributed to individual teachers instead.

> "Regarding the comparison between internal medicine and surgery, we only found minor differences. On this basis, it does not seem justified to conclude that CBL works different in various medical disciplines"
