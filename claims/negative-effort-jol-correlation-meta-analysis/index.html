Evidence¶

Anique B. H. de Bruin 2023¶

Anique B. H. de Bruin, Felicitas Biwer, Luotong Hui, Erdem Onan, Louise David, Wisnu Wiradhany. (2023). Worth the Effort: the Start and Stick to Desirable Difficulties (S2D2) Framework. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09766-w

q3 · i2 · review · r2

Meta-analytic synthesis reported in the review: the correlation was "weaker for prospective than for retrospective judgments, and stronger for problem-solving than for paired associates tasks."

"a recent meta-analysis (Baars et al., 2020) revealed that learners' effort ratings correlate negatively with judgments of their learning when engaging in a learning task (r = − 0.355)."

Anique B. H. de Bruin 2023 (2)¶

Anique B. H. de Bruin, Felicitas Biwer, Luotong Hui, Erdem Onan, Louise David, Wisnu Wiradhany. (2023). Worth the Effort: the Start and Stick to Desirable Difficulties (S2D2) Framework. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09766-w

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports this moderator from the same meta-analysis: under goal-driven interpretation, higher effort leads to higher perceived learning and more use of desirable difficulties; the article notes this approach is less common among learners.

"the subset of studies where learners adopted a goal-driven interpretation of effort showed no negative correlation between effort and learning."

Related Claims¶

  • Effort and monitoring judgments show a medium negative correlation (r = −.355) during learning — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Goal-driven manipulations weaken the negative effort–monitoring correlation, which is nonsignificant under incentives, time pressure or self-agency ratings taken singly — related
  • Effort measurement type moderates the correlation: self-agency ratings show a positive association and subjective ratings a stronger negative one than objective measures — related
  • In the multivariate meta-regression, goal-driven manipulation, judgment timing, and task type remain significant moderators — related
  • Monitoring judgment timing moderates the correlation: concurrent judgments show a stronger negative association than prospective judgments — related
  • Task type moderates the correlation: problem-solving tasks show a stronger negative association than word/paired-associate tasks — related
  • Effort appraisals correlate negatively with monitoring judgments: high mental effort is associated with poorer performance judgments — related
  • Publication bias analyses suggest the negative correlation is robust but trim-and-fill adjusts it to r = −.227 — related
  • Perceived mental effort serves as a cue for monitoring judgments, which mediate the effort–outcome relation (David et al., 2024 meta-analysis) — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Mental effort and judgments of learning show a medium-sized negative correlation, moderated by regulation mode and task type — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Prior meta-analysis found a negative medium-sized effort–monitoring correlation (r = -0.35), attenuated under goal-driven manipulations — related
  • Perceived mental effort is negatively associated with monitoring judgments (β = -0.19) in a meta-analytic structural equation model — related
