## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Many students incorrectly claimed the quantum eraser setup differs from two orthogonal polarizers only in fewer photons reaching D1, and could not articulate why the quantum eraser shows interference at D1. ## Evidence

### Emily Marshman and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Emily Marshman and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Interactive tutorial to improve student understanding of single photon experiments involving a Mach-Zehnder Interferometer. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/37/2/024001](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/37/2/024001)

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Findings from individual think-aloud interviews conducted during preliminary QuILT development with 15 students in quantum mechanics courses. The quote documents the quantum-eraser difficulty; Table 1 shows 41% (UG) and 47% (G) pretest and 5% (UG) and 29% (G) posttest for this difficulty.

> "Some students incorrectly claimed that the q uantum eraser setup (see Fig. 4) is not different from the setup in which two orthogonal polarizers are placed in the U and L paths (see Fig. 3) except fewer photons would reach D1 because some will be absorbed by polarizer 3."
