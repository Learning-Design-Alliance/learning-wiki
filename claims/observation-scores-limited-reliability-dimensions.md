## Evidence

### Garet 2017

Garet, M.S., Wayne, A.J., Brown, S., Rickles, J., Song, M., and Manzeske, D. (2017). The Impact of Providing Performance Feedback to Teachers and Principals, Executive Summary (NCEE 2018-4000). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Psychometric analysis of observation ratings from the study's four annual observation windows. The article notes 39 to 47 percent of variation was measurement error for CLASS, and that single-observation reliability was .33 for CLASS and .51 for FFT.

> "In Year 2, for example, depending on the assumptions used, reliability estimates for the four-window average overall scores were between .53 and .61 for the CLASS."

### Garet 2017 (2)

Garet, M.S., Wayne, A.J., Brown, S., Rickles, J., Song, M., and Manzeske, D. (2017). The Impact of Providing Performance Feedback to Teachers and Principals, Executive Summary (NCEE 2018-4000). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Dimension-level reliability analysis of the two observation rubrics. The article concludes the measures "provided limited information to indicate the areas of practice educators most needed to improve".

> "While most teachers received ratings that differed across dimensions, the differences were not sufficiently reliable to identify dimensions for improvement, even when averaged over the year (.35 to .43 for the CLASS and .18 to .30 for the FFT in Year 2)."
