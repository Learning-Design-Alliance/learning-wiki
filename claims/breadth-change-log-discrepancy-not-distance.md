# Improvement in estimation accuracy was predicted by the log-linear discrepancy of each number, not by its distance from the feedback items

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In all three feedback conditions, pretest-to-posttest improvement was well predicted by the discrepancy between logarithmic and linear representations of each number (R2 from .62 to .76). [→ Opfer 2007](#opfer-2007)
`q2 i?` The generalization-gradient prediction failed in the 725-feedback condition, where improvement was greater for items further from the feedback items. [→ Opfer 2007 (2)](#opfer-2007-2)

## Evidence

### Opfer 2007

Opfer, J. E., & Siegler, R. S. (2007). Representational change and children's numerical estimation. Cognitive Psychology 55 (2007) 169–195. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2006.09.002

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Regression of pretest-to-posttest accuracy change per number on log-linear discrepancy across all 22 items in each feedback group. The no-feedback control showed no such relation (R2 = .12, n.s.), ruling out regression to the mean.

> "The discrepancy between the logarithmic and linear functions provided an excellent Wt to the improvement in all three feedback conditions, and the eVect was in the predicted direction in all conditions: in the 5-feedback group, R2 D .76, F(1, 21) D 64.37, p < .001; in the 725-feedback group, R2 D .62, F(1, 21) D 32.67, p < .001; and in the 150-feedback group, R2 D .73, F(1, 21) D 52.69, p < .001"

### Opfer 2007 (2)

Opfer, J. E., & Siegler, R. S. (2007). Representational change and children's numerical estimation. Cognitive Psychology 55 (2007) 169–195. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2006.09.002

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Regressing improvement on distance from feedback items, the 5- and 150-feedback groups fit the generalization gradient hypothesis (R2 = .72 and .82), but the 725-feedback group showed the opposite direction, so the article treats discrepancy, not distance, as the key determinant.

> "the relation in the 725-feedback condition was not only much weaker, R2 D .33, F(1, 21) D 9.87, p < .01—it was actually in the opposite direction of that predicted by the generalization gradient hypothesis"
