## Evidence

### Cook 1913

Cook, W. A. (1913). The problem of sex education. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 4(5), 253-260. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22The+problem+of+sex+education%22+Cook+1913

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument in the 'When Shall We Begin?' section. The author notes only one-fifth to one-twentieth of children in different communities graduate from high school but many others attend for a time, and argues reached pupils will become teachers of their less-favored fellows.

> "That high school pupils are peculiarly ripe for this instruction is almost a truism, (1) because of the new dangers that are characteristic of their physiologic age, (2) because of their emotional susceptibility."

### Cook 1913 (2)

Cook, W. A. (1913). The problem of sex education. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 4(5), 253-260. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22The+problem+of+sex+education%22+Cook+1913

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument on administration in the same section. The author believes there should ideally be some differentiation on the basis of age between pupils under and over 14, but notes the difficulty of making younger pupils feel they are being dealt with frankly.

> "The only way to establish our limit intelligently is to start in at the top of the school and work carefully down, surely not the reverse."
