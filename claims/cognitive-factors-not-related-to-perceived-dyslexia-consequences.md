## Evidence

### Bazen 2022

Bazen, Loes L.; de Bree, Elise H.; van den Boer, Madelon; de Jong, Peter F. (2022). Perceived negative consequences of dyslexia: the influence of person and environmental factors. Annals of Dyslexia. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11881-022-00274-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the regression analyses of the 123-student sample, word reading fluency betas were .068 (academic, p=.411), -.071 (anxiety, p=.384), and .103 (depression, p=.193); "Word reading fluency did not significantly contribute to any of the three types of perceived negative consequences."

> "Word reading fluency did not significantly contribute to any of the three types of perceived negative consequences."

### Bazen 2022 (2)

Bazen, Loes L.; de Bree, Elise H.; van den Boer, Madelon; de Jong, Peter F. (2022). Perceived negative consequences of dyslexia: the influence of person and environmental factors. Annals of Dyslexia. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11881-022-00274-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis in the same sample: spelling correlations were .014, -.061, -.022 and verbal intelligence -.098, -.011, .039 across academic, anxiety, and depression domains; "Verbal intelligence and spelling skills were not related to any of the perceived consequences."

> "Verbal intelligence and spelling skills were not related to any of the perceived consequences."
