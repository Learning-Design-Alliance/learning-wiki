## Evidence

### Nim C 2025

Nim C, Smith N, Starmer D, Wang S, Choi G, Alayed A, AlShareef J, Gnjatic A, Sloan K, Wong K, Funabashi M. (2025). Chiropractic students’ characteristics influencing confidence and competence in modulating spinal manipulation force–time characteristics of specific target forces: a secondary analysis of a cross-sectional study. Chiropractic & Manual Therapies. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12998-025-00577-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional study of 149 chiropractic students performing SMT on a force-sensing table; multivariable linear regression of confidence (100 mm VAS) on demographic, anthropometric and experience factors found "men were much more likely to be confident than females". No effect sizes printed.

> "Based on both univariable and multivariable analyses, men were much more likely to be confident than females, especially in higher force ranges (i.e., 400 N and 800 N)."
