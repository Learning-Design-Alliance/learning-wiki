# In a four-week randomized crossover study, EasyMED training produced OSCE gains comparable to human SP training (16.89 vs 15.36 points)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Fourteen fourth/fifth-year medical undergraduates in a randomized crossover design improved OSCE scores by 16.89 points (Group A) and 15.36 points (Group B) over four weeks under matched content and rubrics, with comparable baseline scores (t(12)=0.16, p=0.88). ## Evidence

### Bingquan Zhang 2026

Bingquan Zhang, Xiaoxiao Liu, Yuchi Wang, Lei Zhou, Qianqian Xie, Benyou Wang. (2026). Human or LLM as Standardized Patients? A Comparative Study in Medical Education. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.1243](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.1243)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Four-week controlled crossover user study of 20 recruited students (final cohort 14; 7 men, 7 women) comparing EasyMED and human SP training phases with pre-, mid-, and post-OSCE tests scored by two blinded examiners; baseline groups did not differ significantly (t(12)=0.16, p=0.88).

> "Group A improved by 16.89 points on average, while Group B improved by 15.36 points."
