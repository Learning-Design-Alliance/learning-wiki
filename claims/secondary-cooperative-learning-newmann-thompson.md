## Evidence

### Blosser 1993

Blosser, P. E. (1993). Using Cooperative Learning in Science Education. ERIC Clearinghouse for Science, Mathematics, and Environmental Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED351207

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Newmann and Thompson reviewed 27 reports of high-quality secondary research involving STAD, TGT, Jigsaw, Learning Together, and Group Investigation against conventional frontal-teaching groups. The quoted sentence carries the significance count and the printed effect-size range. The range spans 28 comparisons across several methods and is reported second-hand from Newmann and Thompson, so no single effect size is coded here (`i?`).

> "Cooperative learning was favored at the .05 level of significance for 25 comparisons (68%). Twenty-eight comparisons were used to compute effect sizes that ranged from .87 to 5.15."

### Blosser 1993 (2)

Blosser, P. E. (1993). Using Cooperative Learning in Science Education. ERIC Clearinghouse for Science, Mathematics, and Environmental Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED351207

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Method comparison within the same secondary review. The authors speculated that Jigsaw's weaker showing may reflect lack of group reward and individual accountability; most studies involved grade 7, and science was the most-studied subject though mathematics and language arts had the highest success rates.

> "The most consistently successful method was STAD, and the least successful, Jigsaw."
