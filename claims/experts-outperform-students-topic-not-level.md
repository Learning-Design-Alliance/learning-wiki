## Evidence

### Buljan Ivan 2021

Buljan Ivan, Marušić Matko, Tokalić Ružica, Viđak Marin, Peričić Tina Poklepović, Hren Darko, Marušić Ana. (2021). Cognitive levels in testing knowledge in evidence-based medicine: a cross sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02449-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cross-sectional comparison of cognitive-level scores (Kruskal-Wallis test with Dunn post hoc; Bonferroni-corrected significance 0.007) across first-year students, third-year students, and experts; the article reports "no significant differences in the scores on lower or higher cognitive levels separately between the three participant groups."

> "There were no significant differences in the scores on lower or higher cognitive levels separately between the three participant groups (Table 4)."

### Buljan Ivan 2021 (2)

Buljan Ivan, Marušić Matko, Tokalić Ružica, Viđak Marin, Peričić Tina Poklepović, Hren Darko, Marušić Ana. (2021). Cognitive levels in testing knowledge in evidence-based medicine: a cross sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02449-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Kruskal-Wallis comparison of topic scores across groups (N = 203). Experts scored highest on Study design (Md = 4, P = 0.002) and Sources of evidence (Md = 3, P = 0.004), while Evidence-based practice and Internal validity showed no significant group differences.

> "For individual EBM topics, experts had significantly higher scores on Study design and Sources of evidence, third-year students outperformed first-year students on Clinical importance and first-year students outperformed third-year students on Diagnostic studies (Table 5)."
