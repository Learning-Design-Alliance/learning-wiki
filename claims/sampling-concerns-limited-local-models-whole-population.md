# The sampling and significance concerns are limited when models target local conditions or when data cover the whole population

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r2`–`r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Not relying on sampling theory is not concerning when data mining builds specific models reflecting local conditions rather than global understandings. [→ Iwatani 2018](#iwatani-2018)
`q2 i?` When information and computing power allow analyzing the entire population, sampling is unnecessary and significance testing loses its rationale. [→ Iwatani 2018 (2)](#iwatani-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Iwatani 2018

Iwatani, Emi. (2018). Overview of Data Mining's Potential Benefits and Limitations in Education Research. Practical Assessment, Research & Evaluation, 23(15). http://pareonline.net/getvn.asp?v=23&n=15

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports this rebuttal citing Zhao and Luan (2006), who call generating global models "an ambitious and even unrealistic task" and frame the contrast as a tradeoff between population generalization and detailed, specific information.

> "That data mining is not based on sampling theory is also not particularly concerning if the technique is used primarily to build specific models that reflect local conditions, rather than to build global understandings."

### Iwatani 2018 (2)

Iwatani, Emi. (2018). Overview of Data Mining's Potential Benefits and Limitations in Education Research. Practical Assessment, Research & Evaluation, 23(15). http://pareonline.net/getvn.asp?v=23&n=15

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review argues statistical significance measures uncertainty from sampling error, so it is unneeded when information covers the whole population, a large adequate sample exists, or conclusions need not generalize far beyond the data.

> "That as long as there is information and computing power necessary to analyze the entire population, there simply is no need for sampling."
