## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Hierarchical clustering of 48 student-lab profiles on five discourse-move variables yields four styles: High on-task high social, High on-task low social, Low on-task low social, and Low on-task high social. [→ Tong Wan 2024](#tong-wan-2024)
`q2 i?` The four clusters differ significantly on all five discourse codes with large eta-squared effects (e.g., chatting off-task η² = 0.772, p < 0.001). [→ Tong Wan 2024](#tong-wan-2024)

## Evidence

### Tong Wan 2024

Tong Wan, Juliette Pimbert, Reshawna L. Chapple, Ying Cao, Pierre-Philippe A. Ouimet. (2024). Characterizing Discourse Group Roles in Inquiry-based University Science Labs. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2405.15746](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2405.15746)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cluster analysis of 48 student-lab profiles from video coding of 12 group-lab units in the 2015 IMPRESS program. Appendix Table 10 reports eta-squared values from 0.280 to 0.772, all marked large effect, with Dunn's rankings such as "A = C > B = D" for on-task codes.

> "The Kruskal-Wallis tests showed that at least one of the four clusters is distinct from the others in terms of all ﬁve discourse codes (p=0.002 for asking a question, and p<0.001 for the others) with large effect"
