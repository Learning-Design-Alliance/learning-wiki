## Subclaims
`q1 i?` One student changed his definition of "fair" between the constant-velocity and uniform-acceleration versions, leading to very different mathematical approaches; another student kept the same head start across both models. [→ Daniel L. Reinholz 2016](#daniel-l-reinholz-2016)

## Evidence

### Daniel L. Reinholz 2016

Daniel L. Reinholz, Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer. (2016). Using Peer Feedback to Promote Reflection on Open-Ended Problems. [doi:10.1119/1.4961181](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4961181)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Case comparison across the two PAR problems in the course: Corey's CV definition shaved a second off Runner B's time (8 m head start), while in the UA problem he defined fair by comparing Runner B's fastest speed to Runner A's average speed, whereas Alex and Bobbie kept their reasoning.

> "While Alex and Bobbie used the same reasoning to determine Runner B's head start in both the CV and UA versions of the PAR problem, Corey changed his definition of "fair," leading him to use very different mathematical approaches to solving the same problem in these different contexts."
