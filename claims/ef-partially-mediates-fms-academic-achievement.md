# Executive function partially mediates the relationship between fundamental motor skills and academic achievement in both Chinese and mathematics

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Bootstrapped indirect effects of FMS on achievement through EF were significant for Chinese (β = 0.110, 95% CI [0.051, 0.175]) and mathematics (β = 0.134, 95% CI [0.070, 0.189]), while direct effects remained significant, indicating partial mediation. ## Evidence

### Wang Lijing 2025

Wang Lijing, Sun Jiancui, Wang Lijuan, Zheng Danheng, Zhou Yulan. (2025). Fundamental motor skills, executive functions, and academic achievement of Chinese schoolchildren: Testing a mediating model. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0336470

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Structural equation modeling with bootstrapping (5000 samples) in Amos, controlling for age, gender, family income, and mother's education. Both models fit well (Chinese RMSEA = 0.027; mathematics RMSEA = 0.011); the indirect effect accounted for 23.76% (Chinese) and 39.88% (mathematics) of the total effect per Table 5.

> "the indirect effects of EF on academic achievement in Chinese and mathematics were statistically significant (β = 0.110, 95% CI [0.051, 0.175]; β = 0.134, 95% CI [0.070, 0.189]), thus confirming that EF partially mediated these models"
