## Subclaims
`q3 i?` RSJ reaching rates were 100%, 80% and 75% for skilled, intermediate and novice groups without significant difference (p = 0.196). [→ Rohr A. 2022](#rohr-a-2022)

## Evidence

### Rohr A. 2022

Rohr A., Perrenot C., Pitta A., Celerier I., Labrousse M., Renard Y., Cadiot G., Brugel M. (2022). Reperfused human cadaver as a new simulation model for colonoscopy: a pilot study. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-022-09763-0

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Construct validity analysis in the Results section of the prospective pilot study (n = 24). Table 2 reports the RSJ reaching-rate comparison as p = 0.196, a non-significant difference; equivalence was not tested.

> "Skilled participants reached RSJ more often than novice and intermediary groups, respectively, 100%, 80% and 75% without significantly differing (Table 2)."

### Rohr A. 2022 (2)

Rohr A., Perrenot C., Pitta A., Celerier I., Labrousse M., Renard Y., Cadiot G., Brugel M. (2022). Reperfused human cadaver as a new simulation model for colonoscopy: a pilot study. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-022-09763-0

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Results-section text accompanying Fig. 1 reporting time-to-landmark comparisons across the three experience groups in the same 24-participant pilot cohort; the authors describe a gradual increase with experience.

> "The time to reach RSJ and DC was significantly shorter for skilled participants (p < 0.01 and p < 0.01, respectively) (Fig. 1). Furthermore, reaching rates and speed gradually increased according to experience."
