# Computational physics master's students undergo two distinct competence transitions: one triggered by project-based computational courses and a second, smaller one during professional collaborative research in thesis work

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Interviewed students described a first transition from novice to journeyman level when encountering project-based computational physics courses, and a second, smaller and less explicit transition when participating in professional collaborative computational research during master's thesis work. ## Evidence

### Karl Henrik Fredly 2026

Karl Henrik Fredly, Tor Ole B. Odden, and Benjamin M. Zwickl. (2026). Physics computational literacy: Programming, modeling, and collaboration at the journeyman level. Physical Review Physics Education Research 22, 010103. https://doi.org/10.1103/6fny-yzyb

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Thematic analysis of retrospective semistructured interviews with 13 computational physics master's students at the University of Oslo, part of the study's Results I. The analysis found "two distinct transitions in students' development of PCL beyond the novice level", the second of which the authors characterize as smaller and less explicit than the first.

> "Based on the interviews with computational physics master's students, we identified two distinct transitions in students' development of PCL beyond the novice level, as well as important factors supporting the ongoing development of their computational physics expertise."
