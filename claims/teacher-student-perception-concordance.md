## Evidence

### Taylor 2007

Taylor, I. M., & Ntoumanis, N. (2007). Teacher motivational strategies and student self-determination in physical education. Journal of Educational Psychology, 99, 747-760. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.99.4.747

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Three multilevel regressions tested whether teacher self-reports of each strategy predicted student perceptions of the same strategy (51 teachers, 787 students). Only involvement was significant (ß = .24); the authors call the coefficient "relatively small."

> "teachers' reports of their use of involvement strategies predicted student perceptions of teacher involvement (ß = .24, p < .001). Interestingly, teacher perceptions of autonomy support and structure were not related with student perceptions of the corresponding strategy"

### Taylor 2007 (2)

Taylor, I. M., & Ntoumanis, N. (2007). Teacher motivational strategies and student self-determination in physical education. Journal of Educational Psychology, 99, 747-760. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.99.4.747

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel regressions tested accuracy of teacher perceptions of student motivation. Teacher perceptions predicted student-reported SDI (ß = .19) and intrinsic (ß = .35), identified (ß = .29), introjected (ß = .12), and amotivation (ß = .20) regulations, but not external regulation (ß = -.08).

> "teacher perceptions of student SDI significantly predicted student reports of their own SDI (ß = .19, p <.001). Furthermore, teacher perceptions of student intrinsic motivation (ß = .35, p <.001), identified regulation (ß = .29, p <.001)"
