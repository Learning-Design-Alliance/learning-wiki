## Evidence

### Remy Dou 2016

Remy Dou, Eric Brewe, Justyna P. Zwolak, Geoff Potvin, Eric A. Williams, and Laird H. Kramer. (2016). Beyond performance metrics: Examining a decrease in students' physics self-efficacy through a social networks lens. Physical Review Physics Education Research 12, 020124. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.12.020124

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Two independent samples t tests compared female and male students' pre and post SOSESC-P scores: "The analysis revealed that no statistically significant gender difference existed at the start of the MI courses or at the end," holding also for the disaggregated sources.

> "The analysis revealed that no statistically significant gender difference existed at the start of the MI courses or at the end."

### Remy Dou 2016 (2)

Remy Dou, Eric Brewe, Justyna P. Zwolak, Geoff Potvin, Eric A. Williams, and Laird H. Kramer. (2016). Beyond performance metrics: Examining a decrease in students' physics self-efficacy through a social networks lens. Physical Review Physics Education Research 12, 020124. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.12.020124

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

A multiple linear regression with ethnicity, major, and gender predicting post scores while controlling for prescores was statistically significant overall, but "Neither ethnicity nor declared major contributed to the variance in students ' post self-efficacy scores," with low subgroup numbers limiting power.

> "Neither ethnicity nor declared major contributed to the variance in students ' post self-efficacy scores, though to be sure, the low number of representatives from certain ethnic groups (e.g., Black) and majors (e.g., English) limited the power of our model"
