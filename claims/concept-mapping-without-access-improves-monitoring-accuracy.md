# Concept mapping during reading, without access to the map during judgment, improves students' relative monitoring accuracy

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` University students' relative monitoring accuracy was higher when constructing concept maps while reading than when making immediate or delayed judgments only. [→ Janneke van de Pol 2020](#janneke-van-de-pol-2020)

## Evidence

### Janneke van de Pol 2020

Janneke van de Pol, et al. (2020). Mapping and Drawing to Improve Students’ and Teachers’ Monitoring and Regulation of Students’ Learning from Text: Current Findings and Future Directions. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09560-y

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Experiment with at-risk university readers across three conditions; concept-mapping students received 8 × 50-min training. The review reports "higher in the concept map condition (G ± 0.68 3)" and that test performance was also higher in this condition.

> "Students ’ relative monitoring accuracy was higher in the concept map condition (G ± 0.68 3) than in the immediate (G ± 0.30) and delayed (G ± 0.33) conditions."
