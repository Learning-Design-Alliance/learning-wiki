# Learning the CVS with self-generated, vicarious, combined, or no errors during PS-I does not differentially affect CVS posttest performance when controlling for prior knowledge

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The overall effect of condition on CVS posttest performance was not statistically significant in the three-condition ANCOVA or the four-condition ANCOVA controlling for pretest. [→ Hämmerle 2026](#hammerle-2026)
`q3 i?` A planned contrast comparing the combination group to the average of the remaining conditions was not statistically significant, with a negative estimated difference. [→ Hämmerle 2026 (2)](#hammerle-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Hämmerle 2026

Hämmerle, L., Kuhlmann, S. L., Krause-Wichmann, T., & Möller, A. (2026). Learning the control-of-variables strategy through self-generated and vicarious errors: the mediating roles of affect, motivation, cognitive load, and error awareness. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09822-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

ANCOVA on CVS posttest (Rasch logit scores) with condition as fixed factor and CVS pretest as covariate, restricted to the self-generated, vicarious, and no-errors conditions of the randomized intervention (n = 255 total). The study reports "The overall effect of condition was not statistically significant, F(2, 176) = 0.94, p = .393."

> "The overall effect of condition was not statistically significant, F(2, 176) = 0.94, p = .393. Pretest performance was a statistically significant predictor of posttest performance F(1, 176) = 223.66, p < .001."

### Hämmerle 2026 (2)

Hämmerle, L., Kuhlmann, S. L., Krause-Wichmann, T., & Möller, A. (2026). Learning the control-of-variables strategy through self-generated and vicarious errors: the mediating roles of affect, motivation, cognitive load, and error awareness. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09822-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Four-condition ANCOVA including the combination group found no significant overall effect (F(3, 231) = 1.48, p = .220); a planned contrast compared the combination group to the average of the remaining conditions. The study reports the contrast "was not statistically significant t(231) = -1.62, p = .108."

> "This contrast was not statistically significant t(231) = -1.62, p = .108, 95% CI [-2.88, 0.29], b = -1.30. The estimated difference was negative, although this pattern did not reach statistical significance."
