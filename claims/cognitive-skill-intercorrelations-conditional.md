# Cognitive inquiry skills are positively intercorrelated, but the functional thinking–CVS correlation diminishes when controlling for content knowledge

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Content knowledge, CVS, and functional thinking are positively correlated after controlling for general cognitive ability, consistent with hypotheses H1.2.1–H1.2.3. [→ Gerdesmann 2026](#gerdesmann-2026)
`q3 i?` The correlation between functional thinking and CVS diminishes when content knowledge is additionally controlled. [→ Gerdesmann 2026 (2)](#gerdesmann-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Gerdesmann 2026

Gerdesmann, D., Schwichow, M., & Koerber, S. (2026). Factors that explain simulation-based inquiry performance in physics learning. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09806-2

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Partial correlations of cognitive skills (N = 205, without control group) controlling for general cognitive ability were positive and significant: CK–FT, CK–CVS, and FT–CVS, consistent with hypotheses H1.2.1–H1.2.3.

> "These correlations are consistent with hypotheses H1.2.1-H1.2.3 and confirm that functional thinking aligns with previously noted patterns between content knowledge and CVS"

### Gerdesmann 2026 (2)

Gerdesmann, D., Schwichow, M., & Koerber, S. (2026). Factors that explain simulation-based inquiry performance in physics learning. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09806-2

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

When controlling for both general cognitive ability and a third skill, the FT–CVS partial correlation dropped from 0.36 to 0.18 (pre) and 0.31 to 0.14 (post), suggesting shared content-knowledge variance.

> "Notably, the correlation between functional thinking and CVS diminishes when controlling for content knowledge in addition to general cognitive ability."
