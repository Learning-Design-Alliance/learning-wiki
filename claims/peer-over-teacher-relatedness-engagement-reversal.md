## Evidence

### Núñez-Regueiro 2024

Núñez-Regueiro, F., & Wang, M.-T. (2024). Adolescent Well-Being and School Engagement as a Function of Teacher and Peer Relatedness: The More (Relatedness) Is Not Always the Merrier. Journal of Educational Psychology, 117(3), 466-484. https://hal.science/hal-04920252v1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Study 1 cubic response surface analysis of school engagement in 643 high school students; the best-fitting asymmetric congruence model showed a rising trend on the LOIC, where "adolescents experiencing higher levels of peer relatedness than teacher relatedness tended to experience higher levels of school engagement".

> "for close-to-average values of peer relatedness, adolescents experiencing higher levels of peer relatedness than teacher relatedness tended to experience higher levels of school engagement"

### Núñez-Regueiro 2024 (2)

Núñez-Regueiro, F., & Wang, M.-T. (2024). Adolescent Well-Being and School Engagement as a Function of Teacher and Peer Relatedness: The More (Relatedness) Is Not Always the Merrier. Journal of Educational Psychology, 117(3), 466-484. https://hal.science/hal-04920252v1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same Study 1 engagement surface: past the relative maximum on the LOIC (an excess of about 1.2 SD in peer relatedness, as printed in the results), the trend reversed, so "reporting more peer than teacher relatedness was associated with lower levels of school engagement".

> "reporting more peer than teacher relatedness was associated with lower levels of school engagement (Figure 4, A3)."
