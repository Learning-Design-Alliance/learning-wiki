# PRiSE students show lasting STEM confidence and skills effects and greater physics and STEM uptake at university three years later

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Longitudinal evaluation of PRiSE at 6-month and 3-year stages shows lasting effects on students' STEM-related confidence and skills. [→ Martin Archer 2021](#martin-archer-2021)
`q3 i?` Three years later, PRiSE students showed greater uptake of both physics and STEM subjects at university than expected of physics students nationally, statistically significant despite the small sample size. [→ Martin Archer 2021 (2)](#martin-archer-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Martin Archer 2021

Martin Archer, Jennifer DeWitt, Carol Davenport, Olivia Keenan, Lorraine Coghill, Anna Christodoulou, Samantha Durbin, Heather Campbell, Lewis Hou. (2021). Going beyond the one-off: How can STEM engagement programmes with young people have real lasting impact? Manuscript in preparation for Research for All. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.14324/rfa.05.1.07](https://doi.org/10.14324/rfa.05.1.07)

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes this to longitudinal evaluation of PRiSE (Physics Research in School Environments), a scalable framework for 6-month independent research projects with 14-18 year-old London students supported by active researchers. No effect size is printed.

> "Longitudinal evaluation (M.O. Archer and DeWitt, 2020) at the 6-month and 3-year stages has shown lasting effects on students' STEM-related confidence and skills."

### Martin Archer 2021 (2)

Martin Archer, Jennifer DeWitt, Carol Davenport, Olivia Keenan, Lorraine Coghill, Anna Christodoulou, Samantha Durbin, Heather Campbell, Lewis Hou. (2021). Going beyond the one-off: How can STEM engagement programmes with young people have real lasting impact? Manuscript in preparation for Research for All. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.14324/rfa.05.1.07](https://doi.org/10.14324/rfa.05.1.07)

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports the 3-year longitudinal comparison of PRiSE participants against national physics student expectations, significant "despite the small sample size". No effect size or test statistic is printed.

> "with longitudinal evidence 3 years later also revealing greater uptake (to a statistically significant level despite the small sample size) from PRiSE students of both physics and STEM subjects at university than would be expected of physics students nationally."
