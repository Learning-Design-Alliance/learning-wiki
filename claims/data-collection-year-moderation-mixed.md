# Data collection year does not significantly predict the digital reading-comprehension association, though it becomes significant when an outlier is included

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the main moderator analysis, data collection year was not a significant predictor of the effect size (p = .121), contrary to the shallowing-based expectation of increasingly negative associations. [→ Altamura 2023](#altamura-2023)
`q2 i?` When the influential outlier was included in the meta-regression, data collection year turned significant (b = -.014; 95% CI: -.27, -.001; p = .043). [→ Altamura 2023 (2)](#altamura-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Altamura 2023

Altamura, L., Vargas, C., & Salmerón, L. (2023). Do New Forms of Reading Pay Off? A Meta-Analysis on the Relationship Between Leisure Digital Reading Habits and Text Comprehension. *Review of Educational Research, 95*(1), 53–88. [doi:10.3102/00346543231216463](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543231216463)

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Multilevel meta-regression on the 39 effect sizes found data collection year did not significantly predict the association between leisure digital reading habits and comprehension, contradicting the article's hypothesis of a more negative association in more recent studies.

> "Lastly, contrary to our expectations, the data collection year was not significant as a predictor (p = .121)."

### Altamura 2023 (2)

Altamura, L., Vargas, C., & Salmerón, L. (2023). Do New Forms of Reading Pay Off? A Meta-Analysis on the Relationship Between Leisure Digital Reading Habits and Text Comprehension. *Review of Educational Research, 95*(1), 53–88. [doi:10.3102/00346543231216463](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543231216463)

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Sensitivity meta-regression including the outlier study (Gumus & Atalmis, 2011) reversed the year result: the coefficient became significant with a negative value, indicating the year moderation is not robust to outlier inclusion.

> "The significance of the moderators remained the same except for the data collection year which turned out significant (𝑏𝑏𝑤𝑤𝑝𝑝𝐴𝐴ℎ 𝑐𝑐𝑜𝑜𝐴𝐴𝑝𝑝𝑝𝑝𝐴𝐴𝑦𝑦 = -.014; 95% CI: -.27, -.001; p =. 043)."
