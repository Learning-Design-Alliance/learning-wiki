# Students in both SP-TCM groups reported higher satisfaction, confidence, motivation, and perceived skill gains than TT students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r3` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2024 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` SP-TCM students showed higher agreement than TT students on satisfaction with the course (Pa = 0.03; Pb = 0.02), enhanced TCM clinical skills (Pa = 0.02; Pb = 0.03), confidence in handling clinical work, motivation, medical history knowledge, medical record writing, syndrome differentiation, and doctor–patient communication items. [→ Huang Meilan 2024](#huang-meilan-2024)

## Evidence

### Huang Meilan 2024

Huang Meilan, Yang Han, Guo Jing, Fu Xiaoxu, Chen Wangshu, Li Bin, Zhou Shan, Xia Ting, Peng Sihan, Wen Lijuan, Ma Xiao, Zhang Yi, Zeng Jinhao. (2024). Faculty standardized patients versus traditional teaching method to improve clinical competence among traditional Chinese medicine students: a prospective randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05779-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Post-course student feedback questionnaire (chi-square tests on Likert items) in the three-group RCT. The article reports higher approval among SP-group students across 11 items, with printed χ² and p-values, and no significant differences between FSP and OSP groups on these items.

> "The students in the FSP and SP-TCM groups showed higher levels of approval for this course compared to those in the TT group in the “satisfaction with the course”, “confidence in handling clinical work,” (χ²=11.09, Pa = 0.03; χ²=10.54, Pb = 0.03) and “Motivation to study TCM” (χ²=10.06, Pa = 0.04; χ²= 11.399, Pb = 0.02)."
