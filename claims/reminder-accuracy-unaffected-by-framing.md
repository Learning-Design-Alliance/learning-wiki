# Task accuracy is far higher with reminders than internal memory and is unaffected by reminder framing

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r3` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Forced-external accuracy substantially exceeded forced-internal accuracy in Experiment 2 (ηp2 = 0.77), while framing had a negligible main effect (ηp2 < 0.01). [→ Lea Fröscher 2022](#lea-froscher-2022)

## Evidence

### Lea Fröscher 2022

Lea Fröscher, Ann‑Kathrin Friedrich, Max Berentelg, Curtis Widmer, Sam J. Gilbert and Frank Papenmeier. (2022). Framing cognitive offloading in terms of gains or losses: achieving a more optimal use of reminders. Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-022-00416-3

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r3`

Experiment 2 repeated-measures ANOVA on accuracy data: main effect of condition (forced-internal vs forced-external) significant, F(1, 299) = 991.36, p < 0.001, with printed effect size ηp2 = 0.77 (a large effect); main effect of framing (ηp2 < 0.01) and interaction non-significant. Participants remembered "almost two-thirds" internally and "nearly all" with reminders.

> "We again obtained a significant main effect of condition, F(1, 299) = 991.36, p < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.77, but no significant main effect of framing, F(1, 299) = 0.33, p = 0.565, ηp2 < 0.01, and a non-significant interaction of framing and condition"
