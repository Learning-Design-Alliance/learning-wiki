Evidence¶

Livingston et al. 2018¶

Livingston, S., Toce, A., Casey, C., Montoya, F., Hart, B. R., & O'Flaherty, C. (2018). Effect of X-Word Grammar and Traditional Grammar Instruction on Grammatical Accuracy. English Language Teaching, 11(3). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v11n3p119

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Error-type analysis of the same three-month classroom comparison, using the ratio of errors to total attempts per construct. The article reports significance for subject-verb agreement (p=.018), borderline significance for tense (p=.091), and non-significance for aspect and verb forms (p=0.134), with larger improvement for the X-Word group on tense and number.

"Statistical comparisons for individual verb constructs indicate that the observed differences are significant for subject verb agreement (p= .018), borderline significant for tense (p= .091), and not significant for aspect and verb forms (p= 0.134)."
