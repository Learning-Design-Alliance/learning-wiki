## Evidence

### Beck Christopher 2014

Beck Christopher, Butler Amy, Burke da Silva Karen. (2014). Promoting Inquiry-Based Teaching in Laboratory Courses: Are We Meeting the Grade? CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.13-12-0245

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r1`

Synthesis of the 16 controlled studies in the review's sample. The review reports "students in inquiry-based laboratory courses had significantly higher learning gains than students in non-inquiry courses", and students enjoyed them more with more positive attitude shifts; no effect size is printed for this statement.

> "In all controlled studies that measured learning gains, students in inquiry-based laboratory courses had significantly higher learning gains than students in non-inquiry courses."

### Beck Christopher 2014 (2)

Beck Christopher, Butler Amy, Burke da Silva Karen. (2014). Promoting Inquiry-Based Teaching in Laboratory Courses: Are We Meeting the Grade? CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.13-12-0245

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r1`

Effect-size estimation from the 10 controlled studies with sufficient data (n = 10), standardizing mean differences against control-group SD. The review reports "the mean effect size was 0.73" overall and 0.62 when averaged by study.

> "For all assessment in those studies, the mean effect size was 0.73 (median = 0.61, SD = 0.71). When we took the average effect size for each study first, in order to avoid pseudoreplication, the mean effect size decreased to 0.62 (median = 0.40, SD = 0.76)"
