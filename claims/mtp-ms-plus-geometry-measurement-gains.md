# Children in MTP-M/S Plus classrooms made significantly greater geometry and measurement gains across the pre-k year than children in business-as-usual classrooms (g = .52), while Basic-classroom children did not differ from either group

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In a school-level randomized pre-k study, Plus-condition children showed significantly greater gains on the Geometry and Measurement Assessment than BaU children (g = .52), and Basic-condition children did not differ from Plus or BaU children. [→ Kinzie 2014](#kinzie-2014)

## Evidence

### Kinzie 2014

Kinzie, M. B., Vick Whittaker, J., Williford, A. P., DeCoster, J., McGuire, P., Lee, Y., & Kilday, C. R. (2014). MyTeachingPartner-Math/Science pre-kindergarten curricula and teacher supports: Associations with children's mathematics and science learning. Early Childhood Research Quarterly, 29, 586-599. http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/j.ecresq.2014.06.007

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Results of a year-long school-level randomized study of 42 pre-k teachers and 444 children in a mid-Atlantic city district, analyzed with two-level HLM controlling for fall scores and demographics. The article reports "signiﬁcantly greater gains on the GMA" for Plus over BaU with "effect size g = .52", and no difference for Basic against either group.

> "those in the Plus group had signiﬁcantly greater gains on the GMA than students in the BaU group (effect size g = .52). Students in the Basic group did not differ from those in the Plus or BaU groups."
