# Smoking and snuffing are inversely associated with high academic achievement in both adolescent girls and boys

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Smokers had lower odds of high achievement (girls AOR 0.18, boys AOR 0.37), as did snuffers (girls AOR 0.25, boys AOR 0.51). [→ Stea TH 2014](#stea-th-2014)

## Evidence

### Stea TH 2014

Stea TH, Torstveit MK. (2014). Association of lifestyle habits and academic achievement in Norwegian adolescents: a cross-sectional study. BMC Public Health 2014, 14:829. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2458-14-829

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional logistic regression of smoking and snuffing (occasional or daily use) against grades, adjusted for BMI and parental education. The girls' smoking association was "AOR: 0.18 (0.12-0.25)"; the authors state no other study has examined snuffing and achievement. No standardized effect size was printed.

> "adjusted analyses showing decreased odds of a high academic score among girls and boys reporting to be smokers (AOR: 0.18 (0.12-0.25) and AOR: 0.37 (0.25-0.54), respectively) and snuffers (AOR: 0.25 (0.17-0.37) and AOR: 0.51 (0.36-0.72), respectively)."
