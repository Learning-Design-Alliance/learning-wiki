## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Immediately after the course, the flipped classroom group scored significantly higher on self-efficacy in EBN practice than the traditional instruction group (p = 0.011). [→ Chu Tsung-Lan 2019](#chu-tsung-lan-2019)

## Evidence

### Chu Tsung-Lan 2019

Chu Tsung-Lan, Wang Jeng, Monrouxe Lynn, Sung Yu-Chih, Kuo Chen-li, Ho Lun-Hui, Lin Yueh-E. (2019). The effects of the flipped classroom in teaching evidence based nursing: A quasi-experimental study. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0210606

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same quasi-experimental study of 151 nurses. Posttest self-efficacy mean was 89.03±15.19 in the experimental group versus 82.15±17.52 in controls, with the experimental group showing "significantly higher scores than the control group" on the SE-EBP scale.

> "T1 scores revealed that all the participants presented better performance, with the experimental group had significantly higher scores than the control group (knowledge p = 0.015; self-efficacy p = 0.011)."
