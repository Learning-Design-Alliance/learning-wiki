## Evidence

### Collins Jack Charles 2017

Collins Jack Charles, Schneider Carl Richard, Naughtin Clare Louise, Wilson Frances, de Almeida Neto Abilio Cesar, Moles Rebekah Jane. (2017). Mystery shopping and coaching as a form of audit and feedback to improve community pharmacy management of non-prescription medicine requests: an intervention study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2017-019462

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Binary logistic regression on the 521 eligible visits with pharmacist involvement, visit number, questioning score, legislative status, shopper identification, pharmacy and scenario as independent variables; model Nagelkerke R2=0.60. "significant positive predictors" at P=0.008, P=0.022, P<0.001 and P<0.001; pharmacist involvement OR=1.489.

> "Involvement of the pharmacist in the interaction, the visit number, the questioning score and the scenario type were all found to be significant positive predictors (P=0.008, P=0.022, P<0.001 and P<0.001, respectively) of achievement of the appropriate outcome."
