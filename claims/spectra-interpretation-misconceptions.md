## Evidence

### Hyunju Lee & Stephen E. Schneider 2015

Hyunju Lee & Stephen E. Schneider. (2015). Using Astronomical Photographs to Investigate Misconceptions about Galaxies and Spectra: Question Development for Clicker Use. Physical Review Special Topics Physics Education Research. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.11.020101

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Survey 1 open-ended item on two galaxy spectra (Figure 5), probing red-shift: many students attributed spectral differences to temperature, elements, or age instead of the more distant galaxy moving away faster.

> "However, many students incorrectly thought the spectra indicated that the objects had different temperature, contained different elements, or had different ages."

### Hyunju Lee & Stephen E. Schneider 2015 (2)

Hyunju Lee & Stephen E. Schneider. (2015). Using Astronomical Photographs to Investigate Misconceptions about Galaxies and Spectra: Question Development for Clicker Use. Physical Review Special Topics Physics Education Research. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.11.020101

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Survey 1 item on four hypothetical galaxy spectra (Figure 6): students rejecting distance ordering said spectra only reveal composition; even students ordering correctly gave misconceived reasons such as line number, width, or brightness.

> "Students who responded that the distances could not be arranged with the given information mostly thought that spectra were only used to understand an object’s composition"
