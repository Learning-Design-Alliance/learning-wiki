## Evidence

### Nofal MR 2018

Nofal MR, Halim N, Le BN, et al. (2018). Unpacking the “black box”: how an SMS-based continuing medical education intervention improved medical knowledge among HIV clinicians in Vietnam. Glob Health Sci Pract. 2018;6(4):668-679. https://doi.org/10.9745/GHSP-D-18-00298

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Adjusted linear regression (Model 2, n=48 intervention participants retained at endline) of intervention-prompted behaviors on endline scores, controlling for baseline scores, gender, age, and experience. Model 2 had the best fit, explaining 51% of variation in endline test scores; in combined models the coefficient was ß=0.43 (P=.005).

> "Based on Model 2, a 1 standard deviation increase in the percentage of SMS quizzes that participants answered correctly was associated with nearly a half-point increase in endline test scores (ß=0.42; P<.001)."

### Nofal MR 2018 (2)

Nofal MR, Halim N, Le BN, et al. (2018). Unpacking the “black box”: how an SMS-based continuing medical education intervention improved medical knowledge among HIV clinicians in Vietnam. Glob Health Sci Pract. 2018;6(4):668-679. https://doi.org/10.9745/GHSP-D-18-00298

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Bivariate correlation analysis among intervention participants (n=48, Table 3) showing quiz performance correlated r=0.36 with endline scores, stronger than its correlation with baseline scores (r=0.11).

> "Further, endline test scores had the strongest correlation (r=0.36) with quiz performance (Column 1, Row 4), and quiz performance had a stronger correlation with endline (r=0.36) than baseline test scores (r=0.11)."
