# Percentage of sufficient compressions, compression depth, and compression frequency show no significant difference between the teaching groups

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The percentage of sufficient compressions (at least 45 mm depth, 90–120 bpm) did not differ between groups at either assessment (p = 0.98; p = 0.93), nor did the number of consecutive sufficient compressions (p = 0.37; p = 0.99) or compression depth. [→ Berger C 2019](#berger-c-2019)

## Evidence

### Berger C 2019

Berger C, Brinkrolf P, Ertmer C, Becker J, Friederichs H, Wenk M, Van Aken H, Hahnenkamp K. (2019). Combination of problem-based learning with high-fidelity simulation in CPR training improves short and long-term CPR skills: a randomised single blinded trial. BMC Medical Education 19:180. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1626-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Compression-quality secondary endpoints from the manikin data at skill assessment and follow-up. No statistically significant differences were detected; equivalence was not tested, and no effect size is printed for these measures.

> "The percentage of sufficient compressions meeting the criteria of at least 45 mm depth and 90–120 bpm showed no significant difference between the groups in both the 2 h and the follow-up skills assessment (intervention: 42,9 ± 34% vs. control: 41,2 ± 33% p = 0.98; IFU: 41,5 ± 31% vs. CFU: 45,1 ± 37% p = 0.93)."
