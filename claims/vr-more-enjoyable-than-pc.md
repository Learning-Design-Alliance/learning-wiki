## Evidence

### Karen B. Schloss 2021

Karen B. Schloss, Melissa A. Schoenlein, Ross Tredinnick, Simon Smith, Nathaniel Miller, Chris Racey, Christian Castro, and Bas Rokers. (2021). The UW Virtual Brain Project: An immersive approach to teaching functional neuroanatomy. Preprint. [doi:10.1037/tps0000281](https://doi.org/10.1037/tps0000281)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1A experience questionnaire (seven Likert items reduced via PCA to "average enjoyment", capturing 66% of the variance) showed "average enjoyment was signiﬁcantly greater for VR than for PC lessons" (d = 1.23). Experiment 1B (d = .86) and Experiment 2 (d = .76) replicated this.

> "Paired samples t-tests comparing devices showed that average enjoyment was signiﬁcantly greater for VR than for PC lessons ( t(59) = 9.50, p < .001, d = 1.23), and there was no signiﬁcant difference for ease of use (t(59) = .86, p = .39, d = .11)."
