## Evidence

### Charles Baily and Noah D. Finkelstein 2009

Charles Baily and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2009). Teaching and understanding of quantum interpretations in modern physics courses. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.6.010101

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pre- and post-instruction survey statement rated on a five-point scale: "An electron in an atom has a definite but unknown position at each moment of time" (N ~ 60 per course). The article reports no difference between courses at post-instruction; equivalence was not formally tested.

> "Surprisingly, at the end of instruction, students from PHYS3A were just as likely to agree with the statement on atomic electrons as students from PHYS3B, despite the emphasis that PHYS3A gave to thinking of an electron as delocalized in other contexts."

### Charles Baily and Noah D. Finkelstein 2009 (2)

Charles Baily and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2009). Teaching and understanding of quantum interpretations in modern physics courses. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.6.010101

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same pre/post survey item in both courses. The article reports the decrease in unfavorable responses was "statistically insignificant" and students remained more likely to agree than disagree at semester's end.

> "Both courses showed a modest (and statistically insignificant) decrease in unfavorable responses to this statement between pre- and post-instruction, yet students from both courses were still more likely to agree than disagree with this statement at the end of the semester."
