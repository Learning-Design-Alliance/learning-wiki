# The evidence base for school mindfulness interventions is promising but too variable for definitive practice recommendations

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` MBIs are a promising approach for supporting students, but implementation varies widely (e.g., program length) and the child/youth evidence base is not developed enough for definitive recommendations about specific practices, citing Renshaw et al. (2017), Felver et al. (2016), and Zenner et al. (2014). [→ Browning 2020](#browning-2020)

## Evidence

### Browning 2020

Browning, A., & Romer, N. (2020). Mindfulness-based practices for schools. WestEd. https://californias3.wested.org/wp-content/uploads/Mindfulness-Based-Practices-for-Schools.pdf

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review statement in the brief's universal supports section. It notes that common MBI practices include deep breathing, body-centered awareness, mindful movement, pausing before speaking, compassion practice, and guided relaxation, but that "there is, however, a lot of variability in implementation of MBIs, such as in terms of program length."

> "The evidence base for MBIs with children and youths is not developed enough to provide definitive recommendations about specific practices within mindfulness approaches and programs."
