## Evidence

### Peter Hugo Nelson 2012

Peter Hugo Nelson. (2012). Teaching introductory STEM with the Marble Game. [doi:10.1016/j.bpj.2012.11.2943](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bpj.2012.11.2943)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Final exam question in Biophysics (Spring 2012, N = 13) asked students to develop a novel marble game model of osmosis from scratch, worth 30% of a two-hour closed-book-with-cheat-sheet exam on a topic not previously discussed. "11/13 students were able to correctly construct an FD diagram", 13/13 derived the FD equation and 12/13 the thermodynamic equation for osmosis.

> "They show that 11/13 students were able to correctly construct an FD diagram from the marble game representation, 13/13 were able to correctly derive the FD equation and 12/13 were able to correctly derive the thermodynamic equation for osmosis."

### Peter Hugo Nelson 2012 (2)

Peter Hugo Nelson. (2012). Teaching introductory STEM with the Marble Game. [doi:10.1016/j.bpj.2012.11.2943](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bpj.2012.11.2943)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same final exam: results on the kMC algorithm sub-question were poor. The article reports that "many students (5/13) answered the algorithm sub -question as if the whole question was about the FD modeling technique", compounded by the difficulty of distinguishing stochastic kMC from deterministic FD representations.

> "many students (5/13) answered the algorithm sub -question as if the whole question was about the FD modeling technique."
