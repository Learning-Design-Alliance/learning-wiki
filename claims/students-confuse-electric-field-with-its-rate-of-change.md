## Evidence

### Álvaro Suárez 2024

Álvaro Suárez, Arturo C. Marti, Kristina Zuza, Jenaro Guisasola. (2024). Learning difficulties among students when applying Ampère-Maxwell's law and its implications for teaching. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010143](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010143)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Phenomenographic analysis of written answers to question 4, where students sketch magnetic field circulation over time from a graph of a time-varying electric field. "In category A (29,2%)" students recognised circulation is "directly proportional to the rate of change of the electric field".

> "In category A (29,2%), we include the answers from students who recognise, according to Ampère-Maxwell's law, that the magnetic field circulation is directly proportional to the rate of change of the electric field."

### Álvaro Suárez 2024 (2)

Álvaro Suárez, Arturo C. Marti, Kristina Zuza, Jenaro Guisasola. (2024). Learning difficulties among students when applying Ampère-Maxwell's law and its implications for teaching. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010143](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010143)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Written-test category analysis for question 4: "In category B (30,8%)" students supposed a directly proportional relationship between the fields; subcategory B1 (21,6%) assumed a positive constant and B2 (9,2%) an ad-hoc Lenz-like opposition. A further 30,8% did not answer.

> "In category B (30,8%), we group together the answers of students who do not manage to establish a connection between the magnetic field circulation and the variation of the electric field over time."
