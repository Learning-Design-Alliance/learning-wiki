# Sequential analysis of discourse reveals meaning-making processes in small-group CSCL interactions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Sequential analysis of naturally occurring discourse identifies meaning-making processes in small-group collaborative learning by examining how utterances respond to and elicit responses. [→ Gerry Stahl 2012](#gerry-stahl-2012)

## Evidence

### Gerry Stahl 2012

Gerry Stahl. (2012). Ethnomethodologically informed. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning 7:1–10. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-012-9142-3

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Stahl argues the conversational-analysis technique of sequential analysis is applicable to CSCL meaning-making processes by tracing how utterances respond to and elicit new responses, revealing small-group cognition in interaction.

> "Such sequential analysis explicates the evidence embodied in instances of discourse that reveal meaning-making processes taking place in small groups. It looks at the semantic, syntactic and pragmatic details of how utterances respond to each other and elicit new responses in the flow of group cognition."
