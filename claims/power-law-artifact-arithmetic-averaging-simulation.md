# Arithmetically averaged data generated by an exponential model are fitted best by the power model in simulation, producing the power law artifact

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In simulations, the power model fitted arithmetically averaged exponential-model data best 90% of the time, with mean squared error more than twice as good as the exponential model's. [→ Myung et al. 2000](#myung-et-al-2000)

## Evidence

### Myung et al. 2000

In Jae Myung, Cheongtag Kim, and Mark A. Pitt. (2000). Toward an explanation of the power law artifact: Insights from response surface analysis. Memory & Cognition, 28(5), 832-840. https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03198418

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Simulation study generating 10 forgetting curves from exponential and power models, arithmetically averaged, with each model fitted 100 times per data-generating model; mean MSe and percentage best fit reported in Table 1. The simulation showed the power model "fitted the exponential data best 90% of the time".

> "The power model also fitted the exponential data best 90% of the time, with fits on average being more than twice as good (.024 vs..053)."
