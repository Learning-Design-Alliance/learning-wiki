# Students' own cognitive and metacognitive activities predict learning, off-task activity lowers metacognitive knowledge, and group mates' relational activities raise domain knowledge

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A 10% increase above the mean in own cognitive activities predicted 6.35 points higher domain knowledge; group mates' relational activities predicted 6.67 points higher. [→ Molenaar 2011](#molenaar-2011)
`q3 i?` A 10% increase in own off-task behaviors predicted 4.31 points lower metacognitive knowledge. [→ Molenaar 2011 (2)](#molenaar-2011-2)

## Evidence

### Molenaar 2011

Molenaar, I., Chiu, M. M., Sleegers, P., & van Boxtel, C. (2011). Scaffolding of small groups' metacognitive activities with an avatar. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning, 6, 601-624. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-011-9130-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multilevel Model 2 for domain knowledge (N=54): % my cognitive activities coefficient 63.53 (26.06)* and % group mates' relational activities 66.76 (28.04)*, both significant; unstandardized coefficients only, impact null.

> "students whose proportion of cognitive activities exceeded its mean by 10% averaged 6.35 points higher on the post-test"

### Molenaar 2011 (2)

Molenaar, I., Chiu, M. M., Sleegers, P., & van Boxtel, C. (2011). Scaffolding of small groups' metacognitive activities with an avatar. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning, 6, 601-624. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-011-9130-z
