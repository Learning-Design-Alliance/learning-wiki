# Students with higher course grades are more likely to participate in low-stakes RBAs in both CBT and PPT conditions, with a non-linear grade-participation relationship

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` On both CBT and PPT posttests, students earning A-D grades were statistically significantly more likely to participate than students earning an F. [→ Nissen 2018](#nissen-2018)
`q3 i?` The grade-participation relationship is non-linear; on the PPT posttest the F-to-D difference was 1.52 logits while the A-to-B difference was only 0.33 logits. [→ Nissen 2018 (2)](#nissen-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Nissen 2018

Nissen, J. M., Jariwala, M., Close, E. W., & Van Dusen, B. (2018). Participation and Performance on Paper- and Computer-Based Low-Stakes Assessments. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1186/s40594-018-0117-4](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40594-018-0117-4)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

HGLM Model 3 included course grade as four dummy variables at level 2; grade variables reduced within-assessment between-student variance by 20% to 26%. The article reports no effect size for this contrast.

> "Model 3 indicates that for both the CBT and PPT posttests all four grades (A-D) were statistically signiﬁcantly more likely to participate than students who received an F in the course."

### Nissen 2018 (2)

Nissen, J. M., Jariwala, M., Close, E. W., & Van Dusen, B. (2018). Participation and Performance on Paper- and Computer-Based Low-Stakes Assessments. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1186/s40594-018-0117-4](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40594-018-0117-4)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The article used dummy variables for grades rather than a continuous variable because preliminary models showed a non-linear grade relationship, which these logit differences illustrate.

> "For example, on the PPT posttest, the diﬀerence between students who earned Fs and Ds was 1.52 logits, whereas for students who earned As and Bs the diﬀerence was only 0.33 logits."
