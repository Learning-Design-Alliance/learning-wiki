Evidence¶

Wickliffe C. Abraham 2019¶

Wickliffe C. Abraham, Owen D. Jones and David L. Glanzman. (2019). Is plasticity of synapses the mechanism of long-term memory storage? npj Science of Learning 4:9. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0048-y

q2 · i? · review · r3

Narrative review attributing this experiment to Bédécarrats et al. Tail-shock-trained donors' RNA was injected into untrained recipients; recipients of trained donor RNA showed significant SWR enhancement while untrained donor RNA recipients did not. The effect depended on DNA methylation, blocked by RG108.

"When tested 24 h post-injection, the animals that received the trained donor RNA exhibited significant enhancement of the SWR, whereas the recipients of the untrained donor RNA did not"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • DNMT inhibitors block memory formation and/or consolidation and disrupt well-consolidated memory, implicating DNA methylation in memory processes — related
  • Memories can persist and be reinstated in the absence of learning-induced synaptic alterations, supporting cell-intrinsic storage — related
  • Retrograde intervention experiments—reversing LTP or depotentiating learning-strengthened synapses—cause memory loss, lending strong support to the synaptic plasticity and memory hypothesis — reports the opposite
