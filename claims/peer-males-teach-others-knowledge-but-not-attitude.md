# Male volunteers who learned MCH topics can teach another group of males with similar knowledge gains, but the attitude improvement does not transfer

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2011 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Group 2, taught by volunteer peer lecturers from Group 1, showed a significant knowledge increase from pre-test to post-test comparable to Group 1. [→ Kadomoto Noriko 2011](#kadomoto-noriko-2011)
`q2 i?` Group 2 showed no significant attitude change, unlike Group 1, and attitude varied across peer-led sessions held by different lecturers. [→ Kadomoto Noriko 2011 (2)](#kadomoto-noriko-2011-2)

## Evidence

### Kadomoto Noriko 2011

Kadomoto Noriko, Iwasa Hajime, Takahashi Miyako, Dulnuan Marcelyn M, Kai Ichiro. (2011). Ifugao males, learning and teaching for the improvement of maternal and child health status in the Philippines: an evaluation of a program. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2458-11-280

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Paired t-test for Group 1 and mixed effect model with session as random effect for Group 2 (N = 105), comparing pre-test to post-test. The article reports significant knowledge gains in both groups but no attitude change in Group 2; no effect sizes are printed.

> "There was a significant increase in knowledge within both Groups 1 and 2 (p < 0.001). However, with attitude, there was a significant increase in Group 1 (p < 0.001), but no similar trend was observed in Group 2 (p = 0.838)."

### Kadomoto Noriko 2011 (2)

Kadomoto Noriko, Iwasa Hajime, Takahashi Miyako, Dulnuan Marcelyn M, Kai Ichiro. (2011). Ifugao males, learning and teaching for the improvement of maternal and child health status in the Philippines: an evaluation of a program. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2458-11-280

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Further analysis of Group 2 by different group sessions held by different lecturers in various barangays, adjusted for age, education and marital status. Attitude change varied by session; no effect sizes are printed.

> "there was no significant interaction effect in knowledge (F = 0.846, p = 0.499), but attitude varied (F = 3.850, p = 0.006) even after having been adjusted for age, education, and marital status."
