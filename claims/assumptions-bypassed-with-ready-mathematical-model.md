# Students often bypass articulating assumptions when a ready-made mathematical model is available

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students used P = IV to predict LED optical power without recognizing the assumption that all electrical energy converts to light, and only acknowledged it when directly questioned. [→ Benjamin M. Zwickl 2014](#benjamin-m-zwickl-2014)

## Evidence

### Benjamin M. Zwickl 2014

Benjamin M. Zwickl, Dehui Hu, Noah Finkelstein, and H. J. Lewandowski. (2014). Model-Based Reasoning in the Upper-Division Physics Laboratory: Framework and Initial Results. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1410.0881](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1410.0881)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Think-aloud interview data: Student B computed a predicted power of 0.04 watts from the data sheet's forward voltage and 20 mA test current, and only after a direct follow-up question stated the assumption that circuit power was all emitted light, which the student admitted was not necessarily the case.

> "A general pattern was that when a mathematical representation for the model could be readily identified (e.g., P = IV ), then an explicit discussion of the assumptions was bypassed."

### Benjamin M. Zwickl 2014 (2)

Benjamin M. Zwickl, Dehui Hu, Noah Finkelstein, and H. J. Lewandowski. (2014). Model-Based Reasoning in the Upper-Division Physics Laboratory: Framework and Initial Results. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1410.0881](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1410.0881)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Think-aloud interviews: despite the LED's roughly 45 nm spectral width, students did not spontaneously list the monochromatic assumption; when asked, all immediately responded they had assumed the peak wavelength, and one student provided a justification only after the assumption was brought to attention.

> "When listing the various assumptions that were made as part of the prediction or measurement, none of the students listed the assumption that the light was monochromatic, although many other assumptions were listed."
