## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2015

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2015). Upper-division student difficulties with the Dirac delta function. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.010108](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.010108)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Analysis of student solutions to instructor-designed A1-type exam questions across 7 semesters asked students to sketch and describe a given delta-function charge density. "23%, N=87 of 372" had an incorrect shape; the dominant error was treating 1- or 2-delta-function densities as point charges.

> "Roughly a quarter of students (23%, N=87 of 372) had an incorrect shape on exams. The most common error was misidentifying volume charge densities that included 1 or 2 δ-functions as point charges (62%, N=54 of 87)."

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2015 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2015). Upper-division student difficulties with the Dirac delta function. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.010108](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.010108)

`q3 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Multiple-response CUE diagnostic administered end-of-semester at CU across four semesters. "35%, N=51 of 146" selected an incorrect shape; at 7 external institutions the rate was 49% (N=79 of 162), with the most common error being a spherical-shell misidentification (76% of incorrect responses).

> "On the CUE diagnostic administered at the end of the semester, the fraction of students selecting the incorrect shape was roughly a third (35%, N=51 of 146)."
