## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Contrary to the expectation that discrimination would decline with more attempts, item discrimination actually increases as more attempts are considered, suggesting good students are distinguished by tenacity. [→ Gerd Kortemeyer 2014](#gerd-kortemeyer-2014)

## Evidence

### Gerd Kortemeyer 2014

Gerd Kortemeyer. (2014). Extending Item Response Theory to Online Homework. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.10.010118](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.10.010118)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of average item discrimination as a function of the maximum number of considered attempts (Fig. 5) in the homework 2PL fits. The article reports discrimination "actually increases as more and more attempts are considered," underlining the formative character of online homework.

> "It is clear that the discrimination actually increases as more and more attempts are considered, which may suggest that good students are distinguished by their tenacity to keep working on a homework problem, rather than their immediate genius."
