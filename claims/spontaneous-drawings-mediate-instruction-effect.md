# Spontaneous drawings at posttest mediate the effect of multimedia instruction on posttest performance, but not the effect of multimedia feedback

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The frequency of spontaneous drawings positively mediated the effect of diagrams in initial instruction on posttest performance (multimedia-text vs text-text). [→ Gök 2025](#gok-2025)
`q2 i?` No indirect effect through drawings was observed for multimedia feedback (text-text vs text-multimedia). [→ Gök 2025 (2)](#gok-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Gök 2025

Gök, S., Zhang, T., Goldstone, R. L., & Fyfe, E. R. (2025). Multimedia Effects in Initial Instruction and Feedback on Problem Solving. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000943

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

An exploratory mediation analysis (SPSS PROCESS, text-text as baseline) with posttest drawings as mediator and posttest performance as outcome showed a significant indirect effect for the multimedia-text comparison, IE = .18, CI = [.07, .33], with a non-significant direct effect.

> "This indirect effect through drawings was significant, IE = .18, CI = [.07, .33], while the direct effect of condition on posttest performance was not significant, DE = -.18, p = .511."

### Gök 2025 (2)

Gök, S., Zhang, T., Goldstone, R. L., & Fyfe, E. R. (2025). Multimedia Effects in Initial Instruction and Feedback on Problem Solving. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000943

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same mediation analysis comparing text-text vs text-multimedia found "no observable indirect effect" through drawings, suggesting multimedia feedback's benefit was not due to participants generating their own diagrams at posttest.

> "However, this was not the case for multimedia feedback ; there was no observable indirect effect when comparing text-text vs text-multimedia"
