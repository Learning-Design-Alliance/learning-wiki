## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Differences in socioeconomic status and school background create unequal social status that lowers self-esteem, silences students, and unbalances discussion. [→ Singaram Veena S. 2010](#singaram-veena-s-2010)

## Evidence

### Singaram Veena S. 2010

Singaram Veena S., van der Vleuten Cees P. M., Stevens Fred, Dolmans Diana H. J. M. (2010). “For most of us Africans, we don’t just speak”: a qualitative investigation into collaborative heterogeneous PBL group learning. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-010-9262-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Student focus groups reported that social status differences produce "unbalanced discussions, quiet students, withdrawing"; a student described a less fortunate peer not speaking out while better-off students' mistakes were brushed off.

> "The diverse socioeconomic backgrounds create unequal social status in the group and leads towards unbalanced discussions, quiet students, withdrawing. Differences in social status impacts on student’s self-esteem making them feel inferior."
