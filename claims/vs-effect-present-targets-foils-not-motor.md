# The VS correct-greater-than-incorrect response is present for both target and foil trials and is not a motor confound

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Correct>incorrect VS activation occurs for targets (HIT>MISS), foils (CR>FP), trials with motor responses (HIT>FP), and trials without motor responses (CR>MISS). [→ Satterthwaite Theodore D. M.D. 2012](#satterthwaite-theodore-d-md-2012)

## Evidence

### Satterthwaite Theodore D. M.D. 2012

Satterthwaite Theodore D. M.D., M.A.; Ruparel Kosha M.S.; Loughead James Ph.D; Elliott Mark A. Ph.D.; Gerraty Raphael T. B.A.; Calkins Monica E. Ph.D.; Hakonarson Hakon M.D. Ph.D.; Gur Ruben C. Ph.D.; Gur Raquel E. M.D., Ph.D.; Wolf Daniel H. M.D., Ph.D. (2012). Being Right Is Its Own Reward: Load and Performance Related Ventral Striatum Activation to Correct Responses During a Working Memory Task in Youth. Neuroimage. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3358426/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Third GLM modeling HIT, CR, MISS, FP separately, with one-sample t-tests in bilateral VS ROIs. The COR>INCOR effect "was seen in trials where there was a motor response present" and "also in trials where there were no motor responses".

> "The correct > incorrect effect was seen in trials where there was a motor response present [HIT>FP contrast; left VS: T(303)=6.91, p<0.001; right VS: T(303)=6.93, p<0.001 ] and also in trials where there were no motor responses [CR>MISS contrast; left VS: T(303)=3.61, p<0.001; right VS: T(303)=2.67, p<0.004]."
