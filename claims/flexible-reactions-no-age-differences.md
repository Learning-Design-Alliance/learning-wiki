## Evidence

### Bamberg 2025

Bamberg, C., Weigelt, S. & Hagelweide, K. (2025). Reversal learning is influenced by cognitive flexibility and develops throughout early adolescence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00308-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

A model with linear and quadratic age terms fitted to flexible reactions contradicted the inverted-U hypothesis H3a: the quadratic term had "p = 0.56" and the linear term "p = 0.16", with no change when adding sex as covariate.

> "We neither found evidence for a quadratic relation between age and ﬂexible reactions (coeff =−0.01, SE = 0.01, 95% CI = [−0.03, 0.01], p = 0.56) nor a linear relation (coeff = 0.01, SE = 0.01, 95% CI = [0.0, 0.03],p = 0.16), with no change when adding sex as covariate"

### Bamberg 2025 (2)

Bamberg, C., Weigelt, S. & Hagelweide, K. (2025). Reversal learning is influenced by cognitive flexibility and develops throughout early adolescence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00308-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

An exploratory basis-spline regression (Fig. 5) found "signiﬁcant basis functions for early-adolescents and adults" with an early-adolescent slope of 0.03, three times the adult slope of 0.01, and a mid-adolescent slope of −0.01.

> "We only found signiﬁcant basis functions for early-adolescents and adults. The slope for early-adolescents, 0.03, was three times as large as for adults, 0.01 (see Supple- mentary Table 4). The slope for mid-adolescents is−0.01."
