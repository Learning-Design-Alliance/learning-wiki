# The Summer Active Reading Programme produced a small positive but statistically non-significant effect on reading comprehension at the primary-secondary transition

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Pupils participating in the programme made slightly more progress in reading comprehension than control pupils (Hedges g = +0.13, 95% CI -0.12 to +0.38, approximately two months' progress), but the difference was not statistically significant. [→ Maxwell et al. 2014](#maxwell-et-al-2014)

## Evidence

### Maxwell et al. 2014

Dr Bronwen Maxwell, Professor Paul Connolly, Sean Demack, Dr Liam O'Hare, Anna Stevens, Lucy Clague. (2014). Summer Active Reading Programme: Evaluation Report and Executive Summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED581463

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Individually randomised controlled trial of 205 pupils from 10 secondary schools in Yorkshire and Humberside, analysed with multilevel models clustered by primary school. The main-effects model gave "Hedges g effect size for main effects model (95% Confidence Intervals): +0.13 (-0.12, +0.38)"; only the KS2 pre-test term was significant.

> "On average, pupils participating in the programme made more progress than those who did not. The size of the difference was small, and could be envisaged as saying that pupils who participated in the programme made approximately two months’ additional progress than those who did not. However, the finding was not statistically significant"
