## Evidence

### Katie A. Gilligan-Lee 2022

Katie A. Gilligan-Lee, Zachary C. K. Hawes and Kelly S. Mix. (2022). Spatial thinking as the missing piece in mathematics curricula. npj Science of Learning 7:10. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00128-9

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Curriculum analysis in the commentary reports that "none of these spatial skills are mentioned" in the US Common Core State Standards for Mathematics, whose geometry standards focus on classifying and identifying shapes rather than dynamic spatial thinking.

> "none of these spatial skills are mentioned in the Common Core State Standards for Mathematics, used by many states in the U.S., where the primary focus of geometry standards is on classifying and identifying simple and complex shapes."

### Katie A. Gilligan-Lee 2022 (2)

Katie A. Gilligan-Lee, Zachary C. K. Hawes and Kelly S. Mix. (2022). Spatial thinking as the missing piece in mathematics curricula. npj Science of Learning 7:10. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00128-9

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Policy review in the commentary contrasts the 2020 Ontario mathematics curriculum, which includes a spatial sense sub-domain for all year groups, with England's 2021 revision of the Early Years Foundation Stage framework removing shape and measurement early learning goals.

> "the 2020 Ontario (Canada) Mathematics curriculum explicitly highlights spatial reasoning as a gateway to success in STEM domains and includes a "spatial sense" sub-domain for all year groups."
