# Agent personality style significantly affects perceived personality factors (all but conscientiousness) and engagement, with agreeableness showing the largest effect

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Personality style (high vs low extroversion-agreeableness) had significant main effects on perceived engagement, openness, extroversion, agreeableness, and neuroticism after multiple-testing correction. [→ Sonlu 2025](#sonlu-2025)
`q2 i?` The significant main effect of personality style on conscientiousness (F = 5.68, p = 0.018) did not survive the Hommel correction (adjusted p = 0.290). [→ Sonlu 2025](#sonlu-2025)
`q2 i?` Agreeableness had the highest effect among the significant style effects, followed by extroversion. [→ Sonlu 2025](#sonlu-2025)

## Evidence

### Sonlu 2025

Sonlu, S., Bendiksen, B., Durupinar, F., & Gudukbay, U. (2025). The Effects of Embodiment and Personality Expression on Learning in LLM-based Educational Agents. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1109/vr59515.2025.00094](https://doi.org/10.1109/vr59515.2025.00094)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Two-way ANOVA (n = 210; 35 per cell) of model type and personality style on BFI-2-XS and LOES-S measures, with Hommel-adjusted p-values. The adjusted tests returned "significant main effects of personality style" on engagement and four perceived traits, with agreeableness the most significant (p = 0.0000211).

> "Adjusted-for ANOVA tests returned significant main effects of personality style on the outcomes of engagement ( F = 9.502, p= 0.0421), openness ( F = 4.474, p= 0.00178), extroversion ( F = 10.148, p= 0.03006), agreeableness ( F = 25.541, p= 0.0000211), and neuroticism ( F = 9.708, p= 0.0378)."
