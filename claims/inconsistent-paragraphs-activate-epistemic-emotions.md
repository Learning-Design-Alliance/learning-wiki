# Paragraphs presenting inconsistent information in refutation texts elicit activating epistemic emotions and suppress deactivating emotions, with text condition × paragraph interactions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Significant text condition × paragraph interactions emerged, with activating-emotion differences in the misinformation-description paragraph (e.g., negative refutation vs. non-refutation, b = 1.58, p < 0.001) and no differences in the identical introduction and closing paragraphs. [→ Jheng 2025](#jheng-2025)

## Evidence

### Jheng 2025

Jheng, Y.-L., Catrysse, L., Van de Cruys, S., Kendeou, P., Poels, K. & Gijbels, D. (2025). Investigating epistemic emotions experienced while reading refutation texts through a fine-grained measure of emotion. npj Science of Learning 10, 30. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00324-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Paragraph-level Poisson GLMMs (condition, paragraph, and interaction as fixed effects) in the 56-participant experiment found "Signiﬁcant interactions were observed between text condition and paragraph"; activating emotions differed in paragraph 2 and paragraph 4, and deactivating emotions differed in paragraph 3, while identical paragraphs 1 and 5 showed no differences.
