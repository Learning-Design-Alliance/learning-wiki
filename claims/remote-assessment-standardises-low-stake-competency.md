## Evidence

### O’Brien A 2023

O’Brien A, Forde C. (2023). Health science staff and student experiences of teaching and assessing clinical skills using digital tools: a qualitative study. Annals of Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1080/07853890.2023.2256656

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview findings on online assessment: staff described standardised patients ensuring everybody "got asked the same questions at the same time", which they judged fairer than varied real-patient assessments. No effect size is reported.

> "However, some staff believed technology introduced standardisation of assessment methods, facilitating equity and fairness in exam procedures previously based on real-world patient presentation that could vary in complexity between individual student assessments."

### O’Brien A 2023 (2)

O’Brien A, Forde C. (2023). Health science staff and student experiences of teaching and assessing clinical skills using digital tools: a qualitative study. Annals of Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1080/07853890.2023.2256656

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative results section on assessment integrity: participants agreed remote assessment suits "low-stake soft skills" but could not substitute in-person assessment for high-stake skills needed for accreditation. No effect size is reported.

> "Despite its suitability for assessing low-stake soft skills, participants agreed that remote assessments could not substitute the integrity of in-person assessment for high-stake skills."
