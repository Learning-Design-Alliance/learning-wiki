# At its current predictive level of about 2% of variance, PGS adds very little to predicting end-of-year achievement beyond progress monitoring

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When PGS predicts only 2% of variance in achievement (current technology, specific measures, younger children), adding PGS to progress monitoring adds very little overall variance predicted in end-of-year achievement. [→ Shero 2021](#shero-2021)

## Evidence

### Shero 2021

Shero, J., van Dijk, W., Edwards, A., Schatschneider, C., Solari, E. J., & Hart, S. A. (2021). The practical utility of genetic screening in school settings. npj Science of Learning, 6:12. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00090-y

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Data-simulation and stepwise regression study (Methods, Research question 1) simulating multivariate normal data across combinations of PGS and progress monitoring effectiveness. The simulation found that "using the PGS adds very little" variance predicted at the current 2% effectiveness level.

> "when the predictive value of PGS is low (i.e., where the technology is currently, 2%), using the PGS adds very little in terms of the overall variance in achievement predicted, even when progress monitoring's predictive value is also low."
