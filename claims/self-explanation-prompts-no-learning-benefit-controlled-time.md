# Self-explanation prompts in an instructional video do not improve retention or comprehension when learning time is controlled

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Learning from video with self-explanation prompts was not more successful than learning without prompts, with Bayes factors supporting the null hypothesis. [→ Beege 2025](#beege-2025)

## Evidence

### Beege 2025

Beege, M., & Ploetzner, R. (2025). Learning from interactive video: the influence of self-explanations, navigation, and cognitive load. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09693-5

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same controlled-time experiment (N = 128), the MANOVA revealed "no significant main effect for self-explanation prompts"; BF10 = 0.20 for both retention and comprehension supported the null. The prompts were specific, inference-demanding, and learners received instruction on self-explanation.

> "A MANOV A with retention and comprehension scores as depended variables revealed no significant main effect for self-explanation prompts ( Wilk' s Λ = 0.999, F(2, 123) = 0.08, p = .926). Bayes factors indicated support for the null-hypothesis regarding retention (BF10 = 0.20) and comprehension ( BF10 = 0.20)"
