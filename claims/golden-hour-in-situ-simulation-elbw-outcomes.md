`q2 i?` After 17 months of interdisciplinary in-situ Golden Hour simulation training, ELBW admission body temperature improved (36.4°C versus 35°C; P < 0.0001) and time to surfactant decreased (2.5 h versus 3.5 h; P = 0.001); no effect size is printed. [→ Yousef Nadya 2022](#yousef-nadya-2022)

## Evidence

### Yousef Nadya 2022

Yousef Nadya, Moreau Romain, Soghier Lamia. (2022). Simulation in neonatal care: towards a change in traditional training? European Journal of Pediatrics 181:1429–1436. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00431-022-04373-3

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

A French monocentric, retrospective, mixed-methods study evaluated in-situ simulation targeting the first hour after NICU admission; 130 team members trained over 17 months. The review reports improved temperature and surfactant timing "after the training"; only P values are printed, no effect size.

> "Results after training showed improvement in body temperature (36.4 °C [35.7–36.9] versus 35 °C [34-36]; P < 0.0001) and a decrease in time to surfactant administration (2.5 h [2.5–3] versus 3.5 h [3-4]; P = 0.001) after the training"
