Evidence¶

Mousavi 2018¶

Mousavi, K., Alavinia, P., & Gholami, J. (2018). Input Providing vs. Output-Prompting Negotiation Strategies in Learning Grammar among Young EFL Learners. International Journal of Instruction, 11(2), 497-512. https://doi.org/10.12973/iji.2018.11234a

q2 · i? · causal · r1

One-way ANOVA compared gains across the input-providing, output-prompting and control groups (18 learners each). Post-hoc analysis showed both experimental groups beat the control, but "the performance of output -prompting group is witnessed to be better than the other two groups on both immediate and delayed posttests". No effect size is printed for the between-group contrast.

"in immediate posttest p-value is .000 ≤ 0.05, and it can be said that the groups are different from each other after treatment, F (2, 51) = .30.06, p = .000."

Mousavi 2018 (2)¶

Mousavi, K., Alavinia, P., & Gholami, J. (2018). Input Providing vs. Output-Prompting Negotiation Strategies in Learning Grammar among Young EFL Learners. International Journal of Instruction, 11(2), 497-512. https://doi.org/10.12973/iji.2018.11234a

q2 · i? · causal · r2

The same one-way ANOVA run on pretest scores showed no significant difference among the three groups before treatment, supporting group comparability at baseline. Descriptive pretest means were 6.88, 6.66 and 6.55.

"since the p-value is .819 ≥ 0.05, it can be said that the groups are homogeneous before treatment, F (2, 51) = .200, p = .819"
