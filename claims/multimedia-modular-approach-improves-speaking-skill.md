## Evidence

### Jose 2012

Jose, G. R., & Dharma Raja, B. W. (2012). Multimedia Modular Approach for Augmenting the Speaking Skill of the Student-Teachers. i-manager's Journal of English Language Teaching, 2(4). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1070215

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A pre-test post-test equivalent group design with 70 B.Ed. student-teachers matched on intelligence test scores. The experimental group (n=35) was taught through a multimedia module while the control group (n=35) received traditional instruction for two months. The article reports a statistically significant difference favoring the experimental group, with the experimental group performing better in the post-test.

> "In the Table 2, since p value is less than 0.01 in the speaking skill, the null hypothesis is not accepted at 1% level of significance. Hence, it is concluded that there is significant difference between control and experimental groups in the speaking skill at the posttest score."
