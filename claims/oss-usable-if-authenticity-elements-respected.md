## Evidence

### Sørensen Jette Led 2015

Sørensen Jette Led, Navne Laura Emdal, Martin Helle Max, Ottesen Bent, Albrecthsen Charlotte Krebs, Pedersen Berit Woetmann, Kjærgaard Hanne, van der Vleuten Cees. (2015). Clarifying the learning experiences of healthcare professionals with in situ and off-site simulation-based medical education: a qualitative study. BMJ Open. [doi:10.1136/bmjopen-2015-008345](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2015-008345)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Authors' interpretation of the four focus groups with 25 healthcare professionals from the ISS/OSS randomised trial, analysed by conventional content analysis: "the physical setting in simulations was of less significance". The conclusion is presented as based on these focus groups.

> "Our analysis indicates that authenticity in all aspects of simulation is important and that the physical setting in simulations was of less significance, indicating that OSS is just as useful if other elements of authenticity in the simulation are respected."
