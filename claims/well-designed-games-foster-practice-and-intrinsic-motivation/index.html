Evidence¶

Huber 2024¶

Huber, S. E., Kiili, K., Nebel, S., Ryan, R. M., Sailer, M., & Ninaus, M. (2024). Leveraging the Potential of Large Language Models in Education Through Playful and Game-Based Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09868-z

q1 · i? · theoretical · r3

Theoretical argument in the game-based learning section, citing game-based learning research and SDT extensions; the article states that in a well-designed game "the learning becomes its own reward." No new empirical data are reported.

"Well-designed games can indeed promote both the required persistence in activities for practice and high quality of engagement that can foster deep human learning and problem solving"

Huber 2024 (2)¶

Huber, S. E., Kiili, K., Nebel, S., Ryan, R. M., Sailer, M., & Ninaus, M. (2024). Leveraging the Potential of Large Language Models in Education Through Playful and Game-Based Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09868-z

q1 · i? · theoretical · r2

Authors' interpretation in the section on games as a culture medium: intrinsic interest fostered by games may protect novices from over-reliance on LLMs, giving them "a flavor of that kind of intrinsic interest" that protects domain experts. No data reported.

"When within well-designed games, in which the basic needs of autonomy, competence, and relatedness are met, learning becomes its own reward (Ryan & Rigby, 2020), the option to outsource cognitive efforts to LLMs becomes less tempting."

Related Claims¶

  • Self-determination theory holds that autonomy support raises intrinsic motivation, and a meta-analysis finds expected tangible rewards undermine it — a broader claim this one bears on
  • GAI use appears as a core condition only in a supplementary autonomy–relatedness configuration with low unique coverage — related
  • Expected tangible rewards reduce later free-choice engagement with a rewarded task, while verbal praise does not — related
  • The comprehensive psychological need satisfaction configuration (competence core) has the highest coverage for high learning engagement, with GAI use unconstrained — related
  • Robot adaptiveness effects on human autonomy are mixed: autonomy-supportive behaviours and as-needed assistance help, but mere choice or high interactivity may not — related
  • High GAI use is not a necessary condition for high learning engagement, and no single condition is strictly necessary — related
  • Positive teacher-student relationships fulfill relatedness needs and support internalization of learning — related
  • The convenience and low opportunity cost of LLMs poses a risk of over-reliance, deskilling, and reduced judgment competence when learners outsource tasks that are essential learning opportunities. — related
  • Cognitive Load Theory lacks a motivational focus, which becomes problematic when interpreting interventions that foster effortful learning strategies — related
  • Experienced effort-by-need frustration is neither a necessary requirement nor a typical concomitant of high-quality learning — related
  • Formative assessment is theorized to foster motivation by supporting autonomy, competence, and relatedness — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • In EERs, autonomy, competence, and relatedness are each supported by specific design features per self-determination theory — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Effective instruction requires both focus support and reward: high focus support with low reward, or reward without focus support, yields poor learning — related
  • Motivation theories in education have converged on a learner-centered focus over recent decades — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Self-determination theory grew incrementally by adding mini-theories as evidence accumulated — related
  • Perceived individual and class-directed autonomy support positively predict students' intrinsic motivation, effort, and persistence — related
  • The authors propose, without testing it here, that competitive environments may provide clear goals, structures, and rewards that foster engagement, particularly for students less familiar with such environments — related
