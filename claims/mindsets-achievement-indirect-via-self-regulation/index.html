Discussion¶

Learner Variables¶

  • Mindset — predictor: learners who differ on it differ in outcomes

Related Claims¶

  • In kindergarteners, trait beliefs and goal orientations form two separate but related factors rather than a coherent growth/fixed mindset — related
  • Kindergarteners who believe traits are malleable show better classroom behavioral self-regulation than children with a stable trait belief — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Mastery orientation relates to classroom behavioral self-regulation only indirectly via executive functions, while trait beliefs relate mainly directly — related
  • Kindergarteners with a mastery goal orientation show better executive functions than children with a performance orientation — related
  • Malleable trait beliefs may be especially beneficial for classroom behavioral self-regulation among mastery-oriented children (marginal interaction) — related
  • Growth in task orientation partially mediates the association between executive function and mathematics achievement in preschoolers — related
  • Children's executive function at the beginning of pre-kindergarten predicts growth in mathematics achievement across the pre-kindergarten year — related
  • No statistically significant sex differences in the direct associations among EF, task orientation, and mathematics or in the mediating effect of task orientation — related
