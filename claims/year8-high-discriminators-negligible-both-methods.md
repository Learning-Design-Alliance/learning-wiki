## Evidence

### Athanasopoulos 2023

Athanasopoulos, P. (2023). Cognitive dissonance or p-prims? Towards identifying the best way to overcome misconceptions in physics. Prestatyn High School, UK. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2308.15601](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2308.15601)

`q2 · i0` · `causal · r1`

Post-test-only analysis of the three high-discriminator questions for the year 8 classes. Mean post-test scores on high discriminators were 9% (cognitive dissonance) and 12% (p-prim); the printed effect size of -0.14 indicates negligible progress in either condition.

> "When looking at the high discriminators only, progress seems to be negligible in either form of instruction. The effect size of cognitive dissonance is calculated to be -0.14 with the 95% confidence interval being (-0.75, 0.47)."
