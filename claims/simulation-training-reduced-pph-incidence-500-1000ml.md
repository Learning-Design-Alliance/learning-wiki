## Evidence

### Nelissen Ellen 2017

Nelissen Ellen, Ersdal Hege, Mduma Estomih, Evjen-Olsen Bjørg, Twisk Jos, Broerse Jacqueline, van Roosmalen Jos, Stekelenburg Jelle. (2017). Clinical performance and patient outcome after simulation-based training in prevention and management of postpartum haemorrhage: an educational intervention study in a low-resource setting. BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12884-017-1481-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Prospective educational intervention study at Haydom Lutheran Hospital, Tanzania, comparing 3622 births before and 5824 births after a half-day simulation-based training introduced in March 2012. The study reports "a significant reduction in the incidence of postpartum haemorrhage (500–1000 ml) from 2.1% to 1.3% (p = 0.003)"; the article also prints Cohen's d = 0.07, described as of small clinical significance.

> "After training there was a significant reduction in the incidence of postpartum haemorrhage (500–1000 ml) from 2.1% to 1.3% (p = 0.003)."
