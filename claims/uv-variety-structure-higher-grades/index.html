Evidence¶

Priniski 2019¶

Priniski, S. J., Rosenzweig, E. Q., Canning, E. A., Hecht, C. A., Tibbetts, Y., Hyde, J. S., & Harackiewicz, J. M. (2019). The Benefits of Combining Value for the Self and Others in Utility-Value Interventions. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000343

q3 · i? · causal · r2

In the RCT (N = 590), regression within a path-modeling framework tested five orthogonal contrasts on final course grades. The variety contrast was significant: "Œ≤ = 0.07, p = .024"; students writing varied assignments averaged M = 3.05 (SD = 0.69) versus M = 2.85 (SD = 0.80) for same-type writers.

"There was a significant main effect of the variety contrast, which compared the two conditions in which students wrote a variety of self- and other-focused UV assignments to the two conditions in which students wrote only one type of assignment, on course grades, Œ≤ = 0.07, p = .024"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Giving students a choice between self-focused and other-focused utility-value assignments produced higher course grades than fixing the assignment type — related
  • The overall intervention contrast comparing all UV conditions to control was not significant, and no individual UV condition significantly outperformed control in the dummy-coded model — related
  • Among students with low performance expectations, self-first variety (a self-focused essay followed by other-focused letters) produced higher grades than other-first variety — related
  • In replication analyses, the variety conditions were particularly beneficial for students with low prior GPAs, while the same-type-only conditions had negligible effect sizes — related
  • UV writing assignments showed more personal focus, more social and cognitive process content, and higher articulated utility value than control summary assignments — related
  • Choice increased task engagement and end-of-semester interest, and task engagement mediated the effect of choice on course performance — related
  • A utility-value writing intervention increases course grades in introductory biology relative to a summarizing control — related
