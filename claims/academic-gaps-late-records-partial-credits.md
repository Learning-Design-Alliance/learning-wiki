# Teachers report that students in foster care arrive with fragmented academic histories, late records, and — in high school — no partial credits after mid-semester school changes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teachers described checkered academic histories full of gaps, records (IEPs, transcripts) arriving weeks or months late, and high school students unable to accumulate partial credits when mid-semester placement changes force school switches. [→ The Center for the Future of Teaching and Learning 2010](#the-center-for-the-future-of-teaching-and-learning-2010)

## Evidence

### The Center for the Future of Teaching and Learning 2010

The Center for the Future of Teaching and Learning. (2010). Ready to Succeed in the Classroom: Findings from Teacher Discussion Groups on their Experiences and Aspirations Teaching Students in the Foster Care System. Prepared for the Stuart Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED511840

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Qualitative finding from the Academic Gaps section of the discussion groups. One teacher called the histories "like Swiss cheese" — full of gaps; teachers said they had very little academic information when new students entered, and many did not even know which students were in foster care.

> "In high school, they cannot accumulate partial credits when a mid-semester placement change causes them to switch schools, and they fall further and further behind their peers."
