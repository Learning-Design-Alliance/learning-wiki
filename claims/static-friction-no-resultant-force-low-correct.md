## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Effect of Misconception on Transfer in Problem Solving. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07686](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07686)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Written responses from 131 students to a question probing understanding of static friction's magnitude and direction, in which no coefficient was provided. The correct answer required recognizing that a 200 N rightward force is balanced by an equal static frictional force; the paper reports only 15% correct and nearly half asserting the coefficient was necessary.

> "Only 15% of the students provided the correct response. In question (9), the coefficient of friction was not provided, and similar to the common misconception in question (4), almost 50% of the students believed that it is impossible to determine the resultant force on the crate without this information."
