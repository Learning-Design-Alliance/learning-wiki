# Receiving benchmark scores after scoring the video promotes active engagement, whereas receiving them beforehand promotes passive engagement

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Scoring the benchmark video before receiving the panel score increases active engagement because examiners feel a need to reach a justifiable judgement of their own. [→ Edwards 2025](#edwards-2025)
`q2 i?` Knowing the panel score before watching reduces engagement, giving reassurance that alters how the video is watched. [→ Edwards 2025 (2)](#edwards-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Edwards 2025

Edwards, R. J., Yeates, P., Lefroy, J., & McKinley, R. (2025). Understanding contexts and mechanisms through which video based benchmarking promotes alignment of examiners' scoring in objective structured clinical exams. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-025-10454-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In the interview study's manipulated "information after" condition, examiners scored blind first; the article reports their "engagement increased" because they needed to reach a justifiable judgement independently.

> "When participants scored the benchmark performances without being aware of the expert judgement (i.e. using the "information after" procedure), they were aware of a need to reach a justifiable judgement on their own accord and their engagement increased"

### Edwards 2025 (2)

Edwards, R. J., Yeates, P., Lefroy, J., & McKinley, R. (2025). Understanding contexts and mechanisms through which video based benchmarking promotes alignment of examiners' scoring in objective structured clinical exams. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-025-10454-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In the "information before" condition of the same study, participants reported reduced pressure and reassurance, which the analysis linked to more passive engagement with the video content.

> "participants felt that knowing the expert panel scores and justification before observing the video (i.e. the "information before" condition) reduced this pressure, giving a sense of reassurance which altered how they watched the video, thereby reducing engagement"
