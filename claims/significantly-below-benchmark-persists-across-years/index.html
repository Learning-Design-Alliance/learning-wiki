Lemke 2025¶

Lemke, M., Soo Ping Chow, A., LeBeau, B., & Quesen, S. (2025). Stuck at red: Persistent early literacy gaps and later state test performance. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED677637

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Longitudinal tracking of Massachusetts early literacy screening data: over 37,000 students had EOY scores across 2 school years and over 4,000 across 3 years. The brief reports "84 percent of those students were still at significant risk another year later". Students at/above benchmark persisted at 90% and 93%.

"Two thirds of students (67%) who were identified as at significant risk at EOY in 1 year were still at significant risk the following year, and 84 percent of those students were still at significant risk another year later"

Related Claims¶

  • Most students below benchmark at the beginning of the year remained below at the end, with persistence highest for historically marginalized student groups (67–80 percent) — related
  • Risk status tends to persist: 75 percent of students significantly below benchmark at BOY remained so at EOY, and earlier grades showed lower year-to-year persistence — related
  • Early screening benchmark status predicts later performance: most beginning-of-year at-risk students stay at risk, and meeting benchmarks by end of grade 1 is associated with much higher grade 3 MCAS odds — related
  • Exploratory analysis found no observed differences in later MCAS performance between students just below and just above screening risk thresholds — related
  • Students at later grade levels were more likely to remain at risk across school years than students at earlier grade levels — related
  • Most students at risk at BOY remain at risk later, but students who meet benchmark by midyear tend to stay on track — related
  • About 20% of children (later developers) make few self-regulation gains in preschool and lag peers by six months to a year and a half — related
  • Nonresponsiveness to kindergarten intervention is persistent and predicts school-identified reading problems through third grade — related
  • Across school years, students identified as at risk in earlier grades were more likely to get on track by the end of the next grade than students in later grades — related
