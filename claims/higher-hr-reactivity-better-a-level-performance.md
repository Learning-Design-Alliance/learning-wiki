## Evidence

### Ginty 2022

Ginty, A. T., Tyra, A. T., Young, D. A., Brindle, R. C., de Rooij, S. R. & Williams, S. E. (2022). Cardiovascular reactions to acute psychological stress and academic achievement. Psychophysiology, 59, e14064. https://doi.org/10.1111/psyp.14064

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Prospective observational study: HR reactivity to the PASAT measured in 131 final-year high school students, regressed on total A-Level score obtained the following year. The article reports "β = 0.192, p = .027" with lower reactivity linked to lower performance.

> "In the first model, with no adjustment, HR reactivity was associated with total A‐Level performance, β = 0.192, p = .027, R 2 = 0.037; individuals with lower HR reactivity had lower A‐Level performance."

### Ginty 2022 (2)

Ginty, A. T., Tyra, A. T., Young, D. A., Brindle, R. C., de Rooij, S. R. & Williams, S. E. (2022). Cardiovascular reactions to acute psychological stress and academic achievement. Psychophysiology, 59, e14064. https://doi.org/10.1111/psyp.14064

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical regression adjusting for age, gender, ethnicity, SES, HADS depression, cardiorespiratory fitness, baseline cardiovascular values, and task ratings; "HR reactivity and CO reactivity continued to be associated with A‐Level scores". Results also survived post-hoc adjustment for school attended.

> "HR reactivity and CO reactivity continued to be associated with A‐Level scores, β = 0.189, p = .034, ΔR 2 = 0.032 and β = 0.277, p = .005, ΔR 2 = 0.067, respectively"
