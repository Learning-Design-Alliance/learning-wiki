# The increase in noticing dialogue was accompanied by a decrease in noticing mathematical activity, described as attentional tunneling — a design consideration for integrated dialogue-plus-content noticing training

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors report that gains in noticing dialogue co-occurred with an observed decrease in noticing mathematics, a phenomenon they identify as attentional tunneling. [→ Benzi Slakmon 2024](#benzi-slakmon-2024)

## Evidence

### Benzi Slakmon 2024

Benzi Slakmon, Rotem Abdu. (2024). Learning to notice collaboration: examining the impact of professional development on mathematics teachers’ enhanced awareness in CSCL settings. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn 19:137–155. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-024-09423-6

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Author interpretation stated in the limitations and future research section, attributing the trade-off to attentional tunneling and calling for further study of when mathematics and dialogue noticing complement or exclude each other. No quantitative data on mathematics noticing are printed, so the report is observational and unquantified.

> "the increase in noticing dialogue was accompanied by an observed decrease in noticing mathematical activity, a phenomenon also known as attentional tunneling (Wickens & Alexander, 2009). Further studies should focus on noticing mathematics and dialogue to understand when these facets complement or exclude each other."
