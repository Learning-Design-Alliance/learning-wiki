# Collaborative testing did not improve retention of course material measured by cumulative questions on subsequent exams

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Cumulative-question performance did not differ significantly between students who took the previous exam collaboratively and those who took it individually, in either cross-over trial (exam 3 p = 0.166; exam 4 p = 0.846). [→ Leight H. 2012](#leight-h-2012)

## Evidence

### Leight H. 2012

Leight H., Saunders C., Calkins R., Withers M. (2012). Collaborative Testing Improves Performance but Not Content Retention in a Large-Enrollment Introductory Biology Class. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.12-04-0048

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Primary retention outcome in the randomized cross-over design: "not significantly different (p = 0.166)" between collaborative (group A) and individual (group B) testers; the reversed trial on exam 4 was likewise not significant (p = 0.846). Equivalence was not tested; no effect size is printed.

> "The average number of correct responses to cumulative questions on exam 3 was not significantly different (p = 0.166) between group A (2.36 ± 0.16), who took the previous exam collaboratively, and group B (2.09 ± 0.12), who took the previous exam individually."
