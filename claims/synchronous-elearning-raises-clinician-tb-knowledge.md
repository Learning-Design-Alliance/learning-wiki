# Synchronous E-learning participation is associated with higher TB knowledge among clinical physicians in a quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Clinical physicians who participated in synchronous E-learning sessions scored 7.3 points higher on the TB quiz than non-participants, and each synchronous session attended added 0.3 points on average. [→ Wang Zi-Yue 2021](#wang-zi-yue-2021)

## Evidence

### Wang Zi-Yue 2021

Wang Zi-Yue, Zhang Li-Jie, Liu Yu-Hong, Jiang Wei-Xi, Jia Jing-Yun, Tang Sheng-Lan, Liu Xiao-Yun. (2021). The effectiveness of E-learning in continuing medical education for tuberculosis health workers: a quasi-experiment from China. Infectious Diseases of Poverty. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40249-021-00855-y

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multiple linear regression on pooled cross-sectional survey data from the China-Gates TB quasi-experiment, controlling for institutional fixed effects and individual characteristics. The study reports that synchronous participants' knowledge "increased by 7.3 points on average (P = 0.023)" and each session added 0.3 points (P = 0.034).

> "the TB knowledge level of clinical physicians who have participated in synchronous learning activities increased by 7.3 points on average (P = 0.023). For each time they participate in a synchronous session, their clinical TB quiz score could increase by 0.3 points on average (P = 0.034)."
