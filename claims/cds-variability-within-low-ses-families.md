# Child-directed speech varies dramatically among low-SES families, up to an 18-fold difference, largely independent of maternal education and overall talkativeness

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Total adult speech ranged from almost 29,000 to fewer than 2,000 words per 10-hour day, and child-directed speech differed up to 18-fold between families. [→ Weisleder Adriana 2013](#weisleder-adriana-2013)
`q3 i?` Differences in child-directed speech were uncorrelated with maternal education (r = .29, p = .13) and with overheard speech (r = .17, p = .38). [→ Weisleder Adriana 2013 (2)](#weisleder-adriana-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Weisleder Adriana 2013

Weisleder Adriana, Fernald Anne. (2013). Talking to children matters: Early language experience strengthens processing and builds vocabulary. Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797613488145

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive analysis of LENA-based all-day home recordings from 29 low-SES Latino families at 19 months (Figure 1), showing striking variability in adult speech accessible to infants within a demographically homogeneous disadvantaged group.

> "in one family, caregivers spoke more than 12,000 words to the infant, while in another the infant heard only 670 words of child-directed speech over an entire day, an 18-fold difference in the amount of child-directed speech available to these two children"

### Weisleder Adriana 2013 (2)

Weisleder Adriana, Fernald Anne. (2013). Talking to children matters: Early language experience strengthens processing and builds vocabulary. Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797613488145

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Correlational checks on the same 29-family sample: child-directed speech was also not correlated with overheard speech (r = .17, p = .38), which the article reads as showing differences reflect caregivers' degree of verbal engagement rather than overall talkativeness.

> "These differences in parental engagement were uncorrelated with maternal education (r = .29, p = .13)"
