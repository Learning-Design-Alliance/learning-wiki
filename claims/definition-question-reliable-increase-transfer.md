## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Use of the automated grading system produced a reliable increase in scores on the definition question (t(280) = 2.077; p = 0.039), even though no definition exercises were in the system. [→ Angelo Kyrilov and David C. Noelle 2015](#angelo-kyrilov-and-david-c-noelle-2015)
`q2 i?` The authors interpret this as practice on simple translation questions transferring to definition questions, since the system contained no definition or successor-state axiom exercises. [→ Angelo Kyrilov and David C. Noelle 2015](#angelo-kyrilov-and-david-c-noelle-2015)

## Evidence

### Angelo Kyrilov and David C. Noelle 2015

Angelo Kyrilov and David C. Noelle. (2015). Using Automated Theorem Provers to Teach Knowledge Representation in First-Order Logic. 4th International Conference on Tools for Teaching Logic. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1507.03670](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1507.03670)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

A planned two-tailed t-test on the third exam question, which asked students to provide a (often recursive) definition for a predicate, found "a reliable increase in scores" for system users; no effect size is printed. The article notes "there were no deﬁnition or successor-state axiom questions in the system."

> "Use of our automated grading system produced a reliable increase in scores for this question (t(280) = 2 .077; p = 0.039)."
