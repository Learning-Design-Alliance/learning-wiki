## Evidence

### Maëlle Neveu 2023

Maëlle Neveu, Marie Geurten, Nancy Durieux, Laurence Rousselle. (2023). Finger Use and Arithmetic Skills in Children and Adolescents: a Scoping Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09722-8

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Longitudinal study following children with TD, DLD, MLD, and DLD+LMD from second to third grade; the review reports the group difference "was of medium effect size" but prints no numeric effect size value.

> "Wylie et al. (2012) showed that children with MLD and DLD + LMD made more frequent use of finger counting and switched to memory-based strategies later than children with TD and DLD. The difference between these groups was of medium effect size."

### Maëlle Neveu 2023 (2)

Maëlle Neveu, Marie Geurten, Nancy Durieux, Laurence Rousselle. (2023). Finger Use and Arithmetic Skills in Children and Adolescents: a Scoping Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09722-8

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Cross-sectional comparison of first-, third-, and fifth-graders with MLD and working memory deficits against TD children; children with MLD used finger counting more often but were less accurate. No effect size printed.

> "Comparing the computation skills of first-, third-, and fifth-graders with MLD and working memory deficits to those of TD children, Geary et al. (2004) showed that children with MLD used finger counting more often but were less accurate than TD children."

### Maëlle Neveu 2023 (3)

Maëlle Neveu, Marie Geurten, Nancy Durieux, Laurence Rousselle. (2023). Finger Use and Arithmetic Skills in Children and Adolescents: a Scoping Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09722-8

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Cross-sectional comparison of calculation strategies in Chinese and American TD kindergartners (Geary et al., 1993); the earlier switch in Chinese children showed group differences the review describes as "medium to large effect sizes" without printing numeric values.

> "this switch from finger-based to memory-based strategies occurred earlier in Chinese children than in their American peers. These group differences had medium to large effect sizes."
