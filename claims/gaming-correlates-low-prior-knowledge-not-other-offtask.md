# Students who gamed the system had lower prior knowledge and achievement, but gaming frequency was unrelated to other off-task behavior, arguing against lack of interest as the cause.

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Gaming frequency correlated significantly with pre-test score (r = -0.27, p = 0.02) and marginally with general academic achievement (r = -0.21, p = 0.10). [→ Baker et al. 2004](#baker-et-al-2004)
`q3 i?` Gaming frequency was not significantly related to other off-task behavior (F(1,68)=0.33, p=0.57); high-gaming students engaged in other off-task behavior at about the same frequency (15%) as never-gaming students (14%). [→ Baker et al. 2004 (2)](#baker-et-al-2004-2)

## Evidence

### Baker et al. 2004

Ryan Shaun Baker, Albert T. Corbett, Kenneth R. Koedinger, Angela Z. Wagner. (2004). Off-Task Behavior in the Cognitive Tutor Classroom: When Students "Game the System". CHI 2004, Vienna, Austria. https://telearn.hal.science/hal-00190176v1

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r1`

In the same observational study, gaming frequency correlated with pre-test score at r = -0.27; general academic achievement was marginally correlated (r = -0.21, p = 0.10) and dropped out when both predictors were used together.

> "there was a significant correlation between how frequently a student gamed the system and their pre-test score, F(1,68)=5.31,p=0.02, r= -0.27."

### Baker et al. 2004 (2)

Ryan Shaun Baker, Albert T. Corbett, Kenneth R. Koedinger, Angela Z. Wagner. (2004). Off-Task Behavior in the Cognitive Tutor Classroom: When Students "Game the System". CHI 2004, Vienna, Austria. https://telearn.hal.science/hal-00190176v1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same study: gaming frequency was unrelated to other off-task behavior, and the 8 high-gaming students engaged in other off-task behaviors at about the same frequency (15%) as never-gaming students (14%). The authors take this as evidence against lack of interest as the explanation.

> "There was also not a significant relationship between gaming the system and other off-task behavior, F(1,68)=0.33, p=0.57."
