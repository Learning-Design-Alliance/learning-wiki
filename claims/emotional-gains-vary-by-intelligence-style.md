## Evidence

### Sanchez-Martin et al. 2017

Jesus Sanchez-Martin, Garcia J. Alvarez-Gragera, Maria Antonia Davila-Acedo, Vicente Mellado. (2017). TEACHING TECHNOLOGY: FROM KNOWING TO FEELING. ENHANCING EMOTIONAL AND CONTENT ACQUISITION PERFORMANCE THROUGH GARDNER'S MULTIPLE INTELLIGENCES THEORY IN TECHNOLOGY AND DESIGN LESSONS. Journal of Technology and Science Education, 7(1), 58-79. https://doi.org/10.3926/jotse.238

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

IS-segmented emotional ANOVA post-test analysis (Table 2) in section 3.4, with small unbalanced subgroups (e.g. Logical-mathematical n = 4 experimental, 5 control). Descriptive point gains are printed; no standardised effect size is reported.

> "Every IS submitted a positive enhancement of the emotional score, being the most favored ones those subjects with Logical-mathematical (16.1 points of enhancement) and Spatial ones (16.34 points of enhancement)."

### Sanchez-Martin et al. 2017 (2)

Jesus Sanchez-Martin, Garcia J. Alvarez-Gragera, Maria Antonia Davila-Acedo, Vicente Mellado. (2017). TEACHING TECHNOLOGY: FROM KNOWING TO FEELING. ENHANCING EMOTIONAL AND CONTENT ACQUISITION PERFORMANCE THROUGH GARDNER'S MULTIPLE INTELLIGENCES THEORY IN TECHNOLOGY AND DESIGN LESSONS. Journal of Technology and Science Education, 7(1), 58-79. https://doi.org/10.3926/jotse.238

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The authors' own qualification of the segmented analysis in section 3.4: subgroup sizes were very low after dividing the universe into two groups and then nine IS subgroups, so results show a tendency only.

> "However, as we said before, many ANOVA p-values are above 0.05 as well, hence the statistical taxative affirmation of our hypothesis cannot be done."
