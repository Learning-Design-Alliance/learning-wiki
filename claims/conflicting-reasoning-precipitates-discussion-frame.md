## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The discussion frame was often precipitated by conflicting lines of reasoning among group members, who chained arguments to convince each other, as illustrated by a Newton's Third Law disagreement. [→ Conlin 2006](#conlin-2006)

## Evidence

### Conlin 2006

Conlin, L. D., Gupta, A., Scherr, R. E., & Hammer, D. (2006). The Dynamics of Students' Behaviors and Reasoning during Collaborative Physics Tutorial Sessions. Physics Education Research Conference proceedings record. https://www.compadre.org/PER/ [doi:10.1063/1.2820949](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.2820949)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Authors' interpretation of episode data: a group in the worksheet frame shifted abruptly into discussion after Maya disagreed that a massive truck and less massive car exert equal forces, mirroring an observation the authors attribute to Tannen and Wallet.

> "one reason is that the discussion frame was often precipitated by conflicting lines of reasoning amongst group members, who would then need to chain arguments together in order to convince each other of their explanation."
