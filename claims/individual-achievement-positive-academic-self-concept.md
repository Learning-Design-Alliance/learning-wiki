## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The effect of individual student achievement on academic self-concept is positive (linear term = .378) and consistent across all 26 countries. [→ Marsh 2003](#marsh-2003)

## Evidence

### Marsh 2003

Herbert W. Marsh & Kit-Tai Hau. (2003). Big-Fish–Little-Pond Effect on Academic Self-Concept: A Cross-Cultural (26-Country) Test of the Negative Effects of Academically Selective Schools. American Psychologist, 58(5), 364-376. https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.58.5.364

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Three-level multilevel model of PISA data. Students one SD above the mean in achievement had academic self-concepts .384 SD above the mean. Country-specific effects "varied from .14 to .63 (M ⫽ .38, SD ⫽ .11 in Table 1)", all statistically significant.

> "Consistent with a priori predictions, the effects of individual student achievement are substantial and positive (.378), whereas the effects of school-average achievement are less substantial and negative (–.206)."
