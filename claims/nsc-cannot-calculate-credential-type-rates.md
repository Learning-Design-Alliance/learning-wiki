# NSC data cannot be used alone to calculate two-year credential-type completion rates because 57.5 percent of completion records have a blank credential name field

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For 57.5 percent of two-year college completion records in the NSC sample the credential name field was blank, and 80.4 percent of students with uncategorizable NSC completions had earned an associate degree per ICCB records. [→ Jenny Nagaoka and Shelby Mahaffie 2020](#jenny-nagaoka-and-shelby-mahaffie-2020)

## Evidence

### Jenny Nagaoka and Shelby Mahaffie 2020

Jenny Nagaoka and Shelby Mahaffie. (2020). Tracking Two-Year College Outcomes: Comparing National Student Clearinghouse and Illinois Community College Board as Sources of Two-Year College Data. Technical Paper, University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/tracking-two-year-college-outcomes

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-tabulation of credential-type completion outcomes between ICCB and NSC data for 3,660 immediate enrollees (Table 4). ICCB showed a 14.1 percent associate degree completion rate versus 3.7 percent identifiable in NSC, demonstrating that NSC data alone cannot accurately calculate credential-type completion rates.

> "we see that 324 of the 403 students (80.4 percent) whose highest completion was a completion of uncategorizable type according to the NSC data had earned an associate degree according to the ICCB data."
