Tsvetomila Mihaylova 2026¶

Tsvetomila Mihaylova, Evanfiya Logacheva, Arto Hellas, Jing Fan, Francisco Castro, Bita Akram, Narges Norouzi, Peter Brusilovsky, Juho Leinonen. (2026). The Effects of Structured LLM-Generated Feedback on Programming Assignment Performance. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16933

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Negative binomial generalized linear mixed-effects model (NB-GLMM) on attempts to success from the same course dataset (Table 1). Fixed effects: General −0.175, Single-Issue −0.090, Diagnostic −0.200, with 94% HDIs excluding zero. The article attributes Single-Issue's smaller reduction to its focus on one issue per message.

"The results indicate that all hint types credibly re- duced the number of attempts to success relative to the No Feedback baseline, with the largest re- duction observed for Diagnostic Feedback ."

Related Claims¶

  • Human tutoring has a medium effect over no tutoring, and in one small study students learned as well when tutors only prompted them as when tutors also explained and gave feedback — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it
  • Advanced students showed reduced attempts with all hint types, while novices did not clearly benefit and made slightly more submissions with Single-Issue Feedback — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Attempt-reduction patterns differed across course parts: Diagnostic and General Feedback were associated with fewer attempts for New Content, Single-Issue for Old Content — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • All three LLM-generated feedback types are associated with faster time to solution than a compiler-errors-only baseline, with Single-Issue Feedback showing the largest reduction — related
  • Feedback effectiveness varied by content recency: guidance-heavy feedback performed less consistently on newer material released after the model's knowledge cutoff — related
  • Feedback effects on time to solution did not differ significantly between novice and advanced students, though predicted trends suggest feedback was more beneficial for advanced students — related
