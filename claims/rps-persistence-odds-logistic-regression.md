# RPS students had higher odds of persisting in engineering after controlling for semester, an association attenuated and non-significant when controlling for final grade

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In stepwise logistic regression, RPS students were 1.9 times more likely to stay in engineering than lecture students (95% C.L. 1.2-3.04). [→ Nathan Davis 2024](#nathan-davis-2024)
`q2 i?` Controlling for final course grade, the RPS persistence odds ratio was 1.52 (95% C.L. 0.94-2.53), not significant at p=0.05. [→ Nathan Davis 2024](#nathan-davis-2024)
`q2 i?` A one standard deviation increase in final grade raised persistence odds by a factor of 1.80 (95% C.L. 1.51-2.18). [→ Nathan Davis 2024](#nathan-davis-2024)

## Evidence

### Nathan Davis 2024

Nathan Davis, Eric Burkholder. (2024). Real-World Problem-Solving Class is Correlated with Higher Student Persistence in Engineering. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2405.03822](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2405.03822)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Stepwise multiple logistic regression on the N=782 sample controlling for semester and final grade (Table 5). Problem-Solving coefficient e^β = 1.90 (p=0.00706); with Final Score added, 1.52 (p=0.0918); Final Score odds ratio 1.80 (p=2.49x10-10).

> "We found that students in the RPS class were 1.9 times more likely to stay in engineering than in the lecture-based class (95% Confidence Limits [C.L.] = 1.2 – 3.04)"

### Nathan Davis 2024 (2)

Nathan Davis, Eric Burkholder. (2024). Real-World Problem-Solving Class is Correlated with Higher Student Persistence in Engineering. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2405.03822](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2405.03822)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Step 2 of the logistic regression model (Table 5) adds final score; the RPS odds ratio drops to 1.52 and the article notes "the correlation is not significant at the p = 0.05 level when controlling for final grade" while calling the effect-size moderate.

> "When controlling for final course grade, we found that the RPS students were 1.52 (95% C.L. = 0.94 – 2.53) times more likely to persist than their peers in the lecture-based courses who earned the same final grades"
