## Subclaims
`q4 i0` Event incentives produced a small positive but statistically non-significant effect on GCSE Maths (effect size 0.08), with no significant effects on English or Science. [→ Sibieta et al. 2014](#sibieta-et-al-2014)

## Evidence

### Sibieta et al. 2014

Luke Sibieta, Ellen Greaves, Barbara Sianesi. (2014). Increasing Pupil Motivation: Evaluation Report and Executive Summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED581249

`q4 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Cluster randomised controlled trial; 15 event-incentive schools compared with 33 control schools on an intention-to-treat basis. Table 2 reports an effect size of 0.08 (-0.01, 0.17) for GCSE Maths for all pupils, described as approximately one month's progress, and 0.04 for English, neither significant at the 5% level.

> "There is some suggestion that when pupils were offered the incentive of an event or trip, there was a small positive impact on Maths attainment at GCSE , but this is not statistically significant from zero."
