# Blended learning improves knowledge in four communication domains but not in communication structure

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The intervention group scored significantly higher in basics of medical communication, patient- vs physician-centered communication, WEMS techniques, and NURSE techniques. [→ Gross Sebastian 2025](#gross-sebastian-2025)
`q3 i?` Knowledge of communication structure did not significantly differ between groups; equivalence was not tested. [→ Gross Sebastian 2025 (2)](#gross-sebastian-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Gross Sebastian 2025

Gross Sebastian, Wunderlich Kurt, Arpagaus Armon, Becker Christoph, Gössi Flavio, Bissmann Benjamin, Zumbrunn Samuel K., Wilde Michael, Hunziker Sabina. (2025). Effectiveness of blended learning to improve medical students’ communication skills: a randomized, controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06938-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Domain sub-scores from the same RCT knowledge assessment (each 0–100), univariate and age/sex-adjusted regression. The article also reports significant adjusted differences of 16.64, 18.85, and 23.46 for the other three domains, each p < 0.001.

> "The intervention group showed significantly higher knowledge regarding the domains basics of medical communication (80.4 ± 19.6 versus 61.4 ± 21.3, adjusted difference 19.26, 95%CI 12.93 to 25.59, p < 0.001)"

### Gross Sebastian 2025 (2)

Gross Sebastian, Wunderlich Kurt, Arpagaus Armon, Becker Christoph, Gössi Flavio, Bissmann Benjamin, Zumbrunn Samuel K., Wilde Michael, Hunziker Sabina. (2025). Effectiveness of blended learning to improve medical students’ communication skills: a randomized, controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06938-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same RCT, communication-structure sub-score. No significant difference "(61.4 ± 30.4 versus 54.2 ± 39.7, adjusted difference 6.92, 95%CI -3.85 to 17.68, p = 0.21)"; the authors attribute this to ambiguity in the video annotation task instructions.

> "However, students’ knowledge regarding the domain communication structure did not significantly differ between groups (61.4 ± 30.4 versus 54.2 ± 39.7, adjusted difference 6.92, 95%CI -3.85 to 17.68, p = 0.21)"
