# Students taught an explicit problem-solving framework (DP1) made goals, features, difficulty, and information-planning decisions significantly more than non-framework students (RP2), who more often focused on a potential solution

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` DP1 students defined goals, criteria and constraints (decision 3), determined core features (decision 4), and identified difficult or uncertain areas (decision 12) statistically significantly more than RP2 students. [→ Kelly Miller 2026](#kelly-miller-2026)
`q2 i?` Decisions 12, 13 and 15, significant across both group contrasts, showed a higher percentage of both experts and DP1 students. [→ Kelly Miller 2026 (2)](#kelly-miller-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Kelly Miller 2026

Kelly Miller, Olivia Miller, Georgia Lawrence. (2026). Teaching Problem Solving in Undergraduate Physics Courses: An Endorsement for Deliberate Practice. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1103/7pqt-gd9c](https://doi.org/10.1103/7pqt-gd9c)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Chi-squared comparison of devise-a-plan responses between the framework-taught (DP1) and non-framework (RP2) introductory classes showed DP1 students 'define goals, criteria and constraints (decision 3)' and identify difficult or uncertain areas (decision 12) significantly more than RP2 students.

> "Figure 4 shows students taught a problem-solving procedure (i.e. DP1 students) define goals, criteria and constraints (decision 3), determine core features or ideas related to the problem (decision 4), identify areas of particular difficulty and/or uncertainty (decision 12)"

### Kelly Miller 2026 (2)

Kelly Miller, Olivia Miller, Georgia Lawrence. (2026). Teaching Problem Solving in Undergraduate Physics Courses: An Endorsement for Deliberate Practice. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1103/7pqt-gd9c](https://doi.org/10.1103/7pqt-gd9c)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

In the same DP1–RP2 comparison, the three decisions significant across both group contrasts — 'decisions 12, 13 and 15' — all showed 'a higher percentage of experts and a higher percentage of DP1 students', the pattern the authors read as DP1 decisions resembling experts'.

> "The other three decisions which show statistically significant differences between both sets of groups (decisions 12, 13 and 15), all show a higher percentage of experts and a higher percentage of DP1 students."

### Kelly Miller 2026 (3)

Kelly Miller, Olivia Miller, Georgia Lawrence. (2026). Teaching Problem Solving in Undergraduate Physics Courses: An Endorsement for Deliberate Practice. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1103/7pqt-gd9c](https://doi.org/10.1103/7pqt-gd9c)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The same chi-squared comparison showed the potential-solution decision was 'referenced more by RP2 students (compared to DP1 students) (83.6% compared to 58.3%)', which the authors read as RP2 students being less aligned with experts on this decision.

> "Decision 8 (focusing on a potential solution) was also referenced more by RP2 students (compared to DP1 students) (83.6% compared to 58.3%), implying that RP2 students are less aligned with experts on this decision."
