## Evidence

### Wei 2025

Wei, Y., Peng, X., Zhong, Y., Pi, F., Zhai, Y., & Bao, L. (2025). Can Contextualized Physics Problems Enhance Student Motivation? Uncovering a Student-Teacher Perception Gap through a Large-Scale Survey. [doi:10.1103/2g1b-hmhq](https://doi.org/10.1103/2g1b-hmhq)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Chi-square comparisons of difficulty-perception responses by grade (Figure 6). The article attributes the grade rise to increasing problem complexity requiring more effort to extract relevant information; no effect size is printed.

> "Eighth graders do not perceive CPP as significantly more difficult than decontextualized physics problems (DPP). However, in all other grades, students strongly perceive CPP as more challenging."

### Wei 2025 (2)

Wei, Y., Peng, X., Zhong, Y., Pi, F., Zhai, Y., & Bao, L. (2025). Can Contextualized Physics Problems Enhance Student Motivation? Uncovering a Student-Teacher Perception Gap through a Large-Scale Survey. [doi:10.1103/2g1b-hmhq](https://doi.org/10.1103/2g1b-hmhq)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Four U-tests comparing boys' and girls' difficulty-perception scores across grades 8–11. All p-values exceed 0.05, so the article reports no significant gender differences; no effect size is printed.

> "The tests yielded p-values of 0.211, 0.963, 0.119, 0.052 for the 8th, 9th, 10th, and 11th graders, respectively. Since all p-values are greater than 0.05, no significant gender differences in difficulty perception were observed."
