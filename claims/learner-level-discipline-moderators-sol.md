## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Learner level did not moderate cognitive-outcome conditions, but for affective outcomes the graduate/professional effect size was statistically significantly larger than the undergraduate comparison in both conditions. [→ Florence Martin 2021](#florence-martin-2021)
`q2 i?` The discipline of education had a statistically significantly larger effect size estimate than other disciplines in three of the four conditions; discipline did not moderate the synchronous-versus-face-to-face cognitive condition. [→ Florence Martin 2021 (2)](#florence-martin-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Florence Martin 2021

Florence Martin, Ting Sun, Murat Turk, and Albert D. Ritzhaupt. (2021). A Meta-Analysis on the Effects of Synchronous Online Learning on Cognitive and Affective Educational Outcomes. International Review of Research in Open and Distributed Learning, Volume 22, Number 3. https://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Mixed-effects moderator analysis comparing undergraduate with graduate/professional learner levels across the meta-analysis's four conditions. The contrast was significant only for affective outcomes; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Although none of the effect sizes was significant, the effect size for graduate/professional was statistically significantly larger than the undergraduate comparison  for both conditions (Q-value = 7.732, p = .005 for asynchronous, and Q-value = 10.570, p = .001 for face-to-face)."

### Florence Martin 2021 (2)

Florence Martin, Ting Sun, Murat Turk, and Albert D. Ritzhaupt. (2021). A Meta-Analysis on the Effects of Synchronous Online Learning on Cognitive and Affective Educational Outcomes. International Review of Research in Open and Distributed Learning, Volume 22, Number 3. https://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Mixed-effects moderator analysis on the synchronous-vs-asynchronous cognitive condition; the article reports parallel significant discipline differences for the asynchronous-affective (Q = 16.773, p < .001) and face-to-face-affective (Q = 15.904, p < .001) conditions, but no moderation for face-to-face cognitive outcomes.

> "results revealed that effect sizes varied as a function of discipline, with the discipline of education having a statistically significantly larger effect size estimate than other disciplines (Q-value = 18.738, p < .001)."
