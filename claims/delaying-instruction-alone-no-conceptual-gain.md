# Merely delaying instruction until after problem solving does not improve conceptual understanding when no additional measures trigger knowledge-gap awareness or deep-feature discovery

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The order of the learning phases did not significantly affect conceptual understanding posttest scores, and the PS-I vs. I-PS difference was not significant. [→ Loibl 2020](#loibl-2020)

## Evidence

### Loibl 2020

Loibl, K., Tillema, M., Rummel, N., & van Gog, T. (2020). The effect of contrasting cases during problem solving prior to and after instruction. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-020-09504-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA on conceptual understanding in the randomized 2×2 experiment (N = 181). Neither factor was significant; the PS-I vs. I-PS pairwise difference was "not significant (p = 0.22)" with overlapping confidence intervals. Equivalence was not tested.

> "the ANCOVA on conceptual understanding showed that neither the order of the learning phases, F(1, 176) = 0.74, p = 0.39, η2 < 0.01, nor the presence or absence of contrasting cases during problem solving, F(1, 176) = 2.65, p = 0.11, η2 = 0.02, significantly affected posttest scores"
