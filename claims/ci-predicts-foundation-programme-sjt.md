## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Linear regression shows a statistically significant positive relationship between average CI scores and UKFPO SJT scores, and first-CI-decile students score significantly lower on the SJT than the other nine deciles. [→ Sawdon Marina 2020](#sawdon-marina-2020)

## Evidence

### Sawdon Marina 2020

Sawdon Marina, McLachlan JC. (2020). '10% of your medical students will cause 90% of your problems': a prospective correlational study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2020-038472

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Prospective correlational linkage of CI scores from one UK medical school (2006–2014) to UKMED SJT data. Regression found a "statistically significant positive relationship (R=0.373, R2=0.139, B=0.066, p<0.001, n=539)"; the first CI decile differed from the other nine deciles on SJT scores (p<0.001). No effect size for the decile contrast is printed.

> "Linear regression analysis shows a relationship between these two parameters (R=0.373, R2=0.139, B=0.066, p<0.001, n=539). T-test showed a statistically significant difference between SJT scores of students scoring in the first decile of the CI and the other nine deciles, (p<0.001)."
