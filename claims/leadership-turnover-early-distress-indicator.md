# Frequent or midyear leadership turnover is one of the earliest and most prevalent indicators of charter school distress

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Leadership turnover, especially when frequent or midyear, was one of the most prevalent and earliest indicators of a school experiencing distress and appeared to inhibit capacity to sustain improvement. [→ National Charter School Resource Center 2020](#national-charter-school-resource-center-2020)

## Evidence

### National Charter School Resource Center 2020

National Charter School Resource Center, lead authors Aimee Evan, Laura Groth, and Hannah Sullivan. (2020). Identifying Indicators of Distress in Charter Schools: Part 1 The Role and Perspective of Charter School Authorizers. Bethesda, MD: Manhattan Strategy Group. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED609911

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Authorizer interview findings: authorizers 'almost universally named school leaders as the foundation of a school's health.' The report reports frequent turnover as an early sign that appeared to inhibit schools' capacity to sustain improvement or retain teachers.

> "Leadership turnover was one of the most prevalent indicators of a school experiencing distress, especially if leadership turnover occurs frequently or in the middle of a school year."
