## Evidence

### Krieglstein 2025

Krieglstein, F., Beege, M., Wesenberg, L., Rey, G. D., & Schneider, S. (2025). The Distorting Influence of Primacy Effects on Reporting Cognitive Load in Learning Materials of Varying Complexity. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09980-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Two-tailed t-tests in the main study comparing the two sequence conditions on the three delayed cognitive load assessments. No comparison reached significance; equivalence was not tested. The authors attribute this to both conditions receiving identical learning units varying only in sequence.

> "There were no significant differences between the increasing and decreasing complexity conditions for ICL, t(98) = 1.04, p = 0.302, ECL, t(98) = 0.74, p = 0.462, and GCL, t(98) = 0.22, p = 0.827."
