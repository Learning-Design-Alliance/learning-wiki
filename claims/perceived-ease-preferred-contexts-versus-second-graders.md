## Evidence

### Amogh Sirnoorkar 2025

Amogh Sirnoorkar, Winter Allen, Syed Furqan Abbas Hashmi, and N. Sanjay Rebello. (2025). Examining Student and AI Generated Personalized Analogies in Introductory Physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2511.04290](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2511.04290)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Survey self-reports of perceived ease coded as Easy, Difficult, or Intermediate across the two explanation cases; the second-grader context showed "around 30% of the students perceived explaining the curve as easy, 61% as difficult."

> "In the second grader context, around 30% of the students perceived explaining the curve as easy, 61% as difficult, and 9% as partly easy and difficult ("Intermediate")."

### Amogh Sirnoorkar 2025 (2)

Amogh Sirnoorkar, Winter Allen, Syed Furqan Abbas Hashmi, and N. Sanjay Rebello. (2025). Examining Student and AI Generated Personalized Analogies in Introductory Physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2511.04290](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2511.04290)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In the preferred-domain case the trend reversed: "around 52% perceived the process as easy, 36% found it difficult," and 95-98% across ease categories generated analogies when explicitly prompted (Figure 10).

> "While around 52% perceived the process as easy, 36% found it difficult, and 12% described it as a mixture of both. Moreover, an overwhelming 95%, 97% and 98% of the students who perceived the process as easy, difficult, and intermediate, generated analogies when explicitly prompted"
