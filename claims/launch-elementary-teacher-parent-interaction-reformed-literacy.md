# Elementary schools led by LAUNCH principals show higher teacher-parent interaction and more reformed literacy practices

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teacher-parent interaction was significantly higher and teachers reported greater use of reformed literacy practices in elementary schools led by LAUNCH principals than in schools led by comparably experienced principals. [→ Hart 2006](#hart-2006)

## Evidence

### Hart 2006

Hart, H. M., Ponisciak, S. M., Sporte, S. E., & Stevens, W. D. (2006). Principal and Teacher Leadership in Chicago: Continuing Analysis of Three Initiatives. Evaluation Report prepared for The Chicago Public Education Fund, Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/principal-and-teacher-leadership-chicago-continuing-analysis-three-initiatives

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional analysis of teacher ratings on CCSR essential-supports scales for elementary LAUNCH schools with both principal and teacher survey data (57 percent had enough data). More-experienced LAUNCH principals showed greater reformed literacy practice while less-experienced ones showed greater teacher-parent interaction.

> "Here we found that teacher-parent interaction was significantly higher in elementary schools led by LAUNCH principals than those led by comparably experienced principals. Teachers in these LAUNCH schools also reported greater use of reformed literacy practices."
