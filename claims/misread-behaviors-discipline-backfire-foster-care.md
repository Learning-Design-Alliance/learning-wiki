# Teachers report that disruptive behaviors of students in foster care are easily misinterpreted and that purely disciplinary responses can make things worse

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teachers observed that behaviors such as demanding attention, disruption, and resisting authority are easy to misinterpret, and that when such behaviors are not understood the response is often purely disciplinary, sometimes making things worse. ## Evidence

### The Center for the Future of Teaching and Learning 2010

The Center for the Future of Teaching and Learning. (2010). Ready to Succeed in the Classroom: Findings from Teacher Discussion Groups on their Experiences and Aspirations Teaching Students in the Foster Care System. Prepared for the Stuart Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED511840

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative finding from the discussion groups' Barriers section. One administrator explained he and colleagues did not realize that "expelling a student from school also led to an automatic change in placement," adding a punitive layer. Teachers also described the "2-to-21" syndrome of age-inconsistent behavior swings.

> "Unfortunately, when these behaviors are not explored or understood, the response is often purely disciplinary — sometimes making things worse."
