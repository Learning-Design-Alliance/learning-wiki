# The effect of a growth mindset intervention does not interact with knowledge type for motivation, cognitive load, or recall

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Mixed ANCOVA showed no significant knowledge type × mindset intervention interaction for interest, enjoyment, difficulty, effort, or recall performance. [→ Kate M. Xu 2024](#kate-m-xu-2024)

## Evidence

### Kate M. Xu 2024

Kate M. Xu, Sarah Coertjens, Florence Lespiau, Kim Ouwehand, Hanke Korpershoek, Fred Paas, David C. Geary. (2024). An Evolutionary Approach to Motivation and Learning: Differentiating Biologically Primary and Secondary Knowledge. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09880-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mixed ANCOVA with baseline growth mindset as covariate in the 144-participant randomized experiment. The article reports "no significant interaction effects" between knowledge type and mindset intervention on any dependent variable; only F and p values are printed.

> "Results indicated there were no significant interaction effects (also see means, marginal means in Table 2), including for interest (F (1, 141) = 2.52, p = 0.115), enjoyment (F (1, 141) = 2.39, p = 0.124), perceived difficulty (F (1, 141) = 2.37, p = 0.126)"
