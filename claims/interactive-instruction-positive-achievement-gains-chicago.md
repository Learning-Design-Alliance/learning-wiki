# Interactive instruction is positively associated with one-year achievement gains in reading and mathematics in Chicago elementary schools

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In Chicago elementary schools, students learned more in reading and mathematics when teachers used interactive instruction frequently, compared to the city average gain. [→ Julia B. Smith 2001](#julia-b-smith-2001)

## Evidence

### Julia B. Smith 2001

Julia B. Smith, Valerie E. Lee, and Fred M. Newmann. (2001). Instruction and Achievement in Chicago Elementary Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/instruction-and-achievement-chicago-elementary-schools

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

HLM analysis linking teacher survey reports of interactive instruction to ITBS achievement gains for over 100,000 students in 384 schools, controlling for student, classroom, and school characteristics. Students in high-interactive schools gained "5.1 percent more than the city average in mathematics and 5.2 percent more in reading."

> "In schools where teachers used interactive instruction frequently, students learned 5.1 percent more than the city average in mathematics and 5.2 percent more in reading."
