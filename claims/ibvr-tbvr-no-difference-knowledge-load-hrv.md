## Evidence

### Lin Wan-Ni 2025

Lin Wan-Ni, Chuang Hai-Hua, Chao Yi-Ping, Hsin Li-Jen, Kang Chung-Jan, Fang Tuan-Jen, Li Hsueh-Yu, Lee Li-Ang. (2025). Image-based and textbook-based virtual reality training on operational skills among junior residents: a proof of concept study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07245-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Within the cross-over pilot, Wilcoxon signed-rank comparisons of quiz scores found "no statistically significant differences in the number of correct answers for small quizzes"; no effect size printed, so equivalence was not tested.

> "there were no statistically significant differences in the number of correct answers for small quizzes, whether in pretests, posttests, or percentage change, between the IBVR and TBVR modules (all p > 0.05; Table 2)"

### Lin Wan-Ni 2025 (2)

Lin Wan-Ni, Chuang Hai-Hua, Chao Yi-Ping, Hsin Li-Jen, Kang Chung-Jan, Fang Tuan-Jen, Li Hsueh-Yu, Lee Li-Ang. (2025). Image-based and textbook-based virtual reality training on operational skills among junior residents: a proof of concept study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07245-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Generalized estimating equations controlling for age, sex, residence level, and time analyzed 5-minute HRV epochs; the metrics "showed no relationship with VR module type or training sequence". Cognitive load questionnaires (CLS, TLX, CLC) were also comparable (all p > 0.05).

> "HR, SDNN, RMSSD, and LF/HR ratio showed no relationship with VR module type or training sequence (all p > 0.05; Table 3)"
