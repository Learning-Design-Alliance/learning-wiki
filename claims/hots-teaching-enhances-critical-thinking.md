# Purposely teaching for higher order thinking skills enhances high school students' critical thinking compared with traditional teaching

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` High school students exposed to teaching strategies designed for enhancing higher order thinking skills showed statistically significant improvement in critical thinking skills components compared with traditionally taught control groups. [→ Miri 2007](#miri-2007)

## Evidence

### Miri 2007

Miri, B., David, BC. & Uri, Z. (2007). Purposely Teaching for the Promotion of Higher-order Thinking Skills: A Case of Critical Thinking. Res Sci Educ 37, 353–369. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11165-006-9029-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Longitudinal case study with a pre-, post-, and post–post experimental design comparing an experimental group of science students (n = 57) with two traditionally taught control groups. The article reports "a statistically significant improvement on critical thinking skills components"; no effect size is printed.

> "we have found that the experimental group showed a statistically significant improvement on critical thinking skills components and disposition towards critical thinking subscales, such as truth-seeking, open-mindedness, self-confidence, and maturity, compared with the control groups."
