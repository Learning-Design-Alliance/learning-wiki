## Subclaims
`q3 i?` No overall effect of project participation was found on ACT Mathematics Composite scores or ACT Plane Geometry/Trigonometry scores. [→ Middleton et al. 2011](#middleton-et-al-2011)
`q3 i?` On the ACT Algebra/Coordinate Geometry subscale, the interaction effect was statistically significant, and a Scheffe post hoc test showed participating teachers' students outperformed control teachers' students in Algebra II (p < .05). [→ Middleton et al. 2011 (2)](#middleton-et-al-2011-2)

## Evidence

### Middleton et al. 2011

James A. Middleton, Mona Toncheff, Susan Haag. (2011). Growth in Secondary Teachers' Content Knowledge and Practice in Discrete Mathematics. Proceedings of the 33rd Annual Meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education. https://www.pmena.org/pmena-proceedings/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The ACT was used as a nationally-validated outcome measure augmenting the AIMS data; the analysis found "no overall effect of project participation" on the Composite or Plane Geometry/Trigonometry subscales, though a main effect for Course was detected on each.

> "Results are mixed, showing no overall effect of project participation on ACT Mathematics Composite scores, or on ACT Plane Geometry/Trigonometry scores."

### Middleton et al. 2011 (2)

James A. Middleton, Mona Toncheff, Susan Haag. (2011). Growth in Secondary Teachers' Content Knowledge and Practice in Discrete Mathematics. Proceedings of the 33rd Annual Meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education. https://www.pmena.org/pmena-proceedings/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

On the ACT Algebra/Coordinate Geometry subscale, a significant main effect was found for Course but not for Participation; the "interaction effect, however was found to be statistically significant", with the Scheffe post hoc test favoring participating teachers' students in Algebra II.

> "The interaction effect, however was found to be statistically significant. A Scheffe post hoc test revealed that in Algebra II, the largest represented course in the study, Participating teachers' students outperformed those of Control teachers (p < .05)."
