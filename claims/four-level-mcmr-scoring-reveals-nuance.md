## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith 2020

Trevor I. Smith, Philip Eaton, Suzanne White Brahmia, Alexis Olsho, Charlotte Zimmerman, Andrew Boudreaux. (2020). Toward a valid instrument for measuring physics quantitative literacy. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.smith_t](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.smith_t)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Four-level scoring (Completely Correct, Some Correct, Both Correct and Incorrect, Completely Incorrect) applied to the six MCMR items on PIQL v2.2. The authors state this tells a very different story than CTT results, which collapse partial correctness into a broad Incorrect category.

> "Fig. 2 also shows us that at least 60% of students provide at least one correct response to each item (Completely Correct, Some Correct, and Both Correct and Incorrect combined), although this is often coupled with an incorrect response (6%–44% of students categorized as Both Correct and Incorrect)."
