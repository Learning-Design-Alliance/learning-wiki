## Evidence

### Lees-Murdock D. J. 2024

Lees-Murdock D. J., Khan D., Irwin R., Graham J., Hinch V., O'Hagan B., McClean S. (2024). Assessing the Efficacy of Active Learning to Support Student Performance Across Undergraduate Programmes in Biomedical Science. British Journal of Biomedical Science. https://doi.org/10.3389/bjbs.2024.12148

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre/post comparison of exam marks per course combining two academic years on each side (Figures 3A, 3B). The article reports exam marks "significantly increased (p < 0.05 to p < 0.01)" in the five named courses; no effect sizes are printed.

> "However, final exam marks post-Smartwork significantly increased (p < 0.05 to p < 0.01) in Biomedical Science, Biology, Dietetics, Human Nutrition and Optometry courses compared to pre-Smartwork counterparts."

### Lees-Murdock D. J. 2024 (2)

Lees-Murdock D. J., Khan D., Irwin R., Graham J., Hinch V., O'Hagan B., McClean S. (2024). Assessing the Efficacy of Active Learning to Support Student Performance Across Undergraduate Programmes in Biomedical Science. British Journal of Biomedical Science. https://doi.org/10.3389/bjbs.2024.12148

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Per-course pre/post comparisons of overall module and class test marks (Figures 2 and 4) found no change for any course individually; the authors suggest this could be due to different enrolment numbers per course.

> "Interestingly, we did not see any change in overall module and class test marks for any course individually."
