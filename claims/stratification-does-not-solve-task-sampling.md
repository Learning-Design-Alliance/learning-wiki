# Stratifying tasks into science-content strata only slightly reduces relative task-sampling variability and increases the number of tasks needed for .80 reliability (27 to 38)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a stratified p x (t:s) x o x m G study, the combined p x (t:s) and p x (t:s) x o components account for 44.52% of variation versus 57.18% unstratified, and a decision study shows more tasks (38 vs 27) are needed for .80 reliability. [→ Shavelson et al. 1999](#shavelson-et-al-1999)
`q2 i?` Stratification introduces non-negligible strata-sampling variability (ps 8.54%, pso 7.97%, psom 1.87% of total variability). [→ Shavelson et al. 1999](#shavelson-et-al-1999)

## Evidence

### Shavelson et al. 1999

Richard J. Shavelson, Maria Araceli Ruiz-Primo, Edward W. Wiley. (1999). Note on Sources of Sampling Variability in Science Performance Assessments. Journal of Educational Measurement, 36(1), 61-71. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Note+on+Sources+of+Sampling+Variability+in+Science+Performance+Assessments

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

A post-hoc stratified G study treating Electric Mysteries and Bugs as two strata (Table 3) found the p x [t:s] x o component accounted for "40 percent of total variation in scores, compared with 31 percent in the un-stratified design". A decision study showed reliability costs: "27 in the un-stratified G study, and 38 in the stratified design".

> "we calculated that the total number of tasks (N.) needed for a reliability of .80 would be 27 in the un-stratified G study, and 38 in the stratified design"
