## Evidence

### Bailey E. G. 2018

Bailey E. G., Baek D., Meiling J., Morris C., Nelson N., Rice N. S., Rose S., Stockdale P. (2018). Learning Gains from a Recurring “Teach and Question” Homework Assignment in a General Biology Course: Using Reciprocal Peer Tutoring Outside Class. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-12-0259

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental comparison of two sections of a general biology course (TQ n = 44, R n = 41) using a mixed ANCOVA with LCTSR score as covariate; course section significantly explained variation in test scores (p = 0.019, ηp2 = 0.07). The article reports "The TQ section outperformed the R section an average of 5.7% on every test".

> "The TQ section outperformed the R section an average of 5.7% on every test (95% confidence interval for mean: 3.5–7.9%)."

### Bailey E. G. 2018 (2)

Bailey E. G., Baek D., Meiling J., Morris C., Nelson N., Rice N. S., Rose S., Stockdale P. (2018). Learning Gains from a Recurring “Teach and Question” Homework Assignment in a General Biology Course: Using Reciprocal Peer Tutoring Outside Class. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-12-0259

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mann-Whitney U-test on students' average test scores (n = 46 TQ, 44 R), used because some midterm data violated normality; no effect size printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect). The article reports the TQ section "4.1% higher".

> "The average score on all tests was 4.1% higher for students enrolled in the TQ section compared with the R section (p = 0.035)."
