# Learning in-depth semantic associations with novel objects facilitates recognition and attenuates viewpoint dependency

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Associating clusters of distinctive semantic features with novel objects facilitates discrimination across viewpoints, and this attenuation occurs for in-depth semantic associations but not for non-semantic verbal labels. [→ Collins 2014](#collins-2014)

## Evidence

### Collins 2014

Collins, Jessica A., & Olson, Ingrid R. (2014). Knowledge is Power: How Conceptual Knowledge Transforms Visual Cognition. Psychonomic bulletin & review. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-013-0564-3

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports three behavioral training studies in which participants learned clusters of three semantic features per novel object and then performed a perceptual matching task with the second object shown at 0°, 30°, 60°, or 120° viewpoints. No effect sizes are printed.

> "the viewpoint dependency of object recognition was attenuated for objects that had been associated with in-depth semantic associations (Curby et al., 2004), but not for those associated with non-semantic verbal labels (Collins & Curby, 2013)"
