Luo 2016¶

Luo, W., Pelletier, J., Duffin, K., Ormand, C., Hung, W., Shernoff, D., Zhai, X., Iverson, E., Whalley, K., Gallaher, C., and Furness, W. (2016). Advantages of Computer Simulation in Enhancing Students' Learning About Landform Evolution: A Case Study Using the Grand Canyon. Journal of Geoscience Education 64, 60-73. https://doi.org/10.5408/15-080.1

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Per-question growth analysis in the quasiexperiment. The paper group manually constructed a cross section from a contour map while the simulation group saw a transparent cutting plane; the authors interpret the model's "visual effect" as more effective at conveying the concept.

"The highest gain for both groups was the question about cross section (paper group, 30%; simulation group, 60%). The students in the paper group were required to construct a cross-section manually from a contour map, whereas the simulation group students were shown a transparent plane cutting through the valley"
