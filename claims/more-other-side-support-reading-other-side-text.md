## Evidence

### Iordanou 2025

Iordanou, K., Fotiou, C., Manoli, A., & Zembylas, M. (2025). Examining myside bias on a controversial historical event after engagement in dialogic argumentation: Insights from a think aloud study. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102209

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Think-aloud study of 30 Greek-Cypriot 6th graders reading authentic own-side and other-side accounts of the 1974 war; opinion statements were coded from think-aloud protocols. The study found "a difference that was statistically significant" in other-side support across texts, with no notable change over time.

> "Statements supporting the “other-side” were more prevalent when reading the “other-side” text (n = 16), compared to the “own-side” text (n = 4), a difference that was statistically significant. There was no notable change over time."
