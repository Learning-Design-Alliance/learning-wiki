## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a well-designed modern physics course that includes the Bohr model, most students (76-80%) described atoms using the Schrödinger model on the final exam, and exclusive Bohr-model use dropped to 13-16% after curriculum improvements. [→ S. B. McKagan 2007](#s-b-mckagan-2007)

## Evidence

### S. B. McKagan 2007

S. B. McKagan, K. K. Perkins, and C. E. Wieman. (2007). Why we should teach the Bohr model and how to teach it effectively. https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.1541 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0707.1541](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0707.1541)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Analysis of final-exam descriptions of a ground-state hydrogen atom across four semesters of a transformed modern physics course (enrollments 189, 184, 94, 153), coded for Bohr, deBroglie, and Schrödinger model use. After curriculum changes, "76 − 80% of students were using the Schr¨ odinger model" and only 13-16% used the Bohr model alone (p < 0.05).

> "After changing the curriculum to address the problems seen in the ﬁrst two semesters, we found that 76 − 80% of students were using the Schr¨ odinger model"
