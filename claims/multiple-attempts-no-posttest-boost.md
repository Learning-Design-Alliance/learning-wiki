# Multiple posttest attempts in the emporium method did not boost posttest scores; single-attempt students scored higher

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2017 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Emporium students who attempted the posttest a single time scored significantly higher on average than students who attempted multiple times. [→ Cousins-Cooper 2017](#cousins-cooper-2017)
`q3 i?` A subset regression excluding multiple-attempt students still showed the emporium method about 12 points higher, providing no statistical evidence that multiple attempts boost the posttest score. [→ Cousins-Cooper 2017 (2)](#cousins-cooper-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Cousins-Cooper 2017

Cousins-Cooper, K., Staley, K. N., Kim, S., & Luke, N. S. (2017). The Effect of the Emporium Instructional Method on Students' Performance in College Algebra and Trigonometry. European Journal of Science and Mathematics Education, 5(1), 1-13. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1129978

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of posttest attempt counts among emporium students (61% attempted once); single-attempt mean was 84.7 versus 77.4 for multiple attempts, "p‐value <0.0001"; the fourth and fifth attempts were likely technical glitches.

> "The average posttest score of the students who attempted a single time was 84.7 points and that of the students who attempted multiple times was 77.4 points. This difference was statistically significant (p‐value <0.0001)."

### Cousins-Cooper 2017 (2)

Cousins-Cooper, K., Staley, K. N., Kim, S., & Luke, N. S. (2017). The Effect of the Emporium Instructional Method on Students' Performance in College Algebra and Trigonometry. European Journal of Science and Mathematics Education, 5(1), 1-13. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1129978

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subset analysis repeating the covariate-adjusted regression after excluding students who attempted the posttest multiple times; the emporium advantage remained with "p‐value = 5.26E‐18".

> "Although the students who attempted the post‐test once were considered in the regression model, the emporium method achieved 12 points higher on average than the traditional method (p‐value = 5.26E‐18)."
