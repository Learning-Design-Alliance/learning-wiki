# Small school contexts may both enable and constrain professional communities: collegiality rises but heavy workloads make collective instructional focus difficult

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Small schools seem to improve collegiality between teachers and encourage collaboration supporting informal supportive practices, while demanding schedules and increased responsibilities make it difficult for teachers to prioritize collective instructional development. [→ Stevens 2006](#stevens-2006)

## Evidence

### Stevens 2006

Stevens, W. D., with Kahne, J. (2006). Professional Communities and Instructional Improvement Practices: A study of small high schools in Chicago. Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/professional-communities-and-instructional-improvement-practices-study-small-high

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Fieldwork across seven CHSRI schools found teachers perceived small schools as highly personalized and collaborative; teachers reported collegial relationships and more cooperation because the staff was small. At the same time, teachers reported extremely demanding schedules and increased responsibilities.

> "Small schools do not necessarily increase teach - ers’ ability to focus on instructional improve- ment. On the one hand, small schools seem to improve collegiality between teachers and encourage collaboration."
