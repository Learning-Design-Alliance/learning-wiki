## Evidence

### Michael C. Wittmann 2005

Michael C. Wittmann, Jeffrey T. Morgan, and Lei Bao. (2005). Addressing student models of energy loss in quantum tunnelling. Submitted for publication to the European Journal of Physics. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/26/6/001](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/26/6/001)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The article interprets Dave's examination sketch, in which the axis of oscillation was higher in region I than in region III and shifted with the exponential decay term. The authors read this as consistent with considering "the height of the axis of oscillation to be a measure of the energy of the particle."

> "The axis around which the wave function oscillates was higher in region I (the incoming wave) than in region III.  In the barrier (region II), Dave sketched an exponential decay (though both exponential increase and decay are mathematically possible and written out in mathematical notation under the function).  The axis around which the wave function oscillates shifted with the exponential decay term in the barrier."

### Michael C. Wittmann 2005 (2)

Michael C. Wittmann, Jeffrey T. Morgan, and Lei Bao. (2005). Addressing student models of energy loss in quantum tunnelling. Submitted for publication to the European Journal of Physics. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/26/6/001](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/26/6/001)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

On the diagnostic survey, a subset of respondents (17 of 34) were asked to sketch the wave function; the article reports that all students who "gave the axis shift response also described energy loss," consistent with the examination results.

> "All of the students who Addressing student models of energy loss in quantum tunnelling gave the axis shift response also described energy loss."
