# Generative (Constructive) engagement by young children produces better learning than attentive (Passive) engagement

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Five-year-olds who explained how a gear machine works while watching (Constructive) outperformed children who only watched (Passive) on causal explanation and exclusion of an irrelevant part. [→ Chi 2018](#chi-2018)

## Evidence

### Chi 2018

Chi, M. T. H., Adams, J., Bogusch, E. B., Bruchok, C., Kang, S., Lancaster, M., Levy, R., Li, N., McEldoon, K. L., Stump, G. S., Wylie, R., Xu, D., & Yaghmourian, D. L. (2018). Translating the ICAP Theory of Cognitive Engagement Into Practice. Cognitive Science, 42. https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12626

`q2 · i?`

The article describes, citing Legare and Lombrozo, a study in which five-year-olds in an explain condition told the experimenter how the crank-fan machine works while it turned, versus a watch condition; the explain group was significantly better on causal explanation and at excluding the irrelevant topper piece.

> "the "explain" group was signiﬁcantly better at giving an explanation of the causal-functional relationship between the crank and the gears to allow the machine to work, and it was also substantially better at excluding the irrelevant non-functional "topper" piece in their explanations"
