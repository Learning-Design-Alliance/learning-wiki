# Question-by-question analysis of the conceptual questions was mostly consistent with the omnibus result, except that prebuilt-simulation students were more likely to answer CQ4 correctly than AI-group students.

## Evidence

### Yossi Ben-Zion 2026

Yossi Ben-Zion, Turhan K. Carroll, Colin G. West, Jesse Wong, and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2026). Leveraging generative artificial intelligence for simulation-based physics experiments: A new approach to virtual learning about the real world. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1103/s8dy-kqy5](https://doi.org/10.1103/s8dy-kqy5)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Question-by-question Fisher's exact tests (Appendix 3) with Cramer's V for each of five conceptual questions; the article found "a statistically significant association be tween lab type and student performance" on every question, with printed Cramer's V values ranging from 0.2 to 0.778.

> "we reject the null hypothesis (p < 0.05) and conclude that there is a statistically significant association be tween lab type and student performance for each of our review questions."

### Yossi Ben-Zion 2026 (2)

Yossi Ben-Zion, Turhan K. Carroll, Colin G. West, Jesse Wong, and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2026). Leveraging generative artificial intelligence for simulation-based physics experiments: A new approach to virtual learning about the real world. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1103/s8dy-kqy5](https://doi.org/10.1103/s8dy-kqy5)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pairwise comparisons on CQ4 found the only significant AI-vs-Sim difference among conceptual questions; the article states the AI group was more likely to answer CQ1, CQ2, and CQ5 correctly than the traditional lab, and Sim similarly for CQ1, CQ2, CQ4, and CQ5.

> "We can conclude that students who did the lab via pre -built simulations were more likely to answer CQ4 correctly than students who did the lab using AI."
