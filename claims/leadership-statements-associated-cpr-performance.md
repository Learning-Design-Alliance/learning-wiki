## Evidence

### Hunziker S 2013

Hunziker S, Pagani S, Fasler K, Tschan F, Semmer NK, Marsch S. (2013). Impact of a stress coping strategy on perceived stress levels and performance during a simulated cardiopulmonary resuscitation: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-227X-13-8

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis within the simulation trial coding leadership statements (task assignment, decisions what/how, commands) from video transcripts; the authors report significant correlations with hands-on time (0.20, p=0.02) and time to start CPR (r= -0.24, p<0.01).

> "There was a significant positive correlation between leadership statements and hands-on time (0.20, p=0.02) and a significant negative correlation between leadership statements and time to start CPR (r= -0.24, p<0.01)"

### Hunziker S 2013 (2)

Hunziker S, Pagani S, Fasler K, Tschan F, Semmer NK, Marsch S. (2013). Impact of a stress coping strategy on perceived stress levels and performance during a simulated cardiopulmonary resuscitation: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-227X-13-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Intention-to-treat comparison of leadership statements between randomized groups found no difference (10.4 vs 10.9 statements, p=0.54 in Table 3); the authors suggest this may partly explain the lack of performance improvement.

> "No differences between the groups also emerged for the number of leadership statements."
