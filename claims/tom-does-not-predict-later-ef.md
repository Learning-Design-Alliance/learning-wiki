# Earlier theory of mind does not uniquely predict later executive function at either transition

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` ToM at 3 did not predict EF at 4 (β = −.10, p = .60), and ToM at 4 did not predict EF at 5 (β = −.12, p = .58). [→ Marcovitch S. 2014](#marcovitch-s-2014)

## Evidence

### Marcovitch S. 2014

Marcovitch S., O'Brien M., Calkins S. D., Leerkes E. M., Weaver J. M., Levine D. W. (2014). A Longitudinal Assessment of the Relation between Executive Function and Theory of Mind at 3, 4, and 5 Years. Cognitive Development. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogdev.2014.07.001

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Latent variable cross predictions in the same 226-child longitudinal model: neither ToM-to-EF path was significant. No effect size was printed, so impact is null; equivalence was not tested.

> "In contrast, earlier ToM abilities did not uniquely predict later EF (from age 3 to age 4: β = −.10, p = .60; from age 4 to age 5: β = −.12, p = .58)."
