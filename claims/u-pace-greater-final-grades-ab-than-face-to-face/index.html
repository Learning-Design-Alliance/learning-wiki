Evidence¶

Fleming 2018¶

Fleming, R., Pedrick, L.E., Soiber, L., Kienzler, S., Fleming, R.R., & Reddy, D.M. (2018). Increasing undergraduate success: A randomized controlled trial of U-Pace instruction. Online Learning, 22(3), 175-191. https://doi.org/10.24059/olj.v22i3.1317

q4 · i? · causal · r3

Binary logistic regression compared objectively determined final grades of A or B versus B- through F across the four randomly assigned conditions, with withdrawals counted as below B. The printed result was "8.62 times higher for U-Pace students than face-to-face students"; no standardized effect size was printed.

"The odds of earning a final grade of A or B were 8.62 times higher for U-Pace students than face-to-face students (Wald χ2 = 65.482, p < .001, Exp(B) = 8.62)"

Fleming 2018 (2)¶

Fleming, R., Pedrick, L.E., Soiber, L., Kienzler, S., Fleming, R.R., & Reddy, D.M. (2018). Increasing undergraduate success: A randomized controlled trial of U-Pace instruction. Online Learning, 22(3), 175-191. https://doi.org/10.24059/olj.v22i3.1317

q4 · i? · causal · r3

Same logistic regression model. Both single-component online conditions outperformed face-to-face on A or B grades: "concept mastery (Wald χ2 = 39.71, p < .001, Exp(B) = 5.68)" and amplified assistance (Exp(B) = 5.692). No standardized effect size was printed.

"The odds of earning a final grade of A or B were also higher for concept mastery (Wald χ2 = 39.71, p < .001, Exp(B) = 5.68) and amplified assistance students (Wald χ2 = 41.089, p < .001, Exp(B) = 5.692) than face-to-face students."

Related Claims¶

  • Neither concept mastery alone nor amplified assistance alone produced greater learning than face-to-face instruction — related
  • Final grades of A or B did not differ between U-Pace and either single-component condition (concept mastery or amplified assistance) — related
  • U-Pace instruction produced greater learning on a proctored cumulative exam than concept mastery, amplified assistance, and face-to-face instruction in an RCT — related
