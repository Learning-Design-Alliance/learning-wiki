# Polygenic score predictions of education strengthen as GWAS discovery sample sizes increase

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across four GWAS published between 2013 and 2022, polygenic score predictions of educational attainment rose from ~2% to ~14% of variance as discovery samples grew from N ~ 130,000 to ~ 3 million. [→ Wilding 2024](#wilding-2024)

## Evidence

### Wilding 2024

Wilding, K., Wright, M., & von Stumm, S. (2024). Using DNA to Predict Education: a Meta-analytic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09928-4

`q2 · i?`

Narrative synthesis of the four GWAS for years spent in education: discovery samples grew from N ~ 130,000 to ~ 3 million, and predictions rose "from ~ 2 to ~ 14% of the variance in educational attainment, a sevenfold increase" (Fig. 1). The article notes the GWAS samples overlap, so they are not independent.

> "These findings consolidated that polygenic score predictions for education strengthen as GWAS samples sizes increase, potentially to the point that polygenic score predictions will approximate the heritability estimates from twin studies"
