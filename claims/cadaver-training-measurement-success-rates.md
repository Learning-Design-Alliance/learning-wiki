(2024). A surgical instrument motion measurement system for skill evaluation in practical laparoscopic surgery training. PLOS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0305693

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Measurement experiment during cadaver surgical training in which 31 urologists performed laparoscopic radical nephrectomy; Table 4 reports median success rates per instrument, e.g., grasping forceps 92.74% and right angle forceps 98.45%.

> "As presented in Table 4, the measurement success rate of all the surgical instruments used in the experiments was approximately 90%."

### Ebina Koki 2024 (2)

Ebina Koki, Abe Takashige, Yan Lingbo, Hotta Kiyohiko, Shichinohe Toshiaki, Higuchi Madoka, Iwahara Naoya, Hosaka Yukino, Harada Shigeru, Kikuchi Hiroshi, Miyata Haruka, Matsumoto Ryuji, Osawa Takahiro, Kurashima Yo, Watanabe Masahiko, Kon Masafumi, Murai Sachiyo, Komizunai Shunsuke, Tsujita Teppei, Sase Kazuya, Chen Xiaoshuai, Senoo Taku, Shinohara Nobuo, Konno Atsushi. (2024). A surgical instrument motion measurement system for skill evaluation in practical laparoscopic surgery training. PLOS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0305693

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Cadaver training measurement experiment: 31 urologists produced 36 cases (five participated twice); hardware malfunction was resolved by replacing the PC and was not considered a major challenge.

> "Among the 36 cases in the experiment, 9 failed owing to malfunctions of the hardware of the measurement equipment, and 2 were excluded from the analysis owing to inadequate cadaver body conditions. Therefore, measurement data from 25 cases were obtained for skill analysis."
