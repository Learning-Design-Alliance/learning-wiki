## Evidence

### Næss S. Ø. 2026

Næss S. Ø., Sivertsen B., Hysing M., Dovran A., Tobiassen S., Hafstad G. S., Kjørstad K. (2026). School Functioning Among Norwegian Children With a History of Maltreatment. Journal of Interpersonal Violence. https://doi.org/10.1177/08862605251351669

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Logistic regression in the same 192-child parent-reported sample comparing children exposed to three or more maltreatment types (n = 69) with those exposed to one or two types (n = 123). The article reports a printed odds ratio of OR = 3.31 (95% CI [1.26, 8.68]) for high absenteeism, i.e., "3.31-fold increased odds" of more than 8 absent days per month.

> "Additionally, children experiencing multivictimization had 3.31-fold increased odds (95% CI [1.26, 8.68]) of having high levels of school absences (more than 8 days per month)."

### Næss S. Ø. 2026 (2)

Næss S. Ø., Sivertsen B., Hysing M., Dovran A., Tobiassen S., Hafstad G. S., Kjørstad K. (2026). School Functioning Among Norwegian Children With a History of Maltreatment. Journal of Interpersonal Violence. https://doi.org/10.1177/08862605251351669

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Independent-samples t-tests treating absence continuously in the same sample: multivictimization group averaged 3.6 days (SD = 5.2) versus 2.2 days (SD = 2.7, p = .014); hours missed also differed (8.3 vs 2.3 hr, p = .003). No standardized effect size printed.

> "Children exposed to multivictimization had an average of 3.6 days of absence (SD = 5.2), compared to 2.2 days (SD = 2.7, p = .014) in the lower exposure group."
