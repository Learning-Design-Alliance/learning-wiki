## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Learning disability diagnoses were significantly more common in both preterm groups across grades, with kindergarten adjusted odds of 2.05 for 32-34 weeks children and elevated odds of 2.41 reported for the preterm groups. [→ Lock Nicole E. 2024](#lock-nicole-e-2024)

## Evidence

### Lock Nicole E. 2024

Lock Nicole E., DeBoer Mark D., Scharf Rebecca J., Miller Sarah E. (2024). Academic performance in moderately and late preterm children in the United States: are they catching up? Journal of Perinatology 44(6). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41372-024-01938-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multivariable logistic regression of school-recorded learning disability diagnoses in the weighted ECLS-K:2011 cohort; the article prints kindergarten odds of "2.05 time higher odds (CI 1.56, 2.69)" for 32-34 weeks children against term children.

> "32–34 6/7 weeks’ children also had the highest odds of a learning disability in kindergarten compared to term children, at 2.05 time higher odds (CI 1.56, 2.69)."

### Lock Nicole E. 2024 (2)

Lock Nicole E., DeBoer Mark D., Scharf Rebecca J., Miller Sarah E. (2024). Academic performance in moderately and late preterm children in the United States: are they catching up? Journal of Perinatology 44(6). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41372-024-01938-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

In the same regression analysis, adjusted learning-disability odds were "elevated throughout kindergarten to fifth grade, with a 2.41 higher odds (CI 1.61, 3.60) in kindergarten"; kindergarten percentages were 32.9%, 26.5%, and 18.3% for 32-34 weeks, 35-36 weeks, and term children.

> "The adjusted odds ratio for a learning disability was elevated throughout kindergarten to fifth grade, with a 2.41 higher odds (CI 1.61, 3.60) in kindergarten"
