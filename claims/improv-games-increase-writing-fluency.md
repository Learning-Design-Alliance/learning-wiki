# A sequence of collaborative improv games increased word and sentence usage in high school students' journal writing

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a quasi-experimental comparison study, students who participated in a sequence of improv story-telling and story-writing games showed greater increases in word and sentence usage in journal writing than students in comparison writing classes. [→ DeMichele 2015](#demichele-2015)
`q2 i?` In a quasi-experimental interrupted time series study, students showed increases in word and sentence usage in journal writing after an eight-day improv game sequence, following a baseline period of little or no growth. [→ DeMichele 2015 (2)](#demichele-2015-2)

## Evidence

### DeMichele 2015

DeMichele, M. (2015). Improv and ink: Increasing individual writing fluency with collaborative improv. International Journal of Education & the Arts, 16(10). http://www.ijea.org/v16n10/

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison study at an inner-city high school summer program with an experimental improv class (n=14) and two comparison writing classes (n=28). The experimental group's word usage rose 162% and sentence usage 209%, versus 61% and 78% in the comparison group, a "101% increase in word usage and a 131% increase in sentence usage over the comparison group".

> "Results indicated that the experimental group demonstrated a 101% increase in word usage and a 131% increase in sentence usage over the comparison group (see figure 1)."

### DeMichele 2015 (2)

DeMichele, M. (2015). Improv and ink: Increasing individual writing fluency with collaborative improv. International Journal of Education & the Arts, 16(10). http://www.ijea.org/v16n10/

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Interrupted time series study in a 9th grade World History class (n=15) at a suburban high school. During the pre-intervention baseline, word usage grew 28% and sentences 13%; "After only eight days of participation in the improv game sequence a 34% increase in word usage and a 51% increase in sentence usage was demonstrated".

> "After only eight days of participation in the improv game sequence a 34% increase in word usage and a 51% increase in sentence usage was demonstrated."
