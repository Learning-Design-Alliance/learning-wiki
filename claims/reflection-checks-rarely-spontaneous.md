## Evidence

### Wilcox 2013

Wilcox, B. R., Caballero, M. D., Rehn, D. A., & Pollock, S. J. (2013). ACER: An Analytic Framework for Students' Use of Mathematics in Upper-Division Physics. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.9.020119](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.9.020119)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Exam analysis of the Reflection component (elements CR1 and CR2). The article reports "8% of 172" made explicit checks; interviews corroborate that when not prompted, half of participants made no attempt to reflect on their solutions.

> "Only a small number of students (8% of 172) made explicit attempts to check their work on exams and almost exclusively by checking limiting behavior."

### Wilcox 2013 (2)

Wilcox, B. R., Caballero, M. D., Rehn, D. A., & Pollock, S. J. (2013). ACER: An Analytic Framework for Students' Use of Mathematics in Upper-Division Physics. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.9.020119](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.9.020119)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Second interview set (five students) explicitly targeting Reflection with a three-expression units/limits task (Fig. 3). All five suggested units checks but most could not execute them without a workaround; all five suggested checking that the potential goes to zero as r → ∞.

> "All ﬁve students suggested checking the units of these expressions, yet all but one had diﬃculty doing so because they did not recall the units of ǫo."
