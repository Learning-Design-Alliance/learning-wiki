# Escape room-based teaching produces significant knowledge gain (Cohen's d = 1.29) among undergraduate medical students on a pre-post assessment

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Medical students' mean post-test score (M = 71.71) was significantly higher than the mean pre-test score (M = 65.07) after escape room teaching, with a large effect size (Cohen's d = 1.29). [→ Baessler F 2025](#baessler-f-2025)

## Evidence

### Baessler F 2025

Baessler F, Hornung TP, Schuster TJ, Hardt N, Felter E, Anzenberger E, Ely DR, Lichtenstein T, Steiner K, Koelkebeck K, Flasbeck V, Spitzer P, Speerforck S, Pinilla S, Khatamzas E, Mueller B, Zafar A. (2025). Game-based learning in undergraduate medical education: evaluation of an interdisciplinary escape room. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07990-2

`q3 · i3` · `design · r2`

Cross-sectional pre-post evaluation of an interdisciplinary medical escape room repeated eight times, with 45 medical students completing 19-item multiple-choice pre- and post-tests scored out of 95. Mean scores rose from 65.07 to 71.71, a "mean knowledge gain of 6.64 points" with Cohen's d = 1.29. No control group was used.

> "The overall mean pre-test score was 65.07 (SD = 5.29) out of 95, while the mean post-test score was 71.71 (SD = 5.03), which was a significant improvement (t = 8.65, p <.001) with a mean knowledge gain of 6.64 points (SD = 6.01, 95% CI [4.84, 8.44]) and a large effect size (Cohen's d = 1.29, 95% CI [0.89, 1.69])."
