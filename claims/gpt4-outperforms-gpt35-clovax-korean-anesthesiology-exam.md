## Evidence

### Lesselroth Blake; Bruthans Jan; Hidki Asmaa; Yoon Soo-Hyuk; Oh Seok Kyeong; Lim Byung Gun; Lee Ho-Jin 2024

Lesselroth Blake; Bruthans Jan; Hidki Asmaa; Yoon Soo-Hyuk; Oh Seok Kyeong; Lim Byung Gun; Lee Ho-Jin. (2024). Performance of ChatGPT in the In-Training Examination for Anesthesiology and Pain Medicine Residents in South Korea: Observational Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/56859

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of 398 text-only questions from the Korean Society of Anesthesiologists in-training examinations 2018-2022, scored against official answer keys. "The overall performance of GPT-4 (67.8%) was significantly higher" with comparisons via Cochran Q test and Bonferroni-corrected pairwise thresholds. No effect size was printed.

> "The overall performance of GPT-4 (67.8%) was significantly higher than that of GPT-3.5 (37.7%) and CLOVA X (37.2%), surpassing the minimum required difference of 9.1% in Cochran Q test. However, GPT-3.5 and CLOVA X did not show significant differences in their overall performance."
