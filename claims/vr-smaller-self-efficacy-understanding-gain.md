## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The VR group's gain in self-efficacy for understanding course material and content was significantly smaller than the control group's (p<0.01), and the VR group also rated their own performance 18% worse despite better psychomotor results. [→ Hing Lie 2023](#hing-lie-2023)

## Evidence

### Hing Lie 2023

Hing Lie, Kachina Studer, Zhen Zhao, Ben Thomson, Dishita G Turakhia, John Liu. (2023). Training for Open-Ended Drilling through a Virtual Reality Simulation. [doi:10.1109/ismar59233.2023.00051](https://doi.org/10.1109/ismar59233.2023.00051)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Affective assessment using a 21-question 7-point Likert scale adapted from the MSLQ, administered pre and post to both groups. Only the self-efficacy (understand) construct showed a statistically significant between-group difference; other affective measures were non-significant.

> "With the exception of the self -efficacy to use equipment where the VR group scored a larger gain, the VR group scored smaller gains than the control group on all other affective measures. In particular, the smaller gain for self -efficacy on understanding course material and co ntent was statistically significant (p<0.01)."
