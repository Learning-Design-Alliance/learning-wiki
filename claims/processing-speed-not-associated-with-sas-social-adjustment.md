# Processing speed in adults with ASD shows no significant association with SAS-II social adjustment, and only a near-significant positive association with GAF functioning

## Evidence

### Haigh Sarah M. 2018

Haigh Sarah M., Walsh Jennifer A., Mazefsky Carla A., Minshew Nancy J., Eack Shaun M. (2018). Processing Speed is Impaired in Adults with Autism Spectrum Disorder, and Relates to Social Communication Abilities. Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-018-3515-z

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis in the ASD sample of processing speed composite against the Social Adjustment Scale-II; the article reports no significant relationship. The authors suggest processing speed may contribute more to communication and reciprocal social interaction deficits than to social disability generally.

> "However, no significant relationships were observed between processing speed and social adjustment impairments on the SAS-II."

### Haigh Sarah M. 2018 (2)

Haigh Sarah M., Walsh Jennifer A., Mazefsky Carla A., Minshew Nancy J., Eack Shaun M. (2018). Processing Speed is Impaired in Adults with Autism Spectrum Disorder, and Relates to Social Communication Abilities. Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-018-3515-z

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis of processing speed composite against Global Assessment of Functioning scores among 57 adults with ASD; the article reports "r (57) = .24, p = .06", a near-significant positive association, with no effect size beyond the correlation.

> "There was a positive association that was nearing significance with participants’ processing speed composite scores and their GAF scores, r (57) = .24, p = .06, indicating better processing speed was associated with higher levels of functioning."
