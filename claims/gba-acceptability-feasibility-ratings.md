## Evidence

### Gazit N 2025

Gazit N, Ben-Gal G, Eliashar R. (2025). Game-Based Assessment of Cognitive Abilities and Personality Characteristics for Surgical Resident Selection: A Preliminary Validation Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/72264

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Feedback questionnaire data from 152 interns and 30 expert surgeons using 5-point relevance scales. The article reports "average ratings of 3.5 or above" and a mean relevance of 3.6 (SD 0.1); whole-test platform comfort means were 4.2 (interns) and 4.0 (surgeons).

> "All games were considered by both the expert surgeons and the interns as relevant for assessing cognitive abilities and personality characteristics in the selection of candidates for surgical training (manifested in average ratings of 3.5 or above; see Table 4). The mean relevance rating across the games and samples was 3.6 (SD 0.1)."

### Gazit N 2025 (2)

Gazit N, Ben-Gal G, Eliashar R. (2025). Game-Based Assessment of Cognitive Abilities and Personality Characteristics for Surgical Resident Selection: A Preliminary Validation Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/72264

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Questionnaire difficulty ratings across games and samples. The article reports "the Dotto game largely perceived as very difficult to extremely difficult" with a mean difficulty of 3.5 (SD 0.7); the lowest time-limit approval was 62% of interns for Dotto.

> "The difficulty ratings varied between games, with the CurioCity and MultiTask games perceived overall as being moderately difficult, and the Dotto game largely perceived as very difficult to extremely difficult. The mean difficulty rating across the games and samples was 3.5 (SD 0.7)"
