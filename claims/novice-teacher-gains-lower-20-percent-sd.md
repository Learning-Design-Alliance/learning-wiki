This review article reports, citing Rivkin, Hanushek and Kain (2000), a Texas achievement-gain analysis. The printed magnitude — student gains "lower by roughly 20 percent of a standard deviation" in 4th and 5th grades — corresponds to a small effect at the d = 0.2 boundary, described as a small but significant relationship.
