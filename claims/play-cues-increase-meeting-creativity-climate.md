# Play cues in workplace meetings significantly increase experienced meeting creativity climate with a small effect size

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Participants in play-cued meetings showed a significant increase in meeting creativity climate (median 29 to 30, r = .15) while the control group did not change significantly. [→ West 2016](#west-2016)

## Evidence

### West 2016

West, Samuel E., Eva Hoff, and Ingegerd Carlsson. (2016). Play and Productivity: Enhancing the Creative Climate at Workplace Meetings with Play Cues. American Journal of Play, 9(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1123863

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Field experiment with real work groups randomly assigned to play-cued (n = 123) or control (n = 41) conditions, using Wilcoxon Signed Rank Test on repeated measures. The play-cued group's creativity climate rose "from a median of 29 to 30" with "a small effect size (r = .15)"; the control group did not change significantly.

> "The intervention group that received the play cues showed a significant increase in meeting creativity climate from a median of 29 to 30 [z = -2.71, p = .007] with a small effect size (r = .15). The control group did not change significantly [z = -.01, p = .994]."
