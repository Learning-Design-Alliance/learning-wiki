# Five of six investigated linguistic task characteristics (position of the unknown, schematic structure, irrelevant information, realistic considerations, lexical consistency) are associated with elementary schoolers' word problem-solving performance

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### T. Vessonen 2024

T. Vessonen, M. Dahlberg, H. Hellstrand, A. Widlund, J. Korhonen, P. Aunio, A. Laine. (2024). Task Characteristics Associated with Mathematical Word Problem-Solving Performance Among Elementary School-Aged Children: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09954-2

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Meta-analysis of K = 69 studies (five databases plus citation searching, PRISMA-reported, pre-registered) pooling standardized mean differences between word problem types. The review reports that five of six linguistic characteristics "were related (g = 0.39 to 4.26)" with performance; no single pooled effect per characteristic is printed in the supplied text.

> "The findings (K = 69) showed that five of the six investigated linguistic task characteristics (i.e., the position of the unknown, schematic structure, irrelevant information, realistic considerations, and lexical consistency) and one of the two numerical task characteristics (i.e., number of operations) were related (g = 0.39 to 4.26) with elementary school-aged children's mathematical word problem-solving."
