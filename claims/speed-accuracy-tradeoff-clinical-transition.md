## Evidence

### Glevitzky 2026

Glevitzky, L., Cyris, M., Sutor, S., Springer, C., Kahl, M., Mekhemar, M., Sälzer, S. & Graetz, C. (2026). Effect of massed versus distributed preclinical training on subgingival instrumentation skill across the preclinical-to-clinical transition. Scientific Reports 16, 23355. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-62235-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Adjusted multivariable models in the two-cohort in vitro study: semester was the strongest predictor of treatment time (β = −59.46 s/tooth, p < 0.001) and negatively predicted ESP ("β=−4.26%, p = 0.002"); the schedule×time interaction was non-significant. No standardised effect size is printed.

> "From post-DTP to S8 both cohorts became faster by ~ 60s/tooth (p < 0.001) while ESP declined (β=−4.26%, p = 0.002) — a speed–accuracy trade-off; the schedule×time interaction was non-significant."

### Glevitzky 2026 (2)

Glevitzky, L., Cyris, M., Sutor, S., Springer, C., Kahl, M., Mekhemar, M., Sälzer, S. & Graetz, C. (2026). Effect of massed versus distributed preclinical training on subgingival instrumentation skill across the preclinical-to-clinical transition. Scientific Reports 16, 23355. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-62235-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests in the longitudinal subset evaluated at both time points (oGDLR n = 22; nGDLR n = 12). The quote reports the oGDLR paired result; in nGDLR the ESP change (−4.8%) did not reach significance (p = 0.13), read as limited power.

> "the oGDLR cohort (n = 22) showed a statistically significant trade-off, with ESP decreasing from 72.7 to 65.5% (p < 0.001) and treatment time decreasing by ~ 53 s/tooth (p < 0.001)"
