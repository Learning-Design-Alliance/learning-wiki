`q1 i?` Impaired nonword reading with impaired letter-sound knowledge has been hypothesised to arise from at least three different distal causes: a phonological deficit, a paired-associate learning deficit, or a visual attentional deficit. ## Evidence

### Genevieve McArthur and Anne Castles 2017

Genevieve McArthur and Anne Castles. (2017). Helping children with reading difficulties: some things we have learned so far. npj Science of Learning (2017) 2:7. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-017-0008-3

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument from the perspective article, which reviews competing distal-cause hypotheses for poor nonword reading. It presents the phonological deficit, paired-associate learning deficit, and visual attentional deficit hypotheses as alternative explanations for the same proximal cause.

> "The prominent “phonological deﬁcit hypothesis ” proposes a pervasive language-based difﬁculty in processing speech sounds that affects the ability to learn to associate written stimuli (e.g., letters) with speech sounds."
