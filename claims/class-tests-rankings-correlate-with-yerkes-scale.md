## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Correlations between class-test intelligence quotients and Yerkes Scale coefficients of mental ability were high in almost all age groups (e.g., r = .95 at age 11) except age fourteen (r = .26), with r = .80 over all 88 cases. [→ Pintner 1917](#pintner-1917)

## Evidence

### Pintner 1917

Pintner, R. (1917). The Mentality of the Dependent Child, Together with a Plan for a Mental Survey of an Institution. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0075825

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis of 88 home children tested with eight group class tests and individually with the Yerkes-Bridges Point Scale, computed by the Spearman Foot-Rule Method per age group and overall. The printed overall value is "r=.8O with a P. E. of about .0280," a large printed correlation.

> "The correlations between the class tests and the Yerkes Scale of all the 88 cases together is, R=.59; r=.8O with a P. E. of about .0280. This coefficient is naturally high because we are dealing with a heterogeneous group including children varying from age 8 to age 14."
