# Virtual patients are as effective as traditional education for knowledge outcomes (pooled SMD=0.11)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Pooled analysis of randomized trials comparing virtual patients with traditional education shows no significant difference in knowledge outcomes (SMD=0.11, 95% CI −0.17 to 0.39). [→ Kononowicz A A 2019](#kononowicz-a-a-2019)

## Evidence

### Kononowicz A A 2019

Kononowicz A A, Woodham L A, Edelbring S, Stathakarou N, Davies D, Saxena N, Tudor Car L, Carlstedt-Duke J, Car J, Zary N. (2019). Virtual Patient Simulations in Health Professions Education: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis by the Digital Health Education Collaboration. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/preprints.14676

`q3 · i?`

Meta-analysis of randomized trials comparing virtual patients with traditional education for knowledge outcomes, using random-effects models. The pooled effect of "SMD=0.11, 95% CI −0.17 to 0.39" crosses zero, indicating no significant difference; heterogeneity was high (I2=74%).

> "The pooled effect for knowledge outcomes (SMD=0.11, 95% CI −0.17 to 0.39, I2=74%, n=927; Figure 2) suggests that virtual patient interventions are as efficient as traditional education."
