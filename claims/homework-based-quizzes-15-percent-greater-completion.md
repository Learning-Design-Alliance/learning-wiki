## Evidence

### F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry 2013

F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry. (2013). How different incentives affect homework completion in introductory physics courses. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1305.2342](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1305.2342)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Matched comparison of N = 73 students' journal-checked completion in fall 2011 mechanics (mean 52.8%, SD 27.2%) versus spring 2012 E&M (mean 37.9%, SD 27.6%), per Table I. The t-test gives "p < 0.0001"; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "We found that, for the 73 students' scores that we were able to compare, on average, homework completion was about 15% greater in fall 2011 mechanics compared to spring 2012 E&M. A t-test done on these results shows that these results are statistically significant (p < 0.0001) with 95% confidence that the difference in scores lies in the range of 8-22%."
