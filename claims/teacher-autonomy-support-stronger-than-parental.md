# Teacher autonomy support predicts students' need satisfaction and self-determined motivation more strongly than parental autonomy support

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the meta-analytic path model including both providers, direct and indirect paths from teacher autonomy support to self-determined motivation types and amotivation were stronger than those from parental autonomy support. [→ Bureau 2022](#bureau-2022)
`q3 i?` All direct predictions from parental autonomy support to motivation types were close to null once teacher autonomy support and needs were taken into account, with parental support retaining only indirect prediction of identified and intrinsic motivation. [→ Bureau 2022 (2)](#bureau-2022-2)
`q3 i?` Trim-and-fill and Egger's regression indicated potential missing studies for teacher autonomy support correlations with introjected, identified, and intrinsic motivation, with corrected effect size estimates below the observed ones. [→ Bureau 2022 (3)](#bureau-2022-3)

## Evidence

### Bureau 2022

Bureau, J. S., Howard, J. L., Chong, J. X. Y., & Guay, F. (2022). Pathways to Student Motivation: A Meta-Analysis of Antecedents of Autonomous and Controlled Motivations. Review of Educational Research, 92(1), 46–72. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211042426

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

W-COV GLS MASEM (Model 1, pairwise deletion, all 10 variables) testing autonomy support → need satisfaction → motivation types. The model including both providers showed "direct and indirect paths from teacher autonomy support to self-determined motivation types and amotivation were stronger than those from parental autonomy support".

> "In the model including both teachers and parental autonomy support, direct and indirect paths from teacher autonomy support to self-determined motivation types and amotivation were stronger than those from parental autonomy support."

### Bureau 2022 (2)

Bureau, J. S., Howard, J. L., Chong, J. X. Y., & Guay, F. (2022). Pathways to Student Motivation: A Meta-Analysis of Antecedents of Autonomous and Controlled Motivations. Review of Educational Research, 92(1), 46–72. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211042426

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Same MASEM model with both autonomy support sources. Direct predictions from parental autonomy support were "close to null when teacher autonomy support and needs were taken into account", with only indirect prediction of identified and intrinsic motivation (βindirect = .13 and .16).

> "all direct predictions from parental autonomy support to motivation types were close to null when teacher autonomy support and needs were taken into account. Parental autonomy support only accounted for some indirect prediction in identified and intrinsic motivation types (βindirect = .13 and .16, respectively)."

### Bureau 2022 (3)

Bureau, J. S., Howard, J. L., Chong, J. X. Y., & Guay, F. (2022). Pathways to Student Motivation: A Meta-Analysis of Antecedents of Autonomous and Controlled Motivations. Review of Educational Research, 92(1), 46–72. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211042426

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Publication bias analyses (trim and fill, Egger's regression, published–unpublished subgroup comparison) on teacher autonomy support correlations. Corrected estimates were ".09, .29, and .29" versus collected estimates ".17, .44, and .48" for introjected, identified, and intrinsic motivation; direction and interpretation unchanged.

> "It was estimated that their true effect sizes be potentially closer to .09, .29, and .29, as opposed to results from the collected data which estimate these effects at .17, .44, and .48."
