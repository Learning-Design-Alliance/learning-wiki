# Gender-interest stereotypes favoring boys in computer science and engineering are endorsed across grades 1–12 and across racial/ethnic groups in a diverse sample

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Students in grades 1–12 endorsed gender-interest stereotypes favoring boys in engineering (d = 0.73) and computer science (d = 0.44), including first graders for engineering (d = 0.71). [→ Master 2021](#master-2021)
`q3 i?` Black, Asian, Latinx, and White girls and boys all endorsed gender-interest stereotypes favoring boys for both fields (all Ps < 0.03). [→ Master 2021 (2)](#master-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Master 2021

Master, A., Meltzoff, A. N., & Cheryan, S. (2021). Gender stereotypes about interests start early and cause gender disparities in computer science and engineering. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 118(48), e2100030118. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2100030118

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Preregistered survey of 1,544 students in grades 1–12 in six Rhode Island schools (43% free/reduced-price lunch). The article reports endorsement "in computer science, t(1,531) = 17.16, P < 0.001, and d = 0.44, and engineering, t(1,529) = 28.47, P < 0.001, and d = 0.73", with engineering endorsement present in first grade (d = 0.71).

> "Students endorsed gender-interest stereotypes favoring boys in computer science, t(1,531) = 17.16, P < 0.001, and d = 0.44, and engineering, t(1,529) = 28.47, P < 0.001, and d = 0.73"

### Master 2021 (2)

Master, A., Meltzoff, A. N., & Cheryan, S. (2021). Gender stereotypes about interests start early and cause gender disparities in computer science and engineering. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 118(48), e2100030118. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2100030118

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Intersectional analysis in the same preregistered survey (Table 1) reports mean difference scores by race/ethnicity and gender; the article states endorsement held "for both computer science and engineering, all Ps < 0.03".

> "Gender-interest stereotypes favoring boys were endorsed by Black, Asian, Latinx, and White girls and boys for both computer science and engineering, all Ps < 0.03"
