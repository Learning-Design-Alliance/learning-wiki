# Perceived instrumentality enhances student motivation only for students with a positive attitude toward their personal future; for students with a negative future outlook, high utility has the opposite effect

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Perceived instrumentality interacts with affective attitudes toward one's individual future: it enhances motivation only under a positive personal future attitude and reverses under a negative outlook. [→ Phalet 2004](#phalet-2004)

## Evidence

### Phalet 2004

Phalet, K., Andriessen, I., & Lens, W. (2004). How Future Goals Enhance Motivation and Learning in Multicultural Classrooms. Educational Psychology Review, Vol. 16, No. 1. https://hdl.handle.net/1874/23520

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Van Calster et al. (1991), a study of Grade 11 and 12 students in which high-instrumentality students were more motivated than low-instrumentality students, and an interaction effect with affective attitudes toward one's individual future was found. No effect size is printed.

> "Perceived instrumentality does enhance student motivation but only for students with a positive attitude toward their personal future. For students with a negative outlook on their future, attaching high utility to school results had the opposite effect on their motivation to study."
