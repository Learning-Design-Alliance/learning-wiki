## Evidence

### Marcel V. J. Veenman 2011

Marcel V. J. Veenman. (2011). Alternative assessment of strategy use with self-report instruments: a discussion. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-011-9080-x

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Discussion of convergent-validity correlations in a study that prompted learners with multiple-choice questions to enact strategies after reading a text fragment. The article argues that validating a process measure with non-process measures such as vocabulary and word reading seems like comparing apples and oranges, and convergent validity would likely increase if non-process measures were excluded.

> "Cromley and Azevedo (this issue) obtained correlations ranging from .23 to .74 between strategy-use MC-questions and other measures for background knowledge, inference, vocabulary, and word reading"

### Marcel V. J. Veenman 2011 (2)

Marcel V. J. Veenman. (2011). Alternative assessment of strategy use with self-report instruments: a discussion. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-011-9080-x

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

External-validity evidence discussed in the discussion section: in the prompted MC-question enactment study, strategy use showed substantial average relations to reading comprehension. This is reported as an R²-type variance figure, not a standardized effect size.

> "In the study of Cromley and Azevedo (this issue), on the other hand, correlations showed that strategy use accounted for 30% of variance in comprehension on the average"
