# Boston public preschool attendance increases high school graduation by 6.0 percentage points for ever graduating, a 9% increase relative to the non-offered graduation rate of 64%, with no detectable impacts on grade repetition or special education.

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Enrollment in a BPS preschool increases the probability that students graduate from a Massachusetts public high school on time by 5.4 percentage points (p < 0.1), growing to 6.0 percentage points for graduating at any time (p < 0.05). [→ Gray-Lobe 2021](#gray-lobe-2021)

## Evidence

### Gray-Lobe 2021

Gray-Lobe, G., Pathak, P. A., & Walters, C. R. (2021). The Long-Term Effects of Universal Preschool in Boston. NBER Working Paper No. 28756. http://www.nber.org/papers/w28756

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

2SLS estimates from Table 4, Panel B, using the randomized sample of BPS preschool applicants, where preschool increases the probability students "graduate from a Massachusetts public high school on time by 5.4 percentage points". Combined with insignificant grade repetition effects, this suggests preschool increases successful completion rather than earlier enrollment.

> "Enrollment in a BPS preschool increases the probability that students graduate from a Massachusetts public high school on time by 5.4 percentage points (p < 0.1), and this effect grows to 6.0 percentage points when we include students who graduate at any time (p < 0.05). The estimated effect on ever graduating high school is a 9% increase relative to the non-offered graduation rate of 64%"

### Gray-Lobe 2021 (2)

Gray-Lobe, G., Pathak, P. A., & Walters, C. R. (2021). The Long-Term Effects of Universal Preschool in Boston. NBER Working Paper No. 28756. http://www.nber.org/papers/w28756

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

2SLS estimates from Table 4, Panel A, on grade progression and special education for the randomized sample. Effects on starting 1st grade on time, appearing in a BPS school in 6th or 9th grade, repeating a grade, and special education classification in 1st and 3rd grades are "no detectable impacts" — small and statistically insignificant.

> "We find no detectable impacts of BPS preschool on grade repetition and special education outcomes."
