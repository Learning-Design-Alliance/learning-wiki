# Assigning two interval priors to two different body parts enables concurrent learning of two independent priors in a coincidence timing task

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across Experiments 2–5, mean response intervals differed between priors when different body parts (fingers, hands, hand vs foot) were assigned to the short and long priors, whereas the prior effect was non-significant in Experiment 1. [→ Yoshiki Matsumura 2024](#yoshiki-matsumura-2024)
`q3 i?` In Experiment 4 (contralateral hand vs foot), responses were significantly biased toward each prior's mean in all trial bins, indicating concurrent learning from early trials. [→ Yoshiki Matsumura 2024 (2)](#yoshiki-matsumura-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Yoshiki Matsumura 2024

Yoshiki Matsumura, Neil W. Roach, James Heron & Makoto Miyazaki. (2024). Body-part specificity for learning of multiple prior distributions in human coincidence timing. npj Science of Learning 9:34. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00241-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

A three-way ANOVA (5 experiments × 2 priors × 4 trial bins) on TR(Mpriors) found a significant experiment × prior interaction (p = 0.011, ηp2 = 0.30); simple effects showed the prior effect significant in Experiments 2–5 but not Experiment 1.

> "The analyses of simple effects for the interaction between experiment and prior revealed that the effect of prior was non-signi ﬁcant in Experiment 1( p =0 . 2 0 ,F(1, 7) = 1.98,ηp 2 = 0.22) but was signi ﬁcant in Experiments 2–5 (ps ≤ 0.0095,Fs(1, 7)≥ 12.50,ηp 2s ≥ 0.64)."

### Yoshiki Matsumura 2024 (2)

Yoshiki Matsumura, Neil W. Roach, James Heron & Makoto Miyazaki. (2024). Body-part specificity for learning of multiple prior distributions in human coincidence timing. npj Science of Learning 9:34. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00241-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Experiment 4 (n=8) assigned the short and long priors to the contralateral hand and foot. TR(Mpriors) was "signiﬁcantly greater for the long prior than for the short prior over all trial bins", and regression indices exceeded zero for both priors in all bins.

> "/C22TRðMpriorsÞ values were signiﬁcantly greater for the long prior than for the short prior over all trial bins(pscor ≤ 0.0026, ts(7) ≥ 3.99, ds ≥ 1.41, paired t-test with Holm correction)"
