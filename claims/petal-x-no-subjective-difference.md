# No statistically significant difference between Petal-X and graphical score charts in perceived transparency, trust, or intent to use, with Bayes factors favoring the null

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Mean ratings on transparency, trust and intent-to-use scales were slightly higher for GSC but not statistically significant; Bayes factors of 4.29, 2.35 and 3.77 favor the null hypothesis. [→ Diego Rojo 2024](#diego-rojo-2024)

## Evidence

### Diego Rojo 2024

Diego Rojo, Houda Lamqaddam, Lucija Gosak, and Katrien Verbert. (2024). Petal-X: Human-Centered Visual Explanations to Improve Cardiovascular Risk Communication. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2406.18690](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2406.18690)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Between-subject experiment with 88 healthcare students; adapted Cramer et al. questionnaire scales with Cronbach's alpha of 0.79 (transparency), 0.85 (trust), 0.90 (intent to use). No significant group differences; equivalence was not tested.

> "Welch's t-tests revealed that although the mean ratings on the transparency, trust and intent to use scales were slightly higher when GSC was used compared to Petal-X, these effects were not statistically significant."

### Diego Rojo 2024 (2)

Diego Rojo, Houda Lamqaddam, Lucija Gosak, and Katrien Verbert. (2024). Petal-X: Human-Centered Visual Explanations to Improve Cardiovascular Risk Communication. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2406.18690](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2406.18690)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Bayesian re-analysis of the same subjective scales found the data more probable under the null, which the authors call moderate evidence for transparency and intent to use and anecdotical evidence for trust.

> "The Bayes factor for the same analysis revealed that the data were 4.29 (transparency), 3.77 (intent to use) and 2.35 (trust) times more probable under the null hypotheses (no difference between the means of each group) compared to the alternative hypothe- ses."
