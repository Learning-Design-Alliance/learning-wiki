# Pass rates and final course grades did not differ significantly between emporium and traditional lecture sections

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The pass rate difference between emporium and traditional sections was not statistically significant. [→ Cousins-Cooper 2017](#cousins-cooper-2017)
`q3 i?` In logistic regression with covariate adjustment, the emporium method's odds ratio for passing was not statistically different from the traditional method's. [→ Cousins-Cooper 2017 (2)](#cousins-cooper-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Cousins-Cooper 2017

Cousins-Cooper, K., Staley, K. N., Kim, S., & Luke, N. S. (2017). The Effect of the Emporium Instructional Method on Students' Performance in College Algebra and Trigonometry. European Journal of Science and Mathematics Education, 5(1), 1-13. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1129978

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Chi-square comparison of pass status (A, B, C as pass) between 970 emporium and 842 traditional students; pass rates were 72.14% versus 69.94% with "p‐value = 0.3206".

> "However, the pass rate of students between the emporium and traditional lecture sections is not statistically significant (p‐value = 0.3206) where A, B and C were considered as the pass status."

### Cousins-Cooper 2017 (2)

Cousins-Cooper, K., Staley, K. N., Kim, S., & Luke, N. S. (2017). The Effect of the Emporium Instructional Method on Students' Performance in College Algebra and Trigonometry. European Journal of Science and Mathematics Education, 5(1), 1-13. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1129978

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Logistic regression of pass status on instruction method and covariates after dichotomizing letter grades; the emporium odds ratio was 0.98 (0.6-1.59) with "p‐value = 0.9301", unchanged when ACT replaced SAT scores.

> "The odds ratio of the emporium method is not statistically different from that of the Traditional method (p‐value = 0.9301)."
