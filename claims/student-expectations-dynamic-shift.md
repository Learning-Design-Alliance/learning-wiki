# Student expectations about learning biology can be dynamic and shift within a single course in response to specific activities

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A student who resisted equations in a diffusion activity responded very positively to a scaling demonstration showing a mathematical relationship between surface area and volume, showing that expectations can shift based on content and contextual cues. [→ Hall 2011](#hall-2011)

## Evidence

### Hall 2011

Hall, K. L., Watkins, J. E., Coffey, J. E., Cooke, T. J., & Redish, E. F. (2011). Examining the Impact of Student Expectations on Undergraduate Biology Education Reform. AERA 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1105.6349](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1105.6349)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Case evidence from one student (Ashlyn) in the same interview: she called equations "unappealing" when discussing diffusion but found the scaling demonstration "really helpful" and "mindboggling," with her demeanor changing from rigid and frowning to smiling and excited.

> "These quotes show that students’ can shift the way they interpret and respond to the class based on the content, instructional environment, or other contextual cues."
