## Evidence

### Brundage 2024

Brundage, M. J., Meltzer, D. E., & Singh, C. (2024). Investigating introductory and advanced students' difficulties with entropy and the second law of thermodynamics using a validated instrument. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020110](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020110)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey response-rate analysis of item 1 (reversible adiabatic expansion) in the STPFaSL-Long validation data; the article notes no previous studies of students' ideas regarding entropy changes in adiabatic processes.

> "Nonetheless, all three groups did poorly on this item; post-test correct-response rates were 69%, 47%, and 31%, respectively, for upper level, Int-calc, and Int-alg groups  (Table IV) . By far the most popular incorrect response was that entropy would increase."

### Brundage 2024 (2)

Brundage, M. J., Meltzer, D. E., & Singh, C. (2024). Investigating introductory and advanced students' difficulties with entropy and the second law of thermodynamics using a validated instrument. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020110](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020110)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Response-rate analysis of isothermal compression (item 5) and expansion (item 63) items; upper-level students scored far better on item 63 than item 5 (61% vs. 42% correct), while the Int-calc group performed significantly worse on the post-test (<40% correct) than pre-test (50%) on both items.

> "However, the performance of the introductory students on item 63 was either identical (on the pre-test) or only marginally better (on the post-test) than their performance on item 5, suggesting that “compression” was, for them, an influential cue that perhaps competed with their inclination to expect entropy increases in all situations."
