# Clinical virtual simulation improves nursing students' knowledge retention and clinical reasoning immediately after a lesson and 2 months later

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` The experimental group made larger knowledge and clinical reasoning gains than the control group immediately after the intervention (d=1.13). [→ José Miguel Padilha 2019](#jose-miguel-padilha-2019)
`q3 i2` The knowledge advantage persisted 2 months later (d=0.75). [→ José Miguel Padilha 2019 (2)](#jose-miguel-padilha-2019-2)

## Evidence

### José Miguel Padilha 2019

José Miguel Padilha, Paulo Puga Machado, Ana Ribeiro, José Ramos, Patrício Costa. (2019). Clinical Virtual Simulation in Nursing Education: Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/11529

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial with 42 second-year nursing students (n=21 per group); unpaired t test comparing groups on knowledge test change from pretest to posttest. The experimental group presented better outcomes, with d=1.13 described as reinforcing "the magnitude effect of the intervention".

> "The results of the students’ t tests showed the existence of statistically significant differences in knowledge retention after the intervention (t40=−3.656; P=.001; d=1.13)"

### José Miguel Padilha 2019 (2)

José Miguel Padilha, Paulo Puga Machado, Ana Ribeiro, José Ramos, Patrício Costa. (2019). Clinical Virtual Simulation in Nursing Education: Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/11529

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Same RCT, follow-up assessment (A2) 2 months after the intervention using the same true-or-false and multiple-choice test; between-group difference remained significant with d=0.75.

> "knowledge retention 2 months later (t40=−2.439; P=.02; d=0.75), and in learning satisfaction (t40=−4.309; P<.001; d=1.33)"
