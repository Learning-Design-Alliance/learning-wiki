## Evidence

### Daumiller 2019

Daumiller, M., Dickhäuser, O., & Dresel, M. (2019). University Instructors' Achievement Goals for Teaching. Journal of Educational Psychology, 111(1), 131–148. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000271

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Structural equation modeling with item-parcel indicators in the 1,066-instructor sample showed "achievement goals explained substantial amounts of variance in the outcome variables (R² = .19–.57)"; all goals except task avoidance and learning avoidance had distinct unique effects.

> "This model yielded a very good fit to the data ( df = 251, χ² = 483.2, CFI = .99, TLI = .98, RMSEA = .03, SRMR = .02). Achievement goals explained substantial amounts of variance in the outcome variables (R² = .19–.57)."

### Daumiller 2019 (2)

Daumiller, M., Dickhäuser, O., & Dresel, M. (2019). University Instructors' Achievement Goals for Teaching. Journal of Educational Psychology, 111(1), 131–148. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000271

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparing a model with freely varying regression paths against one with paths restricted across the three status groups showed no significant fit loss, so "the predictive effects of the different goals on the outcome variables were similar for the three sta- tus groups of instructors".

> "Since the more restricted model did not describe the data significantly worse (ΔCFI  = .003, ΔTLI = .001, 2ΔLL(80) = 83.13, p = .38), it can be assumed that the predictive effects of the different goals on the outcome variables were similar for the three sta- tus groups of instructors."
