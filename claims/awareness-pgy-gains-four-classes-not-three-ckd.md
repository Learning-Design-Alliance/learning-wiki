# Awareness of CKD dose-adjustment needs was greater for PGY2 and PGY3 than PGY1 for allergy, endocrine, gastrointestinal, and rheumatologic classes but not for analgesic, cardiovascular, or neuropsychotropic classes

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` PGY2 residents had significantly greater awareness mean scores than PGY1 for the overall score and for allergy, endocrine, gastrointestinal, and rheumatologic classes. [→ Surana Sikander 2017](#surana-sikander-2017)
`q2 i?` PGY3 residents had significantly greater awareness mean scores than PGY1 for the overall score and the same four medication classes. [→ Surana Sikander 2017 (2)](#surana-sikander-2017-2)
`q2 i?` There were no statistically significant PGY-group mean differences in awareness for analgesic, cardiovascular, and neuropsychotropic classes. [→ Surana Sikander 2017 (3)](#surana-sikander-2017-3)

## Evidence

### Surana Sikander 2017

Surana Sikander, Kumar Neeru, Vasudeva Amita, Shaikh Gulvahid, Jhaveri Kenar D., Shah Hitesh, Malieckal Deepa, Fogel Joshua, Sidhu Gurwinder, Rubinstein Sofia. (2017). Awareness and knowledge among internal medicine house-staff for dose adjustment of commonly used medications in patients with CKD. BMC Nephrology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12882-017-0443-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Post-hoc LSD comparisons after ANOVA and ANCOVA in the 341-resident survey, awareness outcome. PGY2 'had significantly greater mean scores than PGY1' for the overall score and four classes, with printed p-values; results were maintained in ANCOVA adjusting for covariates.

> "LSD post-hoc analyses showed that PGY2 had significantly greater mean scores than PGY1 for total score of all medications (p = 0.01), and also for allergy (p = 0.03), endocrine (p = 0.001), gastrointestinal (p = 0.004) and rheumatologic (p < 0.001) medication classes."

### Surana Sikander 2017 (2)

Surana Sikander, Kumar Neeru, Vasudeva Amita, Shaikh Gulvahid, Jhaveri Kenar D., Shah Hitesh, Malieckal Deepa, Fogel Joshua, Sidhu Gurwinder, Rubinstein Sofia. (2017). Awareness and knowledge among internal medicine house-staff for dose adjustment of commonly used medications in patients with CKD. BMC Nephrology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12882-017-0443-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Post-hoc LSD comparisons for PGY3 versus PGY1 in the same awareness analysis. PGY3 had 'significantly greater mean scores than PGY1for total score of all medications' and for the four classes, with printed p-values.

> "LSD post-hoc analyses showed that PGY3 had significantly greater mean scores than PGY1for total score of all medications (p < 0.001), and also allergy (p = 0.001), endocrine (p < 0.001), gastrointestinal (p < 0.001), and rheumatologic (p < 0.001) medication classes."

### Surana Sikander 2017 (3)

Surana Sikander, Kumar Neeru, Vasudeva Amita, Shaikh Gulvahid, Jhaveri Kenar D., Shah Hitesh, Malieckal Deepa, Fogel Joshua, Sidhu Gurwinder, Rubinstein Sofia. (2017). Awareness and knowledge among internal medicine house-staff for dose adjustment of commonly used medications in patients with CKD. BMC Nephrology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12882-017-0443-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

ANOVA results for the awareness outcome in the same survey found 'no statistically significant mean differences for PGY groups' for these three classes; equivalence was not tested.

> "There were no statistically significant mean differences for PGY groups for analgesic, cardiovascular, and neuropsychotropic medication classes."
