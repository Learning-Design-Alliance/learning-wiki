# For heat flow scenarios, both joint presentation conditions produced more alignable differences than sequential presentation, but only the instructed joint-presentation condition outperformed sequential presentation on causal relevance and similarity rating

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For heat flow, learners in both the JP and JP+I conditions listed more alignable differences than SP learners, while JP and JP+I did not differ; only JP+I outperformed SP on causal relevance of differences and similarity rating. [→ Mason 2016](#mason-2016)

## Evidence

### Mason 2016

Lucia Mason & Maria Caterina Tornatora (2016). Analogical encoding with and without instructions for case comparison of scientific phenomena. Educational Psychology, 36(2), 391-412. https://doi.org/10.1080/01443410.2014.953038

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized experiment with 333 students (165 5th graders, 168 7th graders) assigned to SP, JP, or JP+I conditions. For the heat flow scenarios the JP and JP+I conditions beat SP on alignable differences ("p = .017, d = .37" and "p = .006, d = .42"), with no JP–JP+I difference.

> "Multiple comparison tests revealed that both learners in JP (p = .017, d = .37) and JP + I (p = .006, d = .42) conditions produced more alignable differences for the two scenarios than learners in the SP condition, while the former did not differentiate."
