Korat et al. 2017¶

Korat, O., Kozlov-Peretz, O., & Segal-Drori, O. (2017). Repeated E-book Reading and Its Contribution to Learning New Words among Kindergartners. Journal of Education and Training Studies, Vol. 5, No. 7. https://doi.org/10.11114/jets.v5i7.2498

q3 · i? · causal · r2

ANCOVA on word explanation scores by group, time, and word type in the randomized e-book study. The Results section reports the posttest difference "in favor of dictionary words compared to non-dictionary words"; the Discussion notes progress for non-dictionary words was "significantly smaller".

"In the experimental group, a significant difference in the expl anation of words was found in posttest 1 and posttest 2 in favor of dictionary words compared to non-dictionary words."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Three repetitions of dictionary word meanings produced significant progress in word explanation, and three more repetitions (six total) brought further progress — related
  • Kindergartners who independently read an e-book with a built-in dictionary improved in receptive word comprehension and word explanation more than controls in regular kindergarten; results for word production were inconsistent — related
  • Word production showed no posttest difference between dictionary and non-dictionary words, with an initial pretest gap favoring non-dictionary words — related
  • Receptive word comprehension improved for both dictionary and non-dictionary words, with no receptive-level difference between the two word types — related
