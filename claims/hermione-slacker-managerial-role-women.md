## Evidence

### Danny Doucette 2020

Danny Doucette, Russell Clark, and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). Hermione and the Secretary: How gendered task division in introductory physics labs can disrupt equitable learning. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ab7831](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ab7831)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative findings from ethnographic observations and interviews in the introductory lab. The authors introduce the Hermione-Slacker mode, in which a student "typically female" shoulders "a disproportionate amount of the work"; interviews with Natalie, Paulette, Zara, Liza, Janet and Kamala illustrate it. No effect size is reported.

> "In this case, a student, typically female, ends up shouldering a disproportionate amount of the work and compensating for the shortcomings of their partner(s). Such students take on the responsibility of ensuring the work gets done when their partners fall short"

### Danny Doucette 2020 (2)

Danny Doucette, Russell Clark, and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). Hermione and the Secretary: How gendered task division in introductory physics labs can disrupt equitable learning. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ab7831](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ab7831)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Observation-phase tally reported by the authors: of approximately 20 students identified in the Hermione role, "none of them were men." The count is the authors' own printed figure from the observation phase.

> "In total, of approximately 20 students in the Hermione role we identified during the observation phase of our work, none of them were men."
