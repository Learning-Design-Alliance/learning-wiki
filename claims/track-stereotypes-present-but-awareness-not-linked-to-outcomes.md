## Evidence

### Brummelman 2024

Brummelman, E., van Atteveldt, N., Wolf, S. & Sierksma, J. (2024). Using social and behavioral science to address achievement inequality. npj Science of Learning, 9, 44. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00259-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The editorial reports, citing Bardach et al., a study of 3,880 secondary school students rating perceived views of their own track. "Pre-registered analyses conﬁrmed the presence of such stereotypes"; mixed-track students fell in between. No effect sizes are printed.

> "Pre-registered analyses conﬁrmed the presence of such stereotypes, showing that students from lower, vocational tracks believed that others had relatively negative perceptions of students in their track"

### Brummelman 2024 (2)

Brummelman, E., van Atteveldt, N., Wolf, S. & Sierksma, J. (2024). Using social and behavioral science to address achievement inequality. npj Science of Learning, 9, 44. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00259-1

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The editorial reports the same study's longitudinal null: stereotype change was "not consistently related" to outcome change, so students becoming more aware of negative stereotypes did not suffer academically. No effect sizes are printed.

> "However, changes in such stereotypes over time were not consistently related to changes in engagement, self-perceived ability, or academic achievement over time."
