## Evidence

### Roser 2017

Roser, M. (2017). iPadegogy of reading: Do iPads influence studens' reading? Teachers and Curriculum, 17(2), 25-32. http://dx.doi.org/10.15663/tandc.v17i2.176

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Survey responses on engagement (motivation, focus, participation) from both groups after the five-week unit. The article reports the 52 percent versus 82 percent focus contrast and concludes the iPad "may have assisted in keeping the students engaged when completing the set activities"; descriptive percentages only, no test.

> "just over half (52 percent) admitted to remaining focused and attentive when required to complete the handwritten bookwork activities. This is a stark contrast to the responses from the treatment participants (82 percent), who, as well as exhibiting high levels of motivation and participation, also accede to actively remaining focused and attentive when completing the activities using their iPad."

### Roser 2017 (2)

Roser, M. (2017). iPadegogy of reading: Do iPads influence studens' reading? Teachers and Curriculum, 17(2), 25-32. http://dx.doi.org/10.15663/tandc.v17i2.176

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same survey's participation items: 82 percent of treatment students reported participating more than in previous units, while almost three quarters (73 percent) of control participants shared that opinion about their unit. Descriptive percentages, no inferential test reported.

> "a high number of students from the treatment group (82 percent) acknowledged that they participated more during the reading unit with their iPad than previous reading units when t heir iPads were not available for use to them."
