Evidence¶

Benzi Slakmon 2024¶

Benzi Slakmon, Rotem Abdu. (2024). Learning to notice collaboration: examining the impact of professional development on mathematics teachers’ enhanced awareness in CSCL settings. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn 19:137–155. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-024-09423-6

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Descriptive per-participant analysis of attending and responding utterance counts before and after the intervention (Table 3, totals 107 to 241 across the 14 teachers). The article reports the counts descriptively; no per-participant effect size or test is printed for individual trajectories.

"In particular, 9 of the 14 participants showed a change in at least one more dialogic utterance per episode after the intervention. Three participants showed milder increases (2, 6, and 13), and two participants showed a mild decrease (5 and 9)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • The increase in noticing dialogue was accompanied by a decrease in noticing mathematical activity, described as attentional tunneling — a design consideration for integrated dialogue-plus-content noticing training — related
  • Dialogic noticing gains occurred in nearly all episodes — with significant increases in episodes 2, 3, 5, 7, and 9 — except episode 6, which did not change — related
  • After a dialogic-focused professional development intervention, mathematics teachers noticed student dialogue significantly more, in both attending (83 to 170 cases) and responding (24 to 71 cases) — related
  • The intervention significantly increased teachers' noticing of six dialogic attributes — persuasive interaction, one leads and another is led, agreement and collective attention, students have equal status, dialogic gap, and monologue — but not of dialogue-expansion attributes — related
  • Student-focused noticing marginally increased (Cohen's d = 0.43) while teacher-focused codes showed no change (d = 0.06)
