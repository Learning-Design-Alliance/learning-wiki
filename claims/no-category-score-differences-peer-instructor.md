# Peer and instructor scores show no significant differences in primary assessment, diagnostic actions, therapeutic actions, or communication

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` None of the four QSAT categories differed significantly between peer and instructor assessment: primary assessment (3.71 ± 0.69 vs 3.80 ± 0.58), diagnostic actions (3.37 ± 0.80 vs 3.61 ± 0.66), therapeutic actions (3.24 ± 0.97 vs 3.03 ± 0.79), and communication (3.66 ± 0.71 vs 3.56 ± 0.66). [→ Yu Ji Hye 2021](#yu-ji-hye-2021)

## Evidence

### Yu Ji Hye 2021

Yu Ji Hye, Lee Mi Jin, Kim Soon Sun, Yang Min Jae, Cho Hyo Jung, Noh Choong Kyun, Lee Gil Ho, Lee Su Kyung, Song Mi Ryoung, Lee Jang Hoon, Kim Miran, Jung Yun Jung. (2021). Assessment of medical students’ clinical performance using high-fidelity simulation: comparison of peer and instructor assessment. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-021-02952-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Independent t-tests on each of the four modified-QSAT categories for 34 groups. Table 5 prints t = -0.593, p = 0.555 (primary assessment), t = -1.319, p = 0.192 (diagnostic), t = 0.940, p = 0.351 (therapeutic), t = 0.633, p = 0.529 (communication); no effect sizes are printed.

> "Likewise, there were no significant differences between peer and instructor assessment for the categories of primary assessment (peer vs. instructor: 3.71 ± 0.69 vs. 3.80 ± 0.58), diagnostic actions (peer vs. instructor: 3.37 ± 0.80 vs. 3.61 ± 0.66), therapeutic actions (peer vs. instructor: 3.24 ± 0.97 vs. 3.03 ± 0.79), and communication (peer vs. instructor: 3.66 ± 0.71 vs. 3.56 ± 0.66)."
