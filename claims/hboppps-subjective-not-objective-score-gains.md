## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Average subjective-question scores were significantly higher in Class 2019 (41.6 ± 0.3) than Class 2017 (38.2 ± 0.4), and Class 2018 also scored higher (41.4 ± 0.3). [→ Liu Xiao-Yu 2022](#liu-xiao-yu-2022)
`q2 i0` The objective-question score of Class 2018 was significantly lower than both Class 2017 and Class 2019, and the article states the improvement of students' ability to answer multi-choice and single answer questions is not significant compared to classical BOPPPS. ## Evidence

### Liu Xiao-Yu 2022

Liu Xiao-Yu, Lu Chunmei, Zhu Hui, Wang Xiaoran, Jia Shuwei, Zhang Ying, Wen Haixia, Wang Yu-Feng. (2022). Assessment of the effectiveness of BOPPPS-based hybrid teaching model in physiology education. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-022-03269-y

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Three-cohort ANOVA comparison of the subjective (essay) component of the final examination. The "average score of subjective questions was significantly higher in Class 2019"; only test statistics were printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "Moreover, the average score of subjective questions was significantly higher in Class 2019 than that of Class 2017 (38.2 ± 0.4 in Class 2017 and 41.6 ± 0.3 in Class 2019, P < 0.01 by ANOVA and Dunnett T3 test)."

### Liu Xiao-Yu 2022 (2)

Liu Xiao-Yu, Lu Chunmei, Zhu Hui, Wang Xiaoran, Jia Shuwei, Zhang Ying, Wen Haixia, Wang Yu-Feng. (2022). Assessment of the effectiveness of BOPPPS-based hybrid teaching model in physiology education. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-022-03269-y

`q2 · i0` · `causal · r1`

Bonferroni-tested comparison of the objective-question component across the three cohorts. Class 2018's objective score was significantly lower than the other two; the article attributes the lack of HBOPPPS objective gain to not focusing on "keywords and definitions of individual facts".

> "However, the objective score of multi-choice and single answer questions earned by Class 2018 (40.5 ± 0.2) was significantly lower than that of the Class 2017 (42.9 ± 0.2, P < 0.01) and Class 2019 (42.3 ± 5.3, P < 0.01 by ANOVA and Bonferroni test"
