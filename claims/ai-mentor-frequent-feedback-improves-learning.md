## Evidence

### Mollick 2023

Mollick, E., & Mollick, L. (2023). Assigning AI: Seven Approaches for Students with Prompts. Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania & Wharton Interactive. [doi:10.2139/ssrn.4475995](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4475995)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument in the AI-as-Mentor section, citing feedback research: "feedback should be timely and goal-oriented" and balanced, highlighting strengths and areas for improvement. The paper presents this as the rationale for the mentor role, not as a new empirical test.

> "Making mistakes can help students learn. particularly if those mistakes are followed by feedback tailored to the individual student (Metcalfe, 2012). To be effective, that feedback should be timely and goal-oriented, helping students achieve their objectives."

### Mollick 2023 (2)

Mollick, E., & Mollick, L. (2023). Assigning AI: Seven Approaches for Students with Prompts. Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania & Wharton Interactive. [doi:10.2139/ssrn.4475995](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4475995)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Authors' argument that AI can offload part of personalized feedback in large classes. The paper notes the AI "doesn't know the students or understand the students' context" and feedback should be coupled with in-class discussion and clear guidelines.

> "While ongoing, tailored feedback is important, it is difficult and time-consuming to implement in a large class setting. The time and effort required to consistently provide personalized feedback to numerous students can be daunting. With guidance and oversight however, some of that work can shift to the AI."
