# During collaborative exam work, monitoring of cognition is the most common monitoring target, followed by monitoring of behavior, with monitoring of motivation or emotion scarce

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across nine groups, monitoring cognition was the most frequent monitoring target (n = 263), followed by monitoring behavior (n = 173), while monitoring motivation or emotion was least common (n = 27, 5.83% of events). [→ Sobocinski 2020](#sobocinski-2020)

## Evidence

### Sobocinski 2020

Sobocinski, M., Järvelä, S., Malmberg, J., Dindar, M., Isosalo, A., & Noponen, K. (2020). How does monitoring set the stage for adaptive regulation or maladaptive behavior in collaborative learning? Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-020-09224-w

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Video-coded analysis of 463 monitoring events from nine groups of three students during a collaborative high-school physics exam. The authors report "Monitoring cognition was the most common event ( n = 263), followed by monitoring behavior (n = 173)"; motivation/emotion monitoring accounted for 5.83% of events.

> "A total of 463 monitoring events were coded across the analyzed 9 groups. Monitoring cognition was the most common event ( n = 263), followed by monitoring behavior (n = 173)."
