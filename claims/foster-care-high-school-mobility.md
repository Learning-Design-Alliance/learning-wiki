# Students in foster care change schools far more often than other students, with many moving multiple times within a school year

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In 2018–19, 34% of California students in foster care moved schools during the school year versus 5% of non-foster students, and 13% moved more than once. [→ Burns 2022](#burns-2022)
`q2 i?` Over four school years, 71% of students in foster care changed schools during the school year at least once, and 22% had four or more such moves, compared to less than 1% of other students. [→ Burns 2022 (2)](#burns-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Burns 2022

Burns, D., Espinoza, D., Adams, J., & Ondrasek, N. (2022). California students in foster care: Challenges and promising practices. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/118.471

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive analysis of California Department of Education enrollment records for 2018–19, counting school moves between September 1 and June 1. The report found "students in foster care were more likely to change schools than other students, and many moved multiple times" (34% vs. 5%; 13% moved more than once).

> "Within the 2018–19 school year, students in foster care were more likely to change schools than other students, and many moved multiple times."

### Burns 2022 (2)

Burns, D., Espinoza, D., Adams, J., & Ondrasek, N. (2022). California students in foster care: Challenges and promising practices. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/118.471

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Four-year descriptive mobility analysis (2015–16 to 2018–19) of enrollment records counting in-school-year moves. The report states "71% of students in foster care in 2018–19 changed schools during the school year in at least 1 of those 4 years, compared to 15% among all other students."

> "Seventy-one percent of students in foster care in 2018–19 changed schools during the school year in at least 1 of those 4 years, compared to 15% among all other students."
