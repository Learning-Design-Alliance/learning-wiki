# The age pattern of kanji writing accuracy differs between cohorts: writing peaked in early adulthood in 2006 but continued increasing into middle adulthood in 2016

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In 2006, middle adults' writing scores did not differ from young adults', whereas in 2016 all age-group differences in writing accuracy were significant, indicating a further increase from early to middle adulthood a decade later. [→ Otsuka 2020](#otsuka-2020)

## Evidence

### Otsuka 2020

Otsuka, S., & Murai, T. (2020). The multidimensionality of Japanese kanji abilities. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-59852-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Tukey post-hoc comparisons on writing-accuracy composite z-scores from the same ANOVA datasets. The article interprets this as "reduced kanji writing ability in young adults... a decade later", attributing it to the spread of digital writing devices and increased attention to English learning.

> "In terms of writing accuracy, the scores for this dimension in middle adults were different from those of high-school and university age (all p < 0.001) but not young adults in 2006 (p = 0.660), whereas significant differences among these age groups were shown in 2016 (all p < 0.001)."
