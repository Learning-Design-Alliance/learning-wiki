## Evidence

### Richard R. Hake 2002

Richard R. Hake. (2002). Lessons From the Physics-Education Reform Effort. Conservation Ecology. Originally published 2001. [doi:10.5751/es-00286-050228](https://doi.org/10.5751/es-00286-050228)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey of pre/post MD/FCI data from 62 introductory physics courses enrolling 6542 students, plotted in Fig. 1. The figure caption reports "<<g>>48IE = 0.48 ± 0.14" for IE versus "<<g>>14T = 0.23 ± 0.04" for T courses; the author notes the difference is "almost two sd’s of <<g>>48IE".

> "The average of <g>’s for the 48 IE courses is <<g>>48IE = 0.48 ± 0.14 (standard deviation) while the average of <g>’s for the 14 T courses is <<g>>14T = 0.23 ± 0.04 (sd)."

### Richard R. Hake 2002 (2)

Richard R. Hake. (2002). Lessons From the Physics-Education Reform Effort. Conservation Ecology. Originally published 2001. [doi:10.5751/es-00286-050228](https://doi.org/10.5751/es-00286-050228)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Matched-data subset analysis responding to Becker's attrition criticism: only posttest scores of students who also took the pretest were averaged. Matched gains ("<<g>>34IE-matched = 0.50 ± 0.13sd" vs "<<g>>4T-matched = 0.24 ± 0.05sd") closely match the full-sample values.

> "an analysis of all the matched data of the survey (4 T courses enrolling 292 students, and 34 IE courses enrolling 3511 students) yields: <<g>>4T-matched = 0.24 ± 0.05sd,      <<g>>34IE-matched = 0.50 ± 0.13sd;"

### William J. Gerace & Ian D. Beatty 2005

William J. Gerace & Ian D. Beatty. (2005). Teaching vs. Learning: Changing Perspectives on Problem Solving in Physics Instruction. 9th Common Conference of the Cyprus Physics Association and Greek Physics Association, Nicosia, Cyprus. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0508131](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0508131)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article reports Hake's survey of FCI pre/post data from 62 courses with 6542 students, in which "traditional lecture-and-homework instruction produces a relatively small average improvement" while interactive-engagement courses improved "twice as large". The article reports Hake's survey second-hand and does not name the measure as normalized gain; the normalized-gain figures are in Hake (2002) above. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "A study by Richard Hake of FCI data from 62 courses with 6542 students has yielded convincing evidence that traditional lecture-and-homework instruction produces a relatively small average improvement. On the other hand, instruction employing “interactive engagement” methods based on PER and other learning research typically produces an average improvement twice as large"
