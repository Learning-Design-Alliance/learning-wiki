# Knowledge construction in MFC begins during the recalling phase, prioritizing global attributes, and is facilitated by later lecturing; behaviorally there is no order effect of blended teaching sequence

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` During recalling, MFC activity decoded the knowledge structure students actually recalled (not the original structure), in both LH and HL conditions. [→ X. Feng 2025](#x-feng-2025)
`q3 i?` Behavioral learning outcomes did not differ between LH (recalling before lecturing) and HL (recalling after lecturing) orders. [→ X. Feng 2025 (2)](#x-feng-2025-2)

## Evidence

### X. Feng 2025

X. Feng, X. Xu, Z. Meng, J. Jiang, M. Pei, Y. Zheng, C. Lu. (2025). A Rapid Cortical Learning Process Supporting Students’ Knowledge Construction During Real Classroom Teaching. Advanced Science. https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202416610

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r1`

Decoding analysis during the LH recalling phase (six students recalled fewer than three events, df = 14). Decoding against the original structure was not significant (d = 0.250), but decoding against recalled structure was, with "Cohen's d = 0.683"; replicated in HL.

> "a significant similarity was observed between the predicted knowledge structure generated by the MFC activity and the knowledge structure actually recalled by students during the recalling phase (t (14) = 2.461, p = 0.030, Cohen's d = 0.683"

### X. Feng 2025 (2)

X. Feng, X. Xu, Z. Meng, J. Jiang, M. Pei, Y. Zheng, C. Lu. (2025). A Rapid Cortical Learning Process Supporting Students’ Knowledge Construction During Real Classroom Teaching. Advanced Science. https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202416610

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

LME comparison of learning outcomes between LH and HL conditions. The article reports no significant main effect ("p = 0.304"); equivalence was not tested. At the brain level, HL recalling showed significant MFC representation (r = 0.191, p = 0.009).

> "The results did not show a significant main effect of condition (F (1, 38) = 1.086, p = 0.304, η 2 = 0.026; Figure 1F), suggesting that, at the behavioral level, there was no order effect associated with the sequence of blended teaching."
