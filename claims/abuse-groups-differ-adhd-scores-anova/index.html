Evidence¶

Sanderud K. 2016¶

Sanderud K., Murphy S., Elklit A. (2016). Child maltreatment and ADHD symptoms in a sample of young adults. European Journal of Psychotraumatology. https://doi.org/10.3402/ejpt.v7.32061

q3 · i? · associational · r2

One-way ANOVA on abuse typologies and overall ADHD score in the national survey. The overall abuse group scored M=16.87 (SD=4.30), emotional abuse M=16.01, sexual abuse M=15.30, and the non-abused group "much lower levels of ADHD (M=13.81, SD=3.72)"; no effect size is printed.

"Post hoc analysis indicated that only the non-abused group differed significantly from the three abuse groups with much lower levels of ADHD (M=13.81, SD=3.72)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • The authors interpret the maltreatment–ADHD association as partly complicated by symptom overlap between ADHD and PTSD, which may make the two conditions difficult to distinguish
  • Males were significantly more likely than females to report probable ADHD in the young adult sample (OR=1.56)
  • In a national sample of young Danish adults, all three maltreatment latent classes showed significantly increased risk of probable ADHD relative to the non-abused class, strongest for the overall abuse class (OR=5.08), then emotional abuse (OR=3.09) and sexual abuse (OR=2.07)
  • 14.5% of the sample scored above the ASRS cutoff for probable ADHD, and the overall abuse latent class was especially associated with ADHD symptoms (35.9% above cutoff vs 10.1% of the non-abused group) — related
