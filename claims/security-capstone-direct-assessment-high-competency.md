## Evidence

### Vijay Kanabar 2015

Vijay Kanabar, PhD, Anatoly Temkin, PhD. (2015). Computer Science Programs, Goals, Student Learning Outcomes and their Assessment. CSECS 2015, Boston, MA. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1601.07793](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1601.07793)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Direct assessment of deliverables in key security courses was conducted by the assessments committee in Sept 2015, with evaluation of student work by someone other than the instructor. The committee reported students "having achieved “High Competency”" on the capstone crypto project, in which each student used a crypto analytic algorithm to record plaintext from ciphertext.

> "We assessed students in this key security course as having achieved “High Competency”. The highest degree of possible rating is not “Exceptional”."

### Vijay Kanabar 2015 (2)

Vijay Kanabar, PhD, Anatoly Temkin, PhD. (2015). Computer Science Programs, Goals, Student Learning Outcomes and their Assessment. CSECS 2015, Boston, MA. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1601.07793](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1601.07793)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Following the direct assessment deliberation, the committee identified an action plan to enhance student competency, including making the final project more challenging with focus on algorithm performance, increased tutorial support for technical written and verbal communication, and remedial explanation of APA style requirements.

> "An action plan was identified to enhance student competency."
