## Evidence

### E. Michael Nussbaum 2015

E. Michael Nussbaum, Marissa C. Owens, Gale M. Sinatra, Abeera P. Rehmat, Jacqueline R. Cordova, Sajjad Ahmad, Fred C. Harris, Jr., Sergiu M. Dascalu. (2015). Losing the Lake: Simulations to Promote Gains in Student Knowledge and Interest about Climate Change. https://doi.org/10.12973/ijese.2015.277a

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized pretest/posttest/delayed-posttest two-group experiment with 119 seventh graders assigned to the game or a control website; generalized linear modeling with GEE showed a significant group-by-time interaction ("Wald χ 2 = 9.83, p = .002"). No effect size is printed for the omnibus interaction.

> "As we had hypothesized, the objective test scores significantly increased in the experimental group from pretest to posttest/delayed posttest but there was no gain in the control group. The omnibus test was significant (Wald χ 2 = 9.83, p = .002)"

### E. Michael Nussbaum 2015 (2)

E. Michael Nussbaum, Marissa C. Owens, Gale M. Sinatra, Abeera P. Rehmat, Jacqueline R. Cordova, Sajjad Ahmad, Fred C. Harris, Jr., Sergiu M. Dascalu. (2015). Losing the Lake: Simulations to Promote Gains in Student Knowledge and Interest about Climate Change. https://doi.org/10.12973/ijese.2015.277a

`q4 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Planned comparisons on the objective test found no pretest group difference but significant posttest ("d = 1.01") and delayed-posttest ("d = 0.75") differences favoring the experimental group.

> "There was no significant difference between the groups at pretest (p = .95), but there was at posttest (p < .001, d = 1.01) and delayed posttest (p =.002, d = 0.75)."
