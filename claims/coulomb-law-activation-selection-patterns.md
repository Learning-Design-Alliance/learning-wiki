## Evidence

### Wilcox 2013

Wilcox, B. R., Caballero, M. D., Rehn, D. A., & Pollock, S. J. (2013). ACER: An Analytic Framework for Students' Use of Mathematics in Upper-Division Physics. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.9.020119](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.9.020119)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Analysis of exam solutions to a canonical disk-of-charge potential problem collected across four semesters of transformed E&M 1 (N=172). The article reports "73% of 172" correctly used Eqn. 2 while the remaining "27% of 172" routed through the electric field, missing elements CA3 and CA4.

> "Roughly three-quarters of our students (73% of 172) correctly approached the exam question using Eqn. 2. The remaining students (27% of 172) attempted to calculate the potential by determining E, either by Gauss's Law or Eqn. 1, and then taking the line integral"

### Wilcox 2013 (2)

Wilcox, B. R., Caballero, M. D., Rehn, D. A., & Pollock, S. J. (2013). ACER: An Analytic Framework for Students' Use of Mathematics in Upper-Division Physics. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.9.020119](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.9.020119)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same exam corpus (N=172) analysis of element CA4: students solving for potential by first computing the electric field. The article reports "15% of 172" attempted this method and "only a few (N=3)" succeeded, indicating the route was valid but rarely completed under exam conditions.

> "Indeed, of the students who attempted this method (15% of 172) only a few (N=3) completed the exam problem successfully."

### Bethany R. Wilcox 2012

Bethany R. Wilcox, Marcos D. Caballero, Rachel E. Pepper and Steven J. Pollock. (2012). Upper-division Student Understanding of Coulomb's Law: Difficulties with Continuous Charge Distributions. [doi:10.1063/1.4789741](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4789741)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Analysis of exam solutions from four semesters of the transformed junior-level E&M 1 course at CU (N=172) coded with the ACER framework. The quote shows "27%, N=46" attempted an indirect route via the electric field.

> "roughly a quarter of the students (27%, N=46) attempted to calculate V by first calculating E and then taking a line integral. These students did not recognize that a direct calculation of V is more efficient."
