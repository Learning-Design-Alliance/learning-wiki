# Engineering students most often use LLMs for academic writing (27%), conceptual clarification (20%), programming help (17%) and brainstorming (13%)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a 2023-24 questionnaire of 100 higher-education students dominated by engineering fields, the most prevalent LLM use pattern (27%) was drafting, improving and refining text, followed by learning and conceptual clarification (20%), programming support (17%) and brainstorming (13%). ## Evidence

### Kudina 2025

Kudina, O. (2025). Using AI in engineering education: a balancing act, driven by clear purpose. Draft version, to appear in The Routledge Handbook of the Philosophy of Engineering, 2nd ed. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16626

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Online questionnaire conducted 2023-24 with 100 respondents, 52% from AI, Robotics, Engineering and Design fields; 81 reported using LLMs. The author reports the top use themes with percentages, including drafting text at 27% and, in Table 2, conceptual clarification at 20%, programming at 17%, brainstorming at 13%.

> "The most prevalent use pattern (27%) concerns the use of LLMs for drafting text, its improvement, and language refinement, e.g., “making my English sound more professional”"
