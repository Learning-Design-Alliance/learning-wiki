# Cueing in the immersive VR environment showed no detrimental effect on learners' mental spatial representation of the environment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` None of three mental spatial representation analyses (first-hit success, total mistakes, T50 search times) supported the hypothesized detrimental effect of cueing. [→ Daniela Decker 2025](#daniela-decker-2025)

## Evidence

### Daniela Decker 2025

Daniela Decker, Martin Merkt. (2025). Look at me! Can a pedagogical agent facilitate orientation and support learning in VR? Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10531-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

In the 91-participant VR experiment, a mental spatial representation test in a replica environment recorded first-hit success, mistakes, and T50 search times. The mistakes analysis "did not reveal an effect of cueing, F < 1"; the first-hit analysis (F(2, 88) = 1.54, p = 0.220) and the T50 analysis (F < 1) also showed no effect.

> "The second analysis regarding the total number of mistakes participants made in the selection of the correct object placeholder also did not reveal an effect of cueing, F < 1."
