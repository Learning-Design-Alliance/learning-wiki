## Evidence

### Reichert et al. 2015

Collin Reichert, Cinzia Cervato, Dale Niederhauser, and Michael D. Larsen. (2015). Understanding Atmospheric Carbon Budgets: Teaching Students Conservation of Mass. Journal of Geoscience Education 63, 222–232. https://doi.org/10.5408/14-055.1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive comparison of misconception use on pretest and final (Fig. 6), with experimental drops of 31% (E-Max) and 23% (E-Min) versus control drops of 9% and 4%. The authors offer the differential decrease as an interpretation, hypothesizing greater cognitive dissonance, and note it is not directly tested.

> "Of the students in the experimental group, 90% used the pattern-matching misconception for identifying maximum stock levels on the pretest, while only 59% used it on the posttest, represented by a drop of 31% on the E-Max question (Fig. 6)."
