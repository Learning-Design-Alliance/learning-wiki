# Learning biologically primary words is rated as more enjoyable and more interesting than learning biologically secondary words

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Primary knowledge words were rated more interesting (d = 0.38) than secondary knowledge words; enjoyment was also higher (d = 0.49). [→ Kate M. Xu 2024](#kate-m-xu-2024)

## Evidence

### Kate M. Xu 2024

Kate M. Xu, Sarah Coertjens, Florence Lespiau, Kim Ouwehand, Hanke Korpershoek, Fred Paas, David C. Geary. (2024). An Evolutionary Approach to Motivation and Learning: Differentiating Biologically Primary and Secondary Knowledge. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09880-3

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Paired-sample t-tests on single-item ratings collected after each of the eight study lists in the same 144-participant randomized experiment. Ratings showed primary words "more interesting" (d = 0.38) and "more enjoyable" (d = 0.49), both p < 0.001.

> "participants rated learning primary knowledge words as more interesting (t = 4.53, p < 0.001, d = 0.38; M = 4.56, SD = 1.79 for primary knowledge words; M = 4.32, SD = 1.80 for secondary knowledge words), more enjoyable (t = 5.83, p < 0.001, d = 0.49"
