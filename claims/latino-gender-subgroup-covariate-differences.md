# Latino girls reported higher belonging and achievement than boys, and Latino subgroup and parent-education differences emerged in the sixth grade

## Evidence

### Morales-Chicas Jessica 2017

Morales-Chicas Jessica, Graham Sandra. (2017). Latinos’ Changing Ethnic Group Representation From Elementary to Middle School: Perceived Belonging and Academic Achievement. Journal of Research on Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1111/jora.12292

`q3 · i?`

Covariate results from Model 2 of the hierarchical regression (n = 1,552 belonging; n = 1,525 GPA). The article also reports "non-Mexican Latinos when compared to Mexican-origin youth showed lower school belonging (b =.09, p < .05)" and dual-origin students had lower GPA (b =.24, p < .01). Coefficients are unstandardized.

> "girls had significantly higher feelings of belonging (b = .15, p < .001) and achievement (b = .33, p < .001) in the sixth grade than did boys. In addition, as parent education level increased, GPA also increased (b = .05, p < .001)."
