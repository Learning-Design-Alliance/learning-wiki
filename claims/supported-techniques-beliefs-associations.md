# Supported techniques show higher associations with self-efficacy, growth mindset, control beliefs and learning goals than unsupported techniques, but low elaboration relates to more exam anxiety

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` High elaboration and retrieval practice correlate more strongly with self-efficacy, growth mindset, control beliefs and learning goals than low elaboration techniques; low elaboration shows no significant association with self-efficacy. [→ Ruiz-Martín Héctor 2024](#ruiz-martin-hector-2024)
`q2 i2` Examination anxiety correlates positively with low elaboration techniques (r = 0.252), has no significant correlation with high elaboration, and correlates negatively with spacing (r = −0.115) and focusing (r = −0.109). [→ Ruiz-Martín Héctor 2024 (2)](#ruiz-martin-hector-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Ruiz-Martín Héctor 2024

Ruiz-Martín Héctor, Blanco Fernando, Ferrero Marta. (2024). Which learning techniques supported by cognitive research do students use at secondary school? Prevalence and associations with students’ beliefs and achievement. Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-024-00567-5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-correlational analysis of the techniques survey against the beliefs survey (n = 2,414, Bonferroni-corrected). The article reports high elaboration and retrieval practice yielding “higher associations with all constructs than ‘low elaboration’ techniques”, with low elaboration non-significant for self-efficacy; no specific effect size is printed for this contrast, only that supported techniques exceeded 0.2 with self-efficacy.

> "In general, “high elaboration” techniques and “retrieval practice” clearly yielded higher associations with all constructs than “low elaboration” techniques, except for “examination anxiety,” which had no significant correlation with “high elaboration” and a weaker association with “retrieval practice” than “low elaboration” techniques. Remarkably, “low elaboration” techniques did not show a significant association with “self-efficacy,”"

### Ruiz-Martín Héctor 2024 (2)

Ruiz-Martín Héctor, Blanco Fernando, Ferrero Marta. (2024). Which learning techniques supported by cognitive research do students use at secondary school? Prevalence and associations with students’ beliefs and achievement. Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-024-00567-5

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

From the same techniques-by-beliefs correlation table, the article reports low elaboration techniques correlating with examination anxiety at r = 0.252, the highest of the technique scales; spacing and focusing correlated negatively.

> "The higher correlation with “examination anxiety” (r > 0.2) was with “low elaboration” techniques (r = 0.252)."
