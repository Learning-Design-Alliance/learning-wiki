# Scaffolding supports involving an analogical tension problem significantly improved introductory physics students' performance on a friction problem involving a strong alternative conception, except for calculus-based intervention group 1

## Evidence

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2017

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2017). Effect of Scaffolding on Helping Introductory Physics Students Solve Quantitative Problems Involving Strong Alternative Conceptions. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05697](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05697)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In-class recitation-quiz study with 410 students in calculus-based (183) and algebra-based (227) introductory mechanics courses, divided into one comparison and three intervention groups; ANOVA comparisons of friction-problem scores are reported in Tables 3 and 4, with the calculus intervention 1 comparison "not better enough to be significantly different" (p=0.06).

> "students who received the scaffolding supports significantly outperformed (with p-values less than 0.05) the comparison group students who did not received scaffolding support, except for the calculus-based intervention group 1 students whose performance was not better enough to be significantly different from the corresponding comparison group (p=0.06)."
