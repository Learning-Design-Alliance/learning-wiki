q2 · i? · review · r?

The review reports, citing Healey, Nagaoka, & Michelman (2014), a comparison holding ACT scores constant: "college graduation rates are 77 percent among those with a high school GPA of 3.5 or higher, but 40 percent among those with a high school GPA of 2.0–2.4." Descriptive rates only; no effect size printed.

"Among students who have the same ACT scores (21–23) and enroll in a four-year college, college graduation rates are 77 percent among those with a high school GPA of 3.5 or higher, but 40 percent among those with a high school GPA of 2.0–2.4."

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review asserts, citing multiple studies including Allensworth & Clark (2018) and Geiser & Santelices (2007), that GPAs reflecting student effort are "by far the strongest predictors of college graduation," more important than any test benchmark including the ACT or SAT.

"High school GPAs, which are largely a reflection of the effort that students put into their classes, are by far the strongest predictors of college graduation."
