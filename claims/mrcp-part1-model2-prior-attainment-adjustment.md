## Evidence

### Johnston Peter W. 2025

Johnston Peter W., Vieira Rute, Cameron Isobel M., Kumwenda Ben, Walker Kim A., Cleland Jennifer A. (2025). Big data analysis: examination of the relationship between candidates' sociodemographic characteristics and performance in the UK's Membership of the Royal College of Physicians Part 1 examination. Advances in Health Sciences Education 30(1), 53-68. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-024-10406-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Model 2 mixed-effects logistic regression (N = 1425) added EPM and SJT scores to the Model 1 covariates. The printed results show male sex OR = 1.620 and higher EPM OR = 4.108, both p < 0.001; SJT was not significant (OR = 1.003, p = 0.315).

> "Model 2: The odds of passing MRCP Part 1 at first sitting were greater for candidates passing who were male (OR = 1.620, 95% CI = 1.243, 2.111) and who had higher EPM scores (OR = 4.108, CI = 3.402, 4.961)."

### Johnston Peter W. 2025 (2)

Johnston Peter W., Vieira Rute, Cameron Isobel M., Kumwenda Ben, Walker Kim A., Cleland Jennifer A. (2025). Big data analysis: examination of the relationship between candidates' sociodemographic characteristics and performance in the UK's Membership of the Royal College of Physicians Part 1 examination. Advances in Health Sciences Education 30(1), 53-68. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-024-10406-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The discussion of Model 2 reports that after entering EPM and SJT scores, the minority-ethnic and gateway-programme effects were no longer present; in the table, minority ethnic OR = 1.166 (p = 0.303) and gateway OR = 0.142 (p = 0.015, direction reversed). The authors interpret this as suggesting reasonable equity among first-sitting passers.

> "odds were still significantly greater for passing in men, as well as candidates with higher EPM scores, but not for candidates from a minority ethnic group, or on a Gateway to Medicine programme."
