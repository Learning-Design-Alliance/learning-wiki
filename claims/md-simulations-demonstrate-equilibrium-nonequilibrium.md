## Evidence

### Daniel V. Schroeder 2015

Daniel V. Schroeder. (2015). Interactive molecular dynamics. Am. J. Phys. 83 (3), 210–218. https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4901185

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

Descriptive result from the article's Section IV on equilibrium states of the two-dimensional Lennard-Jones system, illustrated in Fig. 2 with snapshots of states left to evolve at constant energy, volume, and particle number. The article also reports a critical point at about 0.35 particles per unit volume and temperature about 0.50.

> "The gas, liquid, and solid phases are readily apparent at suitable densities and temperatures."

### Daniel V. Schroeder 2015 (2)

Daniel V. Schroeder. (2015). Interactive molecular dynamics. Am. J. Phys. 83 (3), 210–218. https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4901185

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

Author's pedagogical claim in Section IV, supported by the velocity-reversal exercise in which tiny numerical round-off errors, amplified by chaotic behavior, set a time limit beyond which the original nonequilibrium state cannot be restored. Chaos occurs even with as few as two atoms.

> "Exploring the evolution of nonequilibrium states can give students a vivid understanding of the arrow of time."
