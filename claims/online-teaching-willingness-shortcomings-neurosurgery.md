## Evidence

### Shen Jun 2025

Shen Jun, Zhang Shaolin, Sun Dongdong, Ge Ruixiang, Chen Sansong, Fang Jincheng, An Qian. (2025). Comparison of face-to-face teaching and online teaching in neurosurgery education for medical students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06817-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-course questionnaire on willingness to accept online teaching, administered to the online group and summarized in Fig. 3; the printed percentages above are reported.

> "Only 20.24% of students expressed a preference for online teaching in clinical courses. The majority of students (48.81%) indicated that they would only accept online teaching if no other alternatives were available, such as during the COVID-19 pandemic."

### Shen Jun 2025 (2)

Shen Jun, Zhang Shaolin, Sun Dongdong, Ge Ruixiang, Chen Sansong, Fang Jincheng, An Qian. (2025). Comparison of face-to-face teaching and online teaching in neurosurgery education for medical students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06817-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Follow-up survey of online-group students on shortcomings, summarized in Fig. 4; other cited disadvantages were lack of a quiet environment (10.71%), unstable network signals (7.14%), and unreliable platforms (5.95%).

> "students identified the primary disadvantage of online teaching as the lack of a classroom atmosphere (36.91%), followed by insufficient interaction with teachers (22.62%) and limited interaction with classmates (16.17%)."
