## Evidence

### Metelmann Camilla 2021

Metelmann Camilla, Metelmann Bibiana, Schuffert Louisa, Hahnenkamp Klaus, Vollmer Marcus, Brinkrolf Peter. (2021). Smartphone apps to support laypersons in bystander CPR are of ambivalent benefit: a controlled trial using medical simulation. Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13049-021-00893-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive result of the controlled simulation trial (Table 2): e.g. 90.5% of controls and 88.3% of the mandatory group spoke to and touched the person to check consciousness; 64.9% vs 63.3% performed all three look-listen-feel breathing checks. No inferential test is printed for these comparisons.

> "Regardless of app use, the majority of participants in all study groups assessed consciousness, airway obstruction and breathing in the correct way (Table 2)."

### Metelmann Camilla 2021 (2)

Metelmann Camilla, Metelmann Bibiana, Schuffert Louisa, Hahnenkamp Klaus, Vollmer Marcus, Brinkrolf Peter. (2021). Smartphone apps to support laypersons in bystander CPR are of ambivalent benefit: a controlled trial using medical simulation. Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13049-021-00893-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive result of the controlled simulation trial (Table 4): correct body position was 85.1% (control), 95.4% (facultative) and 100% (mandatory); correct arm posture was 97.3%, 93.7% and 95.0%. No inferential test is printed for these comparisons.

> "The majority of participants showed a correct body position and arm posture during the chest compressions (Table 4). All participants in the mandatory group had a correct body position."
