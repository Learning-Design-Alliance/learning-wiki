## Evidence

### Cutrona 1984

Cutrona, C. E., Russell, D., & Jones, R. D. (1984). Cross-Situational Consistency in Causal Attributions: Does Attributional Style Exist? Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 47(5), 1043-1058. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.47.5.1043

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r1`

Study 2 subsample analysis: the 22 women (about 25% of the sample) with the lowest ASQ variance scores were selected. Stability (average r(20) = .254) and total scores (average r(20) = .210) improved slightly, but internality and globality correlations dropped below .10 to .14, and even these women changed attributions over time.

> "However, for intemality [average r(20) = .080] and giobality [average r(20) = .135], the correlations between attributional style and the other attribution measures were actually lower for the consistent subjects than for the sample as a whole."
