## Evidence

### Asher 2025

Asher, M. W., & Harackiewicz, J. M. (2025). Using Choice and Utility Value to Promote Interest: Stimulating Situational Interest in a Lesson and Fostering the Development of Interest in Statistics. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000921

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r?`

Meta-regression across Studies 1–7 on maintained situational interest in regression showed both manipulations raised it, with "d = 0.15" for utility value and "d = 0.16" for choice, and no significant difference between them. A negative non-significant D-UV × baseline interest interaction (b = −0.08, p = .082) suggested larger effects for lower-interest participants.

> "the directly-communicated utility value manipulation increased participants’ feelings of maintained situational interest in regression by .15 standard deviations, d = 0.15, p < .001. The choice manipulation increased maintained situational interest by .16 standard deviations, d = 0.16, p < .001. These two effect sizes did not significantly differ, p = .881."
