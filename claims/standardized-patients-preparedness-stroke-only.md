## Subclaims
`q3 i?` On a self-rated preparedness scale, the standardized-patient group felt better prepared for the stroke scenario (Kruskal-Wallis; P < 0.0001), but there was no significant difference for chest pain (P = 0.067) or acute dyspnea/asthma (P = 0.899). ## Evidence

### Herbstreit Frank 2017

Herbstreit Frank, Merse Stefanie, Schnell Rainer, Noack Marcel, Dirkmann Daniel, Besuch Anna, Peters Jürgen. (2017). Impact of standardized patients on the training of medical students to manage emergencies. Medicine 96(5):e5933. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000005933

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Self-assessment questionnaire using a numerical rating scale administered after the course in the randomized trial. Only the stroke scenario showed a significant perceived-preparedness difference; the other two scenarios did not.

> "those taught using standardized patients for the stroke scenario felt better prepared (Kruskal-Wallis; P < 0.0001). However, there was no significant difference between subcohorts for chest pain or acute dyspnea/asthma scenarios (Kruskal-Wallis; P = 0.067 and P = 0.899, respectively)"
