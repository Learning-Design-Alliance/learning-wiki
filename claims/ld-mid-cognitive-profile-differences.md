## Evidence

### Ioannis Agaliotis & Afroditi Teli 2016

Ioannis Agaliotis & Afroditi Teli. (2016). Teaching Arithmetic Combinations of Multiplication and Division to Students with Learning Disabilities or Mild Intellectual Disability: The Impact of Alternative Fact Grouping and the Role of Cognitive and Learning Factors. Journal of Education and Learning, 5(4). https://doi.org/10.5539/jel.v5n4p90

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mann-Whitney baseline comparison of cognitive and learning factors between students with LD (n = 39) and MID (n = 14); significant differences favoring LD in verbal skills, processing speed and counting, with only test statistics printed.

> "there were statistically significant differences in verbal skills abilities (U = 120.000, z = -3.090, p = .002), processing speed (U = 84.500, z = -2.579, p = .010) and counting (U = 162.000, z = -2.623, p = .009)"

### Ioannis Agaliotis & Afroditi Teli 2016 (2)

Ioannis Agaliotis & Afroditi Teli. (2016). Teaching Arithmetic Combinations of Multiplication and Division to Students with Learning Disabilities or Mild Intellectual Disability: The Impact of Alternative Fact Grouping and the Role of Cognitive and Learning Factors. Journal of Education and Learning, 5(4). https://doi.org/10.5539/jel.v5n4p90

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Baseline Mann-Whitney comparisons showing no significant LD-MID differences in working memory, phonological short-term memory, or knowledge of properties of operations; equivalence was not tested, only non-significance reported.

> "there were no significant differences in working memory (U = 142.000, z = -.935, p = .350), phonological short-term memory (U = 251.500, z = -. 467, p = .641), and properties of operations (U = 221.5, z = -.919, p = .358)"
