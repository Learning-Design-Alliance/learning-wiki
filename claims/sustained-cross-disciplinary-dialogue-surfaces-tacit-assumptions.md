### Edward F. Redish and Todd J. Cooke 2012

Edward F. Redish and Todd J. Cooke. (2012). Learning Each Other's Ropes: Negotiating interdisciplinary authenticity. To be published in CBE-LSE. [doi:10.1187/cbe.12-09-0147](https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.12-09-0147)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

In the case-study section, the authors state that each discipline brings distinct but often tacit perceptions about content, epistemological orientation, and competencies, and that "hidden assumptions" surface only through frequent conversation.

> "Often these perceptions are tacit. Only through frequent conversations can these hidden assumptions be brought to light."

### Edward F. Redish and Todd J. Cooke 2012 (2)

Edward F. Redish and Todd J. Cooke. (2012). Learning Each Other's Ropes: Negotiating interdisciplinary authenticity. To be published in CBE-LSE. [doi:10.1187/cbe.12-09-0147](https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.12-09-0147)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The abstract reports that the multi-year conversation between the physicist and biologist "led us both to a deeper understanding of each other's discipline" and to significant changes in how each presents classes; this is a self-reported case study with no quantitative measures.

> "These extended discussions have led us both to a deeper understanding of each other's discipline and to significant changes in the way we each think about and present our classes."
