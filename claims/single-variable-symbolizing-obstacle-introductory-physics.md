## Evidence

### Suzanne White Brahmia 2016

Suzanne White Brahmia, Andrew Boudreaux, Stephen E. Kanim. (2016). Obstacles to mathematization in introductory physics. Originally published 2013. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1601.01235](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1601.01235)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Isomorphic-pair comparison in the large-N multiple-choice assessment: item VIIc uses numbers while VIId replaces one decimal with the variable N. The authors state that substituting even one generalized variable for a numeric value inhibits appropriate reasoning at least as much as replacing whole numbers with decimals, quoting "just one symbolic variable quantity presents a significant obstacle."

> "We see a significant and large difference on items VIIc and VIId, providing strong evidence that the presence of just one symbolic variable quantity presents a significant obstacle to engineering students’ algebraic reasoning."

### Suzanne White Brahmia 2016 (2)

Suzanne White Brahmia, Andrew Boudreaux, Stephen E. Kanim. (2016). Obstacles to mathematization in introductory physics. Originally published 2013. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1601.01235](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1601.01235)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Pre/post administration of the Woozles item, in which students convert a verbal proportional statement into an equation, within the large-N assessment of freshman engineering students. The authors state that fewer than half of the students selected the equation matching the verbal statement and that the most common incorrect choice involved a reversal of the variables, quoting "no improvement after one semester of mechanics."

> "Results on item VIII are consistent with the findings of Cohen and Kanim, 45 and performance shows no improvement after one semester of mechanics."
