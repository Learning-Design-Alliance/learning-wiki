Evidence¶

Treiman Rebecca 2013¶

Treiman Rebecca, Kessler Brett. (2013). Learning to Use an Alphabetic Writing System. Language Learning and Development. https://doi.org/10.1080/15475441.2013.812016

q2 · i? · review · r3

Study of Brazilian prephonological spellers with a mean age of 4 years and 3 months, identified with a rearrangement procedure. The review reports that children "tended to use letters and digrams" common in written Portuguese, e.g. ‹VAVI› for dedo.

"We found that these children tended to use letters and digrams (pairs of adjacent letters) that are common in the words of Portuguese."

Treiman Rebecca 2013 (2)¶

Treiman Rebecca, Kessler Brett. (2013). Learning to Use an Alphabetic Writing System. Language Learning and Development. https://doi.org/10.1080/15475441.2013.812016

q2 · i? · review · r2

Cross-language comparison of U.S. and Brazilian prephonological spellers (about 5 months older than the Kessler et al. sample). Children's spellings mirrored the letter statistics of their own language, showing language-specific graphic learning.

"Portuguese words contain a higher proportion of vowel letters than English words, and the prephonological children in Brazil used a higher proportion of vowel letters than the prephonological children in the U.S."
