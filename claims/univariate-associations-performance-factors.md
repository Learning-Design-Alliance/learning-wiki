## Evidence

### Sridi Chayma 2026

Sridi Chayma, Aribi Mayssa, Lassoued Latifa, Kacem Imene, Chelly Farah, Belhadj Narjes, Fki Amene, Elleuch Nour, Chouchene Imed, Maoua Maher, Ben Dhiab Mohamed. (2026). Sociodemographic, lifestyle, and educational environment factors associated with academic performance among Tunisian medical students: a cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08518-4

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Univariate chi-square/Kruskal-Wallis comparisons across the five performance categories in the cross-sectional study (n = 701). The article reports the three significant lifestyle factors with the p-values quoted; alcohol, physical activity, sleep and medical history were not significant.

> "Psychoactive substance use, tobacco use, and stress level were significantly associated with academic performance (p < 0.001, p < 0.001, and p = 0.035, respectively)."

### Sridi Chayma 2026 (2)

Sridi Chayma, Aribi Mayssa, Lassoued Latifa, Kacem Imene, Chelly Farah, Belhadj Narjes, Fki Amene, Elleuch Nour, Chouchene Imed, Maoua Maher, Ben Dhiab Mohamed. (2026). Sociodemographic, lifestyle, and educational environment factors associated with academic performance among Tunisian medical students: a cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08518-4

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Univariate analyses across performance categories (n = 701), reported in Table 5. The article lists the five significant academic factors with their p-values as quoted; no effect sizes were printed.

> "Significant associations were observed between academic performance and academic year (p < 0.001), previous academic failure (p < 0.001), daily revision hours (p < 0.001), academic satisfaction (p = 0.001), and motivation (p < 0.001)"

### Sridi Chayma 2026 (3)

Sridi Chayma, Aribi Mayssa, Lassoued Latifa, Kacem Imene, Chelly Farah, Belhadj Narjes, Fki Amene, Elleuch Nour, Chouchene Imed, Maoua Maher, Ben Dhiab Mohamed. (2026). BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08518-4

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Univariate comparison of DREEM subscale bands against performance categories (n = 701), reported in Table 6. The article states only SPA was significant (p = 0.036); total DREEM score and other subdomains were not.

> "Among the DREEM subdomains, only student perception of atmosphere (SPA) was significantly associated with academic performance (p = 0.036)."
