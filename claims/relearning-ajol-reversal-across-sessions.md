## Evidence

### Philip A. Higham 2020

Philip A. Higham, Bettina Zengel, Laura Bartlett, and Julie A. Hadwin (2020). The Benefits of Successive Relearning on Multiple Learning Outcomes. https://osf.io/nx4va/?view_only=049ee04aab4f420e9fa0091b3b69b9d0

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

A 3 (session) × 2 (practice task) repeated-measures ANOVA on aggregate judgments of learning collected after each practice session. The session main effect "was qualified by an interaction between session and practice task, F(2, 410) = 124.49, p < .001, ηp2 = .378".

> "It yielded a main effect of session, F(2, 410) = 261.00, p < .001, ηp2 = .560, which was qualified by an interaction between session and practice task, F(2, 410) = 124.49, p < .001, ηp2 = .378."

### Philip A. Higham 2020 (2)

Philip A. Higham, Bettina Zengel, Laura Bartlett, and Julie A. Hadwin (2020). The Benefits of Successive Relearning on Multiple Learning Outcomes. https://osf.io/nx4va/?view_only=049ee04aab4f420e9fa0091b3b69b9d0

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Follow-up t-tests on the interaction compared aJOLs between tasks in Session 3. "The difference reversed in Session 3", with relearn aJOLs (M = 61.44) exceeding restudy (M = 58.17), d = -0.426.

> "the difference reversed in Session 3 (restudy: M = 58.17. SD = 15.17; relearn: M = 61.44, SD = 15.03), t(205) = -6.12, p < .001, d = -0.426."
