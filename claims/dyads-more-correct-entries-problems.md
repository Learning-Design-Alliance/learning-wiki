## Evidence

### Hausmann 2006

Hausmann, R. G. M., van de Sande, B., & VanLehn, K. (2006). Are Self-explaining and Coached Problem Solving More Effective When Done by Pairs of Students Than Alone? Pittsburgh Science of Learning Center and the Learning Research and Development Center, University of Pittsburgh. https://www.andes.pitt.edu/

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

In the same randomized comparison (individuals n = 11, dyads n = 14), dyads entered more correct entries, "M = 49.21, SD = 4.61" versus 44.36, F(1, 23) = 4.60, p = .04, d = .90.

> "the dyads ( M = 49.21, SD = 4.61) also entered more correct entries than the individuals (M = 44.36, SD = 6.70). The difference was both statistically and practically significant,  F(1, 23) = 4.60, p = .04, d = .90."

### Hausmann 2006 (2)

Hausmann, R. G. M., van de Sande, B., & VanLehn, K. (2006). Are Self-explaining and Coached Problem Solving More Effective When Done by Pairs of Students Than Alone? Pittsburgh Science of Learning Center and the Learning Research and Development Center, University of Pittsburgh. https://www.andes.pitt.edu/

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the Discussion, the authors summarize the session outcome: "the dyads were able to solve more problems" than individuals within the two-hour cap. Table 1 shows finishing counts by problem, with Problem 3's difference reaching significance.

> "Compared to individuals, the dyads were able to solve more problems, take less time per correct step, and request less assistance from Andes."
