## Evidence

### Farran 2025

Farran, E. K., Fink, E., Hughes, C., Gilligan-Lee, K. A., & The NewFAMS team. (2025). Limited sex differences in spatial language in parent-child dyads. Learning and Instruction, 95, 102006. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.102006

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the longitudinal regression for child spatial language quality (N = 111), step 3 added the gender × mother spatial language quantity interaction, "F-change(1, 99) = 6.67, p = .011, ΔR2 change = .055"; the overall model was not significant (p = .193).

> "For spatial language quality,
Model 3 was not significant, F(11, 99) = 1.382, p = .193, ΔR2 = .037, but
did significantly add to the model, F-change(1, 99) = 6.67, p = .011, ΔR2
change = .055."

### Farran 2025 (2)

Farran, E. K., Fink, E., Hughes, C., Gilligan-Lee, K. A., & The NewFAMS team. (2025). Limited sex differences in spatial language in parent-child dyads. Learning and Instruction, 95, 102006. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.102006

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r3`

Exploratory correlations of mother spatial language quantity at 24 months with child spatial language quality at 48 months were non-significant for both sexes: boys "r = .217, N = 63, p = .087" (negative), girls "r = .229, N = 48, p = .118" (positive).

> "the relationship was negative for boys (r =
 .217, N = 63, p = .087) and positive for girls (r = .229, N = 48, p =
.118). This interaction is therefore interpreted with caution."
