## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across eight question pairs on electric field direction, mean post-pre gains did not differ significantly (p > 0.05) among the VR, video, and static image treatment groups. [→ J. R. Smith 2018](#j-r-smith-2018)

## Evidence

### J. R. Smith 2018

J. R. Smith, A. Byrum, T. M. McCormick, N. Young, C. Orban, and C. D. Porter. (2018). A controlled study of stereoscopic virtual reality in freshman electrostatics. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/perc.2017.pr.089](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.089)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Controlled randomized treatment study in calculus-based freshman physics at OSU: 301 students in the electric field portion were randomly assigned to VR, video, or static image treatments with identical assessments. The authors report this null result was surprising, as they "had expected the VR treatment to signiﬁcantly outperform the two other treatments".

> "For eight different question pairs there were no signiﬁcant (> 0.05) differences between the three treatment types."
