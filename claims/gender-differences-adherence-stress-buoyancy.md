## Evidence

### Puolakanaho 2018

Puolakanaho, A., Lappalainen, R., Lappalainen, P., Muotka, J. S., Hirvonen, R., Eklund, K. M., Ahonen, T. P. S., & Kiuru, N. (2018). Reducing Stress and Enhancing Academic Buoyancy among Adolescents Using a Brief Web-based Program Based on Acceptance and Commitment Therapy: A Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Youth and Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-018-0973-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Adherence analysis of the randomized sample. The article reports that "32% of the participants in the iACTface group and 40% in the iACT group" — specifically male participants — failed to meet the adherence criterion; dropouts were mostly male (t(55.7) = 3.34, p = 0.001).

> "In both the intervention groups, a remarkable number of male participants did not fulfill the adherence criterion for the per-protocol analyses: 32% of the participants in the iACTface group and 40% in the iACT group."

### Puolakanaho 2018 (2)

Puolakanaho, A., Lappalainen, R., Lappalainen, P., Muotka, J. S., Hirvonen, R., Eklund, K. M., Ahonen, T. P. S., & Kiuru, N. (2018). Reducing Stress and Enhancing Academic Buoyancy among Adolescents Using a Brief Web-based Program Based on Acceptance and Commitment Therapy: A Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Youth and Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-018-0973-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Baseline gender comparison in the per-protocol sample. Girls reported higher overall stress (M = 3.39 vs 2.51; t(202) = 4.86, p = 0.000) and lower academic buoyancy (M = 3.29 vs 3.88; t(203) = 5.48, p = 0.000); no differences appeared in school stress or academic skills.

> "gender differences were observed in the initial level of overall stress (Girls: M = 3.39, SD = 1.37, n = 109; Boys: M = 2.51, SD = 1.21, n = 95; 2-tailed t-test: t(202) = 4.86, p = 0.000)"
