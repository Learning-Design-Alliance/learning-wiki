# Career Academies show no discernible effects on staying in school: about 10% of academy students dropped out before the end of grade 12 versus about 12% of comparison students (effect size 0.14, not statistically significant or substantively important).

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### staying-1

U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, What Works Clearinghouse. (2015, September). Dropout Prevention intervention report: Career Academies. Retrieved from https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/70

`q3 · i0` · `review · r3`

The WWC review reports the staying in school domain finding from the Kemple & Snipes (2000) randomized controlled trial, with 1,454 students in nine schools. Dropout was 10.1% for the intervention group versus 12.4% for the comparison group; the effect size of 0.14 (improvement index +6, p > .10) fell below the substantively important threshold, yielding a rating of no discernible effects.

> "About 10% of the students enrolled in Career Academies had dropped out of high school before the end of grade 12, compared to about 12% of students in the comparison group. The effect size of 0.14 was not statistically significant or substantively important."
