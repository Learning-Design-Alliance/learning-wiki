# Cool executive function measures show ascending age-related growth across adolescence, with a growth spurt around age 14–15

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Working memory (backward digit span) improves significantly with age in adolescents aged 12–17, with a developmental spurt around age 15. [→ Poon K 2018](#poon-k-2018)

## Evidence

### Poon K 2018

Poon K (2018) Hot and Cool Executive Functions in Adolescence: Development and Contributions to Important Developmental Outcomes. Front. Psychol. 8:2311. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.02311

`q2 · i3`

Cross-sectional ANCOVA of 136 adolescents in six age groups (12–17), controlling general intellectual ability, on backward digit span. The article reports "Signiﬁcant age eﬀects was identiﬁed" with F(5, 135) = 6.26, p < 0.001, η p2 = 0.19, and post-hoc tests showing 17-year-olds outperforming 12- to 14-year-olds.

> "Signiﬁcant age eﬀects was identiﬁed [ F(5, 135) = 6.26, p < 0.001, η p2 = 0.19] with older adolescents demonstrating higher working memory capacity."
