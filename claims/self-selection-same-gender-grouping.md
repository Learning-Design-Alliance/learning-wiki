## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Experimental (affinity-based) groups tended toward same-gender membership, while control groups were mostly mixed-gender in the first semester. [→ Diego Ramírez 2023](#diego-ramirez-2023)
`q2 i?` After the option to change groups, the number of single-gender groups in the control condition increased. [→ Diego Ramírez 2023 (2)](#diego-ramirez-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Diego Ramírez 2023

Diego Ramírez, Eugenio J. Guzmán-Lavín, Javier Pulgar, Cristian Candia. (2023). Affinity-Based Groups in Secondary Education: Increased Stability at the Expense of Collaboration. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2309.15212](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2309.15212)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive analysis of group composition across the four courses in the first semester, showing a same-gender tendency only in the self-selected (experimental) condition, per Table 3.

> "Regarding gender composition, experimental groups in both grades showed a tendency for same-gender grouping: four all-female and five all-male groups in the first semester. Only three groups had a mix of genders."

### Diego Ramírez 2023 (2)

Diego Ramírez, Eugenio J. Guzmán-Lavín, Javier Pulgar, Cristian Candia. (2023). Affinity-Based Groups in Secondary Education: Increased Stability at the Expense of Collaboration. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2309.15212](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2309.15212)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive comparison of second-semester control-group composition: control groups had only one all-female group in the first semester, but single-gender grouping increased once students could change groups.

> "However, when students had the chance to change groups in the second semester, the number of single-gender groups increased to four all-female and three all-male groups across both grades."
