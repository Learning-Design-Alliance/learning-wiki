# ICL ratings track the manipulated complexity sequence: increasing across units in the increasing condition and decreasing in the decreasing condition

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In the increasing complexity condition, ICL ratings rose significantly across the three learning units (F(2, 104) = 111.81, ηp² = 0.68). [→ Krieglstein 2025](#krieglstein-2025)
`q3 i3` In the decreasing complexity condition, ICL ratings fell significantly across the three learning units (F(2, 92) = 176.25, ηp² = 0.79). [→ Krieglstein 2025](#krieglstein-2025)

## Evidence

### Krieglstein 2025

Krieglstein, F., Beege, M., Wesenberg, L., Rey, G. D., & Schneider, S. (2025). The Distorting Influence of Primacy Effects on Reporting Cognitive Load in Learning Materials of Varying Complexity. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09980-0

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVAs in the main study on the three during-learning ICL ratings per condition. Increasing condition: F(2, 104) = 111.81, ηp² = 0.68, all pairwise differences significant. Decreasing condition: F(2, 92) = 176.25, ηp² = 0.79, all pairwise differences significant. Descriptives confirmed the expected trends (Table 4).

> "a significant difference between the three measurement points was found, F(2, 104) = 111.81, p < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.68."
