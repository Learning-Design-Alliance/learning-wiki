Evidence¶

Panadero 2025¶

Panadero, E., Delgado, P., Zamorano, D., Pinedo, L., Fernández-Ortube, A., & Barrenetxea-Mínguez, L. (2025). Putting excellence first: How rubric performance level order and feedback type influence students' reading patterns and task performance. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102168

q3 · i? · causal · r2

LMM analysis of visits and fixation times across rubric order conditions in the RCT. The 1–4 condition visited PL1 and PL2 more (t = 2.66, p = .01; t = 2.35, p = .02), while "participants in the 4–1 condition visited PL4 more frequently than those in the 1–4 condition"; fixation times showed a parallel pattern.

"conversely, participants in the 4–1 condition visited PL4 more frequently than those in the 1–4 condition (t = 3.82, p < .001; see Fig. 3)"

Panadero 2025 (2)¶

Panadero, E., Delgado, P., Zamorano, D., Pinedo, L., Fernández-Ortube, A., & Barrenetxea-Mínguez, L. (2025). Putting excellence first: How rubric performance level order and feedback type influence students' reading patterns and task performance. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102168

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Chi-square tests on think-aloud references (all 76 references: χ2(3) = 5.72, p = .13; adjusted n = 26: χ2(3) = 1.23, p = .75) found no order effect. The study reports that "rubric order did not affect how frequently students verbally referenced the performance levels."

"These results suggest that rubric order did not affect how frequently students verbally referenced the performance levels."
