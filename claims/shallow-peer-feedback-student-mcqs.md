## Evidence

### Grainger R 2018

Grainger R, Dai W, Osborne E, Kenwright D. (2018). Medical students create multiple-choice questions for learning in pathology education: a pilot study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1312-1

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Analysis of comments on student MCQs in PeerWise using a three-level rubric. The authors report "Thirty-six percent of MCQs (320/885) received at least one comment" and that most comments were made by just 23 students who each submitted more than ten comments.

> "Thirty-six percent of MCQs (320/885) received at least one comment. Of the total 843 comments, 34% (287/843) were classified as level three comments (leading to discussion of, or improvements to the MCQ), while 59% (497/843) of comments were classified as level one comments"

### Grainger R 2018 (2)

Grainger R, Dai W, Osborne E, Kenwright D. (2018). Medical students create multiple-choice questions for learning in pathology education: a pilot study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1312-1

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Survey items on the peer learning environment among 62 respondents, including the Constructivist Online Learning Environment Survey subscale (Cronbach's α = 0.908). The authors report "Only 24% (15/62) of students agreed that other students valued their contribution" and 68% reported no peer interaction.

> "Only 24% (15/62) of students agreed that other students valued their contribution and only 37% (23/62) of students agreed that collaboration with peers was beneficial."
