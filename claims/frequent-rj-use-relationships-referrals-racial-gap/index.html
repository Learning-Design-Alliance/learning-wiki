Evidence¶

Fronius 2019¶

Fronius, Darling-Hammond, Persson, Guckenburg, Hurley, & Petrosino. (2019). Restorative Justice in U.S. Schools: An Updated Research Review. WestEd Justice & Prevention Research Center. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED595733

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports Gregory et al.'s (2016) findings as attributed: frequent RJ implementers had better student relationships, students "felt respected by their teachers," and teachers "generally issued fewer referrals." No effect sizes are printed.

"Gregory and colleagues (2016) indicate that teachers who implemented RJ frequently had better relationships with their students. The students felt respected by their teachers, and teachers generally issued fewer referrals."

Fronius 2019 (2)¶

Fronius, Darling-Hammond, Persson, Guckenburg, Hurley, & Petrosino. (2019). Restorative Justice in U.S. Schools: An Updated Research Review. WestEd Justice & Prevention Research Center. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED595733

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review characterizes the racial-gap finding as "preliminary indications" only, with the explicit caveat that "disparate discipline patterns were not completely removed from the school."

"The authors also report preliminary indications that frequent use of RJ led to reductions in the racial discipline gap, although disparate discipline patterns were not completely removed from the school."
