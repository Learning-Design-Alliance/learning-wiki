Evidence¶

Chang 2026¶

Chang, Y., Liu, Q., Lu, Y. & Miao, E. (2026). Leveraging generative AI to facilitate peer feedback in collaborative argumentation learning. International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00586-w

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Kruskal-Wallis H test of relative argument-quality gains (seven 5-point rubric dimensions) across the three conditions. The omnibus test was significant ("H = 9.69, p = 0.008"), with pairwise PS-GenAI PF > PF, p = 0.007; no standardised effect size is printed in the pairwise sentence.

"For argument quality, there was a significant difference between the three groups (H = 9.69, p = 0.008 < 0.05, ε2 = 0.17), with the PS-GenAI PF group scored the highest for argument quality (Mean = 0.16, SD = 0.10)"

Chang 2026 (2)¶

Chang, Y., Liu, Q., Lu, Y. & Miao, E. (2026). Leveraging generative AI to facilitate peer feedback in collaborative argumentation learning. International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00586-w

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Pairwise comparison within the same Kruskal-Wallis analysis of argument-quality dimension gains. Elaboration favored the GenAI-PF group over the PF group; the PS-GenAI PF group did not significantly exceed the PF group on this dimension, and no effect size is printed for the pairwise contrast.

"Pairwise comparisons showed that elaboration was significantly higher in the GenAI-PF group (Mean = 0.32, SD = 0.32) than in the PF group (Mean = 0.08, SD = 0.18), p = 0.026 < 0.05."
