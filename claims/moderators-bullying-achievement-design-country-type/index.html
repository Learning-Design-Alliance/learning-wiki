Evidence¶

Muthanna 2021¶

Samara, M., Da Silva Nascimento, B., El-Asam, A., Hammuda, S., & Khattab, N. (2021). How Can Bullying Victimisation Lead to Lower Academic Achievement? A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of the Mediating Role of Cognitive-Motivational Factors. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 18(5), 2209. doi:10.3390/ijerph18052209

q3 · i1 · quant-synthesis · r2

Meta-ANOVA moderator analysis for study design on the bullying–achievement relationship. The article reports longitudinal OR = 0.43 (k = 7) versus cross-sectional OR = 0.85 (k = 7), Qb = 11.26, p = 0.001.

"This indicated that bullying involvement had a stronger effect on academic achievement in longitudinal studies (k = 7, OR = 0.43, p = 0.000) compared with cross-sectional studies (k = 7, OR = 0.85, p = 0.006)."

Muthanna 2021 (2)¶

Samara, M., Da Silva Nascimento, B., El-Asam, A., Hammuda, S., & Khattab, N. (2021). How Can Bullying Victimisation Lead to Lower Academic Achievement? A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of the Mediating Role of Cognitive-Motivational Factors. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 18(5), 2209. doi:10.3390/ijerph18052209

q3 · i1 · quant-synthesis · r2

Meta-ANOVAs found significant moderation by bullying assessment method (peer nomination OR = 0.23 strongest), age category (Qb = 7.30, p = 0.026), country (Qb = 21.41, p = 0.000, Portugal strongest) and bullying type (Qb = 26.10, p = 0.000, general bullying strongest).

"The heterogeneity assessment indicated that the relationship was also significantly moderated by the assessment method for bullying data (Qb = 12.62, p = 0.002)."
