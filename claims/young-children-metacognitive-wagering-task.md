## Evidence

### Vo 2014

Vo, V. Y., Li, R., Kornell, N., Pouget, A., & Cantlon, J. F. (2014). Young Children Bet On Their Numerical Skills: Metacognition in the Numerical Domain. Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614538458

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the wagering experiment with 45 children (M = 6y7m), children made binary discriminations then wagered tokens on their accuracy. The article reports "Children made significantly more high bets following correct trials than incorrect trials" in both conditions.

> "Children made significantly more high bets following correct trials than incorrect trials (number: t(88) = −4.08, p < .001; emotion: t(88) = −3.76, p < .001)."

### Vo 2014 (2)

Vo, V. Y., Li, R., Kornell, N., Pouget, A., & Cantlon, J. F. (2014). Young Children Bet On Their Numerical Skills: Metacognition in the Numerical Domain. Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614538458

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Metacognitive sensitivity measured via phi, gamma, and A'ROC exceeded chance for all age groups in both conditions; for all subjects, number phi = 0.22 and emotion phi = 0.22 (Table 1). Discrimination accuracy was above chance (number M = 0.80; emotion M = 0.77).

> "Phi coefficients were significantly greater than zero in both the number and emotion conditions for all age groups (Table 1)."
