## Evidence

### Mohri P 2025

Mohri P, Koyluoglu YO, Seker ME, Sancak SN, Dikeç M, Mazlumoglu AA, Gurol Y, Sungur IC. (2025). Medical students’ perception of paid E-learning. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0317340

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cross-sectional survey of 119 medical students; descriptive Likert-scale result. "74.1% of students reported scores of eight, nine, and ten with an average score of 8.6 ± 1.58" on a scale where 10 is most expensive.

> "When asked about the perceived expensiveness of paid e-learning resources on a 1–10 Likert scale, 74.1% of students reported scores of eight, nine, and ten with an average score of 8.6 ± 1.58 in a scale of 1 to 10 with 1 being cheap and 10 being expensive."

### Mohri P 2025 (2)

Mohri P, Koyluoglu YO, Seker ME, Sancak SN, Dikeç M, Mazlumoglu AA, Gurol Y, Sungur IC. (2025). Medical students’ perception of paid E-learning. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0317340

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive survey result on access methods among the same 119 respondents. "40% of medical students reported relying on repeated free demo sessions" and only 27% paid the fees, which the authors describe as less-than-ideal access circumstances.

> "40% of medical students reported relying on repeated free demo sessions, 27% reported paying the fees, 16% reported occasionally breaching the access protocols, 9% reported having access covered by scholarships and 8% reported using a single account with their friends when accessing paid e-learning resources."
