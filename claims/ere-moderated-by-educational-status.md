## Evidence

### Tetzlaff and Peters 2025

Leonard Tetzlaff, Bianca Simonsmeier, Tabea Peters, Garvin Brod. (2025). A cornerstone of adaptivity – A meta-analysis of the expertise reversal effect. Learning and Instruction 98 (2025) 102142. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102142

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Moderator analysis across the 60 included studies; educational status explained "R2 = 25.92 %" of variance for low prior knowledge learners and "R2 = 8.47 %" for high prior knowledge learners. The article cautions the effect seems especially weak in primary schools but the number of studies there is too low for meaningful conclusions.

> "The educational status of the sample used in the study significantly moderated the size of the effect for both low prior knowledge learners (R2 = 25.92 %) and high prior knowledge learners (R2 = 8.47 %). Both groups showed especially pronounced effect sizes in studies comparing groups with different educational status (coded “several”)."
