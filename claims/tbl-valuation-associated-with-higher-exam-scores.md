## Subclaims
`q2 i?` MED4 students rating TBL 'Very useful/useful' (74.6% ± 9.7%) and 'Somewhat useful' (72.8% ± 10.0%) scored higher on the exam than those rating it 'Not very useful/not useful' (63.4% ± 9.5%). [→ Heltne 2024](#heltne-2024)

## Evidence

### Heltne 2024

Heltne, Sofie Fagervoll; Hovdenakk, Sigrid; Kvernenes, Monika; Tenstad, Olav. (2024). Study preferences and exam outcomes in medical education: insights from renal physiology. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05964-4

Figure 2 cohort comparison in the survey study (n = 72 respondents). One-way ANOVA across TBL-usefulness cohorts gave "F (2, 69) = 6.121, p = 0.0036"; post hoc p = 0.0024 (very useful/useful vs not useful) and p = 0.0286 (somewhat useful vs not useful). No effect size printed.

> "Mean exam scores for the three cohorts (right panel): ‘Very useful/useful’; 74.6% ± 9.7%, n = 40), ‘Somewhat useful’; 72.8% ± 10.0%, n = 20, ‘Not very useful/not useful’; 63.4% ± 9.5%, n = 12. ANOVA: F (2, 69) = 6.121, p = 0.0036."
