Morgan 2024¶

Morgan, I., Gillispie, C., & Waller, A. (2024). Supporting Young Learners Through Family Engagement: Engaging First. EdTrust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED658182

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Survey 2 subgroup comparison. Lower-income parents were also more likely to say a more welcoming staff attitude could improve engagement (25% vs. 16%).

"They were more likely than parents with higher incomes to say that school staff are not welcoming to parents (40% vs. 26% for high-income parents), and that they’ve had a bad experience with their child’s school (45% vs. 26% for high-income parents)."

Morgan 2024 (2)¶

Morgan, I., Gillispie, C., & Waller, A. (2024). Supporting Young Learners Through Family Engagement: Engaging First. EdTrust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED658182

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Survey 2 subgroup comparison. Multilingual parents emphasized more respect from teachers for their racial, ethnic, or cultural background (22% vs. 10%) as what would help increase engagement.

"Unfortunately, families that spoke another language at home were more likely than families that spoke only English at home to say that the school’s staff are not welcoming to parents (44% vs. 33%) and that they have had previous bad experiences with their child’s school (46% vs. 35%)."
