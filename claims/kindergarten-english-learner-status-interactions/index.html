Evidence¶

Foorman 2017¶

Foorman, B., Herrera, S., Dombek, J., Schatschneider, C., & Petscher, Y. (2017). The relative effectiveness of two approaches to early literacy intervention in grades K–2 (REL 2017–251). Washington, DC: U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Regional Educational Laboratory Southeast. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

Kindergarten reading outcomes by English learner status and intervention group (table 4); the average FRA Phonological Awareness outcome was "45 points higher in embedded intervention schools than in standalone intervention schools" among English learner students. The printed effect size is 0.32 standard deviation (12 percentile rank points), though not significant after the Benjamini–Hochberg correction.

"The average FRA Phonological Awareness outcome among kindergarten English learner students was 45 points higher in embedded intervention schools than in standalone intervention schools"

Foorman 2017 (2)¶

Foorman, B., Herrera, S., Dombek, J., Schatschneider, C., & Petscher, Y. (2017). The relative effectiveness of two approaches to early literacy intervention in grades K–2 (REL 2017–251). Washington, DC: U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Regional Educational Laboratory Southeast. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs

q3 · i1 · causal · r3

Same kindergarten subgroup analysis (table 4); the SESAT Word Reading outcome was "higher for the standalone intervention than for the embedded intervention among non–English learner students". The printed effect size is 0.31 standard deviation (11 points, 12 percentile rank points), also not significant after the Benjamini–Hochberg correction.

"The SESAT Word Reading outcome was higher for the standalone intervention than for the embedded intervention among non–English learner students"

Foorman 2017 (3)¶

Foorman, B., Herrera, S., Dombek, J., Schatschneider, C., & Petscher, Y. (2017). The relative effectiveness of two approaches to early literacy intervention in grades K–2 (REL 2017–251). Washington, DC: U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Regional Educational Laboratory Southeast. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

Within-intervention comparison by English learner status (table 5); the SESAT Word Reading outcome was "higher among English learner students in embedded intervention schools than among non–English learner students in embedded intervention schools". The printed effect size is 0.27 standard deviation (9 points), with no differences in other reading outcomes within the same intervention group.

"In kindergarten the SESAT Word Reading outcome was higher among English learner students in embedded intervention schools than among non–English learner students in embedded intervention schools."
