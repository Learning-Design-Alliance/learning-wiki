# A tracing strategy combining segmentation and prediction produced more efficient learning than a non-interactive instructional condition

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Learners using tracing strategies, in which key frames keep information available and learners mentally reconstruct or construct segments, performed more efficiently than learners in a non-interactive instructional condition. [→ Wouters 2007](#wouters-2007)

## Evidence

### Wouters 2007

Wouters, P., Tabbers, H. K., & Paas, F. (2007). Interactivity in Video-based Models. Educational Psychology Review, 19, 327–342. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-007-9045-4

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review describes a study by Paas et al. (in press) combining segmentation and prediction in traced animation; the review attributes the efficiency advantage to the trace keeping information available, decreasing extraneous cognitive load.

> "The results showed that learners in the tracing strategies performed more efficient than learners in the non-interactive instructional condition."
