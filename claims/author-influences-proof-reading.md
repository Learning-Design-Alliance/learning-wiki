## Evidence

### Keith Weber 2013

Keith Weber, Matthew Inglis & Juan Pablo Mejia-Ramos. (2013). How mathematicians obtain conviction: Implications for mathematics instruction and research on epistemic cognition. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2013.865527

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Task-based interview study by Weber with eight mathematicians evaluating eight purported proofs plus open-ended questions about determining validity. "Six of the eight mathematicians claimed that it would"; the article notes this corroborates Muller-Hill's claims.

> "One question asked if the author of the deductive argument that they were reading would influence how they read the argument. Six of the eight mathematicians claimed that it would."

### Keith Weber 2013 (2)

Keith Weber, Matthew Inglis & Juan Pablo Mejia-Ramos. (2013). How mathematicians obtain conviction: Implications for mathematics instruction and research on epistemic cognition. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2013.865527

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Survey of 54 mathematicians experienced in refereeing manuscripts, asking whether they feel confident a proof is correct because a trusted authoritative source wrote it. The article reports the response was mixed: 39% agreed, 41% disagreed; no effect size is printed.

> "The response to this survey item was mixed; 39% of the surveyed mathematicians agreed with it while 41% disagreed with it"
