# Disrupting the event structure of dynamic visualizations — deleting event boundaries or inserting commercials between boundaries — reduces recall of the depicted events

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants viewing videos from which event boundaries had been deleted recalled a lower proportion of subevents than participants viewing other versions. [→ Spanjers 2010](#spanjers-2010)
`q2 i?` Inserting commercials between event boundaries reduced recall of a mini-series episode, while inserting more commercials at event boundaries had a positive effect on recall. [→ Spanjers 2010 (2)](#spanjers-2010-2)

## Evidence

### Spanjers 2010

Spanjers, I. A. E., van Gog, T., & van Merriënboer, J. J. G. (2010). A Theoretical Analysis of How Segmentation of Dynamic Visualizations Optimizes Students' Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-010-9135-6

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports Schwan and Garsoffky's (2004) experiment with videos of cleaning a pistol or upgrading a computer, unaltered or with black screens replacing event boundaries or parts between them; the boundary-deleted version yielded lower recall of subevents. No effect size is printed.

> "Moreover, participants who viewed the video from which event boundaries had been deleted recalled a lower proportion of the subevents than the participants from the other groups, which might be due to disruption of the event structure, reduced coherence of the video, and a lower proportion of relevant steps shown."

### Spanjers 2010 (2)

Spanjers, I. A. E., van Gog, T., & van Merriënboer, J. J. G. (2010). A Theoretical Analysis of How Segmentation of Dynamic Visualizations Optimizes Students' Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-010-9135-6

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review reports Boltz's (1992) experiment showing an episode of a mini-series with commercials inserted at or between event boundaries; the between-boundaries version was recalled less well. No effect size is printed.

> "She hypothesized that insertion of commercials between event boundaries would hinder perceiving the underlying event structure of the episode. Her results were in line with this hypothesis: Participants who saw this version recalled less from the episode than participants who saw another version."
