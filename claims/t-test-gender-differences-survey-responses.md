# Two-sample t-tests found male and female survey responses significantly different for quiz convenience and video lectures but not for most MPL questions

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Two-sample t-tests on male versus female responses showed significant differences on whether online quizzes are as convenient as paper-based quizzes and on video-lecture convenience, while responses on the MPL self-learning, capstone-help, problem-solving and teamwork questions were not significantly different. [→ Khandakar 2021](#khandakar-2021)

## Evidence

### Khandakar 2021

Khandakar, A., Chowdhury, M. E. H., Khalid, M. S., and Zorba, N. (2021). Case study of Innovative Teaching Practices and their Impact for Electrical Engineering Courses during COVID-19 Pandemic. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2107.00746](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2107.00746)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Two-sample t-test on survey responses from 19 male and 14 female students (Table 2). The quiz-convenience question was significantly different between genders, with the printed means μm = 1.42 and μf = 1.64; the video-lecture question was also significantly different, while the MPL questions were not significantly different.

> "The response is mixed with a majority of the male have responded that the online quizzes are as convenient as written quizzes whereas some females disagreed."
