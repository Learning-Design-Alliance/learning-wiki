# In the Standard condition, gaze shifts away from text and fixations to extraneous details are negatively associated with reading comprehension

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Higher gaze shifts away from text (r(57) = -0.62) and higher fixations to extraneous details (r(57) = -0.42) were associated with lower comprehension scores in the Standard condition. [→ Eng 2020](#eng-2020)

## Evidence

### Eng 2020

Eng, C. M., Godwin, K. E., & Fisher, A. V. (2020). Keep it simple: streamlining book illustrations improves attention and comprehension in beginning readers. npj Science of Learning, 5:14. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-020-00073-5

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Correlational analysis within the main experiment's Standard condition relating eye-gaze patterns to comprehension scores; the printed effect sizes were r(57) = −0.62 for gaze shifts and r(57) = −0.42 for extraneous fixations, both negative and significant, shown in scatterplots (Fig. 4).

> "increased gaze shifts away from the text, r(57) = −0.62, 95% CI [−0.75, −0.43], p < 0.0005, and higher fixations to extraneous details r(57) = −0.42, 95% CI [−0.61, −0.18], p < 0.0005, were negatively associated with children's comprehension scores in the Standard condition"
