## Evidence

### Brezis Mayer 2017

Brezis Mayer, Lahat Yael, Frankel Meir, Rubinov Alan, Bohm Davina, Cohen Matan J, Koslowsky Meni, Shalomson Orit, Sprung Charles L, Perry-Mezare Henia, Yahalom Rina, Ziv Amitai. (2017). What can we learn from simulation-based training to improve skills for end-of-life care? Insights from a national project in Israel. Israel Journal of Health Policy Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13584-017-0169-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Quantitative content analysis using the Roter Interaction Analysis System (RIAS) of recorded simulations from the first 10 workshops (47 staff physicians, 33 residents, 40 nurses; 120 recorded 7-min sessions), coded by two trained coders with "Cronbach alpha 0.9". Table 1 reports overall mean cognitive utterances of 140 (SD 55) versus affective 13 (SD 11) per simulation, a 10% ratio; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Analysis of video-clips showed predominance of cognitive utterances - by an order of magnitude more prevalent than affective expressions, as shown in Table 1."
