Evidence¶

Albert Francis A. 2024¶

Albert Francis A., Seidu Abdul-Aziz, Mason Hannah M., Anderson Emma, Alele Faith O., Heggarty Paula, Hollins Aaron, Gupta Tarun Sen, Hays Richard B., McArthur Lawrie, Malau-Aduli Bunmi S. (2024). A systematic review of medical practitioners' retention and application of basic sciences to clinical practice. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05952-8

q3 · i? · review · r3

Review synthesis of cross-sectional predictor studies (Table 2 reports β = -0.20, p < 0.01 for age; β = -0.23, p < 0.001 for curriculum type in the physiology study); the quoted synthesis sentence prints no effect size.

"Age and curriculum type were predictors of physiological knowledge recall [39]. Scores showed an inverse relationship with age, highlighting that younger doctors had a better recall of knowledge compared to their older counterparts [39]."

Albert Francis A. 2024 (2)¶

Albert Francis A., Seidu Abdul-Aziz, Mason Hannah M., Anderson Emma, Alele Faith O., Heggarty Paula, Hollins Aaron, Gupta Tarun Sen, Hays Richard B., McArthur Lawrie, Malau-Aduli Bunmi S. (2024). A systematic review of medical practitioners' retention and application of basic sciences to clinical practice. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05952-8

q3 · i? · review · r3

Review synthesis across four disciplines; Table 2 prints retention interval β = −0.31, p < 0.01 for the doctors' study. The quoted sentence itself prints no effect size, so impact is null.

"Retention interval was inversely related to scores and predicted knowledge recall/ performance in four basic science disciplines (anatomy, physiology, biochemistry, and pharmacology)."
