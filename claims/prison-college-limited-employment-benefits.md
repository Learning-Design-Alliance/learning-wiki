# Prison higher education alone was reported as insufficient for post-release employment, with criminal-record and reentry barriers persisting

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A former student reported that education did not overcome societal barriers to employment for people reentering after incarceration. [→ Jillian Baranger 2018](#jillian-baranger-2018)
`q2 i?` Questionnaire responses on employment help were mixed: two students agreed higher education helped them gain employment post-release, one strongly disagreed, and one rated neutrally. [→ Jillian Baranger 2018 (2)](#jillian-baranger-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Jillian Baranger 2018

Jillian Baranger, Danielle Rousseau, Mary Ellen Mastrorilli, James Matesanz. (2018). Doing Time Wisely: The Social and Personal Benefits of Higher Education in Prison. The Prison Journal. https://doi.org/10.1177/0032885518776380

`q2 · i?`

Student interview on reentry limitations. Kristen cited barriers including criminal background, parole or probation, and lack of networks that education would not transcend, particularly for employment.

> "“I don’t think there’s any place in society for people reentering in general. I think that if you’re a welder you might have a better chance, you know?”"

### Jillian Baranger 2018 (2)

Jillian Baranger, Danielle Rousseau, Mary Ellen Mastrorilli, James Matesanz. (2018). Doing Time Wisely: The Social and Personal Benefits of Higher Education in Prison. The Prison Journal. https://doi.org/10.1177/0032885518776380

`q2 · i?`

Written Likert-scale questionnaire with the four former students. Responses on employment help were split: two agreed, one strongly disagreed, one neutral. No effect size is printed.

> "In terms of employment, two students agreed that participating in higher education in prison helped them gain employment post-release, while one student strongly disagreed. The fourth participant rated this question neutrally."
