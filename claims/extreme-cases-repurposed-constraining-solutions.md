### Mark Eichenlaub 2019

Mark Eichenlaub, Edward F. Redish. (2019). Blending physical knowledge with mathematical form in physics problem solving. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1007/978-3-030-04627-9_6](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-04627-9_6)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Bert, working the half-Atwood problem with a sign error, used extreme case reasoning (M ≫ m) not to evaluate his answer but to determine that "M must go in the denominator," placing structural constraints on the correct solution.

> "In suggesting that M must go in the denominator, Bert has repurposed the extreme cases game. Instead of evaluating potential solutions, he is placing constraints on what the unknown correct solution must look like. Like Myra, he blends his physical intuition and symbolic forms to achieve this."
