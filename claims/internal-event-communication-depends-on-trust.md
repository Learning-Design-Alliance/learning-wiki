# Communication about internal events depends on the student judging the teacher as helpful, and cannot be carried out by a predetermined curriculum

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Stanisław D. Głażek 2008

Stanisław D. Głażek. (2008). Heuristic model of teaching. https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.4796 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0804.4796](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0804.4796)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Model-based argument (Section IV.C): since no set of well-defined events exists for internal events, the teacher must hypothesize from the student's overt and verbal behavior, and cooperation is voluntary, hinging on the student's judgment of the teacher built over time. The article attributes these regularities to observed examples, not to a new experiment.

> "If S judges T as helpful, the dialog will proceed because S expects a beneﬁt. If T is judged as not helpful, S will not be motivated to cooperate."

### Stanisław D. Głażek 2008 (2)

Stanisław D. Głażek. (2008). Heuristic model of teaching. https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.4796 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0804.4796](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0804.4796)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical conclusion of the MOT (Section IV.C): because communication about internal events depends on unique individuals, their relationship, and unpredictable contexts, the article concludes teaching involves an art of communicating despite ambiguities, requiring extensive preparation and continuous training. This conclusion disagrees, the article notes, with most common educational practices.

> "it cannot be carried out in steps enforced by a predetermined curriculum according to a rigid schedule."
