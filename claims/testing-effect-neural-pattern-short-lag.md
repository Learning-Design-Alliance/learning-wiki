`q2 i?` The authors argue their retrieval-based subsequent-memory results, obtained with a 5-minute interval between tests, align with prior studies using one-day or one-week delays, suggesting the neural underpinnings are not modulated by lag. [→ Liu Xiaonan L. 2014](#liu-xiaonan-l-2014)

## Evidence

### Liu Xiaonan L. 2014

Liu Xiaonan L., Liang Peipeng, Li Kuncheng, Reder Lynne M. (2014). Brain Correlates of Testing Effect. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0092025

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion, comparing their 5-minute interval with the one-day and one-week delays used by Wing et al. and van den Broek et al., who reported the same regions (PFC, hippocampus, temporal gyrus) during successful retrieval on intermediate tests.

> "Our results, which are consistent with studies using longer intervals, suggest that the neural underpinnings of the testing effect are not modulated by the lag between intermediate and final tests."
