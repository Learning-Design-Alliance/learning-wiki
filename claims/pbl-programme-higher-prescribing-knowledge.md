# Students in an integrated PBL pharmacology/CPT programme had significantly higher prescribing knowledge scores than students in a traditional programme

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Final-year students who completed an integrated PBL programme had significantly higher overall prescribing knowledge scores (76% vs 67%) than students who completed a traditional discipline-based programme. [→ Brinkman David J. 2021](#brinkman-david-j-2021)
`q2 i?` PBL students scored significantly higher on 'mechanisms of action' and 'side-effects' subdomains, but not on 'interactions and contraindications'. [→ Brinkman David J. 2021 (2)](#brinkman-david-j-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Brinkman David J. 2021

Brinkman David J., Monteiro Teresa, Monteiro Emilia C., Richir Milan C., van Agtmael Michiel A., Tichelaar Jelle. (2021). Switching from a traditional undergraduate programme in (clinical) pharmacology and therapeutics to a problem-based learning programme. European Journal of Clinical Pharmacology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00228-020-03027-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Observational pre/post comparison of two cohorts (traditional n=36, PBL n=54) of final-year medical students completing a validated online 24-MCQ prescribing knowledge assessment before graduation. PBL students scored significantly higher, with "mean 76% (SD 9) vs 67% (SD 15); p = 0.002"; no effect size was printed.

> "Overall, students in the PBL programme had significantly higher knowledge scores than students in the traditional programme (mean 76% (SD 9) vs 67% (SD 15); p = 0.002)."

### Brinkman David J. 2021 (2)

Brinkman David J., Monteiro Teresa, Monteiro Emilia C., Richir Milan C., van Agtmael Michiel A., Tichelaar Jelle. (2021). Switching from a traditional undergraduate programme in (clinical) pharmacology and therapeutics to a problem-based learning programme. European Journal of Clinical Pharmacology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00228-020-03027-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Subdomain analysis in the same pre/post cohort comparison: PBL students scored higher on "'mechanisms of action' (90% (SD 14) vs 81% (SD 17); p = 0.006)" and on side-effects (90% vs 74%, p < 0.001); only p-values and means were printed, no effect size.

> "Also, students in the PBL programme had a significantly better knowledge of 'mechanisms of action' (90% (SD 14) vs 81% (SD 17); p = 0.006) and 'side-effects' (90% (SD 12) vs 74% (SD 20); p < 0.001)."
