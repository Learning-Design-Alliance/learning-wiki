# EBM-trained medical students rate all six self-reported EBM skills significantly better than untrained students, with differences emerging mainly after the second year

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Medical students who participated in an EBM course rated all six skills in searching and evaluating medical literature significantly better than students without EBM training. [→ Csertő Mónika 2019](#cserto-monika-2019)
`q2 i?` Among first- and second-year medical students the difference was limited: only critical appraisal of a scientific publication and identifying knowledge gaps differed significantly, while among third- to sixth-year students all six skills differed significantly. ## Evidence

### Csertő Mónika 2019

Csertő Mónika, Berényi Károly, Decsi Tamás, Lohner Szimonetta. (2019). Self-reported attitudes, knowledge and skills of using evidence-based medicine in daily health care practice: A national survey among students of medicine and health sciences in Hungary. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0225641

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional survey (n = 1080 medical students; 259 EBM-trained, 821 non-trained). Mann-Whitney comparisons of self-rated skill means, e.g. critical appraisal 2.79 vs 2.29 with p<0.001. The article reports "all the six items" differed significantly.

> "Medical students who participated in an EBM course rated all the six items of their skills in searching and evaluating medical literature significantly better than students who did not receive training in EBM (Table 2)."

### Csertő Mónika 2019 (2)

Csertő Mónika, Berényi Károly, Decsi Tamás, Lohner Szimonetta. (2019). Self-reported attitudes, knowledge and skills of using evidence-based medicine in daily health care practice: A national survey among students of medicine and health sciences in Hungary. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0225641

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Subgroup analysis of first- and second-year medical students in the same survey; only two of six skills differed significantly (p = 0.007 and p = 0.025). The article states that among third- and fourth-year and fifth- and sixth-year subgroups all six skills were significantly better in EBM course participants.

> "such students participating in an EBM course rated only their skills in critical appraisal of the content of a scientific publications (p = 0.007) and in identifying knowledge gaps (p = 0.025) significantly better as compared to student who have not yet participated in such a course."
