## Evidence

### García-Crespo 2021

García-Crespo, F. J., Fernández-Alonso, R., & Muñiz, J. (2021). Academic resilience in European countries: The role of teachers, families, and student profiles. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0253409

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel logistic regression of PIRLS 2016 data from 117,539 students in 23 EU samples; the reading confidence coefficient was statistically significant and positive in all countries (Table 7: 100.0%). The article reports students with "greater confidence in reading tended to be more likely to be resilient".

> "in every country, students with greater confidence in reading tended to be more likely to be resilient that those who did not."

### García-Crespo 2021 (2)

García-Crespo, F. J., Fernández-Alonso, R., & Muñiz, J. (2021). Academic resilience in European countries: The role of teachers, families, and student profiles. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0253409

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Across-country summary of the confidence index's impact: the article reports it "increased the probability of student resilience by between 62 and 130 percentage points" (lowest Belgium-Flemish, highest Austria and Poland). No standardized effect size is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "the reading self-confidence index, which increased the probability of student resilience by between 62 and 130 percentage points"
