# The positive practice–exam score relationship holds when accounting for education level, though no individual education level differs significantly from the A Levels baseline

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Practice remains significantly related to exam scores in an additive model with education level (standardized coefficient = 0.37; 0.37 SDs per SD of practice), while no education level shows a statistically significant change from the Completed A Levels baseline. ## Evidence

### Darren Butler 2025

Darren Butler, Conrad Borchers, Michael W. Asher, Yongmin Lee, Sonya Karnataki, Sameeksha Dangi, Samyukta Athreya, John Stamper, Amy Ogan, and Paulo F. Carvalho. 2025. Does the Doer Effect Exist Beyond WEIRD Populations? Toward Analytics in Radio and Phone-Based Learning. In LAK25: The 15th International Learning Analytics and Knowledge Conference (LAK 2025). ACM. https://doi.org/10.1145/3706468.3706505

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Additive linear regression on the same N = 234 learner dataset (Table 2), with education level as a proxy for prior knowledge. The printed standardized effect size for doing is 0.37 (CI95 [0.25, 0.49]); the model accounts for approximately 19.9% of variance in exam points (Adjusted R2 = 0.175), and education coefficients ranged from −0.62 (S1) to 0.19 (S5 or above), all non-significant.

> "The number of unique practice questions answered was significantly related to exam scores (𝐶𝐼 95% = 0.37,𝐶𝐼 95% [0.25, 0.49], p < 0.001), indicating a positive relationship where 1𝑆𝐷𝑠 more practice corresponds to 0.37𝑆𝐷𝑠 higher exam scores. No education levels show a statistically significant change in exam scores from the baseline of ‘Completed A Levels’ (𝑝′𝑠 > .05)."
