# Peak incisor force showed a significant seniority × scenario interaction: attending physicians applied more force than PGY in the normal airway and residents more than PGY in the rigid neck scenario

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` The seniority × scenario interaction on maximum incisor force was significant with a medium effect size (partial ηp2 = 0.13), with VS exceeding PGY in the normal airway and R exceeding PGY in the rigid neck scenario. [→ Yau Sze-Yuen 2021](#yau-sze-yuen-2021)

## Evidence

### Yau Sze-Yuen 2021

Yau Sze-Yuen, Chang Yu-Che, Wu Meng-Yu, Liao Shu-Chen. (2021). Does seniority always correlate with simulated intubation performance? Comparing endotracheal intubation performance across medical students, residents, and physicians using a high-fidelity simulator. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0257969

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Repeated-measures ANOVA of peak incisor force across 50 learners in four scenarios; the mean single-peak force was "52.7N (SD = 50.7N)". The seniority × scenario interaction was significant with "partial ηp2 = 0.13", while the seniority main effect was not.

> "The interaction between seniority and scenario was significant (F(6, 92) = 2.30, p < 0.05) with a medium effect size (partial ηp2 = 0.13, Fig 4), but not the main effect of seniority (F(3, 46) = 1.72, p = n.s.). VS (m = 42.9N, 95% CI 31.2–54.6) applied significantly more force on the incisor compared to PGY (m = 23.6N, 95% CI 16.3–30.9) in the normal airway, while R (m = 74.7N, 95% CI 62.0–87.4) applied significantly more force compared to PGY (m = 48.63N, 95% CI 35.9–61.3) in the rigid neck scenario (p < 0.05)."
