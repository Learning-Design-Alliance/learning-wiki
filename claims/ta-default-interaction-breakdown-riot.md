## Evidence

### Eric Matthew Hickok 2016

Eric Matthew Hickok. (2016). Teaching practices of graduate teaching assistants. San José State University Master's Project. Originally published 2015. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07740](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07740)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Observational analysis of over twenty hours of RIOT-coded TA-student interactions across four courses (Fig. 3.1), reported as totals rather than averages. The author found TAs "spent nearly 45% of the total time Not Interacting with their students." No effect sizes are reported.

> "Looking at the sum total of my observations, the TAs spent just under a third of the time talking at their students: either Clarifying Instruction or Explaining Content. Less than a fifth of the total time was spent talking with or observing their students. TAs spent nearly 45% of the total time Not Interacting with their students."

### Eric M. Hickok and Cassandra A. Paul 2025

Eric M. Hickok and Cassandra A. Paul. (2025). Examining teaching assistant pedagogies in traditional laboratories and recitations. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2509.09136](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2509.09136)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Summary analysis combining total times from the lab (467.6 min) and recitation (411.6 min) RIOT observations. The article reports "just over 34% of the time "talking at" their students", about 17% talking with or observing, and 45% not interacting, with comparable time spent with the whole class and small groups.

> "we find that the TAs spent just over 34% of the time "talking at" their students. About 17% of the total time was spent "talking with" or observing their students. TAs spent 45% of the total timeNot Interactingwith their students."
