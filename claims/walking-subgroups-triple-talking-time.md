## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors' arithmetic estimate is that a 60-min indoor seminar with nine students gives about 7 min talking per student, while a 60-min outdoor seminar with three sub-groups gives about 20 min talking — a tripling of the time talking. [→ Bälter O. 2018](#balter-o-2018)

## Evidence

### Bälter O. 2018

Bälter O., Hedin B., Tobiasson H., Toivanen S. (2018). Walking Outdoors during Seminars Improved Perceived Seminar Quality and Sense of Well-Being among Participants. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph15020303

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Analytical estimate in the Discussion section, not a measured result: the authors compute per-student talking time for a nine-student indoor seminar versus three outdoor sub-groups and call it "a tripling of the time talking". No effect size is printed.

> "A 60-min indoor seminar with nine students will result, on average, in 7 min talking and 53 min listening for the students. A 60-min outdoor seminar with three sub-groups will result in 20 min talking and 40 min listening. This would be a tripling of the time talking"
