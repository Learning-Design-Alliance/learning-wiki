## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Most students were unsure about steradians and angular intensity, ignored the angular intensity specification, and fell back on a P = IV model that produced a prediction about 500 times larger than the angular-intensity model. [→ Benjamin M. Zwickl 2014](#benjamin-m-zwickl-2014)

## Evidence

### Benjamin M. Zwickl 2014

Benjamin M. Zwickl, Dehui Hu, Noah Finkelstein, and H. J. Lewandowski. (2014). Model-Based Reasoning in the Upper-Division Physics Laboratory: Framework and Initial Results. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1410.0881](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1410.0881)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Think-aloud interviews in the LED optical power activity: despite an explanatory comment on the data sheet and prior exposure in a prerequisite lab, students were unsure what steradians meant; Student A needed a 10-minute discussion to estimate the solid angle, and Students B and C explicitly said they did not know the units or the polar intensity graph.

> "it caused most students to largely ignore key information in the data sheet, and construct significantly less accurate models of the optical output power (theP =IV model produced a prediction about 500 times larger than using angular intensity in the model)."
