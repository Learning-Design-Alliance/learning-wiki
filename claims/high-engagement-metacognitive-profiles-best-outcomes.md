## Evidence

### Yoana Omarchevska 2026

Yoana Omarchevska, Brendan A. Schuetze, Juliane Richter, Sascha Schanze, Nina Ulrich, Katharina Scheiter. (2026). From Passive to Metacognitive: The Role of Mode of Engagement in Enabling Lasting Learning Using Digital Media. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10120-z

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Post-hoc comparisons of model-estimated standardized latent knowledge scores with Bonferroni correction (α = .005) in the immediate-knowledge SEM; effect sizes comparable to Cohen's d on the latent scale, with "d = 0.87" and "d = 0.98". The Metacognitive-but-not-Constructive profile showed the same distances.

> "the High Overall Engagement immediate knowledge test was significantly higher than Disengaged (p < .005, d = 0.87) and Moderate Engagement (p < .005, d = 0.98)."

### Yoana Omarchevska 2026 (2)

Yoana Omarchevska, Brendan A. Schuetze, Juliane Richter, Sascha Schanze, Nina Ulrich, Katharina Scheiter. (2026). From Passive to Metacognitive: The Role of Mode of Engagement in Enabling Lasting Learning Using Digital Media. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10120-z

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Post-hoc comparisons on the delayed knowledge test with Bonferroni correction; the largest printed effects ("d = 1.11") distinguish the high performers from the Low Metacognition profile, whose students showed very low metacognitive engagement and focused on passive and active activities.

> "the High Overall Engagement was significantly higher performing than the Low Metacognition profile ( p = .003, d = 1.11) and Moderate Engagement profiles ( p = .002, d = 0.98)"

### Yoana Omarchevska 2026 (3)

Yoana Omarchevska, Brendan A. Schuetze, Juliane Richter, Sascha Schanze, Nina Ulrich, Katharina Scheiter. (2026). From Passive to Metacognitive: The Role of Mode of Engagement in Enabling Lasting Learning Using Digital Media. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10120-z

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In both immediate and delayed post-hoc comparisons the two top profiles did not differ significantly; the article interprets this via the doer effect literature, noting high amounts of engagement predict better learning. Equivalence was not formally tested.

> "the High Overall Engagement and Metacognitive, but not Constructive profiles were not significantly different from one-another."
