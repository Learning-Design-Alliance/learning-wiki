## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Hazlett 2025

Hazlett, Z. S., Jimenez, P. C., & Knight, J. K. (2025). Self-Testing and Follow-Through of Learning Strategies Supports Student Success. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.24-04-0128

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Kruskal–Wallis with Dunn's post-hoc test on quiz grades across report-accuracy and self-testing-completion categories (Figure 3) shows high-completion non-reporters significantly underperformed equally high-completing accurate reporters. The article also reports 81% of reports were accurate overall. No effect size is printed.

> "those who did not report self-testing but did >75% of the practice problems (darkest green) significantly underperformed those who accurately reported self-testing and completed the same amount of practice problems (darkest blue; p < 0.05; Kruskal–Wallis, post-hoc Dunn's test)."

### Hazlett 2025 (2)

Hazlett, Z. S., Jimenez, P. C., & Knight, J. K. (2025). Self-Testing and Follow-Through of Learning Strategies Supports Student Success. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.24-04-0128

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Within the non-reporting category in Figure 3, quiz grades did not differ significantly regardless of measured self-testing amount; the authors conjecture these categories include isolated high performers and that inaccurate reporters may use other strategies that could not be objectively measured.

> "However, among those who did not report self-testing (all green bars, Figure 3), there were no significant differences in quiz grade, regardless of the actual amount of self-testing."
