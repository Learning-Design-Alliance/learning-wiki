## Evidence

### Kevin L. Van De Bogart and MacKenzie R. Stetzer 2018

Kevin L. Van De Bogart and MacKenzie R. Stetzer. (2018). Investigating student understanding of bipolar junction transistor circuits. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1806.00404](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1806.00404)

`q3 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Transistor supply voltage modification task administered in the U1 physics electronics course over two years (N = 27); the incorrect 22% typically argued a reduced supply voltage meant less current through the resistors.

> "the majority of students (78%) correctly recognized that changing the collector voltage VCC would not alter the current through point W (i.e., the collector current) in part 1 of the task, and nearly all of these students (74% of total) supported their answer with correct reasoning."

### Kevin L. Van De Bogart and MacKenzie R. Stetzer 2018 (2)

Kevin L. Van De Bogart and MacKenzie R. Stetzer. (2018). Investigating student understanding of bipolar junction transistor circuits. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1806.00404](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1806.00404)

`q3 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Same task, part 2: increasing VEE reduces the potential difference across the 2-kΩ emitter resistor; 80% of these students supported their answers with correct reasoning per Table IV (89% correct, 70% correct reasoning).

> "For the second part of the task, nearly all (~90%) students recognized that increasing the emitter voltage would subsequently decrease the collector current."
