Evidence¶

Haycock 1998¶

Haycock, Kati. (1998). Good Teaching Matters: How Well-Qualified Teachers Can Close the Gap. Thinking K-16, v3 n2. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED457260

q2 · i? · review · r1

First-ever Dallas Independent School District study of teacher effects on assessment performance (Jordan, Mendro, and Weerasinghe, 1997), a longitudinal analysis of students assigned consecutive effective or ineffective teachers. The printed reading contrast is 59th-to-76th versus 60th-to-42nd percentile; no standardized effect size is printed.

"the average reading scores of a group of Dallas fourth graders who were assigned to three highly effective teachers in a row rose from the 59th percentile in fourth grade to the 76th percentile by the conclusion of sixth grade."

Haycock 1998 (2)¶

Haycock, Kati. (1998). Good Teaching Matters: How Well-Qualified Teachers Can Close the Gap. Thinking K-16, v3 n2. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED457260

q2 · i? · associational · r?

The mathematics portion of the same Dallas longitudinal teacher-effects study, comparing a group taught by three highly effective teachers with a slightly higher-achieving group taught by three of the least effective teachers, who fell to the 27th percentile. No standardized effect size is printed.

"a group of beginning third-graders in Dallas who averaged around the 55th percentile in mathematics scored around the 76th percentile at the end of fifth grade after being assigned to three highly effective teachers in a row."
