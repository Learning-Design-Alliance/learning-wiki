## Evidence

### Spencer 2019

Spencer, M., Wagner, R. K., & Petscher, Y. (2019). The Reading Comprehension and Vocabulary Knowledge of Children with Poor Reading Comprehension despite Adequate Decoding: Evidence from a Regression-Based Matching Approach. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000274

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Regression-based matching analysis (multilevel models) for the SCD-2540 subsample in the Results section. Controlling for SAT-10, the group effect was non-significant (0.11, p = .543), so vocabulary "was best characterized as developmentally delayed."

> "The addition of the grouping variable suggested that weak PPVT differences were observed between groups when controlling for SAT-10 (0.11, p = .543; Table 4). Thus, children’s vocabulary weakness was best characterized as developmentally delayed."

### Spencer 2019 (2)

Spencer, M., Wagner, R. K., & Petscher, Y. (2019). The Reading Comprehension and Vocabulary Knowledge of Children with Poor Reading Comprehension despite Adequate Decoding: Evidence from a Regression-Based Matching Approach. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000274

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Same regression-based matching analysis for the stringent SCD-540 subsample. The significant group effect (6.01, p < .001) explained about 0–1% of variance at student and district levels; the authors attribute the significance to the large sample size.

> "the grouping variable suggested that significant differences in PPVT scores were observed when controlling for SAT-10 (6.01, p < .001); however, the inclusion of this grouping variable did not translate into and understanding of individual differences explained according to the random effect reduction in variances"
