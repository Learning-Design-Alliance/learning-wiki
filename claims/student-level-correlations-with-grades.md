# Student level correlated weakly but significantly with AI and teacher attempt grades, but not with self-evaluations or teacher final grades

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Student level correlated positively with AI-assigned grades (Spearman's ρ = .08, p = .013, n = 918) and with teacher grades for Attempt I (ρ = .11, p < .001, n = 962) and Attempt II (ρ = .10, p = .001, n = 963). [→ Ning Yu 2025](#ning-yu-2025)
`q2 i?` No statistically significant correlations were found between student level and student self-evaluation scores or teacher final grades (p > .05). [→ Ning Yu 2025 (2)](#ning-yu-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Ning Yu 2025

Ning Yu, Jie Zhang, Sandeep Mitra, Rebecca Smith, Adam Rich. (2025). AI-Educational Development Loop (AI-EDL): A Conceptual Framework to Bridge AI Capabilities with Classical Educational Theories. State University of New York (SUNY) Brockport. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2508.00970](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2508.00970)

`q2 · i1` · `design · r2`

Spearman rank-order correlation analyses in the pilot, with both AI and teacher blinded to student names or academic status. Graduate students were slightly more likely to receive higher grades; the AI-grade correlation was ρ = .08 (p = .013, n = 918).

> "a statistically significant positive correlation was found between student level and teacher-assigned grades for student Attempt I submission (Spearman's ρ = .11, p < .001, n = 962), as well as for student Attempt II resubmission (Spearman's ρ = .10, p = .001, n = 963)."

### Ning Yu 2025 (2)

Ning Yu, Jie Zhang, Sandeep Mitra, Rebecca Smith, Adam Rich. (2025). AI-Educational Development Loop (AI-EDL): A Conceptual Framework to Bridge AI Capabilities with Classical Educational Theories. State University of New York (SUNY) Brockport. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2508.00970](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2508.00970)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Null correlational result from the same Spearman analyses (Table 5: self-evaluation ρ = 0.13, p = .281, n = 74; teacher final grade ρ = 0.10, p = .307, n = 114); no significant effect was detected.

> "no statistically significant correlations were found between student level and either student self-evaluation scores or teacher final grades (p > .05), suggesting that overall performance evaluation by students or the teacher was not influenced by student academic level."
