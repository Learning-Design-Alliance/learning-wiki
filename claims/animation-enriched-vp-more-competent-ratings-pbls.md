## Evidence

### Lehmann Ronny 2019

Lehmann Ronny, Lutz Thomas, Helling-Bakki Astrid, Kummer Sebastian, Huwendiek Sören, Bosse Hans Martin. (2019). Animation and interactivity facilitate acquisition of pediatric life support skills: a randomized controlled trial using virtual patients versus video instruction. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1442-5

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Primary outcome: two group-blinded independent pediatricians gave overall competency ratings of videotaped PBLS demonstrations; ratings of 'competent' in consent by both raters were analyzed. "The overall competency rating of the three study groups differed highly significant" (χ2 = 14.366, p = 0.001); percentages were 65.7% (VPanim), 43.2% (Vid), 19.4% (VPstat). No effect size is printed for this comparison.

> "The overall competency rating of the three study groups differed highly significant with χ2 = 14.366, p = 0.001 (Table 4). The VP group using animated media (group VPanim) received distinctly more ‘competent’ ratings than the video group (group Vid), which on her part performed better than the VP group using only static media (group VPstat)."
