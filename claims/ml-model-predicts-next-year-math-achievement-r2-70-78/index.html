Evidence¶

Lavelle-Hill 2024¶

Lavelle-Hill, R., Frenzel, A. C., Goetz, T., Lichtenfeld, S., Marsh, H. W., Pekrun, R., Sakaki, M., Smith, G., & Murayama, K. (2024). How the Predictors of Math Achievement Change Over Time: A Longitudinal Machine Learning Approach. Journal of Educational Psychology, 116(8), 1383–1403. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000863

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Next-year prediction analysis of PALMA data: Elastic Net and Random Forest models trained on 80% of each data set with five-fold cross-validation and evaluated on a 20% hold-out test set. The best model's out-of-sample prediction R-squared ranged from .70 to .78 across the four transitions.

"We found that the best machine learning model could predict future achievement scores well, with the prediction R-squared values: Year 5 → 6 = .70 (mean absolute error [MAE]9 = 34.99), Year 6 → 7 = .73 (MAE = 34.44), Year 7 → 8 = .78 (MAE = 32.74), and Year 8 → 9 = .77 (MAE = 35.97)."
