## Evidence

### Ishii Kaori 2020

Ishii Kaori, Aoyagi Kenryu, Shibata Ai, Javad Koohsari Mohammad, Carver Alison, Oka Koichiro. (2020). Joint Associations of Leisure Screen Time and Physical Activity with Academic Performance in a Sample of Japanese Children. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17030757

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Sex-stratified logistic regression analyses from the same longitudinal study, adjusted for grade and degree of obesity. Boys in the low screen time/high physical activity group had an odds ratio of OR = 4.12 (1.19–14.25, p < 0.05), i.e., "4.12 (1.19–14.25, p < 0.05) times greater odds"; girls showed no significant associations.

> "Among boys, compared to the reference group, participants in the low screen time/high physical activity group had 4.12 (1.19–14.25, p < 0.05) times greater odds of having high academic performance. No significant associations were found among girls."
