# In the classical ball-drop context, advanced students' expectations for what 100 more data points would do to the histogram spread are almost evenly split among more narrow, more accurate, and no change, with a few expecting a wider distribution

`q2 i?` In the classical context, student responses to the more-data question were almost evenly distributed between more narrow, more accurate, and no-change codes, with some participants expecting a wider distribution. [→ White 2020](#white-2020)

## Evidence

### White 2020

White, C. L., Stump, E. M., Holmes, N. G., & Passante, G. (2020). Student evaluation of more or better experimental data in classical and quantum mechanics. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.white](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.white)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Results of the classical ball-drop interviews (N = 19), coded into four categories. Responses were "almost evenly distributed between the more narrow, more accurate, and no change codes", and some participants indicated "that the distribution will become wider".

> "In response to more data, we see that participant responses are almost evenly distributed between the more narrow, more accurate, and no change codes. We even see some participants indicating that the distribution will become wider."
