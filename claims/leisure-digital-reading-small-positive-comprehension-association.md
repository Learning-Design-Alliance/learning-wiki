# Leisure digital reading habits show a small positive association with reading comprehension (r = .055), much smaller than for print reading habits

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across 39 effect sizes from 26 studies, the overall association between leisure digital reading habits and reading comprehension is small, positive, and statistically significant (r = .055). [→ Altamura 2023](#altamura-2023)
`q2 i?` The association is far smaller than the medium-to-strong associations reported for print reading habits in prior meta-analytic work. [→ Altamura 2023](#altamura-2023)

## Evidence

### Altamura 2023

Altamura, L., Vargas, C., & Salmerón, L. (2023). Do New Forms of Reading Pay Off? A Meta-Analysis on the Relationship Between Leisure Digital Reading Habits and Text Comprehension. *Review of Educational Research, 95*(1), 53–88. [doi:10.3102/00346543231216463](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543231216463)

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Three-level random-effects meta-analysis of 39 effect sizes from 26 studies (469,564 participants), excluding one influential outlier. The pooled correlation was "significant and positive, although the effect was rather small (r = .055)" with a 95% CI of .003 to .107. Heterogeneity was very large (Q(38) = 2591.93, p < .001; I2 = 98.9).

> "The overall association between leisure digital reading habits and reading comprehension was significant and positive, although the effect was rather small (r = .055; 95% CI: .003, .107; p = .03; k = 39, n = 25)."
