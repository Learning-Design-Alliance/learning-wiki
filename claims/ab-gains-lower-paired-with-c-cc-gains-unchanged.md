Descriptive analysis of the nine-pair gain matrix (Table 4b) for the 74 IGI-treatment students divided into thirds by initial CSEM score. The article reports the asymmetry (e.g., A-with-C gain 0.29 vs C-with-A 0.41) without inferential statistics.

> "It is striking that the gain of A and B students is signiﬁcantly lower when they paired with C students. ‘A’ students have similar gains whether they paired with A or B students while B students beneﬁt more from pair- ing with A than with another B student. Interestingly, the gain of C students (low- est third) is virtually the same regardless of who they paired with"
