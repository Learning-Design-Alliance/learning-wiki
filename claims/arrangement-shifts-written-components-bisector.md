## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students were more likely to explain their reasoning with the components method on aligned-arrangement questions and about equally likely to use components or bisector methods on divergent-arrangement questions, significant (p < .05) only for the no-grid pair. ## Evidence

### Jeffrey M. Hawkins 2009

Jeffrey M. Hawkins, John R. Thompson, Michael C. Wittmann, Eleanor C. Sayre, Brian W. Frank. (2009). Students’ Responses To Different Representations Of A Vector Addition Question. 2009 Physics Education Research Conference Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3266745

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of written explanations from the same UMaine algebra-based administration, coded into the same method categories as drawings. The article notes "This difference is not one that we had predicted" and calls for further work on why written and drawn explanations differ.

> "There was also a significant difference (Fisher’s exact test, p < .05) between the use of the components and bisector methods in students’ written explanations [Fig. 3] between questions with aligned and divergent arrangements. This difference in method use was only significant for the pair of questions without a grid."
