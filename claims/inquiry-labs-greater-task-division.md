# Inquiry labs showed substantially greater task division within groups than traditional labs, with comparable division across group gender compositions

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Intragroup variances were much larger in inquiry lab groups than traditional lab groups for all codes besides Paper, indicating greater task division. [→ Katherine N. Quinn 2020](#katherine-n-quinn-2020)
`q2 i?` Within inquiry labs, intragroup variances were comparable across single- versus mixed-gender group compositions, indicating similar degrees of task division. [→ Katherine N. Quinn 2020 (2)](#katherine-n-quinn-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Katherine N. Quinn 2020

Katherine N. Quinn, Michelle M. Kelley, Kathryn L. McGill, Emily M. Smith, Zachary Whipps, and N.G. Holmes. (2020). Group roles in unstructured labs show inequitable gender divide. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010129](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010129)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Intragroup deviation analysis (Appendix A) comparing each student profile to their group's average profile. Levene's test confirmed the variance disparity, with none of the p-values for each code exceeding 10⁻⁵.

> "the intragroup variances are remarkably larger in the inquiry lab groups than in the traditional lab groups for all codes besides paper, indicating a greater range of behaviors and an increase in task division"

### Katherine N. Quinn 2020 (2)

Katherine N. Quinn, Michelle M. Kelley, Kathryn L. McGill, Emily M. Smith, Zachary Whipps, and N.G. Holmes. (2020). Group roles in unstructured labs show inequitable gender divide. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010129](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010129)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same intragroup variance analysis within inquiry labs, comparing single- versus mixed-gender group compositions via Levene's test. No significant difference in degree of task division by composition was found.

> "We found comparable intragroup variances regardless of the group's composition for all behavior codes (every code's p-value from Levene's test exceeding the p = 0.01 cutoff, with p-values ranging from p = 0.03− 0.9)"
