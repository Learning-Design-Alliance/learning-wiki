## Evidence

### Edwards Daniel 2013

Edwards Daniel, Friedman Tim, Pearce Jacob. (2013). Same admissions tools, different outcomes: a critical perspective on predictive validity in three undergraduate medical schools. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-173

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Squared semi-partial regression coefficients displayed in Figure 2 show relative unique contributions per institution and year level. "each of the admissions tools used contributes to explaining the variance in outcomes independent of the influence of other tools".

> "Importantly, this figure shows that each of the admissions tools used contributes to explaining the variance in outcomes independent of the influence of other tools. Essentially, this shows incremental validity."

### Edwards Daniel 2013 (2)

Edwards Daniel, Friedman Tim, Pearce Jacob. (2013). Same admissions tools, different outcomes: a critical perspective on predictive validity in three undergraduate medical schools. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-173

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression models (Table 3) showed combined R2 ranging from 3 per cent (Institution C Year 1) to 36 per cent (Institution A Year 2), with the article itself noting this is "a relatively small proportion" of performance variance.

> "It should be noted however, that the three variables were only able to explain between 3 and 36 per cent of variance in GPA, indicating that these admissions variables only explain a relatively small proportion on how students will perform in their studies."
