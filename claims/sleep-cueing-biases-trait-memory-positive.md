## Subclaims
`q3 i3` A significant three-way interaction of time, valence, and face type on trait memory (F(1, 53) = 15.00, η²p = 0.22), with post-sleep simple effects showing more positive (d = 0.68) and fewer negative (d = −0.36) memories for the cued than uncued name. [→ Zhao 2025](#zhao-2025)

## Evidence

### Zhao 2025

Zhao, H., Lin, X., Yuan, K., Hu, X., Wang, X., Su, W., Wang, Q., & Lu, L. (2025). Updating social evaluation during sleep. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00356-9

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures 2×2×2 ANOVA on memory bias (face type × valence × time) in the nap experiment. A "significant three-way interaction effect among time, valence, and face type" (F(1, 53) = 15.00, p < 0.001, η²p = 0.22) was followed by simple effects showing post-sleep more positive memories for the cued name (d = 0.68) and fewer negative (d = −0.36).

> "we detected a signiﬁcant three-way interaction effect among time, valence, and face type, F(1, 53) = 15.00, p < 0.001, η²p =0 . 2 2"

### Zhao 2025 (2)

Zhao, H., Lin, X., Yuan, K., Hu, X., Wang, X., Su, W., Wang, Q., & Lu, L. (2025). Updating social evaluation during sleep. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00356-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Awareness check in the same nap experiment: participants reported zero recollections of sleep-played traits, which the authors take to show "the cues were processed unconsciously without entering conscious awareness". Note the sleep-played traits differed from the pre-sleep learned traits that were tested.

> "none of the participants recalled any of the positive traits presented during sleep, as indicated by zero reports of the traits played during sleep in the memory test"
