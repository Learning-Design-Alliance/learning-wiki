# T1 dropout was largely non-selective: gender, age and patient experience were not significantly associated with dropout, though T1 participants had slightly higher T0 self-assessments on three items

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Dropout analysis found no significant differences by gender (Chi²=0.81, p=.368), age (F(1.257)=0.02, p=.899) or patient experience (Chi²=1.85, p=.174), but three of seven T0 competency items showed higher values among T1 participants (differences of 0.3 to 0.4 scale points). ## Evidence

### von Lengerke Thomas 2011

von Lengerke Thomas, Kursch Angelika, Lange Karin, APG-Lehrteam MHH. (2011). The communication skills course for second year medical students at Hannover Medical School: An evaluation study based on students' self-assessments. GMS Zeitschrift für Medizinische Ausbildung 28(4), Doc54. [doi:10.3205/zma000766](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma000766)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Dropout analysis comparing the 180 T1-participants with T0-only respondents on gender, age, patient experience, learning needs and T0 competencies; the three significant competency comparisons "showed higher values in the T1-participants". One significant learning-needs difference emerged (strengths/weaknesses item, M=4.1 vs 3.8, F(1.260)=3.99, p=.047).

> "Although all three significant (of seven total) comparisons in T0-competencies between T1-participants and non-participants showed higher values in the T1-participants, the level of these differences (0.3 to 0.4 scale points) is not likely an indication for strong “expert knowledge” on the side of the T1-participants."
