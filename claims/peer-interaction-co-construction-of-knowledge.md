# Peer collaboration can produce co-construction of knowledge: pairs answered correctly questions neither member could answer individually, with 29% of responses moving from both-incorrect individually to correct as a pair

## Evidence

### Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Improving Students Understanding of Quantum Measurement Part 2: Development of Research-based Learning Tools. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06926](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06926)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Prior study cited as the authors' own research: introductory physics students first answered CSEM questions individually after traditional instruction, then discussed and re-answered in pairs; 29% of responses showed co-construction. Reported here as background motivating the concept-test design.

> "The fraction of responses on each question that went from both incorrect individually to the correct response from the pair is 29% and shows evidence for co-construction."
