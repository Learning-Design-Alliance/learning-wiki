# Training-time ratio moderates the competency-based simulation advantage: large effects when competency training takes less time, non-significant when it takes more

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` In a post hoc subgroup analysis, studies where the competency-based group trained for less time showed a large pooled effect (SMD = 1.00), while equal-time studies showed a moderate non-significant effect (SMD = 0.67, P = 0.07) and longer-time studies a small non-significant one (SMD = 0.18, P = 0.68). ### Vermylen JH 2026

Vermylen JH, Cohen ER, Cook DA, McGaghie WC, Issenberg SB, Arnold J, Ballard H, Bayoumi M, Beestrum M, Bremner R, Crawford S, Einstein N, Mannarino C, Misra A, Tomita TM, Waldron H, Yanko F, Kessler DO. `q2 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Post hoc subgroup analysis of the 12 competency-versus-noncompetency studies, grouping by the ratio of competency-arm to noncompetency-arm training time. Equal-time studies (2 studies) gave SMD = 0.67 (P = 0.07); longer-time studies (4 studies) gave SMD = 0.18 (P = 0.68).

> "For the 6 studies with a time ratio <1 (shorter time for competency-based simulation), the pooled SMD was 1.00 (95% CI = 0.53 to 1.7, P < 0.001) indicating a large effect favoring the competency-based group"
