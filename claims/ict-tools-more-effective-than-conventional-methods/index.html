J. Durga Prasad Rao & Raksha Singh 2011¶

J. Durga Prasad Rao & Raksha Singh. (2011). Measuring effectiveness of Information, Communication and Technology (ICT) tools in teaching school children: A case study from Chattisgarh state, India. i-manager's Journal of School Educational Technology, 6(3). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1102746

Authors' findings-and-conclusion section following the ANOVA results; it states DLP use is "as effective as" Computer/laptop use and that ICT tools were more effective than Chart and Chalk Talk (Figure 1).

"It is further clarified that the use of DLP in teaching school pupils is as effective as when the teacher uses the Computer/laptop in teaching. The use of ICT tools is more effective as compared to Chart and Chalk Talk method"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Pupils taught with DLP achieved slightly higher average gain scores than pupils taught with Computer/laptop, and both ICT groups outperformed Chart and Chalk Talk groups — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Multimedia application is more effective than conventional media for sixth-semester students' listening comprehension, per a t-test comparison — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Secondary students taught in an e-classroom with ICT resources significantly outperform students taught by conventional methods on post-test achievement — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Adoption of the four teaching methods depends on teachers' technical competence, and the DLP option was judged most feasible for Chhattisgarh schools facing low teacher motivation and competence — related
  • Mobile learning applications improve undergraduate academic achievement compared with traditional lecture-based instruction — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Undergraduate students taught IUPAC nomenclature of organic chemistry through an e-content package scored significantly higher on the post-test than students taught by conventional methods — a broader claim this one bears on
