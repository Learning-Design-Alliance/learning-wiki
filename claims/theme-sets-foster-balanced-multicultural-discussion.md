# Multimodal theme-sets foster more balanced, multicultural small-group and whole-class discussion

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Discussions in the theme-set course were more focused and engaging, with L2 students contributing more willingly, while control discussions were punctuated by silence. [→ Ruefman 2015](#ruefman-2015)

## Evidence

### Ruefman 2015

Ruefman, D. (2015). Bridging the Gaps: Multimodal Theme-Sets in the Global Composition Classroom. The CATESOL Journal, 27(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1111750

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Instructor observations of semistructured small-group and whole-class discussions in both courses. In the control group "most of the conversation was coming from the instructor and native-speaking students," whereas theme-set course contributions appeared more balanced.

> "Conversations appeared to end quickly, and when transitioning to the whole-class discussion, most of the conversation was coming from the instructor and native-speaking students."
