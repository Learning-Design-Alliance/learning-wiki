## Evidence

### Farnaz S 2020

Farnaz S, Zoghoul S, Alahmad M, Al Qudah H. (2020). The influence of gender on clinical examination skills of medical students in Jordan: a cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02002-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey item on perceived supervisor support completed by 188 Jordanian final-year medical students. The results state "More than half of male students (51%) felt they were rarely or never supported to conduct intimate clinical examinations by their supervisors compared to 22% of females"; the authors attribute this to tutors selecting female students more often.

> "More than half of male students (51%) felt they were rarely or never supported to conduct intimate clinical examinations by their supervisors compared to 22% of females"

### Farnaz S 2020 (2)

Farnaz S, Zoghoul S, Alahmad M, Al Qudah H. (2020). The influence of gender on clinical examination skills of medical students in Jordan: a cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02002-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Three-point Likert self-efficacy ratings in the same 188-student survey. The results note "There was no notable difference between male and female students" for general examinations, while for intimate examinations more students reported confidence than had performed the exam more than five times.

> "We asked students if they are confident doing the specified clinical examinations on their own without supervision (Table 5). There was no notable difference between male and female students"
