# Post-error behavioural adjustments can occur automatically, dissociated from explicit error detection

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Skilled typists slowed after real errors that were corrected by false feedback and did not slow after inserted errors, while accepting responsibility for errors they did not make. [→ Frith Chris D 2012](#frith-chris-d-2012)

## Evidence

### Frith Chris D 2012

Frith Chris D. (2012). The role of metacognition in human social interactions. Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B: Biological Sciences. https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2012.0123

`q2 · i?`

A study of skilled typists (Logan & Crump, as reviewed here) in which experimenters supplied false visual feedback by correcting some errors and inserting others. The review reports a dissociation between automatic post-error slowing driven by real errors and explicit awareness, since typists accepted responsibility for inserted errors.

> "The typists slowed down after corrected errors and did not slow down after inserted errors, showing that this outcome of self-monitoring was driven by real errors and was not affected by the false feedback."
