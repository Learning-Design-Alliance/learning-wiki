# Students taught with modeling instruction within the inverted curriculum scored highest on the ACT science subscore (M=24.3) compared with the inverted-curriculum-alone (M=23.7) and traditional (M=23.1) contexts

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` The modeling instruction within inverted curriculum group outscored the traditional baseline group on ACT science by 1.2 points, an effect size of Cohen's d = 0.29. [→ Dye 2013](#dye-2013)
`q2 i?` The inverted curriculum alone group scored between the traditional and modeling groups (M=23.7). [→ Dye 2013](#dye-2013)

## Evidence

### Dye 2013

Dye, J., Cheatham, T., Rowell, G. H., Barlow, A. T., & Carlton, R. (2013). The Impact of Modeling Instruction within the Inverted Curriculum on Student Achievement in Science. Electronic Journal of Science Education, 17(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1188400

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r1`

Retrospective comparison of ACT science subscores for 808 students in eight cohorts at one private high school (402 traditional, 214 inverted, 192 modeling within inverted). The quote reports the ordered group means; the largest pairwise difference (Treatment 2 vs. Traditional) yielded "an effect size of 0.29".

> "On average, the traditional instructional context group scored the lowest ( M=23.1), followed by the students with the inverted curriculum alone (M=23.7), and finally the students who experienced modeling instruction within the inverted curriculum scored the highest ( M=24.3)."
