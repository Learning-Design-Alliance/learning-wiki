## Evidence

### Tsikas Stefanos A. 2024

Tsikas Stefanos A., Dauer Karina. (2024). Examining interviewer bias in medical school admissions: The interplay between applicant and interviewer gender and its effects on interview outcomes. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0309293

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Regression model with an interaction between the voluntary-service indicator and the committee-type-by-gender interaction, plotted in Fig 3 panel (a) for female candidates, adjusted for Abitur grade, waiting time, and interview year. Female candidates without service received approximately 11 points across committee types.

> "In MM and MF committees, female candidates with voluntary service achieved slightly above 12 points, a statistically significant difference. Within FF groups, this difference is nearly nonexistent."

### Tsikas Stefanos A. 2024 (2)

Tsikas Stefanos A., Dauer Karina. (2024). Examining interviewer bias in medical school admissions: The interplay between applicant and interviewer gender and its effects on interview outcomes. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0309293

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Fig 3 panel (b) shows predicted interview grades for male candidates by voluntary-service status and committee type from the same interaction regression. The FF pattern for males was not statistically significant due to wide confidence intervals from few interviews.

> "In contrast, FF committees granted males with a voluntary service about one point more than CM without service and almost 1.5 points more than females with voluntary service. The wide confidence intervals, however, render this finding statistically insignificant."
