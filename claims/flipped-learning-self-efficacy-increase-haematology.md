## Evidence

### Algarni Abdulrahman Abdulrahman 2024

Algarni Abdulrahman Abdulrahman. (2024). Biomedical students’ self-efficacy and academic performance by gender in a flipped learning haematology course. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05421-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A quasi-experimental pre/post study of 86 third-year medical students (41 flipped, 45 control) measured self-efficacy before and after a 10-week course. The mean self-efficacy increase was 4.22 in the intervention group (t40 = 7.59, p <.05) versus 0.18 in the control group (t44 = 0.34, p =.74); the "significant difference (t84 = 5.27, p <.05)" between groups. No effect size was printed.

> "An independent-samples t test of the difference between the mean increase in self-efficacy of the intervention group and the mean increase in self-efficacy of the control group found a significant difference (t84 = 5.27, p <.05)."
