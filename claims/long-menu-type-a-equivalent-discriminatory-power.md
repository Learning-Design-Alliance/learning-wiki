## Subclaims
`q3 i0` There was no significant difference in discriminatory power (point biserial correlation) between long-menu and Type A versions of the same questions, with a printed effect size of 0.03. [→ Cerutti Bernard 2019](#cerutti-bernard-2019)

## Evidence

### Cerutti Bernard 2019

Cerutti Bernard, Stollar Fabiola, Escher Monica, Blondon Katherine, Aujesky Susanne, Nendaz Mathieu, Galetto-Lacour Annick. (2019). Comparison of long-menu and single-best-answer multiple choice questions in computer-based summative assessments: a randomised controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1651-6

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Randomised sequential trial in real summative exams at the University of Geneva, stopped for futility after 88 experimental items from 22 exams. The Table 1 means were 0.220 (long-menu) vs 0.210 (Type A), with "effect size 0.03" and p = 0.7948, i.e. a negligible printed effect size.

> "we concluded that there was no significant difference regarding the discriminatory powers of the type A and long-menu formats"
