# Participants whose goals matched EBM content rated course relevance higher (80% vs 65%), and relevance was only weakly correlated with teaching-quality ratings

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Berger B 2010

Berger B, Steckelberg A, Meyer G, Kasper J, Mühlhauser I. (2010). Training of patient and consumer representatives in the basic competencies of evidence-based medicine: a feasibility study. BMC Medical Education 10:16. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-10-16

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Acceptability assessment using visual analogue scales per module, with learning goals elicited by telephone interviews before each course. Participants with goals such as "critical appraisal skills" (n = 130) rated relevance higher than those with "networking" (n = 25) or "empowerment" (n = 18) goals [65% (SD 2)].

> "rated the relevance of the whole course for their personal learning goals on average at 80% (SD 4) on a visual analogue scale ranging from 0 to 100%."

### Berger B 2010 (2)

Berger B, Steckelberg A, Meyer G, Kasper J, Mühlhauser I. (2010). Training of patient and consumer representatives in the basic competencies of evidence-based medicine: a feasibility study. BMC Medical Education 10:16. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-10-16

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis across 123 participants relating relevance ratings to teaching-quality and content evaluations. The article interprets this as relevance judgements being largely independent of teaching performance, with about 17% of variance attributed to didactics and content.

> "There was only a weak correlation between relevance for personal learning goals and subjective evaluation of teaching quality or content of the course modules (r = 0.41, n = 123)."
