# A test that measures one attribute is not automatically valid for a perfectly correlated different attribute

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The article argues that even perfect correlation between two attributes does not make a test valid for both, because validity requires the measured attribute to causally produce the score variation. [→ Borsboom et al. 2004](#borsboom-et-al-2004)

## Evidence

### Borsboom et al. 2004

Denny Borsboom, Gideon J. Mellenbergh, and Jaap van Heerden. (2004). The Concept of Validity. Psychological Review. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.111.4.1061

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument in the Causality Versus Correlation section. Thunder and lightning correlate perfectly because both result from an electrical discharge, yet variations in lightning play no causal role in producing variations in thunder. The support is a conceptual counterexample, not data.

> "When one is validly measuring the presence of thunder, one is not validly measuring the presence of lightning for the simple reason that one is not measuring the presence of lightning at all."
