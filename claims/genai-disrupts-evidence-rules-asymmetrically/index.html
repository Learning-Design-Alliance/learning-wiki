Evidence¶

Gao 2026¶

Gao, Y., Chen, Z., Li, M., & Zhai, X. (2026). Generative AI as a Design Variable: An Evidence-Centered Framework for Principled Governance in STEM Assessment. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24837

q1 · i? · theoretical · r3

Theoretical analysis within the paper's ECD framework, not an empirical study. The authors argue that when GenAI contributes to products, "it becomes unclear whether they reflect student competence or AI capability," so correctness scores are no longer interpretable as indicators of the targeted construct.

"GenAI complicates the evidence model by undermining evidence rules—the procedures that map observed student work products to latent constructs—because when GenAI contributes to those products, it becomes unclear whether they reflect student competence or AI capability."

Gao 2026 (2)¶

Gao, Y., Chen, Z., Li, M., & Zhai, X. (2026). Generative AI as a Design Variable: An Evidence-Centered Framework for Principled Governance in STEM Assessment. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24837

q1 · i? · theoretical · r3

Authors' analytical claim about the dependence of GenAI substitution on task cognitive demands, supported by cited prior findings (e.g., ChatGPT narrowly passing an introductory physics course per Kortemeyer, 2023) rather than by new data in this paper.

"for tasks requiring factual recall or standard algorithmic problem solving, GenAI can substitute for student cognition almost completely; for tasks requiring disciplinary judgement applied to novel or ambiguous problems, its substitution capacity is more limited"
