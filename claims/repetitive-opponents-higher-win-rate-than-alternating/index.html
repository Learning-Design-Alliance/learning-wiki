Evidence¶

Zhang 2026¶

Zhang, Y., & Dyson, B. J. (2026). Recover or discover: How outcome feedback shapes response patterns within game spaces. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00414-w

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

Paired t-test pooling Pre phase data across all three experiments (n = 218 analysed): win rates against repetitive opponents exceeded those against alternating opponents, "Cohen's d = 0.391". Behaviour types were otherwise collapsed in the analyses.

"A paired t-test on Pre phase across all experiments suggested that playing against repetitive opponents enabled a higher win rate (67.86%) than against alternating opponents (61.53%; t[217] = 5.778, p < .001, Cohen's d = 0.391)."

Related Claims¶

  • Calmness against unexploitable opponents rises when they are played in the context of exploitable, but not exploiting, opponents
  • Win-calmness appears only against exploitable opponents; exploiting opponents instead reproduce the reversed pattern
  • Only against exploitable opponents does individual win rate correlate with win-calmness (positively) and loss-restlessness (negatively)
  • Loss-restlessness is not observed even in structured environments, though restlessness is generally higher against exploiting opponents
  • Against unexploitable opponents, neither win-calmness nor loss-restlessness appears; calmness is instead lower after wins and restlessness lower after losses
  • In win maximization, performance is more consistent following wins than losses in both repetition- and alternation-exploitable opponents — related
  • Students with higher desire to win strategically selected lower-rated opponents for an easy win — related
