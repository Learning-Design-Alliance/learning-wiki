Evidence¶

Glazerman 2010¶

Glazerman, S., E. Isenberg, S. Dolfin, M. Bleeker, A. Johnson, M. Grider, and M. Jacobus. (2010). Impacts of Comprehensive Teacher Induction: Final Results From a Randomized Controlled Study (NCEE 2010-4027). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee

q4 · i? · causal · r3

Teacher mobility surveys and administrative data tracked retention for four years after random assignment in summer 2005. The report finds "no impact on teacher retention" in the original school, district, or profession for either treatment duration.

"There was no impact on teacher retention over the first four years of the teachers’ careers. This was true of retention in the original school, the original school district, and the teaching profession."
