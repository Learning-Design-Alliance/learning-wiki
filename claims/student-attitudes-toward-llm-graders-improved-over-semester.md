## Evidence

### Pavlin G. Poliˇcar 2025

Pavlin G. Poliˇcar, Martin Špendl, Tomaž Curk, Blaž Zupan. (2025). Automated Assignment Grading with Large Language Models: Insights from a Bioinformatics Course. [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btaf196](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaf196)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

End-of-semester anonymous survey of 42 students using five-point Likert items after a session presenting preliminary findings: pre-course attitude averaged 3.2, and 'after completing the course, students were much more open to LLM graders.'

> "Student responses were mixed, with an average score of 3.2. Encouragingly, after completing the course, students were much more open to LLM graders, with the average score increasing to over 4.0."

### Pavlin G. Poliˇcar 2025 (2)

Pavlin G. Poliˇcar, Martin Špendl, Tomaž Curk, Blaž Zupan. (2025). Automated Assignment Grading with Large Language Models: Insights from a Bioinformatics Course. [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btaf196](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaf196)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same end-of-semester survey: 'students strongly felt it would be unacceptable not to have the option to request a manual review.' Over 92% of students also reported using LLM-enabled tools while working on the assignments.

> "Consistent with prior research [14], students strongly felt it would be unacceptable not to have the option to request a manual review."
