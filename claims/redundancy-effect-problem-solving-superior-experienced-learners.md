## Evidence

### Kalyuga 2001

Kalyuga, S., Chandler, P., Tuovinen, J., & Sweller, J. (2001). When Problem Solving Is Superior to Studying Worked Examples. Journal of Educational Psychology, 93(3), 579-588. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.93.3.579

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 used the same 24 apprentices, randomly reallocated, writing Boolean switching equations. At Stage 2 "The problem solving group performed significantly better than the worked examples group," and was also significantly more efficient, F(1, 22) 4.97, p < .05.

> "there was a significant effect for Stage 2. F(1. 22) 5.26, MSE 6.66, p < .05. (The problem solving group performed significantly better than the worked examples group.)"
