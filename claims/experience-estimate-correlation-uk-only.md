## Subclaims
`q3 i0` Assessors' estimate of trainees' surgical experience correlated with actual experience among UK trainees (R = 0·52, P = 0·017) but not among Sierra Leonean trainees (R = −0·09, P = 0·697). [→ Liu B. 2019](#liu-b-2019)

## Evidence

### Liu B. 2019

Liu B., Hunt L. M., Lonsdale R. J., Narula H. S., Mansaray A. F., Bundu I. and Bolkan H. A. (2019). Comparison of surgical skill acquisition by UK surgical trainees and Sierra Leonean associate clinicians in a task‐sharing programme. BJS Open 3(2): 218–223. https://doi.org/10.1002/bjs5.50122

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r1`

Spearman rank correlation (Table 3) in the blinded study: for UK trainees R = 0·52 (P = 0·017); for Sierra Leonean trainees R = −0·09 (P = 0·697). The correlation coefficient R = 0·52 is the printed effect size for the UK group.

> "The correlation analysis revealed that years of surgical experience appeared to have a greater influence on the performance of UK trainees (Table  3)."
