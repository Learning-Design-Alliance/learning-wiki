# Students from flipped sections have a significantly higher 3-year graduation rate (31% vs. 21%) that persists through 4- and 5-year rates

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Riedl Ann 2021

Riedl Ann, Yeung Fan, Burke Tina. (2021). Implementation of a Flipped Active-Learning Approach in a Community College General Biology Course Improves Student Performance in Subsequent Biology Courses and Increases Graduation Rate. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.20-07-0156

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Registrar-provided graduation rates for study participants tracked for 5 years after the academic year of the study. The article reports a 31% versus 21% three-year graduation rate difference, p = 0.00067, noting the non-flipped rate matched the institutional rate.

> "the 31% three-year graduation rate for students who participated in flipped sections was significantly higher than the 21% three-year graduation rate for students who participated in non-flipped sections (p = 0.00067)"

### Riedl Ann 2021 (2)

Riedl Ann, Yeung Fan, Burke Tina. (2021). Implementation of a Flipped Active-Learning Approach in a Community College General Biology Course Improves Student Performance in Subsequent Biology Courses and Increases Graduation Rate. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.20-07-0156

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Supplemental grade-category analysis of 3-year graduation rates. The article reports the flipped advantage was significant for every grade category except F and W, exemplified by A students graduating at 46% versus 32%, p = 0.000027.

> "“A” students from flipped sections graduated at a 46% rate, while “A” students from non-flipped sections graduated at a 32% rate (p = 0.000027)"
