# Learner–learner interaction was perceived as significantly more work than learner–content interaction in an online asynchronous undergraduate course

## Subclaims
`q4 i2` Students assigned to learner–learner interaction rated their perception of workload significantly higher than students assigned to learner–content interaction, with a large effect size. [→ Hauert 2026](#hauert-2026)

## Evidence

### Hauert 2026

Hauert, S., Luo, T., & Yen, C.-J. (2026). Assessing the impact of substituting interaction types: an empirical study of the interaction equivalency theory. Education Tech Research Dev, 74, 1–27. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10563-1

`q4 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial with 66 community-college students randomly assigned to learner–content (n = 33) or learner–learner (n = 33) groups with no attrition; two-tailed independent samples t test on 5-point Likert workload surveys. The learner–content group mean (M = 2.02, SD = .63) was lower than the learner–learner group mean (M = 2.65, SD = 0.99), with "Cohen's d as .78".

> "The difference in perception of workload between the two interaction groups was statistically significant at the .05 level, t(64) = -3.12, p = .003. Moreover, the size of the Cohen's d as .78 suggested a large effect size"
