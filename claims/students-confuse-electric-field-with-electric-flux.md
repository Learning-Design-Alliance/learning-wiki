## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Student understanding of Symmetry and Gauss' law. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07725](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07725)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Written and interview analysis of a cube-in-uniform-field item; 58% of 168 students answered item 3 correctly per Table 1. The most common difficulty was assuming "that the electric ﬁeld is zero at point B on the side surface of the cube" despite the stated uniform 20 N/C field; students claimed the area vector of the side surface is perpendicular to the field lines.

> "that the electric ﬁeld is zero at point B on the side surface of the cube although the problem statement explicitly mentioned"

### Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Student understanding of Symmetry and Gauss' law. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07725](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07725)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Written-test analysis of a multiple-selection flux item about a cube in a uniform 20 N/C field; 59% of 168 students answered correctly per Table 1. The article reports that "students did not realize that both statements (I) and (II) are correct", with many choosing only option (I) or (II) but not both.

> "The most common difﬁculty with question (4) was that students did not realize that both statements (I) and (II) are correct."
