## Evidence

### L. D. Carr and S. B. McKagan 2008

L. D. Carr and S. B. McKagan. (2008). Graduate quantum mechanics reform. https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.2628 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0806.2628](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0806.2628)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Three-year longitudinal implementation at CSM in which interactive methods (eliciting responses from every student, group work at boards, long pauses, online notes with open questions) gradually increased. Because the authors were unwilling to teach the benchmark course traditionally, they cannot clearly demonstrate students learned more than otherwise.

> "The answer is a resounding yes. Students were very responsive to Socratic and other modiﬁcations. Attendance was 100% except in case of illness or attend- ing a professional conference. Students formed a real esprit de corps , and felt they had a say in their own education, according to their evaluations."
