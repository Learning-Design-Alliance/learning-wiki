## Evidence

### O. Bitter 2022

O. Bitter, E.V. Hansen, S. Kravitz, V. Velan, and Y. You (2022). Transforming U.S. Particle Physics Education: A Snowmass 2021 Study. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2204.08983](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2204.08983)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Among 129 junior respondents asked about intended versus eventual research area (Figure 7), "About one quarter of the respondents reported that they changed their sub-ﬁeld during graduate school", with funding and position scarcity the dominant cited reason.

> "About one quarter of the respondents reported that they changed their sub-ﬁeld during graduate school. Of the respondents who oﬀered a reason for their ﬁeld switch, two thirds discussed the lack of positions and/or lack of funding in their desired sub-ﬁeld"

### O. Bitter 2022 (2)

O. Bitter, E.V. Hansen, S. Kravitz, V. Velan, and Y. You (2022). Transforming U.S. Particle Physics Education: A Snowmass 2021 Study. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2204.08983](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2204.08983)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The same sub-field flowchart analysis found asymmetric switching: "switches from theory to experiment were much more common than from experiment to theory", within HEA and HEP and between them. A further third of switchers cited their advisor as the reason.

> "We also note that switches from theory to experiment were much more common than from experiment to theory (both within HEA and HEP, and between the two)."
