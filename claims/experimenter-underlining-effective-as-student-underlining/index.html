In a controlled experiment comparing student-generated underlining with experimenter-generated underlining, the authors found that "subjects who generated their own underlining did not perform significantly better" than those given experimenter-generated underlining. The study also manipulated the relevance of the underlined content (high vs. low relevant text), suggesting that the selection of what to mark — rather than the generative act of marking — drives any effect on test performance.

Discussion¶

Generation is not the active ingredient. A common intuition holds that annotating text oneself is a generative activity that deepens processing relative to passively reviewing someone else's marks. This finding is in tension with that intuition for underlining specifically, though a single non-significant difference with no recorded sample size cannot show that generating the underlining adds nothing. This aligns with the broader literature on text marking, where underlining is among the weaker learning strategies and its value depends heavily on the quality of selection.

Selection quality is the moderator. The study also manipulated the relevance of underlined content, but the entry does not report what that manipulation found. If relevance matters as the authors' design suggests, poorly chosen marks — whether made by students or experimenters — can be useless or harmful, while well-chosen marks help regardless of who makes them. Novices often select poorly when marking on their own, which may explain why self-generated underlining frequently fails to beat provided marks.

Scope limits. The claim concerns underlining specifically and immediate test performance; it does not establish that all forms of annotating are equivalent to provided marks. Richer annotation (marginal notes, elaborative comments, self-questioning) involves different cognitive processes than drawing lines and may show genuine generation benefits. Single-study support in one text domain also warrants replication before strong design prescriptions are drawn.
