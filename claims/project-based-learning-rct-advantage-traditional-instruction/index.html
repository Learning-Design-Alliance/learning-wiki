q4 · i? · causal · r?

Schneider et al. (2022) randomized evaluation of high school chemistry and physics project-based programs centered on real-world questions, with students performing experiments and creating models. The advantage was "close to 0.2 standard deviation" on an independent summative science assessment. No standardized effect size statistic is printed.

"This randomized-control evaluation involving more than 4000 students, showed an advantage of the project-based condition over traditional teaching of close to 0.2 standard deviation on a summative science assessment developed by an independent third party."

q4 · i? · causal · r?

Krajcik et al. (2022) randomized comparison of elementary project-based arrangements on physics and biology topics against business-as-usual courses. Project-based students "outperformed the students in the traditional condition" by a little over 0.2 standard deviations. No standardized effect size statistic is printed.

"This study involved more than 2000 students and used a randomized control set-up. Again, students in the project-based conditions outperformed the students in the traditional condition, now with a difference of a little over 0.2 standard deviation on a summative science assessment"
