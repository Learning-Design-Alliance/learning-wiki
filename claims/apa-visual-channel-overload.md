# APA presentations tend to overload the visual channel, especially with high element interactivity content

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` An APA with its gestures imposes heavy demand on the visual channel (written words, agent image, background pictures), creating cognitive overload that compromises learning efficiency. [→ Woo 2009](#woo-2009)
`q2 i?` Only instruction with high element interactivity is likely to suffer the cognitive overload effect; low element interactivity content leaves sufficient cognitive capacity. [→ Woo 2009 (2)](#woo-2009-2)

## Evidence

### Woo 2009

Woo, H.L. (2009). Designing multimedia learning environments using animated pedagogical agents: factors and issues. Journal of Computer Assisted Learning, 25, 203-218. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2729.2008.00299.x

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Theoretical analysis within the CTMLAPA model (Moreno 2005): the three simultaneous visual elements create heavy demand on the visual channel, and when the channel fails to cope, cognitive overload compromises learning efficiency. This is an argument, not a measured result.

> "For an APA presentation, there are at least three forms of visual elements that our eyes have to handle - the written words, the agent image (including its gesture and facial expression), and other pictures that reside in the background."

### Woo 2009 (2)

Woo, H.L. (2009). Designing multimedia learning environments using animated pedagogical agents: factors and issues. Journal of Computer Assisted Learning, 25, 203-218. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2729.2008.00299.x

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review cites Sweller's (2002) argument that learners with low element interactivity content have sufficient cognitive capacity, so poor design effects are not readily felt, whereas high element interactivity content overtaxes capacity. Theoretical claim, no measured data.

> "only instruction that has high element interactivity is likely to suffer the cognitive overload effect"
