## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Self-reported familiarity with TIC principles rose from 9% (3/35) 'very familiar' at baseline to 70% (23/33) 'very' or 'extremely' familiar after training (p < 0.0001). [→ Brown Taylor 2026](#brown-taylor-2026)

## Evidence

### Brown Taylor 2026

Brown Taylor, Ashista Henry, Sudario Gabriel, Clery Michael, Dubosh Nicole M., Harries Aaron, Misa Nana Yaa. (2026). Trauma-informed procedural training in emergency medicine: a multi-site randomized control pilot study. BMC Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12873-026-01535-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Matched pre/post surveys of 33 students in the multi-site pilot; baseline familiarity was low, with "only 9% (3/35)" very familiar. McNemar test on dichotomized Likert responses; only p-values printed, no effect sizes.

> "After the training, 70% (23/33) of students felt “very familiar” or “extremely familiar” with the principles (Table 1)."

### Brown Taylor 2026 (2)

Brown Taylor, Ashista Henry, Sudario Gabriel, Clery Michael, Dubosh Nicole M., Harries Aaron, Misa Nana Yaa. (2026). Trauma-informed procedural training in emergency medicine: a multi-site randomized control pilot study. BMC Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12873-026-01535-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre-post survey analysis via McNemar's test showed "statistically significant increases" in both comfort measures (Table 1: 0.46→0.94 and 0.31→0.91); no effect sizes are printed.

> "Students demonstrated statistically significant increases in their comfort with performing procedures for patients with emotional distress or a history of trauma and in their ability to guide a patient through a grounding exercise (Table 1)."

### Brown Taylor 2026 (3)

Brown Taylor, Ashista Henry, Sudario Gabriel, Clery Michael, Dubosh Nicole M., Harries Aaron, Misa Nana Yaa. (2026). Trauma-informed procedural training in emergency medicine: a multi-site randomized control pilot study. BMC Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12873-026-01535-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multiple-choice knowledge questions compared pre/post with Wilcoxon signed-rank test; accuracy increased "from 67% to 73% (p = 0.0238)". No effect size printed.

> "The training was also effective in increasing student’s knowledge surrounding the application of TIC principles to clinical practice and procedures as a universal precaution, with accuracy percentages increasing from 67% to 73% (p = 0.0238)."
