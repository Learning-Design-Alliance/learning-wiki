# Prior attendance at non-cadaveric ultrasound courses raises pre-course confidence but not post-course confidence, and non-attendees improve more for hip and ankle but not knee injections

## Evidence

### Zure Mert 2025

Zure Mert, Topaloğlu Mahir, İş Enes Efe, Sezikli Selim, Sarıkaya Deniz, Dıraçoğlu Demirhan. (2025). Advancing skills and confidence in ultrasound-guided injection techniques: the impact of a cadaveric workshop. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07270-z

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Between-group comparison by previous MSK USG course attendance among 109 workshop participants; the difference was most pronounced in knee injections. Only p-values are printed; no effect size.

> "Across all three joints, participants who attended previous courses consistently showed higher pre-course scores (p-value < 0.001 for all joints), this difference is most pronounced in knee injections (Fig. 1 top)."

### Zure Mert 2025 (2)

Zure Mert, Topaloğlu Mahir, İş Enes Efe, Sezikli Selim, Sarıkaya Deniz, Dıraçoğlu Demirhan. (2025). Advancing skills and confidence in ultrasound-guided injection techniques: the impact of a cadaveric workshop. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07270-z

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Null finding from the same between-group comparison: post-course confidence did not significantly differ between prior attendees and non-attendees at any joint. Equivalence was not tested; no effect size printed.

> "Post course confidence scores showed no statistically significant superiority over one group for hip (p-value = 0.067), knee (p-value = 0.05), and ankle (p-value = 0.085) joint injections (Fig. 1 middle)."

### Zure Mert 2025 (3)

Zure Mert, Topaloğlu Mahir, İş Enes Efe, Sezikli Selim, Sarıkaya Deniz, Dıraçoğlu Demirhan. (2025). Advancing skills and confidence in ultrasound-guided injection techniques: the impact of a cadaveric workshop. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07270-z

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Improvement comparison by prior course attendance: non-attendees improved significantly more for hip and ankle injections; the knee contrast was not significant (the article prints p-value = 8.81, an apparent typographical inconsistency).

> "Statistically superior improvements were observed in those who did not attend a previous course over those that did for hip (p-value = 0.005) and ankle (p-value = 0.003) but not for knee injections (p-value = 8.81) (Fig. 1 bottom)."
