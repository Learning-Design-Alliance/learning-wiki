## Evidence

### Zijing Wu and Qiang Li 2026

Zijing Wu and Qiang Li. (2026). Evaluation indicator system for AI certificate programs. International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00604-x

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Monte Carlo simulation (N = 10,000) perturbing AHP pairwise comparisons with normally distributed noise (SD = 20%) and FAHP ratings with 20% adjacent-category shift probability. Results show tight concentration of the composite score and high rank stability for top indicators (C6: 99.8%).

> "The A1 composite score distribution (Fig. 2, Panel B) was tightly concentrated (simulated mean = 3.7681 vs. baseline = 3.7822; CV = 1.31%). The 95% CI [3.673, 3.867] fell entirely within the “ Agree” evaluation range (3.5–4.5)."

### Zijing Wu and Qiang Li 2026 (2)

Zijing Wu and Qiang Li. (2026). Evaluation indicator system for AI certificate programs. International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00604-x

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Deterministic sensitivity analysis under ±10% to ±30% weight variation (Table 7). The article attributes the stability to structural gaps in original weights among sub-indicators, so relative importance relationships remain unchanged.

> "the top 10 sub-indicators (AI Core Competency Achievement Rate among Students, Alignment of Curriculum Content with AI Frontier Technologies, Cross-Cultural Student Satisfaction Ratings, Industry/International Certification Recognition of Learning Outcomes, Faculty-Student Interaction Response Efficiency) exhibited no ranking changes under ± 30% weight adjustments, indicating extremely strong ranking stability"
