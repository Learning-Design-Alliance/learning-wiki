## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Children exposed to parental internalizing disorder have lower GPA; the association is −0.43 unadjusted, −0.24 after socioeconomic adjustment, and −0.20 after also adjusting for other psychiatric and drug/alcohol diagnoses. [→ Nordmo 2023](#nordmo-2023)

## Evidence

### Nordmo 2023

Nordmo, M., Kleppestø, T., Sunde, H. F., Flatø, M., Demange, P. & Torvik, F. A. (2023). The association between parental internalizing disorders and child school performance. npj Science of Learning 8:34. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00182-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Regression models on the Norwegian population register sample. Unadjusted standardized β = −0.43 [−0.44, −0.42]; adjusted model 2 "reduced the association considerably (β = −0.24"; adjusted model 3 gave β = −0.20 [−0.21, −0.19]. Standardized GPA (mean = 0, SD = 1) outcome; β coefficients are the printed effect measures.

> "The largest association was found in the unadjusted model −0.43 [95 % CI: −0.44, −0.42]. Adjusting for child birth order and birth year (adjusted model 1) did not inﬂuence the association −0.43 [95% CI: −0.44, −0.42]. Adding parental socioeconomic status (adjusted model 2) reduced the association considerably (β = −0.24; 95% CI:−0.25, −0.23)."
