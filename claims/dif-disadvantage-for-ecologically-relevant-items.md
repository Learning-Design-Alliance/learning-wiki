## Subclaims
`q3 i2` The odds of low-SES students answering correctly, given average math performance, are 0.91 for ecologically relevant items versus 1.02 (word problems) and 1.06 (notation) for neutral items; controlling for item features, the effect has Cohen's d = 0.70. ## Evidence

### Marjolein Muskens 2024

Marjolein Muskens, Willem E. Frankenhuiss & Lex Borghans. (2024). Math items about real-world content lower test-scores of students from families with low socioeconomic status. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00228-8

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Mantel-Haenszel DIF analyses of 161 TIMSS items comparing relevant-content items with neutral word problems and mathematical-notation items. A follow-up linear regression on DIF-odds controlling for item features reports "b = −0.09, t(160) =−2.55, p = 0.012, Cohen’s d = 0.70", described by the authors as a medium effect size.

> "The odds for low-SES students to respond correctly, in comparison to high-SES students and given their average math performance, is signi ﬁcantly lower (0.91) in math items with low-SESecologically relevant content than in the other items (1.02 and 1.06) (see Table3)."
