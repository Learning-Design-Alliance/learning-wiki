# A dual A/B-marks aggregation scheme tracked advanced performance but confused students and produced low-mark anxiety

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The authors' first marking scheme, with separate A (advanced) and B (basic) marks summed for final grades, successfully tracked advanced-tier performance but confused students, allowed passing while omitting much of the basic tier, and produced anxiety about low marks among adequately performing students. [→ Timo A. Nieminen 2017](#timo-a-nieminen-2017)

## Evidence

### Timo A. Nieminen 2017

Timo A. Nieminen, Serene H.-J. Choi, and Anton Rayner. (2017). Tiered Assessment in Upper-Level Undergraduate Physics. 20th Australian Institute of Physics Congress, Engineers Australia. Originally published 2012. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1708.06053](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1708.06053)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Practice-based report from the authors' trial of the A/B marks scheme in their electromagnetics courses; the article describes no formal evaluation, only the authors' assessment of problems observed in use. Coarse marking scales meant a pass sat near 50% of B marks, and adequately performing students viewed their mark as about 50%, or 25% including A marks.

> "This scheme proved to have three main problems in practice. Firstly, stud ents were confused by two sets of “marks”"
