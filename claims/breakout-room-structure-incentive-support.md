# Breakout rooms succeeded when structured, incentivized, and supported, but failed when group members did not participate

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students reported that breakout room success depended on structure and group composition; grade incentives, accountability to share with the class, and TA/LA support were important factors, while unstructured rooms led students to work alone. [→ Danny Doucette 2021](#danny-doucette-2021)

## Evidence

### Danny Doucette 2021

Danny Doucette, Sonja Cwik, and Chandralekha Singh. (2021). "Everyone is new to this": Student reflections on different aspects of online learning. [doi:10.1119/10.0005795](https://doi.org/10.1119/10.0005795)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview data from fall 2020 students. One student said lab activities were successful "because the collaborative activities were mandatory to do and they were graded. So people came to them and worked on them."

> "Structure and incentivized preparation for breakout sessions seem to have been essential for science courses, but less important for non-science courses in which students felt they could participate in conversations even if they hadn’t completed the out of class preparatory work."
