# Fluid reasoning, not trait self-control, predicts low-stakes standardized mathematics and physics test scores

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Fluid reasoning significantly predicts standardized mathematics (β = 0.52) and physics (β = 0.58) scores, whereas trait self-control does not (math β = −0.01, p = 0.710; physics β = −0.02, p = 0.187). [→ Schmidt FTC 2020](#schmidt-ftc-2020)

## Evidence

### Schmidt FTC 2020

Schmidt FTC, Lindner C, Etzel JM and Retelsdorf J (2020) Self-Control Outdoes Fluid Reasoning in Explaining Vocational and Academic Performance—But Does It? Front. Psychol. 11:757. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00757

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multivariate regression on N = 3,146 trainees; Table 2 prints fluid reasoning slopes of 0.52 (math) and 0.58 (physics), both p = 0.000, versus non-significant TSC slopes, confirming "the coeﬃcients for ﬂuid reasoning were overall larger than those for trait self-control".

> "In line with previous research, ﬂuid reasoning proved to be relatively more important for achievement in standardized tests; the coeﬃcients for ﬂuid reasoning were overall larger than those for trait self-control."
