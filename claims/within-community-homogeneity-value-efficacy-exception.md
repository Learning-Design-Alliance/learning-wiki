## Evidence

### Liwen Zhang 2026

Liwen Zhang, Wei Si, Ke-ke Shang, Jiangli Zhu, Xiaomin Ji. (2026). Mapping the AI Divide in Undergraduate Education: Community Detection in Disciplinary Networks and Survey Evidence. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.3390/bdcc10090319](https://doi.org/10.3390/bdcc10090319)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Internal one-way ANOVAs per community (Tables D.10–D.13, E.14–E.17) on 301 valid questionnaires. Skill and usage efficacy show no significant within-community differences; AIGC literacy dimensions are homogeneous within all four communities.

> "students from different schools within the same community show no significant differences in the two core AIGC literacy dimensions of Using AI and Evaluating AI"

### Liwen Zhang 2026 (2)

Liwen Zhang, Wei Si, Ke-ke Shang, Jiangli Zhu, Xiaomin Ji. (2026). Mapping the AI Divide in Undergraduate Education: Community Detection in Disciplinary Networks and Survey Evidence. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.3390/bdcc10090319](https://doi.org/10.3390/bdcc10090319)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Within-community ANOVA (Tables D.12, D.13): value efficacy differs significantly within Community 3 (F=3.718, p=0.007**) and Community 4 (F=4.379, p=0.002**). The authors attribute this to disciplinary backgrounds and educational philosophies.

> "Within Community 3, the School of Social and Behavioural Sciences has the highest mean value efficacy (4.40±0.28), which is significantly higher than that of the School of Information Management (3.52±0.44)"
