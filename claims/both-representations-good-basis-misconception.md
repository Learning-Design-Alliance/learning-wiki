# Students who correctly identify good bases for the fine-structure and Zeeman terms individually incorrectly claim that both the coupled and uncoupled representations are good bases for their sum

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Some students transfer correct individual-perturbation basis choices to the combined perturbation, claiming both representations form a good basis for H′fs + H′Z. [→ Emily Marshman 2019](#emily-marshman-2019)

## Evidence

### Emily Marshman 2019

Emily Marshman, Christof Keebaugh, and Chandralekha Singh. (2019). Student difficulties with the corrections to the energy spectrum of the hydrogen atom for the intermediate field Zeeman effect. arXiv:2006.05000. [doi:10.1119/perc.2018.pr.marshman](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2018.pr.marshman)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Think-aloud interview evidence from students responding to Q1 after traditional instruction. The student reasoned from correct individual answers to an incorrect combined conclusion about "the coupled" and "the uncoupled" representations.

> "One interviewed student incorrectly claimed that "the coupled are a good ba- sis for ˆH ′f s and uncoupled are a good basis for ˆH ′Z , so both coupled and uncoupled form a good basis for ˆH ′f s + ˆH ′Z.""
