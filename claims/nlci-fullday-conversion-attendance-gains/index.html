In the North Lawndale Cluster Initiative study, pre-k attendance at the four converting schools was about 4 percentage points higher than at propensity-weighted comparison schools in 2015-16 and 2016-17, about seven additional days of attendance; the difference was statistically significant in 2015-16, and in 2016-17 significant in some model specifications but only marginally significant in others. → Ehrlich 2021

Evidence¶

Ehrlich 2021¶

Ehrlich, S.B., Allensworth, E.M., & Tansey, J. (2021). Meeting families' needs: Attendance rates in full-day vs. half-day pre-k. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/meeting-families-needs

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Quasi-experimental comparison of four NLCI schools with comparison schools weighted by their propensity scores (the summary calls it a matching strategy; the methods section describes propensity weighting), using hierarchical linear models with students nested within schools and student demographic controls. Baseline equivalence on pre-k attendance was established in 2012-13.

"Pre-k attendance rates in the NLCI schools improved in the 2015-16 school year and were about 4 percentage points higher than at comparison schools through the 2016-17 school year. The differ -ences were statistically significant despite the small number of schools"
