# Students in categories A–C locate authority for knowledge outside themselves while students in D–F take responsibility themselves, a difference the authors relate to deep and surface approaches

## Evidence

### Booth 1999

Booth, S. & Ingerman, Å. (1999). Making sense of Physics in the first year of study. Chalmers University of Technology. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/9911071](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/9911071)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The authors' ethical-aspect analysis of the categories, drawing on Perry's developmental scheme: in categories A–C "the authority clearly lies outside of the student", whereas in D–F the responsibility is taken and the agenda set mainly by the student.

> "In the first group of categories (A,B,C) the authority clearly lies outside of the student, the responsibility and problem formulation privilege are mainly taken by the teachers and other persons "in power", not necessarily known to the student."

### Booth 1999 (2)

Booth, S. & Ingerman, Å. (1999). Making sense of Physics in the first year of study. Chalmers University of Technology. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/9911071](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/9911071)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The authors' interpretation linking study approaches to authority: a student who perceives authority as external seeks to satisfy it, while a student who sees authority partly with himself focuses on the meaning of and relationships between knowledge fragments.

> "This leads us to relate the dichotomous approach to study – deep approaches vs. surface approaches (Marton et al., 1984) – to the individual's perception of authority and the source of the sense of control and/or self-assurance."
