# Children's adult-reported and observed effortful control in early elementary school prospectively predicts academic achievement 6 years later

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Children high in effortful control at T1 (ages 4-8) achieved at relatively high levels 6 years later, using both reported and observed EC measures. [→ Valiente Carlos 2011](#valiente-carlos-2011)

## Evidence

### Valiente Carlos 2011

Valiente Carlos, Eisenberg Nancy, Haugen Rg, Spinrad Tracy L., Hofer Claire, Liew Jeffrey, Kupfer Anne. (2011). Children's Effortful Control and Academic Achievement: Mediation Through Social Functioning. Early Education and Development, 22(3), 411-433. https://doi.org/10.1080/10409289.2010.505259

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal SEM study of 214 children (M age 73 months at T1), with EC measured via parent and teacher reports and a puzzle persistence task at T1, and teacher- and child-reported grades at T3. The authors state the findings were found "both within and across reporter when both reported and observed data for EC were used."

> "Children who were rated by adults as having high levels of EC and who persisted on a mildly frustrating task when in early elementary school (i.e., kindergarten to third grade) achieved academically at relatively high levels when in middle school or high school (i.e., 6 years after T1)."
