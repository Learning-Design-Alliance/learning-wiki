# Trained raters given quality children's books rate their instructional qualities significantly higher than raters given non-quality books

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Experimental-group ratings of instructional qualities (teaching, advice and orders, fear and anxiety, ideological discourse, gender discrimination, rose-coloured world, traditional values, extremely emotional elements, violence) were significantly higher than control-group ratings, all p < .001. ## Evidence

### Çer and Sahin 2016

Erkan Çer, Ertuğrul Şahin. (2016). Validity of a Checklist for the Design, Content, and Instructional Qualities of Children's Books. Journal of Education and Practice, Vol.7, No.24. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1112922

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized experiment with 120 prospective Turkish language teachers (60 per group) who rated books after checklist training. Independent samples t-tests on instructional-quality subscales showed the experimental group rated quality books higher on every subscale; the article reports all comparisons as significant at p < .001 with no effect sizes printed.

> "As seen in Table 1, the independent samples t-test results indicate that there was a statistically significant difference between the assessments of the students in the experimental and control groups about the quality and non-quality children's books. The rating scores in the experimental group are higher than those in the control group in all respects."
