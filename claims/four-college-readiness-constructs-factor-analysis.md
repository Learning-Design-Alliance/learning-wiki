# The strongest college-readiness predictors measure four broad constructs: academic preparation, aspirations and expectations, socioeconomic status, and teacher perceptions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Exploratory factor analysis on training data and confirmatory factor analysis on test data show highly predictive variables load on a general factor plus roughly four subfactors corresponding to academic preparation, postsecondary aspirations and expectations, socioeconomic status, and teacher perceptions of readiness. [→ James Soland 2017](#james-soland-2017)

## Evidence

### James Soland 2017

Research & Practice in Assessment, Volume Twelve, Summer 2017. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/combining-academic-noncognitive-and-college-knowledge-measures-to-identify-students-not-on-track-for-college-a-data-driven-approach/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Factor analysis of roughly 40 variables identified as strongly predictive by random forest and lasso models; the bifactor model fit best (chi-square 2403, RMSEA .056) among candidate models compared on fit statistics.

> "Beyond the general factor, the variables load on roughly four factors. The first of these factors is associated primarily with test scores and other measures of academic preparation like grades."
