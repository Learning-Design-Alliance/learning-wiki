## Evidence

### Baranipour Jafar 2026

Baranipour Jafar, Hosseini Seyed Mohammad Reza, Sharifzadeh Gholam Reza, Poodineh Moghadam Mahdieh. (2026). Comparison of the effects of microlearning and webinars on knowledge and self-efficacy of emergency medical technicians (EMTs) in the diagnosis and treatment of cardiac arrhythmias. BMC Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12873-025-01428-0

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated measures ANOVA on self-efficacy in the 75-EMT quasi-experiment. The article reports "a significant increase in the mean self-efficacy score" in both intervention groups; only test statistics are printed, no effect size.

> "The results of the repeated measures analysis of variance indicated that in the microlearning (F = 61.5, P < 0.001) and webinar (F = 64.6, P < 0.001) groups, a significant increase in the mean self-efficacy score was observed during the three study periods."

### Baranipour Jafar 2026 (2)

Baranipour Jafar, Hosseini Seyed Mohammad Reza, Sharifzadeh Gholam Reza, Poodineh Moghadam Mahdieh. (2026). Comparison of the effects of microlearning and webinars on knowledge and self-efficacy of emergency medical technicians (EMTs) in the diagnosis and treatment of cardiac arrhythmias. BMC Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12873-025-01428-0

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Null result from the same study's control group, which received only routine Emergency Center Training Unit programs. "No significant changes were observed" in self-efficacy; equivalence was not tested and no effect size is printed.

> "In the control group, no significant changes were observed in the mean self-efficacy score between the three study periods (F = 1.6, P < 0.22)."
