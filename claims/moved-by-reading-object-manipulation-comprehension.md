# Physically or imaginatively manipulating objects during reading improves children's reading comprehension

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The Moved by Reading intervention, in which children act out sentences with toys physically, by imagining, or via computer manipulation, yields substantially better comprehension than re-reading. [→ Fred Paas & John Sweller 2011](#fred-paas-john-sweller-2011)

## Evidence

### Fred Paas & John Sweller 2011

Fred Paas & John Sweller. (2011). An Evolutionary Upgrade of Cognitive Load Theory: Using the Human Motor System and Collaboration to Support the Learning of Complex Cognitive Tasks. Educ Psychol Rev (2012) 24:27–45. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-011-9179-2

`q3 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Studies by Glenberg et al. on the Moved by Reading intervention: children read texts about a farm scenario and, cued by a green traffic light, acted out critical sentences with toys physically, by imagining, or by computer-assisted manipulation, compared with a re-reading control condition.

> "The results of the different studies revealed substantially better comprehension performance in the manipulation conditions than in the control conditions."
