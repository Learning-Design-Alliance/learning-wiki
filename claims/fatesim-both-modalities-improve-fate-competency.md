## Evidence

### Weimer Johannes Matthias 2024

Weimer Johannes Matthias, Sprengart Franziska Marietta, Vieth Thomas, Göbel Sebastian, Dionysopoulou Anna, Krüger Rebecca, Beer Jan, Weimer Andreas Michael, Buggenhagen Holger, Kloeckner Roman, Pillong Lukas, Helfrich Johanna, Waezsada Elias, Wand Philipp, Weinmann-Menke Julia. (2024). Simulator training in focus assessed transthoracic echocardiography (FATE) for undergraduate medical students: results from the FateSim randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06564-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the FateSim randomized controlled trial of 128 third-year students, the simulator group and human-subject control group both improved on the theory post-test and ended at similar scores; the article reports the increase as "a significant (p < 0.001) objective increase in competency".

> "Throughout the test, both groups achieved a significant (p < 0.001) objective increase in competency and demonstrated a similar level of competency at T2 (study: 56 ± 7 vs. control: 57 ± 8; p = 0.41)"

### Weimer Johannes Matthias 2024 (2)

Weimer Johannes Matthias, Sprengart Franziska Marietta, Vieth Thomas, Göbel Sebastian, Dionysopoulou Anna, Krüger Rebecca, Beer Jan, Weimer Andreas Michael, Buggenhagen Holger, Kloeckner Roman, Pillong Lukas, Helfrich Johanna, Waezsada Elias, Wand Philipp, Weinmann-Menke Julia. (2024). Simulator training in focus assessed transthoracic echocardiography (FATE) for undergraduate medical students: results from the FateSim randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06564-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Self-evaluation questionnaires at T1 and T2 in the randomized study showed both training modalities produced a "significant increase in competency up to T2", with no significant between-group difference in final self-rated competency.

> "Both groups reported a significant increase in competency up to T2 (Delta p < 0.001) and reached a similarly high self-reported competency (study: 2.6 ± 0.7 vs. control: 2.7 ± 0.7; p = 0.23)."
