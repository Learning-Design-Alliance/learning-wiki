## Evidence

### Gyeong-Geon Lee 2024

Gyeong-Geon Lee, and Xiaoming Zhai. (2024). Using ChatGPT for Science Learning: A Study on Pre-service Teachers' Lesson Planning. [doi:10.1109/tlt.2024.3401457](https://doi.org/10.1109/tlt.2024.3401457)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Rubric evaluation of all 29 ChatGPT-integrated lesson plans by two researchers using a modified TPACK-based Technology Integration Assessment Rubric; the article reports "M = 3.29 (SD = .91)" overall.

> "The average TIAR score for these plans was M = 3.29 (SD = .91), exceeding the mid -point of 2.5 on a 4 -point scale."

### Gyeong-Geon Lee 2024 (2)

Gyeong-Geon Lee, and Xiaoming Zhai. (2024). Using ChatGPT for Science Learning: A Study on Pre-service Teachers' Lesson Planning. [doi:10.1109/tlt.2024.3401457](https://doi.org/10.1109/tlt.2024.3401457)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Paired samples t-test comparing the four TIAR item scores across the 29 lesson plans; the article reports "t (28) = 2.3173, p = .0280", significant only for items 2 versus 3, indicating weaker ChatGPT function selection.

> "There was a significant difference only in the scores for item 2 and item 3; t (28) = 2.3173, p = .0280 ( < .05 )."
