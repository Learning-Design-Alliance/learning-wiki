# Without assigned roles, all group members generally participate in many core dimensions of group leadership, which emerges as an interactive group phenomenon

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Leadership in online university course groups is an emergent interactive group phenomenon, with all members generally participating in many core dimensions of leadership when roles are not assigned. [→ Gerry Stahl & Friedrich Hesse 2010](#gerry-stahl-friedrich-hesse-2010)

## Evidence

### Gerry Stahl & Friedrich Hesse 2010

Gerry Stahl & Friedrich Hesse. (2010). A prism of CSCL research. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-010-9087-3

`q2 · i?`

The editorial reports, citing Gressick and Derry, a study of university students in a semester-long largely online course that adopted a reciprocal or interactive definition of leadership and combined quantitative and qualitative analyses to distinguish specific forms of leadership. No statistics are printed here.

> "leadership is an emergent interactive group phenomenon and that — if allowed to interact without assigned roles — all group members generally participate in many core dimensions of group leadership"
