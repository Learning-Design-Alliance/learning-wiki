# Cognitive efficiency motives lead perceivers to sacrifice accuracy in person perception

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Perceivers often sacrifice accuracy in person perception to save time and mental effort, via shortcuts such as anchoring, stereotyping, dispositional attributions, and the availability heuristic. [→ Gehlbach 2018](#gehlbach-2018)

## Evidence

### Gehlbach 2018

Gehlbach, H., & Vriesema, C. C. (2018). Meta‐bias: A practical theory of motivated thinking. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9454-6

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument in the article's statement of the meta-bias theory: the authors posit an efficiency motive, drawing on the "cognitive misers" framing of Fiske and Taylor and on Kahneman's System 1 account, under which shortcuts save effort at the expense of accuracy. No empirical test is reported in this article itself.

> "First, perceivers will often sacrifice accuracy in person perception for the sake of cognitive efficiency."
