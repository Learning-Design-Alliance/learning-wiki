# Individual trajectories suggest heavy LLM use focused on support other than full answer generation can coexist with improving scores, whereas reliance on LLM-generated summaries was associated with declining scores

## Evidence

### Minju Park 2026

Minju Park, Ivan Orozco Vasquez, and Cristina Conati. (2026). Characterizing Students' LLM Usage Behaviors and Their Association with Learning in Critical Thinking Tasks. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04534

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Single-student case within the High-Reliance group of the two-term course dataset. S61 used all usage categories except Generating Answers, and the article reports the scores "69% to 72% to 79%" as illustrating that extensive non-generation LLM use can coexist with improved performance.

> "Notably, S61's midterm scores steadily increased across the term (69% to 72% to 79%), illustrating that extensive LLM use—when focused on forms of support other than full answer generation—can coexist with, and may even accompany, improved performance."

### Minju Park 2026 (2)

Minju Park, Ivan Orozco Vasquez, and Cristina Conati. (2026). Characterizing Students' LLM Usage Behaviors and Their Association with Learning in Critical Thinking Tasks. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04534

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Single-student case within the High-Reliance group. S2 used only the Summarizing category, and midterm scores "declined consistently—from 65% to 48% to 38%"; the authors suggest S2 increasingly relied on LLM-generated summaries rather than engaging with the full readings.

> "S2 used only Summarizing(S), and their performance provides a clear illustration of the potential consequences of this pattern: their midterm scores declined consistently—from 65% to 48% to 38%."
