# Haphazard mixing of substance and location predicates leads students to confused reasoning about negative energy

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A student group that combined a 'potential energy is relative' resource (location ontology) with a '-5 is more than -1' resource (substance ontology) produced reasoning that was internally incoherent and resistant to instructor intervention. [→ Benjamin W. Dreyfus 2013](#benjamin-w-dreyfus-2013)

## Evidence

### Benjamin W. Dreyfus 2013

Benjamin W. Dreyfus, Benjamin D. Geller, Julia Gouvea, Vashti Sawtelle, Chandra Turpen, and Edward F. Redish. (2013). Ontological metaphors for negative energy in an interdisciplinary context. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.10.020108](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.10.020108)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Case study of Phillip's group on the kinesin energy bar-chart task in the NEXUS/Physics course (second pilot year video data). Phillip avoided negative energy by taking absolute values, conflating that with shifting the zero, which does not preserve energy conservation.

> "Thus, this example represents a mixing of substance and location predicates in a way that leads to confusion. This confusion can be manifested both in canonically incorrect results and in internal incoherence."
