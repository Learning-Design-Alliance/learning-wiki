## Evidence

### Kozak Agnessa 2017

Kozak Agnessa, Freitag Sonja, Nienhaus Albert. (2017). Evaluation of a Training Program to Reduce Stressful Trunk Postures in the Nursing Professions: A Pilot Study. Annals of Work Exposures and Health. https://doi.org/10.1093/annweh/wxw002

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

CUELA field measurement of 23 participants' early shifts at baseline and complete data for 19 participants at 6-month follow-up in 12 geriatric nursing wards, a pre-experimental design without a control group. The median proportion of time in inclinations >20° fell from 35.4% (IQR 27.6–43.1) to 25.3% (20.7–34.1), a "significantly reduced, by 29% (P < 0.001)" result. The authors note that a control group would be needed "to exclude alternative explanations, and to establish a causal relationship."

> "After the intervention, the median proportion of time spent in sagittal inclinations exceeding 20° was significantly reduced, by 29% (P < 0.001), from 1772 to 1708 median trunk movements per shift."

### Kozak Agnessa 2017 (2)

Kozak Agnessa, Freitag Sonja, Nienhaus Albert. (2017). Evaluation of a Training Program to Reduce Stressful Trunk Postures in the Nursing Professions: A Pilot Study. Annals of Work Exposures and Health. https://doi.org/10.1093/annweh/wxw002

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same CUELA pre/post measurement: median duration of SI ≥ 20° fell from 104 min (IQR 90–134) to 77 min (66–103) per shift, with a Hodges–Lehman estimate of −27.3 min and P < 0.001; the article reports the shift time was "reduced by 27 min per shift".

> "The median time spent in sagittal inclinations exceeding 20° was reduced by 27 min per shift (Table 4)."
