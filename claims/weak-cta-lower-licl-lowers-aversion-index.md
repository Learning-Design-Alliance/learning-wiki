## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Using lower LiCl concentrations (0.05 or 0.03 mol/l) in CTA training produced a lower aversion index than the standard 0.15 mol/l dose, removing the ceiling effect. [→ Levitan 2016](#levitan-2016)

## Evidence

### Levitan 2016

Levitan, D., Gal-Ben-Ari, S., Heise, C., Rosenberg, T., Elkobi, A., Inberg, S., Sala, C. & Rosenblum, K. (2016). The differential role of cortical protein synthesis in taste memory formation and persistence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/npjscilearn.2016.1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Between-group dose comparison in the CTA protocol: groups trained with 0.15, 0.05 and 0.03 mol/l LiCl were compared on aversion index. The article reports a main effect of group (ANOVA, P = 0.0001) and "P = 0.026 between 0.15 and 0.05 mol/l"; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Using these lower concentrations of LiCl resulted in lower aversion index (AI) compared with the higher dose of LiCl, allowing detection of both enhancement and impairment of memory (0.15 mol/l, n = 15; 0.05 mol/l, n = 5; 0.03 mol/l, n = 9. Main effect of group, ANOVA, P = 0.0001. P = 0.026 between 0.15 and 0.05 mol/l)."
