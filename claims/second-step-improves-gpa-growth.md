# Students in Second Step treatment schools showed a significantly greater increase in GPA over four semesters than students in control schools

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The growth rate of achievement (GPA) in treatment schools was higher than in control schools after controlling for gender, ethnicity, and SES (γ101 = 0.035230, p < 0.05). [→ Top 2016](#top-2016)
`q3 i?` Second Step participation status explained 15% of the variance in the growth rate of school achievement (GPA) across four semesters. [→ Top 2016 (2)](#top-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Top 2016

Top, N., Liew, J., & Luo, W. (2016). Effects of Second Step curriculum on behavioral and academic outcomes in 5th and 8th grade students: A longitudinal study on character development. Novitas-ROYAL (Research on Youth and Language), 10(1), 24-47. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1167262

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Three-level longitudinal growth model analysis of GPA data from official school records for 5,189 students across four semesters. The study reports that "The difference between treatment and control schools in the growth rate of achievement (γ101 = 0.035230) is statistically significant, at p < 0.05".

> "The difference between treatment and control schools in the growth rate of achievement (γ101 = 0.035230) is statistically significant, at p < 0.05"

### Top 2016 (2)

Top, N., Liew, J., & Luo, W. (2016). Effects of Second Step curriculum on behavioral and academic outcomes in 5th and 8th grade students: A longitudinal study on character development. Novitas-ROYAL (Research on Youth and Language), 10(1), 24-47. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1167262

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pseudo-R² calculation comparing reduced and full three-level growth models on GPA data. The study found that "the predictor schools' curricula status (SECSTEP) explained 15 % of the variance in the growth rate of school achievement (GPA) throughout 4 semesters from Fall 2012 to Fall 2014".

> "the predictor schools' curricula status (SECSTEP) explained 15 % of the variance in the growth rate of school achievement (GPA) throughout 4 semesters from Fall 2012 to Fall 2014"
