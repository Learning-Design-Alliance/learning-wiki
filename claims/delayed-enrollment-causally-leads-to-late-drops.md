# Cross-lagged panel models support a causal hypothesis that delayed enrollment leads to subsequent late drops, with no reverse reinforcement

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The estimated effect of delayed enrollment on later late drops is about 0.8 SDs of more course drops per SD of late course enrollment. [→ Borchers 2025](#borchers-2025)
`q2 i?` There was no reinforcement of late course dropping onto delayed enrollment, and the link is slightly stronger for students enrolling in higher course loads and stable across years of study. [→ Borchers 2025](#borchers-2025)

## Evidence

### Borchers 2025

Borchers, C., Xu, Y., & Pardos, Z. A. (2025). Workload Overload? Late Enrollment Leads to Course Dropout. Journal of Educational Data Mining, Volume 17, No 1. https://github.com/CAHLR/enrollment-procrastination-edm

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-lagged panel models on consecutive student semesters of enrollment transaction data, comparing cross-lagged effects in both directions; the article states the method tests but does not establish causality like experiments. The 0.8 SD estimate and moderator statements are as printed in the article's summary of results; the full results section was truncated in the supplied text.

> "Our results align with a causal hypothesis for the link between delayed enrollment and late course dropping, estimating the effect to be about 0.8SDs of more course drops per SD of late course enrollment. This link is slightly stronger for students who enroll in courses with a higher course load and is stable across years of study. There was no reinforcement of late course dropping and delayed enrollment, bolstering support for a causal hypothesis."
