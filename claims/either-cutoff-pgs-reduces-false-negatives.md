# Adding PGS as an either-cutoff screener alongside progress monitoring raises negative predictive value (fewer false negatives) at all effectiveness levels, while generally lowering PPV

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Classifying students as at-risk by meeting either the progress monitoring or the PGS cutoff raised NPV in every situation except when PGS was at its theoretical limit and progress monitoring was least effective, and lowered PPV. [→ Shero 2021](#shero-2021)

## Evidence

### Shero 2021

Shero, J., van Dijk, W., Edwards, A., Schatschneider, C., Solari, E. J., & Hart, S. A. (2021). The practical utility of genetic screening in school settings. npj Science of Learning, 6:12. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00090-y

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Density-function simulation (Results, Research question 2; Table 3) of an either-cutoff screener at the tenth-percentile disability definition, with PPV and NPV computed for each combination of PGS and progress monitoring effectiveness.

> "introducing PGS as a secondary method of being identi ﬁed as at-risk lowered positive predictive while raising NPV for every situation except when PGS reached its theoretical limit and progress monitoring was at its least effective."

### Shero 2021 (2)

Shero, J., van Dijk, W., Edwards, A., Schatschneider, C., Solari, E. J., & Hart, S. A. (2021). The practical utility of genetic screening in school settings. npj Science of Learning, 6:12. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00090-y

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Same either-cutoff simulation results: NPV gains depend on both PGS and progress monitoring effectiveness, growing as PGS becomes more accurate and shrinking as progress monitoring becomes more accurate.

> "The increase to NPV ranged from 3.4% when progress monitoring was least effective and PGS was at its theoretical limit, to 0.3% when progress monitoring is highest and PGS effectiveness is weakest."
