The review reports from Schoonheim-Klein et al.'s prospective study of 119 third-year dental students that "Angoff I and II had pass rates of 86.6%, while the Borderline Regression method achieved 97.5%", i.e. more favourable results for Borderline Regression.

Lin Galvin Sim Siang, Hisham Abdul Rauf Badrul, Majid Muhammad Nazmi Abdul, Foong Chan Choong, Ho Ting Khee, Friedlander Lara T. (2026). Refining competency benchmarks: a scoping review of Angoff standard-setting in dental education. BMC Oral Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-026-08195-9

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports from Moreno-Lopez and Hope's retrospective UK study of dental students in years 2–4 that discrepancies between the methods were observed, attributed possibly to poor use of grading scales; the Borderline Regression method was considered an acceptable alternative for small cohorts.

"Moreno-Lopez R et al. [29] observed discrepancies between the Angoff and Borderline Regression methods, suggesting potential concerns with the application of Angoff, particularly in small OSCE cohorts, possibly due to poor use of grading scales."
