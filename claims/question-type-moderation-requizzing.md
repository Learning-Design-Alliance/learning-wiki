## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Re-quizzing significantly predicted closed-response final subscores (ΔR2 = 0.012), with no significant preparation interactions. [→ Walck-Shannon 2019](#walck-shannon-2019)

## Evidence

### Walck-Shannon 2019

Walck-Shannon, E. M., Cahill, M. J., McDaniel, M. A., & Frey, R. F. (2019). Participation in Voluntary Re-quizzing Is Predictive of Increased Performance on Cumulative Assessments in Introductory Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education, 18(2). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.18-08-0163

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Post hoc hierarchical regression predicting the closed-response subscore (37/100 points) of the final exam (n = 310); "significantly increased the explanatory power of the model"; interactions step not significant (p = 0.110).

> "the addition of delayed re-quizzing (step 2) significantly increased the explanatory power of the model (F(1, 306) = 6.217, p = 0.010, ΔR2 = 0.012)."

### Walck-Shannon 2019 (2)

Walck-Shannon, E. M., Cahill, M. J., McDaniel, M. A., & Frey, R. F. (2019). Participation in Voluntary Re-quizzing Is Predictive of Increased Performance on Cumulative Assessments in Introductory Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education, 18(2). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.18-08-0163

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Post hoc regression predicting the free-response subscore (63/100 points) of the final exam (n = 310); step 2 "did not significantly increase the explanatory power of the model" (statistics as printed), but the step 3 interaction improved fit (F(2, 304) = 7.276, p = 0.001, ΔR2 = 0.027), benefiting lowest-chemistry students.

> "the addition of delayed re-quizzing (step 2) did not significantly increase the explanatory power of the model (F(1, 306) = 6.217, p = 0.010, ΔR2 = 0.012)."
