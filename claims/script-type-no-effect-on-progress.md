## Evidence

### Gagnon 2022

Gagnon, D. J., Baker, R. S., Gagnon, S., Swanson, L., Spevacek, N., Andres, J., Harpstead, E., Scianna, J., Slater, S., San Pedro, M. O.C.Z. (2022). Exploring players' experience of humor and snark in a grade 3-6 history practices game. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2210.09906](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2210.09906)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA and Kruskall-Wallis tests in the randomized four-script experiment testing whether snark or humor caused players to play longer and complete more of the game (RQ2). No statistically significant differences were detected; equivalence was not tested. The article also reports no significant effect on active time (χ²(df=3)=6.75, p=0.08).

> "There were no significant effects between game script types in terms of how likely players were to complete the game, χ2 (df = 3) = 1.33, p = 0.72, their highest level reached, F(3, 7112) = 0.01, p = 0.998"
