# Learning conditions predict math grades across racial and socioeconomic groups, with larger associations for FRPL-eligible and Black students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` FRPL-eligible students were 34% more likely and Black students 35% more likely to earn a B or better when rating learning conditions positively, versus 21% for White students not eligible for FRPL. [→ Gripshover 2022](#gripshover-2022)
`q2 i?` The relationship between learning conditions and math grades was not moderated by race or by FRPL eligibility. [→ Gripshover 2022 (2)](#gripshover-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Gripshover 2022

Gripshover, S., Londerée, A., Ahuvia, I., Shyjka, A., Kroshinsky, F., Ryan, N., Farrington, C., & Paunesku, D. (2022). Learning Conditions Are an Actionable, Early Indicator of Math Learning. PERTS & University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publication/learning-conditions-are-an-actionable-early-indicator-of-math-learning

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Subgroup analysis of the 2019–20 survey and grade data comparing positive vs. negative learning conditions within demographic groups; the printed percentages are descriptive likelihoods, not standardized effect sizes.

> "students who are eligible for Free and Reduced Price Lunch (FRPL) because of their family income were 34% more likely to earn a B or better if they rated learning conditions positively. Additionally, Black students were 35% more likely to earn a B or better if they rated learning conditions positively."

### Gripshover 2022 (2)

Gripshover, S., Londerée, A., Ahuvia, I., Shyjka, A., Kroshinsky, F., Ryan, N., Farrington, C., & Paunesku, D. (2022). Learning Conditions Are an Actionable, Early Indicator of Math Learning. PERTS & University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publication/learning-conditions-are-an-actionable-early-indicator-of-math-learning

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Moderation test reported in the results text for the same sample; the report prints no test statistic or effect size for the moderation, so impact is null.

> "The relationship between learning conditions and math grades was not moderated by race or by qualifying for FRPL vs. not qualifying."
