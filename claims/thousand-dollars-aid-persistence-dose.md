## Evidence

### Nguyen 2018

Nguyen, T.D., Kramer, J.W., & Evans, B.J. (2018). The Effects of Grant Aid on Student Persistence and Degree Attainment: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of the Causal Evidence (CEPA Working Paper No. 18-04). Stanford Center for Education Policy Analysis. http://cepa.stanford.edu/wp18-04

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

OLS regression of each study's treatment effect on the average aid amount per study (Table 5); the authors call the estimates rough due to limited studies and assume a linear aid-impact relationship. The year-to-year persistence coefficient is statistically significant at 1.2 percentage points.

> "For the year-to-year persistence estimate, we find that $1,000 of aid increases year-to-year persistence by a statistically significant 1.2 percentage points."

### Nguyen 2018 (2)

Nguyen, T.D., Kramer, J.W., & Evans, B.J. (2018). The Effects of Grant Aid on Student Persistence and Degree Attainment: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of the Causal Evidence (CEPA Working Paper No. 18-04). Stanford Center for Education Policy Analysis. http://cepa.stanford.edu/wp18-04

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Same OLS dose analysis across studies for degree completion outcomes; the printed result is a statistically insignificant 0.8 percentage-point increase in on-time and delayed completion per $1,000 of aid. The authors flag limited degrees of freedom and possible non-linearity.

> "For degree completion, we find $1,000 of aid increases on-time and delayed degree completion by a statistically insignificant 0.8 percentage points."
