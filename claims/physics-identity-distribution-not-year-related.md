## Evidence

### Laura M. Akesson 2026

Laura M. Akesson, Jessica L. Rosenberg, Nancy Holincheck, and Benjamin W. Dreyfus. (2026). Defining a physics person: Tensions in the identity development of undergraduate women in physics. Physical Review Physics Education Research 22, 020102. https://doi.org/10.1103/j9bh-pz14

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Distribution of the single-item overlap measure (A–G) across n=120 CUWiP survey respondents: 15.0% chose low identity (A–C), 62.5% mid, and 22.5% high, with "the majority of students (85.0%) classified themselves in the range of mid-to-high physics identities."

> "A total of 18 students (15.0%) chose A, B, or C, a low physics identity. The remaining 102 students reported mid (D or E, n ¼ 75, 62.5%) or high (F or G, n ¼ 27, 22.5%) physics identity."

### Laura M. Akesson 2026 (2)

Laura M. Akesson, Jessica L. Rosenberg, Nancy Holincheck, and Benjamin W. Dreyfus. (2026). Defining a physics person: Tensions in the identity development of undergraduate women in physics. Physical Review Physics Education Research 22, 020102. https://doi.org/10.1103/j9bh-pz14

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Comparison of identity responses across undergraduate years found no significant relationship with year in school; the authors note juniors and seniors "were not more likely to have a greater physics identity."

> "Interestingly, physics identity responses (A –G) were not significantly related to the year in school."
