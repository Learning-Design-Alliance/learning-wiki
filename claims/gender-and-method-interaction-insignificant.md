## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Two-factor ANOVA showed no significant gender effect on posttest (F(1,74)=.001, p=.979) or delayed test (F(1,74)=.000, p=.999), and no significant method × gender interaction on either measure (p=.681 and p=.962). [→ Tuncer 2014](#tuncer-2014)

## Evidence

### Tuncer 2014

Tuncer, M., & Bahadır, F. (2014). Effect of screen reading and reading from printed out material on student success and permanency in Introduction to Computer Lesson. TOJET: The Turkish Online Journal of Educational Technology, 13(3). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1034226

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Two-factor ANOVA for independent samples across 78 students tested method and gender effects. For delayed tests the article reports no significant group difference and that the "mutual effect of method (Experiment-Control) and gender on delayed test success scores is insignificant."

> "A significant difference between these two groups’ delayed test average scores was not noticed (F (1,74) =.086, p>.05). Another finding on Table12 is that mutual effect of method (Experiment-Control) and gender on delayed test success scores is insignificant [F (1,74) =.002, p>.05]."
