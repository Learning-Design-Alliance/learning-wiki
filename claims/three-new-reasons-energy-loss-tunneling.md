## Evidence

### S. B. McKagan 2008

S. B. McKagan, K. K. Perkins, and C. E. Wieman. (2008). A Deeper Look at Student Learning of Quantum Mechanics: the Case of Tunneling. https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.3194 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0802.3194](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0802.3194)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Case-study interviews (12 tunneling interviews with six students) plus corroborating observations in the transformed courses identified reasons 3-6 in Table I: using the EM-wave energy-amplitude relation, confusion over how the electron regains energy reentering the wire, thinking the transmitted part has less energy, and confusion over the energy-wave function relationship.

> "However, in interviews, students talked about energy loss in terms of diﬀerent, and much more subtle, reasons than those reported in the literature."

### S. B. McKagan 2008 (2)

S. B. McKagan, K. K. Perkins, and C. E. Wieman. (2008). A Deeper Look at Student Learning of Quantum Mechanics: the Case of Tunneling. https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.3194 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0802.3194](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0802.3194)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r3`

Interview finding for reason 6: "four exhibited some form of this diﬃculty"; it was also observed in problem-solving sessions and, unlike reasons 3-5, also in students in traditional courses. No effect size is printed.

> "Out of six students who were interviewed extensively on the relationship between the energy and wave function in tunneling, four exhibited some form of this diﬃculty."
