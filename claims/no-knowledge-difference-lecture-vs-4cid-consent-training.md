# Lecture-based and 4C/ID-based training produced comparable knowledge retention among emergency care professionals

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Post-test multiple-choice knowledge scores did not differ significantly between the lecture group and the 4C/ID workshop group. [→ Khan Abdus Salam 2025](#khan-abdus-salam-2025)

## Evidence

### Khan Abdus Salam 2025

Khan Abdus Salam, Khan Muhammad Nasir Ayub, Khan Muhammad I. (2025). Comparing the Lecture-Based Learning With the Four-Component Instructional Design (4C/ID) Model of Learning in Enhancing the Skills of Consent-Taking in the Emergency Department: A Quasi-experimental Study. Cureus. https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.94133

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental study of 21 emergency care professionals randomized to a lecture (Group A, n=10) or a 4C/ID workshop (Group B, n=11); independent t-tests compared 25-item MCQ scores. Post-test scores 'remained comparable' (p=0.883); no effect size was printed.

> "Both groups improved after their respective interventions, but post-test scores remained comparable (Group A: 21.90 ± 2.38 vs. Group B: 21.73 ± 2.87; p=0.883)."
