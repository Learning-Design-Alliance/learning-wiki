## Evidence

### Najjuma Josephine N. 2024

Najjuma Josephine N., Muhumuza Albert, Santorino Data, Sekyere Samuel Owusu, Ocheke Amaka N., Yiltok Simon J., Bajunirwe Francis. (2024). Barriers and facilitators to interprofessional simulation-based learning in a Ugandan medical school: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06521-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of student focus groups identified lack of leadership clarity as a session-level barrier: "Assigning leadership positions in interprofessional teams may be difficult due to varying levels of seniority and may lead to reluctance or conflicts." A student described a nursing student leading medical students as posing suppression.

> "Assigning leadership positions in interprofessional teams may be difficult due to varying levels of seniority and may lead to reluctance or conflicts."

### Najjuma Josephine N. 2024 (2)

Najjuma Josephine N., Muhumuza Albert, Santorino Data, Sekyere Samuel Owusu, Ocheke Amaka N., Yiltok Simon J., Bajunirwe Francis. (2024). Barriers and facilitators to interprofessional simulation-based learning in a Ugandan medical school: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06521-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Focus group participants reported unequal profession numbers cause "imbalanced role assignments," leaving some members feeling underutilized and facilitators concentrating on the majority profession; one student noted a class of about 100 medical students versus 33 nursing students.

> "Participants mentioned that some rotations or classes had bigger numbers than others. For instance, there could be more nursing students than medical students and sometimes more medical students than nursing students, resulting in imbalanced role assignments."

### Najjuma Josephine N. 2024 (3)

Najjuma Josephine N., Muhumuza Albert, Santorino Data, Sekyere Samuel Owusu, Ocheke Amaka N., Yiltok Simon J., Bajunirwe Francis. (2024). Barriers and facilitators to interprofessional simulation-based learning in a Ugandan medical school: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06521-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis found perceived hierarchies between medical and nursing students generate anxiety and inferiority complex, hindering collaboration; medical students also reported "breaks in the communication" and uncertainty about what nursing students needed to know, leaving nursing students feeling left out.

> "Some students reported feeling anxious or fearful when working with others from different professions, especially when hierarchical barriers exist. This can hinder effective collaboration and communication."
