# Permutation tests show early-starting immersion and non-immersion learners are indistinguishable from the earliest learners, while later starters learn significantly more slowly

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Immersion learners with exposure ages of 2, 5, and 8 years were not significantly different from simultaneous bilinguals, while later learners were significantly lower. [→ Hartshorne Joshua K. 2018](#hartshorne-joshua-k-2018)

## Evidence

### Hartshorne Joshua K. 2018

Hartshorne Joshua K., Tenenbaum Joshua B., Pinker Steven. (2018). A critical period for second language acquisition: Evidence from 2/3 million English speakers. Cognition. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6559801/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Permutation analysis (1000 permutations) of learning trajectories against years of experience for immersion learners. Early exposure ages "ps > 0.31" versus later learners "ps < 0.01", as printed.

> "These analyses revealed that the performance curves for immersion learners with average exposure ages of 2, 5, and 8 years were not significantly different from those of simultaneous bilinguals (exposure age = 0; ps > 0.31), while the curves for later learners were significantly lower (ps < 0.01)."

### Hartshorne Joshua K. 2018 (2)

Hartshorne Joshua K., Tenenbaum Joshua B., Pinker Steven. (2018). A critical period for second language acquisition: Evidence from 2/3 million English speakers. Cognition. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6559801/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same permutation analysis for non-immersion learners. Exposure ages 5-11 "indistinguishable from our earliest non-immersion learners (4 years; ps > 0.31)", later learners "ps < 0.01".

> "Similarly, non-immersion learners with ages of exposure of 5–11 years were indistinguishable from our earliest non-immersion learners (4 years; ps > 0.31), whereas later learners learned significantly more slowly (ps < 0.01)."
