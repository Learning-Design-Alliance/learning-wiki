# Age significantly moderates MBI effects on executive functions across all RCTs (larger benefits with older age) and on negative behaviour in active-control RCTs (larger benefits with younger age)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across all 33 RCTs, age significantly moderated improvements in Executive Functions (Q = 5.60, p = .018), with larger effect sizes associated with older age. [→ Dunning 2019](#dunning-2019)
`q3 i?` In the 17 active-control RCTs, age significantly moderated improvements in Negative Behaviour (Q = 5.27, p = .021), with larger effect sizes associated with younger age. [→ Dunning 2019 (2)](#dunning-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Dunning 2019

Dunning, D. L., Griffiths, K., Kuyken, W., Crane, C., Foulkes, L., Parker, J., & Dalgleish, T. (2019). Research Review: The effects of mindfulness-based interventions on cognition and mental health in children and adolescents – a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12980

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random effects meta-regression across all 33 RCTs testing age as a moderator. The review reports age "was a significant moderator of improvements in Executive Functions" favouring older participants; no effect size is printed for the moderation.

> "individual, random effects meta-regressions showed that age was a significant moderator of improvements in Executive Functions (Q = 5.60, p = .018), with larger effect sizes in favour of the MBI associated with older age."

### Dunning 2019 (2)

Dunning, D. L., Griffiths, K., Kuyken, W., Crane, C., Foulkes, L., Parker, J., & Dalgleish, T. (2019). Research Review: The effects of mindfulness-based interventions on cognition and mental health in children and adolescents – a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12980

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-regression within the 17 active-control RCTs. The review reports that "age significantly moderated improvements in Negative Behaviour" with larger effects for younger children, despite the overall null effect in that category.

> "For the 17 RCTs with active control groups, age significantly moderated improvements in Negative Behaviour ( Q = 5.27, p = .021), with larger effect sizes associated with younger age."
