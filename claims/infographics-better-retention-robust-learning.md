# Graphics+text learners showed a higher pretest-to-post-test gain but a higher post-test-to-delayed-loss than infographic learners, suggesting infographics better support robust learning

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Subjects using graphic+text showed a higher gain from pretest to post-test and, at the same time, a higher decrease from post-test to delayed post-test than infographic users. [→ Lyra 2016](#lyra-2016)
`q2 i?` The authors interpret this pattern as evidence that graphic+text "was not as effective as infographics regarding knowledge retention". [→ Lyra 2016 (2)](#lyra-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Lyra 2016

Lyra, K. T., Isotani, S., Reis, R. C. D., Marques, L. B., Pedro, L. Z., Jaques, P. A., & Bitencourt, I. I. (2016). Infographics or Graphics+Text: Which Material is Best for Robust Learning?. Proceedings of the IEEE International Conference on Advanced Learning Technologies (ICALT), to appear. [doi:10.1109/icalt.2016.83](https://doi.org/10.1109/icalt.2016.83)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Between-condition comparison of gain and decrease across stages in the randomized study (Table I: infographic pretest 22.07, post-test 34, delayed 32.07; graphic+text 19.54, 33.92, 30.77); the difference between groups is not statistically significant.

> "subjects using graphic+text showed a higher gain from pretest to post-test and, at the same time, a higher decrease from post-test to delayed post-test, as shown in Fig. 5"

### Lyra 2016 (2)

Lyra, K. T., Isotani, S., Reis, R. C. D., Marques, L. B., Pedro, L. Z., Jaques, P. A., & Bitencourt, I. I. (2016). Infographics or Graphics+Text: Which Material is Best for Robust Learning?. Proceedings of the IEEE International Conference on Advanced Learning Technologies (ICALT), to appear. [doi:10.1109/icalt.2016.83](https://doi.org/10.1109/icalt.2016.83)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Authors' interpretation of the Stage-to-Stage decrease pattern, stated without a significance test of the group difference; n is not reanalyzed for this contrast.

> "even if students acquired more knowledge using graphic+text, this type of visualization was not as effective as infographics regarding knowledge retention"
