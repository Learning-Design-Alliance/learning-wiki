## Evidence

### Lucía de Hoyos 2025

Lucía de Hoyos, Ellen Verhoef, Aysu Okbay, Janne R. Vermeulen, Celeste Figaroa, Miriam Lense, Simon E. Fisher, Reyna L. Gordon & Beate St Pourcain. (2025). Preschool musicality is associated with school-age communication abilities through genes related to rhythmicity. npj Science of Learning 10:39. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00329-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Single-predictor regression analyses in the ALSPAC cohort (N = 4169–6737 per measure) comparing reduced and full models; associations passed the multiple-testing threshold of p < 0.000324, with musicality measures explaining "up to 6% of the phenotypic variance" in communication outcomes. Incremental R2 was the reported statistic; no standardised effect size was printed.

> "Aftercorrecting for covariates (sex, age and the ﬁrst ten ancestry-informative principal components, see Methods), preschool and school-age musicality measures explained up to 6% of the phenotypic variance in school-age communication and social commu- nication measures"

### Lucía de Hoyos 2025 (2)

Lucía de Hoyos, Ellen Verhoef, Aysu Okbay, Janne R. Vermeulen, Celeste Figaroa, Miriam Lense, Simon E. Fisher, Reyna L. Gordon & Beate St Pourcain. (2025). Preschool musicality is associated with school-age communication abilities through genes related to rhythmicity. npj Science of Learning 10:39. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00329-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

The same regression screen in ALSPAC showed the two infant nursery-rhyme measures (0.5 and 1.5 years) fell below 1% explained variance; the article reports they were "not taken forward in subsequent analysis" and were excluded from later SEM models.

> "infant musicality measures (nursery rhymes) explained less than 1% of the variance in the outcome measures (Supplementary Fig. 1) and were, thus, not taken forward in subsequent analysis."
