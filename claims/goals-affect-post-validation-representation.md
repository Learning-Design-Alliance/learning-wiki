## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The combined pattern—goal effects on overall reading times and on memory for congruent but not incongruent targets, with no goal effects on validation-specific processing—suggests reading goals influence representation-building after inconsistency detection rather than validation itself. [→ Moort 2022](#moort-2022)

## Evidence

### Moort 2022

Moort, M. L. van, Koornneef, A. W., Wilderjans, T. F., & Broek, P. W. van den. (2022). Validation processes and reading purpose: Is validation against knowledge and prior text influenced by reading goal? Journal of Educational Psychology, 114(7), 1533-1552. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000769

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

This is the authors' interpretation (type e) of the combined online and offline results of the self-paced reading experiment with recognition memory testing the next day. The article states that "reading goals may not inﬂuence validation processes directly but affect subsequent representation-building processes after the detection of an (in)consistency."

> "These results suggest that read-ing goals may not inﬂuence validation processes directly but affect subsequent representation-building processes after the detection of an (in)consistency— particularly in the case of incongruencies with prior text."
