# Students perceived text input as superior for accuracy, control, and editability, with transcription errors a key voice drawback

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 studies · 2 qualitative `r2`, 1 associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### accuracy-category

Kaitlin Riegel, Yan Cathy Hua, Paul Denny, Victor-Alexandru Pădurean, Juho Leinonen, James Prather, and Adish Singla. 2026. Say What? Examining Text and Voice Input Modalities for Prompt-Based Programming in Computing Education. Proceedings of the 31st ACM Conference on Innovation and Technology in Computer Science Education V. 1 (ITiCSE 2026). https://doi.org/10.1145/3803400.3809397

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Aspect-based sentiment analysis of open-ended reflection responses (890 comment pieces; inter-rater F1 = 0.65 on 156 independently annotated entries) found "the ability to input prompts with control and accuracy" was the dominant category, heavily favouring text; Table 2 shows 53 positive vs 2 negative text sentiments and 6 positive vs 51 negative voice sentiments in this category.

> "The dominant category that emerged was the ability to input prompts with control and accuracy, heavily in favour of text-based prompting."

### editability-category

Kaitlin Riegel, Yan Cathy Hua, Paul Denny, Victor-Alexandru Pădurean, Juho Leinonen, James Prather, and Adish Singla. 2026. Say What? Examining Text and Voice Input Modalities for Prompt-Based Programming in Computing Education. Proceedings of the 31st ACM Conference on Innovation and Technology in Computer Science Education V. 1 (ITiCSE 2026). https://doi.org/10.1145/3803400.3809397

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The ABSA analysis of reflection responses identified editability as a major advantage of text; the article reports that "voice-based revision was perceived strictly negatively" (Table 2: 47 positive vs 2 negative for text; 0 positive vs 12 negative for voice in this category).

> "The ability to edit and review text prompts before submitting was a major reported advantage."

### nnes

Kaitlin Riegel, Yan Cathy Hua, Paul Denny, Victor-Alexandru Pădurean, Juho Leinonen, James Prather, and Adish Singla. 2026. Say What? Examining Text and Voice Input Modalities for Prompt-Based Programming in Computing Education. Proceedings of the 31st ACM Conference on Innovation and Technology in Computer Science Education V. 1 (ITiCSE 2026). https://doi.org/10.1145/3803400.3809397

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

A post-hoc analysis of all 890 comment pieces using keyword search, LLM categorisation, and human consolidation identified 17 entries that "explicitly mentioned NNES status as a reason for preferring text input"; 15 expressed concerns about their accent being accurately recognised.

> "Using a combination of keyword search, LLM categorisation (pre-trained Qwen3.5-4B in non-thinking mode [38]), and human consolidation, we identified 17 entries that explic- itly mentioned NNES status as a reason for preferring text input."
