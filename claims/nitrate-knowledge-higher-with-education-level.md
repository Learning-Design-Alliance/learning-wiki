# Nitrate knowledge, measured by a 23-point index, was significantly greater in participants with a PhD and tended to be greater in those with a masters degree compared with undergraduate-level qualifications

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Median Nitrate Knowledge Index scores differed significantly by education group (undergraduate 10 (2-14); masters 13 (8-15); PhD 13 (9-17); p=0.01), with post hoc tests showing PhD greater than undergraduate (p=0.01) and a masters-versus-undergraduate trend (p=0.054). ## Evidence

### Shannon Oliver M 2019

Shannon Oliver M, Grisotto Giorgia, Babateen Abrar, McGrattan Andrea, Brandt Kirsten, Mathers John C, Siervo Mario. (2019). Knowledge and beliefs about dietary inorganic nitrate among UK-based nutrition professionals: development and application of the KINDS online questionnaire. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2019-030719

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Kruskal-Wallis comparison of Nitrate Knowledge Index scores across three education groups (n=124 with qualification data), Results education-differences section. The article reports medians that "were significantly different between groups (p=0.01)", with Bonferroni-adjusted post hoc PhD-versus-undergraduate p=0.01 and masters trend p=0.054. No effect size reported.

> "The median (IQR) scores for the Nitrate Knowledge Index for undergraduate, masters and PhD-level participants were 10,2–14 13,8–15 13,9–17 and were significantly different between groups (p=0.01)."
