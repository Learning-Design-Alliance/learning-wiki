# Both treatment groups improved near within domain transfer, but the One Model Group's benefit depended on having a content-expert teacher while the Two Models Group's did not

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1`–`r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Both groups improved on the butterfly effect near transfer item from pretest to posttest, with no main effect of Condition. [→ Jacobson 2020](#jacobson-2020)
`q3 i2` A Condition × Teacher interaction arose solely because the One Model Group benefited from the content-expert teacher on near transfer, whereas the Two Models Group showed no teacher-expertise effect. [→ Jacobson 2020 (2)](#jacobson-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Jacobson 2020

Jacobson, Michael, Goldwater, Micah, Markauskaite, Lina, Lai, Polly, Kapur, Manu, Roberts, Gareth, & Hilton, Courtney. (2020). Schema abstraction with productive failure and analogical comparison: Learning designs for far across domain transfer. Learning and Instruction, 65, Article number: 101222. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2019.101222

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Linear mixed-model analysis of the butterfly effect near within domain transfer item in the quasi-experimental study. Both groups improved significantly by posttest regardless of whether they learned with one or two models.

> "there was a main effect for Time, F(1,92) = 7.585, p = 0.007, η2 = .076, but no main effect for Condition, F(1,92) = 0.425 , p = 0.516, η 2 = .005, nor an interaction between Condition and Time, F(1,92) = 0.067 , p = 0.796, η2 = .001."

### Jacobson 2020 (2)

Jacobson, Michael, Goldwater, Micah, Markauskaite, Lina, Lai, Polly, Kapur, Manu, Roberts, Gareth, & Hilton, Courtney. (2020). Schema abstraction with productive failure and analogical comparison: Learning designs for far across domain transfer. Learning and Instruction, 65, Article number: 101222. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2019.101222

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

The Condition by Teacher Content Expertise interaction on near transfer was significant (F(1,92) = 3.967, p = 0.049, η2 = .041); follow-up comparisons showed the difference was "solely due to" teacher expertise within the One Model Group, while the Two Models Group "showed no effect of teacher expertise".

> "There was a pairwise difference between the content-expert and the non-expert for participants in the One Model Group (M = 0.283, SE = 0.127), t = 2.23, p = 0.028, with students exposed to the content-expert teacher showing higher performance for the near within domain transfer."
