## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Learners from experimental schools preferred a learner-centred group approach and emphasised verbal interaction and peer explanations; control school learners reported they were never given opportunities to discuss mathematics ideas. [→ Dhlamini 2013](#dhlamini-2013)

## Evidence

### Dhlamini 2013

Dhlamini, J., & Mogari, D. (2013). The effect of a group approach on the performance of high school mathematics learners. Pythagoras, 34(2), Art. #198. https://doi.org/10.4102/pythagoras.v34i2.198

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-intervention semi-structured interviews with 17 of 18 purposively sampled learners (two per school, all performance categories and involvement levels represented). Learner 1 said she 'gets it better' when the explanation came from a fellow group member; control learners reported didactic, teacher-dominated teaching.

> "Responses suggest that learners preferred a learner-centred approach (group approach) in which they engage in opportunities to share and discuss their mathematics ideas."
