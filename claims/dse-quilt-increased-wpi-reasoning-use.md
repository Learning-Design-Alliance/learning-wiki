# Undergraduates used WPI reasoning more often after than before the DSE QuILT on three of four DSE polarizer questions, and graduates on two of four, in one uncontrolled pre-post study; Cramer's V values were mostly medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` MacNemar's tests showed undergraduates were significantly more likely to provide WPI reasoning after the DSE QuILT on three of four questions and graduates on two of four, with Cramer's V effect sizes mostly medium (e.g., 0.62, 0.43). [→ Alexandru Maries 2017](#alexandru-maries-2017)

## Evidence

### Alexandru Maries 2017

Alexandru Maries, Ryan Sayer, and Chandralekha Singh. (2017). Effectiveness of interactive tutorials in promoting “which-path” information reasoning in advanced quantum mechanics. Physical Review Physics Education Research 13, 020115. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.13.020115

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

MacNemar's tests on the percentage of students using WPI reasoning among those providing reasoning, before vs after the DSE QuILT (46 UG, 59 GS). Printed effect sizes include UG Q2 V=0.62 (p<0.001) and GS Q3 V=0.43 (p=0.016); several comparisons were not significant (e.g., UG Q3 p=0.057).

> "The p values listed in Table VI show that undergraduate students were statistically significantly more likely to provide reasoning related to WPI after working on the DSE QuILT on three out of the four questions, and for the graduate students it was two out of the four questions. The effect sizes (Cramer 's V) shown in Table VI suggest that for most questions the magnitude of the effect is medium."
