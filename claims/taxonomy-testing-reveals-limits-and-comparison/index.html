q2 · i? · design · r2

Multi-group dialogic testing of the taxonomy against five concrete GenAI learning activity examples during a collaborative workshop, followed by written dialogue. Representing all five examples diagrammatically showed that "the clear difference between the two apparently similar learning activities" concerned teacher pre-configuring and student reflection.

"Representing all examples (Figs. 3, 4, 5, 6, 7) thus shows the clear difference between the two apparently similar learning activities in Examples 1 and 2, as regards teacher pre-configuring of GenAI and student reflection on GenAI decision-making."

q1 · i? · design · r2

Authors' theoretical reflection during the postdigital dialogue on the affordances and limits of the taxonomy as an epistemic form. The taxonomy's left-to-right hierarchical layout "is not designed to capture more complex relationships" such as the recursive, iterative engagement observed in Examples 1 and 2. They suggest a circular or wheel structure as an alternative.

"A taxonomy, as an epistemic form, is designed to represent a hierarchical struc- ture and hierarchical relationships between its elements; it is not designed to cap- ture more complex relationships."
