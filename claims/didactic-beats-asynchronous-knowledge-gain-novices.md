# Didactic lectures produced greater knowledge gain than asynchronous computer-based modules for novice learners in an acute care course

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Senior medical students gained more knowledge from didactic instruction (mean gain 28.39% ± 18.06) than from asynchronous modules (9.93% ± 23.22), a significant difference. [→ Jordan Jaime 2013](#jordan-jaime-2013)

## Evidence

### Jordan Jaime 2013

Jordan Jaime, Jalali Azadeh, Clarke Samuel, Dyne Pamela, Spector Tahlia, Coates Wendy. (2013). Asynchronous vs didactic education: it's too early to throw in the towel on tradition. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-105

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Prospective observational quasi-experimental study of 44 fourth-year medical students; dependent t-test of mean gain scores showed didactic instruction outperformed asynchronous modules, "p = 0.0001". No effect size printed.

> "Mean gain in knowledge was 28.39% ± 18.06 for didactic instruction and 9.93% ± 23.22 for asynchronous instruction. A two-tailed dependent t-test revealed a statistically significant mean difference between didactic and asynchronous modalities of 18.46% with 95% CI [10.40 to 26.50]; p = 0.0001."
