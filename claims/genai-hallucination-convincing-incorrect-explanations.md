## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Ivory 2026

Ivory, M., Finnerty, S., Dunn, K., Philpot, R., & Warmelink, L. (2026). The Integrity of Psychology Assessments in the AI Age: A Critical Examination. Journal of Academic Ethics. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10805-026-09729-0

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

In the paper review assessment (PSYC305), basic prompting reviewed a nonexistent paper with an unresolvable DOI; with the paper uploaded, ChatGPT fabricated the sample size, stating 378 participants whereas the paper reported 329.

> "Further Gen-AI is prone to the generation of incorrect information and the hallucination of sources and references that do not exist."

### Ivory 2026 (2)

Ivory, M., Finnerty, S., Dunn, K., Philpot, R., & Warmelink, L. (2026). The Integrity of Psychology Assessments in the AI Age: A Critical Examination. Journal of Academic Ethics. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10805-026-09729-0

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Observation from the WBA transcripts (e.g. a developmental psychology question in PSYC205 WBA 6 where the correct answer was (a) but ChatGPT answered 'd. All of the above' with a plausible justification); the authors warn students without prior knowledge may become misinformed.

> "when the Gen-AI provided incorrect answers, the associated explanations were often convincing, particularly on content outside the expertise of the researchers."
