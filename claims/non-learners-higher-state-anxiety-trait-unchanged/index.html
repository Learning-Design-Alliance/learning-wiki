Evidence¶

Marino 2025¶

Marino, C. E., Rjabtsenkov, P., Sharp, C., Ali, Z., Pineda, E., Bavdekar, S. Y., Garg, T., Jordan, K., Halvorsen, M., Aponte, C., Blue, J., Zhu, X. & Suarez-Jimenez, B. (2025). Using virtual reality to study spatial mapping and threat learning. npj Science of Learning 10:14. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00305-6

q2 · i2 · associational · r1

Pre/post STAI Form Y-1 state anxiety was compared with a 2 × 2 × 3 ANOVA (time, group, reinforcement rate) in the 70-participant VR study. Significant main effects of group (ηp² = 0.10) and time (ηp² = 0.14) were found; "non-learners, compared to learners, had signiﬁcantly higher combined state anxiety scores".

"We found that non-learners, compared to learners, had signiﬁcantly higher combined state anxiety scores (p =2×1 0 -4, 95%CI[3.165, 10.105])."

Marino 2025 (2)¶

Marino, C. E., Rjabtsenkov, P., Sharp, C., Ali, Z., Pineda, E., Bavdekar, S. Y., Garg, T., Jordan, K., Halvorsen, M., Aponte, C., Blue, J., Zhu, X. & Suarez-Jimenez, B. (2025). Using virtual reality to study spatial mapping and threat learning. npj Science of Learning 10:14. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00305-6

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Trait anxiety (STAI Y-2) was compared between groups with a 2 × 3 ANOVA in the same sample; scores ranged from 21.00 to 60.00 with mean = 36.39 and SD = 9.13, and "we found no signiﬁcant differences in trait anxiety scores". No effect size was printed.

"However, we found no signiﬁcant differences in trait anxiety scores. The trait anxiety scores ranged from 21.00 to 60.00, with a mean = 36.39 and SD = 9.13."
