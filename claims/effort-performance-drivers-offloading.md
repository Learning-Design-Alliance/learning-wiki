# Both performance-oriented goals and effort minimization motivate offloading, and effort's effect disappears when performance monitoring is considered simultaneously

## Evidence

### Yufei Guo & Qun Ye 2026

Yufei Guo & Qun Ye. (2026). Meta-cognitive insights into cognitive offloading: mechanisms, interventions, and educational implications. Humanities and Social Sciences Communications. https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-026-06621-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Sachdeva and Gilbert's (2020) experiment incentivized self-reliant memorization by telling participants they would receive greater rewards for using their own efforts. Higher rewards reduced offloading, supporting the effort-minimization hypothesis alongside performance concerns.

> "The results showed that higher task rewards reduced of ﬂoading tendencies, demonstrating that motivational incentives can override effort-minimization pre- ferences (Clay et al., 2022; Milyavskaya et al., 2019)."

### Yufei Guo & Qun Ye 2026 (2)

Yufei Guo & Qun Ye. (2026). Meta-cognitive insights into cognitive offloading: mechanisms, interventions, and educational implications. Humanities and Social Sciences Communications. https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-026-06621-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Gilbert et al. (2020, Experiment 3), that in memory research effort typically explains offloading choices when examined in isolation, but its effect vanishes when performance monitoring is modeled jointly. The review attributes this to effort transforming into performance pursuit.

> "However, when simultaneously considering performance monitoring and effort level explanations, effort's effect disappears (Gilbert et al., 2020, Experiment 3)."
