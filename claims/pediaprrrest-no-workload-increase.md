# Using the PediAppRREST app was not associated with increased team leaders' perceived workload compared with usual practice

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Team leaders' median NASA RTLX workload scores did not differ significantly between the app group and the control group (P=.57). [→ Corazza F 2020](#corazza-f-2020)

## Evidence

### Corazza F 2020

Corazza F, Snijders D, Arpone M, Stritoni V, Martinolli F, Daverio M, Losi MG, Soldi L, Tesauri F, Da Dalt L, Bressan S. (2020). Development and Usability of a Novel Interactive Tablet App (PediAppRREST) to Support the Management of Pediatric Cardiac Arrest: Pilot High-Fidelity Simulation-Based Study. JMIR mHealth and uHealth. https://doi.org/10.2196/19070

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Primary-outcome workload measurement in the same single-center nonrandomized simulation pilot; team leaders rated perceived workload with the NASA RTLX after managing a nonshockable PCA scenario with (11 teams) or without (5 teams) the app. The article reports the workload "was comparable between the 2 groups" with median scores of 67.5 versus 66.7 and P=.57; no effect size is printed.

> "Team leaders’ perceived workload was comparable between the 2 groups; median NASA RTLX score was 67.5 (IQR 65.0-81.7) for the control group and 66.7 (IQR 54.2-76.7) for the intervention group (P=.57)."
