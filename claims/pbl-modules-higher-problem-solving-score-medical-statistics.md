# Medical students given access to problem-based learning modules within a blended learning course had a significantly higher problem solving score than students in the blended-learning-only course

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In a randomized controlled pilot trial with 53 medical students, students with access to PBL modules within the blended learning course had a significantly higher problem solving score (p = 0.012; effect size 0.69). [→ Bukumiric Zoran 2022](#bukumiric-zoran-2022)

## Evidence

### Bukumiric Zoran 2022

Bukumiric Zoran, Ilic Aleksandra, Pajcin Mirjana, Srebro Dragana, Milicevic Sasa, Spaic Dragan, Markovic Nenad, Corac Aleksandar. (2022). Effects of problem-based learning modules within blended learning courses in medical statistics – A randomized controlled pilot study. PLoS ONE 17(1): e0263015. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0263015

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled pilot trial of 53 third-year medical students randomized to hPBL (n = 26) or BL (n = 27); Table 3 reports problem solving score means 22.5±1.7 (hPBL) vs 21.1±2.3 (BL). The article reports a "significantly higher problem solving score" with effect size 0.69.

> "Students in hPBL group had a significantly higher problem solving score (p = 0.012, effect size 0.69) and total medical statistics score (p = 0.046, effect size 0.57) (Table 3)."
