## Subclaims
`q2 i?` TAs frequently chose answer choices incorporating a correct idea but missing another as the MCI, a strategy that often led them to choices rare among students (e.g., Q1, Q2, Q14). [→ Nafis I. Karim 2018](#nafis-i-karim-2018)

## Evidence

### Nafis I. Karim 2018

Nafis I. Karim, Alexandru Maries, and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Exploring one aspect of pedagogical content knowledge of teaching assistants using the Conceptual Survey of Electricity and Magnetism. Physical Review Physics Education Research 14, 010117. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.14.010117

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of TA MCI selections and think-aloud interviews across CSEM questions. The article reports that selecting the choice most similar to the correct answer "often misled the TAs into selecting an answer choice that was not very common," citing Q1, Q2, and Q14 as examples.

> "While sometimes using this strategy to identify the MCI may provide a reasonable answer choice (i.e., one that is fairly common among students), it often misled the TAs into selecting an answer choice that was not very common—as was the case on Q1, Q2, and Q14 (and other questions that are discussed below)."
