## Evidence

### Stegers-Jager K. M. 2016

Stegers-Jager K. M., Brommet F. N., Themmen A. P. N. (2016). Ethnic and social disparities in different types of examinations in undergraduate pre-clinical training. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-016-9676-7

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Detailed station-level analysis of the second-year OSCE in the cohort study. The results report Dutch students more likely to pass the communication station (92 %) than each of the three non-Western minority groups, with unadjusted ORs and confidence intervals as printed. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "On the communication station, Dutch students were more likely to pass (92 %) than Turkish/Moroccan/African students (79 %; unadjusted OR 0.35, 95 % CI 0.21–0.60), Surinamese/Antillean (84 %; unadjusted OR 0.49, 95 % CI 0.26–0.93) and Asian students (82 %; unadjusted OR 0.40, 95 % CI 0.24–0.68)."

### Stegers-Jager K. M. 2016 (2)

Stegers-Jager K. M., Brommet F. N., Themmen A. P. N. (2016). Ethnic and social disparities in different types of examinations in undergraduate pre-clinical training. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-016-9676-7

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Station-level analysis of the third-year OSCE. The results report that the different minority groups underperformed on different stations, with printed pass rates and unadjusted ORs; Surinamese/Antillean students underperformed only on physical examination and Western students only on communication. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "Turkish/Moroccan/African students underperformed compared with Dutch students only on physical examination in neurology (pass rate 34 vs 49 %, unadjusted OR 0.55, 95 % CI 0.32–0.94), while Asian students underperformed on history taking (pass rate: 62 vs 78 %; unadjusted OR 0.47, 95 % CI 0.29–0.76)"
