## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Teachers with master's degrees or above had higher initial adherence than teachers with Associates or High School degrees, a difference the article describes as substantial by Cohen's d without printing its value. [→ Sutherland et al. 2018](#sutherland-et-al-2018)
`q3 i?` Teachers with Associates or High School degrees had lower initial competence than teachers with Bachelor's or master's degrees or higher, with differences the article describes as moderate by Cohen's d without printing values. [→ Sutherland et al. 2018 (2)](#sutherland-et-al-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Sutherland et al. 2018

Kevin S. Sutherland, Maureen A. Conroy, Bryce D. McLeod, James Algina. (2018). Factors Associated with Teacher Delivery of a Classroom-Based Tier 2 Prevention Program. Prevention Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11121-017-0832-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Structural regression of the adherence status latent variable on pretest predictors in the 92-teacher growth model. The article reports the master's-or-above contrast was significant (z = 3.247, p = .001) and "substantial" by Cohen's d, whose value is not printed.

> "teachers with master ’s degrees or above had higher adherence at the beginning of the intervention than did teachers with Associates or High School degrees. Cohen’s d indicates the difference between the two groups was substantial."

### Sutherland et al. 2018 (2)

Kevin S. Sutherland, Maureen A. Conroy, Bryce D. McLeod, James Algina. (2018). Factors Associated with Teacher Delivery of a Classroom-Based Tier 2 Prevention Program. Prevention Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11121-017-0832-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Structural regression of the competence status latent variable in the same 92-teacher growth model. Both education contrasts were significant (Bachelor's z = 2.535, p = .011; master's z = 2.148, p = .032) with Cohen's d values described as moderate but not printed.

> "the regression coeffi- cients for the two education variables were significant and positive indicating that teachers with Associates or High School degrees had lower competence at the beginning of the intervention than did teachers with Bachelor degrees or teachers with master ’s degrees or higher. Cohen’ s d indicates that the differences were moderate."
