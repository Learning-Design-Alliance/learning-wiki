# Effect sizes for achievement, retention and attitude were heterogeneous across studies, prompting the shift from fixed to random effects modelling

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Cochran's Q tests exceeded the chi-square critical values for all three outcomes, indicating heterogeneous distributions of effect sizes. [→ Özgür Anil 2015](#ozgur-anil-2015)
`q3 i?` The authors chose the random effects model to eliminate errors stemming from the heterogeneous quality of the sample. [→ Özgür Anil 2015 (2)](#ozgur-anil-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Özgür Anil 2015

Özgür Anil, Veli Batdi. (2015). A Comparative Meta-Analysis of 5E and Traditional Approaches in Turkey. Journal of Education and Training Studies. https://doi.org/10.11114/jets.v3i6.1038

`q3 · i?`

Heterogeneity testing for the achievement studies: Q=93.709 with 12 degrees of freedom against a critical χ2 value of 21.026 at 95% significance. The article reports the same pattern for retention (Q=41.535) and attitude (Q=42.302).

> "Thus, the critical value of χ2 distribution of (χ2(0.95)=21.026<93.709) was seen to exceed the Q-statistic. This finding indicates that the distribution of the studies' effect sizes is heterogeneous."

### Özgür Anil 2015 (2)

Özgür Anil, Veli Batdi. (2015). A Comparative Meta-Analysis of 5E and Traditional Approaches in Turkey. Journal of Education and Training Studies. https://doi.org/10.11114/jets.v3i6.1038

`q3 · i?`

The article's stated methodological decision: because the effect size distributions were heterogeneous, pooled estimates were computed under the random effects model rather than the fixed effects model for all three outcomes.

> "For this reason, it was thought that the analysis should be performed not with a fixed effects model, but according to the random effects model, so tha t errors stemming from the heterogeneous quality of the sample could be eliminated."
