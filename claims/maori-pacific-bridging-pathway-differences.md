## Evidence

### Wikaire Erena 2016

Wikaire Erena, Curtis Elana, Cormack Donna, Jiang Yannan, McMillan Louise, Loto Rob, Reid Papaarangi. (2016). Patterns of privilege: A total cohort analysis of admission and academic outcomes for Māori, Pacific and non-Māori non-Pacific health professional students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0782-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational cohort analysis of admission pathway variables for the 2686-student cohort. The article attributes much of this to the Certificate in Health Sciences, a Māori and Pacific specific bridging programme, completed by 29% of Māori and 39% of Pacific students.

> "significantly fewer nMnP students had completed a bridging foundation programme (5 %) compared to half of Pacific students (50 %, p < 0.0001) and two-fifths of Māori students (43 %, p < 0.0001)."

### Wikaire Erena 2016 (2)

Wikaire Erena, Curtis Elana, Cormack Donna, Jiang Yannan, McMillan Louise, Loto Rob, Reid Papaarangi. (2016). Patterns of privilege: A total cohort analysis of admission and academic outcomes for Māori, Pacific and non-Māori non-Pacific health professional students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0782-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same observational analysis of type of admission (school leaver vs alternative admission) in the 2002–2012 cohort, with chi-square tests against the nMnP reference group.

> "A significantly higher proportion of nMnP students (72 %) had enrolled in bachelor level study as direct school leavers compared to less than half of Māori students (49 %, p < 0.0001) and just over one third of Pacific students (39 %, p < 0.0001)."
