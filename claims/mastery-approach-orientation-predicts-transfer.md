# Existing mastery-approach orientation predicts likelihood of transfer, with each unit increase raising transfer odds by 45% among students who received a worked example

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among students who received the worked example, higher initial mastery-approach orientation predicted higher odds of solving the transfer problem (odds ratio 1.45 per unit). [→ Belenky 2012](#belenky-2012)

## Evidence

### Belenky 2012

Daniel M. Belenky and Timothy J. Nokes-Malach. (2012). Motivation and Transfer: The Role of Mastery-Approach Goals in Preparation for Future Learning. The Journal of the Learning Sciences. https://doi.org/10.1080/10508406.2011.651232

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Binary logistic regression on the 52 worked-example participants in the laboratory experiment, with all four initial achievement goal orientations as predictors of solving the transfer item. Only mastery-approach was significant: "the predicted odds of a student successfully transferring became 45% higher" per unit. No standardized effect size was printed.

> "In this model only the mastery-approach term was significant, B = 0.37, Exp (B) = 1.45, Wald's χ 2(1, N = 52) = 7.44, p \< .05. This indicates that for every unit increase in mastery-approach score, the predicted odds of a student successfully transferring became 45% higher"
