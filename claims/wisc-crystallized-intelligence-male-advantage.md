# Males show a small but significant advantage on WISC crystallized intelligence (gC, d = 0.14), driven mainly by the Information subtest

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` The pooled gC effect significantly favours males (d = 0.14), roughly 2 IQ points, independent of WISC version. [→ D. Giofrè · K. Allen · E. Toffalini · S. Caviola 2022](#d-giofre-k-allen-e-toffalini-s-caviola-2022)

## Evidence

### D. Giofrè · K. Allen · E. Toffalini · S. Caviola 2022

D. Giofrè · K. Allen · E. Toffalini · S. Caviola. (2022). The Impasse on Gender Differences in Intelligence: a Meta-Analysis on WISC Batteries. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-022-09705-1

`q3 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analysis of 41 studies (72 samples) for the gC factor; the standardization-sample estimate was virtually the same (d = 0.13), and the version moderator was not significant.

> "The estimated mean effect size was significant but small, with higher scores in males than in females, d = 0.14 [0.08, 0.19], p < 0.001."

### D. Giofrè · K. Allen · E. Toffalini · S. Caviola 2022 (2)

D. Giofrè · K. Allen · E. Toffalini · S. Caviola. (2022). The Impasse on Gender Differences in Intelligence: a Meta-Analysis on WISC Batteries. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-022-09705-1

`q3 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Subtest-level meta-analysis (Table 2) of gC subtests; Vocabulary (d = 0.138) and Comprehension (d = 0.096) were small but significant, Similarities was not significant.

> "a notable discrepancy emerged between Information, which presented the largest estimated difference in favour of males, d = 0.26 [0.17, 0.36], and the other subtests, which presented very small to negligible estimated effect sizes"
