## Evidence

### Kyle M. Whitcomb 2020

Kyle M. Whitcomb, Matthew W. Guthrie, Chandralekha Singh, and Zhongzhou Chen. (2020). Toward more accurate measurement of the impact of online instructional design on students' ability to transfer physics problem-solving skills. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2007.15051](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2007.15051)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Propensity-score-matched comparison (MatchIt, exam scores as covariates) of 2018 and 2017 students with 0-1 brief attempts on modules 2-5 of both sequences, tested with Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted Fisher's exact tests. Differences were "similar in nature but larger in magnitude" than the earlier study; significant differences appeared on Tutorial Pre and Example 1 Pre in the rotational kinematics sequence, while angular momentum differences were less prominent, possibly due to high success rates in both samples.

> "The differences are similar in nature but larger in magnitude compared to what was observed in our earlier study that did not consider alternative learning strategies [10], suggesting that the earlier study could have underestimated the transfer ability of the student population due to some students adopting performance-avoidance goals."
