## Evidence

### O’Dea R. E. 2018

O’Dea R. E., Lagisz M., Jennions M. D. & Nakagawa S. (2018). Gender differences in individual variation in academic grades fail to fit expected patterns for STEM. Nature Communications. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-06292-0

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

School-subset meta-regression with subject type as moderator (lnRR non-STEM 0.075, CI 0.049 to 0.102; STEM 0.031, CI 0.011 to 0.051). The article reports the girls' "advantage of 7.8% in mean grades in non-STEM was more than double their 3.1% advantage in STEM".

> "Girls’ significant advantage of 7.8% in mean grades in non-STEM was more than double their 3.1% advantage in STEM."

### O’Dea R. E. 2018 (2)

O’Dea R. E., Lagisz M., Jennions M. D. & Nakagawa S. (2018). Gender differences in individual variation in academic grades fail to fit expected patterns for STEM. Nature Communications. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-06292-0

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

School-subset meta-regression of lnCVR by subject (STEM: lnCVR −0.079, CI −0.115 to −0.043; non-STEM: −0.149, CI −0.199 to −0.099). The article reports "the sexes were more similar in STEM than non-STEM subjects" in grade variability.

> "Variation in grades among girls was significantly lower than that among boys in every subject type, but the sexes were more similar in STEM than non-STEM subjects"
