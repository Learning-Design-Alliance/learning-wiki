## Evidence

### Fernando Núñez-Regueiro 2024

Fernando Núñez-Regueiro, Natacha Boissicat, Fanny Gimbert, Céline Pobel-Burtin, Marie-Caroline Croset, Marie-Line Bosse, and Cécile Nurra. (2024). Involving the Body to Improve Letter Knowledge and Script: An Experimental Study in French Kindergarten. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09923-9

`q4 · i2` · `causal · r3`

Interaction model in the RCT: positive interaction between arm-body training and pre-test dictation level (b = 0.754, OR = 2.215, d = .438, p = .039); group differences in propensity flipped sign at roughly ¼ SD around the pretest mean.

> "the effect of arm-body training on acquisitions in letters written under dictation (as identified before, Table 3) was detrimental for low-skilled students only, and was beneficial for high-skilled students."

### Fernando Núñez-Regueiro 2024 (2)

Fernando Núñez-Regueiro, Natacha Boissicat, Fanny Gimbert, Céline Pobel-Burtin, Marie-Caroline Croset, Marie-Line Bosse, and Cécile Nurra. (2024). Involving the Body to Improve Letter Knowledge and Script: An Experimental Study in French Kindergarten. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09923-9

`q4 · i1` · `causal · r3`

Two further interaction effects on overall legibility (chi2(2) = 6.9, p = .031); graphical projections showed the arm-training advantage over pencil-and-paper peaked for students about 1 SD below the pretest mean.

> "students exposed to pencil-and-paper training (b = -0.509, OR = 0.601, d = -.280, p = .022) and arm-body training (b = 0.481, OR = 1.618, d = .265, p = .023) experienced decreased acquisitions on this skill compared to the other groups, but only when their initial skills level were lower-than-average."
