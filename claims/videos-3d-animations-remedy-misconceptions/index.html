Evidence¶

Unal Cakiroglu 2017¶

Unal Cakiroglu, Huseyin Yilmaz. (2017). Using Videos and 3D Animations for Conceptual Learning in Basic Computer Units. Contemporary Educational Technology, 8(4), 390-405. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1158165

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Pretest-posttest comparison within both groups of the quasi-experiment (CG n = 15, HG n = 19). The article reports "significant impact in remedying misconceptions" with no effect size printed; descriptive gains included harddisk correct answers rising from 5 to 29 in CG and 17 to 44 in HG.

"The change in pre -test and post-test results of the CG and HG students showed that us ing videos and 3D animations both in the classroom and in the homework activities provided significant impact in remedying misconceptions."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • The quantity of videos and 3D animations was related to success in remedying misconceptions: where materials were quite limited, incorrect or partly true answers remained high — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Vocational high school students held various misconceptions about basic computer system units, including processor, main board, ethernet card, video card, sound card, memory, hard disk, optical disks, and monitor — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Students reported that videos and 3D animations facilitated conceptual understanding via concretization, pausing, slowing down, replaying, and enlarging features — related
  • Using videos and 3D animations in classroom activities versus as homework produced no statistically significant difference in post-test conceptual understanding scores — related
