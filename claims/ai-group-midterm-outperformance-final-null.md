## Evidence

### Márton Benedek and Balázs R. Sziklai 2025

Márton Benedek and Balázs R. Sziklai. (2025). Impact of AI Tools on Learning Outcomes: Decreasing Knowledge and Over-Reliance. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2510.16019](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2510.16019)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Midterm test scores from the randomized course experiment, reported in the Results section. The authors note "we have no baseline to compare the two groups to" and that the gap-widening trend on the second midterm may reflect changes in material difficulty.

> "The AI group outperformed the offline group in the first and second midterm. From this data alone we should not draw any deep conclusions as we have no baseline to compare the two groups to."

### Márton Benedek and Balázs R. Sziklai 2025 (2)

Márton Benedek and Balázs R. Sziklai. (2025). Impact of AI Tools on Learning Outcomes: Decreasing Knowledge and Over-Reliance. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2510.16019](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2510.16019)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Final exam scores from the same randomized course experiment, taken after both groups were permitted AI assistance. The article reports the offline group outperformed the AI group but "the difference is not statistically significant"; equivalence was not tested.

> "It is also notable, that the trend breaks on the final exam, where the offline group (armed with AI assistance) outperformed the AI group, although the difference is not statistically significant."
