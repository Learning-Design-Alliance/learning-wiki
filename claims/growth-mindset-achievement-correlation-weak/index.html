Evidence¶

Victoria F. Sisk 2018¶

Victoria F. Sisk, Alexander P. Burgoyne, Jingze Sun, Jennifer L. Butler, and Brooke N. Macnamara. (2018). To What Extent and Under Which Circumstances Are Growth Mind-Sets Important to Academic Achievement? Two Meta-Analyses. Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617739704

q3 · i1 · quant-synthesis · r3

Random-effects meta-analysis of 273 effect sizes from 162 independent samples (N = 365,915) found the average correlation between growth mind-set and achievement was "r = .10, 95% confidence interval (CI) = [.08, .13], p < .001". Assuming mind-set measure reliability of .80, the estimate was r = .12.

"The meta-analytic average correlation (i.e., the average of various population effects) between growth mind-set and academic achievement is r = .10, 95% confidence interval (CI) = [.08, .13], p < .001."

  • Developmental stage moderates the mind-set–achievement correlation, which is nonsignificant for adults — related
  • Academic risk status does not significantly moderate the mind-set–achievement correlation — related
  • Mind-set interventions show weak overall effects on achievement, with some support that low-SES or academically at-risk students might benefit — related
  • Socioeconomic status does not significantly moderate the mind-set–achievement correlation — related
  • Type of achievement measure moderates the correlation only when laboratory measures are excluded; course-exam correlation drops to r = .04 — related
