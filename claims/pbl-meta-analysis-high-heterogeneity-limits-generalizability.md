## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Heterogeneity was high across studies (knowledge I2 = 95%; skill I2 = 96%), and authors state mean effect sizes are not broadly generalizable. [→ Wang 2016](#wang-2016)

## Evidence

### Wang 2016

Wang, J., Xu, Y., Liu, X., Xiong, W., Xie, J., & Zhao, J. (2016). Assessing the effectiveness of problem-based learning in physical diagnostics education in China: a meta-analysis. Scientific Reports. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5093758/

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Authors' interpretation of the heterogeneity statistics (knowledge I2 = 95%, P < 0.001; skill I2 = 96%, P < 0.001): "the present mean effect sizes are not broadly generalizable". Random-effects models were applied accordingly.

> "The high levels of heterogeneity in the effect sizes indicate that more than one population is generating the effect sizes and that the present mean effect sizes are not broadly generalizable."
