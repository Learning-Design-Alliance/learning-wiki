# The best-fitting FCI post-test model included only FCI pretest and self-efficacy, with no gender predictor

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For FCI post-test scores, the simplest best-fitting model contained only FCI pretest and self-efficacy; adding gender or test anxiety gave a more complex model within ∆AIC < 2 of the simpler one. [→ Jared B. Stang 2020](#jared-b-stang-2020)
`q2 i?` In that model, FCI pretest (β = 0.57) and self-efficacy (β = 0.18) together explained 45% of the variance in FCI post-test scores. [→ Jared B. Stang 2020 (2)](#jared-b-stang-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Jared B. Stang 2020

Jared B. Stang, Emily Altiere, Joss Ives, and Patrick J. Dubois (2020). Exploring the contributions of self-efficacy and test anxiety to gender differences in assessments. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.stang](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.stang)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

AIC model-selection analysis predicting standardized FCI post-test scores: 'the simplest and best-ﬁtting model had only FCI pretest and self-efﬁcacy as predictors', with gender or test anxiety adding complexity without improvement.

> "the simplest and best-ﬁtting model had only FCI pretest and self-efﬁcacy as predictors—including gender o r test anxiety as predictors resulted in a more complex model, but was within ∆AIC < 2 of the simpler model"

### Jared B. Stang 2020 (2)

Jared B. Stang, Emily Altiere, Joss Ives, and Patrick J. Dubois (2020). Exploring the contributions of self-efficacy and test anxiety to gender differences in assessments. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.stang](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.stang)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Coefficients from the best-fitting FCI post-test model: 'βFCI pretest = 0.57 (p < . 001)' and 'βself-efﬁcacy = 0 .18 (p < . 001)', with 'R2 = .45'.

> "the coefﬁcients were βFCI pretest = 0.57 (p < . 001) and βself-efﬁcacy = 0 .18 (p < . 001). For this model, R2 = .45, indicating that FCI pretest and self-efﬁcacy explain 45% of the variance in FCI post-test scores"
