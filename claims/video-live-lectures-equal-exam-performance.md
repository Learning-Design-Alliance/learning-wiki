## Subclaims
`q2 i0` Correct-answer rates were nearly identical for questions taught live (78.283%) versus on video (78.605%), with no significant difference. [→ Brockfeld 2018](#brockfeld-2018)
`q1 i0` The observed effect size ω = 0.003337 was significantly smaller than the minimum detectable effect ω = 0.012128, so any true difference is negligible. [→ Brockfeld 2018 (2)](#brockfeld-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Brockfeld 2018

Brockfeld, T., Müller, B., & de Laffolie, J. (2018). Video versus live lecture courses: a comparative evaluation of lecture types and results. Medical Education Online, 23(1). https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2018.1555434

`q2 · i0`

Crossover analysis of 205 course participants' answers to 301 exam questions assigned to lecture days; 61,705 question responses total. The article reports the difference as n.s. with p = 0.407 and effect size ω = 0.003337, calling the video group "on average 0.332 percentage points better".

> "A total of 36,735 of 46,926 questions (78.283%) were correctly answered by the live group, while 11,617 of 14,799 questions (78.605%) were correctly answered by the video group."

### Brockfeld 2018 (2)

Brockfeld, T., Müller, B., & de Laffolie, J. (2018). Video versus live lecture courses: a comparative evaluation of lecture types and results. Medical Education Online, 23(1). https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2018.1555434

`q1 · i0`

Authors' power analysis in the Discussion: at n = 61,705 the study could have detected ω = 0.012128, and the observed ω = 0.003337 was significantly smaller, so any live–video difference is negligible.

> "For an α = 0.05 and an assumed β of 0.2, an effect strength of only ω = 0.012128 could have been measured at n = 61,705. The effect strength in this study was ω = 0.003337 significantly smaller."
