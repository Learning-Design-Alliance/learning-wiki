## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Beyond knowledge level (NR score explaining 27% of FS variance), DKO use and its interaction with gender explain an additional 8% of FS score variance, indicating risk-avoidance tendency disturbs the knowledge measurement. [→ Ravesloot C. J. 2015](#ravesloot-c-j-2015)

## Evidence

### Ravesloot C. J. 2015

Ravesloot C. J., Van der Schaaf M. F., Muijtjens A. M. M., Haaring C., Kruitwagen C. L. J. J., Beek F. J. A., Bakker J., Van Schaik J. P. J., Cate Th. J. Ten. (2015). The don’t know option in progress testing. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-015-9604-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Sequential multiple regression on data from the randomized cross-over study of 337 radiology residents taking parallel test halves under FS and NR conditions. The article reports "NR score was found to explain 27 % of the variance of FS" with DKO and Gender × DKO adding 8% [F(2,330) = 41.5, p < 0.0005], supporting construct-irrelevant variance.

> "NR score was found to explain 27 % of the variance of FS [F(1,332) = 219.2, p < 0.0005], DKO-score, and the interaction of DKO and Gender were found to explain 8 % [F(2,330) = 41.5, p < 0.0005]"

### Ravesloot C. J. 2015 (2)

Ravesloot C. J., Van der Schaaf M. F., Muijtjens A. M. M., Haaring C., Kruitwagen C. L. J. J., Beek F. J. A., Bakker J., Van Schaik J. P. J., Cate Th. J. Ten. (2015). The don’t know option in progress testing. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-015-9604-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Model 4 of the same sequential regression on the 337-resident cross-over data. The article reports the NR × DKO interaction "increases R square with another 1.6 %" [F(1,329) = 16.6, p < 0.0005], with b = −0.11 indicating the DKO disturbance is larger for better-performing residents.

> "adding the interaction NR × DKO (Model 4) increases R square with another 1.6 % [F(1,329) = 16.6, p < 0.0005], resulting in a final R2 of 69 %"
