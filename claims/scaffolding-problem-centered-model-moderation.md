# Scaffolding yields strong pre–post gains with most problem-centered instructional models, with project-based learning highest (ḡ = 1.21), but exceptions for inquiry-based learning (ḡ = 0) and modeling/visualization (ḡ = 0.28)

## Evidence

### Belland Brian R. 2017

Belland Brian R., Walker Andrew E., Kim Nam Ju. (2017). A Bayesian Network Meta-Analysis to Synthesize the Influence of Contexts of Scaffolding Use on Cognitive Outcomes in STEM Education. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654317723009

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Context-of-use moderator analysis (Figure 5): project-based learning had the highest estimate (ḡ = 1.21) and the highest probability of the best (44%, Table 3), but the article states this ES needs to be interpreted cautiously because of the wide credible interval.

> "The highest pre–post effect size was for project-based learning (ḡ = 1.21; see Figure 5). Due to the low number of coded outcomes for the characteristics, the range of true effects (credible interval) is wide."

### Belland Brian R. 2017 (2)

Belland Brian R., Walker Andrew E., Kim Nam Ju. (2017). A Bayesian Network Meta-Analysis to Synthesize the Influence of Contexts of Scaffolding Use on Cognitive Outcomes in STEM Education. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654317723009

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Context-of-use moderator analysis (Figure 5): inquiry-based learning (ḡ = 0) and modeling/visualization (ḡ = 0.28) fell well below other problem-centered models; the article notes coded outcomes per model were very small apart from problem solving, producing a very large range of true effects.

> "Most pre–post effect sizes were quite large, with the exception of inquiry-based learning (ḡ = 0) and modeling/visualization (ḡ = 0.28)."
