## Evidence

### Linda Qerimi 2025

Linda Qerimi, Sarah Malone, Eva Rexigel, Jochen Kuhn and Stefan Küchemann. (2025). Comparing Visual Qubit Representations in Quantum Education: The Bloch sphere Enhances Task Efficiency. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2507.21721](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2507.21721)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

ANCOVA on the follow-up subsample (N = 98; Quantum Bead n = 53, Bloch sphere n = 45) with Posttest 1 as covariate; means were nearly identical (Quantum Bead M = 3.44, Bloch sphere M = 3.49) and no group effect emerged.

> "The covariate Posttest 1 significantly predicted the follow-up posttest score (Posttest 2) ( F (1, 97) = 18 .82, p < .001), indicating that higher scores in the immediate posttest were associated with better mid-term performance. However, there was no significant group ef- fect on follow-up performance when controlling for prior performance ( F (1, 97) = 0 .03, p = .863)."

### Linda Qerimi 2025 (2)

Linda Qerimi, Sarah Malone, Eva Rexigel, Jochen Kuhn and Stefan Küchemann. (2025). Comparing Visual Qubit Representations in Quantum Education: The Bloch sphere Enhances Task Efficiency. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2507.21721](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2507.21721)

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Mixed ANOVA with time (Pretest, Posttest 1, Posttest 2) as within-subjects factor and representation group as between-subjects factor; the printed effect size was η2G = .013, a negligible effect, and post-hoc tests showed differences between pretest and both posttests (p < .001) but none between the posttests, indicating a stabilisation rather than continued learning.

> "The analysis yielded a significant main effect of time, F (2, 188) = 4 .23, p = .016, η2G = .013, indicating a learning effect."
