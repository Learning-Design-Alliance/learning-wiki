# The predictive value of cognitive load ratings for problem performance was mixed: mental effort predicted performance in Experiment 1, perceived difficulty in Experiment 2, but never both at once

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In Experiment 1, immediate and delayed mental effort ratings significantly predicted performance while difficulty ratings did not; in Experiment 2 this pattern reversed. [→ Schmeck 2015](#schmeck-2015)

## Evidence

### Schmeck 2015

Schmeck, A., Opfermann, M., van Gog, T., Paas, F., & Leutner, D. (2015). Measuring cognitive load with subjective rating scales during problem solving: differences between immediate and delayed ratings. Instructional Science, 43, 93–114. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-014-9328-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Linear regression analyses predicting problem-solving performance from the rating measures (Tables 4 and 9). In Experiment 1 (N = 168) effort ratings were significant predictors (delayed effort p = 0.049); in Experiment 2 (N = 107) neither cognitive load measure reached significance, with difficulty only marginal (p = 0.059 and p = 0.094).

> "we found that mental effort predicted performance in Experiment 1 and that perceived difﬁculty predicted performance in Experiment 2, but unfortunately, in neither of the two experiments, both dimensions of cognitive load predicted performance at the same time."
