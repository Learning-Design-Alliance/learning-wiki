# After number-line modeling, both students computed 2.5 as the median, locating the middle between 2 and 3

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · qualitative `r2` · `q1`
> **Currency** · newest study 2020 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In episode 8, after modeling the data on a number line and sorting its elements, Alice identified the space between 2 and 3 and computed 2.5 as the median of the even-numbered data set. [→ Amiruzzaman 2020](#amiruzzaman-2020)
`q1 i?` Alice reasoned that the middle number must lie between 2 and 3 and can be found by adding 2 and 3 and dividing in half, yielding 2.5 as the median. [→ Amiruzzaman 2020 (2)](#amiruzzaman-2020-2)
`q1 i?` Bob independently agreed that the middle number lies between 2 and 3 and is 2.5, matching Alice's response. [→ Amiruzzaman 2020 (3)](#amiruzzaman-2020-3)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Episode 8 of the teaching experiment, in which students plotted data on number lines. Alice "began to model the data on the number -line" and used it to sort the elements of the data set before locating the middle.

> "Alice began to model the data on the number -line, as in the prior t ask, and used the number -line to sort the elements of the data on the number -line."

### Amiruzzaman 2020 (2)

Amiruzzaman, M., & Kosko, K. W. (2020). Exploring students' understanding of median. In M. B. Wood, E. E. Turner, M. Civil, & J. A. Eli (Eds.), Proceedings of the 38th annual meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education. Tucson, AZ: The University of Arizona. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2004.10302](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2004.10302)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Same episode and task, an even-numbered data set. Alice reasoned the middle number lies between 2 and 3 and computed that "2.5 is the median" by adding and halving, which the authors interpret as partitioning the data set as a unitized whole.

> "this set has no middle value…but, we can find it…the middle number has to be in the middle of 2 and 3…we can add 2 and 3 and divide them in half…yes, then 2.5 is the median…(giggling)"

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

> "Similar to Alice, Bob said, "I see now…the middle number has to be  between 2 and 3. I think, it is 2.5…it is the middle of 2 and 3…I am sure."
