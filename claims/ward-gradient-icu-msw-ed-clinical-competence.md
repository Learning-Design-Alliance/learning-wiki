## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Mean clinical competence scores differed significantly across wards, with ICU nurses highest, internal medicine and surgical ward nurses intermediate, and emergency-department nurses lowest (P<0.05 for all dimensions). [→ Babaei Keivan 2023](#babaei-keivan-2023)

## Evidence

### Babaei Keivan 2023

Babaei Keivan, Sadeghian Efat, Khodaveisi Masoud. (2023). The Predictors of Clinical Competence among Hospital Nurses: A Cross-Sectional Study in Hamadan, West Iran. Ethiopian Journal of Health Sciences. https://doi.org/10.4314/ejhs.v33i1.5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional comparison across three ward types among the 270 nurses; Table 1 shows ICU total CC 49.48±3.8, medical-surgical ward 38.88±6.3, and emergency department 32.84±4.4, with Kruskal-Wallis/ANOVA tests reported at "P<0.05".

> "The differences among nurses in emergency ward, internal medicine ward, and intensive care unit respecting the mean scores of CC and all its dimensions were also significant so that these mean scores in nurses in intensive care unit were significantly greater than other nurses and in nurses in internal medicine ward were significantly greater than nurses in the emergency ward (P<0.05)."
