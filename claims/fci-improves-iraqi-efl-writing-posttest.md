## Evidence

### Ramyar O. Qader and Fadime Yalcin Arslan 2019

Ramyar O. Qader and Fadime Yalcin Arslan. (2019). THE EFFECT OF FLIPPED CLASSROOM INSTRUCTION IN WRITING: A CASE STUDY WITH IRAQI EFL LEARNERS. Teaching English with Technology, 19(1), 36-55. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1204642

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental non-equivalent control group study of 66 Iraqi sophomore EFL students; independent-sample t-test on post-test writing scores showed a significant difference between the flipped (x =6.17, N=34) and traditional (x =5.31, N=32) groups, with the printed result "(T=2.013, P=0.048)". No effect size is reported.

> "an independent-sample t-test revealed that at the end of the FCI program there was a significant difference betw een the mean scores of the experimental group ( x =6.17, N=34, SD=1.72) and the control group ( x =5.31, N=32, SD=1.76)"

### Ramyar O. Qader and Fadime Yalcin Arslan 2019 (2)

Ramyar O. Qader and Fadime Yalcin Arslan. (2019). THE EFFECT OF FLIPPED CLASSROOM INSTRUCTION IN WRITING: A CASE STUDY WITH IRAQI EFL LEARNERS. Teaching English with Technology, 19(1), 36-55. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1204642

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive statistics (Table 2) for the same quasi-experiment show the experimental group's mean increased from 4.35 to 6.17 while the control group "only sligh tly increased" from 4.64 to 5.31; these are descriptive means with no test statistic attached to the gains.

> "students in the experimental group had a mean average that increased from ( x =4.35, N=34, SD=1.85 to x =6.17, N=34, SD=1.72), whereas the mean average of students in the control group only sligh tly increased from ( x =4.64, N=32, SD=1.72 to x =5.31, N=32, SD=1.76)"
