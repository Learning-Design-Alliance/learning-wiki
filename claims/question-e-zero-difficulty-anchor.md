## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Sackett 1922

Sackett, L. W. (1922). A Scale in Ancient History. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/edu

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Small verification study in the article's zero-point section: a class of 27 high-school pupils attempted the easy-question list, and question E was answered by every pupil. The article cautions this "does not establish the fact" of zero difficulty universally but makes it a safe assumption.

> "This list was also submitted to a class of 27 high school pupils who attempted to answer the question. It was found that question E was answered by all the pupils; i. e., it has zero difficulty for them."

### Sackett 1922 (2)

Sackett, L. W. (1922). A Scale in Ancient History. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/edu

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Judge-ranking procedure reported in the article's zero-point section and Table II: ten judges ranked the ten easy questions, producing ten judgments per question, with question E indicated as easiest. The rankings were used with standard deviations of judgment to interpolate the zero point.

> "These questions were submitted to ten competent judges who ranked them as to easiness, leading to ten judgments for each question. These rankings are shown in Table II. Standing out prominent in these judgments is the indication that question E is the easiest of the group."
