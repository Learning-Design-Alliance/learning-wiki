## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors argue that leverage of a motivational strategy depends on the learner's value system: if intrinsic and extrinsic rewards are not attractive or believed, and losses for lack of effort seem inconsequential, the strategy has little leverage. [→ du Boulay 2016](#du-boulay-2016)

## Evidence

### du Boulay 2016

du Boulay, B., & del Soldato, T. (2016). Implementation of Motivational Tactics in Tutoring Systems: 20 years on. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education, 26, 170-182. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0052-1

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

This is a theoretical argument (type e), not an empirical test. The authors take for granted in MORE that learners wanted to learn, but now acknowledge motivation is crucially affected by the learner's value system, expected and actual experience and feelings (citing Pintrich 2003).

> "For a motivational strategy to have a real chance of success learners have to personally value what may be gained by their effort and fear what might be lost by their lack of it."
