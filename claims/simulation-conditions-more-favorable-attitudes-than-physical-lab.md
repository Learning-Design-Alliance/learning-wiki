## Evidence

### Yossi Ben-Zion 2026

Yossi Ben-Zion, Turhan K. Carroll, Colin G. West, Jesse Wong, and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2026). Leveraging generative artificial intelligence for simulation-based physics experiments: A new approach to virtual learning about the real world. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1103/s8dy-kqy5](https://doi.org/10.1103/s8dy-kqy5)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Three-phase analysis of five Likert attitude questions (collapsed to three response categories) using Fisher's exact tests and Cramer's V; omnibus p-values were significant for every attitude question, and "more positive attitudes" held for the prebuilt simulation group as well.

> "Fisher's pairwise exact tests indicate that students who did the AI lab reported more positive attitudes than students who did the traditional lab for all AQs"

### Yossi Ben-Zion 2026 (2)

Yossi Ben-Zion, Turhan K. Carroll, Colin G. West, Jesse Wong, and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2026). Leveraging generative artificial intelligence for simulation-based physics experiments: A new approach to virtual learning about the real world. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1103/s8dy-kqy5](https://doi.org/10.1103/s8dy-kqy5)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pairwise comparisons between the two simulation conditions on the five attitude questions found "significant difference only for AQ 1"; the authors note the prebuilt simulation was faster and easier, and students appreciated finishing sooner.

> "Comparing between the two simulation groups, however (AI and prebuit), there is significant difference only for AQ 1 (padjAQ1,AI-Sim = .035)."
