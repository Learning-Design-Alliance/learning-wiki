# Rubric-guided revisions measurably shifted the inquiry-level composition of the three targeted laboratory classes toward higher levels

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Revisions to laboratory class three reduced confirmation inquiry from 57.8% to 26.7% and increased guided inquiry from 6.7% to 42.2%, adding 8.9% open inquiry where there was none. [→ Grissom 2015](#grissom-2015)
`q2 i?` Revisions to laboratory class four decreased structured inquiry (64.4% to 47.8%) and increased guided (6.7% to 16.7%) and open inquiry (0% to 11.1%). [→ Grissom 2015 (2)](#grissom-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Grissom 2015

Grissom, A. N., Czajka, C. D., and McConnell, D. A. (2015). Revisions of Physical Geology Laboratory Courses to Increase the Level of Inquiry: Implications for Teaching and Learning. Journal of Geoscience Education 63, 285–296. https://doi.org/10.5408/14-050.1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Activity-by-activity rubric analysis weighting each activity's points by the total laboratory points; Table II lists the original and revised activity inventories with their assigned inquiry levels.

> "The revisions to laboratory class three reduced the amount of conﬁrmation inquiry from 57.8% to 26.7% while increasing the amount of guided inquiry from 6.7% to 42.2%."

### Grissom 2015 (2)

Grissom, A. N., Czajka, C. D., and McConnell, D. A. (2015). Revisions of Physical Geology Laboratory Courses to Increase the Level of Inquiry: Implications for Teaching and Learning. Journal of Geoscience Education 63, 285–296. https://doi.org/10.5408/14-050.1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Point-weighted rubric classification of the rocks laboratory activities before and after revision, reported in Table III and Figures 1 and 2.

> "Revisions to laboratory class four resulted in a decrease in structured inquiry activities (64.4% to 47.8%) and increased guided inquiry (6.7% to 16.7%) and open inquiry (0% to 11.1%; Table III and Figs. 1 and 2)."

### Grissom 2015 (3)

Grissom, A. N., Czajka, C. D., and McConnell, D. A. (2015). Revisions of Physical Geology Laboratory Courses to Increase the Level of Inquiry: Implications for Teaching and Learning. Journal of Geoscience Education 63, 285–296. https://doi.org/10.5408/14-050.1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Rubric analysis of the geologic time laboratory (Table IV); the shift occurred only in the Earth history portion because relative and absolute dating activities remained unchanged.

> "The revisions reduced the amount of conﬁrmation in the geologic time laboratory class from 22% to 4.5% while increasing guided inquiry from 18% to 33% (Figs. 1 and 2)."
