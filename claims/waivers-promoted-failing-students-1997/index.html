Related Claims¶

  • In 1997, 31% of third graders and about 20% of sixth and eighth graders were excluded from Chicago's promotional test-score policy, with bilingual education the main exclusion reason in third grade and special education in sixth and eighth — related
  • Despite higher passing rates, retention rates did not fall because fewer students who failed the cutoff were promoted with waivers — related
  • Retained students made up 15 percent of tested third graders, 10 percent of sixth graders, and 4.6 percent of eighth graders in 1998 — related
  • Only 43% of retained third graders and 47% of retained sixth graders met the test criteria by summer 1998 despite four chances, yet over two-thirds were promoted the next year largely due to waivers — related
  • Passing rates improved from 1997 to 1998 in third and sixth grades, but retention rates did not decline because fewer students received waivers — related
  • Early-grade retention rose after the policy, and roughly 20 percent of first and second graders who were retained experienced a second retention in third grade — related
  • Participatory directions keep zero scores and test failures rare in third-grade children — related
