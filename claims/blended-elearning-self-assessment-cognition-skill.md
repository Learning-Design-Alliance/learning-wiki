## Evidence

### Chang 2014

Chang, Shu, Liang, Tseng, and Hsu. (2014). Is Blended E-Learning Better than Traditional Classroom Learning for Vocational High School Students? Vol 15 | No 2 April/14. http://el.evta.gov.tw/

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r?`

MANCOVA with pretest self-assessment as covariance compared the two groups (n = 65 total). The article reports cognition had the largest effect (η2 = .177), a high correlation, and skill a medium correlation (η2 = .092); the experimental group had significantly higher adjusted means on cognition and skill.

> "there were significant differences in cognition (F = 13.309; p < .01) and skill ( F = 6.246; p < .05) between the two groups, but there was no significant difference in attitude ( F = 3.455; p = .068)"

### Chang 2014 (2)

Chang, Shu, Liang, Tseng, and Hsu. (2014). Is Blended E-Learning Better than Traditional Classroom Learning for Vocational High School Students? Vol 15 | No 2 April/14. http://el.evta.gov.tw/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Same MANCOVA analysis: the attitude between-group contrast was not significant (F = 3.455; p = .068; η2 = .053). No effect size interpretation beyond the printed η2 is offered; the authors explain attitude develops more slowly.

> "there was no significant difference on attitude between both groups because the development of attitude was slow er, which confirmed the viewpoint proposed by Linn and Miller (2005)"
