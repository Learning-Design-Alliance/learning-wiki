## Evidence

### Smith Daniel T 2018

Smith Daniel T, Tiffin Paul A. (2018). Evaluating the validity of the selection measures used for the UK’s foundation medical training programme: a national cohort study. BMJ Open 2018;8:e021918. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2018-021918

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Model 3 of the multilevel multivariable analysis using all cases (n=14 128 applicants nested in a cross-classification), adding significant univariable background variables. Table 3 prints SJT z-score OR 1.122 (p=0.075) and EPM decile OR 1.113 in this adjusted model.

> "After controlling for age, disability, the number of applications made and the medical school, the EPM decile score remained a significant predictor of obtaining an outcome 6 while SJT z-score did not."

### Smith Daniel T 2018 (2)

Smith Daniel T, Tiffin Paul A. (2018). Evaluating the validity of the selection measures used for the UK’s foundation medical training programme: a national cohort study. BMJ Open 2018;8:e021918. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2018-021918

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Background-variable findings from model 3 of the same cohort analysis; table 3 prints OR 1.660 for no disability (p=0.002), OR 0.904 for age, and OR 0.544 for number of applications (p=0.002).

> "Trainees with no disability recorded by HESA in their final year of medical school had an approximately 66% higher odds of an outcome 6 compared with those with a disability recorded. Older trainees were less likely to be awarded an outcome 6: each additional year reduced the odds by approximately 9%."
