# The source of SES data moderates the SES-achievement correlation, which is largest when data come from parents and smallest when reported by students

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Mean ES was .38 with parent-reported SES, .24 with secondary sources, and .19 with student-reported SES; all pairwise differences were significant. [→ Sirin 2005](#sirin-2005)

## Evidence

### Sirin 2005

Sirin, S. R. (2005). Socioeconomic Status and Academic Achievement: A Meta-Analytic Review of Research. Review of Educational Research, 75(3), 417-453. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543075003417

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Moderator analysis of SES data source across 62 student-level studies; Qb(2, 64) = 775.55, p < .001. The printed effect sizes were mean correlations of r = .38 (parents), r = .24 (secondary sources), and r = .19 (students), with Bonferroni-adjusted pairwise comparisons all significant at p < .005.

> "the average ES was .38 when the SES data were gathered from parents, .24 when the data were gathered from secondary sources, and .19 when the data were gathered from students them- selves."
