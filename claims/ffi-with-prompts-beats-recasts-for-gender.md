# Form-focused instruction combined with prompts is more effective than FFI combined with recasts or no feedback, especially on written tasks

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among FFI classes, the prompts condition outperformed the recasts and no-feedback conditions, most clearly on written tasks and to a lesser degree on oral tasks. [→ Roy Lyster 2004](#roy-lyster-2004)

## Evidence

### Roy Lyster 2004

Roy Lyster. (2004). DIFFERENTIAL EFFECTS OF PROMPTS AND RECASTS IN FORM-FOCUSED INSTRUCTION. Studies in Second Language Acquisition, Volume 26, Issue 3. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263104263021

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Classroom comparison in which each of the three FFI teachers implemented a different feedback treatment: "recasts, prompts, or no feedback." Results favored prompts over recasts and no feedback, with the advantage strongest on written tasks and weaker on oral tasks.

> "Results of the written tasks in particular, and to a lesser degree the oral tasks, revealed that FFI is more effective when combined with prompts than with recasts or no feedback, as a means of enabling L2 learners to acquire rule-based representations of grammatical gender and to proceduralize their knowledge of these emerging forms."
