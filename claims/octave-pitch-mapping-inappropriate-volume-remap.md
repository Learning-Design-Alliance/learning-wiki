## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The authors judged that using sounds one octave apart to represent stellar intensities was perhaps inappropriate because the pitch differences are easier to distinguish than the corresponding visual intensity differences, and remapped intensity to sounds at different volumes. ## Evidence

### Stefania Varano 2024

Stefania Varano, Federico Di Giacomo. (2024). Design of a Multisensory Planetarium. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2407.05801](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2407.05801)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

This is the authors' interpretation following the pilot test, not a measured comparison. They concluded the octave-separated pitch mapping exaggerated the perceptual contrast relative to visual intensity differences and "decided to map the intensity of light into sounds at different volumes, to offer a more appropriate perceptual equivalent."

> "we realized that the use of sounds one octave apart from each other as substitute stimuli of the different intensities of stars visible in a naked-eye observation was perhaps inappropriate, since the difference between the two representing sounds is much easier to argue than the difference in visual intensity of the stars"
