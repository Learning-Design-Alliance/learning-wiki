# The effect of computer-based teaching on academic success does not differ significantly by subject area

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` All subject-area groups (science, mathematics, social sciences, others) showed large effect sizes, and the intergroup test found no significant difference (Z = 13.86; p > .05). [→ Veli Batdı 2015](#veli-batdı-2015)

## Evidence

### Veli Batdı 2015

Veli Batdı. (2015). A Meta-Analytic Study Concerning the Effect of Computer-Based Teaching on Academic Success in Turkey. Educational Sciences: Theory & Practice. https://doi.org/10.12738/estp.2015.5.2491

`q3 · i?`

Moderator analysis of 77 studies (one foreign-language study excluded) in four subject-area categories. Table 4 reports science 1.14, mathematics 1.06, social sciences 1.11, others 1.02, total 1.10; QB = .30, homogeneous under FEM.

> "when the studies in the analysis were categorized into groups with regard to subject area and their effect sizes were considered, no significant difference was found in terms of subject area ( Z = 13.86; p > .05)."
