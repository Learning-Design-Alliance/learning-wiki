Evidence¶

Vage A 2024¶

Vage A, Spence AD, McKeown G, Gormley GJ, Hamilton PK. (2024). Simulate to stimulate? A systematic review of stress, learning, and performance in healthcare simulation. The Ulster Medical Journal. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11591218/

q2 · i? · review · r3

Systematic review synthesis of 20 included studies (926 total participants; individual sizes 13 to 120) identified via EMBASE, Ovid MEDLINE and PsycINFO searches to November 2023. The review reports "11 (55 %) detailed impaired performance". Heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis, so no pooled effect size is available.

"The identified studies have shown that exposure to high-stress simulation scenarios, consisting of noise, simulated patients, the presence of faculty, and increased task difficulty can elicit a variety of effects on participant performance, 11 (55 %) detailed impaired performance, eight (40 %) reported no effect on performance, and one (5 %) reported enhanced performance."
