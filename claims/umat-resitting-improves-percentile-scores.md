## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among candidates who sat at least twice, total UMAT percentile score improved by a mean of 10.7 ± 0.2 percentiles between first and second attempt, with section gains of 8.3, 8.3 and 7.7 percentiles for UMAT-1, UMAT-2 and UMAT-3. [→ Puddey Ian B 2014](#puddey-ian-b-2014)

## Evidence

### Puddey Ian B 2014

Puddey Ian B, Mercer Annette, Andrich David, Styles Irene. (2014). Practice effects in medical school entrance testing with the undergraduate medicine and health sciences admission test (UMAT). BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-14-48

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Paired comparison of first and second UMAT attempts among the N = 18,328 candidates who sat at least twice in the 2000–2012 cohort. "Total UMAT score improved by a mean of 10.7 ± 0.2 percentiles (Paired T-test P < 0.001)"; UMAT-1, UMAT-2 and UMAT-3 rose by 8.3 ± 0.2, 8.3 ± 0.2 and 7.7 ± 0.2 percentiles.

> "For those who sat at least twice (N = 18,328), the total UMAT percentile score between a first and second attempt improved in 71.1%, decreased in 28.7% and was identical in 0.2% of candidates. Total UMAT score improved by a mean of 10.7 ± 0.2 percentiles (Paired T-test P < 0.001)."
