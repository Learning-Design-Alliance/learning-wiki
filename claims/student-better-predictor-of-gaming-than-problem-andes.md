# In Andes log data, student is a better predictor of gaming than problem, with the combined model explaining 60.8% of variance

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a linear regression on Andes log data, student is more strongly associated with gaming than problem, and the model accounts for 60.8% of the variance. [→ Kasia Muldner · Winslow Burleson · Brett Van de Sande · Kurt VanLehn 2010](#kasia-muldner-winslow-burleson-brett-van-de-sande-kurt-vanlehn-2010)
`q2 i?` Student self-correlation of gaming across problems (r = .963) is higher than problem self-correlation across students (r = .89), and the difference is reliable. [→ Kasia Muldner · Winslow Burleson · Brett Van de Sande · Kurt VanLehn 2010 (2)](#kasia-muldner-winslow-burleson-brett-van-de-sande-kurt-vanlehn-2010-2)

## Evidence

### Kasia Muldner · Winslow Burleson · Brett Van de Sande · Kurt VanLehn 2010

Kasia Muldner · Winslow Burleson · Brett Van de Sande · Kurt VanLehn (2010). An analysis of students’ gaming behaviors in an intelligent tutoring system: predictors and impacts. User Model User-Adap Inter. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11257-010-9086-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear regression on Andes log data from 318 problems and 286 students, with percentage of gaming per student-problem as the dependent variable. The article reports the model was "significant (F=16915, p < 0.001)" with R2 = .608; student's standardized coefficient (.658) exceeds problem's (.325).

> "In this model, both student and problem yield a significant correlation with the dependent variable, but student is more strongly associated with gaming (student: standardized coefficient = .658"

### Kasia Muldner · Winslow Burleson · Brett Van de Sande · Kurt VanLehn 2010 (2)

Kasia Muldner · Winslow Burleson · Brett Van de Sande · Kurt VanLehn (2010). An analysis of students’ gaming behaviors in an intelligent tutoring system: predictors and impacts. User Model User-Adap Inter. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11257-010-9086-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Self-correlation analysis randomly split problems solved by each student into two buckets and correlated average gaming across buckets. Problem self-correlation was lower (r = .89), and the article reports the difference reliable via Z scores (Z = 6.9, p < 0.05).

> "A correlation analysis yielded a high degree of association between the two buckets (r = .963, p < 0.001)."
