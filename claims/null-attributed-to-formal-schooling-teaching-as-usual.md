## Evidence

### James-Brabham 2024

James-Brabham, E., Jay, T., & Sella, F. (2024). No evidence that playing a linear number board game improves numerical skills beyond teaching as usual: a randomized controlled trial in four- to five-year-old primary school children. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000857

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion, not a tested result: UK children at age five receive formal mathematical teaching and are assessed against the Early Years Foundation Stage, unlike US samples in many prior board-game studies; no data test this hypothesis directly.

> "We hypothesize the lack of difference among conditions may be a result of the level of formal learning children in the current study had already experienced."

### James-Brabham 2024 (2)

James-Brabham, E., Jay, T., & Sella, F. (2024). No evidence that playing a linear number board game improves numerical skills beyond teaching as usual: a randomized controlled trial in four- to five-year-old primary school children. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000857

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Authors' framing of the RCT outcome in the Discussion: despite a larger sample than most prior studies, individual randomization, stratified blocked randomization, and masking, no post-intervention differences among conditions were apparent.

> "This pre-registered study failed to (conceptually) replicate previous studies showing linear number board games improved early numeracy skills (e.g., Siegler & Ramani, 2008; Ramani & Siegler, 2008; Siegler & Ramani, 2009; Ramani et al., 2012)."
