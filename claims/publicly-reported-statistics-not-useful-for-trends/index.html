Evidence¶

Luppescu 2011¶

Luppescu, S., Allensworth, E. M., Moore, P., de la Torre, M., Murphy, J., with Jagesic, S. (2011). Trends in Chicago's Schools Across Three Eras of Reform. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/trends-chicagos-schools-across-three-eras-reform-full-report

q2 · i? · associational · r3

Methodological analysis in Chapter 2 identifying five issues that make public statistics non-comparable: test and scoring changes, the imprecise percent-meeting-standards metric, promotion-policy effects on grade composition, changing exclusion rates, and demographic change. No effect size is printed.

"the publicly reported statistics that are used by CPS and other school districts to gauge progress are simply not useful for measuring trends over time"

Luppescu 2011 (2)¶

Luppescu, S., Allensworth, E. M., Moore, P., de la Torre, M., Murphy, J., with Jagesic, S. (2011). Trends in Chicago's Schools Across Three Eras of Reform. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/trends-chicagos-schools-across-three-eras-reform-full-report

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Comparison of publicly reported proficiency trends with the report's own student-level rescaled trends, from the executive summary. The discrepancy is attributed to changes in test content and scoring that make year-over-year comparisons nearly impossible without complex statistical adjustment. No effect size is printed.

"publicly reported statistics indicate that CPS has made tremendous progress in elementary math and reading tests, while this analysis demonstrates only incremental gains in math and almost no growth in reading"
