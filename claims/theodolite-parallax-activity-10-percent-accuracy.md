# The theodolite parallax activity yields distance measurements within about 10% for model-star distances up to 16 m, though pupil-group accuracy ranged from 10% to about 50%

## Evidence

### Markus Pössel 2017

Markus Pössel. (2017). Simple classroom experiments for Astronomical Parallax. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1706.06555](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1706.06555)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Sample measurements made by the author for this article, reconstructed by drawing triangles to scale and compared against tape-measure distances (figure 5). The article reports "distance measurements within about 10% are readily obtainable" and all errors below 14%.

> "For interstellar distances up to about 16 m, we have found that distance measurements within about 10% are readily obtainable."

### Markus Pössel 2017 (2)

Markus Pössel. (2017). Simple classroom experiments for Astronomical Parallax. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1706.06555](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1706.06555)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Observation from pupil groups performing the theodolite activity during internships at Haus der Astronomie; the article reports mean accuracy "ranged between 10% and about 50%" with scale-drawing reconstruction. No effect size is printed.

> "When we had different groups of pupils working on this activity, mean accuracy ranged between 10% and about 50% (reconstruction via scale drawing)."
