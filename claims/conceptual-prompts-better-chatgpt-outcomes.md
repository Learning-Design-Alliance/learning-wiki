## Evidence

### Qurat-ul-Ann Mirza and N. Sanjay Rebello 2025

Qurat-ul-Ann Mirza and N. Sanjay Rebello. (2025). Help or Hype? Students' Engagement and Perception of Using AI to Solve Physics Problems. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2509.25642](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2509.25642)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative analysis of student ChatGPT transcripts and reflections from the in-person assessment. The article reports that complete, conceptual prompts "generally re- ceived more accurate and helpful responses from ChatGPT," whereas declining students omitted key variables such as acceleration and horizontal displacement.

> "Students who provided Chat- GPT with prompts that included complete and detailed prob- lem contexts, and askedconceptual questions focused on idea or principle (e.g. "What does it mean that ...?") generally re- ceived more accurate and helpful responses from ChatGPT."

### Qurat-ul-Ann Mirza and N. Sanjay Rebello 2025 (2)

Qurat-ul-Ann Mirza and N. Sanjay Rebello. (2025). Help or Hype? Students' Engagement and Perception of Using AI to Solve Physics Problems. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2509.25642](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2509.25642)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Figure 1 case comparison of the prompts of the students with maximum and minimum score gains; Student A specified the magnitude of the acceleration while Student B omitted the term magnitude. The authors call this anecdotal evidence of greater improvement for conceptual help-seekers.

> "Comparison of students' initial prompts: Student A, who used a conceptual prompt, saw a 10-point score increase. Student B, who used a procedural prompt, saw an 8-point score decrease."
