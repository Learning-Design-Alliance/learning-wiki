# Non-users declined significantly more than super users (600+ questions), d = 0.56

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Mean percentage-point shift differed significantly between non-users (M = -19.0) and super users (M = -9.1), a medium effect size (d = 0.56). [→ Roberts N 2015](#roberts-n-2015)

## Evidence

### Roberts N 2015

Roberts N, Spencer-Smith G, Vänskä R, Eskelinen S. (2015). From challenging assumptions to measuring effect: Researching the Nokia Mobile Mathematics Service in South Africa. South African Journal of Education, 35(2). https://doi.org/10.15700/saje.v35n2a1045

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Independent t-test on the 1,950-learner dataset comparing the highest usage category to non-users; the article prints "t (804) = 7.6, p = 8.3 x 10-14" and "d = 0.56".

> "Finally, there was a significant difference in the percentage point shifts between the group of non- users (zero questions) (M = -19.0, SD = 17.1) compared to the group of super users (600+ questions) (M = -9.1, SD = 17.9); t (804) = 7.6, p = 8.3 x 10-14. The magnitude of Cohen's effect size (d = 0.56) indicates that the effect of the service here was medium."
