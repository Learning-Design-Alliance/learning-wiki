Evidence¶

Fanguy 2017¶

Fanguy, M., Costley, J., & Baldwin, M. (2017). Pinch Hitter: The Effectiveness of Content Summaries Delivered by a Guest Lecturer in Online Course Videos. International Review of Research in Open and Distributed Learning, 18(7). http://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl/article/view/3210

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Scheffe post-hoc comparison in the same 118-student quasi-experiment found the guest-lecturer summary condition scored only .038 points above control, a non-significant difference, indicating the guest delivery eliminated the summary benefit.

"The quiz mean of the lecture with summary by a guest lecturer was .038 higher than the basic lecture quiz mean, though this relationship is not statistically significant."

Fanguy 2017 (2)¶

Fanguy, M., Costley, J., & Baldwin, M. (2017). Pinch Hitter: The Effectiveness of Content Summaries Delivered by a Guest Lecturer in Online Course Videos. International Review of Research in Open and Distributed Learning, 18(7). http://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl/article/view/3210

q3 · i? · causal · r1

The same Scheffe test showed the guest-lecturer condition scored .960 points below the summaries-only condition, a statistically significant difference (p < .05), despite identical summary content and timing across the two treatment groups.

"Also, the lecture with a summary and different professor group had a quiz mean .960 lower than the lecture with summary group."
