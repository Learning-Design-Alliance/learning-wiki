Evidence¶

Alharbi 2024¶

Alharbi, A., Nurfianti, A., Mullen, R. F., McClure, J. D., & Miller, W. H. (2024). The effectiveness of simulation-based learning (SBL) on students’ knowledge and skills in nursing programs: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education, 24. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06080-z

q2 · i? · review · r2

Narrative synthesis of the retention theme across the included studies. The review reports six studies with "15% improvements" and two with "a 5% decline", with follow-up periods varying from two to five months; results are characterised as mixed.

"Within the theme of retention, eight studies were assessed the retention. Six of these studies reported 15% improvements, while two observed a 5% decline following the intervention."

Alharbi 2024 (2)¶

Alharbi, A., Nurfianti, A., Mullen, R. F., McClure, J. D., & Miller, W. H. (2024). The effectiveness of simulation-based learning (SBL) on students’ knowledge and skills in nursing programs: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education, 24. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06080-z

q2 · i? · review · r2

Review discussion of longitudinal limitations. The review states only 8 of 33 studies measured durability, with follow-ups "spanning just two to five months post-intervention", which it judges inadequate for long-term maintenance claims.

"Only 8 of the 33 included studies measured outcome durability over time, with retention follow-up assessments spanning just two to five months post-intervention."
