# Watching a multimedia presentation increases the probability of answering mid-term test questions correctly

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Viewers had a higher probability of answering test questions correctly than non-viewers. [→ Tani 2022](#tani-2022)

## Evidence

### Tani 2022

Tani, M., Manuguerra, M., & Khan, S. (2022). Can videos affect learning outcomes? Evidence from an actual learning environment. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-022-10147-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental analysis of 568 first-year economics students (22,720 question-level observations) using generalised linear mixed models. Model 1 estimated a marginal effect of watching the presentation of "0.0206" (p = .007), described as "a 2% increase in the probability of answering correctly".

> "watching the presentation emerges as having a strong positive effect on the probability of answering the questions correctly in the mid-term test. The point estimate is not small (a 2% increase in the probability of answering correctly, keeping all other variables fixed)"
