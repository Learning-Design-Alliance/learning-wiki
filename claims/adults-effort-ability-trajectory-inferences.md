## Evidence

### Ying Hu 2026

Ying Hu, Yuhang Shu & Xin Zhao. (2026). Children consider changes in performance over time when reasoning about academic achievements. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00401-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Adult comparison group in Study 1: adults significantly selected the improving character on the hardworking question (M = 0.80, p < 0.001) and the constant character on the smart question (M = 0.20, p < 0.001), with a significant measure effect (B = −2.80, p < 0.001).

> "Most adults inferred that the improving character was more hard-working (Hardworking:M = 0.80,SD = 0.40, intercept-only binomial linear mixed effects model, p < 0.001), while the constant character was smarter (Smart: M = 0.20, SD = 0.40, intercept-only binomial linear mixed effects model, p < 0.001)."

### Ying Hu 2026 (2)

Ying Hu, Yuhang Shu & Xin Zhao. (2026). Children consider changes in performance over time when reasoning about academic achievements. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00401-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Adult evaluation responses in Study 1: significantly above-chance favoring of the improving character only on the prize question (M = 0.77, p < 0.001); preference (p > 0.132) and success responses did not differ from chance.

> "Intercept-only binomial linear mixed effects models revealed that adults signiﬁcantly favored the improving character on the prize question (M = 0.77,SD = 0.42,B = 1.26,SE = 0.26, 95% CI [0.75, 1.77], p < 0.001), but their responses on the preference question (M = 0.59, SD = 0.50) and the success question ( M =0 . 5 2 ,SD = 0.50) did not differ from chance"
