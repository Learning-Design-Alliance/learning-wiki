## Subclaims
`q3 i?` 15- to 16-year-olds adapted referential expressions to assumed addressee knowledge significantly more than 11- to 12-year-olds (AGE GROUP significant, p < 0.001). [→ Arvidsson C. 2022](#arvidsson-c-2022)

## Evidence

### Arvidsson C. 2022

Arvidsson C., Pagmar D., Uddén J. (2022). When did you stop speaking to yourself? Age-related differences in adolescents' world knowledge-based audience design. Royal Society Open Science 9(11): 220305. https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.220305

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Online production task with 58 adolescents from two Stockholm-area schools, 29 per age group; AD score compared between age groups. Older group showed "in 93% versus 83% of unknown condition trials" describing, and less redundant describing in the known condition.

> "Compared to their younger peers, 15- to 16-year-olds described referents they assumed to be unknown to addressees more often (in 93% versus 83% of unknown condition trials), and described referents they assumed to be known to their addressees less often (in 16% versus 30% of known condition trials)."
