Karpicke 2017¶

Karpicke, J. D. (2017). Retrieval-Based Learning: A Decade of Progress. Learning and Memory: A Comprehensive Reference, 2nd edition, Volume 2. https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-809324-5.21055-9

The chapter reports Karpicke and Zaromb (2010), where second-phase performance was about 75% in both conditions: "performance was consistently better when subjects had practiced retrieval (the recall condition)" than after generation.

"On the ﬁnal tests, performance was consistently better when subjects had practiced retrieval (the recall condition) relative to when they generated the targets without thinking back to the original study episode (the generate condition)."

Karpicke 2017 (2)¶

Karpicke, J. D. (2017). Retrieval-Based Learning: A Decade of Progress. Learning and Memory: A Comprehensive Reference, 2nd edition, Volume 2. https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-809324-5.21055-9

q2 · i? · review · r3

The chapter reports Whiffen and Karpicke (2017), where both conditions reexperienced all items: "the list discrimination condition produced about a 10% advantage relative to restudy" on final free recall.

"On a ﬁnal free recall test, the list discrimination condition produced about a 10% advantage relative to restudy."

  • Retrieval practice disrupted within-list order reconstruction of eight-item word lists, just as generation did — related
  • On the final cumulative test, JOLs with word stems and retrieval practice improve List-5 recall over restudy, while restudy shows a List-1 advantage — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Whether initial retrieval practice enhances semantic organization in final free recall is unclear, with results that may depend on how retrieval practice is implemented — related
  • Initial retrieval conditions that provide less cue support, such as free recall rather than recognition or fewer letter cues, tend to produce better retention despite lower initial success — related
