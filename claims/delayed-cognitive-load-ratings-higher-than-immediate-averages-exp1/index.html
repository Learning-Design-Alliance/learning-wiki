Evidence¶

Schmeck 2015¶

Schmeck, A., Opfermann, M., van Gog, T., Paas, F., & Leutner, D. (2015). Measuring cognitive load with subjective rating scales during problem solving: differences between immediate and delayed ratings. Instructional Science, 43, 93–114. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-014-9328-3

q3 · i3 · causal · r1

Repeated-measures classroom experiment with 168 German undergraduate educational science students solving six weekday-problems of increasing complexity. The paired t-test showed "signiﬁcant differences between the average of ratings after each task versus single delayed ratings" for invested mental effort, d = 0.85.

"two paired samples t-tests revealed signiﬁcant differences between the average of ratings after each task versus single delayed ratings of both invested mental effort ( M = 3.43, SD = 0.81 vs. M = 4.32, SD = 1.24, t(167) = 13.54, p\ 0.001, d = 0.85), and perceived task difﬁculty"

Schmeck 2015 (2)¶

Schmeck, A., Opfermann, M., van Gog, T., Paas, F., & Leutner, D. (2015). Measuring cognitive load with subjective rating scales during problem solving: differences between immediate and delayed ratings. Instructional Science, 43, 93–114. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-014-9328-3

q3 · i2 · causal · r1

Same Experiment 1 repeated-measures classroom study (N = 168): the paired t-tests revealed "signiﬁcant differences between the average of ratings after each task versus single delayed ratings" for perceived task difﬁculty (M = 2.96 vs. M = 3.74, d = 0.78).

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Delayed ratings of affective variables (interest and motivation) do not differ from the average of immediate ratings, unlike cognitive load ratings — related
  • The delayed-higher-than-immediate difference for cognitive load ratings replicates regardless of whether problem complexity increases or decreases across the sequence (Experiment 2) — a broader claim this one bears on
  • The predictive value of cognitive load ratings for problem performance was mixed: mental effort predicted performance in Experiment 1, perceived difficulty in Experiment 2, but never both at once — related
  • Delayed cognitive load ratings appear to be based more on the more complex problems in the series than on a recency effect — related
  • Delayed cognitive load assessments after learning exceed the average of during-learning assessments only when complexity decreases across the learning material — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Invested mental effort and perceived task difficulty are strongly correlated yet relate differently to relative task difficulty, indicating the two ratings measure different aspects of overall cognitive load — related
  • Taking an immediate plus delayed final test yields higher delayed test performance with less invested effort than taking only a delayed test (Experiment 2) — related
  • Delayed effort ratings are higher than the average of immediate ratings, suggesting itemized appraisals are more sensitive to effort fluctuations — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
