## Evidence

### Nye 2014

Nye, B. D., Graesser, A. C., & Hu, X. (2014). AutoTutor and Family: A Review of 17 Years of Natural Language Tutoring. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-014-0029-5

`q2 · i3` · `review · r2`

Narrative review synthesis by the authors of AutoTutor evaluation studies across domains. The review reports a printed effect size of 0.8 σ (standard deviation units) above static-material controls; gains are stated to be on par with expert human tutors in computer-mediated conversation.

> "AutoTutor has produced learning gains that are on average about 0.8 σ (standard deviation units) above controls who read static instructional materials (e.g., textbooks) for an equivalent amount of time"

### Nye 2014 (2)

Nye, B. D., Graesser, A. C., & Hu, X. (2014). AutoTutor and Family: A Review of 17 Years of Natural Language Tutoring. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-014-0029-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Review reports the bystander Turing test evaluation in which outsiders judged individual conversational turns from transcripts; no effect size is printed, so impact is null.

> "a bystander Turing test was conducted to determine whether outsiders could discriminate if a specific tutoring turn was produced by AutoTutor or a human tutor. Bystanders could not differentiate between AutoTutor and the human tutor"
