## Evidence

### Kudish Philip 2016

Kudish Philip, Shores Robin, McClung Alex, Smulyan Lisa, Vallen Elizabeth A., Siwicki Kathleen K. (2016). Active Learning Outside the Classroom: Implementation and Outcomes of Peer-Led Team-Learning Workshops in Introductory Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-01-0051

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Chi-square analysis of institutional records for the before-program cohort (Fall 2000–Spring 2004) who took introductory biology as first-year students and graduated in 4 years, comparing URM and non-URM persistence. The study reports "31% of URM students graduated with a major or minor in biology compared with 48% of non-URM students."

> "for those who took Biology 1 or 2 in those 4 years before SGMs were introduced, 31% of URM students graduated with a major or minor in biology compared with 48% of non-URM students (chi-square = 5.9, p < 0.05; Figure 4A)."

### Kudish Philip 2016 (2)

Kudish Philip, Shores Robin, McClung Alex, Smulyan Lisa, Vallen Elizabeth A., Siwicki Kathleen K. (2016). Active Learning Outside the Classroom: Implementation and Outcomes of Peer-Led Team-Learning Workshops in Introductory Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-01-0051

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Chi-square analysis of the after-program cohort (Fall 2008–Spring 2012) shows URM persistence rose to "50% biology majors or minors and 59% NSE majors or minors," eliminating the URM/non-URM difference. SAT Math scores of URM groups did not differ between cohorts (t = −1.66, p = 0.10).

> "the persistence of URM students increased to 50% biology majors or minors and 59% NSE majors or minors. As a result, there were no longer differences between URM and non-URM students in the rates of graduating with either biology or NSE degrees (Figure 4, A and B)."
