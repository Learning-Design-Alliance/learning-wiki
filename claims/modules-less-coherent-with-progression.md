# As students progress through the introductory sequence, PIQL response modules become less coherent rather than more expert-like

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Contrary to an expectation that progressing students' correct responses would converge into one coherent module, the number of small modules increases from PreMech to PostMech to PostEM. [→ Trevor I. Smith 2020](#trevor-i-smith-2020)

## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith 2020

Trevor I. Smith, Philip Eaton, Suzanne White Brahmia, Alexis Olsho, Andrew Boudreaux, Chris DePalma, Victor LaSasso, Christopher Whitener, Scott Straguzzi. (2020). Using psychometric tools as a window into students' quantitative reasoning in introductory physics. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/perc.2019.pr.smith_t](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2019.pr.smith_t)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-data-set comparison of MAMCR heatmaps: the large PostEM module contains responses from both large PreMech modules and both large PostMech modules, and the number of small modules increases across the sequence. The article contrasts this with a hypothetical expert group whose responses would form one coherent module.

> "Our data show that as students progress toward expertise during the introductory sequence, modules become less coherent, not more."
