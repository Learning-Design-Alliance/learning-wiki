## Evidence

### Kersey 2019

Kersey, A. J., Csumitta, K. D., & Cantlon, J. F. (2019). Gender similarities in the brain during mathematics development. npj Science of Learning 4:19. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0057-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

One-sample t tests on same-gender minus different-gender intersubject-correlation difference maps across the child sample, with average similarity thresholds of r(500) ≥ 0.115, p ≤ 0.01. The article concludes children's neural activity patterns reflect one heterogeneous group rather than two gender groups.

> "This whole-brain t test revealed no regions that showed a difference in neural activity (threshold: t(103) ≥ 2.62, voxel-wise p ≤ 0.01, corrected to p < 0.05 with a cluster threshold of 19 voxels)."
