## Evidence

### Littlecott Hannah J 2015

Littlecott Hannah J, Moore Graham F, Moore Laurence, Lyons Ronan A, Murphy Simon. (2015). Association between breakfast consumption and educational outcomes in 9–11-year-old children. Public Health Nutrition. https://doi.org/10.1017/S1368980015002669

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel binary logistic regression on the baseline sample (n 3093) and follow-up sample (n 3055) from the Primary School Free Breakfast Initiative trial, linking dietary recall to SATs. The article reports breakfast consumption "significantly and positively associated with educational performance" at both time points.

> "The subsequent models, which included dietary behaviours, showed that breakfast consumption, number of healthy breakfast items consumed for breakfast, number of sweets and crisps consumed throughout the rest of the day and number of fruit and vegetable portions consumed throughout the rest of the day, adjusted for gender and individual- and school-level FSM entitlement, were all significantly and positively associated with educational performance."
