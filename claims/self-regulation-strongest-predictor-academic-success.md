## Evidence

### Martincová J 2025

Martincová J, Staňková I, Snopek P, Zemánková L. (2025). Engagement, self-efficacy and self-regulation as predictors of academic success in non-medical healthcare education. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08043-4

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis in the 540-student Czech survey; self-regulation was measured with the 40-item DAUS1 questionnaire (α = 0.89). The article states "the strongest correlation was found between self-regulation of learning and academic success (r =.55, p <.01)".

> "Finally, the strongest correlation was found between self-regulation of learning and academic success (r =.55, p <.01), supporting existing literature that emphasizes the role of self-directed learning in academic achievement"

### Martincová J 2025 (2)

Martincová J, Staňková I, Snopek P, Zemánková L. (2025). Engagement, self-efficacy and self-regulation as predictors of academic success in non-medical healthcare education. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08043-4

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Stepwise regression (ENTER then STEPWISE) predicting study success; multicollinearity, tolerance and VIF conditions reported as met. The article reports a standardized effect size of β = 0.294 for self-regulation of learning, the largest of the predictors, from the printed "Stand. coeff. beta = 0.294".

> "According to the standardized regression coefficient beta, self-regulation of learning (Stand. coeff. beta = 0.294) and engagement (Stand. coeff. beta = 0.260) have the most significant effect on subjectively perceived academic success."
