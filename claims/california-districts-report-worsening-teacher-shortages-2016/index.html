Evidence¶

Anne Podolsky and Leib Sutcher 2016¶

Anne Podolsky and Leib Sutcher. (2016). California Teacher Shortages: A Persistent Problem. Learning Policy Institute. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED606761

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Survey of 211 districts in the CSBA Delegate Assembly (response rate over 84%), generally reflecting California district demographics. The report states "Approximately 75% of districts report having a shortage of qualified teachers" and worsening trends.

"Approximately 75% of districts report having a shortage of qualified teachers for the 2016–17 school year. Over 80% of these districts say that shortages have gotten worse since the 2013–14 school year"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Three out of four surveyed California districts reported teacher shortages in fall 2016, and most said shortages were worsening — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Increased retirements and resignations contributed to shortages in most large districts surveyed — related
  • New California credentials stagnated at about 11,500 per year while estimated annual hires exceeded 20,000, so demand far outpaced supply — related
  • California's teacher workforce is aging, with about 40% age 50 or older and 14% age 60 or older — related
  • Districts with shortages most often lack special education, mathematics, and science teachers, and most lack middle and high school teachers — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
