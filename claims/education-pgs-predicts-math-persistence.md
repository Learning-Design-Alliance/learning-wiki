## Evidence

### K. Paige Harden 2020

K. Paige Harden, Benjamin W. Domingue, Daniel W. Belsky, Jason D. Boardman, Robert Crosnoe, Margherita Malanchini, Michel Nivard, Elliot M. Tucker-Drob and Kathleen Mullan Harris. (2020). Genetic associations with mathematics tracking and persistence in secondary school. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-020-0060-2

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Poisson regression of number of advancing steps (0-3) on education-PGS in the Add Health transcript sample, reported in Results. The association was "only modestly attenuated" after family- and school-SES covariates (b = 0.096), and school fixed-effects models also showed higher-PGS students took more advancing steps than schoolmates (b = 0.117).

> "Students with higher education-PGS took more advancing steps (Fig. 1b; b = 0.139, SE = 0.011, 95% CI = [0.117, 0.159]); Supplementary Table 4)."

### K. Paige Harden 2020 (2)

K. Paige Harden, Benjamin W. Domingue, Daniel W. Belsky, Jason D. Boardman, Robert Crosnoe, Margherita Malanchini, Michel Nivard, Elliot M. Tucker-Drob and Kathleen Mullan Harris. (2020). Genetic associations with mathematics tracking and persistence in secondary school. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-020-0060-2

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Covariate model reported in Results: students tracked below Algebra 1 in 9th grade persisted less than those in Algebra 1, whereas students in Geometry or higher did not differ from Algebra 1 students (b = −0.059, CI including 0). Controlling for tracking, the education-PGS remained associated with persistence (b = 0.087).

> "Students who were tracked to Pre-Algebra or lower in the 9th-grade persisted less in math than those in Algebra 1 ( b = −0.221, SE = 0.038, 95% CI = [−0.293, −0.147])."
