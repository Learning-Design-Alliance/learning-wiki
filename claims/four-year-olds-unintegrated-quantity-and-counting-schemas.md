# Four-year-olds hold nonnumerical quantity knowledge and counting knowledge in separate, unintegrated structures and fail number comparison questions

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` At age 4, children can count to five and judge relative quantity of arrays, yet respond at chance when asked which number word is more, indicating the two knowledge sets are not merged. [→ Case 1996](#case-1996)

## Evidence

### Case 1996

Case, R., & Okamoto, Y., in collaboration with Griffin, S., McKeough, A., Bleiker, C., Henderson, B., & Stephenson, K. M. (1996). The Role of Central Conceptual Structures in the Development of Children's Thought. Monographs of the Society for Research in Child Development, 61(1-2, Serial No. 246). https://www.jstor.org/stable/1166077

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The Monograph reports, citing prior research, that preschoolers possess both a global quantity schema and a counting schema but cannot integrate them; on the number-word comparison question they "respond at chance level" despite successful counting and quantity judgments. No effect size is printed.

> "Thus, when asked, "Which is more/which is bigger, 4 or 5?" they respond at chance level, even though they can successfully count to five and make relative quantity judgments about arrays containing five versus four objects (Siegler & Robinson, 1982)."
