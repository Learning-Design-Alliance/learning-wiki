## Evidence

### Matsuda 2015

Matsuda, N., Cohen, W. W., & Koedinger, K. R. (2015). Teaching the Teacher: Tutoring SimStudent Leads to More Effective Cognitive Tutor Authoring. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-014-0020-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Qualitative analysis of errors made on test problems after the 20th training problem, counting all conflicting production applications. Four error types were observed, and all were less frequent under Authoring by Tutoring.

> "Overall, there were notably fewer errors of all types observed for Authoring by Tutoring than Authoring by Demonstration."

### Matsuda 2015 (2)

Matsuda, N., Cohen, W. W., & Koedinger, K. R. (2015). Teaching the Teacher: Tutoring SimStudent Leads to More Effective Cognitive Tutor Authoring. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-014-0020-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same error analysis, inconsistent transformation errors and wrong type-in errors appeared exclusively under Authoring by Demonstration, suggesting tutoring prevented these error categories entirely.

> "Inconsistent transformation errors (ITR in Table 1) and wrong type-in errors (WT) were observed only for Authoring by Demonstration."
