# AI and peer feedback differed in complementary ways: AI gave general, rubric-driven, actionable feedback while peers gave more specific, contextual feedback

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` 38% of student reflections coded AI and human feedback as complementary, with AI often described as overly general (31%) and peer feedback as more specific or detailed (28%). [→ Sperber 2025](#sperber-2025)
`q2 i?` Some students found peers more valuable because peers understood the assignment, course context, and subject matter more deeply than AI (15% of reflections). [→ Sperber 2025 (2)](#sperber-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Sperber 2025

Sperber, Lisa; MacArthur, Marit; Minnillo, Sophia; Stillman, Nicholas; Whithaus, Carl. (2025). Peer and AI Review + Reflection (PAIRR): A human-centered approach to formative assessment. Computers and Composition 76. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compcom.2025.102921

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Thematic coding of the stratified sub-sample of 131 students' reflections in MaxQDA; AI feedback was often described as overly general or generic (31% of reflections) while peer feedback was frequently described as more specific or detailed (28% of reflections).

> "38% of reflections were coded as “Feedback Consensus: AI and Human Complementary.”"

### Sperber 2025 (2)

Sperber, Lisa; MacArthur, Marit; Minnillo, Sophia; Stillman, Nicholas; Whithaus, Carl. (2025). Peer and AI Review + Reflection (PAIRR): A human-centered approach to formative assessment. Computers and Composition 76. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compcom.2025.102921

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Qualitative coding of student reflections; students trusted peers more because of contextual knowledge, and valued peers as an authentic audience (16% of reflections) and for emotional support and relationships.

> "some students found greater value in peer feedback because their peers understood the assignment, course context, and subject matter more deeply than AI (15% of reflections “Understands Assignment/Context”)"
