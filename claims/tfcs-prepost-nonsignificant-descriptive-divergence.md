# Within-group pre-to-post TFCS changes were not statistically significant in either group, though the experimental group's views developed positively and the control group's negatively

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2017 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Paired TFCS pre-post tests were non-significant in both groups (experimental t(19)=-0.832, p>.05; control t(17)=0.182, p>.05), while experimental means rose (95.95 to 99.15) and control means fell (87.39 to 86.44). [→ Buber 2017](#buber-2017)

## Evidence

### Buber 2017

Buber, A., & Coban Unal, G. (2017). The effects of learning activities based on argumentation on conceptual understanding of 7th graders about “force and motion” unit and establishing thinking friendly classroom environment. European Journal of Educational Research, 6(3), 367-384. https://doi.org/10.12973/eu-jer.6.3.367

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Paired samples t-tests on TFCS within each group of the quasi-experiment. The article reports that although not significant, posttest scores of the experimental group increased compared to pretest while control group posttest results were less than pretest.

> "In TFCS results, there isn’t significant difference between pre and posttests of experiment group (t(19)= -0.832, p>.05). There isn’t significant difference between pre and posttests of control group, either (t(17)=0.182, p>.05)."
