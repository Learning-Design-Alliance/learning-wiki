Evidence¶

Michael W. Asher & Paulo F. Carvalho 2026¶

Michael W. Asher & Paulo F. Carvalho. (2026). Conditions for Effective Learning Without Upfront Instruction: How Practice with Feedback Supports Memory, Generalization, Motivation, and Metacognition. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10103-6

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Study 1, a three-cell randomized online experiment (N = 297) comparing lecture, correct-response feedback, and explanatory feedback on multiple-choice practice. The preregistered regression showed a memory advantage of "17 percentage points (or 0.49 SD)" for explanatory feedback and 18 points for correct-response feedback over lecture; the two practice conditions did not differ (b =.01, p =.882).

"participants who received explanatory feedback outperformed those in the lecture condition on memory items by 17 percentage points (or 0.49 SD), t(291) = −3.39, p =.001. The advantage of correct-response feedback over lecture was similar: 18 percentage points, t(291) = 3.56, p <.001."
