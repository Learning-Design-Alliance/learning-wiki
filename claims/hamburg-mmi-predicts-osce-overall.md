## Subclaims
`q3 i1` MMI results correlated significantly with overall OSCE score (ρ = .23, p < .05), a small association whose confidence-interval lower bound was close to zero. [→ Knorr M 2018](#knorr-m-2018)

## Evidence

### Knorr M 2018

Knorr M, Schwibbe A, Ehrhardt M, Lackamp J, Zimmermann S, Hampe W. (2018). Validity evidence for the Hamburg multiple mini-interview. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1208-0

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Study 3: first OSCE after one and a half years; 301 students registered, 89 with MMI data (84% of MMI-admitted). Spearman correlation "ρ = .23, p < .05" (95% CI .03/.41); ρ of .23 is a small effect. OSCE internal consistency was α = .53.

> "However, MMI results correlated significantly with GP psychosocial competencies (ρ = .32, p < .05), GP suitability (ρ = .42, p < .01) and OSCE score (ρ = .23, p < .05)."

### Knorr M 2018 (2)

Knorr M, Schwibbe A, Ehrhardt M, Lackamp J, Zimmermann S, Hampe W. (2018). Validity evidence for the Hamburg multiple mini-interview. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1208-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Robust regression (M estimator) for OSCE overall score: MMI b = 7.05 (SE 3.46, t = 2.04, p < .05, df = 85); gender and age non-significant (Table 5). No effect size printed.

> "The robust regression models for each of the three outcomes showed that the MMI remained a significant predictor when the control variables were part of the model."
