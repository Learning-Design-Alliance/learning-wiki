# Encouragement messages increase university students’ self-efficacy before exams (Cohen’s d = 0.12), with no difference between first- and second-exam delivery

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` Treated students reported higher self-efficacy than controls before their exam, β1 = 0.304 (p < 0.001), d = 0.12, significant at the preregistered Bonferroni level of 0.0167; the printed effect size is below the small-effect threshold. [→ Keller Tamás 2021](#keller-tamas-2021)

## Evidence

### Keller Tamás 2021

Keller Tamás, Szakál Péter. (2021). Not just words! Effects of a light-touch randomized encouragement intervention on students’ exam grades, self-efficacy, motivation, and test anxiety. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0256960

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Secondary analysis on the endline-survey subsample (8,296 observations; 6,908 students) of the same crossover field experiment, single-item self-efficacy measured 0–10 before exams. The article reports the encouragement “increased students’ self-efficacy by β1 = 0.304 (p < 0.001)”, d = 0.12, a printed effect size below d = 0.2.

> "Receiving the encouragement message increased students’ self-efficacy by β1 = 0.304 (p < 0.001) unit, which is a Cohen’s d effect size of 0.12."

### Keller Tamás 2021 (2)

Keller Tamás, Szakál Péter. (2021). Not just words! Effects of a light-touch randomized encouragement intervention on students’ exam grades, self-efficacy, motivation, and test anxiety. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0256960

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Same endline subsample analysis. The second-exam self-efficacy effect remained significant (“the joint linear effect of β1 and β3 … is 0.277”), and the article reports no carry-over effect (β3 = 0.027; p = 0.818), so delivery timing did not matter.

> "the joint linear effect of β1 and β3 (the treatment effect concerning students’ second exam) is 0.277; the coefficient is significant at the 1% significance level."
