`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Video-based observational assessment of simulated patient handovers by trained raters using a modified, mnemonic-agnostic competency instrument; 472 handovers evaluated (M2 n = 248; M5 n = 224), with one record per student analyzed (M2 n = 243; M5 n = 218). The comparison is descriptive; the article hedges with "appear similar or slightly higher".

> "Compared to scores achieved by learners in the low-complexity handover exercise (M2), scores for teamwork competencies observed in the moderate-complexity handover exercise (M5) appear similar or slightly higher."

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Table 5 shows clarifying questions mean 60% (SD 40.2%) in M2 (n = 243) versus 49% (SD 39.4%) in M5 (n = 218). The authors offer the interruption inclusion as a possible explanation, not a tested one.

> "Compared to M2, lower M5 scores for asking clarifying questions may be related to the inclusion of interruptions in the M5 learning activity."
