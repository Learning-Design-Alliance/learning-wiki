# Educated peers or non-physicians teach most basic practical skills as effectively as physicians, with exceptions for full-time faculty and expert teaching of bladder catheterization

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the majority of reviewed studies, being taught by instructed peers or trained personnel, generalists or specialists, led to similar student skill performance. [→ Vogel Daniela 2016](#vogel-daniela-2016)
`q2 i?` Two exceptions contrast this pattern: students performed better in bladder catheterization after expert teaching, and students taught by full-time faculty performed better than those taught by part-time faculty. [→ Vogel Daniela 2016 (2)](#vogel-daniela-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Vogel Daniela 2016

Vogel Daniela, Harendza Sigrid. (2016). Basic practical skills teaching and learning in undergraduate medical education – a review on methodological evidence. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001063](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001063)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Review synthesis of nine controlled studies comparing instructor types, citing no OSCE differences for peers versus physicians, generalists versus specialists, or teaching associates versus faculty.

> "With the majority of studies providing evidence that basic practical skills can be taught equally well by educated peers or non-physicians we believe that the recommendation to recruit personnel other than physicians to teach basic practical skills can be given."

### Vogel Daniela 2016 (2)

Vogel Daniela, Harendza Sigrid. (2016). Basic practical skills teaching and learning in undergraduate medical education – a review on methodological evidence. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001063](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001063)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Review synthesis noting the countervailing results: the bladder catheterization expert-teaching study and the full-time versus part-time faculty trial, both cited against the general equivalence pattern.

> "However, one study demonstrated that students showed better performance of bladder catheterization after being taught by an expert [58] and students who had been taught by full-time faculty performed better than students who were taught by part-time faculty [64]."
