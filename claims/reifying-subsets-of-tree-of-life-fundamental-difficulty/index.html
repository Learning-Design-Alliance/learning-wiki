Evidence¶

Laura R. Novick and Kefyn M. Catley 2016¶

Laura R. Novick and Kefyn M. Catley. (2016). Fostering 21st-Century Evolutionary Reasoning: Teaching Tree Thinking to Introductory Biology Students. CBE—Life Sciences Education 15:ar66. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-06-0127

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Authors' hypothesis from the Discussion, based on the pattern of change scores in Table 5: the smallest increases (Δ = 0.09–0.10) were for polytomy and subsets of the ToL, which also had among the lowest posttest scores.

"we suspect that understanding that any specific cladogram is a subset of the complete, unimaginably large ToL may be the fundamental difficulty students have to overcome in acquiring tree-thinking expertise."

Laura R. Novick and Kefyn M. Catley 2016 (2)¶

Laura R. Novick and Kefyn M. Catley. (2016). Fostering 21st-Century Evolutionary Reasoning: Teaching Tree Thinking to Introductory Biology Students. CBE—Life Sciences Education 15:ar66. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-06-0127

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Error analysis of the polytomy relatedness question about rabbits, moles, and raccoons: students who erred judged moles more closely related to rabbits than to raccoons based on visual levels, reifying the depicted adjacency rather than reasoning from the MRCA.

"all of the students who got this question wrong on the posttest (96% in the business-as-usual condition, 84% in the experimental instruction condition) gave this incorrect answer."
