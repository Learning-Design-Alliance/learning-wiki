### Jeongyeon Kim and John Mitchell 2026

Jeongyeon Kim and John Mitchell. (2026). How AI Coders Discuss, Disagree, and Reach Consensus: Challenges and Opportunities for LLM-Based Qualitative Coding. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11109 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.11109](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2609.11109)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative coding of AI-agent discussion transcripts using the Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Mode Instrument and discursive-move frameworks, compared with human moves from the literature (Table 6). The authors report LLMs also support "collaborative reflection and seeking consensus."

> "LLMs emulate basic patterns of interaction, such as maintaining stances, acknowledging others’ perspectives, reassessing evidence, explicitly contrasting ideas, expressing a tentative stance, and proposing alternatives."

### Jeongyeon Kim and John Mitchell 2026 (2)

Jeongyeon Kim and John Mitchell. (2026). How AI Coders Discuss, Disagree, and Reach Consensus: Challenges and Opportunities for LLM-Based Qualitative Coding. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11109

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative comparison of AI-agent and human discursive moves. The authors find LLMs "engage in significantly less questioning compared to humans" and operate within a more fixed framework, missing adaptability to implicit cues and evolving conversational dynamics.

> "It is worth noting that LLMs engage in significantly less questioning compared to humans. While humans frequently ask clarifying or probing questions to refine understanding or actively explore the perspectives of other participants, LLMs tend to prioritize delivering coherent and direct answers over engaging in exploratory dialogue and mutual learning."
