## Evidence

### Martínez F 2017

Martínez F, Tobar C, Taramasco C. (2017). Implementation of a Smartphone application in medical education: a randomised trial (iSTART). BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1010-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Intention-to-treat analysis of electronically supervised test timing using predictive mean matching; both time outcomes favoured the application group without reaching significance.

> "Intention-to-treat analyses showed a nonsignificant reduction of 8.5 min for the first outcome and 5.7 s for the latter (p = 0.08 for both)."

### Martínez F 2017 (2)

Martínez F, Tobar C, Taramasco C. (2017). Implementation of a Smartphone application in medical education: a randomised trial (iSTART). BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1010-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Complete-case analysis of the secondary endpoint, average time to answer clinical vignettes, among interns who attended the second supervised assessment.

> "Among participants who attended the second assessment, a 10-min reduction in overall time and a 6.7 s reduction in mean time per question were found, and both reached statistical significance (p = 0.04)."
