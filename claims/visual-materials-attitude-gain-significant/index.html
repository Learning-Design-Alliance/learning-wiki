Evidence¶

Aykut Emre Bozdogan 2011¶

Aykut Emre Bozdogan. (2011). The Effects of Instruction with Visual Materials on the Development of Preservice Elementary Teachers' Knowledge and Attitude Towards Global Warming. TOJET: The Turkish Online Journal of Educational Technology, 10(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ932241

q2 · i3 · causal · r1

Related-samples t-test of GWAS scores for the 23 experimental-group preservice teachers (Table 4): mean rose from 3.58 to 3.82, a statistically meaningful gain with printed effect size d=0.93.

"This difference between pre and posttest scores was statistically meaningful and in favor of the posttest (t (22) = -4.445, p<.001). Correlation between the pre and posttest scores of the experimental group was 0.732, and impact factor was d=0.93."

Aykut Emre Bozdogan 2011 (2)¶

Aykut Emre Bozdogan. (2011). The Effects of Instruction with Visual Materials on the Development of Preservice Elementary Teachers' Knowledge and Attitude Towards Global Warming. TOJET: The Turkish Online Journal of Educational Technology, 10(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ932241

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Related-samples t-test of GWAS scores for the 24 control-group preservice teachers (Table 5): mean moved from 3.60 to 3.70 with no statistically meaningful difference; the article prints d=0.33 but reports the test as non-significant.

"This difference between pre and posttest scores was not statistically meaningful (t (23) = -1.647, p>.05). Correlation between the pre and posttest scores of the control group was 0.761, and impact factor was d=0.33."

Related Claims¶

  • When pretest scores were controlled, the posttest attitude difference between the visual-materials and traditional groups was not statistically significant, though the experimental group's adjusted mean was higher — related
  • All six interviewed experimental-group preservice teachers said visual materials changed their outlook on global warming and made them follow related news more attentively — related
  • Instruction with visual materials rectified more global warming misconceptions than traditional instruction (about 40% versus about 12% success) — related
  • Environmentally friendly behaviors increased far more in the visual-materials group (about 65%) than in the control group (about 4%) — related
  • Both experimental and control preservice teachers held knowledge gaps and misconceptions about global warming before instruction, most commonly that global warming happens due to ozone layer depletion — related
  • Post-test environmental attitudes did not differ significantly between experimental and control groups in the ANCOVA — related
  • Descriptive gains show the experimental group's attitude change exceeded the control group's, and pretesting had no effect on the intervention — related
