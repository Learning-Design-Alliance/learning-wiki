Katharina Loibl · Timo Leuders · Inga Glogger-Frey · Nikol Rummel 2025¶

Katharina Loibl · Timo Leuders · Inga Glogger-Frey · Nikol Rummel. (2025). CID: a framework for the cognitive analysis of composite instructional designs. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09665-9

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

The article reports Glogger-Frey et al.'s (2015) study with one problem-solving or worked-solution task before instruction on density/ratios, finding PS-I "less beneficial for transfer" than WS-I. The article contrasts it with the long-phase 2017 study, says the two differed only in phase duration, and notes that research with a shared sample is needed to test whether this cross-study comparison is valid.

"The results of the first study (Glogger-Frey et al., 2015; short PS1/WS1) revealed that PS-I was less beneficial for transfer than explaining the worked solution before instruction"

Katharina Loibl · Timo Leuders · Inga Glogger-Frey · Nikol Rummel 2025 (2)¶

Katharina Loibl · Timo Leuders · Inga Glogger-Frey · Nikol Rummel. (2025). CID: a framework for the cognitive analysis of composite instructional designs. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09665-9

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

The article reports Glogger-Frey et al.'s (2017) study with two problems in the initial phase, finding PS-I "more beneficial for transfer" than WS-I. The CID framework proposes, as a theoretical explanation rather than a tested result, that working on two problems in different contexts gives an opportunity for analogical encoding, so the intermediate knowledge should be more generalized.

"The second study, with the long problem-solving or worked-solution phase (Glogger-Frey et al., 2017), revealed that PS-I was more beneficial for transfer than explain-ing the worked solution before instruction (WS-I)."
