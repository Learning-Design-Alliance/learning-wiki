## Evidence

### McPherson A 2018

McPherson A, Mackay L, Kunkel J, Duncan S. (2018). Physical activity, cognition and academic performance: an analysis of mediating and confounding relationships in primary school children. BMC Public Health, 18:936. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-018-5863-1

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

SEM analysis (Model 1) of baseline data from 601 New Zealand children aged 6.5–10.8 years, with PA as a latent variable of three pedometer indicators and academic performance as a latent variable of e-asTTle reading and maths scores. The printed effect size is a standardised coefficient of β = 0.225 (small-to-medium), and the "model accounted for 5.1% variance" in academic performance.

> "Greater academic performance was marginally associated with higher levels of PA (standardised coefficient = 0.225, p < 0.001) (Table 2.). The model accounted for 5.1% variance in academic performance."
