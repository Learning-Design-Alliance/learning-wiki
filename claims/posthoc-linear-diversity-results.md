## Evidence

### Schelfhout Stijn 2019

Schelfhout Stijn, Wille Bart, Fonteyne Lot, Roels Elisabeth, De Fruyt Filip, Duyck Wouter. (2019). The effects of vocational interest on study results: Student person – environment fit and program interest diversity. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0214618

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Post hoc program-level regression repeating the GPA-on-diversity analysis after excluding the four programs with very similar, high-social low-realistic RIASEC profiles, reported in the Results post hoc subsection. The linear term was "significant, F(1, 33) = 6.13, p = .02, R2 = .16"; the PASS linear trend explained 8% of variance but did not reach significance (p = .10).

> "Statistically, the linear regression of GPA on interest diversity reached significance, F(1, 33) = 6.13, p = .02, R2 = .16, while the curvilinear one no longer did, F(2, 32) = 3.10, p = .06."

### Schelfhout Stijn 2019 (2)

Schelfhout Stijn, Wille Bart, Fonteyne Lot, Roels Elisabeth, De Fruyt Filip, Duyck Wouter. (2019). The effects of vocational interest on study results: Student person – environment fit and program interest diversity. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0214618

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Correlational post hoc analysis within the four programs (Educational Sciences, Speech Language and Hearing Sciences, East European Languages and Cultures, African Studies), whose RIASEC profiles inter-correlated highly (e.g., .97) and ranked very high on the social and very low on the realistic dimension. The printed correlations "AD COR correlates -.47 with GPA and -.88 with PASS" correspond to r = -.47 (GPA) and r = -.88 (PASS), the latter a large printed effect size.

> "For these specific programs, a low interest diversity is tied to better study results, as is shown by the correlation between their interest diversity and their average study results: AD COR correlates -.47 with GPA and -.88 with PASS."
