## Evidence

### Smith 2000

Smith, Howard A. (2000). Challenges in Affecting US Attitudes Towards Space Science. COSPAR 2000, Warsaw. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0008224](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0008224)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Analysis of the NSF Science and Engineering Indicators knowledge questions, three of which were astronomy-related. The survey found "fewer than half of all adults, 48%, knew that the earth circles the sun in one year"; 73% knew the Earth goes around the Sun at all.

> "Perhaps most surprisingly, fewer than half of all adults, 48%, knew that the earth circles the sun in one year  -- and 28% of those adults with graduate/professional degrees, the most knowledgeable category, did not know this fact."

### Smith 2000 (2)

Smith, Howard A. (2000). Challenges in Affecting US Attitudes Towards Space Science. COSPAR 2000, Warsaw. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0008224](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0008224)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The same S&EI survey asked whether "The Universe began with a huge explosion"; only 32% answered true, and fewer than half of the self-described attentive public answered correctly. For comparison, 93% knew smoking causes lung cancer.

> "Only 32% of all adults answered true to number 1, including fewer than half of those who considered themselves as "attentive" to science topics."
