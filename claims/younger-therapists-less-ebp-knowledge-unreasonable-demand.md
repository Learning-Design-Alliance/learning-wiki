## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Therapists aged 20–29 or with 1–5 years of experience were more likely to agree that EBP implementation placed unreasonable demands on their tasks, with odds ratios of 3.2 and 3.3 versus more experienced groups. [→ Dao Hiep Thi 2018](#dao-hiep-thi-2018)
`q2 i?` Younger participants (20–29) were less likely to agree they were confident or able in steps 1, 3, 4, and 5, and more likely to have inadequate English reading skill. [→ Dao Hiep Thi 2018](#dao-hiep-thi-2018)

## Evidence

### Dao Hiep Thi 2018

Dao Hiep Thi, Pichaiyongwongdee Sopa, Sullivan Patricia E., Prasertsukdee Saipin, Apinonkul Benjawan. (2018). Are physical therapists in Viet Nam ready to implement evidence-based practice? A survey. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1428-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-tab trend analyses (Table 6) of associations between demographics and EBP responses. The printed odds ratios for the unreasonable-demand item were 3.2 (95% CI 1.6–6.2, p=.001) and 3.3 (95% CI 1.7–6.7, p=.001). The article also reports younger participants were less confident in steps 1, 3, 4, and 5. No standardized effect size printed.

> "The results show the trends of associations that young participants (20–29 years of age) or those having less than 5 years of experience were more likely to agree that EBP implementation placed unreasonable demands on their tasks compared to those who were 30–39 years of age or had 6 to 14 years of experience."
