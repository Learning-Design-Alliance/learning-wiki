## Evidence

### Servais Elliot L. 2025

Servais Elliot L., Rashidi Laila, Porwal Priyanshi, Garibaldi Mark, Hung Andrew J. (2025). Novel force feedback technology improves suturing in robotic-assisted surgery: a pre-clinical study. Surgical Endoscopy 39(2):1217-1226. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-024-11472-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized single-blinded between-subjects study on the da Vinci 5 with 29 novice surgeons randomized to force feedback (n = 15) or non-FFB (n = 14). Omnibus three-way mixed-model ANOVA on system-log force data showed the overall reduction, "1.96 N with FFB vs 2.53 N, non-FFB; p = 0.005". No effect size was printed for this finding.

> "The overall mean force for all the exercises combined applied by the surgeons was significantly reduced when given force feedback capability (1.96 N with FFB vs 2.53 N, non-FFB; p = 0.005; Fig. 3A)."

### Servais Elliot L. 2025 (2)

Servais Elliot L., Rashidi Laila, Porwal Priyanshi, Garibaldi Mark, Hung Andrew J. (2025). Novel force feedback technology improves suturing in robotic-assisted surgery: a pre-clinical study. Surgical Endoscopy 39(2):1217-1226. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-024-11472-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exercise-level analysis of force logs from the robotic system data log in the same randomized study. The force feedback cohort was "significantly lower than that of surgeons in the non-FFB cohort across each individual exercise (p < 0.001)". No effect size was printed.

> "Furthermore, the mean force applied by surgeons in the FFB cohort was significantly lower than that of surgeons in the non-FFB cohort across each individual exercise (p < 0.001; Fig. 3B)."
