## Evidence

### Diamond Adele 2011

Diamond Adele, Lee Kathleen. (2011). Interventions shown to Aid Executive Function Development in Children 4–12 Years Old. Science. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1204529

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing multiple CogMed studies, that children improve on practiced games with transfer to other working-memory tasks, and that "Benefits usually don’t generalize to unpracticed EF skills."

> "Youngsters improve on games they practice and this transfers to other working-memory tasks. Groups studied have been typically-developing children (13), and those with ADHD (11, 14) or poor working-memory spans (10). Benefits usually don’t generalize to unpracticed EF skills."

### Diamond Adele 2011 (2)

Diamond Adele, Lee Kathleen. (2011). Interventions shown to Aid Executive Function Development in Children 4–12 Years Old. Science. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1204529

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports that in three CogMed studies, "controls who played the same training games without difficulty increasing" did not show the same gains, supporting the continual-challenge principle.

> "Three studies (10–12) included controls who played the same training games without difficulty increasing; those controls did not show the same gains."
