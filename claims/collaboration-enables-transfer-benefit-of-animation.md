# For transfer, only learners studying collaboratively benefited from animated over static graphics

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The transfer advantage of animated over static graphics was present for learners studying in dyads but not for learners studying individually (animation × learning-setting interaction). [→ Rebetez 2010](#rebetez-2010)

## Evidence

### Rebetez 2010

Rebetez, C., Betrancourt, M., Sangin, M., & Dillenbourg, P. (2010). Learning from animation enabled by collaboration. Instructional Science, 38(5), 471–485. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-009-9117-6

`q3 · i?`

Reported finding of the three-factor between-subjects experiment manipulating instruction type (static vs animated) and learning setting (individual vs collaborative). The article reports that for transfer "only learners studying collaboratively benefited from animated over static graphics"; no effect size is printed.

> "The findings indicate that animation was overall beneficial to retention, while for transfer, only learners studying collaboratively benefited from animated over static graphics."
