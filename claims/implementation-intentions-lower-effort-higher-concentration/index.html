Evidence¶

David 2026¶

David, L., Biwer, F., Crutzen, R., & de Bruin, A. (2026). Understanding and supporting university students' use of distributed practice via implementation intentions. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00448-0

q3 · i? · causal · r?

Daily ESM ratings of study effort across the course: control mean 4.37 (SD 1.02), II group 4.06 (SD 1.16); the mixed-effects model showed "lower levels of daily study effort" in the II group. Effort also rose significantly in week 4 for both groups.

"Results of the mixed effect model indicated a signiﬁcant main effect of group ( β = – 0.42, SE = 0.16, p = .007; 95% CI [ −0.73;−0.12]), showing that participants in the II group reported lower levels of daily study effort compared to those in the control group."

David 2026 (2)¶

David, L., Biwer, F., Crutzen, R., & de Bruin, A. (2026). Understanding and supporting university students' use of distributed practice via implementation intentions. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00448-0

q3 · i? · causal · r?

Concentration ratings from the daily ESM questionnaire showed no overall group main effect, but a significant group × week interaction in week 4, with the II group reporting "higher levels of concentration in the ﬁnal week compared to the control group". Week 2 and 3 interactions were not significant.

"However, a signiﬁcant group × week interaction was observed in week 4 ( β = 0.25, SE = 0.12, p = 0.042, 95% CI [0.02; 0.94]), indicating that participants in the II group reported higher levels of concentration in the ﬁnal week compared to the control group."
