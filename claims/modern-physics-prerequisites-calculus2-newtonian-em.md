### Buzzell 2025

Buzzell, A., Barthelemy, R., & Atherton, T. (2025). Modern physics courses: Understanding the content taught in the U.S. arXiv. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.21.010139](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.21.010139)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of publicly available course catalogs determined the highest level of mathematics required prior to or during enrollment in modern physics. Courses were coded on a seven-level scale from no mathematics required to advanced mathematics.

> "The analysis of the course descriptions, concluded that the majority of students enrolled in Modern Physics have already taken Calculus II (37.1%), followed by the next largest percentage of students having already taken Calculus III (22.8%) as shown in Figure 4."

### Buzzell 2025 (2)

Buzzell, A., Barthelemy, R., & Atherton, T. (2025). Modern physics courses: Understanding the content taught in the U.S. arXiv. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.21.010139](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.21.010139)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Researchers traced prerequisite course codes through course catalogs iteratively, coding topics from prerequisite course descriptions until no further physics prerequisites were required, capturing the full prerequisite chain.

> "As for physics backgrounds, students most commonly have previously been enrolled in courses that have introduced students to Newtonian physics (94%), Electricity & Magnetism (84.4%), and Waves/Optics (77.2%) (see Figure 5)."
