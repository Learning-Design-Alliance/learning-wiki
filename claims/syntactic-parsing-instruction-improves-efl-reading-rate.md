# A 12-week syntactic parsing instruction produced a significantly greater reading-rate gain in the experimental group than in the control group (Cohen's d = 0.52)

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` After 12 weeks of instruction, the experimental group's average reading rate rose from 130wpm to 151wpm while the control group rose from 133wpm to 138wpm, a significant between-group difference (t=2.324, p=.023) with a medium effect size (Cohen's d = 0.52). [→ Chen et al. 2018](#chen-et-al-2018)
`q3 i?` Before instruction the two groups' reading rates did not differ significantly (p=.493), supporting comparability at baseline. [→ Chen et al. 2018 (2)](#chen-et-al-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Chen et al. 2018

Xiaokan Chen, Hongmei Li, Min Gui. (2018). Instructional Effects of Syntactic Parsing on Chinese College Students’ EFL Reading Rates. Journal of Education and Training Studies, Vol. 6, No. 11. https://doi.org/10.11114/jets.v6i11.3470

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Pretest-posttest intact-class comparison: 39 of 43 experimental and 37 of 44 control participants included at posttest after the 70% comprehension cutoff. The conclusion reports t=2.324, p=0.023, and "effect size was medium (Cohen’s d = 0.52)"; gains were 21wpm versus 5wpm.

> "The average reading rate of the experimental group is 151wpm, while the average reading rate of the control group is 138wpm, yielding a significant difference in average reading rate between groups (p= .023<0.05)."

### Chen et al. 2018 (2)

Xiaokan Chen, Hongmei Li, Min Gui. (2018). Instructional Effects of Syntactic Parsing on Chinese College Students’ EFL Reading Rates. Journal of Education and Training Studies, Vol. 6, No. 11. https://doi.org/10.11114/jets.v6i11.3470

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Baseline comparison of pretest reading rates (experimental 130wpm, control 133wpm) with 34 experimental and 35 control participants included. No significant difference was found; equivalence was not otherwise tested.

> "An independent t-test was employed to identify whether the participants’ reading rates were comparable before the instruction. No significant difference was observed between the groups (p= .493>0.05)."
