Evidence¶

Parras 2024¶

Parras, G. G., Delgado-García, J. M., López-Ramos, J. C., Gruart, A., & Leal-Campanario, R. (2024). Cerebellar interpositus nucleus exhibits time-dependent errors and predictive responses. npj Science of Learning 9, 12. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00224-y

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Electrophysiological recording study in awake rabbits during delay classical eyeblink conditioning at three sessions (initial, halfway, final). The authors report that "error responses were signiﬁcantly reduced in the middle (halfway) stage" while predictive responses emerged in the final session; no effect size is printed.

"Our results demonstrate that IPn neurons exhibit a long-lasting error response for almost one second after the CS-US offset when learning is not established. These error responses were signiﬁcantly reduced in the middle (halfway) stage of the learning process, while the predictive response of the US occurred once the learning is well established."

Related Claims¶

  • On CS-alone trials, the significant neuronal difference at the US time vanishes by the final session, while a genuine eyelid conditioned response emerges during stimulus omission — related
  • Conditioning brings eyelid EMG onset forward by roughly 150 ms, and IPn firing precedes the detected EMG change — related
  • IPn firing decreases in spontaneous and late time windows and increases during CS and CS-US windows as conditioning progresses — related
  • Percentage of conditioned eyelid responses increases across conditioning sessions, from 7% (initial) to 25% (halfway) to 81% (final) — related
  • Classical conditioning shifts the strongest IPn-EMG cross-correlations from negative to positive lags, improving IPn-eyelid synchrony
