## Evidence

### Thomas M. Cavanagh 2023

Thomas M. Cavanagh, Christa Kiersch. (2023). Using commonly-available technologies to create online multimedia lessons through the application of the Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-022-10181-1

`q2 · i1` · `review · r2`

The review reports a meta-analysis (Rey et al., 2019) of "56 studies and 7713 participants" finding segmenting positively impacted transfer (d = .36) and reduced cognitive load (d = .23), with increased time on learning (d = − .92).

> "A meta-analysis of 56 studies and 7713 participants found that, overall, segmenting positively impacted retention (k = 67, d = .032, 95% CI [.20, .43]) and transfer (k = 56, d = .36, 95% CI [.24, .48]), reduced cognitive load (k = 20, d = .23, 95% CI [.06, .39]), and increased time spent on learning (k = 19, d = − .92, 95% CI [− 1.64, − .20])"
