## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Odds ratios comparing female to male participation ranged from 1.14 to 1.31, with confidence intervals including 1.0, so the gender difference was not statistically significant. [→ Nissen 2018](#nissen-2018)

## Evidence

### Nissen 2018

Nissen, J. M., Jariwala, M., Close, E. W., & Van Dusen, B. (2018). Participation and Performance on Paper- and Computer-Based Low-Stakes Assessments. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1186/s40594-018-0117-4](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40594-018-0117-4)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

HGLM Model 3 gender coefficients. The article states all odds ratios "predict higher female participation" but their confidence intervals include 1, and that they fall within the range of odds ratios found in three prior studies, suggesting "a consistent but small eﬀect".

> "The odds ratios with 95% conﬁdence intervals comparing female to male participation rates calculated by the HLM software were 1.31 [0.96, 1.79] for the CBT pretest, 1.23 [0.84, 1.81] for the CBT posttest, 1.14 [0.67, 1.95] for the PPT pretest, and 1.25 [0.90, 1.75] for the PPT posttest."
