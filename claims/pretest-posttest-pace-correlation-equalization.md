# Pretest scores correlate positively with learning pace, but posttest scores show no such correlation, indicating slower students catch up when given more time and content

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The pretest score correlates positively with learning pace, consistent with prior knowledge driving learning rate. [→ T. Sampson 2013](#t-sampson-2013)
`q2 i?` The posttest score does not correlate with learning pace, indicating an equalization of learning outcome after slower students spend more time learning. [→ T. Sampson 2013 (2)](#t-sampson-2013-2)

## Evidence

### T. Sampson 2013

T. Sampson, and M. Hilke. (2013). Student Variability in Learning Advanced Physics. arXiv:1307.6144v1 [physics.ed-ph]. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1307.6144](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1307.6144)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Observational analysis of quiz logs plotted in figure 7 with linear regressions for pretest (blue) and posttest (red) scores against pace. The pretest–pace correlation was positive; no correlation coefficient or significance test is printed for either regression, so no i is claimed.

> "In figure 7, we show the pretest and posttest test scores as a function of the student pace. Interestingly, while the pretest score correlates positively with the pace, the posttest score does not."

### T. Sampson 2013 (2)

T. Sampson, and M. Hilke. (2013). Student Variability in Learning Advanced Physics. arXiv:1307.6144v1 [physics.ed-ph]. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1307.6144](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1307.6144)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The same figure-7 analysis: the zero posttest correlation is the authors' interpretation as an equalization of outcomes. The authors caution the study was "too small to provide a statistical significant conclusion because the statistical error on the slope is too large".

> "For the slower students, after going through a learn process, which forces them to spend more time learning the same topic, this leads to an equalization of the learning outcome so that at the end students cannot be distinguished anymore based on their learning pace."
