Malhotra 2026¶

Malhotra, M., & Chhabra, I. (2026). Ensuring academic integrity through automated online exam proctoring a decade long systematic review. Discover Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s44217-026-01224-3

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Reviewed study (Chuang et al.) with 42 US undergraduates in learning sessions, online testing in typical and cheating-inducing environments, and interviews; VFOA features from webcam video, FCBF feature selection, and logistic regression models. The LR model "achieved an average accuracy of 75.6%."

"The study found that both time delay and head pose variations relative to the computer screen were significant predictors of cheating behaviors. The final LR model, incorporating these predictors, achieved an average accuracy of 75.6% in detecting cheating incidents during online exams."

Related Claims¶

  • Head-pose-estimation-based proctoring systems report widely varying accuracies, from 75.6% for cheating detection to 100% for yaw-angle estimation — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Eye-gaze-tracking-based proctoring systems report cheating-detection accuracies around 95–98% in their original studies — related
  • A webcam-only automatic cheating detector achieved recall of 78.6%, precision of 84.6%, and accuracy of 83.3% in a MOOP experiment — related
  • Remote proctoring is perceived as an effective deterrent against cheating in nursing assessments, with 98–100% of graduate NP students agreeing webcam proctoring and lockdown browsers deterred cheating — related
  • Head and eye movements increase over time and with task difficulty during computer-based learning sessions. — related
  • The decision to cheat is influenced by perceived monitoring and likelihood of getting caught rather than severity of punishment — related
  • Proctoring effects on exam performance are mixed, and technical difficulties and cheating were reported in open-book online exams — related
