Evidence¶

Paul 2025¶

Paul, U., Mantravadi, A., Shah, J., Shah, S., Mylavarapu, S. V., Rashid, M. P., & Gehringer, E. (2025). Scaling Success: A Systematic Review of Peer Grading Strategies for Accuracy, Efficiency, and Learning in Contemporary Education. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11677

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports, citing Staubitz et al.'s MOOC platform study, that "80% of participants reported feeling more motivated" to write useful feedback when reviews would be evaluated. Self-report percentage; no effect size.

"Staubitz et al. [82] demonstrated that 80% of participants reported feeling more motivated to write useful feedback once they became aware that their reviews would be evaluated."

Paul 2025 (2)¶

Paul, U., Mantravadi, A., Shah, J., Shah, S., Mylavarapu, S. V., Rashid, M. P., & Gehringer, E. (2025). Scaling Success: A Systematic Review of Peer Grading Strategies for Accuracy, Efficiency, and Learning in Contemporary Education. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11677

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review attributes to Gehringer et al. a student rating of the review-of-review process at "3.9 out of 5" for motivating careful, constructive reviews. Descriptive rating only.

"Gehringer et al. [73] found that students rated the “review-of-review” process 3.9 out of 5 in terms of motivating them to write more careful and constructive reviews."

Paul 2025 (3)¶

Paul, U., Mantravadi, A., Shah, J., Shah, S., Mylavarapu, S. V., Rashid, M. P., & Gehringer, E. (2025). Scaling Success: A Systematic Review of Peer Grading Strategies for Accuracy, Efficiency, and Learning in Contemporary Education. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11677

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports, citing Toll et al., that author scoring of reviews motivated more time and higher-quality feedback, and that expectations of inaccurate scores fell from 52% expecting to 31% experiencing it. Percentages are descriptive; no effect size.

"Toll et al. [74] showed that allowing authors to score the reviews they received motivated reviewers to spend more time and produce higher-quality feedback. Notably, in their study, while 52% of students initially expected to receive inaccurate scores, only 31% actually experienced it."
