# EEG alpha power attenuation is an indicator of visual imagery and is more affected by visual than abstract mentation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Several authors regard alpha activity attenuation or blocking as an indicator of visual imagery in general. [→ Bértolo 2005](#bertolo-2005)
`q2 i?` Alpha power is more affected by visual mentation than by abstract mentation, and imagery causes less alpha suppression than direct inspection of the imaged pattern. [→ Bértolo 2005 (2)](#bertolo-2005-2)

## Evidence

### Bértolo 2005

Bértolo, H. (2005). Visual imagery without visual perception? Psicológica, 26, 173-188. https://www.uv.es/psicologica

`q2 · i?`

The review's section on alpha activity and visual imagery states that "Some authors regard alpha activity attenuation (i.e. decreasing of the power) or blocking as an indicator of visual imagery in general", citing multiple EEG and MEG studies. The alpha rhythm is described as mainly generated in visual cortex at 8-13 Hz.

> "Some authors regard alpha activity attenuation (i.e. decreasing of the power) or blocking as an indicator of visual imagery in general"

### Bértolo 2005 (2)

Bértolo, H. (2005). Visual imagery without visual perception? Psicológica, 26, 173-188. https://www.uv.es/psicologica

`q2 · i?`

The review reports EEG frequency-band findings attributed to Lehman and colleagues, including that "the alpha power is more affected by visual mentation then by abstract mentation", and Salenius et al.'s MEG finding that forming a visual image caused less suppression than direct inspection of the imaged pattern.

> "the alpha power is more affected by visual mentation then by abstract mentation"
