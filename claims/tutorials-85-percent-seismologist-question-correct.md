## Evidence

### Rachel E. Scherr 2002

Rachel E. Scherr, Peter S. Shaffer, and Stamatis Vokos. (2002). The challenge of changing deeply-held student beliefs about the relativity of simultaneity. https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1507792

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Post-test results on the Seismologist question administered after tutorial instruction, combined across courses at the same level. The article reports "About 85% of the introductory and advanced undergraduate students answered correctly," which the authors note is better than graduate students' performance on the qualifying examination.

> "After students have completed the two tutorials, performance on this ques tion is very good.  About 85% of the introductory and advanced undergraduate students answered correctly."

### Rachel E. Scherr 2002 (2)

Rachel E. Scherr, Peter S. Shaffer, and Stamatis Vokos. (2002). The challenge of changing deeply-held student beliefs about the relativity of simultaneity. https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1507792

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Pretest and post-traditional-instruction results on the Seismologist question (Table I: introductory N=88 and N=79; advanced N=48 and N=63). Student responses were similar before and after lecture instruction, indicating traditional instruction alone did not address the difficulty.

> "Without tutorial instruction, relatively few undergraduates (between 20% -30% at the introductory level, and about 40% at the advanced level) answered correctly about the time order of events in the frame of the  assistant."
