# Administrators' dual role as evaluator and coach can undermine the observation process's professional learning benefits

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Because ratings carry stakes, some teachers mask instructional weaknesses rather than seek support from the administrator who also evaluates them. [→ Susan E. Sporte 2013](#susan-e-sporte-2013)

## Evidence

### Susan E. Sporte 2013

Susan E. Sporte, W. David Stevens, Kaleen Healey, Jennie Jiang, and Holly Hart. (2013). Teacher Evaluation in Practice: Implementing Chicago's REACH Students. UChicago CCSR Research Report. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/teacher-evaluation-practice-implementing-chicagos-reach-students

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview data from teachers in six randomly selected CPS schools. One teacher reported feeling forced to present favorably rather than have honest discussions; another said a request for help with classroom management was used against her in an observation rating.

> "Because there is such an emphasis placed on assessing the quality of teachers, there is no incentive for teachers to admit insecurity or talk about areas in which he or she struggles."
