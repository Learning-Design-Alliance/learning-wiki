# No significant gender difference in gaming the system appears in the non-game control condition or in the problem-solving step

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the non-game condition, no significant difference between male and female students' gaming was observed in either the problem-solving (F(1,81)=0.17, p=0.68) or self-explanation steps (F(1,81)=0.07, p=0.79). [→ Baker 2025](#baker-2025)
`q3 i?` In the game condition, there was no significant gender difference in gaming on the problem-solving step (F(1,68)=0.096, p=0.76). [→ Baker 2025 (2)](#baker-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Baker 2025

Baker, R. S., Richey, J. E., Zhang, J., Karumbaiah, S., Andres-Bray, J. M., Nguyen, H. A., Andres, J. M. A. L., & McLaren, B. M. (2025). Gaming the system mediates the relationship between gender and learning outcomes in a digital learning game. Instructional Science, 53, 203–238. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09679-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Study 1 ANOVA in the non-game tutor condition found no gender difference in gaming in either step, suggesting "students engaged similarly with the non-game tutor regardless of gender." Equivalence was not tested; only non-significance is reported.

> "In the non-game condition, no significant difference between male and female students’ gaming the system behaviors was observed in either the problem-solving (F(1,81) = 0.17, p = 0.68) or self-explanation steps (F(1,81) = 0.07, p = 0.79)."

### Baker 2025 (2)

Baker, R. S., Richey, J. E., Zhang, J., Karumbaiah, S., Andres-Bray, J. M., Nguyen, H. A., Andres, J. M. A. L., & McLaren, B. M. (2025). Gaming the system mediates the relationship between gender and learning outcomes in a digital learning game. Instructional Science, 53, 203–238. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09679-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Study 1 ANOVA in the game condition found no significant gender difference in gaming rates on the problem-solving step; the gender difference was specific to the self-explanation step.

> "There was no significant difference on the problem-solving step (F(1,68) = 0.096, p = 0.76)."
