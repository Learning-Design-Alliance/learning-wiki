# Senior nursing students taught with an integrative team-based-PBL-plus-simulation curriculum self-rated higher core nursing competencies than students under traditional teaching

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students exposed to the integrative curriculum reported higher self-rated core nursing competencies across all six domains than students exposed to the traditional curriculum. [→ Huang Cheng-Yi 2020](#huang-cheng-yi-2020)

## Evidence

### Huang Cheng-Yi 2020

Huang Cheng-Yi, Wang Ya-huei. (2020). Toward an Integrative Nursing Curriculum: Combining Team-Based and Problem-Based Learning with Emergency-Care Scenario Simulation. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17124612

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of two groups in an 18-week single-case action-research study of 58 senior nursing students in an Emergency Care course, using the self-rated Scale of Core Nursing Competencies (Cronbach's alpha 0.78) displayed in a radar chart. The integrative group's "higher core nursing competencies" means ranged 4.55–4.70 versus 1.50–3.15 for the traditional group; no statistical test or effect size is reported.

> "compared to those not receiving the intervention in their final academic year (means = 2.20, 3.15, 1.54, 1.50, 1.52, and 2.78), the students exposed to the integrative curriculum felt that after the intervention, they had higher core nursing competencies (means = 4.70, 4.57, 4.57, 4.60, 4.60, and 4.55)"
