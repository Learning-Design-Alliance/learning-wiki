Jeff Bale, James Day, J. Ives, and D. A. Bonn. (2026). Constraints From Simulation Improve Experiential Outcomes in Laboratory Environment. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2605.30624](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.30624)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Tukey's HSD post-hoc comparison following MANOVA on pair-level records found no significant quality-metric differences in rounds 1 and 2 (adjusted p = .417 and .075), but a significant difference by round three favoring the simulation group; the authors note the comparison's power was modest (0.62). No effect size is printed.

> "by the third round, the adjusted p-values indicated that the simulation group had significantly higher values compared to the physical apparatus group, with means of 68.6±6.7 for the simulation and 48.6±5.2 for the physical apparatus."
