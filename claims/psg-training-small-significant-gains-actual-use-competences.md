# After training, physiotherapists showed small but significant self-reported improvements in informing and involving patients and in client-centred competences, with no significant change in intention to use the PSG

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · design `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2017 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Self-reported actual use improved significantly for 'informing patients about the reason for using the PSG' and 'involving patients in treatment planning' (both P < 0.01). [→ Stevens A 2017](#stevens-a-2017)
`q2 i?` Client-centred competences (enabling client participation subscale) improved with a mean difference of 0.66 (P = .026). [→ Stevens A 2017 (2)](#stevens-a-2017-2)
`q2 i?` Intention-to-use subscales (attitude, subjective norms, perceived behavioural control, behaviour intention) showed no significant improvement. [→ Stevens A 2017 (3)](#stevens-a-2017-3)

## Evidence

### Stevens A 2017

Stevens A, Köke A, van der Weijden T, Beurskens A. (2017). Ready for goal setting? Process evaluation of a patient-specific goal-setting method in physiotherapy. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-017-2557-9

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Non-parametric Related Samples Wilcoxon Signed-Rank tests on a 4-item 0–10 actual-use questionnaire completed before training (N = 51) and at follow-up (N = 48) in a non-controlled intervention study.

> "After follow-up, there were small, but significant improvements in ‘informing patients about the reason for using the PSG’ (P < 0.01) and ‘involving patients in treatment planning’ (P < 0.01)."

### Stevens A 2017 (2)

Stevens A, Köke A, van der Weijden T, Beurskens A. (2017). Ready for goal setting? Process evaluation of a patient-specific goal-setting method in physiotherapy. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-017-2557-9

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Paired-samples t-test on the 7-item 'enabling client participation' subscale, scored 1–4, before (mean 22.68, SD 3.09) and after training (mean 23.68, SD 2.63); the article reports a small but significant improvement (P < 0.05).

> "Subscale: enabling client participation (sum score of 7 items, range 7–28)	22.68 (3.09)	23.68 (2.63)	0.66 (1.96)	.026** (.08–1.23)"

### Stevens A 2017 (3)

Stevens A, Köke A, van der Weijden T, Beurskens A. (2017). Ready for goal setting? Process evaluation of a patient-specific goal-setting method in physiotherapy. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-017-2557-9

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

31-item Theory of Planned Behaviour questionnaire (four subscales) analysed with paired-samples t-tests or Wilcoxon Signed-Rank tests; no statistically significant improvement was detected. Equivalence was not tested.

> "The mean and median sum scores on the subscales showed no significant improvement after follow-up."
