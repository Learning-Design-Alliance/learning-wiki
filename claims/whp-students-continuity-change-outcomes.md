# Students learning from World History Project demonstrate more understanding of continuity and change over time in history than comparison students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` WHP students scored higher on continuity and change over time in both summative student work (g = .60) and everyday student work (g = .36). [→ Iwatani 2023](#iwatani-2023)

## Evidence

### Iwatani 2023

Iwatani, E., Hardy, A., Means, B., Daal, S., & Wei, X. (2023). Evaluation of World History Project. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/199

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Analysis of 1,509 rubric-scored student work samples from 25 teachers in the 2022-23 comparison found significant outcome differences favoring WHP only for continuity and change over time, with the printed Hedges' g values of .60 and .36.

> "We found statistically significant impact estimates on student outcomes for historical thinking skills, favoring the use of World History Project in: continuity over time in history summative student work ( M WHP = .21, M Comp = .06, β = .15, SE = 0.06, p = .014, Hedges' g = .60) and everyday student work ( M WHP = .11, M Comp = .04, β = .07, SE = 0.03, p = .032, Hedges' g = .36)"
