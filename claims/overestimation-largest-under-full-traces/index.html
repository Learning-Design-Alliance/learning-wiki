Evidence¶

Fernandes 2026¶

Fernandes, D., Buschek, D., Tankelevitch, L., Kosch, T., & Welsch, R. (2026). Explaining Too Much? Understanding How Large Language Model Reasoning Traces Influence Performance and Metacognition. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25856 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.25856

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Global performance estimates collected after all ten problems exceeded achieved performance in each condition; the Full-trace overestimation test printed d = 1.21, with Answer-only d = .85 and Summary-trace d = .97 reported alongside.

"InFull-tracecondition, participants estimated 7.91 items correct (𝑆𝐷= 1.71), while achieving 5.46 correct items, also showing significant overestimation,𝑡( 182)= 16.35,𝑝<. 001,𝑑= 1.21."

Fernandes 2026 (2)¶

Fernandes, D., Buschek, D., Tankelevitch, L., Kosch, T., & Welsch, R. (2026). Explaining Too Much? Understanding How Large Language Model Reasoning Traces Influence Performance and Metacognition. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25856. doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.25856

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

Omnibus ANOVA on overestimation (perceived minus achieved) was significant with η² = .021; Tukey-adjusted comparisons showed Full-trace overestimation significantly larger than Answer-only (Mdiff = 0.70, padj = .003), while Summary-trace did not differ from either.

"the magnitude of overestimation differed significantly by condition,𝐹( 2, 556)= 5.83,𝑝=. 003,𝜂2 =. 021."
