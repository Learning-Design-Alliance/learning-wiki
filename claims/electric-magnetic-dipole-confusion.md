## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2017

Chandralekha Singh. (2017). Improving Students' Understanding of Magnetism. Originally published 2007. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1701.01523](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1701.01523)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Pre/post multiple-choice results for the question asking the net force on a charge at rest due to an electric dipole and a bar magnet: post-test performance of "41%" was "not significantly different from the pre-test performance of 38%".

> "As can be seen from Table 1, the post-test performance on question (1) is 41% which is not significantly different from the pre-test performance of 38% (before instruction)."

### Chandralekha Singh 2017 (2)

Chandralekha Singh. (2017). Improving Students' Understanding of Magnetism. Originally published 2007. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1701.01523](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1701.01523)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Written free-response explanations and think-aloud individual interviews, including probes about cutting a magnet in two, indicated students "often have difficulty distinguishing between the poles of a magnet and point charges".

> "Both written explanations and interviews suggest that students often have difficulty distinguishing between the poles of a magnet and point charges."
