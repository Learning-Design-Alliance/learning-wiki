# Experimental and control groups were homogeneous in idiom knowledge before treatment

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` A pretest t-test showed no significant difference between control (M=2.05, SD=1.60) and experimental (M=2.20, SD=1.32) groups, indicating little prior knowledge of the target idioms. [→ Afrouz Aeineh 2014](#afrouz-aeineh-2014)

`q2 i?` An independent samples t-test on the idiom pretest found no significant difference between groups (p value .55, higher than .05). [→ Abdorreza Tahriri 2014](#abdorreza-tahriri-2014)

`q2 i?` An independent-samples t-test on pretest scores found no significant difference between the control and experimental groups (t = -1.045, p = .284). [→ Ahmet Basal 2016](#ahmet-basal-2016)

`q2 i?` An independent-samples t-test on the 40-item idiom pretest found no significant difference between the EG and CG before the treatment. [→ Malihe Neissari 2017](#malihe-neissari-2017)

## Evidence

### Afrouz Aeineh 2014

Afrouz Aeineh, Saeed Moeeni, Hamideh Merati. (2014). The Effect of Learner-generated Illustrations on the Immediate And Delayed Recall of English Idioms. Advances in Language and Literary Studies. https://doi.org/10.7575/aiac.alls.v.5n.3p.28

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Independent-samples t-test on pretest scores before treatment (t=.323, df=38, Sig.=.749); the authors state the mean score and P value "strongly proves that the two groups are homogeneous" and that neither group had much prior knowledge of the treatment materials.

> "there was no significant difference in scores for the control group (M=2.05, SD=1.60) and the experimental group, M=2.20, SD=1.32; P=.74 (two tailed)"

### Abdorreza Tahriri 2014

Abdorreza Tahriri, Masoud Khalili Sabet, Afrouz Aeineh. (2014). The Effect of Portfolio Assessment on Learning Idioms in Writing. International Journal of Education & Literacy Studies, 2(2). http://dx.doi.org/10.7575/aiac.ijels.v.2n.2p.53

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Second homogeneity check on idiom pretest scores (Table 3 means: control 4.13, experimental 4.46). The article concludes "there was no significant difference in terms of idiom competence of the two groups", establishing that the taught idioms were unfamiliar to both groups.

> "According to table 4, the p value is .55 which is higher than .05. Therefore , it can be concluded that there was no significant difference in terms of idiom competence of the two groups."

### Ahmet Basal 2016

Ahmet Basal, Selahattin Yilmaz, Asli Tanriverdi, & Lutfiye Sari. (2016). Effectiveness of Mobile Applications in Vocabulary Teaching. Contemporary Educational Technology, 7(1), 47-59. https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/cet

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Baseline comparison in the Procedures section: the pretest on the 40 target idioms showed no significant difference between groups, and Table 3 reports t = -1.045, df = 52, p = .284, with means of 15.24 (control) and 17.48 (experimental). Equivalence was not formally tested beyond this t-test.

> "Based on this pre test results, there  were no significant difference between the two groups, indicating that their knowledge level was similar on the selected idioms."

### Malihe Neissari 2017

Malihe Neissari, Hamid Ashraf & Mohammad Reza Ghorbani. (2017). Humorous Videos and Idiom Achievement: Some Pedagogical Considerations for EFL Learners. Iranian Journal of Language Teaching Research, 5(3), 109-127. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1156181

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Baseline check in the quasi-experimental study: the teacher-made 40-item idiom pretest was administered to both groups at the outset of the term. The t-test found no significant difference (EG M = 9.55, CG M = 9.93, t(64) = -0.85, p = 0.39); equivalence was not formally tested beyond this non-significant result.

> "there was no significant difference between the EG ( M = 9.55, SD = 1.99) and the CG [ M = 9.93, SD = 1.52; t (64) = -0.85, p = 0.39] before the treatment."
