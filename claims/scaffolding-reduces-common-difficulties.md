# Common unscaffolded difficulties on the tire swing problem (treating it as equilibrium, missing the centripetal acceleration, misusing kinematics equations) were reduced by the solved-problem scaffolding

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Without scaffolding, students showed errors such as treating the problem as an equilibrium situation and setting v2/r=g; with scaffolding, more students identified both the gravitational force and the centripetal acceleration. [→ Lin 2016](#lin-2016)

## Evidence

### Lin 2016

Lin, S.-Y. and Singh, C. (2016). Using Analogies to Learn Introductory Physics. Department of Physics and Astronomy, University of Pittsburgh. Originally published 2008. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06388](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06388)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Qualitative error analysis of student work across groups, described in the results section. The article lists unscaffolded mistakes (missing the gravitational force term, treating the problem as equilibrium, erroneously using "v2/r=g") and states these "mistakes were reduced when the scaffolding" was provided.

> "With the scaffolding, more students were able to identify the existence of both the gravitational force and the centripetal acceleration, and most students could apply the principle of conservation of mechanical energy to find the speed correctly."
