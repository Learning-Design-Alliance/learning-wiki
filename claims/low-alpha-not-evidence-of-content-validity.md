# A low alpha does not necessarily indicate high content validity, because reliability and validity can both be high

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The reliability-validity trade-off is not a logical necessity: semantically distinct measures of the same concept can correlate highly, and a low alpha may stem from causes other than content diversity, such as unreliable items. [→ Steffen Zitzmann 2025](#steffen-zitzmann-2025)

## Evidence

### Steffen Zitzmann 2025

Steffen Zitzmann, Gabe A. Orona. (2025). Why We Might Still be Concerned About Low Cronbach's Alphas in Domain-specific Knowledge Tests. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10015-5

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument using an analogy of head circumference and body height as semantically distinct but highly correlated indicators of toddler physical development: "reliability and validity can both be high," so low alpha cannot be inferred to indicate validity. The authors add that low alpha may instead reflect unreliability of items.

> "Our point here is that reliability and validity can both be high, calling the logic of what has been termed reliability-validity trade-off into question."
