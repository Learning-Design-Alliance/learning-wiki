## Evidence

### Dairo Yetunde M. 2024

Dairo Yetunde M., Hunter Kirsty, Ishaku Timothy. (2024). The impact of simulation-based learning on the knowledge, attitude and performance of physiotherapy students on practice placement. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05718-2

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview data from the study's 'Working together' theme: 12 of 18 eligible students consented; inductive thematic analysis of semi-structured virtual interviews found unanimous positive views of group work, describing exposure to "team-based work" as easing support-seeking on placement.

> "Participants unanimously expressed a positive view of working in groups. One participant described it as “fantastic.” They believed that the exposure to team-based work during the simulation placement made it easier for them to seek support when needed and assist other team members as required when on practice placement."
