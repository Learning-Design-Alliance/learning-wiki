# Student aptitude strongly predicts writing performance but not clicker improvement, with no treatment × student interactions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Higher-aptitude students (average multiple-choice exam score) scored higher on in-class and exam writing and on postactivity clicker scores, but aptitude did not correlate with pre-to-post clicker improvement; no treatment advantaged stronger or weaker students. [→ Linton 2014](#linton-2014)

## Evidence

### Linton 2014

Linton, D. L., Pangle, W. M., Wyatt, K. H., Powell, K. N., & Sherwood, R. E. (2014). Identifying Key Features of Effective Active Learning: The Effects of Writing and Peer Discussion. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.13-12-0242

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

GLMM and correlational analyses using average multiple-choice exam score as an aptitude measure; exam-writing association χ2 = 367.3, p < 0.0001; in-class writing χ2 = 111.6, p < 0.0001; clicker improvement χ2 = 1.7, p = 0.19; postactivity clicker scores r = 0.24, p < 0.0001. No standardized effect size for the writing associations.

> "Students' average multiple-choice exam scores were a very strong predictor of how well students performed in the writing during an exam, regardless of the treatment they received in lectures (Figure 4; χ2 = 367.3, df = 1, p < 0.0001)."
