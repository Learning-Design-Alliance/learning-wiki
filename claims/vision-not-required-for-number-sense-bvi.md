## Evidence

### Steinbach 2022

Steinbach, S. (2022). Looking beyond vision: Supports for students who are blind or visually impaired in mathematics. NWEA. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/looking-beyond-vision-supports-for-students-who-are-blind-or-visually-impaired-in-mathematics/

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

The review attributes to Ahlberg & Csocsán (1999), a study of 25 Hungarian children aged 5 to 9 born blind, the finding that BVI children "start their understanding by knowing numbers as words first" rather than using fingers for number exploration.

> "Ahlberg & Csocsán (1999) discovered that BVIs do not use their fingers to explore numbers like children with sight generally do, but rather, start their understanding by knowing numbers as words first; their fingers are primarily used for exploration of the world around them."
