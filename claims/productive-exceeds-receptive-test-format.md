# Students gained more productive than receptive vocabulary knowledge, a disparity the authors attribute to the design and format of the tests

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Contrary to the expected receptive-before-productive development cline, students scored higher on the productive test than the receptive test. [→ Mohamad A. Yusuf 2014](#mohamad-a-yusuf-2014)
`q1 i?` The authors attribute the disparity to the gap-filling format with initial-letter clues, sentence-level context, lenient scoring, and the context-free multiple-choice receptive format. [→ Mohamad A. Yusuf 2014 (2)](#mohamad-a-yusuf-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Mohamad A. Yusuf 2014

Mohamad A. Yusuf, Tam S. Sim & Su’ad A. (2014). Students’ Proficiency and Textual Computer Gloss Use in Facilitating Vocabulary Knowledge. English Language Teaching, 7(11). http://dx.doi.org/10.5539/elt.v7n11p99

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparison of mean scores on the two tests in the discussion section; productive total mean 12.19 versus receptive total mean 9.96 at immediate test. The article notes this reverses the documented receptive-to-productive cline. No effect size printed.

> "From the data, it can be seen that st udents gained more productive vocabulary knowledge compared to receptive knowledge."

### Mohamad A. Yusuf 2014 (2)

Mohamad A. Yusuf, Tam S. Sim & Su’ad A. (2014). Students’ Proficiency and Textual Computer Gloss Use in Facilitating Vocabulary Knowledge. English Language Teaching, 7(11). http://dx.doi.org/10.5539/elt.v7n11p99

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Authors' interpretation, not a tested result: the gap-filling format with initial letters, sentence-level context and lenient scoring may have inflated productive scores, while the context-free multiple-choice receptive test was harder. Offered as a probable explanation.

> "The conclusion that can be made is that the design and format of the tests could play a significant role in the outcome of the tests."
