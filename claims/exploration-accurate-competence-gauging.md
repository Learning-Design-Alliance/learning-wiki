## Evidence

### DeCaro 2012

DeCaro, M. S., & Rittle-Johnson, B. (2012). Exploring mathematics problems prepares children to learn from instruction. Journal of Experimental Child Psychology, 113, 552-568. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2012.06.009

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA on subjective ratings of understanding across intervention blocks: "A significant Order × Block interaction was found, F(1, 152) = 5.81, p = .017"; after Block 1 solve-instruct children rated their understanding lower, and after Block 2 ratings were as high as instructed-first children's (F < 1).

> "A signiﬁcant Order × Block interaction was found, F(1, 152) = 5.81, p = .017,
g2p = .04. No other condition effects reached signiﬁcance (Fs < 2.83). As shown in Table 4, after Block
1, children in the solve–instruct condition rated their understanding as lower than children in the instruct–solve condition"

### DeCaro 2012 (2)

DeCaro, M. S., & Rittle-Johnson, B. (2012). Exploring mathematics problems prepares children to learn from instruction. Journal of Experimental Child Psychology, 113, 552-568. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2012.06.009

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis of final subjective ratings with retention-test conceptual knowledge in the same 159-child experiment: the solve-instruct correlation "remained signiﬁcant for the solve–instruct group (rp = .27, p = .020)" while the instruct-solve correlation was not significant (rp = .13, p = .249); r = .27 is a small-to-moderate effect.

> "At retention test, the correlation between ratings of understanding and conceptual knowledge remained signiﬁcant for the solve–instruct group (rp = .27, p = .020). However, the
relationship was no longer signiﬁcant for the instruct–solve group (rp = .13, p = .249)."
