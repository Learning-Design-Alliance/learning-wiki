# High and low CI practice do not differ significantly in overall relatively permanent gains (Δ pre-retention)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q4` · `i0` negligible

## Subclaims
`q4 i0` The overall long-term (pre-to-retention) gain is not significantly greater following high CI (24.56 ± 4.4%) than low CI (21.9 ± 9.8%) practice (ES = −0.13, p = 0.18). [→ Achraf Ammar 2024](#achraf-ammar-2024)

## Evidence

### Achraf Ammar 2024

Achraf Ammar, Khaled Trabelsi, Mohamed Ali Boujelbane, Atef Salem, Omar Boukhris, Jordan M. Glenn, Piotr Zmijewski, Haitham A. Jahrami, Hamdi Chtourou, Wolfgang I. Schöllhorn. (2024). The Effects of Contextual Interference Learning on the Acquisition and Relatively Permanent Gains in Skilled Performance: A Critical Systematic Review with Multilevel Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09892-z

`q4 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Pooled Δ pre-retention comparison in the multilevel meta-analysis of 36 studies: high CI gained 24.56 ± 4.4% versus low CI 21.9 ± 9.8%, a non-significant difference (ES = −0.13, p = 0.18) with a negligible printed effect size.

> "However, this difference was not large enough (ES =  − 0.35) to produce an overall greater long-term gain following high (24.56 ± 4.4%), compared to low (21.9 ± 9.8%) CI (ES =  − 0.13, p = 0.18)."
