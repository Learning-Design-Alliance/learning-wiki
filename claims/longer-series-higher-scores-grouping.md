# Longer letter series yield somewhat higher average scores because they offer opportunities for occasional high scores via mental grouping

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Seven-place series are somewhat more favorable than six-place, and six-place than five-place, in average scores. [→ Whipple 1910](#whipple-1910)
`q2 i?` Correct reports of 6 or 7 letters are almost invariably cases where vowels permitted syllable formation, i.e., high scores reflect mental grouping. [→ Whipple 1910 (2)](#whipple-1910-2)

## Evidence

### Whipple 1910

Whipple, G. M. (1910). The effect of practise upon the range of visual attention and of visual apprehension. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://books.google.com/books?q=Whipple+effect+of+practise+range+of+visual+attention+apprehension

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of the three series in Table 1: general averages were 5.06 (B), 4.53 (E), 4.97 (N), 4.71 (T), 4.82 overall. The article says this is not because longer series are easier but because they offer opportunities for occasional scores of 6 or 7.

> "the 7-place series are somewhat more favorable than the 6-place, and these somewhat more favorable than the 5-plaee series in average scores."

### Whipple 1910 (2)

Whipple, G. M. (1910). The effect of practise upon the range of visual attention and of visual apprehension. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://books.google.com/books?q=Whipple+effect+of+practise+range+of+visual+attention+apprehension

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Analysis of instances where 6 or 7 letters were reported correctly: these were almost invariably cases in which the presence of vowels permitted the formation of syllables, so the advantage reflects grouping rather than a wider grasp.

> "these high scores are due to mental grouping of the elements exposed."
