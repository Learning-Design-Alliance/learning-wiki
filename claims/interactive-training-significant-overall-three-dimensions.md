## Subclaims
`q2 i?` University students taught non-cognitive skills via an interactive training program scored significantly higher than students taught by traditional methods and controls on the overall scale and on empathy, growth mindset, and grit. [→ Gheith and Aljaberi 2017](#gheith-and-aljaberi-2017)

## Evidence

### Gheith and Aljaberi 2017

Eman Gheith & Nahil M. Aljaberi. (2017). The Effectiveness of an Interactive Training Program in Developing a Set of Non-Cognitive Skills in Students at University of Petra. International Education Studies, 10(6). https://doi.org/10.5539/ies.v10n6p60

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA of scores from 174 students randomly distributed into three groups (57 experimental, 58 traditional, 59 control) on a 54-item researcher-designed scale. The article reports "statistically significant differences" with no effect size printed; Table 1 shows F-values of 9.1, 6.99, and 6.8 for empathy, growth mindset, and grit.

> "The F-values indicate statistically significant differences between the different methods used on Groups A-C in the overall test, and on three dimensions: empathy, growth mindset, and grit."
