# Whether students used group resources to try to understand procedures strongly predicted individual-test success

> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 causal `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` Among students who used the group's resources to try to understand the procedures, the probability of a high individual-test score was 0.71; among those who used resources only to obtain the correct answer, it was zero. [→ Webb 1992](#webb-1992)

`q3 i?` Among students who showed difficulty and received assistance, those who tried to understand the help obtained much higher individual test scores than those who merely used the assistance without understanding it. [→ Webb 1993](#webb-1993)

## Evidence

### Webb 1992

Webb, Noreen. (1992). Collaborative Group versus Individual Assessment in Mathematics: Group Processes and Outcomes. Review of Group Assessment Issues. Project 2.3: Enhancing the Utility of Performance Assessments: Domain-Independent R&D. National Center for Research on Evaluation, Standards, and Student Testing. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED353299

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Coding of tape-recorded group processes (93% interrater agreement) classified how students used group assistance. Of 21 students using group resources, only 7 tried to understand the procedures; effort to understand, not receiving help itself, predicted individual-test performance.

> "Among students who used the group's resources to try to understand the procedures, the probability of obtaining a high score on the individual test was 0.71 (5 out of 7 students). Among students who used the group's resources only to obtain the correct answer to the problems, the probability of obtaining a high score on the individual test was zero (0 out of 14 students)."

### Webb 1993

Webb, N. M. (1993). Collaborative Group Versus Individual Assessment in Mathematics: Processes and Outcomes. Educational Assessment, 7(2), 131-152. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326977ea0102_3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Analysis of coded group behavior for the 15 students (28%) who showed difficulty and received assistance. Table 2 shows mean individual scores of .71 for the seven who tried to understand versus .19 for the eight who did not; the article reports these as descriptive means without a test statistic.

> "students who tried to understand the assistance that they received obtained much higher scores on the individual test than students who did not try to understand the assistance they received"
