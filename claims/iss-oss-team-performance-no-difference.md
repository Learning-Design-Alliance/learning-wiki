## Evidence

### Sørensen JL 2015

Sørensen JL, van der Vleuten C, Rosthøj S, Østergaard D, LeBlanc V, Johansen M, Ekelund K, Starkopf L, Lindschou J, Gluud C, Weikop P, Ottesen B. (2015). Simulation-based multiprofessional obstetric anaesthesia training conducted in situ versus off-site leads to similar individual and team outcomes: a randomised educational trial. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2015-008344

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Four consultants outside the trial hospital rated 20 recorded scenarios using the Team Emergency Assessment Measure; linear mixed models gave estimated overall differences of 0.1 (p=0.36) for item rating and 0.7 (p=0.18) for global rating.

> "TEAM: No significant differences were found in the team scoring of performance between the ISS versus the OSS group (table 8)."

### Sørensen JL 2015 (2)

Sørensen JL, van der Vleuten C, Rosthøj S, Østergaard D, LeBlanc V, Johansen M, Ekelund K, Starkopf L, Lindschou J, Gluud C, Weikop P, Ottesen B. (2015). Simulation-based multiprofessional obstetric anaesthesia training conducted in situ versus off-site leads to similar individual and team outcomes: a randomised educational trial. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2015-008344

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post hoc linear mixed-model analysis found differences in mean of 0.3 (0.1 to 0.5, p=0.0003) for TEAM item rating and 0.9 (0.3 to 1.5, p=0.005) for global rating between the first and second scenarios.

> "A significant increase was found in the team scoring of performance from the first simulation (emergency caesarean section) to the second (postpartum haemorrhage) (table 8)."
