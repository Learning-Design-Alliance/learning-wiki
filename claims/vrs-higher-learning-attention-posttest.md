## Evidence

### Chang W 2025

Chang W, Lin C-C, Crilly J, Lee H-L, Chen L-C, Han C-Y. (2025). Virtual Reality Simulation for Undergraduate Nursing Students for Care of Patients With Infectious Diseases: Mixed Methods Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/64780

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental IMMS comparison (ARCS subscales) between the VRS group and control group at posttest in phase 1. The article reports only the test statistic "(t105=2.30; P=.02)" for the Attention difference, with no effect size.

> "At T1, only the Attention dimension differed significantly between the VRS and control groups, being higher in the VRS group (t105=2.30; P=.02), as shown in Table 3."

### Chang W 2025 (2)

Chang W, Lin C-C, Crilly J, Lee H-L, Chen L-C, Han C-Y. (2025). Virtual Reality Simulation for Undergraduate Nursing Students for Care of Patients With Infectious Diseases: Mixed Methods Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/64780

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

IMMS total-score analysis in phase 1 showing a slight pooled pre-to-post motivation increase but "no significant differences between the groups" at either time point; equivalence was not tested.

> "The learning motivation of all students increased slightly from T0 (mean 3.84, SD 0.47) to T1 (mean 3.94, SD 0.40) (t106=–3.10; P=.002), with no significant differences between the groups at either T0 (t76.50=0.09; P=.93) or T1 (t80.95=1.43; P=.16)."
