Evidence¶

Ashley Chen 2024¶

Ashley Chen, Suchita E. Kumar, Rhea Varkhedi, Dillon H. Murphy. (2024). The Effect of Playback Speed and Distractions on the Comprehension of Audio and Audio-Visual Materials. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09917-7

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Experiment 3 assigned 218 undergraduates to narration with textual or pictorial PowerPoint slides at 1x or 2x speed, followed by the 25-item Roman Empire comprehension test. The linear regression found "no significant effect of speed" on performance.

"There was no significant effect of speed, F(1, 202) = 0.18, p = 0.668, /u1D6FD = -0.07, revealing comparable performance between the 1x speed (M = 0.57, SD = 0.20) and 2x speed group (M = 0.51, SD = 0.18)"

Ashley Chen 2024 (2)¶

Ashley Chen, Suchita E. Kumar, Rhea Varkhedi, Dillon H. Murphy. (2024). The Effect of Playback Speed and Distractions on the Comprehension of Audio and Audio-Visual Materials. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09917-7

q2 · i? · causal · r2

The same Experiment 3 regression tested the video type × interest interaction on test performance; the article reports that when interest was high, textual-slide participants scored better, F(1, 202) = 5.61, p = 0.019.

"there was an interaction between video type and interest such that when interest was high, participants who received textual rather than pictorial slides scored better on the test, F(1, 202) = 5.61, p = 0.019, /u1D6FD = 0.12."
