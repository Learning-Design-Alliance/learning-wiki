## Evidence

### Edward Price and Noah Finkelstein 2006

Edward Price and Noah Finkelstein. (2006). Preparing graduate students to be educators. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0609003](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0609003)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre/post conceptual electricity and magnetism assessment (BEMA, augmented version, matched students only) of CU Teaching and Learning Physics students, shown in Figure 2. The course normalized gain was "38%" with a two-tailed paired t-test indicating "statistically significant differences of p<0.02"; all students had taken one to three E/M courses previously.

> "The course normalized gain is 38%,35 and a two-tailed, paired t-test on the pre- and post-test scores indicates statistically significant differences of p<0.02."

### Edward Price and Noah Finkelstein 2006 (2)

Edward Price and Noah Finkelstein. (2006). Preparing graduate students to be educators. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0609003](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0609003)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparison within the same CU course cohort between students serving as Learning Assistants in Tutorials in Introductory Physics and classmates in other teaching or research environments. Learning Assistants posted normalized gains of "55%" versus "27%", with "p<0.1" on a two-tailed t-test.

> "Those students who participated in more formalized teaching roles, as Learning Assistants36 in the Colorado implementation of Tutorials in Introductory Physics,30 posted normalized learning gains of 55%, while their classmates who taught or conducted research in other environments posted normalized learning gains of 27%, with p<0.1 for a two-tailed t-test comparing the two groups."
