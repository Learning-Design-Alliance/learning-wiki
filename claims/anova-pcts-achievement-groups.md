## Evidence

### Wan Chong Choi & Iek Chong Choi 2022

Wan Chong Choi & Iek Chong Choi. (2022). The Influence and Relationship between Computational Thinking, Learning Motivation, Attitude, and Achievement of Code.org in K-12 Programming Education. [doi:10.1145/3578837.3578871](https://doi.org/10.1145/3578837.3578871)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

One-way ANOVA comparing PCTS scores across Low, Medium, and High PAT achievement groups (10, 20, and 10 students as grouped by quartiles). Computational Perspectives was also significant (F = 6.063, p = 0.005); Bonferroni pairwise comparisons showed the High group's mean of 4.24 exceeded Low (3.36) and Medium (3.47).

> "Computational Concepts: The ANOVA results indicated a statistically significant difference between groups in the domain of Computational Concepts, with an F -ratio of 7.064 and a p-value of 0.003."

### Wan Chong Choi & Iek Chong Choi 2022 (2)

Wan Chong Choi & Iek Chong Choi. (2022). The Influence and Relationship between Computational Thinking, Learning Motivation, Attitude, and Achievement of Code.org in K-12 Programming Education. [doi:10.1145/3578837.3578871](https://doi.org/10.1145/3578837.3578871)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same ANOVA analysis for the Computational Practices dimension. The article reports no statistically significant difference between achievement groups at the 0.05 level; no effect size is printed.

> "The results showed an F -ratio of 3.056 with a p -value of 0.059. This indicated that, although there was some variance in computational practices among the groups, the differences were not statistically significant at the 0.05 level"
