## Evidence

### Mavragani Amaryllis 2025

Mavragani Amaryllis, Coco Laura, Mungoli Neelesh, Gerdes John, Schooley Benjamin, Sharp Dakota, Miller Juliana. (2025). The Design and Evaluation of a Simulation Tool for Audiology Screening Education: Design Science Approach. JMIR Formative Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/47150

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Mann-Whitney U analysis of the 25-item pretest/posttest survey in the randomized pilot (16 surveys per group). Two items reached the P<.10 level the authors use for technology pilot studies: landmark location "P=.08" and dB level "P=.07".

> ""I am confident in my ability to locate landmarks while completing otoscopy" (Mann-Whitney U=86.5, n1=n2=16; P=.08, 2-tailed t test) and "I am confident which dB level should be tested during a hearing screening" (Mann-Whitney U=84.0, n1=n2=16; P=.07)"

### Mavragani Amaryllis 2025 (2)

Mavragani Amaryllis, Coco Laura, Mungoli Neelesh, Gerdes John, Schooley Benjamin, Sharp Dakota, Miller Juliana. (2025). The Design and Evaluation of a Simulation Tool for Audiology Screening Education: Design Science Approach. JMIR Formative Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/47150

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same survey analysis: 19 of the 25 items showed no significant group differences even at the lenient P<.10 threshold. No effect sizes were printed, so the null findings are reported as non-detection, not equivalence.

> "The results were not significant across 19 survey questions at the P<.10 significance level, underscoring the specific impact of the simulator on the aforementioned confidence measures."
