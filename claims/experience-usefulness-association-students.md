# Among students, longer AI-use experience is monotonically associated with higher perceived usefulness (Spearman's r = 0.327), though self-selection may explain part of the pattern

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Among students, mean perceived usefulness rose monotonically from 2.78 among non-users to 4.06 among those with more than two years of experience; Spearman's rank correlation was r = 0.327. [→ Braun 2026](#braun-2026)

## Evidence

### Braun 2026

Braun, Y. S., & Khafizov, S. M. (2026). The AI Adaptation Gap in Higher Education: Students, Faculty, and Administrative Staff: AI Use, Trust, and Institutional Policy at a University Specializing in Teacher Education. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25063

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive monotonic trend analysis across student experience categories (n = 1454) found "Spearman's rank correlation was r = 0.327" with perceived usefulness rising from 2.78 among non-users to 4.06 among users with more than two years of experience.

> "The mean increased from 2.78 among non-users (n = 214) to 3.51 among those with less than six months of experience ( n = 375), 3.81 among those with six to twelve months ( n = 328), 3.83 among those with one to two years ( n = 367), and 4.06 among those with more than two years ( n = 170). Spearman's rank correlation was r = 0.327, p < 0.001, n = 1454."
