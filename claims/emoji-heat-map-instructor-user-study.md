## Evidence

### Blobstein 2025

Blobstein, A., Facciotti, M. T., Igo, M., Karger, D., Ravi, P., Sripathi, K., & Gal, K. (2025). #let's-discuss: Analyzing Students' Use of Emoji when Interacting with Course Readings. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-024-00401-5

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Two-part user study with five members of the FYBIO teaching staff (two instructors, two TAs, one class assistant) comparing tasks with and without the emoji heat map on lecture 5 reading material. Participants stated the tool made identifying high-engagement sections less time-consuming. No statistical analysis was performed.

> "They stated that without the heat map, it would have been more time-consuming and diﬃcult to identify sections of the reading material that exhibit high-engagement student reactions without the help of the tool."

### Blobstein 2025 (2)

Blobstein, A., Facciotti, M. T., Igo, M., Karger, D., Ravi, P., Sripathi, K., & Gal, K. (2025). #let's-discuss: Analyzing Students' Use of Emoji when Interacting with Course Readings. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-024-00401-5

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Satisfaction and ease-of-use questionnaire (USE-based, five-point Likert) administered to the five study participants, summarized in Table 3. The article reports "a unanimous answer to the question of whether the emoji heat map will save me time" with score 5. These are descriptive ratings, not effect sizes.

> "A notable point is a unanimous answer to the question of whether the emoji heat map will save me time, where all the participants answer it will (5)."
