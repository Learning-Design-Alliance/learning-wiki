Yanick Zengaffinen 2026¶

Yanick Zengaffinen, Andreas Opedal, Donya Rooein, Kv Aditya Srivatsa, Shashank Sonkar, Mrinmaya Sachan. (2026). Can LLMs Model Incorrect Student Reasoning? A Case Study on Distractor Generation for Multiple-Choice Questions. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15547 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2603.15547

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Targeted error-simulation test over Eedi's 429 misconception-annotated problems, manually verified; the article notes this does not guarantee the most plausible interpretation is selected, as only 62% of faithful distractors appear in the human-authored set.

"When prompted to simulate a problem-specific student error (sampled from Eedi’s misconception annotations for each of the 429 problems), DEEPSEEK-V3.2 achieves a mean accuracy of 0.92±0.02 , manually verified against any faithful interpretation of the error"

Secondary comparison of solution step counts for problems where the targeted distractor was versus was not produced; only a p-value is printed, so no effect size is reported.

"the underly- ing problems tend to have longer correct solutions (p <0.05, Mann-Whitney U test, comparing so- lution step counts), suggesting the model is less likely to reproduce the targeted distractor on prob- lems with longer solutions."

Related Claims¶

  • LLM distractor-generation strategies differ by domain: error description and simulation dominate on math while conceptual linking and discriminability checks dominate on science — related
  • On the misconception-annotated math dataset, LLMs predominantly follow a solve-first, error-injection process aligned with misconception-based distractor design — related
  • Instruction-tuning a student model on a single misconception overgeneralizes the error across problems and degrades correct-solving accuracy — related
  • Cognitive modeling of exact student responses is hardest for multi-select problems and rare-error answers, with select-all accuracy of only 12-15% and cognitive accuracy dropping to as low as 3.7% when answers are incorrect — related
  • Higher-struggling-level problems elicit more diverse but less student-like LLM-generated errors — related
  • LLMs predict common distractors above chance but fall 15 percentage points below chance at predicting which errors students rarely select — related
  • Simulated student reasoning shifted teachers from task-solvers to teacher-reasoners who interpreted misconceptions as student logic — related
  • Behavioral similarity between LLMs and learners does not guarantee mechanistic similarity — related
