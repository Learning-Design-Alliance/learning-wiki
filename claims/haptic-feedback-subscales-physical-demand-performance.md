# Haptic-feedback simulation training reduces physical demand and improves perceived performance in both simulation and live OR settings, with setting-specific effects on effort and mental demand

## Evidence

### Gomez ED 2024

Gomez ED, Husin HM, Dumon KR, Williams NN, Kuchenbecker KJ. (2024). Simulation training with haptic feedback of instrument vibrations reduces resident workload during live robot-assisted sleeve gastrectomy. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-024-11459-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

ANCOVA on individual NASA-TLX subscales from 60 simulated cases (Haptic: n = 30, Control: n = 30), adjusted for PGY level and sleep, found significantly lower physical demand, better perceived performance, and lower effort in the haptic group; mental demand, temporal demand, and frustration were not significant (all p > 0.19).

> "the haptic group had a significantly lower scores of physical demand (2.77 ± 0.204 vs. 3.99 ± 0.20, F(1) = 14.436, p < 0.001), higher levels of satisfaction regarding their performance (2.46 ± 0.27 vs. 3.41 ± 0.27, F(1) = 4.896, p = 0.031, and lower effort required (3.18 ± 0.17 vs. 4.29 ± 0.17, F(1) = 17.012, p < 0.001)"

### Gomez ED 2024 (2)

Gomez ED, Husin HM, Dumon KR, Williams NN, Kuchenbecker KJ. (2024). Simulation training with haptic feedback of instrument vibrations reduces resident workload during live robot-assisted sleeve gastrectomy. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-024-11459-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

ANCOVA of NASA-TLX subscales across all 76 live OR cases (Haptic: n = 45, Control: n = 31), adjusted for PGY level, showed significantly lower mental demand and physical demand in the haptic group, plus better self-rated performance (F(1) = 15.843, p < 0.001); frustration showed only a trend (p = 0.091).

> "the haptic group reported a significantly lower mental demand (3.49 ± 0.17 vs. 4.39 ± 0.21, F(1) = 10.804, p = 0.002), lower physical demand (3.18 ± 0.17 vs. 4.06 ± 0.20, F(1) = 11.201, p = 0.001)"
