## Evidence

### Kenneth Holstein and Shayan Doroudi 2021

Kenneth Holstein and Shayan Doroudi. (2021). Equity and Artificial Intelligence in Education: Will "AIEd" Amplify or Alleviate Inequities in Education?. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2104.12920](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2104.12920)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The chapter reports, citing Reich and Ito (2017), that open access to educational resources does not mean everyone benefits equally: MOOCs are disproportionately used by high socio-economic status students, attributed to socio-cultural barriers even when technologies are free.

> "MOOCs are disproportionately used by students with high socio-economic status, many of whom already have advanced degrees."

### Kenneth Holstein and Shayan Doroudi 2021 (2)

Kenneth Holstein and Shayan Doroudi. (2021). Equity and Artificial Intelligence in Education: Will "AIEd" Amplify or Alleviate Inequities in Education?. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2104.12920](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2104.12920)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The chapter reports, citing Kizilcec et al. (2017), an intervention study in which social belonging and value-affirmation interventions increased MOOC persistence and completion for students from less developed countries. The authors conclude designers should reduce gaps in who chooses to use their technology, since simply improving access is not enough.

> "Kizilcec et al. (2017) showed that simple social belonging and value-affirmation interventions could increase the persistence and completion rate of MOOCs for students from less developed countries, potentially eliminating the gap in MOOC completion between students in less versus more developed countries."
