# Total exposure time per session was significantly lower in the relearn condition than in the restudy condition, ruling out an exposure confound

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Mean total exposure time per session was significantly less in the relearn condition (M = 29.50) than in the restudy condition, d = -1.335. [→ Philip A. Higham 2020](#philip-a-higham-2020)

## Evidence

### Philip A. Higham 2020

Philip A. Higham, Bettina Zengel, Laura Bartlett, and Julie A. Hadwin (2020). The Benefits of Successive Relearning on Multiple Learning Outcomes. https://osf.io/nx4va/?view_only=049ee04aab4f420e9fa0091b3b69b9d0

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Relearn exposure durations were summed across passes within session and collapsed over sessions and weeks, then compared with the fixed restudy duration via t-test. Relearn exposure "was significantly less (M = 29.50; SD = 4.12) than the duration in the restudy condition".

> "This value represented the mean total exposure time per session, which was significantly less (M = 29.50; SD = 4.12) than the duration in the restudy condition, t(211) = -19.44, p < .001, d = -1.335."
