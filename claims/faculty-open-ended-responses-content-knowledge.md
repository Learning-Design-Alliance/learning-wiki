# In open-ended faculty responses, specific content knowledge was the most common answer (21.1%), exceeding any single scientific literacy skill, while quantitative and graphing skills drew only 12.87%

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When asked to list the three most important skills for scientific literacy, faculty responses categorized as specific content knowledge (21.1%) outnumbered any single TOSLS skill category. [→ Gormally C. 2012](#gormally-c-2012)
`q2 i?` The most frequent skill-aligned responses concerned understanding the nature of science (15.44%), followed by research design (15.09%), evaluating source validity (13.21%), and use/misuse of scientific information (8.58%); only 12.87% of responses aligned with quantitative and graphing skills. [→ Gormally C. 2012](#gormally-c-2012)

## Evidence

### Gormally C. 2012

Gormally C., Brickman P., Lutz M. (2012). Developing a Test of Scientific Literacy Skills (TOSLS): Measuring Undergraduates' Evaluation of Scientific Information and Arguments. CBE—Life Sciences Education 11(4):364–377. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.12-03-0026

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Classification of open-ended survey responses (n = 188 faculty) into categories by the coauthors. The article reports "the most frequent responses were related to understanding the nature of science (NOS; 15.44%)" and that "only 12.87% of all responses aligned with quantitative and graphing skills."

> "Responses categorized as specific content knowledge accounted for more responses than any one other skill described (21.1%)."
