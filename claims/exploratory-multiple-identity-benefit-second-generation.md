# Exploratory analyses suggest second-generation students performed better under the multiple identity condition regardless of stereotype activation; first-generation students did not

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A post-hoc moderation analysis (PROCESS Model 2) revealed an interaction between second-generation status and identity intervention, b = 1.96, p = 0.097, 90% CI [0.02, 3.91], with second-generation students scoring higher in the multiple identity condition under both stereotype threat (22.64 vs. 20.12) and control (23.33 vs. 22.39); the corresponding interaction for first-generation students was b = –2.56, 90% CI [–5.71, 0.58]. ## Evidence

### Mocevic 2025

Mocevic, H., and B. Gniewosz. 2025. "Stereotype Threat and a Multiple Identity Intervention Among First‐ and Second‐Generation Students of Migrant Descent." Journal of Community Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1002/jcop.70065

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory post-hoc analysis (PROCESS Model 2), not modeling the stereotype activation × identity interaction, with the same covariates. The printed b coefficients are unstandardized, so no effect size; the p-value sits at the 90% CI boundary and the finding is exploratory, hence weak strength.

> "This post‐hoc analysis revealed a significant interaction between second‐generation status and the identity intervention, b = 1.96, SE = 1.18, p = 0.097, 90% CI [0.02, 3.91], indicating that second‐generation students benefited from the multiple identity condition, regardless of stereotype activation (see Table 6), while first‐generation students did not, b = –2.56, SE = 1.91, p = 0.097, 90% CI [−5.71, 0.58]."
