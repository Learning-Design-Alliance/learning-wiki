## Evidence

### Benjamin M. Zwickl 2014

Benjamin M. Zwickl, Dehui Hu, Noah Finkelstein, and H. J. Lewandowski. (2014). Model-Based Reasoning in the Upper-Division Physics Laboratory: Framework and Initial Results. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1410.0881](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1410.0881)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Think-aloud laboratory interviews with eight students from an upper-division optics lab course, with three analyzed in detail and coded with the modeling framework. The article reports the making measurements code occupied only about 4 minutes while other modeling activities occurred for comparable lengths of time.

> "As Fig. 5 shows, each of the students spent a significant fraction of their time using and modeling the measurement tools in this particular upper-division lab activity."

### Benjamin M. Zwickl 2014 (2)

Benjamin M. Zwickl, Dehui Hu, Noah Finkelstein, and H. J. Lewandowski. (2014). Model-Based Reasoning in the Upper-Division Physics Laboratory: Framework and Initial Results. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1410.0881](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1410.0881)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Qualitative transcript excerpts from the think-aloud activity "span four diﬀerent aspects of model- ing in relation to the measurement tools", e.g., Student B constructing a quantitative measurement model converting photodetector voltage to optical power using responsivity and transimpedance gain, and revising both apparatus and model to handle an offset voltage.

> "Examples are provided that span four diﬀerent aspects of model- ing in relation to the measurement tools: construction, identiﬁcation of assumptions and limitations, interpretation of data, and revision."
