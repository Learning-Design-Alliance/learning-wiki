# Early expressive vocabulary predicts emergent literacy outcomes only until inhibitory control measures are added, after which it is no longer significant

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Time 1 expressive vocabulary was a significant predictor of both phonological awareness and early orthographic knowledge in block 3, but lost significance when the two inhibitory control measures entered in block 4. [→ Gandolfi 2021](#gandolfi-2021)

## Evidence

### Gandolfi 2021

Gandolfi, E., Traverso, L., Zanobini, M., Usai, M. C., & Viterbori, P. (2021). The longitudinal relationship between early inhibitory control skills and emergent literacy in preschool children. Reading and Writing. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11145-021-10131-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the same hierarchical regressions on the whole sample, vocabulary entered in block 3 was significant for both outcomes (phonological awareness Beta = .392, p = .005; orthographic knowledge Beta = .404, p = .008), but in the full block 1–2–3–4 model its coefficients dropped to non-significance (Beta = .256, p = .092; Beta = .205, p = .208).

> "Although early expressive vocabulary was also a significant predictor for both outcomes, when inhibitory control measures were added to the model, it was no longer significant."
