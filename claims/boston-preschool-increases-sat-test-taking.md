### Gray-Lobe 2021

Gray-Lobe, G., Pathak, P. A., & Walters, C. R. (2021). The Long-Term Effects of Universal Preschool in Boston. NBER Working Paper No. 28756. http://www.nber.org/papers/w28756

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

2SLS estimates from Table 5, column (1), using the randomized sample, showing a "statistically significant 8.5 percentage point increase in the rate of SAT test-taking". The size of this impact is similar to the effect on on-time college enrollment, suggesting SAT-taking may help account for the college attendance results.

> "Column (1) of Table 5 shows that among non-offered BPS preschool applicants who attend a Massachusetts public high school, roughly two-thirds take the SAT. Preschool attendance causes a statistically significant 8.5 percentage point increase in the rate of SAT test-taking"

### Gray-Lobe 2021 (2)

Gray-Lobe, G., Pathak, P. A., & Walters, C. R. (2021). The Long-Term Effects of Universal Preschool in Boston. NBER Working Paper No. 28756. http://www.nber.org/papers/w28756

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

2SLS estimates from Table 5 on unconditional quartile thresholds, where non-takers are coded as zeros so effects combine extensive- and intensive-margin impacts. Preschool "boosts the likelihood of an SAT Reasoning score in the top quartile by 3.6 percentage points", with a 5.7 percentage point gain in top-quartile SAT Math (p < 0.01).

> "Specifically, preschool attendance boosts the likelihood of an SAT Reasoning score in the top quartile by 3.6 percentage points. This effect is driven by a substantial increase in top-quartile Math scores."
