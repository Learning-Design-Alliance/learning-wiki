# Strongly organized ECE programs create contexts more supportive of teaching, learning, and family engagement than weakly organized programs

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Qualitative observation, interviews, and photo documentation across four Chicago ECE programs showed that programs with strong essential supports created far more supportive contexts for teaching, learning, and family engagement than weakly organized programs. [→ Pacchiano 2018](#pacchiano-2018)

## Evidence

### Pacchiano 2018

Pacchiano, D.M., Wagner, M.R., & Lewandowski, H., with Ehrlich, S.B. & Stein, A.G. (2018). Early Education Essentials: Illustrations of Strong Organizational Practices in Programs Poised for Improvement. Chicago, IL: The Ounce of Prevention Fund and the University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/early-education-essentials-illustrations-strong-organizational-practices-programs

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative component of the 2017 validation study: researchers blind to survey status spent three consecutive days observing, interviewing leaders, teachers, staff, and parent groups, and photographing common areas in two strong and two weak programs. The report concludes strongly organized programs "created contexts far more supportive of teaching, learning and family engagement."

> "Simply put, strongly organized programs created contexts far more supportive of teaching, learning and family engagement than the contexts created by weakly organized programs."
