# Native signers (children of at least one Deaf parent) outperform non-native signers on ASL syntactic judgment and analogical reasoning tasks

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Native vs. non-native signing status was a significant predictor of both syntactic judgment and analogical reasoning scores. [→ Henner J 2016](#henner-j-2016)
`q3 i?` Non-native signers scored on average 12–13 points lower than native signers across the two tasks. [→ Henner J 2016 (2)](#henner-j-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Henner J 2016

Henner J, Caldwell-Harris CL, Novogrodsky R and Hoffmeister R (2016) American Sign Language Syntax and Analogical Reasoning Skills Are Influenced by Early Acquisition and Age of Entry to Signing Schools for the Deaf. Front. Psychol. 7:1982. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01982

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the mixed effects regression with age-of-entry groups and signing status, native vs. non-native status was significant for syntactic judgment [χ2(7)= 45.18, p < 0.001] and analogical reasoning [χ2(7)= 56.45, p < 0.001].

> "Native vs. non-native status was also signiﬁcant for syntactic judgment abilities [ χ2(7)= 45.18, p < 0.001] and analogical reasoning skills [ χ2(7)= 56.45, p < 0.001]."

### Henner J 2016 (2)

Henner J, Caldwell-Harris CL, Novogrodsky R and Hoffmeister R (2016) American Sign Language Syntax and Analogical Reasoning Skills Are Influenced by Early Acquisition and Age of Entry to Signing Schools for the Deaf. Front. Psychol. 7:1982. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01982

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The regression results (Table 3) showed non-native signers scored 12 and 13 points lower on the syntactic judgment and analogical reasoning tasks respectively compared to native signers.

> "Non-native signers on average scored 12 and 13 points lower on the syntactic judgment task and analogical reasoning tasks compared to native signers."
