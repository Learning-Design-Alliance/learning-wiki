# All three revision groups improved OSCE scores between baseline and post-revision catheterisation attempts, with mean gains of 8.7% (no resources), 19.8% (traditional resources) and 15.9% (mobile application simulator)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Within-group performance in male urinary catheterisation improved significantly after a one-hour revision period in all three randomised groups: 8.7% (p = 0.003), 19.8% (p = 0.0001) and 15.9% (p = 0.001). [→ Bartlett Richard D. 2017](#bartlett-richard-d-2017)

## Evidence

### Bartlett Richard D. 2017

Bartlett Richard D., Radenkovic Dina, Mitrasinovic Stefan, Cole Andrew, Pavkovic Iva, Denn Peyton Cheong Phey, Hussain Mahrukh, Kogler Magdalena, Koutsopodioti Natalia, Uddin Wasima, Beckley Ivan, Abubakar Hana, Gill Deborah, Smith Daron. (2017). A pilot study to assess the utility of a freely downloadable mobile application simulator for undergraduate clinical skills training: a single-blinded, randomised controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1085-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Single-blinded randomised controlled pilot trial at a single London medical school; 27 final-3-year students (n = 9 per group) scored by allocation-blinded examiners against a 46-point OSCE mark scheme. Paired t-tests showed all groups improved, with the "traditional resources" group gaining most (9.1 raw marks, 19.8%).

> "Mean scores improved between baseline and post-revision attempts by 8.7% (p = 0.003), 19.8% (p = 0.0001), and 15.9% (p = 0.001) for no resources, traditional resources, and MAS, respectively."
