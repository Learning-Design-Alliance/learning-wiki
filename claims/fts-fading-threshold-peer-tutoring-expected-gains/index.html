Under the proposed FTS, once fading causes some students' game progress to fall below a set threshold they are regarded as non-expert peers, and peer-tutoring collaboration with expert peers is expected to considerably improve their game progress and significantly increase knowledge gained. → Obikwelu 2013

Obikwelu 2013¶

Obikwelu, C, Read, J, and Sim, G. (2013). Children’s Problem-Solving in Serious Games: The “Fine-Tuning System (FTS)” Elaborated. The Electronic Journal of e-Learning Volume 11 Issue 1. https://www.ejel.org

q1 · i? · the article prints no effect size for this finding · design · r1

This is a design proposal (§10, illustrated in Figure 2), not a tested result: the paper describes the expected relationship between fading, game progress and knowledge gained, stating the collaboration "would considerably improve the game -progress" of the non-expert peer. No empirical data support this expectation yet.

"This collaboration would considerably improve the game -progress made by the non -expert peer and thus significantly increase the knowledge gained in the field through game -play."
