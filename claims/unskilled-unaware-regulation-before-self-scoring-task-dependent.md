# Before self-scoring, the unskilled-and-unaware effect in regulation accuracy appears on multiplication but not on division tasks

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Skill group significantly predicted absolute regulation accuracy before self-scoring on the multiplication task, but low- and high-performing students did not significantly differ on the division task. [→ Sophie Oudman 2021](#sophie-oudman-2021)

## Evidence

### Sophie Oudman 2021

Sophie Oudman, Janneke van de Pol, Tamara van Gog. (2021). Effects of self-scoring their math problem solutions on primary school students' monitoring and regulation. Metacognition and Learning (2022) 17:213–239. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-021-09281-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Multilevel regression on regulation judgments (need for additional instruction, practice, both, or nothing) coded against researcher-determined actual need. The effect held on multiplication only; on division "low- and high-performing students did not significantly differ".

> "Skill group was a statistically significant predictor of absolute regulation accuracy on the multiplication task before self-scoring, with high-performing students making more accurate regulation judgments than low-performing students. On the division task, however, low- and high-performing students did not significantly differ in their absolute regulation accuracy (Table 2)."
