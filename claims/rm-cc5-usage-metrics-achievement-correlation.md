# RM-CC5 usage metrics correlated positively with achievement, and together accounted for an additional 7.3% of variance in Grade 5 scores beyond prior achievement, though causality and relative contributions could not be inferred

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Usage metrics added 7.3% of explained variance in Grade 5 WVGSA beyond the 66% accounted for by Grade 4 WVGSA (small positive linear relationship); the study was not designed to infer causal mechanisms. [→ Nicole Shechtman 2019](#nicole-shechtman-2019)

## Evidence

### Nicole Shechtman 2019

Nicole Shechtman, Jeremy Roschelle, Mingyu Feng, Corinne Singleton. (2019). An Efficacy Study of a Digital Core Curriculum for Grade 5 Mathematics. AERA Open. https://doi.org/10.1177/2332858419850482

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory two-level MLM extension with multiply imputed data, adding time logged in, objectives met, problems given, and problem accuracy to the model of Grade 5 WVGSA. Table 4 reports correlations of metrics with Grade 5 WVGSA (e.g., objectives met r = .548, accuracy r = .547, all p < .0001) and the Grade 4–Grade 5 correlation of .798. The article states "it was impossible to tease apart causality."

> "Together, these added variables accounted for an additional 7.3% of the variance. Thus, after controlling for prior knowledge, these RM-CC5 usage metrics had a small positive linear relationship with Grade 5 WVGSA."
