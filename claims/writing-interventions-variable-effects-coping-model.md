## Evidence

### Kim M. Mitchell 2023

Kim M. Mitchell, Sharon Zumbrunn, Danielle N. Berry, Lisa Demczuk. (2023). Writing Self-Efficacy in Postsecondary Students: a Scoping Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09798-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's synthesis of the nine interventional studies (18%), which tested sentence combining, mood primers, risk taking, modeling, faculty training, strategy instruction, and scaffolding, reports condition-dependent effects; many studies had small samples with under 20 participants per condition.

> "Findings across the studies showed that interventions had variable effects on both writing performance and writing self-efficacy, sometimes showing improvement and sometimes showing no change based on condition"

### Kim M. Mitchell 2023 (2)

Kim M. Mitchell, Sharon Zumbrunn, Danielle N. Berry, Lisa Demczuk. (2023). Writing Self-Efficacy in Postsecondary Students: a Scoping Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09798-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review's summary of Zimmerman and Kitsantas's six-group randomized intervention (n = 12 per group) reports significant main effects for modeling and social feedback on performance and self-efficacy with no interaction effects, and a decline in self-efficacy over time greatest in the no-model, no-feedback group.

> "Coping group had the highest performance assessments and self-efficacy levels, followed by mastery, followed by no model"
