## Evidence

### Reichert et al. 2015

Collin Reichert, Cinzia Cervato, Dale Niederhauser, and Michael D. Larsen. (2015). Understanding Atmospheric Carbon Budgets: Teaching Students Conservation of Mass. Journal of Geoscience Education 63, 222–232. https://doi.org/10.5408/14-055.1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Proportional log odds ratio models (Table IV) fit to per-question change scores for four budget questions; Question 4's coefficient was 1.13 (p = 0.007, adjusted p = 0.035) and Question 5 was not modeled due to few changes. Two alternative methods gave consistent conclusions.

> "Questions 1–3 have nonsigniﬁcant results. Question 4 has a signiﬁcant positive impact before and after adjustment for multiple testing."

### Reichert et al. 2015 (2)

Collin Reichert, Cinzia Cervato, Dale Niederhauser, and Michael D. Larsen. (2015). Understanding Atmospheric Carbon Budgets: Teaching Students Conservation of Mass. Journal of Geoscience Education 63, 222–232. https://doi.org/10.5408/14-055.1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

McNemar's test for correlated proportions on pretest-to-final percentages for all five questions; Questions 2 (p = 0.18), 3 (p = 0.58), and 5 (p = 0.68) did not show statistically significant increases.

> "Question 1 (pretest 48%, ﬁnal 86%, p < 0.001) and Question 4 (pretest 6%, ﬁnal 22%, p < 0.001) showed statistically signiﬁcant increases in the percentage of correct answers."
