Xia Zhichao 2018¶

Xia Zhichao, Zhang Linjun, Hoeft Fumiko, Gu Bin, Gong Gaolang, Shu Hua. (2018). Neural Correlates of Oral Word Reading, Silent Reading Comprehension, and Cognitive Subcomponents. International Journal of Behavioral Development. https://doi.org/10.1177/0165025417727872

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Pearson's correlations between behavioral measures (n = 21) controlling for age, gender and performance IQ, reported in the Results section. The correlations with morphological awareness and orthographical processing were not significant.

"Word reading efficiency was also significantly correlated with several subcomponents including phonological representation (r = 0.643, p = 0.004), phonological awareness (r = 0.516, p = 0.029), orthography-phonology mapping (r = 0.662, p = 0.003) and orthography-semantic mapping (r = 0.536, p = 0.022)."

Xia Zhichao 2018 (2)¶

Xia Zhichao, Zhang Linjun, Hoeft Fumiko, Gu Bin, Gong Gaolang, Shu Hua. (2018). Neural Correlates of Oral Word Reading, Silent Reading Comprehension, and Cognitive Subcomponents. International Journal of Behavioral Development. https://doi.org/10.1177/0165025417727872

q2 · i? · associational · r1

The same behavioral correlation analysis in the Results section shows sentence comprehension correlated with morphological awareness (r = 0.502, p = 0.034) and orthographic processing (r = 0.505, p = 0.032), and with two of three phonological subcomponents.

"Sentence reading comprehension was also significantly correlated with orthography-semantic mapping (r = 0.772, p < 0.001) and two out of the three phonological subcomponents (phonological representation: r = 0.508, p = 0.032; phonological awareness: r = 0.203, p = 0.418; orthography-phonology mapping: r = 0.621, p = 0.006)."

  • Reading-defined regions uniquely predict distinct cognitive subcomponents: LSTG for phonetic representation, LSMG for phonological awareness, and LITG for orthography-phonology mapping — related
  • For sentence comprehension regions, LPHP thickness is correlated with morphological awareness and RV1 thickness with orthography-semantic mapping — related
  • The word-reading and comprehension cortical regions are specific to their respective reading level: cross-level correlations were nonsignificant after FDR correction — related

  • Semantic ability correlates with phonological awareness, rapid naming, and both decoding measures in children with dyslexia — related
  • The oral–silent reading difference increases with reading experience, plausibly because a less extensive orthographic lexicon forces dyslexic readers to rely on phonological processing during silent reading — related
  • Efficiency in phonological and orthographic processing contributes significantly to individual differences in adult ESL reading measures — related
