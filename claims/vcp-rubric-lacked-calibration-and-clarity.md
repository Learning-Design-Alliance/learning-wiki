# The VCP rubric was difficult for teams to use because it lacked standardized calibration and its process and content categories were unclear

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Rubric scores were challenging to interpret over time or across districts because the VCP rubric lacked a standardized calibration and scoring system, and the distinction between process and content was unclear to many district leaders. [→ Perry 2019](#perry-2019)

## Evidence

### Perry 2019

Perry, R., Marple, S., & Reade, F. (2019). What accelerates a community of practice? Inflection points that changed the course of the Math in Common Initiative. San Francisco, CA: WestEd. https://www.wested.org/project/math-in-common-evaluation/

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Qualitative evaluation of the VCP rubric used for developmental purposes only from 2014-15 onward. An Ed Partners staff member reflected that VCP "worked for a while, but we weren't getting people focused on improvement."

> "One challenge was that the rubric lacked a standardized calibration and scoring system, making interpretation of scores over time, or across districts, difficult."
