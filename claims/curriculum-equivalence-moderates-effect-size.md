# Effect sizes were larger in studies where online and face-to-face conditions differed in curriculum materials and instructional approach (+0.42) than where curriculum and instruction were nearly identical (+0.20; Q = 5.40, p < .05).

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Of six methodological variables tested as moderators, only equivalence of curriculum and instruction was significant (Q = 5.40, p < .05): studies with identical or almost identical curriculum and instruction across conditions had smaller effects (+0.20) than studies where conditions varied in multiple instructional aspects (+0.42). ## Evidence

### Means 2009

Means, Toyama, Murphy, Bakia, & Jones. (2009). Evaluation of Evidence-Based Practices in Online Learning: A Meta-Analysis and Review of Online Learning Studies. U.S. Department of Education. https://www.ed.gov/about/offices/list/opepd/ppss/reports.html

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Methodological-moderator analysis of the 51 contrasts testing six variables: sample size, type of knowledge tested, study design strength, unit of assignment, instructor equivalence, and curriculum/instructional equivalence. Only the last was significant, with effect sizes of +0.20 versus +0.42; differences could include activity organization or inclusion of resources such as simulations or lectures in one condition only.

> "Only equivalence of curriculum and instruction emerged as a significant moderator variable (Q = 5.40, p < .05). Studies in which analysts judged the curriculum and instruction to be identical or almost identical in online and face-to-face conditions had smaller effects than those studies where the two conditions varied in terms of multiple aspects of instruction (+0.20 compared with +0.42, respectively)."
