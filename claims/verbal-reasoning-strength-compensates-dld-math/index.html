Evidence¶

Kleemans 2025¶

Kleemans, T., Vissers, C. T. W. M., & Segers, E. (2025). Linguistic precursors of sixth-grade geometric and fraction skills in children with and without Developmental Language Disorder. Learning and Instruction, 95, 102019. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.102019

q2 · i3 · associational · r2

Follow-up MANCOVA within the DLD group (strength vs. no strength in verbal reasoning, covariates: SES, age, sex). DLD children with the strength scored within the normal range on geometry, F(1,41) = 17.57, ηp2 = .30.

"children with DLD that have strength in verbal reasoning skills performed significantly better in geometric skills (F (1,41) = 17.57, p < .001, ηp2 = .30) and fraction skills (F(1,41) = 42.05, p < .001, ηp2 = .51) compared to DLD children without this strength."

Kleemans 2025 (2)¶

Kleemans, T., Vissers, C. T. W. M., & Segers, E. (2025). Linguistic precursors of sixth-grade geometric and fraction skills in children with and without Developmental Language Disorder. Learning and Instruction, 95, 102019. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.102019

q2 · i3 · associational · r2

Same MANCOVA: for fraction skills the strength effect was F(1,41) = 42.05, p < .001, ηp2 = .51. Verbal reasoning was the only predictor in which the proportion of DLD children with strengths did not differ from TD peers.

"children with DLD that have strength in verbal reasoning skills performed significantly better in geometric skills (F (1,41) = 17.57, p < .001, ηp2 = .30) and fraction skills (F(1,41) = 42.05, p < .001, ηp2 = .51) compared to DLD children without this strength."
