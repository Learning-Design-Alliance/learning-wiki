## Evidence

### Gartland Nicola 2022

Gartland Nicola, Aljofi Halah E., Dienes Kimberly, Munford Luke Aaron, Theakston Anna L., van Tongeren Martie. (2022). The Effects of Traffic Air Pollution in and around Schools on Executive Function and Academic Performance in Children: A Rapid Review. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph19020749

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The Sunyer et al. Barcelona cohort study (n = 2715, ages 7–10) directly measured indoor and outdoor EC, UFP, and NO2 at schools. The review reports high-pollution schools showed 7.4% versus 11.5% working memory growth over one year, p = 0.0024; only the p-value and percentages are printed.

> "children at high-pollution schools had a smaller growth in cognitive development than children from the paired low-pollution schools (7.4% (95% CI 5.6–8.8%) versus 11.5% (95% CI 8.9–12.5%) improvement in working memory over 1 year, p = 0.0024)."

### Gartland Nicola 2022 (2)

Gartland Nicola, Aljofi Halah E., Dienes Kimberly, Munford Luke Aaron, Theakston Anna L., van Tongeren Martie. (2022). The Effects of Traffic Air Pollution in and around Schools on Executive Function and Academic Performance in Children: A Rapid Review. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph19020749

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The same cohort study found no baseline executive function differences between high- and low-pollution schools, with significant differences emerging at one-year follow-up, supporting a developmental-trajectory interpretation rather than pre-existing group differences.

> "Importantly, differences in baseline measures of executive function were not observed, but there were significant differences at the 1-year follow-up."
