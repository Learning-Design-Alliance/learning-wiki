## Evidence

### Dawson-Tunik 2005

Dawson-Tunik, T. L., Commons, M., Wilson, M., & Fischer, K. W. (2005). The shape of development. European Journal of Developmental Psychology, 2(2), 163–195. https://doi.org/10.1080/17405620544000011

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear regression of LAS stage estimate on educational attainment across the entire 747-interview sample. "The linear equation is:" printed with F(1, 745) = 2894.06, R² = .80, p < .01.

> "The linear equation is: Stage estimate ¼ 10:34 þ 1:45ed Fð1; 745Þ ¼ 2894:06; R2 ¼ :80; p5:01"

### Dawson-Tunik 2005 (2)

Dawson-Tunik, T. L., Commons, M., Wilson, M., & Fischer, K. W. (2005). The shape of development. European Journal of Developmental Psychology, 2(2), 163–195. https://doi.org/10.1080/17405620544000011

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The article's own interpretation of the fitted linear equation. "every additional year of education results in a little more than one sixth of a complexity level of development"; the article also reports a quadratic residual term explaining an additional 2% of variance.

> "In terms of complexity levels, this means that, on average, every additional year of education results in a little more than one sixth of a complexity level of development."

### Dawson-Tunik 2005 (3)

Dawson-Tunik, T. L., Commons, M., Wilson, M., & Fischer, K. W. (2005). The shape of development. European Journal of Developmental Psychology, 2(2), 163–195. https://doi.org/10.1080/17405620544000011

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis contrasting predictors above age 18. "Above age 18, the correlation between educational attainment and complexity level is moderate, at .46", while the age correlation in adulthood was .16. No standardized effect size beyond this correlation is printed for the contrast, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).
