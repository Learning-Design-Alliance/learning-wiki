# Students perceive video-based feedback as more learner-centered and there is some evidence it better improves physical exam and communication skills

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among 140 students, supervisors more often asked about learning needs in the video format (4.48 vs. 3.62, p=0.001) and stimulated problem-solving participation more (4.56 vs. 4.00, p=0.003). [→ Junod Perron Noëlle 2016](#junod-perron-noelle-2016)
`q2 i?` Students reported marginally greater improvement in physical examination skills (4.51 vs. 4.20, p=0.063) and communication skills (4.49 vs. 4.19, p=0.060) with video-based feedback; neither reached significance. [→ Junod Perron Noëlle 2016 (2)](#junod-perron-noelle-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Junod Perron Noëlle 2016

Junod Perron Noëlle, Louis-Simonet Martine, Cerutti Bernard, Pfarrwaller Eva, Sommer Johanna, Nendaz Mathieu. (2016). Feedback in formative OSCEs: comparison between direct observation and video-based formats. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.3402/meo.v21.32160

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Student ratings collected immediately after feedback via a 14-item questionnaire (video-based n=109, direct observation n=42) analyzed with ANOVA. Table 3 shows learning-needs ratings "4.48 (0.96)	3.62 (1.55)" (p=0.001) and problem-solving "4.56 (0.64)	4.00 (1.09)" (p=0.003).

> "The 140 students who received feedback from supervisors involved in both formats perceived that in the video-based feedback, the supervisor made them more active by evaluating their learning needs, and by involving them more actively in the problem-solving."

### Junod Perron Noëlle 2016 (2)

Junod Perron Noëlle, Louis-Simonet Martine, Cerutti Bernard, Pfarrwaller Eva, Sommer Johanna, Nendaz Mathieu. (2016). Feedback in formative OSCEs: comparison between direct observation and video-based formats. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.3402/meo.v21.32160

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`
