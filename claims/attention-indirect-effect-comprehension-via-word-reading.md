# First-grade attention has no direct effect on third-grade reading comprehension but an indirect effect through word reading, not listening comprehension

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There was no significant direct effect of pre-treatment attention on grade 3 reading comprehension; the only significant indirect path ran through grade 1 sight word reading growth and grade 3 sight word reading. [→ Miller Amanda C. 2014](#miller-amanda-c-2014)

## Evidence

### Miller Amanda C. 2014

Miller Amanda C., Fuchs Douglas, Fuchs Lynn S., Compton Donald L., Kearns Devin, Zhang Wenjuan, Yen Loulee, Patton Samuel, Kirchner Danielle. (2014). Behavioral Attention: A Longitudinal Study of Whether and How It Influences the Development of Word Reading and Reading Comprehension among At-Risk Readers. Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness, 7(3), 232-249. https://doi.org/10.1080/19345747.2014.906691

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bias-corrected bootstrap tests (5,000 samples) of indirect effects from grade 1 attention to grade 3 reading comprehension in the path model of 110 at-risk readers. The specific indirect effect through SW growth and grade 3 SW was .06 (CI = .01 to .12); paths through listening comprehension were not significant. No standardized effect-size magnitude beyond the path coefficient was printed.

> "Only one indirect path was significant: Attention indirectly influenced grade 3 reading comprehension through its contribution to growth in sight word reading and grade 3 sight word reading."
