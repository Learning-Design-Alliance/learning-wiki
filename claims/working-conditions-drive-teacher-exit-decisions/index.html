Evidence¶

Podolsky 2016¶

Podolsky, A., Kini, T., Bishop, J., & Darling-Hammond, L. (2016). Solving the Teacher Shortage: How to Attract and Retain Excellent Educators. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/solving-teacher-shortage

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Analysis of the nationally representative Teacher Follow-up Survey reported in the report's working-conditions section. The report states "approximately 25%" of voluntary leavers rated assessment and accountability dissatisfaction as extremely or very important; no effect size is printed.

"Approximately 25% of public school teachers who left the profession in 2012 reported that dissatisfaction with the influence of school assessment and accountability measures on their teaching or curriculum was extremely or very important in their decision to leave."

Podolsky 2016 (2)¶

Podolsky, A., Kini, T., Bishop, J., & Darling-Hammond, L. (2016). Solving the Teacher Shortage: How to Attract and Retain Excellent Educators. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/solving-teacher-shortage

q2 · i? · review · r2

The report's school-leadership subsection synthesizes survey research on why teachers stay or leave, stating administrative support is "often the top reason teachers identify", and notes the report identifies four consistently cited working-condition factors.

"Administrative support is often the top reason teachers identify for leaving or staying in the profession, or in a given school, outweighing even salary considerations for some teachers."
