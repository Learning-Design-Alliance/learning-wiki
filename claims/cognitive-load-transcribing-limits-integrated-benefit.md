# Authors attribute the integrated component's limited efficacy to cognitive load from transcribing between notations for at-risk learners

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors propose that transcribing between fraction and decimal notations creates cognitive load interfering with decimal learning for at-risk fourth graders with little rational number experience. [→ Malone et al. 2019](#malone-et-al-2019)

## Evidence

### Malone et al. 2019

Malone, A. S., Fuchs, L. S., Sterba, S. K., Fuchs, D., & Foreman-Murray, L. (2019). Does an integrated focus on fractions and decimals improve at-risk students' rational number magnitude performance? *Contemporary Educational Psychology, 59*, 101782. [doi:10.1016/j.cedpsych.2019.101782](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cedpsych.2019.101782)

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' interpretation offered in the Discussion section, not a tested result. The article states this as a possible explanation hedged with "perhaps because," acknowledging that further investigation appears warranted given prior evidence that decimals may be more accessible than fractions.

> "Our findings do not support this possibility perhaps because transcribing between the two notations creates cognitive load that interferes with decimal learning for at-risk fourth graders, who have little prior experience with rational numbers along with a history of whole-number mathematics difficulty."
