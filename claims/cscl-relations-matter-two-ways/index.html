Evidence¶

Sanna Järvelä 2022¶

Sanna Järvelä, Carolyn P. Rosé. (2022). Relations Matter – CSCL Research Informing and Developing CL Competencies. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn (2022) 17:185–189. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-022-09373-x

q1 · i? · theoretical · r2

Theoretical argument of the special-issue editors framing the edition's four papers; the editorial states this thesis with the companion claim that "CSCL environments and the processes housed within them have the ability to foster key competencies among learners". No independent evidence is offered in the editorial itself.

"On the one hand, a substantial part of what is lacking in and for successful collaborative interactions can be supported through interventions designed in light of the knowledge we create from our empirical studies."

Related Claims¶

  • The editors report that knowledge brokering via collaborative entanglement is more successful than linear knowledge transfer for mobilising education research — related
  • Affordances of technology for collaborative learning are created through design to the extent that appropriate principles have been applied in an effective way — related
  • Bures, Abrami and Schmid's empirical analysis argues that multiple forms of scaffolding may interfere with each other and that labeling should be designed flexibly to match existing structure — related
  • Computer-supported collaboration can promote important cognitive results among kindergarten students when properly structured — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Diffusion-based centralities elucidate information exchange and uptake in large-scale collaboration, validated against achievement and qualitative analysis, according to the editors' account of Saqr and López-Pernas's study — related
  • Argumentation is one of the most-attended CSCL subareas, reviewed comprehensively by Scheuer, Loll, Pinkwart and McLaren in this issue — related
  • The editors identify sequentiality of CSCL discourse — how one action takes up a previous action in online interaction — as a central, under-theorized problem — related
  • Wise and Schwarz's eight provocations, per Rummel, emphasize important CSCL dimensions but do not develop a common position, prompting a proposed taxonomy for a shared field agenda. — related
  • Timing of support poses the assistance dilemma, and no empirically validated principles yet exist for when and how much support to give collaborating students — related
  • An empirical qualitative study of paired distance collaboration informed the design of LeCS — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Special-issue papers present theoretical frameworks for guiding online teaching practices, including framing of instruction, cognitive load in CSCL, and engagement — a broader claim this one bears on
  • CSCL is often based on learners' written argumentative discourse about problem perspectives with the goal of acquiring knowledge — related
