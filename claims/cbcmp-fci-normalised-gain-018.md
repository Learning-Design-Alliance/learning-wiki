# An interactive-engagement mechanics course using MasteringPhysics yields a normalised FCI gain of about 0.18 with a statistically significant pre-to-post improvement

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` FCI post scores were significantly higher than pre scores (paired t-test p = 4.1×10^-5, 46 df, mean difference 8.6%), with an overall normalised gain G = 0.18. [→ Allan L. Alinea and Wade Naylor 2014](#allan-l-alinea-and-wade-naylor-2014)

## Evidence

### Allan L. Alinea and Wade Naylor 2014

Allan L. Alinea and Wade Naylor. (2014). Polarisation of physics on global courses. [doi:10.1088/0031-9120/50/2/210](https://doi.org/10.1088/0031-9120/50/2/210)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Pre/post FCI administration to 47 students over three cohorts (2011-2013) in the CBCMP course, analysed with a paired t-test in R. The article reports "a p-value of 4.1× 10−5" and a normalised gain of G = 0.18, which the authors note is only about the average of a traditional course.

> "At the 95% conﬁdence level we found a p-value of 4.1× 10−5 for 46 degrees of freedom with a mean diﬀerence of 8.6%. The null hypothesis was not satisﬁed and suggests the results do lead to a positive increase in the post scores."
