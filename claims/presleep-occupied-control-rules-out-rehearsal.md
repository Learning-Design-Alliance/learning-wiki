# Preventing presleep rehearsal with distractor tasks does not alter the sleep benefit, ruling out unintentional rehearsal as a confound

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Drosopoulos 2007

Drosopoulos, S., Schulze, C., Fischer, S., & Born, J. (2007). Sleep's Function in the Spontaneous Recovery and Consolidation of Memories. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General. https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.136.2.169

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Experiment 1 supplementary condition: participants performed distractor tasks (Snood, auditory monitoring) until sleep onset. The "presleep-occupied group, like the sleep group in the main experiment, showed an improved recall selectively for the first A-B list", η2 = .21, excluding rehearsal as a confound.

> "these analyses confirmed that the presleep-occupied group, like the sleep group in the main experiment, showed an improved recall selectively for the first A-B list, F(1, 18) ⫽ 4.88, p ⫽ .040, ␩2 ⫽ .21, for the Presleep-Occupied Versus Wake ⫻ List interaction"
