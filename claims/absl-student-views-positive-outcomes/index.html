Evidence¶

Akbaş 2019¶

Akbaş, Y.; Şahin, İ. F.; Meral, E. (2019). Implementing Argumentation-Based Science Learning Approach in Social Studies: Academic Achievement and Students' Views. Review of International Geographical Education Online (RIGEO), 9(1), 209-245. https://doi.org/10.33403/rigeo.529139

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Content analysis of semi-structured interviews with 10 experimental-group students selected by maximum variation purposive sampling. Categories included expressing ideas (f=4), understanding the lesson better (f=4), becoming more active (f=4), and learning with fun (f=3).

"As seen in Table 8, students expressed their opinions better, understood the lessons better, became more active in the class, learned subjects with fun, and learned the subjects with cause and effects relations through the ABSL approach."

Akbaş 2019 (2)¶

Akbaş, Y.; Şahin, İ. F.; Meral, E. (2019). Implementing Argumentation-Based Science Learning Approach in Social Studies: Academic Achievement and Students' Views. Review of International Geographical Education Online (RIGEO), 9(1), 209-245. https://doi.org/10.33403/rigeo.529139

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Interview question on interest and attitudes; content analysis found positive-change categories: more fun lesson (f=6), meaningful lesson (f=2), facilitating communication (f=2), ensuring critical thinking (f=1). "Students did not state any negative changes and no change ideas."

"Based on students’ responses to the question about the effects of the ABSL approach implemented course process on their interest and attitudes towards social studies, it is noteworthy that there were positive changes in students' interest and attitudes tow ards social studies after implementation."

Akbaş 2019 (3)¶

Akbaş, Y.; Şahin, İ. F.; Meral, E. (2019). Implementing Argumentation-Based Science Learning Approach in Social Studies: Academic Achievement and Students' Views. Review of International Geographical Education Online (RIGEO), 9(1), 209-245. https://doi.org/10.33403/rigeo.529139

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Interview question on effects on success in social studies; permanent learning was cited by 8 students and easier learning by 3 students in the content analysis of the 10 interviews.

"As specified in Table 13, the ABSL approach did positive impacts on students’ success in social studies course since the approach provided opportunities for permanent and easier learning."
