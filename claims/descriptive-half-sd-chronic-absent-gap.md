# Descriptively, prior-year chronically absent students achieved over half a standard deviation below their peers at the start of the following year

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Before any controls, mean starting achievement declines consistently by prior-year attendance category, with chronically absent students over half an SD below peers. [→ King 2020](#king-2020)

## Evidence

### King 2020

King, G., & Jensen, N. (2020). Looking Back: How Prior-Year Attendance Impacts Starting Achievement. (The Center for School and Student Progress at NWEA Working Paper). https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/looking-back-how-prior-year-attendance-impacts-starting-achievement/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Preliminary descriptive analysis using boxplots of standardized RIT scores by attendance bin (Figure 2), unadjusted for covariates. Chronically absent students were "over half a standard deviation below their peers"; Figure 1 further shows CA and HA students falling further behind each subsequent year.

> "The boxplots, standardized across grade, subject, and year demonstrate that students who were chronically absent achieved at over half a standard deviation below their peers (prior to controlling for any other variables or separating by subject)."
