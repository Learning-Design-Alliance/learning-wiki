# AI-related changes to consumer software threaten the anonymity of remotely collected research data

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The integration of AI features into operating systems, browsers, and security software can transfer participant data to clouds, potentially making remote studies non-anonymous. [→ Skulmowski 2024](#skulmowski-2024)

## Evidence

### Skulmowski 2024

Skulmowski, A. (2024). AI-Related Threats to Information Sovereignty and Challenges for Research Ethics. Educational Psychology Review, 36, 101. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09939-1

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument in this commentary: browser spell-checking, cloud-based malware scanners, and AI-powered PCs that capture screenshots are named as channels through which study data may leave participants' devices. The author offers no empirical measurement; the claim rests on documented product changes and cited re-identification literature.

> "With a stronger integration of AI features into operating systems, thereby allowing even more data to be gathered, it may eventually become impossible to use certain digital devices in a privacy-preserving manner."
