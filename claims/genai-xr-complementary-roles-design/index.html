Evidence¶

Xiao 2026¶

Xiao, Y., Chen, M., Li, Y., Powers, N., Wiesenfeld, M., Smith, G., Farzin, S., Liu, S. (2026). Generative AI and Extended Reality in Collaborative Architectural Design Education: An Exploratory Studio Study. arXiv preprint arXiv:2609.13494. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13494 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.13494

q1 · i? · causal · r1

Thematic analysis of semi-structured group interviews with the four GenARch teams (~35 minutes each), following Braun and Clarke's reflexive six-phase approach. Students reported that full-scale embodied viewing gave "a clearer sense of proportion and spatial presence compared with screen-based representations," while GenAI images externalized rough conceptual ideas.

"GenAI primarily supported ideation and visual reference generation, while XR enabled contextual and spatial understanding. In particular, XR helped students visualize the site landscape and perceive architectural elements at realistic scale."

Related Claims¶

  • Teachers' tool preferences are stage-dependent, with pre-gen valued for ideation, mid-gen for structuring, post-gen for finalization, motivating hybrid workflows — related
  • Students rated the flipped-learning component highest and the GenAI module lower, with GenAI rated stronger for ideation than for time saving or direct output use — related
  • Prompts referencing architectural styles or local architectures yielded less desired image outcomes than future-themed prompts, motivating selectable checkpoints in GAI-A — related
