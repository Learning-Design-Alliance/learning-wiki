Evidence¶

Dunning 2019¶

Dunning, D. L., Griffiths, K., Kuyken, W., Crane, C., Foulkes, L., Parker, J., & Dalgleish, T. (2019). Research Review: The effects of mindfulness-based interventions on cognition and mental health in children and adolescents – a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12980

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Begg's funnel plots and Egger's regressions conducted for each meta-analysis. The review reports "publication bias was evident for Negative Behaviour and Anxiety/Stress" and, in active-control analyses, "in the Mindfulness category only."

"For all 33 RCTs, Egger’s tests showed that publication bias was evident for Negative Behaviour and Anxiety/Stress (Table 1). For the 17 RCTs with active control groups there was evidence of publication bias in the Mindfulness category only (Table 2)."

Related Claims¶

  • When only RCTs with active control groups are analysed, MBI benefits are restricted to Mindfulness (d = .42), Depression (d = .47) and Anxiety/Stress (d = .18) — related
  • Across all RCTs, mindfulness-based interventions significantly improve mindfulness, executive functioning, attention, depression, anxiety/stress and negative behaviours in youth, with small effect sizes — related
  • Risk of bias moderates the Mindfulness effect in active-control RCTs, with higher-risk studies producing larger effect sizes — related
  • School-based mindfulness interventions produce small-to-moderate significant pre-post improvements in youth mental health and well-being compared to control groups — related
  • Funnel-plot asymmetry was significant in both meta-analyses, indicating possible publication or selection bias — related
  • The meta-analysis finds no major publication bias in the teacher personality–outcome associations — related
  • Funnel-plot asymmetry ran contrary to small-study effects because larger-sample studies reported larger effect sizes, likely reflecting longer training programs — related
  • Publication bias is present in the embodied vocabulary learning literature, suggesting pooled effects may overestimate the true benefit — related
  • Total training hours significantly moderate MBI effects on negative behaviour across all RCTs, with more hours associated with larger reductions — related
  • No publication bias is detected in the collected Covid-19 achievement estimates — related
  • Publication bias is indicated for the performance-approach and performance-avoidance anxiety effects but not for the depression effects — related
  • Funnel-plot asymmetry indicates small-study effects for some emotion–learning models — related
  • Publication bias was significant for the overall DT analysis but not for the fluency, flexibility, or originality indicator analyses — related
  • No evidence of publication bias was found in the achievement goal–dishonesty literature — related
  • Large-scale RCTs show school mindfulness training does not benefit overall well-being or social-emotional functioning and can harm those most at risk — related
  • Isolated school mindfulness programs show mixed results, with one multi-site trial showing no improvement and increased anxiety in some subgroups — related
