## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A pair of students could only articulate a prediction about the Stage 1 output after constructing a model of the circuit as comprising noninverting and inverting subsystems, and this prediction enabled their use of the Split-Half strategy. ## Evidence

### Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer 2015

Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer, Kevin L. Van De Bogart, MacKenzie R. Stetzer, and H. J. Lewandowski. (2015). The role of modeling in troubleshooting: an example from electronics. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.021](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.021)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative analysis of a 90-second transcript excerpt from one pair's interview. The students' preliminary model was sufficient to predict the whole-circuit output but not the midpoint output; the authors argue the lack of a sufficiently sophisticated model prevented S1 from articulating a prediction in Line 2. This connection is described as "consistent with the EMF."

> "Only after constructing a model of the circuit as being comprised of a noninverting subsystem (Lines 3–7) was S1 able to make a prediction about the expected output of Stage 1 (Line 8), thus facilitating the Split-Half strategy."
