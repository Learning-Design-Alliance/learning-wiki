Evidence¶

Ying Dong 2026¶

Ying Dong. (2026). Generative AI technologies and educational outcomes: a comprehensive meta-analysis comparing traditional and AI-driven approaches. Humanities and Social Sciences Communications. https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-026-06903-y

q3 · i3 · quant-synthesis · r2

Random-effects meta-analysis of learning-motivation comparisons among the included studies. The pooled estimate was "g = 0.81; 95%CI = 0.20 ~ 1.41, large effect", reported alongside the achievement, thinking, and writing outcomes in Fig. 3.

"learning motivation (g = 0.81; 95%CI = 0.20 ~ 1.41, large effect)"

Fang L 2026¶

Fang L, Li Z, Xiong B and Lu Z (2026). Effects of generative artificial intelligence on students' learning motivation: evidence from a meta-analysis. Front. Psychol. 17:1832451. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1832451

q4 · i2 · quant-synthesis · r3

Random-effects meta-analysis of 56 effect sizes from 42 controlled experimental and quasi-experimental studies of 6,059 participants (2023–2025). The pooled effect was "Hedge's g = 0.764" with a 95% CI of [0.566, 0.962]; heterogeneity was I2 = 93.7%, Tau2 = 0.515, Q[55] = 866.132, p < 0.001.

"As Hedge's g = 0.764, 95% CI =[0.566,0.962],95% PI=[−0.689,2.217]; z=7.571,p <0.001, studentsusingGenAIshowedmorelearningmotivationthanother students."
