## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For each measure, conceptual scoring increased the proportion of bilingual children scoring within or above the average range (standard score ≥ 85). [→ Gross Megan 2014](#gross-megan-2014)
`q3 i?` For simultaneous bilinguals, conceptual scoring achieved good classification (at least 90%) on all measures, and all sequential bilinguals scored within the average range on at least one receptive measure when both languages were used. [→ Gross Megan 2014 (2)](#gross-megan-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Gross Megan 2014

Gross Megan, Buac Milijana, Kaushanskaya Margarita. (2014). Conceptual scoring of receptive and expressive vocabulary measures in simultaneous and sequential bilingual children. American Journal of Speech-Language Pathology. https://doi.org/10.1044/2014_AJSLP-13-0026

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Classification analysis modeled on Bedore et al. (2005), computing proportions of the 39 simultaneous and 19 sequential bilinguals scoring at least 85 with and without conceptual scoring; proportions rose on every measure (e.g., Spanish expressive: 31% to 92% for simultaneous bilinguals). No effect size printed.

> "For each measure, conceptual scoring increased the proportion of typically-developing bilingual children scoring within or above the average range. For the simultaneous bilinguals, conceptual scoring resulted in “good” classification for all measures, with at least 90% of children scoring in the average range."

### Gross Megan 2014 (2)

Gross Megan, Buac Milijana, Kaushanskaya Margarita. (2014). Conceptual scoring of receptive and expressive vocabulary measures in simultaneous and sequential bilingual children. American Journal of Speech-Language Pathology. https://doi.org/10.1044/2014_AJSLP-13-0026

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Classification analysis of the sequential bilingual group using conceptual scores from both languages; all 19 scored within the average range on at least one receptive measure and 89% on at least one expressive measure, as quoted. No effect size printed.

> "However, when considering conceptual scores derived from both English and Spanish measures, all sequential bilinguals scored within the average range on at least one receptive measure and 89% scored within the average range on at least one expressive measure."
