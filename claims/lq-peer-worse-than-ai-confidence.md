# Low-quality peer interactions performed worse than AI on learning confidence but not significantly on interest in the material

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` LQ-PP participants reported lower learning confidence than AI participants (M=3.18 vs 4.30; d=-1.56, p=.002). [→ Morris 2026](#morris-2026)

## Evidence

### Morris 2026

Morris, C., & Maes, P. (2026). When Peers Outperform AI (and When They Don't): Interaction Quality Over Modality. [doi:10.22318/cscl2026.989526](https://doi.org/10.22318/cscl2026.989526)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Independent-samples t-test comparing low-quality peer dyad participants with AI-condition participants on post-session survey measures. Confidence showed "t(21)=-3.62, p=.002, d=-1.56" favoring AI.

> "This pattern held across learning confidence (LQ-PP: M=3.18, SD=0.75 vs. AI: M=4.30, SD=0.67; t(21)=-3.62, p=.002, d=-1.56) and interest in the material (LQ-PP: M=3.36, SD=0.92 vs. AI: M=3.75, SD=0.75; t(21)=-1.08, p=.29, d=-0.46)."

### Morris 2026 (2)

Morris, C., & Maes, P. (2026). When Peers Outperform AI (and When They Don't): Interaction Quality Over Modality. [doi:10.22318/cscl2026.989526](https://doi.org/10.22318/cscl2026.989526)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same between-group comparison, interest measure: the difference was not significant (p=.29); equivalence was not tested. Descriptive means favored AI slightly.

> "This pattern held across learning confidence (LQ-PP: M=3.18, SD=0.75 vs. AI: M=4.30, SD=0.67; t(21)=-3.62, p=.002, d=-1.56) and interest in the material (LQ-PP: M=3.36, SD=0.92 vs. AI: M=3.75, SD=0.75; t(21)=-1.08, p=.29, d=-0.46)."
