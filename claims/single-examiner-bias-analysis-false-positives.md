# Simulated single-examiner analyses of ethnic bias produce massive false positives because examiner marks are confounded with candidate performance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2013 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person · **overdue for a recheck since 2016**: tool and model accuracy changes quickly

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A simple comparison of a single examiner's marks to White versus non-White candidates apparently shows bias in a very large number of examiners, an interpretation the authors call wrong. [→ McManus I C 2013](#mcmanus-i-c-2013)
`q2 i?` A more sophisticated single-examiner method comparing marks with other examiners at other stations still shows a massive excess of apparent white ethnicity bias due to content specificity. [→ McManus I C 2013 (2)](#mcmanus-i-c-2013-2)

## Evidence

### McManus I C 2013

McManus I C, Elder Andrew T, Dacre Jane. (2013). Investigating possible ethnicity and sex bias in clinical examiners: an analysis of data from the MRCP(UK) PACES and nPACES examinations. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-103

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Simulation using PACES diets 1-26 data considering only one examiner's marks. Because non-White candidates on aggregate perform less well, many examiners falsely appear biased; the authors put "White ethnicity bias" in scare quotes.

> "Figure 3 (top) shows that with such a method a very large number of examiners apparently show bias towards white candidates."

### McManus I C 2013 (2)

McManus I C, Elder Andrew T, Dacre Jane. (2013). Investigating possible ethnicity and sex bias in clinical examiners: an analysis of data from the MRCP(UK) PACES and nPACES examinations. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-103

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Second simulation comparing a single examiner's marks with a basket of marks from other examiners at other stations. The article attributes the artefact to content specificity: marks on other stations poorly predict the station examined.

> "Figure 3 (bottom) shows that the method still has a massive excess of examiners apparently showing a white ethnicity bias."
