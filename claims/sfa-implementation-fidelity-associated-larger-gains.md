# Schools rated as implementing SfA with 'refined' fidelity achieved higher end-of-Year-1 literacy scores, especially on the phonics check (g=.41)

## Subclaims
`q2 i0` Schools rated 'refined' or 'refined/routine' for implementation fidelity scored higher on the Woodcock (g=.20, 95% CI [-.01, .40]) and particularly the phonics check (g=.41, 95% CI [.21, .61]) at the end of Year 1 than schools rated routine or mechanical; no fidelity effect was found on Woodcock at end of Reception (g=.11). ## Evidence

### Miller et al. 2017

Sarah Miller, Andy Biggart, Seaneen Sloan, Liam O'Hare. (2017). Success for All: Evaluation report and executive summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED581417

`q2 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory analysis of intervention schools only, using a dummy fidelity variable (refined or refined/routine coded 1, n=7; routine or mechanical coded 0, n=8) rated by SfA in 15 schools. No fidelity effect was found on Woodcock at end of Reception (g=.11, an effect below d=0.2).

> "there was some evidence that schools rated as ‘refined’ resulted in higher scores on both the Woodcock (Hedges’ g=.20, 95% CI [-.01. .40]) and particularly the phonics check at post -test (Hedges’ g=.41, 95% CI [.21, .61] ), i.e. the end of Year 1."
