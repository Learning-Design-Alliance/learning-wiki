Evidence¶

Kim Young-Suk Grace 2017¶

Kim Young-Suk Grace, Schatschneider Christopher. (2017). Expanding the developmental models of writing: A direct and indirect effects model of developmental writing (DIEW). Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000129

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Table 3 of the SEM study with 193 Grade 1 children displays direct, indirect, and total effects of component skills on writing based on the final complete mediation model. Discourse-level oral language, spelling, and handwriting had direct effects; the other skills' effects were indirect.

"The largest effects were found for discourse-level oral language (.46), working memory (.43), and spelling (.37), followed by vocabulary (.19), handwriting (.17), theory of mind (.12), inference (.10), and grammatical knowledge (.10)."

Related Claims¶

  • Discourse-level oral language, spelling, and handwriting fluency completely mediate the relations of language and cognitive skills to writing quality in Grade 1 — related
  • Discourse-level oral language is directly predicted by theory of mind, vocabulary, and working memory, but not inference or grammatical knowledge, in Grade 1 — related
  • Inference, but not theory of mind, is independently related to writing quality after accounting for transcription skills and working memory — related
  • Working memory is a foundational cognitive skill for language and transcription skills, but its relation to writing is primarily mediated by other component skills — related
  • Writing fluency completely mediates the relations of Grade 1 spelling and handwriting fluency to Grade 2 writing quality — related
  • Grade 1 vocabulary is directly related to Grade 2 writing quality over and above writing fluency — related
  • Kindergarten working memory is indirectly related to Grade 2 writing fluency and quality via Grade 1 spelling, while attentional control shows no direct or indirect relation — related
