# Tutors perceive Tutor CoPilot as helpful for on-the-spot explanations but flag suggestions that are not grade-level appropriate and 'too smart'

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 1 causal `r3`, 1 qualitative `r1` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Treatment tutors generally found Tutor CoPilot helpful, particularly for well-phrased explanations and breaking down complex concepts on the spot. [→ Rose E. Wang 2025](#rose-e-wang-2025)
`q1 i?` Tutors reported that suggestions occasionally lacked alignment with students' grade levels and were sometimes perceived as 'too smart,' requiring simplification and adaptation. [→ Rose E. Wang 2025 (2)](#rose-e-wang-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Rose E. Wang 2025

Rose E. Wang, Ana T. Ribeiro, Carly D. Robinson, Susanna Loeb, and Dora Demszky. (2025). Tutor CoPilot: A Human-AI Approach for Scaling Real-Time Expertise. arXiv:2410.03017v2. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2410.03017](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2410.03017)

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Qualitative interviews with approximately 20 treatment tutors conducted a week after the study concluded, exploring usage and how use evolved over time. The article reports themes, including usefulness for explaining difficult topics such as differentiating mean and median. No effect size applies.

> "Tutors generally found Tutor CoPilot helpful, particularly for its ability to provide well-phrased explanations and break down complex concepts on the spot."

### Rose E. Wang 2025 (2)

Rose E. Wang, Ana T. Ribeiro, Carly D. Robinson, Susanna Loeb, and Dora Demszky. (2025). Tutor CoPilot: A Human-AI Approach for Scaling Real-Time Expertise. arXiv:2410.03017v2. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Same interview study with treatment tutors. Tutors noted areas for improvement, mentioning that the tool's suggestions occasionally lacked alignment with students' grade levels. This is qualitative self-report; no effect size applies.

> "A common issue was that the responses were sometimes perceived as "too smart," requiring tutors to simplify and adapt them for clarity."
