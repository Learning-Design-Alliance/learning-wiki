Evidence¶

Gopnik 2012¶

Gopnik, A., & Wellman, H. M. (2012). Reconstructing constructivism: Causal models, Bayesian learning mechanisms and the theory theory. Psychological Bulletin, 138(6), 1085-1108. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028044

q3 · i? · review · r3

In Gopnik et al. (2004), puppets W and I covaried but interventions on each had no effect on the other, ruling out W→I and I→W. Children chose 'something else' as the cause, unlike in a control condition, and many searched behind the apparatus for the hidden cause.

"So 4-year-olds had concluded that some unobserved common cause, U, influenced W and I, and therefore W←U→I."

Gopnik 2012 (2)¶

Gopnik, A., & Wellman, H. M. (2012). Reconstructing constructivism: Causal models, Bayesian learning mechanisms and the theory theory. Psychological Bulletin, 138(6), 1085-1108. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028044

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review attributes to Schulz and Somerville (2006) the finding that children exposed to a machine activating only 2 of 6 times posited a hidden variable behind the inconsistency, extending unobserved-cause inference to probabilistic evidence.

"Similarly, Schulz and Somerville (2006) found that when children saw an indeterministic machine – that is a machine that went off only 2 of 6 times--they inferred that some hidden variable was responsible for the failures."
