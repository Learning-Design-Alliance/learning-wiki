# Probability of failing is higher under traditional methodology

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` The probability of failing the subject is 1.538 times greater when students are taught using the traditional method (Fisher exact test, p = .002). [→ Torres-Martín 2022](#torres-martin-2022)

`q3 i?` The overall mean effect size for pass rates is an odds ratio of 1.55, corresponding to a risk ratio of 1.08, in favor of the flipped classroom. [→ Torstein Låg 2019](#torstein-lag-2019)

## Evidence

### Torres-Martín 2022

Torres-Martín, C., Acal, C., El-Homrani, M., & Mingorance-Estrada, Á. C. (2022). Implementation of the flipped classroom and its longitudinal impact on improving academic performance. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-022-10095-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Fisher exact test on fail/pass categories for 522 traditional and 635 flipped classroom students (Table 6). The article reports the probability of failing is "1.538 greater" under traditional methodology, with relative risk 1.388.

> "The probability of failing the subject is 1.538 greater when the student body has been given class using the traditional method. The fraction of failed students opposing those that managed to pass the subject is 1.388 times larger when they carried out their classes applying the traditional methodology."

### Torstein Låg 2019

Torstein Låg, Rannveig Grøm Sæle. (2019). Does the Flipped Classroom Improve Student Learning and Satisfaction? A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. AERA Open. https://doi.org/10.1177/2332858419870489

`q3 · i?`

Meta-analysis of 45 samples reporting pass/fail or DFW rates. The article reports "an odds ratio of 1.55" (risk ratio 1.08), with mean pass rates of 77.63% flipped versus 75.91% traditional, calling the effect statistically significant but rather small.

> "The overall mean effect size for pass rates was an odds ratio of 1.55 (95% CI [1.34, 1.78], Z = 6.06, p < .001). This corresponds to a risk ratio of 1.08"
