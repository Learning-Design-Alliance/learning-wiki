## Evidence

### Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero 2020

Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero. (2020). Using machine learning to understand physics graduate school admissions. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/perc.2019.pr.young](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2019.pr.young)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Numerical decision-boundary result from plotting all GPA–physics GRE pairs through the two-factor model, with actual applicant data overlaid. The article reports "a rough cutoff around 700" and notes some students well above it were not admitted.

> "While there does not appear to be a hard cutoff physics GRE score, there appears to be a rough cutoff around 700."

### Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero 2020 (2)

Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero. (2020). Using machine learning to understand physics graduate school admissions. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/perc.2019.pr.young](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2019.pr.young)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

A second feature of the same plotted decision surface: the article finds "a negative slope between GPA of 3.6 and 4.0", which it reads as GPA compensating for sub-threshold physics GRE scores.

> "Interestingly, the decision boundary has a negative slope between GPA of 3.6 and 4.0, suggesting a high GPA can partially compensate for a physics GRE score below the soft threshold."
