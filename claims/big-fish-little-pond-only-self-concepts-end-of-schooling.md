# Schoolmates' achievement (big-fish-little-pond effect) relates only to academic self-concepts, not to labor market beliefs or disengagement

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The achievement of students' schoolmates was relevant only for academic self-concepts and not for beliefs about labor market entry or school disengagement. [→ Dumont 2017](#dumont-2017)

## Evidence

### Dumont 2017

Dumont, Hanna; Protsch, Paula; Jansen, Malte; Becker, Michael. (2017). Fish swimming into the ocean. How tracking relates to students' self-beliefs and school disengagement at the end of schooling. The Journal of educational psychology 109 (2017) 6, S. 855-870. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000175

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The Berlin tracking study found the big-fish-little-pond effect was "only relevant for students' academic self-concepts" and not for labor market self-beliefs or disengagement, delimiting the effect's scope at the end of schooling.

> "The achievement of students' schoolmates (i.e., the big-fish-little-pond effect) was only relevant for students' academic self-concepts and not for students' self-beliefs regarding labor market entry or their school disengagement."
