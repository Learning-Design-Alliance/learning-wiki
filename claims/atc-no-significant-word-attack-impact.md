# ATC produced no statistically significant difference from business-as-usual controls on the confirmatory Word Attack measure over two years

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a cluster RCT of 12 schools (548 students), the intent-to-treat impact of ATC on Woodcock-Johnson III Word Attack was not statistically significant (standardized effect size 0.10, p=.62). [→ Wolf 2016](#wolf-2016)

## Evidence

### Wolf 2016

Wolf, B., Latham, G., Laurenzano, M., Ross, S. M., Tam, W., & Cheung, A. C. K. (2016). Evaluation of Around the Corner: Funded by the Investing in Innovation Fund (i3) Grant #112209. Center for Research and Reform in Education, Johns Hopkins University. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED574582

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Cluster randomized trial with six ATC and six control schools; multiple linear regression with pretest covariate and school-level clustered standard errors. The confirmatory contrast showed "no statistically significant differences" with an effect size of 0.10 (p=.62).

> "We found no statistically significant differences for ATC and control students in early reading skills, as measured by the confirmatory measure, the Word Attack subtest. The estimated standardized effect size indicates that ATC may have positively impacted students' early reading skills, however, the estimated standardized effect size (0.10, p=.62) was smaller than anticipated."
