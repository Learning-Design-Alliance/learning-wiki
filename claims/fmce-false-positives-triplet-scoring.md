## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith and Michael C. Wittmann 2008

Trevor I. Smith and Michael C. Wittmann. (2008). Applying a resources framework to analysis of the Force and Motion Conceptual Evaluation. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.1838 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0711.1838](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0711.1838)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Analysis of response patterns (e.g., a–d–a) on the Reversing Direction triplets, attributed to difficulty distinguishing instantaneous velocity from change in velocity. Scoring questions individually produces false positives; no effect size is printed.

> "For all of these reasons it is widely accepted that responses to the questions in the Reversing Direction cluster must be examined in conjunction with one another rather than individually, otherwise a student with serious problems understanding direction reversal will get 2/3 correct on this question triplet."

### Trevor I. Smith and Michael C. Wittmann 2008 (2)

Trevor I. Smith and Michael C. Wittmann. (2008). Applying a resources framework to analysis of the Force and Motion Conceptual Evaluation. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.1838 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0711.1838](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0711.1838)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Analysis of the Newton III collision questions: the correct answer on question 31 can arise from mass dependence and action dependence reasoning cancelling out. The authors suggest clustering responses 30–32 into a triplet sub-cluster to avoid measuring false positives.

> "Many students who answer "e" on question 31 (the correct answer) answer incorrectly on questions 30, 32, and 34."
