## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In one Fall 2010 calculus course, 3% of students lacked an item asking them to compute log base 3 of 27, and these students were more than twice as likely to fail the course. [→ Marc Harper 2013](#marc-harper-2013)
`q2 i?` In College Algebra, students lacking the binomial-expansion item alge032 were at least twice as likely to fail, with odds-ratio z-scores of 4.6 in 2009 and 3.7 in 2010. [→ Marc Harper 2013 (2)](#marc-harper-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Marc Harper 2013

Marc Harper, Alison Ahlgren Reddy. (2013). Detecting Concepts Crucial for Success in Mathematics Courses from Knowledge State-based Placement Data. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1312.1127](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1312.1127)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational finding from Fall 2010 calculus course data: a common scenario in which most students hold an item but a small minority (under 10%) lack it, and that minority fails at more than double the rate. Such items sit at the base of a significant branch of the domain.

> "3% of the students lacked an item that simply asked them to compute log 3 27 (or numerically similar variant), and these students were more than twice as likely to fail the course."

### Marc Harper 2013 (2)

Marc Harper, Alison Ahlgren Reddy. (2013). Detecting Concepts Crucial for Success in Mathematics Courses from Knowledge State-based Placement Data. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1312.1127](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1312.1127)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Item-level analysis of the College Algebra binomial-expansion item (expand e.g. (5t+6)^2) across Fall 2009 and 2010, reported among worked examples with per-item odds-ratio z-scores.

> "This item has similar statistics to the previous item with an odds-ratio z -score of 4.6 in 2009 and 3.7 in 2010. Students lacking this item were at least twice as likely to fail."
