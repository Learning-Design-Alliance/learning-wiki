## Evidence

### Darie S. 2026

Darie S., Wainman B.C., Wohl G.R., Wang A.Y., Zhang B.Z., Li A., Ahmad E., Palombella A.S., Rockarts J.L., de SA D.L., Nguyen L.N., DeCarlo B., Downer N., Brewer‐Deluce D. (2026). Preliminary insights on high‐fidelity embalming solutions for surgical skills training—An evaluation using the McMaster Embalming Scale and mechanical tests. Anatomical Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.70185

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

One-way ANOVAs of the scalpel incision and suture puncture tests on skin samples mounted in an Instron ElectroPuls E1000 under 10 N tension, three and six samples per donor respectively. The article reports SRF skin "required a significantly greater load to cut in comparison to the SSS embalmed tissue" and to puncture versus all other tissues.

> "There were significant effects of solution for both skin‐based tests (scalpel and suture) such that the SRF embalmed tissue required a significantly greater load to cut in comparison to the SSS embalmed tissue (F(3, 7) = 4.986; p = 0.037; partial η 2 = 0.681, Figure 7A) and puncture in comparison to all other tissues (F(3, 18) = 8.573; p < 0.001; partial η 2 = 0.588, Figure 7B)."

### Darie S. 2026 (2)

Darie S., Wainman B.C., Wohl G.R., Wang A.Y., Zhang B.Z., Li A., Ahmad E., Palombella A.S., Rockarts J.L., de SA D.L., Nguyen L.N., DeCarlo B., Downer N., Brewer‐Deluce D. (2026). Preliminary insights on high‐fidelity embalming solutions for surgical skills training—An evaluation using the McMaster Embalming Scale and mechanical tests. Anatomical Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.70185

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

One-way ANOVA of the intercostal puncture test, in which a chest tube/stylet construct was pushed through each donor's intercostal sample on an Instron 5967. The article reports "ICL embalmed tissue required significantly more force to puncture than the SSS tissue" (partial η 2 = 0.466).

> "There was also a significant effect of solution for the intercostal puncture test where ICL embalmed tissue required significantly more force to puncture than the SSS tissue (F(3, 16) = 3.886, p = 0.029, partial η 2 = 0.466, Figure 7C)."
