# Sign priming effects in addition occur for large, small, and +1 problems but not for tie or +0 problems in older adults

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` For addition in older adults, presenting the sign before the operands significantly facilitated large (43 ms), small (55 ms), and +1 (34 ms) problems, but not tie (24 ms, p = .31) or +0 (25 ms, F < 1) problems. [→ Thevenot 2018](#thevenot-2018)

## Evidence

### Thevenot 2018

Thevenot, C., Dewi, J., Bagnoud, J., Wolfer, P., Fayol, M., & Castel, C. (2018). The use of automated procedures by older adults with high arithmetic skills during addition problem solving. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Planned comparisons in the older-adult sample of the sign priming experiment. Priming was significant for large, small, and +1 additions, whereas "tie problems (24 ms, F(1, 42) = 1.04, p = .31)" and +0 problems showed no significant facilitation.

> "For addition in older adults, presenting the sign before the operands was facilitating for large problems (43 ms, F(1, 42) = 5.44, ηp2 = .11, p = .02), small problems (55 ms, F(1, 42) = 7.11, ηp2 = .15, p = .01) and problems involving 1 (34 ms, F(1, 42) = 17.19, ηp2 = .29, p < .001), but not for tie problems (24 ms, F(1, 42) = 1.04, p = .31) nor for problems involving 0 (25 ms, F(1, 42) < 1)."
