# A brief VR Moon-phases activity did not affect students' environmental attitudes compared with desktop and hands-on conditions

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Environmental attitude scores (EAI subscales) did not differ significantly among the three treatment groups. [→ Madden J. 2020](#madden-j-2020)

## Evidence

### Madden J. 2020

Madden J., Pandita S., Schuldt J. P., Kim B., S. Won A., Holmes N. G. (2020). Ready student one: Exploring the predictors of student learning in virtual reality. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229788

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A 15-item subset of the Environmental Attitude Inventory (Cronbach's α = .83) measuring activism, environmental threat, and human utilization of nature was administered after the activity. The hypothesis that VR's astronaut-like Earth perspective would raise environmental concern was not supported.

> "A one-way ANOVA found no significant differences in environmental attitudes among treatment groups (means ± standard error were VR = 5.24 ± 0.09, Desktop = 5.25 ± 0.1, Hands-on = 5.35 ± 0.08; F(2, 169) 0.41, p = .66)"
