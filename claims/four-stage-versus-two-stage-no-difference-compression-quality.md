## Subclaims
`q3 i?` No statistically significant difference was found between the 2-stage and 4-stage approaches in compression rate, compression depth, or compressions with correct hand placement. [→ Jenko Matej 2012](#jenko-matej-2012)

## Evidence

### Jenko Matej 2012

Jenko Matej, Frangež Maja, Manohin Aleksander. (2012). Four-stage teaching technique and chest compression performance of medical students compared to conventional technique. Croatian Medical Journal. https://doi.org/10.3325/cmj.2012.53.486

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Parallel-group study of 126 first-year medical students (64 in 2-stage, 62 in 4-stage), each performing a 2-minute manikin scenario immediately after a 4.5-hour CPR/AED course. The results report no difference between the 2-stage and 4-stage approach in "compression rate" (P = 0.180), "compression depth" (P = 0.368), or correct hand placement (P = 0.765).

> "There was no difference between the 2-stage and the 4-stage approach in the compression rate (126 ± 13 min-1 vs 124 ± 16 min -1, P = 0.180, independent samples t test), compression depth (43 ± 7 mm vs 44 ± 8 mm, P = 0.368"
