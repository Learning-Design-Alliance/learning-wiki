# Dual enrollment college algebra near the cut-off was associated with a 23 percent higher likelihood of earning a college degree

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students near the eligibility cut-off who enrolled in a rigorous dual enrollment college algebra class were 23 percent more likely to earn a college degree than similar students who did not take the class. [→ Cecilia Speroni 2011](#cecilia-speroni-2011)

## Evidence

### Cecilia Speroni 2011

Cecilia Speroni. (2011). High School Dual Enrollment Programs: Are We Fast-Tracking Students Too Fast? National Center for Postsecondary Research. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/high-school-dual-enrollment-programs-are-we-fast-tracking-students-too-fast

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

In the same Florida tracking study of seniors who took a college algebra placement test, dual enrollment participants near the cut-off were "23 percent more likely to earn a college degree" than similar students who did not take the class; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Students who passed the test (with a score very near the cut-off for eligibility) and enrolled in a rigorous dual enrollment college algebra class were 16 percent more likely to go to college and 23 percent more likely to earn a college degree than similar students who did not take the class."
