# Incentives adapted to local context trigger a sense of being fairly treated and perceived organisational support in community health volunteers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2017 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In poor communities where volunteering costs time and money, incentives with financial value such as meals, stipends, free health care and micro credit funds triggered a sense of being fairly treated, resulting in high accountability to perform, better retention and active participation. [→ Vareilles Gaëlle 2017](#vareilles-gaelle-2017)
`q3 i?` Non-monetary incentives including moral and verbal support, tokens of appreciation, recognition signs and events, on-the-job training, certificates and equipment, in a safe- and friendly-working environment, triggered perception of organisational support and contributed to better retention and task performance. [→ Vareilles Gaëlle 2017 (2)](#vareilles-gaelle-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Vareilles Gaëlle 2017

Vareilles Gaëlle, Pommier Jeanine, Marchal Bruno, Kane Sumit. (2017). Understanding the performance of community health volunteers involved in the delivery of health programmes in underserved areas: a realist synthesis. Implementation Science. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-017-0554-3

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Synthesis of incentive findings across the included studies. The review reports that "the provision of incentives with a financial value" such as meals and stipends recognises CHVs' efforts and "triggers a sense of being fairly treated".

> "the provision of incentives with a financial value, such as meals, stipends, free access to health care and micro credit funds meaningfully recognises the CHVs for their efforts and triggers a sense of being fairly treated"

### Vareilles Gaëlle 2017 (2)

Vareilles Gaëlle, Pommier Jeanine, Marchal Bruno, Kane Sumit. (2017). Understanding the performance of community health volunteers involved in the delivery of health programmes in underserved areas: a realist synthesis. Implementation Science. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-017-0554-3

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Same synthesis, non-monetary incentives. Providing "moral and verbal support, tokens of appreciation, recognition signs and events, on-the-job training, certificates and equipment" in a safe- and friendly-working environment triggered perception of organisational support, contributing to better retention and task performance.

> "providing non-monetary incentives (including moral and verbal support, tokens of appreciation, recognition signs and events, on-the-job training, certificates and equipment) as well as a safe- and friendly-working environment triggers the perception of organisational support"
