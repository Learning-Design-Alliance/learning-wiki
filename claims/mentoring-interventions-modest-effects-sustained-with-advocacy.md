### Grant 2014

Grant, K. E., Farahmand, F., Meyerson, D. A., Dubois, D. L., Tolan, P. H., Gaylord-Harden, N. K., Barnett, A., Horwath, J., Doxie, J., Tyler, D., Harrison, A., Johnson, S., & Duffy, S. (2014). Development of Cities Mentor Project: An Intervention to Improve Academic Outcomes for Low-Income Urban Youth Through Instruction in Effective Coping Supported by Mentoring Relationships and Protective Settings. Journal of Prevention & Intervention in the Community, 42:3, 221-242. https://doi.org/10.1080/10852352.2014.916586

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

A meta-analysis of 73 independent evaluations of mentoring program effects found a modest pooled effect of ".21 þ=/C0 .05" that did not deteriorate at follow-up. Interventions with an advocacy role and a teaching or information provision role for mentors, and matching based on similarity of interests rather than demographics, were more effective.

> "Results revealed that men- toring interventions modestly predicted positive social, behavioral, and edu- cational outcomes for youth (.21 þ=/C0 .05), and effect sizes did not differ across outcome type. In addition, for evaluations that included a follow-up, effects did not deteriorate over time."
