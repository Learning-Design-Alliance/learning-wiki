# STEM and non-STEM students did not differ in sentiment toward using ChatGPT in collaborative interdisciplinary learning

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A t-test on DistilBERT sentiment labels of 167 reflections found no significant difference between STEM and non-STEM students' opinions of ChatGPT use. [→ Gaoxia Zhu 2023](#gaoxia-zhu-2023)

## Evidence

### Gaoxia Zhu 2023

Gaoxia Zhu, Xiuyi Fan, Chenyu Hou, Tianlong Zhong, Peter Seow, Annabel Chen Shen-Hsing, Preman Rajalingam, Low Kin Yew, Tan Lay Poh. (2023). Embrace Opportunities and Face Challenges: Using ChatGPT in Undergraduate Students' Collaborative Interdisciplinary Learning. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2305.18616](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2305.18616)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Sentiment analysis of 167 written reflections using the DistilBERT model, with disagreements between model and human coding resolved by author consensus, followed by a t-test comparing STEM and non-STEM sentiment scores. No effect size is printed; equivalence was not tested.

> "The results showed that there was no significant difference in the opinions of STEM and non-STEM students on the use of ChatGPT in collaborative interdisciplinary learning ( t(137.2) = .92, p = .35)."
