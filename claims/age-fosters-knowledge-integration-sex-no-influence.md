## Evidence

### Thorn CJ 2016

Thorn CJ, Bissinger K, Thorn S, Bogner FX. (2016). “Trees Live on Soil and Sunshine!”- Coexistence of Scientific and Alternative Conception of Tree Assimilation. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0147802

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Ordered logistic regressions (Table 1) included age as a continuous predictor; the printed age coefficients were negative for scientific conceptions in both questions, with no effect on alternative conceptions.

> "Students’ age yielded a significant negative effect on the expression of scientific conceptions in both questions but no effect on the expression of alternative conceptions (Table 1)."

### Thorn CJ 2016 (2)

Thorn CJ, Bissinger K, Thorn S, Bogner FX. (2016). “Trees Live on Soil and Sunshine!”- Coexistence of Scientific and Alternative Conception of Tree Assimilation. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0147802

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Binomial linear models of coexistence (Table 2) showed age and educational background effects in question A only; no significant effects of educational background or age were found within question B.

> "Older students provided significantly fewer fragmented conceptions than younger students in question A. 6th graders provided significantly more co-existing conceptions than 10th graders and students of other academic studies."
