# Parents' work-family conflict is stably negatively associated with their own engagement over time, with no crossover partner effects

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Each parent's work-family conflict negatively predicted their own engagement at every wave, with effects stable across time, but partner effects of one parent's conflict on the other's engagement were nonsignificant. [→ Tiago Ferreira 2018](#tiago-ferreira-2018)

## Evidence

### Tiago Ferreira 2018

Tiago Ferreira, Joana Cadima, Marisa Matias, Joana Marina Vieira, and Teresa Leal; Karine Verschueren; Paula Mena Matos. (2018). Trajectories of Parental Engagement in Early Childhood Among Dual-Earner Families: Effects on Child Self-Control. Developmental Psychology, 54(4), 731–743. https://doi.org/10.1037/dev0000458

`q3 · i?`

Conditional latent growth curve models with work-family conflict as a time-varying covariate, tested separately for mothers and fathers. The article reports "a significant negative effect of WFC on mothers’ engagement rates, stable across T1, T2, and T3" with identical unstandardized coefficients at each wave.

> "Regarding the time-varying covariate, there was a significant negative effect of WFC on mothers’ engagement rates, stable across T1 (b /H11005/H11002.15, p /H11021.001, 95% CI [ /H11002.23, /H11002.07], /H9252/H11005/H11002.19), T2 (b /H11005/H11002.15, p /H11021.001, 95% CI [ /H11002.23, /H11002.07], /H9252/H11005/H11002.22), and T3 ( b /H11005/H11002.15, p /H11021.001, 95% CI [ /H11002.23, /H11002.07], /H9252/H11005/H11002.18)."
