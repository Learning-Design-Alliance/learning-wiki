# Nine months after retraining, CPR knowledge and skills were statistically higher than nine months after the first training

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Theoretical scores nine months after each training were higher after retraining (T19m = 17.2 (±3.8) vs T29m = 19.4 (±3.0), p < 0.01), and practical scores were likewise higher (T19m = 16.5 (±1.0) vs T29m = 16.9 (±0.8), p < 0.01). ## Evidence

### de Araujo Nyagra Ribeiro R 2022

de Araujo Nyagra Ribeiro R, de Araújo Raul Amaral H, Moretti Miguel Antonio A, Chagas Antonio Carlos Palandri C. (2022). Nursing training and retraining on cardiopulmonary resuscitation: a theoretical-practical intervention. Revista da Escola de Enfermagem da USP. https://doi.org/10.1590/1980-220X-REEUSP-2021-0521

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Wilcoxon comparison of nine-month follow-up scores between the two training cycles in the same cohort. The article reports the theoretical mean "was higher after retraining (T19m= 17.2 (±3.8) vs T29m= 19.4 (±3.0), p < 0.01)" and, for skills, "statistically higher nine months after the second training (T19m = 16.5 (±1.0) vs T29m = 16.9 (±0.8), p < 0.01)".

> "The average of correct answers in the theoretical evaluation performed nine months after each training, despite having a drop in both periods, was higher after retraining (T19m= 17.2 (±3.8) vs T29m= 19.4 (±3.0), p < 0.01)."
