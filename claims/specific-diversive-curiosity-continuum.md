# Specific and diversive curiosity are labels on a continuum of information generality within one knowledge-gap-driven process, not categorically distinct types

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The framework treats specific curiosity as information-seeking to close a knowledge gap and argues there is no pure exploratory motivation to increase knowledge gaps, since all epistemic behavior is guided by closing some gap. [→ Kou Murayama 2019](#kou-murayama-2019)

## Evidence

### Kou Murayama 2019

Kou Murayama, Lily FitzGibbon & Michiko Sakaki. (2019). Process Account of Curiosity and Interest: A Reward-Learning Perspective. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09499-9

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument: even seemingly random exploration (scanning a bookshelf, infant exploration) is explained as guided by the expected reward value of generic information, with the generality of expected information lying on a continuum rather than a categorical divide.

> "From our perspective, there is no pure exploratory motivation to increase knowledge gaps, as all of our epistemic behavior is, either explicitly or implicitly, guided by the desire to close a knowledge gap or to gain information"
