## Evidence

### Frolli et al. 2023

Frolli, A., Cerciello, F., Esposito, C., Russo, M., & Bisogni, F. (2023). Learning Italian as a second language in a sample of Ukrainian children: A game-based learning approach. *Pediatric Reports, 15*(3), 502–511. [doi:10.3390/pediatric15030046](https://doi.org/10.3390/pediatric15030046)

`q2 · two-group controlled classroom comparison over four weeks` · `i3 · large effect, partial η² = .891 (time × group) and .827 (group), as printed; 171.91 vs 139.29 words, F(1,46) = 219.215` · `n=48` · `causal · r1`

48 Ukrainian children aged 6–7 (mean 6.5), about one month resident in Italy, were taught basic Italian vocabulary for four weeks in two conditions: game-based (Kahoot! and outdoor games) or traditional instruction. Vocabulary size was counted before and after with the MacArthur–Bates CDI, a word checklist rather than a direct test, and the paper says only that the sample "was divided into two groups", not that assignment was random. Both groups started near 40 words. The authors attribute the difference to motivation and attention, noting that incentive systems fuelled the students' motivation to win. They separately note that Russian was used only in cases of extreme necessity or for complex grammatical topics absent from Russian and Ukrainian, giving articles as their example; no article-specific outcome was measured.

## Discussion

**The headline result is the weaker half.** Six- and seven-year-olds in an immersion setting one
month after arrival are an unusual population on every axis, four weeks is a short comparison, and
"game-based" confounds format, novelty, competition and being outdoors. It motivates building a
game; it does not evidence that a given game will work.

**The note on articles is a hypothesis, not a finding.** The paper reports that articles needed
explanation in Russian because neither Russian nor Ukrainian has them, and measured no article
outcome. That learners from article-less first languages find the article system hard is plausible,
and fits what
[A learner's first language predicts L2 phoneme perception more strongly than their proficiency
level does](l1-predicts-l2-phoneme-perception-more-than-proficiency.md) shows in phonology, but this
study does not test it, so it cannot justify moving articles in a difficulty ordering.

**On the vocabulary numbers themselves.** Both groups roughly tripled or quadrupled their word
count, which is what an immersion context does; the comparison is between two rates inside that, not
between practice and nothing. Read alongside
[Pairing contextual encounters with explicit instruction produces stronger vocabulary outcomes than
either alone](pairing-contextual-encounters-with-explicit-instruction-produces-stronger-vocabulary-outcomes-than-either-alone.md)
[+M], which is the better-evidenced statement about vocabulary instruction generally.
