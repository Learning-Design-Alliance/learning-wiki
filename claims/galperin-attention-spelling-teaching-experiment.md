# Stepwise formation teaching reduced spelling-checking errors in inattentive children from a mean of 6.4 to 0.2 errors per task

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Children characterized as outstandingly inattentive to spelling errors reduced mean errors from 6.4 to 0.2 per task after stepwise formation of monitoring activity. [→ Arievitch 2005](#arievitch-2005)

## Evidence

### Arievitch 2005

Arievitch, I.M., & Haenen, J.P.P. (2005). Connecting Sociocultural Theory and Educational Practice: Galperin's Approach. Educational Psychologist, 40(3), 155-165. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4003_2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Small-scale experiment by Galperin and Kabylnitskaia (1974) with 23 nine-year-old third graders taught to monitor spelling via material, verbal, and covert-speech levels following an orientation chart. The article reports the pre-test mean was "6.4 errors per task" decreasing to "0.2 errors per task" at post-test; no effect size or significance test is printed.

> "In the pre-test the mean was 6.4 errors per task (spelling checking in a given text) within a range of 3 to 12, while in the post-test the mean decreased to 0.2 errors per task within a range of 0 to 3."
