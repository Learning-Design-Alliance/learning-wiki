# The immediate visual causal model advantage does not differ between mental health patients and lay people (no three-way interaction)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The relative advantage of the visual causal model over auditory-only presentation was the same for patients and lay people. [→ Kim 2013](#kim-2013)
`q3 i?` Within the patient-only data, the visual causal model still produced a larger immediate gain than auditory-only presentation. [→ Kim 2013 (2)](#kim-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Kim 2013

Kim, N. S., Khalife, D., Judge, K. A., Paulus, D. J., Jordan, J. T., & Yopchick, J. E. (2013). Visual Causal Models Enhance Clinical Explanations of Treatments for Generalized Anxiety Disorder. Journal of Health Communication, 18(Suppl 1), 103-117. https://doi.org/10.1080/10810730.2013.829136

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mixed-factor ANOVA on the full randomized sample (86 patients, 104 lay people); the three-way interaction was not significant (all other ps > .24 reported alongside).

> "there was no three-way interaction of assessment, presentation, and participant type, indicating that the relative advantage of the visual causal model presentation compared with the auditory-only presentation did not differ between patients and lay people"

### Kim 2013 (2)

Kim, N. S., Khalife, D., Judge, K. A., Paulus, D. J., Jordan, J. T., & Yopchick, J. E. (2013). Visual Causal Models Enhance Clinical Explanations of Treatments for Generalized Anxiety Disorder. Journal of Health Communication, 18(Suppl 1), 103-117. https://doi.org/10.1080/10810730.2013.829136

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Follow-up ANOVA on patient data only (F[1, 84] = 5.3; p = .02) replicated the critical Assessment × Presentation interaction, showing the dual-mode advantage within patients alone.

> "even patients' net gain in knowledge following the visual causal model presentation (M = 7.6, SE = 0.8) was larger than that following the auditory-only presentation (M = 4.9, SE = 0.8; t[84] = 2.3; p = .02)"
