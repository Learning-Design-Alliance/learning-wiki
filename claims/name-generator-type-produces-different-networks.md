## Evidence

### Adrienne L. Traxler 2024

Adrienne L. Traxler, Camila Mani Dias do Amaral, Charles Henderson, Evan LaForge, Chase Hatcher, Madison Swirtz, Ramón Barthelemy. (2024). Person-centered and qualitative approaches to network analysis in physics education research. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020132](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020132)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes to a comparative study (Milardo) that interaction and exchange generator types yielded networks of uncorrelated size without substantial overlap. The review concludes the three generator types "should generally not be used as proxies for each other."

> "In a comparative study [71], interaction and exchange networks had uncorrelated sizes and did not have substantial overlap of members."

### Adrienne L. Traxler 2024 (2)

Adrienne L. Traxler, Camila Mani Dias do Amaral, Charles Henderson, Evan LaForge, Chase Hatcher, Madison Swirtz, Ramón Barthelemy. (2024). Person-centered and qualitative approaches to network analysis in physics education research. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020132](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020132)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing van der Poel, that in a study of prompts for emotional, instrumental, and social support, "3-5 name generators could span most of the variance" in sampled personal support networks. It also notes the exchange approach may underestimate dormant connections.

> "In a study of various prompts for emotional, instrumental, and social support, van der Poel [72] found that 3-5 name generators could span most of the variance and role relationships in the sampled personal support networks."
