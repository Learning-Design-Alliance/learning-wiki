Evidence¶

Alexandre Clin Deffarges 2026¶

Alexandre Clin Deffarges, Nataliya Kosmyna, and Pattie Maes. (2026). Socrates went Nuclear: Comparing Interaction Strategies for AI systems in a Learning Context using Brain Sensing. arXiv preprint arXiv:2609.00584. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00584 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.00584

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Rule-based classification of each discussion into eight behavioral clusters, applied to the 170 exercise attempts in each conversational mode. Copy-paste dominated Mode 1 (52.9%) while Mode 2 spread across ask for definition (28.8%), copy-paste (21.8%), and no discussion (22.4%).

"52.9% of all exercise interactions in Mode 1 were classified ascopy-paste question, and the remaining interactions were mostly ask for definition(22.4%)."

Alexandre Clin Deffarges 2026 (2)¶

Alexandre Clin Deffarges, Nataliya Kosmyna, and Pattie Maes. (2026). Socrates went Nuclear: Comparing Interaction Strategies for AI systems in a Learning Context using Brain Sensing. arXiv preprint arXiv:2609.00584. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00584

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Linguistic similarity analysis comparing each final training answer with the chatbot messages of the corresponding exercise. The authors state this confirms Mode 1 answers were heavily derived from the chatbot while Mode 2 answers were formulated by participants themselves.

"Mean similarity between participant answers and chatbot messages was 0.374 in Mode 1 versus 0.044 in Mode 2 (𝑝< 10−18), and 25.3% of Mode-1 final training answers were nearly identical to the chatbot text, against 0% in Mode 2."
