# Mechanical/formula-based selection decisions outperform holistic/clinical (human-judgement) decisions, with an average effect size advantage of +.11 SD across 161 studies

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Across meta-analyses comparing mechanical and clinical methods in health-related, job performance, academic selection, and work attainment contexts, the mechanical method outperformed the clinical approach with an average effect size difference of ESD = +.11. [→ Kreiter Clare 2018](#kreiter-clare-2018)

## Evidence

### Kreiter Clare 2018

Kreiter Clare, O'Shea Marie, Bruen Catherine, Murphy Paul, Pawlikowska Teresa. (2018). A meta-analytic perspective on the valid use of subjective human judgement to make medical school admission decisions. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2018.1522225

`q2 · i1`

Meta-analytic summary in Table 3 of 161 studies from Grove et al. (136 studies, health-related/behavioural decisions, ESD = +.09) and Kuncel et al. (job performance ESD = +.16; academic GPA ESD = +.10; work attainment/training ESD = +.07), totaling roughly 20,000 subjects.

> "Across applications, the mechanical method outperformed the clinical approach with an average effect size advantage of +.11 SD. Across studies, the mechanical method is shown to provide superior decisions compared to those made with the clinical/holistic approach."
