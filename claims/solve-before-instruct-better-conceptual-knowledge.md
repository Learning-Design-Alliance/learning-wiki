# Solving unfamiliar problems before explicit instruction improves children's conceptual knowledge of mathematical equivalence at posttest and 2-week retention

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Children given the solve-instruct order scored higher on conceptual knowledge (M = 67%) than children given the instruct-solve order (M = 60%), at both posttest and retention test. [→ DeCaro 2012](#decaro-2012)

## Evidence

### DeCaro 2012

DeCaro, M. S., & Rittle-Johnson, B. (2012). Exploring mathematics problems prepares children to learn from instruction. Journal of Experimental Child Psychology, 113, 552-568. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2012.06.009

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized 2 (order) × 2 (explanation) experiment with 159 children who scored below 80% on a pretest. ANCOVA controlling pretest scores and age showed solve-instruct scored higher: "(M = 67%, SE = 2) than children given the instruct–solve order (M = 60%, SE = 2)"; effect at both posttest (F = 4.39, p = .038) and retention (F = 3.97, p = .048).

> "A main effect of order was found for conceptual knowledge, F(1, 152) = 5.07, p = .026, g2p = .03. As shown in Fig. 1, children given the solve–instruct order scored higher on the conceptual knowledge measure (M = 67%, SE = 2) than children given the instruct–solve order (M = 60%, SE = 2)."

### DeCaro 2012 (2)

DeCaro, M. S., & Rittle-Johnson, B. (2012). Exploring mathematics problems prepares children to learn from instruction. Journal of Experimental Child Psychology, 113, 552-568. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2012.06.009

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Separate per-occasion ANCOVAs verified the order effect held at each test: "At both posttest and retention test, there was a main effect of order," with η²p = .03 at each occasion; no explanation-condition effect or interaction at either occasion.

> "At both posttest
and retention test, there was a main effect of order, F(1, 152) = 4.39, p = .038, g2p = .03, and
F(1, 152) = 3.97, p = .048, g2p = .03, respectively."
