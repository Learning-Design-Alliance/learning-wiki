## Evidence

### Norman Gray 2013

Norman Gray, Nicolas Labrosse, Sarah Honeychurch, Steve Draper, Niall Barr (2013). Tagging and Linking Lecture Audio Recordings: Goals and Practice. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1311.1725](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1311.1725)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Server-log analysis of six one-semester astronomy courses in 2012-13, with demonstrations and encouragement but no prescribed tagging activities: the authors found 'only a very small number of students have gone on to add tags'. Figure 3 lists the few taggers' per-lecture counts.

> "However many students subscribed to the podcasts, only a very small number of students have gone on to add tags."

### Norman Gray 2013 (2)

Norman Gray, Nicolas Labrosse, Sarah Honeychurch, Steve Draper, Niall Barr (2013). Tagging and Linking Lecture Audio Recordings: Goals and Practice. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1311.1725](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1311.1725)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The same server-log data across the six courses show tagging concentrated in three students (KM, KE and MS) who 'did so fairly consistently', while 'the two students who liked most, added no tags themselves'; most students added none.

> "The three students who tagged extensively (KM, KE and MS) did so fairly consistently, and the two students who 'liked' most, added no tags themselves."
