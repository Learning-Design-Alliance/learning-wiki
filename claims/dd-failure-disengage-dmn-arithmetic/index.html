Evidence¶

Rosenberg-Lee 2014¶

Rosenberg-Lee, M., Ashkenazi, S., Chen, T., Young, C. B., Geary, D. C., & Menon, V. (2014). Brain hyper-connectivity and operation-specific deficits during arithmetic problem solving in children with developmental dyscalculia. Developmental Science. https://doi.org/10.1111/desc.12216

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Authors' interpretation of the gPPI Group main effect and activation deactivation profiles in the same fMRI sample. The study reports "Children with DD showed prominent hyper-connectivity of the IPS with bilateral angular gyrus, posterior cingulate cortex and ventromedial prefrontal cortex", interpreted as failure to disengage the DMN from task-positive IPS regions.

"Children with DD showed prominent hyper-connectivity of the IPS with bilateral angular gyrus, posterior cingulate cortex and ventromedial prefrontal cortex. Collectively, these areas form core parts of the DMN"

Related Claims¶

  • Children with DD show hyper-connectivity, not reduced connectivity, between the IPS and fronto-parietal and default mode networks during arithmetic — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Children with DD show greater brain activation than TD children in parietal, occipito-temporal and prefrontal regions during both addition and subtraction — related
  • DD children show operation-specific hyperactivation: greater IPS, superior parietal lobule and fusiform activity during subtraction, while during addition only the right superior parietal lobule differs (lower in DD) — related
  • Greater IPS connectivity correlates with worse reaction-time performance during addition but not subtraction in children — related
  • Adults with dyscalculia show hyper-connectivity between primary visual cortex and inferior occipital cortex and fusiform gyrus — related
  • Longitudinal changes in IPS connectivity occur independently of changes in regional activation — related
  • Left IPS connectivity with right IPS, superior parietal lobule, and fusiform gyrus increases over childhood — related
