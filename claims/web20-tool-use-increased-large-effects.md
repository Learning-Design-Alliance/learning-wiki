## Evidence

### E. Ergonul and S. Buyukcoban 2026

E. Ergonul and S. Buyukcoban. (2026). Fostering interactive medical education: integrating Web 2.0 tools through a universal design for learning framework. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2026.2639193

`q2 · i3` · `design · r2`

Wilcoxon signed-rank analysis of 5-point frequency-of-use ratings (1 = never to 5 = very frequently) before and two months after training among 262 faculty; e.g. Google Forms mean rose from 2.18 to 3.15 (Z = -12.448, p < .001). The article states "large effects for these increases (Google Forms r = 0.77, Padlet r = 0.64, Wooclap r = 0.62)".

> "As shown in Table 6, the analysis confirmed a statistically significant increase in the frequency of use of the tools covered in the training. The effect sizes indicated large effects for these increases (Google Forms r = 0.77, Padlet r = 0.64, Wooclap r = 0.62)."

### E. Ergonul and S. Buyukcoban 2026 (2)

E. Ergonul and S. Buyukcoban. (2026). Fostering interactive medical education: integrating Web 2.0 tools through a universal design for learning framework. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2026.2639193

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-training survey percentages among the same 262 participants: "50% and 29.5% of participants, respectively, reporting frequent or very frequent use" of Google Forms and Wooclap. No effect size is printed for this descriptive rate.

> "Notably, Google Forms and Wooclap exhibited high post-training adoption rates, with 50% and 29.5% of participants, respectively, reporting frequent or very frequent use."
