# Professional development texts on data use provide few opportunities for educators to learn statistics, treating statistical concepts superficially

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Both data-use and formative-assessment texts offered few opportunities to learn statistics, generally defining descriptive statistics rather than addressing them in the context of data use. [→ Mandinach 2012](#mandinach-2012)
`q2 i?` Nuanced statistical aspects such as effect size, significance, measurement error, sample size, and sampling error were infrequently discussed. [→ Mandinach 2012](#mandinach-2012)

## Evidence

### Mandinach 2012

Mandinach, E. B., & Gummer, E. S. (2012). Navigating the Landscape of Data Literacy: It IS Complex. White paper. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED582807

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

From the materials analysis of 33 professional development texts coded with an eight-code framework. The analysis found texts "provided few opportunities for educators to learn about issues of statistics in data use," most often covering descriptive statistics only generally defined.

> "Both the data use and formative assessment texts provided few opportunities for educators to learn about issues of statistics in data use. The texts most frequently discussed descriptive statistics, including mean, median, mode, and range"
