# Teachers' growth mindset is positively associated with their self-efficacy (r = .180)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Teachers' growth mindset correlates positively with self-efficacy (r = .180, p < .001), a small effect. [→ Bardach 2024](#bardach-2024)

## Evidence

### Bardach 2024

Bardach, L., Bostwick, K. C. P., Fütterer, T., Kopatz, M., Memarpour Hobbi, D., Klassen, R. M., & Pietschnig, J. (2024). A Meta-Analysis on Teachers' Growth Mindset. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09925-7

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Two-level random-effects meta-analysis of k = 29 effect sizes (N = 6,779) on the self-efficacy outcome, using precision-weighted correlations. The pooled estimate was "r = .180, p < .001" with I² = 62.51%, consistent with theoretical premises linking growth mindset to self-efficacy judgments.

> "we observed numerically somewhat larger nontrivial effects for self-efficacy (r = .180, p < .001)"
