## Evidence

### Sirin 2005

Sirin, S. R. (2005). Socioeconomic Status and Academic Achievement: A Meta-Analytic Review of Research. Review of Educational Research, 75(3), 417-453. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543075003417

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Moderator analysis over 35 student-level correlations reporting separate results for White (k = 11) and minority (k = 24) students; Qb(1, 35) = 164.86, p < .001. The printed effect sizes were mean correlations of r = .27 (White) versus r = .17 (minority).

> "The mean ES for White student samples (.27) was significantly larger than the mean ES for minority student samples (.17)."

### Sirin 2005 (2)

Sirin, S. R. (2005). Socioeconomic Status and Academic Achievement: A Meta-Analytic Review of Research. Review of Educational Research, 75(3), 417-453. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543075003417

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Modified weighted least squares regression on 56 independent correlations with minority ratio as predictor; Q(1, 56) = 131.70, p < .001. β is a regression coefficient, not a standardized effect size.

> "The increase in the number of minorities in a study sample was negatively associated with SES–achievement correlations, β=−.30."
