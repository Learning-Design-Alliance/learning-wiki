# DAGs differ from SEMs in that they are non-parametric and make causal assumptions explicit

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Although DAGs and SEMs look visually similar, they differ fundamentally: DAGs are non-parametric and serve identification only, whereas SEMs are assumption-laden statistical models aimed at estimation. [→ Weidlich 2024](#weidlich-2024)

## Evidence

### Weidlich 2024

Weidlich, J., Hicks, B., & Drachsler, H. (2024). Causal reasoning with causal graphs in educational technology research. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-023-10241-0

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument in the section on the relation between SEMs and DAGs: the paper argues that SEM research often uses 'predict' language to avoid causal claims, creating a disconnect between directional arrows and associational interpretation, and that DAGs resolve this tension by forcing causal assumptions into the open.

> "While the primary goal of SEM is estimation, DAGs are used for identification purposes only. Here, estimation comes later and is methodologically decoupled."
