# Continuing-generation and first-generation students’ IPLS drawings differ in element centrality with large effect sizes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Continuing-generation students gave more central positioning to writing, learning, computers, mathematics, and collaboration; first-generation students gave more central positioning to explicitly identified self depictions, with large effect sizes (d = 2.57 for Computer, d = 2.43 for Learning, d = 2.00 for Self). [→ W. Brian Lane 2025](#w-brian-lane-2025)

## Evidence

### W. Brian Lane 2025

W. Brian Lane, Charlotte Dries, Gabriella Khazal, and Tiffany Snow. (2025). Network analysis of students’ drawn representations of an introductory lab. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2505.02681](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.02681)

`q2 · i3` · `qualitative · r3`

Subnetwork comparison of continuing-generation (N = 55) and first-generation (N = 18) drawings. Table II prints large effect sizes for this contrast, including d = 2.57 for Computer, d = 2.43 for Learning, and d = 2.00 for Self, with all p values < .001.

> "Examining the elements in Table II with large effect sizes based on college generation, we observe that continuing-generation students gave more central positioning to the writing process, the goal of learning, the use of computers, the use of mathematics, and collaboration. The first-generation students gave more central positioning to explicitly identified depictions of self instead of drawing generic student figures"
