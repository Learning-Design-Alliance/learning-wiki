# An emotion-control strategy is expected to lower state anxiety early in training, with a stronger negative relationship for trainees high in trait anxiety

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q1` argument or single case

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Trainees provided with emotion-control training are expected to show lower state anxiety early in training, leading to higher self-efficacy and performance; the effect is expected to be stronger for individuals high in trait anxiety. [→ Bell 2008](#bell-2008)

## Evidence

### Bell 2008

Bell, B. S., & Kozlowski, S. W. J. (2008). Active learning: Effects of core training design elements on self-regulatory processes, learning, and adaptability. Journal of Applied Psychology, 93(2), 296-316. http://digitalcommons.ilr.cornell.edu/articles/410/

`q1 · i?`

Theoretical argument in the Emotion-Control section: negative emotions consume attentional resources and hinder learning, especially early in training when cognitive demands are high; self-efficacy is said to mediate the anxiety-performance relationship. The moderation expectation rests on trait anxiety implying greater situational susceptibility.

> "Specifically, it is expected that the negative relationship between emotion-control training and state anxiety may be stronger for individuals high in trait anxiety, because of their enhanced susceptibility to anxiety-provoking events."
