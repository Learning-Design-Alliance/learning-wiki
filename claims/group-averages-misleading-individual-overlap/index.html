Stewart 1917¶

Stewart, S. F. (1917). A study of physical growth and school standing of boys. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0070804

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Descriptive comparison of individual records within the freshman group. Among thirteen-year-old freshmen the heaviest boy weighed 199 pounds and the lightest 73.9 pounds against a group average of 96.9 pounds; similar overlapping of eighth-grade boys is reported.

"of the freshman boys who were thirteen years of age, the heaviest boy weighed 199* pounds while the lightest weighed 73.9 pounds. The group average was 96.9 pounds."

Related Claims¶

  • Reading score distributions within A–F letter grades overlap heavily, so letter grades misrepresent the achievement of many individual students — related
  • Physiologically accelerated children are mentally accelerated: height correlates with mental age +0.53 for 49 girls with age held constant — related
  • Combining reading, writing and spelling scores still leaves immense grade overlap: on average 33.2% of pupils reach the next grade's range and 35.3% fall below their own grade's range — related
  • Among boys of the same age, those one year above normal grade average both heavier and taller than boys of normal grade, and in some cases boys one year below normal also average heavier and taller — related
