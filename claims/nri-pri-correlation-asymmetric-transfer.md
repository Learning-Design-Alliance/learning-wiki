# Correct answers on the numerical and pictorial items are correlated but far from identical, with asymmetric transfer between representations

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Performance on the two isomorphic items correlates at r = 0.45 ± 0.04, and students who answered the numerical item correctly usually also answered the pictorial item correctly, while only about half of PRI-correct students were NRI-correct. [→ Gayle Geschwind 2023](#gayle-geschwind-2023)

## Evidence

### Gayle Geschwind 2023

Gayle Geschwind, Michael Vignal, and H. J. Lewandowski. (2023). Representational differences in how students compare measurements. [doi:10.1119/perc.2023.pr.geschwind](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2023.pr.geschwind)

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis of the same post-test dataset (N = 670). The Pearson coefficient is r = 0.45 ± 0.04; Table II reports 38 students correct only on NRI, 134 only on PRI, and 131 on both, so "only about half of the students who correctly answered the PRI also correctly answered the NRI."

> "For these items, we find r = 0.45 ± 0.04, which shows a fairly significant correlation in that if a student correctly answered one item, they are more likely to have correctly answered the other."
