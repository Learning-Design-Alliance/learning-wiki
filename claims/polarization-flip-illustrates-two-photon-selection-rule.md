# Changing the counterpropagating beams' circular polarizations from σ+σ+ to σ+σ− eliminates the Doppler-free two-photon signal, experimentally illustrating the Δl = 2 selection rule

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` With two counterpropagating beams of identical circular polarization σ+ a strong Doppler-free signal is observed, whereas with opposite polarizations σ+ and σ− no Doppler-free signal is observed, while the Doppler-broadened profile persists. [→ V Jacques 2009](#v-jacques-2009)

## Evidence

### V Jacques 2009

V Jacques, B Hingant, A Allafort, M Pigeard and J F Roch. (2009). Non-linear spectroscopy of rubidium: An undergraduate experiment. https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3115 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0902.3115](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0902.3115)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Polarization-manipulation experiment in the undergraduate two-photon setup: a second half-wave plate in front of the reflecting mirror flips the retroreflected beam to σ− (figure 7). The disappearance of the Doppler-free peak while the Doppler-broadened profile remains illustrates the Δl = 2 selection rule.

> "As a result, the absorption of two photons from opposite directions would lead to ∆l = 0, and no Doppler-free signal is observed, as depicted on ﬁgure 7. However, the Doppler-broadened proﬁle is still present because it corresponds to the absorption of two photons from the same beam (∆ l = 2)."
