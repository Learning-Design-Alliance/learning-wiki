## Evidence

### Huh Yeon-Ju 2026

Huh Yeon-Ju, Kim Ju Whi, Yoon Narae, Jeong Seoi, Kong Hyoun-Joong, Myung Sun Jung. (2026). Immersive Virtual Reality Training to Improve Novice Physicians’ Emergency Response Skills: Randomized Controlled Trial. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/71455

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Embedded qualitative focus group interviews with 4 interns (2 VR, 2 HFS) at 5 months posttraining, thematically analyzed against Kirkpatrick level 3 constructs. Findings are descriptive perceived transfer, not objectively verified behavior change, from a small homogeneous sample.

> "They reported sustained improvements in their perceived ability to handle desaturation emergencies months after the training (median self-rating scores on a 10-point scale: 0.75 (range 0‐2) before training, 3.25 (range 2‐5) at 1 month, and 7.25 (range 5.5‐8) at 5 month)"
