## Evidence

### Wilson Marenda A. 2019

Wilson Marenda A., Odem Max A., Walters Taylor, DePass Anthony L., Bean Andrew J. (2019). A Model for Holistic Review in Graduate Admissions That Decouples the GRE from Race, Ethnicity, and Gender. CBE—Life Sciences Education 18(1). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.18-06-0103

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear regression and Spearman correlation analyses of 393 matriculated-student records (2007–2012, after outlier removal) plotted in Figure 1 and summarized in Table 2. The authors report the correlation with Verbal Reasoning scores was "independent of gender (Figure 1B), race and ethnicity (Figure 1C), or citizenship status (Figure 1D)"; mean Verbal GRE percentile was 65.67 (SD 24.2).

> "Statistical analyses of the data showed a horizontal trend line between Quantitative Reasoning GRE scores and admissions committee scores, and a negatively sloped trend line between Verbal Reasoning GRE scores and admissions committee scores (Figure 1A)."
