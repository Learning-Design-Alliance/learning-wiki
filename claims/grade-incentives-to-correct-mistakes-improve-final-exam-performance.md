## Evidence

### Benjamin R. Brown 2016

Benjamin R. Brown, Andrew Mason, and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Improving performance in quantum mechanics with explicit incentives to correct mistakes. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010121](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010121)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

A quasi-experimental study across four years of an upper-level quantum mechanics course compared 31 incentivized students with 33 comparison students. The incentivized group scored significantly higher on the posttest, with "t=3.265 and p= 0.002".

> "A similar analysis comparing the posttest performance of the two groups shows that incentivized group performance is statistically significantly better than the comparison group (with t=3.265 and p= 0.002)."

### Benjamin R. Brown 2016 (2)

Benjamin R. Brown, Andrew Mason, and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Improving performance in quantum mechanics with explicit incentives to correct mistakes. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010121](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010121)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

A repeated measures ANOVA on the same quasi-experimental data found a statistically significant group-by-time interaction, "F(1,62)=11.6, p=0.001, η2 = 0.16", indicating the incentivized group improved more from pretest to posttest.

> "we conducted a repeated measures ANOVA 42 and investigated the interaction between the groups and the growth from the pretest to the posttest scores and find a statistically significant difference (with F(1,62)=11.6, p=0.001, η2 = 0.16)."
