## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For subjects using infographics, the study found a significant and positive correlation (p < .05) between number of correct answers in the post-test and positive self-assessment of pleasure. [→ Lyra 2016 (2)](#lyra-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Lyra 2016

Lyra, K. T., Isotani, S., Reis, R. C. D., Marques, L. B., Pedro, L. Z., Jaques, P. A., & Bitencourt, I. I. (2016). Infographics or Graphics+Text: Which Material is Best for Robust Learning?. Proceedings of the IEEE International Conference on Advanced Learning Technologies (ICALT), to appear. [doi:10.1109/icalt.2016.83](https://doi.org/10.1109/icalt.2016.83)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Correlational analysis within the randomized study, using SAM pleasure ratings (1–9) collected seven times in Stage B versus post-test correct answers; the correlation held only for the infographic group (n=14).

> "For subjects using infographics as learning material, we found a significant and positive correlation (p < .05) between the number of correct answers and the positive self-assessment of pleasure"

### Lyra 2016 (2)

Lyra, K. T., Isotani, S., Reis, R. C. D., Marques, L. B., Pedro, L. Z., Jaques, P. A., & Bitencourt, I. I. (2016). Infographics or Graphics+Text: Which Material is Best for Robust Learning?. Proceedings of the IEEE International Conference on Advanced Learning Technologies (ICALT), to appear. [doi:10.1109/icalt.2016.83](https://doi.org/10.1109/icalt.2016.83)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Null correlational finding for the graphic+text group (n=13) in the same Stage B analysis; the article reports no correlation coefficient, only that significance was not reached.

> "In contrast, we did not found the same significant correlation for students who used graphic+text as learning material"
