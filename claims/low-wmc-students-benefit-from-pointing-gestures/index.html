Evidence¶

Juan C. Castro-Alonso 2024¶

Juan C. Castro-Alonso, Paul Ayres, Shirong Zhang, Björn B. de Koning, Fred Paas. (2024). Research Avenues Supporting Embodied Cognition in Learning and Instruction. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09847-4

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing Marstaller and Burianová (2013), an assessment of the working memory capacity of 58 undergraduate psychology students (83% females), finding benefits of pointing gestures restricted to low-capacity students. The review interprets gesturing as redundant scaffolding for high-capacity students; no effect size is printed.

"assessed the working memory capacity of 58 undergraduate psychology students (83% females) and found that only low-working memory capacity students benefitted from making pointing gestures when explaining number equations."
