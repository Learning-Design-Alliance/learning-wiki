# DLL children can catch up to monolingual norms in English vocabulary, but need substantial time and exposure to do so

## Evidence

### Hammer 2014

Hammer, C. S., Hoff, E., Uchikoshi, Y., Gillanders, C., Castro, D., & Sandilos, L. E. (2014). The Language and Literacy Development of Young Dual Language Learners: A Critical Review. Early childhood research quarterly. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecresq.2014.05.008

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Goldberg, Paradis, and Crago (2008), that DLL children in Canada "caught up to monolingual norms of the PPVT-III after an average of 34 months of exposure to English," while Dutch-Turkish DLLs entering school at ages three to four accelerated but did not catch up by study's end.

> "five-year-old DLL children from a variety of home language backgrounds and who lived in Canada caught up to monolingual norms of the PPVT-III after an average of 34 months of exposure to English."

### Hammer 2014 (2)

Hammer, C. S., Hoff, E., Uchikoshi, Y., Gillanders, C., Castro, D., & Sandilos, L. E. (2014). The Language and Literacy Development of Young Dual Language Learners: A Critical Review. Early childhood research quarterly. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecresq.2014.05.008

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports that in Hammer et al.'s longitudinal Head Start study, both learner groups' standardized vocabulary scores increased over two years; simultaneous learners moved from below-average to the lower end of the average English range, and sequential learners reached the average Spanish range.

> "Hammer et al. (2008a) observed that both sequential and simultaneous learners’ standardized vocabulary scores increased over their two years in Head Start."
