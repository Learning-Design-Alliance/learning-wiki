## Evidence

### Chartash David 2026

Chartash David, Bievre Nicolas, Marušić Ana, Ota Yuma, Asada Yoshikazu, Kubo Saori, Kanno Takeshi, Yagi Machiko Saeki, Matsuyama Yasushi. (2026). Evaluation of a Problem-Based Learning Program’s Effect on Artificial Intelligence Ethics Among Japanese Medical Students: Mixed Methods Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/84535

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparative word-frequency analysis of pre/post patient-explanation essays from 118 students; transition analysis showed the increases were driven by new adoption, e.g., 37 (31.4%) students newly used "learning" and 20 (16.9%) newly used "bias".

> "In particular, a high number of students used “learning” (net increase of 18 students), “input” (+17), “bias” (+15), and “utilization” (+13)."

### Chartash David 2026 (2)

Chartash David, Bievre Nicolas, Marušić Ana, Ota Yuma, Asada Yoshikazu, Kubo Saori, Kanno Takeshi, Yagi Machiko Saeki, Matsuyama Yasushi. (2026). Evaluation of a Problem-Based Learning Program’s Effect on Artificial Intelligence Ethics Among Japanese Medical Students: Mixed Methods Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/84535

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Word-frequency and transition analysis of physician-precaution essays (N=118); "information" rose by a net 25 students and "personal info" by 14, while vague terms such as "possibility" (dropped by 24 students) and "reference" (dropped by 30) decreased.

> "Notably, 31 (26.3%) students newly adopted the term “personal info” in their posttest responses, suggesting a shift in their awareness of privacy issues."
