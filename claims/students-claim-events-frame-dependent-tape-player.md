## Evidence

### Rachel E. Scherr 2002

Rachel E. Scherr, Peter S. Shaffer, and Stamatis Vokos. (2002). The challenge of changing deeply-held student beliefs about the relativity of simultaneity. https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1507792

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Classroom observations of the modified tutorial's tape player exercise, in which wavefront F starts music and wavefront R silences it. The reported exchange between two advanced undergraduates and a graduate student is described as typical; students refute one another vigorously and some reject the scenario as impossible.

> "They conclude, erroneously, that special relativity im plies that events that occur in one frame do not necessarily occur in all frames.  Few students recognize spontaneously that they can resolve the conflict by discarding absolute simultaneity."

### Rachel E. Scherr 2002 (2)

Rachel E. Scherr, Peter S. Shaffer, and Stamatis Vokos. (2002). The challenge of changing deeply-held student beliefs about the relativity of simultaneity. https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1507792

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

In-depth individual demonstration interviews with advanced undergraduates and graduate students. The article reports this nearly complete stillness can last about thirty seconds, and students tend not to articulate thoughts, ask questions, or respond to the interviewer.

> "In interview situations, where there are no classmates with whom to discuss the intellectual conflict , many otherwise animated students respond to the tape player scenario with silence."
