## Evidence

### Ahtisham 2026

Ahtisham, B.; Vanacore, K.; Zhou, Z.; Lee, J.; Kizilcec, R. F. (2026). LLM Reasoning Predicts When Models Are Right: Evidence from Coding Classroom Discourse. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2602.09832](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.09832)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-model comparison across annotations from GPT-5, o3, Gemini 2.5 Pro, and Claude 4.5 Sonnet (Figure 1). For Claude 4.5 Sonnet, other models dropped to F1 as low as 0.70 while Random Forest maintained 0.75; the Random Forest "consistently outperformed the other models across all four LLMs".

> "all misconception classification models performed well (F1>0.67) on classifications from all LLMs. The Random Forest classifier consistently outperformed the other models across all four LLMs."

### Ahtisham 2026 (2)

Ahtisham, B.; Vanacore, K.; Zhou, Z.; Lee, J.; Kizilcec, R. F. (2026). LLM Reasoning Predicts When Models Are Right: Evidence from Coding Classroom Discourse. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2602.09832](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.09832)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Per-source-model analysis of detectability in the cross-LLM evaluation. The authors suggest Claude 4.5 Sonnet reasoning "may be more linguistically homogenous with valid reasoning, reducing the effectiveness of simple keyword-based detection."

> "Misclassifications generated by reasoning models like o3 and GPT-5 were the easiest to detect, with the Ran- dom Forest achievingF1 scores exceeding 0.85."
