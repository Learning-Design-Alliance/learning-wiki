## Evidence

### Amirthanayagam Anumithra 2026

Amirthanayagam Anumithra, Zecca Massimiliano, Chitrakar Anisha, Chavda Vijay, Moss Esther L. (2026). Looks like a surgeon? Impact of anthropometry on the kinematics of surgical novices: counterbalanced cross-over design study of minimally invasive surgery. Journal of Robotic Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11701-026-04077-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive comparison of anthropometric measurements among 57 recruited surgical novices (33 females, 24 males), with kinematic data available for 54. The study found "The male students had significantly greater anthropometric measurements as compared to the female participants" with the printed means and p values; no standardised effect size was printed.

> "The male students had significantly greater anthropometric measurements as compared to the female participants (Fig. 1): mean height 181.1 ± 8.30 cm versus 165.6 ± 5.42 cm (p < 0.0001); mean arm span 182.7 ± 10.33 cm versus 163.2 ± 10.2 cm (p < 0.0001); mean elbow height 118.3 ± 6.58 cm versus 107.9 ± 4.9 cm (p < 0.0001); and mean hand size 18.88 ± 1.30 cm versus 16.89 ± 0.96 cm (p < 0.0001)."
