Evidence¶

W. H. Winch 1910¶

W. H. Winch. (1910). Accuracy in school children. Does improvement in numerical accuracy 'transfer'? The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Winch+accuracy+school+children

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Experiment in a Standard III municipal boys' class (average age 10 years 0 months) with ten practice exercises in rule sums versus English composition. Practised-group per-test marks of 6.2, 5.7, 7.6, 6.0 exceeded the non-practised 5.6, 5.2, 7.1, 5.7.

"Group A obtains an average score of 21.8 (mean variation 8.1) and 23.6 (mean variation 9.2) whilst Group B obtains 22.0 (mean variation 8.1) and 25.5 (mean variation 8.5) in the preliminary and final tests respectively."

W. H. Winch 1910 (2)¶

W. H. Winch. (1910). Accuracy in school children. Does improvement in numerical accuracy 'transfer'? The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Winch+accuracy+school+children

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Section-level analysis of the same boys' school experiment, including percentage improvement per section (Table XV), showed gains for the practised group in most sections but losses in two, which the author says cannot ground a transfer doctrine.

"But the excess improvement of B over A, section by section, is uncertain; indeed, in two cases, the non-practised section does better work than the corresponding section of the practised group."
