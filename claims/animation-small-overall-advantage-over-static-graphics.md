# Animation produces a small overall learning advantage over static graphics in multimedia instruction

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q4` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q4 i1` Across 140 pairwise comparisons, animated graphics yielded a small positive effect on knowledge acquisition compared to static graphics (Hedges's g = 0.226). [→ BERNEY 2016](#berney-2016)

## Evidence

### BERNEY 2016

BERNEY, Sandra, BETRANCOURT, Mireille. (2016). Does animation enhance learning? A meta-analysis. Computers and education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2016.06.005

`q4 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 61 primary studies (N = 7036) comparing animated vs. static graphics in multimedia instructional material. The review reports "an overall positive effect of animation over static graphics" with Hedges's g = 0.226, 95% CI = 0.12e0.33.

> "An overall positive effect of animation over static graphics was found, with a Hedges's g effect size of 0.226 (95% confidence interval = 0.12e0.33)."
