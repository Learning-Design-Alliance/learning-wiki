# Displaying data with icons or concise summaries led parents to choose schools with higher academic performance than graphs, numbers, or detailed displays

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Using icons instead of graphs or just numbers, and presenting concise summaries instead of detailed displays, led parents to choose schools with higher academic performance. [→ Steven Glazerman 2020](#steven-glazerman-2020)

## Evidence

### Steven Glazerman 2020

Steven Glazerman, Ira Nichols-Barrer, Jon Valant, Jesse Chandler, Alyson Burnett. (2020). The Choice Architecture of School Choice Websites. Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness, vol. 13, issue 2. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/the-choice-architecture-of-school-choice-websites

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same randomized factorial experiment, the article reports that icon-based displays and concise summaries "led parents to choose schools with higher academic performance" relative to graphs or numbers and detailed displays. No effect size is printed.

> "Other design choices such as using icons to represent data, instead of graphs or just numbers, or presenting concise summaries instead of detailed displays, also led parents to choose schools with higher academic performance."
