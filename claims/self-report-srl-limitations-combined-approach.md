# Self-report measures of SRL capture perceptions of strategy use rather than implementation quality, and a combined trace-data approach may be best

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The authors argue that self-report only measures learners' perceptions of the frequency of strategy use, not how successfully the learner implements the strategy, and that the best approach may combine trace data with survey or interview data. [→ Broadbent 2023](#broadbent-2023)

## Evidence

### Broadbent 2023

Broadbent, J., Panadero, E., Lodge, J. M., & Fuller-Tyszkiewicz, M. (2023). The self-regulation for learning online (SRL-O) questionnaire. Metacognition and Learning, 18, 135–163. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09319-6

`q2 · i?`

In the limitations section, the authors discuss the contested status of self-report for measuring SRL, citing concerns that learners may only accurately report strategies they are familiar with or have recently used; they state "self-report only measures learners’ perceptions of the frequency of strategy use".

> "Arguably, self-report only measures learners’ perceptions of the frequency of strategy use, not how successfully the learner implements the strategy – i.e., the "quality" of the implementation"
