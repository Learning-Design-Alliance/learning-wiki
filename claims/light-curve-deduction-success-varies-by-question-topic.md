`q2 i?` Students studying planetary size were most successful at deducing relative planetary properties, while groups studying rings, atmospheres, and reflected light did not tend to demonstrate strongly meaningful results. [→ McConnell 2010](#mcconnell-2010)

## Evidence

### McConnell 2010

McConnell, N. J., Medling, A. M., Strubbe, L. E., Moth, P., Montgomery, R. M., Raschke, L. M., Hunter, L., & Goza, B. (2010). A College-Level Inquiry-Based Laboratory Activity on Transiting Planets. ASP Conference Series. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1009.3940](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1009.3940)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Instructor observation of group investigations at Hartnell: students studying planetary size could generally explain that a larger planet blocks more light and produces a larger dip, but rarely articulated the reverse inference. No effect size reported.

> "Some students were able to deduce relative planetary properties from light curves (Goal #3). The most successful tended to be those who studied planetary size."
