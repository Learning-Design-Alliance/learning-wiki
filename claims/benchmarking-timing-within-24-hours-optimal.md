# Benchmarking shortly before the OSCE keeps information cognitively available, but immediately beforehand limits reflection; examiners judged roughly 24 hours before optimal

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Engaging with benchmarking shortly before the OSCE makes the information more cognitively available because the performance is still recallable. [→ Edwards 2025](#edwards-2025)
`q2 i?` Benchmarking immediately before the OSCE reduces assimilation into the marking schema because there is insufficient opportunity to reflect; participants judged approximately 24 h before optimal. [→ Edwards 2025 (2)](#edwards-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Edwards 2025

Edwards, R. J., Yeates, P., Lefroy, J., & McKinley, R. (2025). Understanding contexts and mechanisms through which video based benchmarking promotes alignment of examiners' scoring in objective structured clinical exams. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-025-10454-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Realist interview data: examiners felt benchmarking "should occur shortly before the OSCE to ensure that the information was cognitively available" while the performance remained recallable.

> "Participants judged that two to three videos, depending on length, seemed an optimal compromise between comparative information and the load placed on participants. Similarly, participants felt that benchmarking should occur shortly before the OSCE to ensure that the information was cognitively available"

### Edwards 2025 (2)

Edwards, R. J., Yeates, P., Lefroy, J., & McKinley, R. (2025). Understanding contexts and mechanisms through which video based benchmarking promotes alignment of examiners' scoring in objective structured clinical exams. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-025-10454-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Examiners recognised OSCE days are pressured and that benchmarking within the hour before may limit reflection time and assimilation; they judged about 24 h before as "the optimal timing for benchmarking".

> "They judged that approximately 24 h before the OSCE may be the optimal timing for benchmarking"
