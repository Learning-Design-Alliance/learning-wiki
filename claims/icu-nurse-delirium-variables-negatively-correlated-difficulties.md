## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Delirium knowledge and self-efficacy correlate negatively with delirium care difficulties (r = −0.346 and r = −0.361), and clinical reasoning correlates negatively (r = −0.456), all p < 0.01. [→ Dong Jing 2025](#dong-jing-2025)

## Evidence

### Dong Jing 2025

Dong Jing, Sui Weijing, Gong Xiaoyan, Wang Li, Ni Qiqi, Yan Ran, Yi Jia, Ding Ying, Zhuang Yiyu. (2025). Impact of Nurses' Knowledge, Self‐Efficacy and Clinical Reasoning Competency on Difficulties in Caring for Patients With Delirium in the Intensive Care Unit: A Cross‐Sectional Study. Journal of Clinical Nursing. https://doi.org/10.1111/jocn.70034

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Pearson/Spearman correlation analysis among 440 ICU nurses in a multicentre cross-sectional survey. The article reports significant correlations (p < 0.01) among knowledge, self-efficacy, clinical reasoning and difficulties, with "significantly and negatively correlated with delirium care difficulties" for knowledge and self-efficacy.

> "Delirium knowledge and self‐efficacy were significantly and positively correlated with clinical reasoning (r = 0.306, p < 0.01 and r = 0.442, p < 0.01, respectively) and significantly and negatively correlated with delirium care difficulties (r = −0.346, p < 0.01 and r = −0.361, p < 0.01, respectively)."
