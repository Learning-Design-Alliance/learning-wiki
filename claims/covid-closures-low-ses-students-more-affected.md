### Hammerstein Svenja 2021

Hammerstein Svenja, König Christoph, Dreisörner Thomas, Frey Andreas. (2021). Effects of COVID-19-Related School Closures on Student Achievement-A Systematic Review. Frontiers in Psychology. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.746289

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Review synthesis of differential effects; Table 1 records "+60% learning loss in low-SES students" (Engzell et al., Netherlands, n = 350,000) and larger effects in low-SES students (Maldonado and De Witte, Belgium). No standardized effect size is printed for this contrast, so impact is coded `i?` (magnitude not reported), which does not mean no impact. The grade-by-SES interaction it also records comes from a separate Australian study (Gore et al., 2021; see the next entry), not from the Engzell sample. The review cites lower access to remote learning, less school learning assistance, and less learning time for low-SES children.

> "Second, children from families with a low socioeconomic status (SES) were more affected than children from families with a high SES were (Maldonado and De Witte, 2020; Engzell et al., 2021)."

`q3 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r1`

Review synthesis reporting the grade-by-SES interaction from the Australian study (Gore et al.), with printed effect sizes d = 0.16 SD loss for younger children in low school-level-SES schools versus d = 0.15 SD gain for those in medium school-level-SES schools; both fall below d = 0.2.

> "one study reported an interaction between grade and SES, that is, for younger children from schools with low school-level SES, learning losses of 0.16 SD were found, while younger children from schools with medium school-level SES experienced learning gains of 0.15 SD (Gore et al., 2021)."
