# Partner-positioned teachers built students' AI literacy, while assistant and substitute teachers' transparency centered on gaming scores

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` AI literacy instruction stood out in partner classrooms more than in assistant or substitute classrooms, where transparency about AI related to gaming scores rather than learning about AI. [→ Hillary Greene Nolan 2024](#hillary-greene-nolan-2024)

## Evidence

### Hillary Greene Nolan 2024

Hillary Greene Nolan, Ph.D, Merijke Coenraad, Ph.D, Viki Young, Ph.D. (2024). Teaching Partner, Grading Assistant, Substitute Teacher: Three Ways Teachers Positioned an Artificial Intelligence Tool in Writing Instruction. Digital Promise, Center for Inclusive Innovation. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/4f2d86a3-9b66-4538-a5ad-977bb8fbacf7

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative interview finding. Partner teachers taught students how the AI works, e.g., one explained "I think it's helped the 7th and 8th graders to learn the formula in order to break the formula, and to add their voice," and projected flawed 4-scored essays to show AI limits.

> "AI literacy, or learning about AI and how it works, stood out as an emphasis in the classrooms of teachers who positioned AI as a partner more than those who positioned it as an assistant or substitute."
