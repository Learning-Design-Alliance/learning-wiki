## Evidence

### Larrain 2018

Larrain, A., Freire, P., Grau, V., López, P., Salvat, I., Silva, M., & Gastellu, V. (2018). The effect of peer-group argumentative dialogue on delayed gains in scientific content knowledge. New Directions for Child and Adolescent Development, 162, 67–87. https://doi.org/10.1002/cad.20263

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Regression testing whether initial argumentation skills predicted students' contribution to group dialogue, controlling for time devoted to group work. "The initial level of argumentation skills ... significantly predicted students' formulation of claims (β = .24, t = 2.24, p = .029)"; the model explained 46% of variance.

> "The initial level of argumentation skills (not pre-scores of DCK) significantly predicted students' formulation of claims (β = .24, t = 2.24, p = .029), but not arguments, counter-arguments, or argumentative questions, when time of group work was controlled."
