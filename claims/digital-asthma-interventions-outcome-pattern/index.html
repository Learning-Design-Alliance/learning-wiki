Evidence¶

Morrison D 2014¶

Morrison D, Wyke S, Agur K, Cameron EJ, Docking RI, MacKenzie AM, McConnachie A, Raghuvir V, Thomson NC, Mair FS. (2014). Digital Asthma Self-Management Interventions: A Systematic Review. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3958674/

q2 · i? · review · r3

Metareview synthesis of RCT results across included reviews: majorities of RCTs favored intervention for self-care markers, quality of life, and medication use; no results favored control groups.

"Digital interventions improved markers of self care (5/6), quality of life (4/7), and medication use (2/3)."

Morrison D 2014 (2)¶

Morrison D, Wyke S, Agur K, Cameron EJ, Docking RI, MacKenzie AM, McConnachie A, Raghuvir V, Thomson NC, Mair FS. (2014). Digital Asthma Self-Management Interventions: A Systematic Review. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3958674/

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Metareview synthesis: symptom and school-absence results split evenly between favoring intervention and no difference; lung function and health service use showed no overall benefit.

"Effects on symptoms (6/12) and school absences (2/4) were equivocal, with no evidence of overall benefits on lung function (2/6), or health service use (2/15)."
