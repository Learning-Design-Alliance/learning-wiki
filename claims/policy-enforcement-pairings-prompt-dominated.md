# Policy–enforcement pairings are dominated by prompt-based constraints, and policy themes differ in enforcement breadth

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Prompt-Based Constraint accounted for 184 of 423 observed policy–enforcement pair instances (43.5%), and a chi-square analysis showed policy themes and enforcement themes were not distributed independently (χ2(20) = 60.77, p < .001, Cramer's V = .190). [→ Minsun Kim 2026](#minsun-kim-2026)
`q2 i?` Policy categories differed in enforcement breadth: Demonstrating Understanding appeared with all 11 enforcement mechanisms, while Direct-Solution Prohibition was connected to 5 mechanisms. [→ Minsun Kim 2026 (2)](#minsun-kim-2026-2)

Chi-square analysis of the theme-level policy–enforcement matrix across 423 pair instances from the 90-system corpus. The authors interpret this as a descriptive pattern rather than a causal claim because the data are multi-label and pair instances from the same paper are not fully independent.

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Category-level policy–enforcement heatmap analysis (Fig. 3) of pair instances across the 12 policy and 11 enforcement categories. Direct-Solution Prohibition was connected to 5 enforcement mechanisms, mainly Prompt-Based Constraint and Rule-Based Gatekeeping.

> "Demonstrat- ing Understanding ( P6 ) showed the broadest enforcement breadth, appearing with all 11 enforcement mechanisms."
