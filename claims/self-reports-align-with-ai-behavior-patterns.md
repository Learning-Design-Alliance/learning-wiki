### Jingwei Yi 2026

Jingwei Yi, Yueqi Xie, Jiyan He, Rui Ye, Junming Huang, Bin Zhu, Sean Rintel, Yu Xie, Xing Xie, and Fangzhao Wu. (2026). Learning behavior accounts for background-related advantage in AI-assisted education. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10101 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.10101](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2607.10101)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre-to-post survey analysis of perceived risks by course, group, and risk dimension in the 318-participant trial; the article reports overreliance concerns "increase more among limited engagement participants than among proactive and critical participants."

> "Concerns about inaccurate AI responses decline more clearly among both limited engagement and proactive and critical experimental participants relative to the control group. This decline suggests that direct experience with AI assistance may increase confidence in response accuracy or usefulness. Concerns about overreliance increase more among limited engagement participants than among proactive and critical participants."

### Jingwei Yi 2026 (2)

Jingwei Yi, Yueqi Xie, Jiyan He, Rui Ye, Junming Huang, Bin Zhu, Sean Rintel, Yu Xie, Xing Xie, and Fangzhao Wu. (2026). Learning behavior accounts for background-related advantage in AI-assisted education. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10101

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Self-reported AI integration methods among experimental participants (Figure 4c, Wilson 95% confidence intervals); the article reports proactive-critical participants "more often report using AI when stuck."

> "Across both courses, proactive and critical participants more often report using AI when stuck, suggesting that AI assistance is used as targeted support after independent effort. Limited engagement participants more often report using AI throughout learning or only at early stages, suggesting less selective use of AI assistance during assignment work."
