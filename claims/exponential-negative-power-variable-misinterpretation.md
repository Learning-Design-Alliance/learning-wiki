# Students misinterpret the spatial variable in a pulse equation, treating the exponential's negative power as reducing amplitude with position

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Interviewed students explained the amplitude decrease by citing the exponential's negative power, failing to recognize that x maps a dimension of the problem rather than the peak location. [→ Richard N. Steinberg 1997](#richard-n-steinberg-1997)

## Evidence

### Richard N. Steinberg 1997

Richard N. Steinberg, Michael C. Wittmann, and Edward F. Redish. (1997). Mathematical Tutorials in Introductory Physics. AIP Conference Proceedings 399, 1075-1092. [doi:10.1063/1.53110](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.53110)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Videotaped demonstration interviews (40 minutes to one hour, conducted after instruction) with volunteer students performing above the class mean. One student's explanation: "Since e is raised to a negative power . . . it's going to reduce the amplitude as x increases." The authors note the exponential represents decrease in space at t=0, not time.

> ""Since e is raised to a negative power . . . it's going to reduce the amplitude as x increases.""
