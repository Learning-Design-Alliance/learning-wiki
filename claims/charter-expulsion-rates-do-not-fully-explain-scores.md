## Evidence

### Cacey S. Stevens 2015

Cacey S. Stevens, Michael Marder, and Sidney R. Nagel. (2015). Patterns in Illinois Educational School Data. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1502.02057](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1502.02057)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Rough counterfactual estimate using 2012-2013 expulsion rates of 0.61% at Chicago charters versus 0.05% at traditional schools, assuming all expelled students would have failed and transferred to neighborhood schools. Some high-performing charters expelled 1.5 - 4.8% of students per year.

> "If those students were not expelled and were instead tested in a charter program, the resulting percent passing in 2013 among charter students would have been 29% and would have increased among neighborhood students to 24%. Thus, the higher scores of charter programs are not fully explained by their higher expulsion rates."
