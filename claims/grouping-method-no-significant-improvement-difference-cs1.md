# In a large CS1 course, random versus expertise-balanced grouping did not significantly affect poor-performing students' improvement across 11 sessions

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Tong Wu 2025

Tong Wu, Xiaohang Tang, Sam Wong, Xi Chen, Clifford A. Shaffer, and Yan Chen. (2025). The Impact of Group Discussion and Formation on Student Performance: An Experience Report in a Large CS1 Course. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1145/3641554.3701973](https://doi.org/10.1145/3641554.3701973)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Session-level analysis across 11 sessions of an 11-session in-class experiment in a large Python CS1 course, comparing improvement of poor-performing students under the two grouping mechanisms. The article reports "Wilcoxon signed-rank test (statistic = 29.00, p-value = 0.7646)", so no statistically significant difference; equivalence was not tested.

> "The overall average improvement was slightly higher for random grouping (46%) compared to expertise-balanced grouping (44%). However, this difference was not statistically significant, as indicated by the Wilcoxon signed-rank test (statistic = 29.00, p-value = 0.7646)."
