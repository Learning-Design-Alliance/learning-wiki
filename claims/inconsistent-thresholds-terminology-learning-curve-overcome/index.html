Evidence¶

Soomro N. A. 2020¶

Soomro N. A., Hashimoto D. A., Porteous A. J., Ridley C. J. A., Marsh W. J., Ditto R. and Roy S. (2020). Systematic review of learning curves in robot‐assisted surgery. BJS Open. https://doi.org/10.1002/bjs5.50235

q2 · i? · review · r3

Review synthesis of threshold definitions among the 27 studies defining whether the operating-time learning curve was overcome. The most common threshold was procedures to reach a plateau, but others used phase changes or expert-set thresholds, and "nine of the 27 studies (33 per cent)" used no statistical assessment.

"In nine of the 27 studies (33 per cent), no statistical or quantitative assessment of the learning curve was reported beyond visual fit or a qualitative description (data not reported)."

Soomro N. A. 2020 (2)¶

Soomro N. A., Hashimoto D. A., Porteous A. J., Ridley C. J. A., Marsh W. J., Ditto R. and Roy S. (2020). Systematic review of learning curves in robot‐assisted surgery. BJS Open. https://doi.org/10.1002/bjs5.50235

q2 · i? · review · r3

Review synthesis of terminology across studies using performance terms. 'Proficiency' marked the overcome point in ten studies and 'competency' in five; in the four studies reporting multiple terms, terms were either used interchangeably or assigned divergent definitions.

"In 13 of 14 studies (93 per cent) that employed performance terms, at least one term was used to describe the point where the learning curve had been overcome; ‘proficiency’ was used for this purpose in ten studies"
