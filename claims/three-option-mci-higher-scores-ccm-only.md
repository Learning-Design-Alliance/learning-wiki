## Evidence

### Chen Dandan 2023

Chen Dandan, Harman Ann E., Sun Huaping, Ye Tianpeng, Gaiser Robert R. (2023). A comparison of 3- and 4-option multiple-choice items for medical subspecialty in-training examinations. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04277-2

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r1`

Independent-samples t-test comparing percent-correct scores between the 2019 4-option and 2020 3-option ITE-CCM administrations (n = 152 and 123 physicians). The article reports "a small-to-medium effect size (Cohen's d = 0.30)" favoring the 3-option format.

> "Physicians who took 3-option MCIs had higher percent-correct scores than those who took 4-option MCIs for the ITE-CCM (67.7 ± 6.7 versus 65.7 ± 6.9, Table 1), t(273) = 2.514, p = 0.013, with a small-to-medium effect size (Cohen's d = 0.30)"

### Chen Dandan 2023 (2)

Chen Dandan, Harman Ann E., Sun Huaping, Ye Tianpeng, Gaiser Robert R. (2023). A comparison of 3- and 4-option multiple-choice items for medical subspecialty in-training examinations. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04277-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Independent-samples t-test comparing percent-correct scores between the 4-option (n = 113) and 3-option (n = 132) ITE-PA administrations. No significant difference was detected; equivalence was not tested. Table 1 means were 71.8 ± 5.0 versus 71.7 ± 5.4.

> "However, for the ITE-PA, there was no significant difference in physician percent-correct scores, t(243) = 0.102, p = 0.919."
