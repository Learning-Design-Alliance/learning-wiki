# Multimedia-based biology instruction produces higher biology achievement than teacher-centred instruction

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Grade 12 students taught with a multimedia biology tool scored significantly higher on a post-test of nervous-system content than students taught with teacher-centred instruction. [→ Koseoglu 2015](#koseoglu-2015)
`q3 i?` Pre-test biology achievement scores did not differ significantly between the multimedia and teacher-centred groups, supporting comparable baseline readiness. [→ Koseoglu 2015 (2)](#koseoglu-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Koseoglu 2015

Koseoglu, P., & Efendioglu, A. (2015). Can a Multimedia Tool Help Students' Learning Performance in Complex Biology Subjects? South African Journal of Education, 35(4). https://doi.org/10.15700/saje.v35n4a1169

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA on post-test scores of 48 Grade 12 students (Mbio n = 26, TCbio n = 22) after a five-week intervention on the nervous system. The article reports a significant group effect, "F (1-45) = 17.071; p < 0.001", favouring the multimedia group. No standardised effect size is printed for this contrast.

> "According to Table 2, it is determined that there is a significant difference between the post-BA scores of the students in the Mbio and TCbio groups [ F (1-45) = 17.071; p < 0.001]."

### Koseoglu 2015 (2)

Koseoglu, P., & Efendioglu, A. (2015). Can a Multimedia Tool Help Students' Learning Performance in Complex Biology Subjects? South African Journal of Education, 35(4). https://doi.org/10.15700/saje.v35n4a1169

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Independent-samples t-test on pre-test scores confirmed baseline equivalence between the multimedia (n = 26) and teacher-centred (n = 22) groups: "t (46) = 1.294, p > 0.05". No effect size is printed for this null result.

> "According to Table 1, it is determined that there is no significant difference between the students in the Mbio and TCbio groups in terms of their pre-BA test scores [ t (46) = 1.294, p > 0.05]."
