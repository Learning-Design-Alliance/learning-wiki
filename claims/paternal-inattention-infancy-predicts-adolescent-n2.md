# Paternal inattention symptoms measured in early infancy predict adolescent N2 amplitude almost 17 years later, suggesting a parent-of-origin effect

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Fathers' inattention symptoms at child age 2-6 months were significantly associated with adolescent N2 amplitude at 17 years, while fathers' hyperactivity-impulsivity symptoms were not. [→ Einziger Tzlil 2021](#einziger-tzlil-2021)

## Evidence

### Einziger Tzlil 2021

Einziger Tzlil, Ben-Shachar Mattan S., Devor Tali, Shmueli Michael, Auerbach Judith G., Berger Andrea. (2021). "My Brain Can Stop": An ERP Study of Longitudinal Prediction of Inhibitory Control in Adolescence. Brain Sciences, 11(1), 100. https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci11010100

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bivariate correlation analysis in the longitudinal sample showed the infant-era paternal link. The article reports the association "remained significant when we controlled for fathers' hyperactivity-impulsivity symptoms"; mothers' symptoms were only at trend level.

> "Fathers' inattention symptoms, measured when their children were 2–6 months of age, were significantly associated with adolescent N2 amplitude at 17 years of age (this correlation remained significant when we controlled for fathers' hyperactivity-impulsivity symptoms)"
