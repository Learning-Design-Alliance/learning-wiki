# ROC analyses show respiration rate, heart rate and consciousness level were the main predictors of nurses' judgements in both formats, with temperature and systolic blood pressure adding no value in paper cases

## Evidence

### Yang Huiqin 2016

Yang Huiqin, Thompson Carl. (2016). Capturing judgement strategies in risk assessments with improved quality of clinical information: How nurses’ strategies differ from the ecological model. BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-016-0243-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

ROC analysis of hierarchical logistic regression models fitted to 97 nurses' aggregate paper-case judgements; the three-cue model reached AUC 0.90 (95% CI 0.88–0.91, pseudo R2 0.45), and adding temperature and systolic blood pressure added no value.

> "In the paper cases the AUC curves showed that heart rate, respiration rate and consciousness level were the three major predictors of 97 participants’ judgements. The AUC area for each model was consistent with the cumulative R-squared of each hierarchical model."

### Yang Huiqin 2016 (2)

Yang Huiqin, Thompson Carl. (2016). Capturing judgement strategies in risk assessments with improved quality of clinical information: How nurses’ strategies differ from the ecological model. BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-016-0243-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ROC analysis of the same participants' aggregate clinical-simulation judgements; the full model reached AUC 0.86 (95% CI 0.84–0.88, pseudo R2 0.39). The article notes temperature may still be an additional contributor in simulations, while systolic blood pressure was of no added value.

> "In clinical simulations the ROC curves showed respiration rate, heart rate, and consciousness level were the three main predictors of participants’ judgements."
