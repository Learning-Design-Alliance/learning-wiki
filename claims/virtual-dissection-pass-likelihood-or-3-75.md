# Second-year medical students who supplemented gross dissection with virtual dissection were over three times more likely to pass the post-dissection test than textbook controls (tendentially significant)

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` In a randomised controlled trial, virtual dissection students had over three times the odds of a positive post-dissection test outcome compared with textbook students (OR 3.75, 95% CI 0.91–15.49; p = 0.06, interpreted as tendentially significant). [→ Boscolo-Berto Rafael 2021](#boscolo-berto-rafael-2021)

## Evidence

### Boscolo-Berto Rafael 2021

Boscolo-Berto Rafael, Tortorella Cinzia, Porzionato Andrea, Stecco Carla, Picardi Edgardo Enrico Edoardo, Macchi Veronica, De Caro Raffaele. (2021). The additional role of virtual to traditional dissection in teaching anatomy: a randomised controlled trial. Surgical and Radiologic Anatomy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00276-020-02551-2

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Multivariable binary logistic regression on the 23 students who completed the post-test, predicting 'passed exam' from group allocation and academic covariates; the model was significant (χ2 = 9.39, p = 0.05) and "correctly classified 78.3% of cases". The odds ratio 3.75 with CI crossing 1 was interpreted as tendentially significant given only 28% power.

> "only the variable ‘experimental group allocation’ made a unique statistically significant contribution to the model (OR 3.75 with 95% CI 0.91–15.49; p = 0.06)"
