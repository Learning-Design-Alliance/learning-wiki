## Evidence

### Michels Nele R. 2019

Michels Nele R., Vanhomwegen Els. (2019). An educational study to investigate the efficacy of three training methods for infiltration techniques on self-efficacy and skills of trainees in general practice. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-019-1023-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Comparison of self-efficacy questionnaire results (Table 1) against OSCE skill results (Table 2) in the randomized three-group study. The authors report "some discordance between the self-efficacy of the participants and their observed skills" with the knee-example quoted verbatim. No effect size is printed.

> "we observed some discordance between the self-efficacy of the participants and their observed skills. For example, after the theoretical lecture participants estimated their own skills for infiltrations of the knee higher than after training on anatomical models, while the opposite was observed in the skills test (see Table 2)."
