# Merely having access to AI advice nearly eliminates people's willingness to suspend judgment under uncertainty, even when the advice is wrong

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2`–`r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants who could seek AI advice suspended judgment far less often than baseline participants (0.36 vs. 0.06 in Study 1a). [→ Chiara Marcoccia 2026](#chiara-marcoccia-2026)
`q3 i?` The suspension collapse replicated with pre-generated AI responses (0.44 vs. 0.03 in Study 1b), ruling out tool malfunction as an explanation. [→ Chiara Marcoccia 2026 (2)](#chiara-marcoccia-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Chiara Marcoccia 2026

Chiara Marcoccia, Walter Quattrociocchi, Valerio Capraro. (2026). AI advice suppresses people's willingness to say "I don't know", even when the advice is wrong and accuracy is incentivized. https://osf.io/nwx6r/overview?view_only=a03ae0ca587c4c84a442c44d55ab97b6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Preregistered randomized experiment (N = 314, Prolific): baseline vs. optional consultation of an LLM whose answers were engineered to be wrong. Baseline participants were "substantially more likely to suspend judgment than participants who could seek AI advice (0.36 vs. 0.06)". No effect size printed.

> "Participants in the baseline condition were substantially more likely to suspend judgment than participants who could seek AI advice (0.36 vs. 0.06; one-tailed t-test: t = 8.295, p < .001; see Figure 1a)."

### Chiara Marcoccia 2026 (2)

Chiara Marcoccia, Walter Quattrociocchi, Valerio Capraro. (2026). AI advice suppresses people's willingness to say "I don't know", even when the advice is wrong and accuracy is incentivized. https://osf.io/nwx6r/overview?view_only=a03ae0ca587c4c84a442c44d55ab97b6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Direct replication (N = 310) replacing live queries with three pre-generated incorrect AI responses per question, eliminating live-query failures. The authors state the replication "rules out transient failures of the AI system as a potential explanation". No effect size printed.

> "Participants in the baseline condition were again substantially more likely to suspend judgment than participants who could seek AI advice (0.44 vs. 0.03; t = 11.772, p < .001; see Figure 1b)."
