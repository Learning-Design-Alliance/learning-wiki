# Students derived the formula Total Distance = Number of Steps x Approximate Step Size and used it predictively, as illustrated by Angelo's reasoning about winning a bet and verifying his ViMAP model

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students generated a formula for total distance by multiplying number of steps by approximate step size, and used it to make predictions about distances they could not actually walk. [→ Dickes 2016](#dickes-2016)

## Evidence

### Dickes 2016

Dickes, A., Farris, A., & Sengupta, P. (2016). Integrating Agent-based Programming with Elementary Science: The Role of Sociomathematical Norms. Proceedings of the 24th International Conference on Computers in Education. Asia-Pacific Society for Computers in Education. [doi:10.58459/icce.2016.1152](https://doi.org/10.58459/icce.2016.1152)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Case study of a teacher-led discussion and the interview excerpt with Angelo: the class derived "Total Distance = Number of Steps x Approximate Step Size", and Angelo used it to argue he could prove wrong a bet that he would walk no more than 100 units, citing his graph and the calculation 15 times 20 equals 300.

> "Two students in the class responded that they are multiplying the “number of steps” by the “approximate step size”, generating a formula for total distance: Total Distance = Number of Steps x Approximate Step Size."
