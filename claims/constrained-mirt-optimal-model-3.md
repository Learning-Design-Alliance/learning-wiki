## Evidence

### John Stewart 2018

John Stewart, Cabot Zabriskie, Seth DeVore, and Gay Stewart. (2018). Multi-Dimensional Item Response Theory and the Force Concept Inventory. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010137](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010137)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical constrained MIRT on a 20-item FCI problem set, comparing models by chi-squared test, AIC and BIC (Table V). Supporting tests included combining L1 with L2 [χ2(1) = 11, p = 0.001 favoring model 3], combining DF3 with L2, and combining C3/C4 with R1/R2 [χ2(1) = 73, p < 0.001].

> "As such, model 3 which contains all of Newton's 3 laws with a separate deﬁnition of the addition of forces, leaves 1D and 3D kine- matics separate, and contains Facts 1-6 represented the best model of students' responses to the FCI. Interestingly, model 3 is probably closest to the model presented in traditional textbooks. Model 3 was also the model which minimized both AIC and BIC."
