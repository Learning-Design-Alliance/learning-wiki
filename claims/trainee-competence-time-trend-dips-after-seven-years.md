## Evidence

### Majuri M 2024

Majuri M, Malin M, Antila A, Luukkonen R, Palosaari M, Reijula K. (2024). Perceived mental health skills and competence among occupational health physicians. Occupational Medicine (Oxford, England). https://doi.org/10.1093/occmed/kqae080

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cross-sectional survey analysis using Spearman correlation between time in training (categorised <1 to >9 years) and self-rated competence among 42 trainees. The correlation was not statistically significant; no effect size beyond the Spearman coefficient was printed. The authors note the post-7-year dip "seems to decrease".

> "among trainees the competence related to mental health issues was assessed better (mean 3.66, SD 1.08) than among those who have just begun the training programme (mean 3.50, SD 0.93), although there is no statistically significant association between mental health competencies and time (Spearman +0.1795, P = 0.2555)"
