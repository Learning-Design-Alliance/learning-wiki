Evidence¶

Schuessler 2025¶

Schuessler, K., Fischer, V., & Walpuski, M. (2025). Investigating construct validity of cognitive load measurement using single-item subjective rating scales. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09692-6

q2 · i3 · associational · r1

Task-level correlation analysis (Ntasks = 32, all p's ≤ .001 as printed in Table 4): the printed effect size r = 0.78 links invested mental effort with perceived task difficulty, and Table 4 reports r = -0.69 with relative task difficulty, the negative sign being a Rasch-scaling artifact.

"The correlation between the two cognitive load measures (invested mental effort and perceived task difficulty) was 0.78 (see Table 4)."

Schuessler 2025 (2)¶

Schuessler, K., Fischer, V., & Walpuski, M. (2025). Investigating construct validity of cognitive load measurement using single-item subjective rating scales. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09692-6

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Dependent-samples comparison of correlations on the same 32 tasks: the mental-effort/relative-difficulty correlation (r = -0.69) differs significantly from the perceived-difficulty/relative-difficulty correlation (r = -0.91); the article prints only the z statistic and p for the comparison.

"reveals a significant difference, z = 3.75, p ≤ 0.001 (please note this is a comparison for dependent rs)."
