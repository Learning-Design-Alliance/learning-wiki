# Students expect open-note testing to confer an exam advantage, and expectations differ by the condition students are assigned to

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The majority of students selected the open-note section as the one that would earn the higher exam mean. [→ Sato 2015](#sato-2015)
`q3 i?` Assigned condition shaped expectations: open-note students were more likely to expect closed-note students to perform better, while closed-note students were more likely to see open-note testing as an advantage. [→ Sato 2015 (2)](#sato-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Sato 2015

Sato, B. K., He, W., Warschauer, M., & Kadandale, P. (2015). The Grass Isn't Always Greener: Perceptions of and Performance on Open-Note Exams. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-08-0121

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre-quiz question recorded on the paper quiz across the nine study courses; students' expectations diverged from the measured null performance result.

> "when asked, “What section do you think will have the higher exam mean?,” the majority of students selected the open-note takers (Figure 2A)."

### Sato 2015 (2)

Sato, B. K., He, W., Warschauer, M., & Kadandale, P. (2015). The Grass Isn't Always Greener: Perceptions of and Performance on Open-Note Exams. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-08-0121

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within the same perception item, responses differed by assigned condition: closed-note students were more likely to see open-note testing as an advantage (60.6% vs. 50.3%, p = 0.006). Reported as percentages with p-values only.

> "The open-note section was more likely to believe that the closed-note students would perform better (11.8% in the open-note section selected this answer vs. 6.1% in the closed-note section, p = 0.004)."
