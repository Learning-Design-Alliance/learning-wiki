# Freshmen high school students taught programming with self-explanation supported by worked examples achieved significantly better programming knowledge than students taught by the traditional method

## Evidence

### Alhassan 2017

Riyadh Alhassan. (2017). The Effect of Employing Self-Explanation Strategy with Worked Examples on Acquiring Computer Programing Skills. Journal of Education and Practice, Vol.8, No.6. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1133008

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experiment with an experimental group (n = 33) using self-explanation with worked examples and a control group (n = 31) taught traditionally; independent-samples t-test gave t = 8.051, Sig. 0.000. The printed effect size was ƞ2 = 0.458, which the article describes as "in the high range according to Cohen (1988)".

> "It is evident from table 2 that there is a statistically significant difference at the level of 0.000 in the mean scores of achievement tests in the knowledge of computer programing skills between the control and experimental groups in favor of the experimental group."
