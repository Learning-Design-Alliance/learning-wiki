Evidence¶

Tjønnås Maria Suong 2022¶

Tjønnås Maria Suong, Guzmán-García Carmen, Sánchez-González Patricia, Gómez Enrique Javier, Oropesa Ignacio, Våpenstad Cecilie. (2022). Stress in surgical educational environments: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-022-03841-6

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Review conclusion synthesizing 31 simulator-based studies and 6 operative-performance studies. The review reports "both negative and positive impacts were demonstrated in the reviewed articles"; for example, one study found stress associated with worse technical performance, while residents under stress were more efficient but less accurate.

"However, the effects of stress on performance were found to be unclear as both negative and positive impacts were demonstrated in the reviewed articles."

Tjønnås Maria Suong 2022 (2)¶

Tjønnås Maria Suong, Guzmán-García Carmen, Sánchez-González Patricia, Gómez Enrique Javier, Oropesa Ignacio, Våpenstad Cecilie. (2022). Stress in surgical educational environments: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-022-03841-6

q2 · i? · review · r3

Review synthesis of expertise comparisons: some studies showed higher stress among novice surgeons during laparoscopic simulation, others observed the opposite, and Klein et al. found similar performance and stress levels for novice and experienced surgeons. The review states "a coherent association between surgical experience and stress levels was not found".

"In the reviewed studies, a coherent association between surgical experience and stress levels was not found."
