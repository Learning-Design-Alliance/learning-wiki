# Collaborative online comic creation improved Mandarin vocabulary performance significantly more than individual online comic creation or paper-based comic creation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The collaborative learning group showed a statistically significant improvement in Mandarin vocabulary performance from pretest to posttest, while the control and individual comics groups did not show significant improvement. [→ Wilujeng 2015](#wilujeng-2015)
`q2 i?` The collaborative learning group scored highest on comic creation rubric scores (M=5.90), followed by the individual comics group (M=1.14) and the control group (M=1.02). [→ Wilujeng 2015 (2)](#wilujeng-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Wilujeng 2015

Wilujeng, N. C. S., & Lan, Y-J. (2015). Online Comic in Mandarin Chinese's Vocabulary Learning: A Case Study of Budi Utama Multilingual School in Yogyakarta, Indonesia. The IAFOR Journal of Education, 3(2). https://journals.iafor.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/iafor-education-vol3-issue2.pdf

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experiment with three Grade 5 classes (N=16 control, N=14 individual, N=16 collaborative). Two-way ANCOVA with previous semester score as covariate found a significant pretest-to-posttest improvement only in the collaborative learning group (F=7.10, p=.009).

> "While the test within group (3) or the collaborative learning group is significant (F(7.10)=.009, p<.05), meaning that the experiment significantly benefited students' vocabulary performance in the collaborative learning group."

### Wilujeng 2015 (2)

Wilujeng, N. C. S., & Lan, Y-J. (2015). Online Comic in Mandarin Chinese's Vocabulary Learning: A Case Study of Budi Utama Multilingual School in Yogyakarta, Indonesia. The IAFOR Journal of Education, 3(2). https://journals.iafor.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/iafor-education-vol3-issue2.pdf

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comic creations were scored by two teachers using a writing rubric (Jacobs et al., 1981) and analyzed with one-way ANOVA. The collaborative learning group scored highest (M=5.90), followed by individual comics (M=1.14) and control (M=1.02).

> "This performance was also reinforced by their comic creation performance where the collaborative learning group got the highest score based on the rubric writing score with the mean of 5.90, followed by the individual comic group with the mean of 1.14, and the control group with the mean of 1.02."
