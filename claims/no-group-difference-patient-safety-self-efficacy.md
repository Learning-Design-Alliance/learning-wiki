# Self-efficacy for patient safety did not differ between the game, e-module, and control groups

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Scores on both self-efficacy subscales (communication in patient safety issues; recognition of patient safety threats) were low and did not differ between groups, contrary to the hypothesis that the game group would be higher. [→ Dankbaar Mary E. W. 2017](#dankbaar-mary-e-w-2017)

## Evidence

### Dankbaar Mary E. W. 2017

Dankbaar Mary E. W., Richters Olivier, Kalkman Cor J., Prins Gerrie, ten Cate Olle T. J., van Merrienboer Jeroen J. G., Schuit Stephanie C. E. (2017). Comparative effectiveness of a serious game and an e-module to support patient safety knowledge and awareness. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0836-5

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Posttest comparison of the 12-item self-efficacy scale (1-100) across game, e-module, and control groups after the intervention; the article reports no statistically significant group difference and no effect size. All students scored higher on communication (M = 68.8) than threat recognition (M = 65.4).

> "Mean scores on the two scales, communication in patient safety issues and recognition of patient safety threats, were low (12-item-scale, M = 67.1, SD = 9.2). Scores did not differ between groups on either scale."
