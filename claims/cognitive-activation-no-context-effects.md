# Self-reported cognitive activation shows no context effects of school characteristics after controlling for individual teacher self-efficacy

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` Collective efficacy, collaboration, participation, and principal leadership were each non-significantly associated with self-reported cognitive activation when controlling for teachers' self-efficacy. [→ Holzberger 2021](#holzberger-2021)

## Evidence

### Holzberger 2021

Holzberger, D., & Prestele, E. (2021). Teacher Self-Efficacy and Self-Reported Instructional Quality: A Multilevel Perspective on the Role of School Characteristics. Learning and Instruction, 76, 101513. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2021.101513

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel regression context-effect analyses on the TALIS 2018 Austrian sample. Collective efficacy in instruction (β = .25, p = .26) and in classroom management (β = .08, p = .61), plus collaboration, participation and leadership, showed no significant unique context effects on cognitive activation.

> "Additionally, more-external school characteristics were not significantly associated with teachers’ self-reported cognitive activation when controlling for teachers’ self-efficacy (collaboration: b(SE) = –0.00(.02), β = –.03, p = .85"
