# In 2015, 71.4% of academic leaders rated online learning outcomes as the same or superior to face-to-face, with perceptions strongly tied to institutional distance engagement

### I. Elaine Allen and Jeff Seaman with Russell Poulin and Terri Taylor Straut 2016

I. Elaine Allen and Jeff Seaman with Russell Poulin and Terri Taylor Straut. (2016). Online Report Card - Tracking Online Education in the United States. Babson Survey Research Group. https://onlinelearningconsortium.org

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Annual national survey of chief academic officers on perceptions of relative learning quality. The report stresses these are personal perceptions, not measures based on agreed quality metrics.

> "The percent of academic leaders rating the learning outcomes in online education as the same or superior to those in face -to-face instruction was 71.4% in 2015."

### I. Elaine Allen and Jeff Seaman with Russell Poulin and Terri Taylor Straut 2016 (2)

I. Elaine Allen and Jeff Seaman with Russell Poulin and Terri Taylor Straut. (2016). Online Report Card - Tracking Online Education in the United States. Babson Survey Research Group. https://onlinelearningconsortium.org

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Survey results by distance enrollment level. At institutions with 10,000 or more distance students, 41.7% rated online as superior and only 16.0% inferior; this is the only group where less than a majority rate online as good as or better.

> "The results from the academic leaders at schools with no distance education enrollments are far more negative , with a majority (51.2%) reporting the relative quality of online as inferior ."
