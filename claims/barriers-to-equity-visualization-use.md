# Barriers to using equity visualizations included lack of classroom diversity and teachers' own equity beliefs

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Some teachers saw little reason to use disaggregated visualizations because their classrooms had little racial or gender diversity, compounded by anonymity suppression of small groups. [→ Ali Raza 2024](#ali-raza-2024)
`q2 i?` A small number of teachers reported that disaggregated participation data did not reflect their personal interests or beliefs about classroom equity. [→ Ali Raza 2024 (2)](#ali-raza-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Ali Raza 2024

Ali Raza, Tamara Sumner and William R. Penuel. (2024). The Science Student Electronic Exit Ticket (SEET) System: Visualizations to Help Teachers Notice and Reflect on Classroom Inequalities. Journal of Learning Analytics, 11(1), 87-100. https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2024.8199

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Interview coding across all three equity visualizations found rationales including less useful due to lack of sample (4), not a large enough sample size (7), and small sample size made it less useful (3). The system does not display data for groups of 2 or fewer students.

> "With respect to their classroom context, some teachers (13) said there was little reason to look at visualizations disaggregated by race/ethnicity and gender since there was very little racial or gender diversity in their classrooms."

### Ali Raza 2024 (2)

Ali Raza, Tamara Sumner and William R. Penuel. (2024). The Science Student Electronic Exit Ticket (SEET) System: Visualizations to Help Teachers Notice and Reflect on Classroom Inequalities. Journal of Learning Analytics, 11(1), 87-100. https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2024.8199

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Inductive coding of interview rationales identified 7 teachers whose equity beliefs diverged, for example viewing equity as equal access to resources rather than disaggregated participation patterns. Other teachers valued the disaggregated data for pursuing gender and racial equity goals.

> "The second barrier to use stemmed from teachers' own equity beliefs. Specifically, a small number of teachers (7) reported that showing classroom participation data disaggregated by race/ethnicity and gender did not reflect their personal interests or their personal beliefs about c lassroom equity."
