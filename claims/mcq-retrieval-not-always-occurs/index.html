Evidence¶

van den Broek 2023¶

van den Broek, G., Gerritsen, S. L., Oomen, I. T. J., Velthoven, E., van Boxtel, F. H. J., Kester, L., & van Gog, T. (2023). Optimizing multiple-choice questions for retrieval practice: Delayed display of answer alternatives enhances vocabulary learning. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000810

q1 · i? · causal · r2

The article's motivating rationale, stated as a premise rather than a tested result in the available text: MCQ benefits depend on retrieval from memory, which easy-to-guess options can bypass. The article offers this as the theoretical motivation for the stepwise manipulation.

"Answering MCQs is beneficial for learning, especially when learners retrieve knowledge from memory to evaluate plausible answer alternatives. However, such retrieval may not always occur (e.g., with easy-to-guess answers)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Competitive, hard-to-guess answer options generally enhance vocabulary retention (Experiments 3a and 3b). — related
  • The stepwise-MCQ benefit is most robust for questions with noncompetitive answer options and for learners who report trying to retrieve the answer during the delay. — related
  • Stepwise-MCQ benefits generalize across translation directions and across both easy-to-guess (noncompetitive) and hard-to-guess (competitive) answer options. — related
  • Stepwise MCQs, which delay answer options by 4 s, enhance vocabulary retention significantly compared to standard MCQs in three of four experiments, measured days after practice. — related
  • Having more multiple-choice alternatives benefits later retention when initial retrieval success is high but hurts learning when it is low — related
