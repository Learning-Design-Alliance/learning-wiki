# Student-calculated WASP-2 parameters agree with accepted values despite a simplified approach

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Parameters calculated from the student light-curve analysis — stellar mass 0.84 ± 0.10 solar masses, stellar radius 0.73 ± 0.10 solar radii, planet radius 1.05 ± 0.27 Jupiter radii, orbital distance 0.031 ± 0.001 AU — compare well with accepted values for WASP-2b. [→ Michael Cowley and Stephen Hughes 2013](#michael-cowley-and-stephen-hughes-2013)

## Evidence

### Michael Cowley and Stephen Hughes 2013

Michael Cowley and Stephen Hughes. (2013). Characterization of transiting exoplanets by way of differential photometry. Physics Education. http://dx.doi.org/10.1088/0031-9120/49/3/293

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Comparison of the activity's calculated values against accepted values [2] in the article's summary table: stellar mass 0.84 ± 0.10 vs 0.85 ± 0.10 solar masses; stellar radius 0.73 ± 0.10 vs 0.78 ± 0.06 solar radii; planet radius 1.05 ± 0.27 vs 0.96 ± 0.3 Jupiter radii; orbital distance 0.031 ± 0.001 AU in both. The article reports the 'results compared well with currently accepted values'.

> "Despite this, the results compared well with currently accepted values."
