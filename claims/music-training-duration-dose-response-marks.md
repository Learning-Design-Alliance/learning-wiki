# Music-control mark difference emerges with training duration: no difference in grade 3, significant by grade 6, with control marks declining over grades

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2009 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In grade 3, music children (M=5.42, SD=.31) did not differ significantly from controls (M=5.33, SD=.37), t(43)=.88, p=.383; in grade 6, music children (M=5.21, SD=.41) scored significantly higher than controls (M=4.80, SD=.45), t(25)=2.48, p<.05. [→ Olive Emil Wetter 2009](#olive-emil-wetter-2009)
`q2 i?` The music group's overall marks were comparable between grades 3 and 6 (5.42 vs 5.21, p=.106), while the control group's declined significantly (5.33 vs 4.80, t(35)=3.90, p<.001). [→ Olive Emil Wetter 2009](#olive-emil-wetter-2009)

## Evidence

### Olive Emil Wetter 2009

Olive Emil Wetter, Fritz Koerner, Adrian Schwaninger. (2009). Does musical training improve school performance? Instr Sci 37:365–374. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-008-9052-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Grade-wise analysis (Fig. 3) of overall average marks across grades 3–6; from fourth grade on the music group achieved significantly better marks than controls; the authors read this as evidence that duration of musical training is relevant.

> "By contrast, the respective values in control group 2 differed signiﬁcantly (5.33 in grade 3 and 4.80 in grade 6 with t(35) = 3.90, p\ .001) as illustrated in Fig. 3."
