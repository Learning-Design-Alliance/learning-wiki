# In the k-modes post-hoc analyses, cluster membership significantly predicts physics identity but non-binary status does not significantly predict cluster membership

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Cluster membership has a significant effect on physics identity in an ANCOVA controlling for non-binary status, with Cluster 1 students reporting stronger physics identity than Cluster 2 students. [→ Minghui Wang 2025](#minghui-wang-2025)
`q2 i?` Non-binary status is not a significant predictor of k-modes cluster membership in a logistic regression. [→ Minghui Wang 2025 (2)](#minghui-wang-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Minghui Wang 2025

Minghui Wang, Meagan Sundstrom, Karen Nylund-Gibson, and Marsha Ing. (2025). Advancing clustering methods in physics education research: A case for mixture models. [doi:10.1103/1fn4-nqvj](https://doi.org/10.1103/1fn4-nqvj)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Post-hoc ANCOVA on the 567-respondent survey data relating k-modes cluster membership to physics identity while controlling for non-binary status; the article reports "B = –0.20, t(560) = –2.79, p <0.01" for the cluster effect.

> "The results indicate that cluster membership has a significant effect on physics identity; specifically, individuals in Cluster 1 (High professional and identity-based support) report stronger perceptions of their physics identity than individuals in Cluster 2 (High professional and low identity-based support; B = –0.20, t(560) = –2.79, p <0.01)."

### Minghui Wang 2025 (2)

Minghui Wang, Meagan Sundstrom, Karen Nylund-Gibson, and Marsha Ing. (2025). Advancing clustering methods in physics education research: A case for mixture models. [doi:10.1103/1fn4-nqvj](https://doi.org/10.1103/1fn4-nqvj)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Post-hoc logistic regression with cluster membership as the dependent variable and non-binary status as the independent variable; the article reports "B = –0.36, p = 0.11", a non-significant result with no printed effect size.

> "The results indicate that non-binary status is not a significant predictor of k-modes cluster membership. That is, individuals identifying as non-binary show no difference in the likelihood of being assigned to Cluster 1 (High professional and identity-based support) versus Cluster 2 (High professional and low identity-based support; B = –0.36, p = 0.11)."
