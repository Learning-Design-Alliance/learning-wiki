Evidence¶

Teodóra Vékony 2022¶

Teodóra Vékony, Claire Pleche, Orsolya Pesthy, Karolina Janacsek and Dezso Nemeth. (2022). Speed and accuracy instructions affect two aspects of skill learning differently. npj Science of Learning 7:27. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00144-9

q2 · i? · causal · r?

Authors' interpretation (type e) from the Discussion of the two-group cued ASRT experiment: the Accuracy Group, at near-ceiling accuracy, acquired stable probability-based and serial-order-based knowledge, "despite minimizing motor (response) errors". No effect size is printed for this interpretive statement.

"Our study showed that participants learning with accuracy instructions acquired stable probability-based and serial-order-based knowledge despite minimizing motor (response) errors during initial learning."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Probability-based knowledge acquired under speed versus accuracy instructions is comparable when tested with equal speed-accuracy emphasis — related
  • Speed and accuracy instructions produced the intended response tendencies: the Speed Group was faster and less accurate, the Accuracy Group slower and near-ceiling accurate — related
  • Errorless discrimination training avoids emotional responses and yields more effective transfer than errorful training (Terrace's pigeon studies) — related
  • Deliberately designing and correcting one's own invalid experiments improves far transfer to a structurally more complex factorial design, whereas spotting and correcting others' errors does not outperform errorless practice on far transfer — related
  • Both errorful learning methods (self- and other-generated invalid designs) improve near transfer of experimental design principles more than errorless practice, and self-generated deliberate errors yield the best near transfer — related
  • Both errorful learning methods produce better negative knowledge (anticipation of problem-specific design errors) than errorless practice, with no difference between self- and other-generated errors — related
  • Students lack metacognitive awareness of the benefits of errorful learning: the three learning groups did not differ in perceived competence, judgments of learning, or rated method effectiveness even after tests — related
  • Serial-order-based sequence learning is resistant to speed and accuracy instructions in both acquisition and testing — related
