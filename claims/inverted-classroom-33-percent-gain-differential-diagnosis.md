# Students in an inverted-classroom differential diagnosis seminar showed a significant overall absolute gain in skills and knowledge of 33% (p < 0.01)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Pre- to post-test results showed a statistically significant overall absolute gain in skills and knowledge of 33% among seminar participants. [→ Bösner S 2015](#bosner-s-2015)

## Evidence

### Bösner S 2015

Bösner S, Pickert J, Stibane T. (2015). Teaching differential diagnosis in primary care using an inverted classroom approach: student satisfaction and gain in skills and knowledge. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0346-x

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre- and post-test (13 extended matching items, 20 key-feature items) completed by 16 students with anonymous matching, analysed with the Wilcoxon matched-pairs signed-ranks test. The article reports a "significant (p < 0.01) overall absolute gain in skills and knowledge of 33%"; no standardised effect size is printed.

> "There was a significant (p < 0.01) overall absolute gain in skills and knowledge of 33%. Gain of fourth year students was slightly higher at 34% than that of fifth year students (31%)."
