Evidence¶

Neitzel 2026¶

Neitzel, A.J., Storey, N., & Wang, X. (2026). Air Reading: A randomized evaluation of a virtual tutoring model in Louisiana and Texas schools. Center for Research and Reform in Education, Johns Hopkins University. https://education.jhu.edu/crre

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Subgroup analyses extended the main OLS model with treatment-by-characteristic interaction terms for grade, gender, English learner status, special education status, economic disadvantage, race/ethnicity, and state. The article reports "no significant differential impacts identified for any of the subgroups"; across all subgroups treatment students outperformed controls.

"While there were no significant differential impacts identified for any of the subgroups, some subgroup-specific impacts were noteworthy. For example, for students in Louisiana there was a substantial effect size of +0.32 corresponding to an estimated gain of 3.6 months of learning, whereas students in Texas showed an effect size of +0.26, translating to 2.3 months of learning."
