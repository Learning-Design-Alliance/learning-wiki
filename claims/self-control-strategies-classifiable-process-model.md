## Evidence

### Duckworth Angela L. 2016

Duckworth Angela L., White Rachel E., Matteucci Alyssa J., Shearer Annie, Gross James J. (2016). A Stitch in Time: Strategic Self-Control in High School and College Students. Journal of educational psychology, 108(3), 329-341. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000062

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Naturalistic survey of N = 577 high school students who described a real self-control incident and responses to three hypothetical academic scenarios. Two trained coders agreed on "81% of valid responses (κ = .57, p < .001)" for everyday incidents and 87% (κ = .58) for hypothetical ones; the most common nominated strategy was cognitive change (38%).

> "Notably, the majority (90%) of these open-ended responses could be categorized using the process model, and all five types of strategies were represented. They were not, however, equally popular, χ2 (4, N = 522) = 135.99, p < .001."
