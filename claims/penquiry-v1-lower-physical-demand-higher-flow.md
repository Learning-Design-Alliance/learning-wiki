# In Study 1, Penquiry V1 reduced physical demand and better maintained learning flow, and received higher TAM ratings, than a keyboard baseline

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 studies · 2 design `r3`, 1 causal `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` V1 imposed significantly lower physical demand than the baseline (4.06 vs. 2.31, p<0.001). [→ tlx-physical](#tlx-physical)
`q3 i?` V1 showed a statistically significant advantage in Maintained Learning Flow (4.06 vs. 5.63, p=0.046). [→ tlx-flow](#tlx-flow)
`q3 i?` V1 received significantly higher TAM ratings than the baseline in most categories including perceived usefulness and intention to use. [→ tam-1](#tam-1)

## Evidence

Same within-subjects Study 1 NASA-TLX results: the printed Maintained Learning Flow row shows baseline 4.06 versus Penquiry V1 5.63, p=0.046. The article reports this as a statistically significant advantage without an effect size.

> "5. Maintained Learning Flow 4.06 5.63 0.046*"

### tam-1

Jeongmin Rhee, Changhee Lee, Hyunwoo Kim, Kiroong Choe, Bohyoung Kim, Sungahn Ko, and Jinwook Seo. 2026. Penquiry: A Pen-based Interactive In-situ Q&A System Leveraging LLMs. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19870

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Study 1 TAM questionnaire (N=16, within-subjects): V1 rated higher on usefulness (5.98 vs. 6.63, p=0.029) and intention to continue use (5.44 vs. 6.31, p=0.008); intuitiveness was n.s. Average SUS for V1 was 80.78, rated "Excellent".

> "The evaluation of TAM showed thatPenquiryV1 received significantly higher ratings than the baseline condition in most categories, including perceived usefulness and intention to use (Table 1)."
