### Noah Leibnitz and Yangqiuting Li 2026

Noah Leibnitz and Yangqiuting Li. (2026). Investigating students’ gender expression and its relation to sense of belonging in introductory physics courses. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2603.17966](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2603.17966)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Interview study of 26 introductory physics students (RQ1). "Roughly two-thirds of the interviewees associated femininity with being gentle, appealing to emotion, and appearing delicate or pretty," and masculinity with logic, low emotional expressiveness, and strength; the article notes these aligned with Bem Sex-Role Inventory traits.

> "Roughly two-thirds of the interviewees associated femininity with being gentle, appealing to emotion, and appearing delicate or pretty. Conversely, they often associated masculinity with use of logic, being less emotionally expressive , and appearing strong."

### Noah Leibnitz and Yangqiuting Li 2026 (2)

Noah Leibnitz and Yangqiuting Li. (2026). Investigating students’ gender expression and its relation to sense of belonging in introductory physics courses. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2603.17966](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2603.17966)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Same interview study, androgyny conceptions: "Most students (17/26) defined androgyny as a blending of characteristics that were both feminine and masculine," while 11/26 conceived of it as lacking both, with overlap (7/26) and 5/26 unfamiliar with the term.

> "Most students (17/26) defined androgyny as a blending of characteristics that were both feminine and masculine, while some students (11/26) conceived of being androgynous as having neither masculine nor feminine traits."
