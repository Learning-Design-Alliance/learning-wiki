# A-level correlations with undergraduate and postgraduate performance are higher after correction for right-censoring, since raw correlations are attenuated by range restriction and grade inflation

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Correcting A-level and GCSE grades for right-censoring raised their correlations with outcomes substantially above conventional Pearson values (e.g. best-three-A-levels with first-year total rising from r = 0.279 to 0.408). [→ McManus IC 2013](#mcmanus-ic-2013)

## Evidence

### McManus IC 2013

McManus IC, Woolf Katherine, Dacre Jane, Paice Elisabeth, Dewberry Chris. (2013). The Academic Backbone: longitudinal continuities in educational achievement from secondary school and medical school to MRCP(UK) and the specialist register in UK medical students and doctors. BMC Medicine 11:242. https://doi.org/10.1186/1741-7015-11-242

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

MCMC-corrected correlations in the UCLMS Cohort Study (Table 1, lower triangle). For example, best-three-A-levels with first-year total rose from r = 0.279 to 0.408 ± 0.039 after correction. The article states corrected values are "much higher than for conventional Pearson correlations" because grades are right-censored.

> "A-level grades and GCSE grades are strongly right-censored, and Table 1 therefore also shows correlations corrected for right-censorship, when, as expected, the values are much higher than for conventional Pearson correlations."
