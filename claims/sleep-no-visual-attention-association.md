# Free-living sleep measures show no association with visual attention task performance in older adolescents

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Neither acute nor weekly sleep parameters were associated with response times or accuracy on the Posner cue-target task, and the ≤7 h vs >7 h rest groups did not differ. [→ Stefansdottir 2020](#stefansdottir-2020)

## Evidence

### Stefansdottir 2020

Stefansdottir, R., Gundersen, H., Rognvaldsdottir, V., Lundervold, A. S., Gestsdottir, S., Gudmundsdottir, S. L., Chen, K. Y., Brychta, R. J. & Johannsson, E. (2020). Association between free-living sleep and memory and attention in healthy adolescents. Scientific Reports 10:16877. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-73774-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Null result from linear regressions in 160 adolescents adjusted for ADHD, videogame use, and valid-cue baseline measures; the authors suggest "the task lacked complexity, the variability of free-living sleep parameters was too high, or both", noting the 9-minute task may have been too short.

> "Surprisingly, we found no associations between response times on the visual attention task and sleep parameters measured the night prior to cognitive testing or over the entire week (Table 5)"
