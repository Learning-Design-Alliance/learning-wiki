## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Mallick R. 2020

Mallick R., Henderson T., Lahue B. J., Kafal A., Bassett P., Scalchunes C. (2020). Subcutaneous immunoglobulin in primary immunodeficiency – impact of training and infusion characteristics on patient-reported outcomes. BMC Immunology. [doi:10.1186/s12865-020-00371-y](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12865-020-00371-y)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression on the 366-respondent survey cohort tested infusion characteristics against TSQM tertile membership. "A shorter infusion preparation duration resulted in better odds of being in the best tertile for convenience" (p < 0.001); Table 5 shows OR = 1.95 for preparation ≤20 min.

> "A shorter infusion preparation duration resulted in better odds of being in the best tertile for convenience (p < 0.001) and global (p = 0.03) domains."

### Mallick R. 2020 (2)

Mallick R., Henderson T., Lahue B. J., Kafal A., Bassett P., Scalchunes C. (2020). Subcutaneous immunoglobulin in primary immunodeficiency – impact of training and infusion characteristics on patient-reported outcomes. BMC Immunology. [doi:10.1186/s12865-020-00371-y](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12865-020-00371-y)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same regression analysis found associations between shorter infusion preparation and actual infusion times and best-tertile side effects membership (p = 0.007 and 0.03); Table 5 shows OR = 1.76 for preparation ≤20 min on the global domain (p = 0.03).

> "There was also an association between shorter infusion preparation, actual infusion times, and being in the best tertile for side effects (p = 0.007 and 0.03, respectively)."
