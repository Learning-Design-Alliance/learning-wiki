# Non-native English learners made significantly greater reading gains than native learners on 7 of 11 measures, while native learners gained more on vocabulary

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Independent of group assignment, non-native learners made significantly greater gains on both word recognition measures, decoding measures, the spelling measure, and reading comprehension, while native learners made significantly larger vocabulary gains. [→ Alamprese 2011](#alamprese-2011)

## Evidence

### Alamprese 2011

Alamprese, J. A., MacArthur, C. A., Price, C., & Knight, D. (2011). Effects of a Structured Decoding Curriculum on Adult Literacy Learners' Reading Development. Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness. https://doi.org/10.1080/19345747.2011.555294

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subgroup gain analysis of the full pre-post sample (Table 3: native N=226, non-native N=123). The article reports these subgroup contrasts with significance markers but no standardized effect size for the native/non-native contrast, so no magnitude is coded.

> "Non-native learners made significantly greater gains on both word recognition measures (WJR-LW and WRAT3-R), two decoding measures (WJR-WA, TOWRE-PDE, and LSS), the experimenter-designed spelling measure (DS), and reading comprehension (NRC). Native learners made significantly larger gains on vocabulary (NWM)."
