## Evidence

### Heskin L. 2021

Heskin L., Simms C., Traynor O., Galvin R. (2021). Designing a synthetic simulator to teach open surgical skills for limb exploration in trauma: a qualitative study exploring the experiences and perspectives of educators and surgical trainees. BMC Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12893-021-01417-7

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Theme 3 of the interview study: participants specified robust, durable, light and portable simulators, injured structures in context among uninjured anatomy, and materials that are elastic, spongy and react like real tissue in pressure and weightiness.

> "Participants reported that the physicality of the simulator needs to look and feel real, have fidelity in its anatomy and tissue handling and have good haptic feedback."

### Heskin L. 2021 (2)

Heskin L., Simms C., Traynor O., Galvin R. (2021). Designing a synthetic simulator to teach open surgical skills for limb exploration in trauma: a qualitative study exploring the experiences and perspectives of educators and surgical trainees. BMC Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12893-021-01417-7

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In Theme 3, trainees requested multiple scenarios per simulator, consequences for operations going wrong, and scenarios inspired by real patient files, recorded complications or malpractice reports, including time-dependent cases like an ischemic limb.

> "The trainees in this study particularly wanted more difficulty in the scenarios as they progressed, they wanted the opportunity to simulate anatomical variants, unpredictable complications and challenging technical scenarios."
