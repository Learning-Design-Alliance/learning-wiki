# Only one of 51 studies measured patient outcomes, demonstrating transfer of skills from the simulated environment to the workplace

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Only one study assessed patient outcome and demonstrated transfer of skill from the simulated environment to the workplace, reporting a significant complication-rate decrease (P < 0·02) and reduced resuscitation time. [→ James 2019](#james-2019)

## Evidence

### James 2019

James, H. K., Chapman, A. W., Pattison, G. T. R., Griffin, D. R. and Fisher, J. D. (2019). Systematic review of the current status of cadaveric simulation for surgical training. The British Journal of Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1002/bjs.11325

`q2 · i?`

Review reports the single Kirkpatrick level 4 study (Martin and colleagues), in which eight surgical trainees' complication rates for core invasive skills decreased significantly (P < 0·02) and resuscitation time fell from approximately 25 to 10 min in 80 patients.

> "The complication rate for all skills decreased significantly immediately and at 3 weeks after instruction (P < 0·02). Initial trauma resuscitation time after training decreased from approximately 25 to 10 min in 80 patients treated by the participants68."
