# In rigorous controlled studies, residency graduates retained at significantly higher rates than non-residency peers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After controlling for school and district characteristics, two rigorous studies found significant differences in retention between residency graduates and non-residency peers (e.g., 82% vs. 72% still teaching in Year 3 or 4 in the TQP study). [→ Guha 2016](#guha-2016)

## Evidence

### Guha 2016

Guha, R., Hyler, M.E., and Darling-Hammond, L. (2016). The Teacher Residency: An Innovative Model for Preparing Teachers. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/teacher-residency

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The Teacher Preparation Quality Grants study used a regression comparing retention of residency graduates to non-resident teachers with similar teaching placements, "controlling for key district and school characteristics". The report also cites the Boston Teacher Residency study: 80% vs. 63% in Year 3 and 75% vs. 51% in Year 5.

> "82% of graduates were still teaching in their same district in Year 3 or 4 vs. 72% of non-residents still teaching in their same district Year 3 or 4"
