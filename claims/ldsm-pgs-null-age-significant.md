# In latent difference score models of ability domains, nearly all PGS-to-change pathways are small and nonsignificant, while age consistently predicts pre-test level and change

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across latent factors for the ability domains, all but one pathway from PGS to the pre-test or latent difference score was small and statistically nonsignificant, and PGS × age interactions were also nonsignificant. [→ Cherry Youn 2022](#cherry-youn-2022)
`q2 i?` In contrast, pathways from age to the pre-test factor model and to the latent difference score were consistently significant. [→ Cherry Youn 2022 (2)](#cherry-youn-2022-2)

### Cherry Youn 2022

Cherry Youn, Andrew D. Grotzinger, Christina M. Lill, Lars Bertram, Florian Schmiedek, Martin Lövdén, Ulman Lindenberger, Michel Nivard, K. Paige Harden, Elliot M. Tucker-Drob. (2022). Genetic associations with learning over 100 days of practice. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00121-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Latent difference score modeling in Mplus (version 8) of the three ability-domain factors, using the full sample with maximum-likelihood estimation, measurement invariance across time, and age group and PGS covariates. The article reports pathways "were small in magnitude and statistically nonsignificant".

> "all but one pathway from PGS to the pre- or the latent difference score were small in magnitude and statistically nonsignificant"

### Cherry Youn 2022 (2)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same LDSM analysis: the article reports "consistently significant pathways (1) from age to the pre-test factor model and (2) from age to the latent difference score", while PGS × age group interactions were nonsignificant.

> "In contrast, we found consistently significant pathways (1) from age to the pre-test factor model and (2) from age to the latent difference score."
