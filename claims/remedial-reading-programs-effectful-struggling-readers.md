# Remedial reading programs show effects around 0.49 for struggling readers and 0.54 for more severe difficulties

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Meta-analyses show remedial reading programs can be effective for struggling readers (effect size 0.49) and for children with more severe reading difficulties (effect size 0.54). [→ Verhoeven 2022](#verhoeven-2022)

## Evidence

### Verhoeven 2022

Verhoeven, L.T.W.; Voeten, M.J.M.; Segers, P.C.J. (2022). Computer-assisted word reading intervention effects throughout the primary grades: A meta-analysis. Educational Research Review, 37, article 100486. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2022.100486

`q2 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

The review reports, citing Scammacca et al. (2015) and Wanzek et al. (2016), that remedial programs "can be effectful for struggling readers (effect size 0.49)" and for more severe difficulties (0.54); the reviewed studies' designs are not described here.

> "Meta-analyses have shown that these programs can be effectful for struggling readers (effect size 0.49) (Scammacca, Roberts, Vaughn, & Stuebing, 2015) and for children with more severe reading difficulties as well (effect size 0.54) (Wanzek et al., 2016)."
