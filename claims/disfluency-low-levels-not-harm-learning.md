# Low to medium disfluency does not harm learning and sometimes descriptively improves it, though the effect is unstable

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across three text-legibility levels, low legibility descriptively increased learning performance versus a legible control, and the only significant recall difference favored medium over high disfluency. [→ Alexander Skulmowski & Kate Man Xu 2022](#alexander-skulmowski-kate-man-xu-2022)

## Evidence

### Alexander Skulmowski & Kate Man Xu 2022

Alexander Skulmowski & Kate Man Xu. (2022). Understanding Cognitive Load in Digital and Online Learning: a New Perspective on Extraneous Cognitive Load. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09624-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Seufert et al. (2017), that "a low level of legibility (and thus, disfluency) resulted in the descriptive result of an increased learning performance when compared to the legible (fluid) control condition"; a follow-up study found only the medium-versus-high disfluency recall difference was significant.

> "out of three levels of varying text legibility, a low level of legibility (and thus, disfluency) resulted in the descriptive result of an increased learning performance when compared to the legible (fluid) control condition."
