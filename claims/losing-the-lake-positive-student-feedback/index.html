Losing the Lake: Simulations to Promote Gains in Student Knowledge and Interest about Climate Change. https://doi.org/10.12973/ijese.2015.277a

Related Claims¶

  • Playing the game produced a small but significant increase in student interest, driven by items about Las Vegas water sources and home water conservation — related
  • Knowledge gains from the game were largest on water-flow, weather-vs-climate, greenhouse-effect, and household-conservation items, but three misconceptions were not effectively addressed — related
  • Playing Losing the Lake significantly increased seventh graders' objective-test content knowledge relative to a control group viewing an earth science website — related
  • Students reported that the virtual-lab lesson made learning fun, helped them figure out concepts themselves, and supported thinking like real scientists — related
  • Both writing-based and AI-based alternative-ending activities motivated students, though with distinct concerns in each group — related
  • Students rated Zoo U highly for engagement and likeability, with every usability area rated at least 3.8 on a four-point scale — related
