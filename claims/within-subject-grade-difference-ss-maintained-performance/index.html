Evidence¶

Rachel G. Pizzie and David J. M. Kraemer 2023¶

Rachel G. Pizzie and David J. M. Kraemer. (2023). Strategies for remediating the impact of math anxiety on high school math performance. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00188-5

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Linear model predicting Pre-/Post-Intervention Grade Difference (Q3 − [Average of Q1 and Q2]), controlling for course subject and teacher, with no significant main effects of anxiety (F(1,174) = 0.01, p = 0.93) or group (F(1,174) = 0.39, p = 0.53), but "a statistically significant interaction between intervention group and math anxiety".

"However, there was a statistically signiﬁcant interaction between intervention group and math anxiety for the Pre-/Post-Intervention Grade Difference scores, F(1,174) = 4.72, p = 0.03."

Rachel G. Pizzie and David J. M. Kraemer 2023 (2)¶

Rachel G. Pizzie and David J. M. Kraemer. (2023). Strategies for remediating the impact of math anxiety on high school math performance. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00188-5

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Within-subject comparison of the same difference-score analysis: ER students' grades decreased with higher anxiety relative to their own prior performance, while the association was "ameliorated in the SS group" (β = −2.19, t(174) = −2.17).

"For the ER intervention group, higher levels of math anxiety were associated with greater decreases in math grades compared to their previous class performance. Similar to the previous analysis, the negative association observed between Pre-/Post-Intervention Grade Difference scores and anxiety is ameliorated in the SS group"
