# In a modified Brown-Peterson paradigm, the lag function is nonmonotonic for cued recall and monotonic for uncued recall, implicating retrieval cues rather than storage differences.

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The cueing by lag interaction was significant, with cued recall producing the inverted U-shaped spacing function and uncued recall a monotonic one. [→ Glenberg 1976](#glenberg-1976)
`q3 i?` Because subjects did not know whether recall would be cued, storage, consolidation, rehearsal, or habituation differences cannot account for the results. [→ Glenberg 1976 (2)](#glenberg-1976-2)

## Evidence

### Glenberg 1976

Glenberg, A. M. (1976). Monotonic and nonmonotonic lag effects in paired-associate and recognition memory paradigms. Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior, 15, 1-16. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5371(76)90002-5)80001-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment II: 35 subjects (11 eliminated for equipment failures) in a Brown-Peterson paradigm with lags of 0, 10, or 30 sec; the ANOVA found "the critical cueing by lag interaction" significant, F(2, 44) = 7.91, among effects all significant at ps < .005.

> "and the critical cueing by lag interaction, may be tapped by a recognition test. If the F(2, 44) = 7.91, were all significant, all encoded stimulus changes from the first to the ps < .005."

### Glenberg 1976 (2)

Glenberg, A. M. (1976). Monotonic and nonmonotonic lag effects in paired-associate and recognition memory paradigms. Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior, 15, 1-16. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5371(76)90002-5)80001-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The author's interpretation of Experiment II: because "the subjects never knew" whether recall would be cued or uncued, there "can be no storage, consolidation, rehearsal" or habituation differences; only the retrieval cues' relationship to encoded information differed.

> "Since the subjects never knew presentation (lag interval), and the interval whether recall was to be cued or uncued there between the second and third presentation can be no storage, consolidation, rehearsal, (retention interval)."
