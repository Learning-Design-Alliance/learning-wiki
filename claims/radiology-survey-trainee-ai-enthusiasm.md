## Evidence

### Kang J 2019

Kang J, Thompson RF, Aneja S, Lehman C, Trister A, Zou J, Obcemea C, El Naqa I. (2019). NCI Workshop on Artificial Intelligence in Radiation Oncology: Training the Next Generation. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1910.13334](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1910.13334)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article reports, citing a single-institution survey of a radiology department, that trainees showed greater intent to learn AI/ML than attendings, with 97% of trainees versus 77% of attending radiologists planning to learn AI/ML.

> "This survey showed that 97% of trainees (residents and fellows) were planning to learn AI/ML as relevant to their job (vs. 77% of attending radiologists)."

### Kang J 2019 (2)

Kang J, Thompson RF, Aneja S, Lehman C, Trister A, Zou J, Obcemea C, El Naqa I. (2019). NCI Workshop on Artificial Intelligence in Radiation Oncology: Training the Next Generation. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1910.13334](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1910.13334)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

In the same single-institution radiology survey the article reports a trainee-versus-attending contrast in willingness to help build algorithms: 74% of trainees versus 60% of attendings.

> "In fact, 74% of trainees (vs. 60% of attendings) were willing to help create or train an ML algorithm to do some of the tasks as a radiologist."
