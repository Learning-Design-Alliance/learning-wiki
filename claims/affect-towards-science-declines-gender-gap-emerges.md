## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Females and males reported equal positive feelings towards science on entering CEGEP, but after one term males felt significantly more positive towards science than females. [→ Dickie 2006](#dickie-2006)

## Evidence

### Dickie 2006

Dickie, L. O., Dedic, H., Rosenfield, S., Rosenfield, E., & Simon, R. A. (2006). Success and Persistence in Science: The Influence of Classroom Climate. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0610243](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0610243)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Nine-item affect scale administered to 398 cohort students at entry and after the first semester. At entry there was no significant gender difference (F(1,398) = .8, p = .36); after one term "males feel[ed] more positive towards science" (means 2.9 vs. 3.2).

> "However, after one term at CEGEP, there was a significant difference, (means 2.9 vs. 3.2, F(1,398) = 15.1, p < .001) with males feeling more positive towards science, as shown in Figure 4."
