## Evidence

### van Leeuwen 2019

van Leeuwen, A., Rummel, N., & van Gog, T. (2019). What information should CSCL teacher dashboards provide to help teachers interpret CSCL situations? International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning 14, 261–289. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-019-09299-x

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative pilot interviews with 10 primary school teachers used contextual inquiry so teachers described what they actually do, not what they would like. Teachers named five information types, including "background information about students" and dyads' grasp of materials, engagement, progress, and interaction difficulties.

> "Teachers indicated five types of information as sources of information or types of events that they act upon in the collaborative classroom"

### van Leeuwen 2019 (2)

van Leeuwen, A., Rummel, N., & van Gog, T. (2019). What information should CSCL teacher dashboards provide to help teachers interpret CSCL situations? International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning 14, 261–289. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-019-09299-x

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Kano-method ratings with the same teachers on MathTutor-loggable indicators. Excluding background information, the other four types mapped onto "cognitive, metacognitive, and social aspects of collaboration" and the four top-rated indicators became the dashboard's input.

> "The following four indicators were judged most highly: 1) the number of tries a dyad needs to solve an assignment, 2) the chance that a dyad displays trial-and-error behavior on an assignment, 3) dyads’ proficiency on fraction skills, and 4) a display of a dyads’ activity over time."
