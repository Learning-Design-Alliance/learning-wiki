## Evidence

### Klimas J. 2017

Klimas J., Small W., Ahamad K., Cullen W., Mead A., Rieb L., Wood E., McNeil R. (2017). Barriers and facilitators to implementing addiction medicine fellowships: a qualitative study with fellows, medical students, residents and preceptors. Addiction Science & Clinical Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-017-0086-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In the learner-concerns domain of the interview study, the article reports "Those who were capable of self-directed learning benefitted from the training the most because of the experiential nature of learning"; a research fellow linked more cases seen and teaching done to greater comfort and better patient care.

> "Those who were capable of self-directed learning benefitted from the training the most because of the experiential nature of learning."

### Klimas J. 2017 (2)

Klimas J., Small W., Ahamad K., Cullen W., Mead A., Rieb L., Wood E., McNeil R. (2017). Barriers and facilitators to implementing addiction medicine fellowships: a qualitative study with fellows, medical students, residents and preceptors. Addiction Science & Clinical Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-017-0086-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Providers with high clinical workloads struggled with training activities; a student participant felt they were slowing their project down because of a busier year. Learners coped by "rotating their tasks or finding some extra time in their schedules".

> "For most learners, training in addiction medicine and research was something performed in addition to their already busy schedules, which included seeing patients and running clinics."
