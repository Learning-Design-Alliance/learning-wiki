# Multiple-response multiple-correct (MRM) questions are much harder than single-response questions, with no significant discrimination differences

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` MRM questions have much lower average difficulty (0.261) than SR (0.74) and MRS (0.64) questions, but there are no statistically significant differences between the CTT discrimination values for the three question types. [→ Trevor I. Smith 2019](#trevor-i-smith-2019)

## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith 2019

Trevor I. Smith, Suzanne W. Brahmia, Alexis Olsho, Andrew Boudreaux, Philip Eaton, Paul J. Kelly, Kyle J. Louis, Mitchell A. Nussenbaum, and Louis J. Remy. (2019). Developing a reasoning inventory for measuring physics quantitative literacy. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1901.03351](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1901.03351)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

CTT comparison of question types in the protoPIQL (Table I): SR difficulty 0.74 ± 0.04, MRS 0.64 ± 0.08, MRM 0.261 ± 0.003; discriminations 0.39, 0.42, 0.42. The article reports "no statistically significant differences between the CTT discrimination values for SR, MRS, and MRM questions."

> "These results indicate that the MRS questions are statistically similar to the SR questions in terms of CTT difﬁculty (both with averages well above the ideal mean of 0.5 [13]), and that the MRM questions are much harder."
