## Evidence

### Stephanie L. Day 2024

Stephanie L. Day, Jin K. Hwang, Tracy Arner, Danielle McNamara, Carol M. Connor. (2024). Choose Your Own Adventure: Interactive E-Books to Improve Word Knowledge and Comprehension Skills. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2403.02496](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2403.02496)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Feedback came from two focus groups with participating teachers, a qualitative source with no effect sizes. Teachers reported students liked naming characters and choosing their own adventure, and that multiple students reread the story choosing different story streams, which user logs corroborated.

> "The teachers (n = 11) reported that they thought the WKe-Books were a fun and engaging tool for their students."

### Stephanie L. Day 2024 (2)

Stephanie L. Day, Jin K. Hwang, Tracy Arner, Danielle McNamara, Carol M. Connor. (2024). Choose Your Own Adventure: Interactive E-Books to Improve Word Knowledge and Comprehension Skills. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2403.02496](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2403.02496)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same focus groups teachers said "small groups may be more efficient and effective" for the reviews and that "time constraints given our other requirements" would be their biggest challenge; teachers also valued the immediate feedback in the books. No quantitative measures accompanied these perceptions.

> "Multiple teachers also noted that WKe-Books could be difficult to implement with limited flexibility to introduce new tools or activities outside of their normal curriculum."
