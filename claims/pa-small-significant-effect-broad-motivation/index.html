Evidence¶

Gladstone 2025¶

Gladstone, J. R., Schroeder, N. L., Heidig, S., Zhang, S., Palaguachi, C., & Pitera, M. (2025). Do Pedagogical Agents Enhance Student Motivation? Unraveling the Evidence Through Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10050-2

q3 · i1 · quant-synthesis · r2

Three-level meta-analysis with correlated and hierarchical effects and robust variance estimation of studies comparing PA to non-PA conditions on unspecified motivation measures. The pooled effect was "g = 0.20, p = 0.03" with significant heterogeneity (Q(83) = 294.90).

"The random effects 3LMA with CHERVE examining the impact of PAs on learners' "motivation" showed that PAs have a small but statistically significant effect (g = 0.20, p = 0.03, kcomparisons = 84, kstudies = 22)."

Gladstone 2025 (2)¶

Gladstone, J. R., Schroeder, N. L., Heidig, S., Zhang, S., Palaguachi, C., & Pitera, M. (2025). Do Pedagogical Agents Enhance Student Motivation? Unraveling the Evidence Through Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10050-2

q3 · i1 · quant-synthesis · r2

Moderator analysis within the broad-motivation 3LMA found facial expressions significantly moderated the effect; PAs with facial expressions showed g = 0.33 (p = 0.03) while those without showed no significant effect.

"We found that whether or not PAs used facial expressions significantly moderated the impact of PAs on learners' "motivation" (F(2.00, 4.52) = 32.06, p < 0.01). Specifically, PAs with facial expressions improved learners' "motivation" (g = 0.33, p = 0.03), whereas those without facial expressions had no significant effect."
