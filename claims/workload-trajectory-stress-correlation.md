## Evidence

### Lahme 2024

Lahme, S. Z., Cirkel, J. O., Hahn, L., Hofmann, J., Neuhaus, J., Schneider, S., & Klein, P. (2024). From enrollment to exams: Perceived stress dynamics among first-year physics students. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020127](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020127)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Weekly self-estimated workload trajectory (Fig. 6) across the three groups, analyzed after 90% winsorization due to extreme outliers. Workload rose to "about fifty hours per week" and dropped back to around twenty hours after exams. No effect size is printed for this descriptive trajectory.

> "During the first four to six lecture weeks, this self-estimated workload increased strongly to a level of about fifty hours per week in all groups. This level continued until the end of the exam weeks, after which the reported workload dropped back to a level of around twenty hours or even less."

### Lahme 2024 (2)

Lahme, S. Z., Cirkel, J. O., Hahn, L., Hofmann, J., Neuhaus, J., Schneider, S., & Klein, P. (2024). From enrollment to exams: Perceived stress dynamics among first-year physics students. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020127](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020127)

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Repeated measures correlation over N = 3,198 observations from all groups and measuring points. The article reports the printed effect size rrm(2843) = 0.62, CI 99% [0.59, 0.65], p <0.001, supporting a coherent picture linking workload and stress perceptions.

> "The repeated measures correlation [84] between the total stress score and the reported workload was calculated over all measuring points and all groups, i.e., N = 3, 198 observations. It is rrm(2843) = 0.62, CI 99% [0.59, 0.65], p <0.001."
