Evidence¶

Hossain 2026¶

Hossain, S., & Nawmi, T. A. (2026). Measuring How Students Rely on Generative AI in Academic Writing: Development and Multi-Source Validation of the Generative AI Reliance Types Scale (GenAI-RTS). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14301 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.14301

q2 · i? · design · r3

Relationships-to-other-variables evidence tested a priori convergent and criterion expectations with Pearson correlations and one-way ANOVAs against an AI literacy measure (α = .78) and ten writing-process and outcome variables. The article reports "Strategic reliance was positively associated with AI literacy" and that reliance types differentiated students across the criterion variables.

"Strategic reliance was positively associated with AI literacy; Critical Evaluation and Dependent reliance emerged as empirically distinct constructs; and the reliance types differentiated students across multiple writing process and outcome variables."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Critical Evaluation and Dependent reliance emerge as empirically distinct constructs, with near-zero correlation between the two facets — related
  • The GenAI-RTS is supported by a five-factor structure in which Strategic reliance comprises Deliberate Use and Critical Evaluation facets alongside Instrumental, Dependent, and Dialogic factors — related
  • Reliance, trust, and dependency are distinct constructs requiring separate measurement from AI-literacy totals — related
  • Undergraduate LLM reliance classifies into four types — Strategic (34.3%), Instrumental (30.9%), Dialogic (30.4%), and Dependent (4.5%) — using a threshold-based classification rule — related
  • Reliance type membership significantly predicts all ten writing-process and outcome variables, supporting criterion validity of the typology — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Strategic users score lowest on standard AI-outcome measures, reflecting instrument validity limits that reward AI-driven outcomes over writing quality — related
  • Available AI-dependence and attachment instruments follow three non-interchangeable measurement logics and do not converge on one stable construct — related
