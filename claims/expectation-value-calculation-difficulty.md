# Most graduate students fail to compute the expectation value of energy, and many who answer the probability question correctly do not exploit it

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Only 39% of students correctly calculated the expectation value of the energy, and a majority of those who had answered the preceding probability question correctly did not use that result. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016)

`q2 i?` Many students who correctly found possible energy values and probabilities could not use that information to determine the expectation value of the energy. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016)

## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Assessing and improving student understanding of quantum mechanics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05664](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05664)

`q2 · i?`

Survey question 2(c) asked for the expectation value of energy in the superposition state; only 39% of students answered correctly. Many who did answer correctly worked out the expectation value from scratch via the integral rather than using probabilities from 2(b).

> "This question turned out to be difﬁcult for students and only 39% provided the correct response. A surprising result is that a majority of students who had answered 2(b) correctly did not see its relevance for 2(c), and did not exploit what they found for 2(b) in an- swering 2(c)."

### Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Assessing and improving student understanding of quantum mechanics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05664](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05664)

`q2 · i?`

On the same expectation-value question, 17% of students wrote only the formal expression and could not proceed. Interviews identified two reasons: not recalling the expectation value as an ensemble average, and believing measurement probabilities depend on time.

> "17% of the students simply wrote ⟨Ψ|E|Ψ⟩ or ⟨Ψ|H|Ψ⟩ and did not know how to proceed."
