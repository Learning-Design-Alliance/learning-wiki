# End-of-physics-2 self-efficacy is directly predicted by prior self-efficacy (b = 0.41), belonging (b = 0.35), peer interaction (b = 0.21) and perceived recognition (b = 0.16)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` All three learning-environment perceptions have direct positive standardized effects on physics 2 self-efficacy (belonging b = 0.35, peer interaction b = 0.21, perceived recognition b = 0.16), with belonging the largest among them. [→ Sonja Cwik 2020](#sonja-cwik-2020)

## Evidence

### Sonja Cwik 2020

Sonja Cwik, Kyle Whitcomb, and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). How the learning environment predicts male and female students' motivational beliefs in algebra-based introductory physics courses. Physics Education Research Conference Proceedings. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.cwik](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.cwik)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Gender-mediation SEM (Fig. 1) on matched survey data from 544 students; the printed standardized regression coefficients are b = 0.41, b = 0.35, b = 0.21 and b = 0.16 — path coefficients, not effect sizes, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect). The authors note self-efficacy is important for students' persistence in the class and future career choices.

> "self-efficacy in physics 2 has direct effects from self-efficacy in physics 1 (b = 0.41), belonging (b = 0.35), peer interaction (b = 0.21), and a small effect from perceived recognition (b = 0.16)."
