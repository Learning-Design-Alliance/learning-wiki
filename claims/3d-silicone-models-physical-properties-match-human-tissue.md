## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Silicone model maximum tensile strengths (0.48 MPa stomach, 0.75 MPa intestine) were relatively close to reported human tissue values (0.5–0.8 MPa gastric; ~0.9 MPa intestine), while porcine models measured lower (0.14 and 0.25 MPa). [→ Xia Jianfu 2025](#xia-jianfu-2025)

## Evidence

### Xia Jianfu 2025

Xia Jianfu, Xia Minjun, Zhu ZhiHao, Wu Junjie, Mao Jinglei, Peng Xiaoping, Yu Shijie, Zhou Menghui, Amadi Sidney Moses, Chen Feng, Zhao Fei, Maimaitiming Maimaitiaili, Aili Aikebaier, Yang Wah, Abudureyimu Kelimu, Wang Zhifei. (2025). Printed simulation models versus organ models in laparoscopic Roux-en-Y gastric bypass training: a prospective validation study of efficacy, learning curve, and cost-effectiveness. BMC Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12893-025-03141-y

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Bench-top mechanical testing of model materials using a dual-column universal testing machine with a 10 kN load sensor, comparing silicone, human and porcine tissues (Fig. 1A-F). The authors conclude "the tensile strengths of silicone models are relatively close to those of human tissues" and note greater strain elongation reduces tearing risk.

> "Figures 1A-F present the maximum tensile strengths: 0.48 MPa (stomach) and 0.75 MPa (intestine) in silicone models, 0.82 MPa (stomach) and 0.52 MPa (intestine) in human models, and 0.14 MPa (stomach) and 0.25 MPa (intestine) in porcine models."

### Xia Jianfu 2025 (2)

Xia Jianfu, Xia Minjun, Zhu ZhiHao, Wu Junjie, Mao Jinglei, Peng Xiaoping, Yu Shijie, Zhou Menghui, Amadi Sidney Moses, Chen Feng, Zhao Fei, Maimaitiming Maimaitiaili, Aili Aikebaier, Yang Wah, Abudureyimu Kelimu, Wang Zhifei. BMC Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12893-025-03141-y

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Bench-top material characterization of 10% PVA/PAM hydrogel: conductivity measured with a digital four-probe tester (10 µA current), thermal conductivity by transient plane heat source, and rheology on an advanced rheometer at 25 °C (Fig. 1G-I). Conductivity "exceeds 1 s/m after 6 h of soaking"; thermal conductivity fell from 0.58 to 0.54 W·m-1·K-1 and viscoelasticity ranged from approximately 0.1 to 0.2.

> "It exceeds 1 s/m after 6 h of soaking, which meets the conductivity requirements of most human tissues"
