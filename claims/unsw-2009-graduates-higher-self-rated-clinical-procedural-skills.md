## Evidence

### Scicluna Helen A 2012

Scicluna Helen A, Grimm Michael C, O’Sullivan Anthony J, Harris Peter, Pilotto Louis S, Jones Philip D, McNeil H Patrick. (2012). Clinical capabilities of graduates of an outcomes-based integrated medical program. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-23

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cohort survey comparison of 2009 outcomes-based graduates with 2007 content-based graduates on the Clinical Capability Questionnaire three months into internship, analysed with independent t-tests. Graduates "rated themselves significantly more capable" on three subscales, with no difference on administrative tasks.

> "new program (2009) graduates rated themselves significantly more capable for the clinical (P < 0.001), procedural (P = 0.002), and operational management skills (P < 0.001) subscales, whereas there was no difference for the administrative tasks subscale (P = 0.126)"

### Scicluna Helen A 2012 (2)

Scicluna Helen A, Grimm Michael C, O’Sullivan Anthony J, Harris Peter, Pilotto Louis S, Jones Philip D, McNeil H Patrick. (2012). Clinical capabilities of graduates of an outcomes-based integrated medical program. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-23

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive self-ratings on the 46-task CCQ Part 1 three months into internship: clinical and procedural skills subscale means "were 4.1 (SD = 0.3) and 4.1 (SD = 0.4) respectively", which the article reads as good capability since 4 equates to unsupervised performance.

> "The mean scores of new program 2009 UNSW graduates for the clinical and procedural skills subscales were 4.1 (SD = 0.3) and 4.1 (SD = 0.4) respectively (Figure 1)"
