## Evidence

### Pettit RK 2015

Pettit RK, McCoy L, Kinney M, Schwartz FN. (2015). Student perceptions of gamified audience response system interactions in large group lectures and via lecture capture technology. BMC Medical Education 15:92. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0373-7

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Learning-domain items of the same 91-student end-of-year survey of gamified TP microbiology sessions, most questions being USMLE/COMLEX-style clinical vignettes. The article reports "at least 90 % of the respondents agreed or strongly agreed that they practiced applying theoretical knowledge to clinical scenarios" and the two companion items.

> "at least 90 % of the respondents agreed or strongly agreed that they practiced applying theoretical knowledge to clinical scenarios, prioritized the concepts they needed to review, and made connections among complex concepts"

### Pettit RK 2015 (2)

Pettit RK, McCoy L, Kinney M, Schwartz FN. (2015). Student perceptions of gamified audience response system interactions in large group lectures and via lecture capture technology. BMC Medical Education 15:92. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0373-7

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same survey's peer-learning item: "Eighty percent of the respondents agreed or strongly agreed that they learned valuable concepts from more knowledgeable peers during the games." The challenge item drew 71%, the lowest positive learning response; peer learning opportunities included results slides, single-clicker team competitions, and peer teaching slides.

> "Eighty percent of the respondents agreed or strongly agreed that they learned valuable concepts from more knowledgeable peers during the games."
