## Evidence

### Mokhtari Saba 2026

Mokhtari Saba, Mokhtari Asieh, Shariat Seyed Vahid, Nazeri Astaneh Ali, Shalbafan Mohammadreza. (2026). Innovative technologies in psychiatry education: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09573-1

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's cross-design comparison of its nine included studies. It attributes the discrepancy to greater susceptibility of non-randomized designs to bias, including confounding, lack of control groups, and reliance on self-reported outcomes.

> "Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) generally reported more conservative or mixed results, with some studies showing no significant differences between technology-based interventions and active comparators such as video-based learning. In contrast, non-randomized and uncontrolled before–after studies more consistently reported positive effects on knowledge, confidence, and skills."

### Mokhtari Saba 2026 (2)

Mokhtari Saba, Mokhtari Asieh, Shariat Seyed Vahid, Nazeri Astaneh Ali, Shalbafan Mohammadreza. (2026). Innovative technologies in psychiatry education: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09573-1

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's synthesis of comparator conditions across the nine studies, highlighting the need for further research comparing new tools with effective existing interventions rather than baseline methods.

> "Reviewing all the studies reveals that most studies with passive control groups, such as lectures and readings, show a clear benefit for educational technologies. Conversely, comparing active learning modalities, such as video tutorials, shows less impact on the use of innovative technologies in psychiatry education."
