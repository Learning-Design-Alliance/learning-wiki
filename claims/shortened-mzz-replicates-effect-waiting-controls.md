# The former waiting control group's scores rose from pre-test to post-test after a shortened 18-session MZZ program, in a pre-post comparison within that group (d = 2.5)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After the waiting control group received an abbreviated MZZ program (18 sessions over six weeks), repeated-measures ANOVA showed a statistically significant rise in the number of successfully solved EMBI tasks from pre-test to post-test within that group, F(1,3) = 12.36, p < .05, d = 2.5. [→ Hasselhorn and Linke-Hasselhorn 2013](#hasselhorn-and-linke-hasselhorn-2013)

## Evidence

### Hasselhorn and Linke-Hasselhorn 2013

Marcus Hasselhorn & Kathrin Linke-Hasselhorn. (2013). Fostering Early Numerical Skills at School Start in Children at Risk for Mathematical Achievement Problems: A Small Sample Size Training Study. International Education Studies, 6(3). http://dx.doi.org/10.5539/ies.v6n3p213

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Second assessment grid: the four former waiting-control children received a shortened MZZ version in which "some of the repetition units were thus either shortened or deleted". All four improved, with gains of 2 to 9 tasks, average increase 5.25, post mean M = 11.0, and a significant change effect, d = 2.5.

> "The repeated measurement analysis of variance on the change of the number of successfully solved EMBI tasks from pre- to posttest reveals a statistically significant effect of the change despite the low test power ( F(1,3) = 12.36, p < .05, d = 2.5)."
