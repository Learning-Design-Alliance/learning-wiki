# AI assistance reduces persistence: persistence costs concentrate among learners who used AI for direct solutions, not hints

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In randomized experiments, participants who lost AI access after brief AI-assisted practice solved fewer problems and gave up more often than those who never had AI assistance, with costs concentrated among direct-solution users. [→ H. Chad Lane 2026](#h-chad-lane-2026)

## Evidence

### H. Chad Lane 2026

H. Chad Lane. (2026). AIED's Unfinished Mission: Centering Agency and Motivation in the Age of Effortless Bypass. AIED 2026, CCIS 3032, pp. 502–516. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-29791-4_38

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The paper reports, citing Liu et al.'s randomized experiments, that brief AI-assisted practice reduced immediate engagement and carried over: those losing access "solved fewer problems and gave up more often" than never-assisted participants. Costs concentrated among direct-solution users; hint or clarification users showed no such impairments.

> "participants who lost access to AI afterward solved fewer problems and gave up more often than participants who never had AI assistance. Critically, these persistence costs were concentrated among learners who used AI to obtain direct solutions"
