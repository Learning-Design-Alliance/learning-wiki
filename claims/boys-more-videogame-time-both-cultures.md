# Boys in both Turkish and British samples spend more time playing videogames than girls

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Boys in both the Turkish and British samples spent significantly more time playing videogames than girls; Turkish boys also played traditional games more, but British boys and girls did not differ on traditional games. [→ Yılmaz 2022](#ylmaz-2022)

## Evidence

### Yılmaz 2022

Yılmaz, E., Yel, S., & Griffiths, M. D. (2022). Comparison of children's social problem-solving skills who play videogames and traditional games: A cross-cultural study. Computers & Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2022.104548

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Chi-square analyses of self-reported daily play time in the two samples found boys played videogames significantly more than girls in both cultures; the article reports "no significant difference was found among the British children (χ2=6.95, p>0.05)" for traditional games.

> "the boys in both the Turkish sample (χ2=54.79, p<0.001) and British sample (χ2=32.87, p<0.001) spent significantly more time playing videogames than girls."
