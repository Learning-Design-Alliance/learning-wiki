# Instructors hold that students should expect to troubleshoot, and that anticipation of the need to troubleshoot marks proficient students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Instructors hold that students should expect circuits involving more than one subsystem not to work, and that this anticipation is a desirable characteristic of experienced students. [→ Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer and H. J. Lewandowski 2016](#dimitri-r-dounas-frazer-and-h-j-lewandowski-2016)

## Evidence

### Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer and H. J. Lewandowski 2016

Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer and H. J. Lewandowski. (2016). Nothing works the first time: An expert experimental physics epistemology. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.020](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.020)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

In the results, instructor Birch says that when circuits "involve more than one subsystem" connecting them "together" reveals they "don't work," and that "[Students] should expect that"; the authors treat anticipation of the need to troubleshoot as a desirable characteristic.

> "as soon as they start making circuits that involve more than one subsystem, you connect them together and ﬁnd they don't work. [Students] should expect that."
