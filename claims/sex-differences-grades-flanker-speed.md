## Evidence

### Beckmann 2021

Beckmann, J.; Lang, C.; du Randt, R.; Gresse, A.; Long, K.Z.; Ludyga, S.; Müller, I.; Nqweniso, S.; Pühse, U.; Utzinger, J.; Walter, C.; Gerber, M. (2021). Prevalence of Stunting and Relationship between Stunting and Associated Risk Factors with Academic Achievement and Cognitive Function: A Cross-Sectional Study with South African Primary School Children. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 18(8), 4218. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18084218

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Sex-stratified ANOVAs in the cross-sectional sample (n = 1045 with grades). Table 1 prints η2 = 0.033 for end-of-year results; the quoted sentence reports the significance of the girls-versus-boys contrast.

> "Stratification by sex revealed significant differences with regard to overall academic achievement (end of year results, p < 0.001) and across all subjects (language, mathematics, and life skills; all p < 0.001), with girls achieving higher grades than boys."

### Beckmann 2021 (2)

Beckmann, J.; Lang, C.; du Randt, R.; Gresse, A.; Long, K.Z.; Ludyga, S.; Müller, I.; Nqweniso, S.; Pühse, U.; Utzinger, J.; Walter, C.; Gerber, M. (2021). Prevalence of Stunting and Relationship between Stunting and Associated Risk Factors with Academic Achievement and Cognitive Function: A Cross-Sectional Study with South African Primary School Children. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 18(8), 4218. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18084218

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Flanker task data (n = 1223) from the same baseline assessment. The article reports "faster reaction times were observed among boys" with similar accuracy; Table 1 prints η2 = 0.019 (congruent) and 0.018 (incongruent).

> "In the Flanker task, accuracy levels were similar in boys and girls. Nevertheless, faster reaction times were observed among boys, both for congruent and incongruent stimuli (p < 0.001)."
