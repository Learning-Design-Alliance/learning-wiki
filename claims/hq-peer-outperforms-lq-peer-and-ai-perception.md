## Evidence

### Morris 2026

Morris, C., & Maes, P. (2026). When Peers Outperform AI (and When They Don't): Interaction Quality Over Modality. [doi:10.22318/cscl2026.989526](https://doi.org/10.22318/cscl2026.989526)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Between-group comparison of survey measures in a remote study of 36 undergraduates learning graph theory, with median-split quality groups per condition. HQ-PP reported "the strongest collaborative experiences (M=4.85, SD=0.38)" with d=1.16 vs LQ-PP and d=2.10 vs AI.

> "High-quality peer-peer interactions (HQ-PP, n=13) reported the strongest collaborative experiences (M=4.85, SD=0.38), significantly outperforming both low-quality peer-peer collaborations (LQ-PP, n=11) (M=3.82, SD=1.25; t(22)=2.58, p=.017, d=1.16) and all AI conditions (M=3.10, SD=1.20; t(23)=4.53, p<.001, d=2.10)."
