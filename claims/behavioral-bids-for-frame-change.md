# A student's animated off-worksheet statements can function as bids for a frame change that the group may accept or ignore

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Momentary departures from the group's dominant behavioral cluster, such as a student speaking in unusually clear, original, animated speech while the group remains in the blue cluster, are interpreted as bids for a frame change; in the episodes analyzed the group accepted the bid the second time it was made. [→ Rachel E. Scherr and David Hammer 2007](#rachel-e-scherr-and-david-hammer-2007)

## Evidence

### Rachel E. Scherr and David Hammer 2007

Rachel E. Scherr and David Hammer. (2007). Student Behavior and Epistemological Framing: Examples from Collaborative Active-Learning Activities in Physics. Submitted to Cognition and Instruction. https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Student+Behavior+and+Epistemological+Framing

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative analysis of Kendra's anomalous statements in group A's episodes. The authors "identify her bid for a frame change based entirely on her behaviors," noting her peers likely read her statement as an implicit request for understanding, more consistent with discussion than worksheet framing.

> "We identify her bid for a frame change based entirely on her behaviors (e.g., she is briefly more animated). The content of her speech, however, is also similar in the two cases: she denies understanding the situation."
