# Judy's epistemological view of a real/ideal gulf was context-dependent: present when discussing conceptual problems, absent during equation-based problem-solving

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 studies · 3 qualitative `r2` · `q1`
> **Currency** · newest study 2013 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims

`q1 i?` Judy disparaged idealizing assumptions when discussing conceptual problems but showed no evidence of thinking about the real/ideal gulf while solving traditional problems, even though the equations encode the same idealizing assumptions. [→ Danielak 2010](#danielak-2010)

`q1 i?` Judy's view that a gulf separates real and ideal circuits is present when she discusses conceptual problems but absent in her discussions of and solutions to traditional equation-based problems. [→ Ayush Gupta 2010](#ayush-gupta-2010)

`q1 i?` Judy's real/ideal gulf stance appears when she discusses conceptual problems but is absent in her discussions of and solutions to traditional quantitative problems. [→ Ayush Gupta 2013](#ayush-gupta-2013)

## Evidence

### Danielak 2010

Danielak, B. A., Gupta, A., & Elby, A. (2010). Incorporating Affect in an Engineering Student's Epistemological Dynamics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1003.4037](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1003.4037)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative pattern analysis of the clinical interview: Judy saw the course as spending too much time on "ideal circuits and theoretical methods" unrelated to "a professional engineer's job," yet displayed positive affect during equation-based problem-solving with no evidence of the gulf view in those moments.

> "Her epistemological view about the real/ideal gulf is thus context-dependent: present when she discusses conceptual problems, but absent when she solves traditional ones."

### Ayush Gupta 2010

Ayush Gupta, Brian A. Danielak, Andrew Elby. (2010). Understanding students' difficulties in terms of coupled epistemological and affective dynamics. 40th ASEE/IEEE Frontiers in Education Conference. [doi:10.1109/fie.2010.5673256](https://doi.org/10.1109/fie.2010.5673256)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview analysis of Judy's four behavioral patterns, including a fat resistive wire equation prompt. The authors conclude her "epistemological view about the real/ideal gulf is thus context-dependent", noting the equations she uses encode the same idealizations she disparages elsewhere.

> "Her epistemological view about the real/ideal gulf is thus context-dependent: present when she discusses conceptual problems, but absent in both her discussions of and solutions to traditional ones."

### Ayush Gupta 2013

Ayush Gupta, Brian A. Danielak, Andrew Elby. (2013). Toward affect-inclusive models of cognitive dynamics: Coupling epistemological resources and emotions. Pre-print for arxiv.org, under review at Physical Review Special Topics – Physics Education Research. https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.1333

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Microanalysis of the interview transcript found that while discussing or solving equation-based problems Judy showed positive affect and "no evidence that in these moments she is ever thinking about the real/ideal gulf," even though those problems involve the same ideal circuits she disparages elsewhere. No effect sizes are reported.

> "Her epistemological view about the real/ideal gulf is thus context-dependent: present when she discusses conceptual problems, but absent in both her discussions of and solutions to traditional ones."
