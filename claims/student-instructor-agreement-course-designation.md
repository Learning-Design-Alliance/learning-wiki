## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Student and instructor course descriptions agreed well (Cohen's kappa = 0.794), and independent coders agreed very highly (kappa = 0.897), leading the authors to frame the LCAS as measuring lab course design rather than mere student perception. [→ Corwin Lisa A. 2015](#corwin-lisa-a-2015)

## Evidence

### Corwin Lisa A. 2015

Corwin Lisa A., Runyon Christopher, Robinson Aspen, Dolan Erin L. (2015). The Laboratory Course Assessment Survey: A Tool to Measure Three Dimensions of Research-Course Design. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-03-0073

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Qualitative content analysis of blind student and instructor descriptions of 16 biology lab courses at 11 US institutions; two authors coded each course as CURE, traditional, or ambiguous. Printed kappas were 0.897 (intercoder) and 0.794 (student-instructor).

> "We calculated a Cohen’s kappa of 0.897 as a measure of intercoder reliability, which is considered very good by conventional standards (Landis and Koch, 1977; Fleiss, 1981). We also calculated a Cohen’s kappa of 0.794 as a measure of student and instructor agreement, which is considered good by conventional standards."
