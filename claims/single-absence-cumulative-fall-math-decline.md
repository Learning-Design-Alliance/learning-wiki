# Each single prior-year absence is associated with a .010 to .016 SD decrease in fall mathematics starting achievement the following year

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Treating absences continuously, each prior-year absence predicted a small but cumulative decline in next-fall math achievement. [→ King 2020](#king-2020)

## Evidence

### King 2020

King, G., & Jensen, N. (2020). Looking Back: How Prior-Year Attendance Impacts Starting Achievement. (The Center for School and Student Progress at NWEA Working Paper). https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/looking-back-how-prior-year-attendance-impacts-starting-achievement/

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Equation 2 models fall-to-fall growth with lagged absences as a continuous variable, run overall and by grade and subject with school-clustered standard errors. Per-absence effect sizes were ".010 to .016 SDs" in math; the article illustrates an 18-day absent 4th grader declining .27 SD.

> "In mathematics, a single absence was associated with a decrease in starting achievement of between .010 to .016 SDs (see Table 5)."
