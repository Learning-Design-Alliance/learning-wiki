## Evidence

### Harb Gerlinde C. 2020

Harb Gerlinde C., Schultz Jon-Håkon. (2020). The nature of posttraumatic nightmares and school functioning in war-affected youth. PLoS ONE 15(11): e0242414. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0242414

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Baseline self-report questionnaire data from the 64 BLP-enrolled students. The study also reports an average sleep latency of 50 minutes, feeling rested in the mornings on average only once per week, and that only 20.3% could return to sleep after nightmares.

> "Students reported an average of 4.7 nights with nightmares per week (SD = 1.36, mode = 6) and an average duration of 3.1 years with a nightmare disturbance (SD = 1.5; mode = 3, range 1–7 years)"

### Harb Gerlinde C. 2020 (2)

Harb Gerlinde C., Schultz Jon-Håkon. (2020). The nature of posttraumatic nightmares and school functioning in war-affected youth. PLoS ONE 15(11): e0242414. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0242414

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Teacher-report questionnaire data on the same 64 students, collected on 10-point scales alongside ratings of concentration, happiness, and behavior problems. Teachers and students both reported impaired academic functioning, including difficulty concentrating and performing to capacity.

> "teachers noticed that approximately half (49%) of the students appeared sleepy in class at least once a week, with 27% of students appearing sleepy several times per week. Furthermore, they reported that 19% of students actually fell asleep in class at least once a week."
