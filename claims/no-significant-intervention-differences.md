# No supplemental intervention was significantly better than another, and gains were not tied to program type

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There was no main effect of tutoring condition and no time-by-condition interaction in the repeated measures MANOVA. [→ Sabatini John P. 2011](#sabatini-john-p-2011)
`q3 i?` The data do not support that any program was superior overall or that skill-specific gains mirrored instructional emphasis. [→ Sabatini John P. 2011 (2)](#sabatini-john-p-2011-2)

## Evidence

### Sabatini John P. 2011

Sabatini John P., Shore Jane, Holtzman Steven, Scarborough Hollis S. (2011). Relative Effectiveness of Reading Intervention Programs for Adults with Low Literacy. Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3269067/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated measures MANOVA of the 148 completers found "no main effect of tutoring condition, F(6, 140) = 0.20, p = .998" and no time-by-condition interaction, so gains did not depend on which of the three programs students received.

> "There was no main effect of tutoring condition, F(6, 140) = 0.20, p = .998, nor any interaction with time, indicating that gains were not conditioned on which instructional program students received."

### Sabatini John P. 2011 (2)

Sabatini John P., Shore Jane, Holtzman Steven, Scarborough Hollis S. (2011). Relative Effectiveness of Reading Intervention Programs for Adults with Low Literacy. Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3269067/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Discussion of research question 2 states "the data do not support the hypothesis that any of these programs was superior to any other overall" and that skill-area gains were not tied to program; MANOVA results for intervention type were not significant.

> "the data do not support the hypothesis that any of these programs was superior to any other overall, nor that gain in particular skill areas were tied to the instructional program. The MANOVA results for intervention type were not significant."
