## Evidence

### Shiels 2026

Shiels, L., Tomlin, D., Payne, J.M. et al. (2026). Benefits of classroom remote microphone technology for inattentive children. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00427-5

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Phase one RCT questionnaire analysis (n=35) using mixed effects models with device condition and timepoint as fixed variables. Table 2 reports student-rated LIFE-R Cohen's d of 1.02 and teacher-rated d of 0.58; the article reports "Both students (P < .001) and teachers (P = .010) reported improved student listening on the LIFE-R during device use".

> "Both students (P < .001) and teachers (P = .010) reported improved student listening on the LIFE-R during device use."

### Shiels 2026 (2)

Shiels, L., Tomlin, D., Payne, J.M. et al. (2026). Benefits of classroom remote microphone technology for inattentive children. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00427-5

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Same phase one questionnaire analysis. Table 2 reports parent-rated hyperactivity Cohen's d of -0.34 (p = 0.007), teacher-rated inattention d = -0.34 (p = 0.037), and teacher-rated hyperactivity d = -0.24 (p = 0.032). The article notes "Parents reported reduced hyperactivity (P = .007) but no change in inattention (P = .107)".

> "Teachers also noted reduced inattention (P = .037) and hyperactivity (P = .032) on the Conners-3."
