## Evidence

### James Wakefield & Jessica K. Frawley 2020

James Wakefield & Jessica K. Frawley. (2020). How does students’ general academic achievement moderate the implications of social networking on specific levels of learning performance? Computers & Education. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360131519302477?via%3Dihub

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regressions proxying reason-specific Facebook time by multiplying daily Facebook minutes by Likert extent/likelihood ratings. Study-related uses (e.g. "Ask a classmate for help in the class") showed significant negative coefficients for low WAM students (e.g. "–0.181***").

> "The relative time spent on a range of reasons for using Facebook has clear implications on student performance, for lower academic achievers, regardless of whether these uses are university study or non -study related ."
