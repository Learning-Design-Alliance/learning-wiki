## Evidence

### Simonsmeier 2022

Simonsmeier, B. A., Flaig, M., Deiglmayr, A., Schalk, L., & Schneider, M. (2022). Domain-specific prior knowledge and learning: A meta-analysis. Educational Psychologist. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2021.1939700

`q3 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Meta-analysis of 697 effect sizes from 69 studies on normalized knowledge gains; the printed effect size rNG+ = -.059 (95% CI [–.150, .034]) was below r = .10 and the confidence interval included zero, based on a large database of 697 effect sizes from 69 studies.

> "the metaanalytically derived mean effect size was very small (rNGþ ¼  -.059, 95% CI [–.150, .034]), and the 95% confidence interval included zero."

### Simonsmeier 2022 (2)

Simonsmeier, B. A., Flaig, M., Deiglmayr, A., Schalk, L., & Schneider, M. (2022). Domain-specific prior knowledge and learning: A meta-analysis. Educational Psychologist. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2021.1939700

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Prediction-interval analysis of the same rNG distribution (I² = 96.93). The article reports the "95% prediction interval (Riley et al., 2011) was large [–.688, .621]", showing strong variability in the effect of prior knowledge on learning.

> "the 95% prediction interval (Riley et al., 2011) was large [–.688, .621], indicating that, without additional information on moderators, it would be difficult to predict the outcomes of future studies."
