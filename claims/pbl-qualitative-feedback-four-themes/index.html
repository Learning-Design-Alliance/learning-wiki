Evidence¶

Manaf et al. 2011¶

Nor Aziah Abdul Manaf, Zuaini Ishak & Wan Nordin Wan Hussin. (2011). Application of Problem Based Learning (PBL) in a Course on Financial Accounting Principles. Malaysian Journal of Learning and Instruction: Vol. 8 (2011): 21-47. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1137300

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Qualitative analysis of open-ended survey responses from the 267 surveyed students. The authors categorized feedback into four themes and illustrate each with verbatim student comments. No effect sizes apply.

"We categorized these student feedback into four themes: PBL is an acceptable way of learning accounting; PBL is a challenging way of learning, but it offers great beneﬁts; PBL helps student to improve team work, communication and other soft skills; and PBL is only suitable for students with some basic accounting knowledge."

Manaf et al. 2011 (2)¶

Nor Aziah Abdul Manaf, Zuaini Ishak & Wan Nordin Wan Hussin. (2011). Application of Problem Based Learning (PBL) in a Course on Financial Accounting Principles. Malaysian Journal of Learning and Instruction: Vol. 8 (2011): 21-47. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1137300

q2 · i? · design · r2

Example of student feedback quoted under the fourth theme: students lacking accounting basics reported giving up easily and finding assignments hard, supporting the scope limit that PBL suits students with some basic accounting knowledge.

"This way of learning is not suitable for the students who don’t have any basic. They easily give up and hard for us to do any assignment."
