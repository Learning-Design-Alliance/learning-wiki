Evidence¶

Keith Weber 2013¶

Keith Weber, Matthew Inglis & Juan Pablo Mejia-Ramos. (2013). How mathematicians obtain conviction: Implications for mathematics instruction and research on epistemic cognition. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2013.865527

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports this study, citing Knuth, Choppin, and Bieda: approximately 400 middle school children completed six justification tasks and gave empirical justifications 36% to 81% of the time. The review attributes the Healy and Hoyles and Recio and Godino rates similarly; the reviewed designs are not described beyond this.

"Knuth, Choppin, and Bieda (2009) presented approximately 400 middle school children with six justification tasks in number theory and found that students provided empirical justifications (i.e., they checked that these assertions were true for specific examples) 36% to 81% of the time, depending upon the task."
