# Neural speech tracking of the lecturer's speech is stronger during high-interest lecture segments

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` The speech stimulus was reconstructed more accurately from EEG in high- vs low-interest segments (d=0.56), and TRF peaks were significantly modulated by interest level. [→ Levy 2025](#levy-2025)

## Evidence

### Levy 2025

Levy, O., Shadi, T., Korisky, A., Bleichner, M. G., & Zion Golumbic, E. (2025). Differential effects of external noise and situational interest on neurophysiological responses during video based learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00392-5

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

TRFs estimated separately for high- vs low-interest segments (n=32) showed early (~160ms) and late (~350ms) peaks larger for high interest and the ~200ms peak larger for low interest (all p's < 0.02, cluster-corrected), with printed decoding "Cohen’s d=0.56".

> "the speech stimulus could be reconstructed more accurately in the high vs. low interest condition [t(31)=3.17, p=0.003, Cohen’s d=0.56"
