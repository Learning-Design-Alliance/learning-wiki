## Evidence

### Casey Inez Canfield & Baruch Fischhoff & Alex Davis 2019

Casey Inez Canfield & Baruch Fischhoff & Alex Davis. (2019). Better beware: comparing metacognition for phishing and legitimate emails. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-019-09197-5

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Item-level analysis (Fig. 3a) plotting each email's actual difficulty against perceived difficulty across both experiments. The article concludes some phishing emails fooled participants while leaving them unaware they were being fooled.

> "for the phishing emails, the proportion correctly identified ranged from 15% to 85%. Thus, some were much more deceptive than others. There was little variance in participants ' mean confidence when evaluating individual emails, in either category (ranging from 80 to 90% confident)."
