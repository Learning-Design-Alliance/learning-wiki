# Differentiated-AI materials exceeded unified-AI materials on reading comprehension within each proficiency stratum, most strongly for high-proficiency learners (d = 1.40)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 studies · 3 causal `r2` · `q3` · `i0`–`i3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Within-proficiency planned contrasts showed differentiated-AI exceeded unified-AI on reading comprehension for low-proficiency learners by d = 0.60. [→ Gao X 2026](#gao-x-2026)
`q3 i0` For intermediate learners the differentiated-AI advantage over unified-AI was small (d = 0.16), with differentiated discourse cues adding little. [→ Gao X 2026 (2)](#gao-x-2026-2)
`q3 i3` For high-proficiency learners differentiated-AI exceeded unified-AI by d = 1.40, the strongest case for added value beyond the unified AI-adapted material. [→ Gao X 2026 (3)](#gao-x-2026-3)

## Evidence

### Gao X 2026

Gao X (2026) From unified to differentiated materials: generative AI–supported adaptation of EAP reading materials. Front. Psychol. 17:1887565. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1887565

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Planned within-stratum contrast on reading comprehension (Table 6) holding proficiency constant, comparing differentiated-AI with unified-AI. The low-proficiency difference was "1.56 points (13.23 vs. 14.79), d = 0.60", a moderate advantage attributed to local access support such as glosses and sentence unpacking.

> "For low-proﬁciency learners, diﬀerentiated-AI exceeded uniﬁed-AI by 1.56 points (13.23 vs. 14.79), d = 0.60."
