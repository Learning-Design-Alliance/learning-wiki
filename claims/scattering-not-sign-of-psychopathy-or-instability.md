# Neurotic, psychopathic and delinquent groups show no consistent excess of scattering, and great scattering cannot be elevated to a pathognomonic sign of psychopathy

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Wallin 1921

Wallin, J. E. W. (1921). Intelligence Irregularity as Measured by Scattering in the Binet Scale. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://psycnet.apa.org/journal/edu

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Group comparisons of 529 'unstable' subjects (155 neurotics, 22 psychopaths, 352 delinquents) against all subjects and normal groups on each scale, by average advance credits and by percentage passing over 10 advance tests. Results differ by scale and criterion; the author questions Mateer's use of scattering as an indication of psychopathy.

> "We have found some individuals among all types—normal, subnormal, feeble-minded, delinquent, neurotic, psychopathic—who scatter very greatly, and others who scatter very little."
