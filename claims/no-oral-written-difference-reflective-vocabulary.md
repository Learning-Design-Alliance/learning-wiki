# Oral and written output-based instruction do not differ significantly in effect on vocabulary learning of reflective EFL learners

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The reflective oral output group (M = 22.38) and reflective written output group (M = 23.23) did not differ significantly on the vocabulary posttest (p = .087); equivalence was not established, as the confidence interval included zero. ## Evidence

### Behnaz Rastegar & Fatemeh Safari 2017

Behnaz Rastegar & Fatemeh Safari. (2017). Output-Based Instruction, Learning Styles and Vocabulary Learning in the EFL Context of Iran. International Journal of Education & Literacy Studies, 5(2). http://dx.doi.org/10.7575/aiac.ijels.v.5n.2p.101

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Independent t-test between the reflective written output group (M = 23.23, SD = 1.47, N = 22) and reflective oral output group (M = 22.38, SD = 1.59, N = 21). The article notes the "negative 95 % lower confidence interval of -.10" means the difference can be zero; the fifth null hypothesis was supported.

> "The results of the independent t-test (t (41) = 1.80, p = .087, r = .270 representing a weak to moderate effect size) (Table 17) indicated that there was not any significant difference between the reflective oral and written output groups’ means on the posttest of vocabulary."
