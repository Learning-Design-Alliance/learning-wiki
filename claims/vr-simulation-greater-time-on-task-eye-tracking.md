# Students spend a significantly greater percentage of time on task in VR simulation than in desktop simulation

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Eye tracking showed students spent a significantly greater percentage of time focusing on the scenarios in VR than on desktop, a mean difference of 6.4% (5.1 to 7.7, p < 0.001). [→ Walls Risheka 2024](#walls-risheka-2024)

## Evidence

### Walls Risheka 2024

Walls Risheka, Nageswaran Priyanka, Cowell Adrian, Sehgal Tunav, White Thomas, McVeigh James, Staykov Stefan, Basett Paul, Mitelpunkt Daniel, Sam Amir H. (2024). Virtual reality as an engaging and enjoyable method for delivering emergency clinical simulation training: a prospective, interventional study of medical undergraduates. BMC Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-024-03433-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Eye tracking data (Tobii VR4 in Pico Neo 3 Pro Eye headsets) measured percentage of time students' gaze was within predefined areas of interest. Time on task was "6.4% higher (5.1 to 7.7, p < 0.001) for the VR technique" after regression adjustment.

> "For time on task measured using eye tracking, the percentage of time on task was 6.4% higher (5.1 to 7.7, p < 0.001) for the VR technique than for standard desktop."
