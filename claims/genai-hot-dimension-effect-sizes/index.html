Evidence¶

Zhao Yan 2025¶

Zhao Yan, Yue Yuhe, Sun Zhonghua, Jiang Qiang, Li Gangsheng. (2025). Does Generative Artificial Intelligence Improve Students’ Higher-Order Thinking? A Meta-Analysis Based on 29 Experiments and Quasi-Experiments. Journal of Intelligence. https://doi.org/10.3390/jintelligence13120160

q3 · i2 · quant-synthesis · r2

Subgroup meta-analysis across the three HOT dimensions (Table 4): problem-solving N = 16, ES = 0.745; critical thinking N = 20, ES = 0.691; creativity N = 23, ES = 0.444, all p < 0.001. The quote reports the problem-solving result; the other two dimension values are printed in the same results section.

"Gen-AI had the most pronounced impact on students’ problem-solving ability, with an effect size (ES) of 0.745, which falls within the moderately significant range (0.5 ≤ ES < 0.8) and is statistically significant (p < 0.001)"

  • Instructional method does not significantly moderate Gen-AI's effect on HOT, though project-based learning shows the largest subgroup effect (ES = 0.717) and lecture-based instruction a small non-significant effect (ES = 0.396) — related
  • Self-regulated learning ability significantly moderates Gen-AI's effect on HOT: high-SRL students show ES = 0.863 versus ES = 0.284 for low-SRL students — related
  • Educational level does not significantly moderate Gen-AI's effect on HOT, though K-12 subgroups show a larger effect (ES = 0.857) than post-secondary (ES = 0.539) — related
