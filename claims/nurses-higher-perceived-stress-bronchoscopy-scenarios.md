## Evidence

### Fosli CA 2025

Fosli CA, Sperling S, Jensen RD, Skaarup SH. (2025). In-situ simulation-based team training reduces incidence of negative events during bronchoscopy. A prospective educational intervention cohort study. Respiratory Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12931-025-03205-w

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Secondary Kirkpatrick level 2 analysis of questionnaire stress ratings by job function among the 13 trained staff, with Bonferroni-corrected secondary testing. Differences with 95% CIs and p-values are printed; no standardized effect size is reported.

> "Nurses had a higher perceived stress level than physicians if the patient was motoric agitated (difference 1.9 (95% CI 0.5–3.4), p = 0.01), if the patient had pneumothorax (difference 2 (95% CI 0.4–3.6), p = 0.02), and if the patient needed oral suction (difference 2 (95%CI 0.2–3.8),p = 0.03) during bronchoscopy."

### Fosli CA 2025 (2)

Fosli CA, Sperling S, Jensen RD, Skaarup SH. (2025). In-situ simulation-based team training reduces incidence of negative events during bronchoscopy. A prospective educational intervention cohort study. Respiratory Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12931-025-03205-w

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same questionnaire dataset comparing nurses' self-ratings with physicians' ratings of nurses' stress; significant in three named scenarios. No standardized effect size printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "The level of perceived stress on nurses was higher when assessed by themselves than when assessed by physicians in all scenarios and the difference reached the statistical significance level in the scenarios in which patients had severe cough (1.9 (95% CI 0.3–3.4), p = 0.02)"
