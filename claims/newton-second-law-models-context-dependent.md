## Evidence

### Salomon F. Itza-Ortiz 2003

Salomon F. Itza-Ortiz, N. Sanjay Rebello and Dean Zollman. (2003). Students' Models of Newton's Second Law in Mechanics and Electromagnetism. Pre-print, Kansas State University. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/25/1/011](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/25/1/011)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Phase I interview 1 used FCI-based horizontal (box pushed at constant speed) and vertical (elevator lifted at constant speed) contexts. Student S3 "used the Newtonian model for the horizontal context" but the Aristotelian model for the vertical context, a mixed model state.

> "This student used the Newtonian model for the horizontal context, but Aristotelian for the verti cal context; therefore, we say the student uses a mixed model state in mechanics contexts."

### Salomon F. Itza-Ortiz 2003 (2)

Salomon F. Itza-Ortiz, N. Sanjay Rebello and Dean Zollman. (2003). Students' Models of Newton's Second Law in Mechanics and Electromagnetism. Pre-print, Kansas State University. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/25/1/011](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/25/1/011)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Final Phase II interview on induction contexts (a loop pulled out of a uniform magnetic field; a rod moving on rails). The table shows "none of the students used the Aristotelian model on the induction context", though S7 and S10 shifted from Newtonian to hybrid.

> "Table 4 shows that none of the students used the Aristotelian model on the induction context."
