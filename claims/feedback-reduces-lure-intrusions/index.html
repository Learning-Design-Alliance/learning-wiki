Evidence¶

Andrew C. Butler and Henry L. Roediger III 2008¶

Andrew C. Butler and Henry L. Roediger III. (2008). Feedback enhances the positive effects and reduces the negative effects of multiple-choice testing. Memory & Cognition, 36(3), 604-616. https://doi.org/10.3758/MC.36.3.604

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Same 72-participant experiment, intrusion analysis on the forced-report cued recall test. Main effect of feedback condition ηp² = .25; no feedback yielded more lure responses than either feedback schedule, with printed effects d = .65 and d = .81; the article states both schedules were equally effective at reducing misinformation.

"there was a main effect o f feedback con dition [ F(2,138) 22.62, MSe 0.031, hp2 .25] in which no feedback produced a significantly higher proportion of intrusions than did the immediate feedback [t(71) 5.90, SEM 0.018, d .65, prep .99] and delayed feedback [t(71) 4.65, SEM 0.019, d .81, prep 1.00]"

Andrew C. Butler and Henry L. Roediger III 2008 (2)¶

Andrew C. Butler and Henry L. Roediger III. (2008). Feedback enhances the positive effects and reduces the negative effects of multiple-choice testing. Memory & Cognition, 36(3), 604-616. https://doi.org/10.3758/MC.36.3.604

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Supplementary t test against the no-test control condition, in which no lures had been shown. Feedback reduced intrusions to baseline, whereas no feedback exceeded it, d = .65.

"an additional t test confirm ed that the no-feedback condition produced a higher proport ion of in-trusions than did the no test contro l condition in which no lures had been shown [t(71) 4.49, SEM 0.019, d .65, prep .99]. Feedback on the multi ple-choice test reduced lure intrusions to this baseline level."
