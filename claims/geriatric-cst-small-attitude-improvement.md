# A brief geriatric clinical skills training produced a slight, non-significant improvement in medical students' overall attitudes toward elderly patients (UCLA-GAS median difference 2 points, P = 0.062)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students evaluated after the geriatric CST scored slightly higher on the UCLA-GAS overall scale than students evaluated before it, but the difference was not statistically significant (median difference 2, 95% CI 0–4, P = 0.062). [→ Goeldlin Adrian O 2014](#goeldlin-adrian-o-2014)

## Evidence

### Goeldlin Adrian O 2014

Goeldlin Adrian O, Siegenthaler Andrea, Moser André, Stoeckli Yvette D, Stuck Andreas E, Schoenenberger Andreas W. (2014). Effects of geriatric clinical skills training on the attitudes of medical students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-14-233

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-randomized before/after comparison of 147 first-clinical-year medical students (71 evaluated before, 76 after the CST), each completing the UCLA-GAS once. Median overall scale rose from 49 (IQR 44–53) to 51 (IQR 47–54); the "median difference of the UCLA-GAS overall scale was 2 points" with P = 0.062, not significant. No effect size printed.

> "The median difference of the UCLA-GAS overall scale was 2 points (95% CI 0 – 4, P = 0.062), indicating a higher score in students evaluated after the geriatric CST (Table 1)."
