Evidence¶

Piya-amornphan N 2020¶

Piya-amornphan N, Santiworakul A, Cetthakrikul S, Srirug P. (2020). Physical activity and creativity of children and youths. BMC Pediatrics. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12887-020-2017-2

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Kruskal-Wallis comparison of TCT-DP scores across the three age groups (n = 521, 487, 439) found scores "increased in accordance with the age group", medians 20, 21 and 24, p < 0.001; only medians and ranges are printed, no effect size.

"The total TCT-DP scores which indicated creativity ability in the present study ranged between 9 and 69 points, and the TCT-DP score increased in accordance with the age group (Table1)."

Related Claims¶

  • Active play correlates with creativity (TCT-DP score) in adolescents aged 14–17, but not in children aged 6–13 — related
  • The three physical activity indicators (active play, family/peers time, sedentary behavior) are all intercorrelated, with weak sedentary-behavior links — related
  • Regression analysis showed creativity score was not dependent on active play — related
  • School-aged children show reduced physical activity and increased sedentary behavior during the summer break across multiple countries
  • Active play is associated with time with family and peers in all age groups (r = 0.485) — related
  • Anthropometric variables, body composition and hand grip strength show no correlation with creativity components in this sample — related
  • Spending more time sedentary 1 to 2 hours after encoding is associated with better long-term memory retention, implying physical activity in that window is detrimental — related
  • A combined model of demographics, aerobic fitness, and the two critical activity windows accounts for over 30% of variance in long-term memory retention — related
