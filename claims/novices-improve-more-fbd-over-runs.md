## Evidence

### Joshua S. Cetron 2020

Joshua S. Cetron, Andrew C. Connolly, Solomon G. Diamond, Vicki V. May, James V. Haxby and David J. M. Kraemer. (2020). Using the force: STEM knowledge and experience construct shared neural representations of engineering concepts. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-020-0065-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

A linear mixed-effects model of FBD accuracy across runs in the same N = 31 sample found a "signi ﬁcant two-way interaction" of run by group, with novices improving 23.61% versus 7.55% for engineering students. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "There was also a signi ﬁcant two-way interaction (β = 0.05, SE = 0.01, p = 0.0005) in which novices improved more than engineering students over time ( Δeng = 7.55%, Δnov = 23.61%, t(29) = 3.47, p = 0.002)."

### Joshua S. Cetron 2020 (2)

Joshua S. Cetron, Andrew C. Connolly, Solomon G. Diamond, Vicki V. May, James V. Haxby and David J. M. Kraemer. (2020). Using the force: STEM knowledge and experience construct shared neural representations of engineering concepts. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-020-0065-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

At the fourth and final fMRI run, "both groups demonstrated mastery of the FBD task"; the group difference was no longer statistically significant, t(29) = 1.34, p = 0.19. Equivalence was not tested.

> "By run 4, both groups demonstrated mastery of the FBD task, and did not perform signiﬁcantly differently from each other ( t(29) = 1.34, p = 0.19)."
