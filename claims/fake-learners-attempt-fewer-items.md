## Evidence

### Alexandron 2019

Alexandron, G., Yoo, L. Y., Ruipérez-Valiente, J. A., Lee, S., & Pritchard, D. E. (2019). Are MOOC Learning Analytics Results Trustworthy? With Fake Learners, They Might Not Be! International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education, 29, 484–506. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-019-00183-1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The same 8.MReVx cohort comparison of item-attempt proportions found "fake learners trying less items" (p < 0.05). Medians: checkpoints 0.69 vs 0.75, quizzes 0.58 vs 0.64, homework 0.47 vs 0.48. The article notes the homework gap is smallest because homework combines high weight with a 'show answer' feature.

> "there is a clear difference between the groups, with fake learners trying less items. This is confirmed with a two-sided Mann-Whitney U test (nfake = 72, ntrue = 406, p.value < 0.05)."
