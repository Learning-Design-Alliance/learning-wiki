## Evidence

### J. D. Heilman 1916

J. D. Heilman. (1916). Psychology in the Schoolroom. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0073918

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article reports that psychology's most important contribution to school administration is methods for determining school-system efficiency, with retardation statistics first published in 1907 in The Psychological Clinic; no figures are printed.

> "The first statistics on pedagogical retardation were published in 1907 in the April number of The Psychological Clinic."

### J. D. Heilman 1916 (2)

J. D. Heilman. (1916). Psychology in the Schoolroom. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0073918

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article describes school surveys using standards or scales of measurement for quality of children's work, crediting psychology and the writings of Thorndike and Starch; it notes some superintendents call them worthless while conceding imperfection.

> "For the purpose of measuring the quality, of the children's work in some of the school branches, use has been made of standards or scales of measurement for the development of which education is indebted to psychology and the writings of such psychologists as Thorndike and Starch."
