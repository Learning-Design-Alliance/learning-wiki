# Age-related increases in dorsolateral PFC recruitment during encoding are most evident when tasks explicitly engage controlled processes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Studies with explicitly controlled encoding tasks found age-related increases in DLPFC recruitment predicting subsequent recollection, whereas incidental-encoding studies found no or limited age differences in lateral PFC. [→ Ghetti Simona; Bunge Silvia A 2012](#ghetti-simona-bunge-silvia-a-2012)

## Evidence

### Ghetti Simona; Bunge Silvia A 2012

Ghetti Simona; Bunge Silvia A. (2012). Neural Changes Underlying the Development of Episodic Memory During Middle Childhood. Developmental cognitive neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2012.05.002

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Review comparing four developmental fMRI encoding studies: Ofen et al. (2007) and Wendelken et al. (2011) used explicit instructions and found age-related DLPFC increases; Ghetti et al. (2010b) found increases only between 8 and 10 years, and Maril et al. (2010) found none from age 7 to 19. The article frames the task-engagement difference as a possibility, not a tested result.

> "it is possible that age-related differences in PFC activation at encoding are most evident when the encoding task explicitly engages controlled processes involved in episodic encoding."
