## Evidence

### Fu Jinbin & Zhao Fang 2026

Fu Jinbin & Zhao Fang. (2026). Artificial Intelligence Connectedness: Theoretical Reconstruction of Connectedness and Its Impacts on Adolescent Mental Health. Behavioral Sciences. https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16081393

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument in the article's ethical analysis section. The authors argue psychological authenticity: experiences of being understood through AI are real, citing a large-scale Replika user survey in which lonely students reported moderate to high perceived social support. They argue ethical asymmetry because 'AI's response does not contain genuine moral concern'.

> "artificial intelligence connectedness is a perceptual bond rather than a complete authentic caring relationship, defined as psychologically real yet ethically asymmetric."

### Fu Jinbin & Zhao Fang 2026 (2)

Fu Jinbin & Zhao Fang. (2026). Artificial Intelligence Connectedness: Theoretical Reconstruction of Connectedness and Its Impacts on Adolescent Mental Health. Behavioral Sciences. https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16081393

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument stating that whether risks materialize depends on multiple conditions including technological design, usage patterns, individual characteristics, and the social support environment. The article lists four dimensions of asymmetric risk: individual psychological, time allocation, power structure, and social market.

> "asymmetry itself does not imply harm, nor should it be directly regarded as a source of risk; it merely signals an ethical tension that warrants attention."
