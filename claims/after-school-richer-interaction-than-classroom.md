## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students compared the after-schools favorably to their regular mathematics classrooms, noting limited activity diversity in class and freedom to move around and consult peers in the after-school. [→ Maura Varley Gutiérrez 2011](#maura-varley-gutierrez-2011)

## Evidence

### Maura Varley Gutiérrez 2011

Maura Varley Gutiérrez, Craig Willey, & Lena L. Khisty. (2011). (In)equitable Schooling and Mathematics of Marginalized Students: Through the Voices of Urban Latinas/os. Journal of Urban Mathematics Education, 4(2), 26–43. http://education.gsu.edu/JUME

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative comparison drawn from student comments in both project sites. Marlena described being able to move around, check other pairs' answers, and ask 'what did you get?' in the after-school, unlike the normal class; facilitators asked questions rather than offering explanations.

> "When we asked students to compare the after-schools to their mathematics classrooms, students frequently noted how their regular classroom experiences were limited in the diversity and quality of activities."
