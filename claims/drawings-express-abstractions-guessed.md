# Students' game drawings expressed abstract scientific concepts and led teammates to guess correctly, including words the students had not seen in class

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students created drawings that encoded abstract science concepts — for example a wormhole from the Astronomy and Gravitation category — and their teammates guessed the words correctly. [→ M. A. M. Souza 2021](#m-a-m-souza-2021)
`q2 i?` Students also made themselves understood on content they had not seen in class, as with a creative drawing of electric field combining a soccer field with a lightning bolt. [→ M. A. M. Souza 2021 (2)](#m-a-m-souza-2021-2)

## Evidence

### M. A. M. Souza 2021

M. A. M. Souza, V. Dutra, T. B. Menezes, J. W. Silva. (2021). Drawing Science: an interdisciplinary, playful, and inclusive proposal. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2109.10748](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2109.10748)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Qualitative results-section analysis of a student's game drawing for an Astronomy and Gravitation card; the team "were able to easily guess" the word. The authors add the teacher should mediate by explaining the correct context afterwards.

> "The student not only managed to dr aw the wormhole, but the members of h is team were able to easily guess what it was about and got the word right."

### M. A. M. Souza 2021 (2)

M. A. M. Souza, V. Dutra, T. B. Menezes, J. W. Silva. (2021). Drawing Science: an interdisciplinary, playful, and inclusive proposal. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2109.10748](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2109.10748)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Results-section analysis of a student drawing of electric field that avoided textbook force-line diagrams; the student "used creativity" and a simple representation, and the team still got it right.

> "In figure 6 we have another interesting drawing in which another student managed to draw the word electric field. He could have used the usual diagrams seen in textbooks where the electric field is represented by means of force lines, only once again he used creativity"
