Evidence¶

Asy’ari 2019¶

Asy’ari, M., Ikhsan, M., & Muhali. (2019). The Effectiveness of Inquiry Learning Model in Improving Prospective Teachers’ Metacognition Knowledge and Metacognition Awareness. International Journal of Instruction, 12(2), 455-470. https://doi.org/10.29333/iji.2019.12229a

q2 · i? · causal · r1

ANOVA on n-gain across the three groups (knowledge: F = 30.838, p = .000; awareness: F = 25.963, p = .000) was used to measure consistency of the inquiry model's impact; the omnibus test showed significant between-group differences. All three classes received inquiry learning and there was no control class, so these are differences in pre-post gains between classes, not differences in an effect of inquiry learning. Only F and p printed.

"Table 6 shows that the score of significance (p) on prospective teachers’ metacognition knowledge and metacognition awareness in learning fluid is lower than alpha of the test (0.05), so that the impact of in quiry learning model as an attempt to increase subjects’ metacognition knowledge and awareness is significantly different in all three experimental groups (p < 0.05)."

Asy’ari 2019 (2)¶

Asy’ari, M., Ikhsan, M., & Muhali. (2019). The Effectiveness of Inquiry Learning Model in Improving Prospective Teachers’ Metacognition Knowledge and Metacognition Awareness. International Journal of Instruction, 12(2), 455-470. https://doi.org/10.29333/iji.2019.12229a

q2 · i? · causal · r1

LSD post-hoc tests on n-gain differences: knowledge differed among all group pairs (p < 0.05), but awareness did not differ between Group B and Group C (p > 0.05), indicating inconsistent impact of the inquiry model across groups.

"On the other hand, subjects’ metacognition awareness is significantly different between Group A and Group B (p < 0.05) and between Group A and Group C (p < 0.05). Therefore, Group B and Group C prospective teachers’ metacognition awareness are not significantly different (p > 0.05) at significance level of 5%."
