# Undergraduate math/physics double majors perceive mathematics as deductive, standardized, and highly structured, whereas physics is inductive, eclectic, and flexible

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students perceive mathematical proofs as highly structured with explicit logic, while physics proofs are free-form with jumps and gaps and physically motivated approximations. [→ Jeffrey M. Rabin 2024](#jeffrey-m-rabin-2024)
`q2 i?` Students report math classes follow a definition-theorem-proof structure while physics classes present math briefly as a tool and apply it without deep justification. [→ Jeffrey M. Rabin 2024 (2)](#jeffrey-m-rabin-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Jeffrey M. Rabin 2024

Jeffrey M. Rabin, Andrew Meyertholen, Brian Shotwell. (2024). Students' Experience of Cultural Differences Between Mathematics and Physics. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Thematic analysis of surveys and semi-structured interviews with 11 math/physics double majors or majors-with-minors at UCSD. Students described math proofs as structured while physics proofs "can contain jumps and gaps" and look more like calculations.

> "Students perceive mathematical proofs as highly structured, wit h the hypotheses and conclu- sion announced at the start and all the logic spelled out explicitly."

### Jeffrey M. Rabin 2024 (2)

Jeffrey M. Rabin, Andrew Meyertholen, Brian Shotwell. (2024). Students' Experience of Cultural Differences Between Mathematics and Physics. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview theme on classroom presentation: students said math topics are built up through definition/theorem/proof, whereas in physics "math is a means to an end" and new techniques may be presented and exemplified through applications without rigorous justification.

> "Students felt that topics in mathematics classes are developed tho roughly through the def- inition/theorem/proof structure, so that their properties are b uilt up gradually and fully justiﬁed."
