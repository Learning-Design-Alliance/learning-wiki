## Evidence

### Strain 2017

Phillip S. Strain. (2017). Four-Year Follow-Up of Children in the LEAP Randomized Trial: Some Planned and Accidental Findings. Topics in Early Childhood Special Education, 37(2), 121–126. https://doi.org/10.1177/0271121417711531

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Follow-up assessment of original RCT participants in both study arms, with assessors blind to prior study group membership and a 32% attrition rate evenly divided between arms. The blind assessment found Leiter means of 76 versus 93, "statistically significant, t(177) = 2.12, p = .05" with d = .42.

> "Leiter mean differences favoring LEAP graduates of 76 versus 93 were statistically significant, t(177) = 2.12, p = .05. Using Cohen’s d, effect size for this finding was .42."

### Strain 2017 (2)

Phillip S. Strain. (2017). Four-Year Follow-Up of Children in the LEAP Randomized Trial: Some Planned and Accidental Findings. Topics in Early Childhood Special Education, 37(2), 121–126. https://doi.org/10.1177/0271121417711531

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Teacher-rated SSRS prosocial measure at 4 years post, where higher scores represent greater perceived social skills. The follow-up testing found means of 31 versus 39.8 favoring LEAP participants, "statistically significant, t(180) = 2.99, p < .01" with d = .52.

> "Differences favoring LEAP participants (31 vs. 39.8) were statistically significant, t(180) = 2.99, p < .01. Again, using Cohen’s d, the effect size for this finding was .52."

### Strain 2017 (3)

Phillip S. Strain. (2017). Four-Year Follow-Up of Children in the LEAP Randomized Trial: Some Planned and Accidental Findings. Topics in Early Childhood Special Education, 37(2), 121–126. https://doi.org/10.1177/0271121417711531

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same blind follow-up battery found Kaufman means of 86 versus 95 favoring LEAP graduates that were "not statistically significant using a two-tailed t test"; the article likewise reports non-significant TOLD (81 vs 88) and Vineland (94 vs 98) differences. Equivalence was not tested.

> "Mean group differences on the Kaufman of 86 versus 95 in favor of LEAP graduates were not statistically significant using a two-tailed t test."
