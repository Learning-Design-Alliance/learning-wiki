## Evidence

### S. Küchemann 2020

S. Küchemann, P. Klein, H. Fouckhardt, S. Gröber, and J. Kuhn. (2020). Improving students' understanding of rotating frames of reference using videos from different perspectives. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010112](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010112)

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Distractor-type analysis of POE single-choice items in the same 21-student study, classifying four error types across the stationary and rotating frames. The article reports a "signiﬁcant medium eﬀect" for the error reduction from Predict to Observe, with Cohen's d = 0.64 and p < 0.05.

> "the average diﬀerence between the total number of errors in P redict (N = 3 .05 ± 1.40) and Observe (N = 2 .19 ± 1.29) exhibits a signiﬁcant medium eﬀect (Cohen's d = 0 .64, p < 0.05, see inset of Fig. 3a)"

### S. Küchemann 2020 (2)

S. Küchemann, P. Klein, H. Fouckhardt, S. Gröber, and J. Kuhn. (2020). Improving students' understanding of rotating frames of reference using videos from different perspectives. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010112](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010112)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Same distractor analysis, comparing Observe to Explain after instruction with a textbook introduction, two augmented photographs and six videos. The article reports a "signiﬁcant very large eﬀect" with Cohen's d = 1.18 and p < 0.001; after instruction 62% of students reported the trajectories correctly.

> "the average diﬀerence between the number of errors in Observe and Explain (N = 0 .81 ± 1.03) exhibits a signiﬁcant very large eﬀect (Cohen's d = 1 .18, p < 0.001)"
