# Professional development curriculum students scored higher on use of silence and frequency of interruptions, and showed a large increase in keeping the discussion relevant

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students receiving the professional development curriculum scored higher than traditional curriculum students on frequency of interruptions and use of silence (main effect of cohort). [→ Joekes K 2011](#joekes-k-2011)
`q2 i?` For ability to keep the discussion relevant, traditional curriculum students showed little change while professional development students showed a large increase (time-by-cohort interaction). [→ Joekes K 2011 (2)](#joekes-k-2011-2)

## Evidence

### Joekes K 2011

Joekes K, Noble LM, Kubacki AM, Potts HWW, Lloyd M. (2011). Does the inclusion of 'professional development' teaching improve medical students' communication skills? BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-11-41

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-hoc item-level repeated measures ANOVAs on the 82-student video-rated sample. Table 2 reports for use of silence Cohort: F1,80 = 7.5, p = 0.004 and for frequency of interruptions Cohort: F1,81 = 4.5, p = 0.036.

> "With frequency of interruptions and use of silence, there was a significant main effect of time, but also of cohort, with students receiving the professional development curriculum scoring higher."

### Joekes K 2011 (2)

Joekes K, Noble LM, Kubacki AM, Potts HWW, Lloyd M. (2011). Does the inclusion of 'professional development' teaching improve medical students' communication skills? BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-11-41

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Item-level repeated measures ANOVA for ability to keep discussion relevant in the same 82-student sample; Table 2 reports Interaction: F1,81 = 18.0, p < 0.001, with means rising from 2.70 to 3.69 in the professional development cohort.

> "For ability to keep the discussion relevant, there was a significant interaction, with students receiving the traditional curriculum showing little change between the two times, but students receiving the professional development curriculum showing a large increase."
