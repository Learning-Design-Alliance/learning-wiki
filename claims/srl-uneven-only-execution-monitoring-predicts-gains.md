Chen C and Liu T (2026) Impact of automated scoring on interpreting performance and self-regulated learning: evidence from a pedagogical experiment. Front. Psychol. 17:1867463. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1867463

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive analysis of the adapted SRL-SRS questionnaire from all 24 experimental-group students showed high self-reported engagement during execution and evaluation but comparatively low planning and emotional motivation. Comparisons are read as relative patterns, since subscales comprise different numbers of differently worded items.

> "Execution and Monitoring (M = 3.92, SD = 0.65) and Evaluation and Reflection (M = 3.87, SD = 0.57) approached 4.0, while Emotional Motivation ( M = 3.06, SD = 0.63) and Pre-learning Planning (M = 3.01, SD = 0.54) hovered around the scale midpoint"

### Chen C and Liu T 2026 (2)

Chen C and Liu T (2026) Impact of automated scoring on interpreting performance and self-regulated learning: evidence from a pedagogical experiment. Front. Psychol. 17:1867463. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1867463

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Pearson correlations between the four SRL dimensions and value-added scores found "Only Execution and Monitoring correlated significantly with score gains", with a printed correlation of r = 0.42. A multiple regression (28.3% of variance, F(4, 19) = 1.87, p = 0.157, non-significant overall) kept Execution and Monitoring the only significant predictor (B = 2.51, β = 0.591, p = 0.039).

> "Only Execution and Monitoring correlated significantly with score gains ( r = 0.42, p = 0.041, 95% CI [0.02, 0.70])."
