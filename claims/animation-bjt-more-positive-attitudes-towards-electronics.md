# Practical engineering students taught the transistor with computer animation express significantly more positive attitudes towards electronics than peers taught with static diagrams.

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After learning the transistor topic, the median of the animation group's attitude index was significantly higher than that of the control group, indicating significantly more positive attitudes towards electronics. [→ Gero 2014](#gero-2014)

## Evidence

### Gero 2014

Gero, A., Zoabi, W., & Sabag, N. (2014). Animation Based Learning of Electronic Devices. Advances in Engineering Education, 4(1), Winter 2014. https://journals.gmu.edu/aaa/issue/winter-2014

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mann-Whitney tests on Likert-like attitude questionnaires administered before and after learning the transistor topic in the same randomized study, chosen because "a normal distribution of the index cannot be assumed". The animation group's posttest median of 82 was significantly higher than the control group's 62, with p < 0.01.

> "There was no significant difference between the experimental group and the control group before learning the subject of the BJT, but afterwards – the median of the experimental group was significantly higher than that of the control group."
