# Pubertal development and female sex are each positively associated with reversal learning performance

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among underage participants, RL performance increases by 0.2 units with every unit increase in self-reported puberty. [→ Bamberg 2025](#bamberg-2025)
`q2 i?` Female sex is associated with higher RL performance (coeff = 0.05, p = 0.045) when added to a model with age. [→ Bamberg 2025 (2)](#bamberg-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Bamberg 2025

Bamberg, C., Weigelt, S. & Hagelweide, K. (2025). Reversal learning is influenced by cognitive flexibility and develops throughout early adolescence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00308-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Exploratory analysis of self-reported puberty scores in N = 75 underage participants found "With every unit increase in puberty, the RL performance increases by 0.2 units" (p = 0.04); self-reported puberty scores are, as the article notes, more imprecise than external medical assessments.

> "With every unit increase in puberty, the RL performance increases by 0.2 units (SE = 0.09, 95%CI = [0.008, 0.38],p = 0.04)."

### Bamberg 2025 (2)

Bamberg, C., Weigelt, S. & Hagelweide, K. (2025). Reversal learning is influenced by cognitive flexibility and develops throughout early adolescence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00308-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In a regression with sex and age together, "coeff female sex= 0.05; SE = 0.02, 95% CI = [0.001, 0.09], p = 0.045"; the article discusses that earlier maturation in females might explain the difference.

> "Sex as well as age show a signiﬁcant association with RL performance when adding them to the same regression model (coeff female sex= 0.05; SE = 0.02, 95% CI = [0.001, 0.09], p = 0.045; coeff age = 0.01; SE = 0.003, 95% CI = [0.01, 0.02],p < .001)."
