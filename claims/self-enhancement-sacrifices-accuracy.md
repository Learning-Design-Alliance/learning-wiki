## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Perceivers sacrifice accuracy when perceiving would threaten their self-esteem or self-concept, via biases such as self-serving biases, the spotlight effect, naïve realism, and confirmation bias. [→ Gehlbach 2018](#gehlbach-2018)

## Evidence

### Gehlbach 2018

Gehlbach, H., & Vriesema, C. C. (2018). Meta‐bias: A practical theory of motivated thinking. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9454-6

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument stating the second meta-bias. The authors align with Kunda's view that self-enhancing perceptions are viable so long as people can ground them in sensible explanations, and note this motive biases self-perceptions more frequently than the efficiency motive. No new empirical test is reported.

> "Second, we will also sacrifice accuracy if we feel compelled to protect, heal, maintain, or enhance our sense of self (Kunda, 1990)."
