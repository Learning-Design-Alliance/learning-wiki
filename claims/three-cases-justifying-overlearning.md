# Excess practice and over-learning are justifiable in three cases: changed mental sets, isolating a new element, and bonds of special practical importance

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Over-learning is desirable when a bond must operate under a changed mental set, when all other computation should be made easy except one new element being learned, and when a bond is of such practical use or facilitative value that it should exceed the ordinary strength criterion. [→ Thorndike 1921](#thorndike-1921)

## Evidence

### Thorndike 1921

Thorndike, E. L. (1921). The psychology of drill in arithmetic: The amount of practice. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 12(4). https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/edu/12/4

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical discussion with examples: operating 7X8 within long division; using easy divisors 2, 3, 4 when teaching averages so the pupil's energy is spent on the new facts; and driving bonds like the subtractions from 10 and the multiplication tables to greater strength for practical life use.

> "In certain cases, however, such excess practice and over-learning are actually desirable. Three cases are of special importance. The first is the case of a bond operating under a changed mental set or adjustment."
