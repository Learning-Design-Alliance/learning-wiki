## Evidence

### Lee V 2026

Lee V, Kuncel NR, Sackett PR. (2026). Graduate grade inflation at a U.S. research-intensive university: A 22-year longitudinal analysis. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0341315

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Theoretical argument in the introduction, grounded in signaling theory: grades serve as an observable indicator of student quality, and inflation removes their discriminating power. The article offers this as a reasoned argument, not a tested result; no effect size is printed.

> "However, grade increases not tied to genuine improvements in student performance create situations where high-performing and mediocre students receive indistinguishable grades (e.g., 4.0), rendering school grades useless as an information source and making it difficult to differentiate between them."
