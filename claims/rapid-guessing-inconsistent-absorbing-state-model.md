# Rapid-guessing behavior on an adaptive achievement test is inconsistent with an absorbing state model of unmotivated test taking

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After students began rapid guessing, most subsequent responses were still solution behaviors (81% after the first rapid guess, 67% after six), contradicting the absorbing state prediction of continued unmotivated responding. [→ Wise 2015](#wise-2015)

## Evidence

### Wise 2015

Wise, S. L., & Kingsbury, G. G. (2015). Modeling Student Test-Taking Motivation in the Context of an Adaptive Achievement Test. Paper presented at the annual meeting of the National Council on Measurement in Education, Chicago. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Modeling+Student+Test-Taking+Motivation+in+the+Context+of+an+Adaptive+Achievement+Test

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of rapid-guessing patterns in mathematics MAP CAT data from 285,230 students in grades 2-12, spring 2012-2013. The article reports that "After the occurrence of the first rapid guess, 81% of the remaining responses were solution behaviors; after 6 rapid guesses, 67% were solution behaviors," contrary to absorbing state predictions.
