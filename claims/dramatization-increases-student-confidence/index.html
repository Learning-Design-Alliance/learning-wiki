Evidence¶

Dowlati 2016¶

Dowlati, E., Musick, D. W., Zhang, L., Thornton, K., & Carvalho, H. (2016). Use of Dramatization to Teach Cardiac Cycle Physiology to Medical Students. Journal of Education and Training Studies. https://doi.org/10.11114/jets.v4i9.1603

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Confidence was rated on a five-category scale (insufficient, okay, good, very good, excellent) at pretest, posttest, and follow-up. The article reports a significant pretest-to-posttest confidence difference for both groups; the printed averages are descriptive with no effect size.

"For Group A, average confidence increased from 1.46 to 2.23, and for Grou p B, average confidence increased from 1.35 to 2.29."

Dowlati 2016 (2)¶

Dowlati, E., Musick, D. W., Zhang, L., Thornton, K., & Carvalho, H. (2016). Use of Dramatization to Teach Cardiac Cycle Physiology to Medical Students. Journal of Education and Training Studies. https://doi.org/10.11114/jets.v4i9.1603

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Cross-group confidence comparison: the article reports that "Group A students were more confident in their knowledge after participating in the drama than were Group B students who experienced lecture alone," with the printed p-value 0.04822 and no effect size.
