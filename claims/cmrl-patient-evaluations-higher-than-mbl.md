# Patients rated CMRL trainees higher than MBL trainees on satisfaction, medical ability, communication skills, and patient care after teaching

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Patient evaluations on trainees after teaching were significantly higher in the CMRL group than the MBL group on all four questionnaire items (e.g., communication skills 7.78 ± 1.02 vs. 7.19 ± 1.04, p = 0.019; patient care 8.69 ± 0.92 vs. 7.67 ± 1.59, p = 0.001). ## Evidence

### Li Min 2025

Li Min, Liu Tao, Wen Yiqiong, Zhang Min, Zhu Wenye, Wang Xuming, Hou Yunjiao, Li Lin, Zu Anju, Sun Shibo. (2025). Combination of mentor-based and resident-based learning may benefit to improving the ability of clinical practice in resident training. BMC Medical Education 25:197. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06798-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Patient questionnaires (0–10 per item; KMO 0.781, Cronbach's α 0.878) collected before and after teaching in the RCT. All four between-group post-teaching comparisons favored CMRL with printed p-values; no effect sizes were printed.

> "scores of CMRL group in patient's evaluation on the trainees after teaching were significantly higher than those of the MBL group (satisfaction with trainees 8.81 ± 0.92 scores vs. 7.89 ± 1.55 scores, p = 0.003; medical ability of trainees 8.42 ± 1.08 scores vs. 7.75 ± 1.54 scores, p = 0.037; communication skills 7.78 ± 1.02 scores vs. 7.19 ± 1.04 scores, p = 0.019; patient care 8.69 ± 0.92 scores vs. 7.67 ± 1.59 scores, p = 0.001; respectively)"
