## Evidence

### Chen Yingqian 2019

Chen Yingqian, Zheng Keguo, Ye Shanshan, Wang Jifei, Xu Ling, Li Ziping, Meng Quanfei, Yang Jianyong, Feng Shi-Ting. (2019). Constructing an experiential education model in undergraduate radiology education by the utilization of the picture archiving and communication system (PACS). BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1827-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of 101 fourth-year medical students (38 experiential, 61 control) after one semester. The experiential group scored significantly higher on short-answer diagnosis questions; the article reports no effect size, only means and p < 0.05.

> "The experiential education group demonstrated significantly higher scores than the control group ((7.4 ± 1.3 and 6.7 ± 1.5, respectively, p < 0.05)."

### Chen Yingqian 2019 (2)

Chen Yingqian, Zheng Keguo, Ye Shanshan, Wang Jifei, Xu Ling, Li Ziping, Meng Quanfei, Yang Jianyong, Feng Shi-Ting. (2019). Constructing an experiential education model in undergraduate radiology education by the utilization of the picture archiving and communication system (PACS). BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1827-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Final examination total scores among 99 attending students (38 experiential, 61 control) showed no statistically significant difference; equivalence was not tested. Baseline GPA was comparable (U = 1240.5, p = 0.614).

> "The average score of the experiential education group was 81.5 ± 10.3, which was not significantly different from the control group (79.2 ± 7.5, p > 0.05)"

### Chen Yingqian 2019 (3)

Chen Yingqian, Zheng Keguo, Ye Shanshan, Wang Jifei, Xu Ling, Li Ziping, Meng Quanfei, Yang Jianyong, Feng Shi-Ting. (2019). Constructing an experiential education model in undergraduate radiology education by the utilization of the picture archiving and communication system (PACS). BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1827-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Sub-domain analysis of the same final examination found no significant difference in multiple-choice question scores between the experiential and control groups; equivalence was not tested.

> "there were no significant differences in the scores for multi-choice questions (MCQs) between the two groups. (34.0 ± 4.8 in the experiential education group and 33.0 ± 3.4 in the control group, p > 0.05)"
