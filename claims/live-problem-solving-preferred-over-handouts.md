## Evidence

### P. Klein 2021

P. Klein, L. Ivanjek, M.N. Dahlkemper, K. Jeličić, M.-A. Geyer, S. Küchemann, and A. Susac. (2021). Studying physics during the COVID-19 pandemic: Student assessments of learning achievement, perceived effectiveness of online recitations, and online laboratories. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010117](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010117)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive analysis of the recitation-format items (Fig. 3), in which 401 recitation attendees marked the formats they experienced (multiple choice) and their optimal format (single choice). The article reports students "are less interested in handouts of solutions, but wish to work together live on exercise sheets"; no statistical test was applied.

> "However, comparing the relative counts within each data series, it can be observed that students are less interested in handouts of solutions, but wish to work together live on exercise sheets."

### P. Klein 2021 (2)

P. Klein, L. Ivanjek, M.N. Dahlkemper, K. Jeličić, M.-A. Geyer, S. Küchemann, and A. Susac. (2021). Studying physics during the COVID-19 pandemic: Student assessments of learning achievement, perceived effectiveness of online recitations, and online laboratories. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010117](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010117)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same descriptive comparison of experienced versus optimal recitation formats among recitation attendees. The article reports that "forums are perceived to be ineﬀective for the recitations", based on relative counts rather than a statistical test.

> "In line with that result, we observe that forums are perceived to be ineﬀective for the recitations."
