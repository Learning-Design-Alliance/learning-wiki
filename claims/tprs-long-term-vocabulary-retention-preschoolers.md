## Evidence

### Kara and Eveyik-Aydin 2019

Ketevan Kara, Evrim Eveyik-Aydın. (2019). Effects of TPRS on Very Young Learners' Vocabulary Acquisition. Advances in Language and Literary Studies, 10(1). https://doi.org/10.7575/aiac.alls.v.10n.1p.135

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Delayed posttests administered two weeks after each weekly cycle with the same 19 children. The article reports receptive means "remained the same or even increased"; no statistical test was applied.

> "The results presented in Table 1 also confirm the long-term effects of the TPRS treatment on receptive knowledge of L2 vocabulary, as the mean scores remained the same or even increased."

### Kara and Eveyik-Aydin 2019 (2)

Ketevan Kara, Evrim Eveyik-Aydın. (2019). Effects of TPRS on Very Young Learners' Vocabulary Acquisition. Advances in Language and Literary Studies, 10(1). https://doi.org/10.7575/aiac.alls.v.10n.1p.135

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Delayed productive posttest data from Table 2 for the same 19 participants. The article attributes the slight productive decrease to possible natural vocabulary attrition under complete non-encounter, while retention percentages stayed high.

> "Even the most challenging item for retention - lettuce – was produced by 63% of the participants (see Table 2), which is still a considerable improvement in comparison with productive pretest results."
