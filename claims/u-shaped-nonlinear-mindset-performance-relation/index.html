Evidence¶

Melody M. Chao 2025¶

Melody M. Chao, Allen H. Huang, Anirban Mukhopadhyay & Janghoon Shon. (2025). Divergent effects of mindsets on performance trajectories. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00355-w

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Regressions of Final CGA and Term GPA on mindset and mindset-squared showed positive squared terms (e.g., Term GPA B = 0.015, p = 0.002), with vertices slightly left of the mean; Fig. 2 depicts "the most growth and the most ﬁxed mindsets outperform" the middle. The effect persisted at course level.

"students with the most growth and the most ﬁxed mindsets outperform those with neutral or slightly ﬁxed (and even slightly growth) mindsets. This U-shaped relationship remains statistically signiﬁcant even at the course level after controlling for course ﬁxed effects (B = 0.016, SE = 0.003, p < 0.001, Column 6)"

Melody M. Chao 2025 (2)¶

Melody M. Chao, Allen H. Huang, Anirban Mukhopadhyay & Janghoon Shon. (2025). Divergent effects of mindsets on performance trajectories. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00355-w

q3 · i? · associational · r2

The authors report that "62.62% of the students fall between+/− 1scale point of the vertex", the region associated with the poorest performance across individual, term, and course levels. This is a descriptive proportion, not an effect size.

"In our study, 62.62% of the students fall between+/− 1scale point of the vertex. Our ﬁndings reveal that it is exactly this neglected majority that might deserve more attention as they are the ones with the poorest performance"
