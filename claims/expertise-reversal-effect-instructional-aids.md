# Expertise reversal effect: instructional aids effective for low-knowledge learners hinder high-knowledge learners across all three layers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Instructional aids beneficial for novices became ineffective or negative for performance and mental effort as expertise increased, and fading-out prompts produced higher learning outcomes than permanent prompts. [→ Joachim Wirth 2020](#joachim-wirth-2020)

## Evidence

### Joachim Wirth 2020

Joachim Wirth, Ferdinand Stebner, Melanie Trypke, Corinna Schuster, & Detlev Leutner. (2020). An Interactive Layers Model of Self-Regulated Learning and Cognitive Load. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09568-4

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Narrative review attributing this to Kalyuga et al. (1998) longitudinal studies on the interaction of expertise levels and instructional formats. The review also cites Nückles et al. (2010), whose fading-out prompts group outperformed the permanent prompts group.

> "instructions that were beneficial for novice learners (Exp. 1) became ineffective or even had negative consequences on performance and mental effort (Exp. 3) when learners' expertise increased"
