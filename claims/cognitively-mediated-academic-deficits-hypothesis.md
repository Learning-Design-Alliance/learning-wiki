## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Deficits in reading and numeracy after bushfire exposure may be mediated by trauma-related disruption of neuropsychological skills such as working memory, speed of processing, and executive functions. [→ Gibbs 2019](#gibbs-2019)

## Evidence

### Gibbs 2019

Gibbs, L., Nursey, J., Cook, J., Ireton, G., Alkemade, N., Roberts, M., Gallagher, H. C., Bryant, R., Block, K., Molyneaux, R., & Forbes, D. (2019). Delayed Disaster Impacts on Academic Performance of Primary School Children. Child Development. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.13200

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r3`

This is the authors' interpretive hypothesis in the Discussion, drawing on literature that trauma and PTSD impair working memory, processing speed, rapid automatized naming, and executive functions. The article offers no direct test of this mediation in the present study.

> "it is possible to hypothesize that the deficits in reading and numeracy demonstrated by the children in this study may be cognitively mediated (either directly or indirectly though the development of PTSD)"
