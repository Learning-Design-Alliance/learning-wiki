# Gestures and bodily actions lighten cognitive load and extend mathematical ideas across group members during collaboration

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Bodily actions such as gestures act as material scaffolding that affords distribution of cognition across modalities, lightens cognitive load, and allows collaborative extension of mathematical ideas over multiple bodies. [→ Juliene Madureira Ferreira 2021](#juliene-madureira-ferreira-2021)

## Evidence

### Juliene Madureira Ferreira 2021

Juliene Madureira Ferreira. (2021). What If We Look at the Body? An Embodied Perspective of Collaborative Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09607-8

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes, citing Goldin-Meadow and colleagues and Roth's review, the claim that gestures facilitate conservation of cognitive resources to improve explanation-task performance. It also reports Walkington et al. (2019) finding learners use collaborative gestures to extend mathematical reasoning across group members.

> "studies argue that such bodily actions carry a property of material scaffolding, as it affords a distribution of cognition across different modalities (for a review see Roth, 2003), lightning cognitive load (Goldin-Meadow et al., 2001)"
