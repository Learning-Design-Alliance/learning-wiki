Evidence¶

de Koning 2025¶

de Koning, B., Zhang, S., & Sepp, S. (2025). Integrating Human Movement in Learning: Advancements in Language Instruction, Multimedia, and Theory. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10027-1

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports the eye-tracking study by Park et al. (2023) comparing tracing, pointing, and no hand movements while learning about the human heart. The review states pointing "led to superior comprehension and was associated with increased attentional focus and cognitive activity." No effect size is printed.

"The results replicated previous findings, demonstrating the benefits of hand movements, particularly pointing, which led to superior comprehension and was associated with increased attentional focus and cognitive activity."

Related Claims¶

  • In a three-group eye-tracking experiment, the pointing group significantly outperformed both the tracing group and the no-touch group on comprehension performance — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Tracing and pointing during a paper-based multimedia lesson improves comprehension test performance in Chinese-speaking adults — related
  • The reviewed tracing studies consistently confirm learning benefits of tracing across retention, comprehension, and transfer measures, though cognitive-load hypotheses were not confirmed in all studies — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Pointing and tracing gestures improved retention and comprehension over no gesturing (review attributes this to Macken & Ginns, 2014) — related
  • Mimicking tracing and pointing gestures on a touchscreen during timed multimedia worked examples hindered adolescent learners' geometry learning — related
  • Pointing outperformed tracing on the terminology test, while neither touch-based group differed significantly from the no-touch group on terminology — related
  • Observing typical gestures significantly benefits narrative comprehension compared with atypical or no gestures, which do not differ significantly — related
