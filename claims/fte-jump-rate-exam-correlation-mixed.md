# FTE jump rate correlates negatively with exam performance in one study but only marginally in another

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the Fall 1997 Physics 152 study, FTE jump rates correlated significantly with exam performance (r = -0.67; first-half jump rates r = -0.59), while in the Spring 1999 Physics 151 study the evidence was statistically marginal after removing an outlier. [→ Ian D. Beatty and William J. Gerace 2005](#ian-d-beatty-and-william-j-gerace-2005)

## Evidence

### Ian D. Beatty and William J. Gerace 2005

Ian D. Beatty and William J. Gerace. (2005). Probing physics students' conceptual knowledge structures through term association. Originally published 2000. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0508133](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0508133)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Fall 1997 Physics 152 study of 18 students (one removed for missing an exam), jump rates plotted against average exam score. In the Spring 1999 study (16 subjects), r = -0.23 overall and r = -0.46 first-half, but removing one outlying point gave r = 0.16 and r = -0.09, which the article calls "statistically marginal."

> "The correlation of coefficient for jump rates versus exam performance is r = –0.67, and for first-half jump rates versus exam performance is r = –0.59, where ±0.13 is the threshold for statistical significance with 17 data points."
