# Eliciting confidence ratings impairs rule-based category learning compared to both no-rating and judgment-of-learning conditions on ambiguous transfer items

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a pre-registered experiment (N = 710), the confidence rating group performed significantly worse on ambiguous transfer items than both the JOL group and the no-rating group. [→ Kit S. Double 2025](#kit-s-double-2025)

## Evidence

### Kit S. Double 2025

Kit S. Double, Micah B. Goldwater, Damian P. Birney. (2025). Reactivity to confidence ratings: evidence of impaired rule-learning. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09413-5

`q3 · i?`

Pre-registered mixed ANOVA on the full sample (N = 710; 235 confidence rating, 251 no-rating, 224 JOL) of a 3 (condition) × 3 (item type) design. The interaction was significant, and "Pairwise comparisons indicated that the only significant differences were for the ambiguous transfer items, where the confidence rating group performed significantly worse than both the JOL group and the no-rating group."

> "There was also a main effect of condition, F(2,707) = 3.41, p =.034, ηp 2 = 0.010 and a significant interaction between condi- tion and item type, F(4,1414) = 2.63, p =.033, ηp 2 = 0.007, see Table 3 for group differences and pairwise comparisons."
