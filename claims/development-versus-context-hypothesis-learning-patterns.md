# Learning-pattern differentiation and integration increase with educational progression within one type of education, while context change induces temporary diffuse patterns

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Within one type of education, learning components become more differentiated and interrelated as students progress; entering a new type of education produces a disorganized factor pattern explained by the context hypothesis. [→ Vermunt 2004](#vermunt-2004)

## Evidence

### Vermunt 2004

Vermunt, J. D. & Vermetten, Y. J. (2004). Patterns in Student Learning: Relationships Between Learning Strategies, Conceptions of Learning, and Learning Orientations. Educational Psychology Review. https://dspacelibrary.uu.nl/handle/1874/11958

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review synthesizing ILS factor studies across school phases (Klatter 1995; Roosendaal and Vermunt 1996; Vermetten et al. 1999a; Severiens 1997). The review concludes the development hypothesis holds "within one type of education" while context explains differences between types. No effect sizes printed.

> "It seems that the development hypothesis generally holds true for stu- dents progressing within one type of education, but that the context hypoth- esis is necessary to explain the different factor structures between different types of education."
