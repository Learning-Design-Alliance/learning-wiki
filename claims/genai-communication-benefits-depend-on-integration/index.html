Evidence¶

Wei 2026¶

Wei, Y., and Perkins, M. (2026). Generative AI and student collaboration: a scoping review of group work processes, outcomes, and risks. International Journal for Educational Integrity. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40979-026-00217-x

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports Yeon et al.'s (2024) 40-minute experiment with 44 university students randomly assigned across three team methods, finding interface-dependent communication benefits.

"When AI agents are directly embedded into group communication platforms, such as chatbots in chat rooms, they enhance communication more effectively than external tools, such as standalone websites (Yeon et al. 2024)."

Wei 2026 (2)¶

Wei, Y., and Perkins, M. (2026). Generative AI and student collaboration: a scoping review of group work processes, outcomes, and risks. International Journal for Educational Integrity. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40979-026-00217-x

q3 · i? · review · r2

The review reports Lin et al.'s (2024) randomised controlled trial finding that AI-supported groups "streamline discussions and allocate more attention to cognitive tasks" relative to human-only teams, while cautioning over-reliance may reduce the need for communication.

"AI-sup - ported groups can streamline discussions and allocate more attention to cognitive tasks compared to human-only teams that devote greater effort to maintaining communica - tion and building a shared understanding"
