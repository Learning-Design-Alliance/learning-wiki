# A post-rotation formative OSCE increased interns' satisfaction with direct observation and feedback for calling consults, obtaining informed consent, and responding to nursing pages, but not for providing sign-out

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Interns' perceptions of receiving sufficient direct observation and feedback significantly increased pre- to postsession for calling a consult, obtaining informed consent, and responding to nursing pages, with no significant difference for providing sign-out. ## Evidence

### Wick Alexandra MD 2026

Wick Alexandra MD, Murray-Bainer Samantha MD, Tischendorf Jessica MD MS, Almasry Mazen MBBS, Coyle Andrew MD, Zamanian Maryam MD MS. (2026). Development and Implementation of an OSCE for Formative Assessment of Core Clinical Skills in Internal Medicine Interns. MedEdPORTAL. https://doi.org/10.15766/mep_2374-8265.11576

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre-post survey analysis of 35 internal medicine interns after a 4-station OSCE, using t tests on 5-point Likert ratings. Satisfaction with observation and feedback rose significantly for three skills, e.g. "calling a consult (mean 3.69 to 4.15, p < .01)"; the largest effect size was for informed consent (Hedges' g 1.261).

> "The interns’ perceptions of receiving sufficient direct observation and feedback significantly increased from pre- to postsession, in calling a consult (mean 3.69 to 4.15, p < .01), obtaining informed consent (mean 3.0 to 4.0, p < .01), and responding to nursing pages (mean 3.41 to 4.06, p < .01)."
