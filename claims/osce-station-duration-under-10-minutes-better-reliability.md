# When OSCEs have 5-10 stations, duration per station under 10 minutes yields significantly better internal consistency (alpha = 0.88) than 10 or more minutes (alpha = 0.64)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Within the 5-10 station subgroup, OSCEs with station durations under 10 minutes achieved higher internal consistency (alpha = 0.88, 95% CI: 0.86-1.00) than those with durations of 10 or more minutes (alpha = 0.64, 95% CI: 0.59-0.69). [→ Peng Qiyun 2025](#peng-qiyun-2025)

## Evidence

### Peng Qiyun 2025

Peng Qiyun, Luo Jiani, Wang Chunyan, Chen Lei, Tan Shenglan. (2025). Impact of station number and duration time per station on the reliability of Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE) scores: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06691-0

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Subgroup meta-analysis restricted to studies with 5-10 stations, comparing duration <10 min versus >=10 min. The under-10-minute group reached the highest pooled alpha (0.88) in the review, attributed by the authors to reduced examiner and participant fatigue.

> "when the station number were set to 5-10, internal consistency was significantly better with duration time under 10 min (Cronbach's alpha = 0.88, 95% CI: 0.86, 1.00) compared to durations exceeding 10 min per station (Cronbach's alpha = 0.64, 95% CI: 0.59, 0.69)."
