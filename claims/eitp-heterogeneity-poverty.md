# Low-poverty schools benefited more from the evaluation pilot than high-poverty schools, in both math and reading

## Evidence

### Matthew P. Steinberg and Lauren Sartain 2015

Matthew P. Steinberg and Lauren Sartain. (2015). Does Teacher Evaluation Improve School Performance? Experimental Evidence from Chicago's Excellence in Teaching Project. Association for Education Finance and Policy. https://doi.org/10.1162/EDFP_a_00173

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Heterogeneity analysis (table 10) of the randomized comparison: the printed year 1 interaction effect sizes are −0.091 standard deviations (significant at the 5 percent level) in math and −0.099 (significant at the 5 percent level) in reading. The article reports "the differential effect of the pilot persisted even after the ﬁrst year."

> "lower-poverty schools (those that are one standard deviation below the mean share of students receiving free or reduced-price lunch) improved by 0.09 standard deviations in math and 0. 10 standard deviations in reading beyond the pilot effect at schools with average poverty level."
