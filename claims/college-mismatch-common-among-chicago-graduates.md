# College mismatch is common among Chicago graduates: only 38 percent of the most qualified students enrolled in a match college, and many mismatch by enrolling in two-year colleges or not enrolling

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Only 38 percent of Chicago students with access to very selective colleges enrolled in a match college, and only 27 percent of students with access to selective colleges enrolled in a selective or very selective college. [→ Jenny Nagaoka 2008](#jenny-nagaoka-2008)
`q2 i?` About 62 percent of Chicago graduates attended a college whose selectivity level was below the level to which they would most likely have been accepted given their qualifications. [→ Jenny Nagaoka 2008 (2)](#jenny-nagaoka-2008-2)

## Evidence

### Jenny Nagaoka 2008

Jenny Nagaoka, Melissa Roderick, and Vanessa Coca. (2008). Barriers to College Attainment: Lessons from Chicago. The Consortium on Chicago School Research at The University of Chicago / Center for American Progress. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/barriers-college-attainment-lessons-chicago

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

The mismatch analysis compared the selectivity of colleges Chicago students were eligible to attend, based on ACT scores, GPAs, and coursework, against the Barron's-rated selectivity of colleges in which they enrolled (Figure 4). Only 16 percent of students with access to selective colleges matched.

> "At the end of senior year, only 38 percent of students with highest qualifications enrolled in a match college, that is, a very selective college."

### Jenny Nagaoka 2008 (2)

Jenny Nagaoka, Melissa Roderick, and Vanessa Coca. (2008). Barriers to College Attainment: Lessons from Chicago. The Consortium on Chicago School Research at The University of Chicago / Center for American Progress. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/barriers-college-attainment-lessons-chicago

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

The same comparison of accessed versus enrolled selectivity found the dominant mismatch pattern was enrolling in two-year colleges or not enrolling at all, rather than attending a four-year college slightly below match.

> "Across all, about two-thirds (62 percent) of students attended a college whose selectivity level was below the kinds of colleges to which they would have most likely been accepted given their level of qualifications."
