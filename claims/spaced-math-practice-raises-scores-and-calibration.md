# Spacing mathematics practice across weeks raised Year 7 students' test scores a month later and made their score predictions more accurate than massed practice

Spaced practice here means distributing the problems on one concept across several assignments rather than massing them in one, the choice a textbook or course makes when it arranges practice sets.

## Evidence

### Emeny et al. 2021

Emeny, W. G., Hartwig, M. K., & Rohrer, D. (2021). Spaced mathematics practice improves test scores and reduces overconfidence. *Applied Cognitive Psychology, 35*(4), 1082–1089. [doi:10.1002/acp.3814](https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.3814)

`q3 · two classroom experiments` · `i? · the abstract prints no effect size` · `n=not stated in the abstract` · `causal · r?`

Two classroom experiments with Year 7 students (11–12 years old) compared [spaced practice](../elements/spaced-learning.md) (three practice sessions one week apart) with massed practice (one session) on the same mathematics problems, followed one month later by a test; students also predicted their test scores. Spaced practice produced higher scores in both experiments, and score predictions were relatively accurate after spaced practice but grossly overconfident after massed practice. This entry was written from the published abstract: the full text was not available to this ingest, so sample sizes, assignment of students or classes to conditions, effect sizes and the authors' stated limitations are not recorded here. The study's materials and results files are on OSF (https://osf.io/vcf6e/).

## Discussion

The overconfidence finding matters for design because learners who mass their practice may judge it to have worked and choose it again. The abstract does not say whether the topics, the timing of the last practice session relative to the test, or the total practice time were equated, so read the size of the benefit from the full article.
