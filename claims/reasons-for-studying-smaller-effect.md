# Problem-driven learning shows significantly smaller effects on students' reasons for studying than on other motivation constructs

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r?` · `q4`

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` In the CHE model, effects on students' reasons for studying were statistically significantly lower than effects on other motivation constructs, with only a trivial positive effect (d < 0.20) for reasons. [→ Wijnia 2024](#wijnia-2024)

## Evidence

### Wijnia 2024

Wijnia, L., Noordzij, G., Arends, L. R. A., Rikers, R. M. J. P., & Loyens, S. M. M. (2024). The Effects of Problem-Based, Project-Based, and Case-Based Learning on Students' Motivation: a Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09864-3

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Correlated and Hierarchical Effects model with robust variance estimation (ρ = 0.60 assumed) across motivation constructs. The CHE model found effects on reasons for studying "statistically significantly lower compared to effects on other motivation constructs"; the three-level test of moderators was not significant, F(4, 283) = 2.08, p = 0.084.

> "results of the CHE model indicated that the effect of problem-driven learning on students' reasons for studying was statistically significantly lower compared to effects on other motivation constructs."
