## Evidence

### Conlin 2019

Conlin, L. D., Kuo, E., and Hallinen, N. R. (2019). Nothing's plenty: The significance of null results in physics education research. Preprint. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020104](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020104)

`q3 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Replication experiment with 136 undergraduates in an algebra-based physics course, randomly given a diagram prompt (n=66) or no prompt (n=70); "no difference in correctness by condition" across the two force problems.

> "However, unlike Heckler's study, there was no difference in correctness by condition, with accuracy across conditions on problem 1 at 38% and accuracy on problem 2 at 55%."

### Conlin 2019 (2)

Conlin, L. D., Kuo, E., and Hallinen, N. R. (2019). Nothing's plenty: The significance of null results in physics education research. Preprint. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020104](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020104)

`q3 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Process-measure analysis in the same replication found solution type affected accuracy: "the formal Newton's 2nd law approach was less accurate than informal alternatives", with most incorrect formal solutions incomplete.

> "In the replication study, it was found that solution type used affected accuracy, where the formal Newton's 2nd law approach was less accurate than informal alternatives."
