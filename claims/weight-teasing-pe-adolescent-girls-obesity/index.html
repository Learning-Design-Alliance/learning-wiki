Evidence¶

Martin A 2017¶

Martin A, Booth JN, McGeown S, Niven A, Sproule J, Saunders DH, Reilly JJ. (2017). Longitudinal Associations Between Childhood Obesity and Academic Achievement: Systematic Review with Focus Group Data. Current Obesity Reports, 6(3), 297–313. https://doi.org/10.1007/s13679-017-0272-9

q2 · i? · qualitative · r?

Exploratory qualitative focus group study with four obese adolescent girls (aged 12–15 years) and their parents/guardians (4 dyads) following a weight management programme. "PE was reported to be an environment where the girls were strongly exposed to body weight-related teasing."

"PE was reported to be an environment where the girls were strongly exposed to body weight-related teasing. Some girls also felt ignored and excluded from activities by PE teachers."

Learner Variables¶

Related Claims¶

  • Obesity is associated with lower maths attainment in adolescent girls but not younger girls or boys
  • Psychosocial factors (bullying, interpersonal skills) and executive cognitive functions may mediate the obesity–achievement association
  • Group formation via the captain technique, soccer dominance in free play, content repetition and public anthropometric exposure generated fear, anger, sadness and disgust, especially among girls — related
  • Discriminatory teacher attitudes, including sexism and fat-phobia, were identified as negative emotional triggers in participants' PE narratives — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
