## Evidence

### Horton William B. 2019

Horton William B., Law Sidney, Darji Monika, Conaway Mark R., Kubiak Nancy T., Kirby Jennifer L., Thigpen S. Calvin. (2019). Construction and preliminary evaluation of the inpatient glycemic control questionnaire (IGCQ): a survey tool assessing perceptions and knowledge of resident physicians. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1657-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

CTT analysis with Kruskal-Wallis tests on multicenter data (Table 4): PGY1 mean 18.2 (SD 3.1), PGY2 19.5 (2.5), PGY3-4 20.0 (2.3), p = 0.006. The article reports "comfort with management scores increased as PGY increased".

> "Specifically, comfort with management scores increased as PGY increased (i.e., resident physicians grow more comfortable managing inpatient glycemic control as they progress through training)."

### Horton William B. 2019 (2)

Horton William B., Law Sidney, Darji Monika, Conaway Mark R., Kubiak Nancy T., Kirby Jennifer L., Thigpen S. Calvin. (2019). Construction and preliminary evaluation of the inpatient glycemic control questionnaire (IGCQ): a survey tool assessing perceptions and knowledge of resident physicians. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1657-0

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Same CTT analyses on the barriers scale found no differences (gender p = 0.276, program p = 0.67, PGY p = 0.169). For comfort, the gender comparison gave p = 0.020 with male mean 19.6 (2.5) vs female 18.5 (3.0).

> "CTT analyses using Kruskal-Wallis test indicated no difference in performance by gender, program, or PGY for the “barriers to IGC” scale."
