### Hapnes Toba 2024

Hapnes Toba, Oscar Karnalim, Meliana Christianti Johan, Terutoshi Tada, Yenni Merlin Djajalaksana, and Tristan Vivaldy. (2024). Inappropriate Benefits and Identification of ChatGPT Misuse in Programming Tests: A Controlled Experiment. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-51979-6_54](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-51979-6_54)

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Likert item Q15 in the student-perspective survey: 47% disagree and 18% strongly disagree that ChatGPT code is easily understood by novices, despite the code being perceived as helpful.

> "From the answers to Q15, most of the participants (47%) do not agree and strongly do not agree (18%) that generated code of ChatGPT will be easily understood by newbies or those who are not proficient in programming."

### Hapnes Toba 2024 (2)

Hapnes Toba, Oscar Karnalim, Meliana Christianti Johan, Terutoshi Tada, Yenni Merlin Djajalaksana, and Tristan Vivaldy. (2024). Inappropriate Benefits and Identification of ChatGPT Misuse in Programming Tests: A Controlled Experiment. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-51979-6_54](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-51979-6_54)

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Likert item Q16: participants, most with tutoring experience, majority-agreed that non-proficient students would use ChatGPT-generated code; the article states these facts suggest "reconfirmation of submitted assignments would be important".

> "However, as teaching assistants, they have majority agreements that most non-proficient students would use ChatGPT-generated codes to fulfill their assignments (Q16), i.e., 29% strongly agree and 41% agree."
