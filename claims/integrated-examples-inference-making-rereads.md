# Integrated worked examples elicited more correct inferences and more solution-linked rereads than split-source examples

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The integrated group generated more correct inferences than the split-source group, mainly transformations of inconsistent into consistent language statements. [→ Mwangi 1998](#mwangi-1998)
`q2 i?` The integrated group made a significantly greater proportion of solution-linked rereads than the split-source group. [→ Mwangi 1998 (2)](#mwangi-1998-2)

## Evidence

### Mwangi 1998

Wangari Mwangi and John Sweller. (1998). Learning to Solve Compare Word Problems: The Effect of Example Format and Generating Self-Explanations. Cognition and Instruction, 16(2). https://www.jstor.org/stable/3233720

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of tape-recorded self-explanation protocols in Experiment 2, coded with an eight-category scheme (interrater reliability .897 by Scott's procedure), found "The integrated group generated more correct inferences than the split-source group, t(17) = 1.73, p = .05".

> "The integrated group generated more correct inferences than the split-source group, t(17) = 1.73, p = .05."

### Mwangi 1998 (2)

Wangari Mwangi and John Sweller. (1998). Learning to Solve Compare Word Problems: The Effect of Example Format and Generating Self-Explanations. Cognition and Instruction, 16(2). https://www.jstor.org/stable/3233720

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Reread-context analysis of Experiment 2 protocols showed the integrated group's rereads were more often solution-linked, "x2(1, N = 182) = 3.95, p < .05", suggesting the split-source group had acquired an inadequate understanding of the worked solutions.

> "The integrated group made a significantly greater proportion of solution-linked rereads (61%) than isolated rereads (39%) as compared to the split-source group (47% and 53%, respectively), x2(1, N = 182) = 3.95, p < .05."
