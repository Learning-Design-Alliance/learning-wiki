## Evidence

### Unggi Lee 2026

Unggi Lee, Jiyeong Bae, Jaehyeon Park, Haeun Park, Taejun Park, Younghoon Jeon, Sungmin Cho, Junbo Koh, Yeil Jeong, Gyeonggeon Lee. (2026). Rewarding How Models Think Pedagogically: Integrating Pedagogical Reasoning and Thinking Rewards for LLMs in Education. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2601.14560](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2601.14560)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quantitative categorization of thinking content into Schoenfeld's Explore, General, and Verify phases across conditions; the article reports the Explore ratio decreasing from 1.66% to 0.75% when thinking reward is added. Descriptive percentages only; no standardized effect size.

> "Ped. Think Reward shows the lowest Explore ratio (0.75%) and highest General ratio (81.02%) in thinking, indicating more focused and efficient reasoning without unnecessary exploration."

### Unggi Lee 2026 (2)

Unggi Lee, Jiyeong Bae, Jaehyeon Park, Haeun Park, Taejun Park, Younghoon Jeon, Sungmin Cho, Junbo Koh, Yeil Jeong, Gyeonggeon Lee. (2026). Rewarding How Models Think Pedagogically: Integrating Pedagogical Reasoning and Thinking Rewards for LLMs in Education. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2601.14560](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2601.14560)

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Qualitative analysis of turn-level and sentence-level labeled reasoning traces; the authors interpret the pattern as "responsive teaching" with instructional planning followed by monitoring and adaptive regulation, contrasting with fragmented intent in non-thinking and thinking-only conditions.

> "tutor turns exhibit the characteristics of responsive teaching, going beyond simple immediate reactions to show that the teacher notices student thinking and orchestrates the lesson to provide appropriate learning opportunities accordingly"
