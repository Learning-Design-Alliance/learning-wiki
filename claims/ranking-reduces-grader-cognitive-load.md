# Ranking and pairwise comparison reduce graders' cognitive load relative to numeric scoring, with limits when submissions are similar or numerous

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students found ranking less cognitively demanding than scoring because it eliminates interpreting an absolute grading scale (Raman et al.; Moffat et al.). [→ Paul 2025](#paul-2025)

## Evidence

### Paul 2025

Paul, U., Mantravadi, A., Shah, J., Shah, S., Mylavarapu, S. V., Rashid, M. P., & Gehringer, E. (2025). Scaling Success: A Systematic Review of Peer Grading Strategies for Accuracy, Efficiency, and Learning in Contemporary Education. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11677

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Raman et al. and Moffat et al., that students found ranking easier than judging against an abstract universal standard, reducing cognitive burden. Perceptual/qualitative finding; no effect size.

> "Raman et al. [31] highlighted that ranking reduces the cognitive burden by eliminating the need to interpret an absolute grading scale. Moffat et al. [30] echoed this, noting that students found it easier to compare peer work relative to one another rather than judge against an abstract universal standard."

### Paul 2025 (2)

Paul, U., Mantravadi, A., Shah, J., Shah, S., Mylavarapu, S. V., Rashid, M. P., & Gehringer, E. (2025). Scaling Success: A Systematic Review of Peer Grading Strategies for Accuracy, Efficiency, and Learning in Contemporary Education. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11677

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes to Raman et al. that pairwise comparison "significantly ease[d] the grading burden" and to Zhang et al. that pairwise ranking let novice reviewers reach accuracy "comparable to expert lecturers". No effect size is printed.

> "Raman et al. [31] found this approach to significantly ease the grading burden, while Zhang et al. [91] reported that pairwise ranking enabled even novice student reviewers to achieve scoring accuracy comparable to expert lecturers."

### Paul 2025 (3)

Paul, U., Mantravadi, A., Shah, J., Shah, S., Mylavarapu, S. V., Rashid, M. P., & Gehringer, E. (2025). Scaling Success: A Systematic Review of Peer Grading Strategies for Accuracy, Efficiency, and Learning in Contemporary Education. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11677

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Nakayama et al.'s item response theory study, that a "5-point grading scale outperformed a 10-point scale" on complexity while maintaining effectiveness. No effect size is printed.

> "Nakayama et al. [85] demonstrated that a 5-point grading scale outperformed a 10-point scale, reducing complexity while maintaining grading effectiveness."
