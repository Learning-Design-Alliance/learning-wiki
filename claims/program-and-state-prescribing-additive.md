## Evidence

### Burk-Rafel J 2026

Burk-Rafel J, Park H, Cornwell M, Holmboe ES, Yamazaki K, Santen SA, Park YS, Andrews JS, Hogan SO, Richardson J. (2026). Residency Competency Ratings and Early-Career Physician Antibiotic Prescribing. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.34365

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Unadjusted cross-classification of residency program quintiles with practice-state quintiles of antibiotic prescribing rates (eFigure 6). State-level contrasts alone were 8.3 and 12.8 more prescriptions per 100 beneficiaries for highest- vs lowest-quintile states.

> "Internal medicine and family medicine PCPs who graduated from programs with the highest prescribing rate and went on to practice in states with the highest prescribing rate wrote 19.2 (95% CI, 17.1-21.3; P < .001) and 22.2 (95% CI, 20.9-23.6; P < .001) more antibiotic prescriptions per 100 beneficiaries"
