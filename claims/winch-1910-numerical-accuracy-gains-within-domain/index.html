Evidence¶

W. H. Winch 1910¶

W. H. Winch. (1910). Accuracy in school children. Does improvement in numerical accuracy 'transfer'? The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Winch+accuracy+school+children

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Practice-medium analysis in the School I girls' class comparing marks from the 1st and 2nd practice exercises with the 5th and 6th; Table X section averages rose across sections, and the author reports "a very consid- erable improvement (approximately 21 '/<.)" in numerical accuracy.

"There can be no doubt that, on the whole, a very consid- erable improvement (approximately 21 '/<.) in numerical ac- curacy has taken place."

W. H. Winch 1910 (2)¶

W. H. Winch. (1910). Accuracy in school children. Does improvement in numerical accuracy 'transfer'? The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Winch+accuracy+school+children

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Numerical-accuracy comparison in School B (Table VII) of the practised versus non-practised groups on preliminary and final problem tests; percentage comparisons favoured the practised group in every section, though the author cautions the estimate is unsatisfactory unless the groups attempted equal numbers of problems.

"after the practice period, the position of the two groups, sec- tion by section, is reversed. Nearly all the sections appear to have gone down; a study of the possible numerical units and of the problematic difficulty in the preliminary and final tests show this to be appearance only."
