Proceedings of the 2026 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI '26). https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791139

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Propensity score matching (nearest-neighbor with mahalanobis) on 1,262 learners, 273 per matched group, with linear regressions controlling demographics and Week 1 performance. "the difference also not significant (𝑏=− 0.065, 𝑝=. 492)"; the authors say differences due to assessment had largely diminished by the final exam.

> "on the final exam, Leb Lango learners scored 56% versus 61% for English learners, with the difference also not significant (𝑏=− 0.065, 𝑝=. 492)"

### Phenyo Phemelo Moletsane 2026 (2)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Second PSM comparison (Leb Lango vs Hybrid), matched samples of 273 learners each; standardized mean difference decreased from 0.371 to 0.003. The final-exam gap "was not statistically significant (𝑏=− 0.107, 𝑝=. 245)".

> "On the final examination, Leb Lango learners scored 56% versus 60% for Hybrid learners, though this difference was not statistically significant (𝑏=− 0.107, 𝑝=. 245)"

### Phenyo Phemelo Moletsane 2026 (3)

Phenyo Phemelo Moletsane, Michael W. Asher, Christine Kwon, Paulo F. Carvalho, and Amy Ogan. (2026). Inclusive Mobile Learning: How Technology-Enabled Language Choice Supports Multilingual Students. Proceedings of the 2026 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI '26). https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791139

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the Leb Lango vs English matched comparison, "Leb Lango learners scored significantly lower than English learners on the practice assessments"; weekly tests showed a similar non-significant trend (58% vs 62%, b=−0.146, p=.064). The authors note Week 1 baseline and schooling were stronger predictors than language.

> "Leb Lango learners scored significantly lower than English learners on the practice assessments (54% vs. 60%; 𝑏=− 0.240, 𝑝<. 01)"
