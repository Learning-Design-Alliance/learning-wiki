## Evidence

### Ayush Gupta 2013

Ayush Gupta, Andrew Elby, Luke D. Conlin. (2013). How substance-based ontologies for gravity can be productive: A case study. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.10.010113](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.10.010113)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Transcript evidence from the Episode 1 small-group discussion. Lynn states she knows objects fall together yet feels "gravity's still pulling the heavier thing down." The authors' grammatical analysis codes gravity as an active external noun participant, an agent metaphor within a substance/matter ontology.

> "Even though I know that it's going to fall at the same time, I feel that deep down in my heart, gravity's still pulling the heavier thing down"

### Ayush Gupta 2013 (2)

Ayush Gupta, Andrew Elby, Luke D. Conlin. (2013). How substance-based ontologies for gravity can be productive: A case study. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.10.010113](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.10.010113)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

The authors' predicate-analysis coding of the same Episode 1 transcript identifies substance attributes applied to gravity, including "less, more, and amount of" as quantity markers and "borrow" as an absorb marker, supporting a substance-based ontology.

> "A predicate analysis of Lynn`s and Daniel`s reasoning reveals multiple categories of substance attributes: less, more, and amount of as markers of the quantity predicate; use and takes as markers of the consume predicate; and borrow as a marker of the absorb predicate."
