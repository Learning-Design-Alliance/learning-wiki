Evidence¶

Noguchi 2024¶

Noguchi, Y. (2024). Harmonic memory signals in the human cerebral cortex induced by semantic relatedness of words. npj Science of Learning, 9:6. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00221-1

q3 · i0 · causal · r2

Experiment 1 EEG analysis of 32-sensor data (n = 34) during delay 5: "signiﬁcant reductions in Sem compared to NonSem trials" in IPI-SD over posterior temporal cortex contralateral to the cued hemifield; e.g., T5 t(33) = −4.54, p = 0.00007, d = −0.15. Survived FDR correction and ANCOVA controlling amplitude.

"the SD of IPIs, a measure for irregularity of EEG waveforms, showed signiﬁcant reductions in Sem compared to NonSem trials (Fig.4c) both when memory items were presented in left and right visualﬁelds"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Semantically associated words induce similar distributions of inter-peak intervals: semantic structure is represented in IPIs, not in oscillation amplitude, and not explained by visual word similarity — related
  • Semantic integration of memorized words induces false memory: false-alarm rates are higher to semantically related lure probes than to unseen new probes — related
  • Semantic integration of memory words inhibits the time-related increase in oscillatory irregularity (IPI-SD) from early to late retention periods — related
  • Semantic relatedness accelerates oscillation speed (reduced mean IPI / increased central frequency) only when memory words are memorized in the right visual field — related
