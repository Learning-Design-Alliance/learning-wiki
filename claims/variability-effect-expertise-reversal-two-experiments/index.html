In Experiment 1, low variability instructional materials enhanced learning regardless of level of instructional guidance (worked examples vs. unguided problem-solving); in Experiment 2, a disordinal aptitude-treatment interaction indicated an expertise reversal effect, with low prior knowledge students who solved problems with low variability outperforming those with high variability, and the opposite pattern for higher prior knowledge students. → Paul Ginns & Jimmie Leppink 2019

Evidence¶

Paul Ginns & Jimmie Leppink 2019¶

Paul Ginns & Jimmie Leppink. (2019). Special Issue on Cognitive Load Theory: Editorial. Educational Psychology Review, 31, 255–259. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09474-4

q2 · i? · review · r2

Two experiments investigating variability in instructional task features combined with instructional guidance (Experiment 1) and learner expertise (Experiment 2), summarized in the editorial. The editorial reports a "disordinal aptitude-treatment interaction" and the quoted expertise reversal contrast. No effect size value is printed.

"students with low prior knowledge who solved problems with low variability outperformed those who solved problems with high variability on a subsequent test, but the opposite pattern of results was found for students with higher prior knowledge."
