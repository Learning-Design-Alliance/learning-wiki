# Text-based reflective use from Copilot Critical Engagement responses does not predict productive engagement, with limited coder reliability and a TA ceiling effect (exploratory null)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 associational `r1`–`r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Technology acceptance did not significantly predict RUItext (b = 0.690, p = 0.118), and RUItext did not significantly predict productive engagement (b = 0.149, p = 0.202) or project score; the text-based indirect effect was not significant. [→ Wang S and Zhao J 2026](#wang-s-and-zhao-j-2026)
`q1 i?` Initial intercoder agreement was limited (exact agreement 57.6%, linear-weighted κ = 0.421, ordinal α = 0.495), and the Copilot TA measure showed a ceiling effect (28.9% maximum scores; 81.6% at least 4.5). [→ Wang S and Zhao J 2026 (2)](#wang-s-and-zhao-j-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Wang S and Zhao J 2026

Wang S and Zhao J (2026) Reflective AI use and student engagement in AI-supported programming: technology acceptance, programming self-efficacy, and behavioral trace evidence. Front. Psychol. 17:1929887. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1929887

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Exploratory complete-case regressions on the Copilot field-study dataset (66 nonblank Critical Engagement responses of 114 records), double-coded by two blinded human coders into a four-level RUItext index. The article reports "Technology acceptance did not signiﬁcantly predictRUItext (b = 0.690, SE = 0.435, and p = 0.118)"; the indirect estimate was 0.101 with a percentile interval from –0.022 to 0.304.

> "Technology acceptance did not signiﬁcantly predictRUItext (b = 0.690, SE = 0.435, and p = 0.118), and RUItext did not signiﬁcantly predict productive engagement or project score."

### Wang S and Zhao J 2026 (2)

Wang S and Zhao J (2026) Reflective AI use and student engagement in AI-supported programming: technology acceptance, programming self-efficacy, and behavioral trace evidence. Front. Psychol. 17:1929887. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1929887

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Reliability of the two coders' independent ratings before adjudication of the 66 nonblank responses; the article states these estimates "indicate limited initial agreement" and do not justify treating RUItext as a fully validated scale. A separate ceiling-effect quote reports 33 of 114 students (28.9%) at the maximum TA score and 93 (81.6%) at least 4.5.

> "Exact agreement was 57.6%, linear-weighted Cohen’sκ was 0.421, and Krippendorﬀ’s ordinal α was 0.495."
