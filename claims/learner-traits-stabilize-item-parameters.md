## Evidence

### Emre Gönülateş and Gerd Kortemeyer 2015

Emre Gönülateş and Gerd Kortemeyer. (2015). A New Item Response Theory Model for Open-Ended Online Homework with Multiple Allowed Attempts. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.10.010118](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.10.010118)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Distribution of estimated difficulty parameters for the 401 homework items (Fig. 8) across 2PL and 2P3TL variants. The article reports that "Introducing the additional student traits stabilizes the model" and items shift toward higher difficulty.

> "Introducing the additional student traits stabilizes the model, where in fact the reduced "copying only" model completely absorbs any divergencies. Items are shifted toward higher diﬃculty, which is not surpris- ing: the copying trait explains why low-ability students might get those items correct"

### Emre Gönülateş and Gerd Kortemeyer 2015 (2)

Emre Gönülateş and Gerd Kortemeyer. (2015). A New Item Response Theory Model for Open-Ended Online Homework with Multiple Allowed Attempts. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.10.010118](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.10.010118)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Distribution of estimated discrimination parameters for the homework items (Fig. 9). The article states "a larger number of items now show positive discrimination" after the learner traits absorb noise, though divergence persists for some items.

> "The additional learner traits absorb some of the noise that drowns out the item discrimination, and a larger number of items now show positive discrimination."
