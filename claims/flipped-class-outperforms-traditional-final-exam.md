# A flipped redesigned introductory physics class achieved higher final exam scores than a traditional lecture-based class, with no midterm difference

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students in the redesigned flipped class scored higher on the final exam than students in the traditional lecture class (Class I 34.4 vs Class II 23.9), while midterm means did not differ significantly. [→ Heeseung Zoe 2014](#heeseung-zoe-2014)
`q3 i?` The final-exam mean difference between the redesigned and traditional classes was statistically significant by Welch T-test (p = 1.925 × 10−7). [→ Heeseung Zoe 2014 (2)](#heeseung-zoe-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Heeseung Zoe 2014

Heeseung Zoe, Kyujin Kwak, Yoonjoo Cho, Yun-Young Seo, Jin-suk Choi, Jin-Hyouk Im, Soogyun Beum and Hai-Woong Lee. (2014). The role of face-to-face lecturing in flipped classrooms. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1404.3566](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1404.3566)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Semester 2012 comparison of a redesigned flipped class (N = 176) with a traditional lecture class (N = 161), students randomly assigned by the university, same midterm and final exams. The article reports "The average score of Class I was 10 % higher than that of Class II" on the final exam (34.4 vs 23.9 in Table IV).

> "The average score of Class I was 10 % higher than that of Class II ."

### Heeseung Zoe 2014 (2)

Heeseung Zoe, Kyujin Kwak, Yoonjoo Cho, Yun-Young Seo, Jin-suk Choi, Jin-Hyouk Im, Soogyun Beum and Hai-Woong Lee. (2014). The role of face-to-face lecturing in flipped classrooms. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1404.3566](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1404.3566)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

T-test and F-test analysis of Semester 2012 exam score distributions. The final-exam difference is significant (p = 1.925 × 10−7, Welch T-test after F-test p = 0.0017), whereas the midterm difference is not (p = 0.1308).

> "The diﬀerence in the means of two classes for the ﬁnal exam is conﬁrmed by T-test, since the p-value is 1 .925 × 10−7 which is much less than the signiﬁcant level."
