# Task-selection skills trained with video modeling examples in biology transfer to task selection in a different domain (math)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Both heuristic-trained and algorithmic-trained students showed significantly better task-selection accuracy on math transfer scenarios than control students. [→ Raaijmakers 2018](#raaijmakers-2018)

## Evidence

### Raaijmakers 2018

Raaijmakers, S. F., Baars, M., Schaap, L., Paas, F., van Merriënboer, J., & van Gog, T. (2018). Training self-regulated learning skills with video modeling examples: Do task-selection skills transfer? Instructional Science, 46, 273–290. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-017-9434-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Transfer was measured with eight scenarios in which students selected a next task for a fictitious peer in math, with databases of 75 or 32 tasks and 5- or 8-step problems. Both trained conditions beat control (heuristic r = .48; algorithmic r = .35).

> "Post-hoc Mann–Whitney U Tests showed that in line with Hypothesis 2a, both the heuristic training, U = 355.5, p  .001, r = .48, and algorithmic training, U = 537.5, p = .001, r = .35, showed signiﬁcantly better task-selection accuracy on the transfer test than the control group"
