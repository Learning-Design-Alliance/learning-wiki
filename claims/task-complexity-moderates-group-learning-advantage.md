# Collaborating learners learn more effectively and efficiently than individual learners on cognitively challenging tasks, but group work becomes disadvantageous on cognitively unchallenging tasks

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In experiments by F. Kirschner et al., collaborating learners on cognitively challenging tasks could divide information processing, expanding cognitive capacity, and learned more effectively and efficiently than individual learners. [→ Janssen 2010](#janssen-2010)
`q2 i?` With cognitively unchallenging tasks that individual learners could carry out alone, the group advantage disappeared and working in a group became disadvantageous for learning effectiveness and efficiency. [→ Janssen 2010 (2)](#janssen-2010-2)

## Evidence

### Janssen 2010

Janssen, J., Kirschner, F., Erkens, G., Kirschner, P. A., & Paas, F. (2010). Making the Black Box of Collaborative Learning Transparent: Combining Process-Oriented and Cognitive Load Approaches. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-010-9131-x

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review describes a series of experiments by F. Kirschner et al. (2009a, b, c) into effects of task-imposed cognitive load on individual versus group learning, reporting that "collaborating learners learned more effectively and efficiently than did individual learners" on cognitively challenging tasks. No effect sizes are printed in the review.

> "collaborating learners learned more effectively and efficiently than did individual learners"

### Janssen 2010 (2)

Janssen, J., Kirschner, F., Erkens, G., Kirschner, P. A., & Paas, F. (2010). Making the Black Box of Collaborative Learning Transparent: Combining Process-Oriented and Cognitive Load Approaches. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-010-9131-x

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

For cognitively unchallenging tasks, the review reports the group advantage disappeared: "Working in a group even became disadvantageous for learning effectiveness and efficiency because group members had to be engaged in cognitive activities related to interindividual communication and coordination."

> "Working in a group even became disadvantageous for learning effectiveness and efficiency because group members had to be engaged in cognitive activities related to interindividual communication and coordination."
