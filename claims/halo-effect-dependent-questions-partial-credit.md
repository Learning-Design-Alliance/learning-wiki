## Evidence

### Ene 2019

Ene, E., & Webb, R. C. (2019). A tale of two test setups: The effect of randomizing a popular conceptual survey in electricity and magnetism. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1908.09221](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1908.09221)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of the dependent trio Q1-Q3 between fixed sequential presentation on WebAssign and separated presentation on the local platform, using the cleaned PRE sample; the authors attribute the difference to a halo effect of Q1 under partial-credit grading.

> "The questions Q2 and Q3 received significantly more incorrect answers if they were presented as a continuation of the question Q1 than if they were presented as separate questions with their own diagrams."

### Ene 2019 (2)

Ene, E., & Webb, R. C. (2019). A tale of two test setups: The effect of randomizing a popular conceptual survey in electricity and magnetism. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1908.09221](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1908.09221)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Regrading of WebAssign Q3 answers under the independent scoring model for a fair comparison, reported in Table 1-6 for the cleaned PRE subpopulation.

> "If Q3 was graded only for correctness, its popularity at pre-instruction was 0.558 when presented in trio, but 0.649 when presented detached from Q1 and Q2."
