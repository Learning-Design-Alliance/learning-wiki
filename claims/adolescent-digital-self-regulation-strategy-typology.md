# Adolescent digital self-regulation is enacted through a typology of four strategy types differing in executive effort versus external scaffolding

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The article identifies an evidence-based typology of four strategy categories—activity-based, context-based (eliminating and limiting), technology-supported, and time-structured self-regulation—that differ in reliance on executive effort versus external scaffolding. ## Evidence

### Wang 2026

Wang, M.-T. (2026). Developing Digital Self-Regulation in Adolescence: A Developmental Self-Determination Model of Digital Self-Regulation. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10183-y

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review's synthesis of prior literature (Table 1) presents four strategy categories. Activity-based strategies are described as "a more effortful form of regulation, drawing heavily on attentional control and autonomous motivation", while context-based, technology-supported, and time-structured strategies progressively externalize regulation.

> "Activity-based self-regulation (e.g., engaging in alternative activities such as exercise, reading, shopping) reflects a more effortful form of regulation, drawing heavily on attentional control and autonomous motivation to redirect behavior toward intrinsically or instrumentally valued goals"
