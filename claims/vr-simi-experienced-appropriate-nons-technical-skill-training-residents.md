## Evidence

### Birkheim SL 2024

Birkheim SL, Calogiuri G, Hvalstad M, Martinsen R. (2024). Exploring the experiences of resident doctors in child and adolescent psychiatry with virtual reality-based simulation training: a qualitative study. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-024-11941-w

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Exploratory qualitative study with participant observations of thirteen resident doctors across three VR-SIMI sessions plus two focus groups (n = 7 and n = 5), analysed with reflexive thematic analysis. The conclusion states "VR-SIMI was experienced as an appropriate method of training NTS". No effect size is reported (qualitative design).

> "VR-SIMI was experienced as an appropriate method of training NTS among resident doctors in child and adolescents’ psychiatry. The findings indicate that the high levels of presence generated through the VR technology elicited- emotional awareness; and arousal, which are crucial for observational learning to occur."

### Birkheim SL 2024 (2)

Birkheim SL, Calogiuri G, Hvalstad M, Martinsen R. (2024). Exploring the experiences of resident doctors in child and adolescent psychiatry with virtual reality-based simulation training: a qualitative study. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-024-11941-w

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The reflexive thematic analysis of the observation and focus-group data identified three main themes, of which the appropriateness of the method and learning processes are elaborated across the Results section.

> "The analysis revealed the following three themes: 1) The importance of pedagogical principles, 2) Playful and motionally affected learning, and 3) Observational learning contributing to lifelong learning."
