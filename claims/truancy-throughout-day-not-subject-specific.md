# Poor attendance occurs throughout the school day and across subjects; first period is most often missed but missing it does not explain overall truancy

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Over half of ninth graders missed two or more weeks in first period, yet over 44 percent missed two or more weeks during periods 4 to 6, so tardiness alone does not account for class absences. [→ Roderick 1997](#roderick-1997)
`q2 i?` Class absences are relatively uniform across subjects—students are just as likely to be truant in English as in Math—suggesting cutting reflects poor overall school environments. [→ Roderick 1997 (2)](#roderick-1997-2)

## Evidence

### Roderick 1997

Roderick, M., Arney, M., Axelman, M., DaCosta, K., Steiger, C., Stone, S., Villarreal-Sosa, L., & Waxman, E. (1997). Habits Hard to Break: A New Look at Truancy in Chicago's Public High Schools. Student Life in High Schools Project, School of Social Service Administration, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/habits-hard-break-new-look-truancy-chicagos-public-high-schools

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of class absences by period of the day for the CPS ninth grade cohort in second semester 1996. The brief reports over half of ninth graders missed two or more weeks in their first class, and "Over 44 percent" missed two or more weeks in periods 4 to 6.

> "Over 44 percent of the ninth graders missed two or more weeks of instruction during the middle of the school day—periods 4 to 6."

### Roderick 1997 (2)

Roderick, M., Arney, M., Axelman, M., DaCosta, K., Steiger, C., Stone, S., Villarreal-Sosa, L., & Waxman, E. (1997). Habits Hard to Break: A New Look at Truancy in Chicago's Public High Schools. Student Life in High Schools Project, School of Social Service Administration, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/habits-hard-break-new-look-truancy-chicagos-public-high-schools

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same period-by-period and subject-by-subject analysis of class absences. The brief reports relatively consistent absence patterns across English, Social Studies, Math, and Science, and interprets this as reflecting poor overall school environments.

> "Students are just as likely to be truant in English as in Math."
