## Evidence

### Ryugo Morita 2025

Ryugo Morita, Ko Watanabe, Jinjia Zhou, Andreas Dengel, and Shoya Ishimaru. (2025). GenAIReading: Augmenting Human Cognition with Interactive Digital Textbooks Using Large Language Models and Image Generation Models. [doi:10.1145/3745900.3746066](https://doi.org/10.1145/3745900.3746066)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Observational grouping of study participants by stated preference; the article reports which condition scored highest per group without inferential statistics. It adds that the lowest text-preference scores were under IGenAI Image.

> "In the text-preference group (TGenAI), the highest average test scores were achieved under theTGenAI Summary condition, demonstrating the benefit of providing a text-based overview."

### Ryugo Morita 2025 (2)

Ryugo Morita, Ko Watanabe, Jinjia Zhou, Andreas Dengel, and Shoya Ishimaru. (2025). GenAIReading: Augmenting Human Cognition with Interactive Digital Textbooks Using Large Language Models and Image Generation Models. [doi:10.1145/3745900.3746066](https://doi.org/10.1145/3745900.3746066)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The image-preference group's best condition was IGenAI Summary, and their lowest scores were in the Baseline condition. No effect sizes or tests are printed for these group patterns.

> "Conversely, in the image-preference group (IGenAI), the IGenAI Summary condition produced the highest test scores."
