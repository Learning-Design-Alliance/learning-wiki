# Five-year-olds recall more words in a Brown-Peterson task when they finger-track a transparent goal cue during the retention interval

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In 5-year-olds, presenting a transparent goal cue significantly improved recall performance when children tracked the cue with their fingers (ηp² = 0.15). [→ Fitamen 2019](#fitamen-2019)

## Evidence

### Fitamen 2019

Fitamen, C., Blaye, A. & Camos, V. (2019). Five-year-old children's working memory can be improved when children act on a transparent goal cue. Scientific Reports 9:15342. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-51869-4

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA on recall scores in the experimental conditions of the study's between-subjects design (170 preschoolers, control condition score as covariate) showed the transparent-cue benefit in the finger-tracking condition for 5-year-olds, with "F(1,35) = 6.36, p = 0.02, ηp2 = 0.15".

> "presenting a transparent goal cue led to a significant improvement in 5-year-olds' recall performance when the children had to track the cue with their fingers, F(1,35) = 6.36, p = 0.02, ηp2 = 0.15."
