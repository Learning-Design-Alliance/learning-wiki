# A 10-minute bout of moderate-intensity exercise improves Stroop inhibitory control with increased left dorsolateral prefrontal activation in young adults

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Compared to resting control, acute moderate exercise significantly increased inhibitory control on the Stroop Task, accompanied by increased left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex activation. [→ Basso Julia C. 2017](#basso-julia-c-2017)
`q2 i?` In older adults, acute exercise enhanced Stroop performance and increased task-related right frontal polar activity, consistent with an increased HAROLD compensatory effect. [→ Basso Julia C. 2017 (2)](#basso-julia-c-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Basso Julia C. 2017

Basso Julia C., Suzuki Wendy A. (2017). The Effects of Acute Exercise on Mood, Cognition, Neurophysiology, and Neurochemical Pathways: A Review. Brain Plasticity. https://doi.org/10.3233/BPL-160040

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

fNIRS study (Yanagisawa and colleagues) in healthy young adults measured during the Stroop Task before and 15 minutes after a 10-minute moderate-intensity bout at 50% VO2 peak or rest. Quotes the fragment "significantly increased inhibitory control as measured by the interference component of the Stroop Task".

> "Compared to the resting control condition, acute moderate exercise significantly increased inhibitory control as measured by the interference component of the Stroop Task (incongruent minus neutral), and this effect was accompanied by increased activation in the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex"

### Basso Julia C. 2017 (2)

Basso Julia C., Suzuki Wendy A. (2017). The Effects of Acute Exercise on Mood, Cognition, Neurophysiology, and Neurochemical Pathways: A Review. Brain Plasticity. https://doi.org/10.3233/BPL-160040

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

fNIRS repetition of the young-adult study in older individuals, showing bilateral prefrontal activation during Stroop interference consistent with the HAROLD effect. Quotes the fragment "increased the HAROLD effect or the older brain's ability for compensatory brain activation".

> "Acute exercise enhanced performance on the Stroop task and increased task-related activity in the right frontal polar area, indicating that exercise increased the HAROLD effect or the older brain's ability for compensatory brain activation"
