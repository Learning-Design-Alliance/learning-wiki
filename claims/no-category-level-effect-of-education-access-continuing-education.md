## Evidence

### Nákity K 2025

Nákity K, Kasza BB, Tatár BB, Szűcs M, Kis D, Barzó P, Domján A. (2025). Assessment of differential diagnostic skills of physiotherapists related to the cervical spine - approaches to improving effectiveness: observational, cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education 25:1065. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07682-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Category-level logistic regressions in the 128-respondent survey found "decision-making was not significantly influenced by education level, access mode, or continuing education on the cervical spine"; equivalence was not tested.

> "On the basis of all MSK and CM case responses, decision-making was not significantly influenced by education level, access mode, or continuing education on the cervical spine."

### Nákity K 2025 (2)

Nákity K, Kasza BB, Tatár BB, Szűcs M, Kis D, Barzó P, Domján A. (2025). Assessment of differential diagnostic skills of physiotherapists related to the cervical spine - approaches to improving effectiveness: observational, cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education 25:1065. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07682-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The Discussion reports "no statistically significant difference in the appropriate treatment decisions for either MSK or CM cases" between direct and indirect access, contrasting with Ojha et al.'s systematic review of direct-access outcomes.

> "In the present study, we found no statistically significant difference in the appropriate treatment decisions for either MSK or CM cases, regardless of whether respondents treated patients through direct or indirect access to physiotherapy."
