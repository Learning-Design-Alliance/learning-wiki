# In an upper-level QM course, student performance on clicker questions rose significantly from ICT to GCT in one single-group pre-post study

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2016 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Average performance on clicker questions was significantly higher after peer interaction (GCT) than on individual responses (ICT), for both unadjusted and adjusted scores. [→ Ryan Sayer 2016](#ryan-sayer-2016)

## Evidence

### Ryan Sayer 2016

Ryan Sayer, Emily Marshman and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). The impact of peer interaction on the responses to clicker questions in an upper-level quantum mechanics course. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.071](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.071)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Case study in an upper-level QM elective with 20 students; Table I reports unadjusted percentages of 69 (ICT) vs 85 (GCT), p = 0.003, and adjusted percentages of 48 vs 73, p = 0.009, showing "an improvement in students’ performance from ICT to GCT". No effect size is printed.

> "Overall, there was an improvement in students’ performance from ICT to GCT, and the difference between the means of ICT and GCT was significant."
