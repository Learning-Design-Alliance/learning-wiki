# Typical engineering assessment practices reward regurgitation and speed and designate conceptual reasoning as peripheral "extra credit," in Michael's experience

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Michael reports that the engineering education system rewards regurgitation and good memory rather than good learning, and that time-pressured wide-coverage exams reward "quick people, not deep people." [→ Brian A. Danielak 2010](#brian-a-danielak-2010)
`q2 i?` On a circuits exam, conceptual questions requiring reasoning and argumentation were designated "extra credit," signaling they test the periphery, though for Michael they are the core. [→ Brian A. Danielak 2010 (2)](#brian-a-danielak-2010-2)

### Brian A. Danielak 2010

Brian A. Danielak, Ayush Gupta, Andrew Elby. (2010). The Marginalized Identities of Sense-makers: Reframing Engineering Student Retention. 40th ASEE/IEEE Frontiers in Education Conference. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1003.4655](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1003.4655)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In the March 17, 2010 interview Michael states the engineering education system "doesn't reward good learning so much as it rewards regurgitation, and good memory," and asks why math and engineering departments cannot assign papers as English and History departments do.

> "the current education system we have [in engineering] doesn't reward good learning so much as it rewards regurgitation, and good memory"

### Brian A. Danielak 2010 (2)

Brian A. Danielak, Ayush Gupta, Andrew Elby. (2010). The Marginalized Identities of Sense-makers: Reframing Engineering Student Retention. 40th ASEE/IEEE Frontiers in Education Conference. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1003.4655](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1003.4655)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In the May 13, 2009 interview Michael describes a circuits exam on which he did particularly well on conceptual questions designated "extra credit," which "helped balance out for the things that I should have been able to just regurgitate."

> "What [that exam] was intended for was, so, if people got none of the conceptual things right they could still do well. But, it sorta was the opposite for me."
