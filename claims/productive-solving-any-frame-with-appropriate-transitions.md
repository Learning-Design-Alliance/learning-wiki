# Productive problem solving can occur in any epistemological frame provided students transition appropriately between frames

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Productive problem solving may occur in any of the four frames as long as students transition appropriately between frames; no frame is inherently productive or unproductive. [→ Bahar Modir 2017](#bahar-modir-2017)

## Evidence

### Bahar Modir 2017

Bahar Modir, John D. Thompson, and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2017). Students’ epistemological framing in quantum mechanics problem solving. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.020108](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.020108)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative observational analysis of video-recorded group problem solving in one semester of a senior-level quantum mechanics class, with illustrative episodes of frame use and transitions. The authors argue "productive problem solving may occur in any frame" with appropriate transitions, illustrated by episodes where students resolve errors after shifting frames.

> "arguing that productive problem solving may occur in any frame as long as students’ transition appropriately between frames."
