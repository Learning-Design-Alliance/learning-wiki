## Evidence

### van der Maas 1992

van der Maas, H. L. J., & Molenaar, P. C. M. (1992). Stagewise Cognitive Development: An Application of Catastrophe Theory. Psychological Review, 99(3), 395-417. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.99.3.395

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review reports Bentler's (1970) cross-sectional study: the Goldschmid and Bentler conservation test administered to 560 children aged 4 to 8 yielded bimodal distributions, which the authors read as suggesting "a discontinuity in the acquisition of conservation."

> "He used the complete Goldschmid and Bentler version of the conservation test (the standard conservation test at present) and assessed 560 children (aged 4 to 8 years) in a cross-sectional design."

### van der Maas 1992 (2)

van der Maas, H. L. J., & Molenaar, P. C. M. (1992). Stagewise Cognitive Development: An Application of Catastrophe Theory. Psychological Review, 99(3), 395-417. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.99.3.395

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review reports Tabor and Kendler (1981), who used Piaget's class inclusion task against the optional shift task as a control expected to be unimodal; "the Piagetian task produced clearly bimodal score distributions" while the control task did not. Field (1987) also presented bimodal distributions for conservation items.

> "It was found that the Piagetian task produced clearly bimodal score distributions, whereas the op- tional shift task did not."
