## Evidence

### Shute 2016

Shute, V. J., Wang, L., Greiff, S., Zhao, W., & Moore, G. (2016). Measuring problem solving skills via stealth assessment in an engaging video game. Computers in Human Behavior, 63, 106-117. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2016.05.047

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive analysis of the validation study measures on a 0-1 scale. The article reports the analyzing givens facet was negatively skewed with a high mean, suggesting it "was the easiest of the four problem solving facets", while evaluating progress and the MicroDYN measures were positively skewed and relatively difficult.

> "The distribution of the analyzing givens and constraints facet was negatively skewed. This, coupled with its relatively high mean score, suggests that it was the easiest of the four problem solving facets for the students."

### Shute 2016 (2)

Shute, V. J., Wang, L., Greiff, S., Zhao, W., & Moore, G. (2016). Measuring problem solving skills via stealth assessment in an engaging video game. Computers in Human Behavior, 63, 106-117. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2016.05.047

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis among the in-game estimates in the validation study. The article reports the overall problem solving estimate correlated r = 0.98 with the planning a solution facet, and attributes this to planning being conceptually the broadest facet, while noting regression results still differ across facets.

> "What is a little surprising is the correlation between the overall Problem Solving estimate and the Planning a Solution facet ( r ¼ 0.98), almost indicating identity."
