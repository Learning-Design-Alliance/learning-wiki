# Graduate students shift to worse categories when categorizing from an introductory student's perspective yet cannot anticipate students' category frequencies

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q2` quasi-experiment
> **Currency** · newest study 2016 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` When asked to categorize from the perspective of a typical introductory student, graduate students placed questions in worse categories than from their own perspective. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016)
`q2 i?` Despite this shift, graduate students were not able to anticipate how frequently introductory students placed each problem in common less-expert-like categories. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)](#chandralekha-singh-2016-2)

`q2 i?` Graduate students recognized that introductory students understand less physics and re-categorized accordingly, but their category-by-category placements still differed significantly from introductory students' own categorizations. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016)

`q2 i?` For specific categories such as conservation of momentum, graduate students' predictions of novice categorization differed significantly from what introductory students actually did. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)](#chandralekha-singh-2016-2)

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Rethinking Tools for Training Teaching Assistants. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1603.02946](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1603.02946)

`q2 · i?`

Histogram comparison (Fig. 2) of graduate students' categorizations from their own versus an introductory student's perspective, alongside introductory students' own categorizations of the same 25 problems. The article reports the shift descriptively with no effect size.

> "Figure 2 shows that the graduate students re-categorized the questions in worse categories when performing the categorization from the perspective of a typical introductory physics student."

### Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Rethinking Tools for Training Teaching Assistants. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1603.02946](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1603.02946)

`q2 · i?`

Item-level analysis of categories such as conservation of momentum showed significant differences between graduate students' novice-perspective categorizations and introductory students' own categorizations, indicating graduate students' estimates of novice category usage were inaccurate.

> "overall they were not able to anticipate the frequency with which introductory students categorized each problem in the common less-expert-like categories."
