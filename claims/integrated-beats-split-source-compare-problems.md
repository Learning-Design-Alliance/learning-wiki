# Children studying integrated worked examples outperformed those studying split-source examples on acquisition and delayed similar-problem tests

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In Experiment 2, students in the integrated group significantly outperformed those in the split-source group on acquisition test problems. [→ Mwangi 1998](#mwangi-1998)
`q2 i?` On delayed similar test problems, the integrated group solved more problems correctly and made fewer incorrect attempts. [→ Mwangi 1998 (2)](#mwangi-1998-2)

## Evidence

### Mwangi 1998

Wangari Mwangi and John Sweller. (1998). Learning to Solve Compare Word Problems: The Effect of Example Format and Generating Self-Explanations. Cognition and Instruction, 16(2). https://www.jstor.org/stable/3233720

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 assigned 27 Year 3 children to integrated (n = 14) or split-source (n = 13) worked-example formats for inconsistent-language compare problems; on acquisition test problems "students in the integrated group significantly outperformed those in the split-source group, t(25) = 2.54".

> "In relation to the number of acquisition test problems solved correctly, students in the integrated group significantly outperformed those in the split-source group, t(25) = 2.54."

### Mwangi 1998 (2)

Wangari Mwangi and John Sweller. (1998). Learning to Solve Compare Word Problems: The Effect of Example Format and Generating Self-Explanations. Cognition and Instruction, 16(2). https://www.jstor.org/stable/3233720

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

On the delayed test administered 10 days after acquisition in Experiment 2, significant format differences favored the integrated group on similar problems, "t(25) = 3.54, and t(25) = 3.38, respectively"; no format effect was found on transfer problems.

> "Significant differences in the number of problems solved correctly and in the number of incorrect attempts made were obtained for similar delayed test problems, t(25) = 3.54, and t(25) = 3.38, respectively."
