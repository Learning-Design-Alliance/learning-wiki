q3 · i0 · associational · r3

Effect sizes from the final RI-CCLPM French solution, approximated as Cohen's d representing expected standardized change following a one standard deviation increase. The contemporaneous effect was "of moderate size (d =0 .33)" and the lagged effect d = 0.25.

"In French, a similar asymmetry emerged, with a contemporaneous effect of skills of moderate size (d =0 .33) and a lagged effect of self-concept of comparable size (d =0 .25)."

q3 · i0 · associational · r3

Effect-size analysis of the RI-CCLPM cross-effects across domains. The alternative cross-effects were "null or negligible in size (d = 0.22)", indicating only the specific temporal effects predicted by the CL-REM were reliably present.

"In contrast, the alternative cross-effects—i.e., lagged effect of skills and contemporaneous effect of self-concept—were null or negligible in size (d = 0.22), suggesting that only specific temporal effects were reliably present."

Related Claims¶

  • Academic skills predict contemporaneous (within-semester) changes in academic self-concept in both mathematics and French — a broader claim this one bears on
  • The temporal asymmetry holds across gender and initial ability levels, with a cautious gender difference in the French lagged effect — related
  • In German secondary-school mathematics, effects of self-concept on achievement are lagged (lag1) whereas effects of achievement on self-concept are more contemporaneous (lag0) — related
  • Academic self-concept predicts lagged (across-semester) changes in academic skills in both mathematics and French — related
  • Across basic and extended RI-CLPMs and CLPMs of five-wave secondary-school data, math self-concept and achievement show consistent reciprocal (bidirectional) directional ordering — related
  • Reciprocal effects are robust to time-invariant confounders, but the French lagged self-concept effect did not survive the lag2 carryover stress test — related
