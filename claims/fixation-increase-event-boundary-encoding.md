## Evidence

### Lee Madison Mason 2025

Lee Madison Mason, Vatral Caleb, Cohn Clayton, Davalos Eduardo, Jessee Mary Ann, Biswas Gautam, Levin Daniel T. (2025). Eye movements as predictors of student experiences during nursing simulation learning events. Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-025-00640-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Non-pre-registered boundary analysis of gaze changes across participant-defined event boundaries in the eye-tracked simulation footage. The authors interpret this as participants possibly allocating more attentional resources at event onset.

> "Our analysis revealed that mean fixation duration was significantly greater during the first 5 s of an event (M = 412, SD = 378) compared to the last 5 s of the prior event (M = 348, SD = 271; b = − 63.38, t(624) = − 2.58, p = 0.0101)"

### Lee Madison Mason 2025 (2)

Lee Madison Mason, Vatral Caleb, Cohn Clayton, Davalos Eduardo, Jessee Mary Ann, Biswas Gautam, Levin Daniel T. (2025). Eye movements as predictors of student experiences during nursing simulation learning events. Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-025-00640-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Boundary comparisons for saccade amplitude and pupil diameter at both 5-s and 1-s windows were non-significant; the article suggests spatial breadth of visual exploration remained stable and cognitive load may not have differed dramatically across boundaries.

> "Additionally, neither saccade amplitude nor pupil diameter varied significantly between event boundaries when comparing the first and last five seconds"
