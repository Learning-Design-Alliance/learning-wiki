# Total time spent on the TUG-K does not correlate with accuracy but correlates negatively with confidence

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2020 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There is no significant correlation between total time spent on the TUG-K and accuracy (r(115) = −0.17, p > 0.05), while time correlates significantly with confidence (r(115) = −0.25, p < 0.01). [→ P Klein 2020](#p-klein-2020)

## Evidence

### P Klein 2020

P Klein, A Lichtenberger, S Küchemann, S Becker, M Kekule, J Viiri, C Baadte, A Vaterlaus and J Kuhn. (2020). Visual attention while solving the test of understanding graphs in kinematics: An eye-tracking analysis. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ab5f51](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ab5f51)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis of total completion time (mean 20.2 ± 4.6 min) against accuracy and confidence scores for N = 115 students. The article reports "r(115) =−0.17, p > 0.05" for accuracy and "r(115) =−0.25, p < 0.01" for confidence; no standardised effect size is printed for these correlations.

> "There is no signiﬁcant correlation between total time spent on the TUG-K and the accuracy score, r(115) =−0.17, p > 0.05, whereas the correlation between time spent on the test and conﬁdence scores is signiﬁcant, r(115) =−0.25, p < 0.01."
