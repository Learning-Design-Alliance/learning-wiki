# Comparing natural musical scale with European equal temperament teaches students that the choice of musical scale is a diverse value judgment

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Equal temperament's frequency ratios are irrational (except for the octave), producing beats, in contrast to the natural scale, while equal temperament allows freedom of change in key; the choice between them is a value judgment with diversity. [→ Tsuyoshi HONDOU 2007](#tsuyoshi-hondou-2007)

## Evidence

### Tsuyoshi HONDOU 2007

Tsuyoshi HONDOU, Masayuki YOSHIZAWA, and Shozo SUTO. (2007). Science-based comparative culture: A new theme of experiment for freshmen in Tohoku University. https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.3527 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0708.3527](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0708.3527)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

Design rationale for the theme's second learning aspect, the variety of value judgments. Students analyze frequency differences between the natural musical scale and equal temperament and are asked why equal temperament was devised in Europe despite its impurity through beating. No outcome data are reported.

> "Thus, we have different musical scales used in the present day. The choice of one musical scale is a result of a “value judgment” which has diversity depending on persons, ethnic groups, period and so on."
