# Video game engagement is predicted by hours of play, playing with others, gender, and task orientation, with hours of play the strongest predictor

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a stepwise linear regression, hours played per week was the most accurate predictor of engagement, followed by playing with others, gender, and task scores, all significant contributors to the model. [→ Bobby Hoffman 2010](#bobby-hoffman-2010)

## Evidence

### Bobby Hoffman 2010

Bobby Hoffman, Louis Nadelson. (2010). Motivational engagement and video gaming: a mixed methods study. Education Tech Research Dev 58:245-270. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-009-9134-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Stepwise linear regression on self-report data from 189 participants, with engagement (Video Game Play total score) as the dependent variable. Hours per week entered first (R²Δ = .251, p = .001), then gender, plays with others, and task scores; the description reports "the most accurate predictor of video game engagement was the number of hours played per week".

> "Based upon beta coefficients the most accurate predictor of video game engagement was the number of hours played per week, followed by playing with others, with gender, and ego scores contributing the least, but still signiﬁcantly to the overall model (see Table 5)."
