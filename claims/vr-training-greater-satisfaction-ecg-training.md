# Students trained via VR report significantly greater satisfaction than students trained on real patients or a mannequin

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Satisfaction scores in the VR group (2.35, SD 0.18) were significantly higher than in the real patient (1.74, SD 0.24) and mannequin (1.79, SD 0.18) groups on a three-point scale (P < 0.001). [→ Kermanian Fatemeh 2026](#kermanian-fatemeh-2026)

## Evidence

### Kermanian Fatemeh 2026

Kermanian Fatemeh, Safiyari Parham, Hassanzadeh Gholamreza, Mojtahedzadeh Rita, Mohammadi Aeen. (2026). Boosting electrocardiography leads location training through virtual reality simulation: effects on skill performance and learner satisfaction. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09854-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Anonymous 16-item satisfaction questionnaire (three-point Likert scale, previously validated with reliability 0.9) administered after training and before the DOPS test. ANOVA gave F = 79.6, Sig. 0.00, with VR highest at 2.35.

> "The results of the survey also indicated that the satisfaction of the students in the VR group was significantly greater than that of the other two groups of patients: (0.24) 1.74, (0.18) 1.76 and (0.18) 2.35 (P value < 0.001)."
