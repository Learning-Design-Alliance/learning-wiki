Evidence¶

Jheng 2025¶

Jheng, Y.-L., Catrysse, L., Van de Cruys, S., Kendeou, P., Poels, K. & Gijbels, D. (2025). Investigating epistemic emotions experienced while reading refutation texts through a fine-grained measure of emotion. npj Science of Learning 10, 30. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00324-3

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Text-level Poisson GLMM analysis of DynamicEmo counts in the 56-participant experiment: negative refutation texts produced "significantly higher" anxiety than all other conditions; frustration showed a parallel pattern (b = 0.84, p < 0.001 vs. non-refutation).

"The anxiety levels of participants were signiﬁcantly higher when reading negative refutation texts compared to positive refutation ( b = −0.47, p = 0.01), standard refutation ( b = −0.54, p = 0.001) and non-refutation texts ( b = 0.65, p < 0.001)."

Jheng 2025 (2)¶

Jheng, Y.-L., Catrysse, L., Van de Cruys, S., Kendeou, P., Poels, K. & Gijbels, D. (2025). Investigating epistemic emotions experienced while reading refutation texts through a fine-grained measure of emotion. npj Science of Learning 10, 30. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00324-3

q3 · i? · causal · r?

Same text-level mixed-model analyses: positive refutation texts raised curiosity relative to standard and non-refutation texts; the article also reports higher boredom and neutral emotion and lower surprise under non-refutation texts.

"Moreover, the curiosity levels of participants exposed to positive refutation texts were signiﬁcantly higher than those of partici- pants reading standard refutation ( b = −0.23, p = 0.03) and non- refutation texts ( b = 0.28, p = 0.006)."
