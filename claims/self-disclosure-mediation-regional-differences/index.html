Differential associations of preferences for chatbot-mediated self-disclosure and instrumental help-seeking with adolescent interpersonal behaviors in the United States and Hong Kong. https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/qm6bg_v1

Region-specific bootstrapped indirect effects (Table 3) show the self-disclosure mediation pattern holds in HK but not the US; Table 3 additionally prints a significant positive US indirect path via maladaptive coping to bullying (β = 0.02, p < 0.05).

"The mediating effects of both adaptive coping and maladaptive coping between self-disclosure and prosocial behaviors were significant in HK adolescents but not significant in US adolescents (AC: βHK = 0.25, p < 0.001; βUS = 0.02, p > 0.05; MC: βHK = 0.02, p < 0.05; βUS = -0.01, p > 0.05)."

Related Claims¶

  • Adolescents with self-disclosure or instrumental help-seeking preferences show higher adaptive coping and prosocial behaviors, and the self-disclosure group shows the lowest maladaptive coping and bullying, in ANOVA comparisons — related
  • Adolescents' preference for instrumental help-seeking from AI chatbots is positively associated with adaptive coping, which relates to more prosocial behavior and less bullying, consistently across US and HK samples — related
  • In the whole sample, preference for self-disclosure with AI chatbots is associated with higher adaptive coping and lower maladaptive coping, linking to more prosocial behavior and less bullying — related
  • The correlates of self-disclosure preference with AI chatbots diverge culturally: positive with adaptive coping in HK but not the US, and positively associated with maladaptive coping in the US but negatively in HK — related
  • US and HK adolescents differ in conversation-topic preferences: HK adolescents more often prefer instrumental help-seeking and self-disclosure, while US adolescents more often report nothing to say to chatbots
  • Students report peer support and distraction as effective coping, rarely self-regulation or professional help-seeking — related
  • Emotion regulation difficulties are reliably associated with maladaptive trajectories including non-suicidal self-injury, aggression, bullying, and social withdrawal in at-risk adolescents — related
