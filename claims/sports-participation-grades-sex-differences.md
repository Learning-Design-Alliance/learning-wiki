## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Boys' odds ratios for all three subjects were larger than girls' at each frequency, and girls' 1–2 times a week math association was not significant (OR = 1.08, 95% CI: 0.99–1.19). [→ Zhang Yao 2023](#zhang-yao-2023)

## Evidence

### Zhang Yao 2023

Zhang Yao, Yan Jin, Jin Xiao, Yang Hongying, Zhang Ying, Ma Huijun, Ma Rui. (2023). Sports Participation and Academic Performance in Primary School: A Cross-Sectional Study in Chinese Children. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph20043678

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Sex-stratified ordered logistic regressions (Tables 3–5) on the same survey. Boys' ORs reached 1.44 (Chinese), 1.38 (math), 1.52 (English) at 3+ times/week, exceeding girls' corresponding ORs of 1.13, 1.17, 1.14.

> "Regarding girls, compared with students who never participate in sports, those students who participate sports in 1–3 times a month, 1–2 times a week, and 3 times a week and above were more likely to achieve better grades in Math learning performance (OR = 1.17, 95% CI: 1.07–1.27), (OR = 1.08, 95% CI: 0.99–1.19) and (OR = 1.17, 95% CI: 1.06–1.30)"

### Zhang Yao 2023 (2)

Zhang Yao, Yan Jin, Jin Xiao, Yang Hongying, Zhang Ying, Ma Huijun, Ma Rui. (2023). Sports Participation and Academic Performance in Primary School: A Cross-Sectional Study in Chinese Children. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph20043678

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Authors' interpretation of the sex-stratified results in the Discussion, attributing the absent dose relationship among girls to "gender differences and subject differences"; no test statistic is printed for this contrast.

> "Another interesting finding is that girls did not find a significant dose relationship in Chinese and math, which may be caused by gender differences and subject differences"
