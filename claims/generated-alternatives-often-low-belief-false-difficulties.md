## Evidence

### Nolan K. Weinlader 2019

Nolan K. Weinlader, Eric Kuo, Benjamin M. Rottman, and Timothy J. Nokes-Malach. (2019). A new approach for uncovering student resources with multiple-choice questions. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1909.01455](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1909.01455)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Part 3 certainty ratings in the interview study show generated alternatives can be disbelieved. For Q1, "none of the 14 students" giving the closer-target explanation for A "believed A to be the most likely answer," illustrating what the article calls false difficulties.

> "none of the 14 students who gave the explanation for A "target 1 is hit first, because it is closer to the battleship " believed A to be the most likely answer."

### Nolan K. Weinlader 2019 (2)

Nolan K. Weinlader, Eric Kuo, Benjamin M. Rottman, and Timothy J. Nokes-Malach. (2019). A new approach for uncovering student resources with multiple-choice questions. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1909.01455](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1909.01455)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Same interview study, Q2: "12 students provided an incorrect explanation for choice B" that shell 2 travels faster, "but only 3 of these students believed B could be the correct answer," again showing generated explanations need not reflect belief.

> "for Q2, 12 students provided an incorrect explanation for choice B: Shell 2 hits first, because it's traveling faster to get there , but only 3 of these students believed B could be the correct answer."
