# Student-reported SRL promotion is associated with students' self-efficacy, utility value and attainment value for SRL at class and student levels, but not with perceived cost

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` At the class level, student-reported SRL promotion was significantly related to self-efficacy (β = 0.44), utility value (β = 0.78) and attainment value (β = 0.58), with similar but weaker effects at the student level (β = [0.18, 0.32]). [→ Johannes Jud 2024](#johannes-jud-2024)
`q3 i0` Student-reported SRL promotion was not significantly associated with students' perceived cost at either level. [→ Johannes Jud 2024](#johannes-jud-2024)

## Evidence

### Johannes Jud 2024

Johannes Jud, Yves Karlen, Carmen Nadja Hirt. (2024). Linking teachers' and students' motivation for self-regulated learning: is there a signal and how is it transmitted? Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-024-09393-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Full-perspective multilevel SEM (167 teachers, 2,785 students); class-level results show "higher values for self-efficacy (β = 0.44), higher utility value (β = 0.78) and higher attainment value (β = 0.58)". Student-level effects were β = [0.18, 0.32]; class-level R² = [0.39, 0.79], student-level R² = [0.02, 0.13].

> "Classes who reported higher promotion of SRL also showed higher values for self-efficacy (β = 0.44), higher utility value (β = 0.78) and higher attainment value (β = 0.58). However, student-reported SRL promotion was not significantly associated with students' costs."
