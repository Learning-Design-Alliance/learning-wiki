# Pure PBL and Hybrid PBL groups outperformed the lecturing group on overall and higher-order performance at posttest in pediatric nursing

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` At posttest, the COTL (lecturing) group's overall and higher-order performance means were significantly lower than those of the PPBL and HPBL groups, with large effect sizes (η² = 0.24 overall, 0.28 higher-order). [→ Salari Mohsen 2018](#salari-mohsen-2018)

## Evidence

### Salari Mohsen 2018

Salari Mohsen, Roozbehi Amrollah, Zarifi Abdolvahed, Tarmizi Rohani Ahmad. (2018). Pure PBL, Hybrid PBL and Lecturing: which one is more effective in developing cognitive skills of undergraduate students in pediatric nursing course?. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1305-0

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA on posttest scores of 95 junior nursing students (PPBL = 30, HPBL = 30, COTL = 35) in a quasi-experimental nonequivalent control group design, with PN I and Prior Performance Test scores as covariates. Table 2 reports F(2, 91) = 14.32, p = .001, η² = 0.24 for overall and F = 17.23, η² = 0.28 for higher-order performance, showing "significantly lower" COTL means.

> "The Tukey HSD test indicated that the means of overall and the higher-order performances for COTL group were significantly lower than those for the PPBL and HPBL groups."
