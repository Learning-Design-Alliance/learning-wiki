Evidence¶

Franz 2023¶

Franz, D. J., Richter, T., Lenhard, W., Marx, P., Stein, R., & Ratz, C. (2023). The Influence of Diagnostic Labels on the Evaluation of Students: a Multilevel Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09716-6

q4 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Single-moderator analysis of diagnostic category in the multilevel meta-analysis of 284 effects. The omnibus test was non-significant; descriptively, intellectual disabilities (g = −0.56), learning disorders (g = −0.47), and behavioral disorders (g = −0.44) were all negative while the residual category (g = −0.17) was the only one not different from zero.

"Overall, we found no significant differences between the four superordinate diagnostic categories (k = 284, F (3, 280) = 2.43, p = .066, Fig. 2)."

Franz 2023 (2)¶

Franz, D. J., Richter, T., Lenhard, W., Marx, P., Stein, R., & Ratz, C. (2023). The Influence of Diagnostic Labels on the Evaluation of Students: a Multilevel Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09716-6

q4 · i2 · quant-synthesis · r2

Subcategory-level moderator analysis in the same meta-analysis; the overall test indicated significant differences between diagnostic subcategories, F(7, 276) = 2.30, p = .027, with combined intellectual-and-learning-disability labels most negative.

"with the most negative effects caused by combined intellectual and learning disability labels (g = −0.77), followed by combined behavioral and emotional disorder labels (g = −0.75)"
