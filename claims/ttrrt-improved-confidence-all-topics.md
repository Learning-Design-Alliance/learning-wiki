## Evidence

### Fitzgerald MC 2022

Fitzgerald MC, Noonan M, Lim E, Mathew Joseph K, Boo E, Stergiou HE, Kim Y, Reilly S, Groombridge C, Maini A, Williams K, Mitra B. (2022). Multi‐disciplinary, simulation‐based, standardised trauma team training within the Victorian State Trauma System. Emergency Medicine Australasia. https://doi.org/10.1111/1742-6723.14068

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Wilcoxon rank-sum comparison of pre- and post-programme confidence medians (n = 120) showed all key topics improved at P < 0.001; "Significantly improved confidence was demonstrated in all key topics", e.g. leading a trauma team rose from median 3 (2–4) to 4 (3–5).

> "Significantly improved confidence was demonstrated in all key topics (Table 3)."

### Fitzgerald MC 2022 (2)

Fitzgerald MC, Noonan M, Lim E, Mathew Joseph K, Boo E, Stergiou HE, Kim Y, Reilly S, Groombridge C, Maini A, Williams K, Mitra B. (2022). Multi‐disciplinary, simulation‐based, standardised trauma team training within the Victorian State Trauma System. Emergency Medicine Australasia. https://doi.org/10.1111/1742-6723.14068

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Self-reported change items among responders: "83 (70.3%) reported increased confidence" in leading a trauma team; the article similarly reports 75 (63.6%) increased confidence working within a team and 72 (60.5%) in raising a concern of perceived clinical risk.

> "With regard to leading a trauma team, 83 (70.3%) reported increased confidence after the programme, 30 (25.2%) reported no change whereas five (4.2%) reported reduced confidence (Fig. 3)."
