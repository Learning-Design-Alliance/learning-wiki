## Evidence

### Harris David J. 2017

Harris David J., Vine Samuel J., Wilson Mark R., McGrath John S., LeBel Marie-Eve, Buckingham Gavin. (2017). The effect of observing novice and expert performance on acquisition of surgical skills on a robotic platform. PLoS ONE 12(11): e0188233. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0188233

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANOVAs on the knot tying transfer task at the second visit. The article reports "no effect of group on time to completion" or knot tying score, with near-null Bayes factors for both measures.

> "In the knot tying transfer task, there was no effect of group on time to completion, F(3,116) = 0.18, p = .91, ꙍ2 = .000, BF10 = 0.06 or knot tying score, F(3,115) = 0.23, p = .88, ꙍ2 = .000, BF10 = 0.06."

### Harris David J. 2017 (2)

Harris David J., Vine Samuel J., Wilson Mark R., McGrath John S., LeBel Marie-Eve, Buckingham Gavin. (2017). The effect of observing novice and expert performance on acquisition of surgical skills on a robotic platform. PLoS ONE 12(11): e0188233. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0188233

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANOVAs on the ball pick-up-and-drop transfer task. The article reports no group effect on "time to completion" or errors, and states "Bayes factors provide moderate to strong evidence that performance was equivalent across groups."

> "For the ball drop task, there was also no effect of group on time to completion, F(3,115) = 1.09, p = .36, ꙍ2 = .002, BF10 = 0.16 or errors, F(3,113) = 1.48, p = .23, ꙍ2 = .012, BF10 = 0.24."
