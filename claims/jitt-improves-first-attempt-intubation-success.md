# Just-in-time simulation training immediately before the procedure improves first-attempt intubation success and reduces attempt duration

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Agrawal 2026

Agrawal, S., Rakshasbhuvankar, A., Rath, C., Brown, J. A., McEvoy, P. M., Rao, S., & Patole, S. (2026). Neonatal endotracheal intubation: development of a comprehensive educational program for procedural skills. Pediatric Research, 99, 484–489. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41390-025-04217-4

`q3 · i?` · `design · r1`

RCT randomising 153 trainees (paediatric anaesthesia fellows, residents, and nurse anaesthetist students) to JiTT expert coaching on a mannequin within one hour before intubating real infants or standard instruction. First-attempt success was "91.4%" vs 81.6%, adjusted OR 2.42.

> "They found that the JiTT group had a higher first-attempt success rate (212/213; 91.4%) compared to the control group (231/283; 81.6%); adjusted odds ratio 2.42 (CI: 1.45–4.04)."

### Agrawal 2026 (2)

Agrawal, S., Rakshasbhuvankar, A., Rath, C., Brown, J. A., McEvoy, P. M., Rao, S., & Patole, S. (2026). Neonatal endotracheal intubation: development of a comprehensive educational program for procedural skills. Pediatric Research, 99, 484–489. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41390-025-04217-4

`q3 · i?` · `design · r1`

Multicentre RCT in which 65 residents performed 139 ETIs, comparing simulation-based JiTT (short video and simulation) with a 5-minute video. Overall success was similar (67% vs 70%, P = 0.71), but "the first-attempt success rate was higher for the JiTT group (54% vs 41%, P = 0.035)"; no effect size is printed.

> "However, the first-attempt success rate was higher for the JiTT group (54% vs 41%, P = 0.035). The mean (SD) duration of attempts was shorter [35 (9) vs 62 (9) seconds, P = 0.048)] in the JiTT group."
