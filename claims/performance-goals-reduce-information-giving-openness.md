# Performance goals produce less openness in information giving than mastery or no goals

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium
> **Currency** · newest study 2007 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Relative to a mastery goal, a performance goal produced lower openness in information giving (ηp² = 0.07), while the mastery goal did not differ from no goal. [→ Poortvliet 2007](#poortvliet-2007)
`q3 i2` This effect replicated in Study 2 (ηp² = 0.06) with a larger sample and a 2x2 goal design. [→ Poortvliet 2007 (2)](#poortvliet-2007-2)

## Evidence

### Poortvliet 2007

Poortvliet, P. M., Janssen, O., Van Yperen, N. W., & Van de Vliert, E. (2007). Achievement Goals and Interpersonal Behavior: How Mastery and Performance Goals Shape Information Exchange. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin. https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167207305536

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Experiment with 74 University of Groningen students randomly assigned to mastery, performance, or no-goal conditions using the Winter Survival Exercise; openness was behavioral, computed as the Spearman correlation between the initial and the sent ranking. The article reports "ηp² = 0.07" for performance vs mastery and ηp² = 0.05 vs no goal.

> "Relative to mastery goal manipulation, performance goal manipulation produced a lower average level of openness in information giving, F(1, 69) = 5.02, p = .03, ηp² = 0.07. Performance goal manipulation also produced a lower average level of openness in information giving than the no-goal condition, F(1, 69) = 3.95, p = .05, ηp² = 0.05."

### Poortvliet 2007 (2)

Poortvliet, P. M., Janssen, O., Van Yperen, N. W., & Van de Vliert, E. (2007). Achievement Goals and Interpersonal Behavior: How Mastery and Performance Goals Shape Information Exchange. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin. https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167207305536

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Study 2 with 125 students randomly assigned to a 2 (definition) x 2 (valence) between-subjects design plus a no-goal condition replicated the openness effect; the article reports "ηp² = 0.06" for performance vs mastery and ηp² = 0.04 vs no goal, calling the replication perfect.

> "compared with the mastery goal manipulation, performance goal manipulation produced a lower average level of openness in information giving, F(1, 122) = 7.39, p < .01, ηp² = 0.06. Performance goal manipulation also led to a lower level of openness in information giving than the no-goal condition, F(1, 122) = 4.74, p = .03, ηp² = 0.04."
