## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For below-median SART children, PC and VR recall did not differ significantly; for above-median children the difference was marginally significant (partial η² = 0.11). [→ Rudling 2026](#rudling-2026)

## Evidence

### Rudling 2026

Rudling, M., Rumetshofer, T., Nirme, J. & Mårtensson, J. (2026). Embodied Word Learning in Schools and Sustained Attention in Virtual Reality. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00395-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Median split on SART (0.24) in the repeated measures ANOVA. For low-SART children VR and PC conditions were equal; only for higher sustained attention was the PC recall score higher, and only marginally (p = 0.054, partial η² = 0.11).

> "The results showed that for the below-median group there was no significant difference between the PC and VR recall scores (F(1,37) = 0.73, p = 0.399, partial 2 = 0.02), whereas for the above-median group, the difference was marginally significant despite the decreased statistical power of these analyses (F(1,31) = 4.03, p = 0.054, partial 2 = 0.11)."

### Rudling 2026 (2)

Rudling, M., Rumetshofer, T., Nirme, J. & Mårtensson, J. (2026). Embodied Word Learning in Schools and Sustained Attention in Virtual Reality. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00395-2

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Independent t-test on combined VR and PC recall comparing SART median-split groups printed a medium effect: Cohen's d = 0.54 (t(71) = 2.31, p = 0.024).

> "The low and high SART score groups also differed significantly in terms of overall word recall (VR and PC combined; t(71) = 2.31, p = 0.024, Cohen's d = 0.54)."
