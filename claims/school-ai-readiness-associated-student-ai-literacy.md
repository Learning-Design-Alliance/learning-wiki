## Evidence

### Guan 2026

Guan, X., Zheng, M., Gašević, D., Guo, W., Liu, Y., Han, X., Gasevic, D., Ma, R., Wu, Q., & Yan, L. (2026). From School AI Readiness to Student AI Literacy: A National Multilevel Mediation Analysis of Institutional Capacity and Teacher Capability. Preprint submitted to Elsevier. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2603.20056](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2603.20056)

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel linear mixed-effects model (random school intercepts) on the linked national sample of 1,007 schools and 2,379,546 students, covariate-adjusted. The article reports the association was "statistically significant" with β = 0.016 and p < 0.001; the standardized coefficient is very small in magnitude.

> "As shown in Figure 5, overall school AI readiness exhibited a statistically significant association with student AI literacy (𝛽= 0.016,𝑡(𝑑𝑓= 948.423) = 4.564,𝑝<0.001) after controlling for economic-administrative region, policy status, and institutional type."

### Guan 2026 (2)

Guan, X., Zheng, M., Gašević, D., Guo, W., Liu, Y., Han, X., Gasevic, D., Ma, R., Wu, Q., & Yan, L. (2026). From School AI Readiness to Student AI Literacy: A National Multilevel Mediation Analysis of Institutional Capacity and Teacher Capability. Preprint submitted to Elsevier. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2603.20056](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2603.20056)

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Preliminary unconditional multilevel model established the baseline variance structure. The article states that 1.18% of variance was "attributable to between-school differences", and that adding school-level covariates reduced between-school variance by 15.7%.

> "The unconditional model yielded an intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) of 0.0118, indicating that 1.18% of the total variance in student AI literacy was attributable to between-school differences."
