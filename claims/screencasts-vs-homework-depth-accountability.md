# Students distinguished screencasts from traditional homework, most often saying screencasts require more work and greater depth of understanding (53%) and impose audience-based accountability

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After eight screencasts, students most commonly reported screencasts require greater depth of understanding (53%), teaching the process (37%), creative expression (37%), and communication (32%), and that an audience imposes accountability for learning the content. ## Evidence

### Susan Nicholson-Dykstra 2013

Susan Nicholson-Dykstra, Ben Van Dusen and Valerie Otero. (2013). Teaching to Learn: iPads as Tools for Transforming Physics Student Roles. [doi:10.1119/perc.2013.pr.053](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2013.pr.053)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

End-of-semester-2 open-ended comparison assignment (Table 4, n=19) in which students identified and explained differences between screencasts and traditional homework. The article reports that most responses suggested screencasts require greater depth of content understanding; percentages are descriptive with no effect size.

> "Students reported that screencasting requires more communication, allows for more creativity and freedom of expression, and engages multiple senses, all ideas involving attention to audience engagement."
