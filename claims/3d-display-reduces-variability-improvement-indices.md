# Glasses-free 3D display learning reduces variability in improvement in spatial-arrangement understanding on total, left–right, and anterior–posterior indices, but not the superior–inferior index

## Evidence

### Muro S 2025

Muro S, Miyosawa K, Yamaguchi K, Okamoto K, Okamoto S, Itamiya T, Akita K. (2025). Evaluation of glasses‐free 3D anatomy learning materials through a randomized control study with a puzzle‐like method. Anatomical Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.70063

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the RCT, a one-sided F-test comparing improvement-index variances between the 2D and 3D display groups found significantly smaller variance in the 3D group for ID; standard deviations were 0.21 versus 0.14. No effect size is printed.

> "A one‐sided F‐test, conducted to compare the variance, found that the variance in the 3D display group was significantly smaller (p=0.022) (Table 2)."

### Muro S 2025 (2)

Muro S, Miyosawa K, Yamaguchi K, Okamoto K, Okamoto S, Itamiya T, Akita K. (2025). Evaluation of glasses‐free 3D anatomy learning materials through a randomized control study with a puzzle‐like method. Anatomical Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.70063

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The RCT's axis-specific analyses show the variance reduction held for IX (p=0.003) and IZ (p=0.003) but not IY (p=0.197), suggesting the effect is tied to axes visualized by the horizontally-restricted rotation allowed in the learning phase.

> "Significant differences in variability between the two groups were observed in the left–right and anterior–posterior axes, which are visualized in 3D through horizontal rotation, but not in the superior–inferior axis."
