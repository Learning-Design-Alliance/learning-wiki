# Multiplicative word-problem intervention was superior to both additive WP intervention and control on the multiplicative WP measure, while additive WP intervention did not differ from control

## Evidence

### Fuchs et al. 2016

Lynn S. Fuchs, Robin F. Schumacher, Jessica Long, Jessica Namkung, Amelia S. Malone, Amber Wang, Carol L. Hamlett, Nancy C. Jordan, Robert S. Siegler, and Paul Changas. (2016). Effects of Intervention to Improve At-Risk Fourth Graders' Understanding, Calculations, and Word Problems with Fractions. Elementary School Journal. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED566704

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Pairwise comparisons on the Multiplicative Word Problems measure in the randomized trial of 213 at-risk fourth graders. The article reports "The ES favoring the M-WP condition over the control group was 1.06, and the ES favoring the M-WP condition over the A-WP condition was 0.89," while A-WP versus control was not significant (ES = 0.16).

> "On the M-WP measure, M-WP intervention students performed significantly better than both contrasting conditions. The ES favoring the M-WP condition over the control group was 1.06, and the ES favoring the M-WP condition over the A-WP condition was 0.89. The comparison between the A-WP condition versus control was not significant (ES = 0.16)."
