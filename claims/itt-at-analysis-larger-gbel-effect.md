# Instrumental-variable (ITT-AT) analysis corrected for non-compliance shows an even larger effect of the game than standard ITT

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` The ITT-adjusted-for-treatment analysis, using randomization as an instrumental variable, yielded a Cohen's d of 0.89, larger than the ITT estimate of 0.71. [→ Boeker Martin 2013](#boeker-martin-2013)

## Evidence

### Boeker Martin 2013

Boeker Martin, Andel Peter, Vach Werner, Frankenschmidt Alexander. (2013). Game-Based E-Learning Is More Effective than a Conventional Instructional Method: A Randomized Controlled Trial with Third-Year Medical Students. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0082328

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Secondary instrumental variable regression analysis correcting standard ITT for non-compliance (6 script and 13 game students did not use their assigned material); the printed effect size was Cohen's d = 0.89, a large effect, with the adjusted difference "3.12 points corresponding to 9.2 % change on the 34-point scale" and Wald test p < 0.001.

> "In the ITT-AT analysis, the means were 26.0 for the script group and 29.1 for the GbEl group with a Cohen's d effect size of 0.89."
