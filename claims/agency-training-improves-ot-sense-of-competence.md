# Among occupational therapists, the self-reported proportion not feeling competent to act as change agents fell from 52.4% before to 14.3% after a seven-hour change-agent training in one uncontrolled pre-post study

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After training, the self-reported proportion of occupational therapists not feeling competent to act as change agents dropped from 52.4% to 14.3%, a statistically significant change. [→ Carrier Annie 2024](#carrier-annie-2024)

## Evidence

### Carrier Annie 2024

Carrier Annie, Éthier Alexandra, Beaudoin Michaël, Hudon Anne, Bédard Denis, Jasmin Emmanuelle, Lavoie-Trudeau Étienne, Verville France. (2024). Agency: occupational therapists’ perspectives on their sense of competence and their skills following training. Canadian Medical Education Journal. https://doi.org/10.36834/cmej.78346

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre-post self-administered questionnaire with 103 occupational therapists attending one of seven training days; McNemar test on Likert items showed the shift from 54 (52.4%) not confident pre-training to 14 (14.3%) post-training, "p < 0,001". Perception-based, no effect size printed.

> "Avant d’entamer la formation, la plupart des ergothérapeutes indiquent ne pas se sentir compétentes pour agir en tant qu’AC (n = 54; 52,4 %; Tableau 2). Or, après la formation, seulement 14 (14,3 %) ergothérapeutes ne se sentent toujours par compétentes (p < 0,001)."
