# In a Stern-Gerlach question, the most common student difficulty was assuming no beam splitting occurs because the magnetic field gradient direction and the spin state are 'orthogonal'

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students incorrectly related the direction of the magnetic field in real space with the direction of state vectors in Hilbert space, predicting no splitting for a |↑z⟩ beam in a −x-direction field gradient. [→ Chandralekha Singh and Emily Marshman 2016](#chandralekha-singh-and-emily-marshman-2016)

## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh and Emily Marshman 2016

Chandralekha Singh and Emily Marshman. (2016). Review of Student Difficulties in Upper-Level Quantum Mechanics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020117](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020117)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Multi-university study reviewed (Ref. [24]): a beam of electrons in spin state |↑z⟩ propagating along y was sent through an apparatus with a horizontal magnetic field gradient in the −x-direction; students sketched the expected phosphor screen pattern. The correct answer is two spots from splitting along x.

> "The most common difficulty was assuming that there should not be any splitting since the magnetic field gradient (in the −x-direction) and the spin state (an eigenstate of ˆSz) are orthogonal to each other."
