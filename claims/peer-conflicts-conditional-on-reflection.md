# Peer conflicts did not always produce conceptual change; they appeared to work only for students prepared to reflect on and reconstruct their conceptions

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Tao 1997

Tao, Ping-Kee; Gunstone, Richard F. (1997). Conceptual Change in Science through Collaborative Learning at the Computer. Paper presented at the Annual Meeting of the National Association for Research in Science Teaching (70th, Oak Brook, IL, March 21-24, 1997). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED406151

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Analysis of peer-conflict instances across the 7 dyads (Table 4), where the number of conflicts ranged from 0 to 8. Sid, Colin and Nigel achieved substantial conceptual change without peer conflicts, while Mick, Terry and Don experienced conflicts but showed no conceptual change.

> "Peer conflicts appeared to have impact on some students but not on others, and some students achieved conceptual change without experiencing any peer conflicts at all.It appeared that the crucial factor was whether students were prepared to reflect on and reconstruct their conceptions."
