# Higher outdoor active play at age 8 years is favorably associated with executive function and academic achievement, whereas high indoor active play is unfavorably associated

[doi:10.3389/fpubh.2024.1412634](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2024.1412634)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Adjusted regression models (Table 4, Model 2) in the GUSTO cohort printed positive associations for medium and high outdoor active play tertiles versus low, with the high-tertile academic estimate not significant [0.204 (−0.040, 0.448)]. No standardized effect size was printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "compared to children who engaged in low levels of outdoor active play at age 8 years, those who engaged in medium and/or high levels had higher mean executive function [0.201 (0.022, 0.380) and 0.211 (0.023, 0.399) z-scores] and higher mean academic achievement [0.273 (0.039, 0.507) and 0.204 (−0.040, 0.448) z-scores]"

[doi:10.3389/fpubh.2024.1412634](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2024.1412634)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same adjusted models printed −0.266 (−0.455, −0.076) for executive function and −0.250 (−0.500, 0.001) for academic achievement for high versus low indoor active play; the academic estimate was borderline (p = 0.051). Indoor and outdoor play at age 5.5 years showed no associations. No standardized effect size was printed.

> "compared to children who engaged in low levels of indoor active play at age 8 years, those who engaged in high levels had lower mean executive function [−0.266 (−0.455, −0.076) z-score] and academic achievement [−0.250 (−0.500, 0.001) z-score]"
