## Evidence

### Sanna Järvelä 2023

Sanna Järvelä, Carolyn P. Rosé. (2023). Reflecting on what counts as collaboration: Reaching forward without losing what is behind. International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning, 18, 457–464. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-023-09415-y

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The editorial reports, citing Reich-Stiebert, Voltmer, Raimann and Stürmer, a pretest–posttest study in a nine-week CSCL course with 1,525 freshmen receiving distance education, randomly assigned to 343 CSCL groups; heterogeneous-group students showed lower gains.

> "Independent of their own language status, students in CSCL groups featuring first-language heterogeneity exhibited lower precourse–postcourse gains in online interaction self-efficacy than students in groups without heterogeneity."

### Sanna Järvelä 2023 (2)

Sanna Järvelä, Carolyn P. Rosé. (2023). Reflecting on what counts as collaboration: Reaching forward without losing what is behind. International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning, 18, 457–464. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-023-09415-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The editorial reports the moderation finding from the same study: time on task-related communication in the initial collaboration phase moderated the heterogeneity–self-efficacy relationship, consistent with a theoretically derived moderation model. Get-to-know-each-other communication time "was ineffective as a significant moderator" and paradoxically increased perceived heterogeneity.

> "the relationships were significant when little time was spent on it but not when a great deal of time was spent on it"
