Evidence¶

Yanghee Kim · Michael Tscholl 2021¶

Yanghee Kim · Michael Tscholl. (2021). Young children's embodied interactions with a social robot. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-021-09978-3

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Qualitative analysis of ten pairs of children in a robot-child-child triad in a public elementary school media lab over six sessions. When the robot asked questions such as "How big is a cat?", children's responses integrally used speech, gestures, and movement.

"First, children embodied their explanations of physical and mathematical properties, such as height, size or speed of an animal."

Yanghee Kim · Michael Tscholl 2021 (2)¶

Yanghee Kim · Michael Tscholl. (2021). Young children's embodied interactions with a social robot. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-021-09978-3

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Case example from Study Two: asked whether a humanoid robot image is an animal, a child answered no, crouched to crawl on four limbs, then stood in a penguin position to qualify that some animals stand on two feet. The authors read this as embodied reasoning across several ideas in sequence.

"We interpret this sequence of embodiments as displaying a reasoning sequence involving the detection of and recovery from an overgeneralization."
