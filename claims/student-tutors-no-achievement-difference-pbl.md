# Students taught by rigorously selected and well trained student tutors show no significant achievement differences from students taught by staff tutors in first-year PBL courses

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across four courses and two cohorts, ANOVA found no significant differences in end-of-course exam achievement between the student tutor and staff tutor conditions, with all effect sizes small. [→ Catherine De Rijdt 2012](#catherine-de-rijdt-2012)

## Evidence

### Catherine De Rijdt 2012

Catherine De Rijdt, Janine van der Rijt, Filip Dochy, Cees van der Vleuten. (2012). Rigorously selected and well trained senior student tutors in problem based learning: student perceptions and study achievements. Instructional Science, 40, 397-411. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-011-9173-6

`q2 · i?`

Quasi-experimental comparison in a first-year PBL law curriculum: students were randomly assigned to staff versus student tutor conditions in four courses across two cohorts, and achievement was measured by end-of-course exam grades. The article reports "not significant" results with small effect sizes; no standardized effect size value is printed for the pooled finding.

> "The differences in achievement between students guided by a student tutor and those guided by a staff tutor are not significant and all effect sizes are small (d B 50) (Table 4)."
