# Spatial skills uniquely predict 27% of the variance in preschool mathematics over and above executive function

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Entering TOSA and VMI after the EF measures significantly added 27.1% of variance (adj. R2 = .271) to prediction of WIAT math scores, leading the authors to reject the null that spatial skills add nothing beyond EF. [→ Verdine 2014](#verdine-2014)

## Evidence

### Verdine 2014

Verdine, B. N., Irwin, C. M., Golinkoff, R. M., & Hirsh-Pasek, K. (2014). Contributions of Executive Function and Spatial Skills to Preschool Mathematics Achievement. Journal of Experimental Child Psychology, 126, 37-51. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2014.02.012

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Step-2 change statistic of the final hierarchical regression in the same 44-child longitudinal sample. The spatial block's unique contribution was adj. R2 = .271, F(2, 39) = 19.82, p < .001; the authors state they "reject the null hypothesis that spatial skills do not predict early mathematics achievement over and above EF alone."

> "To determine the unique contribution of spatial skills, the TOSA and VMI scores were then entered in the second step of the regression, and significantly added to the variance accounted for on the WIAT ( adj. R2 = .271, F(2, 39) = 19.82, p = <.001)"
