## Evidence

### Sejer EPF 2024

Sejer EPF, Ladelund AK, Bruun FJ, Slavensky JA, Mortensen EL, Kesmodel US. (2024). Preterm birth and subsequent intelligence and academic performance in youth: A cohort study. Acta Obstet Gynecol Scand. https://doi.org/10.1111/aogs.14796

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Linear regression of BPP conscription test scores in the Danish cohort (Table 4), adjusted for the same confounder set. The article reports adjusted score differences of −5.13, −2.73, and −0.76 with 95% confidence intervals; descriptive means were 36.87 (extremely preterm) vs. 42.75 (term).

> "Statistically significant differences in intelligence were observed with BPP scores −5.13 (95% confidence interval − 6.21 to −4.05) in the extremely preterm group, −2.73 (−3.15 to −2.30) in the very preterm group, and − 0.76 (−0.91 to −0.62) in the moderate to late preterm group compared to the term group after adjusting for potential confounders."
