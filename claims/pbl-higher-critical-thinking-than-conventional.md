# Grade-ten students taught with problem-based learning achieved higher mathematics critical thinking scores than students taught with conventional learning

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students who acquired PBL scored significantly higher on the mathematics critical thinking test than students who acquired conventional learning (PBL mean 37.7, MCC achievement 80%; CL mean 32.57, MCC achievement 50%). [→ Widyatiningtyas et al. 2015](#widyatiningtyas-et-al-2015)

## Evidence

### Widyatiningtyas et al. 2015

Reviandari Widyatiningtyas, Yaya S. Kusumah, Utari Sumarmo, Jozua Sabandar. (2015). The Impact of Problem-Based Learning Approach to Senior High School Students' Mathematics Critical Thinking Ability. IndoMS-JME, Volume 6, No. 2, July 2015, pp. 30-38. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22IndoMS-JME%2C+Volume+6%2C+No.+2%2C+July+2015%2C+pp.+30-38%22

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Only-posttest static-group comparison of 140 grade-ten students (70 PBL, 70 CL) from one excellent and one moderate school; classes were existing, non-randomly selected. Descriptive results show PBL mean "37. 7" with 80% MCC achievement versus CL mean 32.57 with 50%. The t-test comparison is reported as significant without a printed test statistic or effect size.

> "The data processing an average score of students' critical thinking skills and PBL 37. 7 percentage MCC achievement by 80%, the average score of 32. 57 and less CL MCC percentage achieved by 50% has been obtained."
