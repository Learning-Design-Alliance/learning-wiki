`q2 i?` Subjects using visual metaphors had greater pre-to-post improvements in confidence on four of the five self-efficacy questions, but began with consistently lower pre-intervention confidence ratings than the text-based group. [→ Kevin Baron 2024](#kevin-baron-2024)

## Evidence

### Kevin Baron 2024

Kevin Baron. (2024). Comparing Visual Metaphors with Textual Code For Learning Basic Computer Science Concepts in Virtual Reality. UW iSchool, March 2024. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2407.11975](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2407.11975)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Results from identical pre/post five-question self-efficacy questionnaires (1-7 confidence scale) for the two three-subject groups. Group A had higher post-intervention average ratings, but smaller shifts because of its higher pre-intervention ratings, except on question 1 about identifying basic data types.

> "when averaged together, subjects from group B had greater improvements in self-efficacy, or confidence, for four out of the five questions answered. Between the groups, subjects in group B had consistently lower confidence ratings in the pre-intervention questionnaire than subjects from group A."
