# Five null studies with spacing but no rest and dissimilar materials showed neither spacing nor interleaving effects

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Five studies that used spacing between different concepts with no rest-from-deliberate-learning and very dissimilar materials found neither a spacing effect nor an interleaving effect. [→ Ouhao Chen & Fred Paas & John Sweller 2021](#ouhao-chen-fred-paas-john-sweller-2021)

## Evidence

### Ouhao Chen & Fred Paas & John Sweller 2021

Ouhao Chen & Fred Paas & John Sweller. (2021). Spacing and Interleaving Effects Require Distinct Theoretical Bases: a Systematic Review Testing the Cognitive Load and Discriminative-Contrast Hypotheses. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09613-w

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Review analysis of five null-effect studies, including Ostrow and Heffernan (2015) with knowledgeable learners and De Croock and Van Merriënboer (2007) with dissimilar troubleshooting scenarios. The review reports "There were five studies that (1)all used spacing between different concepts or skills with no rest-from-deliberate-learning, resulting in no spacing effect."

> "There were five studies that (1)all used spacing between different concepts or skills with no rest-from-deliberate-learning, resulting in no spacing effect; (2) all used materials that were very dissimilar, resulting in no need to contrast and discriminate materials and so finding no interleaving effect."
