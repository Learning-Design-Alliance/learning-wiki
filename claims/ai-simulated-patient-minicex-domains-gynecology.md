## Subclaims
`q3 i2` AI-group students scored higher on medical interviewing skills (7.70 vs 7.25, d = 0.69) and organization/efficiency (7.60 vs 7.10, d = 0.88). [→ Gao Lingling 2026](#gao-lingling-2026)
`q3 i?` No statistically significant differences were observed in professionalism, clinical judgment, counseling skills, or overall clinical competence; this does not show the AI practice had no effect on them, and the study notes it may have been underpowered to detect differences. ## Evidence

### Gao Lingling 2026

Gao Lingling, Zhang Li, Zhang Yanxin, Wu Min, Xu Jianbo, Lu Dan. (2026). Utilizing generative artificial intelligence to create simulated patient for history taking in gynecology. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09219-2

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Gynecology-specific Mini-CEX on a 9-point scale, graded by two independent faculty examiners in the RCT. The trial reports higher interviewing skills with d = 0.69 and organization/efficiency 7.60 vs 7.10 (p = 0.0092, d = 0.88).

> "Students in the AI group achieved significantly higher scores in medical interviewing skills compared with the control group (7.70 ± 0.66 vs. 7.25 ± 0.64, p = 0.0342, Cohen’s d = 0.69)."

### Gao Lingling 2026 (2)

Gao Lingling, Zhang Li, Zhang Yanxin, Wu Min, Xu Jianbo, Lu Dan. (2026). Utilizing generative artificial intelligence to create simulated patient for history taking in gynecology. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09219-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Null Mini-CEX domains in the same RCT: the trial reports "No statistically significant differences" across four domains; no effect sizes were printed for these comparisons, and the study may have been underpowered for them.

> "No statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups in the domains of “Humanistic qualities/professionalism” (p = 0.3572), “Clinical judgment” (p = 0.6391), “Counseling Skills” (p = 0.6041), or “Overall Clinical Competence” (p = 0.7739)."
