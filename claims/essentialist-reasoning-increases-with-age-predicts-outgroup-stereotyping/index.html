Pauker 2010¶

Pauker, K., Ambady, N., & Apfelbaum, E. P. (2010). Race Salience and Essentialist Thinking in Racial Stereotype Development. Child Development, 81(6), 1799–1813. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2010.01511.x

q3 · i3 · associational · r2

Chi-square analyses comparing the frequency of reasoning types (none, superficial, essentialist, idiosyncratic) across four age groups in 68 subjects, reported in the Essentialist Reasoning subsection. Reasoning type "changed across age groups" for both self and other judgments with large printed effect sizes, ϕ = .56 and ϕ = .68; older children primarily used essentialist reasoning (e.g., 6–7-year-olds: Mself = 78.6%, Mother = 85.7%).

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Children begin applying racial stereotypes to out-groups around age 6, with out-group stereotyping increasing across age groups while in-group stereotyping shows no reliable age differences — related
  • Racial justification (merely noticing and describing racial differences) did not uniquely predict out-group stereotyping, unlike functional sorting by race — related
  • Sorting by race, racial constancy for self, and racial constancy for others uniquely predicted out-group stereotyping in a hierarchical regression controlling for age, sex, ethnicity, and classification skill — related
  • Children in the two oldest age groups used out-group stereotypes above chance (50%), with the largest effect among 7–10-year-olds (r = .80) — related
