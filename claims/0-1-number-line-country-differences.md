# On 0-1 fraction number line estimation, Belgian and Chinese students estimate far more accurately than U.S. students, with no Country × Grade interaction

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Country differences in 0-1 number line estimation accuracy were large (partial eta² = .20), with Chinese mean PAE = 6%, Belgian 7%, U.S. 16%. [→ Torbeyns 2015](#torbeyns-2015)
`q3 i2` 8th graders estimated more accurately than 6th graders (PAE 7% vs 12%, partial eta² = .06). [→ Torbeyns 2015](#torbeyns-2015)

## Evidence

### Torbeyns 2015

Torbeyns, J., Schneider, M., Xin, Z., & Siegler, R. S. (2015). Bridging the Gap: Fraction Understanding is Central to Mathematics Achievement in Students from Three Different Continents. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2014.03.002

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

A 3 (Country) × 2 (Grade) ANOVA on percentage absolute error in the 187-student cross-cultural study showed a large main effect of Country with printed effect size partial eta² = .20; Belgian accuracy (mean PAE = 7%) was as high as Chinese and much higher than U.S.

> "Belgian, Chinese, and U.S. students’ estimation accuracy differed, F(2, 186) = 22.67, p < .01, partial eta² = .20. Chinese students were much more accurate than U.S. students, mean PAE = 6% and 16%."
