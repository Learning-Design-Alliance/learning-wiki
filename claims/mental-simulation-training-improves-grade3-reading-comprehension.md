# Mental simulation training improved general reading comprehension in Grade 3, with a significant training group × time interaction varying by grade

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q2` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Third graders who received mental simulation training showed a larger reading comprehension gain than third graders in the control training group. [→ Björn B. de Koning 2017](#bjorn-b-de-koning-2017)
`q2 i2` The training group × time interaction on reading comprehension was significant (ηp² = 0.05) and varied as a function of grade. [→ Björn B. de Koning 2017 (2)](#bjorn-b-de-koning-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Björn B. de Koning 2017

Björn B. de Koning, Lisanne T. Bos, Stephanie I. Wassenburg & Menno van der Schoot. (2017). Effects of a Reading Strategy Training Aimed at Improving Mental Simulation in Primary School Children. Educational Psychology Review, 29, 869–889. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-016-9380-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pretest-posttest control group study of 143 third and fourth graders from five Dutch primary schools, with classes (not individual children) randomly assigned to training groups. Gain-score analysis showed "third graders in the mental simulation training showed a larger gain in reading comprehension scores than third graders in the control training group"; the printed t(30) = 0.14, p = 0.01.

> "Analysis of the gain scores showed that third graders in the mental simulation training showed a larger gain in reading comprehension scores than third graders in the control training group"

### Björn B. de Koning 2017 (2)

Björn B. de Koning, Lisanne T. Bos, Stephanie I. Wassenburg & Menno van der Schoot. (2017). Effects of a Reading Strategy Training Aimed at Improving Mental Simulation in Primary School Children. Educational Psychology Review, 29, 869–889. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-016-9380-4

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

A 2 × 2 × 2 ANOVA on CITO reading comprehension proficiency scores with time within-subject and training group and grade between-subject factors. The article reports "the training group × time interaction was significant" with F(1,92) = 5.12, p = 0.03, ηp² = 0.05, and that it varied by grade (F(1,92) = 4.55, p = 0.04, ηp² = 0.05).

> "the training group × time interaction was significant ( F(1,92) = 5.12, p = 0.03, ηp 2 = 0.05), and this interaction appeared to vary as a function of grade"
