# The benefit of preliminary VR training was limited to questions posed in VR, with no improvement on 2D computer-based posttest questions

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` Trained students scored significantly higher on questions posed in VR at the midpoint of instruction (d = 0.22, p = 0.02), roughly 16% above untrained students, but the two groups scored similarly on non-VR posttest questions. [→ C. D. Porter 2020](#c-d-porter-2020)

## Evidence

### C. D. Porter 2020

C. D. Porter, J. R. H. Smith, E. M. Stagar, A. Simmons, M. Nieberding, C. M. Orban, J. Brown, A. Ayers. (2020). Using Virtual Reality in Electrostatics Instruction: The Impact of Training. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020119](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020119)

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Figure 6 analysis of the same randomized study comparing trained (N = 142) and untrained (N = 137) groups across four question sets posed in different media. "the large and statistically signiﬁcant (d = 0.22, p = 0.02) diﬀerence" was at the mid-VR questions; the untrained group scored about 16% lower there.

> "Perhaps the most interesting feature of Fig. 6 is the large and statistically signiﬁcant (d = 0.22, p = 0.02) diﬀerence between the two treatment groups’ scores on the questions posed in VR at the midpoint, during electrostatics instruction in VR."
