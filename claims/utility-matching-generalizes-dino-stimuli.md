## Evidence

### Ilia Sucholutsky 2024

Ilia Sucholutsky, Katherine M. Collins, Maya Malaviya, Nori Jacoby, Weiyang Liu, Theodore R. Sumers, Michalis Korakakis, Umang Bhatt, Mark Ho, Joshua B. Tenenbaum, Brad Love, Zachary A. Pardos, Adrian Weller, Thomas L. Griffiths. (2024). Representational Alignment Supports Effective Teaching. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2406.04302](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2406.04302)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Simulation generalization experiment repeating the two classroom pool types with dino stimuli (Tables 4 and 5), e.g., unstructured pool Utility 0.36 ± 0.01 versus Random 0.29 ± 0.00 and Min Err 0.35 ± 0.01 average accuracy. The authors state the curves "generalize nicely to different grid sizes and stimuli type"; no effect size is printed.

> "We find that our utility curves generalize nicely to different grid sizes and stimuli type, yielding student outcomes that on average appear to boost student accuracy particularly for the students in the top-performing group than baselines which do not account for representation misalignment (MOOC)."
