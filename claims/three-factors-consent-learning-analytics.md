## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Higher institutional trust, lower data-collection concern, and greater comfort with instructor data use each increase the likelihood of consenting. [→ Warren Li 2022](#warren-li-2022)

## Evidence

### Warren Li 2022

Warren Li, Kaiwen Sun, Florian Schaub, Christopher Brooks. (2022). Disparities in Students' Propensity to Consent to Learning Analytics. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education 32:564–608. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-021-00254-2

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression on 272 email respondents with feature selection (RFE with 20-fold CV and backwards elimination) identified "one’s trust in the institution, concern in the amount of personal data collected, and comfort with instructor use of data" as impactful factors (Table 3 reports coefficients and odds ratios).

> "BE indicates a subset of 3 factors that have an effect on the response variable and we consider the following to be impactful in students’ decision to consent: one’s trust in the institution, concern in the amount of personal data collected, and comfort with instructor use of data for instructional purposes."
