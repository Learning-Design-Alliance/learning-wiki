# Tobit models decompose the Head Start impact into a 48-percentage-point increase in probability of an observed quality score and a 2-point increase in quality conditional on observation

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Random assignment to Head Start increased the probability of having an observed quality score by 48 percentage points and, conditional on observation, increased quality of care by about 2 points. [→ Friedman-Krauss 2013](#friedman-krauss-2013)

## Evidence

### Friedman-Krauss 2013

Friedman-Krauss, A. H., Connors, M. C., & Morris, P. A. (2013). Estimating impacts of treatment random assignment on classroom quality in the Head Start Impact Study: The problem of missing data. SREE Spring 2013 Conference. https://www.sree.org/conferences/2013s/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Tobit models used to parse the estimated impact of random assignment into a dichotomous impact on use of formal care and a continuous impact on quality. Table 3 reports marginal effects of 0.484 (probability of observed score) and 1.970 (quality given observed), both p<.001. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "Results of the Tobit analyses indicate that random assignment to Head Start increased the probability of having an observed quality score by 48 percentage points. Additionally, conditional on having an observed quality score, random assignment to Head Start increases the quality of care by 2 points"
