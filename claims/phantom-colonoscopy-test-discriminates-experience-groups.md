## Evidence

### Preisler Louise MD 2015

Preisler Louise MD, Svendsen Morten Bo Søndergaard MScEng, Nerup Nikolaj MD, Svendsen Lars Bo MD, DMSc, Konge Lars MD, PhD. (2015). Simulation-Based Training for Colonoscopy: Establishing Criteria for Competency. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000000440

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Results of the phantom-model test comparing novices with experienced endoscopists. The experienced group scored significantly higher ("2.48 (SD 1.09), P < 0.001") than the novices (0.32, SD 0.31); no effect size was printed. Participants failing a case were scored at the 15-minute maximum.

> "The mean aggregated score per minute in the novice group was 0.32 (SD 0.31) and for the experienced group was 2.48 (SD 1.09), P < 0.001 (Figure 2A)—ICC, average measures = 0.87, single measure = 0.77."

### Preisler Louise MD 2015 (2)

Preisler Louise MD, Svendsen Morten Bo Søndergaard MScEng, Nerup Nikolaj MD, Svendsen Lars Bo MD, DMSc, Konge Lars MD, PhD. (2015). Simulation-Based Training for Colonoscopy: Establishing Criteria for Competency. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000000440

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Completion-rate analysis of the phantom-model test's two tasks. Case failures were due to the cutoff time, and the authors note cecal intubation rates correlate with experience. All 10 experienced participants completed both tasks.

> "In the easy case, 1 novice, and in the more difficult case, 6 novices failed to reach the cecum. All experienced participants completed both the cases."
