## Evidence

### Daker 2021

Daker, R.J., Gattas, S.U., Sokolowski, H.M., Green, A.E., & Lyons, I.M. (2021). First-year students' math anxiety predicts STEM avoidance and underperformance throughout university, independently of math ability. npj Science of Learning 6:17. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00095-7

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Moderation regression for % STEM Courses with the same covariates as the primary models; the printed interaction effect size was "Cohen’s d = −.061", negligible, so no moderation of the participation outcome.

> "non-STEM Grades did not moderate the relation between Math Anxiety and % STEM Courses ( β = −.025, t(173) = −.40, p = .687, Cohen’s d = −.061)"

### Daker 2021 (2)

Daker, R.J., Gattas, S.U., Sokolowski, H.M., Green, A.E., & Lyons, I.M. (2021). First-year students' math anxiety predicts STEM avoidance and underperformance throughout university, independently of math ability. npj Science of Learning 6:17. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00095-7

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Moderation regression for STEM Grades in the same sample: the printed interaction was "β = −.140, t(173) = −2.90, p = .004, Cohen’s d = −.441"; among students 1 SD above the mean in non-STEM Grades, high math-anxious students were predicted to score 8.48 points worse than low math-anxious peers.

> "but did moderate the relation between Math Anxiety and STEM Grades ( β = −.140, t(173) = −2.90, p = .004, Cohen’s d = −.441)"
