## Evidence

### Jungck John R. 2010

Jungck John R., Gaff Holly, Weisstein Anton E. (2010). Mathematical Manipulative Models: In Defense of “Beanbag Biology”. CBE-Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.10-03-0040

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Workshop demonstration of the Pólya urn scheme described by Cohen (1976), regularly performed by Lou Gross at conferences: participants predict random fluctuation, observe drift toward one color, and pooled results show a uniform limiting distribution. No effect sizes or participant counts are printed.

> "Once the experiment is complete, however, combining data across all participants reveals a uniform distribution: all physically possible proportions of green vs. blue beads are equally likely."

### Jungck John R. 2010 (2)

Jungck John R., Gaff Holly, Weisstein Anton E. (2010). Mathematical Manipulative Models: In Defense of “Beanbag Biology”. CBE-Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.10-03-0040

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

The authors' interpretation of the urn demonstration: most participants initially predict fluctuating frequencies, revise predictions as their cups drift, and the pooled uniform result contradicts their expectations. This is an observational account from workshop experience, not a controlled study.

> "Comparing participants' initial predictions to their results reinforces a final feature of manipulative models: the ability to surprise. A seemingly simple game can yield insight into counterintuitive results that students would otherwise reject as implausible."
