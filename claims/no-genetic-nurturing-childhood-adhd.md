# No genetic nurturing affects childhood ADHD symptoms: the non-transmitted ADHD PGS is unrelated to all outcomes, with power (0.80) to detect R² of 0.7–1.6%

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The non-transmitted ADHD PGS was not associated with any childhood outcome, indicating parental ADHD-related genes did not shape the rearing environment in a way affecting children's development. [→ de Zeeuw Eveline L. 2020](#de-zeeuw-eveline-l-2020)
`q2 i?` Power analyses showed 0.80 power to detect non-transmitted PGS effects explaining 1.6%, 0.7% and 0.9% of variance in achievement, ADHD symptoms at home and at school respectively. [→ de Zeeuw Eveline L. 2020 (2)](#de-zeeuw-eveline-l-2020-2)

## Evidence

### de Zeeuw Eveline L. 2020

de Zeeuw Eveline L., Hottenga Jouke-Jan, Ouwens Klaasjan G., Dolan Conor V., Ehli Erik A., Davies Gareth E., Boomsma Dorret I., van Bergen Elsje. (2020). Intergenerational Transmission of Education and ADHD: Effects of Parental Genotypes. Behavior Genetics, Volume 50, Issue 4. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10519-020-09992-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Across the childhood regressions (model 2), the non-transmitted ADHD PGS "was not associated with any of the outcomes"; the authors read this as the ADHD-related parental environment not affecting children's development. No effect size for the null is quoted in this sentence.

> "The non-transmitted ADHD PGS was not associated with any of the outcomes, indicating that the environment that parents provided to their children based on the genes that play a role in their own ADHD symptoms did not affect their children’s development (see Fig. 2b)."

### de Zeeuw Eveline L. 2020 (2)

de Zeeuw Eveline L., Hottenga Jouke-Jan, Ouwens Klaasjan G., Dolan Conor V., Ehli Erik A., Davies Gareth E., Boomsma Dorret I., van Bergen Elsje. (2020). Intergenerational Transmission of Education and ADHD: Effects of Parental Genotypes. Behavior Genetics, Volume 50, Issue 4. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10519-020-09992-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Power analysis based on effective sample sizes accounting for family clustering: "sufficient power (0.80) to detect an R2 explained by the non-transmitted PGS of 1.6%, 0.7% and 0.9%" for achievement, ADHD symptoms at home and at school, strengthening confidence in the null genetic-nurturing results.

> "The analyses indicated that there was sufficient power (0.80) to detect an R2 explained by the non-transmitted PGS of 1.6%, 0.7% and 0.9% for, respectively, academic achievement, ADHD symptoms at home and ADHD symptoms at school (Fig. 3)."
