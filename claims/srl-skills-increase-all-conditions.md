# Self-reported SRL questionnaire scores rose over a five-week goal-setting intervention in all conditions, with no differences between conditions

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Self-reported SRL questionnaire (OSLQ) scores rose from before to after the five-week intervention (time effect η2p = .116), with no main effect of condition and no time-by-condition interaction; since every condition received the goal-setting intervention and there was no untreated group, the rise cannot be attributed to the intervention. ## Evidence

### Martins van Jaarsveld 2025

Martins van Jaarsveld, G., Wong, J., Baars, M., Specht, M., & Paas, F. (2025). Enhancing goal attainment in higher education with a scripted conversational agent: Effects of monitoring and reflection support in digital learning. Computers and Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2025.105441

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Randomised trial with 84 undergraduates at one Dutch university, in which all four conditions received weekly goal setting from a scripted conversational agent, with or without added monitoring and reflection prompts. SRL was measured by self-report on four OSLQ subscales (goal setting, task strategies, time management, self-evaluation) before and after the intervention, so the outcome is self-reported SRL, not measured skill. A mixed-design ANOVA found a time effect (F(1, 80) = 10.453, p = .002, η2p = .116) and no condition effect (F(3, 80) = 0.639, p = .592) or interaction (F(3, 80) = 0.607, p = .612); the article states the trend "was the same for all conditions". Every condition shared the goal-setting intervention and there was no untreated group measured over time, so the rise cannot be attributed to the intervention rather than to time, repeated measurement or the semester.

> "Results revealed a significant main effect of time, F(1, 80) = 10.453, p = .002, η2p = .116, a moderate effect, indicating that SRL skills increased during the intervention."
