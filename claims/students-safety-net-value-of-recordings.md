## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students who worked, fell ill, were carers, or had mental health problems or disabilities described recordings as relieving the stress of missing lectures and keeping them on top of studies. [→ MacKay 2018](#mackay-2018)

## Evidence

### MacKay 2018

MacKay, J. R. D. (2018). Show and ‘Tool’: How lecture recording transforms staff and student perspectives on lectures in higher education. University of Edinburgh. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2019.05.019

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Free-text survey responses from first-year students. A student carer reported that access to missed lectures 'has allowed me to keep on top of my studies... without the worry and anxiety of missing vital topics/lectures'; the safety net idea was prevalent among carers and students with access challenges.

> "When lectures are made available to view at a later date, they can be very helpful and even crucial for people with mental health problems or disabilities that make attending *every* lecture difficult."
