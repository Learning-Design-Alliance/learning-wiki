# Learning gains vary by Moon phases topic but do not differ significantly across conditions

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Broken down by question topic, pre-to-post scores showed some variation between conditions but no statistically or practically significant differences (Fisher's exact test p-values greater than 0.1 for all pre/post scores by question). [→ J. H. Madden 2019](#j-h-madden-2019)
`q3 i?` Some topic areas showed gains over 40% consistent across condition (orbit period, phase period, illumination) while others showed gains less than 10% (scale, Moon rotation, phase diagram, rise/set time). [→ J. H. Madden 2019](#j-h-madden-2019)

## Evidence

### J. H. Madden 2019

J. H. Madden, A. S. Won, J. P. Schuldt, B. Kim, S. Pandita, Y. Sun, T. J. Stone, and N. G. Holmes. (2019). Virtual Reality as a Teaching Tool for Moon Phases and Beyond. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1119/perc.2018.pr.madden](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2018.pr.madden)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Per-question analysis of the randomized three-condition study (Fig. 2). Gains over 40% appeared for orbit period, phase period, and illumination; gains under 10% for scale, Moon rotation, phase diagram, and rise/set time; a negative shift occurred on the "Why phases occur" question.

> "Upon being broken down by question the pre- to post-test scores show some variation between conditions but no statistically or practically signiﬁcant differences (Fig. 2). A Fisher’s exact test gives p-values greater than 0.1 for all pre/post scores by question."
