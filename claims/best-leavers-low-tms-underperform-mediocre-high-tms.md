## Evidence

### Kadmon Guni 2016

Kadmon Guni, Kadmon Martina. (2016). Academic Performance of Students with the Highest and Mediocre School-leaving Grades: Does the Aptitude Test for Medical Studies (TMS) Balance Their Prognoses? GMS Journal for Medical Education 33(1):Doc7. [doi:10.3205/zma001006](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001006)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Study 3 comparison against best school leavers who had not taken the TMS found the differences with mediocre leavers were not significant overall, but 27 non-takers (31%) averaged below the mediocre high-TMS group's mean and 18 (20%) fell below its 25th percentile (Figure 2).

> "In fact, the mean performance of 27 best school leavers without TMS (31%) was lower in the pre-clinical examinations than the mean performance of the mediocre school leavers with TMS scores >105. Furthermore, 18 of the former (20%) performed less well than the 25 percentile of the latter (see Figure 2 (Fig. 2), Point A)."
