# In a randomized between-subject experiment, MOOC learners using PeerCollab reported significantly higher sense of belonging on all six scale items than learners using the standard MOOC forum.

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` PeerCollab participants reported significantly more sense of belonging than control forum participants on each of the six adapted scale items, with the largest differences for mattering to others and happiness in the group. ## Evidence

### Dilrukshi Gamage and Mark E Whiting 2021

Dilrukshi Gamage and Mark E Whiting. 2021. Together we learn better: leveraging communities of practice for MOOC learners. In Asian CHI Symposium 2021, May 8–13, 2021, Yokohama, Japan. ACM, New York, NY, USA. https://doi.org/10.1145/3429360.3468176

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized between-subject experiment (n=22) comparing the same Coursera course taken with PeerCollab versus the built-in forum; sense of belonging measured post-course with an adapted 6-item validated scale. The article reports "mean differences significant for each item" with all two-sided permutation t-test p-values ≤ 0.01 (Table 1).

> "The PeerCollab condition saw significantly more sense of belonging, with mean differences significant for each item 1. The most notable effect comes from items 5 and 6 which reflect PeerCollab community members felt that they mattered to each other"
