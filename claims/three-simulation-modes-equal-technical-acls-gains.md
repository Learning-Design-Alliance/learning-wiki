## Evidence

### Yu Jiafeng 2026

Yu Jiafeng, Wang Qian, Yin Ling, Yuan Fang, Kan Yufei, Wang Zengkun, Yu Yonghao, Li Qing, Yu Yang. (2026). Prospective randomized controlled trial of computer simulation versus high-fidelity mannequin simulation in advanced cardiovascular life support (ACLS) training for anesthesiology novice clinicians. BMC Anesthesiology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12871-026-04080-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-group comparison in the randomized trial: Group C's megacode score rose from 14.24 ± 4.31 at T0 to 21.05 ± 3.39 at T1 (P < 0.001); the article reports the same pattern for Groups M and SM.

> "In Group C, the score at T1 (21.05 ± 3.39) was significantly higher than at T0 (14.24 ± 4.31) (P < 0.001) (Fig. 2A)."

### Yu Jiafeng 2026 (2)

Yu Jiafeng, Wang Qian, Yin Ling, Yuan Fang, Kan Yufei, Wang Zengkun, Yu Yonghao, Li Qing, Yu Yang. (2026). Prospective randomized controlled trial of computer simulation versus high-fidelity mannequin simulation in advanced cardiovascular life support (ACLS) training for anesthesiology novice clinicians. BMC Anesthesiology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12871-026-04080-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Between-group ANOVA comparison at baseline and immediately after training found no statistically significant differences in megacode scores among the three groups, so equivalence was not tested beyond non-significance.

> "Additionally, no statistically significant differences in megacode scores were identified between the three groups at either T0 or T1 (Supplementary Table 4, Fig. 2B)."
