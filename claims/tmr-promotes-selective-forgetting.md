## Evidence

### Julia Carbone & Susanne Diekelmann 2024

Julia Carbone & Susanne Diekelmann. (2024). An update on recent advances in targeted memory reactivation during sleep. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00244-8

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes this to Schechtman and colleagues, who cued to-be-forgotten vs to-be-remembered images during a nap. "memory performance was reduced for the to-be-forgotten images" associated with re-presented cues; no effect size printed.

> "During a subsequent afternoon nap, sound cues used to signal forgetting were presented again. After sleep, memory performance was reduced for the to-be-forgotten images associated with the presented cues relative to those cues not presented during sleep."

### Julia Carbone & Susanne Diekelmann 2024 (2)

Julia Carbone & Susanne Diekelmann. (2024). An update on recent advances in targeted memory reactivation during sleep. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00244-8

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes to Whitmore and colleagues the finding that manipulating cue intensity during sleep produced arousals, and "sound cues tha t induced sleep arousals selectively weakened the associated memories". No effect size printed.

> "They observed that reactivation with sound cues tha t induced sleep arousals selectively weakened the associated memories47."
