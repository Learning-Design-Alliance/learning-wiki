## Evidence

### Jere-Folotiya 2014

Jere-Folotiya, J., Chansa-Kabali, T., Munachaka, J. C., Sampa, F., Yalukanda, C., Westerholm, J., Richardson, U., Serpell, R., & Lyytinen, H. (2014). The effect of using a mobile literacy game to improve literacy levels of grade one students in Zambian schools. Education Tech Research Dev, 62, 417-436. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-014-9342-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized ANCOVA of posttest spelling scores with pretest as covariate in the RESUZ study of Lusaka grade one students (posttest N = 312). The article reports a statistically significant improvement for directly and indirectly exposed students versus control; no effect size is printed in this quote.

> "GraphoGame TM produced signiﬁcant improvements in the performance of the students who were exposed to it directly (when the individual student was exposed) or indirectly (when the teacher alone played the game) compared to students in the control group"

### Jere-Folotiya 2014 (2)

Jere-Folotiya, J., Chansa-Kabali, T., Munachaka, J. C., Sampa, F., Yalukanda, C., Westerholm, J., Richardson, U., Serpell, R., & Lyytinen, H. (2014). The effect of using a mobile literacy game to improve literacy levels of grade one students in Zambian schools. Education Tech Research Dev, 62, 417-436. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-014-9342-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA contrast across combined intervention groups (C, LG, LG+TG & LG+TIG, TG&TIG) with spelling pretest as covariate. The linear trend across groups ordered by exposure intensity was "statistically signiﬁcant"; no effect size is printed for this contrast.

> "Fig. 3 represents the order of estimated marginal means for the Spelling test, which were also tested against the linear trend and found to be statistically signiﬁcant (difference = 1.285, SE = .431, p\ .01)"
