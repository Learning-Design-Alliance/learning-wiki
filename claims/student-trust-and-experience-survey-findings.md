## Evidence

### Jeff Bale 2026

Jeff Bale, James Day, J. Ives, and D. A. Bonn. (2026). Constraints From Simulation Improve Experiential Outcomes in Laboratory Environment. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2605.30624](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.30624)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Open-ended survey question 4 was coded by two authors into three categories with 100% agreement; Table IV shows 68.2% of physical-apparatus students and 71.1% of simulation students trusted simulations more. No effect size reported.

> "both groups overwhelmingly claim to trust the results from simulation labs more than their analogous physical labs, with no difference in levels of trust between the two groups."

### Jeff Bale 2026 (2)

Jeff Bale, James Day, J. Ives, and D. A. Bonn. (2026). Constraints From Simulation Improve Experiential Outcomes in Laboratory Environment. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2605.30624](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.30624)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Likert survey results (Table V) show simulation users tilted toward finding the lab less difficult (Q6 median 4.21 vs 3.43), reported being able to do a bit more than usual (Q9 median 3.88 vs 3.07), and wanted more future simulations (Q8 median 3.76 vs 3.12); questions 5 and 7 showed little difference between groups.

> "Students who used simulations reported wanting “slightly more simulations than physical labs in the future,”"
