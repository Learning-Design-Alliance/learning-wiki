`q2 i?` Minoritized students were more likely than non-minoritized students to experience internet connectivity problems often or very often (20% vs. 12%), and lower-income students about twice as likely as high-income students (22% vs. 11%). [→ Means 2020](#means-2020)

## Evidence

### Means 2020

Means, B., Peters, V., Neisler, J., & Griffiths, R. (2020). STEM Courses During the COVID Pandemic: Lessons from Spring 2020. Digital Promise Global. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/7900cb79-6ea5-44d0-aad6-34aa81e9bf4b

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Weighted chi-square analyses of the national survey of 620 STEM students compared minoritized vs. non-minoritized groups and income bands, finding "20%" vs. "12%" and "22%" vs. "11%" for frequent connectivity problems; hardware/software problems followed the same pattern (11% combined Hispanic/Black vs. 5% White).

> "students from minoritized race/ethnicity groups were more likely to experience internet connectivity problems often or very often (20%) compared to students from non-minoritized groups (12%). Students from lower-income households (annual incomes under $50,000) were also twice as likely to experience internet connectivity problems often or very often (22%) compared to students from households earning $100,000 or more (11%)."

### Means 2021

Means, B., Peters, V., Neisler, J., Wiley, K., & Grifths, R. (2021). Lessons from remote learning during COVID-19. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/116

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same national student survey (n = 1,008, weighted sample 23% Hispanic, 15% Black, 37% under $50,000 income). The survey found minoritized students more likely to experience connectivity problems "often or very often (22%)" versus 12% of non-minoritized students; hardware/software problems showed the same pattern (12% vs. 5%).

> "were more likely to experience internet connectivity problems often or very often (22%) compared to students from non-minoritized groups (12%)."
