## Evidence

### Yossi Ben-Zion 2025

Yossi Ben-Zion, Jianqiang Liu, Chun-Ming Wang, Atanu Rajak, and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2025). Global Blind Spot in Understanding Trigonometric Derivatives: A Multinational Analysis. [doi:10.1103/t2fv-2spd](https://doi.org/10.1103/t2fv-2spd)

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

One-way ANOVA comparing confidence (5-point Likert) across Q4 response groups in the 769-student survey. The article reports F(2, 766) = 12.35, p < .001, η2 = 0.03; mean confidence was 3.78 (radians), 3.76 (both), 2.96 (degrees).

> "The results indicated a signifi- cant difference between the response groups( F (2, 766) = 12.35, p < .001, η2 = 0.03), suggesting that students' con- fidence levels vary depending on their conceptual under- standing."

### Yossi Ben-Zion 2025 (2)

Yossi Ben-Zion, Jianqiang Liu, Chun-Ming Wang, Atanu Rajak, and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2025). Global Blind Spot in Understanding Trigonometric Derivatives: A Multinational Analysis. [doi:10.1103/t2fv-2spd](https://doi.org/10.1103/t2fv-2spd)

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Multivariate linear regression predicting confidence (overall model R2 = 0.258, F(13, 635) = 16.989, p < .001). The article reports β = 0.623, p = 0.005 for both-units versus radians, and β = −1.114, p < .001 for explicit uncertainty.

> "students who incorrectly selected "both are correct" as a valid unit for the Harmonic Oscillator expression exhibited significantly higher confidence compared to those who correctly identified "radians" ( β = 0 .623, p= 0 .005)"
