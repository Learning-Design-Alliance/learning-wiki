## Evidence

### Fu Yangyang 2025

Fu Yangyang, Wang Qiuju, Wang Xiaofeng, Zhong Haoxuan, Chen Junqi, Fei Haoyu, Yao Yipeng, Xiao Yao, Li Wenfu, Li Na. (2025). Unlocking academic success: the impact of time management on college students' study engagement. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-025-02619-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Bootstrap mediation analysis (PROCESS model 6) in the survey of 1,016 college students. The article reports the chain path "through the “chain mediation of self-control and mobile phone dependence” was significant (β = 0.021, 95% CI [0.012, 0.032])", the smallest of the three indirect paths (5.26%).

> "The third indirect path through the “chain mediation of self-control and mobile phone dependence” was significant (β = 0.021, 95% CI [0.012, 0.032]), indicating that time management improves self-control, which reduces mobile phone dependence, ultimately leading to higher study engagement."

### Fu Yangyang 2025 (2)

Fu Yangyang, Wang Qiuju, Wang Xiaofeng, Zhong Haoxuan, Chen Junqi, Fei Haoyu, Yao Yipeng, Xiao Yao, Li Wenfu, Li Na. (2025). Unlocking academic success: the impact of time management on college students' study engagement. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-025-02619-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same bootstrap mediation analysis. The article reports the "total effect of time management on study engagement was significant (β = 0.399, 95% CI [0.314, 0.473])", with indirect effects carrying just over half of it, indicating partial mediation.

> "The total effect of time management on study engagement was significant (β = 0.399, 95% CI [0.314, 0.473]), with the indirect effects accounting for 51.37% of the total effect."
