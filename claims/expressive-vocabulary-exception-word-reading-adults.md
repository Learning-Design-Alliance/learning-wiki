## Evidence

### Hall 2012

Hall, R., Greenberg, D., Laures-Gore, J., & Pae, H. K. (2012). The Relationship Between Expressive Vocabulary Knowledge and Reading Skills for Adult Struggling Readers. Journal of Research in Reading. [doi:10.1111/j.1467-9817.2012.01537.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9817.2012.01537.x)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical regression predicting exception word reading (N = 232): nonword reading explained 9.7% and general word reading added 16.8% (total 26.5%); expressive vocabulary added a significant 1.8% at the final step.

> "When entered at the final step, expressive vocabulary accounted for a small but significant (p < .05) additional 1.8% of the variance (see Table 4)."

### Hall 2012 (2)

Hall, R., Greenberg, D., Laures-Gore, J., & Pae, H. K. (2012). The Relationship Between Expressive Vocabulary Knowledge and Reading Skills for Adult Struggling Readers. Journal of Research in Reading. [doi:10.1111/j.1467-9817.2012.01537.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9817.2012.01537.x)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical regressions with general word reading and nonword reading as dependent variables (N = 232) found no significant expressive vocabulary contribution; for nonword reading only general word reading entered at step 1 was significant (36.5% of variance).

> "Results indicated that expressive vocabulary did not contribute to variance in the adults' general word reading and nonword reading skills."
