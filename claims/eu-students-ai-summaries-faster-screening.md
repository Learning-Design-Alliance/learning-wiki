# For EU health-sciences students, answering questions from AI-generated short summaries took significantly less time than reading full abstracts in both use cases

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` EU students in the full-abstract group needed more time than the short-summary-only group in UC1 and UC2, and more time than the optional-full-abstract group in UC2 (F=13.466 and F=9.705, both p<0.001). [→ Gregor Stiglic 2023](#gregor-stiglic-2023)

## Evidence

### Gregor Stiglic 2023

Gregor Stiglic, Leon Kopitar, Zhe He, Primoz Kocbek, Lucija Gosak, Prithwish Chakraborty, Pablo Meyer, Jiang Bian. (2023). Improving Primary Healthcare Workflow Using Extreme Summarization of Scientific Literature Based on Generative AI. DSHealth at SIGKDD '23, August 6 – 10, 2023, Long Beach, CA. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2307.15715](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2307.15715)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized three-group human evaluation with 113 participants (75 EU); one-way ANOVA of mean time per use case. Table 1 reports UC1 means of 643.3 s (G1) vs 387.74 s (G3), "G1>G2, G1>G3".

> "Looking at EU students, a statistically significant difference in time needed was observed in UC1 (F=13.466, p<0.001) as well as UC2 (F=9.705, p<0.001), where post hoc pairwise comparisons using Tukey HSD showed that for both UC1 and UC2 the G1 group needs more time than G3 and in the case of UC2 the G1 group needs longer than G2."
