## Evidence

### Al-Zahrani Sami H. MD 2015

Al-Zahrani Sami H. MD, Al-Khail Bahaa A. Aba MD, DPH. (2015). Resident physician's knowledge and attitudes toward biostatistics and research methods concepts. Saudi Medical Journal. https://doi.org/10.15537/smj.2015.10.11842

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional survey comparing knowledge scores of residents who had versus had not ever attended an EBM course; attendees scored higher (p=0.05), though attitudes toward EBM courses "did not differ significantly" (p=0.07).

> "Physicians who reported ever attending an EBM course were more likely to score higher (p=0.05), their attitudes toward EBM courses did not differ significantly from those of their counterparts who had never attended an EBM course (p=0.07)."

### Al-Zahrani Sami H. MD 2015 (2)

Al-Zahrani Sami H. MD, Al-Khail Bahaa A. Aba MD, DPH. (2015). Resident physician's knowledge and attitudes toward biostatistics and research methods concepts. Saudi Medical Journal. https://doi.org/10.15537/smj.2015.10.11842

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple linear regression model for covariates predicting mean knowledge scores in the same 162-resident survey found prior EBM and biostatistics courses and male gender associated with knowledge scores; no effect size is printed.

> "Multiple linear regression analyses showed that prior courses in EBM and biostatistics, as well as male gender were associated with knowledge scores (Table 3)."
