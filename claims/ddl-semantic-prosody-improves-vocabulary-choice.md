## Evidence

### Mansoory 2014

Mansoory, N. & Jafarpour, M. (2014). Teaching Semantic Prosody of English Verbs through the DDL Approach and its Effect on Learners' Vocabulary Choice Appropriateness in a Persian EFL Context. Advances in Language and Literary Studies, 5(2). https://doi.org/10.7575/aiac.alls.v.5n.2p.149

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVA on pretest-posttest scores of two intact classes (n=41) in a quasi-experiment. The article reports the TEST × GROUP interaction was "highly significant: F=59.33, df=1, 39, p<0.05"; no effect size is printed.

> "The result also showed that the interaction was highly significant: F=59.33, df=1, 39, p<0.05 (in the repeated-measures ANOVA) (tables 3 and 4), and the differences were larger than what was expected."

### Mansoory 2014 (2)

Mansoory, N. & Jafarpour, M. (2014). Teaching Semantic Prosody of English Verbs through the DDL Approach and its Effect on Learners' Vocabulary Choice Appropriateness in a Persian EFL Context. Advances in Language and Literary Studies, 5(2). https://doi.org/10.7575/aiac.alls.v.5n.2p.149

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive statistics in Table 2 for the 20-student control and 21-student treatment classes. The article reports the treatment group's means "improved from 8.14 to 13.43" while the control group improved only slightly; these are descriptive means with no test statistic in this quote.

> "In the control group, the means improved slightly from 7.85 to 8.65. The standard deviation (SD) remained almost stable (1.424 and 1.755). In the treatment group, the means, from the pretest to the posttest, improved from 8.14 to 13.43."
