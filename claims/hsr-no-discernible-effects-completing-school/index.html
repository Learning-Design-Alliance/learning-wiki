Evidence¶

High School Redirection 2007¶

High School Redirection. (2007). WWC Intervention Report: High School Redirection. U.S. Department of Education, What Works Clearinghouse. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc

q3 · i? · review · r?

Across the three randomized trials, no study found a statistically significant or substantively important effect on the combined diploma-or-GED outcome; the report states "No study found statistically significant or substantively important effects in this domain," supporting the no-discernible-effects rating.

"The Stockton study found that, by the end of the third follow-up year, High School Redirection youth were more likely than control group youth to have completed high school or earned a GED certificate—40% versus 32%—a difference that was not statistically significant."

Related Claims¶

  • In the Cincinnati trial, High School Redirection had a small, statistically significant positive effect on receiving a high school diploma but not on the combined diploma-or-GED outcome — related
  • High School Redirection shows mixed effects on staying in school, with significant effects in the Stockton trial but not in Wichita or Cincinnati — related
  • High School Redirection has potentially positive effects on progressing in school, driven by a significant credit-accumulation advantage in Stockton — related
  • Career Academies have potentially positive effects on completing school: about 96% of academy students earned a diploma or GED within 8 years of expected graduation versus about 94% of comparison students (effect size 0.27, not statistically significant but substantively important). — related
  • The National Guard Youth ChalleNGe Program has potentially positive effects on completing school for at-risk youth, with 61% of the program group reporting a diploma or GED versus 36% of controls 21 months after random assignment — related
  • Job Corps has potentially positive effects on completing school: more youth without credentials earned a diploma or GED — related
