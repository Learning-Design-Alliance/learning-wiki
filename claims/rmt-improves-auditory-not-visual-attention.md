## Evidence

### Shiels 2026

Shiels, L., Tomlin, D., Payne, J.M. et al. (2026). Benefits of classroom remote microphone technology for inattentive children. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00427-5

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Phase one crossover RCT (n=35) using the IVA-QS continuous performance task. Table 1 reports auditory attention Cohen's d of 0.54 (p = 0.003), sustained auditory attention d = 0.62 (p = 0.003), and auditory response control d = 0.60 (p = 0.001); the article reports "improvements were seen with RMT for auditory attention (P = .003), sustained auditory attention (P = .003), and auditory response control (P = .001)".

> "In the attention task, improvements were seen with RMT for auditory attention (P = .003), sustained auditory attention (P = .003), and auditory response control (P = .001)."

### Shiels 2026 (2)

Shiels, L., Tomlin, D., Payne, J.M. et al. (2026). Benefits of classroom remote microphone technology for inattentive children. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00427-5

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same phase one RCT crossover analysis; the IVA-QS visual subscales showed no significant device effects (e.g., visual attention d = 0.22, p = 0.355), consistent with a benefit specific to auditory stimuli, though a non-significant result does not show the absence of a visual effect.

> "No significant improvements were observed in any of the visual attention or response control measures."
