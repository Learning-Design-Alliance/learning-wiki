# Preschoolers in a multiliteracies folk-tale program showed increased critical-thinking behaviors in qualitative journal data

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Through qualitative analysis of researcher journals, children in the program appeared to acquire ways of dealing with familiar issues and to identify gaps in problematic situations, generating critical thinking skills. [→ Papadopoulos 2020](#papadopoulos-2020)

## Evidence

### Papadopoulos 2020

Papadopoulos, I., & Bisiri, E. (2020). Fostering Critical Thinking Skills in Preschool Education: Designing, Implementing and Assessing a Multiliteracies-Oriented Program Based on Intercultural Tales. Multilingual Academic Journal of Education and Social Sciences, 8(1), 87–105. https://doi.org/10.46886/MAJESS/v8-i1/7263

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Qualitative analysis of teacher/researcher journals kept in every session of the seven-month program with 25 Greek preschoolers. The researchers report children seemed to be "acquiring an understanding of the ways to be used for dealing with familiar issues" and trying to identify gaps in problematic situations.

> "The children appeared to be trying to identify gaps in “problemat ic” situations and generate their skills of critical thinking."
