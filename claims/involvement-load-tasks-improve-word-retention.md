# Tasks with higher involvement load, involving search and evaluation, are conducive to better word retention, with marginal glosses efficient for incidental learning

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Restrepo Ramos 2015

Restrepo Ramos, F. D. (2015). Incidental vocabulary learning in second language acquisition: A literature review. PROFILE Issues in Teachers' Professional Development, 17(1), 157-166. http://dx.doi.org/10.15446/profile.v17n1.43957

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Xu's (2010) study assigned 125 ESL freshmen from a Chinese university, divided into four groups by entrance-examination scores, to four reading tasks on one passage with 10 target words (glosses; sentence marking; glosses plus dictionary; no aid); the review reports higher involvement load aided retention.

> "Results suggested that tasks with higher involvement load, that is, tasks that involve search and evaluation, such as reading with glosses and dictionary use, are conducive to better word retention. Overall, the marginal glosses showed to be more efficient in fostering incidental learning"

## Discussion

The review's list of Xu's four tasks does not match the original study. Xu (2010), "An Empirical Study on the Effect of Task on L2 Incidental Vocabulary Acquisition through Reading", *Asian Social Science, 6*(7), 126-131 ([doi:10.5539/ass.v6n7p126](https://doi.org/10.5539/ass.v6n7p126)), compared reading with marginal glosses, marginal glosses plus making sentences with the target words, a dictionary, and no external aid; its higher-involvement task is the glosses-plus-sentences one, and it concludes that marginal glosses were more efficient than dictionary use, which beat no aid. The task list on [Reading with marginal glosses is the most beneficial task condition for incidental vocabulary acquisition gains](marginal-glosses-most-beneficial-task.md), taken from another review, agrees with the original.
