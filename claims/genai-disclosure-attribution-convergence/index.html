Qian 2026¶

Qian, Y. (2026). Governing generative AI in higher education: emerging policy approaches and support ecosystems at innovative U.S. Universities. International Journal for Educational Integrity. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40979-026-00233-x

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Document analysis of official university GenAI guidance pages across the 50-institution corpus. The convergence section reports that "Institutions commonly instruct students to acknowledge, cite, or otherwise document AI assistance" when AI use is permitted, framing transparency as the integrity norm and an instructional design practice.

"Institutions commonly instruct students to acknowledge, cite, or otherwise document AI assistance rather than present AI-generated text, ideas, code, or outputs as entirely their own"

Related Claims¶

  • Australian university policy environments yield mostly restrictive or indeterminate GenAI classifications, with no combination clearly permitted — related
  • Disclosure status alone often changes little in substantive classification, and false declarations change fewer classifications than expected — related
  • Students fear that declaring AI use will lead to lower marks even when permitted, creating a 'Disclosure Trap' that drives AI use underground — related
  • Cross-university divergence is highest for disclosed language rewriting and a disclosed AI-drafted paragraph, while an explicit assessment prohibition produces unanimity — related
  • Institutional GenAI guidance is more pro-use than computing course syllabus policies, where 92% of syllabi set explicit permissions and half prohibit use outright — related
  • Students frequently do not complete mandatory AI use declarations, perceiving them as admissions of wrongdoing (attributed to Gonsalves) — related
  • Among the 50 most innovative U.S. universities, instructor-set syllabus-level governance is the dominant GenAI convergence, with 33 of 50 providing syllabus or course-policy guidance — related
  • A majority of faculty believe student evaluation methods require significant change, and preferences tilt toward disclosure-based permission regimes over bans — related
  • Students co-designing a GenAI course policy prioritized training for students and instructors, standardized disclosure procedures, and stronger institutional support — related
  • Strict AI prohibitions can intensify students' moral conflict without preventing AI use — related
  • Both institutional and course-level guidance emphasize transparency and acknowledging or citing GenAI use, with 83% of syllabi providing explicit citation guidance — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
