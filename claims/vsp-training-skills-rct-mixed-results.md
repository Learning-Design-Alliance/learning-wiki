## Evidence

### Sten CW 2024

Steen CW, Söderström K, Stensrud B, Nylund IB, Siqveland J. (2024). The effectiveness of virtual reality training on knowledge, skills and attitudes of health care professionals and students in assessing and treating mental health disorders: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05423-0

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

RCT of 102 health care professionals randomized to a VSP intervention or no training; three standardized patients rated participants with a checklist. The review reports "significantly improved posttest skills" with moderate and small effect sizes; no numeric effect sizes are printed.

> "The VSP intervention group demonstrated significantly improved posttest skills in alcohol screening and brief intervention compared to the control group, with moderate and small effect sizes, respectively."

### Sten CW 2024 (2)

Steen CW, Söderström K, Stensrud B, Nylund IB, Siqveland J. (2024). The effectiveness of virtual reality training on knowledge, skills and attitudes of health care professionals and students in assessing and treating mental health disorders: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05423-0

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r1`

Posttest-only RCT of 30 family physicians comparing two VSP interventions with a text-based program, assessed by a diagnostic form. The review reports significant effects with large effect sizes for depression and large, and moderate to large, effects for PTSD by avatar type.

> "The results revealed a significant effect on diagnostic accuracy for major depressive disorder for both groups receiving VR training, compared to the text-based program, with large effect sizes observed."

### Sten CW 2024 (3)

Steen CW, Söderström K, Stensrud B, Nylund IB, Siqveland J. (2024). The effectiveness of virtual reality training on knowledge, skills and attitudes of health care professionals and students in assessing and treating mental health disorders: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05423-0

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

RCT of 67 medical students comparing a VSP intervention group to a video module group on the frequency of suicide risk questions asked of a standardized patient. The review reports minimal to small effect sizes that "did not achieve statistical significance for any outcomes".

> "Minimal to small effect sizes were noted in favor of the VSP intervention, though they did not achieve statistical significance for any outcomes."
