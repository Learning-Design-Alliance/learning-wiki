# The grade advantage of regular PR users is only partly due to improved exam performance: regular users outperformed their complement on exam scores by a modest 3% in Fall 2012, a pattern not as evident in Winter 2013 (5% higher).

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Regular users outperformed their complement on exam scores at only a modest 3% level in Fall 2012, so higher homework and in-lecture response scores also contribute to the overall grade effect. [→ Evrard 2013](#evrard-2013)

## Evidence

### Evrard 2013

Evrard, A. E., Mills, M., Winn, D., Jones, K., Tritz, J., and McKay, T. A. (2013). Problem Roulette: Studying Introductory Physics in the Cloud. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1309.7678](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1309.7678)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Secondary comparison of Fall 2012 Phys 240 exam scores for regular users versus complement. The article states higher scores in on-line homework and in-lecture response scores are also important to the overall grade effect; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Regular users outperformed their complement on exam scores, but only at a modest level of 3%. The eﬀect on overall grade is thus only partly due to improved exam performance."

### Evrard 2013 (2)

Evrard, A. E., Mills, M., Winn, D., Jones, K., Tritz, J., and McKay, T. A. (2013). Problem Roulette: Studying Introductory Physics in the Cloud. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1309.7678](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1309.7678)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Winter 2013 replication check on Phys 240 exam scores: the authors attribute the difference partly to possible conscientiousness correlates of regular users and note limited sample statistics may play a role.

> "The Fall 2012 pattern is not as evident in the Winter 13 class of Phys 240, where we ﬁnd that regular users score an average of 5% higher on examinations."
