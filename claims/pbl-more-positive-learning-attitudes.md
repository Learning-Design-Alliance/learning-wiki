## Evidence

### Ibrahim Nahla Khamis 2014

Ibrahim Nahla Khamis, Banjar Shorooq, Al-Ghamdi Amal, Al-Darmasi Moroj, Khoja Abeer, Turkistani Jamela, Arif Rwan, Al-Sebyani Awatif, Musawa Al-anoud, Basfar Wijdan. (2014). Medical students' preference of problem-based learning or traditional lectures in King Abdulaziz University, Jeddah, Saudi Arabia. Annals of Saudi Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5144/0256-4947.2014.128

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional survey of 460 students; attitude items in Table 2 (six items, five-point Likert) compared PBL with lectures. The article reports "Highly statistical significant differences were present" (P<.001) for motivation, learning better, and professional discussion items.

> "Students preferred PBL for gaining more motivation to learn, constructive learning, and helping them to discuss information in a professional way. Highly statistical significant differences were present between students' opinion of both methods (P<.001)."

### Ibrahim Nahla Khamis 2014 (2)

Ibrahim Nahla Khamis, Banjar Shorooq, Al-Ghamdi Amal, Al-Darmasi Moroj, Khoja Abeer, Turkistani Jamela, Arif Rwan, Al-Sebyani Awatif, Musawa Al-anoud, Basfar Wijdan. (2014). Medical students' preference of problem-based learning or traditional lectures in King Abdulaziz University, Jeddah, Saudi Arabia. Annals of Saudi Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5144/0256-4947.2014.128

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Summary result line from the Results section of the same survey: motivation favoured PBL over lecturing at P<.05, consistent with the Table 2 motivation item (PBL mean 3.85 vs lectures 2.98, t = 10.859, P<.001). No effect size printed.

> "PBL motivates students to learn better than lecturing (P<.05)."
