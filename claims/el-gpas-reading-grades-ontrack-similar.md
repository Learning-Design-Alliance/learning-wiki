# GPAs were very similar for students who did and did not enter CPS as ELs, with a reading-grade gap that closed by seventh grade and similar Freshman OnTrack rates

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students who entered kindergarten as ELs received lower reading grades from second to sixth grade, but closed the gap by seventh grade, with no differences in reading or other core-class grades. [→ de la Torre 2019](#de-la-torre-2019)
`q2 i?` Students who began as ELs had Freshman OnTrack rates similar to students who were never classified as ELs. [→ de la Torre 2019 (2)](#de-la-torre-2019-2)

## Evidence

### de la Torre 2019

de la Torre, M., Blanchard, A., Allensworth, E.M., & Freire, S. (2019). English Learners in CPS: A new perspective. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Descriptive analysis of course grades in core subjects across the three CPS cohorts, kindergarten through eighth grade. The report states GPAs were very similar overall, with the reading-grade difference limited to grades two through six. No effect size is printed.

> "Students who entered kindergarten as ELs received lower grades in reading during these years, on average, compared to other students. But by seventh grade, they closed the gap, and there were no differences in students' grades in reading, or in other core classes."

### de la Torre 2019 (2)

de la Torre, M., Blanchard, A., Allensworth, E.M., & Freire, S. (2019). English Learners in CPS: A new perspective. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Descriptive comparison of ninth-grade Freshman OnTrack status, which the report describes as a predictor of on-time graduation based on course credits and failures. No effect size is printed.

> "When they got to high school, they also had similar Freshman OnTrack rates—a predictor of on-time high school graduation, based on course credits (and failures)—as students who were never classified as ELs."
