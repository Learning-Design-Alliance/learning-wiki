# Gender moderates the effect of physical activity on interference task performance, with benefits observed for females but not males

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Active minutes significantly predicted MSIT performance for females but not for males, a gender-moderated association. [→ Papasideris M. 2021](#papasideris-m-2021)

## Evidence

### Papasideris M. 2021

Papasideris M., Ayaz H., Safati A. B., Morita P. P., Hall P. A. (2021). Examining the relationships among adolescent health behaviours, prefrontal function, and academic achievement using fNIRS. Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience, 50, 100983. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2021.100983

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Moderated regression (Section 3.1) in the adolescent study; the article reports corresponding effect sizes of 0.384 for females and 0.061 for males. Gender did not moderate the fast-food, sleep, or substance-use associations with MSIT performance.

> "Gender was a significant moderator of the relationship between active minutes and MSIT performance (ΔR2 = .077, F = 4.939 (1,54), ρ = .031), such that active minutes had a significant effect on MSIT performance for females (β = 1.018, SE = .412, ρ = .017)"
