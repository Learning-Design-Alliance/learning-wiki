Evidence¶

Melissa Roderick 2008¶

Melissa Roderick, Jenny Nagaoka, Vanessa Coca, Eliza Moeller, with Karen Roddie, Jamiliyah Gilliam, and Desmond Patton. (2008). From High School to the Future: Potholes on the Road to College (Case Studies). Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/high-school-future-potholes-road-college

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Qualitative longitudinal case study of two high-achieving CPS students (Moises and Grady), two of only five students in the 105-study who were qualified for a very selective college, searched thoroughly, and enrolled in their choice. The authors state students need "strong parental support combined with structured sup- port from high schools."

"in addition to academic quali- fications and personal determination, students need strong parental support combined with structured sup- port from high schools to undertake an extensive and effective college search."

Related Claims¶

  • Thoughtful, well-supported students distinguish college fit from college match, treating match as one but not the only component of fit — related
  • Only 41 percent of CPS seniors who aspired to a four-year degree enrolled in a four-year college the fall after graduation — related
  • Students with strong qualifications took themselves out of four-year college planning, choosing early two-year enrollment as a low-risk solution when searches felt overwhelming and costly — related
  • Even students with stellar qualifications and strong plans can fail to enroll after career uncertainty, incomplete financial aid paperwork, and no extended adult conversation about college combine to make choice feel too risky — related
  • College mismatch is common among Chicago graduates: only 38 percent of the most qualified students enrolled in a match college, and many mismatch by enrolling in two-year colleges or not enrolling — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Becoming too focused on a single college option left a highly qualified student with no fallback when that option fell through, and she did not enroll in college — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Even the top students in a school can go almost unnoticed by adults in college planning, missing steering to selective colleges and relying on luck to find a good fit — related
  • One-fifth of students in academically advanced programs do not apply to any four-year college — related
  • Nearly two-thirds of graduates from academically advanced programs graduate with access to a selective or very selective four-year college — related
  • College undermatch is widespread among CPS students at all qualification levels, with only 27 percent of selective-access graduates matching — related
  • Early educators face financial, academic, and structural barriers to college enrollment and degree completion, disproportionately affecting students of color — related
