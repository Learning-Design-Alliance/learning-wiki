## Evidence

### Rawson 2011

Rawson, K. A., & Dunlosky, J. (2011). Optimizing Schedules of Retrieval Practice for Durable and Efficient Learning: How Much Is Enough? Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 140(3), 283–302. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023956

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 mixed-factor ANOVA (initial criterion between subjects; number of relearning sessions within subjects) on final cued recall in Session 5. Only the number of relearning sessions was significant, with ηp2 = .46 and "other Fs ⬍ 1".

> "A 2 (one or three correct recalls in Session 1) ⫻ 2 (one or three relearning sessions) mixed-factor ANOVA revealed only a main effect of the number of relearning sessions, F(1, 50) ⫽ 42.38, MSE ⫽ 191.41, p ⬍ .001, ␩p2 ⫽ .46 (other Fs ⬍ 1)."

### Rawson 2011 (2)

Rawson, K. A., & Dunlosky, J. (2011). Optimizing Schedules of Retrieval Practice for Durable and Efficient Learning: How Much Is Enough? Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 140(3), 283–302. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023956

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 comparison of final recall for practiced items against baseline control items never presented during practice. Both relearning conditions exceeded baseline ("t(51) ⫽ 6.59" with one session; "t(51) ⫽ 9.75" with three); no effect size was printed.

> "Performance was significantly greater in both relearning conditions than in the baseline control condition, t(51) ⫽ 6.59 with one relearning session and t(51) ⫽ 9.75 with three relearning sessions, establishing significant effects of practice beyond any learning of items acquired outside of the experiment."
