# Dedicated sense-of-belonging courseware features were underused: most students were unaware of the Lumen Community and vaguely remembered the Introduce Yourself module

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` 40 of the 50 interviewed students were unaware of the Lumen Community feature, and only 10 said they had tried it. [→ Koch 2025](#koch-2025)
`q2 i?` Students only vaguely remembered the Introduce Yourself module; it supported near-term belonging but did not usually leave a lasting impression. [→ Koch 2025 (2)](#koch-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Koch 2025

Koch, M., & Peters Hinton, V. (2025). Voices of Undergraduate Students Taking Introductory Statistics Courses [Project Report]. Digital Promise.

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview finding on the Lumen Community peer feature: "40 out of the 50 interviewed students were unaware of its existence" and only 10 had tried it. Students who knew it wanted more instructor guidance and found response times slow.

> "While the Lumen Community feature was designed for collaborative engagement, 40 out of the 50 interviewed students were unaware of its existence. When asked directly about this feature, only 10 students said they had tried it out."

### Koch 2025 (2)

Koch, M., & Peters Hinton, V. (2025). Voices of Undergraduate Students Taking Introductory Statistics Courses [Project Report]. Digital Promise.

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Students did not navigate to or mention the Introduce Yourself module unless prompted and vaguely remembered it. The report concludes it "supported students' sense of belonging in the near term but did not usually leave a lasting impression."

> "The student responses suggest that the Introduce Yourself module supported students' sense of belonging in the near term but did not usually leave a lasting impression on students."
