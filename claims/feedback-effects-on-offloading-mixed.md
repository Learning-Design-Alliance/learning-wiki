## Evidence

### Yufei Guo & Qun Ye 2026

Yufei Guo & Qun Ye. (2026). Meta-cognitive insights into cognitive offloading: mechanisms, interventions, and educational implications. Humanities and Social Sciences Communications. https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-026-06621-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Gilbert et al. (2020), that without feedback learners overused reminders, while advice feedback reduced reminder bias; feedback valence also shifted offloading preferences in alignment with its valence.

> "However, when participants received metacognitive advice feedback (e.g., “Based on your performance, of ﬂoading is a good choice now”), they reduced reminder bias in memory tasks, demon- strating more accurate metacognitive evaluations and wiser off- loading strategies."

### Yufei Guo & Qun Ye 2026 (2)

Yufei Guo & Qun Ye. (2026). Meta-cognitive insights into cognitive offloading: mechanisms, interventions, and educational implications. Humanities and Social Sciences Communications. https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-026-06621-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Grinschgl et al. (2020), a pattern-copy task in which fabricated ranking feedback altered metacognitive beliefs — low-ranking feedback reduced self-efficacy and confidence — but did not increase offloading, a phenomenon the review calls not previously observed.

> "Comparative ranking feedback signiﬁcantly shifted participants' metacognitive beliefs about working memory capacity. Contrary to expectations, however, individuals with lower metacognitive beliefs did not exhibit increased of ﬂoading behaviors"
