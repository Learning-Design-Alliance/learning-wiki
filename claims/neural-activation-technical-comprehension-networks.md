# During technical text reading, activation in language, spatial, and knowledge-integration regions is positively related to comprehension, while activation in episodic/autobiographical retrieval regions is negatively related

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Regions for language processing, spatial imagery, episodic encoding, and semantic knowledge retrieval/reorganization were more highly activated in readers with better technical passage comprehension. [→ Keller 2024](#keller-2024)
`q3 i?` Greater activation in ventromedial prefrontal cortex and related episodic/autobiographical retrieval regions was associated with poorer comprehension. [→ Keller 2024 (2)](#keller-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Keller 2024

Keller, T. A., Mason, R. A., Legg, A. E., & Just, M. A. (2024). The neural and cognitive basis of expository text comprehension. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00232-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Voxelwise correlation analysis of fMRI data from 31 participants reading 16 technical passages identified regions including left IFG, left SPL, bilateral DLPFC, and bilateral hippocampus where "activation ... was more highly activated in readers who displayed better comprehension".

> "A set of regions associated with language processing and memory, spatial imagery, episodic encoding, and semantic knowledge retrieval and reorganization was more highly activated in readers who displayed better comprehension of the technical passages."

### Keller 2024 (2)

Keller, T. A., Mason, R. A., Legg, A. E., & Just, M. A. (2024). The neural and cognitive basis of expository text comprehension. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00232-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

> "Other brain areas showed a nega tive relation between compre hension of the technical passages a nd activation during the reading of the passages (greater activation w as associated with poorer compre hending of participants)."
