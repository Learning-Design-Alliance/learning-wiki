Evidence¶

Whipple 1910¶

Whipple, G. M. (1910). The effect of practise upon the range of visual attention and of visual apprehension. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://books.google.com/books?q=Whipple+effect+of+practise+range+of+visual+attention+apprehension

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Individual-differences analysis in Table 3 of the apprehension tests with three observers (Ruger, Geissler, Vermilyea): F excelled at dots and poetry but was poorest at nonsense syllables, drawings, pictures and objects; G showed the reverse pattern.

"V, it will be seen, is more than twice as efficient as G in the perception of groups of dots, but G is more than twice as efficient as V in the perception of drawings."

Whipple 1910 (2)¶

Whipple, G. M. (1910). The effect of practise upon the range of visual attention and of visual apprehension. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://books.google.com/books?q=Whipple+effect+of+practise+range+of+visual+attention+apprehension

q2 · i? · causal · r1

The article's summary conclusion from the apprehension data: one may state only that a given individual excels in attentive observation of particular kinds of material, not in all-around observation.

"Individual differences are so striking and so specific as to negative the notion of a general ability to apprehend, or even of a general ability to apprehend visually."

Related Claims¶

  • Systematic practise gives little warrant for markedly improving adults' quick visual perception; the slight poetry gain is attributed to familiarity with the poem — related
  • The average range of visual attention for isolated letters lies between 4 and 5 letters (4.82), with only slight individual differences — related
  • Practise has a very slight effect on the range of attention, explicable by habituation and grouping tricks rather than any expansion of the range itself — related
  • Practice effects in visual reproduction are specific: they transfer to new conditions only in proportion to identical ability-conditioning factors. — related
