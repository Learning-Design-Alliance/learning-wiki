# Automatization is item-based: learning is specific to trained stimuli and transfers poorly to different stimuli

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Because instance theory holds that learning is item-based, subjects learn specific responses to specific stimuli, and what they learn should not transfer well to different stimuli, unlike process-based views. [→ Logan 1988](#logan-1988)

## Evidence

### Logan 1988

Logan, G. D. (1988). Toward an instance theory of automatization. Psychological Review, 95(4), 492-527. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.95.4.492

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument contrasting instance-based with process-based automatization: process-based learning should transfer equally to trained and novel stimuli, whereas instance-based learning should not. The article cites the consistent-versus-varied-mapping literature as evidence for specificity.

> "subjects learn specific responses to specific stimuli, and what they learn should not transfer well to different stimuli"
