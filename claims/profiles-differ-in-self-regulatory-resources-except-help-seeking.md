# The four profiles differ in academic self-efficacy, time/study management, and effort regulation, with HL highest, but not in help seeking

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` HL (high literacy, low dependency) scored highest on academic self-efficacy, time/study management, and effort regulation; help seeking did not differ across profiles. [→ Hilit Maizel 2026](#hilit-maizel-2026)

## Evidence

### Hilit Maizel 2026

Hilit Maizel, Maya Kalman Halevi, Miri Sarid, and Rony Tutian. (2026). Understanding Student Dependency on AI: The Role of AI Literacy, Academic Self-Efficacy, and Resource Management Strategies. Educ. Sci. 2026, 16, 1123. https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci16071123

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

One-way ANOVAs comparing the four clusters with Tukey HSD post hoc tests. The HL profile scored significantly higher than all other profiles on academic self-efficacy and higher than HH and MM on effort regulation. "Help seeking did not significantly differ across profiles (F(3, 474) = 2.01, p = 0.112, η2 = 0.01)".

> "academic self-efficacy (F(3, 474) = 22.60, p < 0.001, η2 = 0.13), time and study environment management (F(3, 474) = 11.21,p < 0.001, η2 = 0.07), and effort regulation (F(3, 474) = 19.00,p < 0.001, η2 = 0.11)"
