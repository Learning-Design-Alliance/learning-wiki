# Processing speed has a significant effect on both letter and category fluency in older adults, while IQ does not

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In ANCOVA models, processing speed significantly affected both fluency measures, but the effect of full-scale IQ (NART) was not significant for either fluency measure. [→ Safa A. Elgamal 2011](#safa-a-elgamal-2011)

## Evidence

### Safa A. Elgamal 2011

Safa A. Elgamal, MBBCh, PhD, Eric A. Roy, PhD, Michael T. Sharratt, PhD. (2011). Age and Verbal Fluency: The Mediating Effect of Speed of Processing. Canadian Geriatrics Journal, Volume 14, Issue 3. https://doi.org/10.5770/cgj.v14i3.17

`q3 · i?`

Univariate ANCOVAs on letter and category fluency controlling for NART and the processing speed composite; Table 4 reports effect of speed p < .001 (letter) and p = .002 (category), with NART effects non-significant (p = .09 and p = .25). The MANCOVA confirmed "the effect of speed was significant (p = .001)" while IQ remained insignificant (p = .24).

> "The two models showed that processing speed had a significant effect on both fluency measures; however, the effect of IQ was not significant (Table 4)."
