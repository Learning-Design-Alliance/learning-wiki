Evidence¶

Mihindo W. Jane 2017¶

Mihindo W. Jane, Wachanga S.W. & Anditi Z. O. (2017). Effects of Computer-Based Simulations Teaching Approach on Students’ Achievement in the Learning of Chemistry among Secondary School Students in Nakuru Sub County, Kenya. Journal of Education and Practice, 8(5). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1133108

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Independent samples t-test of post-test CAT scores by gender in the experimental groups (female mean .6432, N=38; male mean .6608, N=49). The article reports "no significance difference ... since t (85) =-.562, p>0.05"; null hypothesis Ho3 was accepted.

"From the results of the independent samples t-test no significance difference was obtained between achievement of male and female who were taught Chem istry through CBS since t (85) =-.562, p>0.05."

Mihindo W. Jane 2017 (2)¶

Mihindo W. Jane, Wachanga S.W. & Anditi Z. O. (2017). Effects of Computer-Based Simulations Teaching Approach on Students’ Achievement in the Learning of Chemistry among Secondary School Students in Nakuru Sub County, Kenya. Journal of Education and Practice, 8(5). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1133108

q2 · i? · causal · r2

ANCOVA of post-test CAT scores by gender with KCPE as covariate, run to compensate for pre-test mean differences between male and female students. The article reports "no statistically significant difference ... F(1, 84 )=.355 ,p>0.05".

"The results in Table 15 showed that there was still no statistically significant difference between th e mean scores of male and female students in Experimental Groups 1 and 2,F(1, 84 )=.355 ,p>0.05."
