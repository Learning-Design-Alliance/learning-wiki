## Evidence

### Postiglione A. 2023

Postiglione A., De Angelis I., Di Blasi M. (2023). AstroGarden of Roma Tre University: from presence to online tour. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2301.03842](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2301.03842)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Kahoot! answers from the European Researchers' Night 2020 YouTube live broadcast, followed live by 70 participants with about 40 entering the Kahoot! game. The authors report "a non-negligible percentage of participants (23%)" holding the summer-Sun-distance misconception; percentages are descriptive, no inferential test reported.

> "diﬀerence between summer and winter relies on the fact that the Su n reaches a greater height in summer, there is, however, a non-negligible percentage of participants (23%) who, in any case, showed that the common misconception that in summer the Sun is closer to Earth."

### Postiglione A. 2023 (2)

Postiglione A., De Angelis I., Di Blasi M. (2023). AstroGarden of Roma Tre University: from presence to online tour. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2301.03842](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2301.03842)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same Kahoot! quiz during the 2020 live broadcast, on why the poles alternate between months of darkness and light: 64% answered "because of the inclination of the Earth rotation axis". Descriptive answer percentages only; the article reports no statistical test.

> "64% of the participants knew that it was because of the inclination of the Earth rotation axis, while 25% found the reason in the Earth rotation, and 7% in the Earth motion around the Sun."

### Postiglione A. 2023 (3)

Postiglione A., De Angelis I., Di Blasi M. (2023). AstroGarden of Roma Tre University: from presence to online tour. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2301.03842](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2301.03842)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same Kahoot! quiz during the 2020 broadcast, on the position of the Sun at the zenith: "83% of the answers stated that at noon the Sun can be found at the zenith only in the region between the Tropics". Descriptive percentages only; no statistical test reported.

> "83% of the answers stated that at noon the Sun can be found at the zenith only in the region between the Tropics; the remaining were divided equally among those who thought that this holds everywhere and those who thought that this holds only at the Poles."
