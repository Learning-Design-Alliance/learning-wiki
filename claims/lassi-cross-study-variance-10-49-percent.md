## Evidence

### Zhou Yuanyuan 2016

Zhou Yuanyuan, Graham Lori, West Courtney. (2016). The relationship between study strategies and academic performance. Int J Med Educ 7:324-332. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.57dc.fe0f

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Retrospective comparison of the present study with five reviewed studies (West 2011, West 2014, Kellogg 2010, Lipsky 1999, Loong 2012) summarized in Table 3; Anxiety was correlated only occasionally. No pooled effect size was computed.

> "Even though the six studies have variations by nature, the correlation matrices were relatively robust across studies. Concentration, Motivation, Time Management and Test Strategies were likely to be significantly correlated with outcome variables that measured academic performance."

### Zhou Yuanyuan 2016 (2)

Zhou Yuanyuan, Graham Lori, West Courtney. (2016). The relationship between study strategies and academic performance. Int J Med Educ 7:324-332. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.57dc.fe0f

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-study summary of R2 and adjusted R2 from regression models with the ten LASSI subscales as predictors (Table 3). The article notes a complete meta-analysis was not conducted because a sufficient number of previous studies were not available.

> "Based on the R2 reported in the six studies, 10-49% of the total variance could be explained by the LASSI subscales. This means that the effect of learning strategies on academic performance is at least small, but it could be substantial."
