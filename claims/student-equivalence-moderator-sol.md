## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the synchronous-versus-asynchronous cognitive condition, non-random assignment had a statistically significantly larger effect size than random assignment, although both individual estimates were insignificant. [→ Florence Martin 2021](#florence-martin-2021)
`q2 i?` In the synchronous-versus-face-to-face affective condition, non-random-assignment studies had significantly larger effect sizes than random-assignment studies and than the study without assignment information. [→ Florence Martin 2021 (2)](#florence-martin-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Florence Martin 2021

Florence Martin, Ting Sun, Murat Turk, and Albert D. Ritzhaupt. (2021). A Meta-Analysis on the Effects of Synchronous Online Learning on Cognitive and Affective Educational Outcomes. International Review of Research in Open and Distributed Learning, Volume 22, Number 3. https://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Mixed-effects moderator analysis on the synchronous-vs-asynchronous cognitive condition comparing random (3 studies) with non-random (4 studies) assignment. No standardized effect size is printed for the moderator contrast; student equivalence also moderated the affective condition (Q = 5.291, p = .021).

> "Although both conditions yielded insignificant effect size estimates, non-random assignment had a statistically significantly larger effect size than random assignment (Q-value = 5.837, p < .016)."

### Florence Martin 2021 (2)

Florence Martin, Ting Sun, Murat Turk, and Albert D. Ritzhaupt. (2021). A Meta-Analysis on the Effects of Synchronous Online Learning on Cognitive and Affective Educational Outcomes. International Review of Research in Open and Distributed Learning, Volume 22, Number 3. https://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Mixed-effects moderator analysis on the synchronous-vs-face-to-face affective condition, based on three random-assignment studies, one non-random-assignment study, and one study without assignment information. Student equivalence did not moderate the face-to-face cognitive condition (Q = 0.136, p < .713).

> "Results revealed that student equivalence moderated the effect size estimates, with studies employing non-random assignment having statistically significantly larger effect size than studies in the other two categories, studies employing random assignment (Q-value = 14.019, p < .001) and the study without information (Q-value = 19.331, p < .001)."
