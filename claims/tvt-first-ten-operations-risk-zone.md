## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For operations 1–10, experienced surgeons showed fewer patient-reported complications after discharge (14.0% vs 18.4%, P = .002), higher 1-year improvement (95.9% vs 91.8%, P < .001) and higher satisfaction (90.9% vs 86.2%, P = .002); from operation 11 onwards trainees matched the experienced level. ## Evidence

### Nüssler EK 2021

Nüssler EK, Löfgren M, Lindkvist H, Idahl A. (2021). The tension‐free vaginal tape operation: Is the inexperienced surgeon a risk factor? Learning curve and Swedish quality reference. Acta Obstet Gynecol Scand. https://doi.org/10.1111/aogs.14033

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Register-based cohort comparison of trainee operations 1–10 against the experienced-surgeon quality reference. The authors call the first 10 operations a "risk zone" and note a sensitivity analysis with other cutoffs did not substantially influence results.

> "For the first 10 trainee operations there were small differences in favor of the experienced surgeons for patient‐reported complications after discharge from the hospital leading to medical control (14.0% vs 18.4%, P = .002), patient‐reported 1‐year improvement (95.9% vs 91.8%, P < .001) and patient 1‐year satisfaction (90.9% vs 86.2%, P = .002)."
