# Content Proximity math scores were consistently higher than traditional MAP Growth math scores, with small effects varying by grade

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Regression-adjusted math score differences favoring the pilot group were statistically significant at the 0.05 level in every grade, ranging from 0.76 adjusted RIT in grade 2 to 6.42 in grade 6, with Hedge's g from 0.05 to 0.37. [→ J. Patrick Meyer 2023](#j-patrick-meyer-2023)

## Evidence

### J. Patrick Meyer 2023

J. Patrick Meyer, Ann Hu, Sylvia Li. (2023). Content Proximity Spring 2022 Pilot Study Research Brief. Psychometric Solutions, NWEA. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/content-proximity-spring-2022-pilot-study-research-report/

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Propensity-score-matched regression analysis (spring score regressed on group indicator, demographics, winter RIT, response time effort, school challenge index) found significant pilot advantages in all grades; "Hedge’s g estimates of effect size shown in Table 2.4 range from 0.05 in Grade 2 to 0.37 in Grade 6."

> "Regression analysis for math indicated that the increased math scores for the pilot group are statistically significant at the 0.05 level for each grade (see Table 2.4). The difference ranged from a low of 0.76 adjusted RIT in grade 2 to a high of 6.42 adjusted RIT in grade 6."
