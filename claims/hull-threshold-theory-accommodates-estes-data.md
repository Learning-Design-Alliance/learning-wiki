# A Hull-type incremental threshold theory can accommodate the data Estes et al. (1960) offered for one-trial learning

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` An incremental theory distinguishing associative strength from a performance threshold, with oscillation and individual-difference variables, predicts the same second-test-trial pattern Estes et al. took as supporting all-or-none learning. [→ Underwood 1962](#underwood-1962)

## Evidence

### Underwood 1962

Underwood, B. J., & Keppel, G. (1962). One-Trial Learning? Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior, 1, 1-13. https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/journal-of-verbal-learning-and-verbal-behavior

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Theoretical analysis (not a new experiment): the authors show that a Hull-type threshold theory, with rate-of-growth individual differences (Fig. 2), "leads to exactly the same prediction as that given by Estes et al." for items correct and incorrect on a first test trial.

> "based on an all-or-none theory, namely, that without intervening reinforcement, the two items which were correct on the first test trial are likely to be correct on the second, and the two that were incorrect on the first test trial are likely to remain incorrect on the second."
