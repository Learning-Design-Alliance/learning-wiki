# Initial learning criterion showed a small persistent effect on relearning rate after relearning: lower-criterion items required more relearning trials

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In Experiment 1, collapsing the Criterion 1–2 versus Criterion 3–4 groups, lower-criterion items required more relearning trials in Session 3 (d = 0.43), evidence for a small persistent effect of initial criterion on relearning rate despite null cued recall. [→ Rawson 2011](#rawson-2011)

## Evidence

### Rawson 2011

Rawson, K. A., & Dunlosky, J. (2011). Optimizing Schedules of Retrieval Practice for Durable and Efficient Learning: How Much Is Enough? Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 140(3), 283–302. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023956

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1 converging measure: number of retrieval-monitoring-feedback trials per item needed to reach one correct recall in Session 3. A collapsed pairwise comparison showed "the lower criterion groups required more relearning trials than did the higher criterion groups" with d = 0.43.

> "such that the lower criterion groups required more relearning trials than did the higher criterion groups, t(83) ⫽ 1.97, p ⫽ .026, d ⫽ 0.43."
