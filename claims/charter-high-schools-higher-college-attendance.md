# Attending a charter high school is associated with an 8 to 10 percentage point higher likelihood of attending college

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students attending a charter high school are 8 to 10 percentage points more likely to attend college than comparable students attending traditional public high schools, controlling for key student characteristics. [→ Kevin Booker 2010](#kevin-booker-2010)

## Evidence

### Kevin Booker 2010

Kevin Booker, Tim R. Sass, Brian Gill, Ron Zimmer. (2010). The Unknown World of Charter High Schools. Education Next, vol. 10, no. 2. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/the-unknown-world-of-charter-high-schools

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of Chicago and Florida data on college attendance following charter high school enrollment. The study reports that charter high school attendees are "8 to 10 percentage points more likely to attend college," with the same controls as the diploma analysis. No standardized effect size is printed for this finding.

> "Similarly, those attending a charter high school are 8 to 10 percentage points more likely to attend college."
