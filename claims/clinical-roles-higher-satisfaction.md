## Evidence

### Wei Yingying 2025

Wei Yingying, Deng Na, Zheng Hongmei, Guo Haijun, Zhang Huiling, Li Bing, Wu Daiqin, Li Wei, Si Xiaoyun. (2025). Comparison of teacher-standardized patients and peer role-play in assessing medical students' history-taking skills. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07758-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Role-based subgroup analysis of the satisfaction questionnaire (241 doctor, 84 patient, 301 evaluator) found doctor roles significantly higher than evaluators on clinical-skill attitudes; communication-skills and clinical-thinking items also favored doctors over evaluators (p < 0.05).

> "In clinical skill development, doctor role participants demonstrated significantly higher scores than evaluators with statistical significance (F = 5.107, p = 0.006)."

### Wei Yingying 2025 (2)

Wei Yingying, Deng Na, Zheng Hongmei, Guo Haijun, Zhang Huiling, Li Bing, Wu Daiqin, Li Wei, Si Xiaoyun. (2025). Comparison of teacher-standardized patients and peer role-play in assessing medical students' history-taking skills. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07758-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same role analysis, teamwork attitudes were significantly higher for doctor and patient roles than evaluators (F = 4.657, p = 0.01), with no significant doctor–patient difference; overall course attitude also favored patient roles over evaluators (F = 3.028, p = 0.049).

> "Regarding teamwork and cooperation aspects, both doctor role and patient role participants achieved significantly higher scores than evaluators with statistically significant intergroup differences (F = 4.657, p = 0.01)"
