## Evidence

### Yeomans 2018

Yeomans, M., Stewart, B. M., Mavon, K., Kindel, A., Tingley, D., & Reich, J. (2018). The Civic Mission of MOOCs: Engagement across Political Differences in Online Forums. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education, 28, 553–589. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-017-0161-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey-based descriptive analysis of U.S. pre-course survey respondents (Saving Schools n = 1,315; American Government n = 1,258) compared against nationally representative polls. The authors state this is "the first direct evidence for ideological diversity" in MOOCs, though the American Government sample's central tendency was more left-leaning than the population.

> "That is, both classes managed to attract students that represent a diverse range of political beliefs, across the ideological spectrum. Though the demographic diversity of MOOCs has been well-established, to our knowledge, this is the first direct evidence for ideological diversity."
