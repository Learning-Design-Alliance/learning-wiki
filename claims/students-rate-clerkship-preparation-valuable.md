## Evidence

### Murray R 2026

Murray R, Engel‐Haber E, Snider B, Kirshblum S. (2026). Assessing knowledge about the Americans with Disabilities Act and comfort level in treating persons with disabilities among fourth‐year medical students following a clerkship in physical medicine and rehabilitation. PM&R. https://doi.org/10.1002/pmrj.13434

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre/post survey item (n = 99 per Figure 4) on perceived curriculum adequacy, showing statistically significant improvement at p < .001. Nearly 90% of students felt the curriculum prepared them to interact with persons with disabilities.

> "After the course, participants reported an improved perception of the adequacy of the medical school curriculum in preparing them to effectively interact with patients with disabilities; see Figure 4 (p < .001)."

### Murray R 2026 (2)

Murray R, Engel‐Haber E, Snider B, Kirshblum S. (2026). Assessing knowledge about the Americans with Disabilities Act and comfort level in treating persons with disabilities among fourth‐year medical students following a clerkship in physical medicine and rehabilitation. PM&R. https://doi.org/10.1002/pmrj.13434

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Postsurvey ratings from the same cohort. All participants agreed they would use the clerkship experience in future clinical encounters; qualitative postsurvey responses also included a desire for additional lecture material and a longer clerkship duration.

> "Almost all (99%) of students somewhat or strongly agreed that the rotation provided valuable insights into caring for persons with disabilities. Overall, all student participants somewhat or strongly agreed that they would use the clerkship experience in the future to enhance their interactions with persons with disabilities."
