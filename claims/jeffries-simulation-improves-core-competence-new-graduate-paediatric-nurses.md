# Total core competence scores of new graduate paediatric surgery nurses rose significantly after Jeffries-framework simulation training, in one uncontrolled pre/post study

`q2 i3` Total CIRN core competence scores increased significantly from pre- to post-training in a single-group design (mean difference 24.08 ± 9.51 points, Cohen's d = 2.36, p < 0.001); with no control group, the gain cannot be attributed to the simulation alone. ## Evidence

### Luo Wenjing 2026

Luo Wenjing, Yang Runyu, Feng Liwei, and Liang Yuanyuan. 2026. "Effect of the Jeffries Simulation Framework on the Core Competence of New Graduate Paediatric Surgery Nurses: A Quasi‐Experimental Study." Nursing Open 13 (9): e70779. https://doi.org/10.1002/nop2.70779

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Single-group pretest/posttest quasi-experiment with 60 new graduate paediatric surgery nurses; clinical preceptors rated core competence with the 55-item CIRN before and after an 8-week Jeffries-framework simulation course. The study reports a "mean difference of 24.08 ± 9.51 points" with p < 0.001 and Cohen's d = 2.36 (Table 2). This is a within-group gain: the authors state that, without a control group, the change cannot be attributed solely to the intervention, since routine ward experience over the 8 weeks and the Hawthorne effect may have contributed.

> "The total score of core competence increased significantly from 118.48 ± 8.84 pre‐training to 142.56 ± 6.11 post‐training, with a mean difference of 24.08 ± 9.51 points."
