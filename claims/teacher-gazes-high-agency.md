# Teacher gazes at student faces and teacher-started eye contacts occur during high teacher agency, and teacher-started eye contacts are shorter when agency is higher

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1` · `q2` · `i1`–`i2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Durations of teacher-started dyadic eye contacts correlated negatively with teacher agency (r = −.224). [→ Haataja 2021](#haataja-2021)
`q2 i1` Across gaze categories, eye contact had a significant effect on teacher agency (η² = 0.018), with teacher agency highest during teacher-started eye contacts and teacher gazes at student faces. [→ Haataja 2021 (2)](#haataja-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Haataja 2021

Haataja, E., Salonen, V., Laine, A., Toivanen, M., & Hannula, M. S. (2021). The Relation Between Teacher-Student Eye Contact and Teachers' Interpersonal Behavior During Group Work: a Multiple-Person Gaze-Tracking Case Study in Secondary Mathematics Education. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09538-w

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Pearson correlations on log-transformed gaze durations from the three-lesson case study showed teacher-started eye contacts were shorter during high teacher agency, the printed effect size being r = −.224 (p = .003); the article reports this result was led by Joanne's data.

> "The correlation between the durations of teacher-started eye contacts and teacher agency was negative (r(171) = − .224, p = .003)."

### Haataja 2021 (2)

Haataja, E., Salonen, V., Laine, A., Toivanen, M., & Hannula, M. S. (2021). The Relation Between Teacher-Student Eye Contact and Teachers' Interpersonal Behavior During Group Work: a Multiple-Person Gaze-Tracking Case Study in Secondary Mathematics Education. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09538-w

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r1`

The pooled within-subject ANOVA on teacher agency found a statistically significant effect with printed effect size η² = 0.018; teacher agency was highest during teacher-started eye contacts and teacher gazes at student faces.

> "The analysis of variance yielded statistically significant effect of teacher-student eye contact on teacher agency ( F(3, 1658) = 9.88, p <. 0 0 1 ,η2 = 0.018), between the gaze categories."
