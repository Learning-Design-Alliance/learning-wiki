# Initially lowest-performing students on phonological decoding grew more than peers regardless of condition, with the largest growth in the morphological-awareness vocabulary condition

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Higher-risk students (at or below the pretest NWF median of 35 sounds per minute) improved more from pretest to posttest than lower-risk students (d = .69 vs. d = .26), and among them the MA condition showed the largest effect (d = 1.01 vs. SR .65 and PAD .63), though between-condition differences were not statistically significant. [→ Filippini 2012](#filippini-2012)

## Evidence

### Filippini 2012

Filippini, A. L., Gerber, M. M., & Leafstedt, J. M. (2012). A Vocabulary-Added Reading Intervention for English Learners At-Risk of Reading Difficulties. International Journal of Special Education, 27(3). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1001055

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Pre-post effect size analysis of higher-risk students defined by the pretest NWF median split in this exploratory study; the MA group's d = 1.01 is a large effect. A Kruskall-Wallis test on posttest NWF mean ranks was not significant (c2(2) = 1.63, p = .443), though mean rank was higher for MA (19.94) and SR (19.40) than PAD (14.96).

> "Among the higher risk students, the MA group had the largest effect size (d = 1.01) compared to SR (d = .65) and PAD ( d = .63)."
