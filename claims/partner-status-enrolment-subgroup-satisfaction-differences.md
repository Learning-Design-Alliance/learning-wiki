# Partnered student parents report higher school and family satisfaction, and part-time enrolment is associated with higher family satisfaction

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Being partnered was positively associated with school satisfaction (.11, t = 2.33, p = .02) and family satisfaction (.14, t = 3.36, p = .001). [→ Tricia M. van Rhijn & Donna S. Lero 2014](#tricia-m-van-rhijn-donna-s-lero-2014)
`q2 i?` Part-time enrolment was positively associated with family satisfaction (.13, t = 3.01, p = .003). [→ Tricia M. van Rhijn & Donna S. Lero 2014](#tricia-m-van-rhijn-donna-s-lero-2014)

## Evidence

### Tricia M. van Rhijn & Donna S. Lero 2014

Tricia M. van Rhijn & Donna S. Lero. (2014). The influence of self-efficacy beliefs for student parents attending university. International Journal of Lifelong Education. http://dx.doi.org/10.1080/02601370.2014.884178

`q2 · i?`

Exploratory covariate analysis adding gender, partner status, enrolment status, and age of youngest child to the structural model of 398 student parents. "Both partner status (.14, t = 3.36, p = .001) and enrolment status (.13, t = 3.01, p = .003) were positively associated with family satisfaction."
