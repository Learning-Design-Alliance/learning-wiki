## Evidence

### Kötter T 2017

Kötter T, Wagner J, Brüheim L, Voltmer E. (2017). Perceived Medical School stress of undergraduate medical students predicts academic performance: an observational study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1091-0

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Prospective longitudinal observational analysis (LUST cohort) matching PMSS scores to M1 grades for 456 students. The printed effect sizes Spearman's ρ = 0.21 and ρ = 0.22 fall in the .20–.36 range, indicating small-to-moderate positive associations between perceived stress and worse exam grades.

> "Spearman's ρ for the bivariate correlation between PMSS T1 and T2 scores and the M1 grade were 0.21 and 0.22, respectively (in both cases p < .01)."

### Kötter T 2017 (2)

Kötter T, Wagner J, Brüheim L, Voltmer E. (2017). Perceived Medical School stress of undergraduate medical students predicts academic performance: an observational study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1091-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Separate linear regression analyses for T1 (Nagelkerkes R2 = .115) and T2 (R2 = .095) controlling for age and gender; PMSS remained a significant predictor in both models. No standardized effect size was printed for the regression coefficients.

> "The linear regression analyses revealed both PMSS T1 and T2 scores as independent predictors of the M1 grade (see Tables 2 and 3) with better M1 grades for students with lower PMSS scores."
