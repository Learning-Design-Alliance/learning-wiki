# Race prompts elicit culturally stereotyped feedback: Black, Hispanic, and Asian students are positioned through cultural or linguistic stereotypes while White students receive argument-structure feedback

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Feedback for Black-prompted essays invoked collective identity, systemic contexts, and social change; Hispanic-prompted feedback emphasized English corrections and cultural frames; Asian-prompted feedback emphasized education, respect, and academic English; White-prompted feedback focused on argument structure and evidence. [→ Tan et al. 2026](#tan-et-al-2026)

## Evidence

### Tan et al. 2026

Mei Tan, Lena Phalen, and Dorottya Demszky. 2026. Marked Pedagogies: Examining Linguistic Biases in Personalized Automated Writing Feedback. In LAK 2026: 16th International Learning Analytics and Knowledge Conference (LAK 2026). ACM, New York, NY, USA. [doi:10.1145/3785022.3785113](https://doi.org/10.1145/3785022.3785113)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Qualitative and lexical analysis (Table 4, words with |z| > 1.96 shared across models) of feedback generated under Black, Hispanic, Asian, and White prompt conditions for the same essays. Distinctive words included systemic, stereotype, culture, family, and formal. No effect size is printed for the pattern.

> "These patterns suggest a Marked Pedagogy in which Black, Hispanic, and Asian students are positioned through cultural or linguistic stereotypes, while White students are positioned as competent writers invited to refine argument structure and reasoning."
