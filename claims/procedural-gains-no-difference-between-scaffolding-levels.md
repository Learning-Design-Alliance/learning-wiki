## Evidence

### Borchers 2025

Borchers, C., Fleischer, H., Schanze, S., Scheiter, K., & Aleven, V. (2025). High scaffolding of an unfamiliar strategy improves conceptual learning but reduces enjoyment compared to low scaffolding and strategy freedom. Computers & Education, 236, 105364. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2025.105364

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same crossover experiment, ANOVA on procedural pre/post scores found no significant difference in gains between the two tutoring systems, "suggesting that students did not improve more or less with either of the tutoring systems".

> "For procedural learning gain, analysis of variance (ANOVA) revealed no significant differences in gains between students using ORCCA and StoichTutor, F(1, 59) = 1.36, p = .249, suggesting that students did not improve more or less with either of the tutoring systems."
