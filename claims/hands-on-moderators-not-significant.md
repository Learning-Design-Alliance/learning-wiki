# No moderator variable significantly differentiates hands-on science effect sizes across subgroups

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Between-groups heterogeneity (QM) was not significant at alpha level .05 for any of the six moderators (study type, treatment time, school level, treatment location, geographical region, grade level). [→ Caglak 2017](#caglak-2017)
`q3 i?` Differences between reference groups and other groups within each moderator were not statistically significant, with 95% CIs for slope parameters capturing zero. [→ Caglak 2017 (2)](#caglak-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Caglak 2017

Caglak, S. (2017). Does hands-on science practices make an ımpact on achievement in science? A meta-analysis. Journal of Education in Science, Environment and Health (JESEH), 3(1), 69-87. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1125775

`q3 · i?`

Mixed-effects moderator analyses across six study-level variables (Table 1). The article reports "the between -groups heterogeneities ( QM) were not significant at alpha level .05", indicating effect sizes for each moderator level were not statistically different from each other.

> "The within -groups heterogeneities (QE) were statistically significant across all of the moderators; the between -groups heterogeneities ( QM) were not significant at alpha level .05."

### Caglak 2017 (2)

Caglak, S. (2017). Does hands-on science practices make an ımpact on achievement in science? A meta-analysis. Journal of Education in Science, Environment and Health (JESEH), 3(1), 69-87. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1125775

`q3 · i?`

Parameter estimates from mixed-effects modeling (Table 2). The article reports slope parameter 95% CI estimates "captured the zero point", so subgroup contrasts were not statistically significant.

> "Even though the mean effect size estimates for the reference groups were statistically significant, the difference between the reference groups and the other groups of interests in each moderator variable were not statistically significant (see Table 2)."
