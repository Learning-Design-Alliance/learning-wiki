## Evidence

### Benjamin D. Nye 2015

Benjamin D. Nye. (2015). Intelligent Tutoring Systems by and for the Developing World: A Review of Trends and Approaches for Educational Technology in a Global Context. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-014-0028-6

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports this field finding from the Cognitive Tutor project, citing Ogan et al. (2012); the underlying study's design is not described in the review. Sharing has serious implications for user models that assume each machine measures one person's work.

> "Recent findings from the Cognitive Tutor project show that computer sharing accounts for over 60 % of use in developing areas, with students leaving their own machines to share a machine instead (Ogan et al. 2012)."

### Benjamin D. Nye 2015 (2)

Benjamin D. Nye. (2015). Intelligent Tutoring Systems by and for the Developing World: A Review of Trends and Approaches for Educational Technology in a Global Context. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-014-0028-6

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review describes multi-input ITS field systems, citing Brunskill et al. (2010) for MultiLearn+ and Alcoholado et al. (2012) for Single Display Groupware. Neither field study indicated that students exchanged or shared input devices extensively under these conditions.

> "Since inputs are inexpensive compared to processors or displays, a four-user system like MultiLearn+ lowers the cost-per-student by almost three-quarters."
