# Across nations, boys outperform girls in mathematics by a small average margin while girls outperform boys in reading by a margin three times larger

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The mean overall sex difference in mathematics favoring boys was 10 to 11 points and remained relatively stable over ten years of PISA assessments. [→ Stoet G 2013](#stoet-g-2013)
`q3 i?` The average sex difference in reading favoring girls was three times larger than the sex difference in mathematics. [→ Stoet G 2013 (2)](#stoet-g-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Stoet G 2013

Stoet G, Geary DC (2013) Sex Differences in Mathematics and Reading Achievement Are Inversely Related: Within- and Across-Nation Assessment of 10 Years of PISA Data. PLoS ONE 8(3): e57988. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0057988

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Secondary analysis of PISA 2000, 2003, 2006 and 2009 data from nearly 1.5 million 15-year-olds in 75 countries, with scores normalized so the OECD average is 500 points (SD 100). The printed average mathematics sex difference favoring boys was "10 to 11 points".

> "Across nations, the mean overall sex difference in mathematics was small but remained relatively stable over the ten years at 10 to 11 points (Figure 1, top)."

### Stoet G 2013 (2)

Stoet G, Geary DC (2013) Sex Differences in Mathematics and Reading Achievement Are Inversely Related: Within- and Across-Nation Assessment of 10 Years of PISA Data. PLoS ONE 8(3): e57988. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0057988

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same PISA secondary analysis, Results section. The article reports the average reading sex difference favoring girls was "three times larger" than the mathematics sex difference; no effect size beyond score points is printed.

> "In contrast to the sex difference in mathematics, the difference in reading, favoring girls, receives relatively little attention, despite the fact that the average sex difference in reading was three times larger than the sex difference in mathematics (Figure 1, Table 2)."
