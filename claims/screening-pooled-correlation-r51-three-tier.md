# Screening tools in the Three Tier model show a moderate pooled correlation with academic achievement outcomes (r = .51), with very high heterogeneity

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across 13 studies and 67 effect sizes, screening and progress monitoring tools correlate moderately with concurrent and predictive academic achievement outcomes (r = .51, 95% CI [.35, .64], p = .001), with very high heterogeneity. [→ Keles 2025](#keles-2025)

## Evidence

### Keles 2025

Keles, S., ten Braak, D., Hagen, Å. M., & Melby-Lervåg, M. (2025). Validity of Early-Grade Screening Tools in the Three Tier (RTI/MTSS) Model: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Literacy, Language, and Mathematics. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10077-5

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Meta-analysis using a CHE model with RVE of 67 effect sizes from 13 validation studies of screening tools in ECEC through grade 2, reporting a "moderate overall pooled effect size for correlations ( r = .51, 95% CI [.35, .64], p = .001)". Heterogeneity was very high (Q(66) = 7579.60, p < .001; 95 PI [−.31, .89]).

> "Using a correlated-hierarchical effects robust variance estimation (CHE-RVE) model, results indicated a moderate overall pooled effect size for correlations ( r = .51, 95% CI [.35, .64], p = .001). However, this estimate should be interpreted cautiously, as heterogeneity was very high."
