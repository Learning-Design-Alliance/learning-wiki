# Reward does not alter the speed or variability of response execution in either stop signal task

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Reward conditions did not significantly affect mean reaction time or its variability on go trials in either task. [→ Sinopoli 2011](#sinopoli-2011)

## Evidence

### Sinopoli 2011

Sinopoli, K.J., Schachar, R., & Dennis, M. (2011). Reward Improves Cancellation and Restraint Inhibition Across Childhood and Adolescence. Developmental Psychology, 47(5), 1479-89. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0024440

`q3 · i?`

Repeated measures ANOVA of go-trial mean reaction time across NO, LOW, and HIGH reward conditions in both cancellation and restraint tasks. The article reports no significant reward effect on response execution speed; no effect size is printed.

> "Reward did not significantly affect the speed of response execution in either task (see Table 3)."

### Sinopoli 2011 (2)

Sinopoli, K.J., Schachar, R., & Dennis, M. (2011). Reward Improves Cancellation and Restraint Inhibition Across Childhood and Adolescence. Developmental Psychology, 47(5), 1479-89. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0024440

`q3 · i?`

Repeated measures ANOVA of the standard deviation of go reaction time across reward conditions and tasks. The article reports no reward effect and no significant reward interactions; no effect size is printed.

> "Reward did not alter the variability of go responses, and there were no significant interactions with reward (see Table 3)."
