## Evidence

### Benjamin Pollard 2020

Benjamin Pollard, Alexandra Werth, Robert Hobbs, and H. J. Lewandowski. (2020). Impact of a course transformation on students' reasoning about measurement uncertainty. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020160](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020160)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Code-level RD probe analysis (Fig. 4); in both semesters the code with the largest pre-to-post change was S4, and the article reports the U1 code "did not significantly change after the transformation," which "could suggest that students articulated their reasoning with greater sophistication."

> "Before the transformation, the other code that increased was U1, while that code did not signiﬁ- cantly change after the transformation. U1 represents a response that does not display more sophisticate reason- ing than the other codes. Therefore, the lack of an increase in U1 after the transformation compared to before could suggest that students articulated their reasoning with greater sophistication in the transformed course."
