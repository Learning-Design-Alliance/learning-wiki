## Evidence

### Lee Jia Jia 2026

Lee Jia Jia, Yeap Li Ling, Mohammed Ali Haider, Yap Chuan Sheng, Hassan Bassam Abdul Rasool, Dujaili Juman Abdulelah, Saw Pui San, Blebil Ali Qais. (2026). Experiential learning to advocacy: A peer-led approach to safe medication disposal in pharmacy education. PLOS One 21(4): e0343961. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0343961

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Item-level McNemar analysis (Table 4) of the 43 workshop participants' pre/post responses; items 2, 3, 6 and 10 improved significantly (e.g., item 6 from 20.93% to 62.79% correct, p < 0.001).

> "Significant improvements were observed for wastewater treatment inefficacy, incineration benefits, disposal of solid medicines and cream/ointments (p < 0.05)."

### Lee Jia Jia 2026 (2)

Lee Jia Jia, Yeap Li Ling, Mohammed Ali Haider, Yap Chuan Sheng, Hassan Bassam Abdul Rasool, Dujaili Juman Abdulelah, Saw Pui San, Blebil Ali Qais. (2026). Experiential learning to advocacy: A peer-led approach to safe medication disposal in pharmacy education. PLOS One 21(4): e0343961. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0343961

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Item 9 (MDI disposal) showed only 11.63% to 25.58% correct (p = 0.146, McNemar), a non-significant change; the authors suggest brief peer-led sessions may lack depth for technical topics.

> "post-intervention knowledge of item 9 remained relatively low, with less than 50% of participants correctly identifying whether pressurized metered-dose inhalers (MDIs) should be discarded in the garbage"
