## Evidence

### Halls Amy 2019

Halls Amy, Kanagasundaram Mohan, Lau-Walker Margaret, Diack Hilary, Bettles Simon. (2019). Using in situ simulation to improve care of the acutely ill patient by enhancing interprofessional working: a qualitative proof of concept study in primary care in England. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2018-028572

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative proof of concept study in three research-active General Practices in south east England; nine staff per practice participated in an in situ cardiac arrest simulation with debriefing, then individual semistructured interviews analysed by inductive thematic analysis. The article concludes the simulation was "well-received by practices and staff". No effect sizes are reported.

> "This study suggests in situ simulation can be an acceptable approach for interdisciplinary team training within primary care, being well-received by practices and staff."
