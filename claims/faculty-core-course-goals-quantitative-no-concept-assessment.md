## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh and Alexandru Maries 2012

Chandralekha Singh and Alexandru Maries. (2012). Core Graduate Courses: A Missed Learning Opportunity?. [doi:10.1063/1.4789732](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4789732)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Individual discussions with nine faculty members who regularly teach first-year graduate core courses about the goals of the courses and student performance on the conceptual problems. The authors summarize that instructors 'did not assess their conceptual understanding' and expected students to make conceptual connections on their own.

> "They expected that students should make conceptual connections on their own but did not assess their conceptual understanding."

### Chandralekha Singh and Alexandru Maries 2012 (2)

Chandralekha Singh and Alexandru Maries. (2012). Core Graduate Courses: A Missed Learning Opportunity?. [doi:10.1063/1.4789732](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4789732)

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

From the individual faculty discussions about course goals and the conceptual pre/post results. One instructor admitted 'he did not expect that students will improve significantly from pre-test to post-test' on conceptual topics, since his course targeted mathematically rigorous problem solving.

> "One graduate course instructor admitted that he did not expect that students will improve significantly from pre-test to post-test after the core courses on conceptual topics because his course focused on helping students learn to solve mathematically rigorous problems."
