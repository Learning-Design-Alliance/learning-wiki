Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • When applied by human evaluators, all four AI-generated rubrics met pooled scoring equivalence with a human-created baseline within a ±5-point margin — related
  • In automated evaluation, equivalence with the human baseline depended on the evaluator model: DeepSeek V3 met equivalence for all rubric sources while GPT-4.1 and GPT-4o scored below the baseline — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Equivalence failures under human evaluators were confined to five assignment-by-question-type cells, four of them on code questions
  • In one study of five LLMs scoring 60 undergraduate essays on IELTS rubric dimensions, systematic underestimation of human scores was the dominant bias trend, with some models also compressing scores into a narrow range
  • Open-weight (DeepSeek) and proprietary (OpenAI) rubrics produced equivalent grading outcomes in every assignment-approach comparison except Assignment 1 free-form — related
