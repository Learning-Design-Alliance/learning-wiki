## Evidence

### Ryu Jong-Sik 2021

Ryu Jong-Sik, Chung Hae Ryong, Meador Benjamin M., Seo Yongsuk, Kim Kyung-O. (2021). The Associations between Physical Fitness, Complex vs Simple Movement, and Academic Achievement in a Cohort of Fourth Graders. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18052293

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Authors' summary of the Pearson correlation results across the full cohort of 110 fourth graders; the sit-and-reach–English correlation in girls (r = 0.295, p = 0.038) was the sole exception among simple fitness measures.

> "In the current dataset, no correlations were found between simple fitness tests (physical activity, BMI, sit-ups, sit-and-reach, standing long jump, 50-m run, and hand grip) and academic achievement, except for the correlations between sit-and-reach and English scores in girls."

### Ryu Jong-Sik 2021 (2)

Ryu Jong-Sik, Chung Hae Ryong, Meador Benjamin M., Seo Yongsuk, Kim Kyung-O. (2021). The Associations between Physical Fitness, Complex vs Simple Movement, and Academic Achievement in a Cohort of Fourth Graders. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18052293

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Pearson correlations in the male subsample; these were the only significant correlations in boys, both small (r = 0.295 and r = −0.260).

> "In males, physical activity was correlated with Korean Literature (r = 0.295, p = 0.022), and the soda pop hand test times were correlated with math scores (r = −0.260, p = 0.045)."
