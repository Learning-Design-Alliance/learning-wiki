## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Online supernaturalness ratings showed a significant main effect of familiarity, with high-familiarity participants rating all scenario types significantly higher than low-familiarity participants. [→ Xiaodong Xu 2025](#xiaodong-xu-2025)

## Evidence

### Xiaodong Xu 2025

Xiaodong Xu, Cheng Jia, Kang Chen & Lijuan Chen. (2025). Unveiling the neural mechanisms of supernatural fiction comprehension using fNIRS. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00327-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Linear mixed-effects analysis of online supernaturalness ratings in the 61-participant experiment (χ² = 12.118, df = 1, p < 0.001), with high-familiarity readers rating "all types of scenarios signiﬁcantly higher" (β = 0.342).

> "The analysis revealed a signiﬁcant main effect of Famil- iarity (χ² = 12.118, df = 1,p < 0.001; see Fig.1c). Post-hoc comparisons using the emmeans package with FDR correction showed that participants with high familiarity consistently rated all types of scenarios signiﬁcantly higher than those with low familiarity (β = 0.342, t(59) = 3.493, pFDR < 0.001)."
