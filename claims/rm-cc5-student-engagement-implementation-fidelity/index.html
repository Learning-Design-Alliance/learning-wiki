Evidence¶

Nicole Shechtman 2019¶

Nicole Shechtman, Jeremy Roschelle, Mingyu Feng, Corinne Singleton. (2019). An Efficacy Study of a Digital Core Curriculum for Grade 5 Mathematics. AERA Open. https://doi.org/10.1177/2332858419850482

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Exploratory RQ4 descriptive analysis of RM-CC5 system use metrics for 881 of 940 treatment students, aggregated at the school level (Table 3). Students met about 36.7 objectives (school SD 5.2), worked an estimated 14.5 problems per day, and spent an estimated 9.8 minutes per day in Smarter Solving. The article concludes "all the RM-CC5 schools were compliant with the program model."

"Across all treatment schools, students were logged into RM-CC5 for an overall estimated average of 56.7 minutes per day, with a school-level SD of about 7.7 minutes."

Related Claims¶

  • Assigning schools to use RM-CC5 as their core Grade 5 mathematics curriculum for a full year did not significantly change state assessment achievement compared with business-as-usual schools — related
  • RM-CC5 usage metrics correlated positively with achievement, and together accounted for an additional 7.3% of variance in Grade 5 scores beyond prior achievement, though causality and relative contributions could not be inferred — related
  • Treatment classrooms used technology far more intensively than control classrooms, whose technology use remained supplemental — related
  • The authors interpret the null result as partly reflecting a tension between adaptive mastery learning and curricular coverage: students who master early topics slowly may not reach later topics tested by the state assessment — related
  • Practitioners identified classroom complexities and fidelity-of-implementation variability as considerations experimental research should account for beyond causal effects — related
  • LMS process indicators documented adaptive engagement during the eight-week intervention, with most experimental students reaching mastery by unit end — related
