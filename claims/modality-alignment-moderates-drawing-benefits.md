## Evidence

### Dechamps 2025

Dechamps, T., & Skulmowski, A. (2025). The Effective Design of Tasks Involving Learning by Drawing: Current Trends and Methodological Progress in Research on Drawing to Learn. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10026-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports that in both Jaeger et al. (2018) experiments on sketching and seductive details with a tectonic-plate text, "no significant effects concerning performance in the multiple-choice test" emerged, although fewer seductive details were recalled in the sketching and summary conditions than in the thinking condition.

> "the results of both experiments indicated that no significant effects concerning performance in the multiple-choice test were generated by the experimental manipulations pertaining to drawing."

### Dechamps 2025 (2)

Dechamps, T., & Skulmowski, A. (2025). The Effective Design of Tasks Involving Learning by Drawing: Current Trends and Methodological Progress in Research on Drawing to Learn. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10026-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review's interpretive argument, citing Van Meter and Garner (2005), that because drawing evokes "nonverbal representational modalities" and initiates pictorial processes, an assessment involving the pictorial modality may be needed to detect its benefits consistently.

> "This may be explained by the fact that the learning strategy of drawing evokes "nonverbal representational modalities" (Van Meter & Garner, 2005, p. 288), hence initiating pictorial processes."
