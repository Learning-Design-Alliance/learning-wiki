# Learners' spatial ability moderates whether realistic visualizations help or hinder learning

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Higher spatial-ability learners benefit from realistic or 3D presentations while lower spatial-ability learners perform better with simplified presentations, per studies the review reports. [→ Skulmowski 2022](#skulmowski-2022)

## Evidence

### Skulmowski 2022

Skulmowski, A., Nebel, S., Remmele, M., & Rey, G. D. (2022). Is a Preference for Realism Really Naive After All? A Cognitive Model of Learning with Realistic Visualizations. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09638-1

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review attributing this to Brucker et al. (2014), who compared dynamic visualizations of fish as contour drawings versus actual film footage. The review reports the ability-moderated reversal in scores; no effect size is printed.

> "participants with lower visuospatial abilities achieved higher scores when learning with the simplified drawings, while participants who had higher visuospatial abilities performed better after having learned with the real fish footage (Brucker et al., 2014)."
