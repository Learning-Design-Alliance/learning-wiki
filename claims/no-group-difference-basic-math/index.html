Evidence¶

Mielicki 2017¶

Mielicki, M. K., Kacinik, N. A., & Wiley, J. (2017). Bilingualism and symbolic abstraction: Implications for algebra learning. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2017.03.002

q3 · i? · associational · r2

In Experiment 1, both groups completed the Woodcock-Johnson III Applied Problems subtest under standard administration. Means were nearly identical and the difference was not significant: "t(59)=0.40,ns".

"There were no significant differences between monolinguals (M =0.59,SD =0.10)andbilinguals(M =0.61,SD =0.19)inproportion scoresonthebasicmathtask;t(59)=0.40,ns."

Mielicki 2017 (2)¶

Mielicki, M. K., Kacinik, N. A., & Wiley, J. (2017). Bilingualism and symbolic abstraction: Implications for algebra learning. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2017.03.002

q3 · i? · associational · r2

In Experiment 2 all participants received the same 12-item subset of the Applied Problems subtest. The general discussion states the groups "did not differ in performance on the basic math task".

"Bilinguals and monolinguals in the current studies did not differ in performance on the basic math task, which fails to support theideathatbilingualsaregenerallybetteratmath"
