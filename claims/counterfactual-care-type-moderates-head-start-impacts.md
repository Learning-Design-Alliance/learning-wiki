# Head Start's vocabulary impacts are concentrated among children who would otherwise receive home-based care, with no average advantage over other center-based alternatives

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` For home care compliers, Head Start enrollment improved receptive vocabulary by roughly 0.20 SD (principal-stratification LATE 0.23), more than 50% larger than the overall ITT estimate, while effects for center care compliers were negligible. [→ Morris 2018](#morris-2018)

## Evidence

### Morris 2018

Morris, P. A., Connors, M., Friedman-Krauss, A., McCoy, D. C., Weiland, C., Feller, A., Page, L., Bloom, H., & Yoshikawa, H. (2018). New Findings on Impact Variation From the Head Start Impact Study: Informing the Scale-Up of Early Childhood Programs. AERA Open. https://doi.org/10.1177/2332858418769287

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Principal stratification analysis (Feller et al., 2016; N = 4,385) using a Bayesian model to predict counterfactual care type, with a printed effect size of roughly 0.20 SD for home compliers. Table 5 reports LATE for home compliers of 0.23 (0.15, 0.30) versus 0.00 (−0.13, 0.14) for center compliers; p (LATE hc > LATEcc) = .99.

> "For the home-based group, we estimate that, after 1 year, enrollment in Head Start improved scores by roughly 0.20 SD—comparable to the difference in effects that we find for DLL versus non-DLL children. This impact is >50% larger than the corresponding HSIS ITT estimates (Puma et al., 2010a)."
