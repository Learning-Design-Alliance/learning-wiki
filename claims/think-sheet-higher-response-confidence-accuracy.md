# Completing a think sheet yields higher response-confidence accuracy than reading a refutation text or a standard text with a misconception list

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Misconceptions and instructional method did not interact to influence response-confidence accuracy, but a contrast analysis showed response-confidence accuracy was higher in the think-sheet group than in the other two groups (η² = .06). [→ Anja Prinz 2021](#anja-prinz-2021)

## Evidence

### Anja Prinz 2021

Anja Prinz, Stefanie Golke, Jörg Wittwer. (2021). Counteracting detrimental effects of misconceptions on learning and metacomprehension accuracy: The utility of refutation texts and think sheets. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-021-09535-8

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Contrast analysis (weights: think-sheet +2, refutation-text −1, control −1) on response-confidence accuracy (gamma correlations) in the randomized experiment, with 14 participants excluded for performance-score invariance: "F(1, 75) = 4.43, p = .039, η2 = .06 (medium effect)".
