# Pre-clerkship medical students' self-confidence and self-perceived competency rose from pre-test to post-test after simulation-based venipuncture training in one single-group pre-post study with no comparison group, and rose further at a one-year delayed assessment

## Evidence

### Kodikara Kaumudee 2023

Kodikara Kaumudee, Seneviratne Thilanka, Premaratna Ranjan. (2023). Pre-clerkship procedural training in venipuncture: a prospective cohort study on skills acquisition and durability. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04722-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Prospective cohort study of 55 second-year medical students who served as their own controls, assessed before and after SBPT with Likert-scale self-ratings. The article reports "a statically significant mean increase in self-confidence and self-competency" after training.

> "A statically significant mean increase in self-confidence and self-competency was observed in the participants following SBPT (p-value < 0.05)."

### Kodikara Kaumudee 2023 (2)

Kodikara Kaumudee, Seneviratne Thilanka, Premaratna Ranjan. (2023). Pre-clerkship procedural training in venipuncture: a prospective cohort study on skills acquisition and durability. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04722-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Delayed assessment one year after training, in the clinical setting. Table 1 reports self-confidence means of 2.89 (0.69) simulated versus 4.09 (0.55) clinical, p < 0.001, and self-competency 2.42 (0.57) versus 3.80 (0.40), p < 0.001; "most (n = 38; 69.10%)" rated confidence at level four.

> "When assessed for self-confidence to perform venipuncture, one year after venipuncture training in the skills lab, most (n = 38; 69.10%) of the participants rated self-confidence as level four."
