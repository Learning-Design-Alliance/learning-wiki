# Number of peer-instructed seminars attended is positively associated with end-of-course exam scores, even when corrected for previous academic achievement

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a 10-week physiology course, the number of seminars attended was positively associated with summative exam scores (B = 0.34; p < 0.01, R2 = 0.20), and the association remained significant after controlling for previous academic achievement (B = 0.15; p = 0.02; R2 = 0.48). [→ Bouwmeester Rianne A. M. 2016](#bouwmeester-rianne-a-m-2016)

## Evidence

### Bouwmeester Rianne A. M. 2016

Bouwmeester Rianne A. M., de Kleijn Renske A. M., van Rijen Harold V. M. (2016). Peer-instructed seminar attendance is associated with improved preparation, deeper learning and higher exam scores: a survey study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0715-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression analysis in a survey study of 124 students (82.6% of the cohort) in a second-year physiology course, linking headcount-measured seminar attendance to summative exam scores. The article reports "B = 0.34; p < 0.01, R2 = 0.20" uncorrected and "B = 0.15; p = 0.02; R2 = 0.48" corrected for previous achievement.

> "Table 6 indicates that the number of seminars attended is positively associated with end-of-course exam scores (B = 0.34; p < 0.01, R2 = 0.20, ΔR2 = 0.19), even when corrected for previous achievement (B = 0.15; p = 0.02; R2 = 0.48, ΔR2 = 0.47)."
