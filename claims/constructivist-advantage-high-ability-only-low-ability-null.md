# The constructivist environment significantly favoured high ability students over high ability transmissive students, but low ability constructivist and low ability transmissive students did not differ significantly

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` High ability constructivist students scored significantly higher than high ability transmissive students (t=2.94 > 2.02). [→ Atomatofa 2016](#atomatofa-2016)
`q2 i?` No significant difference in mean achievement scores between low ability constructivist and low ability transmissive students (t=1.27 < 1.96); equivalence was not tested. [→ Atomatofa 2016 (2)](#atomatofa-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Atomatofa 2016

Atomatofa, R., Okoye, N., & Igwebuike, T. (2016). Learning Environments as Basis for Cognitive Achievements of Students in Basic Science Classrooms in Nigeria. Universal Journal of Educational Research 4(6): 1471-1478. [doi:10.13189/ujer.2016.040627](https://doi.org/10.13189/ujer.2016.040627)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

t-test of high ability groups: constructivist (n=26, mean 30.31) vs transmissive (n=28, mean 26.39), df=52, t=2.94 vs t-critical 2.02. The article reports the difference "in favor of the constructivist group". No effect size printed.

> "A significant difference in the mean achievement scores between high ability constructivist and high ability transmissive group."

### Atomatofa 2016 (2)

Atomatofa, R., Okoye, N., & Igwebuike, T. (2016). Learning Environments as Basis for Cognitive Achievements of Students in Basic Science Classrooms in Nigeria. Universal Journal of Educational Research 4(6): 1471-1478. [doi:10.13189/ujer.2016.040627](https://doi.org/10.13189/ujer.2016.040627)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

t-test of low ability groups: constructivist (n=44, mean 20.52) vs transmissive (n=48, mean 19.41), df=90, t=1.27 vs t-critical 1.96; hypothesis two was accepted for this comparison. No effect size printed.

> "No significant difference in mean achievement scores between low ability constructivist and low ability transmissive group."
