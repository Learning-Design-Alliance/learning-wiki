# In the author's cited longitudinal classroom experiments, lessons with retrieval practice raised course grades from a C to an A level, and three spaced retrieval opportunities were optimal even nine months later

## Evidence

### Pooja K. Agarwal 2024

Pooja K. Agarwal. (2024). Personal reflections on science communication and sharing retrieval practice research with teachers. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09839-w

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

The commentary attributes this to longitudinal classroom experiments conducted over nearly 10 years with 1,500 K–12 students in a public school district near St. Louis, Missouri, comparing retrieval-practice lessons to re-reading or no-retrieval lessons. Reported here as the authors' summary of that work.

> "we found that teachers' lessons that included retrieval practice—compared to re-reading statements or lessons without retrieval practice—benefitted students' long-term learning and raised course grades from a C to an A level (Roediger et al., 2011)"

### Pooja K. Agarwal 2024 (2)

Pooja K. Agarwal. (2024). Personal reflections on science communication and sharing retrieval practice research with teachers. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09839-w

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The commentary attributes this follow-up classroom experiment finding to McDaniel et al. (2011): three spaced retrieval opportunities were optimal, with retention measured nine months later. No effect size is printed in this article.

> "lessons with three spaced opportunities for retrieval practice was optimal for increasing learning, even when measured nine months later (McDaniel et al., 2011)"
