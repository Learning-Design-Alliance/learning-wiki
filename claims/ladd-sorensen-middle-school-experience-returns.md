# In a North Carolina middle school study, a teacher with 12 years of experience raised scores 0.08 SD in ELA and 0.18 SD in math versus a novice teacher, with returns persisting through 21–27 years

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r2` · `q3` · `i0`–`i2`

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` A teacher with 12 years of experience raised test scores 0.08 SD in ELA and 0.18 SD in math compared to a teacher with no prior experience. [→ Kini 2016](#kini-2016)
`q3 i0` Math and ELA teachers with 21–27 years of experience were still 0.04 SD more effective than when they had five years of experience. [→ Kini 2016 (2)](#kini-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Kini 2016

Kini, T., & Podolsky, A. (2016). Does Teaching Experience Increase Teacher Effectiveness? A Review of the Research. Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/our-work/publications-resources/does-teaching-experience-increase-teacher-effectiveness-review-research

`q3 · i2` · `review · r2`

A study of 250,000 North Carolina middle school students over five years using teacher fixed effects found clear returns to experience through at least 12 years, with printed effect sizes of 0.08 standard deviations in ELA and "0.18 standard deviations in math" versus a novice teacher, per the review's summary.

> "A teacher with 12 years of experience raised test scores from 0.08 standard deviations in ELA to 0.18 standard deviations in math as compared to a teacher with no prior experience."

### Kini 2016 (2)

Kini, T., & Podolsky, A. (2016). Does Teaching Experience Increase Teacher Effectiveness? A Review of the Research. Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/our-work/publications-resources/does-teaching-experience-increase-teacher-effectiveness-review-research

`q3 · i0` · `review · r2`

The same middle school study found returns level off after 12 years, yet printed an effect size showing "math and ELA teachers with 21–27 years were still 0.04 standard deviations more effective" than at five years of experience; this printed effect size of 0.04 standard deviations is below d = 0.2.

> "Although the returns level off after 12 years for math and ELA teachers, the study found that math and ELA teachers with 21–27 years were still 0.04 standard deviations more effective than when they had five years of experience."
