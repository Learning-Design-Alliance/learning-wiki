# Screen time and television viewing are associated with poorer cognition and academic achievement in children, whereas sedentary behaviors such as reading and drawing may improve them

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` High levels of screen time, especially television watching, are associated with poorer cognitive functions and academic achievement in children and adolescents. [→ Naveed Sehrish 2020](#naveed-sehrish-2020)
`q2 i?` Some sedentary behaviors such as reading and drawing may improve cognitive functions and academic performance, explaining weak associations of total objectively measured sedentary time with cognition. [→ Naveed Sehrish 2020 (2)](#naveed-sehrish-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Naveed Sehrish 2020

Naveed Sehrish, Lakka Timo, Haapala Eero A. (2020). An Overview on the Associations between Health Behaviors and Brain Health in Children and Adolescents with Special Reference to Diet Quality. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17030953

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review of observational studies reporting that "a longer time spent watching television" is associated with poorer cognitive functions and academic achievement in children and adolescents. No effect sizes printed.

> "High levels of screen time, and especially a longer time spent watching television, has been associated with poorer cognitive functions and academic achievement in children and adolescents [70,71,72]."

### Naveed Sehrish 2020 (2)

Naveed Sehrish, Lakka Timo, Haapala Eero A. (2020). An Overview on the Associations between Health Behaviors and Brain Health in Children and Adolescents with Special Reference to Diet Quality. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17030953

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Review reports that "some sedentary behaviors, such as reading and drawing, may improve" cognition and academic performance, which the review offers to explain inconsistent associations of total sedentary time with cognition.

> "In contrast, some sedentary behaviors, such as reading and drawing, may improve cognitive functions and academic performance in children [73]."
