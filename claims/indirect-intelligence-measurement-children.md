# Testing elementary psychic states is a poor direct measure of adult intelligence, but attention or memory measures are legitimate indirect indices in children

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Because higher ideation is not yet developed in children, measuring attention or memory via psychic correlation is a legitimate indirect route to the intellectual level of children. [→ De Sanctis 1911](#de-sanctis-1911)

## Evidence

### De Sanctis 1911

De Sanctis, S. (1911). Mental development and the measurement of the level of intelligence. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 2, 498-507. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0069835

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument from the lecture text, not a tested result. The author reasons that since higher ideation is undeveloped in children, indirect measures of attention or memory may stand for intellectual level, while the goal "is not always reached... by this indirect measure."

> "In order to test the intelligence of children it is necessary, then, to put one's faith in the principle of psychic correlations. Assuming this principle one can legitimately take as the measure of the intellectual level the measure of attention or of memory."
