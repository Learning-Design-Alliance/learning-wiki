# Preschool students who miss more school have lower kindergarten readiness scores on math, letter recognition, and social-emotional measures, controlling for incoming skills

## Evidence

### Ehrlich 2014

Ehrlich, S. B., Gwynne, J. A., Pareja, A. S., & Allensworth, E. M. (2014). Preschool Attendance in Chicago Public Schools: Relationships with Learning Outcomes and Reasons for Absences. UChicago CCSR Research Report. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED555650

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of 1,265 four-year-olds in CPS's Preschool Longitudinal Study (2010-11), using Woodcock-Johnson III fall scores as controls and the KRT spring assessment. The report finds missing more school "still significantly associated with lower scores in math, letter recognition, and social-emotional development" after controls.

> "The more days of preschool a student misses at age four, the lower s/he scores on the math, letter recognition, and social-emotional portions of CPS's Kindergarten Readiness Tool (KRT) at the end of the school year, controlling for entering skills upon entering preschool."
