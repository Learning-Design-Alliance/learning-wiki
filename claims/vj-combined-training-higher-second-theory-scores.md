# Combined virtual-then-jaw (V-J) training yields higher second theoretical examination scores than single-method training in preclinical implant education

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After operation training, the V-J group scored significantly higher on the second theoretical exam than the other groups, and the J-V group scored higher than the J and V groups. [→ Zhang Baoping 2020](#zhang-baoping-2020)

## Evidence

### Zhang Baoping 2020

Zhang Baoping, Li Sihong, Gao Shuting, Hou Mingfang, Chen Hong, He Lulu, Li Yiting, Guo Yumeng, Wang Errui, Cao Rui, Cheng Jingyang, Li Ruiping, Zhang Kailiang. (2020). Virtual versus jaw simulation in Oral implant education: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02152-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial of 80 undergraduates in four groups (n = 20 each); one-way ANOVA of second theoretical exam scores (V-J 62.90 ± 3.70; J-V 60.05 ± 2.73) showed "statistical significance in the difference (P < 0.001)". No effect size printed.

> "the results show statistical significance in the difference (P < 0.001). The V-J group was found to be significantly superior to other groups, whereas the J-V group performed better than the J and V groups."
