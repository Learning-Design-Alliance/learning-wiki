## Evidence

### Newman 1995

Richard S. Newman and Mahna T. Schwager. (1995). Students' Help Seeking During Problem Solving: Effects of Grade, Goal, and Prior Achievement. American Educational Research Journal, 32(2), 352-376. https://www.jstor.org/stable/1163435

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Logistic regression analysis of Day 3 audiotaped requests from 118 students, with frequencies dichotomized; the article reports significant grade predictions, chi-square (1) = 6.99, p < .01, and 4.32, p < .05, for the "vague, nonspecific way a lack of understanding" contrast.

> "Third graders were more likely than sixth graders to request the answer (ANS; Ms = 1.0 and 0.4, respectively) and to express in a vague, nonspecific way a lack of understanding (LOU; Ms = 0.5 and 0.2, respectively)."

### Newman 1995 (2)

Richard S. Newman and Mahna T. Schwager. (1995). Students' Help Seeking During Problem Solving: Effects of Grade, Goal, and Prior Achievement. American Educational Research Journal, 32(2), 352-376. https://www.jstor.org/stable/1163435

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Grade (2) x goal (2) x achievement (3) x request-type MANOVA of Day 3 requests showed a grade x type interaction, F(3,104) = 5.71, p < .001; simple effects with the Bonferroni technique showed the hint differences. The article notes "no grade differences in either requests for explanations or spontaneously generated requests."

> "Although sixth graders asked for more hints of both types (Ms = 2.6 and 2.0, for big and little hints, respectively) than did third graders (Ms = 1.8 and 1.1), there were no grade differences in either requests for explanations or spontaneously generated requests"
