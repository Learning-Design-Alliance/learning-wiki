Mastery goals correlate positively (r = .17) and performance-avoidance goals negatively (r = −.17) with mathematics achievement, while performance-approach goals barely do¶

Claim · All claims
Evidence · 1 study · review r3 · q3 · i1 small · n=30 meta-analyses, 66 correlations from 13,853 effect sizes (~4.66 million participants)

In Huang's (2011) meta-analysis, mastery goals correlate r = .17 with mathematics achievement, performance-approach goals .07 and performance-avoidance goals −.17 (k = 19 each). These are correlations compiled across meta-analyses: they describe how strongly the characteristic goes with mathematics achievement, not what causes it.

Evidence¶

Breit et al. 2025¶

Breit, M., Schneider, M., & Preckel, F. (2025). Mathematics achievement and learner characteristics: A systematic review of meta-analyses. Learning and Individual Differences, 118, 102621. doi:10.1016/j.lindif.2024.102621

q3 · systematic review of meta-analyses · i1 · small effect, r = .17, .07, −.17 · n=30 meta-analyses, 66 correlations from 13,853 effect sizes (~4.66 million participants) · review · r3

The review compiles zero-order correlations from published meta-analyses (each with at least ten effect sizes, the largest one kept where several covered a variable), found by a systematic PsycINFO search in July 2024. It pools nothing further and controls for nothing: many of these characteristics correlate with one another, so a correlation here says how strongly the characteristic goes with mathematics achievement, not that it causes it. Mastery goals r = .17, performance-approach .07, performance-avoidance −.17, each on 19 effect sizes in mixed-age samples (Huang 2011).

Discussion¶

The ordering matches achievement-goal theory: mastery goals go with persistence and absorption in the task, avoidance goals with self-concern and anxiety. All three correlations are small.
