## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Individual differences in verbal and visual working memory, entered as covariates, did not explain modality or redundancy effects in study time or knowledge gain. [→ Knoop‐van Campen CAN 2018](#knoopvan-campen-can-2018)

## Evidence

### Knoop‐van Campen CAN 2018

Knoop‐van Campen CAN, Segers E, Verhoeven L. (2018). The modality and redundancy effects in multimedia learning in children with dyslexia. Dyslexia. 2018;24:140–155. https://doi.org/10.1002/dys.1585

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the experiment's ANCovas, verbal and visual working memory were covariates; no significant main effects of verbal working memory (e.g., F(1, 42) = 0.54, p = .466, η2 p = .013) or visual working memory on learning time, and no interactions. Children with dyslexia scored significantly lower on verbal working memory (d = 1.24).

> "In contrast to our expectation, we did not find individual differences in working memory that would explain modality or redundancy effects in study time of knowledge gain."
