# Undifferentiated GenAI integrity rules convert linguistic disadvantage into integrity risk for EAL students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Integrity rules that treat GenAI as a single category of unauthorised assistance systematically disadvantage multilingual cohorts by imposing higher compliance burdens, increasing suspicion of newly fluent prose, and enabling selective enforcement on weak evidence. [→ Grace Li 2026](#grace-li-2026)

## Evidence

### Grace Li 2026

Grace Li. (2026). GenAI Assessment and Language Equity: Drawing the Line between Support and Substitution. Journal of Academic Ethics. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10805-026-09760-1

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Normative argument in the introduction: the risk is structural rather than behavioural, flowing from how language assistance operates in multilingual cohorts, not from EAL students being 'more likely to behave dishonestly'. The article offers doctrinal reasoning, not empirical measurement.

> "integrity rules that do not distinguish language support from substantive substitution can systematically disadvantage multilingual cohorts. They can do so by imposing higher compliance burdens on the students most likely to use legitimate language support, by increasing suspicion where an EAL student’s prose suddenly becomes fluent"
