## Evidence

### Kovacevic Pedja 2019

Kovacevic Pedja, Dragic Sasa, Kovacevic Tijana, Momcicevic Danica, Festic Emir, Kashyap Rahul, Niven Alexander S., Dong Yue, Gajic Ognjen. (2019). Impact of weekly case-based tele-education on quality of care in a limited resource medical intensive care unit. Critical Care. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13054-019-2494-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Before-after cohort study of all MICU patients in 2015 (before, n = 667) versus 2016–2017 (after), reported in the article's abstract-level results summary. The article reports the mortality percentages, length-of-stay figures, and cost savings descriptively; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "The intervention was associated with reduction in ICU (43% vs 27%) and hospital (51% vs 44%) mortality, length of stay (8.3 vs 3.6 days), cost savings ($400,000 over 2 years), and a high level of staff satisfaction and engagement with the tele-education program."

### Kovacevic Pedja 2019 (2)

Kovacevic Pedja, Dragic Sasa, Kovacevic Tijana, Momcicevic Danica, Festic Emir, Kashyap Rahul, Niven Alexander S., Dong Yue, Gajic Ognjen. (2019). Impact of weekly case-based tele-education on quality of care in a limited resource medical intensive care unit. Critical Care. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13054-019-2494-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Multivariate regression analyses reported in the Results section, adjusting for age, sex, mechanical ventilation, and vasopressors. The article reports the associations as statistically significant without printing coefficients or effect sizes in the supplied text.

> "When adjusted for age, sex, mechanical ventilation, and vasopressors, tele-education remained associated with a significant decrease in hospital mortality (Additional file 1: Table S3) and shorter ICU and hospital length of stay (Additional file 1: Table S4, electronic data supplement)."
