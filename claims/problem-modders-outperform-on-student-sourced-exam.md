## Evidence

### Ameya S. Kolarkar and Aimee A. Callender 2016

Ameya S. Kolarkar and Aimee A. Callender. (2016). Learning Physics By Creating Problems: An Experiment. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1609.03517](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1609.03517)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Fall 2014 classroom study in a 1st year physics course for non-majors with 73 students; modders were a self-selected group (about 10% of the class). A two-tailed t-test on Exam 4 found "the modders performed significantly better than the non modders"; no effect size was printed.

> "A t-test was conducted to compare test performance between those who completed the modifications and those who did not on exam 4 performance, t(70) = 5.04, p = 0.00088, and the modders performed significantly better than the non modders."

### Ameya S. Kolarkar and Aimee A. Callender 2016 (2)

Ameya S. Kolarkar and Aimee A. Callender. (2016). Learning Physics By Creating Problems: An Experiment. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1609.03517](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1609.03517)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Baseline comparison in the same Fall 2014 study: a two-tailed t-test on Exam 1, before modifications were implemented, showed "no difference in test performance" between the future groups; equivalence was not formally tested.

> "For exam 1, there was no difference in test performance between those who later made modifications and those who did not, t(70) = 0.68, p = 0.55."
