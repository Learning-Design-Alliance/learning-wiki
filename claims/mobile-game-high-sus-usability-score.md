## Evidence

### Arachchilage 2013

Arachchilage, N. A. G., Love, S., & Maple, C. (2013). Can a Mobile Game Teach Computer Users to Thwart Phishing Attacks. International Journal for Infonomics (IJI), Volume 6, Issues 3/4. [doi:10.20533/iji.1742.4712.2013.0083](https://doi.org/10.20533/iji.1742.4712.2013.0083)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

SUS questionnaire administered after 15 minutes of engagement with the mobile game activity, measuring subjective satisfaction of the prototype interface on a five-point Likert scale. The article prints the score but no effect size or test statistic.

> "The score was significantly high with 84 percent (83.62 out of 100) [5]."

### Nalin Asanka Gamagedara Arachchilage 2015

Nalin Asanka Gamagedara Arachchilage. (2015). User-Centred Security Education: A Game Design to Thwart Phishing Attacks. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1511.03459](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1511.03459)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Subjective satisfaction evaluation of the prototype using the SUS scoring approach introduced by Brooke; the score was "signiﬁcantly high, 83.62 out of 100". No standardised effect size was printed.

> "Initially, we evaluated the participants subjective satis- faction of the mobile game prototype using SUS (System Usability Scale) scoring approach introduced by Brooke [6]. The score was signiﬁcantly high, 83.62 out of 100 ( ≈ 84%) [6]."
