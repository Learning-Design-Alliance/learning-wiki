`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports meta-analytic findings (citing Allan 2014; Jacob and Parkinson 2015; Titz 2014) that EF–academic performance relations are "generally small to medium in magnitude". No effect size values are printed in the quoted text.

> "Meta-analytic findings indicate that relations between EF and academic performance are generally small to medium in magnitude, with inhibition showing small effects and broader EF factors showing medium-sized effects on reading and mathematics achievement"

### Theobald 2026 (2)

Theobald, M., Kerner auch Koerner, J., Breitwieser, J., Buhr, L., Karbach, J., Könönen, T., Nobbe, L., Reinelt, T., Schaaf, M., Schmiedek, F., Wieland, L. M., & Blume, F. (2026). Executive Functions, Metacognition, Self-Regulation, and Self-Regulated Learning – What are We Talking About? A Review and Introduction of the EMERGE Model. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10157-0

`q3 · i?` · `review · r?`

The review reports, citing Jacob and Parkinson 2015, a ranked pattern across EF subcomponents: "cognitive flexibility (attention shifting) tends to show the strongest medium-sized effects", then WM, then response inhibition. No numeric effect sizes are printed.

> "Within this work, cognitive flexibility (attention shifting) tends to show the strongest medium-sized effects, followed by slightly smaller medium effects for WM and small effects for response inhibition (Jacob and Parkinson 2015)."
