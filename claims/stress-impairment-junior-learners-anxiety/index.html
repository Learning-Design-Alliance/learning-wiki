Evidence¶

Vage A 2024¶

Vage A, Spence AD, McKeown G, Gormley GJ, Hamilton PK. (2024). Simulate to stimulate? A systematic review of stress, learning, and performance in healthcare simulation. The Ulster Medical Journal. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11591218/

q2 · i? · review · r3

Narrative synthesis across the review's impaired-performance category. The review highlights "the deleterious effects of overstimulating" students and junior residents, suggesting learner level moderates the stress-performance relationship.

"Of the 11 studies showing impaired performance, nine (82 %) involved healthcare students and/or junior medical residents; highlighting the deleterious effects of overstimulating those populations for whom learning is the gateway to proficiency."

Vage A 2024 (2)¶

Vage A, Spence AD, McKeown G, Gormley GJ, Hamilton PK. (2024). Simulate to stimulate? A systematic review of stress, learning, and performance in healthcare simulation. The Ulster Medical Journal. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11591218/

q2 · i? · review · r3

Review synthesis of correlational patterns within the 11 impaired-performance studies; the association between rising perceived anxiety or stress and poorer performance is reported as a trend, with no pooled effect size printed.

"In 10 (91 %) studies, increasing levels of perceived anxiety, cognitive load and/or stress were associated with poorer performance."

Related Claims¶

  • Inducing a high-stress environment during healthcare simulation is generally associated with impaired performance — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Studies finding unaltered performance under simulation stress tended to involve more experienced participants and smaller samples — related
  • Stress biomarker and self-report measures show negative correlations with simulation performance across outcome categories — related
  • Negative emotions, most commonly stress, were inversely correlated with clinical performance in some simulated or educational acute care settings — related
  • The single study reporting enhanced performance under stress may reflect stressors that failed to induce sufficient stress — related
