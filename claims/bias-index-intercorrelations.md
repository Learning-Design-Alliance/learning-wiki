## Evidence

### McManus I C 2013

McManus I C, Elder Andrew T, Dacre Jane. (2013). Investigating possible ethnicity and sex bias in clinical examiners: an analysis of data from the MRCP(UK) PACES and nPACES examinations. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-103

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Secondary correlational analysis of examiner bias indices across both examination formats, reporting r = -.270 and r = -.265, both p < .001.

> "The extent of sex bias and ethnic bias showed a significant negative correlation in PACES (r = −.270, p < .001) and nPACES (r = −.265, p < .001), suggesting that examiners who rated white candidates more highly also rated female candidates more highly."

### McManus I C 2013 (2)

McManus I C, Elder Andrew T, Dacre Jane. (2013). Investigating possible ethnicity and sex bias in clinical examiners: an analysis of data from the MRCP(UK) PACES and nPACES examinations. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-103

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Correlational analysis showing the hawkishness-ethnic bias link held only in nPACES; the article flags this format difference, so the association is mixed across examinations.

> "hawkishness only correlated with ethnic bias in nPACES (r = .302, p < .001) but not in PACES (r = .021, NS)"
