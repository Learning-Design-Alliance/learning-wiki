# Lessons in Character shows no discernible effects on knowledge, attitudes, and values

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Lessons in Character 2006

Lessons in Character. (2006). WWC Intervention Report, What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/InterventionReport/270

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

WWC analysis of the Dietsch, Bayha, & Zheng (2005) trial's four SCCP-II-based attitude scales (student respect, friendship and belonging, shaping the environment, support and care by teachers and staff); none reached significance.

> "The average response across each of these four scales was neither statistically significant (as calculated by the WWC) nor large enough to be substantively important using WWC criteria."

### Lessons in Character 2006 (2)

Lessons in Character. (2006). WWC Intervention Report, What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/InterventionReport/270

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The quasi-experimental DeVargas (1998) study of 61 fifth-grade students in Fort Worth, Texas measured moral judgment with the Sociomoral Reflection Measure—Short Form; the WWC-calculated effect size was 0.11, not significant.

> "The DeVargas study reported neither statistically significant nor substantively important differences between the Lessons in Character group and the comparison groups on a measure of moral development."
