# Comparison and integration of different types of group awareness information is a research gap despite firmly established positive tool effects

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Positive effects of cognitive group awareness tools are firmly established, but the awareness information used is inconsistent and rarely compared across types. [→ Schnaubert 2019](#schnaubert-2019)

## Evidence

### Schnaubert 2019

Schnaubert, L., & Bodemer, D. (2019). Providing different types of group awareness information to guide collaborative learning. International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning, 14, 7–51. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-018-9293-y

`q2 · i?`

The article's framing of its research gap as a narrative statement: positive tool effects are established, yet "there is no consistency with regard to the awareness information used" and attempts to compare types are rare. No empirical evidence for this synthesis is reported in this study itself.

> "While positive effects of such tools are firmly established, there is no consistency with regard to the awareness information used and a wide range of target concepts exist."
