## Evidence

### Lund Frederike 2012

Lund Frederike, Schultz Jobst-Hendrik, Maatouk Imad, Krautter Markus, Möltner Andreas, Werner Anne, Weyrich Peter, Jünger Jana, Nikendei Christoph. (2012). Effectiveness of IV Cannulation Skills Laboratory Training and Its Transfer into Clinical Practice: A Randomized, Controlled Trial. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0032831

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Clinical validation analysis of the randomized trial's video ratings: students were classified as competent (ratings 1–2), borderline (3–4), or incompetent (5–6). The intervention group had "nine IG students were rated as "competent"" versus seven in the control group, and fewer incompetent students (eight vs nineteen).

> "Using the IPPI item "Overall ability to perform the procedure [including technical and professional skills]" [41] as a way of clinical validation, nine IG students were rated as "competent", 24 as "borderline", and eight as "incompetent", while in the CG seven students were regarded to be "competent", 14 to be "borderline", and 19 to be "incompetent"."
