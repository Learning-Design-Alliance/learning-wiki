# Teachers reported that trusting students to generate and pursue their own questions worked better than expected and revealed new student capabilities and gaps

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Many teachers found that student-generated questions matched or exceeded what they would have asked, while also discovering students lacked experience in questioning, sensemaking, and independent research. [→ Iwatani 2020](#iwatani-2020)

## Evidence

### Iwatani 2020

Iwatani, E., Means, B., Romero, M. R., & Vang, M. C. (2020). Deepening Science Engagement with Challenge Based Learning: Research Report. San Mateo, CA: Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/f928a607-e645-49d6-abbf-3d9b42cf7383

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Qualitative findings from teacher interviews. Teachers observed students could productively generate science questions, reason deeply, and collaborate, but also struggled with self-directed questioning, secondary research skills, and applying concepts to new problems.

> "For many teachers, asking students to do more of the questioning and thinking worked out better than they had expected, which was a pleasant surprise."
