Evidence¶

Liao 2026¶

Liao, H.-H., Liu, T.-C., & Lin, Y.-C. (2026). External and internal smartphone distractions: Effects on phone use and learning performance in college classrooms. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09794-3

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Independent-samples t-test (Welch's correction) comparing phone-use frequency between the notification-revealed (N = 42) and notification-hidden (N = 42) groups in a classroom video-lesson experiment. The quote shows the "significantly higher PUF" with d = 1.824.

"the NR group exhibited a significantly higher PUF than the NH group (alternative hypothesis: NR > NH; t = 8.358, df = 45.276, p < .001, Cohen's d = 1.824; H1 supported)"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Phone-use frequency and duration increase phone-use mental effort, which negatively affects learning performance, mediating the behavior–performance link
  • Habitual phone use heightens smartphone vigilance but does not directly increase checking frequency; it lengthens use duration only when notifications are hidden — related
  • Notification-hidden students showed longer average phone-use duration and no performance difference versus notification-revealed students — related
  • Phone-use behavior under hidden notifications is highly heterogeneous: about half of NH students abstained entirely, and most NR checks were brief glances — related
  • Smartphone settings moderate how vigilance translates into phone use: vigilance raises checking frequency more when notifications are revealed, and use duration more when notifications are hidden — related
