# Peer teaching and lectures increase learning effort: peer teaching more than quadruples the odds of over two hours weekly ECG self-study versus self-directed learning

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Compared with self-directed learning, peer teaching increased the odds of spending more than 2 h/week on independent ECG self-study more than four-fold (OR 4.42; 95% CI 2.64 to 7.38) and lectures increased them about two-fold (OR 2.14; 95% CI 1.33 to 3.45). [→ Raupach Tobias 2013](#raupach-tobias-2013)

## Evidence

### Raupach Tobias 2013

Raupach Tobias, Brown Jamie, Anders Sven, Hasenfuss Gerd, Harendza Sigrid. (2013). Summative assessments are more powerful drivers of student learning than resource intensive teaching formats. BMC Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/1741-7015-11-61

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary outcome (learning time) in the adjusted multivariate regression of the 534-student trial; Table 2 prints peer teaching OR 4.42 (2.64 to 7.38) and lectures OR 2.14 (1.33 to 3.45) for learning time, and OR 1.94 and 1.81 for additional material use. The article reports "the odds of spending more than 2 h/week on independent ECG self-study were more than four times those in the self-directed learners".

> "Among students receiving peer teaching, the odds of spending more than 2 h/week on independent ECG self-study were more than four times those in the self-directed learners. In contrast, students receiving lectures had only 1.8 times the odds"
