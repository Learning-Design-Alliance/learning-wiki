## Evidence

### J. Strauss 2014

J. Strauss. (2014). Presenting particle physics and quantum mechanics to the general public. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1505.05032](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1505.05032)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article reports, citing Pawan Sinha's Project Prakash, that newly sighted children "exhibits profound impairments": they cannot organize regions of different colors and brightness into larger assemblies, and perceive a ball and its shadow as distinct objects. No effect size is printed.

> "the experience of a Prakash child soon after gaining sight is different. The newly sighted exhibits profound impairments."

### J. Strauss 2014 (2)

J. Strauss. (2014). Presenting particle physics and quantum mechanics to the general public. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1505.05032](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1505.05032)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article reports, citing Sinha, that with the motion cue "the confusing soup of pixels is recognized as a meaningful pattern"; the brain recognizes an object as the invariant of part of a visual scene under translation and/or rotation. Reported second-hand, no statistics printed.

> "the confusing soup of pixels is recognized as a meaningful pattern with the introduction of one particular visual cue: motion."
