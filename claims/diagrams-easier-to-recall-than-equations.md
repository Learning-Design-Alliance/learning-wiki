## Evidence

### Eugene T. Torigoe 2015

Eugene T. Torigoe. (2015). Emphasizing Expert Practice with Spaced Recall. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.082](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.082)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview remarks from the two students during the first spaced recall session, in which both generally remembered the diagram and its labels well. The article reports "remembering diagrams was much easier than remembering the equations." No effect size printed.

> "They both felt that remembering diagrams was much easier than remembering the equations."

### Eugene T. Torigoe 2015 (2)

Eugene T. Torigoe. (2015). Emphasizing Expert Practice with Spaced Recall. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.082](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.082)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Observation across all recall sessions of the heat engine diagram (Figure 1): the bar-width feature was not salient, and one student replaced the bars with arrows. The article states the solution video presents that information but students never used it.

> "Neither student in any of the recall sessions (including the immediate recall) used the width of the bars to represent the relative magnitude of the heat flows and work, even though the solution video presents that information."
