# The frequency effect was significant for the picture identification test but not for the L1 meaning-matching test

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Picture identification scores improved significantly across sessions, but increasing read-aloud frequency did not significantly improve L1 meaning-matching performance. [→ Lin 2014](#lin-2014)

## Evidence

### Lin 2014

Lu-Chun Lin. (2014). Learning Word Meanings from Teachers' Repeated Story Read-Aloud in EFL Primary Classrooms. English Language Teaching, 7(7). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v7n7p68

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Subtest analysis of the same 45-child quasi-experiment: picture identification showed a significant session effect ("F(2,88) = 9.44, p < .001, partial ŋ2 = .17"; effect sizes 0.65 and 0.44 for 1st vs. 3rd and 1st vs. 4th), while the L1 meaning-matching test showed none.

> "the effect of the frequency of teachers' read-aloud was only significant for the picture identification test, F(2,88) = 9.44, p < .001, partial ŋ2 = .17. The children correctly identified more target words on the picture identification test in the third and fourth sessions than they did in the first session (Table 4). There was, however, no significant difference between the third and fourth sessions. Increasing the frequency of reading aloud to students, on the other hand, did not significantly improve the students' performance on the L1 meaning-matching test."
