# Learning of second language morphosyntactic rules is highly skill-specific: comprehension practice yields comprehension skill and production practice yields production skill

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The learning of morphosyntactic rules in a second language is highly skill-specific, so that practicing a rule in comprehension does not automatically confer production skill and vice versa. [→ Robert M. DeKeyser 1997](#robert-m-dekeyser-1997)

## Evidence

### Robert M. DeKeyser 1997

Robert M. DeKeyser. (1997). Beyond Explicit Rule Learning: Automatizing Second Language Morphosyntax. Studies in Second Language Acquisition, Volume 19, Issue 2. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263197002040

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Controlled experiment in an artificial language: all subjects received identical instruction and equal amounts of comprehension and production practice, while the assignment of rules to modality varied between groups. The study reports that "the learning of morphosyntactic rules is highly skill-specific."

> "All subjects were taught the same rules and then practiced them, and all subjects had the same amount of comprehension and production practice, but which ruleswere practiced in comprehension and which in production varied between groups. Results showthat the learning of morphosyntactic rules is highly skill-specific"
