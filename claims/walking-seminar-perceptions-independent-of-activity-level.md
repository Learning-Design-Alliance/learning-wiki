## Evidence

### Bälter O. 2018

Bälter O., Hedin B., Tobiasson H., Toivanen S. (2018). Walking Outdoors during Seminars Improved Perceived Seminar Quality and Sense of Well-Being among Participants. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph15020303

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis combining the seminar questionnaire with the Active-Q physical activity questionnaire answered by 115 of the 131 students (average energy expenditure 11.5 MJ/day, SD 2). The article reports "no other correlation between any of the seminar questions and the level of physical activity, or Body-Mass Index (BMI)"; no effect size coefficients are printed.

> "However, there is no other correlation between any of the seminar questions and the level of physical activity, or Body-Mass Index (BMI). That is, the students were approximately equally positive to the walking seminars regardless of their normal physical activity level and BMI."

### Bälter O. 2018 (2)

Bälter O., Hedin B., Tobiasson H., Toivanen S. (2018). Walking Outdoors during Seminars Improved Perceived Seminar Quality and Sense of Well-Being among Participants. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph15020303

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparison between Active-Q responders and non-responders within the student survey on the Q2 well-being and Q3 quality items; the printed means are "5.7 vs. 5.1" and "5.3 vs. 4.6" with t-tests at p < 0.05. No effect size is printed.

> "Those who answered Active-Q felt significantly better after the seminar (Q2, 5.7 vs. 5.1, t-test p-value < 0.05) and also thought that the quality had improved more (Q3, 5.3 vs. 4.6, t-test p-value < 0.05) compared to those who did not answer Active-Q."
