## Evidence

### Hapnes Toba 2024

Hapnes Toba, Oscar Karnalim, Meliana Christianti Johan, Terutoshi Tada, Yenni Merlin Djajalaksana, and Tristan Vivaldy. (2024). Inappropriate Benefits and Identification of ChatGPT Misuse in Programming Tests: A Controlled Experiment. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-51979-6_54](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-51979-6_54)

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Open-ended survey question Q18 analyzed via LDA topic modeling (7 topics, coherence score 0.692) in the same experiment; participants named "the strategy to efficiently usage of memory (storage) and function arrangement" using a list or a dictionary data structure.

> "The participants describe that computer codes generated by ChatGPT have structural and syntactical advances in comparison to those written by beginners."

### Hapnes Toba 2024 (2)

Hapnes Toba, Oscar Karnalim, Meliana Christianti Johan, Terutoshi Tada, Yenni Merlin Djajalaksana, and Tristan Vivaldy. (2024). Inappropriate Benefits and Identification of ChatGPT Misuse in Programming Tests: A Controlled Experiment. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-51979-6_54](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-51979-6_54)

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Continuation of the Q18 topic-modeling analysis: participants judged ChatGPT code "mostly tidier and shorter (efficient), than those developed by students" and in some cases beyond typical first-year course materials.

> "Further, the generated ChatGPT code itself is mostly tidier and shorter (efficient), than those developed by students. In some cases, it is obvious to observe that the generated codes by ChatGPT are beyond the typical course materials for first-year students."
