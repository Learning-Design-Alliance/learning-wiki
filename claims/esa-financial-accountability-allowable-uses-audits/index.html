Evidence¶

Berry 2024¶

Berry, W. & Chait, R. (2024). Education savings accounts and accountability: A landscape analysis across states. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED663763

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Financial accountability analysis of the 15 statutes finds all specify allowable expenses, with a common list including private school tuition and fees, textbooks, educational therapies, tutoring services, and educational technology; less frequent allowances include summer and after-school program fees and workforce credentials.

"The statutes of all ESA programs contain language that explicitly specifies what types of services and materials ESA funds can be used for."

Berry 2024 (2)¶

Berry, W. & Chait, R. (2024). Education savings accounts and accountability: A landscape analysis across states. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED663763

q2 · i? · review · r2

Statute analysis reports "only 3 of the 15 state statutes" list prohibited uses (e.g., noneducational consumer electronics), and that all but 2 of the 15 statutes contain explicit audit language; eight states require annual audits and five require random audits. The brief notes much of this language is open to interpretation.

"While almost all ESA statutes detail allowable uses of funds, only 3 of the 15 state statutes explicitly list expenses that ESA funds cannot be used for."
