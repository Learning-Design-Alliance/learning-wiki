# Explicit attention-focusing and discrepancy-detection interventions largely reduce susceptibility; mindfulness interventions do not

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Explicit attention-focusing interventions (indicating discrepancies, veracity judgments) were largely positive across eight reviewed articles. [→ Rau 2025](#rau-2025)
`q3 i?` Mindfulness interventions yielded null or negative effects, with one study finding increased susceptibility. [→ Rau 2025 (2)](#rau-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Rau 2025

Rau, M. A., & Premo, A. E. (2025). Systematic Review of Educational Approaches to Misinformation. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10012-8

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's synthesis of eight attention-focusing articles reports that "Instructions to indicate discrepancies were effective (Putnam et al., 2017)", and slow reading and highlighting also improved discrepancy detection and memory for original information.

> "Results for the explicit attention-focusing interventions were largely positive. Instructions to indicate discrepancies were effective (Putnam et al., 2017), although one study found only marginal benefits (Umanath et al., 2019)."

### Rau 2025 (2)

Rau, M. A., & Premo, A. E. (2025). Systematic Review of Educational Approaches to Misinformation. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10012-8

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports that for the two mindfulness interventions, "their mindfulness intervention increased susceptibility to misinformation" in one study, with null effects in the other; mindfulness's nonjudgmental attitude may backfire for misinformation resistance.

> "Alberts et al. (2017) found null effects on participants' susceptibility. By contrast, Qi et al. (2018) found that their mindfulness intervention increased susceptibility to misinformation."
