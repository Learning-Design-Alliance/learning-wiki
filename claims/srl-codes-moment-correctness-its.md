# In-tutor correctness is lower after processing and error-realization utterances and higher after enacting utterances in stoichiometry ITS

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Attempts following utterances coded as processing information or realizing errors had significantly lower correctness, while attempts following enacting utterances had significantly higher correctness; planning was not significant. [→ Conrad Borchers 2024](#conrad-borchers-2024)

## Evidence

### Conrad Borchers 2024

Conrad Borchers, Jiayi Zhang, Ryan S. Baker, and Vincent Aleven. 2024. Using Think-Aloud Data to Understand Relations between Self-Regulation Cycle Characteristics and Student Performance in Intelligent Tutoring Systems. In The 14th Learning Analytics and Knowledge Conference (LAK '24). ACM. https://doi.org/10.1145/3636555.3636911

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Regression analysis of 630 attempts from ten students in two stoichiometry ITS. Processing OR = 0.53 (p = .014), enacting OR = 1.55 (p = .046), realizing errors OR = 0.34 (p = .015); "The effect of planning on correctness was not significant (𝑝 =.869)".

> "students had a significantly lower probability of having a correct attempt in the system after utterances with codes for processing and realizing errors in them. Conversely, students had a significantly higher probability of having a correct attempt in the system after utterances with enacting in them."
