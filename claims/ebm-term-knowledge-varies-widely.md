## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among medical students, 'sample size' was the best-understood term (65.09% could explain it) and 'intention-to-treat analysis' the least understood (6.96%). [→ Csertő Mónika 2019](#cserto-monika-2019)

## Evidence

### Csertő Mónika 2019

Csertő Mónika, Berényi Károly, Decsi Tamás, Lohner Szimonetta. (2019). Self-reported attitudes, knowledge and skills of using evidence-based medicine in daily health care practice: A national survey among students of medicine and health sciences in Hungary. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0225641

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Self-reported term understanding on a 5-point scale (Table 4, n = 1080 medical students). Printed percentages: sample size 65.09% "understand and I could explain to others"; intention-to-treat analysis 6.96%.

> "The most known term among medical students was 'sample size'; about two third of medical students answered that they could explain meaning of the term to others. In contrast, only 7% of medical students thought that they could explain the meaning of the term 'intention-to-treat analysis'."

### Csertő Mónika 2019 (2)

Csertő Mónika, Berényi Károly, Decsi Tamás, Lohner Szimonetta. (2019). Self-reported attitudes, knowledge and skills of using evidence-based medicine in daily health care practice: A national survey among students of medicine and health sciences in Hungary. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0225641

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of term-knowledge ratings between EBM-trained and non-trained medical students in the survey, scored as a 5-point scale; the article reports p<0.001 for all investigated terms.

> "those participating in an EBM course rated their knowledge regarding EBM-related terms to be significantly better than students who did not receive a training in EBM yet (with p<0.001 for all investigated terms)."

### Csertő Mónika 2019 (3)

Csertő Mónika, Berényi Károly, Decsi Tamás, Lohner Szimonetta. (2019). Self-reported attitudes, knowledge and skills of using evidence-based medicine in daily health care practice: A national survey among students of medicine and health sciences in Hungary. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0225641

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Health sciences students (n = 911): two terms showed no significant difference between trained and non-trained students. Equivalence was not tested.

> "although there was no significant difference in the knowledge of trained and non-trained students in case of the terms 'lost to follow-up' (p = 0.15) and 'number needed to treat' (p = 0.05)."
