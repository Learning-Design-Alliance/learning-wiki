## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The interview reports that innovations demonstrated to improve outcomes — such as menu-based self-explanation, worked examples, and personalized problem scenarios — have seen only limited, sometimes design-inconsistent, uptake in the commercial product. [→ Kenneth R. Koedinger & Vincent Aleven 2016](#kenneth-r-koedinger-vincent-aleven-2016)

## Evidence

### Kenneth R. Koedinger & Vincent Aleven 2016

Kenneth R. Koedinger & Vincent Aleven. (2016). An Interview Reflection on “Intelligent Tutoring Goes to School in the Big City”. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education 26, 13–24. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0082-8

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

This is the interviewee's retrospective interpretation about technology transfer into Carnegie Learning products, citing studies of self-explanation, worked examples, and personalized scenarios. It is an interpretive account with no effect size, so impact is null.

> "there has been some limited influence on the product. However, it has not always been in line with particulars of the research design (e.g., Carnegie Learning added an upfront example in each unit, but the recommended approximate 50–50 example-problem ratio and use of fading were not incorporated)."
