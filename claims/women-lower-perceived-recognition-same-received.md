## Evidence

### Meagan Sundstrom and N. G. Holmes 2025

Meagan Sundstrom and N. G. Holmes. (2025). Bias in physics peer recognition does not explain gaps in perceived recognition. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1038/s41567-025-02789-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-025-02789-w)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Linear mixed models on survey data from 1,721 students across 27 introductory physics courses at eight US institutions, with course as a random intercept. The models show "women report significantly lower perceived recognition than men" with the printed coefficients above; no standardized effect size is reported.

> "That is, for students receiving the same amount of peer recognition (and having the same academic year, academic major, and race or ethnicity), women report significantly lower perceived recognition than men (lab: b = –0.24 [95% confidence interval: –0.35, –0.12], SE = 0.06, t = –3.92, p < 0.001; lecture: b = –0.30 [–0.45, –0.15], SE = 0.08, t = –4.03, p < 0.001)."
