## Evidence

### Theodore A. Brzinski III and Karen E. Daniels 2015

Theodore A. Brzinski III and Karen E. Daniels. (2015). Stretching Rubber, Stretching Minds: a polymer physics lab for teaching entropy. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1508.00538](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1508.00538)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Instructional lab component in an undergraduate thermal physics course: students rapidly stretched and released a rubber sheet, recording the largest surface temperature observed with a contactless IR thermometer and repeating measurements for reproducibility. The article reports sign agreement, with stretching producing "an increase in temperature, while adiabatic unstretching leads to a temperature drop".

> "Qualitatively, students should observe an agreement in the sign of the eﬀect: adiabatic stretching leads to an increase in temperature, while adiabatic unstretching leads to a temperature drop."

### Theodore A. Brzinski III and Karen E. Daniels 2015 (2)

Theodore A. Brzinski III and Karen E. Daniels. (2015). Stretching Rubber, Stretching Minds: a polymer physics lab for teaching entropy. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1508.00538](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1508.00538)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same lab component, quantitative comparison to the students' analytic prediction. The article reports that "students' estimates will generally be one or more orders of magnitude greater than the change measured, depending on their assumptions"; no effect size is printed.

> "students' estimates will generally be one or more orders of magnitude greater than the change measured, depending on their assumptions."
