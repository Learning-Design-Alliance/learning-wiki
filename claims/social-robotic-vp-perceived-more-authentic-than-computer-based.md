# Medical students perceive an LLM-driven social robotic virtual patient platform as more authentic than a computer-based semilinear virtual patient platform

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On summary theme scores, the social robotic platform was rated higher than VIC for authenticity of the patient encounter (mean 4.5 vs 3.9; P=.04). [→ Borg A 2025](#borg-a-2025)
`q2 i?` The robotic platform was rated more authentic for decision-making within the encounter (mean 4.4 vs 3.9; P=.03). [→ Borg A 2025 (2)](#borg-a-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Borg A 2025

Borg A, Georg C, Jobs B, Huss V, Waldenlind K, Ruiz M, Edelbring S, Skantze G, Parodis I. (2025). Virtual Patient Simulations Using Social Robotics Combined With Large Language Models for Clinical Reasoning Training in Medical Education: Mixed Methods Study. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/63312

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quantitative questionnaire comparison (N=15 medical students) in a paired proof-of-concept study, analysed with paired 2-tailed t tests on 5-point scales. The authenticity summary theme favored the social robot, "OR 2.9, 95% CI 0.0-1.0; P=.04"; no standardised effect size is printed.

> "Summary scores within themes demonstrated superiority of the social robotic platform compared with VIC both regarding authenticity (mean 4.5, SD 0.7 vs mean 3.9, SD 0.5; OR 2.9, 95% CI 0.0-1.0; P=.04)"

### Borg A 2025 (2)

Borg A, Georg C, Jobs B, Huss V, Waldenlind K, Ruiz M, Edelbring S, Skantze G, Parodis I. (2025). Virtual Patient Simulations Using Social Robotics Combined With Large Language Models for Clinical Reasoning Training in Medical Education: Mixed Methods Study. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/63312

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Questionnaire item-level result from the same paired comparison of the robotic platform versus VIC, printed with means, OR and P value: "OR 3.2, 95% CI 0.1-1.2; P=.03"; no standardised effect size is printed.

> "The students perceived the social robotic platform as more authentic compared with VIC with regard to decision-making (mean 4.4, SD 0.6 vs mean 3.9, SD 0.9; OR 3.2, 95% CI 0.1-1.2; P=.03)"
