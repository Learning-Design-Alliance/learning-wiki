## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Self-reported empathy, measured multidimensionally with the JSPE-S, QCAE, and AMSP, significantly but modestly predicted OSCE performance in both cohorts (Year 3: β = 0.17; Year 5: β = 0.15). [→ Carrard Valerie 2026](#carrard-valerie-2026)

## Evidence

### Carrard Valerie 2026

Carrard Valerie, Bourquin Céline, Félix Sylvie, Viret Francine, Takeuchi Yusuke L., Berney Alexandre. (2026). Simulated-patient evaluations during formative interviews predict exam performance better than self-reported empathy and the ability to recognize others’ emotions. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09934-w

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Independent SEM for the Year 3 cohort (N = 468) showed self-reported empathy significantly predicted OSCE performance ("self-reported empathy (β = 0.17, p=.013)"), a small effect size. The authors attribute the significant link to the multidimensional empathy measurement approach.

> "For the Year 3 cohort, simulated-patient evaluations demonstrated a strong significant effect (β = 0.54, p<.001), while self-reported empathy (β = 0.17, p=.013) and the ability to recognize others’ emotions (β = 0.15, p=.002) showed smaller but significant effects."
