# Nonsegmented spoken–written presentations show a larger advantage over spoken-only presentation than segmented ones

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Nonsegmented presentations were associated with a larger weighted mean effect size (g = 0.53) than segmented presentations (g = 0.15), a statistically significant between-levels difference (QB(2) = 12.72, p < .001). [→ Adesope 2012](#adesope-2012)

## Evidence

### Adesope 2012

Adesope, O. O., & Nesbit, J. C. (2012). Verbal Redundancy in Multimedia Learning Environments: A Meta-Analysis. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0026147

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Moderator analysis of segment size (Table 3): nonsegmented studies (k = 7) reached Hedges's g = 0.53 versus g = 0.15 for segmented studies (k = 22); post hoc analysis found nonsegmented presentations significantly different from segmented ones.

> "Table 3 shows that although both segmented and nonseg- mented studies were associated with statistically detectable effect sizes, the nonsegmented presentations were associated with a larger effect size ( g = 0.53) than were the segmented ones ( g = 0.15). This result suggests that verbal redundancy is more beneficial when texts are not segmented."
