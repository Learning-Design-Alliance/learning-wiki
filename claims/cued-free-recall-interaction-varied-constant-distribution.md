# In Experiment 1, the type-of-recall by varied-constant by distribution interaction was significant, with a massed-distributed effect only in constant cued recall and a distribution effect in both free-recall conditions

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The three-way interaction between type of recall, varied-constant repetition, and distribution was statistically significant, supporting the prediction that descriptive cues eliminate the massed-distributed effect in varied conditions while contextual cues preserve it in free recall. [→ Glenberg 1979](#glenberg-1979)
`q3 i?` In cued recall, varied-constant repetition, distribution, and their interaction were all significant; in free recall, varied-constant and distribution were significant but their interaction was not (F ratio of only .18). [→ Glenberg 1979 (2)](#glenberg-1979-2)

## Evidence

### Glenberg 1979

Glenberg, A. M. (1979). Component-levels theory of the effects of spacing of repetitions on recall and recognition. Memory & Cognition, 7(2), 95-112. https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03197590

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Experiment 1 assigned 64 introductory psychology students to free or cued recall of repeated paired associates under massed or distributed presentation, with constant or varied stimulus terms. The three-way interaction was statistically significant; no effect size is printed.

> "The interaction between type of recall, varied-constant, and distribution was statistically significant."

### Glenberg 1979 (2)

Glenberg, A. M. (1979). Component-levels theory of the effects of spacing of repetitions on recall and recognition. Memory & Cognition, 7(2), 95-112. https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03197590

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Two identical 8 by 2 by 2 ANOVAs (counterbalancing by varied-constant by distribution, within subjects) were run on the cued and free recall data. Cued recall showed all three effects significant; free recall showed main effects significant but an interaction F ratio of only .18. No effect sizes are printed.

> "For cued recall, the effects of varied-constant repe­tition [F(l ,24) =10.69, MSe =1.35], distribution [F(1 ,24) = 5.81, MSe = .84], and their interaction [F(1 ,24) =5.44, MSe =.63] were all significant. For free recall, the effect of varied-constant [F(1 ,24) = 6.29, MSe = 1.61] and distribution [F(1,24) = 5.67, MSe = 1.41] were significant. The F ratio for the interaction was only .18."
