## Evidence

### Levin 2022

Levin, J., Baker, B., Lee, J., Atchison, D., & Kelchen, R. (2022). An Examination of the Costs of Texas Community Colleges. Regional Educational Laboratory Southwest. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Authors' synthesis of the equity and cost function findings: spending is progressive for several need factors, but weights greater than 1 for first-generation, economically disadvantaged, older, and English learner students indicate projected adequate costs exceed actual spending. The models predict colleges with the highest first-generation shares would need over $1,400 more per student.

> "the additional amount of spending for students with these characteristics may not be enough to provide an equal opportunity for them to meet success points milestones"
