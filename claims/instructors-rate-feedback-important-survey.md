# Most medical instructors rate feedback as important or very important, with no significant differences by gender or location

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a 2017 survey of 464 instructors at five medical faculties, 72% rated feedback as very important and 23% as important, with no significant differences by gender or location. [→ Kunz Kevin 2019](#kunz-kevin-2019)

## Evidence

### Kunz Kevin 2019

Kunz Kevin, Burkert Mirka, Heindl Felix, Schüttpelz-Brauns Katrin, Giesler Marianne. (2019). The frequency of using certain feedback methods in the teaching of medicine: a survey of teachers at the medical faculties in Baden-Wurttemberg. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001253](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001253)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Online survey of 464 instructors at the Freiburg, Heidelberg, Mannheim, Tuebingen and Ulm medical faculties in the 2017 summer semester, using a questionnaire with four-point Likert items. "A majority of instructors find feedback to be important (23%) or very important (72%)"; only 5% did not find feedback important.

> "A majority of instructors find feedback to be important (23%) or very important (72%). A total of 5% of the teachers do not find feedback important."
