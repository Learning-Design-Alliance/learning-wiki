# The group advantage reverses when partners' abilities differ greatly, and depends on the mode of communication

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Adding noise to one partner's signals eliminated the group advantage, but this disadvantage did not occur when confidence was communicated non-verbally. [→ Frith Chris D 2012](#frith-chris-d-2012)

## Evidence

### Frith Chris D 2012

Frith Chris D. (2012). The role of metacognition in human social interactions. Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B: Biological Sciences. https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2012.0123

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Experiments (Bahrami et al., as reviewed here) degraded one partner's perceptual ability by adding noise. The review reports loss of group advantage with unequal partners under free verbal communication, but that a supplied non-verbal confidence system "eliminated the disadvantage of working with a less-competent partner".

> "This loss of advantage occurred even when the noise was always added to the same individual in the pair so that his performance was consistently poor. This effect critically depended on how the partners communicated their confidence."
