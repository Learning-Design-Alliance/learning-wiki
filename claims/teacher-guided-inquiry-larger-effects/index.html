Evidence¶

Lazonder 2016¶

Lazonder, A. W., & Harmsen, R. (2016). Meta-analysis of inquiry-based learning: Effects of guidance. Review of Educational Research, 86(3), 681-718. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315627366

q2 · i1 · quant-synthesis · r?

A meta-analysis of 72 studies whose background section reports the Furtak, Seidel, Iverson, and Briggs (2012) meta-analysis in science: minimal-guidance inquiry showed d = 0.25 over traditional instruction, while teacher-directed inquiry comparisons showed "a considerably higher overall mean effect size (d = 0.65)."

"Students engaged in an inquiry with minimal guidance from their teacher tended to learn more than students exposed to "traditional instruction" (d = 0.25). However, studies comparing teacher-directed inquiry learning with traditional instruction had a considerably higher overall mean effect size (d = 0.65)."

Lazonder 2016 (2)¶

Lazonder, A. W., & Harmsen, R. (2016). Meta-analysis of inquiry-based learning: Effects of guidance. Review of Educational Research, 86(3), 681-718. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315627366

q2 · i0 · quant-synthesis · r?

A meta-analysis of 72 studies whose background section reports the Carolan, Hutchins, Wickens, and Cumming (2014) meta-analysis, which found "a small but significant overall benefit (g = 0.15)" for more guided over less guided inquiry learning, failing to replicate the larger teacher-directed effect size.

"The magnitude of this effect could not be replicated in the meta-analysis by Carolan, Hutchins, Wickens, and Cumming (2014), who found a small but significant overall benefit (g = 0.15) of more guided inquiry learning over less guided inquiry learning."
