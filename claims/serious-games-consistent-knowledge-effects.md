# Standalone serious games show large, relatively consistent effects on knowledge acquisition compared with other gamification categories

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q4 i3` In the knowledge subgroup analysis, standalone serious games yielded a large effect (SMD=0.84, 95% CI [0.43, 1.25]) with moderate heterogeneity (I2=61%), the most consistent gamification category. [→ Chen W.-L. 2026](#chen-w-l-2026)

## Evidence

### Chen W.-L. 2026

Chen W.-L., Winitchayothin S., Cheng C.-H., Tonapa S.I., Chou F.-H., & Zimmerman P.-A. (2026). A systematic review and meta-analysis of the effect of gamified clinical skill learning on learning outcomes in undergraduate nursing education. The Journal of Nursing Research. https://doi.org/10.1097/jnr.0000000000000772

`q4 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Subgroup analysis of four RCTs (n=135) of standalone serious games within the knowledge meta-analysis. The review reports a large pooled effect, SMD = 0.84, quoting "a large effect size (SMD=0.84, 95% CI [0.43, 1.25])" with moderate heterogeneity (I2=61%).

> "These interventions (four studies, n=135) exhibited a large effect size (SMD=0.84, 95% CI [0.43, 1.25]) with moderate heterogeneity (I 2=61%). The purpose-built educational games used in these studies integrated learning content within immersive narrative and simulation environments."
