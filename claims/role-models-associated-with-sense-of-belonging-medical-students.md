## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Medical students with role models reported stronger classroom comfort, faculty understanding, and peer support than students without role models, with peer support showing the largest difference (d = -.405), and all associations except having role models and faculty understanding remained significant after Bonferroni correction. ## Evidence

### Spaans I 2025

Spaans I, Verbree A-R, de Kleijn R, Dilaver G. (2025). Role models in medicine and beyond: a survey study exploring the relationship between role models, academic performance and sense of belonging. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08257-6

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Cross-sectional survey comparing medical students with (n = 259) and without (n = 217) role models using the SOBS. Peer support showed the largest group difference (t = −4.40 (474), p <.001, d = -.405; ρ =.256 for number of role models), classroom comfort d = -.371, faculty understanding d = -.252.

> "Medical students with role models reported a stronger sense of belonging than their peers without role models. With p ≤.05, having role models and the average number of role models both corresponded positively with all three subconstructs of sense of belonging."
