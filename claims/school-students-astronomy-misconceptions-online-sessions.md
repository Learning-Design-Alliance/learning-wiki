# Roughly a third of school students in synchronous online sessions show misconceptions about seasons, polar day/night, and the zenith position of the Sun

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among 173 participating students of 8 classes, about 37% showed the misconception that seasons are due to distance from the Sun, about 34% could not explain polar months of darkness and light, and about 31% placed the Sun's zenith outside the Tropics. ## Evidence

### Postiglione A. 2023

Postiglione A., De Angelis I., Di Blasi M. (2023). AstroGarden of Roma Tre University: from presence to online tour. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2301.03842](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2301.03842)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Kahoot! answers from synchronous Zoom sessions with individual class groups; 173 participants of 8 different classes took part, 152 of whom joined the Kahoot! quiz. Percentages are descriptive; no inferential statistics are reported.

> "For this type of audience, about 37% shows the misconception that the season is due to the distance from the Sun; about 34% cannot explain correctly why months of darkness and light alternate at the poles, and about 31% states that the Sun can be at its zenith even outside the region between the Tropics."
