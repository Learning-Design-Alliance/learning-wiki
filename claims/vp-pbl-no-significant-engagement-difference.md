# Sign tests found no statistically significant difference between video and text in students' sense of making doctor-like decisions or being the doctor, and Mann-Whitney tests found no difference by entry stream

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Luke A Woodham 2015

Luke A Woodham, Rachel H Ellaway, Jonathan Round, Sophie Vaughan, Terry Poulton, Nabil Zary. (2015). Medical Student and Tutor Perceptions of Video Versus Text in an Interactive Online Virtual Patient for Problem-Based Learning: A Pilot Study. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.3922

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Sign tests on dependent ordinal Likert responses comparing video and text role items (e.g., Z = –0.139, P = .89 for feeling like the caring doctor); no statistically significant difference was detected, and equivalence was not tested.

> "In each case, the null hypothesis (ie, that the distributions were the same) could not be rejected at the 5% level (P<.05), indicating that the use of video did not significantly impact on student responses to this statement when compared to their response for text."

### Luke A Woodham 2015 (2)

Luke A Woodham, Rachel H Ellaway, Jonathan Round, Sophie Vaughan, Terry Poulton, Nabil Zary. (2015). Medical Student and Tutor Perceptions of Video Versus Text in an Interactive Online Virtual Patient for Problem-Based Learning: A Pilot Study. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.3922

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Two-tailed Mann-Whitney U tests compared graduate entry (n=46) and undergraduate entry (n=73) students on each Likert item; no distribution difference reached the 5% level, so responses were pooled in analysis.

> "In each case, the null hypothesis (ie, that the 2 groups had the same distribution) could not be rejected indicating that the graduate entry or undergraduate entry status of students, and thus the different nature of the prior experience of these 2 groups, did not significantly impact on their experiences of the intervention"
