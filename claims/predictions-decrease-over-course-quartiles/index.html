Evidence¶

de Bruin 2017¶

de Bruin, A. B. H., Kok, E. M., Lobbestael, J., & de Grip, A. (2017). The impact of an online tool for monitoring and regulating learning at university: overconfidence, learning strategy, and personality. Metacognition and Learning, 12, 21–43. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-016-9159-5

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

2x4 repeated measures ANOVA on absolute accuracy at t=0 and t=2 with performance quartile as between-subjects factor. The quartile main effect was F(3, 125) = 49.06, p < .001, ηp2 = .54; the interaction was not significant, F(3, 125) = 1.79, p = .15.

Related Claims¶

  • The four exam-performance quartile groups differed significantly on first-unit exam scores, validating the grouping used for behavioral comparisons — related
  • Most students who ultimately failed the exam still expected to pass three days before it, showing persistent overconfidence despite knowledge gain — related
  • The level of reflection support (practice-only, grade comparison, or reflection prompts) did not differentially affect the improvement of secondary students' calibration accuracy over time — related
  • Overconfidence (bias) decreased from pretest to posttest across all groups including control, with no group differences — related
  • The Monitoring Exercise protected against overconfidence in the second exam score prediction but did not affect exam scores — related
