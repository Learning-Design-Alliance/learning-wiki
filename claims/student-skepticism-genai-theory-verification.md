## Evidence

### Karl Henrik Fredly 2026

Karl Henrik Fredly, Tor Ole B. Odden and Benjamin M. Zwickl. (2026). How undergraduate physics students use generative AI for computational modeling. [doi:10.1103/vg1v-dkrh](https://doi.org/10.1103/vg1v-dkrh)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of the 19 interviews found "all students were skeptical of using genAI to solve problems they had with physics theory or equations", based on their experience and perceived lack of relevant training material; when used, it was for explanations they could easily double-check.

> "Although the students had mixed opinions on how genAI should be used for programming, all students were skeptical of using genAI to solve problems they had with physics theory or equations."

### Karl Henrik Fredly 2026 (2)

Karl Henrik Fredly, Tor Ole B. Odden and Benjamin M. Zwickl. (2026). How undergraduate physics students use generative AI for computational modeling. [doi:10.1103/vg1v-dkrh](https://doi.org/10.1103/vg1v-dkrh)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview analysis of resource use found students "all later verified the information with trusted sources", having little confidence in genAI's factual information; some students preferred textbooks as more reliable.

> "Even though most students found genAI helpful for finding information, they all later verified the information with trusted sources such as instructors, textbooks, or quality online resources, as they had little confidence in factual information provided by genAI."
