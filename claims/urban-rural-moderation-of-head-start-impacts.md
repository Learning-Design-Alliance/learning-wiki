## Subclaims
`q3 i0` The effect of random assignment to Head Start on receptive vocabulary was 0.14 SD in urban versus 0.07 SD in rural communities (a 0.07-SD difference); oral comprehension impacts were 0.10 SD rural versus −0.02 SD urban. [→ Morris 2018](#morris-2018)

## Evidence

### Morris 2018

Morris, P. A., Connors, M., Friedman-Krauss, A., McCoy, D. C., Weiland, C., Feller, A., Page, L., Bloom, H., & Yoshikawa, H. (2018). New Findings on Impact Variation From the Head Start Impact Study: Informing the Scale-Up of Early Childhood Programs. AERA Open. https://doi.org/10.1177/2332858418769287

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Impact-variation analysis with urbanicity as a site-level predictor (McCoy et al., 2016; N = 3,503), built on the Bloom and Weiland (2015) multilevel models. The article reports Head Start was "more than twice as effective" for receptive vocabulary in urban communities, with the oral comprehension pattern reversed (0.10 SD rural vs. −0.02 SD urban); the 0.07-SD difference is below d = 0.2.

> "the effect of random assignment to Head Start versus a control condition was 0.14 SD in urban communities versus 0.07 SD in rural communities, a 0.07-SD difference in impacts"
