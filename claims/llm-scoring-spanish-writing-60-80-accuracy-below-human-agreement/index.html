Evidence¶

Curi 2026¶

Curi, M. E., Capdehourat, G., Amigo, I., Romano, M., Serra, R., Silveira, A., Peri, A. (2026). A Human-in-the-Loop Framework for AI-Assisted Scoring in Large-Scale Writing Assessment. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05143 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.05143

q2 · i? · design · r2

Analysis of the 2024 calibration phase (50 texts, each scored 10 times by the AI model) comparing mean AI accuracy to average human inter-rater agreement per rubric item (Fig. 3). The article reports "AI model accuracy ranges between 60% and 80%" with gaps of about 5% for most items.

"As expected, the model's results fall slightly below the agreement observed between human raters. However, for more than half of the rubric items, this difference is approximately 5%, and for the remaining items, it never exceeds 15%. In absolute terms, AI model accuracy ranges between 60% and 80%."

Related Claims¶

  • Human inter-rater agreement on the writing rubric is imperfect: no rubric item reaches full agreement and the most discrepant rater generally falls below 80% — related
  • Automated essay scoring can achieve high agreement with human raters but faces calibration constraints — related
  • An LLM-based AI Evaluator agrees with expert human raters on collaboration transcripts at a level similar to inter-expert agreement — related
  • AI grading of the exam is highly stable across five independent runs at the total-score level (ICC(A,1) = 0.967), with lower but still strong cell-level stability (ICC(A,1) = 0.836) — related
  • Out-of-the-box LLMs agree with human essay grades far less than human raters agree with each other — related
  • Spellcheck and autocorrect are not foolproof: efficacy is bounded near 80% and the most common student writing error is now correctly spelled but inappropriate words — related
