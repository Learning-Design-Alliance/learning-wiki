# Brazilian EE legislation shifted from Law 6938/1981's naturalistic, conservation-restricted discourse to the socio-environmental perspective of Law 9795/1999 and the 2013 DCNEB

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Law 6938/1981 restricted EE to physical, chemical, and biological aspects and silencing social issues, while Law 9795/1999 and the DCNEB shifted meanings toward critical consciousness, social issues, and environmental justice. `q2 i?` The DCNEB explicitly conceives EE from a socio-environmental perspective of environmental justice and sustainability. [→ Elizabeth Bozoti Pasin and Reinaldo Luiz Bozelli 2016 (2)](#elizabeth-bozoti-pasin-and-reinaldo-luiz-bozelli-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Elizabeth Bozoti Pasin and Reinaldo Luiz Bozelli 2016

Elizabeth Bozoti Pasin and Reinaldo Luiz Bozelli. (2016). An Analysis of Curriculum and Undergraduate Students' Discourses about Environmental Education: A Debate Concerning Teacher Training. International Journal of Environmental & Science Education, 11(18), 12197-12234. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1122668

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Document analysis of the foundation discourse of Brazilian EE legislation. The article states Law 6938's environment "only embraced the chemical, physical, and biological aspects," silencing social issues, and did not require EE in curricula.

> "Law 6938 expressed a discourse in which the environment only embraced the chemical, physical, and biological aspects, and the purpose of the EE was to raise awareness of the need for the preservation and maintenance of ecological balance; it was restricted to these aspects, silencing social issues."
