## Evidence

### Jin Piaopiao 2026

Jin Piaopiao, Weng Xiaoyu, Zhang Xiaoyu, Fang Tian, Geng Lei, Chen Shaohua, Liang Tingbo, Hu Qida. (2026). A highly feasible simulation model based on nipple-preserving pork portions for practicing superficial mass resection. BMC Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12893-026-03840-0

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Perception-based comparison using feedback questionnaires from all 56 trainees who rated both models on a 1–10 scale; no objective performance data were collected for the silicone model. The pork model achieved "significantly greater fidelity than the silicone model".

> "the pork-based model resulted in significantly greater fidelity than the silicone model (9.7 ± 0.5 for the pork-based model vs. 5.1 ± 1.9 for the silicone model, p < 0.01; paired t test)"

### Jin Piaopiao 2026 (2)

Jin Piaopiao, Weng Xiaoyu, Zhang Xiaoyu, Fang Tian, Geng Lei, Chen Shaohua, Liang Tingbo, Hu Qida. (2026). A highly feasible simulation model based on nipple-preserving pork portions for practicing superficial mass resection. BMC Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12893-026-03840-0

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Perceived operational difficulty ratings were statistically indistinguishable between models (p = 0.99); equivalence was not tested. This addresses concerns that higher fidelity adds difficulty for beginners.

> "the fidelity improvement did not lead to additional operational difficulty (7.0 ± 1.4 for the pork-based model vs. 7.0 ± 1.6 for the silicone model, p = 0.99; paired t test)"

### Jin Piaopiao 2026 (3)

Jin Piaopiao, Weng Xiaoyu, Zhang Xiaoyu, Fang Tian, Geng Lei, Chen Shaohua, Liang Tingbo, Hu Qida. (2026). A highly feasible simulation model based on nipple-preserving pork portions for practicing superficial mass resection. BMC Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12893-026-03840-0

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Overall satisfaction on the 1–10 scale favored the pork-based model over the silicone model by paired t test. The comparison was perception-based only, as the authors state it "might need further validation using performance-related objective ratings".

> "the overall satisfaction rating from the residents showed that the pork-based model outperformed the silicone model (9.9 ± 0.3 for pork-based model vs. 4.5 ± 1.3 for silicone model, p < 0.01; paired t test)"
