# Public research-extensive universities spend hundreds of millions in institutional grant aid on high-income students, with nearly equal spending on the top two and bottom two income quintiles

## Evidence

### Haycock 2010

Haycock, K., Lynch, M., & Engle, J. (2010). Opportunity Adrift: Our Flagship Universities Are Straying From Their Public Mission. The Education Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED507851

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Ed Trust analysis of NPSAS:04 and NPSAS:08 data reported in the institutional aid section, finding spending "a combined total of $361 million" on the highest-income students plus $400 million on the next band.

> "In 2007, the research-extensive public universities spent a combined total of $361 million on grant aid for students from families earning more than $115,000 per year and another $400 million on students from families earning from $80,400 to $115,000"

### Haycock 2010 (2)

Haycock, K., Lynch, M., & Engle, J. (2010). Opportunity Adrift: Our Flagship Universities Are Straying From Their Public Mission. The Education Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED507851

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same NPSAS-based quintile analysis (Figure 11) shows aggregate 2007 institutional grant aid of $394.2 million for the bottom quintile and $361.4 million for the top quintile, supporting the near-equal spending pattern the report calls "literally beyond belief."

> "these institu-tions are spending almost exactly the same amount of money to provide grant aid to students in the top two quintiles of family income as they are spending on students in the bottom two quintiles"
