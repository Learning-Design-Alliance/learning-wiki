## Evidence

### Najjuma Josephine N. 2024

Najjuma Josephine N., Muhumuza Albert, Santorino Data, Sekyere Samuel Owusu, Ocheke Amaka N., Yiltok Simon J., Bajunirwe Francis. (2024). Barriers and facilitators to interprofessional simulation-based learning in a Ugandan medical school: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06521-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of 9 key informant interviews and 3 uniprofessional focus group discussions with medical and nursing students at MUST found that "Participants from all groups emphasized the importance of interprofessional collaboration during simulation-based learning." Students reported learning skills across professions, such as neuro examinations and nasogastric tube care.

> "Participants from all groups emphasized the importance of interprofessional collaboration during simulation-based learning."

### Najjuma Josephine N. 2024 (2)

Najjuma Josephine N., Muhumuza Albert, Santorino Data, Sekyere Samuel Owusu, Ocheke Amaka N., Yiltok Simon J., Bajunirwe Francis. (2024). Barriers and facilitators to interprofessional simulation-based learning in a Ugandan medical school: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06521-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis identified preparation for future teamwork, breaking down professional silos, and roles clarification as subthemes: "Interprofessional simulation prepares students for future collaboration in healthcare settings." Participants said co-training helps students know each profession's scope of practice and prevents role confusion.

> "Interprofessional simulation prepares students for future collaboration in healthcare settings. Simulation sessions involving different professions bring the experience closer to real-life scenarios, preparing students for the collaborative nature of healthcare practice."
