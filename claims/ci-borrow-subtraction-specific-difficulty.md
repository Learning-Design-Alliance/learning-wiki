# The subtraction borrowing effect is more pronounced in children with cochlear implants than in hearing peers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A significant borrow × group interaction showed the borrowing effect on error rates was larger for children with CI (25.0% vs. 16.1% errors) than for NH controls. [→ Pixner 2014](#pixner-2014)

## Evidence

### Pixner 2014

Pixner, S., Leyrer, M., and Moeller, K. (2014). Number processing and arithmetic skills in children with cochlear implants. Frontiers in Psychology 5:1479. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01479

`q3 · i?`

Two-way ANOVA (borrow vs. non-borrow problems × group, CE covariate) on the subtraction task error rates; the "borrowing effect was indeed more pronounced for children with CI as compared to NH controls". The main group effect on errors was not reliable (p = 0.10).

> "the significant interaction of borrow and participant group [F(1,91) = 4.82, p < 0.05] indicated that the borrowing effect was indeed more pronounced for children with CI as compared to NH controls (25.0% vs. 16.1% errors, respectively"
