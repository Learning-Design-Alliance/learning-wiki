## Subclaims
`q3 i3` First-half SPS is significantly correlated with second-half procedure success rates (r = 0.70; P < .001). [→ Chung KC 2025](#chung-kc-2025)
`q3 i?` In unadjusted analysis, each 1-point increase in SPS was associated with a 7.5% increase in the surgeon's future success rate (95% CI, 5.5%-8.1%; P < .001); no standardized effect size was printed for this estimate. [→ Chung KC 2025 (2)](#chung-kc-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Chung KC 2025

Chung KC, Heiman A, Malay S, and the PRAISE Study Group. (2025). Proficiency and Difficulty Scoring Tools for Finger Replantation. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.40453

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r3`

SPS validation analysis in the multicenter retrospective cohort (653 digits, 65 surgeons): each surgeon's caseload was split chronologically in half, and early SPS was correlated with the later half's success rates, yielding "r = 0.70; P < .001".

> "Using Pearson correlation coefficient, we found a significant correlation between first-half SPS and second-half success rates (r = 0.70; P < .001)."

### Chung KC 2025 (2)

Chung KC, Heiman A, Malay S, and the PRAISE Study Group. (2025). Proficiency and Difficulty Scoring Tools for Finger Replantation. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.40453

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Unadjusted SPS validation in the same cohort, reported alongside the Figure plotting SPS against 1-month success rates. The article prints the per-point estimate, "7.5% (95% CI, 5.5%-8.1%)", but no standardized effect size.

> "For each 1-point increase in SPS, a surgeon's future success rate increased by 7.5% (95% CI, 5.5%-8.1%; P < .001) (Figure)."
