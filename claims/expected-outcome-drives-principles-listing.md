# Students list principles sources of uncertainty more often for experiments predicting a distribution of outcomes than for single-value experiments, while paradigm alone does not consistently explain principles listing

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Within both paradigms, distribution-outcome experiments elicited more principles sources than single-value experiments (classical: 56% vs. 6%; quantum: 58% vs. 32%). [→ Emily M. Stump 2023](#emily-m-stump-2023)
`q2 i?` The fraction of students listing principles sources did not differ consistently between classical and quantum scenarios. [→ Emily M. Stump 2023 (2)](#emily-m-stump-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Emily M. Stump 2023

Emily M. Stump, Matthew Dew, Gina Passante, and N. G. Holmes. (2023). Context affects student thinking about sources of uncertainty in classical and quantum mechanics. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020157](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020157)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Scenario-based survey responses coded for principles; comparison controlling for paradigm. "56% of students listed a principles source for Brownian mo- tion (distribution) compared with 6% for projectile motion (single value)"; for quantum scenarios, 58% (single-slit) versus 32% (Stern-Gerlach).

> "For the classical paradigms, 56% of students listed a principles source for Brownian mo- tion (distribution) compared with 6% for projectile motion (single value)."

### Emily M. Stump 2023 (2)

Emily M. Stump, Matthew Dew, Gina Passante, and N. G. Holmes. (2023). Context affects student thinking about sources of uncertainty in classical and quantum mechanics. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020157](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020157)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cross-paradigm comparison of scenario responses: Stern-Gerlach (quantum) 32% versus projectile motion (classical) 6%, but "students do not consistently draw more heavily on principles in a quantum context than in a classical context" because Brownian motion (56%) and single-slit (58%) were nearly identical.

> "students do not consistently draw more heavily on principles in a quantum context than in a classical context"
