# PF students did not outperform DI students when learning social science research methods in two quasi-experimental studies with 10th graders, failing to replicate the STEM-domain PF effect

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across two quasi-experimental studies (212 and 152 10th graders) comparing Productive Failure to direct instruction for social science research methods, PF students did not outperform DI students. [→ Valentina Nachtigall · Katja Serova · Nikol Rummel 2020](#valentina-nachtigall-katja-serova-nikol-rummel-2020)

## Evidence

### Valentina Nachtigall · Katja Serova · Nikol Rummel 2020

Valentina Nachtigall · Katja Serova · Nikol Rummel. (2020). When failure fails to be productive: probing the effectiveness of productive failure for learning beyond STEM domains. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-020-09525-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The authors' two quasi-experimental studies with 212 and 152 10th graders, conducted in an out-of-school lab, contrasted a PF condition (problem solving before instruction) with a DI condition (instruction before problem solving). The article states that "PF students did not outperform DI students", i.e. the PF effect was not replicated in this non-STEM domain.

> "in the present two studies, PF students did not outperform DI students on learning social science research methods. Thus, the findings did not replicate the PF effect on learning in a non-STEM domain."
