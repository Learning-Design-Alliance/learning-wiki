Expanding conceptual evidence-building with the science of learning: integrating evidence weights, alignment estimates and iterative updates for educational innovations. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10732-w

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Case study 1: the authors' structured rapid review of the I Can Read early childhood literacy product against the five Science of Reading Big Ideas, using weight-of-evidence ratings by two independent researchers. The review found high alignment for phonemic awareness, phonics instruction, fluency and comprehension, and low alignment for vocabulary. No effect sizes are reported.

> "While the degree of alignment between four of the big Ideas of Science of Reading and I Can Read materials was high, it was low for one of the Big Ideas, Vocabulary."

### Natalia Kucirkova 2026 (2)

Natalia Kucirkova, Phil Vahey, David Patterson, David Dockterman. (2026). Expanding conceptual evidence-building with the science of learning: integrating evidence weights, alignment estimates and iterative updates for educational innovations. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10732-w

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

In the same case study, after the misalignment finding was shared with the I Can Read team, the company revised activities and designed new ones for vocabulary alignment. The authors interpret this as showing that the iterative theory-of-change process "can result in significant improvements to educational materials". No outcome data are reported.

> "they engaged in a significant effort to ensure that the revision of existing activities and the design of new activities were in alignment with the research on effective vocabulary instruction"
