# Priming with pre-class online videos did not significantly improve medical students' test performance on the primed topic compared with the non-primed topic

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Immediate post-test improvement was 21.4% for concordant (primed) questions versus 15.2% for discordant questions, not statistically significant (p = 0.66). [→ Rose E 2018](#rose-e-2018)

## Evidence

### Rose E 2018

Rose E, Jhun P, Baluzy M, Hauck A, Huang J, Wagner J, Kearl YL, Behar S, Claudius I. (2018). Flipping the Classroom in Medical Student Education: Does Priming Work? Western Journal of Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2017.8.35162

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Prospective descriptive study in which students served as their own control, tested on two topics but primed on one, analyzed with Mann-Whitney U tests per group per month. The concordant groups' "immediate post-test scores improved by 21.4%" versus 15.2% discordant, p = 0.66.

> "In the concordant groups, the immediate post-test scores improved by 21.4%, compared with 15.2% in the discordant groups (p = 0.66)."

### Rose E 2018 (2)

Rose E, Jhun P, Baluzy M, Hauck A, Huang J, Wagner J, Kearl YL, Behar S, Claudius I. (2018). Flipping the Classroom in Medical Student Education: Does Priming Work? Western Journal of Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2017.8.35162

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same within-subject clerkship study comparing concordant versus discordant delayed post-test gains three weeks after the session, analyzed with Mann-Whitney U statistics; the discordant gain was numerically larger and the difference was not significant.

> "In the delayed post-test the concordant scores improved by 10.5% and discordant scores by 16.9 percent (p=0.61)."
