# Connectivism is an iteration of architectonic/constructivist thought rather than a new theory of learning

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The article argues, on theoretical grounds, that connectivism emerges from the architectonic tradition and therefore should not be treated as a new learning theory lacking foundations. [→ Dennis 2020](#dennis-2020)

## Evidence

### Dennis 2020

Dennis, J. (2020). Languaging Network Learning: The Emergence of Connectivism in Architectonic Thought. International Review of Research in Open and Distributed Learning, 21(3). https://www.irrodl.org/

`q1 · i?`

Theoretical argument in the article's conclusion: tracing connectivism's genealogy through hypertextuality and intertextuality back to architectonic thought, the author concludes that "connectivism or network learning is another iteration of architectonics." No empirical data are offered; the support is philosophical-historical argument.

> "Like constructivism, connectivism or network learning is another iteration of architectonics. As such, we find that the idea of connectivism appears to emerge out of one of our earliest theories of hypertextuality. This evidence rebuts the argument that connectivism is a new theory of learning."
