# School contributions to within-year (single-year fall-to-spring) growth correlate at roughly .10 or below with school contributions to growth across elementary school (grades 2–6)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Correlations between Z-scores for short-term (single-year) and long-term (grades 2–6) school growth contributions are highest for third grade at roughly .095 in math and reading. [→ Soland 2019](#soland-2019)

## Evidence

### Soland 2019

Soland, J., Thum, Y.M., & King, G. (2019). Reconciling long-term education policy goals with short-term school accountability models: Evidence and implications from a longitudinal study. (The Collaborative for Student Growth at NWEA Working Paper). https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/reconciling-long-term-education-policy-goals-with-short-term-school-accountability-models-paper/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

CP model analysis comparing within-year Z-scores to grades 2–6 growth Z-scores for the same schools, using a consistent student sample. Correlations were "highest for third grade with estimates of roughly .095" in both math and reading; no effect size beyond the correlation is printed, so impact is null.

> "In math and reading, the correlations between Z scores for short- and long-term growth are highest for third grade with estimates of roughly .095. These results indicate that the gains attributable to schools within a single year are only tenuously associated with growth produced during elementary school."
