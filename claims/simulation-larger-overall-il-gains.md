# The participatory simulation group showed significantly larger overall (combined knowledge and application) gains than the problem-based group after four weeks

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After a four-week implementation, the PS group's overall mean gain exceeded the PB group's overall mean gain (T = 1.852, p = .04, one-tailed). [→ Newell 2008](#newell-2008)

## Evidence

### Newell 2008

Newell, T. S. (2008). Examining Information Problem-Solving, Knowledge, and Application Gains within Two Instructional Methods: Problem-Based and Computer-Mediated Participatory Simulation. School Library Media Research. https://www.ala.org/aasl/slmr

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Two-group randomized pretest–posttest trial with 54 seventh- and eighth-grade students (27 per group) comparing problem-based and participatory simulation instruction; independent-sample t-test on gains found "a significant difference (T = 1.852, p = .04*)" in overall gains favoring the simulation group.

> "There was a significant difference (T = 1.852, p = .04*) between the overall PB and PS gains after the 4-week implementation period"
