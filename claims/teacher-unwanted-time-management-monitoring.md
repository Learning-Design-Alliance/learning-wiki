# Teachers do not want to spend time on management communication and transition approvals, and lack sufficient time to monitor all students

> **Currency** · newest study 2021 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teachers in all sessions reported spending significant time on classroom management and communicating activity directions, and desired a system to prompt students directly so they could focus on teaching. [→ Jennifer K. Olsen & Nikol Rummel & Vincent Aleven 2021](#jennifer-k-olsen-nikol-rummel-vincent-aleven-2021)
`q2 i?` Teachers reported being unable to monitor everything while working with individual students, and believed a system could track fine-grained behaviors they miss. [→ Jennifer K. Olsen & Nikol Rummel & Vincent Aleven 2021 (2)](#jennifer-k-olsen-nikol-rummel-vincent-aleven-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Jennifer K. Olsen & Nikol Rummel & Vincent Aleven 2021

Jennifer K. Olsen & Nikol Rummel & Vincent Aleven. (2021). Designing for the co-Orchestration of Social Transitions between Individual, Small-Group and Whole-Class Learning in the Classroom. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education 31:24–56. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-020-00228-w

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Study 1 co-design finding: communicating who works with whom, where, and on what takes teacher time away from helping students who are behind; teachers also did not want to approve every transition ('don't want to have to go back and touch something or press something'). Qualitative; no effect size printed.

> "The teachers in all of the sessions believed that “it might be nice to have a system [prompt the students] ” (T6) so that they could focus on the teaching."

### Jennifer K. Olsen & Nikol Rummel & Vincent Aleven 2021 (2)

Jennifer K. Olsen & Nikol Rummel & Vincent Aleven. (2021). Designing for the co-Orchestration of Social Transitions between Individual, Small-Group and Whole-Class Learning in the Classroom. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education 31:24–56. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-020-00228-w

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Study 1 finding on insufficient monitoring time: while working one-on-one, teachers cannot watch other students and only see snapshots when visiting groups; they expressed the need for more monitoring support than they can currently achieve alone. Qualitative; no effect size printed.

> "Moreover, in all three of the sessions, the teachers discussed their limitations and believed that “a system would be able to do the stuff, the fine details that we miss, that we do not know that we have missed” (T2) and “could eliminate human/teacher error ” (T4)."
