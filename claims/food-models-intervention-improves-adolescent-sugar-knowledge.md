## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Adolescents receiving two 45-minute food-model lessons had significantly higher total knowledge of sugar guidelines, food-group servings, and added sugar content at follow-up than controls (F(1, 201) = 104.84, p < 0.001, η2 = 0.34). [→ Santaló María Isabel 2019](#santalo-maria-isabel-2019)

## Evidence

### Santaló María Isabel 2019

Santaló María Isabel, Gibbons Sandra, Naylor Patti-Jean. (2019). Using Food Models to Enhance Sugar Literacy among Older Adolescents: Evaluation of a Brief Experiential Nutrition Education Intervention. Nutrients. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu11081763

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Cluster-randomized controlled trial with 16 classes randomized to intervention or control; 203 consenting students analyzed with repeated measures ANOVA and intention-to-treat. Total knowledge rose from 18.6 to 54.5% correct in intervention versus 15.7 to 20.1% in control, with printed effect size η2 = 0.34, which the article describes as "large in all knowledge dependent variables".

> "Adolescents’ knowledge of added sugar in foods and beverages and servings per food group in a healthy diet was limited at baseline but improved significantly in the intervention condition (F(1, 201) = 104.84, p < 0.001) compared to controls."
