# In the CRT experiment, confidence appraisals predicted solving accuracy more strongly than goal-driven effort appraisals

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In experiment 1, the appraisal-accuracy correlation was significantly stronger for confidence than for goal-driven effort appraisals (η2 = 0.06); confidence did not differ significantly from data-driven effort or difficulty. [→ Emely Hoch 2023](#emely-hoch-2023)

## Evidence

### Emely Hoch 2023

Emely Hoch, Yael Sidi, Rakefet Ackerman, Vincent Hoogerheide, Katharina Scheiter. (2023). Comparing Mental Effort, Difficulty, and Confidence Appraisals in Problem-Solving: A Metacognitive Perspective. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09779-5

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r?`

Same CRT experiment (284 participants, four appraisal groups). ANOVA on within-participant appraisal-accuracy (Goodman–Kruskal γ) correlations showed a "significant medium effect of appraisal type" with η2 = 0.06; only the confidence–goal-driven effort contrast was significant (p < 0.001).

> "There was a significant medium effect of appraisal type, F(3, 272) = 5.61, MSE = 0.17, p < 0.001, η2 = 0.06. Post hoc pairwise comparisons (t-tests with Bonferroni adjustment for p-values) showed that the correlation with accuracy was significantly stronger for confidence than for goal-driven effort appraisals, p < 0.001."
