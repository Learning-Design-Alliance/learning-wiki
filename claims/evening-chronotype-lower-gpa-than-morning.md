## Evidence

### Bacaro 2025

Bacaro, V., De Lise, F., Natale, V., Tonetti, L., & Crocetti, E. (2025). Night Owls and Early Birds: The Role of Adolescents' Chronotype on Educational Identity Trajectories. International Journal of Psychology, 60(5), e70108. https://doi.org/10.1002/ijop.70108

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

One-way ANOVA on self-reported GPA at T4 across three chronotype groups yielded a significant effect (F(2) = 3.83, p = 0.022, η² = 0.011). Tukey post hoc comparisons showed evening < morning, with intermediate not differing from either ("significantly lower GPA at T4"). The η² of 0.011 indicates a very small effect.

> "adolescents with an evening chronotype reported significantly lower GPA at T4 than those with a morning chronotype, while adolescents with an intermediate chronotype did not differ from the other two groups"
