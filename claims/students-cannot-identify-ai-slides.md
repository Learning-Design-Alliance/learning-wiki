# Students cannot reliably distinguish AI-generated slides from human-created slides

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students' human-vs-AI guesses did not differ between AI and human slides (median guess 4.0 for both). [→ anchor-guess](#anchor-guess)

## Evidence

### anchor-guess

Juho Leinonen, Lisa Zhang, and Arto Hellas. 2026. AI-Generated Slides: Are They Good? Can Students Tell?. In Proceedings of Western Canada Conference on Computing Education 2026 (WCCCE 2026). ACM, New York, NY, USA. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13532

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same in-class survey: after each lecture segment students guessed on a 7-point scale whether slides were AI- or human-made, uninformed about which segments were AI; "the median for the guesses for both AI and human slides was 4.0", U = 28171.5, p = 0.1.

> "While the median for the guesses for both AI and human slides was 4.0, students seemed not to be able to guess the source (AI vs. human) of the slides based on the Mann-Whitney U test results (U = 28171.5, p = 0.1)."
