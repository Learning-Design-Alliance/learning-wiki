# CBT condition was a truly nonsignificant predictor of RBA pretest scores in hierarchical linear models

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Adding CBT condition to the pretest HLM had no meaningful impact: the CBT intercept was -0.40 (p=0.63), and adding condition increased intercept variance, indicating a truly nonsignificant variable. [→ Jayson M. Nissen 2018](#jayson-m-nissen-2018)

## Evidence

### Jayson M. Nissen 2018

Jayson M. Nissen, Manher Jariwala, Xochith Herrera, Eleanor W. Close, and Ben Van Dusen. (2018). Performance differences for in-class and online administration of low-stakes research-based assessments. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/perc.2017.pr.044](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.044)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Hierarchical linear modeling of pretest scores from the 1,310-student between-groups experiment, with students nested in sections. The pretest model showed a CBT condition intercept of -0.40 with p=0.63, described as having "no meaningful impact" on score.

> "The intercept for CBT condi- tion was very small, -0.40, and was not statistically reliably different from 0 (p=0.63). This result indicated that whether or not students completed the pretest online or in class had no meaningful impact on their score."
