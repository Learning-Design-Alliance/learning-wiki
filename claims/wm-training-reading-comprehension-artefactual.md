## Subclaims
`q4 i0` Reading comprehension showed a small significant treated-control effect at posttest (g = 0.15), but excluding comparisons with pretest-to-posttest declines in the control group reduces it to a trivial g = 0.08. [→ Melby-Lervåg 2016](#melby-lervag-2016)

## Evidence

### Melby-Lervåg 2016

Melby-Lervåg, M., Redick, T. S., & Hulme, C. (2016). Working Memory Training Does Not Improve Performance on Measures of Intelligence or Other Measures of “Far Transfer”: Evidence From a Meta-Analytic Review. Perspectives on Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616635612

`q4 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analysis of reading comprehension outcomes versus treated controls. Six comparisons across four studies showed decreases in reading comprehension from pretest to posttest in the control group; when these problematic comparisons were excluded the effect became trivial (g = 0.08).

> "For reading comprehension, the effect size for studies with treated controls is small though significant (g = 0.15)."
