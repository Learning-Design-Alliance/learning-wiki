# Correlations between course evaluations and grade measures become non-significant when grades are corrected using modified Hofstee cut points

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The percentage of students passing with arbitrary cut points correlated with median course evaluation (rho = 0.18, P = 0.017), but with a modified Hofstee cut point this became non-significant (rho = 0.06, P = 0.41); likewise the A-grade correlation became non-significant (rho = 0.09, P = 0.27). ## Evidence

### Gilbert R. O. 2025

Gilbert R. O., Gilbert D. R. (2025). Student evaluations of teaching do not reflect student learning: an observational study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06896-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Secondary analysis calculating modified Hofstee cut scores as an objective alternative to the arbitrary 70% pass point for each of the 160 courses; the study reports the pass-rate/evaluation relationship "became non-significant" under the calculated cut point.

> "The percentage of students passing courses with arbitrary cut points was correlated to the median course evaluation (rho = 0.18, P = 0.017) but had the modified Hofstee cut point been used, this relationship became non-significant (rho = 0.06, P = 0.41)."
