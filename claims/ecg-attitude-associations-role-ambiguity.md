### Skaliaris Lampros 2026

Skaliaris Lampros, Kipourgos George, Andreopoulou Evangelia, Gkotsi Angeliki, Albani Eleni, Tzenalis Anastasios. (2026). Knowledge and Attitudes Towards ECG Interpretation Among Intensive Care Nurses: A Greek Cross‐Sectional Study. Nursing in Critical Care. https://doi.org/10.1111/nicc.70505

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Chi-squared association between ECG evaluation frequency and attitude items in the survey of 100 ICU nurses; the article reports the printed effect size "Cramer's V = 0.35" (p < 0.001) for this association.

> "Nurses who frequently evaluated ECGs in their daily practice were more likely to consider interpretation an essential nursing responsibility (p < 0.001, Cramer's V = 0.35)."

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive attitude result from the same survey; the article reports that "25% expressed neutrality or disagreement with ECG interpretation as part of nursing responsibilities". No effect size applies to this descriptive proportion.

### Skaliaris Lampros 2026 (3)

Skaliaris Lampros, Kipourgos George, Andreopoulou Evangelia, Gkotsi Angeliki, Albani Eleni, Tzenalis Anastasios. (2026). Knowledge and Attitudes Towards ECG Interpretation Among Intensive Care Nurses: A Greek Cross‐Sectional Study. Nursing in Critical Care. https://doi.org/10.1111/nicc.70505

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Chi-squared comparison of perceived preparedness by ICU type; the article reports the printed effect size "Cramer's V = 0.29" (p = 0.010) for the 40% vs. 22% contrast. The authors suggest this may reflect heightened awareness of clinical complexity rather than lower ability.

> "Cardiac ICU nurses were more likely to perceive themselves as inadequately prepared compared with general ICU nurses (40% vs. 22%; p = 0.010, Cramer's V = 0.29)."
