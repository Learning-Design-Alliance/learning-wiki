# Reading times for unknown words decrease significantly after three to four encounters, and by eight encounters unknown words are read like known words

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` For L2 readers, the decrease in gaze duration for target nonwords started to be significant from the third encounter (r = .47), and in number of fixations and total reading time from the fourth encounter (r = .42 and r = .53). [→ Pellicer-Sánchez 2016](#pellicer-sanchez-2016)
`q3 i?` The difference between target and control reading measures disappeared in all measures only at the eighth encounter. [→ Pellicer-Sánchez 2016](#pellicer-sanchez-2016)

## Evidence

### Pellicer-Sánchez 2016

Pellicer-Sánchez, Ana. (2016). Incidental L2 Vocabulary Acquisition From and While Reading: An Eye-Tracking Study. Studies in Second Language Acquisition, 38(1), 97-130. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263115000224

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Post hoc Mann-Whitney comparisons with Bonferroni correction (.05/7 = .007) on eye-tracking data from 23 L2 participants across eight encounters, comparing the first encounter with each subsequent one. Printed effect sizes r = .47, r = .42, r = .53.

> "the decrease in gaze duration for targets started to be significant from the third encounter, Z = –3.18, p = .002 , r = .47. In the case of number of fixations and total reading time, it was not until the fourth encounter that the effect of frequency of exposure started to be significant"
