# No moderation effects were found: the physics GRE score does not change the relationship between GPA and admission, and no demographic moderation effects were detected

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The physics GRE score does not moderate the GPA–admission relationship (b2 = 0.024, 95% CI including zero). [→ Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero 2020](#nicholas-t-young-and-marcos-d-caballero-2020)
`q2 i?` Institutional selectivity marginally moderates the physics GRE–admission relationship (zero outside the 93% confidence interval for b2,selectivity), a result the authors say warrants further analysis. [→ Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero 2020 (2)](#nicholas-t-young-and-marcos-d-caballero-2020-2)
`q2 i?` None of the demographic moderation coefficients (physics GRE or GPA moderating gender or race) differ statistically from zero. [→ Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero 2020 (3)](#nicholas-t-young-and-marcos-d-caballero-2020-3)

## Evidence

### Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero 2020

Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero. (2020). The Physics GRE does not help applicants “stand out”. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2008.10712](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2008.10712)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bootstrapped logistic moderation analysis with the physics GRE as moderator of the GPA–admission relationship. The article reports b2 = 0.024 (−0.114, 0.154), an interval containing zero, and "we do not ﬁnd evidence that the physics GRE score moderates" admission.

> "we ﬁnd that b2 = 0.024 (−0.114, 0.154). As zero is included in the conﬁdence interval, we do not ﬁnd evidence that the physics GRE score moderates the relationship between an applicant’s GPA and whether they are admitted."

### Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero 2020 (2)

Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero. (2020). The Physics GRE does not help applicants “stand out”. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2008.10712](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2008.10712)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Moderation analysis of institutional selectivity on the physics GRE–admission relationship in the same bootstrapped models. The article states "the result is marginally signiﬁcant" and that zero is not contained in the 93% conﬁdence interval for b2,selectivity.

> "However, in the case of institutional selectivity moderating physics GRE and admit, the result is marginally signiﬁcant. Indeed, zero is not contained in the 93% conﬁdence interval forb2,selectivity."

### Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero 2020 (3)

Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero. (2020). The Physics GRE does not help applicants “stand out”. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2008.10712](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2008.10712)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bootstrapped moderation analysis for gender and race with both the physics GRE and GPA as moderators of admission. The article reports that "none of the b2 values are statistically different from zero", listing the coefficient estimates and confidence intervals for each.

> "When investigating whether any moderation effects exist, we do not ﬁnd that to be the case. That is, we ﬁnd that none of the b2 values are statistically different from zero."
