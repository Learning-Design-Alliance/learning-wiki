## Evidence

### Mogali Sreenivasulu Reddy 2025

Mogali Sreenivasulu Reddy, Yan Qi, Jiang Nan, Teng Peng, Xu Youran, Qian Kaoliang, Lu Ming, Hu Jun. (2025). Case-Based Virtual Reality Simulation for Severe Pelvic Trauma Clinical Skill Training in Medical Students: Design and Pilot Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/59850

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Descriptive results of a 15-item 5-point Likert questionnaire (Cronbach alpha 0.85, adapted from a validated Technology Acceptance Model instrument) administered to 40 pilot students. Undergraduate vs graduate mean ratings for Q1 were 4.40 (SD 0.66) and 4.80 (SD 0.40).

> "The respondents showed strong agreement; 95% (n=38) agreed or strongly agreed that the interactive software simulated a real patient scenario (Q1 in Figures 6 and 7)."

### Mogali Sreenivasulu Reddy 2025 (2)

Mogali Sreenivasulu Reddy, Yan Qi, Jiang Nan, Teng Peng, Xu Youran, Qian Kaoliang, Lu Ming, Hu Jun. (2025). Case-Based Virtual Reality Simulation for Severe Pelvic Trauma Clinical Skill Training in Medical Students: Design and Pilot Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/59850

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Descriptive questionnaire results in the same pilot; Table 2 prints Q3 means of 4.55 (SD 0.50) undergraduates and 4.80 (SD 0.40) graduates, and Q4 means of 4.85 (SD 0.36) and 4.70 (SD 0.46).

> "All students (n=40, 100%) felt they had to make real-life doctor decisions by the end of the simulation (Q3) and found the VR simulation interesting and useful (Q4)."
