## Evidence

### Juan J. Merelo 2023

Juan J. Merelo, Pedro A. Castillo, Antonio M. Mora, Francisco Barranco, Noorhan Abbas, Alberto Guillén and Olia Tsivitanidou. (2023). Chatbots and messaging platforms in the classroom: an analysis from the teacher's perspective. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1007/s10639-023-11703-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10639-023-11703-x)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Chi-square test of independence on the 282-response first survey, cross-tabulating sector (university vs vocational) against post-COVID changes in messaging-app habits; the relation was significant at p = 0.048, with cross-tabulated frequencies given in Table 2.

> "The relation between the latter variable and the instructors’ sector was signiﬁcant, X 2(4,N =282) = 9.598, p = 0 .048."

### Juan J. Merelo 2023 (2)

Juan J. Merelo, Pedro A. Castillo, Antonio M. Mora, Francisco Barranco, Noorhan Abbas, Alberto Guillén and Olia Tsivitanidou. (2023). Chatbots and messaging platforms in the classroom: an analysis from the teacher's perspective. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1007/s10639-023-11703-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10639-023-11703-x)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Two further chi-square tests of independence on the same 282-response survey linked post-COVID changes to discipline and to gender. Pearson's residual analyses (Figs. 8-10) showed, e.g., female educators who started using messaging apps contributing about 31% of the gender test result.

> "The relationships between changes in the use of messaging apps, due to the covid-19 pandemic, and the instructors’ discipline, X 2(24,N =282) = 44.856, p = 0.006, as well as the gender, X 2(8,N =282) = 16 .249, p= 0.039 were also signiﬁcant."
