## Evidence

### Gerd Kortemeyer and Wolfgang Bauer 2024

Gerd Kortemeyer and Wolfgang Bauer. (2024). Cheat sites and artificial intelligence usage in online introductory physics courses: what is the extent and what effect does it have on assessments? arXiv preprint. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010145](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010145)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Observational comparison of course assessment records from two Fall 2023 online introductory physics courses: t-test comparing score distributions of nine unsupervised online quizzes against two supervised on-campus quizzes, "significantly different (p ≈ 4.7 · 10−47)". Means in Table I: OnlExams 83.10%, CamExams 53.80%. No effect size is printed.

> "Figure 4 shows the score distributions for the nine exams that were conducted online and the two exams that were conducted on-campus under supervision. In a t-test, these distributions are significantly different (p ≈ 4.7 · 10−47)."
