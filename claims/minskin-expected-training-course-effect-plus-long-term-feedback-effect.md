## Evidence

### Badertscher Nina 2011

Badertscher Nina, Rosemann Thomas, Tandjung Ryan, Braun Ralph P. (2011). minSKIN Does a multifaceted intervention improve the competence in the diagnosis of skin cancer by general practitioners? Study protocol for a randomised controlled trial. Trials. https://doi.org/10.1186/1745-6215-12-165

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Authors' stated expectation in the protocol abstract and Discussion section, attributed to prior evidence that the effect of continuing medical education is superior to single interventions. No effect size is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "We expect an improvement of the competence in skin cancer diagnosis by GPs in both groups after the full day training course. Beside this immediate effect, we also expect a long term effect in the intervention group because of the continuous problem based feedback."

### Badertscher Nina 2011 (2)

Badertscher Nina, Rosemann Thomas, Tandjung Ryan, Braun Ralph P. (2011). minSKIN Does a multifaceted intervention improve the competence in the diagnosis of skin cancer by general practitioners? Study protocol for a randomised controlled trial. Trials. https://doi.org/10.1186/1745-6215-12-165

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Discussion-section expectation of a group difference at T2 and a within-group T1-to-T2 gain in the intervention group, presented as evidence of continuous problem based teaching effect. No results or effect sizes are printed in this protocol.

> "Therefore, we expect at T2 a significant difference in the competence in the diagnosis of skin cancer between the intervention group and the control group. We even expect that in the intervention group, GPs will score better at T2 than they did at T1, this would show the effect of the continuous problem based teaching."
