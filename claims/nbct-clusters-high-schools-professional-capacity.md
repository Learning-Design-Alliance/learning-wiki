# High schools with clusters of at least three NBCTs score higher on professional capacity measures

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` High schools with clusters of at least three national-board-certified teachers scored significantly higher on collective responsibility, reflective dialogue, and teacher-teacher trust; in elementary schools the trend was evident but not statistically significant, though NBCT-cluster elementary schools were more likely to use reformed literacy practices. ## Evidence

### Hart 2006

Hart, H. M., Ponisciak, S. M., Sporte, S. E., & Stevens, W. D. (2006). Principal and Teacher Leadership in Chicago: Continuing Analysis of Three Initiatives. Evaluation Report prepared for The Chicago Public Education Fund, Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/principal-and-teacher-leadership-chicago-continuing-analysis-three-initiatives

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional comparison of schools with and without NBCT clusters using CCSR teacher survey measures of professional capacity. The elementary-level trend did not reach statistical significance, but elementary NBCT-cluster schools were significantly more likely to use reformed literacy practices.

> "High schools with clusters of at least three national-board-certified teachers (NBCTs) scored significantly higher on several measures of professional capacity, including collective responsibility, reflective dialogue, and teacher-teacher trust."
