Evidence¶

Strain 2017¶

Phillip S. Strain. (2017). Four-Year Follow-Up of Children in the LEAP Randomized Trial: Some Planned and Accidental Findings. Topics in Early Childhood Special Education, 37(2), 121–126. https://doi.org/10.1177/0271121417711531

q2 · i? · associational · r2

The follow-up analysis of placement decisions in both study arms found a "disturbing pattern of “child-independent” decisions": some districts placed children in autism classes because of a past label, others placed progress-makers inclusively, and parent advocacy occasionally altered policy.

"where children were placed was driven by a district-level decision conditioned on opinions about “other” individuals with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and/or historical information."

Strain 2017 (2)¶

Phillip S. Strain. (2017). Four-Year Follow-Up of Children in the LEAP Randomized Trial: Some Planned and Accidental Findings. Topics in Early Childhood Special Education, 37(2), 121–126. https://doi.org/10.1177/0271121417711531

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Table 1 cross-tabulates all study participants by preschool outcome quartile against inclusive versus autism class placement; contrary to expectation, the top quartile was least represented in inclusive settings (18% vs 21%), and the author concludes there is "no correspondence" between preschool growth and placement.

"Overall, the distribution of children in Table 1 demonstrates no correspondence between relative growth on study outcome measures in preschool and kindergarten placements."
