## Evidence

### Cousins 2019

Cousins, J. N., van Rijn, E., Ong, J. L., Wong, K. F., & Chee, M. W. L. (2019). Does splitting sleep improve long-term memory in chronically sleep deprived adolescents? npj Science of Learning 4:8. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0047-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Polysomnography averaged over two recorded nights (SR21, SR23) of the second restriction week: "TST was again signiﬁcantly lower in the split sleep group" (about 12 min less) and "REM sleep duration was signiﬁcantly reduced by splitting sleep" (64.0 vs 75.36 min, t(53) = 2.697, p = 0.009). Memory did not correlate with any sleep metric.

> "TST was again signiﬁcantly lower in the split sleep group (split sleep: M = 354.71 ± 9.57 min; continuous sleep: M = 366.69 ± 9.41 min), t(53) = 4.682, p < 0.001. Analysis of macrostructure showed that REM sleep duration was signiﬁcantly reduced by splitting sleep"
