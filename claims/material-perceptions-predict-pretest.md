## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Sohn Sangho 2019

Sohn Sangho, Lee Young-Mee, Jung Jaehun, Cha Eun Shil, Chun Byung Chul. (2019). The flipped classroom model for an undergraduate epidemiology course. Korean Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.3946/kjme.2019.122

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Multiple regression of pre-test scores on preparedness, relevance, difficulty, and length of materials (Table 3); relevance coefficients were 7.971 (p<0.001) for lectures and 7.638 (p=0.004) for readings. Coefficients are unstandardised; no effect size printed.

> "Among the factors, the consistency and difficulty of both the online lectures and assigned readings showed a significant correlation with learning outcomes (Table 3)."

### Sohn Sangho 2019 (2)

Sohn Sangho, Lee Young-Mee, Jung Jaehun, Cha Eun Shil, Chun Byung Chul. (2019). The flipped classroom model for an undergraduate epidemiology course. Korean Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.3946/kjme.2019.122

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same multiple regression (Table 3): difficulty coefficients were -6.333 (p<0.001) for online lectures and -6.01 (p=0.044) for assigned readings. Unstandardised coefficients; no effect size printed.

> "Among the factors, the consistency and difficulty of both the online lectures and assigned readings showed a significant correlation with learning outcomes (Table 3)."

### Sohn Sangho 2019 (3)

Sohn Sangho, Lee Young-Mee, Jung Jaehun, Cha Eun Shil, Chun Byung Chul. (2019). The flipped classroom model for an undergraduate epidemiology course. Korean Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.3946/kjme.2019.122

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Multiple regression (Table 3): online lecture length coefficient 2.92 (p=0.008), while preparedness was non-significant (p=0.618 for lectures, p=0.79 for readings). Unstandardised coefficients; no effect size printed.

> "Interestingly, although many students cited a lack of time as a barrier to effective independent study, lengthy online lectures were significantly predictive of better learning outcomes (Table 3)."
