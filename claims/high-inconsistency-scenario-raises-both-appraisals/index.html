Evidence¶

Chen Y 2026¶

Chen Y (2026) When universities advocate GAI but practice falls short: student appraisals and creative process engagement in art education. Front. Psychol. 17:1955192. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1955192

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Classroom-based between-subjects scenario experiment with 160 art undergraduates randomly assigned to high- or low-inconsistency conditions; independent-samples t-tests compared appraisals. High-inconsistency participants reported "significantly higher challenge appraisal" with Cohen's d = 1.22.

"Participants in the high-inconsistency condition reported significantly higher challenge appraisal (M = 4.58, SD = 1.30) than those in the low-inconsistency condition ( M = 2.96, SD = 1.37), t(158) = 7.68, p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 1.22."

Chen Y 2026 (2)¶

Chen Y (2026) When universities advocate GAI but practice falls short: student appraisals and creative process engagement in art education. Front. Psychol. 17:1955192. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1955192

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Same scenario experiment (82 low-, 78 high-inconsistency after screening); t-test on hindrance appraisal. The high-inconsistency group showed "significantly higher hindrance appraisal" with Cohen's d = 1.06.

"Participants in the high-inconsistency condition also reported significantly higher hindrance appraisal ( M = 4.71, SD = 1.27) than those in the low-inconsistency condition (M = 3.30, SD = 1.38), t(158) = 6.71, p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 1.06."
