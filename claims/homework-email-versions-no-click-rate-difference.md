# Four homework reminder email versions showed no statistically significant difference in click rate over three weeks

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across three weeks of a homework email reminder field experiment, all four email versions had click rates within 2% of each other and the difference was not statistically significant. [→ Zavaleta-Bernuy 2021](#zavaleta-bernuy-2021)

## Evidence

### Zavaleta-Bernuy 2021

Zavaleta-Bernuy, A., Zheng, Q.Y., Shaikh, H., Nogas, J., Rafferty, A., Petersen, A., & Williams, J.J. (2021). Using Adaptive Experiments to Rapidly Help Students. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1007/978-3-030-78270-2_75](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-78270-2_75)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Panel regression with week fixed effects aggregating the uniform-random portion of the three-week field experiment on student click rate. The article reports "the average click rate in our sample across the three weeks is around 19%" and no significant difference among arms; results were robust to adding student fixed effects.

> "All four arms had click rates are within 2% of each other and their diﬀerence is not statistically signiﬁcant regardless of time and participant eﬀects."
