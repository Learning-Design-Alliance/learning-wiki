# Students evaluate GenAI explanations for assumed background and audience, reading unfamiliar cultural references and excessive scaffolding as signals about who the explanation was written for

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 qualitative `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants flagged explanations that assumed prior knowledge or domain familiarity (e.g., sports and gaming references) the student did not have. [→ assumed-background](#assumed-background)
`q2 i?` Some students read excessive explanation and repetition as condescending social signals of assumed low competence. [→ over-explanation](#over-explanation)

## Evidence

### assumed-background

Seth Bernstein and Naaz Sibia. 2026. Flawed but Memorable: Student Critical Reception of Interest-Personalized GenAI Analogies in Computing Education. Koli Calling '26. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06095 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.06095](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2609.06095)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

RQ2 findings from reflexive thematic analysis of the ten-participant study. The article reports "assumed domain familiarity, particularly around sports and gaming references," with P9 recalling sports analogies she could not use and P2 noting jargon assumptions even in a game he knew.

> "A related failure was assumed domain familiarity, particularly around sports and gaming references."

### over-explanation

Seth Bernstein and Naaz Sibia. 2026. Flawed but Memorable: Student Critical Reception of Interest-Personalized GenAI Analogies in Computing Education. Koli Calling '26. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06095

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

The article reports that participants described excessive explanation as condescending; P9 said it felt like it was "trying to baby me," and P6 counted far too many words. Length and repetition were read as signals of assumed competence.

> "This suggests students readlengthandrepetitionas social signals of assumed competence, not just as stylistic choices, complicating a simple "more scaffolding is safer" design heuristic."
