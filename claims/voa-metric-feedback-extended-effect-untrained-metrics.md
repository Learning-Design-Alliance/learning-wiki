## Evidence

### Fazlollahi AM et al 2022

Fazlollahi AM et al. (2022). Effect of Artificial Intelligence Tutoring vs Expert Instruction on Learning Simulated Surgical Skills Among Medical Students: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2021.49008

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Group comparisons of the 4 trained VOA metrics across practice resections (Figure 2B-D). Three of 4 metrics improved significantly in the VOA group; "no significant difference among groups in bleeding rate owing to wide participant variability in this metric". No standardized effect size printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "Of the 4 VOA metrics used for competency training, 3 demonstrated improvement in VOA group and significant differences compared with the instructor and control groups (maximum bipolar force application, mean instrument tip separation distance, and mean bipolar acceleration)"

### Fazlollahi AM et al 2022 (2)

Fazlollahi AM et al. (2022). Effect of Artificial Intelligence Tutoring vs Expert Instruction on Learning Simulated Surgical Skills Among Medical Students: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2021.49008

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Secondary analysis of ICEMS benchmark metrics across practice resections in the randomized trial: feedback targeted at only 4 AI-selected metrics generalized to 8 of 16 untrained metrics. No standardized effect size printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "Of the 16 ICEMS metrics not trained by the VOA, 8 significantly improved in the VOA group compared with instructor and control groups, suggesting that feedback on 4 AI-selected safety and instrument movement metrics resulted in improved bimanual psychomotor performance in other benchmark metrics"
