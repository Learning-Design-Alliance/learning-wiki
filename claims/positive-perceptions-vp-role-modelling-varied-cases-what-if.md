# Students rated the integrated VP session positively, with role modelling (M = 4.44) and varied case practice (M = 4.35) rated higher than “what-if” discussions (M = 3.76)

## Evidence

### Li Zhien 2026

Li Zhien, Asoodar Maryam, Keulers Tom, Joosten-ten Brinke Desirée, Dolmans Diana. (2026). Ensuring Knowledge Transfer: The Critical Role of “What-If” Discussions in Virtual Patient Simulation. Perspectives on Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5334/pme.2363

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive survey analysis (n = 21) using a 19-item instrument rated on a 5-point Likert scale. Students rated overall experience “highly (M = 4.48, SD = 0.51)” and “what-if” discussions “also positive (M = 3.76, SD = 0.81)”. No inferential tests were reported for survey scales.

> "The overall experience was rated highly (M = 4.48, SD = 0.51). The same holds for the other instructional components, role modelling (M = 4.44, SD = 0.39), practice with varied cases (M = 4.35, SD = 0.61) and perceived knowledge transfer (M = 4.33, SD = 0.70). Ratings for the “what-if” discussions were also positive (M = 3.76, SD = 0.81)."
