# Within-group theoretical scores rose from pre-test to post-test after operation training in the combined V-J and J-V groups, but did not change in single-method groups

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Paired comparisons show significant pre-to-post theoretical gains for V-J (57.05 to 62.90, P < 0.001) and J-V (55.05 to 60.05, P < 0.001), but no significant change for V or J groups. [→ Zhang Baoping 2020](#zhang-baoping-2020)

## Evidence

### Zhang Baoping 2020

Zhang Baoping, Li Sihong, Gao Shuting, Hou Mingfang, Chen Hong, He Lulu, Li Yiting, Guo Yumeng, Wang Errui, Cao Rui, Cheng Jingyang, Li Ruiping, Zhang Kailiang. (2020). Virtual versus jaw simulation in Oral implant education: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02152-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Paired sample t-tests comparing first and second theoretical exams within each training group; gains were significant only for the combined groups, with "the difference in the V and J groups was not significant". No effect size printed.

> "the first score of the V-J group (57.05 ± 3.92) is lower than the second (62.90 ± 3.70, P < 0.001); we find similar results for the J-V group (55.05 ± 3.80; 60.05 ± 2.73; P < 0.001). However, the difference in the V and J groups was not significant"
