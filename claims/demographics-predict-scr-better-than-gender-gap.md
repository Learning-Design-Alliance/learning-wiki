## Evidence

### Eamonn Corrigan 2024

Eamonn Corrigan, Martin Williams, and Mary A. Wells. (2024). Beyond gender: The intersectional impact of community demographics on the continuation rates of male and female students into high school physics. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1139/cjp-2023-0319](https://doi.org/10.1139/cjp-2023-0319)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Comparison of the RQ1 and RQ2 mixed-effects model fits (conditional R2 of 0.122 and 0.105 in both table pairs). The article reports the gender-gap models fit markedly worse; R2 from regression is not treated as a standardised effect size.

> "In addition, the conditional R2 values for these models are considerably smaller (Tables II and III), suggesting that community-level demographics are much less effective at predicting the gender gap in SCR between male and female students compared to predicting overall variations in SCR for the entire student population."

### Eamonn Corrigan 2024 (2)

Eamonn Corrigan, Martin Williams, and Mary A. Wells. (2024). Beyond gender: The intersectional impact of community demographics on the continuation rates of male and female students into high school physics. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1139/cjp-2023-0319](https://doi.org/10.1139/cjp-2023-0319)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

RQ2 models: median family income had negative significant estimates for both grades (β11 = −0.88 ± 0.35; β12 = −0.84 ± 0.35), and STEM-degree share widened the gap only for grade 12 (β12 = 1.09 ± 0.29); no standardised effect sizes are printed.

> "In other words, as median family income increases, while holding all other variables in the model constant, SCR for female students tends to increase relative to that of male students, shrinking the gender gap."
