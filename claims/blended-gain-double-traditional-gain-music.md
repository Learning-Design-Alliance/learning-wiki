# Both groups improved significantly from pre-test to post-test, but the blended learning group's average gain (16.11) was roughly twice the control group's gain (9.62)

> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Paired samples t-tests showed significant pre-to-post gains in both groups (experimental gain 16.11, control gain 9.62; both p = .000), with the experimental gain described as twice as much as the control group's. [→ Edward et al. 2019](#edward-et-al-2019)

## Evidence

### Edward et al. 2019

Chamila Nishanthi Edward, David Asirvatham & Md Gapar Md Johar. (2019). The Impact of Teaching Oriental Music Using Blended Learning Approach. Malaysian Journal of Learning and Instruction, 16(1), 81-103. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1219911

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Paired samples t-test on gain for each group: experimental t(119) = 17.852, control t(239) = 12.825, both Sig. (2-tailed) .000; 95% CIs [14.364, 18.289] and [8.139, 12.825] exclude zero. The article states "there was a considerable improvement in the control group as well".

> "Experimental group average gain difference is 16.11 and the control group average gain difference is 9.62. Thus, the result shows that there is a substantial gain difference between experimental groups who studied under BL than those who studied under traditional method."
