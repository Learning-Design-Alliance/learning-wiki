Evidence¶

Huang Pin-Hsiang 2024¶

Huang Pin-Hsiang, Arianpoor Arash, Taylor Silas, Gonzales Jenzel, Shulruf Boaz. (2024). Insights into undergraduate medical student selection tools: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Educational Evaluation for Health Professions. https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2024.21.22

q3 · i2 · quant-synthesis · r2

Random-effects meta-analysis of MMI studies (9 articles, 12 effect sizes per Table 1), with printed pooled values d = 0.417 and d = 0.195. The abstract states "Panel interviews showed no significant effect"; panel CIs intersected zero for academic outcomes.

"The effect sizes of MMI’s were 0.417 (95% CI, 0.092 to 0.743) for early program OSCE/OSLER/clinical exams and 0.195 (95% CI, 0.019 to 0.372) for early program academic results."

Huang Pin-Hsiang 2024 (2)¶

Huang Pin-Hsiang, Arianpoor Arash, Taylor Silas, Gonzales Jenzel, Shulruf Boaz. (2024). Insights into undergraduate medical student selection tools: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Educational Evaluation for Health Professions. https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2024.21.22

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Random-effects meta-analysis of panel interview studies (12 articles, 20 effect sizes). No panel interview effect reached statistical significance; equivalence was not tested.

"Panel interviews had effect sizes smaller than 0.2 for early and end of program academic results, and an effect size of 0.372 for end of program OSCE/OSLER/clinical exams. However, these 3 effect sizes had 95% CIs intersecting zero."
