## Evidence

### Frenzel 2024

Frenzel, A. C., Dindar, M., Pekrun, R., Reck, C., & Marx, A. K. G. (2024). Joy is reciprocally transmitted between teachers and students: Evidence on facial mimicry in the classroom. Learning and Instruction, 91, 101896. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.101896

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis linking instructors' post-session self-reported joy to the class-average mimicry index across 13 instructors. The article reports "r = 0.82, p < 0.001" and notes the correlation held when controlling for instructors' levels of joy expression (partial correlation = 0.70, p = 0.011).

> "In contrast, for instructors, the correlation was clearly positive (r = 0.82, p < 0.001), indicating that instructors subjectively experienced teaching as more enjoyable when their own joy expression co-occurred more frequently with their students' joy expression."

### Frenzel 2024 (2)

Frenzel, A. C., Dindar, M., Pekrun, R., Reck, C., & Marx, A. K. G. (2024). Joy is reciprocally transmitted between teachers and students: Evidence on facial mimicry in the classroom. Learning and Instruction, 91, 101896. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.101896

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis of 69 students nested within instructors, treating self-reported session joy as ordinal. Table 3 reports the student mimicry index correlation as r = -0.033 [CI -0.368; 0.301], p = 0.845, which was not significant; equivalence was not tested.

> "For students, we used the dyad-specific mimicry index and their individual self-reported session joy. We considered the nestedness of students within instructors using the command TYPE = COMPLEX in Mplus."
