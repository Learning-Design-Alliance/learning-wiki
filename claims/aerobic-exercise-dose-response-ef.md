## Evidence

### Diamond Adele 2011

Diamond Adele, Lee Kathleen. (2011). Interventions shown to Aid Executive Function Development in Children 4–12 Years Old. Science. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1204529

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports a sustained aerobic running study in 8–12 year-olds showing greater cognitive flexibility and creativity gains than standard PE, with no effect on non-EF skills.

> "Aerobic running (with exercises becoming more demanding over time) improved 8–12 year-olds’ cognitive flexibility and creativity, and significantly more so than did standard physical education, yet didn’t affect non-EF skills (19)."

### Diamond Adele 2011 (2)

Diamond Adele, Lee Kathleen. (2011). Interventions shown to Aid Executive Function Development in Children 4–12 Years Old. Science. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1204529

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Davis et al.'s randomized assignment of sedentary overweight 7–11 year-olds to no treatment, 20- or 40-minutes/day aerobic games; only the high-dose group improved on EFs and math.

> "Only the high-dose aerobics group improved on EFs (only on the most EF-demanding measure) and math, compared with no-treatment controls. Dose-response benefits of aerobic exercise were found for the most difficult EF task and for math."
