## Evidence

### Quintas-Hijós Alejandro 2020

Quintas-Hijós Alejandro, Peñarrubia-Lozano Carlos, Bustamante Juan Carlos. (2020). Analysis of the applicability and utility of a gamified didactics with exergames at primary schools: Qualitative findings from a natural experiment. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0231269

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Focus groups and interviews with students across the four schools in the natural experiment. Students' positive attitude was qualified by frustration at systematic technological failures such as phones switching off and Wi-Fi interruptions.

> "Students generally showed a very positive attitude toward exergame. However, they indicated that they found systematic technological failures frustrating: “What I didn’t like was that my phone switched off when I held it in my hand” (5B48-s)"

### Quintas-Hijós Alejandro 2020 (2)

Quintas-Hijós Alejandro, Peñarrubia-Lozano Carlos, Bustamante Juan Carlos. (2020). Analysis of the applicability and utility of a gamified didactics with exergames at primary schools: Qualitative findings from a natural experiment. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0231269

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Teacher interviews in the same natural experiment attributed the exergame's motivating effect to students' affinity with technology, noting that students are used to working with new technologies.

> "Another male teacher also considered exergame to be motivating thanks to students’ affinity to technology: “with benefits firstly in motivation because our students get their hooks into anything technological. They’re used to working with new technologies” (6B9-t)."
