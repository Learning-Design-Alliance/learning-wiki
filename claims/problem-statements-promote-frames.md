# Problem statement phrasing promotes different initial frames and may elicit different difficulties

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Problem statements act as the jumping-off point for student framing: procedure-hinting calculate phrasing promotes algorithmic frames while phrasing specifying neither numbers nor procedures promotes conceptual frames, potentially eliciting different difficulties or different proportions of them. [→ Bahar Modir 2019](#bahar-modir-2019)

## Evidence

### Bahar Modir 2019

Bahar Modir, John D. Thompson, and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2019). Framing difficulties in quantum mechanics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020146](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020146)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Analytical comparison of two contrasting square-well energy problem statements in the interactions section; the authors argue student responses are interaction effects between student ideas and the question posed, so phrasing may elicit different difficulties or different proportions of them.

> "The ﬁrst problem encourages students to think mathe- matically (“calculate”) and algorithmically (by hinting at a procedure). The second is more conceptual, specifying neither numbers nor procedures."
