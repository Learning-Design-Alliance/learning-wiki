# Schools implementing ST Math with fidelity in at least one grade level showed larger math advantages (ES 0.31-0.35) than matched comparison clusters

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In the fidelity subsample (239 schools, 14 states), combined-grade proficiency effects were 0.35 SD and scale-score effects 0.31 SD, significant after BH correction. [→ Wendt 2019](#wendt-2019)
`q3 i?` By grade in the fidelity subsample, proficiency effects ranged from a fifth to nearly half a standard deviation and scale-score effects from 0.27 to 0.43, all significant after correction. [→ Wendt 2019 (2)](#wendt-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Wendt 2019

Wendt, S., Rice, J., & Nakamoto, J. (2019). A cross-state evaluation of MIND Research Institute's ST Math program and math performance. San Francisco, CA: WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED601907

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

HLM analysis of 497 treatment and 497 comparison grade-level clusters across 14 states, adjusting for baseline achievement, school demographics, and grade nesting. The fidelity subsample showed an effect size of "0.35 of a standard deviation" for proficiency rates and 0.31 for scale scores, both p < .01 with BH correction.

> "The magnitude (or effect size) of the difference for math proficiency rates was 0.35 of a standard deviation and 0.31 for math scale scores."

### Wendt 2019 (2)

Wendt, S., Rice, J., & Nakamoto, J. (2019). A cross-state evaluation of MIND Research Institute's ST Math program and math performance. San Francisco, CA: WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED601907

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Grade-specific regressions in the fidelity sample (N = 168, 156, 173 matched pairs). The report describes proficiency effects as "from a fifth to nearly a half of a standard deviation higher" and states scale-score effect sizes ranged from 0.27 to 0.43, all significant after the BH correction; no single standardized effect size is printed for this pooled statement.

> "students in grade-level clusters 3 through 5 in schools that implemented the ST Math program with fidelity had z-score proficiency rates in math that were, on average, from a fifth to nearly a half of a standard deviation higher than those of students in grade-level clusters not provided with the ST Math program"
