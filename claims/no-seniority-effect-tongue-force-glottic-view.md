## Evidence

### Yau Sze-Yuen 2021

Yau Sze-Yuen, Chang Yu-Che, Wu Meng-Yu, Liao Shu-Chen. (2021). Does seniority always correlate with simulated intubation performance? Comparing endotracheal intubation performance across medical students, residents, and physicians using a high-fidelity simulator. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0257969

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Repeated-measures ANOVA of peak tongue force across 50 learners; the mean was "52.9N (SD = 34.3N)" and neither the seniority main effect nor interactions were significant. Equivalence was not tested.

> "The mean peak force applied on the tongue was 52.9N (SD = 34.3N) (S1 Table). However, the main effect of seniority, and interactions were not significant (F ≤ 1.41, p = n.s)."

### Yau Sze-Yuen 2021 (2)

Yau Sze-Yuen, Chang Yu-Che, Wu Meng-Yu, Liao Shu-Chen. (2021). Does seniority always correlate with simulated intubation performance? Comparing endotracheal intubation performance across medical students, residents, and physicians using a high-fidelity simulator. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0257969

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Chi-square analysis of Cormack Lehane grades across seniority for direct laryngoscopy only, since video-laryngoscope views differ from the original grading concept; no significant difference was found. Equivalence was not tested.

> "The majority of intubations were grade IV (94/200, 47%), followed by grade I (57/200, 28.5%) (Table 1). A chi-square test of independence indicated that Cormack Lehane grade did not differ by seniority (χ2(9) = 7.32, p > 0.05)."
