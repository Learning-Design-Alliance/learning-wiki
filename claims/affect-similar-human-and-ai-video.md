## Evidence

### Lia 2024

Lia, Z. R.-Y., Barry, C., & Cukurova, M. (2024). Adult learners' recall and recognition performance and affective feedback when learning from an AI-generated synthetic video. Technical Report. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2412.10384](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2412.10384)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Affective assessment adapted from Wiggins et al. (1988) with 16 items (e.g., outgoing, friendly, dominant) administered to participants in the two video conditions of the experiment. The article reports "the insignificant results of the paired t-tests for all items"; no effect size was printed for these tests.

> "Both video conditions demonstrated similar mean scores in 16 affective items, as Figure 9 illustrated, based on the insignificant results of the paired t-tests for all items"

### Lia 2024 (2)

Lia, Z. R.-Y., Barry, C., & Cukurova, M. (2024). Adult learners' recall and recognition performance and affective feedback when learning from an AI-generated synthetic video. Technical Report. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2412.10384](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2412.10384)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Pearson correlation between the 16-item affective ratings of the two video conditions in the same experiment. The article reports "C2 and C4 presented moderate correlation (Pearson's r = .62, p < .01)", with the printed 95% CI [.58, .65] in Table 5.

> "Moreover, C2 and C4 presented moderate correlation (Pearson's r = .62, p < .01), demonstrated in Table 5, according to the strength of correlation coefficients"
