# Providing worked examples of journal entries early (rather than delayed) yields better early journal quality and more conceptual knowledge

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a quasi-experimental field experiment in 5th-grade mathematics, providing journal examples early led to qualitatively better early journal entries and more conceptual knowledge, with no effects on procedural knowledge. [→ Nückles 2020](#nuckles-2020)

## Evidence

### Nückles 2020

Nückles, M., Roelle, J., Glogger-Frey, I., Waldeyer, J., & Renkl, A. (2020). The Self-Regulation-View in Writing-to-Learn: Using Journal Writing to Optimize Cognitive Load in Self-Regulated Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09541-1

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Quasi-experimental field experiment in high school mathematics (5th grade) with journal writing as homework after four lessons, comparing early versus delayed example provision across classrooms. "providing examples early led to qualitatively better learning journal entries" and more conceptual knowledge, with no procedural-knowledge effects.

> "providing examples early led to qualitatively better learning journal entries in the beginning (first two lessons) and to the acquisition of more conceptual knowledge from the first two and the last two lessons (no effects on procedural knowledge)"
