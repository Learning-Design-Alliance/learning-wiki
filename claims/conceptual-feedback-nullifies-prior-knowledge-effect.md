# Conceptual feedback significantly reduces the effect of prior knowledge on achievement

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A significant negative interaction between pretest score and conceptual feedback group indicates that prior knowledge mattered far less for achievement when students received conceptual feedback. [→ Fynn Töllner 2026](#fynn-tollner-2026)

## Evidence

### Fynn Töllner 2026

Fynn Töllner, Poldi Kuhl, Michael Besser. (2026). Feedback complexities interact with prior knowledge. Learning and Instruction 105 (2026) 102413. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2026.102413

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Interaction analysis in the N = 190 multilevel model: the conceptual feedback × pretest interaction was significant (estimate −0.609, p = .046), approximately cancelling the pretest effect (0.623), so an additional correctly solved pretest task translated to only 0.014 additional posttest tasks in that group.

> "An analysis of the interactions of the feedback groups with the pretest score revealed a significant interaction only for the conceptual feedback group."
