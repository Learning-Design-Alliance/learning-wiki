# Most heterogeneity in the effects of student SES and school SES on achievement is due to differences between countries, not between cycles or sampling error

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For student SES on mathematics achievement, 96.8% of heterogeneity was between countries (I2 Level-2), with 0.0% between cycles. [→ Yigiter 2025](#yigiter-2025)
`q3 i?` For school SES on mathematics achievement, 88.6% of heterogeneity was between countries (I2 Level-2). [→ Yigiter 2025 (2)](#yigiter-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Yigiter 2025

Yigiter, Mahmut Sami. (2025). The effect of socioeconomic status on academic achievement: A big data study across countries and time with integrative data analysis. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0335485

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Heterogeneity tests (Q and I2 statistics, Table 3) on the pooled effect sizes for student SES on mathematics achievement. The article reports "the majority of the observed heterogeneity (96.8%) due to differences between countries" and inter-cycle heterogeneity of 0.0%.

> "These findings indicate that heterogeneity across years is minimal, with the majority of the observed heterogeneity (96.8%) due to differences between countries."

### Yigiter 2025 (2)

Yigiter, Mahmut Sami. (2025). The effect of socioeconomic status on academic achievement: A big data study across countries and time with integrative data analysis. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0335485

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Heterogeneity analysis for school SES on mathematics achievement in Table 3, where "cross-country differences (88.6%)" dominate, with 9.7% due to sampling error and 1.7% between cycles; findings were statistically significant (p < .001).

> "It is seen that cross-country differences (88.6%) are also a significant source of heterogeneity in the effect of school SES on mathematics achievement."
