## Evidence

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Challenges in Analogical Reasoning. Department of Physics and Astronomy, University of Pittsburgh. Originally published 2008. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06423](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06423)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

In the 37-student analogy study, "The most common incorrect response, by 16% of the students" was that the frictional force equals μsN; other incorrect responses used μs or μk, and some students who wrote F = mg sinθ = 7500 N correctly then multiplied 7500 N by a friction coefficient.

> "The most common incorrect response, by 16% of the students, was that the magnitude of the frictional force is sNm ."

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Challenges in Analogical Reasoning. Department of Physics and Astronomy, University of Pittsburgh. Originally published 2008. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06423](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06423)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Qualitative evidence from individual discussions in the earlier problem-pair study: even when attention was drawn to identical free-body diagrams, some students used convoluted reasoning and asserted friction has a special formula, so the free-body diagram applies only to tension problems.

> "In individual discussions, students often noted that the problem with friction must be solved differently from the problem involving tension because there is a special formula for the frictional force."
