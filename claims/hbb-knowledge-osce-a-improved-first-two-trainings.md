# HBB knowledge and OSCE A pass rates rose significantly from pre-test to post-test after trainings one and two only, with no significant change after the third session, in one uncontrolled pre-post study

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Knowledge scores and OSCE A pass rates rose significantly from pre-test to post-test after training one (knowledge 17→23; OSCE A 3→29 passes) and training two (knowledge 22→30; OSCE A 24→34 passes), but did not change significantly after training three, in one group with no comparison group. [→ Mubeen Kiran 2021](#mubeen-kiran-2021)

## Evidence

### Mubeen Kiran 2021

Mubeen Kiran, Baig Marina, Abbas Sadia, Adnan Farzana, Lakhani Arusa, Bhamani Shireen Shehzad, Rehman Bushra, Shahid Shahnaz, Jan Rafat. (2021). Helping babies breathe: assessing the effectiveness of simulation-based high-frequency recurring training in a community-based setting of Pakistan. BMC Pediatrics. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12887-021-03014-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

McNemar tests of pass/fail among the 34 community midwives who completed all three trainings. Knowledge was the 18-item HBB knowledge check (14/18 to pass); OSCE A assessed routine care and first Golden Minute interventions (9/12 to pass). Only p-values were printed, so no effect size is reported.

> "However, for knowledge and OSCE A significant improvement was observed after training one and training two only."
