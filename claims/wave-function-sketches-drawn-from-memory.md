## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh and Guangtian Zhu 2016

Chandralekha Singh and Guangtian Zhu. (2016). Cognitive Issues in Learning Advanced Physics: An Example from Quantum Mechanics. arXiv:1602.05619. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05619](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05619)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

From the survey-and-interview investigation: students produced sketches with discontinuities, cusps, exponential decay inside the well, and conflated potential-energy and wave function axes. The article notes the wave function itself is not physical and cannot be observed experimentally.

> "Interviews and written explanations suggest that many students drew diagrams of the wave function from mem- ory without thinking about the physical meaning of the wave function."

### Chandralekha Singh and Guangtian Zhu 2016 (2)

Chandralekha Singh and Guangtian Zhu. (2016). Cognitive Issues in Learning Advanced Physics: An Example from Quantum Mechanics. arXiv:1602.05619. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05619](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05619)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

From a semi-structured think-aloud interview: one student claimed a qualitative sketch was impossible without numerically solving a transcendental equation, then drew parallel curves and a line, saying only that intercepts would give the wave function.

> "It appeared that the student had memorized a procedure but had not developed a qualitative “feel” for what the bound and scattering state wave functions should look like for a ﬁnite square well."
