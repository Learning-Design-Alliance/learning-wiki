# Constructivist teacher beliefs positively predict primary teachers' classroom use of computers

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Teachers' constructivist beliefs have a significant positive effect on classroom use of computers, controlling for demographics and technology-related variables. [→ Hermans 2008](#hermans-2008)

## Evidence

### Hermans 2008

Hermans, R., Tondeur, J., van Braak, J., & Valcke, M. (2008). The impact of primary school teachers’ educational beliefs on the classroom use of computers. Computers & Education, 51(4), 1499–1509. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2008.02.001

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel analysis (Model 3) of questionnaire data from 525 primary teachers in 68 Flemish schools, adding teacher beliefs to a model already containing gender, computer experience and computer attitudes. The article reports "a signiﬁcant positive eﬀect on the ‘Class Use of Computers’" with χ² = 21.69, p < 0.001; no standardised effect size is printed.

> "The analysis results conﬁrm the latter and support the assumption that constructivist beliefs about teaching and learning have a signiﬁcant positive eﬀect on the ‘Class Use of Computers’ ( v2 = 21.69, df =1 , p < 0.001)."
