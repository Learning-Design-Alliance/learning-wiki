# Grasping virtual objects during L2 vocabulary learning enhances test performance relative to audiovisual-only learning

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Audiovisual observation and grasping (AVOG) learning significantly enhanced vocabulary test performance relative to audiovisual (AV) learning (d = 1.18). [→ Manuela Macedonia 2023](#manuela-macedonia-2023)

## Evidence

### Manuela Macedonia 2023

Manuela Macedonia, Brian Mathias, Astrid E. Lehner, Susanne M. Reiterer, Claudia Repetto. (2023). Grasping Virtual Objects Benefits Lower Aptitude Learners' Acquisition of Foreign Language Vocabulary. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09835-0

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Mixed-effects analysis of vocabulary test scores from 46 adults trained in a VR cave under three conditions. The article reports "b = 0.99; p < .001, d = 1.18" for the AVOG-versus-AV contrast, with AV as the reference level.

> "The linear mixed effects model revealed that A VOG learning significantly enhanced vocabulary test performance relative to A V learning (b = 0.99; p < .001, d = 1.18)"
