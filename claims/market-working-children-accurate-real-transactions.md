## Evidence

### Banerjee 2025

Banerjee, A. V., Bhattacharjee, S., Chattopadhyay, R., Duflo, E., Ganimian, A. J., Rajah, K. & Spelke, E. S. (2025). Children's arithmetic skills do not transfer between applied and academic mathematics. Nature. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08502-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Undercover-purchase study of 201 market-working children in Kolkata: enumerators bought unusual quantities of two goods and checked computed totals and change. The article reports "95%, 97% and 98% of children were correct by their second try" across the three transactions, mostly without paper aids.

> "Overall, 95%, 97% and 98% of children were correct by their second try for the first, second and third transactions, respectively"

### Banerjee 2025 (2)

Banerjee, A. V., Bhattacharjee, S., Chattopadhyay, R., Duflo, E., Ganimian, A. J., Rajah, K. & Spelke, E. S. (2025). Children's arithmetic skills do not transfer between applied and academic mathematics. Nature. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08502-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Replication with 400 market-working children across 39 markets in Delhi using the same undercover-purchase design. The article reports "96%, 99% and 97% succeeded" by the second try; only one child used a calculator, 2% received help, and no child made written calculations.

> "Overall, 96%, 99% and 97% succeeded in the first, second and third transactions, respectively, by their second try"
