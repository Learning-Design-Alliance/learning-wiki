# Deficient-processing and all-or-none theories of spacing are challenged by the long-interval data, and simulations of ACT-R and SAM models could not fit the ridgeline of optimal retention

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Deficient processing theory fits poorly because gap effects appear on a scale of days and weeks whereas working memory operates on seconds or minutes. [→ Cepeda et al. 2008](#cepeda-et-al-2008)
`q1 i?` Simulations of Pavlik and Anderson's ACT-R model and Raaijmakers' SAM model could not fit both the increase in optimal gap with RI and the decrease in gap/RI ratio with increasing RI. [→ Cepeda et al. 2008](#cepeda-et-al-2008)

## Evidence

### Cepeda et al. 2008

Nicholas J. Cepeda, Edward Vul, Doug Rohrer, John T. Wixted, and Harold Pashler. (2008). Spacing effects in learning: A temporal ridgeline of optimal retention. Psychological Science, 19(11). https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' own simulations of the ACT-R and SAM models, conducted to determine whether these quantitative theories can characterize the ridgeline of optimal retention; the article also argues deficient-processing and all-or-none accounts "would not seem to fit well with present data". Encoding variability and study-phase retrieval accounts are described as potentially consistent.

> "We were not able to fit both the increase in optimal gap as a function of RI and the decrease in gap/RI ratio as a function of increasing RI."
