# Students who learned WPI reasoning in the MZI context spontaneously used it on DSE polarizer questions, using it correctly 79% of the time

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies (3 entries) · 2 causal `r1` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2020 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` After the MZI tutorial, students from the MZI→DSE cohort often used WPI reasoning on DSE polarizer questions 2-5, and used it correctly 79% of the time, which the authors interpret as significant positive transfer. [→ Alexandru Maries 2020](#alexandru-maries-2020)
`q3 i?` Without prior WPI instruction, almost no students used WPI reasoning: only one graduate student out of 45 used it on one MZI polarizer question, and none of the 46 undergraduates did. [→ Alexandru Maries 2020 (2)](#alexandru-maries-2020-2)

`q3 i?` Among students who provided reasoning on the DSE pretest after the MZI QuILT, WPI reasoning use ranged from 20% to 60% for graduate students and 37% to 62% for undergraduates, and was used correctly 79% of the time. [→ Alexandru Maries 2017](#alexandru-maries-2017)

## Evidence

### Alexandru Maries 2020

Alexandru Maries, Ryan Sayer and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). Can students apply the concept of "which-path" information learned in the context of Mach Zehnder Interferometer to the double-slit experiment?. [doi:10.1119/10.0001357](https://doi.org/10.1119/10.0001357)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Percentage of MZI→DSE cohort undergraduates and graduates using WPI reasoning on DSE polarizer questions Q2-Q5 (Table 2), out of those who provided reasoning; usage was highest on the last two questions (e.g., 62% US, 44% GS on Q5).

> "out of all instances in which a graduate or undergraduate student used WPI reasoning to answer a question, he/she used it correctly 79% of the time, thus suggesting significant positive transfer of WPI reasoning learned in the MZI context to the DSE context."

### Alexandru Maries 2020 (2)

Alexandru Maries, Ryan Sayer and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). Can students apply the concept of "which-path" information learned in the context of Mach Zehnder Interferometer to the double-slit experiment?. [doi:10.1119/10.0001357](https://doi.org/10.1119/10.0001357)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Baseline on the MZI pretest before the tutorial: WPI reasoning was essentially absent, indicating students were not aware of this reasoning type without prior instruction.

> "For the MZI polarizer questions, we found that without any prior instruction related to WPI, only one graduate student (out of 45) used WPI reasoning in only one question. None of the 46 undergraduate students used this type of reasoning."

### Alexandru Maries 2017

Alexandru Maries, Ryan Sayer, and Chandralekha Singh. (2017). Effectiveness of interactive tutorials in promoting “which-path” information reasoning in advanced quantum mechanics. Physical Review Physics Education Research 13, 020115. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.13.020115

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Analysis of DSE pretest responses from the MZI→DSE cohort (46 UG, 45 GS) after the MZI QuILT but before the DSE QuILT. Table II reports WPI-use percentages per question; the article states students "used it correctly 79% of the time". On average 67% of UG and 28% of GS provided any reasoning.

> "out of all instances in which a graduate or undergraduate student used WPI reasoning and described their reasoning to answer a question, he/she used it correctly 79% of the time, thus indicating appropriate usage of WPI reasoning learned in the MZI context to answer questions in the DSE context."
