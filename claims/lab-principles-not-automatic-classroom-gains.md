# Laboratory-established learning principles do not automatically translate to learning gains for all students in real classrooms

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The worked example effect translated to real classrooms in some contexts and for some students, but not necessarily for all students. [→ Booth 2015](#booth-2015)

## Evidence

### Booth 2015

Booth, J., Oyer, M., Paré-Blagoev, E. J., Elliot, A. J., Barbieri, C., Augustine, A., & Koedinger, K. R. (2015). Learning Algebra by Example in Real-World Classrooms. Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness. https://doi.org/10.1080/19345747.2015.1055636

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' interpretation (type e) from the Discussion, drawing on their two experiments plus prior null classroom translations (Davenport et al., 2007; Dynarski et al., 2007). They conclude "laboratory results do not automatically translate to classrooms" and call for more studies with ecological validity.

> "even well thought-out incorporation of a well-established learning principle into the complexities of the classroom environment will not necessarily lead to appreciable learning gains for all students."
