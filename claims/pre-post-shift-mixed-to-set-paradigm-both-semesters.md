# Students in both the original and transformed introductory lab course shifted predominantly from mixed to set-like reasoning about measurement uncertainty from pre-test to post-test

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Benjamin Pollard 2020

Benjamin Pollard, Alexandra Werth, Robert Hobbs, and H. J. Lewandowski. (2020). Impact of a course transformation on students' reasoning about measurement uncertainty. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020160](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020160)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre/post PMQ administration to matched students in Spring 2017 (original) and Spring 2018 (transformed) semesters; Fisher's Exact tests of paradigm distributions found "significant differences between pre and post" for mixed and set-like responses, all with p ≪ 0.05. Proportions of point-like responses were statistically similar (p = 1 for 2017 and p = 0.34 for 2018).

> "for both semesters, there were signiﬁcant diﬀerences between pre and post for the number of mixed and the number of set- like responses. Fisher’s Exact conﬁrms those diﬀerences, all with p ≪ 0.05."
