# Role framing shifts toward non-use in team contexts, with non-use more than doubling

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` The role-framing distribution differed significantly between individual and team contexts (Cramer's V = 0.253, small effect), with non-use rising from 12.9% to 33.7%. [→ Sehrish Basir Nizamani 2026](#sehrish-basir-nizamani-2026)

## Evidence

### Sehrish Basir Nizamani 2026

Sehrish Basir Nizamani, Zannah Ziew, Saad Nizamani, and Khyati Goyal. (2026). Less Deliberate in Teams: Student LLM Use Across Individual and Collaborative Work. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30860

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r1`

Survey-based comparison of role framing (Assistant, Peer, Expert, Did not use) aggregated across the three individual and three team assignments; the quote reports the printed effect size "V=0.253, small effect" and the framing percentages.

> "Across team assignments, the proportion reporting non-use more than doubled to 33.7%, while Assistant framing fell to 38.0% and Peer framing fell to 26.5%. A chi-square test confirmed that this shift in role framing distribution was statistically significant (𝜒 2(3)= 35.57, 𝑝<.0001,𝑉=0.253, small effect)."
