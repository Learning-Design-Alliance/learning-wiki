## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Women (M = 3.05) had significantly higher university GPAs than men (M = 2.93) overall; a significant student-level by gender interaction showed first-year men with higher GPAs than first-year women, while second- and third-year women outperformed men. [→ Bloodhart 2020](#bloodhart-2020)

## Evidence

### Bloodhart 2020

Bloodhart, B., Balgopal, M. M., Casper, A. M. A., Sample McMeeking, L. B., & Fischer, E. V. (2020). Outperforming yet undervalued: Undergraduate women in STEM. PLoS ONE 15(6): e0234685. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0234685

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Univariate ANOVA of registrar GPAs including student level as a second predictor. The printed effect size ηp2 = .003 is very small, below the threshold for a rating of small on the ηp2 scale used here.

> "Results showed a significant main effect of student gender, with women (M = 3.05) having significantly higher university GPAs than men (M = 2.93) overall, F (1, 1448) = 4.06, p < .05, ηp2 = .003."

### Bloodhart 2020 (2)

Bloodhart, B., Balgopal, M. M., Casper, A. M. A., Sample McMeeking, L. B., & Fischer, E. V. (2020). Outperforming yet undervalued: Undergraduate women in STEM. PLoS ONE 15(6): e0234685. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0234685

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bonferroni-corrected post-hoc tests of the significant level-by-gender interaction (F(3, 1448) = 9.36, p < .001, ηp2 = .019). First-year men led, second- and third-year women led, and seniors did not differ; the article prints no standardized effect size for these pairwise contrasts.

> "The post-hoc test of the interaction showed men in their first year of college have significantly higher university-wide GPAs than women in their first year, while women in their second and third years have higher GPAs than men in those years, and there is no statistical difference between the GPAs of senior-level women and men."
