# Children admitted by random lottery to high-fidelity Montessori schools performed better or the same as waitlisted controls on academic, executive function, and social measures, never worse

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In two lottery-based studies, Montessori children outperformed or matched waitlisted controls on academic achievement, executive function, social cognition, and positive peer play. [→ Angeline S. Lillard 2019](#angeline-s-lillard-2019)

## Evidence

### Angeline S. Lillard 2019

Angeline S. Lillard. (2019). Shunned and Admired: Montessori, Self-Determination, and a Case for Radical School Reform. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09483-3

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports the Lillard and Else-Quest (2006) lottery study of 5- and 12-year-olds at an AMI-recognized public Montessori school in Milwaukee versus waitlist controls, where "they performed better than waitlisted controls" on the named subtests and social measures, and never worse on any measure.

> "at age 5, they performed better than waitlisted controls on the Woodcock-Johnson Letter-Word, Word Attack, and Applied Problems subtests, and on tests of executive function, social problem-solving, and social cognition"
