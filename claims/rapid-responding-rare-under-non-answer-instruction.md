# Rapid responding was rare (0.3% of responses under 5 seconds) on this instructed low-stakes test, far below previously reported low-stakes levels

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Only 0.3% of all responses took less than 5 seconds, which the authors attribute to instructions discouraging rapid and random responding. [→ Srdjan Verbić & Boris Tomić 2008](#srdjan-verbic-boris-tomic-2008)

## Evidence

### Srdjan Verbić & Boris Tomić 2008

Srdjan Verbić & Boris Tomić. (2008). Test item response time and the response likelihood. Institute for Education Quality and Evaluation. http://sepp.ceo.edu.rs/moodle/mod/resource/view.php?id=25

`q2 · i1`

Response-time analysis of the 32-item computer-based physics trial test. The authors report 0.3% of responses under 5 seconds, "much lower level of rapid responding than previously reported for low-stakes tests: 5% (Wise & DeMars, 2006), 5-15% (Yang, 2007)", attributing this to the non-answer instruction.

> "We have identified rapid-responding behavior for fairly small proportion of students: 0.3% of all responses took less than 5 seconds."
