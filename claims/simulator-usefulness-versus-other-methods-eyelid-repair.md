# Residents rated the mannequin-based simulator more useful than past practice methods but less useful than operating room experience, and equivalent to cadaver head

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The surgical simulator was preferred over past methods (fruit peels, surgical skill boards, gloves, pig feet; p = 0.03), rated lower than operating room experience (p = 0.02), and rated equivalent to cadaver head (median 2.5 vs 2.5, p = 0.74). [→ Zhao Jiawei 2021](#zhao-jiawei-2021)

## Evidence

### Zhao Jiawei 2021

Zhao Jiawei, Ahmad Meleha, Gower Emily W., Fu Roxana, Woreta Fasika A., Merbs Shannath L. (2021). Evaluation and implementation of a mannequin-based surgical simulator for margin-involving eyelid laceration repair – a pilot study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-021-02600-3

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Post-training questionnaire of the 6 simulation participants; usefulness of each method ranked on a 5-point Likert scale and compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test (Table 2: simulator median 2.5, operating room 5 p = 0.02, cadaver head 2.5 p = 0.74, past methods 1 p = 0.03). No effect size printed.

> "Residents reported that they preferred using our surgical simulator in learning margin-involving laceration repair more than past methods such as fruit peels, surgical skill boards, gloves, and pig feet (p = 0.03), and they felt that the simulator was as useful as cadaver heads and emergency department/consult experience"
