# Vocational students who received collaboration instruction plus an online joint-reflection tool showed significantly higher domain-knowledge learning gains than control students, while instruction alone did not differ from control

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Elise H. Eshuis 2019

Elise H. Eshuis, Judith ter Vrugte, Anjo Anjewierden, Lars Bollen, Jakob Sikken, Ton de Jong. (2019). Improving the quality of vocational students' collaboration and knowledge acquisition through instruction and joint reflection. International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning (2019) 14:53–76. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-019-09296-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In a pretest-intervention-posttest study of secondary vocational students, an ANOVA on learning gains (printéd ηp2 = .075) showed "learning gains were significantly higher in the instruction with tool condition compared to the control condition (p = .038)". Paired t-tests showed significant gains in all conditions (d = 1.19, 1.10, 0.57).

> "an ANOVA with learning gains as dependent variable and condition as independent variable revealed that these learning gains differed significantly between conditions, F(2, 84) = 3.39, p =. 0 3 8 ,ηp2 = .075. Post-hoc comparisons (Bonferroni) showed that learning gains were significantly higher in the instruction with tool condition compared to the control condition (p = .038)."
