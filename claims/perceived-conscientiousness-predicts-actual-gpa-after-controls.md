## Evidence

### Talamas SN 2016

Talamas SN, Mavor KI, Perrett DI. (2016). Blinded by Beauty: Attractiveness Bias and Accurate Perceptions of Academic Performance. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0148284

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Partial correlation analysis on 100 faces controlling for age of face, sex of face and perceived attractiveness. The study reports "a significant correlation between perceived conscientiousness and actual academic performance (r = 0.22, p = 0.035)"; findings were unchanged with alternative control sets.

> "Partial correlations revealed (see Fig 1) a significant correlation between perceived conscientiousness and actual academic performance (r = 0.22, p = 0.035)."

### Talamas SN 2016 (2)

Talamas SN, Mavor KI, Perrett DI. (2016). Blinded by Beauty: Attractiveness Bias and Accurate Perceptions of Academic Performance. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0148284

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Multiple linear regression on the 100-face sample testing perceived competence variables against actual GPA. Perceived conscientiousness "significantly predicted actual academic performance" with β = 0.48, p = 0.027; the overall four-predictor model was not significant (adjusted R2 = 0.08, p = 0.082).

> "In a simple regression model perceived conscientiousness significantly predicted actual academic performance (B = 1.59, SE = 0.712, 95% CI [0.18, 3.00], p = 0.027, β = 0.48)"
