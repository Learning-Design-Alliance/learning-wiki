## Evidence

### Thorpe Alistair PhD; Kassianos Angelos P PhD; Plackett Ruth PhD; Krishnamurthy Vinodh PhD; Kambouri Maria A PhD; Sheringham Jessica FFPH 2025

Thorpe Alistair PhD; Kassianos Angelos P PhD; Plackett Ruth PhD; Krishnamurthy Vinodh PhD; Kambouri Maria A PhD; Sheringham Jessica FFPH, PhD. (2025). Comparison of Physician Assistant and Medical Students' Clinical Reasoning Processes Using an Online Patient Simulation Tool to Support Clinical Reasoning (eCREST): Mixed Methods Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/68981

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Retrospective comparison on eCREST Case 1: initial relevant diagnoses considered averaged about 45% (2.7/6) overall; PAs (mean 51.5, SD 14.2) exceeded medical students (mean 43.4, SD 14.4). For case 2 the groups did not differ (P=.19).

> "Compared with medical students (mean 43.4, SD 14.4), PA students (mean 51.5, SD 14.2) included a greater proportion of relevant diagnoses in their initial diagnosis (P<.001)."

### Thorpe Alistair PhD; Kassianos Angelos P PhD; Plackett Ruth PhD; Krishnamurthy Vinodh PhD; Kambouri Maria A PhD; Sheringham Jessica FFPH 2025 (2)

Thorpe Alistair PhD; Kassianos Angelos P PhD; Plackett Ruth PhD; Krishnamurthy Vinodh PhD; Kambouri Maria A PhD; Sheringham Jessica FFPH, PhD. (2025). Comparison of Physician Assistant and Medical Students' Clinical Reasoning Processes Using an Online Patient Simulation Tool to Support Clinical Reasoning (eCREST): Mixed Methods Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/68981

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Retrospective comparison on eCREST Case 1: overall proportion of relevant diagnoses rose from about 45% initially to 64% (3.8/6) on average; final proportions were similar between groups (P=.23).

> "Notably, the average improvement was steeper among medical students (mean 19.5, SD 16.6) than PA students (mean 14.2, SD 16.7; P=.05)."
