# Schools with higher collective efficacy show stronger associations with self-reported instructional quality at the school level than at the teacher level

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` At Level 2, collective efficacy in instruction was strongly associated with school-mean cognitive activation (β = .66) and collective efficacy in classroom management with school-mean classroom management (β = .78). [→ Holzberger 2021](#holzberger-2021)

## Evidence

### Holzberger 2021

Holzberger, D., & Prestele, E. (2021). Teacher Self-Efficacy and Self-Reported Instructional Quality: A Multilevel Perspective on the Role of School Characteristics. Learning and Instruction, 76, 101513. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2021.101513

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Random intercept models on aggregated school-level variables (246 schools). Level 2 standardized coefficients were β = .66 for efficacy in instruction on cognitive activation and β = .78 for efficacy in classroom management on classroom management, with R² of .49 and .61 at Level 2.

> "uniquely significant parameters indicated that schools with higher collective efficacy concerning instruction were also the schools where teachers reported higher cognitive activation (Level 2)"
