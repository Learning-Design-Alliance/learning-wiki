# After controlling for Bloom’s level, question familiarity had no effect on MLab exam performance, while Bloom’s level was a significant predictor

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Regression models controlling for both familiarity category and Bloom’s level found Bloom’s level significantly predicted MLab exam scores, while familiarity (VF or F vs. NF) had no significant effect. [→ Sato Brian K. 2017](#sato-brian-k-2017)

## Evidence

### Sato Brian K. 2017

Sato Brian K., Lee Amanda K., Alam Usman, Dang Jennifer V., Dacanay Samantha J., Morgado Pedro, Pirino Giorgia, Brunner Jo Ellen, Castillo Leanne A., Chan Valerie W., Sandholtz Judith H. (2017). What’s in a Prerequisite? A Mixed-Methods Approach to Identifying the Impact of a Prerequisite Course. CBE—Life Sciences Education 16(1). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-08-0260

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple regression on MLab exam question scores controlling for familiarity and Bloom’s level, run separately for instructor- and lecture-slide familiarity designations (r2 = 0.15 in both). Bloom’s 5 vs. Bloom’s 1 carries an unstandardized coefficient of −0.47, p = 6.2e–4; VF vs. NF is p = 0.07. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "While Bloom’s level was a significant predictor of performance on exam questions, familiarity had no effect, highlighting that the one significant result seen with the raw data was due to a slight imbalance in average Bloom’s levels between the questions in each familiarity category"
