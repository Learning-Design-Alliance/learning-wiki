# A longitudinal small-group EBM intervention improved medical students' overall attitudes and skills scores across three assessment points over 15 months

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A single intervention of 5 EBM steps taught in small-group sessions over 15 months improved overall attitudes (2.4 to 3.6 to 4.0) and overall skills (2.2 to 3.4 to 3.8) across assessments before, mid, and after the course, with mid and post ratings significantly improved versus baseline. [→ Kyriakoulis Konstantinos 2016](#kyriakoulis-konstantinos-2016)

## Evidence

### Kyriakoulis Konstantinos 2016

Kyriakoulis Konstantinos, Patelarou Athina, Laliotis Aggelos, Wan Andrew C, Matalliotakis Michail, Tsiou Chrysoula, Patelarou Evridiki. (2016). Educational strategies for teaching evidence-based practice to undergraduate health students: systematic review. Journal of Educational Evaluation for Health Professions. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5066070/

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Longitudinal pre-post evaluation of EBM integration into a medical school curriculum for 4th-year medical students (n=259) in Thailand, with three assessments (before, middle, after). Table 2 reports "Overall attitudes: 2.4 (1.7, 3.0) vs. 3.6 (3.0, 4.0) vs. 4.0 (3.4, 4.2)" and skills rising from 2.2 to 3.8.
