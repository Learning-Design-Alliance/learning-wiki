# Growth mindset mediates only a small share (about 2.9–3.2%) of the effect of SES on academic achievement across 73 countries

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` SES-driven differences in growth mindset explain only 2.9% of the total SES achievement gap in math (PIE = -0.017, p < 0.01). [→ Madia 2025](#madia-2025)
`q3 i?` The pure indirect effect accounts for about 3.2% of the total SES effect in science and in reading. [→ Madia 2025 (2)](#madia-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Madia 2025

Madia, J. E., Gruijters, R. J., Raabe, I. J. & Hübner, N. (2025). Growth mindset and socioeconomic inequality in academic achievement across seventy-three PISA countries. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00365-8

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Four-way decomposition analysis of PISA 2022 data from 73 countries (503,656 observations in math), controlling for gender, migration status, and country fixed effects. The pure indirect effect through growth mindset was -0.017, with "Prop. PIE = 0.029" of the total SES effect.

> "we find a significant but small effect of -0.017 (p < 0.01). This means that SES-driven differences in mindset explain only 2.9% of the total achievement gap (Prop. PIE = 0.029)"

### Madia 2025 (2)

Madia, J. E., Gruijters, R. J., Raabe, I. J. & Hübner, N. (2025). Growth mindset and socioeconomic inequality in academic achievement across seventy-three PISA countries. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00365-8

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Cross-subject comparison from the same decomposition analysis, reported in the Results section: mediation proportions were similar across the three PISA domains, with confidence intervals as printed.

> "the pure indirect effect (PIE) accounts for ~2.9% (CI 95%: 2.7, 3.0) of the total effect in mathematics, 3.2% (CI 95%: 3.1%, 3.4%) in science, and 3.2% (CI at 95%: 3.0%, 3.4%) in reading"
