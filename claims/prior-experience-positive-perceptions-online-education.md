# Prior online learning experience (familiarity and perceived usefulness) is positively associated with medical students' evaluation of and satisfaction with ongoing formal online education

## Evidence

### Wang Cixiao 2020

Wang Cixiao, Xie A’na, Wang Weimin, Wu Hongbin. (2020). Association between medical students’ prior experiences and perceptions of formal online education developed in response to COVID-19: a cross-sectional study in China. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2020-041886

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple linear regression on the composite evaluation score (Cronbach's α=0.95) from the survey of 99,559 students, with institution fixed effects and demographic covariates. The article reports "β=3.11; 95% CI 2.92 to 3.30; p<0.01" for PU and "β=0.46" for familiarity.

> "In the regression (see table 5), we found that both perceptions of usefulness (β=3.11; 95% CI 2.92 to 3.30; p<0.01) and high familiarity (β=0.46; 95% CI 0.45 to 0.58; p<0.01) had signiﬁcant associations with a positive evaluation of ongoing online education."

### Wang Cixiao 2020 (2)

Wang Cixiao, Xie A’na, Wang Weimin, Wu Hongbin. (2020). Association between medical students’ prior experiences and perceptions of formal online education developed in response to COVID-19: a cross-sectional study in China. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2020-041886

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression on satisfaction dichotomised between Likert score 4 and 3, from the same 99,559-student survey. The article reports "OR 2.55; 95% CI 2.37 to 2.75; p<0.01" for PU and "OR 1.14" for familiarity.

> "It was found that both positive perceptions of usefulness (OR 2.55; 95% CI 2.37 to 2.75; p<0.01) and high familiarity (OR 1.14; 95% CI 1.13 to 1.14; p<0.01) are significantly associated with increased satisfaction with their ongoing online education, but the interaction of PU and familiarity had no significant association with students’ satisfaction."
