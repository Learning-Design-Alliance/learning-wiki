## Evidence

### Pan 2024

Pan, S. C., Han, J. Y. & Fung, F. M. (2024). Prequestioning Enhances Undergraduate Students' Learning in an Environmental Chemistry Course. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2403.02788](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2403.02788)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Per-session analyses (Table 3) of tested versus untested postquestions in the 10-student course study; significant sessions included Lithosphere (d = 1.26), Waste Management (d = 0.84), Environmental Toxicology (d = 0.82), Ozone Layer (d = 1.62), and Food Chain (d = 0.91).

> "there was a statistically significant difference (i.e., p < .05) in favor of the tested condition—that is, evidence of a prequestioning effect—in five lecture sessions (with effect sizes ranging from Cohen’s d of 0.57 to 1.62)."
