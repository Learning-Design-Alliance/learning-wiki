## Evidence

### Gil-Espinosa F.J. 2020

Gil-Espinosa F.J., Chillón P., Fernández-García J.C., Cadenas-Sanchez C. (2020). Association of Physical Fitness with Intelligence and Academic Achievement in Adolescents. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17124362

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional linear regression analysis in 403 adolescents using school grades in Spanish language, mathematics, geography and history, English and physical education as achievement outcomes, adjusted for sex and age. The article reports all β ≥ 0.089 with p ≤ 0.038.

> "Cardiorespiratory fitness, muscular strength, flexibility and overall fitness were positively associated with academic achievement in all academic subjects (all β ≥ 0.089, p ≤ 0.038), except muscular strength, which was not significantly associated with Spanish language or mathematics (all β ≤ 0.050, p ≥ 0.200)."

### Gil-Espinosa F.J. 2020 (2)

Gil-Espinosa F.J., Chillón P., Fernández-García J.C., Cadenas-Sanchez C. (2020). Association of Physical Fitness with Intelligence and Academic Achievement in Adolescents. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17124362

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same regression analysis shows muscular strength's associations were subject-specific: significant for geography and history, English, physical education and grade point average, but the article reports no significant association with mathematics or Spanish Language.

> "With regard to muscular strength, we found significant associations with geography and history, English, physical education and grade point average, but not with mathematics or Spanish Language."
