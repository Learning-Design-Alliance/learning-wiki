## Evidence

### Jennifer Richards 2014

Jennifer Richards, Andrew Elby, and Ayush Gupta. (2014). Incorporating Disciplinary Practices Into Characterizations of Progress in Responsive Teaching. Manuscript in the review process. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1502.04420](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1502.04420)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative case-study analysis of a videotaped 15-minute episode from April 2010. The authors characterize the teacher's attention as focused on "the causal factors (such as force-like entities) responsible for the motion they predicted", illustrated by his press for a force after a student mentioned weight.

> "In the first key drop episode (April 2010), Mr. S primarily attended and responded to a particular form of scientific knowledge with respect to students’ ideas, namely the causal factors (such as force-like entities) responsible for the motion they predicted."

### Jennifer Richards 2014 (2)

Jennifer Richards, Andrew Elby, and Ayush Gupta. (2014). Incorporating Disciplinary Practices Into Characterizations of Progress in Responsive Teaching. Manuscript in the review process. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1502.04420](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1502.04420)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Transcript-based analysis of an exchange in the same April 2010 episode: after Diane offered a racecar story, Mr. S pressed only for the causal factor, and his emphasis on "sp::eed" plus moving on "suggest that he was satisfied with this level of explanation."

> "His verbal emphasis on Diane’s identification of “sp::eed” as the relevant causal factor (line 19), followed by his moving on to other ideas, suggest that he was satisfied with this level of explanation."
