# Postgraduate aesthetic medicine trainees overwhelmingly rate a 3D simulation app as visually similar to 2D models and superior for understanding the 3D structure of facial aging

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Trainees rated the 3D app model as visually similar to the paper-based 2D model (96% agree/strongly agree) and reported the 3D model better assisted facial aging assessments (96% agree/strongly agree), with 100% agreeing it was useful for understanding the 3D structure of facial aging. [→ Hassan Haidar 2025](#hassan-haidar-2025)

## Evidence

### Hassan Haidar 2025

Hassan Haidar, Khalil Hassan, Shatta Bashar, Magrin Anna Maria Fenech, Novo Pereira Ines, Matin Atif. (2025). The Role of Educational Interactive Virtual Simulation App in Aesthetic Medicine and Cosmetic Dermatology Preclinical Skills. Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology. https://doi.org/10.1111/jocd.70496

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Descriptive questionnaire results from 25 QMUL postgraduate trainees (4 male, 21 female) on the 3D Model Visualization and Assessment subscale; percentages only, no inferential tests. "96% of the respondents either strongly agreed or agreed" on visual similarity; 100% agreed the app was useful for understanding 3D facial aging structure.

> "96% of the respondents either strongly agreed or agreed that the visual similarity between the 3D application model of the patient's face and that of the paper‐based 2D model was in fact similar"
