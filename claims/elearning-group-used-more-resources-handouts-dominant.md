# E-learning group students used significantly more learning resources in parallel (median 2 vs 1), while handouts were the dominant single resource in both groups

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` E-learning students tended to use more than one learning resource (1 vs 2 median, p < 0.05 Wilcoxon signed-rank test), and among single-source users, 57.7% and 56.3% respectively relied solely on handouts. [→ Dombrowski T 2018](#dombrowski-t-2018)

## Evidence

### Dombrowski T 2018

Dombrowski T, Wrobel C, Dazert S, Volkenstein S. (2018). Flipped classroom frameworks improve efficacy in undergraduate practical courses – a quasi-randomized pilot study in otorhinolaryngology. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1398-5

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Resource-use questionnaire administered to both quasi-randomized groups. The article separately reports that e-learning students used more resources ("1 vs 2 median, p < 0.05 Wilcoxon signed-rank test") and that handouts were the sole resource for a majority of single-source users in both groups.

> "Among students using a single source only for learning (50.4% of the non-e-learning group vs 14.7% of the e-learning group), 57.7 and 56.3% respectively relied solely on handouts."
