# School level moderates cognitive effects of game-based learning in STEM: games are less effective for secondary than primary school students

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Secondary school students gain less cognitively from game-based learning than primary school students (g = −0.67, p < .001), with school level accounting for 34% of heterogeneity. [→ Michaela Arztmann 2023](#michaela-arztmann-2023)

## Evidence

### Michaela Arztmann 2023

Michaela Arztmann, Lisette Hornstra, Johan Jeuring & Liesbeth Kester (2023). Effects of games in STEM education: a meta-analysis on the moderating role of student background characteristics. Studies in Science Education, 59:1, 109-145. https://doi.org/10.1080/03057267.2022.2057732

`q3 · i2`

Mixed-effects moderator analysis (Knapp-Hartung) for cognition with k = 40 studies; the omnibus test was significant (p < .001) and "accounted for 34% of the amount of heterogeneity". Primary school is the reference category (k = 28) versus secondary (k = 12).

> "The findings indicate that games are less effective for secondary school students (g = −0.67, p = <.001) than for primary school students"
