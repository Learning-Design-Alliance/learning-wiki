## Evidence

### Kılıç Gülşen 2026

Kılıç Gülşen, Işık Sevcan Avcı, Çevik Banu. (2026). Nursing students’ first clinical encounters with delirium: a qualitative study of experiential learning and professional development. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09980-4

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Students reported a shift from fear to comfort: "At first I was very scared, but later I felt more comfortable," with clinical experience directly enhancing professional confidence.

> "Initial fear and anxiety gradually decreased as clinical exposure increased. Subsequent encounters fostered greater confidence and a more controlled, comfortable approach to the clinical environment."

### Kılıç Gülşen 2026 (2)

Kılıç Gülşen, Işık Sevcan Avcı, Çevik Banu. (2026). Nursing students’ first clinical encounters with delirium: a qualitative study of experiential learning and professional development. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09980-4

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Students reflected on growing ability to cope, with one noting experience from the first patient improved communication with the second.

> "Initial challenges eventually fostered a stronger sense of professional self-efficacy. As fear and hesitation subsided through repeated clinical exposure, students transitioned toward a more controlled and proactive approach, actively participating in the care process with increased comfort and competence."
