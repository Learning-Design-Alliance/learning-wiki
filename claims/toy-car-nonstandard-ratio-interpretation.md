## Evidence

### Andrew Boudreaux 2014

Andrew Boudreaux, Stephen E. Kanim, and Suzanne White Brahmia. (2014). Student facility with ratio and proportion: Mapping the reasoning space in introductory physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1511.08960](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1511.08960)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Free-response written Toy car question given on the first day of the WWU general education physics course for non-science majors; '43% correctly interpreted 0.04 as the number of seconds required for the car to travel one centimeter,' while other responses rejected 0.04 as not meaningful or confused it with speed.

> "was administered to 82 students on the first day of the general education physics course for non-science majors at WWU, 43% correctly interpreted 0.04 as the number of seconds required for the car to travel one centimeter."

### Andrew Boudreaux 2014 (2)

Andrew Boudreaux, Stephen E. Kanim, and Suzanne White Brahmia. (2014). Student facility with ratio and proportion: Mapping the reasoning space in introductory physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1511.08960](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1511.08960)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Forced-choice version administered at the beginning of the academic year to N = 628 science and engineering majors at Rutgers enrolled in calculus-based mechanics, E&M, or general chemistry; '74% answered correctly (choice c),' 18% confused 0.04 with speed, and 8% chose the not-valid option.

> "Of these students, 74% answered correctly (choice c), 18% confused 0.04 with the speed of the car (choice a, b, or d), and 8% indicated that 0.04 is not valid (choice e)."
