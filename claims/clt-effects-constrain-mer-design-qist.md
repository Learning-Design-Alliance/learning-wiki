## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Using visual and audio channels simultaneously (the modality principle) benefits learning, while redundant material can hinder learning and split-source presentations impose avoidable load. [→ Simon Goorney 2024](#simon-goorney-2024)

## Evidence

### Simon Goorney 2024

Simon Goorney, Jonas Bley, Stefan Heusler, Jacob Sherson. (2024). A Framework for Curriculum Transformation in Quantum Information Science and Technology Education. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ad7e60](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ad7e60)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article summarises psychology research within Cognitive Load Theory as reported in its Representations section: working memory has separate visual-spatial and audio channels, and "it was shown that it is beneficial for learning to make use of both these memory channels". This is a narrative review-style assertion attributed to prior research, not a new experiment.

> "it was shown that it is beneficial for learning to make use of both these memory channels by giving visual and audio information at the same time, for example in a typical presentation, as opposed to giving information in written words and pictures"
