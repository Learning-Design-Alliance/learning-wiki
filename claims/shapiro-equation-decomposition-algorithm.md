# A gradient-matching algorithm decomposes almost any composite student equation into primitive physics equations

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Shapiro's algorithm can decompose almost any composite equation into its primitive constituents by searching for primitive equations whose gradient combination matches the student equation's gradient. [→ VanLehn 2015](#vanlehn-2015)

## Evidence

### VanLehn 2015

VanLehn, K. (2015). Reflections on Andes' Goal-free User Interface. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0067-7

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

A mathematical algorithm developed by sabbatical visitor Joel Shapiro for the Andes system, replacing combinatorial pre-generation of algebraic combinations, which "exploded combinatorially". The article says it decomposed almost any composite equation and calls it one of the small treasures of ITS research.

> "The algorithm searched for a set of primitive equations such that a linear combination of their gradients at the solution point equaled the gradient of the student’s equation at the solution point."
