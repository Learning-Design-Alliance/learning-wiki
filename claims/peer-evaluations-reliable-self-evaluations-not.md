# Peer evaluations of student teaching were reliable and consistent with instructor and TA evaluations, but self-evaluations were not reliable

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Student evaluations of other pairs' teaching were reliable and consistent with instructor and TA evaluations, while students always rated themselves highly. [→ C. Singh 2016](#c-singh-2016)

## Evidence

### C. Singh 2016

C. Singh, L. Moin, and C. Schunn. (2016). Increasing interest and awareness about teaching in science undergraduates. arXiv:1603.02949 [physics.ed-ph]. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1603.02949](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1603.02949)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Observation from the course evaluation protocol of 15 Likert-scale questions, with all classroom teaching videotaped and each student required to observe and critique at least one other pair per round. The authors found "self-evaluations were not reliable and stu- dents always rated themselves highly."

> "We found that the student evaluation of other pairs were quite reliable and consistent with the instructor and TA evaluation. Students did a good job evaluating the positive and negative aspects of other group’s instruction. However, self-evaluations were not reliable and stu- dents always rated themselves highly."
