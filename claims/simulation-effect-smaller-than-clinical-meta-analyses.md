# Simulation training effects on board communication were smaller than typical simulation effects in clinical settings, possibly due to self-assessed outcomes and brief dosage

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors interpret their effect sizes (d=0.40–0.54) as slightly smaller than meta-analytic effects of simulation training in other contexts and offer candidate explanations: prior high confidence, self-assessed outcomes ('response shift'), and the brief single-session intervention. [→ Faulkner Nicholas 2020](#faulkner-nicholas-2020)

## Evidence

### Faulkner Nicholas 2020

Faulkner Nicholas, Wright Breanna, Lennox Alyse, Bismark Marie, Boag Jane, Boffa Sophie, Waxman Bruce, Watson-Kruse Janet, Paine Geoff, Bragge Peter. (2020). Simulation-based training for increasing health service board members' effectiveness: a cluster randomised controlled trial. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2019-034994

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion, not a tested result. They propose three possible reasons the effect size was slightly lower, including that "the use of self-assessed outcomes may have reduced the effect size" and that "the intervention was brief in nature".

> "These effects are broadly consistent with, although slightly smaller than, those observed in meta-analyses examining the effects of simulation training in other contexts, which have typically found medium to large effect sizes when comparing simulation training to no training."
