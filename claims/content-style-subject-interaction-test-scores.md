## Evidence

### Tiffany D. Do 2025

Tiffany D. Do, Usama Bin Shafqat, Elsie Ling, and Nikhil Sarda. (2025). PAIGE: Examining Learning Outcomes and Experiences with Personalized AI-Generated Educational Podcasts. PREPRINT. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1145/3706598.3713460](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713460)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The two-way ANOVA on test scores from the 180-student study found "a significant interaction effect betweencontent style and textbook subject", with F(4, 171) = 2.27, p < 0.01, prompting per-subject analyses. No effect size is printed.

> "we found a significant interaction effect betweencontent style and textbook subject,𝐹(4, 171) = 2.27,𝑝 < 0.01, which indicated that we should analyze scores of each subject separately."

### Tiffany D. Do 2025 (2)

Tiffany D. Do, Usama Bin Shafqat, Elsie Ling, and Nikhil Sarda. (2025). PAIGE: Examining Learning Outcomes and Experiences with Personalized AI-Generated Educational Podcasts. PREPRINT. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1145/3706598.3713460](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713460)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In Psychology the main effect of content style was significant (F(2, 57) = 3.17, p = 0.04) but "there were no significant pairwise comparisons"; in Government the article reports no significant main effect of content style on test score, F(2, 57) = 2.96, p = 0.06.

> "A post-hoc test revealed that there were no significant pairwise comparisons, however the Personalized condition (M=5.80, SD=1.24) trended as having higher scores than both the Generalized condition (M=4.75, SD=1.62),𝑝 = 0.07, and the Textbook condition (M=4.8, 1.57),𝑝 = 0.09."
