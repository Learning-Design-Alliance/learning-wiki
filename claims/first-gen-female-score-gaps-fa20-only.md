# In FA20, first-generation students had significantly lower overall course scores but not exam or quiz scores, and female students had significantly lower exam scores than male students, neither difference observed in FA19.

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In FA20, first-generation students had significantly lower overall course scores (p = 0.02) but not exam or quiz scores, a difference not observed in FA19; female students had significantly lower exam scores than male students (p = 0.02), also not observed in FA19. [→ Zhongzhou Chen and Tom Zhang 2021](#zhongzhou-chen-and-tom-zhang-2021)

## Evidence

### Zhongzhou Chen and Tom Zhang 2021

Zhongzhou Chen and Tom Zhang. (2021). Analyzing the Heterogeneous Impact of Remote Learning on Students' Ability to Stay on Track During the Pandemic. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2108.00601](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2108.00601)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Mann-Whitney U comparison of normalized summative scores in FA20 (Table VII): first-generation course p = 0.02 (exam p = 0.11, quiz p = 0.16); gender exam p = 0.02 (course p = 0.71). Neither difference was observed in FA19 (Table VIII). No effect sizes printed.

> "In FA20, ﬁrst generation students had signiﬁcantly lower overall course scores but not exam or quiz scores. Since this diﬀerences was not observed in FA19, the diﬀerence in FA20 is likely linked to the penalty from late submissions as well as signiﬁcantly less extra credit for early submissions. In FA20, female students had signiﬁcantly lower exam scores than male students, which was also not observed for FA19."
