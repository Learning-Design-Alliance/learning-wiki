q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Random‐effects proportional meta‐analysis comparing newer LLM models from companies with ≥5 data points against OpenAI models. Reports Claude 3–3.7 at "86.2%, CI = 81.7%–89.7%", DeepSeek R1 at 86.0%, Gemini at 75.5%, Copilot at 69.4%. Heterogeneity was high for Gemini and Copilot (I² ≥ 76%) and low for Anthropic and DeepSeek (I² ≤ 20%).

"Anthropic's Claude 3 through 3.7 (86.2%, CI = 81.7%–89.7%) and DeepSeek's R1 (86.0%, CI = 78.0%–90.3%) models modestly underperformed compared with OpenAI's GPT‐4o_o1 models. The performance of the Google Gemini series (75.5%, CI = 68.0%–81.7%) and Microsoft Copilot (69.4%, CI = 54.0%–81.4%) trailed that of the leading companies."

Related Claims¶

  • GPT‐4 through o1 models achieve pooled accuracy of 87% in the anatomical sciences and ≥83% in five of six biomedical disciplines, consistently above older versions — related
  • Newer OpenAI LLM versions achieve significantly higher pooled accuracy on biomedical MCQs than earlier versions (GPT‐4o–o1: 90%; GPT‐4: 82%; GPT‐3/3.5: 59%) — related
  • LLM version progression explains about half of the variance in biomedical MCQ accuracy, with GPT‐4o_o1 over six times as likely as GPT‐3/3.5 to answer correctly — related
  • GPT-4o and GPT-5.2 achieve higher accuracy and more fully correct answers on university IVDA multiple-choice questions than the smaller Mistral-7B and DeepSeek-R1 models — related
  • The author reports that prompt phrasing and the specific foundational model had secondary impact on final material quality compared with the structure of the workflow itself. — related
  • GPT-4 achieves passing accuracy on USMLE Steps 1–3 (mean 86%), significantly exceeding GPT-3.5 (mean 56%) — related
  • GPT‐4 through o1 models show significantly lower pooled accuracy in pathology (75%) than in other biomedical disciplines — related
