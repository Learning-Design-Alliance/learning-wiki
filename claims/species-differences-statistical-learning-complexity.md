## Evidence

### Conway 2025

Conway, C. M., Jenkins, H. E., Milne, A. E., Singh, S. & Wilson, B. (2025). Addressing the theory crisis in statistical learning research. npj Science of Learning 10, 68. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00359-6

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The authors' comparative-literature evaluation within this Perspective, covering incidental-learning studies in tamarins, marmosets, squirrel monkeys, rhesus macaques, baboons, and chimpanzees; adjacent dependencies are learned by all tested animals, nonadjacent dependencies and supra-regular grammars are harder.

> "the central phenomenon, that core statistical learning abilities exist across the primate taxa (and likely more broadly in the animal kingdom), appears to be highly robust. However, as the complexity of these statistical relationships increases, species differences become pronounced."

### Conway 2025 (2)

Conway, C. M., Jenkins, H. E., Milne, A. E., Singh, S. & Wilson, B. (2025). Addressing the theory crisis in statistical learning research. npj Science of Learning 10, 68. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00359-6

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article reports this operant-training demonstration as showing that incidental-condition failures on supra-regular grammars do not reflect an absolute capacity limit, but the authors caution that different learning systems and motivational, perceptual, and attentional differences are likely recruited.

> "using an explicit, operant training approach in which rhesus macaques were rewarded for correct responses over many thousands of trials, monkeys were capable of learning these types of grammars"
