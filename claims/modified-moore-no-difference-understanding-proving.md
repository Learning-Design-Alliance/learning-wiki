# Modified Moore learning approach and conventional teaching produced no significant difference in undergraduate mathematical understanding or proving ability in advanced abstract algebra

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` There was no difference between MLA and conventional classes in mathematical understanding ability (F = 0.548, Sig. 0.462) or mathematical proving ability (F = 0.086, Sig. 0.771). [→ Maya and Sumarmo 2011](#maya-and-sumarmo-2011)
`q2 i?` Within low and medium prior-ability subgroups there were likewise no differences between the two classes on either ability. [→ Maya and Sumarmo 2011](#maya-and-sumarmo-2011)

## Evidence

### Maya and Sumarmo 2011

Rippi Maya, Utari Sumarmo. (2011). Mathematical Understanding and Proving Abilities: Experiment With Undergraduate Student By Using Modified Moore Learning Approach. IndoMS. J.M.E, Vol. 2 No. 2, pp. 231-250. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1078509

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-test experimental control group study of 56 undergraduates in Advanced Structure Algebra; two path ANOVA on understanding and proving test scores. MUA means were 65.65 (MLA) vs 68.77 (CNV); MPA 56.35 vs 55.17; both null hypotheses accepted. Equivalence was not tested.

> "However according to the result of two path ANOVA in Table 3 , there was no difference between students’ MUA of MLA class and of CNV class. Similar findings of students’ MPA namely there was no difference of students’ MPA as well"
