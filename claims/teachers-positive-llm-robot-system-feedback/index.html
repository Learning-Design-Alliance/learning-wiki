Evidence¶

Tozadore 2025¶

Tozadore, D., Ertug, N., Chaker, Y., & Abderrahim, M. (2025). RoboBuddy in the Classroom: Exploring LLM-Powered Social Robots for Storytelling in Learning and Integration Activities. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16706

q2 · i? · design · r2

Separate post-study interviews with the four teachers in the exploratory 1-week study. The article reports enthusiasm about "ease of use for generating differentiated lessons" and suggestions such as interactive games and progress tracking; one teacher called it a "game changer".

"teachers were enthusiastic about the AI tool’s ease of use for generating differentiated lessons, especially for multilingual students. They suggested adding interactive games and progress tracking."

Tozadore 2025 (2)¶

Tozadore, D., Ertug, N., Chaker, Y., & Abderrahim, M. (2025). RoboBuddy in the Classroom: Exploring LLM-Powered Social Robots for Storytelling in Learning and Integration Activities. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16706

q2 · i? · design · r2

In the discussion of teacher feedback (RQ4), the authors report that teachers identified accurate operation without extra preparation time as crucial to their approval. This is the authors' report of interview content, with no quantitative measure.

"The fact that the system was able to perform accurately and without taking extra time for teachers was pointed as crucial for their approval of our proposal."
