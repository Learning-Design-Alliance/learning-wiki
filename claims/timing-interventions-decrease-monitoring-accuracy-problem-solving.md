# Timing interventions significantly decrease monitoring accuracy in problem-solving tasks and differ from all other intervention types

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r?` · `q4`

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` Interventions targeting the timing of metacognitive judgment significantly decreased students' monitoring accuracy (g = −0.40) and had a significantly lower effect size than all other intervention types. [→ Noortje Janssen · Ard W. Lazonder 2024](#noortje-janssen-ard-w-lazonder-2024)

## Evidence

### Noortje Janssen · Ard W. Lazonder 2024

Noortje Janssen · Ard W. Lazonder. (2024). Meta-analysis of Interventions for Monitoring Accuracy in Problem Solving. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09936-4

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Moderator analysis in the 35-study meta-analysis; Table 3 reports timing k = 2, g = −0.40, 95% CI [−0.52, −0.29]. Pairwise comparisons showed "timing had a significantly lower effect size than all the other intervention types" (z from −4.56 to −2.76).

> "On the other hand, the timing interventions significantly decreased students’ monitoring accuracy. The other interventions did not significantly impact monitoring accuracy."
