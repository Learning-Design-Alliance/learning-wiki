## Subclaims

`q3 i0` In experimental studies, positive-activating emotions show no significant overall association with political learning. [→ Elisabeth Graf 2024 (2)](#elisabeth-graf-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Elisabeth Graf 2024

Elisabeth Graf, Johanna L. Donath, Elouise Botes, Martin Voracek, Thomas Goetz. (2024). The Associations Between Discrete Emotions and Political Learning: A Cross-Disciplinary Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09893-y

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Multilevel random-effects meta-analysis of cross-sectional studies (k = 12, N = 42 effect sizes) synthesizing associations between positive-activating emotions and political learning, finding "an overall positive association between emotions and learning (r = 0.13".

> "the model based on positive-activating emotions (e.g., enthusiasm, hope, curiosity) revealed an overall positive association between emotions and learning (r = 0.13, 95% CI [0.06, 0.19])"

### Elisabeth Graf 2024 (2)

Elisabeth Graf, Johanna L. Donath, Elouise Botes, Martin Voracek, Thomas Goetz. (2024). The Associations Between Discrete Emotions and Political Learning: A Cross-Disciplinary Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09893-y

`q3 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Multilevel random-effects meta-analysis of experimental studies (k = 6, N = 16 effect sizes) on positive-activating emotions and political learning; the model "did not reveal a significant overall correlation" and correlations mainly represented null-effects.

> "Our model, including effect sizes from positive-activating emotions and experimental designs, did not reveal a significant overall correlation (r = 0.06, 95% CI [− 0.12, 0.23]"
