Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Consistency condition was significant in all GLMM models, with higher consistency judgement accuracy for consistent than inconsistent texts
  • First-pass reading measures at the target word showed no main effect on inconsistency identification, and lookbacks from the target word were associated with lower accuracy in consistent texts
  • Overall reading time did not differ between consistent and inconsistent texts, but participants judged consistent texts more accurately (d = 0.84), indicating inconsistencies were more often overlooked than falsely perceived
  • Reanalysis of the inconsistent target word (longer rereading time and higher revisit probability) is associated with higher consistency judgement accuracy in inconsistent texts, but not in consistent texts
  • Longer first-pass reading time at the final word of the target sentence was negatively associated with consistency judgement accuracy
  • Students who did not report the discrepancy in peer feedback showed longer first-pass reading times — related
