Evidence¶

Kumar and Raja 2012¶

S. Praveen Kumar & B. William Dharma Raja. (2012). Remedial Instruction to Enhance Mathematical Ability of Dyscalculics. i-manager's Journal on Educational Psychology, Vol. 6 No. 1 May - July 2012. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1102291

q1 · i? · causal · r1

Narrative review by the authors, citing Ota (2008), Moomaw and Coup (2008), Michaelson and Thomas (2007), Lugo (2005), Mohankumar and Rajaguru (2001), Mishra (1991) and Nwaizu and Ifeanyi (1991); the article offers no pooled effect sizes for this assertion.

"Certain research studies reveal that the vast majority of problems experienced by mathematically disabled learners can be through appropriate, explicit and early instruction and intervention."

Related Claims¶

  • Expertise reversal effect: learners with developed fluency do not benefit from unnecessarily explicit instruction and benefit more from open problem-solving approaches — related
  • Instructional focus (number and operations vs. word-problem solving) and intervention implementer (researcher vs. school personnel) did not significantly predict effect sizes or moderate the dose–response relation — related
  • Remedial instruction significantly enhanced the mathematical ability of dyscalculic primary students in a single-group study — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
