## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Evaluations of short-series programmes such as summer schools show no change in STEM aspirations from before to after, and other short series show mixed or unrobust aspiration impacts. [→ Martin Archer 2021](#martin-archer-2021)
`q2 i?` A six-week STEM careers intervention series broadened understanding of science-related jobs but did not significantly change students' aspirations or views on science. [→ Martin Archer 2021 (2)](#martin-archer-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Martin Archer 2021

Martin Archer, Jennifer DeWitt, Carol Davenport, Olivia Keenan, Lorraine Coghill, Anna Christodoulou, Samantha Durbin, Heather Campbell, Lewis Hou. (2021). Going beyond the one-off: How can STEM engagement programmes with young people have real lasting impact? Manuscript in preparation for Research for All. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.14324/rfa.05.1.07](https://doi.org/10.14324/rfa.05.1.07)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing prior evaluations, that "many summer school programmes show no change in STEM aspirations" from before to after, and notes a critical lack of longitudinal studies in the widening access sector.

> "many summer school programmes show no change in STEM aspirations from before to after (e.g. Bhattacharyya et al., 2011; MacIver and MacIver, 2015)."

### Martin Archer 2021 (2)

Martin Archer, Jennifer DeWitt, Carol Davenport, Olivia Keenan, Lorraine Coghill, Anna Christodoulou, Samantha Durbin, Heather Campbell, Lewis Hou. (2021). Going beyond the one-off: How can STEM engagement programmes with young people have real lasting impact? Manuscript in preparation for Research for All. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.14324/rfa.05.1.07](https://doi.org/10.14324/rfa.05.1.07)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The review attributes this to L. Archer et al. (2014), a six-week careers intervention series for Year 9 students at a London girls' school evaluated with before/after surveys, observations, discussion groups and teacher interviews. No effect size is printed.

> "While the series did appear to have a positive effect on broadening students' understanding of the range of jobs that science can lead to or be useful for, it did not significantly change students' aspirations or views on science."
