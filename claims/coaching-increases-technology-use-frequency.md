# Coached teachers report increases in how often students and teachers use technology, compared to non-coached peers

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teachers who received at least eight weeks of coaching more frequently reported increases in days students used technology, number of students using it, and courses/subjects using it (printed effect sizes = .4, p < .001). [→ Mahsa Bakhshaei 2020](#mahsa-bakhshaei-2020)

## Evidence

### Mahsa Bakhshaei 2020

Mahsa Bakhshaei, Angela Hardy, Jason Ravitz, John Seylar. (2020). Instructional Coaching Holds Promise as a Method to Improve Instruction with Technology. SITE 2020 - Online, April 7-10, 2020. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/5465e54a-b290-4210-97fb-514a3aa13e21

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Year 2 post-survey of the DLP comparing coached (target) teachers with non-coached (control) teachers within participating schools; participation was voluntary, not randomly assigned. Coached teachers reported larger increases (e.g., 70% vs. 56% for days of student use), with the article printing "Effect sizes = .4, p < .001" without naming a standardized metric, so no impact magnitude is coded.

> "Over the year, teachers who received coaching, compared to their peers who didn't participate, more frequently reported an increase in the number of days students used technology (70% vs. 56%), the number of students who used technology (70% vs. 58%) and the number of courses/subjects in which they used technology (62% vs. 48%) (Effect sizes = .4, p < .001)."
