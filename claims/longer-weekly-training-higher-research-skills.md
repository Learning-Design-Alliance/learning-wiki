## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among trained undergraduates, the long-training group (≥10 weekly hours) scored higher on literature reading, data analysis, and paper writing than the short-training group, with no significant differences in English score or GPA. [→ Jiang Juan 2025](#jiang-juan-2025)

## Evidence

### Jiang Juan 2025

Jiang Juan, Deng Pengbo, Li Ruijia, Yang Qian, Hu Chengping, Li Yuanyuan. (2025). The effect of research training on research skills, academic performance, and stress level among medical undergraduates in China: a cross-sectional survey. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07809-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Within-group comparison of the 95 trained undergraduates, split by weekly training hours (<10 vs ≥10). The "long-training group demonstrated better performance" on all three research-skill measures; English test (P = 0.876) and GPA (P = 0.424) showed no significant differences.

> "the long-training group demonstrated better performance in self-evaluation scores on scientific literature reading ability (7.0 [6.0–8.0] vs. 6.0 [5.0–7.0], P = 0.022), research data analysis ability (6.0 [5.8-7.0] vs. 5.0 [3.0–6.0], P = 0.012), and scientific paper writing ability (7.0 [6.0-8.3] vs. 5.0 [4.0–7.0], P = 0.001)."
