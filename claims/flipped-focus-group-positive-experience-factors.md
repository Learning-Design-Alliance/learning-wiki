## Evidence

### Galway Lindsay P 2014

Galway Lindsay P, Corbett Kitty K, Takaro Timothy K, Tairyan Kate, Frank Erica. (2014). A novel integration of online and flipped classroom instructional models in public health higher education. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-14-181

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Qualitative thematic analysis of one 40-minute focus group with all 11 students, conducted after the final in-class session. A second theme reported changes in attitudes, with students noting greater interest in environmental health.

> "High amounts of interaction with other students and the instructor, small class size, the use of active in-class learning activities and reflective responses, and engagement with content online before attending in-class sessions were all positively-cited aspects of the course and its design."
