# Adaptable task progression is descriptively more beneficial than fixed task progression in simulation-based learning

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Simulations with adaptable task progression (g = 1.10) showed descriptively higher effects than those with fixed, non-personalized task progression (g = 0.87), though the difference was not statistically significant. [→ Chernikova 2025](#chernikova-2025)

## Evidence

### Chernikova 2025

Chernikova, O., Sommerhoff, D., Stadler, M., Holzberger, D., Nickl, M., Seidel, T., Kasneci, E., Küchemann, S., Kuhn, J., Fischer, F., & Heitzmann, N. (2025). Personalization through adaptivity or adaptability? A meta-analysis on simulation-based learning in higher education. Educational Research Review 46 (2025) 100662. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2024.100662

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random-effects meta-analysis (Hedges g) of studies coded for task progression; Table 1 reports adaptable task progression g = 1.10 (N = 34) versus not personalized g = 0.87 (N = 109); adaptive task progression was not identified in any study (N = 0).

> "descriptive values indicate that adaptable scaffolding seems to be in general slightly less beneficial than adaptive scaffolding, but adaptable task progression increases effects of simulation for learners"
