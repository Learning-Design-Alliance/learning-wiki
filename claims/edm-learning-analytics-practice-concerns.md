# Learning analytics and EDM researchers raise practical concerns: impoverished ontologies, easy-to-collect data bias, interdisciplinary demands, and negative consequences for students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` EDM studies of self-regulated learning often assume a flat ontology of simple behaviors, limiting explanatory power across contexts; big data and conceptually rich data are not identical. [→ Iwatani 2018](#iwatani-2018)
`q2 i?` Dashboards showing class-mean standing were associated with underperformance by high-achieving students, illustrating unintended negative consequences. [→ Iwatani 2018 (2)](#iwatani-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Iwatani 2018

Iwatani, Emi. (2018). Overview of Data Mining's Potential Benefits and Limitations in Education Research. Practical Assessment, Research & Evaluation, 23(15). http://pareonline.net/getvn.asp?v=23&n=15

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Reimann et al.'s (2014) caution that "big data" and "more data" are not identical with conceptually rich data, recommending enriched ontologies and multi-source data analyzed via methods like system dynamics and agent-based modeling.

> "Reimann et al. (2014) noticed that many studies tend to assume a "flat ontology" that relies heavily and makes assumptions about simple user behaviors such as clicking, logging in, moving their eyes, typing, and uttering."

### Iwatani 2018 (2)

Iwatani, Emi. (2018). Overview of Data Mining's Potential Benefits and Limitations in Education Research. Practical Assessment, Research & Evaluation, 23(15). http://pareonline.net/getvn.asp?v=23&n=15

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review cites this finding and related worries (Gasevic et al., 2015) that constant reminders of poor performance may cause distress or narrow teaching to superficial metrics; it urges quantitative researchers to minimize negative implications.

> "Corrin and de Barba (2014) found that high-achieving students tended to underperform in a class, when dashboards informed them of their standing relative to the class mean."
