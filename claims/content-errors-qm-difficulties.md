## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Content errors occur when students are in the frame intended by the problem statement but fail to activate the correct resources; three difficulties in transition-requiring problems and nine in simpler problems were classified as content errors only. [→ Bahar Modir 2019](#bahar-modir-2019)
`q2 i?` On an expectation-value problem, 67% of graduate students answered part (2) correctly but only 39% answered part (3) correctly, illustrating students in the right frame missing key content. [→ Bahar Modir 2019 (2)](#bahar-modir-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Bahar Modir 2019

Bahar Modir, John D. Thompson, and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2019). Framing difficulties in quantum mechanics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020146](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020146)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Definitional statement opening the content error section; the authors illustrate with interview data from first-year graduate students (Singh's study) where a student needed the interviewer to supply the correct TISE before completing the solution.

> "A third kind of error occurs when students are in the appropriate frame intended by the problem statement, but have not activated enough of (or the correct) resources to complete the problem."

### Bahar Modir 2019 (2)

Bahar Modir, John D. Thompson, and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2019). Framing difficulties in quantum mechanics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020146](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020146)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview and survey data from Singh's study of first-year graduate students on an expectation-value problem; the problem statement starts students in an algorithmic physics frame by directing them to calculate, and students instead explicitly calculated integrals.

> "Although 67% of the students were able to answer part (2) correctly, only 39% were able to answer part (3) correctly, and many were not able to use the information to apply in part (3)."

### Bahar Modir 2019 (3)

Bahar Modir, John D. Thompson, and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2019). Framing difficulties in quantum mechanics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020146](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020146)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Written responses on the Stern-Gerlach question, whose frame the authors code as conceptual physics; a typical incorrect response connected the field-gradient direction to a single spin outcome rather than reasoning about the prepared superposition of spin up and spin down.

> "Only 41% of the students (N= 202) answered correctly, and the rest of the students predicted that there will be only a single spot on the screen."
