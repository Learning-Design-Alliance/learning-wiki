## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Overall ATS attitude scores increased significantly from pretest to posttest in the Spring 2014 course, by 0.32 pretest SDs. [→ Masel J. 2015](#masel-j-2015)

## Evidence

### Masel J. 2015

Masel J., Humphrey P. T., Blackburn B., Levine J. A. (2015). Evidence-Based Medicine as a Tool for Undergraduate Probability and Statistics Education. CBE—Life Sciences Education 14(4). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-04-0079

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVA on summed positive ATS scores from 29 Likert items, precourse versus postcourse, in the same 40-student course; overall attitude mean rose from 63.71 to 69.50 (p = 0.002). The article reports "the ATS increased by 0.32 pretest SDs"; no d, g, r or η² is printed for this gain, so impact is uncoded.

> "We were delighted that, from pretest to posttest, QRQ increased by 0.63 pretest SDs (p < 0.001; Table 4), and the ATS increased by 0.32 pretest SDs (p = 0.002; Table 5)."
