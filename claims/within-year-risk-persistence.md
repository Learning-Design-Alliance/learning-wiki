# About 60 percent of students significantly below benchmark at BOY remained so at EOY, and improving to benchmark by MOY predicted meeting benchmark at EOY

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Mariann Lemke 2024

Mariann Lemke, Dan Murphy, Aaron Soo Ping Chow, and Angela Acuña. (2024). Early Literacy Performance in Massachusetts: Results of Ongoing Analysis of Literacy Screening Assessments. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED663754

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Longitudinal within-year analysis tracking students' benchmark status from BOY through MOY to EOY in 2022/23, with differences by grade noted in the report.

> "Overall, about 60 percent of students who started the year significantly below benchmark were still significantly below benchmark at EOY. About 30 percent of students who were significantly below benchmark at BOY met benchmark by EOY, and another 16 percent improved to the next performance level"

### Mariann Lemke 2024 (2)

Mariann Lemke, Dan Murphy, Aaron Soo Ping Chow, and Angela Acuña. (2024). Early Literacy Performance in Massachusetts: Results of Ongoing Analysis of Literacy Screening Assessments. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED663754

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The report's student progress analysis compares EOY outcomes for students at benchmark at MOY versus those still classified as at risk at MOY, finding a significant difference in later benchmark attainment.

> "Students who improved to the meeting benchmark level MOY were significantly more likely to meet benchmark at EOY than those who were still classified as at risk at MOY."
