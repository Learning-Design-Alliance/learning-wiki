Related Claims¶

  • A declarative task placed early in training is associated with a small, transient reduction in post-rest gain (Experiment 3), with only weak statistical support — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • An intervening declarative cued-recall task placed late in finger-tapping training does not reduce the post-rest performance gain (Experiment 1) — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Trial-level reactive inhibition (progressively larger sequence RTs within trials) emerges during training and dissipates across rest breaks in the FTT — related
  • Even a substantially increased declarative-load intervening task placed late in training does not impair the post-rest gain (Experiment 2) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • No significant association exists between intervening-task performance and post-rest motor gain in any experiment — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • No retrograde interference: forgetting after months is comparable for early- and late-trained tasks (Experiment 1B) — related
