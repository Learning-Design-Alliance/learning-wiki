q2 · i2 · review · r2

The review narrates meta-analytic findings on EF–achievement associations, citing Jacob and Parkinson (2015) and Robson et al. (2020) with "overall average correlations" of r = .31 (reading), r = .30 (math), and r = .37 (academic performance).

"Meta-analyses consistently report positive associations between EFs and aca - demic outcomes (Jacob & Parkinson, 2015: overall average correlations between EFs and reading: r = .31 and EFs and math: r = .30; Robson et al., 2020: overall average correlations between EFs and academic performance: r = .37)."

Related Claims¶

  • Executive functions and working memory correlate moderately to strongly with mathematics achievement (r = .28 to .40), from preschool through elementary school — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Meta-analytic findings show small-to-medium relations between EF and academic performance, with cognitive flexibility showing the strongest effects — related
  • Executive functions show significant associations with reading outcomes in elementary school children (meta-analysis of 60 studies, 275 effect sizes) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Math achievement correlates more strongly with EF components than EF components correlate with each other in early childhood — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • EF explains substantial achievement variance (about 20–60% in school children) while SRL shows positive but low-effect-size relations to achievement — related
  • All types of working memory functions show substantial relationships with IQ, with average correlations greater than .50 — related
