# Awareness of standard precautions increased significantly in both education groups, with no statistically significant difference between the simulation and peer role-play methods.

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Awareness of standard precautions rose significantly within both groups (SP: z = −4.178, p < 0.001; PRP: z = −4.628, p < 0.001) but did not differ significantly between groups (z = −1.304, p = 0.192). [→ Kim Eunyoung 2020](#kim-eunyoung-2020)

## Evidence

### Kim Eunyoung 2020

Kim Eunyoung, Kim Sang Suk, Kim Sunghee. (2020). Effects of Infection Control Education for Nursing Students Using Standardized Patients vs. Peer Role-Play. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18010107

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Measured with the 36-item, 8-dimension Awareness of Standard Precaution questionnaire (Cronbach's α = 0.95), both groups' awareness scores "statistically increased" from pretest to posttest. The between-group comparison (z = −1.304, p = 0.192) was not significant; no effect size was printed.

> "we found that their scores statistically increased from 4.67 ± 0.30 (pretest) to 4.90 ± 0.19 (posttest) in the simulation using SP group (z = −4.178, p < 0.001), and from 4.57 ± 0.31 (pretest) to 4.87 ± 0.23 (posttest) in the PRP group (z = −4.628, p < 0.001)."
