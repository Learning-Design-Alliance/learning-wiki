# Displaced students in receiving schools with high student-teacher trust and teacher personal attention showed larger reading and math gains than those in low-support schools

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students in schools with low teacher personal attention were two months behind expected in reading, while those in the highest-quartile schools learned somewhat more than expected; low-trust schools showed students more than three months behind in reading. [→ Marisa de la Torre 2009](#marisa-de-la-torre-2009)

## Evidence

### Marisa de la Torre 2009

Marisa de la Torre, Julia Gwynne. (2009). When Schools Close: Effects on Displaced Students in Chicago Public Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago Urban Education Institute. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/when-schools-close-effects-displaced-students-chicago-public-schools

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Moderator analysis using CCSR survey measures of student-teacher interaction (Figure 10): teacher personal attention reading ranged -2.11 (Q1) to +0.90 (Q4), student-teacher trust reading -3.23 (Q1) to +0.64 (Q4); effects significant for reading (p < 0.01) and math (p < 0.05 or 0.01), more pronounced for reading than math.

> "students who enrolled in schools where there was a high level of trust between students and teachers had achievement levels of over half a month higher in reading and one month in math than expected. Students who were in schools with a low level of trust between students and teachers were more than three months behind their expected level in reading"
