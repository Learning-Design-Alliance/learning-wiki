## Evidence

### Hausknecht 2005

Hausknecht, J. P., Halpert, J. A., Di Paolo, N. T., & Moriarty Gerrard, M. O. (2005). Retesting in Selection: A Meta-Analysis of Practice Effects for Tests of Cognitive Ability. Journal of Applied Psychology (in press). https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/apl

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

WLS regression moderator analysis; 53 of 107 samples received some formal instruction, median 60 days contact time. No subgroup analysis was performed given the null result and the variable's continuous nature.

> "The WLS regression results in Table 3 show that the coefficient for formal instruction was not statistically significant."

### Hausknecht 2005 (2)

Hausknecht, J. P., Halpert, J. A., Di Paolo, N. T., & Moriarty Gerrard, M. O. (2005). Retesting in Selection: A Meta-Analysis of Practice Effects for Tests of Cognitive Ability. Journal of Applied Psychology (in press). https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/apl

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Subgroup analysis of study context (k = 88 research, k = 19 operational). The article states the 95% confidence interval overlapped and Hypothesis 4 was not supported.

> "results revealed an overall effect size for studies conducted in research contexts (δ = .24) that was similar to that found for studies conducted in operational contexts (δ = .29)."

### Hausknecht 2005 (3)

Hausknecht, J. P., Halpert, J. A., Di Paolo, N. T., & Moriarty Gerrard, M. O. (2005). Retesting in Selection: A Meta-Analysis of Practice Effects for Tests of Cognitive Ability. Journal of Applied Psychology (in press). https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/apl

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Subgroup analysis by cognitive ability dimension. The article reports the ordering of δ values but concludes the estimates are not statistically different; Hypothesis 6 was not supported.

> "Practice effects were largest for tests of analytical ability (δ = .32), followed by quantitative ability (δ = .30), and verbal ability (δ = .19). Although these results are in the expected direction, the overlapping confidence intervals indicate that these estimates are not statistically different from one another."
