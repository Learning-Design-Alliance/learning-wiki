## Evidence

### Josephine C. Meyer 2022

Josephine C. Meyer, Gina Passante, Steven J. Pollock, and Bethany R. Wilcox. (2022). The interdisciplinary quantum information classroom: Themes from a survey of quantum information science instructors. arXiv:2202.05944. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2202.05944](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2202.05944)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Authors' summary finding from thematic analysis of the instructor survey and six follow-up interviews; interview quotes (e.g., Carl's 'I didn't want to dumb it down') illustrate the accessibility-versus-rigor trade-off.

> "each course is crafted by its instructor to tell a different story about QIS and to uniquely balance goals such as accessibility and academic rigor, such that no canonical introduction to QIS course emerges from our dataset."

### Josephine C. Meyer 2022 (2)

Josephine C. Meyer, Gina Passante, Steven J. Pollock, and Bethany R. Wilcox. (2022). The interdisciplinary quantum information classroom: Themes from a survey of quantum information science instructors. arXiv:2202.05944. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2202.05944](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2202.05944)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Cross-case finding from the six faculty interviews: Ben, Carl, David, and Franz all named interpreting and executing a quantum circuit diagram as an essential goal, while the importance of actual quantum programming ranged from essential (Ben, Edwin) to irrelevant (Carl).

> "Interestingly, none of the instructors have as a goal for stu- dents the ability to develop their own quantum algorithms."
