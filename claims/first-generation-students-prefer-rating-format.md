# First-generation university students, but not non-first-generation students, prefer rating formats over pick-one formats

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small
> **Currency** · newest study 2018 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` First-generation university status interacted with response format on process favourability, face validity and applicant differentiation; first-generation students favoured rating formats (d = 0.45, 0.51, 0.42) while non-first-generation students judged the formats similarly. [→ De Leng Wendy E. 2018](#de-leng-wendy-e-2018)

## Evidence

### De Leng Wendy E. 2018

De Leng Wendy E., Stegers-Jager Karen M., Born Marise Ph., Themmen Axel P. N. (2018). Influence of response instructions and response format on applicant perceptions of a situational judgement test for medical school selection. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1390-0

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Interaction ANOVAs in the N = 372 survey: first-generation status × response format on process favourability (F(1, 341) = 5.23, p = .023, η2 = .02), face validity (F(1, 338) = 9.80, p = .002, η2 = .03) and applicant differentiation (F(1, 340) = 4.25, p = .040, η2 = .01). Non-first-generation students showed d = 0.05 to −0.15.

> "First-generation university students judged an SJT with a rating format more favourably than an SJT with a pick-one format on process favourability (d = 0.45), the face validity item (d = 0.51) and the applicant differentiation item (d = 0.42)."
