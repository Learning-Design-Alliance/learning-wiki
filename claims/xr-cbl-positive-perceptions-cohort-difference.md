## Evidence

### Aytaç Güneş 2026

Aytaç Güneş, Kalehuawehe Kalāwena, Pagat Shawnea, Oktay Cemil, Rettenmeier Christoph, Lee U-Young, Romine Rebecca, Lozanoff Scott. (2026). Extended reality and web-based imaging in case-based anatomy education: a two-year study of knowledge retention on anomalies and variations. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09470-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Likert-scale feedback survey (5-point, agree/neutral/disagree groupings, Fig. 6) administered to both cohorts (Cohort 2021: n = 61; Cohort 2022: n = 67) in the same quasi-experimental study. The article reports the cohort difference in satisfaction without test statistics or effect sizes.

> "Students in both cohorts responded positively, noting that the cases were well-organized and clearly emphasized the significance of normal and variant anatomy. Although the structure, content, and delivery of the CBL modules remained consistent across both cohorts, Class of (CO) 2025 reported higher levels of satisfaction and perceived learning gains compared to CO 2024."
