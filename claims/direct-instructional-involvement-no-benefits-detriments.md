Sebastian, J., & Allensworth, E. (2013). How Do Secondary Principals Influence Teaching and Learning? Principal's Research Review, 8(4). https://www.nassp.org/prr

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the Chicago high school pathway analysis, principals' direct instructional involvement showed no benefits and some detriments for instruction quality, especially classroom academic demands. The article offers two possible explanations without adjudicating between them.

> "High school principals' direct involvement with instruction—over and above their work to improve parent involvement, school climate, professional community, or program quality—showed no benefits and even some detriments for the quality of instruction, particularly at the level of classroom academic demands."
