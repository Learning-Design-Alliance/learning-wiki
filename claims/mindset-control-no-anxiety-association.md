# Growth mindset and control beliefs show no significant association with examination anxiety, contrary to theoretical expectations

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Examination anxiety shows no significant correlation with growth mindset or with control beliefs, and no significant correlation with learning goals. [→ Ruiz-Martín Héctor 2024](#ruiz-martin-hector-2024)

## Evidence

### Ruiz-Martín Héctor 2024

Ruiz-Martín Héctor, Blanco Fernando, Ferrero Marta. (2024). Which learning techniques supported by cognitive research do students use at secondary school? Prevalence and associations with students’ beliefs and achievement. Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-024-00567-5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis among belief scales (n = 2,414, Bonferroni-corrected). The article reports that growth mindset “did not yield a significant association with ‘examination anxiety’” and that control beliefs did not significantly correlate with examination anxiety either (r = 0.038); no effect size is printed for the null findings.

> "It is worth noting that “growth mindset” did not yield a significant association with “examination anxiety,” which is not consistent with the idea that a “growth mindset” (i.e., believing that the ability to do well in school is malleable and can be changed) helps students face school challenges"
