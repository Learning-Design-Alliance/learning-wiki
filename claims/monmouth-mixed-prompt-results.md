`q2 i?` Beginning prompt 3 to ending prompt 1 improved 23.54% (two-tailed .0222), and the identical prompt 2 improved 16.16% (two-tailed .0423), while beginning prompt 1 to ending prompt 3 declined 17.32% (two-tailed .0407). [→ DeSimone and Buzza 2013](#desimone-and-buzza-2013)

## Evidence

### DeSimone and Buzza 2013

Frank DeSimone and John Buzza. (2013). Experiential Learning: Improving The Efficacy Of An Undergraduate Business Degree. American Journal Of Business Education, 6(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1054187

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre-post prompt comparison for 67 Monmouth entrepreneurial studies students (39 Entrepreneurship, 28 Small Business Management), scored by the same two Wagner graduate assistants. The article reports this 23.54% gain as "a highly significant result within a 2% confidence" and separately reports the prompt 2 gain (16.16%, .0423) and the prompt 1 decline (17.32%, .0407).

> "The beginning-of-the-semester prompt 3 had an average mean score of 2.44. while the end of the semester prompt 1 score had an average mean score of 3.01, or a 23.54% improvement of the end of the semester scores over the beginning-of-the-semester scores, The two-tailed t-test showed a .0222 significance, or a highly significant result within a 2% confidence."
