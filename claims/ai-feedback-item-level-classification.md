## Evidence

### Marcos Abreu 2026

Marcos Abreu, Alvaro Suárez, Cecilia Stari, Arturo C. Marti. (2026). Exploring the potential of ChatGPT for feedback and evaluation in experimental physics. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/ae8110](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/ae8110)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Qualitative coding of AI feedback for 57 reports, performed independently by two experienced physics teachers with consensus resolution, across six rubric items (e.g., Objectives 87% correct, Data analysis 84%, Overall assessment 81%). No effect size is printed.

> "For each item, responses were classified into three categories: correct application, reasonable but superficial, and invalid evaluation. Table 1 summarizes the percentage distribution of these categories across rubric items."

### Marcos Abreu 2026 (2)

Marcos Abreu, Alvaro Suárez, Cecilia Stari, Arturo C. Marti. (2026). Exploring the potential of ChatGPT for feedback and evaluation in experimental physics. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/ae8110](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/ae8110)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Results-section analysis of the Overall assessment item, where correct responses were 81% and invalid 7% with 4% explicit and 3% inferred evidence limitations per Table 1. The article reports no effect size for this contrast.

> "In this item, which summarizes a global evaluation of the reports, the percentage of responses classified as correct was the lowest among all rubric items. In line with this, responses associated with retrieval limitations were the highest."
