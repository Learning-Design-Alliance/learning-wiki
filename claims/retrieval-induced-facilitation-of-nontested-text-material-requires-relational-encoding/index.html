Evidence¶

Karpicke 2017¶

Karpicke, J. D. (2017). Retrieval-Based Learning: A Decade of Progress. Learning and Memory: A Comprehensive Reference, 2nd edition, Volume 2. https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-809324-5.21055-9

q2 · i? · review · r3

The chapter reports Chan et al. (2006), whose initial questions covered only some portions of texts: "practicing retrieval enhanced performance on both the tested and nontested material".

"On a later criterial test, practicing retrieval enhanced performance on both the tested and nontested material, relative to control conditions that did not practice retrieval."

Karpicke 2017 (2)¶

Karpicke, J. D. (2017). Retrieval-Based Learning: A Decade of Progress. Learning and Memory: A Comprehensive Reference, 2nd edition, Volume 2. https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-809324-5.21055-9

q2 · i? · review · r3

The chapter reports Chan (2010), showing that "these transfer effects persist after delays of 1 day and 1 week".

"Chan et al. called this phenomenon retrieval-induced facilitation (see also Little et al., 2011 ). Importantly, Chan (2010) showed that these transfer effects persist after delays of 1 day and 1 week."

Karpicke 2017 (3)¶

Karpicke, J. D. (2017). Retrieval-Based Learning: A Decade of Progress. Learning and Memory: A Comprehensive Reference, 2nd edition, Volume 2. https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-809324-5.21055-9

q2 · i? · review · r3

The chapter reports Chan (2009), where randomizing sentence order disrupted relational encoding: "there was no longer a retrieval-induced facil- itation effect for nontested material".

"For these texts, retrieval practice enhanced subsequent retention for tested portions of the material, but there was no longer a retrieval-induced facil- itation effect for nontested material."
