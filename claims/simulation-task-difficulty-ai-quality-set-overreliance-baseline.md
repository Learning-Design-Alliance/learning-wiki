# In the agent-based model, task difficulty and AI quality set the baseline level of overreliance, with difficulty the dominant global factor

## Evidence

### Ahana Biswas 2026

Ahana Biswas. (2026). Modeling AI Overreliance as a Complex Adaptive System. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19616

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Simulation sweep over task difficulty crossed with continuous AI quality (Fig. 2), averaged over the final 10 steps across seeds with 95% CIs. Overreliance rises with difficulty from "≈0.02→0.38", and at hard tasks quality moves it "from 0.38 (poor AI) to 0.16 (good AI)".

> "The phase diagram (Fig. 2) shows overreliance climbing with difficulty (≈0.02→0.38); at hard tasks AI quality moves it from 0.38 (poor AI) to 0.16 (good AI)."

### Ahana Biswas 2026 (2)

Ahana Biswas. (2026). Modeling AI Overreliance as a Complex Adaptive System. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19616

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Global Morris elementary-effects screening of behavioral overreliance across the swept parameter ranges (Fig. 6a). The article reports that "difficulty (µ ∗ = 0.29) and AI quality (0.12) lead"; these µ* values are screening measures, not effect sizes.

> "A global Morris analysis (Fig. 6a) confirms difficulty (µ ∗ = 0.29) and AI quality (0.12) lead."
