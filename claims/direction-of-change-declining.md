## Evidence

### Ying Hu 2026

Ying Hu, Yuhang Shu & Xin Zhao. (2026). Children consider changes in performance over time when reasoning about academic achievements. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00401-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Study 2 decreasing condition (declining vs. constant character, matched final performance): the final model showed a significant age effect (B = −1.76, p < 0.001), with older children increasingly favoring the constant character; adults favored the constant character on all three evaluation questions.

> "the ﬁnal model revealed a signiﬁcant age effect (B = −1.76, SE = 0.48, 95% CI [−2.71, −0.81], p < 0.001; β = −2.78); with age, children were signiﬁcantly more likely to favor the constant character than the decreasing character."

### Ying Hu 2026 (2)

Ying Hu, Yuhang Shu & Xin Zhao. (2026). Children consider changes in performance over time when reasoning about academic achievements. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00401-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Study 2 decreasing-condition inference analysis: age significantly predicted selecting the constant character as more hardworking (B = −1.33, p < 0.001) and as smarter (B = −0.71, p = 0.013), with a significant age-by-measure interaction.

> "The simple slope analysis revealed that age signiﬁcantly predicted selection of the constant character on both measures, though the age effect was stronger on the hardworking question ( B = −1.33, SE = 0.38, p < 0.001) than on the smart question (B = −0.71, SE = 0.29, p = 0.013)."
