# Students given a partial diagram (empty tube) scored significantly worse on the standing-wave quiz than students prompted to draw a diagram or given no support

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` Students in the diagram-only (DO) group performed statistically significantly worse than the prompt-only (PO) and no-support (NS) groups on the quiz problem, while PO and NS did not differ significantly. [→ Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2013](#alexandru-maries-and-chandralekha-singh-2013)

`q2 i?` Students in the diagram-only group performed significantly worse on the quiz problem than students in the prompt-only and no-support groups, with no significant difference between the latter two. [→ Chandralekha Singh and Alexandru Maries 2023](#chandralekha-singh-and-alexandru-maries-2023)

## Evidence

### Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2013

Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2013). A good diagram is valuable despite the choice of a mathematical approach to problem solving. 2013 PERC Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2013.inv.006

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quiz comparison of three intervention groups (PO N=50, DO N=39, NS N=29) in an algebra-based introductory physics course, scored with a rubric with at least 90% inter-rater reliability. The DO group averaged 6.6 versus 8.0 (PO) and 8.5 (NS); "students in DO performed statistically significantly worse than students in PO and NS".

> "T-tests on the data in Table 2 reveal that students in DO performed statistically significantly worse than students in PO and NS (p=0.007 for PO-DO comparison and p<0.001 for DO-NS comparison). The difference between PO and NS is not statistically significant."

### Chandralekha Singh and Alexandru Maries 2023

Chandralekha Singh and Alexandru Maries. (2023). Helping students become proficient problem solvers Part II: An example from waves. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2304.06188](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2304.06188)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of three recitation interventions (PO, DO, NS) on a standing-waves quiz in an algebra-based introductory course of 118 students. Scheffe-corrected pairwise p values were 0.016 (PO-DO), < 0.001 (DO-NS), and 0.414 (PO-NS); the article reports "students in DO performed significantly worse than students in the other two groups".

> "ANOVA indicates that the groups show statistically significantly different performance and pair -wise t -tests between the different groups (corrected for multiple comparisons using the Scheffe method), shown in Table 3, indicate that students in DO performed significantly worse than students in the other two groups"
