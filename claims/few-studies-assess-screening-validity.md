# Very few studies (18 of 127) provide validity data for screening tools used in tier assignment, and none test whether screening itself improves achievement

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Of 127 eligible studies, only 18 provided convergent validity data (correlations, sensitivity, specificity), and no study examined the effect of screening itself on academic outcomes. [→ Keles 2025](#keles-2025)

## Evidence

### Keles 2025

Keles, S., ten Braak, D., Hagen, Å. M., & Melby-Lervåg, M. (2025). Validity of Early-Grade Screening Tools in the Three Tier (RTI/MTSS) Model: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Literacy, Language, and Mathematics. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10077-5

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Systematic search of seven databases yielding 14,165 items; after two-stage screening with overall agreement rates of 95.3% (title/abstract) and 87.5% (full text), 127 studies were included. The review states "No study was detected on the effect of screening itself (RQ3)", and the preregistered RQ3 was dropped because eligible studies lacked outcome data.

> "While 109 of these studies provided information only on the tools used as part of tier assignment (answering RQ1), 18 of them provided some screening validity data (i.e., bivariate correlations, sensitivity, and specificity) to synthesize (answering both RQ1 and RQ2). No study was detected on the effect of screening itself (RQ3)."
