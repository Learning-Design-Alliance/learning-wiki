## Evidence

### Spencer et al. 2019

Trina D. Spencer, Douglas B. Petersen, M. Adelaida Restrepo, Marilyn Thompson, and Maria Nelly Gutierrez Arvizu. (2019). The Effect of Spanish and English Narrative Intervention on the Language Skills of Young Dual Language Learners. Topics in Early Childhood Special Education. https://doi.org/10.1177/0271121418779439

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Visual analysis of the multiple-baseline data showed considerable individual differences: some children responded quickly, others took longer; all eventually exceeded a retell score of eight, and four reached scores of 15 to 18.

> "Evidenced by the magnitude and immediacy of change, five of the children showed strong effects while three participants showed slower and more moderate improvements."

### Spencer et al. 2019 (2)

Trina D. Spencer, Douglas B. Petersen, M. Adelaida Restrepo, Marilyn Thompson, and Maria Nelly Gutierrez Arvizu. (2019). The Effect of Spanish and English Narrative Intervention on the Language Skills of Young Dual Language Learners. Topics in Early Childhood Special Education. https://doi.org/10.1177/0271121418779439

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Comparison of CELF-P-2 Spanish standard scores with intervention response: children with lower Spanish scores (e.g., Adan and Jaden) made substantial English gains while some with average scores showed moderate gains. The authors attribute this limited relationship to possible latent bias in norm-referenced tests.

> "Children’s response to intervention did not correspond to their performance on the Spanish norm-referenced language tests administered at the beginning of the study."
