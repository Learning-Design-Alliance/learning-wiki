# The knowledge test showed measurement invariance across gender and school grade, with girls and ninth-graders scoring better at small effect sizes

## Subclaims
`q2 i0` Pre- and post-test scores differed significantly between boys and girls (r = 0,17 pre; r = 0,18 post) and between seventh and ninth grades (r = 0,23 pre; r = 0,21 post), which the authors interpret as generic differences rather than test discrimination. [→ Borovnik Lesjak Vesna 2019](#borovnik-lesjak-vesna-2019)

## Evidence

### Borovnik Lesjak Vesna 2019

Borovnik Lesjak Vesna, Šorgo Andrej, Strnad Matej. (2019). Development, validation and assessment of the test on knowledge about basic life support and use of automated external defibrillator among schoolchildren. Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13049-019-0683-6

`q2 · i0` · `design · r3`

Mann-Whitney U comparisons in Study 2 (603 students stating gender; 608 with both tests). Group differences were significant (p "< 0,001") with small printed effect sizes r of 0,17 to 0,23 across gender and grade on pre- and post-test.

> "Gender	Pre-test	36,561,5	- 4,19	< 0,001	0,17	
Post-test	36,522,5	- 4301	< 0,001	0,18	
School Grade	Pre-test	33,740	− 5704	< 0,001	0,23	
Post-test	35,103	− 5178	< 0,001	0,21"

### Borovnik Lesjak Vesna 2019 (2)

Borovnik Lesjak Vesna, Šorgo Andrej, Strnad Matej. (2019). Development, validation and assessment of the test on knowledge about basic life support and use of automated external defibrillator among schoolchildren. Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13049-019-0683-6

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion of Study 2 findings: girls scored better than boys and ninth-graders better than seventh-graders, but small effect sizes suggest "differences were generic, namely due to inherent differences between genders' scholastic achievements and age".

> "Measurement invariance of the developed instrument across gender and two different grades can therefore intuitively be applied."
