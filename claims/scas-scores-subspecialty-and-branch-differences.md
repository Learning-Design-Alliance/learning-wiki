# Subspecialty research assistants scored significantly higher on the SCAS than main-specialty assistants, and basic medical sciences scored higher than surgical branches

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Subspecialty research assistants had a significantly higher median scale score (106) than main-specialty assistants (91; Mann-Whitney U, p < 0.001). [→ KOÇAK Cemal 2025](#kocak-cemal-2025)

## Evidence

### KOÇAK Cemal 2025

KOÇAK Cemal, GÜVERCİN Hande, ESEN Ecem, ÇÖL Meltem. (2025). Development study of scientific competence and activity scale in medical faculty research assistants. Turkish Journal of Medical Sciences. https://doi.org/10.55730/1300-0144.6026

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Comparison of SCAS scores between main-specialty (n = 270) and subspecialty (n = 29) research assistants using the Mann-Whitney U test; the article reports medians of 91 versus 106 with p < 0.001 (Table 5).

> "While the median score on the scale for main specialty research assistants was 91, the median score for subspecialty research assistants was 106, which was found to be statistically significantly higher."

### KOÇAK Cemal 2025 (2)

KOÇAK Cemal, GÜVERCİN Hande, ESEN Ecem, ÇÖL Meltem. (2025). Development study of scientific competence and activity scale in medical faculty research assistants. Turkish Journal of Medical Sciences. https://doi.org/10.55730/1300-0144.6026

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pairwise follow-up to a significant branch difference (Kruskal-Wallis p = 0.024) among 299 research assistants; the article reports the basic-versus-surgical contrast with medians 102 and 87.5.

> "Accordingly, the scale score of basic medical science research assistants (median: 102) was significantly higher than that of surgical medical science research assistants (median: 87.5)."
