## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Weekly self-reported stress and patient safety behavior during the clinical rotation showed no significant differences between the three groups, contrary to hypotheses about biofeedback exercises and patient missions. [→ Dankbaar Mary E. W. 2017](#dankbaar-mary-e-w-2017)

## Evidence

### Dankbaar Mary E. W. 2017

Dankbaar Mary E. W., Richters Olivier, Kalkman Cor J., Prins Gerrie, ten Cate Olle T. J., van Merrienboer Jeroen J. G., Schuit Stephanie C. E. (2017). Comparative effectiveness of a serious game and an e-module to support patient safety knowledge and awareness. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0836-5

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Weekly questionnaire study over a 10-week Internal Medicine rotation (Table 2, n = 68): reported stress means 2.2/2.4/2.1 and patient safety behavior 1.7/1.6/1.6 on a 5-point scale for control, e-module, and game groups; p = 0.2 and p = 0.7 over three groups. No effect size printed.

> "Average reported stress in the game group was lower than in the e-module group, but overall there were no significant differences between the three groups on stress or patient safety behavior and scores were low"
