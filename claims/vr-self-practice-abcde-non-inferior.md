# Individual self-practice of the ABCDE approach in an immersive and interactive VR application is non-inferior to individual self-practice with traditional equipment for first-year medical and nursing students

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` First-year medical and nursing students who self-practiced the ABCDE approach for 20 minutes in an immersive interactive VR application performed non-inferiorly on a practical simulator-manikin test compared to students practicing with traditional equipment. [→ Berg Helen 2020](#berg-helen-2020)

## Evidence

### Berg Helen 2020

Berg Helen, Steinsbekk Aslak. (2020). Is individual practice in an immersive and interactive virtual reality application non-inferior to practicing with traditional equipment in learning systematic clinical observation? A randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02030-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Non-inferiority parallel group randomized controlled trial with 289 first-year medical and nursing students randomized to individual VR (n=149) or traditional practice (n=140). Primary outcome: "all eight observations documented in the correct ABCDE order" on a practical test, with 24.8% in VR vs 27.1% in TP, absolute difference 2.3% points within the 13% non-inferiority limit.

> "A total of 37 (24.8%) participants in VR and 38 (27.1%) in TP had all eight observations documented in the correct ABCDE order. The absolute difference was 2.3% points, the one-sided 95% CI upper level was 10.8% which was within the limit of 13% points and thus demonstrate the non-inferiority of VR practice"
