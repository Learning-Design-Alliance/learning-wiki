# Focused PFA training is associated with a lower 6-year revision rate after patellofemoral arthroplasty (8% for trained trial surgeons vs 26% for nontrial surgeons)

`q2 i?` Knees operated on by PFA-trained trial surgeons had a 6-year revision rate of 8% vs 26% for knees operated on by nontrial surgeons, with an adjusted RR of 0.35 (95% CI, 0.22-0.56; P < .001). [→ Rasmussen Louise Eggers MD 2025](#rasmussen-louise-eggers-md-2025)

## Evidence

### Rasmussen Louise Eggers MD 2025

Rasmussen Louise Eggers MD, Hoffmann Adam Gorm MSc, Blanche Paul PhD, Espersen Frederik MD, Justesen Tobias Freyberg MD, Rasmussen Lasse Enkebølle MD, Hangaard Stine MD PhD, Christensen Robin PhD, Odgaard Anders MD DMSc. (2025). Surgeon Training and Revision Rates After Patellofemoral Arthroplasty. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.17825

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Population-based Danish registry cohort of 482 primary PFA procedures (2008-2015), 274 by trial surgeons and 208 by nontrial surgeons, with complete 6-year follow-up. "8% revision rate (22 of 274)" vs "26% (54 of 208)". The article prints no standardized effect size for this contrast; the crude RR appears in Table 2 as 0.31.

> "We observed an 8% revision rate (22 of 274) within 6 years of the index procedure for trial surgeon PFA procedures compared with 26% (54 of 208) for nontrial surgeon PFA procedures (Figure 2)."

### Rasmussen Louise Eggers MD 2025 (2)

Rasmussen Louise Eggers MD, Hoffmann Adam Gorm MSc, Blanche Paul PhD, Espersen Frederik MD, Justesen Tobias Freyberg MD, Rasmussen Lasse Enkebølle MD, Hangaard Stine MD PhD, Christensen Robin PhD, Odgaard Anders MD DMSc. (2025). Surgeon Training and Revision Rates After Patellofemoral Arthroplasty. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.17825

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Main analysis of the same cohort used multiple logistic regression with standardization (g-computation), adjusted for 7 preoperative confounders, with robust bootstrap standard errors. The printed adjusted "RR of 0.35 (95% CI, 0.22-0.56; P < .001)" is a relative risk, not a standardized effect size, so no impact magnitude is coded.

> "After adjusting for potential confounders, the main analysis found an RR of 0.35 (95% CI, 0.22-0.56; P < .001) (Table 2)."
