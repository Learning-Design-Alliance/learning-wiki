# ACCESS produces a small improvement in self-reported well-being relative to delayed treatment among college students with ADHD

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` The rate of change in AIM-A General Well-Being was significantly higher in the ACCESS group (d = .26). [→ Eddy et al. 2021](#eddy-et-al-2021)

## Evidence

### Eddy et al. 2021

Laura D. Eddy, Arthur D. Anastopoulos, Melissa R. Dvorsky, Paul J. Silvia, Jeffrey D. Labban & Joshua M. Langberg (2021). An RCT of a CBT Intervention for Emerging Adults with ADHD Attending College: Functional Outcomes. Journal of Clinical Child & Adolescent Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1080/15374416.2020.1867989

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Latent growth curve analysis of AIM-A General Well-Being in the RCT showed a small between-condition effect ("d = .26 [.01, .51]") favoring immediate ACCESS.

> "For AIM-A General Well Being, the immediate ACCESS (b = 6.04, SE = 1.77, p = .001) condition significantly increased in well-being over time, but the DTC (b = -.95, SE = 2.85, p = .738) condition did not. The rate of change was significantly higher in the ACCESS group, Wald (1) = 9.19, p = .002, d = .26 [.01, .51]."
