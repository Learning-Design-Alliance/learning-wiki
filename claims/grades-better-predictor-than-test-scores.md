## Subclaims
`q2 i?` High school grades are stronger and more consistent predictors of college persistence and graduation than entrance exam scores or coursetaking, suggesting grades capture noncognitive attributes beyond content mastery. [→ Farrington 2012](#farrington-2012)

## Evidence

### Farrington 2012

Farrington, C.A., Roderick, M., Allensworth, E., Nagaoka, J., Keyes, T.S., Johnson, D.W., & Beechum, N.O. (2012). Teaching adolescents to become learners. The role of noncognitive factors in shaping school performance: A critical literature review. Chicago: University of Chicago Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/teaching-adolescents-become-learners-role-noncognitive-factors-shaping-school

`q2 · i?` · `review · r?`

Review synthesis of predictive studies (Box 1.1), citing University of California data showing high school grades predicted college GPA and graduation better than SAT scores, class rank, and family background. The review attributes the pattern to grades capturing "qualities of motivation and perseverance" and study habits.

> "Likewise, high school grades are stronger and more consistent predictors of college persistence and graduation than college entrance examination scores or high school coursetaking (Geiser & Santelices, 2007; Roderick, Nagaoka, & Allensworth, 2006)."
