# Children with developmental dyscalculia are twice as likely to have high mathematics anxiety as children with typical mathematics performance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` 22% of children with DD had high MA versus 10% of children with typical mathematics performance, a significant difference. [→ Devine 2017](#devine-2017)

## Evidence

### Devine 2017

Devine, A., Hill, F., Carey, E., & Szűcs, D. (2017). Cognitive and Emotional Math Problems Largely Dissociate: Prevalence of Developmental Dyscalculia and Mathematics Anxiety. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000222

`q3 · i?`

Cross-sectional study of 1757 primary and secondary school children assessed with the mAMAS and MaLT tests. The frequency of high MA differed significantly between the DD and TM groups (χ2 = 14.42, p < 001; φ = .094), with "22% percent of students in the DD group had high MA" versus 10% of the TM group.

> "When using a threshold of high MA at or above the 90th percentile, 10% of students with typical mathematics performance had high MA; however, 22% percent of students in the DD group had high MA."
