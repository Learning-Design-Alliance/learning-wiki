# Gender equality indicators moderate gender differences among top-performing math students: tertiary enrollment ratios predict the share of female students and their achievement profiles

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r3` · `q4`

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` A rise in the tertiary enrollment ratio by 1% was associated with an increase of 0.04% in the percentage of female students in the top 5% in mathematics (b = 0.04). [→ Keller 2021](#keller-2021)
`q4 i?` Higher tertiary enrollment ratios predicted smaller math–reading profile differences for both genders and smaller science–reading differences for female top performers; women's share in senior and middle management predicted smaller female math–science profile differences. [→ Keller 2021 (2)](#keller-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Keller 2021

Keller, L., Preckel, F., Eccles, J. S., & Brunner, M. (2021). Top-Performing Math Students in 82 Countries: An Integrative Data Analysis of Gender Differences in Achievement, Achievement Profiles, and Achievement Motivation. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/edu0000685

`q4 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multivariate metaregression (mixed-effects models) with gender equality indicators as moderators of the country-level proportions. The printed coefficient was b = 0.04 for tertiary enrollment ratios, controlling for the other indicators; no standardized effect size was printed.

> "Tertiary enrollment ratios positively predicted the proportion of female students in the top 5% in mathematics (b = 0.04), indicating that a rise in the tertiary enrollment ratio by 1% was associated with an increase in the percentage of female students in the top 5% in mathematics by 0.04% (under control of the other gender equality indicators; Table 4)."

### Keller 2021 (2)

Keller, L., Preckel, F., Eccles, J. S., & Brunner, M. (2021). Top-Performing Math Students in 82 Countries: An Integrative Data Analysis of Gender Differences in Achievement, Achievement Profiles, and Achievement Motivation. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/edu0000685

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Metaregression of profile-score heterogeneity on gender equality indicators. Printed coefficients: bfemale = -0.15, bmale = -0.12 (math–reading), bfemale = -0.10 (science–reading); women's share in senior and middle management predicted female math–science scores (bfemale = -0.99). No standardized effect sizes were printed.

> "The results in Table 6 show that enrollment ratios in tertiary education were associated with female and male students' math–reading profile scores (bfemale = –0.15, bmale = –0.12) and female students' science–reading profile scores (bfemale = –0.10)."
