## Evidence

### Maynard 2015

Maynard, B. R., Brendel, K. E., Bulanda, J. J., Thompson, A. M., & Pigott, T. D. (2015). Psychosocial Interventions for School Refusal Behavior with Primary and Secondary School Students: A Campbell Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. SREE Spring 2015 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED562325

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random-effects meta-analysis of anxiety outcomes across the included psychosocial intervention studies, using standardized anxiety instruments. The review reports "a very small nonsignificant effect" (g = 0.06, 95% CI [-0.63, 0.75], p> .05).

> "The mean effect at post-test on anxiety was g = 0.06 (95% CI [-0.63, 0.75], p> .05), demonstrating a very small nonsignificant effect."

### Maynard 2018

Maynard, B. R., Heyne, D., Brendel, K. E., Bulanda, J. J., Thompson, A. M., & Pigott, T. D. (2018). Treatment for School Refusal Among Children and Adolescents: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Research on Social Work Practice, 28(1), 56-67. https://doi.org/10.1177/1049731515598619

`q3 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random-effects meta-analysis of four psychosocial treatment studies assessing anxiety with standardized instruments at posttest. The pooled effect size was g = 0.06, negligible and not significantly different from zero; heterogeneity was significant (Q = 11.13, p = .01; I2 = 73.05).

> "Results indicated that the overall mean effect of the psychoso- cial studies at posttest was not significantly different from zero (g ¼ 0.06, 95% confidence interval [CI] ¼ [/C00.63, 0.75], p ¼ .86)."
