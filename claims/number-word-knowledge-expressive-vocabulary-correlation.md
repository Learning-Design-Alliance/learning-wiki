# Number-word knowledge is strongly correlated with expressive vocabulary, independent of age, in children aged 30–60 months.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In Experiment 1, number-word knowledge related to expressive vocabulary when partialled for age, r(23) = .65, p < .01. [→ Negen 2012](#negen-2012)

## Evidence

### Negen 2012

Negen, J., & Sarnecka, B. W. (2012). Number-Concept Acquisition and General Vocabulary Development. Child Development. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2012.01815.x

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Experiment 1 tested 26 children (mean age 3;7) with the Give-N task and the Woodcock-Johnson Picture Vocabulary Test in counterbalanced order. The printed partial effect size r = .65 ("r(23) = .65, p < .01") shows vocabulary relates to knower-level beyond age; the simple correlation was r(24) = .746.

> "However, number-word knowledge was also related to expressive vocabulary when partialled for age, r(23) = .65, p < .01, meaning that the children with larger vocabularies tended to know more cardinal number-word meanings, independent of their age."
