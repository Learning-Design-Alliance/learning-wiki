## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Pharmacy students taught with CBL achieve significantly higher exam scores than students taught with LBL, with a moderate pooled effect (SMD = 0.58, 95% CI [0.39, 0.77]). [→ Chen Min 2025](#chen-min-2025)

## Evidence

### Chen Min 2025

Chen Min, Li Xinyao, Liu Qingming, Zhou Jing, Fan Xiaofang, Liu Yantao, Lin Yunzhu, Ni Jiaqi. (2025). The effectiveness of case-based learning compared with lecture-based learning in pharmacy education: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07927-9

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analysis of nine RCTs involving 1,127 pharmacy students (514 CBL, 613 LBL) reporting exam scores. Moderate heterogeneity (I2 = 51%) required a random-effect model; the pooled SMD = 0.58 (95% CI [0.39, 0.77]) favored CBL over LBL.

> "The results showed that the CBL group demonstrated significantly higher exam performance compared to the LBL group (SMD = 0.58, 95% CI [0.39, 0.77], I2 = 51%, P < 0.05)"
