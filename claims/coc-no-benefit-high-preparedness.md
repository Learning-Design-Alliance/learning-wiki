## Evidence

### Hawkins Whitney 2021

Hawkins Whitney, W.; Goddard, K.; Favero, C. (2021). A Cocurricular Program That Encourages Specific Study Skills and Habits Improves Academic Performance and Retention of First-Year Undergraduates in Introductory Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.20-06-0117

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental grade comparison across preparedness groups; the high-preparedness group showed no significant exam or final-grade benefit from full CoC attendance.

> "There was no significant benefit of attending the CoC program for all 8 weeks for students with high preparedness (Figure 1)."

### Hawkins Whitney 2021 (2)

Hawkins Whitney, W.; Goddard, K.; Favero, C. (2021). A Cocurricular Program That Encourages Specific Study Skills and Habits Improves Academic Performance and Retention of First-Year Undergraduates in Introductory Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.20-06-0117

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same study, low-preparedness group on exam 1: 15 full participants vs 40 nonparticipants; the article calls this "a trend toward a benefit" with p = 0.086, not significant.

> "For the low-preparedness group, there was a trend toward a benefit on exam 1 for the 15 students who were full participants in CoC (62.7 ± 11.17) relative to the 40 students who did not participate (55.8 ± 13.65), t(53) = 1.747, p = 0.086."
