Evidence¶

Manu Kapur 2012¶

Manu Kapur, Nikol Rummel. (2012). Productive failure in learning from generation and invention activities. Instr Sci 40:645-650. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-012-9235-4

q2 · i? · review · r2

Two studies in which small groups of undergraduate students engaged in a variance learning task based on Kapur's; the editorial reports groups may need at least one high-math-ability member.

"Results suggested that groups may need at least one member with high math ability to take advantage of the invention learning setting."

Manu Kapur 2012 (2)¶

Manu Kapur, Nikol Rummel. (2012). Productive failure in learning from generation and invention activities. Instr Sci 40:645-650. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-012-9235-4

q2 · i? · causal · r?

Group-composition comparison in the same two undergraduate variance-invention studies: mixed-ability groups generated a broader RSM range than both homogeneous group types, relating to better canonical-formula uptake.

"Groups consisting of both high and low math ability members generated a broader range of RSMs during the invention task than than all-low ability and all-high ability groups, and this related to better uptake of the canonical formula when it was presented after the invention task."
