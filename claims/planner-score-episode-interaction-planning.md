# Reported planner scores interact with episode: high planners plan more at the start and less at the end of task execution

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The effect of planner scores on online planning activities varies across episodes, with higher planners applying more planning early and less late in the writing process. [→ Tillema 2011](#tillema-2011)

## Evidence

### Tillema 2011

Tillema, M., van den Bergh, H., Rijlaarsdam, G., & Sanders, T. (2011). Relating self reports of writing behaviour and online task execution using a temporal model. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-011-9072-x

`q2 · i?`

In the multilevel regression of think-aloud protocols from 20 students, a significant planner score x episode interaction emerged for planning activities. The article reports that students one standard deviation above the average planner score were more likely to plan at the start of task execution than lower-scoring students.

> "Students with higher planner scores (+sd) are more likely to apply planning activities at the start of task execution than students with lower planner scores."
