# The two phases of the argumentative task required different organizing principles, and groups changed or failed to change accordingly

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Group 3 shifted from constructing personal lines of reasoning to direct initiation-response interaction when the second phase was initiated. [→ Overdijk 2008](#overdijk-2008)
`q2 i?` Group 2 did not arrive at a different principle for organization when entering the second phase, proceeding as before. [→ Overdijk 2008 (2)](#overdijk-2008-2)

## Evidence

### Overdijk 2008

Overdijk, M. & van Diggelen, W. (2008). Appropriation of a shared workspace: Organizing principles and their application. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-008-9038-4

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Episode analysis of group 3 shows that in the transition from the first to the second phase "the group arrived at a different shared principle": organization in the textual modality changed from expanding a sequence without participant change to pairing of initiation and response.

> "With the initiation of the second phase, the students started to interact directly with each other by means of initiation and response."

### Overdijk 2008 (2)

Overdijk, M. & van Diggelen, W. (2008). Appropriation of a shared workspace: Organizing principles and their application. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-008-9038-4

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Episode analysis of group 2 shows the strong graphical principle persisted into the second phase; near its end Patrick added links between successive contributions, reinforcing the principle and emphasizing reading direction.

> "When the group was asked to enter the second phase of the discussion they proceeded to interact as they did during the first phase."
