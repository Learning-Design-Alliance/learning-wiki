Evidence¶

Rexhäuser 2026¶

Rexhäuser, D., Radkowitsch, A., Richters, C., Glogger-Frey, I., Vogel-Blaschka, D., Meier, J., & Abele, S. (2026). Effects of specificity of self-explanation prompts for worked examples on computer-supported learning of collaborative diagnostic reasoning. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-026-09468-9

q3 · i? · causal · r2

RM-ANOVA in a pre-post experiment with 154 trainees (77 dyads) comparing worked example groups against the problem-solving group; the significant time point × condition interaction supported H1.1a, so the authors report "a significant improvement compared with the problem-solving group". No effect size printed for this finding.

"In terms of declarative knowledge of CDR strategies (H1.1a), the worked example group demonstrated a significant improvement compared with the problem-solving group (significant interaction between time point and condition)."

Related Claims¶

  • Worked examples did not improve application of CDR strategy knowledge relative to problem-solving — related
  • Worked examples improved the exchange of relevant information in the CDR process, driven by the initial-situation step — related
  • Static self-explanation prompts in the Geometry Cognitive Tutor produced greater gains on Reason and Not Enough Info test items than problem-solving practice alone — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Students who self-explain worked examples learn more effectively than those who do not, and the quality of self-explanations relates to explaining deep structure — related
