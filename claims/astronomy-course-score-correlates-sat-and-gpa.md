## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Combined SAT score correlates with course score at R=0.49, with the math component showing the clearer relation. [→ James Schombert 2010](#james-schombert-2010)
`q2 i?` Course score correlates with overall college GPA at Pearson r = 0.65, which the author reports as stronger than any known g-component correlation to college scores. [→ James Schombert 2010 (2)](#james-schombert-2010-2)

## Evidence

### James Schombert 2010

James Schombert. (2010). Introductory Astronomy as a Measure of Grade Inﬂation. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1008.4410](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1008.4410)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Observational analysis of composite SAT versus ASTR122 total score across four terms, shown as a scatter plot (Fig. 3) and density distribution (Fig. 4). The printed statistic is "R=0.49", and a density plot showed ASTR122 score rising with SAT score.

> "The correlation is evident (R=0.49) and also visible in SAT M versus course score (less obvious in SAT R versus course score which is unsurprising due to the mathematical nature of the course)"

### James Schombert 2010 (2)

James Schombert. (2010). Introductory Astronomy as a Measure of Grade Inﬂation. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1008.4410](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1008.4410)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Observational density-distribution analysis (Fig. 5) of overall GPA versus ASTR122 course score. The printed statistic is "The Pearson correlation coeﬃcient is 0.65", which the author notes is as high as any known g-component correlation to college scores.

> "Figure 5 displays this data, again as a density distribution due to the large number of data points . The Pearson correlation coeﬃcient is 0.65"
