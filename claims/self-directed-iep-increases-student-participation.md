# Self Directed IEP Increases Student Participation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

When students with disabilities lead their own Individualized Education Program (IEP) meetings — preparing materials, setting goals, and presenting to the team — they participate more actively in special education planning than students whose IEPs are run entirely by adults.

## Discussion

**Mechanism.** Self-directed IEP instruction (e.g., the published Self-Directed IEP curriculum from the Self-Determination Project at the University of Oklahoma, https://www.ou.edu/zarrow) teaches students to introduce attendees, review past goals, report progress, state new goals, and ask questions. Participation gains are plausibly mediated by the same autonomy and competence needs described in [Self-Determination Theory](../theories/self-determination-theory.md): giving students authentic control over a high-stakes meeting supports ownership of the planning process, consistent with the broader finding that [autonomy supports intrinsic motivation](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md). The component skills — introducing attendees, stating strengths and needs, articulating goals — must be explicitly taught and rehearsed, paralleling the role of [modeling and demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md) in [cognitive apprenticeship](../theories/cognitive-apprenticeship.md).

**Scope and boundary conditions.** The claim concerns *participation* (meeting involvement, goal articulation, self-advocacy behavior), not directly academic achievement. Effects should be expected to depend on explicit instruction and rehearsal — students do not spontaneously lead meetings without being taught the component skills — and on accommodations that make the meeting format accessible (see [Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md)). Participation gains may not generalize to younger students or to students with significant cognitive disabilities without substantial adaptation. Outcomes are also likely moderated by adult facilitators' willingness to cede meeting control; a student-led agenda within an otherwise adult-dominated meeting structure may produce only nominal participation.

**Measurement caution.** "Participation" is operationalized differently across studies — counts of student utterances, ratings of leadership steps performed, or self-report self-advocacy scales — so effect sizes are not directly comparable across operationalizations. Studies using observer ratings of scripted leadership steps tend to show the clearest gains, but these may partly reflect rehearsal of the checklist rather than generalized self-advocacy.

**Open questions.** Whether self-directed IEP participation produces durable post-school self-determination outcomes (employment, independent living) is a separate, longer-term question that the current evidence base does not settle.
