# Lower SES predicted slower reading and math growth, with the largest SES differences in growth occurring in Grade 1

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` SES had the largest and most consistent effect on growth slopes, with negative standardized coefficients indicating lower-SES students grew more slowly. [→ Scammacca Nancy 2020](#scammacca-nancy-2020)
`q3 i?` SES differences in growth effect sizes were largest in Grade 1 (0.26–0.41 SD in reading) and shrank to 0.05 SD or less in later grades. [→ Scammacca Nancy 2020 (2)](#scammacca-nancy-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Scammacca Nancy 2020

Scammacca Nancy, Fall Anna-Mária, Capin Philip, Roberts Greg, Swanson Elizabeth (2020). Examining Factors Affecting Reading and Math Growth and Achievement Gaps in Grades 1–5: A Cohort-Sequential Longitudinal Approach. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000400

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Conditional LGC models using free or reduced-price lunch status as the SES proxy. The negative coefficients indicated that "students of lower SES grew more slowly than higher SES students"; low SES also predicted lower intercepts in some cohorts.

> "Standardized coefficients ranged from −.12 to −.22 for the effect of SES on reading growth and −.09 to −.20 for math growth, though SES was not a significant predictor of reading slope in the Grade 3 cohort."

### Scammacca Nancy 2020 (2)

Scammacca Nancy, Fall Anna-Mária, Capin Philip, Roberts Greg, Swanson Elizabeth (2020). Examining Factors Affecting Reading and Math Growth and Achievement Gaps in Grades 1–5: A Cohort-Sequential Longitudinal Approach. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000400

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive effect sizes by SES group for within-grade growth, computed from raw data with the overall-sample standard deviation. Reading differences favored higher-SES students mainly in Grade 1; math differences favored higher-SES students in Grades 1–2 by 0.16 SD.

> "Reading growth was 0.41 SD greater across Grade 1 for students with higher SES, but differences across Grades 2–4 ranged from 0.14 to 0.10 SD and no difference was found in Grade 5."
