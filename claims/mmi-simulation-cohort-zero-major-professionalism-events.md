## Evidence

### Wildermuth Anne 2024

Wildermuth Anne, Battista Alexis, Anderson LaKesha N. (2024). Simulation in admissions interviews: applicant experiences and programmatic performance prediction. Canadian Medical Education Journal. https://doi.org/10.36834/cmej.78961

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Cohort comparison of programmatic outcomes at a midwestern PA program: a class of 66 students admitted via MMI with simulation stations was compared with two prior cohorts admitted via panel interviews. The article reports "4.8% (n = 3 of 62)" and "5.5% (n = 3 of 55)" of prior-cohort students had major professionalism events, versus none in the MMI cohort; no effect size or significance test is printed.

> "No students in this class experienced a major professionalism issue requiring adjudication by the program’s Progress and Promotions Committee in their didactic year after matriculation. This contrasts with the two prior years’ cohorts admitted using panel interviews and traditional questions in which 4.8% (n = 3 of 62) and 5.5% (n = 3 of 55) experienced a major professionalism issue"
