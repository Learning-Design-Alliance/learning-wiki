# Effort-rating item wording changes what learners reflect on: requirement wording correlates negatively and engagement wording positively with judgments of learning

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Requirement-worded effort items yield negative correlations with judgments of learning, while engagement-worded items yield positive correlations. [→ Tina Seufert 2020](#tina-seufert-2020)

## Evidence

### Tina Seufert 2020

Tina Seufert. (2020). Building Bridges Between Self-Regulation and Cognitive Load—an Invitation for a Broad and Differentiated Attempt. Educational Psychology Review, 32, 1151–1162. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09574-6

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports that in several special-issue studies, "the requirement-wording results in a negative correlation with judgments of learning" whereas "the engagement-wording results in positive correlations." No coefficients are printed in the review.

> "While the requirement-wording results in a negative correlation with judgments of learning and thus would reflect an effort as difficulty view, the engagement-wording results in positive correlations, reflecting an effort as important view."
