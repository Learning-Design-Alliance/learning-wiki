# Elementary schools strong in three or more of the five essential supports were ten times more likely to substantially improve student math and reading scores (attributed to Bryk et al., 2010)

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Elementary schools strong in three or more of the five essential supports were 10 times more likely than schools weak in most supports to substantially improve student math and reading test scores over the next few years. [→ Ehrlich 2016](#ehrlich-2016)

`q2 i?` Schools strong on clusters of the five essential supports were 10 times more likely to improve learning of reading and mathematics than schools weak in these supports. [→ Sebring P. 2014](#sebring-p-2014)

## Evidence

### Ehrlich 2016

Ehrlich, S.B., Pacchiano, D.M., Stein, A.G., & Luppescu, S. (2016). Essential organizational supports for early education: The development of a new survey tool to measure organizational conditions. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research and the Ounce of Prevention Fund. http://consortium.uchicago.edu/surveys

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The brief reports, citing Bryk et al. (2010), that responses to the K-12 5Essentials surveys "strongly predicted which schools were most and least likely to improve over time." This is prior research reported as background for the early education adaptation, not a new analysis in this brief.

> "elementary schools strong in three or more of these supports were 10 times more likely than schools weak in most supports to substantially improve student math and reading test scores over the next few years"

### Sebring P. 2014

Sebring P., & Montgomery, N. (2014). The Five Essential Supports for School Improvement: Mobilizing the Findings. Pensamiento Educativo. Revista de Investigación Educacional Latinoamericana, 51(1), 63-85. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/five-essential-supports-school-improvement-mobilizing-findings [doi:10.7764/pel.51.1.2014.7](https://doi.org/10.7764/pel.51.1.2014.7)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

The UChicago Consortium's 2010 book-length research on public elementary schools compared schools strong on clusters of the five essential supports with schools weak in these supports, reporting a ten-fold difference in likelihood of improving reading and mathematics learning. The overview reports this as the study's headline finding without printing an effect size.

> "Schools strong on clusters of these supports were 10 times more likely to improve learning of reading and mathematics, compared to schools weak in these supports."
