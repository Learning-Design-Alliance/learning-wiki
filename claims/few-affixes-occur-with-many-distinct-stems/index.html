Evidence¶

Korochkina 2025¶

Korochkina, M. & Rastle, K. (2025). Morphology in children's books, and what it means for learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00313-6

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Type-frequency analysis of the 48 most common affixes in CYP-LEX (Fig. 2; full values in Supplementary Data 1). Ten suffixes but "only four prefixes (un-, re-, in-, im-)" reach 300 distinct words in each band; type frequency rises with book target age.

"there are ten suffixes (-er, -ly, -y, -ion, -ate, -al, -ness, -able, -ic, -ity) but only four prefixes (un-, re-, in-, im-) that are encountered in at least 300 distinct words in each age band."

Korochkina 2025 (2)¶

Korochkina, M. & Rastle, K. (2025). Morphology in children's books, and what it means for learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00313-6

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

Same type-frequency analysis for prefixes: in 13+ books "the type frequency of 65% of the preﬁxes is lower than 300 words", which the authors read against prior work as suggesting children's experience with these prefixes may be too impoverished to enable learning.

"It is striking that even in the 13+ books, the type frequency of 65% of the preﬁxes is lower than 300 words, suggesting that children’s experience with these preﬁxes may be too impoverished to enable learning"
