# Publication bias analyses suggest possible small-sample bias for SCED studies, with trim-and-fill reducing the adjusted mean effect to 1.01

## Evidence

### Dahl-Leonard 2025

Dahl-Leonard, K., Hall, C., Cho, E., Capin, P., Roberts, G. J., Kehoe, K. F., Haring, C., Peacott, D., & Demchak, A. (2025). Examining the Effects of Family-Implemented Literacy Interventions for School-Aged Children: A Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-09985-3

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Publication bias analysis of the 10 SCED studies using a regular Egger's test and the trim-and-fill method. The adjusted effect after adding three imputed studies was "reduced to 1.01 (95% CI [−0.01–2.04], p = 0.05)". For group design studies, Eggers' regression found no significant intercept (p = 0.12) and the funnel plot showed no asymmetry.

> "the visual inspection of the funnel plot indicated the possibility of small sample bias, and the adjusted mean effect after including three additional studies was reduced to 1.01 (95% CI [−0.01–2.04], p = 0.05) based on the trim-and-fill method."
