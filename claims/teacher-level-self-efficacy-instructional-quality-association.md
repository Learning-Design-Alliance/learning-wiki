## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Teachers reporting higher self-efficacy in instruction report higher cognitive activation, and teachers reporting higher self-efficacy in classroom management report better classroom management (standardized β = .30 and β = .28). [→ Holzberger 2021](#holzberger-2021)

## Evidence

### Holzberger 2021

Holzberger, D., & Prestele, E. (2021). Teacher Self-Efficacy and Self-Reported Instructional Quality: A Multilevel Perspective on the Role of School Characteristics. Learning and Instruction, 76, 101513. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2021.101513

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Random intercept multilevel regression of Austrian TALIS 2018 teacher survey data (4,255 teachers, 246 schools). Congruent Level 1 associations were significant: self-efficacy in instruction predicted cognitive activation (β = .30) and self-efficacy in classroom management predicted classroom management (β = .28); incongruent paths were near zero.

> "Moreover, schools with higher collective efficacy concerning classroom management were also the schools where teachers reported better classroom management (Level 2), and teachers who reported higher self-efficacy concerning classroom management also reported better classroom management"
