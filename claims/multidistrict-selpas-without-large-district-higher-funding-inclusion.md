# Multidistrict SELPAs without a large district received higher per pupil special education funding and had higher preK and K–12 inclusion rates than those with a large district

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Multidistrict SELPAs without a large district received higher per pupil state and federal special education apportionments than those with a large district ($600 versus $522). [→ Doutre 2021](#doutre-2021)
`q2 i?` Multidistrict SELPAs without a large district placed higher proportions of preK students in regular early childhood programs than those with a large district (63 versus 50 percent). [→ Doutre 2021 (2)](#doutre-2021-2)
`q2 i?` Multidistrict SELPAs without a large district had a higher percentage of K–12 students spending 80 percent or more of the school day in general education than those with a large district (57 versus 53 percent). [→ Doutre 2021 (3)](#doutre-2021-3)

## Evidence

### Doutre 2021

Doutre, S. M., Barrett, T., & Rice, J. (2021). California's Special Education Local Plan Areas: Funding patterns, inclusion rates, and student outcomes. Regional Educational Laboratory West at WestEd. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive comparison of 24 versus 59 multidistrict SELPAs; table 3 reports per pupil apportionment means of $600 versus $522 with a printed difference statistic of 0.68, marked as meaningful. The authors attribute the gap largely to historical per pupil rates in the funding formula.

> "Per pupil state and federal special education apportionment ($)   522 (506–547) 600 (494–1,230) 0.68*"
