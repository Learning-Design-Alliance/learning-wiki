# Gender significantly predicts academic performance: boys score higher on cognitive aptitudes, girls higher on emotional aptitudes and GPA at all three timepoints, and gender explains 8.85% of T3 GPA variance

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Mann–Whitney tests show boys higher on cognitive aptitudes and girls higher on self-reported emotion regulation and GPA at all three timepoints; scenarios are the exception (p = 0.224). [→ Vera María; Cortés José A 2021](#vera-maria-cortes-jose-a-2021)
`q2 i?` In the dominance analysis, gender explains 8.85% of T3 GPA variance, ranking third overall, ranging from 0.43% in child talent development to 21.10% in marketing. [→ Vera María; Cortés José A 2021 (2)](#vera-maria-cortes-jose-a-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Vera María; Cortés José A 2021

Vera María; Cortés José A. (2021). Emotional and Cognitive Aptitudes and Successful Academic Performance: Using the ECCT. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 18(24), 13184. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph182413184

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Mann–Whitney tests on the 796-student sample (Table 2; e.g., T3 GPA z = −8.82, p = 0.000; scenarios z = −1.22, p = 0.224, the only non-significant comparison).

> "As Table 2 shows, boys have higher scores for cognitive aptitudes, whereas girls have higher scores for emotional aptitudes and academic performance in the three time periods."

### Vera María; Cortés José A 2021 (2)

Vera María; Cortés José A. (2021). Emotional and Cognitive Aptitudes and Successful Academic Performance: Using the ECCT. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 18(24), 13184. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph182413184

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Gender was included in the dominance models because it had a significant effect on the study variables; its whole-sample contribution of 8.85% ranks third after the two past-GPA measures.

> "dominance analyses show that gender explains 8.85% of the variance in T3 academic performance in the whole sample, ranging from 0.43% in child talent development to 21.10% in marketing."
