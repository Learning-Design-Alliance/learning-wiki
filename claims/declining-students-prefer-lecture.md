# Students whose final exam scores declined relative to the midterm in the 1/2-lecture class preferred face-to-face lecture over group problem solving

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Class 2 students with −10 < FR-M < 0 rated group problem solving lower than their Class 1 counterparts (GPS T-test p = 0.06091, significant at the 10% level), while lecture satisfaction did not differ (p = 0.4223). [→ Heeseung Zoe 2014](#heeseung-zoe-2014)

## Evidence

### Heeseung Zoe 2014

Heeseung Zoe, Kyujin Kwak, Yoonjoo Cho, Yun-Young Seo, Jin-suk Choi, Jin-Hyouk Im, Soogyun Beum and Hai-Woong Lee. (2014). The role of face-to-face lecturing in flipped classrooms. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1404.3566](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1404.3566)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Semester 2013 questionnaire analysis of students with −10 < FR-M < 0 (n = 34 per class), comparing satisfaction with lecture and group problem solving. The GPS difference between classes is significant only at a less conservative 10% level; the lecture difference is not (p = 0.4223).

> "However, for the group problem solving, T-test under the equal variance assumption is used and its p-value is 0.06091. If we consider a less conservative signiﬁcant level, i.e. at 10% or higher, then this test for GPS is rejected clearly."
