# Rural Washington districts show much lower AP enrollment and slower CTE DC growth than urban districts, with lower dual credit completion

## Evidence

### Kristen Hengtgen 2023

Kristen Hengtgen, Hector Biaggi. (2023). Increasing Access to Advanced Coursework. EdTrust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED641932

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of Washington State Report Card and NCES Common Core of Data comparing AP enrollment by locale. The brief reports rural AP enrollment "was much lower" than urban (9% vs 22% in 2021-22 per the figure), with slight declines in both.

> "The percentage of students enrolled in AP classes in rural districts was much lower than the percentage of students enrolled in AP classes in urban districts, and both types of districts saw slight declines from 2019 to 2022."

### Kristen Hengtgen 2023 (2)

Kristen Hengtgen, Hector Biaggi. (2023). Increasing Access to Advanced Coursework. EdTrust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED641932

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Locale analysis of CTE DC enrollment (urban 33% to 39%; rural 28% to 29%) and DC completion (urban 61% to 62%; rural 47% to 49%). The brief attributes rural disparities to districts lacking "the resources to attract and hire enough qualified instructors for DC courses."

> "Urban districts also had a higher proportion of students in CTE DC courses than rural districts in 2018-19; and urban districts experienced more growth in CTE DC enrollment than rural districts during that same period, which helped widen the gap in access between urban and rural districts."
