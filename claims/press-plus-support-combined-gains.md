# Chicago students gain most in achievement when strong academic press and strong social support occur together, and learn less when either is strong and the other weak

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2`–`r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students in high-press schools reporting high support made the greatest reading gains (1.82 GEs), far exceeding mismatched combinations. [→ Valerie E. Lee 1999](#valerie-e-lee-1999)
`q2 i?` The same combined pattern holds for math achievement and is even more pronounced. [→ Valerie E. Lee 1999 (2)](#valerie-e-lee-1999-2)

## Evidence

### Valerie E. Lee 1999

Valerie E. Lee, Julia B. Smith, Tamara E. Perry, Mark A. Smylie. (1999). Social Support, Academic Press, and Student Achievement: A View from the Middle Grades in Chicago. Chicago Annenberg Research Project, Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/social-support-academic-press-and-student-achievement-view-middle-grades-chicago

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

HLM analysis grouping schools by press level and students by support level. High-press/high-support students gained "1.82 GEs" in reading; high-support/low-press students gained 0.94 GEs and high-press/low-support students 1.05 GEs.

> "students who attend schools with high levels of academic press and who also report high levels of social support make the greatest gains in reading achievement on the ITBS—1.82 GEs (1 year, 8.2 months)."

### Valerie E. Lee 1999 (2)

Valerie E. Lee, Julia B. Smith, Tamara E. Perry, Mark A. Smylie. (1999). Social Support, Academic Press, and Student Achievement: A View from the Middle Grades in Chicago. Chicago Annenberg Research Project, Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/social-support-academic-press-and-student-achievement-view-middle-grades-chicago

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

The math version of the same combined analysis (Figure 4) shows the high-press/high-support group at 2.39 GEs versus 0.63 GEs for low-press/low-support students; the article states the relationships are "even more pronounced" for math.

> "As shown in Figure 4 (page 17), similar relationships exist for math achievement. With math, these relationships are even more pronounced."
