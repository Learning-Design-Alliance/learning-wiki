## Evidence

### Massoth C 2019

Massoth C, Röder H, Ohlenburg H, Hessler M, Zarbock A, Pöpping DM, Wenk M. (2019). High-fidelity is not superior to low-fidelity simulation but leads to overconfidence in medical students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1464-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Chi-squared comparisons of pre- and post-course belief items. Pre-course belief favored HF in both groups (84% vs 69%, p = 0.038); post-course "only 38% of students in the LF group still held that opinion" while 88% of HF students did (p < 0.001).

> "After the course, 88% of students in the HF group still considered HF simulation to be the superior learning method and thus recommended it for future training and education, but only 38% of students in the LF group still held that opinion (p < 0.001)."
