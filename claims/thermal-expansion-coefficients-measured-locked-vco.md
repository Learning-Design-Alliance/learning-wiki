`q2 i?` Heating the cavity and recording the locked oscillator frequency versus temperature yields linear thermal expansion coefficients for copper, aluminum, and super invar, with measured values systematically larger than accepted values by approximately 0.5 × 10−6 °C−1. C. E. Liekhus-Schmaltz and J. D. D. Martin. (2012). Understanding Pound-Drever-Hall locking using voltage controlled radio-frequency oscillators: An undergraduate experiment. arXiv. [doi:10.1119/1.3671074](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.3671074)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Measurement procedure in which the locked voltage controlled oscillator tracks the cavity resonance while the cavity is heated in a Styrofoam box; a linear fit of frequency versus temperature gives the coefficient via Eq. (17). The article reports the measured values are "systematically larger than the accepted values" and suggests heating the air rather than the cavity could reduce the effect.

> "The measured linear thermal expansion coeﬃcients are systematically larger than the accepted values by approx- imately 0 .5 × 10−6 ◦C−1, possibly due to inadequate in- sulation of the cavity temperature sensing element."
