# Bottom-ranked students prefer anonymous comparison on an absolute leaderboard but public comparison on a relative leaderboard

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Students' preference for public versus anonymous leaderboard display differed by leaderboard type and position: bottom-ranked students preferred anonymity under the absolute leaderboard but favoured public ranking under the relative leaderboard. [→ Bai 2021](#bai-2021)

## Evidence

### Bai 2021

Bai, Shurui, Khe Foon Hew, Michael Sailer, and Chengyuan Jia. 2021. "From top to bottom: how positions on different types of leaderboard may affect fully online student learning performance, intrinsic motivation, and course engagement." Computers & Education 173: 104297. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2021.104297.

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Thematic analysis of open-ended surveys with a 100% response rate in both quasi-experiments (24 and 26 respondents; coder agreement 82%). Under the absolute leaderboard, most bottom-ranked students "would feel more relieved if their names were hidden"; under the relative leaderboard, most bottom-ranked students liked the public comparison better.

> "However, most of the bottom-ranked students stated that they would feel more relieved if their names were hidden. Anonymous comparison may be more motivating because it saves face"
