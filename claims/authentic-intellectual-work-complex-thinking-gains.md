# Instruction organized to promote authentic intellectual work was associated with more complex student thinking and greater standardized test gains

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When teachers organized instruction to promote authentic intellectual work, students returned assignments demonstrating more complex thinking, and such assignments were associated with greater gains on standardized tests. [→ Sebring 2021](#sebring-2021)

## Evidence

### Sebring 2021

Sebring, Sall, Kheraj, Davis, Allensworth, de la Torre, & Beechum. (2021). Research and Engagement Agenda, 2021–25. UChicago Consortium on School Research. https://www.5-essentials.org/cps/5e/2018/s/610268/essentials/families/#trends?c=district&o=-change

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The agenda reports, citing Newmann, Bryk, & Nagaoka (2001), that authentic-intellectual-work instruction yielded assignments "demonstrating more complex thinking", and separately states such assignments were associated with greater standardized test gains. No effect sizes are printed.

> "In the early 2000s, Consortium studies showed that when teachers organized instruction to promote authentic intellectual work, students returned assignments demonstrating more complex thinking."
