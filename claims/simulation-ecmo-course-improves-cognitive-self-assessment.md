# A three-day high-fidelity simulation-based ECMO course significantly improved physicians' cognitive self-assessments of ECMO management (p<0.001)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Mean cognitive self-assessment across 11 aspects of ECMO management rose from below 4.0 pre-course to above 4.5 post-course on a 5-point Likert scale, a statistically significant increase by Wilcoxon signed-rank test. [→ Puslecki M 2021](#puslecki-m-2021)

## Evidence

### Puslecki M 2021

Puslecki M, Dabrowski M, Ligowski M, Zakhary B, Said AS, Ramanathan K, Cooley E, Puslecki L, Stefaniak S, Ziemak P, Kiel-Puslecka I, Dabrowska A, Klosiewicz T, Sip M, Zalewski R, Ladzinska M, Mrowczynski W, Ladzinski P, Szlanga L, Baumgart K, Kupidlowski P, Szarpak L, Jemielity M, Perek B. (2021). Comprehensive assessment of a nationwide simulation-based course for artificial life support. PLoS ONE 16(10): e0257162. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0257162

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre/post within-course comparison of 115 physicians completing ELSO cognitive self-assessment forms (11 questions, 5-point Likert) before and after the 2020 courses; the article reports a statistically significant increase with no printed effect size, so no magnitude is stated. "significantly lower before (less than 4.0) than after the courses (more than 4.5)".

> "Therefore, the mean value for all aspects taken together was significantly lower before (less than 4.0) than after the courses (more than 4.5) (p<0.001). Of note, medians of the baseline values were either 4 (in eight out of 11 aspects) or 3."
