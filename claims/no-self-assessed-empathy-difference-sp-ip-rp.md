# Self-assessed empathy (JSPE and IRI subscales) does not differ significantly between students trained with a simulated, incognito, or real patient

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Third-year medical students' self-assessed empathy on the JSPE and IRI subscales showed no statistically significant differences between the SP, IP and RP training groups. [→ Britz Vanessa 2024](#britz-vanessa-2024)

## Evidence

### Britz Vanessa 2024

Britz Vanessa, Sterz Jasmina, Koch Yannik, Schreckenbach Teresa, Stefanescu Maria-Christina, Zinßer Uwe, Verboket Rene Danilo, Sommer Katharina, Ruesseler Miriam. (2024). Impact of simulated patient-based communication training vs. real patient-based communication training on empathetic behaviour in undergraduate students – a prospective evaluation study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05801-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Prospective evaluation of 146 third-year medical students allocated to SP, IP or RP communication training; ANOVA across groups on JSPE and IRI subscales (Table 1, e.g. JSPE Perspective Taking p = 0.177). The article reports "no statistically significant differences between the groups within the items of the subscales"; no effect size printed.

> "Table 1 demonstrates the results of the self-assessment via the JSPE and IRI subscales. The higher the score, the higher was the empathetic self-assessment of the student. There were no statistically significant differences between the groups within the items of the subscales."
