Evidence¶

Mosley 2016¶

Mosley, P., Ardito, G., & Scollins, L. (2016). Robotic Cooperative Learning Promotes Student STEM Interest. American Journal of Engineering Education, 7(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1131333

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Textual analysis (relative frequencies and word densities) of sixth-graders' responses to five class-blog prompts about the robotics project. Peak relative-frequency activity occurred in the two prompts about Pace mentors; the article reports "the girls wrote with more relative frequencies about the mentors than did the boys".

"What is most interesting is that the girls wrote with more relative frequencies about the mentors than did the boys."

Mosley 2016 (2)¶

Mosley, P., Ardito, G., & Scollins, L. (2016). Robotic Cooperative Learning Promotes Student STEM Interest. American Journal of Engineering Education, 7(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1131333

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Same relative-frequency analysis of collaboration terms across blog prompts. "Group" exceeded "team" until the fourth prompt; for girls "group" stayed consistently high, while for boys "team" surpassed "group", which the authors read as different understandings of collaboration.

"For the boys, however, the relative frequency of "team" surpasses that of "group.""
