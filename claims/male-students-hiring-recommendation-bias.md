## Evidence

### Graves 2017

Graves, A.L., Hoshino-Browne, E., & Lui, K.P.H. (2017). Swimming against the Tide: Gender Bias in the Physics Classroom. Journal of Women and Minorities in Science and Engineering 23(1), 15-36. [doi:10.1615/jwomenminorscieneng.2017013584](https://doi.org/10.1615/jwomenminorscieneng.2017013584)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

2(lecturer sex) x 2(student sex) ANOVA on the hiring recommendation item in the video-lecture experiment. The interaction was "F(1, 117) = 5.44, p = .021"; male students recommended male lecturers (M = 3.69) more strongly than female lecturers (M = 2.95), significant at F(1, 117) = 8.99, p = .003.

> "these two variables interacted significantly, F(1, 117) = 5.44, p = .021. Simple main effect tests indicated that female students recommended female lecturers (M = 3.38, SD = 1.12, n = 21) and male lecturers (M = 3.14, SD = 1.11, n = 21) approximately equally"

### Graves 2017 (2)

Graves, A.L., Hoshino-Browne, E., & Lui, K.P.H. (2017). Swimming against the Tide: Gender Bias in the Physics Classroom. Journal of Women and Minorities in Science and Engineering 23(1), 15-36. [doi:10.1615/jwomenminorscieneng.2017013584](https://doi.org/10.1615/jwomenminorscieneng.2017013584)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Correlational analysis within the same experiment linking the hiring item to the mean of the other evaluation items, with a printed effect size of "r(119) = .84, p < .001"; the correlation held separately for female students, r(40) = .86, and male students, r(77) = .83.

> "the correlation was very strong in the positive direction, r(119) = .84, p < .001. Thus, students who evaluated the professor and her or his lecture more positively also recommended the professor more strongly to be hired"
