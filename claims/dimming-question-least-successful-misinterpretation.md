# The stellar-dimming conceptual question in Essay 3 was the least successful, with only 36% of responses correct and 21% of students misinterpreting the question as asking about the duration of dimming

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On the Goal IIg question about what affects stellar dimming during a transit, only 36% of responses (14 of 39) were correct, and 21% (8 of 39) interpreted the question as asking how much time the dimming is affected rather than how the intensity changes. [→ Dcruz 2014](#dcruz-2014)

## Evidence

### Dcruz 2014

Dcruz, N. L. (2014). Essays on Eclipses, Transits and Occultations as Teaching Tools in the Introductory Astronomy College Course. Revista Latino-Americana de Educação em Astronomia - RELEA, n. 17, p. 39-66. [doi:10.37156/relea/2014.17.039](https://doi.org/10.37156/relea/2014.17.039)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Analysis of 39 responses from students answering the stellar-dimming option question in Essay 3 on transiting exoplanets; the author reports "36% of the responses were correct (14 of 39)" and calls this disappointing.

> "36% of the responses  were correct (14 of 39), which is disappointing (Goal IIg). 13% of responses (5 of 39) were unclear."

### Dcruz 2014 (2)

Dcruz, N. L. (2014). Essays on Eclipses, Transits and Occultations as Teaching Tools in the Introductory Astronomy College Course. Revista Latino-Americana de Educação em Astronomia - RELEA, n. 17, p. 39-66. [doi:10.37156/relea/2014.17.039](https://doi.org/10.37156/relea/2014.17.039)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The same analysis found "21% of responses (8 of 39)" misread the dimming question as about duration, allowing correct answers for wrong reasons — an error the author notes would not have been revealed by a multiple-choice format.

> "21% of responses (8 of 39) indicated that the question was interpreted as asking for how much time the star’s dimming is affected, not how the intensity of the dimming changes, despite having a figure included in the handout to accompany this question."
