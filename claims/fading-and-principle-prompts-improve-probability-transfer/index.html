Atkinson, Renkl & Merrill 2003¶

Atkinson, R. K., Renkl, A., & Merrill, M. M. (2003). Transitioning from studying examples to solving problems: Effects of self-explanation prompts and fading worked-out steps. Journal of Educational Psychology, 95(4), 774–783. doi:10.1037/0022-0663.95.4.774

Transitioning From Studying Examples to Solving Problems: Effects of Self-Explanation Prompts and Fading Worked-Out Steps. Journal of Educational Psychology, 95(4), 774–783. Full paper, Experiments 1–2, pp. 775–781.

Experiment 1 randomized 78 university students in a 2×2 design. Tasks taught probability principles. Backward fading omitted later solution steps progressively; prompts asked learners to identify principles. Near/far transfer were study-defined probability tasks, not unrestricted real-world transfer. Experiment 2 compared prompting versus no prompting within fading, rather than repeating all four cells. Full paper checked for these contrasts; no adaptive learner-specific fading threshold or delayed safety outcome is established.
