# Students' social support is positively related to one-year reading and math achievement gains in Chicago middle grades, controlling for background and school characteristics

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students with high social support gained more in reading and math on the ITBS than students with low support, after controls. [→ Valerie E. Lee 1999](#valerie-e-lee-1999)

## Evidence

### Valerie E. Lee 1999

Valerie E. Lee, Julia B. Smith, Tamara E. Perry, Mark A. Smylie. (1999). Social Support, Academic Press, and Student Achievement: A View from the Middle Grades in Chicago. Chicago Annenberg Research Project, Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/social-support-academic-press-and-student-achievement-view-middle-grades-chicago

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

HLM analysis of 1997 Consortium survey and ITBS data from 28,318 sixth and eighth graders in 304 Chicago schools, controlling for student background and school demographics. High-support students gained "1.42 GEs" in reading and "1.67 GEs" in math versus 0.56 and 0.93 GEs for low-support students.

> "among students who experienced high levels of social support, reading achievement increased on an average of 1.42 GEs (1 year, 4.2 months). Among these students, math achievement increased on an average of 1.67 GEs (I year, 6.7 months)."
