## Evidence

### Giesbrecht Ed M. 2022

Giesbrecht Ed M., Rushton Paula W., Dubé Evemie. (2022). Wheelchair service provision education in Canadian occupational therapy programs. PLoS ONE 17(2): e0262165. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0262165

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey results on evaluation approaches (Table 3) report that "Educators were mostly using written evaluation" with steps 4 (Funding & Ordering) and 8 (Follow-up) least evaluated; other methods included practical evaluation and oral presentations.

> "Educators were mostly using written evaluation to evaluate students’ knowledge in each step with step 4 and 8 being the less evaluated."

### Giesbrecht Ed M. 2022 (2)

Giesbrecht Ed M., Rushton Paula W., Dubé Evemie. (2022). Wheelchair service provision education in Canadian occupational therapy programs. PLoS ONE 17(2): e0262165. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0262165

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Discussion of evaluation findings states that "The Funding, Fitting, and Follow-up steps were not evaluated at least half of the time", even when taught, which the authors attribute to educators needing to prioritize content within larger courses.

> "Student evaluations did not necessarily incorporate every step in the wheelchair provision process even if it was taught. The Funding, Fitting, and Follow-up steps were not evaluated at least half of the time."
