## Evidence

### Mokhtari Saba 2026

Mokhtari Saba, Mokhtari Asieh, Shariat Seyed Vahid, Nazeri Astaneh Ali, Shalbafan Mohammadreza. (2026). Innovative technologies in psychiatry education: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09573-1

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Randomized study of 144 second- and third-year medical students (72 VR, 72 control) experiencing a ~4.5-minute culturally contextualized VR simulation of psychosis. The review reports significant stigma reduction, empathy increase, and knowledge gains, each at p < 0.001; RoB 2 rated some concerns.

> "The results showed significant reductions in stigma (p < 0.001) and increases in empathy (p < 0.001), along with improvements in knowledge related to psychosis (p < 0.001) for the VR group compared to the control group. Notably, reported side effects were minor and temporary, with no lasting negative symptoms."
