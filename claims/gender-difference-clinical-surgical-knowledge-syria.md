## Evidence

### Ataya J 2026

Ataya J, Ismail A, Kousa A, Shahoud Z, Alhalbi R, Ghanem R, Ranjous Y, Zaitouna M. (2026). Assessing theoretical knowledge related to surgical and clinical skills in Syrian medical students: A cross-sectional study. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000051241

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Welch independent-samples t-test comparing male (n=237) and female (n=263) students. Males scored higher with a mean difference of "0.50 points"; the printed effect size "Hedges' g = 0.186" is below d = 0.2, hence negligible.

> "Mean total knowledge scores were also higher among males than females (9.05 [SD 2.85] vs 8.55 [SD 2.52]); the mean difference was 0.50 points (95% CI 0.02–0.97; t(473.6) = 2.06, P = .040; Hedges' g = 0.186, 95% CI 0.010–0.361)."
