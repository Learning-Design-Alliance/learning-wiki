## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the peer instruction literature, questions posed at higher cognitive levels produce greater learning gains than lower-order questions, making question cognitive level a consequential adaptation. [→ Offerdahl E. G. 2018](#offerdahl-e-g-2018)

## Evidence

### Offerdahl E. G. 2018

Offerdahl E. G., McConnell M., Boyer J. (2018). Can I Have Your Recipe? Using a Fidelity of Implementation (FOI) Framework to Identify the Key Ingredients of Formative Assessment for Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.18-02-0029

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review reports, citing Smith et al. (2009) and Knight et al. (2013), that within peer instruction, "higher-level questions lead to greater learning gains than lower-order questions." The article presents this as substantial empirical evidence about the productivity of adapting PI's question-difficulty component.

> "For this critical component of PI, there is substantial empirical evidence to predict how productive an adaptation might be in terms of student learning; higher-level questions lead to greater learning gains than lower-order questions (e.g., Smith et al., 2009; Knight et al., 2013)."
