# ChatGPT improves programming learning outcomes when integrated through structured pedagogical frameworks and teacher facilitation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Studies reviewed in the chapter report that structured ChatGPT interventions improved student performance, critical thinking, debugging, motivation, self-efficacy, and coding accuracy. [→ Grume 2026](#grume-2026)
`q2 i?` ChatGPT-4 automated grading correlated 0.91 with instructor scores while completing evaluations faster than manual grading. [→ Grume 2026 (2)](#grume-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Grume 2026

Grume, J. C., Miranda, J. P. P., De Leon, A. P., Salenga, J. L., Hernandez, H. E., Castro, M. A., Maniago, V. G. M., Canlas, J. D., & Quiambao, J. B. (2026). Pedagogical Promise and Peril of AI: A Text Mining Analysis of ChatGPT Research Discussions in Programming Education. In M. B. Garcia (Ed.), Pedagogical Innovations in Computer Science Education (pp. 305-330). IGI Global Scientific Publishing. https://doi.org/10.4018/979-8-3373-6546-6.ch010

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative synthesis of reviewed intervention studies including the R5E model (higher post-test gains in computational thinking), PyChatAI bilingual feedback, a mobile learning system, and a GPT-based code review system. The chapter concludes ChatGPT "contributes to programming education when supported by pedagogical guidance and teacher facilitation."

> "These studies show that ChatGPT contributes to programming education when supported by pedagogical guidance and teacher facilitation."

### Grume 2026 (2)

Grume, J. C., Miranda, J. P. P., De Leon, A. P., Salenga, J. L., Hernandez, H. E., Castro, M. A., Maniago, V. G. M., Canlas, J. D., & Quiambao, J. B. (2026). Pedagogical Promise and Peril of AI: A Text Mining Analysis of ChatGPT Research Discussions in Programming Education. In M. B. Garcia (Ed.), Pedagogical Innovations in Computer Science Education (pp. 305-330). IGI Global Scientific Publishing. https://doi.org/10.4018/979-8-3373-6546-6.ch010

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The chapter reports, citing Bernik et al. (2025), a comparative study of large language models in programming assignment grading, in which "ChatGPT-4 graded programming submissions with a 0.91 correlation to instructor scores." No effect size is printed for the speed advantage.

> "Bernik et al. (2025) demonstrated that ChatGPT-4 graded programming submissions with a 0.91 correlation to instructor scores while completing evaluations faster than manual grading."
