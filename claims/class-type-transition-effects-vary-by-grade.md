## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Estimated transition effects show type D classes have a high positive effect on math ability from Grade 6 to Grade 7, whereas from Grade 7 to Grade 8 type C classes contribute the most to students' math ability. [→ Francesco Bartolucci 2009](#francesco-bartolucci-2009)

## Evidence

### Francesco Bartolucci 2009

Francesco Bartolucci, Fulvia Pennoni and Giorgio Vittadini. (2009). Assessment of school performance through a multilevel latent Markov Rasch model. https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.4961 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0909.4961](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0909.4961)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Interpretation of the estimated cluster intercepts affecting transition probabilities of the latent Markov process (Table 9), from the application section of the model fitted to the Lombardy cohort; the dominant class typology differs between the two grade transitions.

> "the classes of type B contribute less on the math ability of their students than those of type C and the classes of type D have a high positive eﬀect on the math ability from Grade 6 to Grade 7. However if we consider th e estimated coeﬃcients related to the transition form Grade 7 to 8, type C classes contribute the most to students’ math ability."
