## Evidence

### Andrew J. Mason 2016

Andrew J. Mason, Charles A. Bertram. (2016). Epistemic Impact on Group Problem Solving for Different Science Majors. US-China Education Review B. Originally published 2014. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.08666](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.08666)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pre-post CLASS analysis of the same 39-student IPLS cohort, comparing cluster-level averaged individual gains by response category. The framework-oriented group showed positive gains on all clusters, while "the performance -oriented group had negative gains across all item clusters" (-0.09 to -0.32).

> "the framework -oriented group exp erienced moderately strong positive gains for the three problem -solving item clusters ( g = +0.34 for the PS -General cluster, +0.46 for PS-Confidence, and +0.16 for PS-Sophistication), as well as weak positive gains for Conceptual Understanding and Applied Conceptual Understanding clusters ( g = +0.08). In contrast, the performance -oriented group had negative gains across all item clusters, with gain values ranging from -0.09 to -0.32."
