Evidence¶

Hecht 2019¶

Hecht, C. A., Harackiewicz, J. M., Priniski, S. J., Canning, E. A., Tibbetts, Y., & Hyde, J. S. (2019). Promoting Persistence in the Biological and Medical Sciences: An Expectancy-Value Approach to Intervention. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000356

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Indirect-effects path models linked the UVI's grade effects to persistence. Significant indirect effects also emerged on biomedical major via engagement, course grade, and enrollment (z = 2.22, p = .03; interaction z = 2.36, p = .02), conditional on group.

"we found a significant indirect effect of the UVI on enrollment in the second course via engagement and course grade (z = 2.25, p = .02) for all students on average, as well as a significant indirect effect of the three-way UVI x FG x URM interaction on enrollment via engagement and course grade (z = 2.40, p = .02)."

Hecht 2019 (2)¶

Hecht, C. A., Harackiewicz, J. M., Priniski, S. J., Canning, E. A., Tibbetts, Y., & Hyde, J. S. (2019). Promoting Persistence in the Biological and Medical Sciences: An Expectancy-Value Approach to Intervention. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000356

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Because no direct UVI persistence effects mirrored the grade effects, the authors conducted a sensitivity analysis (Imai, Keele & Yamamoto, 2010) and concluded the indirect effects were fairly robust to unmeasured confounding.

"an unmeasured confounding variable would have to account for at least 13.67% of the variance in both course grade and enrollment in the second course or 19.87% of the variance in both course grade and major in order to reduce the indirect effects of the UVI via course grade to zero."
