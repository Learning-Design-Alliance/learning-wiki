## Evidence

### Schunk 1995

Schunk, D. H. (1995). Self-efficacy, motivation, and performance. Journal of Applied Sport Psychology, 7(2), 112-137. http://www.taylorandfrancis.com/

`q2 · i?`

The review reports Chi's (1993) study of male college students on a physical exertion task comparing self-efficacy theory predictions with goal theory. The review states these results suggest goal orientations and self-efficacy perceptions combine in ways not originally predicted by self-efficacy theory.

> "high task-oriented subjects report greater perceived fitness competence (i.e., self-efficacy) regardless of how well they perform, whereas high ego-oriented subjects report lower perceived competence after being told they performed poorly."
