# Gender differences in mathematics and science achievement are negligible among top-performing math students, while female students show better reading achievement

## Subclaims
`q4 i0` The mean gender gap in mathematics achievement among top-performing math students is negligible (d = 0.05) and homogeneous across countries. [→ Keller 2021](#keller-2021)
`q4 i0` The mean gender gap in science achievement among top-performing math students is negligible (d = 0.01) and homogeneous. [→ Keller 2021 (2)](#keller-2021-2)
`q4 i1` Female top-performing math students outperform male students in reading (d = -0.23), with heterogeneous effect sizes across countries. [→ Keller 2021 (3)](#keller-2021-3)

## Evidence

### Keller 2021

Keller, L., Preckel, F., Eccles, J. S., & Brunner, M. (2021). Top-Performing Math Students in 82 Countries: An Integrative Data Analysis of Gender Differences in Achievement, Achievement Profiles, and Achievement Motivation. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/edu0000685

`q4 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analytic synthesis of country-specific Cohen's d values from six PISA cycles. The weighted mean "gender gap in mathematics achievement was d = 0.05", negligible by the article's own effect-size ranges; effect sizes were homogeneous, so no moderator analysis was run.

> "The overall weighted mean effect size of the gender gap in mathematics achievement was d = 0.05 (see Table 3), representing a negligible gender difference in top-performing math students."

### Keller 2021 (2)

Keller, L., Preckel, F., Eccles, J. S., & Brunner, M. (2021). Top-Performing Math Students in 82 Countries: An Integrative Data Analysis of Gender Differences in Achievement, Achievement Profiles, and Achievement Motivation. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/edu0000685

`q4 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Same synthesis protocol applied to science achievement. The weighted mean "gender difference in science achievement was d = 0.01", with almost all country-level effect sizes negligible or small and homogeneous, so no moderator analysis was conducted.

> "The overall weighted mean effect size of the gender difference in science achievement was d = 0.01 (Table 3), showing that male and female students performed similarly in science."

### Keller 2021 (3)

Keller, L., Preckel, F., Eccles, J. S., & Brunner, M. (2021). Top-Performing Math Students in 82 Countries: An Integrative Data Analysis of Gender Differences in Achievement, Achievement Profiles, and Achievement Motivation. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/edu0000685

`q4 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analytic synthesis for reading achievement found "female students showed better reading performance than male students did" with a printed effect size d = -0.23 (small). Effects were heterogeneous, but gender equality indicators did not significantly explain the variation.

> "The overall weighted mean effect size of the gender difference in reading achievement was d = –0.23 (see Table 3), indicating that, on average, female students showed better reading performance than male students did."
