# Forecasts of intervention efficacy by scientists, platform staff, and teachers were overly optimistic by a factor of 30 or more, though rank orders correlated across forecasters

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Duckworth 2025

Duckworth, A. L., Ko, A., Milkman, K. L., et al. (2025). A national megastudy shows that email nudges to elementary school teachers boost student math achievement, particularly when personalized. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2418616122

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Forecasting study collecting ex-ante impact predictions from 15 behavioral scientists, 29 Zearn Math staff, and 100 US elementary school teachers recruited through Qualtrics, each with preregistration. The article reports actual average effect was 0.03 extra lessons, optimism "by a factor of 30 or more".

> "All forecasts of intervention efficacy collected were overly optimistic. Scientists who collaborated on intervention designs predicted that compared to the reminder-only megastudy control, the 14 interventions they were asked to evaluate would produce an average of 1.07 extra math lessons completed"

### Duckworth 2025 (2)

Duckworth, A. L., Ko, A., Milkman, K. L., et al. (2025). A national megastudy shows that email nudges to elementary school teachers boost student math achievement, particularly when personalized. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2418616122

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Correlations among the three forecasters' rank orderings of the interventions, and of each against actual efficacy [scientists r(12) = 0.28, P = 0.33; staff r(12) = 0.38, P = 0.18; teachers r(12) = 0.07, P = 0.81]. Scientists and staff accurately predicted personalized-data interventions would be more effective.

> "there was remarkable rank-order consistency in how scientists, Zearn Math staff, and classroom teachers predicted individual interventions would perform [scientists and staff: r(12) = 0.86, P < 0.001; scientists and teachers: r(12) = 0.71, P = 0.005; staff and teachers: r(12) = 0.66, P = 0.01]"
