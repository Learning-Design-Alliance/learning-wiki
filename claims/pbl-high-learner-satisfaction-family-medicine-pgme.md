## Evidence

### Clemens Megan 2026

Clemens Megan, Boparai Josheil Kaur, Dawe Russell, Avery Susan. (2026). Problem-based and related learning approaches in family medicine residency: a scoping review of four countries. Canadian Medical Education Journal. https://doi.org/10.36834/cmej.81284

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Synthesis of eight studies from the scoping review assessing learner satisfaction (Kirkpatrick Level 1). The review reports that studies "primarily reporting high satisfaction with PBL," with learners appreciating similarity to real-life cases and the clinical reasoning required. No effect size is printed.

> "Eight studies evaluated learner satisfaction, corresponding to Level 1 of the adapted Kirkpatrick's Model, with each study primarily reporting high satisfaction with PBL."

### Clemens Megan 2026 (2)

Clemens Megan, Boparai Josheil Kaur, Dawe Russell, Avery Susan. (2026). Problem-based and related learning approaches in family medicine residency: a scoping review of four countries. Canadian Medical Education Journal. https://doi.org/10.36834/cmej.81284

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports Haidet et al.'s controlled trial comparing PBL with slide-and-lecture teaching: attitude improvements occurred "irrespective of whether they attended the PBL versus lecture-style delivery" (p < 0.0001), so PBL held no satisfaction or attitude advantage. Only a p-value is printed.

> "Specifically, Haidet et al. showed that learners demonstrated significant improvements in attitudes toward sensitivity, specificity and predictive value content (p < 0.0001) irrespective of whether they attended the PBL versus lecture-style delivery."
