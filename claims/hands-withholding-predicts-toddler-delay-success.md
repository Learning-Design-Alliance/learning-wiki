# Actively withholding the hands from the reward predicts success on delay-of-gratification tasks in two- and three-year-olds

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A higher percentage of time spent actively withholding the hands (e.g., holding one hand with the other, hands under the table) predicts passing snack and gift delay tasks. [→ Mulder 2019](#mulder-2019)

## Evidence

### Mulder 2019

Mulder, H., van Ravenswaaij, H., Verhagen, J., Moerbeek, M., & Leseman, P. P. M. (2019). The process of early self-control: an observational study in two- and three-year-olds. Metacognition and Learning, 14, 239–264. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-019-09199-3

`q3 · i?`

Logistic regression (snack delay, Model 3, N = 52, χ2(2) = 18.27***) showed hands withholding predicted task success (B = −0.03, p < .05, OR = .97); the gift delay model was also significant (χ2(2) = 13.89**, OR = .97). The article states withholding behaviours "were predictive of task success".
