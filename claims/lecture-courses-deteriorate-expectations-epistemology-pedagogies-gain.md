## Evidence

### Chatzikyriakidou 2025

Chatzikyriakidou, K., Hall, K. L., Redish, E. F., & Cooke, T. J. (2025). A critical review of pre-post surveys designed to measure student epistemology in undergraduate science courses. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2511.15575](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2511.15575)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Redish et al. (1998) and follow-up studies, that "student expectations tended to deteriorate" in traditional large lecture classes, even ones with measurable conceptual gains. No effect sizes are printed for this synthesis.

> "The original MPEX paper reported that student expectations tended to deteriorate over the first semester of calculus-based introductory physics (Redish et al., 1998). Follow-up studies using the MPEX and CLASS-Phys at many other universities confirmed this result in traditional large lecture classes"

### Chatzikyriakidou 2025 (2)

Chatzikyriakidou, K., Hall, K. L., Redish, E. F., & Cooke, T. J. (2025). A critical review of pre-post surveys designed to measure student epistemology in undergraduate science courses. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2511.15575](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2511.15575)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes to cited studies (e.g., Brewe et al.; Gatch; Otero and Gray) that classes showing significant gains focused on epistemological issues, with modeling instruction and workshop-style active-engagement instruction effective. The review prints no effect sizes.

> "Typically, the classes that showed significant gains were designed to focus on epistemological issues. In particular, the method that organizes around modeling (Hestenes, 1987) was effective in large introductory physics classes"
