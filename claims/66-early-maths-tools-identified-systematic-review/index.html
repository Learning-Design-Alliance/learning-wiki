Evidence¶

Laura A. Outhwaite 2024¶

Laura A. Outhwaite, Pirjo Aunio, Jaimie Ka Yu Leung, Jo Van Herwegen. (2024). Measuring Mathematical Skills in Early Childhood: a Systematic Review of the Psychometric Properties of Early Maths Assessments and Screeners. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09950-6

q3 · i? · review · r3

Pre-registered systematic review (PRISMA protocol) searching seven scholarly databases and two grey literature sources, with backward, updated and forward citation searches; 89 studies were included in the synthesis. The review reports "41 mathematical assessments and 25 screeners were identified".

"Overall, 41 mathematical assessments and 25 screeners were identified."

Related Claims¶

  • Only four screeners demonstrated acceptable diagnostic accuracy for distinguishing children with or at-risk of mathematical learning difficulties — related
  • Content validity evidence was reported for only eight assessments and four screeners, and over half of tools were developed in WEIRD societies or in English — related
  • Only one mathematical assessment and one screener (NSS) met the acceptability threshold for predictive validity — related
  • Concurrent validity evidence is sparse: only 11 assessments and one screener aligned with other validated measurement tools — related
  • Only 15 of the identified measurement tools met common acceptability thresholds for more than two areas of psychometric evidence — related
  • Most early maths measurement tools target number and arithmetic skills, with less than half covering shape, space, and measure — related
  • Most identified early maths measurement tools are child-direct measures administered individually with a trained assessor in paper-based format — related
