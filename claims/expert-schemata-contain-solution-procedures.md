# Expert problem schemata contain solution procedures and conditions of applicability, whereas novice schemata lack explicit solution procedures

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Experts associate category labels with procedural knowledge, immediately naming alternative principles and the conditions under which they apply. [→ Chi et al. 1981](#chi-et-al-1981)
`q2 i?` Novices' production rules contain no explicit solution-procedure actions; their actions are attempts to find specific unknowns. [→ Chi et al. 1981 (2)](#chi-et-al-1981-2)

## Evidence

### Chi et al. 1981

Michelene Chi, Paul Feltovich, Robert Glaser. (1981). Categorization and Representation of Physics Problems by Experts and Novices. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog0502_2

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Study Three protocol analysis: two experts and two novices elaborated 20 category labels (3 minutes each); expert M. G.'s "inclined plane" protocol immediately mentioned Newton's laws and Conservation of Energy plus the conditions under which each applies.

> "Therefore, the expert appears to associate her principles with proced&l knowledge about their applicability."

### Chi et al. 1981 (2)

Michelene Chi, Paul Feltovich, Robert Glaser. (1981). Categorization and Representation of Physics Problems by Experts and Novices. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog0502_2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Production-rule conversion of the same Study Three protocols: novice rules' actions were attempts to find specific unknowns such as "find mass," and one novice produced rules with no explicit actions, suggesting a schema with fewer explicit procedures.

> "None of the novices' rules depicted in Table 9 contain any actions that are explicit solution procedures."
