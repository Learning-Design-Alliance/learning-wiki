## Evidence

### Keith C. Herman and Wendy M. Reinke 2016

Keith C. Herman and Wendy M. Reinke. (2016). Improving Teacher Perceptions of Parent Involvement Patterns: Findings From a Group Randomized Trial. School Psychology Quarterly. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/spq0000169

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Latent profile analyses of teacher ratings of parent contact and comfort at baseline and follow-up; a parametric bootstrapping likelihood ratio test confirmed the four-class model fit better than three classes. Roughly half the sample fell into Low Contact/Medium Comfort (54.8% and 47.3%).

> "The four class solution included three classes that we hypothesized: (1) High Con- tact/High Comfort, (2) Low Contact/Medium Comfort, and (3) Low Contact/Low Comfort."

### Keith C. Herman and Wendy M. Reinke 2016 (2)

Keith C. Herman and Wendy M. Reinke. (2016). Improving Teacher Perceptions of Parent Involvement Patterns: Findings From a Group Randomized Trial. School Psychology Quarterly. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/spq0000169

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Equality tests of means across follow-up classes using posterior probability-based multiple imputations (Table 2). The Low Contact/Low Comfort class had significantly lower academic performance across all comparisons; WJ Reading means ranged from 92.42 to 103.47.

> "Notably, the mean standard score on the math and reading subtests of the WJ was 10 points lower for students in the Low Contact/ Low Comfort compared to the Low Contact/ High Comfort class."
