## Evidence

### Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork 2009

Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork. (2009). Making Things Hard on Yourself, But in a Good Way: Creating Desirable Difficulties to Enhance Learning. Psychology and the Real World. https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/research/

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The chapter's synthesis of laboratory research on retrieval practice (citing Landauer and Bjork, 1978, and Carrier and Pashler, 1992) states that "a test or retrieval attempt, even when no corrective feedback is given" beats rereading in the long term, and that repeated testing beats repeated study at delay despite short-term advantages of restudying.

> "a test or retrieval attempt, even when no corrective feedback is given, can be considerably more effective in the long term than reading material over and over."

### Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork 2009 (2)

Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork. (2009). Making Things Hard on Yourself, But in a Good Way: Creating Desirable Difficulties to Enhance Learning. Psychology and the Real World. https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/research/

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The chapter argues that a student's ability to judge, while rereading a chapter, whether information will be recallable on an exam is severely limited, whereas attempting to answer another student's questions can reveal what has and has not been learned.

> "tests have metacognitive benefits in terms of indentifying whether information has or has not been understood and/or learned."

### Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork 2009 (3)

Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork. (2009). Making Things Hard on Yourself, But in a Good Way: Creating Desirable Difficulties to Enhance Learning. Psychology and the Real World. https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/research/

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The chapter reports, citing Kornell, Hays, and Bjork (2009), that unsuccessful retrieval attempts still enhance subsequent learning, so tests add value beyond assessment even when the learner answers incorrectly.

> "tests can potentiate the effectiveness of subsequent study opportunities even under conditions that insure learners will be incorrect on the test (Kornell, Hays, & Bjork, 2009)."
