# Reading strategy training was followed by a statistically significant increase in FLRA levels in the experiment group, while the control group's change was not significant

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The experiment group's mean FLRAS score rose from 52.27 to 63.5, a statistically significant increase in favor of the pre-test scores (t = -3.193, p = .005). [→ Çapan and Pektaş 2013](#capan-and-pektas-2013)

## Evidence

### Çapan and Pektaş 2013

Seyit Ahmet Çapan & Rumeysa Pektaş. (2013). An Empirical Analysis of the Relationship between Foreign Language Reading Anxiety and Reading Strategy Training. English Language Teaching, 6(12). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v6n12p181

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Paired samples t-test on pre/post FLRAS scores of the 20-student experiment group in a one-semester reading course; Table 1 prints pre-test X = 52.27 (S = 13.02) and post-test X = 63.5 (S = 8.29), t = -3.193, p = .005. The article reports "a statistically significant difference in favor of the pre-test scores".

> "However, the change in the mean scores of the experiment group did show a statistically significant difference in favor of the pre-test scores (See Table 1)."

### Çapan and Pektaş 2013 (2)

Seyit Ahmet Çapan & Rumeysa Pektaş. (2013). An Empirical Analysis of the Relationship between Foreign Language Reading Anxiety and Reading Strategy Training. English Language Teaching, 6(12). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v6n12p181

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Paired samples t-test on the 19-student control group following the prescribed curriculum only; Table 1 prints pre-test X = 58.89 and post-test X = 62.05, t = -1.412, p = .175. No effect size is printed, so the null is reported as a non-significant test, not equivalence.

> "the change in the pre- and post-test FLRAS scores of the control group was not statistically significant"
