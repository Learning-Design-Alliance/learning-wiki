# School membership as measured by the PSSM is positively associated with adolescent self-concept

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` In Study 1, High PSSM students scored significantly higher than Low PSSM students on SPPC self-concept domains (MANOVA Wilks lambda = .518, p < .0001), with PSSM–GSW correlation r = .65. [→ Hagborg 1994](#hagborg-1994)
`q2 i2` In Study 2 high-school students, the High PSSM group scored significantly higher on both SPPA subscales, with correlations of r = .31 (SC) and r = .38 (GSW). [→ Hagborg 1994 (2)](#hagborg-1994-2)

## Evidence

### Hagborg 1994

Winston J. Hagborg. (1994). An Exploration of School Membership among Middle- and High-School Students. Journal of Psychoeducational Assessment. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=An+Exploration+of+School+Membership+among+Middle-+and+High-School+Students

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Study 1 contrasted 50 randomly selected eighth-graders split at the PSSM median. Univariate ANOVAs were significant for every SPPC subscale except Athletic Competence; Table 1 reports PSSM correlations with GSW of r = .65 and SC of r = .53.

> "Self-concept differences between Low and High students were tested with a MANOVA and were found to be statistically significant: Wilks lambda = .518, Rao's R (6, 43) 6.67, p < .0001."

### Hagborg 1994 (2)

Winston J. Hagborg. (1994). An Exploration of School Membership among Middle- and High-School Students. Journal of Psychoeducational Assessment. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=An+Exploration+of+School+Membership+among+Middle-+and+High-School+Students

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Study 2 used independent-sample t-tests (df 118) for high-school students; Table 4 reports PSSM correlations of r = .31 with Scholastic Competence and r = .38 with Global Self-Worth, both p < .001.

> "Among the high-school students, the High group was significantly higher than the Low group on grades, homework time, and both SPPA subscales"
