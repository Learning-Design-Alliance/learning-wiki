## Evidence

### Alele Faith O. 2024

Alele Faith O., Albert Francis A., Anderson Emma, Seidu Abdul-Aziz, Mason Hannah, Heggarty Paula, Hollins Aaron, Sen Gupta Tarun, McArthur Lawrie, Hays Richard B., Malau-Aduli Bunmi S. (2024). The perceived relevance, utility and retention of basic sciences in general practice. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05750-2

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Inductive thematic analysis of interviews with 11 GP registrars (six female, seven AMGs), conducted via Microsoft Teams and analysed in NVivo 14. Registrars "perceived basic sciences as the 'backbone of clinical medicine'" and reported applying them subconsciously, with anatomy and physiology seen as most relevant.

> "The registrars perceived basic sciences as the 'backbone of clinical medicine', with the first year of medical training being the 'gateway' year that provides the foundational knowledge and the building blocks for the clinical concepts learned in later years"
