Evidence¶

Rossi 2019¶

Rossi, S., Vidal, J., Letang, M., Houdé, O., & Borst, G. (2019). Adolescents and Adults Need Inhibitory Control to Compare Fractions. Journal of Numerical Cognition, 5(3), 314-336. https://doi.org/10.5964/jnc.v5i3.197

q3 · i? · causal · r1

A two-way mixed ANOVA on probe RTs found "no effect of age, F(1, 48) = 1.12, p = .29, BF01 = 1.48, and no two-way interaction, F < 1, BF01 = 3.20". The authors note the Bayes factor suggests moderate evidence for the null (about 3 times more likely under the null).

Related Claims¶

  • No significant differences in probe error rates between test and control trials were found, suggesting the negative priming RT effect is not due to speed-accuracy trade-offs — related
  • The negative priming effect on probe RTs was statistically significant in both adolescents (Cohen's d = 1.07) and adults (Cohen's d = 0.73) — related
  • Adolescents and educated adults require more time to compare common-denominator fractions after a common-numerator prime than after a control prime (negative priming effect) — related
  • Participants required more time and committed more errors when comparing fractions with common numerators than with common denominators — related
