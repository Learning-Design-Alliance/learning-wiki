## Evidence

### Bedny 2013

Gregory Z Bedny and Steven R Harris. (2013). Safety and reliability analysis methods based on systemic-structural activity theory. Proc IMechE Part O: J Risk and Reliability 227(5). https://doi.org/10.1177/1748006X13485563

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Analytical quantitative analysis of the press operation, combining probabilistic measures from subjective expert judgments (Zarakovsky data) with objective time measurement. The article reports this combination "arguably provides a more comprehensive safety assessment"; what is assessed is the probability of potential danger points, not of accidents per se.

> "The combination of probability and complexity evaluation methods arguably provides a more comprehensive safety assessment, allowing improved quantification of the degree of danger and thus more accurately targeted risk-reduction measures."

### Bedny 2013 (2)

Gregory Z Bedny and Steven R Harris. (2013). Safety and reliability analysis methods based on systemic-structural activity theory. Proc IMechE Part O: J Risk and Reliability 227(5). https://doi.org/10.1177/1748006X13485563

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Numerical calculation for the example operation: total performance time T = 10.64 sec, executive time Tex = 5.84, and lNl = 0.6/5.84 = 0.1; the active waiting period measure is 'Naw = 3/10.64 = 0.28. Both logical conditions are memory-based, so 'Lltm = 1, and 'Lch = 1 indicates variable, randomly occurring decisions.

> "This measure demonstrates the relationship between the logical and executive components of activity, giving the fraction of logical components in the task performance associated with potentially dangerous points of the task"
