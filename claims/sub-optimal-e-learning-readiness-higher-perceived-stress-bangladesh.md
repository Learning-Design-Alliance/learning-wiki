# Sub-optimal e-learning readiness is significantly associated with higher odds of moderate and high perceived e-learning stress among Bangladeshi university students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students with sub-optimum readiness had significantly higher adjusted odds of moderate perceived e-learning stress (AOR = 2.29) than students with optimum readiness. [→ Kabir Humayun 2021](#kabir-humayun-2021)
`q2 i?` Students with sub-optimum readiness had significantly higher adjusted odds of high perceived e-learning stress (AOR = 4.25) than students with optimum readiness. [→ Kabir Humayun 2021](#kabir-humayun-2021)

## Evidence

### Kabir Humayun 2021

Kabir Humayun, Hasan Md. Kamrul, Mitra Dipak Kumar. (2021). E-learning readiness and perceived stress among the university students of Bangladesh during COVID-19: a countrywide cross-sectional study. Annals of Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1080/07853890.2021.2009908

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional multinomial logistic regression of 1145 e-learning-enrolled university students in Bangladesh, mild stress as reference, controlling for demographic and e-learning covariates. Sub-optimal readiness gave "moderate (AOR = 2.29, 95% CI: 1.39–3.78)" and "high levels (AOR = 4.25, 95% CI: 2.32–8.09)" of stress versus optimum readiness; no standardised effect size was printed.

> "The results indicated that suboptimal level of e-learning readiness was significantly associated with both moderate (AOR = 2.29, 95% CI: 1.39–3.78) and high levels (AOR = 4.25, 95% CI: 2.32–8.09) of perceived e-learning stress compared to the students with the optimum level of readiness controlling for all other covariates."
