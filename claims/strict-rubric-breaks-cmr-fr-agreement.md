## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When the CMR CUE is scored with a strict perfect/right-wrong rubric, its average falls significantly below the FR average, showing nuanced partial-credit grading is needed for format agreement. [→ Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2014](#bethany-r-wilcox-and-steven-j-pollock-2014)

## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2014

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2014). Coupled Multiple-response vs. Free-response Conceptual Assessment: An Example from Upper-division Physics. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1407.6310](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1407.6310)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary analysis within the same split-class CMR/FR comparison (N=45 CMR): rescoring the CMR version with a rubric giving full credit only for the correct method and all necessary reasoning elements produced a significantly lower average than the FR version's 54.6%.

> "Using this 'perfect' scoring rubric, the score on the CMR version falls to 42.8 ± 2.8 % ( σ = 18.5%). The difference between this score and the average on the FR version is statistically significant (Student's t-test, p < 0.01)."
