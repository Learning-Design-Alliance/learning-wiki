# About 60% of teachers repaying student loans report high or very high loan-related stress, which is correlated with overall job-related stress

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### García 2023

García, E., Wei, W., Patrick, S. K., Leung-Gagné, M., & DiNapoli, M. A., Jr. (2023). In debt: Student loan burdens among teachers. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/497.986

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

NTPS 2020–21 stress item asked of teachers owing some or all of their loans (Figure 9): "about 60% of teachers who owe some or all of their student loans reported feeling highly or very highly stressed about them."

> "about 60% of teachers who owe some or all of their student loans reported feeling highly or very highly stressed about them. Only about 14% of teachers who are repaying their loans noted that their loan-related stress levels were low or very low, whereas 26.1% reported a moderate loan-related stress level."

### García 2023 (2)

García, E., Wei, W., Patrick, S. K., Leung-Gagné, M., & DiNapoli, M. A., Jr. (2023). In debt: Student loan burdens among teachers. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/497.986

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The report describes a correlation between loan-related and job-related stress: "Larger shares of teachers who indicated high or very high levels of loan-related stress reported feeling stressed and disappointed with their jobs" than less loan-stressed teachers.

> "Larger shares of teachers who indicated high or very high levels of loan-related stress reported feeling stressed and disappointed with their jobs, compared to teachers who felt less stressed by loans."
