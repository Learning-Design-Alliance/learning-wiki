## Evidence

### Wood Caroline E. 2016

Wood Caroline E., Hardeman Wendy, Johnston Marie, Francis Jill, Abraham Charles, Michie Susan. (2016). Reporting behaviour change interventions: do the behaviour change technique taxonomy v1, and training in its use, improve the quality of intervention descriptions? Implementation Science 11:84. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-016-0448-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Study 3, 39 writers each writing before training without the taxonomy and after training with it. Three of four measures improved significantly; replicability of active ingredients did not (F(1,74) = 3.57, p = 0.06).

> "Significant findings were for clarity of active ingredients, F(1,74) = 6.51, p < 0.05, clarity of mode of delivery, F(1,74) = 5.71, p < 0.05, and replicability of mode of delivery, F(1,74) = 5.46, p < 0.05."
