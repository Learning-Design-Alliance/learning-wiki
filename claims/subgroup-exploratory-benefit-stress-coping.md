## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Male participants and participants in the highest stress quartile appeared to benefit more from the intervention on hands-on time, but these subgroup effects were not statistically significant. [→ Hunziker S 2013](#hunziker-s-2013)

## Evidence

### Hunziker S 2013

Hunziker S, Pagani S, Fasler K, Tschan F, Semmer NK, Marsch S. (2013). Impact of a stress coping strategy on perceived stress levels and performance during a simulated cardiopulmonary resuscitation: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-227X-13-8

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory linear regression analyses with interaction terms for subgroups (Figure 3); males showed a larger, non-significant coefficient than females, and the highest stress quartile showed a larger coefficient (13.08, 95% CI −6.12, 32.28) than lower quartiles (4.15). The authors state "The effect of the intervention did not reach statistical significance in any of these subgroups."

> "Male participants appeared to benefit more from the intervention compared to females (beta coefficient (95% CI) 9.05 (−2.69, 20.79) vs. 3.88 (−7.65, 15.41)."
