# Design factors moderating AI's effect on learning matter only through whether they engage the learner's evaluation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The many design considerations the field reports as separately shaping AI's learning effect bear on learning only to the degree they engage the learner's vigilance; none works around a vigilance that fails. [→ Marcus Kubsch 2026](#marcus-kubsch-2026)

## Evidence

### Marcus Kubsch 2026

Marcus Kubsch. (2026). AI as a Partner in Learning about, Doing, and Engaging with Science: Vigilance as the Key to Productive Augmentation. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16822

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument positioned against the field's growing list of design considerations (Deng et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025, as cited): the author proposes calibrated vigilance as the single governing, refutable condition beneath the list. No empirical test of the proposal is reported.

> "I argue that one condition sits beneath the list and governs the rest: whether the learner brings calibrated vigilance to the exchange. It is the base condition because it is the precondition for learning at all; the design considerations matter just to the degree that they bear on whether vigilance is exercised."
