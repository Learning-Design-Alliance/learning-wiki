Buber 2017¶

Buber, A., & Coban Unal, G. (2017). The effects of learning activities based on argumentation on conceptual understanding of 7th graders about “force and motion” unit and establishing thinking friendly classroom environment. European Journal of Educational Research, 6(3), 367-384. https://doi.org/10.12973/eu-jer.6.3.367

q1 · i? · causal · r1

Qualitative content analysis of semi-structured interviews with six experimental-group students, coded by two researchers with agreement percentages from 81 to 93 percent. Most responses were scientifically accurate, but misconceptions persisted in Work, Simple Machines and Frictional Force subjects.

"when students’ responses were analyzed, 63 percent of them was in CSU, 9 percent of them was in PSU, 9 percent of them was in TWU, 16 percent of them is was in M, 1 percent o f them was in NQD and 2 percent of them was in NI categories."

Related Claims¶

  • Argumentation-based learning activities did not produce a statistically significant difference in 7th graders' conceptual understanding of Force and Motion compared with regular instruction — related
  • Students in computer-based inquiry environments still held misconceptions about force and energy after instruction — related
  • Students reported that the virtual-lab lesson made learning fun, helped them figure out concepts themselves, and supported thinking like real scientists — related
  • Neither group showed a statistically significant pre-to-post change in Force and Motion misconceptions over the five-week unit — related
  • Middle school students in inquiry-based computer-based learning environments gain more conceptual understanding of force and energy than peers in regular inquiry-based classrooms — related
  • Natural-monument misconceptions (e.g., \"It is the name used for mausoleums\") were largely eliminated post-test, with 18 experimental and 12 control students reaching complete understanding — related
  • Students reported that communication technologies made the unit's topics more understandable and supported knowledge retention, with some reporting behavior change — related
