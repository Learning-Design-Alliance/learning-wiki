## Evidence

### Martin-Storey 2014

Martin-Storey, A., & Crosnoe, R. (2014). Perceived Neighborhood Safety and Adolescent School Functioning. Applied Developmental Science, 18(2), 61-75. https://doi.org/10.1080/10888691.2014.876276

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

SEM models of SECCYD data (n = 924) predicting age-15 Woodcock-Johnson test scores with and without external neighborhood measures; the authors attribute the null to "the very strong continuity in test scores over time". No effect size printed.

> "The link between perceived neighborhood safety and standardized test scores was not significant when other confounding variables were controlled. Indeed, no neighborhood measures were associated with test scores"

### Martin-Storey 2014 (2)

Martin-Storey, A., & Crosnoe, R. (2014). Perceived Neighborhood Safety and Adolescent School Functioning. Applied Developmental Science, 18(2), 61-75. https://doi.org/10.1080/10888691.2014.876276

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

SEM models predicting grade-9 GPA in the full SECCYD sample (n = 924) with all covariates; the safety coefficient was non-significant. Equivalence was not tested; no effect size printed.

> "Next, no significant association was found between perceived safety and GPA, when individual, family, and school-level variables were controlled."
