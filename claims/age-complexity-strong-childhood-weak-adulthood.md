## Subclaims
`q2 i3` In the full dataset the correlation between age and complexity level is strong and linear up to age 18. [→ Dawson-Tunik 2005](#dawson-tunik-2005)
`q2 i1` In adulthood the correlation between age and complexity level is weak. [→ Dawson-Tunik 2005 (2)](#dawson-tunik-2005-2)

## Evidence

### Dawson-Tunik 2005

Dawson-Tunik, T. L., Commons, M., Wilson, M., & Fischer, K. W. (2005). The shape of development. European Journal of Developmental Psychology, 2(2), 163–195. https://doi.org/10.1080/17405620544000011

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Conventional correlation analysis of the complete 747-interview dataset, reported alongside the Rasch results. Up to age 18 the age–complexity correlation is r = .92 (p < .01, n = 371); "but it is weak in adulthood (r = .16, p 5 .01, n = 376)".

> "we ﬁnd the relation between age and complexity level to be strong and linear up to age 18 (r = .92, p 5 .01, n = 371), but it is weak in adulthood (r = .16, p 5 .01, n = 376)"

### Dawson-Tunik 2005 (2)
