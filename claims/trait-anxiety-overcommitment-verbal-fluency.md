## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Trait anxiety correlated best with over-commitment (ρ = 0.73), followed by effort-reward imbalance (ρ = 0.57), depressiveness (ρ = 0.51) and psychological demand (ρ = 0.49). [→ Hahn Henry 2017](#hahn-henry-2017)
`q2 i3` Verbal fluency correlated negatively with trait anxiety (ρ = -0.45), but only for the alternating-categories subtask, and fluency did not alleviate acute test anxiety before oral exams. [→ Hahn Henry 2017 (2)](#hahn-henry-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Hahn Henry 2017

Hahn Henry, Kropp Peter, Kirschstein Timo, Rücker Gernot, Müller-Hilke Brigitte. (2017). Test anxiety in medical school is unrelated to academic performance but correlates with an effort/reward imbalance. PLoS ONE 12(2): e0171220. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0171220

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Spearman rank correlation analysis of the cohort's STAI-T scores against psychosocial distress measures; trait anxiety correlated most strongly with over-commitment at ρ = 0.73, with the other printed coefficients as quoted.

> "Trait anxiety correlated best with a feeling of over-commitment (ρ = 0.73) followed by an imbalance between high efforts spent and low rewards received (ρ = 0.57), scores for depressiveness (ρ = 0.51) and the perceived psychological demand associated with attending medical school (ρ = 0.49)"

### Hahn Henry 2017 (2)

Hahn Henry, Kropp Peter, Kirschstein Timo, Rücker Gernot, Müller-Hilke Brigitte. (2017). Test anxiety in medical school is unrelated to academic performance but correlates with an effort/reward imbalance. PLoS ONE 12(2): e0171220. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0171220

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Correlation analysis of Regensburger word fluency test subtests against STAI-T; the negative correlation at ρ = -0.45 was limited to subtask 4, testing alternating categories. The authors state this disproves their hypothesis that verbal fluency alleviates test anxiety before oral exams.

> "verbal fluency correlated negatively with trait anxiety (ρ = -0.45)"
