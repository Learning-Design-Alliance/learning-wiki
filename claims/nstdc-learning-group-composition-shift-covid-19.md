## Evidence

### Bauer KM 2021

Bauer KM, Corcorran MA, Budak JZ, Johnston C, Spach DH. (2021). Leveraging E-Learning Infrastructure in Times of Rapid Change: Use of the National Sexually Transmitted Diseases Curriculum in the Era of COVID-19. Sex Transm Dis. https://doi.org/10.1097/OLQ.0000000000001462

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Observational comparison of learning group characteristics from the standardized Health Professional Application for Training form, categorized by type. The proportion of academic groups "increased from 20.0% (n = 8) and 22.5% (n = 9)" to 37.9% each; no effect size was reported.

> "The proportion of academic-undergraduate and academic-graduate learning groups increased from 20.0% (n = 8) and 22.5% (n = 9), respectively, in March 2019 to February 2020 to 37.9% (n = 11) and 37.9% (n = 11), respectively, in March to April 2020."

### Bauer KM 2021 (2)

Bauer KM, Corcorran MA, Budak JZ, Johnston C, Spach DH. (2021). Leveraging E-Learning Infrastructure in Times of Rapid Change: Use of the National Sexually Transmitted Diseases Curriculum in the Era of COVID-19. Sex Transm Dis. https://doi.org/10.1097/OLQ.0000000000001462

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Observational comparison of US Census Bureau region for learning groups created March–April 2020 versus the prior 12 months, when 42.5% (n = 17) of 40 groups were in the South. In the pandemic period the "Midwest region" led with 41.4%; no effect size was reported.

> "Among the 29 learning groups created in March to April 2020, 41.4% (n = 12) were located in the Midwest region and 31.0% (n = 9) in the South region."
