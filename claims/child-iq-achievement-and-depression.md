# Child IQ shows a strong positive association with academic achievement and a small inverse association with depressive symptoms in both sexes

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Fully adjusted models show positive associations between IQ and achievement (0.62 in boys, 0.6 in girls) and small inverse associations with depressive symptoms (-0.09 and -0.11). [→ López-López José A. 2021](#lopez-lopez-jose-a-2021)

## Evidence

### López-López José A. 2021

López-López José A., Kwong Alex S.F., Washbrook Liz, Tilling Kate, Fazel Mina S., Pearson Rebecca M. (2021). Depressive symptoms and academic achievement in UK adolescents: a cross-lagged analysis with genetic covariates. Journal of Affective Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2021.01.091

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Covariate estimates from fully adjusted RI-CLPM models in the ALSPAC cohort, with IQ measured at the eight-year clinic visit using the Weschler Intelligence Scale for Children (verbal and non-verbal IQ averaged). The printed values are standardised regression coefficients, not effect sizes. The discussion also reports a small inverse association between child IQ and depressive symptoms.

> "fully adjusted models provided evidence of direct associations between family SES and academic achievement in boys (0.09, 0.05, 0.13) and girls (0.07, 0.04, 0.1), and even stronger positive associations between IQ and achievement (0.62, 0.59 to 0.65 in boys and 0.6, 0.57 to 0.63 in girls)."
