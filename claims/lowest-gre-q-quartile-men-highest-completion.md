## Evidence

### Petersen Sandra L. 2018

Petersen Sandra L., Erenrich Evelyn S., Levine Dovev L., Vigoreaux Jim, Gile Krista. (2018). Multi-institutional study of GRE scores as predictors of STEM PhD degree completion: GRE gets a low mark. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0206570

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Quartile analysis of 1038 men, rank-ordered GRE Q scores combined across institutions (Table 4). Completion rates were 56.2% (Q1), 59.4% (Q2), 60.6% (Q3), 74.0% (Q4 lowest); the article reports Q1–Q3 rates significantly lower than Q4 (p<0.0001, p<0.0001, p<0.001).

> "In contrast to women, men in the lowest quartile of GRE Q scores finished at a higher rate than counterparts in all three higher quartiles."

### Petersen Sandra L. 2018 (2)

Petersen Sandra L., Erenrich Evelyn S., Levine Dovev L., Vigoreaux Jim, Gile Krista. (2018). Multi-institutional study of GRE scores as predictors of STEM PhD degree completion: GRE gets a low mark. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0206570

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Per-institution analysis shown in Fig 2 of male completion rates by GRE Q quartile at the four state flagship universities; the text states the lowest-quartile men finished at a higher rate than highest-quartile men in every institution.

> "The pattern of men in the lowest quartile for GRE Q finishing at a higher rate than those in the highest quartile was seen in each of the four institutions in the study (Fig 2)."
