## Evidence

### Schoen Deborah E. 2016

Schoen Deborah E., Gausia Kaniz, Glance David G., Thompson Sandra C. (2016). Improving rural and remote practitioners’ knowledge of the diabetic foot: findings from an educational intervention. Journal of Foot and Ankle Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13047-016-0157-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Paired pre-test/post-test analysis of 117 rural and remote healthcare professionals who completed both electronic KAP surveys, tested with a two-tailed paired t-test. The article reports "a significant change in mean knowledge score (0.9, 95 % CI 0.5–1.3) (p < 0.001)".

> "The mean knowledge score for the 117 participants was 6.9 ± 1.6 in the pre-test and 7.8 ± 1.4 in the post-test, a significant change in mean knowledge score (0.9, 95 % CI 0.5–1.3) (p < 0.001) on a two-tailed paired t-test."

### Schoen Deborah E. 2016 (2)

Schoen Deborah E., Gausia Kaniz, Glance David G., Thompson Sandra C. (2016). Improving rural and remote practitioners’ knowledge of the diabetic foot: findings from an educational intervention. Journal of Foot and Ankle Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13047-016-0157-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same matched cohort of 117 participants: "post-test knowledge score increased from the pre-test score (68/117; 58 %)", the article's headline individual-level result for the workshop.

> "For most participants with paired data, their post-test knowledge score increased from the pre-test score (68/117; 58 %)."

### Schoen Deborah E. 2016 (3)

Schoen Deborah E., Gausia Kaniz, Glance David G., Thompson Sandra C. (2016). Improving rural and remote practitioners’ knowledge of the diabetic foot: findings from an educational intervention. Journal of Foot and Ankle Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13047-016-0157-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Distribution of individual score change in the matched cohort: "No change in knowledge score occurred for 18 % (21/117)" and 25 % scored higher before than after training; the article reports no pattern by professional group except Home and Community Care Workers.

> "No change in knowledge score occurred for 18 % (21/117), and 25 % (28/117) had a pre-test score greater than post-test score (Fig. 2)."
