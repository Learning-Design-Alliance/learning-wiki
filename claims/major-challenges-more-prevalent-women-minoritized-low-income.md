# Four or more major challenges were more prevalent among women, Hispanic and minoritized students, and lower-income students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` 17% of women versus 7% of men reported four or more major problems continuing their STEM course online. [→ Means 2020](#means-2020)
`q2 i?` Hispanic students (19%) were more likely than non-Hispanic White students (7%) to report four or more major problems. [→ Means 2020 (2)](#means-2020-2)
`q2 i?` Students from households earning under $50,000 (19%) were more likely than middle- (10%) and upper-income (8%) students to report four or more major challenges. [→ Means 2020 (3)](#means-2020-3)

## Evidence

### Means 2020

Means, B., & Neisler, J. (2020). Unmasking Inequality: STEM Course Experiences During the COVID-19 Pandemic. Digital Promise Global. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/c978880f-6afe-4778-9df2-dac931a9c471

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey analysis of number of major challenges by student group (Table 2); the report suggests part of the gender difference may relate to childcare responsibilities.

> "The proportion of women reporting four or more major problems was more than twice that of the proportion of men (17% versus 7%)."

### Means 2020 (2)

Means, B., & Neisler, J. (2020). Unmasking Inequality: STEM Course Experiences During the COVID-19 Pandemic. Digital Promise Global. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/c978880f-6afe-4778-9df2-dac931a9c471

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same challenge-scale analysis by race/ethnicity (Table 2); the two percentages for Hispanic students correspond to the two- and four-year college subgroups as printed.

> "Hispanic students were more likely to report having experienced four or more major problems (19% and 16%, respectively) than were non-Hispanic white students (7%)."

### Means 2020 (3)

Means, B., & Neisler, J. (2020). Unmasking Inequality: STEM Course Experiences During the COVID-19 Pandemic. Digital Promise Global. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/c978880f-6afe-4778-9df2-dac931a9c471

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same challenge-scale analysis by household income (Table 2); the report also found students with four or more prior online courses less likely to report four or more problems (only 7% did so).

> "Students in the lowest of the three annual household income groups (annual income of less than $50,000) were more likely to report having had four or more major challenges (19% versus 10% for students in the middle-income group and 8% of those in the highest-income group)."
