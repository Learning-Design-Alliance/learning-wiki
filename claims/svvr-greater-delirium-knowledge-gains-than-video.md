## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Nurses receiving SVVR training showed greater knowledge gains than the noninteractive video group immediately postintervention (B = 0.77, p = 0.001, d = 0.54) and at 1-month follow-up (B = 0.58, p = 0.022, d = 0.32). [→ Hung Yin-Ling 2026](#hung-yin-ling-2026)

## Evidence

### Hung Yin-Ling 2026

Hung Yin-Ling, Zhou Yi-Chao, Feng Hsin-Pei, Lin Chia-Huei, Chien Wu-Chien, Tzeng Wen-Chii. (2026). Spherical Video‐Based Virtual Reality Training for Nurses’ Delirium Care Competence: A Randomized Controlled Trial With Qualitative Evaluation. Journal of Nursing Management. https://doi.org/10.1155/jonm/8355655

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r3`

Two-group parallel RCT with 108 registered nurses (54 per arm) at a Taiwanese medical center, analyzed by generalized estimating equations. The between-group effect was "moderate immediately after the intervention (Cohen's d = 0.54)" and "small to moderate at follow-up (d = 0.32)".

> "For delirium care knowledge, Table 3 shows significant group‐by‐time interactions at both T1 (B = 0.77, 95% CI [0.32, 1.21], p = 0.001) and T2 (B = 0.58, 95% CI [0.09, 1.08], p = 0.022), indicating that increases from baseline were greater in the experimental group than in the control group."
