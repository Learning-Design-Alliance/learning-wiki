# Higher physical activity and fitness are linked to better cognition and academic achievement in children in some but not all studies, with possibly stronger effects in children with ADHD, low fitness, or overweight

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Cross-sectional studies show inconsistent associations of objectively measured physical activity with cognition and academic achievement in children. [→ Naveed Sehrish 2020](#naveed-sehrish-2020)
`q2 i?` Exercise interventions provide some evidence that increasing after-school or classroom physical activity improves cognitive functions and academic achievement, but not all interventions show effects. [→ Naveed Sehrish 2020 (2)](#naveed-sehrish-2020-2)
`q2 i?` Physical activity effects may be stronger among children with ADHD, poor academic performance, low fitness, or overweight/obesity than in mainstream children. [→ Naveed Sehrish 2020 (3)](#naveed-sehrish-2020-3)

Narrative review of observational studies: some found associations between "objectively measured physical activity, cognition, and academic achievement", others showed "weak, if any, associations". No effect sizes printed.

> "While some studies have found an association between objectively measured physical activity, cognition, and academic achievement [43,44,45], others have shown weak, if any, associations of objectively measured physical activity with cognition and academic achievement in children [46,47,48,49]"

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Review reports intervention evidence that "increasing after-school or classroom physical activity" may improve cognitive functions and academic achievement; the review also notes not all intervention studies found improved cognition or achievement.

> "Exercise interventions also have provided some evidence that increasing after-school or classroom physical activity improve cognitive functions and academic achievement in children [50,51,52]."

### Naveed Sehrish 2020 (3)

Naveed Sehrish, Lakka Timo, Haapala Eero A. (2020). An Overview on the Associations between Health Behaviors and Brain Health in Children and Adolescents with Special Reference to Diet Quality. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17030953

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Review's interpretation of intervention and observational evidence: physical activity "may have weak effects on cognition in mainstream children" but a stronger effect among children with ADHD, poor academic performance, low fitness, or overweight/obesity.

> "These results may suggest that physical activity may have weak effects on cognition in mainstream children, but a stronger effect on brain and cognition among children with attention deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) [58,59], poor academic performance [60], low fitness levels, or who are overweight or obese [61,62,63]."
