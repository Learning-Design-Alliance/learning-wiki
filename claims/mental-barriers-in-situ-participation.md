# Mindset and emotional barriers, including fear of being tested, deter learners from participating in in situ simulation

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants reported that learners get nervous, fear being tested and fear looking stupid among peers, which prevents participation; mental barriers were mentioned by 87.5% of participants. [→ Jee Marcus 2023](#jee-marcus-2023)

## Evidence

### Jee Marcus 2023

Jee Marcus, Murphy Ella, Umana Etimbuk, O'Connor Paul, Khamoudes Daniel, McNicholl Brian, O'Donnell John J, James Binchy, Irish Trainee Emergency Research Network (ITERN). (2023). Exploring barriers and enablers to simulation-based training in emergency departments: an international qualitative study (BEST-ED Study). BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2023-073099

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview finding from the capability dimension of the 32-participant qualitative study; the article reports "an overwhelming theme that mindset plays a big role" and that all participants agreed. Behavioural, emotional, mental barriers: 28 (87.5%) in Table 1.

> "People get nervous, they think they are going to be tested (in an in situ simulation), additionally that they might not have adequate knowledge and they do not like that (Participant 006)."
