## Evidence

### Nikendei Christoph 2015

Nikendei Christoph, Diefenbacher Katja, Köhl-Hackert Nadja, Lauber Heike, Huber Julia, Herrmann-Werner Anne, Herzog Wolfgang, Schultz Jobst-Hendrik, Jünger Jana, Krautter Markus. (2015). Digital rectal examination skills: first training experiences, the motives and attitudes of standardized patients. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0292-7

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Theme I of the thematic interview analysis with 4 SPs. SPs stressed that students can actively train DRE on real human beings and get "feedback from the patients’ perspective"; one SP compared practising on an SP with going directly to a patient, which he thought takes away a certain insecurity.

> "the advantages of participating in the DRE training and the DRE program were that the students received feedback from the patients’ perspective, which might lead to the students feeling more secure in implementing DRE."
