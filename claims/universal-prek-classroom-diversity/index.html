Evidence¶

Plasencia 2024¶

Plasencia, S., & Melnick, H. (2024). Strategies to foster integration in early childhood education. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/639.507

q2 · i? · review · r2

The report reviews research on universal PreK, stating that "school-based PreK classrooms tend to be about as diverse as 1st-grade classrooms in the same state," contrasting this with means-tested programs.

"Research on universal PreK programs suggests that school-based PreK classrooms tend to be about as diverse as 1st-grade classrooms in the same state. Black and Latino/a children in means-tested programs, by contrast, are less likely than 1st-graders in their state to interact with children from other racial backgrounds."

Plasencia 2024 (2)¶

Plasencia, S., & Melnick, H. (2024). Strategies to foster integration in early childhood education. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/639.507

q2 · i? · review · r2

The report reviews studies of New York City's universal preschool program, which found that "public PreK classrooms in community-based organizations" were more likely to predominantly serve children who are Black or Latino/a, while school-based classrooms were as diverse as 1st-grade classrooms.

"Studies of New York City’s universal preschool program found that while school-based PreK classrooms were as diverse as 1st-grade classrooms, public PreK classrooms in community-based organizations (many of which offered subsidized child care that is means-tested) were more likely to predominantly serve children who are Black or Latino/a."
