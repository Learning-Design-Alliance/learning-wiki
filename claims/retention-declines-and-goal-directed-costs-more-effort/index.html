Related Claims¶

  • Associative thinking (semantic distance of generated word associations) predicts paired-associate foreign-language vocabulary learning beyond general cognitive ability in undergraduates — related
  • Only goal-directed, not free, association correlates with vocabulary retention; the associative thinking–learning link is driven by deliberate rather than spontaneous association — related
  • Prior learning of word pairs reduces the semantic distance of goal-directed (but not free) associations, suggesting interference from newly acquired knowledge — related
  • Adolescents trained on word pairs in the afternoon show higher word-pair retention after 24 hours than evening-trained adolescents, but the difference is not sustained at 7 days — related
  • The retrieval (TTST) advantage appeared at both short (5 min) and long (24 h) retention delays, with no reliable evidence that it differed between them — related
  • A review reports that pretesting helps paired-associate learning only when correct-answer feedback is immediate, but helps trivia-fact learning even when feedback is delayed up to 24 hours — related
