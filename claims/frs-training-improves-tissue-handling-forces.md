## Evidence

### von Bechtolsheim Felix 2024

von Bechtolsheim Felix, Franz Andreas, Schmidt Sofia, Schneider Alfred, La Rosée Felicitas, Radulova-Mauersberger Olga, Krause-Jüttler Grit, Hümpel Anja, Bodenstedt Sebastian, Speidel Stefanie, Weitz Jürgen, Distler Marius, Oehme Florian. (2024). The development of tissue handling skills is sufficient and comparable after training in virtual reality or on a surgical robotic system: a prospective randomized trial. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-024-10842-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures general linear model analysis of the randomized trial's three test points (Baseline, Midterm, Final) for both groups; the article reports significant overall peak-force improvement in all tasks for both groups (e.g., Robot Flap p < 0.01; VR Suture and Knot p < 0.01). No effect sizes are printed.

> "The learning curves of peak-force exertion showed a significant and comparable improvement in both groups for all tasks (Table 4; Fig. 2e)."

### von Bechtolsheim Felix 2024 (2)

von Bechtolsheim Felix, Franz Andreas, Schmidt Sofia, Schneider Alfred, La Rosée Felicitas, Radulova-Mauersberger Olga, Krause-Jüttler Grit, Hümpel Anja, Bodenstedt Sebastian, Speidel Stefanie, Weitz Jürgen, Distler Marius, Oehme Florian. (2024). The development of tissue handling skills is sufficient and comparable after training in virtual reality or on a surgical robotic system: a prospective randomized trial. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-024-10842-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same repeated-measures analysis of mean non-zero force: the Robot group improved significantly only in the Suture and Knot task (p < 0.01), and the VR group's Flap-task learning curve was not significant (p > 0.05). No effect sizes are printed.

> "Regarding the mean non-zero force, the Robot group showed a tendency but no significant improvement in the Flap task, Precise Cut task, or Dissection task (Table 4; Fig. 2c)."
