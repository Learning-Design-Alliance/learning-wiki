Evidence¶

Hatami 2015¶

Ali Hatami. (2015). The effect of collaborative learning and self-assessment on self-regulation. Educational Research and Reviews. https://doi.org/10.5897/ERR2015.2349

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Tukey post hoc comparisons on posttest achievement in the three-group quasi-experiment; Table 4 prints significance levels of 0.044 (traditional vs cooperative), 0 (traditional vs self-regulated), and 0.03 (cooperative vs self-regulated). All pairwise differences were "significant".

"Table 4 shows that there is a significant difference between the traditional and collaborator groups, between traditional and self-regulated groups, between self-regulated and collaborator groups with regard to their academic achievement. It is in the way that the mean scores of self-motivated learning strategies in the post-test for the self-regulated group and the traditional group are the maximum and the minimum, respectively."

Hatami 2015 (2)¶

Ali Hatami. (2015). The effect of collaborative learning and self-assessment on self-regulation. Educational Research and Reviews. https://doi.org/10.5897/ERR2015.2349

q2 · i? · causal · r1

The article's conclusion states the intervention benefited achievement "at low and high levels of cognitive domains", measured by a self-developed ten-question geometry test with five questions at each level.

"In addition, cooperative learning and self-assessment have a positive impact on academic achievement at low and high levels of cognitive domains."
