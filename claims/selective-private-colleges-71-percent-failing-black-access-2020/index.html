Ed Trust analysis of IPEDS Fall 2019-2021 and Census 2020 data grading each institution's Black enrollment against state demographic benchmarks. The analysis found "almost three-quarters (71%) of institutions had failing grades in 2020," with an average failing score of 40.

"Figure 6 shows that almost three-quarters (71%) of institutions had failing grades in 2020. The average failing score was 40, and nearly four-fifths of the F grades were scores of 50 and below."

Related Claims¶

  • The average Black Student Access score across the 122 selective private institutions was a D grade in both 2000 and 2020, moving only from 62 to 68 over two decades. — related
  • Black undergraduate enrollment increased at 74% of selective private institutions from 2000 to 2020, but the average increase (2.3 percentage points) was smaller than the average decrease (3.7 percentage points) among institutions where enrollment declined. — related
  • Among institutions that increased Black enrollment from 2000 to 2020, more than half (56%) increased it more than their state-based demographic benchmark rose, while at 65% of declining institutions enrollment fell even as the Black resident population increased. — related
  • All eight Ivy League institutions earned D or F grades for Black student access in both 2000 and 2020, with average scores of 45.1 in 2000 and 52 in 2020. — related
  • Minority-serving institutions, especially HBCUs and PBIs, held most of the top Black student access grades: 100% of HBCUs and PBIs in the sample earned A grades in both 2000 and 2020, and 6 or 7 of the top 10 scores were held by HBCUs or PBIs. — related
