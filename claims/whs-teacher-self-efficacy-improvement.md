# Teachers using We Have Skills! show greater improvement in self-efficacy than control teachers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium
> **Currency** · newest study 2014 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Teachers in the WHS intervention group improved significantly more in self-efficacy (mean change = .54) than control teachers (mean change = .15), with a large effect size (Cohen's d = .79). [→ Marquez 2014](#marquez-2014)

## Evidence

### Marquez 2014

Marquez, B., Marquez, J., Vincent, C. G., Pennefather, J., Sprague, J. R., Smolkowski, K., & Yeaton, P. (2014). The Iterative Development and Initial Evaluation of We Have Skills!, an Innovative Approach to Teaching Social Skills to Elementary Students. Education and Treatment of Children, 37(1), 137-161. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1070194

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

A randomized controlled trial with 70 K-3 classrooms (37 intervention, 33 control) measured teacher self-efficacy using the TSES at pre- and posttest. ANCOVA controlling for pretest showed "teacher self-efficacy improved significantly more for the intervention group" with Cohen's d = .79.

> "teacher self-efficacy improved significantly more for the intervention group (mean change = .54) than for the control group (mean change = .15), F(1, 66) = 10.2, p = .002 (Cohen's d = .79)"
