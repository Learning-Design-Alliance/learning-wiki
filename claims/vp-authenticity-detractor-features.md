## Evidence

### Coristine A 2025

Coristine A, Li D, Foroutan DB, Ojo TF, Menon NP, Aditya I, Cook DA, Overgaard J, Pankratz VS, Del Fiol G, Aakre CA. (2025). Virtual Patients Using Large Language Models: Scalable, Contextualized Simulation of Clinician-Patient Dialogue With Feedback. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/68486

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Inductively developed feature lists marked by raters across 180 dialogues and feedback episodes. Enhancers included expressing explicit preference (106/180, 58.9%) and appropriate emotion (38/180, 21.1%).

> "the most frequent detractors were that GPT was verbose or used atypical vocabulary (93/180, 51.6%), was overly agreeable (56/180, 31.1%), repeated the question as part of the response (47/180, 26.1%)"

### Coristine A 2025 (2)

Coristine A, Li D, Foroutan DB, Ojo TF, Menon NP, Aditya I, Cook DA, Overgaard J, Pankratz VS, Del Fiol G, Aakre CA. (2025). Virtual Patients Using Large Language Models: Scalable, Contextualized Simulation of Clinician-Patient Dialogue With Feedback. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/68486

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Rater-marked feedback detractor features across 180 conversations. Feedback enhancers included being notably specific or actionable (75/180, 41.7%) and suggesting a useful clinical action (63/180, 35%).

> "detractors included excessively positive feedback relative to actual performance (42/180, 23.3%), failure to mention an important weakness or strength (41/180, 22.8%), inaccuracies due to claimed omissions that were actually done (39/180, 21.7%)"
