Evidence¶

Scammacca Nancy 2020¶

Scammacca Nancy, Fall Anna-Mária, Capin Philip, Roberts Greg, Swanson Elizabeth (2020). Examining Factors Affecting Reading and Math Growth and Achievement Gaps in Grades 1–5: A Cohort-Sequential Longitudinal Approach. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000400

q3 · i3 · associational · r2

Hedges's g effect sizes computed from raw data for growth within each grade across cohorts. The article also reports "more growth occurred from the start of the year to mid-year than from mid-year to end-of-year time points, particularly in math."

"The effect size for reading growth declined from g = 1.37 in Grade 1 to g = 0.50 in Grade 5. In math, growth declined from g = 1.93 to g = 0.73."
