## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Pediatric ETI success was 57.7% for pediatricians versus 96.2% for anesthetists (p < 0.001), with time (83.4 s vs 33.6 s), performance score (median 2 vs 12.3), complications (median 2.0 vs 0), end grade (median 3.5 vs 7.3), and sufficiency rating (15.4% vs 94.2%) all favoring anesthetists. ## Evidence

### van Sambeeck SJ 2019

van Sambeeck SJ, van Kuijk SMJ, Kramer BW, Vermeulen PM, Vos GD. (2019). Endotracheal intubation skills of pediatricians versus anesthetists in neonates and children. European Journal of Pediatrics. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00431-019-03395-8

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Child-manikin scenario in the same cross-sectional simulation study (52 vs 52). The article reports all outcomes significantly better for anesthetists; 8 (15.4%) pediatricians versus 49 (94.2%) anesthetists were considered sufficiently able (p < 0.001). Two pediatricians (4%) refused to perform the procedure because they felt not capable.

> "The difference in a successful pediatric ETI was statistically significant (pediatricians 57.7% versus anesthetists 96.2%, p < 0.001)."
