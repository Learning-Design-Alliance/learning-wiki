# LiPS has no discernible effects on reading comprehension for students with learning disabilities

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` No statistically significant effects were found on either reading comprehension measure (WRMT–R Passage Comprehension, GORT–III Reading Comprehension); the WWC average effect size of 0.21 was not substantively important. [→ What Works Clearinghouse 2010](#what-works-clearinghouse-2010)

## Evidence

### What Works Clearinghouse 2010

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2010). What Works Clearinghouse Intervention Report: Lindamood Phoneme Sequencing® (LiPS®). Students with Learning Disabilities. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/279

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Posttest findings from the Torgesen et al. (2001) RCT (n = 50) on two comprehension measures. Neither comparison was statistically significant; the WWC-calculated average effect size across the two outcomes was 0.21, below the 0.25 substantively-important threshold.

> "Torgesen et al. (2001) found no statistically significant effects on either of the two measures of reading comprehension: the Passage Comprehension subtest of the Woodcock Reading Mastery Test–Revised and the Reading Comprehension subtest of the Gray Oral Reading Test–III."
