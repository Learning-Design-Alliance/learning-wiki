# Giving students a choice between self-focused and other-focused utility-value assignments produced higher course grades than fixing the assignment type

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students given a choice between UV assignment types earned higher grades than students in fixed-type conditions (β = 0.06, p = .040). [→ Priniski 2019](#priniski-2019)

## Evidence

### Priniski 2019

Priniski, S. J., Rosenzweig, E. Q., Canning, E. A., Hecht, C. A., Tibbetts, Y., Hyde, J. S., & Harackiewicz, J. M. (2019). The Benefits of Combining Value for the Self and Others in Utility-Value Interventions. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000343

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same RCT's performance analyses, the choice contrast compared the choice condition with the four fixed-type conditions. The quoted sentence reports the significant effect, "Œ≤ = 0.06, p = .040", and the printed means for both sides of the comparison.

> "There was also a main effect of the choice contrast on course performance, Œ≤ = 0.06, p = .040, such that students who were given a choice between UV assignment types earned higher grades in the course (M = 3.06, SD = 0.59) than students in the four conditions in which UV assignment types were fixed (M = 2.95, SD = 0.75)"
