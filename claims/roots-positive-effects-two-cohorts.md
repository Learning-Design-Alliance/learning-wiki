# ROOTS participants demonstrated positive intervention effects on proximal and distal mathematics measures across two cohorts

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Cohort 1 ROOTS participants showed gains on RAENS (g = .75), TEMA standard scores (g = .25), and ASPENS (g = .55), all p < .01, relative to controls. [→ Shanley et al. 2017](#shanley-et-al-2017)

## Evidence

### Shanley et al. 2017

Lina Shanley, Ben Clarke, Christian T. Doabler, Evangeline Kurtz-Nelson, and Hank Fien. (2017). Early Number Skills Gains and Mathematics Achievement: Intervening to Establish Successful Early Mathematics Trajectories. The Journal of Special Education, 51(3), 177–188. https://doi.org/10.1177/0022466917720455

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Impact analyses from the two cohorts of the randomized ROOTS efficacy trial, reported in the intervention effects subsection. Cohort 1 effects on the three named measures were g = .75, g = .25, and g = .55, all p < .01.

> "Cohort 1 participants demonstrated gains on the ROOTS Assessment of Early Numeracy Skills (RAENS; g = .75, p < .01), the Test of Early Mathematics Achievement standard scores (g = .25, p < .01), and the ASPENS curriculum-based measures (g = .55, p < .01; Clarke et al., 2016)."

### Shanley et al. 2017 (2)

Lina Shanley, Ben Clarke, Christian T. Doabler, Evangeline Kurtz-Nelson, and Hank Fien. (2017). Early Number Skills Gains and Mathematics Achievement: Intervening to Establish Successful Early Mathematics Trajectories. The Journal of Special Education, 51(3), 177–188. https://doi.org/10.1177/0022466917720455

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Cohort 2 impact analyses in the same trial replicated the three Cohort 1 effects with larger values (g = 1.08, g = .31, g = .64) and extended them to the NSB (g = .40) and kindergarten SAT-10 (g = .24).

> "These findings were replicated and extended in Cohort 2 with ROOTS participants demonstrating gains on these three measures (g = 1.08, p < .01; g = .31, p < .01; and g = .64, p < .01, respectively), in addition to the NSB (g = .40, p < .01) and standard scores on the kindergarten version of the Stanford Achievement Test (g = .24, p < .05; Doabler et al., 2016)."
