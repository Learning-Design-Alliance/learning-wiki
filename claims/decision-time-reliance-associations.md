# Decision time is negatively associated with over-reliance and positively associated with under-reliance on ChatGPT recommendations

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Longer decision time on incorrect-recommendation trials was associated with lower over-reliance (ρ = −0.41). [→ Puppart 2025](#puppart-2025)
`q2 i2` Longer decision time on correct-recommendation trials was associated with higher under-reliance (ρ = 0.35). [→ Puppart 2025](#puppart-2025)

## Evidence

### Puppart 2025

Puppart, B., & Aru, J. (2025). A Brief AI Literacy Intervention Does Not Significantly Reduce Over-Reliance and Increases Under-Reliance on ChatGPT: A Randomized Study. University of Tartu. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2503.10556](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2503.10556)

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Correlational analysis (Spearman's ρ) across the 36-participant sample relating reliance decisions to decision time per trial type. Both correlations were statistically significant: "ρ = −0.41, p = .013" for over-reliance and "ρ = 0.35, p = .038" for under-reliance.

> "Over-reliance was negatively associated with decision time on trials with incorrect recommendations (ρ = −0.41, p = .013), whereas under-reliance was positively associated with decision time on trials with correct recommendations (ρ = 0.35, p = .038)."
