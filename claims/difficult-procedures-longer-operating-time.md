## Evidence

### Skjold-Ødegaard B 2024

Skjold-Ødegaard B, Ersdal HL, Assmus J, Søreide K. (2024). Internal and external factors affecting the performance score of surgical trainees doing laparoscopic appendectomy: a prospective, observational cohort study in a structured training programme. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-024-11007-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear mixed model on 95 of 142 evaluation forms (47 excluded where the trainer took over steps). The final model printed B 32.2 (95% CI 10.1, 54.3), p = 0.006 for difficulty; medians were "90 (75–100) min for the “difficult” procedures vs 59 (25–120) min".

> "Only the definition of a “difficult” procedure led to a significant increase in the operating time (Table 3) with operating time 90 (75–100) min for the “difficult” procedures vs 59 (25–120) min for the non-difficult."

### Skjold-Ødegaard B 2024 (2)

Skjold-Ødegaard B, Ersdal HL, Assmus J, Søreide K. (2024). Internal and external factors affecting the performance score of surgical trainees doing laparoscopic appendectomy: a prospective, observational cohort study in a structured training programme. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-024-11007-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same linear mixed model printed B −2 (95% CI −11.9, 7.9), p = 0.694 for trainee experience; the authors state "no significant difference between the inexperienced and the experienced trainees". Equivalence was not tested.

> "Comparing the operating time in this study, we found no significant difference between the inexperienced and the experienced trainees."
