# Stories increase interest in law among non-native speakers but not native speakers

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The treatment condition shows statistically higher interest in engaging with legal content than the control condition for non-native speakers (p = .001) but not for native speakers (p = .459). [→ Hang Jiang 2024](#hang-jiang-2024)

## Evidence

### Hang Jiang 2024

Hang Jiang, Xiajie Zhang, Robert Mahari, Daniel Kessler, Eric Ma, Tal August, Irene Li, Alex 'Sandy' Pentland, Yoon Kim, Deb Roy, Jad Kabbara. (2024). Leveraging Large Language Models for Learning Complex Legal Concepts through Storytelling. [doi:10.18653/v1/2024.acl-long.388](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.acl-long.388)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

RCT interest ratings among 136 participants. Non-native speakers reading stories rated interest 4.03±1.20 vs. 3.84±0.92 for control; native speakers 3.78±0.99 vs. 3.67±1.12. The group difference in significance is a moderator by speaker status.

> "Mann-Whitney U tests reveal that statistical differences are found for the participant interest between the treatment group and control group for non-native speakers (U = 18662.5, p = .001); however, not for the native English speaker (U = 13800.0, p= .459)."
