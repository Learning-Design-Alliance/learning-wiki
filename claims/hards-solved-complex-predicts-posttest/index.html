Evidence¶

Malkiewich 2016¶

Malkiewich, L. J., Lee, A., Slater, S., & Chase, C. C. (2016). Tenacious Assessments: Using In-Game Behaviors to Measure Student Persistence and Challenge Navigation. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Tenacious+Assessments+Using+In-Game+Behaviors

q2 · i3 · associational · r1

Correlational analysis of gameplay log measures against paper posttest scores for students playing a block-based coding game (log-data subset N=18). "Hards Solved Complex (r = 0.76, p < .01)" led the challenge-approach measures correlated with learning.

"These included Hards Solved Complex (r = 0.76, p < .01), Hards Started Complex (r = 0.71, p < .01), and Levels Solved Complex (r = 0.70, p < .01). All three of these correlations maintained significance after the Benjamini-Hochberg correction."

Malkiewich 2016 (2)¶

Malkiewich, L. J., Lee, A., Slater, S., & Chase, C. C. (2016). Tenacious Assessments: Using In-Game Behaviors to Measure Student Persistence and Challenge Navigation. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Tenacious+Assessments+Using+In-Game+Behaviors

q2 · i3 · associational · r1

Step-wise linear regression entering pretest first, then the most strongly correlated measures. Hards Solved Complex "explained 50.2% additional unique variance in posttest score, controlling for pretest"; Hards Started Complex added no further variance because the two were highly correlated (r = 0.90).

"Hards Solved Complex, when added to the model, explained 50.2% additional unique variance in posttest score, controlling for pretest, F(1,15) = 20.09, p < .01."
