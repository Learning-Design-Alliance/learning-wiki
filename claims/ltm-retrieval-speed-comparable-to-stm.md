# With appropriate retrieval cues, selective retrieval from LTM after brief presentation can reach speeds comparable to retrieval from STM

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Trained subjects retrieved digit groups from LTM via retrieval-structure location cues, with a new digit group taking about 2 s to recall, reducible to within 1 s with task familiarity. [→ Ericsson 1993](#ericsson-1993)

## Evidence

### Ericsson 1993

K. Anders Ericsson and Walter Kintsch. (1993). Long-Term Working Memory. https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Long-Term+Working+Memory

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review reports, citing Ericsson and Chase (1982) and Müller (1917), that trained subjects memorizing digit matrices by rows could retrieve rows in many different orders, and "The time required to recall a new digit group was estimated to be about 2 s", falling within 1 s for a familiar subject. It concludes LT-WM retrieval can attain speeds similar to STM through practice.

> "The time required to recall a new digit group was estimated to be about 2 s, but a subject familiar with this particular recall task completed the retrieval within 1 s (Müller, 1917)."
