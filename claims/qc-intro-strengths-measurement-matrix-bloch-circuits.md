# After an introductory quantum computing course, students commonly show strengths in measurement, matrix operations, the Bloch sphere, common gates, and interpreting quantum circuit diagrams

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Interviewed students confidently explained that measurement collapses qubit states into zeros and ones with probability values, and were confident with matrix operations, the Bloch sphere, Pauli X and Z, Hadamard, and CNOT gates, and step-by-step interpretation of quantum circuit diagrams. ## Evidence

### Tunde Kushimo 2023

Tunde Kushimo, Beth Thacker. (2023). Investigating students' strengths and difficulties in Quantum Computing. [doi:10.1109/qce57702.2023.20322](https://doi.org/10.1109/qce57702.2023.20322)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative case study: thematic analysis of two one-hour semi-structured interviews with five physics majors who completed the introductory course; patterns counted as findings only if common to at least 60% (N=3) of interviewees. Students "confidently explained" measurement collapse, used matrix methods, drew Bloch sphere states, and gave step-by-step circuit explanations.

> "All the interviewed students confidently explained that the results from measuring qubits are always in the form of zeros and ones with some probability values; it cannot be in a superposition state"
