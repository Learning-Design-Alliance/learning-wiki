## Evidence

### Brezis Mayer 2017

Brezis Mayer, Lahat Yael, Frankel Meir, Rubinov Alan, Bohm Davina, Cohen Matan J, Koslowsky Meni, Shalomson Orit, Sprung Charles L, Perry-Mezare Henia, Yahalom Rina, Ziv Amitai. (2017). What can we learn from simulation-based training to improve skills for end-of-life care? Insights from a national project in Israel. Israel Journal of Health Policy Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13584-017-0169-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Pre- and post-workshop questionnaire data on skills, satisfaction, perceived challenges and attitude change. Participants "rated the simulations as representative reflection of field experiences", found the professional actors so realistic they often forgot the artificial setting, rated the learning experience high, and most would strongly recommend it. Subjective, short-term self-evaluation; no effect size.

> "They believed that the simulation-based workshop was likely to ameliorate future handling of EOL situations"
