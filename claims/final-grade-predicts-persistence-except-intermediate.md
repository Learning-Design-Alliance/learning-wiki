# Final grade positively predicts persistence overall, but grades in the middle of the pack show no statistically significant correlation with persistence

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A simple logistic regression confirms a positive correlation between final grade and persistence for the full data set (OR = 1.5, P < 0.001, N = 273). [→ Justyna P. Zwolak 2018](#justyna-p-zwolak-2018)
`q2 i?` Within the intermediate grade category, final grades are not significantly correlated with persistence. [→ Justyna P. Zwolak 2018 (2)](#justyna-p-zwolak-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Justyna P. Zwolak 2018

Justyna P. Zwolak, Michael Zwolak, and Eric Brewe. (2018). Educational commitment and social networking: The power of informal networks. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010131)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Simple logistic regression on the full sample of 273 students in four MI-M sections. The article reports "a positive correlation between final grade and persistence" with OR = 1.5 and P < 0.001; the OR is a rescaled unstandardised ratio, so no standardised effect size is printed.

> "A simple logistic regression with grade as a sole predictor for persistence (Mgr :persistence∼grade) conﬁrms a positive correlation between ﬁnal grade and persistence for our data (odd ratio OR = 1.5; estimate B = 1.36, standard error of estimateSE = 0.19, signiﬁcance levelP <0.001; sample size N = 273)"

### Justyna P. Zwolak 2018 (2)

Justyna P. Zwolak, Michael Zwolak, and Eric Brewe. (2018). Educational commitment and social networking: The power of informal networks. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010131)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression within the intermediate grade group (grades B, B-, C+; N = 90 for final grades). The article reports no statistically significant correlation for either final grades or their midterm proxy; equivalence was not tested.

> "we ﬁnd no statistically signiﬁcant correlation between grades “in the middle of the pack” and persistence, whether we use ﬁnal grades or their midterm proxy"
