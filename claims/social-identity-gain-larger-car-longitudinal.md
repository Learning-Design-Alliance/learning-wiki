## Evidence

### Griffin 2025

Griffin, S. M., Lebedová, A., Cruwys, T., McMahon, G., Foran, A. M., Skrodzka, M., Gallagher, S., Ginty, A. T., & Muldoon, O. T. (2025). Identity change and the transition to university: Implications for cortisol awakening response, psychological well‐being and academic performance. Applied Psychology: Health and Well‐Being, 17(1), e12608. https://doi.org/10.1111/aphw.12608

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal path analysis with FIML predicting T2 CAR (change from T1 controlled; 38 participants had analysable T2 CAR data). New group memberships predicted a larger CAR, "b = .035, p = .025, indicative of a better ability to cope with stress"; no standardised effect size printed.

> "H3 was partially supported: New group memberships were associated with a larger CAR, b = .035, p = .025, indicative of a better ability to cope with stress."

### Griffin 2025 (2)

Griffin, S. M., Lebedová, A., Cruwys, T., McMahon, G., Foran, A. M., Skrodzka, M., Gallagher, S., Ginty, A. T., & Muldoon, O. T. (2025). Identity change and the transition to university: Implications for cortisol awakening response, psychological well‐being and academic performance. Applied Psychology: Health and Well‐Being, 17(1), e12608. https://doi.org/10.1111/aphw.12608

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same path model: the continuity pathway showed no longitudinal association with T2 CAR ("a larger CAR (T2; p = .161); this was not supported"). H2 was not supported for any outcome.

> "We hypothesized (H2) that maintained group memberships (T2) would be associated with lower depressive symptoms (T2; p = .893), greater life satisfaction (T2; p = .153), better academic performance (T3; p = .401) and a larger CAR (T2; p = .161); this was not supported."

### Griffin 2025 (3)

Griffin, S. M., Lebedová, A., Cruwys, T., McMahon, G., Foran, A. M., Skrodzka, M., Gallagher, S., Ginty, A. T., & Muldoon, O. T. (2025). Identity change and the transition to university: Implications for cortisol awakening response, psychological well‐being and academic performance. Applied Psychology: Health and Well‐Being, 17(1), e12608. https://doi.org/10.1111/aphw.12608

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Fit of the full SIMIC path model estimated with maximum likelihood in AMOS: the article reports "good fit, χ 2(29) = 35.93, p = .176" with RMSEA = .040 and CFI = .969 (NFI = .881).

> "The full model, as displayed in Figure 2, demonstrated good fit, χ 2(29) = 35.93, p = .176; RMSEA = .040 [.0001, .077], CFI = .969, NFI = .881, AIC = 157.93 and AICc = 241.02."
