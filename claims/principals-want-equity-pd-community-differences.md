# More than two thirds of principals want equity-focused professional development, with significant differences by community type

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Principals wanted professional development in meeting the needs of students with disabilities (71%), supporting diverse learners (69%), equitably serving all students (69%), and meeting the needs of English learners (64%). [→ Levin 2020](#levin-2020)
`q2 i?` Rural principals (55%) were much less likely than city (75%), suburban (77%), or town (72%) principals to want professional development on supporting students from diverse backgrounds; suburban principals (75%) were more likely to want English learner content. [→ Levin 2020 (2)](#levin-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Levin 2020

Levin, S., Leung, M., Edgerton, A. K., & Scott, C. (2020). Elementary school principals’ professional learning: Current status and future needs. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. http://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/professional-learning-principals

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey findings on four equity-related professional development topics (Figure 4), reported with descriptive percentages for all respondents.

> "more than two thirds of principals wanted professional development in meeting the needs of students with disabilities (71%), leading schools to support diverse learners (69%), and equitably serving all students (69%)."

### Levin 2020 (2)

Levin, S., Leung, M., Edgerton, A. K., & Scott, C. (2020). Elementary school principals’ professional learning: Current status and future needs. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. http://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/professional-learning-principals

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Subgroup analysis by community type (Figure 6); the article reports this and the suburban English learner difference as the two statistically significant community-type differences for equity topics.

> "Rural principals (55%) were much less likely to want professional development that helped them to support students from diverse backgrounds than principals in cities (75%), suburbs (77%), or towns (72%)"
