## Evidence

### Homeyra Sadaghiani 2013

Homeyra Sadaghiani, John Miller, Steven Pollock, and Daniel Rehn. (2013). Constructing a Multiple-Choice Assessment For Upper-Division Quantum Physics From An Open-Ended Tool. [doi:10.1119/perc.2013.pr.066](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2013.pr.066)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Item analysis of the Q12-derived MC items at CPP and CUB. The open-ended QMAT Q12 average was very low (CPP=23%, N=19; CUB=25%, N=53). Percentages are printed descriptively; no effect size or significance test is reported.

> "While many students correctly identified the false nature of the statement in MC-Q18, (CPP = 71%, CUB = 52%), many were not able to detect all of the possible correct options (d, e, f) in Q19. (CPP = 18%, CUB = 15%)."

### Homeyra Sadaghiani 2013 (2)

Homeyra Sadaghiani, John Miller, Steven Pollock, and Daniel Rehn. (2013). Constructing a Multiple-Choice Assessment For Upper-Division Quantum Physics From An Open-Ended Tool. [doi:10.1119/perc.2013.pr.066](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2013.pr.066)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Item-analysis finding from the first MC implementation: on multi-correct items, 0% of students in both samples selected all correct options and no incorrect options, motivating combining options (d) and (e) in later versions. Option (f) was selected by only one student across both data sets and was removed.

> "MC questions with multiple correct answer choices showed undesirable item difficulty (0% of students in both samples selected all correct options and no incorrect options), so options (d) and (e) were combined in later versions of the instrument."
