# Cold calling and volunteering are rated the most anxiety-inducing practices, and the most anxiogenic practices tend to be rated as having the least educational value

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` At the start of the semester, cold calling (3.99 ± 1.18) and volunteering to answer a question (3.16 ± 1.20) were rated the most anxiety-inducing practices, while cold calling was rated as having the least value for learning (2.41 ± 1.23). [→ Hood 2021](#hood-2021)

## Evidence

### Hood 2021

Hood, S., Barrickman, N., Djerdjian, N., Farr, M., Magner, S., Roychowdhury, H., Gerrits, R., Lawford, H., Ott, B., Ross, K., Paige, O., Stowe, S., Jensen, M., & Hull, K. (2021). “I Like and Prefer to Work Alone”: Social Anxiety, Academic Self-Efficacy, and Students’ Perceptions of Active Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-12-0271

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive survey results from the same N = 227 sample at the start of term, rating practices on a 1–5 anxiety scale. Cold calling and volunteering were rated "the most anxiety-inducing practices"; nongraded activities completed alone were the least.

> "At the start of the semester, students rated cold calling (3.99 ± 1.18, 95% CI [3.78, 4.20]) and volunteering to answer a question (3.16 ± 1.20, 95% CI [2.95, 3.37]) as the most anxiety-inducing practices, whereas nongraded activities completed alone (1.49 ± 0.79, 95% CI [1.28, 1.70]) were the least anxiety inducing."

### Hood 2021 (2)

Hood, S., Barrickman, N., Djerdjian, N., Farr, M., Magner, S., Roychowdhury, H., Gerrits, R., Lawford, H., Ott, B., Ross, K., Paige, O., Stowe, S., Jensen, M., & Hull, K. (2021). “I Like and Prefer to Work Alone”: Social Anxiety, Academic Self-Efficacy, and Students’ Perceptions of Active Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-12-0271

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Ranking comparison of 11 EBIPs for anxiety versus contribution to learning, using the same sample's ratings. The inverse association reached significance only in end-of-term rankings; the beginning-of-term association was not significant.

> "Although this pattern was not significant using responses collected at the beginning of term (Kendall’s tau-b (10) = −0.33, p = 0.16), it was in the end-of-term rankings (Kendall’s tau-b (10) = −0.48, p = 0.04, r2 = 0.23)."
