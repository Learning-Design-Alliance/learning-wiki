## Evidence

### Phalet 2004

Phalet, K., Andriessen, I., & Lens, W. (2004). How Future Goals Enhance Motivation and Learning in Multicultural Classrooms. Educational Psychology Review, Vol. 16, No. 1. https://hdl.handle.net/1874/23520

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review's synthesis of empirical research on future time perspective and perceived instrumentality, largely correlational studies among nonminority and minority students. It concludes that "students with a positive perception of the instrumentality of school work" show higher motivation, strategy use, and performance. No effect sizes are printed.

> "students with a positive perception of the instrumentality of school work to reach future career goals (a) are more motivated for school tasks, (b) make more use of effective learning strategies, and (c) work harder and perform better in school."

### Phalet 2004 (2)

Phalet, K., Andriessen, I., & Lens, W. (2004). How Future Goals Enhance Motivation and Learning in Multicultural Classrooms. Educational Psychology Review, Vol. 16, No. 1. https://hdl.handle.net/1874/23520

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Lens et al. (in press), a study finding that high perceived utility of the learning task predicts "higher on deep-level learning and lower on surface-level learning," with correspondingly better school results; Simons et al. (2003) report similar findings.

> "Lenset al. (in press) found that when the present learning task is experienced as having high instrumentality or utility, students score higher on deep-level learning and lower on surface-level learning."
