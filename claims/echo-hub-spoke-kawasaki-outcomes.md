## Evidence

### Puckett 2021

Puckett, H. M., Bossaller, J. S., & Sheets, L. R. (2021). The impact of project ECHO on physician preparedness to treat opioid use disorder: a systematic review. Addiction Science & Clinical Practice, 16, 6. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-021-00215-z

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Self-report survey within a multimodal Pennsylvania program combining ECHO training, emergency medical training, and peer-based support. The review also reports 83% decreased professional isolation and 88% improved MOUD ability; all measures self-reported.

> "Of 121 Project ECHO participants, all of whom were primary care physicians, reported:Increased knowledge of evidence-based OUD management (n = 109/121, 90%)"

### Puckett 2021 (2)

Puckett, H. M., Bossaller, J. S., & Sheets, L. R. (2021). The impact of project ECHO on physician preparedness to treat opioid use disorder: a systematic review. Addiction Science & Clinical Practice, 16, 6. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-021-00215-z

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Observational service-outcome data from the same hub-and-spoke program, where the hub was a psychiatric-hospital opioid treatment program supporting 12 affiliated sites. The review reports 166/180 patients began MOUD upon intake; the review attributes the wait-time reduction to ECHO-enabled communication.

> "Prior to implementation wait times were sometimes more than two weeks, and after implementation wait time was reduced to 24–48 h, which increased initial program adherence."
