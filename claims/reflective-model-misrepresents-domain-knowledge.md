# Reflective modeling of formative knowledge constructs risks misrepresenting domain knowledge as a general factor such as intelligence

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` A high alpha on a domain knowledge test may reflect general learner-related factors, so the latent factor could be misinterpreted as general intelligence rather than domain knowledge. [→ misrep-1](#stadler-2021)

## Evidence

### Stadler 2021

Stadler, Matthias, Michael Sailer, and Frank Fischer. 2021. “Knowledge as a formative construct: a good alpha is not always better.” New Ideas in Psychology 60: 100832. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.newideapsych.2020.100832.

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical argument from the article's misrepresentation section. It reasons that high alpha may indicate "a lot of the variance is due to general learner-related factors (e.g., intelligence, study diligence, interest)" so the instrument does not differentiate conceptual material.

> "In this case, a measure of domain knowledge would be mis- represented as a measure of, for instance, general intelligence."
