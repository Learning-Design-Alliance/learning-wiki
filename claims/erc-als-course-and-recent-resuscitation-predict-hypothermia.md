## Evidence

### Fischer H 2014

Fischer H, Bachmann K, Strunk G, Neuhold S, Zapletal B, Maurer C, Fast A, Stumpf D, Greif R. (2014). Translation of ERC resuscitation guidelines into clinical practice by emergency physicians. Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/1757-7241-22-9

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Multivariate logistic regression of the Austria-wide questionnaire survey shows both course attendance and recent resuscitation independently predict hypothermia initiation, with "OR = 1.670, p <0.05" for course attendance.

> "physicians who have attended an ERC-ALS provider course since 2005 have initiated more mild therapeutic hypothermia after successful resuscitation (OR = 1.670, p <0.05) as well as participants who resuscitated a victim of cardiac arrest within the last year (OR = 2.324, p < 0.01)"

### Fischer H 2014 (2)

Fischer H, Bachmann K, Strunk G, Neuhold S, Zapletal B, Maurer C, Fast A, Stumpf D, Greif R. (2014). Translation of ERC resuscitation guidelines into clinical practice by emergency physicians. Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/1757-7241-22-9

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Same regression model (Table 2) shows age per year negatively predicts hypothermia initiation, "OR = 0.913, p <0.01", while years of clinical experience positively predict it (OR = 1.086, p < 0.05).

> "while older physicians initiated less hypothermia (OR = 0.913, p <0.01)"
