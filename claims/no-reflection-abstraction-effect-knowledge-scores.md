## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Neither the interaction (F(1, 22) = .720, p = .40) nor the main effects of reflection type (F(1, 22) = 1.64, p = .21) or order of abstraction (F(1, 22) = .50, p = .49) were significant for knowledge test scores. [→ DiBenedetto 2017](#dibenedetto-2017)

## Evidence

### DiBenedetto 2017

DiBenedetto, C. A., Blythe, J. M., & Myers, B. E. (2017). Effects of the Order of Abstraction and Type of Reflection on Content Knowledge when Teaching Experientially in a High School Classroom. Journal of Agricultural Education, 58(2), 67-82. https://doi.org/10.5032/jae.2017.02067

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

ANOVA on knowledge test scores in the 2 x 2 randomized study (n = 26) found no significant main effects; the interaction was also non-significant (F(1, 22) = .720, p = .40). Equivalence was not tested.

> "There was no significant main effect of the type of reflection, F (1, 22) = 1.64, p = .21, or order of abstraction on the knowledge test scores  F (1, 22) = .50, p = .49, resulting in a failure to reject the second and third null hypotheses."
