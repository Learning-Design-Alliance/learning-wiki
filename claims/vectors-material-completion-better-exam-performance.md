# Completing supplemental math material 1 (vectors) is associated with better exam performance, and the association grows with the material's relevance to exam content

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The regression coefficient of supplemental math material 1 completion is positive and statistically significant, indicating additional vector practice is positively associated with better exam performance. [→ Yifan Lu 2025](#yifan-lu-2025)
`q2 i?` As the Relevance Index increases across exams, the regression coefficient for material 1 becomes larger, with clear separation of fitted lines for Exams 1 and 3 but not Exam 2 (RI = 0.2). [→ Yifan Lu 2025 (2)](#yifan-lu-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Yifan Lu 2025

Yifan Lu, K. Supriya, Shanna Shaked, Elizabeth H. Simmons, and Alexander Kusenko. (2025). Incentivizing supplemental math assignments and using AI-generated hints is associated with improved exam performance. arXiv. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1103/v93w-ytyc](https://doi.org/10.1103/v93w-ytyc)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Multiple linear regression (OLS with HC3 covariance) on exam z-scores for 188 and 194 students in Sections A and B; material 1 completion was significant for Exam 3 (coefficient 0.24, p = 0.001), while materials 3 and 4 showed no significant association.

> "Notably, the coefficient of supplemental math mate- rial 1 completion is positive and statistically significant, showing that additional practice on vectors is indeed pos- itively associated with better exam performance."

### Yifan Lu 2025 (2)

Yifan Lu, K. Supriya, Shanna Shaked, Elizabeth H. Simmons, and Alexander Kusenko. (2025). Incentivizing supplemental math assignments and using AI-generated hints is associated with improved exam performance. arXiv. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1103/v93w-ytyc](https://doi.org/10.1103/v93w-ytyc)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Cross-exam comparison of Relevance Index values (Exam 1 RI = 0.6, Exam 2 RI = 0.2, Exam 3 RI = 0.8) against material 1 regression coefficients; Fig. 5 shows fitted-line separation for high-RI exams that is absent for Exam 2.

> "we found that as RI increases, the regression coefficient becomes not only significant but also larger (see Tab. III, Tab. VII, and Tab. VIII). This effect is visually demonstrated in Fig. 5"
