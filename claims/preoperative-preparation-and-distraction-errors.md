## Evidence

### Esanu V 2026

Esanu V, Marziali E, Gaspar O, Agoston S, Carciumaru T, Stoia A, Paun C, Colosi HA, Dindelegan G, Dirven C, Meling TR, Vasilic D, Volovici V. (2026). Microvascular errors of technique: a systematic review. Acta Neurochirurgica. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00701-026-06810-w

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Narrative synthesis of ten studies reporting pre-operative errors, observed consistently across simulation-based and in vivo models. The review reports setup-related errors involved "incorrect instrument selection, poor microscope calibration, and inadequate preparation of the operative field."

> "Setup-related errors involved incorrect instrument selection, poor microscope calibration, and inadequate preparation of the operative field."

### Esanu V 2026 (2)

Esanu V, Marziali E, Gaspar O, Agoston S, Carciumaru T, Stoia A, Paun C, Colosi HA, Dindelegan G, Dirven C, Meling TR, Vasilic D, Volovici V. (2026). Microvascular errors of technique: a systematic review. Acta Neurochirurgica. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00701-026-06810-w

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

The review attributes these findings to Carr et al. (ORDI cognitive-distraction study) and Chadha et al. (randomized trial of mental rehearsal), reporting them second-hand with no effect sizes printed.

> "Carr et al. [4] studied the effects of ORDI (operating room cognitive distractions) and concluded that operating under stress led to faster completion but lower accuracy, while Chadha et al. [6] demonstrated that mental rehearsal before operating reduced the number of errors."
