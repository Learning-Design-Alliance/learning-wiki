## Evidence

### Niederhauser A 2019

Niederhauser A, Züllig S, Marschall J, Schweiger A, John G, Kuster SP, Schwappach DLB. (2019). Change in staff perspectives on indwelling urinary catheter use after implementation of an intervention bundle in seven Swiss acute care hospitals: results of a before/after survey study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2018-028740

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r1`

Repeated cross-sectional survey of 1579 staff at baseline and 1527 at follow-up in seven Swiss hospitals. Table 2 reports the overall knowledge score effect size as 0.29, described by the authors as small to moderate; the quote confirms the significant increase.

> "The mean number of correctly answered knowledge questions increased significantly between the two survey periods (T0: 10.4, T1: 11.0; p<0.001)."

### Niederhauser A 2019 (2)

Niederhauser A, Züllig S, Marschall J, Schweiger A, John G, Kuster SP, Schwappach DLB. (2019). Change in staff perspectives on indwelling urinary catheter use after implementation of an intervention bundle in seven Swiss acute care hospitals: results of a before/after survey study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2018-028740

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r1`

Stratified t-tests on the same two survey waves. Table 2 prints effect sizes of 0.35 (nurses), 0.33 (without managerial function), 0.40 (frequent users) and 0.16 (physicians, p=0.047), consistent with the subgroup pattern stated in the text.

> "Subgroup analysis indicates that knowledge scores increased in particular for nurses, staff members without managerial function and staff members frequently placing catheters."
