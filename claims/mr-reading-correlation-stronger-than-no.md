# Math Reasoning is more strongly correlated with reading comprehension than Numerical Operations is, and the two math measures are highly correlated

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` MR correlated more strongly with reading (r = 0.46) than NO did (r = 0.29), a significant difference. [→ Wu 2017](#wu-2017)
`q3 i3` NO and MR were highly correlated (r = 0.64). [→ Wu 2017 (2)](#wu-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Wu 2017

Wu, S. S., Chen, L., Battista, C., Watts, A. K. S., Willcutt, E. G., & Menon, V. (2017). Distinct influences of affective and cognitive factors on children's non-verbal and verbal mathematical abilities. Cognition, 166, 118-129. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2017.05.016

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r3`

Pearson correlations (Table 2) in the full sample of 330 children; r = 0.46 for MR-reading versus r = 0.29 for NO-reading, compared with a Fisher Z test.

> "although both MR and NO were significantly correlated with reading comprehension, MR was more strongly correlated with reading than (r = 0.46) than NO (r = 0.29), Fisher Z = 2.54, p = 0.011."

### Wu 2017 (2)

Wu, S. S., Chen, L., Battista, C., Watts, A. K. S., Willcutt, E. G., & Menon, V. (2017). Distinct influences of affective and cognitive factors on children's non-verbal and verbal mathematical abilities. Cognition, 166, 118-129. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2017.05.016

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r3`

Descriptive/correlational analysis of the whole sample (Table 2), showing the two WIAT-II math subtests share substantial variance.

> "The two math achievement measures (NO and MR) were highly correlated with one another (r = 0.64, p < 0.001) and also significantly positively correlated with all four WM subscales."
