# Students who always skip breakfast have increased risk of low achievement on numeracy (RR = 1·78) and reading (RR = 1·63) after adjustment for confounding

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among students who always skipped breakfast, adjusted relative risk of low achievement was greatest for numeracy (RR = 1·78, 95% CI 1·64, 1·94) and reading (RR = 1·63, 95% CI 1·49, 1·77), compared with students who never skip breakfast. [→ Sincovich A 2025](#sincovich-a-2025)

## Evidence

### Sincovich A 2025

Sincovich A, Sechague Monroy N, Smithers LG, Brushe M, Boulton Z, Rozario T, Gregory T. (2025). Breakfast skipping and academic achievement at 8–16 years: a population study in South Australia. Public Health Nutr 28(1), e20. https://doi.org/10.1017/S1368980024002258

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Poisson regression analysis of linked 2021 population data (Wellbeing and Engagement Collection, NAPLAN, school enrolment census) for 28,651 government-school students, adjusting for student, family and community-level confounders with school-level clustering. The adjusted model showed "Greatest risk for low achievement was observed on numeracy (RR = 1·78, 95 % CI 1·64, 1·94)".

> "Greatest risk for low achievement was observed on numeracy (RR = 1·78, 95 % CI 1·64, 1·94) and reading (RR = 1·63, 95 % CI 1·49, 1·77) among students who reported always skipping breakfast."
