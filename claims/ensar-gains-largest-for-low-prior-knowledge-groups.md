Between-group comparison from the seven-group deployment reported in Section 5 and plotted in Figs. 3 and 4: the article states that "0-3 range show notable improvements, with some groups achieving average improve- ments greater than 5 points", i.e. some groups starting with low initial grades gained more than 5 points on average on the 10-point scale.

### Abhishek Purohit 2024 (2)

Abhishek Purohit, José Jorge Christen, Richard Kienhoefer, Simon Armstrong, Maninder Kaur, Araceli Venegas-Gomez. (2024). Qureka! Box - An ENSAR methodology based tool for understanding quantum computing concepts. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2410.21219](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.21219)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Group-level comparison in Section 5: the two undergraduate groups showed the highest grade improvements, which the authors read as validating ENSAR for beginners across different academic streams. No per-group statistics are printed beyond the ranking.

> "Group 3, which included students from Engineering, Business, Law, and Psychology, showed the greatest improvement, followed closely by Group 4, comprised of undergraduates from mixed academic streams."

### Abhishek Purohit 2024 (3)

Abhishek Purohit, José Jorge Christen, Richard Kienhoefer, Simon Armstrong, Maninder Kaur, Araceli Venegas-Gomez. (2024). Qureka! Box - An ENSAR methodology based tool for understanding quantum computing concepts. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2410.21219](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.21219)

The same deployment analysis says the group with the most prior knowledge tends to have lower average improvements, which the authors describe as expected since those starting with higher knowledge have less room for improvement.

> "Group 6 which consisted of quantum technology professionals having prior education in QC and higher average grades in the initial assessment tends to have lower average grade improvements."
