Evidence¶

Lea Waters 2015¶

Lea Waters, Adam Barsky, Amanda Ridd, Kelly Allen. (2015). Contemplative Education: A Systematic, Evidence-Based Review of the effect of Meditation Interventions in Schools. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-014-9258-2

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review's summary of the academic achievement literature: only three studies exist, two using standardised tests and one using student self-assessments; effect sizes were calculable for two studies, with Cohen's d ranging from 0.44 to 0.83.

"In summary, research into school meditation programmes is still in its infancy, and only three published studies have examined the relationship between meditation and academic achievement."

Lea Waters 2015 (2)¶

Lea Waters, Adam Barsky, Amanda Ridd, Kelly Allen. (2015). Contemplative Education: A Systematic, Evidence-Based Review of the effect of Meditation Interventions in Schools. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-014-9258-2

q2 · i2 · review · r2

The review reports this non-random experiment trial (Table 2 lists it as non-random experiment trial). Table 3 prints the effect sizes: composite d=.79, math d=.85, English d=.44 (dIGPP, SDpost). The review attributes the finding to Nidich et al. (2011).

"Nidich et al. (2011) reported strong significant improvements in English and Math scores on the California Standards Test for 125 sixth- and seventh-grade students who underwent 3 months of twice daily (i.e. before and after school) 12-min TM practice in comparison to a randomly assigned control group."
