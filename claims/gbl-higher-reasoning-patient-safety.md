# Game-based learning produced significantly higher reasoning skill scores than the flipped classroom at both two-week and two-month post-tests, with a large effect (partial η² = 0.146)

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Surgical nursing students and nurses taught with puzzle-based game-based learning achieved significantly higher reasoning skill scores in managing patient safety incidents than the flipped classroom control group at both Post-test 1 (p < 0.001) and Post-test 2 (p < 0.001), with a large educational effect (Partial η² = 0.146). [→ Mehrdad M 2025](#mehrdad-m-2025)

## Evidence

### Mehrdad M 2025

Mehrdad M, Jambarsang S, Sadeghian H, Jabinian F, Keshmiri F. (2025). Effects of game-based learning and flipped classroom strategies on performance and reasoning in patient safety education for surgical nursing students. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0334545

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Repeated Measures ANOVA in a quasi-experimental cluster-randomized study of 100 surgical nursing students and nurses (50 per group), reasoning measured by a Key Features examination at baseline, two weeks, and two months. The intervention group outperformed controls with "a high educational effect (Partial η² = 0.146)"; group differences at both post-tests were p < 0.001.

> "The results demonstrated a significant improvement in management reasoning skills among the intervention group compared to the control group over time, with a high educational effect (Partial η² = 0.146)."
