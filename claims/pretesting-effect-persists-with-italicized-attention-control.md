# The pretesting effect persists when attention to key content is equated by italicizing tested sentences in both conditions

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` When all testable sentences were italicized in both conditions, pretested items were still recalled better (M = 71%) than italicized-only items in the extended study condition (M = 54%). [→ Richland 2009](#richland-2009)

## Evidence

### Richland 2009

Richland, L. E., Kornell, N., & Kao, L. S. (2009). The Pretesting Effect: Do Unsuccessful Retrieval Attempts Enhance Learning? Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied, 15(3), 243–257. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016496

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2: 61 undergraduates (2 excluded) read the same passage with all 10 testable sentences italicized in both conditions, equating attention direction. The result shows "recall of tested and italicized items in the test and study condition" was significantly greater, d = 0.61.

> "recall of tested and italicized items in the test and study condition (M ⫽ 71%, SE ⫽ 5.6) was significantly greater than recall of italicized-only items in the extended study condition (M ⫽ 54%, SE ⫽ 3.7), t(57) ⫽ 2.3, p ⬍ .022, d ⫽ 0.61."
