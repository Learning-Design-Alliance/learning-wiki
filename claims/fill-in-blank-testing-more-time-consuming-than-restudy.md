### de Jonge 2015

de Jonge, M., Tabbers, H. K., & Rikers, R. M. J. P. (2015). The Effect of Testing on the Retention of Coherent and Incoherent Text Material. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-015-9300-z

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1 learning-phase cycle counts: restudy 2.85 cycles versus testing 1.60 cycles, "F(1, 62)=39.52, p<.0 01, ηp2=.39", indicating the test was more time consuming.

> "During the second block, participants in the restudy condition studied the text 2.85 times ( SD=.85), while participants in the testing condition went through the test 1.60 times ( SD=.73). Analysis showed that, for the second block, the difference in number of cycles was significant, F(1, 62)=39.52, p<.0 01, ηp2=.39,"

### de Jonge 2015 (2)

de Jonge, M., Tabbers, H. K., & Rikers, R. M. J. P. (2015). The Effect of Testing on the Retention of Coherent and Incoherent Text Material. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-015-9300-z

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Experiment 2 learning-phase cycle counts: restudy 2.29 versus testing 1.45 cycles, "F(1, 62)=21.0, p<.001, ηp2 =.25". Testing-condition participants scored 54% correct on the practice test.

> "During the second block, participants in the restudy condition studied the list of facts 2.29 times (SD=.94), while participants in the testing condition went through the test 1.45 times (SD=.44). As in Experiment 1, taking the test was more time consuming compared to simply restudying the list of facts, F(1, 62)=21.0, p<.001, ηp2 =.25."
