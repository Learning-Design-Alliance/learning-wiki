## Evidence

### Colin S. Wallace 2017

Colin S. Wallace, Timothy G. Chambers, Edward E. Prather. (2017). An item response theory evaluation of the Light and Spectroscopy Concept Inventory national data set. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1709.05255](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1709.05255)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Distribution of IRT-estimated gains for all 3205 students (Fig. 5): minimum -1.8 logits, maximum 4 logits, average 1.1 logits, standard deviation 0.93 logits. The article reports "less than 10%" of students had shifts ≤ 0 logits; no effect size is printed.

> "These data show a range in the shift of abilities, with less than 10% of the assessed population exhibiting a shift ≤ 0 logits, which would be consistent with students moving backward or achieving no improvement in their understanding."
