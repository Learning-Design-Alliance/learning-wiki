# Phonological input during pre-listening vocabulary activities had no significant effect on vocabulary test scores

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students who completed vocabulary activities with phonological input scored similarly on vocabulary tests to those without phonological input. [→ Mihara 2015](#mihara-2015)

## Evidence

### Mihara 2015

Mihara, K. (2015). Effects of Phonological Input as a Pre-Listening Activity on Vocabulary Learning and L2 Listening Comprehension Test Performance. TESL-EJ 19(2). https://tesl-ej.org/pdf/ej74/a1.pdf

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

ANOVA of vocabulary test scores across eight tests for 60 students in two experimental classes found no statistically significant interaction between classes. Mean scores were nearly identical (5.812 vs. 5.794), suggesting "phonological input may not have a great influence on vocabulary learning or vocabulary test scores."

> "The results indicate that the interaction between Classes 1 and 2 was not statistically significant. The students in Class 2, who carried out a vocabulary activity without phonological input and took orthographical-cue vocabulary tests, obtained roughly the same mean scores as did those in Class 1, who performed an activity with phonological input and took phonological-cue vocabulary tests (the mean scores were 5.812 for Class 1 and 5.794 in Class 2, respectively)."
