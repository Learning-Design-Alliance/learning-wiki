## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Transitions between epistemological frames occur quickly, on the order of a few seconds, and can be prompted by external factors such as the instructor’s framing or by disagreement within a group. [→ Bahar Modir 2017](#bahar-modir-2017)

## Evidence

### Bahar Modir 2017

Bahar Modir, John D. Thompson, and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2017). Students’ epistemological framing in quantum mechanics problem solving. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.020108](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.020108)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Observational analysis of video data from group problem-solving episodes, where transitions were identified by locating preceding and following frames. The authors report transition timing is "on the order of a few seconds" and illustrate triggers including the instructor’s correct answer and a groupmate’s bid for a different frame.

> "Broadly speaking, we notice that the timing of transitions is relatively short (on the order of a few seconds, less than length of a few turns at talk)."
