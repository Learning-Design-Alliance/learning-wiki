## Evidence

### Touw 2020

Touw, K. W. J., Vogelaar, B., Thissen, F., Rovers, S., & Resing, W. C. M. (2020). Progression and individual differences in children's series completion after dynamic testing. The British Journal of Educational Psychology, 90(1), 184-205. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12272

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

CRT tree analysis of IRT-based gain scores with initial ability, condition, working memory, gender, and age as predictors; Table 6 shows condition (normalized importance 100.0%) and pre-test score (37.6%) as most important. "condition and initial ability seem to be the most important predictors of children's progression".

> "Children in the training condition can be differentiated further by their initial ability: Children with a lower initial ability showed more improvement from the pre-test to post-test than children with a higher initial ability."

### Touw 2020 (2)

Touw, K. W. J., Vogelaar, B., Thissen, F., Rovers, S., & Resing, W. C. M. (2020). Progression and individual differences in children's series completion after dynamic testing. The British Journal of Educational Psychology, 90(1), 184-205. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12272

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same CRT tree analysis split trained higher-initial-ability children by standardized AWMA Listening Recall score, a finding shown in Figure 6 and stated in the Results text. AWMA listening recall normalized importance was 13.0%.

> "The trained children with a higher initial ability can be differentiated further by their auditory working memory: Those with lower scores for their auditory working memory showed more improvement from the pre-test to post-test than the children with higher scores."
