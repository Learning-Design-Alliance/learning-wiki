# Teams using AI-assisted writing are smaller-sized, with the association stronger for Nature publications

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Poisson regression shows a significant negative relationship between AI usage score and team size (PLoS IRR 0.779; Nature IRR 0.545). [→ Haoyang Wang 2026](#haoyang-wang-2026)

## Evidence

### Haoyang Wang 2026

Haoyang Wang, Mingze Zhang, Yi Bu, Star Xing Zhao, Meijun Liu. (2026). Smaller, Younger, and More Impactful: How AI-Assisted Writing Transforms Research Teams. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27404

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Poisson regression on post-ChatGPT PLoS and Nature publications with author-level controls and time, discipline, and journal fixed effects (Table 3). The Nature coefficient is -0.607 (p<0.01), a 45.5% decrease from no AI assistance to full AI assistance.

> "For the PLoS publications, the coefficient for AI usage score is -0.250 (𝑝<0.01), corresponding to an IRR of 0.779. This indicates that a one-unit increase in AI usage score, representing the extreme shift from no AI assistance to full AI assistance, is associated with a 22.1% (=1-0.779) decrease in team size."

### Haoyang Wang 2026 (2)

Haoyang Wang, Mingze Zhang, Yi Bu, Star Xing Zhao, Meijun Liu. (2026). Smaller, Younger, and More Impactful: How AI-Assisted Writing Transforms Research Teams. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27404

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Propensity score matching (1:1 nearest neighbor without replacement, caliper 0.2 SD) comparing AI-assisted and human-written post-GPT publications. The matched PLoS team-size difference was significant (5.81 vs 6.08), but the Nature difference was "slightly insignificant (𝑝=0.384)".

> "For the Nature publications, the AI-assisted group has a mean team size of 8.50, compared to 8.63 for the human-written (post-GPT) group. The difference between them is -0.131 but slightly insignificant (𝑝=0.384)."
