# Male students report significantly higher overall technological self-efficacy than female students (p = 0.001)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among the 241 students who identified as male or female (two non-binary respondents of the 243 were excluded from the gender comparison), male students reported higher generalized digital confidence (M=4.05) than female students (M=3.89), a statistically significant difference (p=0.001). [→ Nicolas Rodriguez-Alvarez 2026](#nicolas-rodriguez-alvarez-2026)

## Evidence

### Nicolas Rodriguez-Alvarez 2026

Nicolas Rodriguez-Alvarez, Alan Martin Blanch-Marsolini, Samuel Vara-Gutierrez, Hugo Gil-Garcia, Javier Calzon-Dueñas, and Fernando Rodriguez-Merino. (2026). The Illusion of Competence: Self-Perceived Digital Literacy and AI Readiness Among European Secondary Students. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26010 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.26010](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2605.26010)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Independent samples t-test with Welch's correction on the survey's gender-based comparison (n=241 after excluding negligible non-binary responses) found males scored higher, a difference the authors call statistically significant.

> "male students consistently reported higher generalized digital confidence (𝑀= 4.05) compared to their female counterparts (𝑀= 3.89). This difference was found to be statistically significant (𝑝= 0.001)."
