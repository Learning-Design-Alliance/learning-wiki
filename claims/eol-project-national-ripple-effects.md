## Evidence

### Brezis Mayer 2017

Brezis Mayer, Lahat Yael, Frankel Meir, Rubinov Alan, Bohm Davina, Cohen Matan J, Koslowsky Meni, Shalomson Orit, Sprung Charles L, Perry-Mezare Henia, Yahalom Rina, Ziv Amitai. (2017). What can we learn from simulation-based training to improve skills for end-of-life care? Insights from a national project in Israel. Israel Journal of Health Policy Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13584-017-0169-9

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Field observation of unanticipated consequences reported retrospectively by the authors: many institutions enacted guidelines and set up palliative care units, and word-of-mouth spread generated workshop demands from intensive care, dialysis, oncology, emergency and family medicine. The authors state they "cannot determine causality" for media coverage leading to national committees.

> "One participant conducted a study of preparedness to EOL at nursing homes (eventually published [92]) that was presented at the Ministry of Health and called forth a national survey of preparedness to EOL at hospitals."
