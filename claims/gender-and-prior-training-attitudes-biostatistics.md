## Evidence

### Al-Zahrani Sami H. MD 2015

Al-Zahrani Sami H. MD, Al-Khail Bahaa A. Aba MD, DPH. (2015). Resident physician's knowledge and attitudes toward biostatistics and research methods concepts. Saudi Medical Journal. https://doi.org/10.15537/smj.2015.10.11842

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional survey analysis of attitudes by participant characteristics (Table 1): females more likely to hold positive attitudes toward biostatistics (p=0.003), while males scored higher on the knowledge test (p=0.006); no effect sizes printed.

> "Further analysis showed that females were more likely to have a positive attitude toward biostatistics (p=0.003; Table 1) and males scored more in the knowledge test regarding terms used in biostatistics (p=0.006; Table 2)."

### Al-Zahrani Sami H. MD 2015 (2)

Al-Zahrani Sami H. MD, Al-Khail Bahaa A. Aba MD, DPH. (2015). Resident physician's knowledge and attitudes toward biostatistics and research methods concepts. Saudi Medical Journal. https://doi.org/10.15537/smj.2015.10.11842

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the same survey, prior biostatistics and epidemiology training was associated with positive attitude toward biostatistics (p<0.001 in both gender); the article reports significance without an effect size.

> "Physicians with prior training in biostatistics and epidemiology had a positive attitude toward biostatistics (p<0.001 in both gender)."
