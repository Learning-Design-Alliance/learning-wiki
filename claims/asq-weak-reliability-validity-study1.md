# The ASQ shows weak reliability and weak concurrent validity with depression in a large college sample

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In Study 1, ASQ total-score correlations with depression were statistically significant but weak (r = .22), and reliability coefficients were lower than previously reported. [→ Cutrona 1985](#cutrona-1985)

## Evidence

### Cutrona 1985

Cutrona, C. E., Russell, D., & Jones, R. D. (1985). Cross-Situational Consistency in Causal Attributions: Does Attributional Style Exist? Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 47(5), 1043-1058. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.47.5.1043

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Reliability and validity evaluation in Study 1, a sample of 1,133 introductory psychology students completing the ASQ and BDI. The total-score correlation with depression was r = .22 (df = 1131), far below the .48 previously reported, and coefficient alpha for the total score was .66 (internality .33).

> "In particular, note the correlation between the total score on the ASQ and depression (r [1131] = .22). This value is substantially lower than the value of .48 reported by Seligman et al. (1979)."
