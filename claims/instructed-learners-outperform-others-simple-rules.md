# Instructed learners outperform all other condition groups in learning simple second language rules

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Adult ESL learners trained under instructed conditions outperformed learners in implicit, incidental, and rule-search conditions on simple (easy) rules. [→ Peter Robinson 1996](#peter-robinson-1996)

## Evidence

### Peter Robinson 1996

Peter Robinson. (1996). Learning Simple and Complex Second Language Rules Under Implicit, Incidental, Rule-Search, and Instructed Conditions. Studies in Second Language Acquisition, Volume 18, Issue 1, pp. 27-67. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263100014674

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Randomized four-condition experiment with 104 adult ESL learners; the results contrast names instructed learners against all other conditions for simple rules. The article reports the advantage without a printed effect size.

> "Implicit learners do not outperform other learners on complex rules, but instructed learners outperform all others in learning simple rules."
