# Lab instruction shifts students’ instrument choices toward the instrument with lower measurement uncertainty for items where students initially preferred the higher-uncertainty instrument

## Evidence

### Micol Alemani 2026

Micol Alemani, Karel Kok, Eva Philippaki. (2026). Students’ reasoning in choosing measurement instruments in an introductory physics laboratory course. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2603.16638](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2603.16638)

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Pre-/post questionnaire in the introductory lab course at the University of Potsdam: pre-test 68% chose the digital caliper, post-test 57% chose the micrometer, "which is the instrument with lower uncertainty"; the printed effect size is w = 0 .34 (χ2(2) = 39.55, p <0.001).

> "For this item, we found a statistical significant difference between the distributions of students’ answers in the pre- and post-test [χ2(2) = 39.55, p <0.001]. This difference has a medium size effect ( w = 0 .34)."

### Micol Alemani 2026 (2)

Micol Alemani, Karel Kok, Eva Philippaki. (2026). Students’ reasoning in choosing measurement instruments in an introductory physics laboratory course. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2603.16638](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2603.16638)

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r1`

For item 3 the article reports post-test choices "strongly shifting from the analog caliper (18 %) to the analog micrometer (75 %)", the lower-uncertainty instrument; the printed effect size is w = 0 .27 (χ2(2) = 24 .86, p < 0.001).

> "In the post-test we found a statistically different distribution [ χ2(2) = 24 .86, p < 0.001] with small to medium size effect ( w = 0 .27) with respect to the pre-test."

### Micol Alemani 2026 (3)

Micol Alemani, Karel Kok, Eva Philippaki. (2026). Students’ reasoning in choosing measurement instruments in an introductory physics laboratory course. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2603.16638](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2603.16638)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

For item 2 students unanimously preferred the digital caliper (82% pre, 87% post), the lower-uncertainty instrument; the pre-post difference was not statistically significant, and equivalence was not tested.

> "The small difference in the distributions of students’ answers observable by comparing the pre- and post-answers is not statistically significant ( p > 0.001)."
