## Evidence

### Kellman 2009

Kellman, P. J., & Garrigan, P. (2009). Perceptual Learning and Human Expertise. Physics of Life Reviews, 6(2), 53-84. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2008.12.001

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Goldstone (2000), training on sorting 2D shapes formed from 5 separate parts into categories based on single components, conjunctions, or ordered conjunctions of components.

> "Performance, measured by response time, improved with training, and although categorization based on a single feature was fastest, the greatest improvement due to training was for categorization based on 5 parts in a specific order."

### Kellman 2009 (2)

Kellman, P. J., & Garrigan, P. (2009). Perceptual Learning and Human Expertise. Physics of Life Reviews, 6(2), 53-84. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2008.12.001

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports that the cited response-time study indicated improvement exceeded predictions from component processing, which the review describes as strong evidence of new perceptual units.

> "A detailed study of response times indicated that the improvement based on unitization was greater than would be predicted based on improvements in processing of individual components. This result furnished strong evidence that new perceptual units were being used."
