## Evidence

### Lee V 2026

Lee V, Kuncel NR, Sackett PR. (2026). Graduate grade inflation at a U.S. research-intensive university: A 22-year longitudinal analysis. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0341315

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Model 8 added degree level and degree level × natural-spline time interactions to the multilevel models; the omnibus joint test across spline terms was significant. Individual interaction coefficients were nonsignificant, which the authors attribute to spline decomposition of time.

> "Results showed that the model with the interactive effects had significantly better fit (χ2 [4] = 20.55, p < .001), providing support for the idea that the magnitude of grade inflation varies by degree level."

### Lee V 2026 (2)

Lee V, Kuncel NR, Sackett PR. (2026). Graduate grade inflation at a U.S. research-intensive university: A 22-year longitudinal analysis. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0341315

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

In the discussion, the authors report a printed correlation of r = −.48 between program intercepts and slopes, offered as an explanation for why master's inflation slightly exceeded doctoral inflation between 1999 and 2020.

> "Indeed, individual intercepts and slopes tend to be negatively correlated such that programs with higher starting intercepts (i.e., mean GPA in year 1999) tend to show lesser growth over time (r = −.48)."
