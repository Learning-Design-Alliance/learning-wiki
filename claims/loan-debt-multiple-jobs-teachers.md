# Over a third (36.7%) of borrowing teachers have worked multiple jobs because of student loans, and the share rises with monthly repayment amounts

## Evidence

### García 2023

García, E., Wei, W., Patrick, S. K., Leung-Gagné, M., & DiNapoli, M. A., Jr. (2023). In debt: Student loan burdens among teachers. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/497.986

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

NTPS 2020–21 question to borrowing teachers on employment decisions (Figure 7): "more than one third of borrowing teachers (36.7%) reported having ever worked multiple jobs at the same time because of student loans." No fewer than 1 in 3 in each subgroup.

> "more than one third of borrowing teachers (36.7%) reported having ever worked multiple jobs at the same time because of student loans."

### García 2023 (2)

García, E., Wei, W., Patrick, S. K., Leung-Gagné, M., & DiNapoli, M. A., Jr. (2023). In debt: Student loan burdens among teachers. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/497.986

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Figure 8 analysis (median monthly repayment $280) shows repayment amount is correlated with multiple-job holding: among teachers with 16–20 years of experience, "45.4% of teachers whose repayment amount exceeds the median" versus "31.1% of teachers paying amounts below the median."

> "larger shares of teachers have worked multiple jobs if their monthly repayment amount is higher than the median compared to teachers paying amounts below the median. For example, among teachers who have 16–20 years of experience, 45.4% of teachers whose repayment amount exceeds the median reported having worked at more than one job simultaneously, compared with 31.1% of teachers paying amounts below the median."
