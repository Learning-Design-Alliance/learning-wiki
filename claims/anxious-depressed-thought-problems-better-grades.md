# After adjustment for comorbid symptoms, more anxious/depressed and thought problems are associated with better, not worse, school grades

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2022 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In fully adjusted models, students reporting more anxious/depressed and thought problems were less likely to perform poorly, with ORs below 1 (e.g., anxious/depressed 0.93–0.95 across subjects). [→ Pagerols M 2022](#pagerols-m-2022)

## Evidence

### Pagerols M 2022

Pagerols M, Prat R, Rivas C, Español-Martín G, Puigbó J, Pagespetit È, Haro JM, Ramos-Quiroga JA, Casas M, Bosch R. (2022). The impact of psychopathology on academic performance in school-age children and adolescents. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-08242-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Mixed-effects ordinal logistic regression (model 2, n = 6602 or similar per table) controlling all CBCL scales simultaneously; anxious/depressed ORs of 0.93–0.95 and thought problems ORs of 0.82–0.87 were significant, meaning students were "less likely to perform poorly".

> "students who reported more anxious/depressed and thought problems were less likely to perform poorly, while those with increased levels of attention problems and delinquent behavior had higher risk for academic impairment"
