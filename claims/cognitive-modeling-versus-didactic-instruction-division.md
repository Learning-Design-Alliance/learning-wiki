# Cognitive modeling raised division skill more than didactic instruction while both raised self-efficacy equally, with self-efficacy mediating treatment effects

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Schunk's (1981) long-division experiment, cognitive modeling and didactic instruction raised self-efficacy equally, but modeling produced greater skill gains and more accurate efficacy judgments. [→ Schunk 1995](#schunk-1995)

## Evidence

### Schunk 1995

Schunk, D. H. (1995). Self-efficacy, motivation, and performance. Journal of Applied Sport Psychology, 7(2), 112-137. http://www.taylorandfrancis.com/

`q3 · i?`

Experiment with low-achieving children in a long-division instructional program comparing cognitive modeling with didactic instruction; skill, persistence, and self-efficacy were assessed before and after instruction. Didactic subjects "tended to overestimate what they could do."

> "Cognitive modeling and didactic instruction raised self-efficacy equally well; however, modeling led to greater gains in division skill and to more accurate perceptions of capabilities"
