## Evidence

### Reavley Nicola J. 2018

Reavley Nicola J., Morgan Amy J., Fischer Julie-Anne, Kitchener Betty, Bovopoulos Nataly, Jorm Anthony F. (2018). Effectiveness of eLearning and blended modes of delivery of Mental Health First Aid training in the workplace: randomised controlled trial. BMC Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-018-1888-3

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r3`

Open-ended responses scored 0-12 against the MHFA Action Plan, double-coded (ICC 0.88 depression, 0.94 PTSD), analysed with mixed-effects models in the 608-participant RCT; the quoted effect sizes are versus the PFA control.

> "There were greater improvements in quality of helping intentions for depression and PTSD in both the MHFA eLearning and blended MHFA groups compared to PFA eLearning. These differences were large in size for depression (d = 1.16 and d = 1.43 respectively) and PTSD (d = 1.17 and d = 1.33 respectively)."

### Reavley Nicola J. 2018 (2)

Reavley Nicola J., Morgan Amy J., Fischer Julie-Anne, Kitchener Betty, Bovopoulos Nataly, Jorm Anthony F. (2018). Effectiveness of eLearning and blended modes of delivery of Mental Health First Aid training in the workplace: randomised controlled trial. BMC Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-018-1888-3

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r3`

Planned contrast between the two MHFA modes from the same mixed-effects analysis; the advantage for the blended mode was confined to the depression vignette.

> "There were also significantly greater improvements (of small effect size) in the blended MHFA group than in the eLearning MHFA group for depression (d = 0.28), although not for PTSD."
