## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For females, overall and level-specific NCEA achievement points were significantly higher for those from food-secure households (e.g. level 1 p = 0.021, level 2 p = 0.044, level 3 p = 0.035); for males the same comparisons were not significant. [→ Leon I. 2023](#leon-i-2023)

## Evidence

### Leon I. 2023

Leon I., Tautolo E.-S., Plank L.D., Rush E. (2023). Pacific Islands Families Study: Household Food Security during Pregnancy and Secondary School Educational Achievement. Nutrients. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu15194131

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Mann–Whitney U-tests of median NCEA achievement points by food security status (PIF definition, Table 4): females food secure vs food insecure p = 0.032 overall, 0.021 level 1, 0.044 level 2, 0.035 level 3; males p = 0.332 overall, 0.253, 0.067, 0.492. The effect did not replicate across sex.

> "For both females and males, achievement points overall and at each level were higher if they came from a food-secure household during gestation (Table 4 and Table 5). However, statistically significant differences at each level of achievement were only seen in females, not males."
