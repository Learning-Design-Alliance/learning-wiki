# Teacher satisfaction with SBI did not differ significantly between experienced and novice implementer cohorts

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Two-sample t-tests on the Teacher Satisfaction Survey found no statistically significant differences between Cohort 1 and Cohort 2 teachers' attitudes toward SBI. [→ Jitendra 2016](#jitendra-2016)

## Evidence

### Jitendra 2016

Jitendra, A. K., Harwell, M. R., Karl, S. R., Dupuis, D. N., Simonson, G., Slater, S. C., & Lein, A. E. (2016). Schema-based instruction: The effects of experienced and novice teacher implementers on seventh-grade students' proportional problem solving. Learning and Instruction, 44, 53-64. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2016.03.001

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Two-sample t-tests of Teacher Satisfaction Survey scores (22 items, coefficient alpha 0.94) comparing Cohort 1 and Cohort 2 teachers. The article reports "results were not significantly different (t = .043, df = 71, p > .05)," with both cohorts showing similarly high mean scores on a 1-to-4 scale.

> "With regard to teachers' attitudes, results were not significantly different (t = .043, df = 71, p > .05). On a scale of 1 to 4, Cohort 1 teachers had a mean score of 3.30 (SD = 0.46) and Cohort 2 teachers had a mean score of 3.29 (SD = 0.41)."
