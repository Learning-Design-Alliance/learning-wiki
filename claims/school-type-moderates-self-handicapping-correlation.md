# School type moderates the self-handicapping–achievement correlation, which is stronger in elementary and middle school than in high school and university

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` The negative correlation is stronger in elementary (r = -.29) and middle school (r = -.34) samples than in high school (r = -.23) and university (r = -.18) samples. [→ Schwinger 2014](#schwinger-2014)

## Evidence

### Schwinger 2014

Schwinger, M., Wirthwein, L., Lemmer, G., & Steinmayr, R. (2014, February 10). Academic Self-Handicapping and Achievement: A Meta-Analysis. Journal of Educational Psychology. Advance online publication. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/a0035832

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Mixed-effects moderator analysis across the 49 effect sizes. School type showed the largest moderator effect ("QB ⫽ 18.83, p ⬍ .01"), with category correlations of r = -.29 (elementary), r = -.34 (middle school), r = -.23 (high school), and r = -.18 (university) showing nonoverlapping confidence intervals between younger and older samples.

> "The largest moderator effect was found for "school type" (QB ⫽ 18.83, p ⬍ .01). The confidence intervals of the categories "elementary school" (r ⫽ ⫺.29; 95% CI: ⫺.35 ⱕ r ⱕ ⫺.23) and "middle school" (r ⫽ ⫺.34; 95% CI: ⫺.41 ⱕ r ⱕ ⫺.25) compared to "high school" (r ⫽ ⫺.23; 95% CI: ⫺.26 ⱕ r ⱕ ⫺.18) and "university students" (r ⫽ ⫺.18; 95% CI: ⫺.21 ⱕ r ⱕ ⫺.14) did not overlap, indicating a statistically significant difference between these correlations."
