### E. F. Redish 2014

E. F. Redish, C. Bauer, K. L. Carleton, T. J. Cooke, M. Cooper, C. H. Crouch, B. W. Dreyfus, B. Geller, J. Giannini, J. S. Gouvea, M. W. Klymkowsky, W. Losert, K. Moore, J. Presson, V. Sawtelle, K. `q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The authors' finding from extensive biologists-physicists-chemists discussions and study of students in biology and physics classes: biology favors "real specific systems" with realistic detail and few equations, while physics treats "mathematical modeling is considered fundamental" with idealized toy models.

> "Biology faculty and physics faculty tend to have dramatically different views about the nature and structure of the knowledge that is appropriate to teach in introductory classes."
