### Thorpe Alistair PhD; Kassianos Angelos P PhD; Plackett Ruth PhD; Krishnamurthy Vinodh PhD; Kambouri Maria A PhD; Sheringham Jessica FFPH 2025

Thorpe Alistair PhD; Kassianos Angelos P PhD; Plackett Ruth PhD; Krishnamurthy Vinodh PhD; Kambouri Maria A PhD; Sheringham Jessica FFPH, PhD. (2025). Comparison of Physician Assistant and Medical Students' Clinical Reasoning Processes Using an Online Patient Simulation Tool to Support Clinical Reasoning (eCREST): Mixed Methods Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/68981

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Qualitative coding of free-text reflections to the question on what students would take to clinical work, coded into six themes; chi-square comparisons of medical versus PA reflections found the open-mindedness difference (P=.01).

> "A greater proportion of medical student reflections related to open-mindedness and flexibility (48% vs 26%; P=.01)."

### Thorpe Alistair PhD; Kassianos Angelos P PhD; Plackett Ruth PhD; Krishnamurthy Vinodh PhD; Kambouri Maria A PhD; Sheringham Jessica FFPH 2025 (2)

Thorpe Alistair PhD; Kassianos Angelos P PhD; Plackett Ruth PhD; Krishnamurthy Vinodh PhD; Kambouri Maria A PhD; Sheringham Jessica FFPH, PhD. (2025). Comparison of Physician Assistant and Medical Students' Clinical Reasoning Processes Using an Online Patient Simulation Tool to Support Clinical Reasoning (eCREST): Mixed Methods Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/68981

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Qualitative coding of free-text reflections: PA students more often reported knowledge-related takeaways (87% vs 70%; P=.03), which the authors attribute to PAs having had approximately 1.5 years of clinical training versus medical students' approximately 5 years.

> "A greater proportion of PA student reflections related to improving knowledge related to the clinical content of the case (87% vs 70%; P=.03)."
