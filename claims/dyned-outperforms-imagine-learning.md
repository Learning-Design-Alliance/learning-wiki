# DynEd has a significantly greater effect than Imagine Learning on several subtests in both years

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Head-to-head comparison of the two randomly assigned packages shows DynEd significantly outperforms Imagine Learning on several subtests in years one and two, while Imagine never significantly outperforms DynEd. [→ Sarah Humpage and Horacio Álvarez-Marinelli 2014](#sarah-humpage-and-horacio-alvarez-marinelli-2014)

## Evidence

### Sarah Humpage and Horacio Álvarez-Marinelli 2014

Sarah Humpage and Horacio Álvarez-Marinelli. (2014). Teachers’ Helpers: Experimental Evidence from Costa Rica on Computers for English Language Learning. SREE Spring 2014 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED562994

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Full-sample difference-in-difference comparison of the two treatment groups (n=331), the sample in which inclusion criteria were applied identically to both packages. Table 4c shows Dyned*t of 0.409*** (picture vocabulary) and 0.540*** (understanding directions) in year one and 0.270* and 0.383** in year two.

> "When comparing DynEd against Imagine Learning, DynEd has a significantly greater effect on several subtests in the first and second years. Imagine does not have a significantly greater effect on any subtest."
