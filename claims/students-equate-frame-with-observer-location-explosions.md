## Evidence

### Rachel E. Scherr 2001

Rachel E. Scherr, Peter S. Shaffer, and Stamatis Vokos. (2001). Student understanding of time in special relativity: simultaneity and reference frames. https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1371254

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

On the Explosions question administered after instruction on written examinations, students interpreted the question about a reference frame as being about a single observer. "The student seems to have interpreted" the frame question as an observer question, treating a set of observers at rest relative to one another as unable to agree on simultaneity.

> "The student seems to have interpreted the question 'Is there a frame in which the events are simultaneous?' to mean 'Is there an observer who sees the events at the same time?'"

### Rachel E. Scherr 2001 (2)

Rachel E. Scherr, Peter S. Shaffer, and Stamatis Vokos. (2001). Student understanding of time in special relativity: simultaneity and reference frames. https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1371254

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The Explosions question was given in written form to introductory students in sophomore modern physics and junior-level electricity and magnetism courses after instruction, and during interviews with 5 advanced undergraduates and 12 graduate students. "The percentage of correct responses on the written question was 40%."

> "The percentage of correct responses on the written question was 40%. In the interviews, 3 of the 5 undergraduates and 7 of the 12 graduate students answered correctly."
