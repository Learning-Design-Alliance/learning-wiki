## Evidence

### Singh 2016

Singh, C. (2016). Student Difficulties with Quantum Mechanics Formalism. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.075](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.075)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Written survey responses to the Hamiltonian eigenvalue statement. "Eleven percent (11%) of the students answering this question" held this measurement misconception; some agreed and some disagreed with the statement on that basis.

> "Eleven percent (11%) of the students answering this question believed (incorrectly) that any statement involv- ing a Hamiltonian operator acting on a state is a state- ment about the measurement of energy."

### Singh 2016 (2)

Singh, C. (2016). Student Difficulties with Quantum Mechanics Formalism. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.075](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.075)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Individual interviews about the Hamiltonian question reveal over-generalization of the Hψ = Eψ reasoning to any observable, attributing ˆQψ = λψ to measurement; the article notes this holds only when ψ is an eigenstate of ˆQ.

> "Individual interviews related to this question suggest that some students believed that whenever an operator ˆQ corresponding to a physical observable Q acts on any state ψ, it will yield the corresponding eigenvalue λ and the same state back"
