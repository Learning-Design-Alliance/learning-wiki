## Subclaims
`q3 i?` GPT-4 could not score accurately when students provided multiple possibilities, missed incorrect transmission probabilities, and failed to recognize accurate vocabulary absent from the rubric. [→ Olvet DM 2026](#olvet-dm-2026)

## Evidence

### Olvet DM 2026

Olvet DM, Kruidering M, Fulton TB, Truong B, Endo K, Lucito R, Willey JM. (2026). Can Generative Artificial Intelligence Reliably Score Open-Ended Question Assessments in Undergraduate Medical Education? Medical Science Educator 36:1539–1552. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40670-026-02638-2

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Error pattern analysis from the Results, in which at least two faculty reviewed responses scored differently by faculty and GPT-4, using GPT-4's generated feedback to identify reasons for discrepancies on question 1A.

> "GPT-4 was unable to score accurately when students provided multiple possibilities. Also, GPT-4 often didn’t recognize when a student provided the incorrect transmission probability for Stephen’s sister or Alex."

### Olvet DM 2026 (2)

Olvet DM, Kruidering M, Fulton TB, Truong B, Endo K, Lucito R, Willey JM. (2026). Can Generative Artificial Intelligence Reliably Score Open-Ended Question Assessments in Undergraduate Medical Education? Medical Science Educator 36:1539–1552. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40670-026-02638-2

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Findings of the faculty error review in the Results section. The article also reports faculty missed minor errors and sometimes gave full credit when students provided only one of two critical answers on question 2A.

> "We found that faculty were overly generous graders and awarded credit for responses that were true but did not answer the question. Students sometimes used confusing language, which also made it difficult for faculty to score."
