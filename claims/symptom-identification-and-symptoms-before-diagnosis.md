## Evidence

### Schwarz P 2025

Schwarz P, Hellmers S, Spanknebel S, Immel D, Hurlemann R, Hein A. (2025). Comparing Patient Simulation With a Humanoid Robot or a Human Actor in Terms of Training Success and Acceptance: Pilot Questionnaire Study. JMIR Formative Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/70363

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pilot questionnaire results (Table 2): one participant per group failed to identify all symptoms. Descriptive percentages only; no statistical test printed.

> "In total, 90% (9/10) of the participants were able to identify all symptoms shown in the video. A total of 91% (10/11) of the participants were able to identify all symptoms shown by the robot."

### Schwarz P 2025 (2)

Schwarz P, Hellmers S, Spanknebel S, Immel D, Hurlemann R, Hein A. (2025). Comparing Patient Simulation With a Humanoid Robot or a Human Actor in Terms of Training Success and Acceptance: Pilot Questionnaire Study. JMIR Formative Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/70363

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Questionnaire item on number of symptoms viewed before diagnosing (Table 2): robot group mean 3.5 (SD 1.16) versus video group mean 4.2 (SD 1.3). The article does not test this difference for significance.

> "In the patient simulation by the human actor in the video, 20% (2/10) of the participants made a diagnosis after 2 symptoms were presented, and 10% (1/10) of the participants made the diagnosis after 3 symptoms. The remaining 70% (7/10) of the participants were shown all 5 symptoms before they made a diagnosis."
