## Evidence

### Broemmel et al. 2010

Amy D. Broemmel, Mary Jane Moran, and Deborah A. Wooten. (2010). The Impact of Animated Books on the Vocabulary and Language Development of Preschool-Aged Children in Two School Settings. University of Tennessee. http://www.movingpicturebooks.com/downloads/the-alphabet-songbook.php

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Three-week quasi-experimental pilot in four mixed-age preschool classrooms at two sites; paired sample t-tests and ANCOVA on curriculum-based vocabulary measures. The article reports "significant for both the control and the experimental groups" and no significant treatment effect (F[1, 20] = 1.77, p = .199, η² = .081).

> "Vocabulary analyses indicated that student vocabulary growth from pretelling to retelling was significant for both the control and the experimental groups."

### Broemmel et al. 2010 (2)

Amy D. Broemmel, Mary Jane Moran, and Deborah A. Wooten. (2010). The Impact of Animated Books on the Vocabulary and Language Development of Preschool-Aged Children in Two School Settings. University of Tennessee. http://www.movingpicturebooks.com/downloads/the-alphabet-songbook.php

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

ANCOVA of retelling vocabulary with pretelling vocabulary as covariate, analyzed for 23 children. The covariate was significantly related to retelling vocabulary (F = 22.165, p = .000, η² = .526); the treatment effect was not significant (F[1, 20] = 1.77, p = .199).

> "However, no significantly different effects on retelling vocabulary (F[1, 20] = 1.77, p = .199) were found between the control and experimental groups (see Table 3)."
