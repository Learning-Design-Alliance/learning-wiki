# Students rarely spontaneously reflect on their SoV solutions in either Cartesian or spherical geometries

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2015

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2015). Upper-division student difficulties with Separation of Variables. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020131)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Exam analysis of Cartesian SoV solutions against the three reflective checks (units, boundary conditions, Laplace's equation): "very few of our students (N=2 of 234) made explicit, spontaneous attempts reﬂect on their solution using any of these checks". The article notes this is a lower bound since checks may not be written down.

> "Overall, we found that very few of our students (N=2 of 234) made explicit, spontaneous attempts reﬂect on their solution using any of these checks."

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2015 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2015). Upper-division student difficulties with Separation of Variables. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020131)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Exam analysis of spherical SoV solutions found rare explicit spontaneous checking: "spontaneous attempts to check their ﬁnal expressions (7%, N=27 of 397) and the majority of these did so only by checking boundary conditions (70%, N=19 of 27)".

> "spontaneous attempts to check their ﬁnal expressions (7%, N=27 of 397) and the majority of these did so only by checking boundary conditions (70%, N=19 of 27)"
