# Minority graduation rates at flagships improved relative to white students from 2005 to 2008, with some institutions closing gaps substantially

## Evidence

### Haycock 2010

Haycock, K., Lynch, M., & Engle, J. (2010). Opportunity Adrift: Our Flagship Universities Are Straying From Their Public Mission. The Education Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED507851

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Ed Trust analysis of IPEDS 2005 and 2008 Graduation Rate Survey data (Figure 8): minority six-year rates rose from 58.0% to 61.1% while white rates rose from 69.3% to 70.5%, moving the success ratio "from 0.84 to 0.87."

> "In 2008, minority freshmen graduated at 87 percent of the rate of white freshmen, a slight improvement over the 84 percent rate in 2005."

### Haycock 2010 (2)

Haycock, K., Lynch, M., & Engle, J. (2010). Opportunity Adrift: Our Flagship Universities Are Straying From Their Public Mission. The Education Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED507851

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Institution-level analysis cited as especially notable gap-closing; the report states SUNY Buffalo's graduation rates for minority and white students alike are above national averages, showing it is possible to "combine excellence and equity."

> "In 2005, minority students at SUNY Buffalo only graduated at 67 percent the rate of their white classmates, but by 2008, minority students graduated at 93 percent the rate of white students."
