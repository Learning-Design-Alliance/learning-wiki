# On loss trials, smaller pupil dilation predicted response change in TD participants while larger dilation predicted it in FXS participants, with no robust effect in DS or WS

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In TD participants, smaller pupil dilation was associated with a higher likelihood of response change on loss trials (b = -0.51). [→ Hallman 2026](#hallman-2026)
`q3 i?` In FXS individuals the opposite pattern held: larger pupil dilation was associated with a higher likelihood of changing response (b = 1.24), with weak evidence (BF = 2.80). [→ Hallman 2026 (2)](#hallman-2026-2)
`q3 i?` No robust evidence for pupil dilation predicting response change was found for DS (BF = 0.45) or WS (BF = 0.36). [→ Hallman 2026 (3)](#hallman-2026-3)

## Evidence

### Hallman 2026

Hallman, A. E. Z., Willfors, C., Frick, M. A., Nordgren, A. & Kleberg, J. L. (2026). Comparing feedback learning, pupil dilation, and gaze allocation in intellectual disability. npj Science of Learning.

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r?`

GLMM fitted on loss-feedback trials only with varying intercepts for participants, Results section, run separately for each syndrome group.

> "Pupil dilation affected response behavior in TD participants, suggesting that smaller pupil dilation was associated with a higher likelihood of response change ( b = −0.51, 95% CI = [ −0.85, −0.18], BF = 9.64, pd =1 )."

### Hallman 2026 (2)

Hallman, A. E. Z., Willfors, C., Frick, M. A., Nordgren, A. & Kleberg, J. L. (2026). Comparing feedback learning, pupil dilation, and gaze allocation in intellectual disability. npj Science of Learning.

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Same loss-trial GLMM, FXS group, Results section. The BF of 2.80 falls in the moderate evidence range per the article's guidelines, so the effect is only weakly supported.

> "The opposite pattern was observed in FXS individuals, where a larger pupil dilation was associated with a higher likelihood of changing response on loss trials (b = 1.24, 95% CI = [0.02, 2.63], BF = 2.80,pd = 0.98)."

### Hallman 2026 (3)

Hallman, A. E. Z., Willfors, C., Frick, M. A., Nordgren, A. & Kleberg, J. L. (2026). Comparing feedback learning, pupil dilation, and gaze allocation in intellectual disability. npj Science of Learning.

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Null result from the same loss-trial GLMMs for the DS and WS groups, Results section. Equivalence was not tested; the article reports absence of robust evidence rather than a measured null effect size.

> "For WS and DS, there was no robust evidence for an effect regarding pupil dilation predicting response change on loss trials (DS: b = 0.43, 95% CI = [−0.52, 1.45], BF = 0.45, pd = 0.81; WS: b = −0.33, 95% CI = [ −1.16, 0.46], BF = 0.36, pd = 0.79)."
