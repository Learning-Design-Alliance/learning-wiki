# Students incorrectly claimed that two orthogonal polarizers in the MZI paths differ from a single polarizer only in that fewer photons reach the detectors

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students treated a setup with two orthogonal polarizers, one in each MZI path, as equivalent to a single polarizer except for reduced photon counts, missing that interference is destroyed. [→ Emily Marshman and Chandralekha Singh 2014](#emily-marshman-and-chandralekha-singh-2014)

## Evidence

### Emily Marshman and Chandralekha Singh 2014

Emily Marshman and Chandralekha Singh. (2014). Developing an Interactive Tutorial on a Quantum Eraser. 2014 PERC Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.040

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Think-aloud interview finding during QuILT development: students saw the two-polarizer setup as different from one polarizer only "except that fewer photons would reach the detectors." In the correct analysis the orthogonal polarizers collapse the path state, WPI is known, and interference is destroyed.

> "Students often incorrectly claimed that the effect of placing two orthogonal polarizers in the two paths of the MZI is not different from the effect of a single polarizer in one path except that fewer photons would reach the detectors."
