# Solvable judgments of solvability discriminate solvable from unsolvable anagrams even after excluding already-solved judgments

> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r3` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` S JOSs discriminate between solvable and unsolvable anagrams above chance even when anagrams that received AS JOSs are excluded from analysis. [→ Burton 2023](#burton-2023)

## Evidence

### Burton 2023

Burton, O. R., Bodner, G. E., Williamson, P., & Arnold, M. M. (2023). How accurate and predictive are judgments of solvability? Explorations in a two-phase anagram solving paradigm. Metacognition and Learning, 18, 1–35. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09313-y

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r3`

Experiment 1 (N = 100 MTurk participants) two-phase anagram paradigm with 16/8/4/2 s durations across four blocks. Repeated-measures ANOVA showed hits exceeded false alarms for AS + S JOSs, η²p = .81 for the discrimination main effect. "averaged across blocks, hits (M = .73, SD = .16) were significantly greater than false alarms".

> "Participants’ AS + S JOSs w ere highly discriminating; averaged across blocks, hits (M = .73, SD = .16) w ere significantly greater than false alarms (M = .24, SD = .18)."
