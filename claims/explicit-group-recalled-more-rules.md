## Evidence

### Van Brakel-Van Lobenstein 2025

Van Brakel-Van Lobenstein, N., Van Leeuwen-Prins, S. T., Verdoes, L., De Waal, G. M., Kamsteeg, M. C. J., Oudejans, R. R. D., Devereaux, J., van Zaalen, Y., Dikken, J., & Renden, P. G. (2025). Teaching nursing skills without detailed protocols: effects of an implicit learning strategy in nursing education. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-025-10421-y

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Study 2 recall check administered one week after the retention and transfer tests to the 52 students with complete data. The t-test showed the implicit group memorized fewer movement-related rules, which the authors interpret as greater reliance on working memory during explicit practice.

> "The Independent Samples t-test on Movement-related rules revealed a significant difference between groups (p < 0.05, d = 0.78), showing that the implicit group memorized fewer movement-related rules about bandaging, 1 week after the retention and transfer test."

### Van Brakel-Van Lobenstein 2025 (2)

Van Brakel-Van Lobenstein, N., Van Leeuwen-Prins, S. T., Verdoes, L., De Waal, G. M., Kamsteeg, M. C. J., Oudejans, R. R. D., Devereaux, J., van Zaalen, Y., Dikken, J., & Renden, P. G. (2025). Teaching nursing skills without detailed protocols: effects of an implicit learning strategy in nursing education. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-025-10421-y

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Study 3 replicated the recall check with 27 students from the 2022–2023 cohort who had trained with minimal instructions, compared against Study 2's explicit group. The implicit group again memorized fewer movement-related rules (d = 0.80), one week after testing.

> "The Independent Samples t-test on movement-related rules revealed a significant difference between groups (p < 0.05, d = 0.80), showing that the implicit group memorized fewer movement-related rules about bandaging, 1 week after the retention and transfer test."
