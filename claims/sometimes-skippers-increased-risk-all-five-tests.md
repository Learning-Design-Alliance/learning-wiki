## Evidence

### Sincovich A 2025

Sincovich A, Sechague Monroy N, Smithers LG, Brushe M, Boulton Z, Rozario T, Gregory T. (2025). Breakfast skipping and academic achievement at 8–16 years: a population study in South Australia. Public Health Nutr 28(1), e20. https://doi.org/10.1017/S1368980024002258

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Adjusted Poisson regression (Table 3) in the same 28,651-student linked population dataset, with never skippers as reference. The article reports "Students who sometimes skipped breakfast were also at increased risk for low achievement" across all five tests, with the quoted adjusted RRs.

> "Students who sometimes skipped breakfast were also at increased risk for low achievement, with risk highest for numeracy (RR = 1·36, 95 % CI 1·27, 1·45), reading (RR = 1·32, 95 % CI 1·23, 1·41) and grammar (RR = 1·32, 95 % CI 1·25, 1·40) test scores, though not as higher risk as that among students who reported skipping breakfast every day."
