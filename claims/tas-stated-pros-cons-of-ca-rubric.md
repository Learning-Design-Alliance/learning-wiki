## Evidence

### Edit Yerushalmi 2016

Edit Yerushalmi, Ryan Sayer, Emily Marshman, Charles Henderson and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Physics graduate teaching assistants' beliefs about a grading rubric: Lessons learned. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.097](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.097)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Coding of TAs' written pros and cons on the grading worksheet (two researchers coded until agreement greater than 90%) found "Over 70% of the TAs noted that the CA-inspired rubric encourages students to use good PS practices and over 30% of the TAs said that it allows the instructor to identify specific student difficulties".

> "Over 70% of the TAs noted that the CA-inspired rubric encourages students to use good PS practices and over 30% of the TAs said that it allows the instructor to identify specific student difficulties."

### Edit Yerushalmi 2016 (2)

Edit Yerushalmi, Ryan Sayer, Emily Marshman, Charles Henderson and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Physics graduate teaching assistants' beliefs about a grading rubric: Lessons learned. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.097](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.097)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

The same coded worksheet analysis found "over half of the TAs mentioned that one con of the rubric was that it was too constraining"; these TAs were usually uncomfortable taking off points when the final answer was correct and wanted freedom to grade as they saw appropriate.

> "over half of the TAs mentioned that one con of the rubric was that it was too constraining – it did not allow for enough flexibility when assigning scores"
