# Speechreading training produces short-term vocabulary gains at immediate post-test that are not sustained at later follow-ups

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` The speechreading group showed significantly greater gains than the control group on overall vocabulary (d = 0.30) and spoken vocabulary (d = 0.19) at T2, with no sustained effects at T3 or T4. [→ Pimperton 2019](#pimperton-2019)

## Evidence

### Pimperton 2019

Pimperton, H., Kyle, F., Hulme, C., Harris, M., Beedie, I., Ralph-Lewis, A., Worster, E., Rees, R., Donlan, C., & MacSweeney, M. (2019). Computerized Speechreading Training for Deaf Children: A Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Speech, Language, and Hearing Research. https://doi.org/10.1044/2019_JSLHR-H-19-0073

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r3`

Intention-to-treat ANCOVA comparisons on the 74-item trained-vocabulary naming task in the RCT. The printed effect sizes were "significant beneficial effects of speechreading training on overall vocabulary (d = 0.30)" and spoken vocabulary (d = 0.19) at T2 only; later time points showed no sustained effects.

> "There were significant beneficial effects of speechreading training on overall vocabulary (d = 0.30) and spoken vocabulary (d = 0.19) at T2 (see Table 2), but no evidence of sustained effects at T3 (d < 0.01 and d = 0.05, respectively) or T4 (d = −0.12 and d = −0.08, respectively)."
