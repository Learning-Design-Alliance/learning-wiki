Evidence¶

Bodke Parag 2026¶

Bodke Parag, Jumle Rahul. (2026). Large language models in undergraduate medical education: a systematic review of examination performance, knowledge acquisition, and learner perceptions. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09999-7

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Observational study using library access logs, reported within the review's knowledge acquisition section; the review reports a p of 0.034. No effect size printed; only one study, so strength is weak.

"Unrestricted LLM access was associated with reduced engagement with validated primary reference texts in one observational study (measured by library access logs; p = 0.034)"

Bodke Parag 2026 (2)¶

Bodke Parag, Jumle Rahul. (2026). Large language models in undergraduate medical education: a systematic review of examination performance, knowledge acquisition, and learner perceptions. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09999-7

q3 · i? · review · r3

Two experimental studies reported in the knowledge acquisition section found lower hallucination identification among unstructured LLM users, with printed correct-identification rates of 23–41%. No standardised effect size printed.

"students who regularly used LLMs without structured guidance demonstrated significantly lower ability to identify LLM-generated hallucinations when presented with them experimentally (correct identification rate: 23%–41%)"
