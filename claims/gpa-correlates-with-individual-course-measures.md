# Overall GPA correlates most strongly with reliably measured individual performance (final exam .65) and less with group project (.49) and participation (.34) grades

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a consumer behavior course, overall GPA correlated .65 with the final exam, .49 with the group project, and .34 with participation (all p < .01). [→ Bacon 2006](#bacon-2006)

## Evidence

### Bacon 2006

Donald R. Bacon and Beth Bean. (2006). GPA in Research Studies: An Invaluable but Neglected Opportunity. Journal of Marketing Education, Vol. 28 No. 1, April 2006 35-42. https://doi.org/10.1177/0273475305284638

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Secondary analysis of academic records of 174 students in the first author's undergraduate consumer behavior course, correlating registrar overall GPA with course components. The authors attribute lower correlations with subjective and group measures to lower reliability and teammate effects.

> "The correlation between overall GPA and participation was .34 (p \< .01), and the correlation with the group project grade was .49 (p \< .01). Overall GPA correlated most highly with the final exam (.65, p \< .01)."
