## Evidence

### Sinha 2014

Sinha, T., Jermann, P., Li, N., & Dillenbourg, P. (2014). Your click decides your fate: Inferring Information Processing and Attrition Behavior from MOOC Video Clickstream Interactions. [doi:10.3115/v1/w14-4102](https://doi.org/10.3115/v1/w14-4102)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Z-tests of mean differences in average IPI on video lecture 4-6, plotted in figure 4: the article reports average IPI "signiﬁcantly higher for students with "High" engagement (|z|=8.296, p<0.01) and "Non In-video dropouts" (|z|=22.54, p <0.01)"; similar findings were confirmed with other randomly chosen course videos.

> "The average IPI is signiﬁcantly higher for students with "High" engagement (|z|=8.296, p<0.01) and "Non In-video dropouts" (|z|=22.54, p <0.01)."

### Sinha 2014 (2)

Sinha, T., Jermann, P., Li, N., & Dillenbourg, P. (2014). Your click decides your fate: Inferring Information Processing and Attrition Behavior from MOOC Video Clickstream Interactions. [doi:10.3115/v1/w14-4102](https://doi.org/10.3115/v1/w14-4102)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Full-course analysis (figure 5) with z-tests across student partitions: "IPI is clearly able to distinguish between Non-dropouts and Dropouts (|z|=9.06, p <0.01)", and "striking differences between "Active" versus "Viewers" (|z|=10.45, p <0.01)", with Viewers expected to have higher IPI as their primary MOOC activity is video lecture viewing.

> "IPI is clearly able to distinguish between Non-dropouts and Dropouts (|z|=9.06, p <0.01)."
