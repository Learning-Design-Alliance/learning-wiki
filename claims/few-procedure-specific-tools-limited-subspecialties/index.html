Li Yibo 2024¶

Li Yibo, Chan Robert, Menon Matthew R.G., Ryan Joanna F., Mador Brett, Campbell Sandra M., Turner Simon R. (2024). Validity Evidence for Procedure-specific Competence Assessment Tools in Orthopaedic Surgery: A Scoping Review. JAAOS Global Research & Reviews. https://doi.org/10.5435/JAAOSGlobal-D-23-00065

q3 · i? · review · r3

Study and tool characterization from the scoping review's results: 22 studies using 24 procedure-specific tools, mostly in simulation settings, assessing procedures from knee arthroscopy to fracture fixation. The discussion notes "Thirteen tools evaluated arthroscopic procedures, and only five evaluated fracture osteosynthesis".

"Thirteen tools evaluated arthroscopic procedures, and only five evaluated fracture osteosynthesis. Orthopaedic subspecialties represented include only trauma, sports medicine, spine, and upper extremity."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Validity evidence for procedure-specific orthopaedic surgery assessment tools is low, with total scores ranging from 1 to 9 of a maximum of 15 — related
  • Validity-evidence patterns in orthopaedic surgery mirror those in general surgery and cardiothoracic reviews, with strong content evidence and weak response-process and consequences evidence — related
  • Educational utility was not explicitly assessed for most tools, and no studies reported data on educational impact — related
  • The Procedure Based Assessment shows poor engagement and deficient orthopaedic utility evidence, and the authors propose it is no longer appropriate for summative assessment
