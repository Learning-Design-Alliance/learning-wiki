Zheng 2026¶

Zheng, Y., Lo, T. W., Lau, E. Y., Eskes, G., Hui, L. L., & Hsiao, J. H. (2026). The Role of Eye Movement Consistency in Aging-Related Decline in Face Recognition. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00437-3

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Partial correlation analyses in the 301-participant face recognition study. The article reports the age–d′ (r(286) = -.149) and age–false alarm (r(286) = .153) correlations remained significant after controlling aging-related cognitive abilities, while the age–RT correlation became insignificant (r(286) = .049).

"older age was associated with poorer recognition d′ (r(297) = -.227, p < .001, padjusted < .001, Figure 1A), higher false alarm rate (r(297) = .237, p < .001, padjusted < .001, Figure 1B), and longer RT (r(297) = .259, p < .001"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • The literature is mixed on age differences in the accuracy of retrospective confidence judgments during recognition memory tests, with older adults sometimes producing more high-confidence false alarms and poorer resolution with deceptive lures — related
  • A less eyes-focused pattern and lower eye movement consistency jointly predict poorer recognition beyond age and cognitive abilities — related
  • A more eyes-focused scanning pattern and higher eye movement consistency are each correlated with better face recognition performance — related
  • Older age is marginally associated with a greater response bias toward affirming faces as seen, independent of eye movement behavior — related
  • Older age is associated with lower eye movement consistency in face recognition, reflected in higher overall entropy and C3 entropy — related
  • Age correlates with detection performance: hits and d′ increase and false alarms decrease with age — reports the opposite
  • C3 entropy partially mediates the relationship between age and face recognition performance — related
