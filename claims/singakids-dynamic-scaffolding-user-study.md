# High-performing students receive more feedback and explanations; low-performing students receive more hints and social-emotional support

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a user study with 35 grade 1-2 students, utterance-level analysis showed significant differences in some scaffolding types: high-performing students received more feedback (69% vs. 43%) and explanations (21% vs. 9%), while low-performing students received more hints (12% vs. 5%) and social-emotional support (31% vs. 17%).
