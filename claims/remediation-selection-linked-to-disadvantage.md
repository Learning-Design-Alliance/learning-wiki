## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students with extremely low test scores (-6 SD) had the highest probability of participation (90%), while students with average or above-average scores were considerably less likely to participate. [→ Jacobs 2025](#jacobs-2025)
`q2 i?` Students with less educated parents were more likely to participate (1.5% more likely than the mean), and students with both parents in employment were less likely to participate (1% less likely than the mean). [→ Jacobs 2025](#jacobs-2025)

## Evidence

### Jacobs 2025

Jacobs, M., Haelermans, C., & Meeter, M. (2025). Effective remediation programs for vulnerable students to overcome learning loss. PLoS One, 20(5). https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0323352

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Logistic regression with school-level clustered standard errors on 66,439 Dutch primary students, predicting participation from background characteristics and previous test scores; predicted probabilities shown in Fig 1. The article reports "the probability decreasing sharply as scores increase", implying lower achievement is an important participation indicator.

> "Students with extremely low test scores (-6 SD) have the highest probability of participation (90%), while those with average (0 SD, 15%) or above average scores (3 SD, 2%; 4 SD, 1%) are considerably less likely to participate."
