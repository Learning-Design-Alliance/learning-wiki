## Evidence

### Mwiinga CH 2018

Mwiinga CH, Zulu JM, Jacobs C, Chongwe G, Chola M, Halwiindi H, Zgambo J, Michelo C. (2018). Towards a framework for analyzing determinants of performance of community health workers in malaria prevention and control: a systematic review. Human Resources for Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12960-018-0284-x

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Thematic finding from nine studies in the review indicating the importance of training prior to implementing malaria interventions. The review reports CHWs "perform better with only introduction to basic concepts" and that training should be competency-based with exercises, demonstration, role play, and case studies.

> "Evidence shows that CHW trainings must be short as they perform better with only introduction to basic concepts in relation to managing diseases and are to take 2–3 days. Longer training may lead to information overload and thus result in less retention [23]."
