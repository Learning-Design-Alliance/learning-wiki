## Evidence

### Ajai 2015

Ajai, J.T. & Imoko, I.I. (2015). Gender differences in mathematics achievement and retention scores: A case of problem-based learning method. International Journal of Research in Education and Science (IJRES), 1(1), 45-50. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1105194

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre-posttest quasi-experimental study of 261 male and 167 female SS1 students in 10 Nigerian schools taught algebra with PBL for four weeks. Post-test means were 47.03 (female) and 45.62 (male); the article reports "t(426)= 1.553, p=.121", so the null hypothesis was not rejected.

> "Gender factor is not significant. This is evident from Table 1 with t(426)= 1.553, p=.121. The implication is that there is no significant difference between the achievement mean scores of male and female students taught algebra using PBL strategy."
