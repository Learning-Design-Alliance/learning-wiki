# Experiment 2: with element interactivity increased, explicit instruction first was superior on both similar and transfer questions

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In Experiment 2 (N = 71), the explicit-instruction-first group scored significantly higher on similar questions (d = 0.57) and on transfer questions (d = 0.56) than the problem-solving-first group. [→ Ashman et al. 2020](#ashman-et-al-2020)
`q3 i2` The transfer advantage emerged in Experiment 2 where an additional conservation-of-energy step raised element interactivity to twelve interacting elements. [→ Ashman et al. 2020 (2)](#ashman-et-al-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Ashman et al. 2020

Greg Ashman & Slava Kalyuga & John Sweller. (2020). Problem-solving or Explicit Instruction: Which Should Go First When Element Interactivity Is High? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09500-5

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Replication with 71 Year 5 students and twelve interacting elements (eight plus four added by a subtraction step). Transfer questions: "significantly higher on transfer questions", t(69) = 2.35, p = 0.02, Cohen's d = 0.56.

> "Similarly, learners in the lecture–problem-solving condition, who received explicit instruction first, scored significantly higher on transfer questions than learners in the problem-solving–lecture condition who received problem-solving first (t(69) = 2.35, p = 0.02, Cohen's d = 0.56)."

### Ashman et al. 2020 (2)

Greg Ashman & Slava Kalyuga & John Sweller. (2020). Problem-solving or Explicit Instruction: Which Should Go First When Element Interactivity Is High? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09500-5

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 similar questions (maximum 48, Cronbach's α = 0.96), 71 students randomly assigned: "significantly higher on similar questions" for explicit instruction first, t(69) = 2.41, p = 0.02, Cohen's d = 0.57. The post-test occurred one day after instruction.

> "Learners in the lecture–problem-solving condition, who received explicit instruction first, scored significantly higher on similar questions than learners in the problem-solving–lecture condition who received problem-solving first (t(69) = 2.41, p = 0.02, Cohen's d = 0.57)."
