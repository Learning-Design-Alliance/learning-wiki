## Evidence

### de Jong Zuzana 2010

de Jong Zuzana, van Nies Jessica AB, Peters Sonja WM, Vink Sylvia, Dekker Friedo W, Scherpbier Albert. (2010). Interactive seminars or small group tutorials in preclinical medical education: results of a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-10-79

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Questionnaire A Likert items from 105 non-RCT participants (68 small group, 36 seminar choosers), compared by student unpaired t test. The authors speculate the seminar choice reflects a wish to use time economically, as advance preparation is less mandatory.

> "significantly more participants in small groups stated a general preference for small group work (93% versus 64%; P = 0.001) and said they believed that participation in small group tutorials enhances retention of information (88% versus 44%; P = 0.000). The percentage of participants expressing a dislike of compulsory elements in teaching was higher among the participants in interactive seminars (56% versus 18%; P = 0.008)."
