Evidence¶

Canning 2018¶

Canning, E. A., Harackiewicz, J. M., Priniski, S. J., Hecht, C. A., Tibbetts, Y., & Hyde, J. S. (2018). Improving Performance and Retention in Introductory Biology with a Utility-Value Intervention. Journal of educational psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000244

q3 · i3 · causal · r3

Manipulation check in the randomized experiment: essays coded 0–4 for specificity and personalness of UV connections, with 91% interrater agreement. The article reports "t(571) = 14.78, β = 0.52, p < .001" — a large effect — and that 88% of control students made no personal UV connections.

"As expected, students in the UV conditions made higher-quality UV connections (i.e., made more personal and more specific connections to curricular content) in their essays (M = 1.96, SD = .91) than those in the control condition (M = .45, SD = .40), t(571) = 14.78, β = 0.52, p < .001."

Canning 2018 (2)¶

Canning, E. A., Harackiewicz, J. M., Priniski, S. J., Hecht, C. A., Tibbetts, Y., & Hyde, J. S. (2018). Improving Performance and Retention in Introductory Biology with a Utility-Value Intervention. Journal of educational psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000244

q3 · i0 · causal · r2

Supplementary internal analysis of UV essays only (excluding the 82 students with no UV essays), regressing average articulated utility value on number of UV assignments. The article reports "t(489) = 2.03, β = .09, p = .043" — a small effect — suggesting practice helps students find better UV connections.

"We found that number of UV essay assignments significantly predicted the average level of articulated utility value in UV essays, t(489) = 2.03, β = .09, p = .043 (M1UV = 2.87, SD1UV = 1.22; M2UVs = 2.99, SD2UVs = .95; M3UVs = 3.14, SD3UVs = .67)."
