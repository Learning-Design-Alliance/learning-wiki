## Evidence

### Eroğlu Haktanır A 2026

Eroğlu Haktanır A, Çelebi A. (2026). Achalasia: Physician practices and knowledge in Türkiye. Turk J Gastroenterol. https://doi.org/10.5152/tjg.2026.25713

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Univariate chi-square comparisons by gender in the 675-physician survey; female physicians more often reported self-perceived deficiencies (85.1% vs. 68.6%; P < .001) and uncertainty about HREM availability (P = .004).

> "Male physicians had higher rates of adequate knowledge about achalasia (81.2% vs. 71.1%; P < .001), more frequent symptom inquiry, and greater recognition of appropriate diagnostic methods, including HREM (P = .033)."

### Eroğlu Haktanır A 2026 (2)

Eroğlu Haktanır A, Çelebi A. (2026). Achalasia: Physician practices and knowledge in Türkiye. Turk J Gastroenterol. https://doi.org/10.5152/tjg.2026.25713

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariable logistic regression on the survey cohort; gender remained an independent predictor of achalasia knowledge, while for HREM familiarity "Gender showed a borderline effect (OR = 1.46; 95% CI: 0.96-2.21; P = .076)".

> "Male physicians were more likely to demonstrate adequate knowledge than females (OR = 1.63; 95% CI: 1.10-2.41; P = .015)."
