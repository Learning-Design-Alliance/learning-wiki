Equal Access to Content Instruction for English Learners: An Example from Science 2012¶

Equal Access to Content Instruction for English Learners: An Example from Science. (2012). WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED580362

q2 · i? · theoretical · r1

The report presents College Board 2011 SAT data (Table 2) and cites Adelman (1999, 2006), stating "students with fewer years of science score lower than those students with more years of science." The comparison contrasts fewer-years with more-years students; no effect size is printed.

"the number of years of science study for high school students is directly related to achievement on college entrance exams (Adelman, 1999, 2006); students with fewer years of science score lower than those students with more years of science"

Equal Access to Content Instruction for English Learners: An Example from Science. (2012). WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED580362

q2 · i? · review · r1

The report cites Bridgeman and Wendler (2005) for the pattern that "Smaller proportions of Hispanic and Black students tend to complete advanced science courses" compared to White and Asian students. The claim is hedged as the source hedges it; no effect size is printed.

"Smaller proportions of Hispanic and Black students tend to complete advanced science courses (e.g., Physics) in high school, compared to White and Asian students (Bridgeman & Wendler, 2005)."
