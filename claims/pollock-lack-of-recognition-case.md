## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Eileen Pollock, the first woman to earn a BS in physics at Yale, left physics for English despite graduating summa cum laude, and her memoir attributes this to her thesis advisor's failure to praise or encourage her. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2021](#chandralekha-singh-2021)

### Chandralekha Singh 2021

Chandralekha Singh. (2021). Inclusive Mentoring: The Mindset of an Effective Mentor. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2112.01227](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2112.01227)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Case-study evidence drawn from Pollock's memoir as quoted in the article. Decades later the advisor, asked about the thesis, called it "exceptional" and said he had never encouraged any undergraduates toward PhDs; the article calls this a missed mentoring opportunity.

> "Yet I don't remember him praising me in any way. I was dying to ask if my ability to solve the problem meant that I was good enough to make it as a theoretical physicist. But I knew that if I needed to ask, I wasn't."
