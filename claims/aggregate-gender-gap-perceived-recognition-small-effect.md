## Evidence

### Meagan Sundstrom and N. G. Holmes 2025

Meagan Sundstrom and N. G. Holmes. (2025). Bias in physics peer recognition does not explain gaps in perceived recognition. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1038/s41567-025-02789-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-025-02789-w)

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r3`

Preliminary two-sided Student's t-tests comparing men's and women's perceived recognition within each instructional context, replicating prior work. Both printed Cohen's d values (0.33 and 0.37) fall in the article's small-effect range (0.2 < d < 0.5).

> "Student's t-tests show that, on aggregate, men report significantly higher perceptions of peer recognition than women in both the lab (t = 6.60, df [degrees of freedom] = 1639.6,p < 0.001, Cohen's d (effect size) = 0.33) and lecture ( t = 5.92, df = 1015.9, p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.37) contexts in this study"
