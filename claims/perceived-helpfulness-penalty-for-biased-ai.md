# Participants systematically undervalue biased AI, rating it less helpful and less recommendable even when it improves their performance

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` In fact-checking, perceived benefit declined as bias grew (g = 0.305) even though accuracy improved, and participants grew less willing to recommend the assistant (g = 0.429). [→ Lai 2025](#lai-2025)

## Evidence

### Lai 2025

Lai, S., Choi, J., Kim, J., Kunievsky, N., Potter, Y., & Evans, J. (2025). Biased AI improves human performance but reduces perceived helpfulness. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2508.09297](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2508.09297)

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r3`

Subjective-evaluation outcomes from the randomized fact-checking experiment: despite veracity improvements with bias magnitude, participants' perceived improvement fell (g = 0.305) and willingness to recommend dropped (Δ = 0.779, g = 0.429, adj. p = 0.006).

> "Yet they perceived reduced benefit as the bias grew (Δ = 0.553, [0.118, 0.987], adj. p = 0.036, g = 0.305), increasingly construing the assistant as an influencing agent rather than an assistive tool"

### Lai 2025 (2)

Lai, S., Choi, J., Kim, J., Kunievsky, N., Potter, Y., & Evans, J. (2025). Biased AI improves human performance but reduces perceived helpfulness. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2508.09297](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2508.09297)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r3`

Classroom field experiment post-homework helpfulness ratings: the novelty-biased style carried a large helpfulness penalty (g = −1.14) despite its grade advantage, while reliability-biased assistants showed no helpfulness penalty (adj. p = 0.450).

> "novelty-biased tutors raised coding grades relative to the default, yet they were rated less helpful among students who used them (Δ = −1.056, [−1.872, −0.240], g = −1.14, adj. p = 0.025)"
