Evidence¶

Alexandron 2019¶

Alexandron, G., Yoo, L. Y., Ruipérez-Valiente, J. A., Lee, S., & Pritchard, D. E. (2019). Are MOOC Learning Analytics Results Trustworthy? With Fake Learners, They Might Not Be! International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education, 29, 484–506. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-019-00183-1

q3 · i? · associational · r1

In the 8.MReVx behavioral comparison, "Fake learners are significantly faster than true learners" (median 97 vs 150 seconds, p < 0.001) and showed higher weekly improvement. On grade, ability, and CFA the article reports no significant difference (p > 0.05).

Related Claims¶

  • Fake learners attempted a smaller proportion of assessment items than true learners, with the smallest gap on homework items. — related
  • Fake learners spent significantly less time on each type of instructional resource than true learners in the MITx 8.MReVx MOOC. — related
  • In the Champaign et al. replication, removing fake learners moved three correlations (Ability vs. Homework Time, Ability vs. Reading Time, Weekly Improvement vs. Homework Time) in the positive direction, but no correlation changed qualitatively. — related
  • A simulated doubling of fake learners (from ~15% to ~26%) significantly increased the bias in Analysis 1A, making 'Doers who are neither Watchers nor Readers' by far the most successful group.
  • Removing fake learners changed the Tetrad causal graph: the links watching → quizScore and reading → IRT disappeared.
  • Trust in reviews is highest for voluntary reviews because fake reviews are usually created for payment — related
