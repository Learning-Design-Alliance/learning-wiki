## Evidence

### Opitz Sebastian 2020

Opitz Sebastian, Harms Ute. (2020). Assessing High Performers in the Life Sciences: Characteristics of Exams Used at the International Biology Olympiad (IBO) and Their Implications for Life Science Education. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-10-0215

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Descriptive cognitive-process coding of practical items shows "two-thirds of all items required students to analyze, evaluate, or create" and fewer than 10% were simple apply tasks; shares only, no test statistic reported.

> "The practical exams were generally characterized by a stronger emphasis on higher-order cognitive processes than the theoretical exams (Figure 4). Here, two-thirds of all items required students to analyze, evaluate, or create, while tasks with relatively simple "cookbook-like" instructions (category apply) were found in less than 10% of tasks."

### Opitz Sebastian 2020 (2)

Opitz Sebastian, Harms Ute. (2020). Assessing High Performers in the Life Sciences: Characteristics of Exams Used at the International Biology Olympiad (IBO) and Their Implications for Life Science Education. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-10-0215

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Knowledge-type coding of practical items shows "three-fourths of practical items encompass elements of procedural knowledge"; descriptive share only, no test statistic reported.

> "A look at the types of knowledge (Figure 5) reveals the emphasis on hands-on activities during practicals, where three-fourths of practical items encompass elements of procedural knowledge."

### Opitz Sebastian 2020 (3)

Opitz Sebastian, Harms Ute. (2020). Assessing High Performers in the Life Sciences: Characteristics of Exams Used at the International Biology Olympiad (IBO) and Their Implications for Life Science Education. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-10-0215

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The article qualifies the create category: create tasks were mostly summaries such as graphs or laboratory outputs, while "few items required students to create products, research plans, or models."

> "The respective tasks typically asked students to prepare graphs, calculations, or other representations as summaries of their earlier analyses or students had to create certain laboratory outputs. However, few items required students to create products, research plans, or models."

### Opitz Sebastian 2020 (4)

Opitz Sebastian, Harms Ute. (2020). Assessing High Performers in the Life Sciences: Characteristics of Exams Used at the International Biology Olympiad (IBO) and Their Implications for Life Science Education. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-10-0215

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Null finding from the item coding: no practical or theoretical items explicitly assessed metacognitive knowledge; reported descriptively, no test statistic.

> "Finally, neither in the practicals nor in the theoretical items did we observe items that explicitly assessed metacognitive knowledge (e.g., strategic thinking, self-knowledge)."
