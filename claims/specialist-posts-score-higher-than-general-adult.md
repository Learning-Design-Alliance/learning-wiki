## Evidence

### Bizrah M 2013

Bizrah M, Mukhtar; Iacoponi, Eduardo; Parker, Elizabeth; Rymer, Janice; Iversen, Amy; Wessely, Simon. (2013). A novel method of assessing quality of postgraduate psychiatry training: experiences from a large training programme. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-85

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of combined trainee-and-trainer scores across placement types in the interview audit of the Maudsley scheme, shown in Figure 3. The article reports the t-test result "p < 0.001, 95% CI: -0.398 to −0.132" favouring specialist posts.

> "the combined score is significantly higher for specialist psychiatry posts (Two tailed t-test, p < 0.001, 95% CI: -0.398 to −0.132) than for general adult psychiatry posts."

### Bizrah M 2013 (2)

Bizrah M, Mukhtar; Iacoponi, Eduardo; Parker, Elizabeth; Rymer, Janice; Iversen, Amy; Wessely, Simon. (2013). A novel method of assessing quality of postgraduate psychiatry training: experiences from a large training programme. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-85

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Domain-level breakdown shown in Figure 4 of the same interview audit. The article names clinical opportunities, clinical environment and research opportunities as the domains with the largest general-adult-versus-specialist differences.

> "the interview domains in which the biggest difference was observed between general adult and specialist psychiatry are clinical opportunities and clinical environment (t-test, p< 0.001), followed by research opportunities (p= 0.001)."
