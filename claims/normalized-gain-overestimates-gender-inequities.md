## Evidence

### Jayson M. Nissen 2016

Jayson M. Nissen, Robert M. Talbot, Amreen Nasim Thompson, and Ben Van Dusen. (2016). A comparison of normalized gain and Cohen's d for analyzing gains on concept inventories. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1612.09180](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1612.09180)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of gender differences in effect sizes within 43 courses from the LASSO dataset, where male students typically had higher pretest means, found g indicated larger male effects in 33 of 43 courses versus 22 for d. A chi-squared test of independence confirmed the disagreement pattern was statistically reliable, χ2(1) = 6.10, p = 0.013.

> "In total, g showed a larger eﬀect on males in 33 out of 43 courses whereas d indicated a larger eﬀect on males in 22 out of 43 courses. Figure 3 illustrates this bias in g in the diﬀerence between Quadrants II and IV. A chi squared test of independences indicated that these diﬀerences were statistically reliable, χ2(1) = 6 .10, p=0.013."
