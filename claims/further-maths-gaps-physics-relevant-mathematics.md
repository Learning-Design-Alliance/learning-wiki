## Evidence

### Mackay 2026

Mackay, Paul and Inyang, Oto-obong. (2026). Transitioning from A-level to higher education physics courses: where are the knowledge gaps?. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2610.00175](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.00175)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Specification analysis across AQA, Edexcel, OCR and Eduqas/WJEC shows all Further Mathematics boards share a compulsory core with optional components, so "a student who chooses a Statistics option may have no exposure" to simple harmonic motion, damped oscillations or circular motion.

> "a student who chooses a Statistics option may have no exposure to advance mechanics content such as simple harmonic motion, damped oscillations or circular motion, all of which are directly relevant to Physics"

### Mackay 2026 (2)

Mackay, Paul and Inyang, Oto-obong. (2026). Transitioning from A-level to higher education physics courses: where are the knowledge gaps?. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2610.00175](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.00175)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Five-year UCAS uptake data for Further Mathematics (Figure 3) show uptake substantially lower than Mathematics across every board; "Even Edexcel, the largest provider, records entries roughly five times lower than its Mathematics cohort".

> "Even Edexcel, the largest provider, records entries roughly five times lower than its Mathematics cohort, underscoring the limited reach of Further Mathematics provision nationally."
