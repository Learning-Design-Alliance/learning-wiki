# Teacher-created video mistake messages were not harmful but produced no significant learning differences compared with answer-only feedback (n = 649)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a randomized experiment, tailored video mistake messages for common wrong answers showed no significant differences from correct-answer-only feedback on completion rates, problems required, or next-question accuracy and attempts. [→ Heffernan 2016](#heffernan-2016)

## Evidence

### Heffernan 2016

Heffernan, N. T., Ostrow, K. S., Kelly, K., Selent, D., Van Inwegen, E. G., Xiong, X., & Williams, J. J. (2016). The Future of Adaptive Learning: Does the Crowd Hold the Key? Int J Artif Intell Educ 26:615–644. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-016-0094-z

`q3 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Randomized controlled experiment (Selent and Heffernan 2015) comparing one-step equation problem sets with tailored 20–30 second teacher video mistake messages versus feedback restricted to the correct answer; no significant differences on the reported measures, so equivalence was not tested.

> "In a sample of 649 students ( n control = 328, n experimental = 321), no significant differences were observed in completion rates for the assignment, the number of problems required for completion"
