## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The PBL-CBL group scored higher than the traditional group on the critical thinking traits measured (e.g., truth-seeking 36.57±6.15 vs. 33.12±5.95, P = 0.005; analytical skills 37.71±3.54 vs. 27.50±4.43, P = 0.000). [→ Jiang Xin 2026](#jiang-xin-2026)

## Evidence

### Jiang Xin 2026

Jiang Xin, Hui Yiqing, Tong Chuan, Li Dongxiao, Zhang Chaoqun, Chang Ying. (2026). Application of the combined PBL-CBL teaching method of anaphylactic shock prevention and care in the fundamental nursing course. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08746-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Anonymous questionnaire based on the California Critical Thinking Disposition Inventory (Cronbach's α = 0.72–0.88) administered to both groups (n=50 each); the quote reports "higher critical thinking scores (truth-seeking, analytical skills, etc.)" in the PBL-CBL group.

> "Questionnaire results showed higher critical thinking scores (truth-seeking, analytical skills, etc.), course satisfaction, and teacher-student interaction in the PBL-CBL group, though it consumed more learning time (P<0.05)."
