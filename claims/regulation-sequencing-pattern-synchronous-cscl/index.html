Evidence¶

Duong Ngo 2024¶

Duong Ngo, Andy Nguyen, Belle Dang, Ha Ngo. (2024). Facial Expression Recognition for Examining Emotional Regulation in Synchronous Online Collaborative Learning. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education 34:650–669. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-023-00378-7

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Process-mining analysis (Fuzzy Miner, Fluxicon Disco) of the coded regulatory activities produced the regulation pattern shown in Fig. 2; the article reports "cognitive interactions set the stage for the learning regulation pattern in synchronous CSCL" and that groups "frequently commence and conclude with other interactions".

"Interestingly, we found that co-regulation often iterates with task execution whereas socially shared regulation mainly occurs after socio-emotional interactions. However, with a small sample size, caution must be applied in generalizing the findings."

Related Claims¶

  • Co-regulation and socially shared regulation occur rarely in synchronous online collaborative learning — related
  • Emotional synchrony frequency differs slightly across regulatory activities but the metric does not discriminate SSRL, cognitive, and task-execution interactions — related
  • Process mining of WBA logs shows students typically start with Handwashing, then Pulse and History, with frequent looping back to basic clinical skills — related
  • Two distinct group deliberation patterns emerge in response to regulation triggers: the Plan and Implementation Approach (PIA) and the Trials and Failure Approach (TFA) — related
  • PIA and TFA types differ structurally in deliberative networks, with PIA centred on establishing strategy, educating, agreeing and monitoring — related
  • PIA groups follow a strategic generate-options-to-implement cycle with fewer random idea attempts, while TFA groups loop through repeated metacognitive interactions and unproductive idea testing — related
  • Surprise is the most frequently shared emotion among learners in synchronous online collaborative English discussion tasks — related
  • Explicit group regulation in online PBL was infrequent, emerged in response to challenges, and often involved teacher support — related
  • Task execution dominates social interactions in synchronous online collaborative learning, mirroring face-to-face and asynchronous findings — related
  • High prior knowledge students engaged more in co-regulation behaviors while low prior knowledge students relied more on question-raising and idea-confirming sequences — related
  • LIWC analysis shows synchronous messages carry more social and emotional language while asynchronous notes carry more cognitive-processing language — related
