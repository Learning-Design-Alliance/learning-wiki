## Evidence

### Wilcox 2013

Wilcox, B. R., Caballero, M. D., Rehn, D. A., & Pollock, S. J. (2013). ACER: An Analytic Framework for Students' Use of Mathematics in Upper-Division Physics. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.9.020119](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.9.020119)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Exam analysis of the Construction component among the 97% of 172 (N=166) solutions containing Construction elements. Common dq errors included integrating over regions with zero charge density, wrong-unit differentials, and using total charge instead of charge density (Table I).

> "Nearly half the students (42% of 166) had diﬃculty expressing the diﬀerential charge element (el- ement CC2) and some (14% of 166) failed to provide limits of integration or gave limits that were inconsistent with their diﬀerential (element CC3)."

### Wilcox 2013 (2)

Wilcox, B. R., Caballero, M. D., Rehn, D. A., & Pollock, S. J. (2013). ACER: An Analytic Framework for Students' Use of Mathematics in Upper-Division Physics. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.9.020119](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.9.020119)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same exam corpus (N=166 with Construction elements). The article notes this finding is somewhat surprising given prior research indicating middle-division students often prefer Cartesian coordinates; one interview participant also started in Cartesian and switched.

> "Of these students, only two did not use the appropriate coordinates (i.e., cylindrical), indicating that students at this level are adept at selecting appropriate coordinate systems in highly symmetric problems (element CC1)."
