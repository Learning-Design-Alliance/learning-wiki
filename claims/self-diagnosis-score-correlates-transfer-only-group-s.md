## Evidence

### Mason 2016

Mason, A., Yerushalmi, E., Cohen, E., & Singh, C. (2016). Learning from mistakes: The effect of students' written self-diagnoses on subsequent problem solving. Physics Education Research Conference proceedings. Originally published 2012. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07253](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07253)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Correlational analysis in the typical-problem setting of the introductory physics study; the article prints an effect size of r = 0.54 (printed as "correlation = 0.54, p-value = 0.01") between self-diagnosis score and transfer score for group S only.

> "we find that the self-diagnosis score was correlated (correlation = 0.54, p-value = 0.01) with subsequent problem-solving performance on the transfer problem, but only for group S, when textbooks and notes were the sole means of guidance available to the students to help them with self-diagnosis"
