## Evidence

### Morgan 2024

Morgan, I., Gillispie, C., & Waller, A. (2024). Supporting Young Learners Through Family Engagement: Engaging First. EdTrust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED658182

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey 2 findings on communication about academic expectations; Figure 3 shows 52% received expectation information, 44% a plan to remain on track, and 41% how to access extra academic support such as tutoring.

> "Specifically, 52% of parents and 51% of teachers (66% of teachers in the first survey) reported that schools provide this information."

### Morgan 2024 (2)

Morgan, I., Gillispie, C., & Waller, A. (2024). Supporting Young Learners Through Family Engagement: Engaging First. EdTrust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED658182

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey 2 parent respondents lacking expectation information rated it their most valued engagement option. The report links this to the mismatch where 90% of parents think their children are at or above grade level while only 32% of fourth graders scored proficient on NAEP.

> "Of those parents who said that schools are not providing information on whether students are meeting academic expectations or information to help students remain on track, nearly all of them said that having this information would be more helpful and valuable to them than all other engagement options included in the survey."
