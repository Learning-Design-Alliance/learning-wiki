## Evidence

### Guido Makransky · Gustav Bøg Petersen 2023

Guido Makransky · Gustav Bøg Petersen. (2023). The Theory of Immersive Collaborative Learning (TICOL). Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09822-5

`q3 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review reports that meta-analyses by Coban et al. (2022) and Wu et al. (2020) found a "small effect size advantage for immersive lessons" over less immersive technology. No pooled effect size value is printed in the review.

> "Meta-analyses have found a small effect size advantage for immersive lessons compared to less immersive technology (Coban et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2020)."

### Guido Makransky · Gustav Bøg Petersen 2023 (2)

Guido Makransky · Gustav Bøg Petersen. (2023). The Theory of Immersive Collaborative Learning (TICOL). Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09822-5

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review reports, citing multiple studies, that using XR without considering instructional design, technology affordances, and desired outcomes "can lead to distraction and less learning" compared to traditional instruction. It also states immersive environments can increase presence without necessarily leading to more learning.

> "However, using XR without considering instructional design (Makransky et al., 2021; Makransky & Mayer, 2022), the affordances of the technology (Petersen et al., 2022), and the desired outcomes (Makransky, Borre-Gude, & Mayer, 2019) can lead to distraction and less learning (Makransky et al., 2021; Makransky, Terkildsen, & Mayer, 2019; Parong & Mayer, 2018) compared to traditional means of instruction."
