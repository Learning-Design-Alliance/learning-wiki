## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who received reminder messages attempted homework at a higher rate than control students, an aggregate effect of about 4 percentage points over four weeks. [→ Angela Zavaleta-Bernuy 2022](#angela-zavaleta-bernuy-2022)

## Evidence

### Angela Zavaleta-Bernuy 2022

Angela Zavaleta-Bernuy, Ziwen Han, Hammad Shaikh, Qi Yin Zheng, Lisa-Angelique Lim, Anna Rafferty, Andrew Petersen, and Joseph Jay Williams. 2022. How can Email Interventions Increase Students’ Completion of Online Homework? A Case Study Using A/B Comparisons. In LAK22: 12th International Learning Analytics and Knowledge Conference (LAK22), March 21–25, 2022, Online, USA. ACM, New York, NY, USA. https://doi.org/10.1145/3506860.3506874

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Panel data regression on time-stamped homework submissions from weeks 5–8 of a randomized A/B deployment in a CS1 course (3784 student×week observations). The article reports the reminder "increased the number of students who started the homework by around 4 percentage points" with coefficient = 0.0319; z = 2.86; p < .01. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "Table 2 shows that the reminder messages increased the number of students who started the homework by around 4 percentage points (coefficient = 0.0319; z = 2.86; p < .01; panel regression with time and participant effects)."
