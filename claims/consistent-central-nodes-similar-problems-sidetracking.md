## Evidence

### Nandana Weliweriya 2016

Nandana Weliweriya, Justyna P. Zwolak, Eleanor C. Sayre, Dean Zollman. (2016). Varied reasoning schema in students’ written solutions. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1611.02262](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1611.02262)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparison of networks combining solutions to three problems classified as structurally similar (type-two prompts), for individual students and all students merged. The article states this "could suggest that students take similar approaches to solve all three problems."

> "In all cases, node E5 ( do algebra ) was the most central node. Also, the four most prominent nodes (A2, C3, E3, E5) are the same for all students and for the merged network, although their order diﬀers slightly."

### Nandana Weliweriya 2016 (2)

Nandana Weliweriya, Justyna P. Zwolak, Eleanor C. Sayre, Dean Zollman. (2016). Varied reasoning schema in students’ written solutions. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1611.02262](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1611.02262)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Network descriptives for structurally similar problems: student C's network had greater diameter and lower density than other students'. The article notes the significant codes for this student "were the same as for students who took a more direct approach."

> "the network characteristics in table 2 reveal that the network for student C has greater diameter and lower density (less connected graph with more nodes), indicating that this student got sidetracked in his solution. This could not be seen by the ACER framework alone"
