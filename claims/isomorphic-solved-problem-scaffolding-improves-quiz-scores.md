# Learning from a solved isomorphic problem with scaffolding significantly improves introductory students' quiz-problem scores in the algebra-based course (all three interventions) and for the struggle-first intervention in the calculus-based course

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` All three intervention groups in the algebra-based course and intervention group 2 in the calculus-based course significantly outperformed the comparison group on the isomorphic quiz problem. [→ Lin 2011](#lin-2011)

## Evidence

### Lin 2011

Lin, S.-Y. and Singh, C. (2011). Using Isomorphic Problems to Learn Introductory Physics. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.7.020104](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.7.020104)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experiment in which 362 students in algebra-based and calculus-based courses were divided by recitation into a comparison group and three scaffolding interventions; ANOVA p-values (Table 4) show the named groups "significantly outperformed the co mparison group". No effect sizes are printed.

> "The p-values presented in Table 4 show that all three intervention (Intv) groups in the algebra -based course and the intervention group 2 in the calculus -based course significantly outperformed the co mparison group"
