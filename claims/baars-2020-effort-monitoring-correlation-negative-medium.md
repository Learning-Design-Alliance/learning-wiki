# Prior meta-analysis found a negative medium-sized effort–monitoring correlation (r = -0.35), attenuated under goal-driven manipulations

## Evidence

### Louise David 2024

Louise David, Felicitas Biwer, Martine Baars, Lisette Wijnia, Fred Paas, Anique de Bruin. (2024). The Relation Between Perceived Mental Effort, Monitoring Judgments, and Learning Outcomes: A Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09903-z

`q2 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

The article reports, citing Baars et al. (2020), that "an overall negative, medium-sized correlation between effort and monitoring judgments (r = -0.35)" was found, with the association non-significant under goal-driven manipulations such as incentives or time pressure.

> "While they found an overall negative, medium-sized correlation between effort and monitoring judgments (r = -0.35), their moderator analysis results showed that this association was no longer significant for studies employing goal-driven manipulations, such as incentives, time pressure, or promoting feelings of self-agency"
