## Evidence

### Romanovska 2022

Romanovska, L., Janssen, R. & Bonte, M. (2022). Longitudinal changes in cortical responses to letter-speech sound stimuli in 8–11 year-old children. npj Science of Learning 7:2. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00118-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Post-hoc mixed regression on individual beta values from the longitudinal sample, with session, baseline age, reading fluency, recalibration effect and pre-test slope as within-subject covariates. The session × baseline age interaction was significant (F(2,13) = 8.702, p < 0.005).

> "younger children showed higher left STG activation during session 2 compared to session 3 (mean difference = 0.596, t(11) = 2.950, p < 0.05), whereas older children showed more STG activation in session 3 compared to session 2 (mean difference = 0.502, t(10) = 2.918, p < 0.05)."

### Romanovska 2022 (2)

Romanovska, L., Janssen, R. & Bonte, M. (2022). Longitudinal changes in cortical responses to letter-speech sound stimuli in 8–11 year-old children. npj Science of Learning 7:2. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00118-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same mixed regression models: both the session × baseline age and session × recalibration effect interactions were significant in the left STG cluster only, with no significant interactions in the lateral inferior pre-CG.

> "The analyses showed signi ﬁcant interactions between session and children ’s baseline age [ F(2,13) = 8.702, p < 0.005] and between session and the recalibration effect [ F(2,17) = 5.808, p < 0.05] in the left STG cluster."
