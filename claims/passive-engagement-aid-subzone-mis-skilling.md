# Passive engagement within correctly classified Aid tasks is a detection-resistant mis-skilling pathway requiring expert epistemic auditing

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · theoretical `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` A learner who correctly identifies an Aid task but engages passively (reading without evaluating, accepting without comparing) internalizes plausible-sounding AI errors, constituting a failure of epistemic activity rather than subzone selection. [→ Tsim 2026](#tsim-2026)
`q1 i?` Passive engagement is indistinguishable from active engagement within a correctly identified Aid task, so supervision requires subzone re-identification from Aid to Non-Negotiable with human experts as epistemic auditors. [→ Tsim 2026 (2)](#tsim-2026-2)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument in Section 3.3.2.3 describing an underappreciated mis-skilling form involving engagement quality rather than subzone selection, where wrong clinical structures form through the interaction rather than despite it.

> "AI-generated content is processed and potentially internalized, but the incomplete knowledge base means plausible-sounding errors pass undetected, and the cognitive interaction that makes Aid-subzone engagement developmentally beneficial fails to instantiate."

### Tsim 2026 (2)

Tsim, F., Gutoreva, A., Weiss, A., & Dubosh, N. (2026). AI as Teammate: Rethinking Task Distribution in Medical Training. Correspondence: fenditsim@gmail.com. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28373

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument posing a practical supervision challenge: because passive and active engagement look identical, the proposed solution has experts audit how the learner engaged with the mode, not which mode was selected, aligning with Vygotsky's ZPD.

> "Within a correctly identified Aid task, a learner passively engaging with AI is indistinguishable from the one exercising actively."
