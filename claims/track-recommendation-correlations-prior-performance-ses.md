# Students' prior performance correlates highly with both track recommendation types, while SES correlates only weakly to moderately

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Track recommendations correlate highly and significantly positively with students' prior performance (r = .76–.84) and less strongly with SES (r = .27–.32) for both recommendation types. [→ van Leest 2021](#van-leest-2021)

## Evidence

### van Leest 2021

van Leest, A., Hornstra, L., van Tartwijk, J., & van de Pol, J. (2021). Test‐ or judgement‐based school track recommendations: Equal opportunities for students with different socio‐economic backgrounds? The British Journal of Educational Psychology, 91(1), 193–216. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12356

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r3`

Bivariate correlations in the full sample showed 'high significant positive correlations' between prior performance and both recommendation types (r = .76–.81); the school leavers' test correlated r = .84 with test‐based recommendations, and SES correlations were lower (r = .32 test‐based; r = .27 judgement‐based).

> "Overall, high significant positive correlations were reported between students' prior performance and both types of track recommendations (test‐based recommendations: rReading = .76, rMathematics= .76; judgement‐based recommendations: rReading = .77, rMathematics= .81)."
