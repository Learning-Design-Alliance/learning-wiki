# Students in homogeneous prior-knowledge groups made greater learning gains and showed more distributed collaboration than heterogeneous groups

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students in homogeneous groups made significantly greater learning gains than students in heterogeneous groups in small-group compost simulations. [→ Sanna Järvelä 2023](#sanna-jarvela-2023)
`q2 i?` Interaction patterns differed: interactions in homogeneous groups were more distributed, while interactions in heterogeneous groups were more centralized around the member with the greatest prior knowledge. [→ Sanna Järvelä 2023 (2)](#sanna-jarvela-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Sanna Järvelä 2023

Sanna Järvelä, Carolyn P. Rosé. (2023). Reflecting on what counts as collaboration: Reaching forward without losing what is behind. International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning, 18, 457–464. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-023-09415-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The editorial reports, citing Puntambekar, Gnesdilow and Yavuz, a study of 102 groups from one teacher's classes across multiple compost simulations, contrasting homogeneous versus heterogeneous groups, analyzed with hierarchical linear modeling (HLM); students in homogenous groups "made significantly greater learning gains".

> "According to their results, stu- dents in homogenous groups made significantly greater learning gains than students in het- erogeneous groups."

### Sanna Järvelä 2023 (2)

Sanna Järvelä, Carolyn P. Rosé. (2023). Reflecting on what counts as collaboration: Reaching forward without losing what is behind. International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning, 18, 457–464. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-023-09415-y

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The editorial reports that social network analysis (SNA) and thematic analysis of discussions identified interaction-pattern differences between the two group types in the same study.

> "the interactions in the homogenous group were more distributed, while the interactions in the heterogenous were more centralized around the member with the greatest prior knowledge"
