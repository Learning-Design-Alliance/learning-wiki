## Evidence

### Hewitson 2021

Hewitson, C. L., Shukur, S. T., Cartmill, J., Crossley, M. J., & Kaplan, D. M. (2021). Camera realignment imposes a cost on laparoscopic performance. Scientific Reports, 11, 17634. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-96965-6

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Mixed-design ANOVA on movement time in the box-trainer peg-transfer experiment (10 experts, 10 controls; condition within-subject). The main effect of realignment was significant ("η2 = 0.65") and the group × condition interaction was not ("η2 = 0.03").

> "Camera realignment increased movement time for both groups to similar degrees as evidenced by a significant main effect of condition [F(1,18) = 32.95, p < 0.001, η2 = 0.65], and a non-significant group × condition interaction [F(1,18) = 0.57, p = 0.46, η2 = 0.03]."

### Hewitson 2021 (2)

Hewitson, C. L., Shukur, S. T., Cartmill, J., Crossley, M. J., & Kaplan, D. M. (2021). Camera realignment imposes a cost on laparoscopic performance. Scientific Reports, 11, 17634. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-96965-6

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Post hoc t-test on movement time in the same 2 × 2 mixed-design experiment confirmed slower movements under realignment, with the printed effect size "d = − 0.77".

> "Post hoc t-tests support the direction of this effect [t(19.0) =  − 5.81, p < 0.01, d =  − 0.77]."
