## Evidence

### Herchenröther Julia 2021

Herchenröther Julia, Tiedemann Elena, Vogel Heiner, Simmenroth Anne. (2021). Which teaching method is more effective in a communication course – role-playing versus simulated patients, taught by tutors or faculty staff? A randomized trial. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001450](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001450)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Questionnaire-based retrospective self-assessment with pre-test-bias correction, analyzed with dependent-sample t-tests: "All of the self-assessed skills in regard to medical history taking showed learning progress, p<.001"; e.g. SP-Lecturer rose from M=67.8% to M=84.1%. No effect size is printed.

> "All of the self-assessed skills in regard to medical history taking showed learning progress, p<.001."

### Herchenröther Julia 2021 (2)

Herchenröther Julia, Tiedemann Elena, Vogel Heiner, Simmenroth Anne. (2021). Which teaching method is more effective in a communication course – role-playing versus simulated patients, taught by tutors or faculty staff? A randomized trial. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001450](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001450)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Overall learning-gain comparison between SP-based groups using Welch's t-test: "no significant difference" between the SP-Lecturer (M=20.6%) and SP-Tutor (M=24.2%) groups. The RP-Tutor versus SP-Tutor gain comparison was likewise non-significant (p=.31).

> "In regard to overall learning success there was no significant difference between the SP-Lecturer group (M=20.6%, n=26) and the SP-Tutor group (M=24.2%, n=25), t(47)=-1.23, p=.22."
