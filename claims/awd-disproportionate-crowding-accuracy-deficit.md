# Adults with dyslexia show disproportionately reduced discrimination accuracy in crowded displays compared to controls

## Evidence

### Moores 2011

Moores, E., Cassim, R. and Talcott, J.B. (2011). Adults with dyslexia exhibit large effects of crowding, increased dependence on cues, and detrimental effects of distractors in visual search tasks. Frontiers in Psychology. [doi:10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2011.10.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2011.10.005)

`q2 · i3`

Four-factor ANOVA on discrimination accuracy in the crowding analysis (set size eight spread vs crowded displays) for 15 AwD and 16 controls. The display type by group interaction was significant, with the article reporting "the performance accuracy of the AwD group decreased more than that of the controls in crowded displays" (η2p =.21).

> "The interaction between display type and group was significant (F1, 29=7.46, p<.01, η2p =.21), demonstrating that the performance accuracy of the AwD group decreased more than that of the controls in crowded displays."

### Moores 2011 (2)

Moores, E., Cassim, R. and Talcott, J.B. (2011). Adults with dyslexia exhibit large effects of crowding, increased dependence on cues, and detrimental effects of distractors in visual search tasks. Frontiers in Psychology. [doi:10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2011.10.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2011.10.005)

`q2 · i3`

Post-hoc two-factor ANOVAs split by group in uncued conditions. AwD showed a strong display type effect (F1,14=34.33, p<.001, η2p=.71) whereas controls showed none (F1,15=2.25, p=.15), demonstrating group-specific crowding vulnerability when stimuli were uncued.

> "In contrast, whereas the controls showed no significant effect of display type in uncued conditions (F 1, 15=2.25, p=.15, η2p =.13), the AwD showed a strong and significant effect (F 1, 14=34.33, p<.001, η2p =.71)."
