Evidence¶

Satoshi Takahashi 2026¶

Satoshi Takahashi, Atsushi Yoshikawa, Megumi Kose, Kenichi Suzuki, Chieko Inoue, Yumi Watanabe, and Mari Sawada. (2026). Automating Constructive Assessment with Large Language Models: Toward Scalable and Repeated Evaluation of Practical Competence. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25790

q3 · i? · design · r2

Experiment 1 with 100 working adults randomly assigned to two counterbalanced groups compared original and ChatGPT-generated HDR problems; Cronbach's α was computed for total scores, showing "a high internal consistency (α=0.78)".

"The original and ChatGPT-generated problems yielded a high internal consistency (α=0.78), suggesting that both measure the same cognitive ability to detect the misuse of knowledge."

Satoshi Takahashi 2026 (2)¶

Satoshi Takahashi, Atsushi Yoshikawa, Megumi Kose, Kenichi Suzuki, Chieko Inoue, Yumi Watanabe, and Mari Sawada. (2026). Automating Constructive Assessment with Large Language Models: Toward Scalable and Repeated Evaluation of Practical Competence. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25790

q3 · i? · design · r2

Experiment 1 score-distribution analysis: mean scores and 95% confidence intervals for original vs. ChatGPT-generated problems showed "extremely similar" trends, and Fisher's exact tests found no significant differences.

"Figure 1 compares the distribution of the descriptive scores for the original and ChatGPT-generated problems. The trends are extremely similar, indicating no difference in difficulty."
