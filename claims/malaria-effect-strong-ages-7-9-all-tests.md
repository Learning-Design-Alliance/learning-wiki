## Evidence

### Klejnstrup 2018

Klejnstrup, Buhl-Wiggers, Jones, & Rand. (2018). Early life malaria exposure and academic performance. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0199542

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Re-estimation of the baseline and household fixed-effects models on children aged 7–9 (119,611 observations; 31,658 with household fixed effects); Table 4 prints coefficients such as -1.709 (English) and -1.449 (numeracy), significant at the 1% and 5% levels, with no standardised effect size.

> "Table 4 shows that when we confine our attention to children aged 7–9 years old, we find that birth-year PfPR has strong negative effects on academic achievement across all three test scores. This supports the argument that strong ceiling effects in the numeracy and Kiswahili test scores are likely to explain the insignificant results."
