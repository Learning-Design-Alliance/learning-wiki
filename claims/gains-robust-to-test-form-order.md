# Gains in source evaluation held regardless of the order in which students took the two assessment forms

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Scores improved significantly in both counterbalanced sections: Section 1 rose from 3.59 to 7.7 points and Section 2 from 4.3 to 6.49 points. [→ Breakstone 2021](#breakstone-2021)

## Evidence

### Breakstone 2021

Breakstone, J., Smith, M., Connors, P., Ortega, T., Kerr, D., & Wineburg, S. (2021). Lateral reading: College students learn to critically evaluate internet sources in an online course. Harvard Kennedy School (HKS) Misinformation Review. https://doi.org/10.37016/mr-2020-56

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Counterbalanced administration across the two course sections, analyzed with a repeated-measures ANOVA controlling for order effects. Section 2, taking the forms in the opposite order, improved from 4.3 points (SE = .32) to 6.49 points (SE = .42); no effect size was printed.

> "Scores improved significantly regardless of the order in which students took the two forms (see Figure 1). For Section 1, which took Form A at pretest and Form B at posttest, mean scores improved from 3.59 points (SE = .31, 95% CI 2.97 to 4.21) to 7.7 points (SE = .42, 95% CI 6.83 to 8.49)."
