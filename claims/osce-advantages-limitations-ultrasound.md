## Evidence

### Neubauer R 2025

Neubauer R, Recker F, Weimer J, Jenssen C, Nürnberg D, Karlas T, Westerway SC, Ruppert JP, Daum N, Gschmack A, Dong Y, Möller K, Nourkami-Tutdibi N, Barth G, Şirli R, Lucius C, Prats M, Sänger SL, Keil M, Zervides C, Hoffmann B, Dietrich CF. (2025). Verifying learning success: assessment and certification of student ultrasound education. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07469-0

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

In its discussion of assessment methods, the review describes OSCEs as frequently used tools in which "Structured checklists provide equivalent scenarios for all examinees with a high degree of objectivity", covering acquisition, optimization, and interpretation, plus context-related skills such as patient interaction.

> "Structured checklists provide equivalent scenarios for all examinees with a high degree of objectivity. During OSCEs, the same test tool can evaluate image acquisition, optimization, and interpretation skills."

### Neubauer R 2025 (2)

Neubauer R, Recker F, Weimer J, Jenssen C, Nürnberg D, Karlas T, Westerway SC, Ruppert JP, Daum N, Gschmack A, Dong Y, Möller K, Nourkami-Tutdibi N, Barth G, Şirli R, Lucius C, Prats M, Sänger SL, Keil M, Zervides C, Hoffmann B, Dietrich CF. (2025). Verifying learning success: assessment and certification of student ultrasound education. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07469-0

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review lists OSCE limitations: students may narrow preparation to tested scenarios, scenarios may not represent realistic hospital settings, and the format requires specialist examiners, OSCE training, and "at least two observers" to avoid bias, raising personnel and resource costs.

> "the limited number of tested scenarios may lead to students preparing only for specific skills, potentially neglecting the clinical relevance of other examinations and broader sonographic principles"
