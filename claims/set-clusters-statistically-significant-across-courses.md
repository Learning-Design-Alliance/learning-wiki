# Student evaluations of Yale's first-semester introductory physics courses form statistically distinct clusters by course, even across instructors and teaching styles

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For every pair of Yale first-semester introductory physics classes except PHYS 170/PHYS 200, at least one p-value (overall rating or work load) is less than 0.05 by Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, indicating the differences between classes are statistically significant. [→ S. G. J. Mochrie 2015](#s-g-j-mochrie-2015)
`q2 i?` Within PHYS 150, PHYS 170, and PHYS 260 evaluations, there is a negative correlation between work load and overall rating: the smaller the work load, the higher the overall rating. [→ S. G. J. Mochrie 2015 (2)](#s-g-j-mochrie-2015-2)

## Evidence

### S. G. J. Mochrie 2015

S. G. J. Mochrie. (2015). Vision and change in introductory physics for the life sciences. [doi:10.1119/1.4947003](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4947003)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Nine-year observational analysis of student evaluations of teaching (SETs) at Yale, 2006-2014 (Tables VIII and IX). Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests compare each pair of classes' overall-rating and work-load distributions; "at least one of the twop-values for each pair of classes is less than 0.05" for all pairs except PHYS 170/PHYS 200. No effect size is printed for this omnibus clustering result.

> "At least one of the twop-values for each pair of classes is less than 0.05 (except for the PHYS 170/PHYS 200 pair) demonstrating that the dif- ferences between classes are indeed statistically signiﬁcant"

### S. G. J. Mochrie 2015 (2)

S. G. J. Mochrie. (2015). Vision and change in introductory physics for the life sciences. [doi:10.1119/1.4947003](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4947003)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Secondary correlational observation from the same nine-year SET data (Fig. 4): the authors note "there is clearly a strong negative correlation" between mean work load and mean overall rating within the PHYS 260 and PHYS 150 evaluations, and note a negative correlation within PHYS 170 as well, though with a smaller range. No correlation coefficient is printed.

> "within the evaluations for both PHYS 260 and PHYS 150, for both of which there is a relatively broad range of numerical values for the mean work load and the mean overall rating, there is clearly a strong negative correlation. between these two measures: the smaller the work load, the higher the overall rating, and vice versa."
