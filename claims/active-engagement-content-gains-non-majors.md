## Evidence

### J. Christopher Moore and Louis J. Rubbo 2011

J. Christopher Moore and Louis J. Rubbo. (2011). Are we teaching students to think like scientists? Originally published 2009. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1101.1090](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1101.1090)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Three-year assessment of the authors' courses: the conceptual physics course used a large-enrollment implementation of Physics by Inquiry, and the astronomy course used Lecture-Tutorials for Introductory Astronomy. Average normalized gains on DIRECT (N=39), TUG-K (N=40) and SPCI (N=28) were "still significant", though lower than reported for some algebra- and calculus-based active-engagement courses.

> "Even with significant disadvantages, substantial gains in content knowledge can still be obtained in conceptual physics and astronomy courses, especially when those courses are designed around a research-­‐verified, active-­‐engagement curriculum."
