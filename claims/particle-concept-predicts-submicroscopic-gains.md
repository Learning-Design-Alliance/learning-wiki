## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among high, middle, and low particle-concept groups, sub-microscopic N-gains differed significantly (ANOVA p = 0.002), with the high group (0.33) exceeding middle (0.29) and low (0.19) groups. [→ Sopandi 2018](#sopandi-2018)

## Evidence

### Sopandi 2018

Sopandi, W., Kadarohman, A., Rosbiono, M., Latip, A., & Sukardi, R. R. (2018). The Courseware of Discontinuous Nature of Matter in Teaching the States of Matter and Their Changes. International Journal of Instruction, 11(1), 61-76. https://doi.org/10.12973/iji.2018.1115a

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

ANOVA comparing N-gain of three groups (high n=65, middle n=38, low n=28 by particle-concept understanding) on sub-microscopic items about changing states of matter. The article reports "a significant difference in the increase of the students' conceptual mastery at sub-microscopic level among the groups"; no effect size is printed.

> "From the test results, a significance score of 0.002 was obtained. It indicates that there is a significant difference in the increase of the students' conceptual mastery at sub-microscopic level among the groups."
