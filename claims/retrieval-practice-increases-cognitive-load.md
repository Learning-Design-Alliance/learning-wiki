# Retrieval practice conditions produced significantly higher cognitive load than rereading

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Cognitive load was significantly lower in the rereading condition than in each of the three retrieval practice conditions, with effect sizes from d = 0.52 to d = 0.79. [→ Redifer 2026](#redifer-2026)

## Evidence

### Redifer 2026

Redifer, J. L., Myers, S. J., Bae, C. L., Naas, A., & Scott, A. (2026). Testing the testing effect with advanced materials while accounting for individual differences. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09758-z

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc Tukey comparisons following a significant condition effect on self-reported cognitive load (perceived difficulty) in the 213-participant between-subjects experiment. The reread-versus-free-recall contrast showed "mean difference = 0.55" with "d = 0.52"; the quiz and generation contrasts reached d = 0.71 and d = 0.79.

> "post-hoc tests indicated that cognitive load was significantly lower in the rereading condition than in all other conditions: free recall condition, mean difference = 0.55, 95% CI [0.01, 1.08], p =.04, d = 0.52"
