# Bias-detection accuracy rose from training to test among untrained control participants after mere exposure to rating study materials (d = 0.21)

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Accuracy rose from training to test phase among control participants without any bias-label training, in a within-group pre-post comparison (d = 0.21). [→ Spinde 2025](#spinde-2025)

## Evidence

### Spinde 2025

Spinde, T., Wu, F., Gaissmaier, W., Demartini, G., & Giese, H. (2025). Enhancing Media Literacy: The Effectiveness of (Human) Annotations and Bias Visualizations on Bias Detection. [doi:10.1016/j.ipm.2025.104244](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ipm.2025.104244)

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r3`

Experiment 1 simple effects: the untrained control group, which received identical instructions to rate sentences but no labels, still improved from training to test with a printed effect size of d = 0.21, so "minimal exposure effects coexist alongside the more pronounced training effects of feedback labels."

> "While both control (t(467) = 4.51, p < .001, d = 0.21) and AI label (t(467) = 4.67, p < .001, d = 0.22) improved accuracy in the test phase"
