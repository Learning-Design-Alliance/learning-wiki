# AI anxiety is negatively associated with academic motivation among university students, in both bivariate and regression analyses

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Zhang Y 2026

Zhang Y, Shi Y and Lu J (2026). The relationship between AI anxiety and academic motivation among university students: the mediating role of emotion regulation and the moderating role of gender. Front. Psychol. 17:1918525. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1918525

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Bivariate Pearson correlation analysis of the cross-sectional survey of 1,484 Chinese university students (Table 1). The study reports that AI anxiety was negatively correlated with academic motivation (r = −0.175, p < 0.001).

> "AI anxiety was negatively correlated with both emotion regulation (r = −0.172, p < 0.001) and academic motivation (r = −0.175, p < 0.001), whereas emotion regulation was positively correlated with academic motivation (r = 0.457, p < 0.001)."

### Zhang Y 2026 (2)

Zhang Y, Shi Y and Lu J (2026). The relationship between AI anxiety and academic motivation among university students: the mediating role of emotion regulation and the moderating role of gender. Front. Psychol. 17:1918525. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1918525

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression analysis from the mediation models (Table 2), controlling for daily AI use duration. The total-effect model showed a significant negative association between AI anxiety and academic motivation (B = −0.177, p < 0.001); the direct association remained negative with emotion regulation in the model (B = −0.101). No standardized effect size is printed for this regression finding.

> "The total-effect model similarly showed a significant negative association between AI anxiety and academic motivation [ B = −0.177, SE = 0.026, 95% CI ( −0.227, −0.127), p < 0.001]."
