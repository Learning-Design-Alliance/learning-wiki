# CBM slope cutoffs of .25 (primary) and .50 (secondary) digits per week define adequate first-grade responsiveness

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In primary prevention, a first-grade student should demonstrate a slope of .25 digits per week or higher to show adequate growth. [→ Powell 2007](#powell-2007)
`q2 i?` At secondary prevention, first-grade students should demonstrate a slope of .50 or higher to be deemed responsive to intervention. [→ Powell 2007 (2)](#powell-2007-2)

## Evidence

### Powell 2007

Powell, S. R., & Seethaler, P. M. (2007). Grade One: Math Computation (Math Case Study #1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED502453

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

The case study reports a normative cutoff for adequate first-grade growth in primary prevention, attributed to "CBM Computation normative data collected on first-grade students." Slope is computed by comparing medians of three probe groups divided by probes minus 1.

> "In primary prevention, a first-grade student should demonstrate a slope of .25 digits per week or higher. This information comes from CBM Computation normative data collected on first-grade students."

### Powell 2007 (2)

Powell, S. R., & Seethaler, P. M. (2007). Grade One: Math Computation (Math Case Study #1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED502453

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

In the secondary prevention section, the case study sets a higher responsiveness cutoff of .50 digits per week. Using this rule, Ilene's slope of 0.625 qualified her to return to primary prevention while Daniel (0.375) and Kelly (0.125) remained.

> "Ms. Lockhart also looks at the CBM slopes for Daniel, Ilene, and Kelly to determine their responsiveness to secondary prevention. At secondary prevention, first-grade students should demonstrate a slope of .50 or higher to be deemed "responsive" to intervention."
