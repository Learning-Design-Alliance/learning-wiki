## Evidence

### Ralf Teusner 2018

Ralf Teusner, Thomas Hille, Thomas Staubitz. (2018). Effects of Automated Interventions in Programming Assignments: Evidence from a Field Experiment. L@S 2018. https://doi.org/10.1145/3231644.3231650

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive Table 4 metrics from the A/B test: time to RFC was 28.8 min for break (-10%) and 30.4 min for RFC (-5%) versus 32.1 min control, and break duration 18.07 min (+25%); the authors state "students take action earlier, regardless of the type".

> "The duration of breaks increases and students take action ear- lier, regardless of the type of the just-in-time intervention."

### Ralf Teusner 2018 (2)

Ralf Teusner, Thomas Hille, Thomas Staubitz. (2018). Effects of Automated Interventions in Programming Assignments: Evidence from a Field Experiment. L@S 2018. https://doi.org/10.1145/3231644.3231650

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Course-end survey of 1,257 students on intervention helpfulness; "a large share (47%) answered they simply ignored them or felt bugged by them (30%)". The authors partly attribute this to no daily intervention limit in the first half of the course.

> "According to the course-end survey most interventions were not perceived as very helpful to the students, as a large share (47%) answered they simply ignored them or felt bugged by them (30%)."
