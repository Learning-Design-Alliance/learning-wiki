# MA scores were non-normally distributed, so high MA was defined empirically as scores at or above the 90th percentile, capturing 11% of the sample

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The mAMAS score distribution differed significantly from normal (W = .95, p <.001), so high MA was defined as scores at or above the 90th percentile (raw score 30+), yielding 11% of the sample. [→ Devine 2017](#devine-2017)

## Evidence

### Devine 2017

Devine, A., Hill, F., Carey, E., & Szűcs, D. (2017). Cognitive and Emotional Math Problems Largely Dissociate: Prevalence of Developmental Dyscalculia and Mathematics Anxiety. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000222

`q3 · i?`

Shapiro-Wilk testing of mAMAS scores in the full sample showed non-normality ("W = .95, p <.001"), leading the authors to reject SD-based definitions and define high MA at or above the 90th percentile; the final high-MA group was 11% of the sample. mAMAS reliability was good (Cronbach's α = .85; ordinal α = 0.89).

> "the distribution of mAMAS scores in the current study was significantly different from normal (N=1757, W = .95, p <.001, skewness =.70; Kurtosis = -.006"
