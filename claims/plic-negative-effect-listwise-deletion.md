# Under listwise deletion, PEPPER students scored significantly lower than SALT students on the PLIC

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The lab-condition effect on PLIC scores was negative and statistically significant under listwise deletion (γ01k = −0.1820, p = 0.016), but not under multiple imputation. [→ Markert 2026](#markert-2026)

## Evidence

### Markert 2026

Markert, E., Verostek, M., Dew, M., Chen, X., Lory-Moran, M., Padavan, S., Walia, H., Wittich, P., and Holmes, N.G. (2026). CURE-like, not cure-all: Varying broad relevance in experimentation labs produces similar student outcomes. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2606.00464](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2606.00464)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Hierarchical linear modeling results (Table IV) using listwise deletion show a negative standardized effect on PLIC for the PEPPER condition: "γ 01k =−0.1820±0.0755,p= 0.016". The effect did not persist under multiple imputation; no standardized effect size beyond the coefficient is printed.

> "Using listwise deletion, we observed no statistically significant effects (p >0.05) for any construct except the PLIC (γ 01k =−0.1820±0.0755,p= 0.016)."
