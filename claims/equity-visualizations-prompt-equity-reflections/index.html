Evidence¶

Ali Raza 2024¶

Ali Raza, Tamara Sumner and William R. Penuel. (2024). The Science Student Electronic Exit Ticket (SEET) System: Visualizations to Help Teachers Notice and Reflect on Classroom Inequalities. Journal of Learning Analytics, 11(1), 87-100. https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2024.8199

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Diary study of 42 middle school science teachers using the SEET system over a two-month professional learning series; teachers recorded 248 noticing and wondering reflections, of which 35 considered students' race and/or gender. The article reports "zero reflections related to race/ethnicity and gender when using the whole class visualization."

"As shown in Table 2, only the equity visualizations (Figure 2a, 2b, 2c) prompted teachers to reflect on how race/ethnicity or gender may impact student classroom experiences. Teachers reported zero reflections related to race/ethnicity and gender when using the whole class visualization."

Ali Raza 2024 (2)¶

Ali Raza, Tamara Sumner and William R. Penuel. (2024). The Science Student Electronic Exit Ticket (SEET) System: Visualizations to Help Teachers Notice and Reflect on Classroom Inequalities. Journal of Learning Analytics, 11(1), 87-100. https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2024.8199

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Semi-structured interviews (10-15 minutes) with each of the 42 teachers after the professional learning series, in which participants ranked all six visualizations and gave rationales. The article reports equity set visualizations prompted "most reflections on race/ethnicity and gender (92%)" versus 8% for whole class visualizations.

"We saw comparable results in the interview data, where equity set visualizations prompted most reflections on race/ethnicity and gender (92%), compared to the whole class visualizations (8%, Table 2)."
