# Workshop participants reported that the ESSA four-tier model under-delivers evidence about for whom and under what conditions technologies work

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants critiqued the four-tier model for not delivering evidence about whether a technology will work in situations like the user's own and for whom and under what conditions it is effective. [→ critique-1](#critique-1)
`q2 i?` Participants noted more efficacy studies than effectiveness studies reach the top tier, and argued some what-works interventions are narrowly applicable to only five percent of students, citing Ginsburg & Smith (2016). [→ critique-2](#critique-2)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Workshop discussion and critique of the four-tier model reported in the brief's Four-Tier Model section. Participants endorsed rigor but asked for defined dimensions of rigor; the quote names the two under-delivered evidence aspects.

> "school procurement leaders place a strong priority on interrogating "will it work in a situation like mine?" and evidence about "for whom and under what conditions" a technology-supported intervention is effective — two aspects of evidence often not delivered under the four-tier model."
