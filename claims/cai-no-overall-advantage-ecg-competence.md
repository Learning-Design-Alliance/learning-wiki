# CAI is not better than face-to-face teaching for acquiring ECG competence overall, with high heterogeneity among studies

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Meta-analysis found no significant difference in acquired ECG competence between computer-assisted and face-to-face instruction (SMD=0.32, 95% CI −0.09 to 0.74). [→ Viljoen Charle André 2019](#viljoen-charle-andré-2019)

## Evidence

### Viljoen Charle André 2019

Viljoen Charle André, Scott Millar Rob, Engel Mark E, Shelton Mary, Burch Vanessa. (2019). Is computer-assisted instruction more effective than other educational methods in achieving ECG competence amongst medical students and residents? A systematic review and meta-analysis. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2018-028800

`q3 · i?`

Random-effects meta-analysis of eight comparative studies pooling standardised mean differences for ECG competence acquisition. The pooled SMD=0.32 (95% CI −0.09 to 0.74) crossed zero, with "I2=88.9%" indicating significant heterogeneity; effect sizes ranged from −1.08 to 1.09.

> "Overall, we found that CAI was not better than face-to-face teaching for acquiring ECG competence (standardised mean difference (SMD)=0.32 (95% CI −0.09 to 0.74); eight studies, n=945; I2=88.9%)"
