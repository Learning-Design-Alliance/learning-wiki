## Evidence

### Michels Nele R. 2019

Michels Nele R., Vanhomwegen Els. (2019). An educational study to investigate the efficacy of three training methods for infiltration techniques on self-efficacy and skills of trainees in general practice. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-019-1023-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-group pre-post comparison on the OSCE in the randomized study of 48 general practice trainees, with medians and minimal-maximal values reported per region (Tables 2–3) and Kruskal-Wallis testing. The article states "the participants' performances significantly improved and this independently of the training strategy". No effect size is printed.

> "As can be seen in Tables 2 and 3, the participants' performances significantly improved and this independently of the training strategy that was used. Participants showed to be better in performing all the different steps belonging to infiltrations and this with a higher proficiency, with a better systematic and more complete."
