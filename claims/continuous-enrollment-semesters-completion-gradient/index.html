Evidence¶

Mahaffie 2024¶

Mahaffie, S., Nagaoka, J., & Usher, A. (2024). Measuring Patterns of Immediate and Continued College Enrollment among CPS Graduates. University of Chicago Consortium on School Research and the To&Through Project. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/measuring-patterns-of-immediate-and-continued-college-enrollment-among-CPSs-graduates

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive analysis (Figure 5) of completion rates for CPS graduates continuously enrolled across each number of semesters in the first four years after high school graduation. Students meeting the existing four-semester persistence definition completed at "a rate of 71%" versus 65% for three continuous semesters, a 6 percentage point gap.

"students who persisted for at least four semesters completed at a rate of 71%, while students who completed at least three semesters of college completed at a rate of 65%"

Mahaffie 2024 (2)¶

Mahaffie, S., Nagaoka, J., & Usher, A. (2024). Measuring Patterns of Immediate and Continued College Enrollment among CPS Graduates. University of Chicago Consortium on School Research and the To&Through Project. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/measuring-patterns-of-immediate-and-continued-college-enrollment-among-CPSs-graduates

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Analysis of completion rates by number of semesters of continuous enrollment in the first four years after graduation, used to test whether any persistence definition variant was more predictive of completion. The authors "did not find clear cutoff points", supporting the choice of a simpler definition on other grounds.

"We confirmed that each additional term of college enrollment was associated with a higher completion rate, but did not find clear cutoff points that indicated any specific semesters had particular predictive value over and above other semesters of enrollment."
