## Evidence

### Bateman J 2013

Bateman J, Allen M, Samani D, Kidd J, Davies D. (2013). Virtual patient design: exploring what works and why. A grounded theory study. Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.12151

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Discussion-section interpretation of the focus group data: elements intended to promote clinical reasoning, such as 'non e-tasks' and the scoring counter, at times frustrated students, while the scoring counter helped some students' learning and assessment focus.

> "Some design properties produced a mixture of positive and negative effects. These included: case pathway choices, such as branching versus linear case narratives; some structured approaches to clinical reasoning using the evidence available, and data quantity and presentation"

### Bateman J 2013 (2)

Bateman J, Allen M, Samani D, Kidd J, Davies D. (2013). Virtual patient design: exploring what works and why. A grounded theory study. Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.12151

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Model-derived prediction from the discussion, not a tested comparison: branched designs raise complexity and e-inertia, with effects on the skipping threshold and case fracturing described as "complex and different for each student".

> "The model predicts that using a branched case design potentially makes cases more realistic, but is associated with increased complexity and a higher frequency of and more types of 'e-inertia' and errors"
