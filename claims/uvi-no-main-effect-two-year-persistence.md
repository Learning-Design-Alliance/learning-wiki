## Evidence

### Hecht 2019

Hecht, C. A., Harackiewicz, J. M., Priniski, S. J., Canning, E. A., Tibbetts, Y., & Hyde, J. S. (2019). Promoting Persistence in the Biological and Medical Sciences: An Expectancy-Value Approach to Intervention. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000356

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Path models in the two-year follow-up of the randomized trial tested the UVI main effect on both binary persistence outcomes. The article reports no significant main effect on either, in contrast to the significant main effect on course grade in the original study. Equivalence was not tested.

> "The main effect of the UVI was not significant for either enrollment in the second course or biomedical major two years post-intervention, in contrast to the significant main effect on course grade originally observed in this study"

### Hecht 2019 (2)

Hecht, C. A., Harackiewicz, J. M., Priniski, S. J., Canning, E. A., Tibbetts, Y., & Hyde, J. S. (2019). Promoting Persistence in the Biological and Medical Sciences: An Expectancy-Value Approach to Intervention. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000356

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

The same basic model included FG, URM, and their interactions with the UVI. The article reports no significant interactions, so the intervention did not differentially affect persistence for first-generation or underrepresented minority students.

> "Neither FG nor URM status significantly interacted with the UVI to predict enrollment or major, suggesting that the UVI did not increase persistence differentially for FG or URM students (ps > .25; see Table 3 for full model output)."
