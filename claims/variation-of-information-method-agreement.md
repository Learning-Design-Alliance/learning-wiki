# The k-means and hierarchical partitions of the same student sample are consistent, with the best agreement (VI = 0.28) between the four-cluster k-means solution and the six-cluster hierarchical solution

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Variation of Information distances between k-means and hierarchical partitions are 0.34, 0.38 and 0.28 for the 4-, 5- and 6-cluster hierarchical solutions respectively, the smallest for the six-cluster comparison. [→ Battaglia 2014](#battaglia-2014)

## Evidence

### Battaglia 2014

Battaglia, Di Paola & Fazio. (2014). Cluster Analysis of Educational Data: an Example of Quantitative Study on the answers to an Open-Ended Questionnaire. Università di Palermo. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1512.08998](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1512.08998)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparison of the two clustering methods on the same N = 124 sample using the Variation of Information criterion, which measures information gained and lost between partitions. The values 0.34, 0.38 and 0.28 led the authors to conclude the best agreement is between the 4-clustering k-means and 6-clustering hierarchical results.

> "We calculated the value of VI to compare the 4-clustering results of k-means method with the 4-clustering, 5-clustering and 6-clustering results of H-ClA method and obtained the values of 0.34, 0.38 and 0.28, respectively ."
