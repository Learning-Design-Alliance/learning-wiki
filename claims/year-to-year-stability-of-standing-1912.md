## Evidence

### L. E. Frailey and C. M. Crane 1912

L. E. Frailey and C. M. Crane. (1912). Correlation of Excellence in Different School Subjects Based on a Study of School Grades. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Educational_Psychology

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparison of the same 32 pupils' group standings in four successive years of English. The authors report 13 of 32 pupils held the same group all four years and no pupil appeared in all four groups, concluding there is "a correlation of excellence in successive years of study."

> "In brief 13 out of 32 pupils were in the same group for all of the four years. Of the remaining 19, fourteen varied only from one group to a connected one in the rank scale, from I to II, II to ILL, etc. No pupil appeared in all four of the groups."

### L. E. Frailey and C. M. Crane 1912 (2)

L. E. Frailey and C. M. Crane. (1912). Correlation of Excellence in Different School Subjects Based on a Study of School Grades. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Educational_Psychology

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The authors' direct comparison of the year-to-year English results with the cross-subject results for the same 32-pupil group, finding year-to-year standing the more stable of the two.

> "And if we compare these results with those of our first problem, we find (5) that a more perfect correlation exists between excellence in one study during succeeding years than exists between excellence in various studies of different nature."
