# The weekday screen time association with grades at age 16 is significant only among girls in the gender-stratified analysis

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among girls, high weekday screen time at age 13 predicted lower odds of A–D grades at age 16 (OR 0.309, 99% CI 0.106–0.901, p = 0.005); for boys the similar odds ratio (0.464) was not significant after multiple-comparison adjustment. [→ Kjellenberg Karin 2025](#kjellenberg-karin-2025)

## Evidence

### Kjellenberg Karin 2025

Kjellenberg Karin, Helgadóttir Björg, Ekblom Örjan, Nyberg Gisela. (2025). Fitness and Screen Time at Age 13 Relates to Academic Performance at Age 16. Acta Paediatrica. https://doi.org/10.1111/apa.70024

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Gender-stratified multilevel logistic regression (girls n = 517, boys n = 489) in the same cohort; the article reports "odds ratio: 0.309" for girls and notes the boys' estimate did not remain significant after adjusting for multiple comparisons.

> "Specifically, girls in the high screen time group on weekdays had lower odds of receiving grades A–D (odds ratio: 0.309), compared to those in the low screen time group."
