## Evidence

### Zhang Li-dan 2026

Zhang Li-dan, Wang Li, Chen Zhuang-gui, Wu He-yan, Nie Xing, Li Zhen, Zhang Chao-jie, Ye Xiao-ting, Chen Hua-bao, Tang Yan-lai, Chen Chun, Xu Ling-ling. (2026). Effectiveness of situational simulation and case-based learning in pediatric intern cardiopulmonary resuscitation training: a pilot study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09050-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled pilot study of 100 pediatric interns at an academic teaching hospital, with 50 per group and blinded outcome assessors. Table 3 reports hands-on CPR means of 94.20 ± 4.17 (intervention) vs. 79.56 ± 6.04 (traditional), t = -14.20, P < 0.001. The article reports "P < 0.05" for the between-group comparison; no effect size is printed.

> "the situational simulation with CBL group resulted in a significant improvement in clinical performance scores: hands-on CPR (79.56 ± 6.04 vs. 94.20 ± 4.17) and bag-valve-mask ventilation (81.14 ± 6.33 vs. 93.47 ± 3.57) (P < 0.05)"
