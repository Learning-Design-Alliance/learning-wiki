# Students who participate in an Opener are significantly more likely to revisit and revise their embedded-assessment work than students with no Opener

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who had an Opener were significantly more likely to revisit and revise their work than students who did not (66% vs 33% revision rate). [→ Zertuche 2012](#zertuche-2012)

## Evidence

### Zertuche 2012

Zertuche, A., Gerard, L., & Linn, M. C. (2012). How do Openers Contribute to Student Learning? International Electronic Journal of Elementary Education, 5(1), 79-92. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1070484

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental comparison of original vs revised embedded-assessment responses in the WISE Hydrogen Fuel Cell Car unit: 105 Opener pairs vs 30 no-Opener pairs. The article reports "In the Opener condition, revision was twice as likely as in the no Opener condition."

> "Students who had an Opener ( M = .68, SD = .48) were significantly more likely to revisit and revise their work than students who did not have an Opener ( M = .33, SD = .48), t(131) = -3.5, p < 0.001."
