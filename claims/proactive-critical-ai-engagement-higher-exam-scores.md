# Among GPT-assisted students, proactive and critical engagement patterns are associated with higher exam scores while limited engagement shows little benefit relative to control

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Experimental participants whose behavior was active-trial, verification, or error-correction were associated with higher exam scores than the control group. [→ Jingwei Yi 2026](#jingwei-yi-2026)
`q2 i?` Abstention was close to the control group and rote-adoption showed limited benefit relative to control. [→ Jingwei Yi 2026](#jingwei-yi-2026)

## Evidence

### Jingwei Yi 2026

Jingwei Yi, Yueqi Xie, Jiyan He, Rui Ye, Junming Huang, Bin Zhu, Sean Rintel, Yu Xie, Xing Xie, and Fangzhao Wu. (2026). Learning behavior accounts for background-related advantage in AI-assisted education. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10101

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Behavior-outcome comparison within the randomized controlled trial of 318 university students, using one-sided Brunner–Munzel tests with Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment comparing each behavior pattern's exam score against the control group; the article reports that "active-trial, verification, and error-correction are associated with higher exam scores."

> "As shown in Figure 2c, abstention is close to the control group, indicating that unused GPT access provides little observable learning advantage. Rote-adoption also shows limited benefit relative to the control group, even though it may help students complete assignment problems more directly. By contrast, active-trial, verification, and error-correction are associated with higher exam scores."
