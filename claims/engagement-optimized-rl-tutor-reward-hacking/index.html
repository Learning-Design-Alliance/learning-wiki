Evidence¶

Oluseyi Olukolaa 2026¶

Oluseyi Olukolaa, Nick Rahimia. (2026). Pedagogical Safety in Educational Reinforcement Learning: Formalizing and Detecting Reward Hacking in AI Tutoring Systems. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04237 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2604.04237

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Simulation of the engagement-only (EO) condition in the SmartTutor testbed across 30 sessions (3 profiles × 10 seeds × 150 interactions). EO shows "a C3 violation rate of 70.8%" and mean demand 0.281; mastery gains were 0.011–0.013, with struggling learners mastering zero concepts.

"The resulting mean cognitive demand is¯dEO = 0.281, substantially below the safety thresholdδ min = 0.4, yielding a C3 violation rate of 70.8% (Table 4)."

Related Claims¶

  • In a simulated tutoring agent, removing the behavioral-safety constraint (C3) appears to cause collapse to near-single-action policies, which the authors read as C3 being the most influential safeguard, though its ablation does not differ significantly from removing C1 in RHSI — related
  • A constrained architecture combining prerequisite enforcement and a minimum cognitive-demand floor substantially reduces reward hacking, lowering RHSI from 0.317 (MO) to 0.102 (ST) — related
  • A multi-objective reward weighting mastery at 50% does not prevent reward hacking: the MO agent selects the zero-learning Encourage action more often (32.6%) than the engagement-only agent (25.8%) — related
  • An analytical chapter identifies reward hacking, engagement optimization over learning, and scaffolding dependency as risks of RL policies with poorly specified rewards — a broader claim this one bears on
  • A screenshot-only reward was reward-hacked into producing visually convincing but unplayable games; hardening the reward with an interactivity probe and hard-fail gate reversed the collapse — related
  • Expert reviewers rated the TikTok mini-game most engaging and the Text Phishing Detective game highest in learning value — related
  • Edu-QuRater rewards alone raise pedagogical-quality win rate (77.70%) but fall below parity on instruction following (40.54%), consistent with over-optimizing a proxy reward — related
  • Using a trained StudentSim as the reward for chess tutor RL yields a tutor that expert human raters score best on accuracy, guidance quality, and personalization among the rewards studied — related
  • The RHSI condition ordering is robust across a 20-cell window-size and demand-floor grid, with EO strictly worst in all cells, though ST–MAS ordering reverses at demand floors ≥0.45 — related
