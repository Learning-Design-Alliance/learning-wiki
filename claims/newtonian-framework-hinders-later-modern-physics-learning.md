### Shabajee 2004

Shabajee, P. and Postlethwaite, K. (2004). What happened to modern physics? School Science Review, 81(297), p51-56. Originally published 2000. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0401016](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0401016)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The authors' argument, citing research by Driver (1985, 1994), that assimilated limited understandings resist change. They state there is 'a great deal of research evidence that once children assimilate a mistaken or limited understanding' of the world it is difficult to change. No new data are reported.

> "There is a great deal of research evidence that once children assimilate a mistaken or limited understanding of their world into th eir cognitive framework, it is difficult to change it"

### Shabajee 2004 (2)

Shabajee, P. and Postlethwaite, K. (2004). What happened to modern physics? School Science Review, 81(297), p51-56. Originally published 2000. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0401016](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0401016)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The authors' argument that lesson examples implying simple 'A leads to B' linear causality misrepresent the physical world, since chaos theory indicates simple linear causality holds only in special circumstances, and 'A cognitive model based on these elements of Newtonian mechanics and simple linear causality will not prepare pupils to embrace modern physics'.

> "A cognitive model based on these elements of Newtonian mechanics and simple linear causality will not prepare pupils to embrace modern physics."
