# Sentiment scores of LLM-generated replies differ significantly from human replies under both classifiers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Paired t-tests showed significant differences between sentiment scores of LLM-generated and human replies regardless of classifier. [→ Liu 2025](#liu-2025)

## Evidence

### Liu 2025

Liu, Z., Xing, W., Jiao, X., & Li, C. (2025). Exploring Fairness and Explainability in LLM-Generated Support for Online Learning Discussion Forums. Journal of Learning Analytics, 12(3), 8–33. https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2025.8885

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Paired t-tests on 8,322 pairs comparing human reply sentiment with each fine-tuned model's replies, using both BERT and SOWB classifiers; the article reports "significant differences between the sentiment scores" for all three models, with Table 6 printing means such as 4.18 (0.41) vs. 3.84 (0.51) for GPT-2 under BERT.

> "It can be observed that regardless of whether BERT or SOWB was used as the sentiment classifier, there are significant differences between the sentiment scores of the responses generated by the three models and the sentiment scores of human responses."
