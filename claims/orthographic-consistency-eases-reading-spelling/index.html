Döhla D and Heim S 2016¶

Döhla D and Heim S (2016). Developmental Dyslexia and Dysgraphia: What can We Learn from the One About the Other? Front. Psychol. 6:2045. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.02045

q2 · i? · review · r2

Cross-linguistic comparison reported by the review: Landerl et al. (1997) compared German and English learners and found high-consistency-language learners "have fewer deﬁcits in reading", while reading fluency was independent of language. PA remains the main predictor of reading across languages.

"Landerl et al. (1997) found out that children learning a language with a high consistency of letter-sound correspondence have fewer deﬁcits in reading than those learning to read less consistent languages whereas skill of reading ﬂuency is independent of language."

Cross-linguistic spelling study reported by the review: Caravolas and Bruck (1993) compared English and Czech first-graders' phonologically accurate pseudoword spellings and found Czech learners "show faster spelling development". Children learning consistent languages also acquire more advanced spelling skills more easily.

"CaravolasandBruck(1993) comparedEnglish and Czech children with respect to their phonologically accurate pseudowordspellingsinﬁrstgrade.Theydiscoveredthatchildren wholearnorthographically consistent languageslikeCzechshow faster spelling development than children who learn the less orthographically consistent language of English."
