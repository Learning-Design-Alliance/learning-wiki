## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Curbing cheating directly, for example by heavier proctoring or antiplagiarism software, gives no reason to assume students would start studying more, so reducing cheating does not necessarily have a positive impact on education. [→ Mathieu Bouville 2009](#mathieu-bouville-2009)

## Evidence

### Mathieu Bouville 2009

Mathieu Bouville. (2009). Why is cheating wrong?. [doi:10.1007/s11217-009-9148-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11217-009-9148-0)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument (section 4) about policy consequences. The article reasons that students who want passable grades with little work may keep their amount of work constant rather than study more if cheating becomes impossible. Philosophical analysis, no empirical data.

> "That cheating has a negative impact on education does not mean that reducing cheating necessarily has a positive impact on education."
