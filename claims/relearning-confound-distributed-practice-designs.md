# Relearning-to-criterion designs confound distribution of practice with amount of second-session practice

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In studies training to criterion in later sessions, longer ISIs necessarily provide more relearning trials, making it impossible to isolate distributed practice effects per se. [→ Cepeda 2006](#cepeda-2006)

## Evidence

### Cepeda 2006

Cepeda, N. J., Pashler, H., Vul, E., Wixted, J. T., & Rohrer, D. (2006). Distributed Practice in Verbal Recall Tasks: A Review and Quantitative Synthesis. Psychological Bulletin, 132(3), 354-380. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354

`q1 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Methodological argument, not a tested result: the authors identify criterion-training procedures (e.g., Bahrick's studies) as confounding ISI with relearning amount and recommend fixed relearning trials to equalize initial learning.

> "Thus, in designs that have this feature, distribution of practice is confounded with the amount of practice time during the second (and subsequent) sessions. This makes it impossible to know whether differences in final-test performance reflect distributed practice effects per se"
