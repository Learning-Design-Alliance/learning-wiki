## Evidence

### Chen et al. 2015

Chin-Chih Chen, Jill V. Hamm, & Thomas W. Farmer. (2015). Exceptionality and Peer Victimization Involvement in Late Childhood: Subtypes, Stability, and Social Marginalization. Remedial and Special Education, 36(5). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED588091

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r3`

Multilevel logistic analysis of spring sixth-grade peer victimization subtypes in a cluster randomized trial with 1,861 students in 36 rural schools, controlling for gender and minority status. Students with disabilities were "more likely to be nominated as victims and bully-victims" with OR = 2.145 and OR = 1.697, and less likely to be noninvolved (OR = .352).

> "Compared to nondisabled students, students with disabilities were more likely to be nominated as victims and bully-victims (OR = 2.145, p < .001; OR = 1.697, p < .05, respectively), but were less likely to be identified as noninvolved students (OR = .352, p < .001)."

### Chen et al. 2015 (2)

Chin-Chih Chen, Jill V. Hamm, & Thomas W. Farmer. (2015). Exceptionality and Peer Victimization Involvement in Late Childhood: Subtypes, Stability, and Social Marginalization. Remedial and Special Education, 36(5). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED588091

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive subtype percentages from the same sixth-grade spring assessment. "Over 74% of students with disabilities were involved in peer victimization" (22.7% bullies, 33.3% victims, 18.2% bully-victims); the majority of academically gifted students were not involved (61.4%).

> "In the spring of sixth grade, over 74% of students with disabilities were involved in peer victimization (22.7% - bullies; 33.3% - victims; 18.2% - bully-victims)."
