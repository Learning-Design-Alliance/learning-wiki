# Economic, educational, and geopolitical inequalities structure differential access to AI capabilities, reproducing an epistemic hierarchy

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Access to AI capabilities is structured by three intersecting inequalities: economic (premium models available only to paying subscribers), educational (interrogation capacity correlated with educational attainment), and geopolitical (uneven connectivity and Western-dominated training data). Theoretical argument, supported by cited literature, that economic inequality structures access at the most basic level: "premium AI models" are subscriber-only while others are confined to less capable free tiers or no access, replicating income-differentiated access to professional expertise. Educational capital and geopolitical connectivity structure access at second and third levels.
