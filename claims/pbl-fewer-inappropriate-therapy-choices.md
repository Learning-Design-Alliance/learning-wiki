## Evidence

### Brinkman David J. 2021

Brinkman David J., Monteiro Teresa, Monteiro Emilia C., Richir Milan C., van Agtmael Michiel A., Tichelaar Jelle. (2021). Switching from a traditional undergraduate programme in (clinical) pharmacology and therapeutics to a problem-based learning programme. European Journal of Clinical Pharmacology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00228-020-03027-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Clinical case-scenario skill assessment in the same observational pre/post comparison; treatment plans scored by a clinical pharmacologist as inappropriate, suboptimal or appropriate. The article reports "significantly fewer inappropriate therapy choices (p = 0.023)" for the PBL group; inappropriate plans were 62% vs 51% of plans. No effect size was printed.

> "Overall, students in the PBL programme made significantly fewer inappropriate therapy choices (p = 0.023) and fewer erroneous prescriptions than students in the traditional programme (p = 0.27) (Table 4)."

### Brinkman David J. 2021 (2)

Brinkman David J., Monteiro Teresa, Monteiro Emilia C., Richir Milan C., van Agtmael Michiel A., Tichelaar Jelle. (2021). Switching from a traditional undergraduate programme in (clinical) pharmacology and therapeutics to a problem-based learning programme. European Journal of Clinical Pharmacology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00228-020-03027-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Prescription-error screening (Dean et al. classification) within the same case-scenario assessment, reported in Table 4: prescriptions including errors were 81% in the traditional group vs 75% in the PBL group, a non-significant Mann-Whitney difference (p = 0.27); equivalence was not tested.

> "Number of drug prescriptions including errors	160 (81)	288 (75)	0.27d"
