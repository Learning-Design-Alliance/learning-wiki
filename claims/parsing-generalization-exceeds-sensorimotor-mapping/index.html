Evidence¶

B. P. Johnson 2023¶

B. P. Johnson, I. Iturrate, R. Y. Fakhreddine, M. Bönstrup, E. R. Buch, E. M. Robertson and L. G. Cohen. (2023). Generalization of procedural motor sequence learning after a single practice trial. npj Science of Learning 8:45. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00194-7

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Experiment 4 (n = 212 additional participants) added a RANDOM group typing random keypresses for one trial before skill B, isolating sensorimotor mapping. The parsing-cue contribution exceeded mapping: "approximately 19% larger in the PARSING RULE than in the RANDOM group (Independent-samples t-test: t (250) = 3.577, p < 0.001".

"Performance at the onset of skill B was approximately 19% larger in the PARSING RULE than in the RANDOM group (Independent-samples t-test: t (250) = 3.577, p < 0.001; Fig. 4c)"

B. P. Johnson 2023 (2)¶

B. P. Johnson, I. Iturrate, R. Y. Fakhreddine, M. Bönstrup, E. R. Buch, E. M. Robertson and L. G. Cohen. (2023). Generalization of procedural motor sequence learning after a single practice trial. npj Science of Learning 8:45. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00194-7

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Combined analysis of Experiment 4 groups with Experiment 3 data; PARSING RULE micro-offline gains were "46% and 81% of those observed in the SAME A and SAME B groups", which the authors describe as highlighting substantial variation in generalization tied to skill structure.

"Micro-of ﬂine gains from the end of the ﬁrst skill to the onset of the second ( GenB0 /C0 Af ) in the PARSING RULE group represented 46% and 81% of those observed in the SAME A and SAME B groups"
