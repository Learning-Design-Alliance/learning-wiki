# Students using Learn Your Way scored significantly higher on an immediate recall assessment than students using a digital textbook

## Evidence

### Courtney Heldreth 2026

Courtney Heldreth, Diana Akrong, Laura M. Vardoulakis, Nicole E. Miller, Yael Haramaty, Lidan Hackmon, Lior Belinsky, Abraham Ortiz Tapia, Lucy Tootill, and Scott Siebert. (2026). An Experimental Evaluation of an AI-Powered Interactive Learning Platform. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.3389/frai.2026.1783117](https://doi.org/10.3389/frai.2026.1783117)

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

In the lab experiment, 60 randomly assigned high school students learned a neuroscience chapter with Learn Your Way (n = 30) or a PDF textbook (n = 30) for 20-40 minutes, then took a 12-point immediate assessment. The directional Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney test showed Learn Your Way "performed significantly better on the immediate recall assessment" (Z = 1.89, p = .029, r = .24).

> "students in the Learn Your Way condition (M dn = 9.5; M = 9.2, SD = 2.2) performed significantly better on the immediate recall assessment compared to students in the Textbook condition (M dn = 8; M = 8.2, SD = 2)"
