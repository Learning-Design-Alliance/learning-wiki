## Evidence

### Michael C. Wittmann 2005

Michael C. Wittmann, Jeffrey T. Morgan, and Lei Bao. (2005). Addressing student models of energy loss in quantum tunnelling. Submitted for publication to the European Journal of Physics. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/26/6/001](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/26/6/001)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Content analysis of one student's final examination response found correct wave functions in each region — sinusoidal in regions I and III, exponential in region II — yet "inconsistencies between his mathematical, graphical, and verbal responses to the question," with the axis shift absent from the mathematics.

> "These responses are all correct, but we note inconsistencies between his mathematical, graphical, and verbal responses to the question. The oscillation axis shift for the wave function is not represented mathematically."

### Michael C. Wittmann 2005 (2)

Michael C. Wittmann, Jeffrey T. Morgan, and Lei Bao. (2005). Addressing student models of energy loss in quantum tunnelling. Submitted for publication to the European Journal of Physics. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/26/6/001](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/26/6/001)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

In the Maryland comparison study, the article reports that "energy-loss" students used mathematical equations inconsistent with their conceptual understanding, and that the wave number, k, was the same in all students' equations, implying a constant energy.

> "“energy-loss” students used mathematical equations inconsistent with their conceptual understanding.  The wave number, k, was the same in all students’ equations, implying a constant energy."
