## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Students described problem representation, pertinent positives and negatives, and illness scripts while working through cases, and integrated peer feedback, though heuristics were not described. [→ N. Mitchell 2025](#n-mitchell-2025)

## Evidence

### N. Mitchell 2025

N. Mitchell, M. Rashid, J. Foulds, and K. Forbes. (2025). Thinking About the Why: A Qualitative Study on Students' Perspectives of Paediatric Team‐Based Learning Discussions. The Clinical Teacher. https://doi.org/10.1111/tct.70161

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Focus-group analysis found students described illness scripts and peer feedback integration; the discussion notes "heuristics, or rapid non‐analytic processing, were not described by our participants, which may be due to being relatively novice".

> "They were able to describe established strategies involved in clinical reasoning, even if not labelled as such; these included articulating a problem representation as they worked through the cases, highlighting the pertinent positives and negatives."
