# CPS four-year high school graduation rose from 58 percent (2007) to 82 percent (2019), with 18-point growth excluding options school graduates

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The four-year high school graduation rate increased from 58 percent in 2007 to 82 percent in 2019 when options school graduates are included, and from 58 percent to 76 percent when they are excluded. [→ Nagaoka 2020](#nagaoka-2020)
`q2 i?` Black and Latino young men had the largest graduation rate increases between 2007 and 2019, at 26 and 28 percentage points respectively, and across all racial/ethnic groups young women were more likely to graduate than young men. [→ Nagaoka 2020 (2)](#nagaoka-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Nagaoka 2020

Nagaoka, J., Mahaffie, S., Usher, A., & Seeskin, A. (2020). The Educational Attainment of Chicago Public Schools Students: 2019. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://toandthrough.uchicago.edu/tool/cps/2019/#/milestones

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Figure 7 takeaway from this descriptive report on four-year graduation rates of CPS ninth-grade cohorts, 2007–2019. With roughly the same number of students (approximately 26,000), the class of 2019 had over 5,000 more graduates than the class of 2007 (21,608 vs. 16,598).

> "The percentage of CPS students completing high school in four years has steadily increased; for the graduating class of 2019, the rate reached 82 percent compared to 58 percent for students who graduated from high school in 2007, an increase of almost 24 percentage points."

### Nagaoka 2020 (2)

Nagaoka, J., Mahaffie, S., Usher, A., & Seeskin, A. (2020). The Educational Attainment of Chicago Public Schools Students: 2019. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://toandthrough.uchicago.edu/tool/cps/2019/#/milestones

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Table 3 takeaway comparing 2004 and 2016 ninth-grade cohort graduation rates by race/ethnicity and gender, with and without options program graduates. The overall 2019 rate for Latina young women was 87 percent, a 19-point increase from 2007.

> "Across all racial/ethnic groups, young women were more likely to graduate from high school than young men. Young men and young women graduated from options schools at similar rates."
