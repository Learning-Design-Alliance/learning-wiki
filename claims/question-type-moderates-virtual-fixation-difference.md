# Visual attention between virtual and non-virtual elements depends on question type: the virtual-over-real fixation difference is more pronounced for conceptual than procedural questions

## Evidence

### David Dzsotjan 2024

David Dzsotjan, Atakan Coban, Christoph Hoyer, Stefan Küchemann, Jürgen Durst, Anna Donhauser, Jochen Kuhn. (2024). Quantum cryptography visualized: assessing visual attention on multiple representations with eye tracking in an AR-enhanced quantum cryptography student experiment. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2410.21975](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.21975)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Descriptive eye-tracking data from the pilot study (N=38) shown in Fig. 6, normalized by the sum of all fixation durations per question. The article states students "on average look longer at the virtual visualizations" with a question-type moderation.

> "The normalized total fixation durations for the virtual and non-virtual elements of the experiment show that students on average look longer at the virtual visualizations. For questions 1 and 2, this difference is more pronounced than for questions 3 and 4."
