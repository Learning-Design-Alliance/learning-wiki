## Evidence

### Celia Anteneodo 2020

Celia Anteneodo, Carolina Brito, Alan Alves-Brito, Simone Silva Alexandre, Beatriz Nattrodt D'Avila, Débora Peres Menezes. (2020). Brazilian physicists community diversity, equity and inclusion: a first diagnostic. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010136](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010136)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Respondents rated identification level (very, little, not at all) with listed motivations, non-exclusive (Fig. 4). The summary reports math/physics at school influenced the decision for 68% and around 62% pointed to contributing to the progress of science.

> "The three main motivating factors are: (i) ease in math- ematics at school, (ii) aﬃnity for physics at school, and (iii) desire to take part in the progress of science."

### Celia Anteneodo 2020 (2)

Celia Anteneodo, Carolina Brito, Alan Alves-Brito, Simone Silva Alexandre, Beatriz Nattrodt D'Avila, Débora Peres Menezes. (2020). Brazilian physicists community diversity, equity and inclusion: a first diagnostic. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010136](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010136)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive survey results on motivations. The article reports "Approximately 80% of people attribute little or no inﬂuence of 'social or ﬁnancial recognition'", and that in most cases models in literature, cinema or family did not motivate the choice, perhaps due to absence of positive models.

> "Approximately 80% of people attribute little or no inﬂuence of “social or ﬁnancial recognition” for choos- ing a career in physics."
