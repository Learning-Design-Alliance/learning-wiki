# A single student's interview dialogue can contain both fixed-mindset-aligned and growth-mindset-aligned descriptions of physics challenge, so student talk does not neatly fit one mindset category

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Student interview dialogue about college physics can simultaneously include statements aligned with a fixed mindset (e.g., feeling bad at physics) and behaviors aligned with a growth mindset (e.g., working hard and seeking help), so dialogue should not be assumed to fit entirely into one category. [→ Little 2018](#little-2018)

## Evidence

### Little 2018

Little, A. J., Humphrey, B., Green, A., Nair, A., & Sawtelle, V. (2018). Exploring Mindset's Applicability to Students' Experiences with Challenge in Transformed College Physics Courses. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1807.11062](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1807.11062)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview-based qualitative analysis of one student (“Leyla”), a biology major in a transformed introductory college physics course, presented in the article's introduction. In the same interview she compared herself unfavorably to peers and described working hard and attending office hours. The article states that "a student might report that they are “bad at physics,” but work hard". No effect size is reported.

> "Leyla's example shows the importance of not assuming that student dialogue will neatly fit entirely into growth or fixed mindset categories. A student might report that they are “bad at physics,” but work hard and be willing to show vulnerability to their instructors in the face of challenge."
