# Introducing intermittent stop trials does not change processing speed (drift rate) in children with or without ADHD

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Fosco 2019

Fosco, W. D., Kofler, M. J., Alderson, R. M., Tarle, S. J., Raiker, J. S., & Sarver, D. E. (2019). Inhibitory Control and Information Processing in ADHD: Comparing the Dual Task and Performance Adjustment Hypotheses. Journal of Abnormal Child Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10802-018-0504-9

`q3 · i0`

Tier 2 Bayesian mixed ANOVA in the same 144-child sample. The finding of "drift rate was equivalent across the stop-signal and no-tone tasks" (d = 0.12) contradicted the dual-task requirement hypothesis prediction of slower processing speed.

> "drift rate was equivalent across the stop-signal and no-tone tasks (BF01 = 3.08; d = 0.12), and the ADHD/Non-ADHD groups showed equivalent changes in drift rate when inhibition demands were added (group x task interaction: BF01 = 4.38)."
