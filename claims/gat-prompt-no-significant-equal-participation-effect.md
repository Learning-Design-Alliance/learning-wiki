# Neither a group awareness tool alone nor combined with adaptive prompts significantly changed the equality of participation in university online collaboration

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Kruskal-Wallis tests found no significant difference in gini-coefficient equality of participation between control, GAT, and GAT + Prompt conditions at the end of either collaborative task, so H1 was rejected. [→ Strauß 2021](#strauß-2021)

## Evidence

### Strauß 2021

Strauß, S., & Rummel, N. (2021). Promoting regulation of equal participation in online collaboration by combining a group awareness tool and adaptive prompts. But does it even matter? International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning (2021) 16:67–104. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-021-09340-y

`q3 · i?`

Field experiment in a university online course: 61 students in 16 groups (topic #3) and 54 students in 13 groups (topic #4), randomly assigned to control, GAT, or GAT + Prompt. The Kruskal-Wallis test "revealed no significant difference between the conditions in terms of equality of participation"; H1 was rejected. Equivalence was not tested.

> "However, a Kruskal-Wallis test revealed no significant difference between the conditions in terms of equality of participation at the end of topic #3 H(2) = 0.463, p > 0.05, or topic #4 H(2) = 0.91, p > 0.05."
