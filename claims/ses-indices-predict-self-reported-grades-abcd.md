# Parental education, income-to-needs ratio, and neighborhood deprivation each predict self-reported school grades in the ABCD sample at both follow-up timepoints, with larger effects for parental education and income than for neighborhood deprivation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

> **Currency** · newest study 2024 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` The odds of reporting a higher grade increase with parental education, income-to-needs ratio, and lower neighborhood deprivation at both the 2-year and 3-year follow-up (N = 5001), with ORs of 1.29–1.37 for education and income and 0.89–0.91 for ADI. [→ Langensee 2024](#langensee-2024)
`q3 i0` Effect sizes for parental education and income-to-needs ratio are larger than for neighborhood deprivation, mirroring earlier ABCD findings that parental education and income effects were twice as big as neighborhood quality. [→ Langensee 2024](#langensee-2024)

## Evidence

### Langensee 2024

Langensee, L., Rumetshofer, T. & Mårtensson, J. (2024). Interplay of socioeconomic status, cognition, and school performance in the ABCD sample. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00233-x

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Ordinal logistic regression models on ABCD data (N = 5001) at 2-year and 3-year follow-up, with self-reported grades as dependent variable. Tables 1–2 print parental education OR 1.29 (1.21, 1.38) and 1.34 (1.26, 1.43), ITN OR 1.37 and 1.36, and ADI OR 0.91 and 0.89, i.e. "the odds ratio for obtaining a higher grade at school increased with increasing levels of each of the three SES measures."

> "For both models, the odds ratio for obtaining a higher grade at school increased with increasing levels of each of the three SES measures."
