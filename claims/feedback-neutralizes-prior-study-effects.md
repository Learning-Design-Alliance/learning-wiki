## Evidence

### Andrew C. Butler and Henry L. Roediger III 2008

Andrew C. Butler and Henry L. Roediger III. (2008). Feedback enhances the positive effects and reduces the negative effects of multiple-choice testing. Memory & Cognition, 36(3), 604-616. https://doi.org/10.3758/MC.36.3.604

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Interaction of feedback condition and prior study on correct responses, F(4,138) = 5.77, ηp² = .14. Correct responses rose with prior study in the no-feedback condition but not with delayed feedback, where "performance was roughly the same in all three of the study conditions".

> "in the delayed-feedback con dition, it did not matter w hether stu dents had previously studied the mater ial at a ll; performance was roughly the same in all three of the study conditions."

### Andrew C. Butler and Henry L. Roediger III 2008 (2)

Andrew C. Butler and Henry L. Roediger III. (2008). Feedback enhances the positive effects and reduces the negative effects of multiple-choice testing. Memory & Cognition, 36(3), 604-616. https://doi.org/10.3758/MC.36.3.604

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Interaction of prior study and feedback on intrusions, F(4,138) = 5.88, ηp² = .15. Prior study reduced intrusions only without feedback; feedback reduced intrusions and made study amount irrelevant.

> "Greater amount of prior stud y decreased the proportion of intrusions in the no-feedback condition (as in Ro e-diger & Mars h, 2005 ), but t he amount o f pr ior stu dy was neutra lized by feedback, which reduced the nu m-ber of intrusions."
