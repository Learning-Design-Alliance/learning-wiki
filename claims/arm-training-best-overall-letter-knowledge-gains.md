# Arm training with nested handwriting exercises yields the greatest overall acquisitions in letter knowledge and script, while arm-body training reduces them

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r3` · `q4` · `i1`–`i2`

## Subclaims
`q4 i2` Arm training had a significant positive effect on the composite measure of letter knowledge and script (d = 0.404). [→ Fernando Núñez-Regueiro 2024](#fernando-nunez-regueiro-2024)
`q4 i1` Arm-body training had a significant negative effect on the composite measure (d = -0.383). [→ Fernando Núñez-Regueiro 2024 (2)](#fernando-nunez-regueiro-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Fernando Núñez-Regueiro 2024

Fernando Núñez-Regueiro, Natacha Boissicat, Fanny Gimbert, Céline Pobel-Burtin, Marie-Caroline Croset, Marie-Line Bosse, and Cécile Nurra. (2024). Involving the Body to Improve Letter Knowledge and Script: An Experimental Study in French Kindergarten. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09923-9

`q4 · i2` · `causal · r3`

Preregistered randomized-controlled experiment in 13 kindergarten classes; linear regression on a latent composite of letter knowledge and script, controlling for baseline, age, gender and letter set. Arm training increased acquisitions by "+0.404 SD" versus the average student.

> "the arm training condition had a significant, positive effect on overall acquisitions in letter knowledge and script on post-tests (ß = 0.148, d = 0.404, p = .008)"

### Fernando Núñez-Regueiro 2024 (2)

Fernando Núñez-Regueiro, Natacha Boissicat, Fanny Gimbert, Céline Pobel-Burtin, Marie-Caroline Croset, Marie-Line Bosse, and Cécile Nurra. (2024). Involving the Body to Improve Letter Knowledge and Script: An Experimental Study in French Kindergarten. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09923-9

`q4 · i1` · `causal · r3`

Same RCT, linear regression on the composite measure: arm-body training decreased acquisitions by "-0.383 SD" relative to average students; the likelihood ratio test for the model was significant.

> "the arm-body training condition had a significant, negative effect on overall acquisitions (ß = -0.138, d = -0.383, p = .033)"
