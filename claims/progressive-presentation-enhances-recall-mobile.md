# Progressive sentence presentation enhances recall compared to full-sentence presentation, particularly in mobile multitasking scenarios

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Progressive presentation of sentences (sequentially revealing subject, verb, object components while retaining prior context) yields higher recall than full-sentence presentation during walking multitasking. [→ Janaka 2025](#janaka-2025)
`q2 i?` All participants preferred progressive presentation over full sentences and over random individual words, citing improved memory for both individual words and sentence structure. [→ Janaka 2025 (2)](#janaka-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Janaka 2025

Janaka, N., Zhao, S., Ram, A., Sun, R., Tan, S. T. J. W., Li, D., & Hsu, D. (2025). Progressive Sentences: Combining the Benefits of Word and Sentence Learning. MobileHCI '25 Adjunct. [doi:10.1145/3737821.3749564](https://doi.org/10.1145/3737821.3749564)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pilot 2 (N=6) within-subject comparison of Progressive vs. Full vs. Word presentation during Walking. Progressive yielded the highest recall though differences were not statistically significant; all participants preferred it, noting "improved memory for both individual words and sentence structure."

> "Although differences were not statistically significant, Progressive yielded the highest recall. All participants preferred it, noting improved memory for both individual words and sentence structure."

### Janaka 2025 (2)

Janaka, N., Zhao, S., Ram, A., Sun, R., Tan, S. T. J. W., Li, D., & Hsu, D. (2025). Progressive Sentences: Combining the Benefits of Word and Sentence Learning. MobileHCI '25 Adjunct. [doi:10.1145/3737821.3749564](https://doi.org/10.1145/3737821.3749564)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pilot 2 (N=6) subjective ratings: Progressive reduced cognitive load compared to Full and helped link L2 and L1 words. Three of six participants suggested adding pauses between words to aid memory consolidation.

> "It also reduced cognitive load compared to Full and helped link L2 and L1 words."
