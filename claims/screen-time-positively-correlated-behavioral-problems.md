## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Screen time correlated positively with the Pediatric Symptom Checklist total score (r = 0.504, p < 0.001) and its attention, internalizing and externalizing subscales, and predicted the PSC total score in regression. [→ Sidiq Mohammad et al 2025](#sidiq-mohammad-et-al-2025)

## Evidence

### Sidiq Mohammad et al 2025

Sidiq Mohammad et al. (2025). Screen time exposure and academic performance, anxiety, and behavioral problems among school children. PeerJ. https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.19409

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Pearson correlations in the 537-child cross-sectional survey, with the PSC-Parent version as the behavioral measure. The printed effect size for the total score is "r 0.504, p < 0.001". Regression also showed screen time associated with the pediatric symptom total score (β 0.178, t = 4.33, p < 0.001).

> "the PSC version of the pediatric symptoms scale total score (r 0.504, p < 0.001) and subscales (attention problem (r = 0.434, p < 0.001), internalizing problems (r = 0.398, p < 0.001), and externalizing problems (r = 0.455, p < 0.001))"
