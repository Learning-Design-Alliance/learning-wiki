# Creative performance weakly predicts metacognitive judgments, with perceived difficulty and task interest serving as additional cues

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Performance, perceived difficulty, and interest all acted as cues for metacognitive judgments in both tasks, with performance a significant but weak predictor. [→ Kamila Urban 2025](#kamila-urban-2025)

## Evidence

### Kamila Urban 2025

Kamila Urban, Marek Urban. (2025). Metacognition and motivation in creativity: examining the roles of self-efficacy and values as cues for metacognitive judgments. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09421-5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The SEM results for the two creativity tasks showed "significant relationships between performance and judgments" as well as negative difficulty and positive interest associations, supporting the hypothesis that all three served as cues.

> "The results reported in Table 5 showed significant relationships between performance and judgments (β = 0.11, p =.016; β = 0.24, p <.001), difficulty and judgments (β = − 0.13, p <.001; β = − 0.08, p =.050), and interest and judgments (β = 0.21, p <.001; β = 0.19, p <.001)."
