# With content balancing and item exposure control imposed, 5-item Chain-CAT is less efficient than the pre-post method

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2025 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Imposing content balancing and item exposure control raises the overall average RMSE at L = 5 from 0.277 to 0.317, about 10%, making it less efficient than pre-post. [→ Jun-ichiro Yasuda 2025](#jun-ichiro-yasuda-2025)

## Evidence

### Jun-ichiro Yasuda 2025

Jun-ichiro Yasuda, Michael M. Hull, Naohiro Mae, Kentaro Kojima. (2025). Chained computerized adaptive testing for the Force Concept Inventory. arXiv. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1103/x2rq-z8lm](https://doi.org/10.1103/x2rq-z8lm)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Numerical simulation with the constrained content balancing method and an item exposure threshold (Nadm = 3 for L = 5) in place. Constraining item selection forces administration of items yielding lower accuracy and precision, so the constrained 5-item Chain-CAT falls below pre-post efficiency.

> "On the other hand, by implementing content balancing and item exposure control, the overall average RMSE is increased from 0.277 to 0.317 when L = 5, an increase of about 10%."
