## Evidence

### Michael Vignal 2023

Michael Vignal, Gayle Geschwind, Benjamin Pollard, Rachel Henderson, Marcos D. Caballero, H. J. Lewandowski. (2023). Survey of physics reasoning on uncertainty concepts in experiments: an assessment of measurement uncertainty for introductory physics labs. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020139](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020139)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Across pilot interviews and betas, the team refined items to strengthen evidentiary arguments. For item 1.1, early MC and CMC iterations were abandoned because "multiple explanations, some correct and some incorrect, would lead to different students selecting the same answer options," leading to a CMR format where students select an answer and its supporting reasoning.

> "most of our item revisions were to improve our mappings by addressing instances in which different students either provided the same response with different justifications or provided different responses with the same justification."

### Michael Vignal 2023 (2)

Michael Vignal, Gayle Geschwind, Benjamin Pollard, Rachel Henderson, Marcos D. Caballero, H. J. Lewandowski. (2023). Survey of physics reasoning on uncertainty concepts in experiments: an assessment of measurement uncertainty for introductory physics labs. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020139](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020139)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Interviews on item 2.2 (reporting uncertainty of the mug's mass) showed students selecting the correct answer because the option "standard error (also known as standard deviation of the mean)" contained the word 'mean.' When the parenthetical was removed, students familiar with 'standard deviation of the mean' were unfamiliar with 'standard error.' The authors chose to retain the parenthetical, a decision also applied to item 4.7.

> "we decided to keep the parenthetical to avoid arbitrarily large discrepancies between classes based on the particular language used in the course."
