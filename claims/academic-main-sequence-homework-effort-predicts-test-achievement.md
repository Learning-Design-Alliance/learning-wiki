# Homework effort correlates with test achievement as a stable linear 'academic main sequence' in introductory physics, at least until about 2016

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On average, more effort on homework (homework grade) correlates with higher test scores, forming a best-fit linear main sequence. [→ James Overduin 2021](#james-overduin-2021)
`q2 i?` Until about 2016 the main sequence was reasonably well fit by a linear function with consistent slope and intercept, so homework effort faithfully predicted later test achievement. [→ James Overduin 2021 (2)](#james-overduin-2021-2)

## Evidence

### James Overduin 2021

James Overduin, Jacob Buchman, Jonathan Perry and Thomas Krause. (2021). The Scourge of Online Solutions and an Academic Hertzsprung-Russell Diagram. The Physics Educator. [doi:10.1142/s2661339521500074](https://doi.org/10.1142/s2661339521500074)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

An observational analysis of anonymized average homework and test grades for 364 Physics 1 and 261 Physics 2 students across twelve semesters (2012-2020), plotted as academic HR diagrams in Fig. 4 with least-squares best-fit lines. The authors state it is "clear that, on average, more eﬀort on homework" correlates with higher test scores.

> "As expected, the academic HR diagram shows sig- niﬁcantly more scatter than the astronomical one. Nevertheless, it is clear that, on average, more eﬀort on homework (student “temperature”) does corre- late with higher test scores (“luminosity”)."

### James Overduin 2021 (2)

James Overduin, Jacob Buchman, Jonathan Perry and Thomas Krause. (2021). The Scourge of Online Solutions and an Academic Hertzsprung-Russell Diagram. The Physics Educator. [doi:10.1142/s2661339521500074](https://doi.org/10.1142/s2661339521500074)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

A time-series plot of best-fit slope m for each semester (Fig. 6), with error bars given by relative standard error, drawn from the same twelve-semester grade dataset. Before about 2016 the fit is stable; the paper notes this period's linearity qualitatively, printing no effect size for the correlation.

> "This ﬁgure conﬁrms that until about 2016, the academic main sequence is reasonably well ﬁt by a simple linear function with consistent values of m and b."
