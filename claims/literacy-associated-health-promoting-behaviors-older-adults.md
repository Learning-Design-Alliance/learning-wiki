# Higher total literacy is associated with more frequent participation in cognitive, physical, and social activities in older adults without dementia

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In regression models adjusted for age, sex, and education, higher total literacy scores were associated with more frequent participation in cognitive, physical, and social activities (all p values <0.05). [→ Bennett JS 2012](#bennett-js-2012)

## Evidence

### Bennett JS 2012

Bennett JS, Boyle PA, James BD, Bennett DA. (2012). Correlates of health and financial literacy in older adults without dementia. BMC Geriatrics. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2318-12-30

`q3 · i?`

Cross-sectional linear regression in a cohort of 556 older persons without dementia, adjusted for age, sex, and education. A 90th- vs 10th-percentile literacy contrast corresponded to a cognitive activity score "0.64 standard deviations higher"; social and physical activity associations were also significant in core models.

> "Based on the estimated association, a person with a total literacy score of 0.86 (90 th percentile) had a cognitive activity score of 0.64 standard deviations higher than a person with a total literacy score of 0.41 (10 th percentile)."
