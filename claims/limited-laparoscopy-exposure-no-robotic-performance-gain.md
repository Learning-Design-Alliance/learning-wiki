# Limited laparoscopy exposure does not significantly improve medical students' initial objective performance on robot-assisted digital laparoscopy tasks

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Medical students with 0, 20, or 40 minutes of laparoscopy training showed no statistically significant differences in time, completion rate, clutch use, out-of-view instruments, ball drops, or manual adjustments on robotic tasks (p > 0.05). [→ Nasit V 2021](#nasit-v-2021)

## Evidence

### Nasit V 2021

Nasit V, Vongsurbchart T, Myszka A, Richter P, Rogula T. (2021). Medical student experience with robot-assisted surgery after limited laparoscopy exposure. Journal of Robotic Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11701-020-01129-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental self-enrolled study of 23 medical students assigned to 0 min (n=11), 20 min (n=6), or 40 min (n=6) laparoscopy training, then tested on Senhance robotic tasks. The article reports "no statistically significant differences in objective performance between the groups (p > 0.05)"; no effect size printed.
