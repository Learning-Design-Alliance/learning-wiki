## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Under traditional grading models, aggregating assignments into course grades increases grade reliability regardless of what the grades measure, because random error decreases as more assignments are taken into account. [→ Connor Gorman 2018](#connor-gorman-2018)

## Evidence

### Connor Gorman 2018

Connor Gorman, David Webb, Kevin Gee. (2018). Hierarchical Linear Modeling Approach to Measuring the Effects of Class Size and Other Classroom Characteristics on Student Learning in an Active-Learning Based Introductory Physics Course. University of California, Davis. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1809.00218](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1809.00218)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The article reports this finding from prior grading research in its measurement-theory discussion, using Classical Test Theory terminology; it implies overall course grades are a better proxy for physics understanding than individual assessment grades, assuming both overwhelmingly measure physics understanding. No effect size is printed.

> "it has been found that even under more traditional models of grading as assignments are aggregated up to course grades the reliability of grades tends to increase regardless of what it is that they are measuring"
