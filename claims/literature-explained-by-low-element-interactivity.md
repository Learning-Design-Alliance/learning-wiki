## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The article argues problem-solving first advantages for conceptual but not procedural knowledge may be explained by element interactivity being "likely to be relatively lower for conceptual than procedural knowledge". [→ Ashman et al. 2020](#ashman-et-al-2020)

## Evidence

### Ashman et al. 2020

Greg Ashman & Slava Kalyuga & John Sweller. (2020). Problem-solving or Explicit Instruction: Which Should Go First When Element Interactivity Is High? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09500-5

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The article's narrative review of studies such as DeCaro and Rittle-Johnson (2012) and Kapur (2014), which it reports found "a difference favouring problem-solving first on conceptual but not procedural knowledge". This is the authors' interpretation, not a new experiment.

> "Other studies (e.g. Kapur 2014) demonstrated similar findings, replicating a difference favouring problem-solving first on conceptual but not procedural knowledge. Together, these findings may be explained by element interactivity that is likely to be relatively lower for conceptual than procedural knowledge."
