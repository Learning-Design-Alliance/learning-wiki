## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In one online physics course, students without writing devices had to type equations or use standard shapes, which slowed group discussions and required extra time. [→ Da Yang Tan & Jer-Ming Chen 2021](#da-yang-tan-jer-ming-chen-2021)

## Evidence

### Da Yang Tan & Jer-Ming Chen 2021

Da Yang Tan & Jer-Ming Chen. (2021). Bringing Physical Physics Classroom On line – Challenges of Online Teaching in the New Normal. The Physics Teacher (submitted). Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1119/5.0028641](https://doi.org/10.1119/5.0028641)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Observation from the same 13-week online course, based on a student-produced whiteboard artefact showing differing technological affordances within one group. The authors report that typing equations "slowed dow n discussions" and extra discussion time had to be given.

> "In our observations, this slowed dow n discussions as students struggled to type out the equations and hence extra time was had to be given for the students to complete their discussions."
