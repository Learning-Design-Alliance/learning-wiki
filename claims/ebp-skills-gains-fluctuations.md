## Evidence

### Ramis Mary-Anne 2019

Ramis Mary-Anne, Chang Anne, Conway Aaron, Lim David, Munday Judy, Nissen Lisa. (2019). Theory-based strategies for teaching evidence-based practice to undergraduate health students: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1698-4

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Review reports Long et al.'s mixed-methods study measuring overall research skills via self-report Likert items from the Research Readiness Self-Assessment tool; nursing cohorts improved (p = 0.001) and the nutrition RCT arm improved (p = 0.002).

> "Significant improvement from pre-test to post-test results was noted in nursing students using the tool (p = 0.001), as well as in the second arm of the study which was an RCT comprising intervention and control groups of undergraduate students studying nutrition (p = 0.002)."

### Ramis Mary-Anne 2019 (2)

Ramis Mary-Anne, Chang Anne, Conway Aaron, Lim David, Munday Judy, Nissen Lisa. (2019). Theory-based strategies for teaching evidence-based practice to undergraduate health students: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1698-4

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Review reports Liabsuetrakul et al.'s one-group pre-test/post-test studies measuring EBM skills by student self-reported Likert answers; scores fluctuated across time points, with a significant decrease at weeks 5 and 13 in one study (p < 0.001).

> "Overall scores for EBM skills were significantly higher from baseline to week one (p < 0.001) [47, 48] and at 37 weeks post intervention (p = 0.003) [48], after students were given more time to reflect and conduct some individual learning."
