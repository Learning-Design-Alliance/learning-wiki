# Co-construction alone does not contribute to shared mental model development and shows a negative unique effect

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Contrary to expectations, co-construction behavior does not contribute to shared mental model development; its unique effect is negative. [→ Van den Bossche 2011](#van-den-bossche-2011)

## Evidence

### Van den Bossche 2011

Van den Bossche, P., Gijselaers, W., Segers, M., Woltjer, G., & Kirschner, P. (2011). Team learning: building shared mental models. Instructional Science, 39, 283-301. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-010-9128-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression analysis of team learning behaviors predicting shared mental model indicators found co-construction's unique effect negative once construction and constructive conflict were taken into consideration; its intercorrelation with sharedness was close to zero.

> "However, these results show that, contrary to the expectations, the co-construction behavior of the team does not contribute to the development of shared mental models. In fact, the unique effect of co-construction is negatively connected to the sharedness of mental models."
