## Evidence

### Li 2026

Li, X., Liu, Z., Jiang, S., Chen, J., & Chen, W. (2026). The impact of an LLM-based educational agent on learning achievement, cognitive dynamics, and student perceptions in computer science education. International Journal of STEM Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40594-026-00641-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mann-Whitney U comparison of practical-task scores between the control class J01 (n = 67) and experimental classes using DBagent. Y02 (Mean = 87.21) and Y03 (Mean = 89.63) "both scored higher than J01 (Mean = 85.97)" with p < .01; Y03 also significantly outperformed control on Task 1 (Z = 3.49, Z = 5.15, p < .001).

> "Y02 (Mean = 87.21) and Y03 (Mean = 89.63) both scored higher than J01 (Mean = 85.97), with significant differences ( p < .01)."

### Li 2026 (2)

Li, X., Liu, Z., Jiang, S., Chen, J., & Chen, W. (2026). The impact of an LLM-based educational agent on learning achievement, cognitive dynamics, and student perceptions in computer science education. International Journal of STEM Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40594-026-00641-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mann-Whitney U test on Task 2 scores shows experimental class Y01 (Mean = 84.42), comprising primarily Geoscience majors, "underperformed J01" (Z = -2.97, p < .001, r = .38). The article attributes this to moderated efficacy tied to learners' domain-specific digital readiness.

> "The negative Z-score and significant metric (Z = -2.97, p < .001, r = .38) indicate that the Y01 (Mean = 84.42) underperformed J01."
