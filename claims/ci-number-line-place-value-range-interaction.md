# Number line estimation error increases more strongly for children with cochlear implants when the range extends from 0–100 to 0–1000

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A significant number line range × group interaction showed estimation error increased more from the 0–100 to the 0–1000 range for children with CI (8.5% vs. 4.2% increase) than for controls, who also had larger overall errors (10.8% vs. 7.0%). [→ Pixner 2014](#pixner-2014)

## Evidence

### Pixner 2014

Pixner, S., Leyrer, M., and Moeller, K. (2014). Number processing and arithmetic skills in children with cochlear implants. Frontiers in Psychology 5:1479. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01479

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Paper-pencil number-to-position task (36 trials, 0–100 and 0–1000 ranges) analyzed as percent absolute estimation error in a two-way ANOVA with CE covariate; the range × group interaction was reliable, F(1,91) = 11.77, p < 0.01, with the "increase of estimation error ... more pronounced for children with CI".

> "The interaction indicated that the increase of estimation error from the 0 to 100 to the 0 to 1000 number line range was more pronounced for children with CI than for hearing controls (8.5% vs. 4.2% increase in misplacement, respectively"
