# The mixed condition's learning-gain advantage was more pronounced for 4th graders than 5th graders

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Three-way interactions between grade, pretest/posttest, and condition were significant, with slope differences more pronounced for 4th graders. [→ Olsen 2017](#olsen-2017)

## Evidence

### Olsen 2017

Olsen, J. K., Rummel, N., & Aleven, V. (2017). Learning Alone or Together? A Combination Can Be Best! Proceedings of the 12th International Conference on Computer Supported Collaborative Learning. https://learning-analytics.info/journals/index.php/ijcscl/article/view/9151

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

HLM three-way interaction analysis from the same 307-student study. The article reports significant three-way interactions with "slope differences between the mixed conditions and the other conditions being more pronounced for the 4 th grade students."

> "there were a significant interactions for both the pretest/posttest, grade, and collaborative/mixed conditions, t(301) = 4.57, p < .05, r = .25, and the pretest/posttest, grade, and individual/mixed conditions, t(301) = 3.19, p < .05, r = .18, with the slope differences between the mixed conditions and the other conditions being more pronounced for the 4 th grade students than the 5 th grade students"
