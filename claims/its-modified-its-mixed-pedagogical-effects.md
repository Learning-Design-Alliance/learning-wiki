## Evidence

### Létourneau 2025

Létourneau, A., Martineau, M. D., Charland, P., Karran, J. A., Boasen, J. & Léger, P. M. (2025). A systematic review of AI-driven intelligent tutoring systems (ITS) in K-12 education. npj Science of Learning 10:29. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00320-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's synthesis of eleven ITS-vs-modified-ITS studies: only three reported effect sizes, mostly small. McCarthy et al.'s 2x2 iSTART study found no condition effects, contradicting Long and Aleven's self-assessment findings. Walkington and Bernacki's personalization benefits were limited to students highly engaged with their interests.

> "Contrary to the three previous studies ’ results which suggested that self-assessment enhanced the effectiveness of the ITS44,46, none of the experimental conditions in this study were reported to inﬂuence performance33."

### Létourneau 2025 (2)

Létourneau, A., Martineau, M. D., Charland, P., Karran, J. A., Boasen, J. & Léger, P. M. (2025). A systematic review of AI-driven intelligent tutoring systems (ITS) in K-12 education. npj Science of Learning 10:29. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00320-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Borchers et al.'s quasi-experiment with 82 ninth-graders using Mathtutor found a significant main effect of matching practice and test format (t(567.96) = -3.28, p = 0.001), though overall ITS vs paper learning gains were similar.

> "learning gains were twice as high when the test format matched the practice environment (M = 0.18, SD = 0.30) compared to when there was no such match and students had to transfer knowledge across formats (M = 0.10, SD = 0.30)"
