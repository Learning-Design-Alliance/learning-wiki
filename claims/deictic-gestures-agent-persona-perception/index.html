Evidence¶

Maik Beege 2020¶

Maik Beege, Manuel Ninaus, Sascha Schneider, Steve Nebel, Julia Schlemmel, Jasmin Weidenmüller, Korbinian Moeller, Günter Daniel Rey. (2020). Investigating the effects of beat gestures and deictic gestures of a lecturer in educational videos. Psychology of learning with digital media, Chemnitz University of Technology. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2020.103955

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Experiment 2 agent-persona perception measured with the API-R (Ryu & Baylor, 2005 revision) in the industrial-revolution video study (N = 121); univariate effects on learning-facilitating (ηp2 = .10), credibility (ηp2 = .05), and engagement (ηp2 = .11) all significant. The lecturer was "perceived to be more beneficial for learning in the deictic condition".

"Univariate ANOVAs showed a significant effect of gesture instruction with a medium effect size for learning facilitating; F(2, 118) = 6.41, p = .002, ηp2 = .10, credibility; F(2, 118) = 3.25, p = .04, ηp2 = .05, and engagement; F(2, 118) = 7.60, p = .001, ηp2 = .11. Sidak-corrected post-hoc analyses indicated that the lecturer was perceived to be more beneficial for learning in the deictic condition in contrast to the control (Mdiff = 0.64; p = .003) and the beat gesture condition (Mdiff = 0.47; p = .03)."
