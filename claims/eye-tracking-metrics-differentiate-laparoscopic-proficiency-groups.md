## Evidence

### Khanfar Aseel F. 2025

Khanfar Aseel F., Motamedi Sanaz, Safford Shawn D., Moore Jason, Menold Jessica, Miller Scarlett. (2025). Visual attention and cognitive workload using different laparoscopic box trainers and mixed-reality feedback. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-025-11881-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment at Penn State Hershey with 24 novices and 4 experts; k-means++ clustering on eye-tracking and workload metrics yielded three groups, and Welch ANOVA showed "the completion times were statistically significantly different between the groups" (p < 0.001). No effect size printed.

> "the completion times were statistically significantly different between the groups, Welch's F(2, 11.798) = 21.122, p < 0.001"

### Khanfar Aseel F. 2025 (2)

Khanfar Aseel F., Motamedi Sanaz, Safford Shawn D., Moore Jason, Menold Jessica, Miller Scarlett. (2025). Visual attention and cognitive workload using different laparoscopic box trainers and mixed-reality feedback. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-025-11881-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

One-way Welch ANOVA across the three novice clusters and the expert group in the same experiment; "All metrics showed a statistically significant difference between the groups" (p < 0.001), with pairwise comparisons showing experts lowest. No effect size printed.

> "All metrics showed a statistically significant difference between the groups (p < 0.001, see Fig. 6)"
