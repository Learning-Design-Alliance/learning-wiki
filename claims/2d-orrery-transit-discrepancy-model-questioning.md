# The 2D Human Orrery wrongly predicts frequent Venus transits, and the discrepancy with the rare real phenomenon forces learners to question the model's validity

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Rollinde 2019

Rollinde, E. (2019). Enacting Planets to Understand Occultation Phenomena. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1910.09403](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1910.09403)

`q2 · i?`

Authors' analysis of the model's failed prediction: the Orrery's 2D geometry cannot represent that Venus's orbital plane is not aligned with Earth's, so transits occur only where the two planes intersect. Real transits occur in pairs separated by 8 years with a periodicity of about 120 years, versus the model's 575 days.

> "It appears that the Human Orrery is in 2D, while Venus orbital plane is not aligned with Earth orbital plane. Thus, the transit may occur only in two specific positions where the two planes intersect."

### Rollinde 2019 (2)

Rollinde, E. (2019). Enacting Planets to Understand Occultation Phenomena. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1910.09403](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1910.09403)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pedagogical interpretation of the failed prediction in the modelling sequence: the mismatch between the Orrery's 575-day prediction and the real ~120-year transit periodicity is deliberately used to make learners question the model, illustrating the modelling cycle's return from predictions to the experimental world.

> "This huge discrepancy forces the learners to question the validity of the model used for this activity."
