## Evidence

### Gur Windmiller 2021

Gur Windmiller, Philip Blanco, and William F. Welsh. (2021). Developing Virtual Reality Activities for the Astro 101 Class and Lab. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2109.01592](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2109.01592)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

In the stellar parallax VR activity, "All students completed a worksheet" calculating distances from the parallax angle measured in the second part; missed guesses from the first part were not counted against grades.

> "All students completed a worksheet where they calculated distances to the stars using the parallax angle they measured in the second part of the activity."

### Gur Windmiller 2021 (2)

Gur Windmiller, Philip Blanco, and William F. Welsh. (2021). Developing Virtual Reality Activities for the Astro 101 Class and Lab. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2109.01592](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2109.01592)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The authors report that "The vast majority of students performed well on these tasks," based on conceptual questions such as whether parallax from Jupiter would be better or worse than from Earth. No sample size or effect size is printed for this outcome.

> "In addition, we asked conceptual questions to test their depth of understanding. For example, "If we could make parallax measurements from Jupiter, would they be better or worse than what we can do from Earth?" The vast majority of students performed well on these tasks."
