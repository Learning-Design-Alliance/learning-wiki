# Deception-cue instructions and facial-expression trainings are largely ineffective at reducing misinformation susceptibility

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across the reviewed deception literature, instructions on specific deception cues and facial-expression trainings were largely ineffective, with mixed and sometimes negative results. [→ Rau 2025](#rau-2025)

## Evidence

### Rau 2025

Rau, M. A., & Premo, A. E. (2025). Systematic Review of Educational Approaches to Misinformation. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10012-8

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's discussion of 30 lie-detection articles concludes that "the reviewed deception-cue instructions and facial-expression trainings were largely ineffective at reducing susceptibility to misinformation", citing null, mixed, and negative findings such as worse performance after instruction with examples in one study.

> "Altogether, the reviewed deception-cue instructions and facial-expression trainings were largely ineffective at reducing susceptibility to misinformation, regardless of the targeted types of cues or pedagogical devices."
