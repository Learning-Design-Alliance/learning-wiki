## Evidence

### E. F. Redish 2014

E. F. Redish, C. Bauer, K. L. Carleton, T. J. Cooke, M. Cooper, C. H. Crouch, B. W. Dreyfus, B. Geller, J. Giannini, J. S. Gouvea, M. W. Klymkowsky, W. Losert, K. Moore, J. Presson, V. Sawtelle, K. V. Thompson, C. Turpen, and R. K. P. Zia. (2014). NEXUS/Physics: An interdisciplinary repurposing of physics for biologists. [doi:10.1119/1.4870386](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4870386)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Interview-based study by the UMD BERG and PERG of students in biology and physics classes, including a case (Ashlyn) who resisted the diffusion scaling argument but responded enthusiastically to a model-horse scaling example once its relevance was clear. "very accepting, and indeed enthusiastic" occurred only when applications were perceived as relevant to biology.

> "students were very accepting, and indeed enthusiastic, about physical and mathematical modeling when they perceived that applications had authentic relevance to the biology they were learning, but were dismissive and resistant when they perceived them as having little or no implications for biology."
