# Retrospective self-assessed learning gains are higher on the knowledge plane than on the attitude plane, with the lowest gains on items about the participants' own mortality

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the outcome-based evaluation, the highest learning gains occurred on knowledge items (CSA gain 78%, 78%, and 75% for naming reasons for incorporating PC, interprofessional collaboration, and the importance of relatives), while the lowest gains occurred on attitude items (CSA gain 27% for dealing with own mortality, 31% for attitude on death and dying, 45% for supporting saying goodbye). `q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Outcome-based evaluation using the retrospective Comparative Self-Assessment Gain method with 24 physicians; gains computed from pre/post self-ratings on the German school grading scale. The description quotes "CSA gain 78%, 78%, and 75%, respectively" for the top knowledge items and 27% for dealing with own mortality.

> "The highest learning gains on the knowledge plane were observed in naming reasons for incorporating PC, being aware of the importance of interprofessional and interdisciplinary collaboration and understanding the importance of relatives and their care (CSA gain 78%, 78%, and 75%, respectively). The lowest learning gains were detected on the attitude plane in dealing with their own mortality (CSA gain 27%)"
