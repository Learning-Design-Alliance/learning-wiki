# Using demographic variables as predictors risks reinforcing biases embedded in training labels, including self-fulfilling prophecies

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · theoretical `r3` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` When training labels encode biased stakeholder decisions, including demographics in prediction may reinforce the beliefs producing those biased decisions. [→ Baker 2023](#baker-2023)
`q1 i?` There is a risk of a self-fulfilling prophecy where the instructor believes race causes worse outcomes and acts to bring that about. [→ Baker 2023 (2)](#baker-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Baker 2023

Baker, R. S., Esbenshade, L., Vitale, J., & Karumbaiah, S. (2023). Using Demographic Data as Predictor Variables: a Questionable Choice. Journal of Educational Data Mining, 15(2). https://jedm.educationaldatamining.org/index.php/JEDM/article/view/619

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument (type e) in section 4.2: "if there is direct bias in the decisions that the model is trying to predict, including these direct biases in prediction may reinforce the beliefs that produce those biased decisions", quoting Paquette et al. (2020) on grade-lifting discretion.

> "In other words, if there is direct bias in the decisions that the model is trying to predict, including these direct biases in prediction may reinforce the beliefs that produce those biased decisions."

### Baker 2023 (2)

Baker, R. S., Esbenshade, L., Vitale, J., & Karumbaiah, S. (2023). Using Demographic Data as Predictor Variables: a Questionable Choice. Journal of Educational Data Mining, 15(2). https://jedm.educationaldatamining.org/index.php/JEDM/article/view/619

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The authors warn, citing Rosenthal & Jacobson (1968), of "a risk of creating a self-fulfilling prophecy" when instructors are told a student will perform worse because of group membership; they also note the Wolff et al. (2013) race variable case as an example of models encoding biased instructor decisions.

> "There is also a risk of creating a self-fulfilling prophecy (Rosenthal & Jacobson, 1968), where the instructor believes race causes worse outcomes and therefore acts in ways that bring about that outcome."
