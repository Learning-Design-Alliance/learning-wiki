# Students answered questions requiring coordination of two or more representations less accurately than questions answerable from a single representation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Accuracy on Question #4, which required coordination of two or more representations, was lowest among the three scored question types. [→ Mike Stieff 2011](#mike-stieff-2011)
`q3 i?` Accuracy differed between question types, and Question #4 was significantly lower than Questions #2 and #3 collapsed, per Scheffé post-hoc test. [→ Mike Stieff 2011 (2)](#mike-stieff-2011-2)

## Evidence

### Mike Stieff 2011

Mike Stieff, Mary Hegarty, Ghislain Deslongchamps. (2011). Identifying Representational Competence with Multi-Representational Displays. Cognition and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1080/07370008.2010.507318

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive accuracy data from the problem-solving interview of ten organic chemistry undergraduates across three animations. Question #4 accuracy was lowest: "student accuracy was the lowest (M = 1.2, SD = .97)".

> "For Question #4, which required the coordination of two or more representations, student accuracy was the lowest (M = 1.2, SD = .97)."

### Mike Stieff 2011 (2)

Mike Stieff, Mary Hegarty, Ghislain Deslongchamps. (2011). Identifying Representational Competence with Multi-Representational Displays. Cognition and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1080/07370008.2010.507318

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

ANOVA over the nine scored interview questions of the ten-participant interview study. "A one-way analysis of variance showed that the accuracy rate differed between the questions, F(2,26) = 7.04, p < .05."

> "A one-way analysis of variance showed that the accuracy rate differed between the questions, F(2,26) = 7.04, p < .05, and a post-hoc analysis using the Scheffé test indicated that the average number of correct responses to Question #4 was significantly lower"
