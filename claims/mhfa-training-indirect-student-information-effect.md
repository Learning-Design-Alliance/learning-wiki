# Students of MHFA-trained teachers report receiving more mental health information from school staff, but student mental health and help received are unchanged

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students of trained teachers were more likely to report receiving mental health information (OR = 2.60, P < 0.001), including via class lessons, printed materials and website referral. [→ Jorm Anthony F 2010](#jorm-anthony-f-2010)

## Evidence

### Jorm Anthony F 2010

Jorm Anthony F, Kitchener Betty A, Sawyer Michael G, Scales Helen, Cvetkovski Stefan. (2010). Mental health first aid training for high school teachers: a cluster randomized trial. BMC Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-244X-10-51

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Cluster randomized trial student outcomes (1,633 students surveyed at pre-test and follow-up). Odds ratios printed for overall information receipt and three delivery modes; no standardized effect size printed.

> "students of the trained teachers were more likely to report that they received information about mental health problems (OR = 2.60, P < 0.001), including a "class lesson from teacher" (OR = 2.76, P = 0.030), "poster, pamphlet, brochure or book" (OR = 4.84, P = 0.003) and "referral to website" (OR = 2.78, P = 0.045)"

### Jorm Anthony F 2010 (2)

Jorm Anthony F, Kitchener Betty A, Sawyer Michael G, Scales Helen, Cvetkovski Stefan. (2010). Mental health first aid training for high school teachers: a cluster randomized trial. BMC Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-244X-10-51

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Cluster randomized trial student outcomes including SDQ subscales; null findings for help received and mental health, with no effect sizes printed, so equivalence was not tested.

> "Contrary to the hypotheses, there was no difference in reported help received from teachers or in the students' mental health. A secondary analysis focussing just on students with worse mental health (above the cut-off on the Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire) at baseline also did not support these hypotheses."
