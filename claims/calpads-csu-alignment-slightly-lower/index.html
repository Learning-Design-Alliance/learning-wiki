Evidence¶

Fong 2018¶

Fong, A., Barrat, V., & Finkelstein, N. (2018). Examination of data usability options for assessing eligibility for higher education in California. San Francisco, CA: WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED592524

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Comparison of about half a million A-G courses per year for 101,343 CSU applicants matched to CALPADS (80% matching). Table 11 prints exact alignment of 69.8%, 71.6%, and 73.1% for 2011-12 through 2013-14.

"Table 11 shows that across all categories about 70% of the courses had the same annualized passed number of units between the CSU admissions dataset and CALPADS, with improvement across the years."

Fong 2018 (2)¶

Fong, A., Barrat, V., & Finkelstein, N. (2018). Examination of data usability options for assessing eligibility for higher education in California. San Francisco, CA: WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED592524

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Grades 10-11 GPA comparison between CALPADS and CSU course records (Figure 2), computed with the same method as the UC comparison. The at-or-above-3 conclusion was the same for 93% of students and conflicted for 7%.

"The GPAs computed using the two data sources were close, and for the majority of students (58%) the difference between the two GPAs was within a tenth of a point. For close to 90% of students the two GPAs were within three tenths of a point."
