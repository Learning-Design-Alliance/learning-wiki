## Evidence

### Laura M. Akesson 2026

Laura M. Akesson, Jessica L. Rosenberg, Nancy Holincheck, and Benjamin W. Dreyfus. (2026). Defining a physics person: Tensions in the identity development of undergraduate women in physics. Physical Review Physics Education Research 22, 020102. https://doi.org/10.1103/j9bh-pz14

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Comparison of the two open-ended survey questions showed that for a physics person, "interest was enough, and was not accompanied with qualifiers or extra requirements," whereas students' self-explanations added negative qualifiers.

> "Although students were resolute in their own interest in physics when explaining their own identity, they were quick to add negative qualifying statements claiming other identities, academic or research weaknesses, or uncertainty that they fit in."

### Laura M. Akesson 2026 (2)

Laura M. Akesson, Jessica L. Rosenberg, Nancy Holincheck, and Benjamin W. Dreyfus. (2026). Defining a physics person: Tensions in the identity development of undergraduate women in physics. Physical Review Physics Education Research 22, 020102. https://doi.org/10.1103/j9bh-pz14

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In comparing self-descriptions with descriptions of a physics person, "18 of 120, 15.0%" wrote about their own academic abilities versus "36 of 120, 30.0%" for a physics person, with the natural-easy notion absent from self-descriptions.

> "A smaller fraction of students (18 of 120, 15.0%) wrote about their academic abilities than those who included academic understanding in their description of a physics person (36 of 120, 30.0%)."
