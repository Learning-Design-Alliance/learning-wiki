# Students assessed by trained raters more often found their grade inadequate despite high concordance between rater and self-assessment grades

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students rated by trained raters disagreed more strongly with their grade (P=5.74x10-3), yet self-assessment concordance with rater grades was similarly high in both groups (Kendall's coefficient 0.83 and 0.80). [→ Weitz G 2014](#weitz-g-2014)

## Evidence

### Weitz G 2014

Weitz G, Vinzentius C, Twesten C, Lehnert H, Bonnemeier H, König IR. (2014). Effects of a rater training on rating accuracy in a physical examination skills assessment. GMS Zeitschrift für Medizinische Ausbildung. [doi:10.3205/zma000933](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma000933)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-assessment questionnaires from 208 students (95% of the video-recorded subset): students under trained raters more often judged their grade inadequate (P=5.74x10-3, Mann-Whitney U).

> "However, students in the group with trained raters disagreed more strongly with their assessment, finding their grade more often inadequate (P=5.74x10-3 from the Mann-Whitney U test)."

### Weitz G 2014 (2)

Weitz G, Vinzentius C, Twesten C, Lehnert H, Bonnemeier H, König IR. (2014). Effects of a rater training on rating accuracy in a physical examination skills assessment. GMS Zeitschrift für Medizinische Ausbildung. [doi:10.3205/zma000933](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma000933)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Self-assessed grades tracked rater grades closely in both groups (Kendall's coefficient 0.83 trained, 0.80 untrained); the authors attribute this to questionnaires being filled in immediately after the grade was announced, so self-assessment was influenced by the raters' judgement.

> "The concordance between the raters' grading and the self-assessment of the students was high in both groups (Kendall's coefficient 0.83 and 0.80 in the group with trained and untrained raters, respectively"
