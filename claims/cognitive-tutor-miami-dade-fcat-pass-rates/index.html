Evidence¶

Ritter 2007¶

Ritter, S., Anderson, J. R., Koedinger, K. R., & Corbett, A. (2007). Cognitive Tutor: Applied research in mathematics education. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 14(2), 249-255. https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03194060

q2 · i? · review · r1

The same Miami-Dade analysis reported particularly dramatic findings for special populations: 35.7% of Exceptional Student Education Cognitive Tutor students passed the FCAT versus 10.9% with another curriculum; the article prints no effect size for this contrast.

"The study showed that 35.7% of students receiving Exceptional Student Education who use Cognitive Tutor passed the FCAT, in comparison with only 10.9% of such students who used a different curriculum."

Ritter 2007 (2)¶

Ritter, S., Anderson, J. R., Koedinger, K. R., & Corbett, A. (2007). Cognitive Tutor: Applied research in mathematics education. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 14(2), 249-255. https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03194060

q2 · i? · design · r2

The same district analysis reported that among students with limited English proficiency, 27% of Cognitive Tutor students passed the FCAT versus 18.9% of such students in another curriculum; no effect size is printed for this contrast.

"For students with limited English proficiency, 27% of Cognitive Tutor students passed the FCAT, as opposed to only 18.9% of such students in another curriculum."
