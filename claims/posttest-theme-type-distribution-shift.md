## Evidence

### Liu Jingxia & Liu Li 2013

Liu Jingxia & Liu Li. (2013). An Empirical Study on the Application of Theme Theory in the Field of Writing Pedagogy. English Language Teaching, 6(5). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v6n5p117

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive analysis of post-test compositions (Table 6): experimental class totals 327 themes versus 220, with clausal themes 20.8% versus 5.8%. The authors interpret this as "better at using different types of theme"; percentages are descriptive, no test reported.

> "However, the experimental class uses more multiple themes and clausal themes. This shows that the experimental class is better at using different types of theme to achieve a variety of sentence structure."

### Liu Jingxia & Liu Li 2013 (2)

Liu Jingxia & Liu Li. (2013). An Empirical Study on the Application of Theme Theory in the Field of Writing Pedagogy. English Language Teaching, 6(5). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v6n5p117

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same Table 6 analysis: textual themes were 26.6% in the experimental class versus 11.4% in the controlled class. The authors attribute this to textual elements doing "important cohesive work in relating the clause to its context"; no statistical test is printed for this contrast.

> "Another significant difference lies in the fact that the experimental class uses more textual themes than the controlled class."
