# Regardless of dyad type, dyads' Extend/revise and Recognize/agree behaviors facilitated fluency, while child-parent dyad type showed a negative effect

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Dyads exhibiting more Extend/revise or Recognize/agree behaviors generated more ideas, regardless of dyad type and activity level; child-parent dyad type showed a negative effect on fluency in the control model. [→ Bai 2026](#bai-2026)

## Evidence

### Bai 2026

Bai, H.; Chan, L.; Luo, A.; Kroesbergen, E.H.; Christie, S. (2026). Creativity in dialogues: How do children interact with parents vs. strangers for generating creative ideas? Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102286

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Multilevel regressions on two-level dyad data (stimulus objects nested within dyads) showed that in the control model "the effects of Extend/revise, Recognize/agree, and dyad type remained robust", and "regardless of dyad type and how active and motivated dyads were, dyads which exhibited more Extend/revise or Recognize/agree behaviors generated more ideas". Dyad type showed a negative effect.
