## Evidence

### Nagyová Valéria 2026

Nagyová Valéria, Blaňár Dominik, Kybic Jan, Schwendicke Falk, Tichý Antonín. (2026). Student Performance in Bitewing Caries Detection: Artificial Intelligence Versus Alternative E-learning. International Dental Journal. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.identj.2026.109567

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Permutation tests on each group's pre/post metrics (Table 4) showed the Dataset Group's sensitivity rose from 0.62 ± 0.14 to 0.78 ± 0.08 and the AI Group's from 0.68 ± 0.15 to 0.73 ± 0.12, while the Lecture Group's specificity rose from 0.94 ± 0.09 to 0.96 ± 0.05.

> "Sensitivity increased significantly in the Dataset Group (from 0.62 ± 0.14 to 0.78 ± 0.08) and the AI Group (from 0.68 ± 0.15 to 0.73 ± 0.12), as opposed to the Lecture Group, where a significant increase in specificity was observed (from 0.94 ± 0.09 to 0.96 ± 0.05)."

### Nagyová Valéria 2026 (2)

Nagyová Valéria, Blaňár Dominik, Kybic Jan, Schwendicke Falk, Tichý Antonín. (2026). Student Performance in Bitewing Caries Detection: Artificial Intelligence Versus Alternative E-learning. International Dental Journal. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.identj.2026.109567

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Confusion matrices of mean TP, FP, TN, and FN counts per group before and after training (Figure 4) showed the AI Group alone improved in all four categories; per the article, this "result[ed] in the greatest improvements in precision and accuracy."

> "The AI Group was the only group that showed improvement across all detection categories (TP, FP, TN, and FN), resulting in the greatest improvements in precision and accuracy."
