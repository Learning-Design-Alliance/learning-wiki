# ITS evaluation results depend strongly on test type: effects are large on locally developed tests (ES = 0.73) and small on standardized tests (ES = 0.13)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · quant-synthesis `r2`–`r3` · `q2`–`q4`

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` Test type was the study feature most strongly related to effect size: average ES was 0.73 for local tests, 0.13 for standardized tests, and 0.45 for both. [→ Kulik 2016](#kulik-2016)
`q3 i?` Homogeneity analyses confirmed the pattern: average g was 0.62 on local tests, 0.09 on standardized tests, and 0.46 on both. [→ Kulik 2016 (2)](#kulik-2016-2)
`q3 i?` Within a single study of Practical Algebra Tutor, effects were larger on aligned local tests (mean ES = 0.99) than on standardized tests (mean ES = 0.36). [→ Kulik 2016 (3)](#kulik-2016-3)

## Evidence

### Kulik 2016

Kulik, J. A., & Fletcher, J. D. (2016). Effectiveness of Intelligent Tutoring Systems: A Meta-Analytic Review. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315581420

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Univariate ANOVAs on the Winsorized data set (Glass's ES, study as unit) identified test type as the strongest moderator of ITS effects across the 50 evaluations. Local, standardized, and combined test categories averaged 0.73, 0.13, and 0.45 respectively.

> "ESs are large in evaluations that used local tests as outcome measures (average ES = 0.73), small in evaluations that used standardized tests (average ES = 0.13), and intermediate in evaluations that used a combination of the two (average ES = 0.45)."

### Kulik 2016 (2)

Kulik, J. A., & Fletcher, J. D. (2016). Effectiveness of Intelligent Tutoring Systems: A Meta-Analytic Review. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315581420

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Supplementary Hedges's-g homogeneity analysis of the Winsorized data set confirmed test type as the strongest moderator, with average g of "0.62 when outcomes were measured on local tests, 0.09 when they were measured on standardized tests, and 0.46 when they were measured on both."

> "average g was 0.62 when outcomes were measured on local tests, 0.09 when they were measured on standardized tests, and 0.46 when they were measured on both."

### Kulik 2016 (3)

Kulik, J. A., & Fletcher, J. D. (2016). Effectiveness of Intelligent Tutoring Systems: A Meta-Analytic Review. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315581420

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

The review attributes this within-study pattern to Koedinger et al. (1997), who evaluated the Practical Algebra Tutor on locally developed problem-solving tests and standardized multiple-choice tests; the review reports, citing that study, that local-test effects were larger. The review reports it second-hand (q 2).

> "The researchers found large effects on the locally developed tests (mean ES = 0.99) and smaller effects on the standardized ones (mean ES = 0.36)."
