# The percentage of assignments listing references rose from 55% in Runs 1-2 to 64% in Run 7 and 71% in Run 8, though the Run 1 versus Runs 7/8 comparison did not reach significance (P=.11)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The proportion of learners including references rose across interventions (Run 1: 55%, Run 7: 64%, Run 8: 71%), and summaries with no sources fell from 28% to 14%, but the Fisher exact comparison of Run 1 versus Runs 7/8 was not statistically significant (P=.11). ## Evidence

### Louise M Blakemore 2020

Louise M Blakemore, Sarah E M Meek, Leah K Marks. (2020). Equipping Learners to Evaluate Online Health Care Resources: Longitudinal Study of Learning Design Strategies in a Health Care Massive Open Online Course. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/15177

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

One-tailed Fisher exact test comparing coded referencing categories (list of sources, wrote about resources, no sources) between Run 1 and combined Runs 7/8, with Run 1 counts normalized to 30 for Figure 2. The article reports a clear trend toward improved referencing despite non-significance.

> "Although the difference in the proportion of assignments of each type did not meet statistical significance between Run 1 and Run 7/8 (P=.11), this was partly because of the small number of assignments in the later runs."
