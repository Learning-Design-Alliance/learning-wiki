## Subclaims
`q3 i0` Students randomly assigned to preliminary VR training (unrelated to electrostatics) had significantly higher pre-to-post gains than untrained students, d = 0.24, p = 0.014. [→ C. D. Porter 2020](#c-d-porter-2020)

## Evidence

### C. D. Porter 2020

C. D. Porter, J. R. H. Smith, E. M. Stagar, A. Simmons, M. Nieberding, C. M. Orban, J. Brown, A. Ayers. (2020). Using Virtual Reality in Electrostatics Instruction: The Impact of Training. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020119](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020119)

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled study of 279 students in a second-semester introductory calculus-based physics course, assigned to VR with or without preliminary training. "The diﬀerence in gains is statistically signiﬁcant with p = 0.014, and an eﬀect size of d = 0 .24."

> "We ﬁnd that the pre-trained group did have higher gains than the untrained group (see Fig. 4). The diﬀerence in gains is statistically signiﬁcant with p = 0.014, and an eﬀect size of d = 0 .24."
