## Evidence

### Ehrlich 2014

Ehrlich, S. B., Gwynne, J. A., Pareja, A. S., Allensworth, E. M., Moore, P., Jagesic, S., & Sorice, E. (2014). Preschool Attendance in Chicago Public Schools: Relationships with Learning Outcomes and Reasons for Absences. UChicago CCSR Research Report. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/preschool-attendance-chicago-public-schools-relationships-learning-outcomes-and-reasons

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of 1,265 four-year-olds in the CPS Preschool Longitudinal Study (2010-11), using Woodcock-Johnson III fall scores as controls and the Kindergarten Readiness Tool at year end. Descriptively, best attenders answered 88 percent of math items correctly versus 75 percent for students missing at least 20 percent of school.

> "Among students with similar incoming skills and backgrounds, students who missed 10 percent or more of school—those considered chronically absent—still performed significantly worse on the math and letter recognition portions of the KRT than students who had the best attendance."

### Ehrlich 2014 (2)

Ehrlich, S. B., Gwynne, J. A., Pareja, A. S., Allensworth, E. M., Moore, P., Jagesic, S., & Sorice, E. (2014). Preschool Attendance in Chicago Public Schools: Relationships with Learning Outcomes and Reasons for Absences. UChicago CCSR Research Report. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/preschool-attendance-chicago-public-schools-relationships-learning-outcomes-and-reasons

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Null result from the same PLS analysis (n=1,265): the pre-literacy subscale showed no significant attendance relationship after controlling for beginning-of-year letter recognition; equivalence was not tested. Students who started with higher pre-literacy skills maintained that advantage regardless of attendance.

> "For pre-literacy outcomes, once we took into account children's letter recognition skills at the beginning of the year, there was no significant relationship between attendance and end-of-year scores."
