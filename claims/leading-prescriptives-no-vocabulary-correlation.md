# Prescriptives that lead the child's attention did not correlate significantly with productive vocabulary

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Only FOLLOW-prescriptives showed a significant correlation with vocabulary; LEADING-prescriptives did not, indicating the attentional direction of the directive matters. [→ Nameera Akhtar 1991](#nameera-akhtar-1991)

## Evidence

### Nameera Akhtar 1991

Nameera Akhtar, Frances Dunham and Philip J. Dunham. (1991). Directive interactions and early vocabulary development: the role of joint attentional focus. Journal of Child Language, Volume 18, Issue 1, pp. 41-49. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305000900013283

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

In the same correlational study of twelve dyads, prescriptives were split into LEADING and FOLLOWING types based on whether they changed or followed the child's focus of attention. The word "Only" indicates the FOLLOW category alone correlated significantly with the 1;10 vocabulary measure; no statistic for the LEADING category is printed in the available text.

> "Prescriptives were coded as either changing (LEADING) or FOLLOWING the child's focus of attention. Only the frequency of mothers' FOLLOW-prescriptives correlated significantly"
