# Among small districts in multidistrict SELPAs, membership in a SELPA without a large district was associated with higher preK inclusion but lower K–12 inclusion

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` PreK inclusion in small districts was greater in multidistrict SELPAs without a large district (68 versus 49 percent). [→ Doutre 2021](#doutre-2021)
`q2 i?` K–12 inclusion in small districts was lower in multidistrict SELPAs without a large district (68 versus 80 percent). [→ Doutre 2021 (2)](#doutre-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Doutre 2021

Doutre, S. M., Barrett, T., & Rice, J. (2021). California's Special Education Local Plan Areas: Funding patterns, inclusion rates, and student outcomes. Regional Educational Laboratory West at WestEd. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

District-level descriptive comparison of 371 small districts in SELPAs without a large district versus 76 in SELPAs with one; table 4 reports preK regular early childhood program inclusion of "68 (45–85)" versus "49 (46 –55)" percent with a printed difference statistic of 1.40, marked as meaningful.

> "Preschool – percent received services 
in a regular early childhood program  49 (46 –55) 68 (45–85) 1.40*"

### Doutre 2021 (2)

Doutre, S. M., Barrett, T., & Rice, J. (2021). California's Special Education Local Plan Areas: Funding patterns, inclusion rates, and student outcomes. Regional Educational Laboratory West at WestEd. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same district-level comparison; table 4 reports K–12 80-percent-or-more inclusion of "80 (13–100)" versus "68 (0–100)" percent with a printed difference statistic of 0.67, a meaningful difference in the opposite direction from preK. No other meaningful differences were found for small districts.

> "K–12 – percent of students who spend 
80 percent or more of the school day in a 
general education setting 80 (13–100) 68 (0–100) 0.67*"
