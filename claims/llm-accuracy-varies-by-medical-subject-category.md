## Evidence

### Chen Yikai 2024

Chen Yikai, Huang Xiujie, Yang Fangjie, Lin Haiming, Lin Haoyu, Zheng Zhuoqun, Liang Qifeng, Zhang Jinhai, Li Xinxin. (2024). Performance of ChatGPT and Bard on the medical licensing examinations varies across different cultures: a comparison study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06309-x

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Subject-stratified analysis in which questions were categorized by each official exam guideline; accuracies per subject are reported in Supplementary Table 1 and plotted in Fig. 2. The text reports the below-70% dips in "the hematolymphoid systems, and behavioral health".

> "Specifically, in the USMLE, both GPT-4o and GPT-4 excelled by scoring over 80% in most subjects. However, their performance dipped in the categories of the hematolymphoid systems, and behavioral health, which was lower than 70%."

### Chen Yikai 2024 (2)

Chen Yikai, Huang Xiujie, Yang Fangjie, Lin Haiming, Lin Haoyu, Zheng Zhuoqun, Liang Qifeng, Zhang Jinhai, Li Xinxin. (2024). Performance of ChatGPT and Bard on the medical licensing examinations varies across different cultures: a comparison study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06309-x

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Authors' stratified analysis of multi-system and subspecialty questions across the four examinations. It also reports GPT-4's accuracy of "66.7% and 30%" for HKMLE orthopedics and NMLE neurology respectively, with GPT-3.5 and Bard at 30% and 40% in NMLE neurology.

> "However, when the questions integrated knowledge from multiple systems, the performance of the three LLMs other than GPT-4o was less than satisfactory, failing to provide satisfactory answers."
