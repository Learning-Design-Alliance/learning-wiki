# No study of principal burnout employed a causal framework or linked burnout to actual turnover

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` None of the 99 reviewed studies used a causal framework, and no study directly linked principal burnout to actual turnover or to organizational outcomes such as school climate, teacher outcomes, or student outcomes. [→ Rogers 2025](#rogers-2025)

## Evidence

### Rogers 2025

Rogers, L. K., Nguyen, T. D., Timmer, M., & Samson, J. (2025). Principal Burnout: A Systematic Review. Working paper. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED666666

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Systematic review of 99 studies found no causal framework and no direct link between burnout and observed turnover or attrition. The nearest evidence was Norris (1983), in which former principals retrospectively surveyed "appeared to exhibit more signs of burnout than currently employed principals."

> "No studies directly linked burnout to actual turnover. While intention or motivation to quit is an important consequence of burnout, the lack of inquiry into other consequences, particularly real turnover represents a major gap in the literature."
