Evidence¶

Al Otaiba 2006¶

Al Otaiba, S., & Fuchs, D. (2006). Who Are the Young Children for Whom Best Practices in Reading Are Ineffective? An Experimental and Longitudinal Study. Journal of Learning Disabilities, 39(5), 414-431. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00222194060390050401

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Longitudinal follow-up of 50 of the original 104 children tested at the end of third grade. The authors report persistence of nonresponsiveness from kindergarten to first grade and reading-related IEP goals by third grade for most nonresponders.

"Virtually our entire sample (91.89%)—and literally all children with IEPs—who were nonresponsive to kindergarten intervention remained nonresponsive in first grade. At third grade, four out of five children who had been nonresponsive to kindergarten intervention had IEP goals in reading."

Al Otaiba 2006 (2)¶

Al Otaiba, S., & Fuchs, D. (2006). Who Are the Young Children for Whom Best Practices in Reading Are Ineffective? An Experimental and Longitudinal Study. Journal of Learning Disabilities, 39(5), 414-431. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00222194060390050401

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Chi-square analysis of third-grade educational placement (Table 10) for the 50 located children, comparing intervention and control students across responsiveness groups on reading difficulties, tutoring, retention, and IEP reading goals.

"There was a statistically significant difference among nonresponsive, sometimes responsive, and always responsive students in terms of school-identified reading problems 2 years beyond first grade, χ2(6, n = 50) = 40.60, p < .001."
