# IBO exam content shifted toward genetics and molecular-level processes, with significantly rising emphasis on the core ideas Regulation and Information and Communication

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Opitz Sebastian 2020

Opitz Sebastian, Harms Ute. (2020). Assessing High Performers in the Life Sciences: Characteristics of Exams Used at the International Biology Olympiad (IBO) and Their Implications for Life Science Education. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-10-0215

`q2 · i2` · `qualitative · r2`

Content coding across six IBO cohorts with ANOVA; molecular-level focus rose from "ca. 20% in earlier years, ca. 40% in 2017" (F(5, 618) = 4.93, p < 0.001, η² = 0.04).

> "The share of items focusing on genetics and other molecular-level processes increased over the years (e.g., ca. 20% in earlier years, ca. 40% in 2017: F(5, 618) = 4.93, p < 0.001, η² = 0.04)."

### Opitz Sebastian 2020 (2)

Opitz Sebastian, Harms Ute. (2020). Assessing High Performers in the Life Sciences: Characteristics of Exams Used at the International Biology Olympiad (IBO) and Their Implications for Life Science Education. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-10-0215

`q2 · i2` · `qualitative · r2`

ANOVA of core-idea codes across IBO years shows significantly higher emphasis on Regulation (η² = 0.10) and Information and Communication (η² = 0.07); Structure & Function (43%), Regulation (53%), and Information and Communication (36%) received stronger attention overall.

> "An increased emphasis in recent years on genetics or other molecular-level pathways can be seen reflected in the significantly higher emphasis on Regulation (F(5, 618) = 2.98, p < 0.001, η² = 0.10) and Information and Communication (F(5, 618) = 8.57, p < 0.001, η² = 0.07)."
