# Trained ninth-graders became more critical toward less reliable links than controls, with the effect persisting three weeks for the least reliable (bad) links

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After training, trained students gave lower ratings than controls to poor and bad links, and the bad-link effect held at post-test 2 three weeks later. [→ Ana Pérez 2018](#ana-perez-2018)

## Evidence

### Ana Pérez 2018

Ana Pérez, Anna Potocki, Marc Stadtler, Mônica Macedo-Rouet, Johanna Paul, Ladislao Salmerón, Jean-François Rouet. (2018). Fostering Teenagers' Assessment of Information Reliability: Effects of a Classroom Intervention focused on Critical Source Dimensions. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2018.04.006

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental classroom study of 137 ninth-graders (64 trained, 73 controls) in four French schools, analyzed with linear mixed-effects models on normalized link ratings. The three-way group × phase × condition interaction was significant, χ² (6) = 12.64, p < .05, dv = .04; "trained students became more critical toward less reliable sources after the training sessions".

> "Overall, these results indicate that compared to controls, trained students became more critical toward less reliable sources after the training sessions, with the effect still holding after a period of three weeks for the least reliable (bad) links."
