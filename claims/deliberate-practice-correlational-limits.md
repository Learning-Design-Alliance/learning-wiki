# Deliberate practice research is largely correlational with large unexplained variability in accumulated practice hours

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Research on deliberate practice tends to be correlational rather than prospective or intervention-based, and large standard deviations in accumulated practice hours imply practice is not the sole factor in developing expertise. [→ Williams 2017](#williams-2017)

## Evidence

### Williams 2017

Williams, A. M., Fawver, B., & Hodges, N. J. (2017). Using the 'expert performance approach' as a framework for examining and enhancing skill learning: Improving understanding of how experts learn. *Frontline Learning Research, 5*(3), 139–154. https://doi.org/10.14786/flr.v5i3.267

`q2 · i1`

The review reports, citing Ford et al. (2012) and Hopwood et al. (2016), "extremely large standard deviations in the number of hours athletes accumulate" across practice types. It notes the deliberate practice literature's findings on variance explained remain controversial (citing Hambrick et al., 2014; McNamara et al., 2014) and that retrospective data may describe social and cultural background rather than causal factors.

> "Numerous researchers have reported extremely large standard deviations in the number of hours athletes accumulate in different types of practices activities during development, implying that practice (at least given issues in measurement) is not the sole factor in developing expertise"
