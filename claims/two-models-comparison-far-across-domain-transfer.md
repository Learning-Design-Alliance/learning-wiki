# Students who compared a climate model with non-climate analog models during productive failure scored significantly higher on far across domain transfer than students who used only the climate model

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` The Two Models Group significantly outperformed the One Model Group on the far across domain transfer (robot mining) posttest item, with a medium-to-large effect size. [→ Jacobson 2020](#jacobson-2020)
`q3 i2` The far transfer advantage held for both the content-expert teacher's classes and the non-expert teachers' classes. [→ Jacobson 2020 (2)](#jacobson-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Jacobson 2020

Jacobson, Michael, Goldwater, Micah, Markauskaite, Lina, Lai, Polly, Kapur, Manu, Roberts, Gareth, & Hilton, Courtney. (2020). Schema abstraction with productive failure and analogical comparison: Learning designs for far across domain transfer. Learning and Instruction, 65, Article number: 101222. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2019.101222

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Linear mixed-model analysis of the posttest robot space mining far transfer item in the quasi-experimental two-condition study (96 analyzed students). The Two Models Group (M = 1.231, SD = 0.777) outperformed the One Model Group (M = 0.719, SD = 0.750); a Bayesian t-test gave "BF10 = 0.052" strong evidence and a frequentist test gave "d = 0.672".

> "There was a main effect of Condition, with students in the Two Models Group who were exposed to two models significantly outperforming students in the One Model Group, F(1,92) = 10.403, p < .001, η 2 = .11."

### Jacobson 2020 (2)

Jacobson, Michael, Goldwater, Micah, Markauskaite, Lina, Lai, Polly, Kapur, Manu, Roberts, Gareth, & Hilton, Courtney. (2020). Schema abstraction with productive failure and analogical comparison: Learning designs for far across domain transfer. Learning and Instruction, 65, Article number: 101222. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2019.101222

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Follow-up pairwise analyses of the far transfer item showed the Two Models condition won under every teacher: the content expert's Two Model class beat the One Model class (d = 0.654) and the early career non-expert's Two Model class beat the experienced non-expert's One Model class (d = 0.693), so the advantage did "not" depend on teacher expertise.

> "It is important to note that the Two Model condition outperformed the One Model condition for both the Content Expert’s class and the Non-expert classes."
