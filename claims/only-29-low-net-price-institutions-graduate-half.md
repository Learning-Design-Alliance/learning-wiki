# Only 29 low-net-price institutions graduate at least half their students, and only five of those enroll at least 30 percent low-income students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Of the 65 institutions with a net price below $4,600, only 29 (19 publics and 10 private nonprofits) have a graduation rate of at least 50 percent, and only five of those 29—all public colleges—enroll a student body that is at least 30 percent low income. [→ Mamie Lynch 2011](#mamie-lynch-2011)

## Evidence

### Mamie Lynch 2011

Mamie Lynch, Jennifer Engle, and José L. Cruz. (2011). Priced Out: How the Wrong Financial-Aid Policies Hurt Low-Income Students. The Education Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED520199

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Successive-criteria analysis of IPEDS 2009 data: the 65 low-net-price institutions were screened on six-year graduation rate (Figure 2, median 49 percent), leaving 29; Figure 3 then screens on Pell enrollment, where "Only five of these 29—all public colleges—meet or exceed the national average of enrolling a student body that is at least 30 percent low income."

> "How many of the previously identified 65 institutions with a net price below $4,600 give all their students at least a 1-in-2 shot at graduating? Only 19 publics and 10 private nonprofits—29 institutions in total—meet this rather low standard (Figure 2)."
