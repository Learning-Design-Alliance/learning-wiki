# Initial-phase task-related communication moderates the negative relationship between first-language heterogeneity and self-efficacy gains, while getting-to-know-each-other time does not

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2`–`r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Time spent on task-related communication was a significant moderator (H × T: p = .01; ΔR2 = .003, F = 6.27), with the negative heterogeneity effect significant only when groups spent little time on task-related communication. [→ Natalia Reich-Stiebert 2023](#natalia-reich-stiebert-2023)
`q3 i?` Time spent getting to know each other did not moderate the heterogeneity effect (interaction p = .38). [→ Natalia Reich-Stiebert 2023 (2)](#natalia-reich-stiebert-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Natalia Reich-Stiebert 2023

Natalia Reich-Stiebert, Jan-Bennet Voltmer, Jennifer Raimann, Stefan Stürmer. (2023). The role of first-language heterogeneity in the acquisition of online interaction self-efficacy in CSCL. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn 18:513–530. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-023-09411-2

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Moderation analysis using SPSS PROCESS (Hayes Model 2) on residual gains, controlling gender, age, and SES heterogeneity; H × T showed "the critical interaction p = .01" and conditional effects of H on gains were significant only at low task-related communication (e.g., −0.45 at low G, low T; −0.62 at high G, low T) and nonsignificant at high T.

> "time spent on task-related communication emerged as a significant moderator of the relationship between CSCL groups' first-language heterogeneity and self-efficacy gains (for the critical interaction p = .01), while time spent getting to know each other was ineffective (p = .38)."

### Natalia Reich-Stiebert 2023 (2)

Natalia Reich-Stiebert, Jan-Bennet Voltmer, Jennifer Raimann, Stefan Stürmer. (2023). The role of first-language heterogeneity in the acquisition of online interaction self-efficacy in CSCL. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn 18:513–530. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-023-09411-2

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same moderation analysis; the H × G interaction was not significant (Table 2 reports ΔR2 = .00, F(1, 1513) = 0.77, p = .38), meaning getting-to-know-each-other time did not moderate the heterogeneity effect. No statistical equivalence was established.

> "With respect to the amount of time that groups spent getting to know each other, on the other hand, we observed no significant interaction effect on students' gains in self-efficacy."
