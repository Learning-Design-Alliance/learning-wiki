## Evidence

### Glazerman 2010

Glazerman, S., E. Isenberg, S. Dolfin, M. Bleeker, A. Johnson, M. Grider, and M. Jacobus. (2010). Impacts of Comprehensive Teacher Induction: Final Results From a Randomized Controlled Study (NCEE 2010-4027). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee

`q4 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Year-3 test score analysis in two-year districts, benchmark sample requiring current- and prior-year scores. The report states "effect sizes of 0.11 in reading and 0.20 in math," moving the average student up 4 and 8 percentile points respectively.

> "These impacts are equivalent to effect sizes of 0.11 in reading and 0.20 in math, which is enough to move the average student from the 50th percentile up 4 percentile points in reading and 8 percentile points in math."

### Glazerman 2010 (2)

Glazerman, S., E. Isenberg, S. Dolfin, M. Bleeker, A. Johnson, M. Grider, and M. Jacobus. (2010). Impacts of Comprehensive Teacher Induction: Final Results From a Randomized Controlled Study (NCEE 2010-4027). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Alternative specification nearly doubles the available sample of study teachers but lacks prior-achievement data, yielding a less precise estimate; the report states "we do not find an impact on math or reading scores" under this approach.

> "If the analyses are conducted without requiring test scores from the prior year,  we do not find an impact on math or reading scores."
