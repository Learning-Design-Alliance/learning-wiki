Evidence¶

Howard 2017¶

Howard, S. J., Burianová, H., Calleia, A., Fynes-Clinton, S., Kervin, L. and Bokosmaty, S. (2017). The method of educational assessment affects children's neural processing and performance: behavioural and fMRI Evidence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-017-0010-9

q2 · i? · causal · r1

PLS analysis of the spelling phase, expected a priori to "involve identical processes across experimental conditions"; the common pattern accounted for 70.91% of covariance. This contrasts with condition-specific mental-search patterns.

"During the spelling phase, all conditions activated a common brain pattern, which comprised the anterior and posterior cingulate gyri, bilateral inferior parietal lobule (angular and supramarginal gyri), precuneus, insula, parahippocampus, hippo- campus, fusiform gyrus, inferior frontal gyrus, and lingual gyrus, accounting for 70.91% of covariance in the data"

Related Claims¶

  • Neural contrasts: confident trials recruit widespread fronto-parietal activation; confident-error trials recruit medial frontal and bilateral inferior prefrontal/insular regions; overconfidence differences are confined to the lingual gyrus — related
  • During mental search, proofreading preferentially engages dorsal frontoparietal working-memory and attention regions, whereas cloze dictation and multiple choice engage semantic-processing and memory-recognition regions — related
  • Cross-condition spelling accuracies correlate highly but non-perfectly, indicating shared and method-specific components of performance — related
  • Cloze dictation and multiple choice also elicit distinct brain patterns during mental search, with cloze dictation engaging word-monitoring regions and multiple choice engaging recognition and inhibition regions — related
