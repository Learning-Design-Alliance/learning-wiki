# High-fidelity simulation on spiritual care significantly improves first-year nursing students' perceptions of spirituality and spiritual care on the SSCRS-Sp one month apart

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After the simulation, first-year nursing students (n = 143) showed statistically significant improvements on most SSCRS-Sp items, with paired t-tests significant at p < 0.05, p < 0.01, or p < 0.001 for items a, c–l, n, o, and q. [→ Domènech-Sorolla 2025](#domenech-sorolla-2025)
`q2 i?` At the subscale level, Spirituality, Spiritual Care, and Personalized Care scores improved significantly (p < 0.001), while Religiosity did not change. [→ Domènech-Sorolla 2025](#domenech-sorolla-2025)

## Evidence

### Domènech-Sorolla 2025

Domènech-Sorolla, J., Martínez-Rodríguez, L., Pedregosa-Fauste, S., Muns-Orenga, A., García-Díaz, F., & Fernández-Pascual, M. D. (2025). Impact of High-Fidelity Simulation on the Acquisition of Spiritual Competencies in a Nursing Degree in Spain: An Experimental Pre–Post Study. Journal of Religion and Health. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10943-025-02285-8

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

A quantitative pretest–posttest single-group study with 143 first-year nursing students; pretest and posttest one month apart using the SSCRS-Sp, analyzed with paired-samples t-tests corroborated by Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. The article reports the significant items and prints group means, e.g. Spirituality "19.9 (2.62)" to "21.4 (2.57)"; no effect sizes were reported.

> "Analysis revealed statistically significant differences for items d, e, and n (p < 0.05); c, g, j, k, and l (p < 0.01); and a, c, f, h, i, o, and q (p < 0.001)."
