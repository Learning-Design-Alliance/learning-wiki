# Disfluency is not clearly defined across the literature, hindering replication and precise measurement

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` What constitutes hard-to-read (disfluent) text is not clearly defined, making effects difficult to replicate and measure precisely; the label has been applied to small, blurred, poorly copied, degraded fonts and a range of different fonts. [→ Bjork 2016](#bjork-2016)

## Evidence

### Bjork 2016

Bjork, R. A., & Yue, C. L. (2016). Commentary: Is disfluency desirable? Metacognition Learning, 11, 133–137. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-016-9156-8

`q1 · i?` · `review · r2`

Strukelj et al. (as summarized in this commentary) identify the definitional problem. The commentary adds that the disfluent label has been applied to small fonts (Rhodes and Castel 2008), blurred text (Yue et al. 2013), poorly copied text (Diemand-Yauman et al. 2011), degraded text, and various fonts, and an ideal theory of disfluency does not yet cover all these variations.

> "they point out a critical issue in the literature on this topic— that what constitutes Bhard to read ^ text is not clearly defined and, as such, difficult to replicate and measure precisely."
