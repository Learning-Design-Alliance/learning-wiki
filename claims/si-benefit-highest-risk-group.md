# SI attendance was associated with a significant DFW decrease in the highest-risk group but smaller, non-significant decreases in mid- and low-risk groups

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the highest-risk group, 31% of non-attendees earned DFW versus 19% of attendees, a significant difference (Fisher's exact p = 0.003). [→ Pelaez 2019](#pelaez-2019)
`q2 i?` In the mid- and low-risk groups the DFW differences between SI attendees and non-attendees (13% vs 8%; 7% vs 3%) were not statistically significant (p = 0.057 and 0.061). [→ Pelaez 2019 (2)](#pelaez-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Pelaez 2019

Pelaez, K., Levine, R. A., Guarcello, M., Laumakis, M., & Fan, J. (2019). Using a Latent Class Forest to Identify At-Risk Students in Higher Education. Journal of Educational Data Mining, 11(1). https://jedm.educationaldatamining.org/index.php/JEDM/article/view/283

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Group-level Fisher's exact tests comparing SI and non-SI students within each LCF cluster (Table 8). Group 1 showed a 12 percentage point DFW decrease with p = 0.003; "31% of those students who did not attend SI earned a DFW grade, whereas only 19% of those who did attend".

> "However, 31% of those students who did not attend SI earned a DFW grade, whereas only 19% of those who did attend earned a DFW grade."

### Pelaez 2019 (2)

Pelaez, K., Levine, R. A., Guarcello, M., Laumakis, M., & Fan, J. (2019). Using a Latent Class Forest to Identify At-Risk Students in Higher Education. Journal of Educational Data Mining, 11(1). https://jedm.educationaldatamining.org/index.php/JEDM/article/view/283

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Fisher's exact tests within Groups 2 and 3 (Table 8) yielded p = 0.057 and p = 0.061, not significant. In Group 2, "About 13% of those who did not attend SI earned a DFW grade, whereas 8% of those who attended at least one SI session earned a DFW grade"; Group 3 showed 7% vs 3%.

> "About 13% of those who did not attend SI earned a DFW grade, whereas 8% of those who attended at least one SI session earned a DFW grade."
