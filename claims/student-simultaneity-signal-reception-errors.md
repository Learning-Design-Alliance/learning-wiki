The review reports a study by Scherr, Shaffer & Vokos (2001) probing university students of scientific and non-scientific degrees on time and simultaneity, listing "typical errors regarding these concepts". No effect size is printed.

> "They presented an account of typical errors regarding these concepts: the misguided belief that two events are simultaneous if an observer receives signals from both at the same time, the conviction that simultaneity is absolute and the assumption that every observer constitutes a different reference frame."
