# After the OSCE, interns' self-reported confidence significantly increased for obtaining informed consent, providing sign-out, and responding to nursing pages, but not for calling a consult

## Evidence

### Wick Alexandra MD 2026

Wick Alexandra MD, Murray-Bainer Samantha MD, Tischendorf Jessica MD MS, Almasry Mazen MBBS, Coyle Andrew MD, Zamanian Maryam MD MS. (2026). Development and Implementation of an OSCE for Formative Assessment of Core Clinical Skills in Internal Medicine Interns. MedEdPORTAL. https://doi.org/10.15766/mep_2374-8265.11576

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre-post survey analysis of the same intern cohort using t tests on 5-point Likert confidence ratings. The largest effect size was for obtaining informed consent (Hedges' g 0.919); "Interns’ confidence post-OSCE did not significantly increase for calling a consult (p = .051)."

> "Following the OSCE, self-reported confidence significantly increased for obtaining informed consent (mean 3.38 to 4.09, p < .01), providing sign-out (mean 3.5 to 4.06, p < .01), and responding to nursing pages (mean 3.75 to 4.18, p < .01)."
