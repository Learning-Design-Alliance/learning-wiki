# VR's score advantage over traditional methods (SMD = 0.81) does not hold against other 2D digital methods (SMD = 0.35, non-significant)

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Subgroup analysis shows significant improvement when VR is compared with traditional interventions (SMD = 0.81, p = 0.02) but no significant advantage over other 2D digital methods (SMD = 0.35, p = 0.25). [→ Zhao Jingjie 2020](#zhao-jingjie-2020)

## Evidence

### Zhao Jingjie 2020

Zhao Jingjie, Xu Xinliang, Jiang Hualin, Ding Yi. (2020). The effectiveness of virtual reality-based technology on anatomy teaching: a meta-analysis of randomized controlled studies. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-1994-z

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Subgroup analysis of the 15 trials by comparator type, reported in Table 2. The review reports the VR-versus-digital contrast as non-significant and the VR-versus-traditional contrast as significant with "SMD = 0.81; 95% CI 0.15–1.47, p = 0.02".

> "test scores of the VR group was not significantly better than using other 2D digital methods (SMD = 0.35; 95% CI -0.25–0.95, p = 0.25), while there was a significant improvement when compared with the traditional intervention group (SMD = 0.81; 95% CI 0.15–1.47, p = 0.02)"
