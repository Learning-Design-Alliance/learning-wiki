## Evidence

### Shima Salehi 2019

Shima Salehi, Eric Burkholder, Peter Lepage, Steven Pollock, and Carl Wieman. (2019). The impact of incoming preparation and demographics on performance in Physics I: a multi-institution comparison. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1905.00389](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1905.00389)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariable linear regression on physics 1 final exam scores at three institutions (HSEC, HSWC, PM), with z-scored continuous predictors. The article reports "the direct effects of demographic variables on student outcomes become insignificant" once preparation measures enter the model; no standardized effect size is printed for the null demographic coefficients.

> "However, when using multivariable regression to control for student incoming preparation as measured by CI pre-scores and math SAT/ACT scores, the direct effects of demographic variables on student outcomes become insignificant."

### M. B. Weissman 2020

M. B. Weissman. (2020). Comment on "Demographic gaps or preparation gaps?: The large impact of incoming preparation on performance of students in introductory physics". Department of Physics, University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2011.06678](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2011.06678)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The comment reports, citing Salehi et al. [1], that college exam scores can be predicted from pre-college math ACT/SAT and concept inventory scores, and that adding demographic variables gives coefficients that are "small for practical purposes" and not statistically significant. No effect size is printed.

> "If demographic variables are added to the predictive model, their coefficients are small for practical purposes and also not large enough to consider statistically significant in this sample."
