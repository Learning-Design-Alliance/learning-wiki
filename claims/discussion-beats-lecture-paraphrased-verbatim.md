## Evidence

### Jaeseo Lim 2020

Jaeseo Lim, Hwiyeol Jo, Byoung-Tak Zhang, Jooyong Park. (2020). Human-Like Active Learning: Machines Simulating the Human Learning Process. 34th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2020), Vancouver, Canada. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2011.03733](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2011.03733)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA in the same 54-participant randomized experiment; the article reports "F (1, 52) = 15.74,p<. 001,η2 =.24" for paraphrased items, the smallest of the three effects.

> "For the paraphrased type questions, the D group (M = 18.93,SD = 3.14) scored signiﬁcantly higher than the L group (M = 15.40,SD = 2.69),F (1, 52) = 15.74,p<. 001,η2 =.24"

### Jaeseo Lim 2020 (2)

Jaeseo Lim, Hwiyeol Jo, Byoung-Tak Zhang, Jooyong Park. (2020). Human-Like Active Learning: Machines Simulating the Human Learning Process. 34th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2020), Vancouver, Canada. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2011.03733](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2011.03733)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Same ANCOVA analysis; the article reports "F (1, 52) = 40.60,p<. 001,η2 =.45" for verbatim items, showing the discussion advantage extended even to rote recall.

> "for the verbatim type questions, the D group (M = 7.35,SD = 1.20) scored signiﬁcantly higher than the L group (M = 4.96,SD = 1.46),F (1, 52) = 40.60,p<. 001,η2 =.45"
