## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Because the Get Help button looks identical in both treatment arms, excluding students who never requested help cannot be affected by treatment randomization and does not cause selection bias. [→ Siedahmed 2025](#siedahmed-2025)

## Evidence

### Siedahmed 2025

Siedahmed, A., Pei, Y., Sales, A., Heffernan, N. T., Gagnon-Bartsch, J. A., Zhang, D., Schuetze, B. A., & Zengilowski, A. (2025). E-TRIALS: Empowering Data-Driven Decisions to Enhance Computer-Based Learning Platforms. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2502.10545](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2502.10545)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument in the Identifying Target Populations section: subsetting on help-seeking can induce post-randomization selection bias, but in E-TRIALS the Get Help button's appearance does not depend on available supports, so pre-button exclusion is treatment-independent.

> "Therefore, the excluded subset of students who never pressed "Get Help" cannot be affected by treatment randomization—the subset would have included the same students under any permutation of the treatment assignment, and its exclusion does not cause selection bias."
