# Implicit prejudice is negatively related to attending microaggressions in Study 1 but does not replicate in Study 2

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In Study 1, implicit prejudice negatively predicted perceived microaggressions in the school-performance scenario only; in Study 2 the association was absent. [→ Civitillo 2025](#civitillo-2025)

## Evidence

### Civitillo 2025

Civitillo, S., S. Glock, L. A. Naudszus, L. Venhoff, D. Lieck, and P. Jugert. (2025). Investigating Preservice Teachers' Competence to Notice Ethnic Microaggressions in the Classroom. Journal of Community Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1002/jcop.70056

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r3`

Study 1 multiple regression controlling for migration background, teaching experience, and gender, regressing perceived microaggressions on implicit prejudice (SA-IAT D score) and explicit measures. The printed coefficient was "β = −0.27, p = 0.041" for the school-performance scenario only.

> "results showed that implicit prejudiced attitudes were negatively related to perceived microaggressions in the school‐performance scenario (β = −0.27, p = 0.041) but not in the normative situations."
