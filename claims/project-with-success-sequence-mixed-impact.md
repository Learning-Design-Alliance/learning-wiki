# Youth reported a mixed pattern of impact on the success sequence: 60–61 percent were more likely to plan a steady full-time job before having a child or marrying, but only 28–35 percent were more likely to plan to delay sexual intercourse until milestones

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` 60–61 percent of youth reported being more likely to plan to have a steady full-time job before having a child or getting married. [→ Nakamoto 2025](#nakamoto-2025)
`q2 i?` Only 28–35 percent reported being more likely to plan to delay having sexual intercourse until completing milestones such as graduating college. [→ Nakamoto 2025 (2)](#nakamoto-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Nakamoto 2025

Nakamoto, J., Carter, C., & Russo, S. (2025). Implementation and impact of the Project With curriculum in juvenile justice facilities and group homes. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED673580

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Exit-survey success-sequence items (sample sizes 170 to 172). "60–61 percent of youth reported that they were" more likely to plan a steady full-time job before having a child or getting married; Lesson 7 includes an activity ordering relationship steps.

> "60–61 percent of youth reported that they were “much more likely” or “somewhat more likely” to plan to have a steady full-time job before they have a child or get married as a result of participating in Project With."

### Nakamoto 2025 (2)

Nakamoto, J., Carter, C., & Russo, S. (2025). Implementation and impact of the Project With curriculum in juvenile justice facilities and group homes. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED673580

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Exit-survey success-sequence items on delaying sexual intercourse until graduating high school, college, or marriage (sample sizes 170 to 172). "only 28–35 percent of the youth reported" being more likely to plan such delay, consistent with the curriculum not directly focusing on these success-sequence parts.

> "only 28–35 percent of the youth reported that they were “much more likely” or “somewhat more likely” to plan to delay having sexual intercourse until they complete various milestones (e.g., graduate college) as a result of participating in Project With."
