## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Because other trainees watched each pair's simulation in real time and joined the debriefing, difficulties were ironed out for subsequent scenarios and procedure time constantly decreased despite increasing difficulty. [→ Favier V. 2020](#favier-v-2020)

## Evidence

### Favier V. 2020

Favier V., Kimmoun A., Gatin A., Gallet P. (2020). Percutaneous tracheostomy simulation training for ENT physicians in the treatment of COVID-19-positive patients. European Annals of Otorhinolaryngology, Head and Neck Diseases. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.anorl.2020.06.002

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Authors' observational interpretation of their 3 training sessions, reported in the Discussion. They report that real-time spectator viewing and shared debriefing meant "procedure time constantly decreased despite the increasing difficulty". Details are in Appendices 2 and 3; no statistics are printed in the supplied text.

> "The fact that the other trainees were able to watch a given pair's simulation in real time and take part in the debriefing ironed out some difficulties for the subsequent scenarios, so that procedure time constantly decreased despite the increasing difficulty"
