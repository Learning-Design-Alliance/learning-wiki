## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Confident and unconfident students differed significantly in maximum (d = 1.21, p = 0.005), average (d = 1.11, p = 0.005) and total (d = 0.82, p = 0.03) visit duration on areas of interest during Predict questions. [→ S. Küchemann 2020](#s-kuchemann-2020)

## Evidence

### S. Küchemann 2020

S. Küchemann, P. Klein, H. Fouckhardt, S. Gröber, and J. Kuhn. (2020). Improving students' understanding of rotating frames of reference using videos from different perspectives. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010112](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010112)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Eye-tracking analysis (Tobii X3-120, 120 Hz) of 21 students during computer-based Predict items with square 50×50-pixel AOIs. Table IV prints maximum visit duration 3.38 s vs 7.33 s (d = 1.21, p = 0.005), average 0.47 vs 0.76 s (d = 1.11), total 25.75 vs 44.56 s (d = 0.82, p = 0.03).

> "there is signiﬁcant very large eﬀect in the maximum visit duration and a signiﬁcant large eﬀect in the average and total visit duration between conﬁdent and unconﬁdent students during the predict questions."

### S. Küchemann 2020 (2)

S. Küchemann, P. Klein, H. Fouckhardt, S. Gröber, and J. Kuhn. (2020). Improving students' understanding of rotating frames of reference using videos from different perspectives. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010112](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010112)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same eye-tracking analysis during Observe items (Table V): the visit-duration effects are not significant (e.g. maximum visit duration p = 0.16) and the number of highly focused AOIs does not differ between groups. The authors attribute the disappearance to partial familiarity with the repeated questions.

> "the diﬀerence in the maximum, average, and total visit duration of conﬁdent and unconﬁdent students is clearly reduced in comparison to the P redict-questions so that the eﬀects are not signiﬁcant during the Observe-questions."
