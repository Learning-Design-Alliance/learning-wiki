# Providing corrective feedback on practice tests does not significantly change the testing effect (g = 0.63 with feedback vs. g = 0.60 without)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q4`

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` Testing effects were robust whether or not feedback was given; the weighted mean effect sizes did not differ significantly between feedback and no-feedback conditions. [→ Adesope 2017](#adesope-2017)

## Evidence

### Adesope 2017

Adesope, O. O., Trevisan, D. A., & Sundararajan, N. (2017). Rethinking the Use of Tests: A Meta-Analysis of Practice Testing. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316689306

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Moderator analysis of feedback on practice tests (Table 2): feedback k = 119 (g = 0.63), no feedback k = 153 (g = 0.60); between-levels QΒ = 0.46, not significant. No effect size difference was printed for this contrast, so impact is unquantified.

> "The weighted mean effect sizes do not differ signifi- cantly when feedback was given (g = 0.63; k = 119) or not (g = 0.60; k = 153)."
