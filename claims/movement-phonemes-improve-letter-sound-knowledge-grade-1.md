# A 4-week embodied learning intervention with movement-phonemes improves grade 1 children's letter-sound knowledge more than regular classroom teaching

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` MOVE improved naming of all letter-sounds from T1 to T2 significantly more than CON (d = 2.0). [→ Damsgaard 2023](#damsgaard-2023)
`q3 i3` MOVE improved conditional letter-sounds significantly more than CON (d = 2.3). [→ Damsgaard 2023](#damsgaard-2023)

## Evidence

### Damsgaard 2023

Damsgaard, L., Nielsen, A.-M. V., Topor, M. K., Hansen, R. A., Jensen, S. K., Markers, R. L., Gejl, A. K., Malling, A. S. B., & Wienecke, J. (2023). Embodied Learning Activities Focusing on Letter-Sound Knowledge Increase Spelling Performance in 1st Grade Children with Low and High Reading Ability. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09791-9

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial with 53 grade 1 children assigned to MOVE (n=24) or CON (n=29), 52 of whom completed the post-test (T2), analyzed with linear mixed models. The direct training effect on letter-sound naming showed "a significantly larger improvement in all letter-sound from T1 to T2 for MOVE compared to CON" with d = 2.0 and d = 2.3 for conditional sounds.

> "Between-group analysis showed a significantly larger improvement in all letter-sound from T1 to T2 for MOVE compared to CON (p < 0.001, mean = 3.9, 95% CI (2.8, 4.9), d = 2.0). There was also a significantly larger improvement for conditional sounds in MOVE compared to CON (p < 0.001, mean = 3.5, 95% CI (2.6, 4.3), d = 2.3)"
