# Listen/understand negatively affected dyads' appropriateness, while Extend/revise's negative effect became insignificant once Inviting behavior was controlled

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Listen/understand and Extend/revise showed negative effects on dyads' appropriateness; in the control model Listen/understand's negative effect remained robust while Extend/revise's became insignificant and Inviting behavior showed a positive effect. [→ Bai 2026](#bai-2026)

## Evidence

### Bai 2026

Bai, H.; Chan, L.; Luo, A.; Kroesbergen, E.H.; Christie, S. (2026). Creativity in dialogues: How do children interact with parents vs. strangers for generating creative ideas? Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102286

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Multilevel regressions on dyad-level appropriateness scores showed that "Both Listen/understand and Extend/revise showed a negative effect (Model 3)"; in the control model "the effect of Listen/understand remained robust" while Extend/revise became insignificant and Inviting behavior showed a positive effect. No standardized effect size was printed.

> "Both Listen/understand and Extend/revise showed a negative effect (Model 3). In the control model, the effect of Listen/understand remained robust, but the effect of Extend/revise became insignificant while dyads' Inviting behavior showed a positive effect (Model 4)."
