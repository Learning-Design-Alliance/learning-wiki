# Long-term prevalence of post-deployment mental health problems is expected to exceed pre-deployed/non-deployed baseline ranges across all four outcome domains

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Based on baseline prevalence in comparison samples and the pooled odds ratios, the review expects long-term post-deployment prevalence of 6.1–14.9% for PTSD, 7.6–18% for depression, 2.4–17.5% for substance use and 10–23% for common mental disorder. [→ Bøg M 2018](#bg-m-2018)

## Evidence

### Bøg M 2018

Bøg M, Filges T, & Jørgensen A.M.K. (2018). Deployment of personnel to military operations: impact on mental health and social functioning. Campbell Systematic Reviews. https://doi.org/10.4073/csr.2018.6

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

This is the review's own derived expectation, combining baseline prevalence in pre-deployed or non-deployed comparison samples with its pooled long-term odds ratios; the printed ranges are expectations, not directly measured post-deployment prevalences.

> "On the basis of the prevalence of mental health problems in pre‐deployed or non‐deployed population based comparison sampleswe would therefore expect the long term prevalence of PTSD in post‐deployed samples to be in the range 6.1 – 14.9%, the long term prevalence of depression to be in the range from 7.6% to 18%, the long term prevalence of substance use to be in the range from 2.4% to 17.5% and the prevalence of common mental disorder to be in the range from 10% to 23%."
