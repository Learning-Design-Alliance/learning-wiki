# During problem reading, experts hypothesize physics principles early and confirm or reject them via derived features, while novices' representation is guided by surface-oriented schemata tied to equations

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Early in reading a problem, the expert entertains a hypothesized physics principle, then extracts additional features to confirm, reject, or choose among hypothesized principles. [→ Chi et al. 1981](#chi-et-al-1981)
`q2 i?` The novice's problem representation in the same task was guided by two surface-oriented schemata, one for springs and one for gravity, with representation proceeding through equations relating variables. [→ Chi et al. 1981 (2)](#chi-et-al-1981-2)

## Evidence

### Chi et al. 1981

Michelene Chi, Paul Feltovich, Robert Glaser. (1981). Categorization and Representation of Physics Problems by Experts and Novices. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog0502_2

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Study Four protocol process analysis: Expert J. L. generated her first principle(s) after reading 20 percent of each problem on average; literal cues like "dropped" and "height X" were interpreted as triggering the Conservation of Energy schema whose slots guide further interrogation of the problem.

> "In general, early in the reading of a problem, the expert usually entertains a hypothesis, a potential physics principle, or a set of plausible competing hypoth- eses."

### Chi et al. 1981 (2)

Michelene Chi, Paul Feltovich, Robert Glaser. (1981). Categorization and Representation of Physics Problems by Experts and Novices. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog0502_2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same Study Four protocol analysis for the novice on the same problem (Problem 16): his protocol generated parallel equations F = Mg and F = -kx which he equated via the common element F; the authors interpret this as representation via surface-oriented schemata and their variables.

> "Our interpretation for the novice in this case is &at his problem represent& tion is guided by two surface-oriented schemata, one associated with "springs" and the other with "gravity.""
