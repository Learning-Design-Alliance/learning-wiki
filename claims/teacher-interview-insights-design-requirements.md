# Teacher interviews identified six classroom insights that shaped the activity's design requirements

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Interviews with three teachers of grades 4-6 revealed that active experience with physical materials works better than lecturing, students need active guidance, activities must be flexible for different needs and skills, group work is welcomed, engaging parents in homework is not possible in all families, and data is almost totally absent in the Swedish grades 4-6 curriculum except for social media dangers. ## Evidence

### Lorenzo Ambrosini 2022

Lorenzo Ambrosini, Miriah Meyer. (2022). Data Bricks Space Mission: Teaching Kids about Data with Physicalization. arXiv. [doi:10.1109/vis4good57762.2022.00007](https://doi.org/10.1109/vis4good57762.2022.00007)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

Qualitative interview study with 1 former and 2 current school teachers who teach grades 4-6 in a public primary school in Norrköping, Sweden, conducted with an iteratively developed interview guide that was piloted on a former teacher. The quote is one of six bullet-point insights the article reports from these interviews.

> "active experience with physical materials works better than lecturing, but time and access to resources in the classroom are difficult barriers to overcome"
