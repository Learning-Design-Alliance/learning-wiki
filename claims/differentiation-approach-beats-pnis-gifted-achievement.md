## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Gifted 6th-grade students taught with the differentiation approach scored significantly higher on current, enriched, and overall objectives than gifted students taught via the Program for Noticing Individual Skills. [→ Altintas 2015](#altintas-2015)

## Evidence

### Altintas 2015

Altintas, E., & Ozdemir, A. S. (2015). Evaluating a Newly Developed Differentiation Approach in Terms of Student Achievement and Teachers' Opinions. Educational Sciences: Theory & Practice. https://doi.org/10.12738/estp.2015.4.2540

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

A quasi-experimental comparison in a public school with gifted 6th graders (n = 11) using the Mann–Whitney U test on Tables and Graphics content. The experimental group taught with the differentiation approach outperformed the control group taught via the Program for Noticing Individual Skills on "current objective scores that favored the experimental group (U = .00, p = .00 < .05)". No effect size was reported.

> "Looking at Table 1, although there were no significant differences between the current objective scores of the gifted students in the control and experimental groups before implementation (U = 12.50, p = .64 > .05), after implementation, there was a significant difference between current objective scores that favored the experimental group (U = .00, p = .00 < .05)."
