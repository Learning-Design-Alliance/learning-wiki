# Random assignment to open- versus closed-note conditions produces no significant difference in overall paper quiz performance in large-enrollment biology lab courses

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students randomly assigned to open- versus closed-note testing on an identical primary-literature-based exam did not differ significantly in overall paper quiz score. [→ Sato 2015](#sato-2015)

## Evidence

### Sato 2015

Sato, B. K., He, W., Warschauer, M., & Kadandale, P. (2015). The Grass Isn't Always Greener: Perceptions of and Performance on Open-Note Exams. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-08-0121

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-randomized within-course comparison in nine biology lab courses (roughly 900 students), with about 394 closed- and 397 open-note paper quiz scores. Regression analysis with class fixed effects found "no significant difference between the two groups" in overall performance.

> "To determine whether student perception matched reality, we compared overall paper quiz performance as well as performance on questions of specific Bloom's levels between open- and closed-note testing conditions. As seen in Figure 2B, there was no significant difference between the two groups"
