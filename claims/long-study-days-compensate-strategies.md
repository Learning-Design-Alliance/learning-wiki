## Evidence

### Theobald 2025

Theobald, M. (2025). Study longer or study effectively? Better study strategies can compensate for less study time and predict goal achievement and lower negative affect. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 95, 405–420. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12725

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

A supplementary analysis of the high-study-time subset (k = 176 observations, 5% of data, from n = 58 participants) found strategy–goal achievement associations vanished; average goal achievement in this subset was about 82% versus about 73% in the rest of the data.

> "when perceived study time exceeded 8 hours, higher levels of self‐reported procrastination were no longer associated with goal achievement (β = −.06, 95% CI [−.21, .08], p = .397). For planning strategies, the association disappeared even earlier, at about 5 hours (β = −.001, 95% CI [−.07, .06], p = .840)."
