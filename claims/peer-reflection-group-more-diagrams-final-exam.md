## Evidence

### Andrew J. Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2015

Andrew J. Mason and Chandralekha Singh. (2015). Impact of guided reflection with peers on the development of effective problem solving strategies and physics learning. [doi:10.1119/1.4947159](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4947159)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of two recitation structures in one course (PR evening section, 93 students; traditional daytime section, 107 students). Table 1 shows averages of 8.6 vs 7.0 problems with diagrams per student; the article reports the difference as significant with no effect size printed.

> "on the final exam with only multiple -choice questions, the PR group drew diagrams on significantly more problems than the traditional group although there is no difference between the number of problems with scratch work"

### Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Using Reflection with Peers to Help Students Learn Effective Problem Solving Strategies. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1603.02944](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1603.02944)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Non-equivalent comparison of two course sections (traditional N=107, PR N=93) on the same 40-question multiple-choice final exam. Table 2 reports averages of 7.0 problems with diagrams per student (traditional) vs 8.6 (PR), p=0.003 between groups; "no partial credit given for drawing the diagrams".

> "We ﬁnd that the PR group on average drew more diagrams than the tradi- tional group. Table 2 compares the average number of problems with diagrams or scratchworks by the tradi- tional group and the PR group on the ﬁnal exam."
