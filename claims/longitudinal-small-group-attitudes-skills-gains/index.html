q3 · i? · review · r2

Longitudinal pre-post evaluation of EBM integration into a medical school curriculum for 4th-year medical students (n=259) in Thailand, with three assessments (before, middle, after). Table 2 reports "Overall attitudes: 2.4 (1.7, 3.0) vs. 3.6 (3.0, 4.0) vs. 4.0 (3.4, 4.2)" and skills rising from 2.2 to 3.8.

Related Claims¶

  • Exposure to a 14-session EBM intervention was associated with higher confidence in critical appraisal skills among medical students (M5 EBM 17.1 vs. M5 non-EBM 8.4, P<0.001) — related
  • A longitudinal multifaceted EBM curriculum for medical students significantly increased Berlin questionnaire and Fresno test knowledge scores over two and three years — related
  • A statistically significant increase in medical students' self-reported EBM knowledge and attitudes was found after a 3-month single-intervention course, with EBM concept understanding rising from 2.20 to 3.17 (P<0.001) — related
  • A multifaceted EBM course for pharmacy students significantly improved overall EBM test scores (11.4 vs. 12.6, P<0.0001) — related
  • A three-session EBM and critical appraisal course produced a highly statistically significant overall pre-post score difference (Z-score=-3.398, P=0.001) among medical students — related
