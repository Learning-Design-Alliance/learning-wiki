## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Non-graduates had higher odds than graduates of passing MRCS Part A (OR 1.98, 95% CI 1.44–2.74) and Part B (OR 1.77, 95% CI 1.15–2.71) at the first attempt after full adjustment. [→ Ricky Ellis 2022](#ricky-ellis-2022)

## Evidence

### Ricky Ellis 2022

Ricky Ellis, Peter A Brennan, Amanda J Lee, Duncan SG Scrimgeour, Jennifer Cleland. (2022). Differential attainment at MRCS according to gender, ethnicity, age and socioeconomic factors: a retrospective cohort study. Journal of the Royal Society of Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1177/01410768221079018

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multivariate logistic regression in the Part B cohort (n = 2600; final model N = 1125) adjusting for prior attainment and all sociodemographic factors. The article reports "being a non-graduate (OR 1.77, 95% CI 1.15–2.71)" as an independent predictor of Part B success; the Part A estimate (OR 1.98) comes from the corresponding Part A model. No standardised effect size is printed.

> "being a non-graduate (OR 1.77, 95% CI 1.15–2.71) and having attended a fee-paying school (OR 1.51, 95% CI 1.08–2.10) were found to be independent predictors of Part B success, with gender no longer being a significant predictor (p > 0.05)."
