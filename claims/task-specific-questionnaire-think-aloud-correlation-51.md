# A task-specific questionnaire built item-by-item from a think-aloud coding taxonomy correlated r=.51 with think-aloud protocols, higher than the -.07 to .42 regularly reported

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When a questionnaire and think-aloud method were aimed at the same learning task and the same learning activities, they correlated r=.51 (one-tailed, marginal significance; p=.05), above the regularly reported range of -.07 to .42. [→ Gonny Schellings 2011](#gonny-schellings-2011)

## Evidence

### Gonny Schellings 2011

Gonny Schellings. (2011). Applying learning strategy questionnaires: problems and possibilities. Metacognition and Learning, 6, 91–109. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-011-9069-5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparative study in which 16 ninth-graders studied a 1,650-word history text thinking aloud, then completed the task-specific questionnaire; Spearman's rho between methods was .51 (p=.05, one-tailed). The author notes this "was promising" against the reported -.07 to .42 range.

> "The two methods showed a correlation of .51 (that reached a one-tailed, marginal significance; p=.05). This correlation might be seen as moderate, but as shown in our overall picture concerning the comparison of questionnaires with think-aloud measures, where correlations between complete instruments range from -.07 to .42, a .51 correlation was promising."
