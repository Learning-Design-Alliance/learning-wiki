# Textbook format choice produces a significant multivariate difference in university students' motivation and self-regulated learning scores

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q2` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` A one-way MANOVA on the five MSLQ subscales showed a significant multivariate difference based on students' textbook format choice, with eta squared = .08. [→ Amanda J. Rockinson-Szapkiw 2013](#amanda-j-rockinson-szapkiw-2013)

## Evidence

### Amanda J. Rockinson-Szapkiw 2013

Amanda J. Rockinson-Szapkiw, Jillian Wendt, and Rebecca Lunde. (2013). Electronic Versus Print Textbooks: The Influence of Textbook Format on University Students' Self-Regulated Learning Strategies, Motivation, and Text Anxiety. American Journal of Distance Education, 27(3), 179-188. https://doi.org/10.1080/08923647.2013.796230

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Omnibus one-way MANOVA comparing the two self-selected format groups on five correlated MSLQ dependent variables, with alpha set at .025 and Pillai's trace used due to homogeneity violations. The printed effect size was eta squared = .08 for Pillai's trace = .08, F(4, 532) = 8.6.

> "Results of the MANOVA demonstrated that there was a signiﬁcant difference in university students’ MSLQ scores based on their choice of textbook format, Pillai’s trace = .08, F(4, 532) = 8.6, p > .001, eta squared = .08."
