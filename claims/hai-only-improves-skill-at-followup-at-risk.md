## Subclaims
`q3 i?` No significant risk × condition interaction effects on SKILL were found for HAI-E at posttest (B = 0.340, p = 0.176) or follow-up (B = 0.333, p = 0.149), nor for HAI-O at posttest (B = 0.288, p = 0.227). [→ Pendry Patricia 2020](#pendry-patricia-2020)

## Evidence

### Pendry Patricia 2020

Pendry Patricia, Carr Alexa M., Gee Nancy R., Vandagriff Jaymie L. (2020). Randomized Trial Examining Effects of Animal Assisted Intervention and Stress Related Symptoms on College Students’ Learning and Study Skills. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17061909

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ITT regressions on the randomized trial found no significant interaction between risk status and either HAI condition on SKILL immediately after the four-week program; the article reports "no significant interaction effects by risk-status and the HAI-E condition at posttest".

> "However, we found no significant interaction effects by risk-status and the HAI-E condition at posttest (B = 0.340, p = 0.176), or follow up (B = 0.333, p = 0.149) on SKILL."

### Pendry Patricia 2020 (2)

Pendry Patricia, Carr Alexa M., Gee Nancy R., Vandagriff Jaymie L. (2020). Randomized Trial Examining Effects of Animal Assisted Intervention and Stress Related Symptoms on College Students’ Learning and Study Skills. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17061909

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same model at six-week follow-up showed at-risk students exposed to therapy dogs without content presentations had "significantly higher use of study skills and strategies compared to the at-risk students in the ASM condition". The authors flag this as an unexpected outcome needing replication.

> "At follow up (R2 = 0.139, F(6,302) = 8.10, p < 0.001, results showed a significant interaction among at-risk students assigned to the HAI-O condition (B = 0.499, p = 0.029), demonstrating significantly higher use of study skills and strategies compared to the at-risk students in the ASM condition, while controlling for attendance."
