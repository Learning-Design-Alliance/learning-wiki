# Student reflections on screencasting clustered into five value themes, with teaching-to-learn (65%) and learning from peers (46%) the most common

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After four screencasts, students' explanations of why they created screencasts yielded five codes: Teaching to Learn (65%), Learn from Peers (46%), Practice Communicating Science (15%), Evaluate Understanding (10%), and Support/Help Peers (10%). `q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Inductive coding of end-of-semester-1 open-ended reflections (Table 3, n=22) on the value of screencasting. The article reports the five themes with percentages and notes responses could evidence multiple categories, so percentages do not add to 100%; no effect size is printed.

> "Based on open-ended responses, 5 codes emerged regarding student perspectives on the value or usefulness of screencasting as a learning activity"
