# An apparent switching advantage in dyslexia (lower switching reaction-time cost) disappears after controlling for processing speed

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Dyslexia showed a significantly lower switching z-mean reaction time cost than controls (Cohen's d = -0.60). [→ Doyle C 2018](#doyle-c-2018)
`q3 i?` The switching reaction time cost difference was no longer significant after controlling for processing speed; equivalence was not tested. [→ Doyle C 2018 (2)](#doyle-c-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Doyle C 2018

Doyle C, Smeaton AF, Roche RAP and Boran L (2018) Inhibition and Updating, but Not Switching, Predict Developmental Dyslexia and Individual Variation in Reading Ability. Front. Psychol. 9:795. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00795

`q3 · i2`

Case-control ANOVA on switching z-mean reaction time cost composites (Number-Letter and Phoneme switch tasks) in the dyslexia vs control sample. Dyslexia showed "a significantly lower switching z-mean reaction time cost score," Cohen's d = −0.60.

> "At the composite level, dyslexia is characterized by a significantly lower switching z-mean reaction time cost score than control participants [ F(1, 54) = 5.03, p = 0.029, Cohen'sd = −0.60]."

### Doyle C 2018 (2)

Doyle C, Smeaton AF, Roche RAP and Boran L (2018) Inhibition and Updating, but Not Switching, Predict Developmental Dyslexia and Individual Variation in Reading Ability. Front. Psychol. 9:795. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00795

`q3 · i?`

ANCOVA controlling for processing speed in the same sample. The switching reaction time cost difference was "no longer signiﬁcant," F(1, 53) = 2.55, p = 0.116; equivalence was not tested.

> "After controlling for individual variation in processing spe ed, signiﬁcant diﬀerences for the switching z-mean reaction time cost score are no longer signiﬁcant [ F(1, 53) = 2.55, p = 0.116]."
