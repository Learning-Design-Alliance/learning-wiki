## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The authors report that navigation activity in the animation+voice setting did not correlate with knowledge test results (printed Spearman r = 0.464, no significance test), and participants rarely used the stop/play flow control, leading to rejection of the cognitive-load-reduction hypothesis. ## Evidence

### Guttormsen Schär 2007

Guttormsen Schär, S., & Zimmermann, P. G. (2007). Investigating Means to Reduce Cognitive Load from Animations: Applying Differentiated Measures of Knowledge Representation. Journal of Research on Technology in Education, 40(1), 64–78. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ826063 [doi:10.1080/15391523.2007.10782497](https://doi.org/10.1080/15391523.2007.10782497)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Correlation analysis between logged use of navigation elements (stop, start, spooling) and re-coded mean knowledge test results in the AV group. The article reports no correlation, though the printed Spearman r = 0.464 is unusually high for that conclusion; no significance test is printed.

> "There was no correlation between the navigation activities in the AV setting and the knowledge tests (Spearmans Rank Korrelation, r=0.464)."
