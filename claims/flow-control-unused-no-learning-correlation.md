# Learner flow control over animations was rarely used and did not correlate with learning performance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Navigation activity in the animation+voice setting did not correlate with knowledge test results, and participants rarely used the stop/play flow control, leading to rejection of the cognitive-load-reduction hypothesis. [→ Guttormsen Schär 2007](#guttormsen-schar-2007)

## Evidence

### Guttormsen Schär 2007

Guttormsen Schär, S., & Zimmermann, P. G. (2007). Investigating Means to Reduce Cognitive Load from Animations: Applying Differentiated Measures of Knowledge Representation. Journal of Research on Technology in Education, 40(1), 64–78. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ826063

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Correlation analysis between logged use of navigation elements (stop, start, spooling) and re-coded mean knowledge test results in the AV group. The article reports no correlation, though the printed Spearman r = 0.464 is unusually high for that conclusion; no significance test is printed.

> "There was no correlation between the navigation activities in the AV setting and the knowledge tests (Spearmans Rank Korrelation, r=0.464)."
