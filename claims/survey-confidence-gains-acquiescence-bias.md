## Evidence

### Malouf William T. 2024

Malouf William T., Kondaveeti Geeth, Phillips Jacline G., Patel Kunjan, Hall Justin A., Fourrey Torrey L., May Nelson, Meegalla Nuwan T., Reger Kevin J., Runyan Christopher M., Hiatt Kevin D. (2024). Facial trauma education in radiology: using surgeon feedback as the benchmark for success. Emergency Radiology 31(6):807-814. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10140-024-02288-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Wilcoxon signed-rank comparison of pre/post surveys from 26 participants showed significantly improved confidence across all facial subunits on a 4-point Likert scale, including mandibular trauma, which the session did not cover.

> "Comparing pre- and post-surveys, participants indicated significantly improved confidence with reporting CT studies of complex facial trauma and significantly improved awareness of surgeon's expectations from imaging reports (Fig. 3)."

### Malouf William T. 2024 (2)

Malouf William T., Kondaveeti Geeth, Phillips Jacline G., Patel Kunjan, Hall Justin A., Fourrey Torrey L., May Nelson, Meegalla Nuwan T., Reger Kevin J., Runyan Christopher M., Hiatt Kevin D. (2024). Facial trauma education in radiology: using surgeon feedback as the benchmark for success. Emergency Radiology 31(6):807-814. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10140-024-02288-0

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Authors' interpretation (type e) offered in the Discussion to explain why survey confidence improved even for the untaught mandible subunit; no test of the bias was performed, though the authors note acquiescence scores can correct for it.

> "We suspect that this finding reflects the presence of acquiescence bias, which is a type of response bias in which participants offer favorable responses in support of a research initiative rather than responses matching their true opinions."
