# Reanalysis suggests the 2-sigma tutoring effect was confounded with mastery learning, yielding a net tutoring effect of about .85 ES

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Assuming additive contributions of tutoring and mastery learning, subtracting the mastery-learning effect from the 2-sigma studies' combined effect yields a net tutoring effect of .85 ES, not 2 ES. [→ Olney 2025](#olney-2025)

## Evidence

### Olney 2025

Olney, A.M., D'Mello, S.K., Person, N., Cade, W., Hays, P., Dempsey, C.W., Lehman, B., Williams, B., Graesser, A. (2025). Efficacy of a Computer Tutor that Models Expert Human Tutors. [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-98462-4_38](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-98462-4_38)

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Theoretical reanalysis in the Introduction: the authors subtract the mastery-learning effect size from the combined tutoring-plus-mastery effect of Bloom's two source dissertations, assuming additivity and no interaction. This is an analytical argument, not a new experiment.

> "This calculation, using the actual average effect sizes in the 2-sigma studies [1,8], yields 1.91 ES for tutoring+mastery learning, 1.06 ES for mastery learning, and a .85 ES net effect of tutoring."
