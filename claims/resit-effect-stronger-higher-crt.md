# The resit effect on study-time investment is stronger for participants with higher Cognitive Reflection Test scores

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium
> **Currency** · newest study 2016 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` The reduction in study-time investment caused by resit availability correlated positively with CRT scores (r = .34 in Experiment 1; r = .25 combined across experiments). [→ Nijenkamp 2016](#nijenkamp-2016)

## Evidence

### Nijenkamp 2016

Nijenkamp, R., Nieuwenstein, M. R., de Jong, R., & Lorist, M. M. (2016). Do Resit Exams Promote Lower Investments of Study Time? Theory and Data from a Laboratory Study. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0161708

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Bayesian correlation analysis in Experiment 1 (N = 50) between CRT scores and the resit effect (NR minus R1 study-time investment), yielding "a correlation of r = .34" with BF = 6.1. A combined analysis across Experiments 1 and 2 replicated this with r = .25, BF = 4.6.

> "A Bayesian test of whether CRT scores were positively correlated with the resit effect yielded a correlation of r = .34 and a Bayes factor of 6.1, indicating that the data produced strong evidence in favor of the existence of a positive relation between CRT scores and the effect of resit exams on study-time investment"
