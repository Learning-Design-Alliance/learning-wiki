# When the textbook was optional, exam scores of students who purchased it did not differ statistically from those who did not

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Two-sample t-tests found no statistical difference on any of the three exams between students who bought the optional textbook and those who did not. [→ Rudolph 2013](#rudolph-2013)

## Evidence

### Rudolph 2013

Rudolph, A. L. (2013). Do You Always Need a Textbook to Teach Astro 101?. Astronomy Education Review. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1311.3635](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1311.3635)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within the optional-textbook terms (Fall 2009-Spring 2011), students self-reported via clickers whether they had purchased the book; weighted averages and standard deviations per exam were compared between purchasers (N=119) and non-purchasers (N=153). Only p-values are printed.

> "The results of those t-tests again strongly confirm the null hypothesis in all three cases: that there is no statistical difference between students who purchased a textbook and those who did not (two-tail p-values of 0.642, 0.900, and 0.618 for the first midterm, second midterm, and final exam, respectively)."
