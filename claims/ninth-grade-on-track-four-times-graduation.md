# Students on track in ninth grade were four times more likely than off-track peers to graduate high school

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Ninth- and tenth-grade students who were on track were 4 times more likely to graduate than off-track students; this predictive gap has held across 2007-08 to 2014-15 cohorts (e.g., 87.2% vs 29.7% in 2014-15). [→ UChicago Consortium on School Research 2024](#uchicago-consortium-on-school-research-2024)

## Evidence

### UChicago Consortium on School Research 2024

UChicago Consortium on School Research. (2024). Family playbook: How can we understand students' progress in school? Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/events-and-resources

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Consortium analysis (citing Seeskin, Mahaffie, & Usher, 2020) of CPS ninth-grade on-track status and four-year graduation; a companion figure shows on-track graduation rates of 85.5% to 88.0% versus about 29.7% to 35.1% for off-track students across 2007-08 through 2014-15 cohorts.
