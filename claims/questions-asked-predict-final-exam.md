## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Bailey E. G. 2018

Bailey E. G., Baek D., Meiling J., Morris C., Nelson N., Rice N. S., Rose S., Stockdale P. (2018). Learning Gains from a Recurring “Teach and Question” Homework Assignment in a General Biology Course: Using Reciprocal Peer Tutoring Outside Class. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-12-0259

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Hierarchical multiple linear regression (n = 57) predicting final exam score; block 3 added the number of questions asked (β = 0.305, p = 0.006 as printed in Table 3). Adjusted R2 = 0.410; no standardized effect size interpretable as impact, so null.

> "the average number of questions the students asked during their TQ sessions significantly improved the fit (p = 0.006). The final model (model 3) had an adjusted R2 of 0.410 and included LCTSR, attendance, and number of questions asked per TQ session as significant predictors of final exam performance."

### Bailey E. G. 2018 (2)

Bailey E. G., Baek D., Meiling J., Morris C., Nelson N., Rice N. S., Rose S., Stockdale P. (2018). Learning Gains from a Recurring “Teach and Question” Homework Assignment in a General Biology Course: Using Reciprocal Peer Tutoring Outside Class. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-12-0259

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Null finding from the same regression analysis: percent of high-level questions did not significantly predict final exam performance. The article notes only about 30% of student questions were high level and suggests more training might increase their use.

> "Interestingly, the percent of questions asked that were high level did not significantly predict performance on the final exam."
