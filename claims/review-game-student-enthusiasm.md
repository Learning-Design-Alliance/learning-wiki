## Evidence

### Chou E 2026

Chou E, Cooper D, Goel P, Tang J, Henkle G, Zemeida S, Wolfson H, Shyu YL, Chang E, Abram A, Gerriets VA, Cusick JK. (2026). The use of voiceovers and review games in teaching immunology. ImmunoHorizons. https://doi.org/10.1093/immhor/vlag042

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-study survey of participating medical students administered anonymously via SurveyMonkey, scored on a 7-point Likert scale and compiled across all years of the study. The study reports students "wished to have review games more frequently (5.62; n = 73)" and that the review game "solidified their knowledge (5.80; n = 54)."

> "The enthusiasm for review games was greater than that of voiceovers, because more students wished to have review games more frequently (5.62; n = 73), versus having more voiceovers used for lectures (4.77; n = 83). Students felt the review game solidified their knowledge (5.80; n = 54) and cleared up any misconceptions they had regarding the material (5.63; n = 54)."
