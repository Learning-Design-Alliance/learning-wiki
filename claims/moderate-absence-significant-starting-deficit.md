# Even moderately absent students (0.1-4.9% of days) start the following year at significantly lower mathematics achievement (.07 to .15 SDs) than no-absence peers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Students with only moderate prior-year absences started the next fall significantly behind no-absence peers in math. [→ King 2020](#king-2020)

## Evidence

### King 2020

King, G., & Jensen, N. (2020). Looking Back: How Prior-Year Attendance Impacts Starting Achievement. (The Center for School and Student Progress at NWEA Working Paper). https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/looking-back-how-prior-year-attendance-impacts-starting-achievement/

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Fixed-effects models on the same pooled district cohorts show moderate absentees (1-8 days missed) started the next year at Cohen's d effect sizes of ".07 to .15 SDs" below no-absence peers in math, supporting the claim that harms begin before the 10% chronic threshold.

> "Interestingly, MA students (0.1-5.0% absences) also started the following school year at a significantly lower achievement level in mathematics than their NA peers (effects ranging from .07 to .15 SDs)."
