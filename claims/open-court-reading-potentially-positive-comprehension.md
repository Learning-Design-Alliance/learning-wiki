# Open Court Reading© has potentially positive effects on comprehension for beginning readers in grades 1–3, with a small extent of evidence

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` One randomized controlled trial (Borman et al., 2008; 679 students in grades 1–3) reported positive effects of Open Court Reading© on the CTBS/5 Terra Nova Reading Composite, with an average effect size large enough to be considered substantively important (at least .25). ## Evidence

### Open Court Reading© 2014

Open Court Reading©. (2014). WWC Intervention Report: Beginning Reading, Updated October 2014. U.S. Department of Education, What Works Clearinghouse. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/347

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

A multisite cluster randomized field trial across five schools in five states during 2005–06, with 300 students in the Open Court Reading© group and 379 in the comparison group, assessed after approximately 7 months. The article reports the average effect size was at least .25, meeting the WWC's substantively important threshold.

> "Borman et al. (2008) reported positive effects of Open Court Reading© on the Reading Composite score of the Comprehensive Test of Basic Skills, 5th Edition (CTBS/5) Terra Nova test for students in grades 1, 2, and 3."
