Roderick 2000 (2)¶

Roderick, M., Nagaoka, J., Bacon, J., & Easton, J. Q. (2000). Update: Ending Social Promotion: Passing, Retention, and Achievement Trends Among Promoted and Retained Students 1995-1999. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://www.consortium-chicago.org

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Tracking of 1997 Summer Bridge passers through the eighth-grade gate. Almost 86 percent of sixth graders who met the cutoff in May 1997 did so again in eighth grade; excluding leavers, 94 percent passed again.

"Although almost half of them did have to at- tend Summer Bridge after eighth grade, 75 percent of this group passed the test criterion by August and fully 85 percent were promoted."

Related Claims¶

  • Students who met the promotional cutoffs, including after Summer Bridge, maintained positive test trajectories two years after promotion — related
  • Summer Bridge's second chance substantially raised promotion rates: by August 1997, 68% of included third graders, 79% of sixth graders, and 83% of eighth graders had met the cutoff — related
  • Passing rates under the Chicago promotional policy increased in all three grades from 1997 to 1999, mostly during the school year rather than Summer Bridge — related
  • Despite higher passing rates, retention rates did not fall because fewer students who failed the cutoff were promoted with waivers — related
  • Summer Bridge produced short-term test-score gains and allowed more students to meet promotion cutoffs, particularly among sixth and eighth graders — related
  • In 1997, 31% of third graders and about 20% of sixth and eighth graders were excluded from Chicago's promotional test-score policy, with bilingual education the main exclusion reason in third grade and special education in sixth and eighth — related
  • Second-year outcomes were worst for retained eighth graders and Transition Center students: only 27% met the criteria by May 1998 and about 16% of officially retained eighth graders dropped out — related
