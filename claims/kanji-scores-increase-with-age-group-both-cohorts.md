## Subclaims
`q2 i?` One-way ANOVAs on composite factor scores showed main effects of age group on reading accuracy, semantic comprehension and writing accuracy in both cohorts, with post-hoc tests showing older groups scoring higher. [→ Otsuka 2020](#otsuka-2020)

## Evidence

### Otsuka 2020

Otsuka, S., & Murai, T. (2020). The multidimensionality of Japanese kanji abilities. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-59852-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional ANOVAs on the sums of z-scores per factor in comparable Level 2 data, four age groups (13-18, 19-22, 23-39, 40-59). Post-hoc tests showed "the scores of middle adults were higher than those of young adults and high-school and university age examinees on reading accuracy and semantic comprehension, in both 2006 and 2016".

> "The one-way ANOVAs with both the 2006 and 2016 data showed the main effects of age group on the scores in each dimension (F(3, 32854) = 985.8, p < 0.001, on reading accuracy, F(3, 32854) = 693.6, p < 0.001, semantic comprehension, F(3, 32854) = 430.7, p < 0.001, writing accuracy, in 2006;"
