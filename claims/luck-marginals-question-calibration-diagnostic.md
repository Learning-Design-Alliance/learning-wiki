# Marginal luck probabilities diagnose question calibration, flagging questions 7–9 as prone to over-performance and question 12 as particularly challenging

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The marginal probability of the luck variable provides a diagnostic of question informativeness: questions 7, 8 and 9 show high probability of very good luck (above 30%), suggesting poor calibration, while question 12 shows bad-luck probability up to 0.76, indicating underperformance is more likely than over-performance. ## Evidence

### Francesca Mangili 2026

Francesca Mangili, Alessandro Antonucci, Rafael Cabañas de Paz. (2026). Causal Modelling of Support Interventions for Student Competency Assessment. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24632

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Group-level marginal inference on the luck variables of the CAT causal model, reported in Table 1, based on the assessment data from 109 students. The first six questions appear more informative than Q7–Q9, since very good luck probability never exceeds 15% in the former but can exceed 30% in the latter.

> "it is very unlikely to experience good or very good luck inQ 12, while the probability of bad luck can be as high as 0.76. This suggests that the scheme is particularly challenging, making underperformance more likely than over-performance."
