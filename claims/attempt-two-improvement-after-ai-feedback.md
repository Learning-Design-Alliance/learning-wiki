# Student performance improved significantly from first to second submission after AI feedback, with 15.18% of responses improving and only 0.31% declining

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teacher-assigned grades for Attempt II were statistically significantly higher than for Attempt I (Z = 10.56, p < .001). [→ Ning Yu 2025](#ning-yu-2025)
`q2 i?` Of 962 responses with both teacher grades, 15.18% improved (n = 146), 84.51% showed no change (n = 813), and 0.31% declined (n = 3). [→ Ning Yu 2025 (2)](#ning-yu-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Ning Yu 2025

Ning Yu, Jie Zhang, Sandeep Mitra, Rebecca Smith, Adam Rich. (2025). AI-Educational Development Loop (AI-EDL): A Conceptual Framework to Bridge AI Capabilities with Classical Educational Theories. State University of New York (SUNY) Brockport. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2508.00970](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2508.00970)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Wilcoxon signed-rank test in the pilot study comparing teacher grades across attempts. Mean teacher grade rose from 1.56 (SD = 0.73) in Attempt I to 1.77 (SD = 0.57) in Attempt II; no effect size is printed.

> "There was a statistically significant difference in the teacher-assigned grades for Attempt I and Attempt II submissions (Z = 10.56, p < .001), indicating that students received significantly higher grades on their resubmissions (Attempt II) compared to their initial submissions (Attempt I)."

### Ning Yu 2025 (2)

Ning Yu, Jie Zhang, Sandeep Mitra, Rebecca Smith, Adam Rich. (2025). AI-Educational Development Loop (AI-EDL): A Conceptual Framework to Bridge AI Capabilities with Classical Educational Theories. State University of New York (SUNY) Brockport. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2508.00970](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2508.00970)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Descriptive tabulation of teacher-assigned grades for Attempt I vs. Attempt II (Table 7), visualized in Figure 5 where blue points above red indicate widespread improvement following AI-supported feedback and revision.

> "out of 962 responses where teacher grades for both Attempt I and II were available, approximately 84.51% of responses showed no change (n = 813), 15.18% demonstrated improvement (n = 146), and only 0.31% declined (n = 3)."
