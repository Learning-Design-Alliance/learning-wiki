Evidence¶

Diamond Adele 2011¶

Diamond Adele, Lee Kathleen. (2011). Interventions shown to Aid Executive Function Development in Children 4–12 Years Old. Science. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1204529

q2 · i? · causal · r?

The review reports a study in which children 5–11 years old were randomly assigned by homeroom class to Tae-Kwon-Do with incrementing challenge or standard PE; gains generalized to multiple contexts.

"Children getting traditional Tae-Kwon-Do training were found to show greater gains than children in standard physical education on all dimensions of EFs studied (e.g., cognitive [distractible—focused] and affective [quitting—persevering]) (28)."

Diamond Adele 2011 (2)¶

Diamond Adele, Lee Kathleen. (2011). Interventions shown to Aid Executive Function Development in Children 4–12 Years Old. Science. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1204529

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports moderators of the Tae-Kwon-Do effect: age and gender, with the oldest children and boys gaining most; participants were socioeconomically advantaged.

"Gains were greatest for the oldest children (Grades 4 & 5) and least for the youngest (K & Grade 1) and greater for boys than girls."

Discussion¶

Learner Variables¶

  • Age and Developmental Stage — moderator: an instructional effect differs with it

Related Claims¶

  • Physical activities combining exercise with character development or mindfulness improved children's EFs more than exercise alone: tae kwon do outperformed standard PE, and a yoga pilot improved planning — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Whether EF gains are produced depends on how an activity is done: traditional tae kwon do reduced aggression while modern competitive martial arts worsened outcomes in juvenile delinquents — related
  • Mindfulness training improves EFs most in 7–9 year-olds with initially poorer EFs, and yoga improves EFs where physical training alone does not — related
  • Whether EF gains are seen depends on the amount of time children spend doggedly practicing and pushing themselves to improve — related
  • Open-skill exercises yield larger executive function benefits than closed-skill exercises for inhibitory control and working memory — related
  • Music interventions show moderately strong evidence for EF change including inhibitory control, possibly stronger with greater intensity and longevity — related
