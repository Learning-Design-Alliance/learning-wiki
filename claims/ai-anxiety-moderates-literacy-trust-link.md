# AI anxiety attenuates the positive association between AI literacy and trust in AI

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` The AI literacy × AI anxiety interaction predicting trust is negative and significant (β = −0.25, p < 0.001). [→ Peichen Hu 2026](#peichen-hu-2026)
`q3 i?` Simple slopes of literacy predicting trust fall from 0.76 at −1 SD anxiety to 0.25 at +1 SD, showing a diminished rather than absent return on literacy. [→ Peichen Hu 2026 (2)](#peichen-hu-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Peichen Hu 2026

Peichen Hu. (2026). Psychological predictors of continued ChatGPT use among university students: the roles of AI literacy, trust, and academic self-efficacy. Front. Psychol. 17:1900190. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1900190

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Latent interaction specified with matched-pair product indicators in the SEM of 450 existing users; the printed interaction was "β for the interaction=− 0.25, p < 0.001". An LMS sensitivity check gave "standardized β =− 0.26, p < 0.001".

> "AI anxiety moderated the AI literacy–trust association in this cross-sectional model (β for the interaction=− 0.25, p < 0.001), supporting H8."

### Peichen Hu 2026 (2)

Peichen Hu. (2026). Psychological predictors of continued ChatGPT use among university students: the roles of AI literacy, trust, and academic self-efficacy. Front. Psychol. 17:1900190. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1900190

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Simple-slope probing at one SD below/above the mean of anxiety in the same model; the printed standardized slopes were "0.76 at one standard deviation below the mean, to 0.50 at the mean, to 0.25 at one standard deviation above". These are simple slopes, not a printed effect size, so no magnitude is coded.

> "Simple-slope analysis showed that the positive association of literacy with trust weakened as anxiety rose, from 0.76 at one standard deviation below the mean, to 0.50 at the mean, to 0.25 at one standard deviation above (Figure3)."
