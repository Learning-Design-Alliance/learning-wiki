## Evidence

### Snelgrove Huon; Gabbott Ben 2020

Snelgrove Huon; Gabbott Ben. (2020). Critical analysis of evidence about the impacts on surgical teams of ‘mental practice’ in systematic reviews: a systematic rapid evidence assessment (SREA). BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02131-3

`q2 · i?`

Analysis of the stated aims and keywords of the six included reviews (Table 3). The authors find the domain's keywords "for the most part linguistically unmarked", with mental practice, mental imagery and mental rehearsal the most common imbrications.

> "The key words used by authors to describe the conceptual domain are for the most part linguistically unmarked, the most common imbrications being ‘mental practice’, ‘mental imagery’ and ‘mental rehearsal’."

### Snelgrove Huon; Gabbott Ben 2020 (2)

`q2 · i?`

In the review-limitations section the authors state that using MP as an umbrella for mental imagery, mental simulation and mental rehearsal "may risk skewing more highly focussed definitions and their findings", and that MP and MI remain inadequately defined.

> "However, this encompassing conception may risk skewing more highly focussed definitions and their findings [44]."
