# Bilingual children acquire vocabulary and syntax in each language more slowly than monolingual children, yet show cognitive advantages and can match or surpass monolingual peers with support

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Children learning multiple languages typically acquire knowledge of Vocabulary and Syntax in each language more slowly than children who know only one language. [→ Sheppard 2015](#sheppard-2015)

## Evidence

### Sheppard 2015

Sheppard, S. M. (2015). Supporting Research-Based Personalization for Reading Success. Digital Promise Global. https://global.digitalpromise.org

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The paper's Student Biographical Background section reports, citing prior studies, that multilingual children acquire Vocabulary and Syntax more slowly in each language. It states that with language and literacy support at home and in school, they can match and even surpass their monolingual peers.

> "However, because they are learning multiple languages, they also typically acquire knowledge of Vocabulary and Syntax (i.e., grammar) in each language more slowly than children who know only one language."

### Sheppard 2015 (2)

Sheppard, S. M. (2015). Supporting Research-Based Personalization for Reading Success. Digital Promise Global. https://global.digitalpromise.org

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

In the interactions section, the paper reports, citing bilingual-advantage studies, that bilingual and multilingual children tend to show better Selective Attention than monolingual children, while initially gaining early literacy skills like Alphabet Knowledge more slowly when schooled in a language other than their Primary Language.

> "Bilingual/multilingual children tend to have better Selective Attention (the ability to select and focus on pertinent information while ignoring irrelevant stimuli) than monolingual children."
