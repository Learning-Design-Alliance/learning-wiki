Evidence¶

Villavicencio 2025¶

Villavicencio, P., Tsay, J. S. & de la Malla, C. (2025). Target configuration determines how and what we learn during sensorimotor adaptation. npj Science of Learning 10:89. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00379-2

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Experiment 1 reaching study (42 participants, 1.3× gain perturbation): both groups rapidly reduced movement amplitude at the shared target, showing "a main effect of phase ( F(3,120) = 134.89,p < 0.0001, η2 = 0.77)".

"Adaptation was evident in both groups, reﬂected in a main effect of phase ( F(3,120) = 134.89,p < 0.0001, η2 = 0.77)"

Villavicencio 2025 (2)¶

Villavicencio, P., Tsay, J. S. & de la Malla, C. (2025). Target configuration determines how and what we learn during sensorimotor adaptation. npj Science of Learning 10:89. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00379-2

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Linear mixed-effects model at the shared 11 cm training target in Experiment 1 revealed "a marked interaction between Group and Phase ( F(3,120) = 5.36, p = 0.0017, η2 = 0.12)", showing target configuration shaped the learning-process profile.

"Theshared target model revealed a marked interaction between Group and Phase ( F(3,120) = 5.36, p = 0.0017, η2 = 0.12)."

Villavicencio 2025 (3)¶

Villavicencio, P., Tsay, J. S. & de la Malla, C. (2025). Target configuration determines how and what we learn during sensorimotor adaptation. npj Science of Learning 10:89. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00379-2

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Between-subjects comparison of aftereffects in Experiment 1: "the Extent group exhibited reduced implicit learning (aftereffects) compared to the Angular group", t(119) = 3.02, p = 0.003, D = 1.15.

"the Extent group exhibited reduced implicit learning (aftereffects) compared to the Angular group (Fig. 2b; Extent vs Angular, between-subjects, aftereffect: t(119) = 3.02, p = 0.003, D = 1.15)."

q3 · i2 · causal · r2
