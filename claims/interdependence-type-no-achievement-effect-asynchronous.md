# Structuring positive interdependence (role, reward, or both) did not affect posttest achievement for undergraduate business majors in asynchronous online small group work

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In one experiment with 280 undergraduate business majors, students in role, reward, role-plus-reward, and no-interdependence groups performed similarly on the posttest, with no significant differences by type of interdependence or affiliation motive. [→ Brewer 2003](#brewer-2003)

## Evidence

### Brewer 2003

Brewer, S. A., & Klein, J. D. (2003). Small Group Learning in an Online Asynchronous Environment. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED484997

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment with 280 undergraduate business majors blocked by high/low affiliation motive and randomly assigned to role, reward, role-plus-reward, or no interdependence triads; achievement measured by individual posttest. "Overall achievement was about 71% on the posttest"; no effect size printed.

> "Results indicated no significant differences in achievement by type of interdependence in small group work, or by affiliation motive (see Table 1). Overall achievement was about 71% on the posttest"

## Discussion

One experiment (Brewer & Klein 2003, 280 undergraduate business majors in triads) is recorded. An earlier version of this page described the participants as adult reentry students; the evidence entry does not say so, so the scope here follows the entry. The null result is a non-significant difference with no effect size printed, which does not establish that the conditions are equivalent.
