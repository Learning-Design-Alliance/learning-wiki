Claims

In cross-validated vocabulary-app review logs, memory models that use each item's sequence of past recall probabilities and review gaps predicted recall with about a third of half-life regression's error

On grouped data from 220 million review logs of a vocabulary app, recurrent-network memory models fed each item's history of outcomes, recall probabilities and review intervals predicted recall probability with a mean absolute error of 0.0307–0.0376, against 0.1070 for half-life regression and 0.0779 for a hand-built state model; the history of recall probabilities did most of the work.
