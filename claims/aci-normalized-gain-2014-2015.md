## Evidence

### Lachlan P. McGinness and C. M. Savage 2016

Lachlan P. McGinness and C. M. Savage. (2016). Developing an Action Concept Inventory. arXiv:1605.01786. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010133](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010133)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Pre/post administration of the 18-question second-draft ACI to the 2014 ANU Physics 2 first-year class (enrolment 108; N=76 completed both tests). The class average rose from "5 .1 ± 0.2" to "6.3 ± 0.3", with a "normalized gain" of "0 .09 ± 0.03"; the authors state the inventory thereby diagnosed low teaching effectiveness.

> "For the 2014 class, the pre and post-instruction class average scores out of a possible 18 were 5 .1 ± 0.2 and 6.3 ± 0.3, where the uncertainties are standard deviations of the means. The corresponding normalized gain, that is the actual post instruction gain as a fraction of the maximum possible gain, is 0 .09 ± 0.03."

### Lachlan P. McGinness and C. M. Savage 2016 (2)

Lachlan P. McGinness and C. M. Savage. (2016). Developing an Action Concept Inventory. arXiv:1605.01786. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010133](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010133)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Pre/post administration of the revised 19-question ACI to the 2015 Physics 2 class (N=87) after lecture and computational laboratory time on quantum mechanics was increased and introductory videos emphasised. The average rose from "5.2±0.2" to "8.2±0.3", a normalized gain of "0.22±0.03".

> "For the 2015 class the pre and post-instruction average scores out of a possible 19 were 5.2±0.2 and 8.2±0.3, for a normalized gain of 0.22±0.03."
