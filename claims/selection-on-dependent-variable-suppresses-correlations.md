## Subclaims
`q1 i2` In a simulation selecting the top 5%, 2%, and 1% of performers, correlations with GRE fell to r = 0.238, r = 0.219, and r = 0.201 respectively, versus 0.501 in the full pool. [→ Alexander Small 2017](#alexander-small-2017)

## Evidence

### Alexander Small 2017

Alexander Small. (2017). Range restriction, admissions criteria, and correlation studies of standardized tests. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1709.02895](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1709.02895)

`q1 · i2` · `theoretical · r3`

Simulation with all students admitted and the most successful subsequently identified, mimicking studies of successful postdocs; Table 4 (10^6 students) reports the printed effect sizes r = 0.238 (top 5%), r = 0.219 (top 2%), and r = 0.201 (top 1%), with spurious negative GRE-Experience correlations (down to -0.155).

> "This illustrates that selection on the dependent variable can give rise to range restriction effects just as can selection on independent variables."
