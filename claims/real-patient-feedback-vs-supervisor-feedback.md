# Students considered real patients' feedback useful but less beneficial than supervisor or trained-actor feedback

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students valued the patient's layperson perspective but rated feedback from real patients as less beneficial than feedback from specifically trained actors and less useful than the supervisor's feedback. [→ Groener JB 2015](#groener-jb-2015)

## Evidence

### Groener JB 2015

Groener JB, Bugaj TJ, Scarpone R, Koechel A, Stiepak J, Branchereau S, Krautter M, Herzog W, Nikendei C. (2015). Video-based on-ward supervision for final year medical students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0430-2

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Interview theme 'Patient's feedback' (22 quotations) from the grounded-theory analysis of student interviews in the pilot study. Students reported the layperson perspective highlighted aspects they had not thought of, while in the post-assessment patient feedback for skills received medians of 4 versus 5 for supervisor feedback.

> "Nevertheless, feedback from patients was considered useful, but 'not as useful as the supervisor's feedback'."
