# Before feedback, no significant physical-virtual difference existed; after ChatGPT feedback on the procedural question (Q3), students' normalized total fixation duration was higher on physical experimental components than on virtual representations, in one pre-post study with no comparison group

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For Q3, no significant physical-virtual difference existed before feedback (p=0.785), but after feedback the normalized total fixation duration on physical components was significantly higher than on virtual components (p=0.068). [→ Atakan Coban 2025](#atakan-coban-2025)

## Evidence

### Atakan Coban 2025

Atakan Coban, David Dzsotjan, Stefan Küchemann, Jürgen Durst, Jochen Kuhn & Christoph Hoyer. (2025). AI Support Meets AR Visualization for Alice and Bob: Personalized Learning Based on Individual ChatGPT Feedback in an AR Quantum Cryptography Experiment for Physics Lab Courses. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1140/epjqt/s40507-025-00310-z](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjqt/s40507-025-00310-z)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Paired sample t-tests for the six groups receiving feedback on Q3 (one group excluded for technical problems); Interval 1 showed no significant difference (t=-0.285, p=0.785), Interval 2 showed "significantly higher" physical AOI attention (t=2.233, p=0.068). No effect size is printed in the quoted result sentence.

> "However, after ChatGPT feedback, during Interval 2, the normalized total fixation duration on physical components is significantly higher than that on virtual components."
