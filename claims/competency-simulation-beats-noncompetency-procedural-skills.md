### Vermylen JH 2026

Vermylen JH, Cohen ER, Cook DA, McGaghie WC, Issenberg SB, Arnold J, Ballard H, Bayoumi M, Beestrum M, Bremner R, Crawford S, Einstein N, Mannarino C, Misra A, Tomita TM, Waldron H, Yanko F, Kessler DO. `q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 12 studies (from 11 articles) comparing competency-based to noncompetency-based simulation, using each study's best outcome. The review reports "a moderate effect size" favoring competency-based training; all but 2 studies favored it, and heterogeneity was high (I2 = 77%).

> "Meta-analysis of the best outcome for each of these 12 studies shows improved performance among participants trained in a competency-based simulation when compared with a noncompetency-based simulation (pooled SMD = 0.67, 95% CI = 0.27 to 1.07, P < 0.001)"
