# Identification is statistically unrelated to the other three narrative characteristics

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Use of 'us' was not significantly related to homogeneous subject (Fisher's p = .73), simplified events (Fisher's p = .33), or essentialist nation concept (χ²(1, N = 38) = 2.335, p = .13), while the other three characteristics were significantly related among each other (Fisher's p < .01 each). ## Evidence

### Mario Carretero & Floor van Alphen 2014

Mario Carretero & Floor van Alphen. (2014). Do Master Narratives Change Among High School Students? A Characterization of How National History Is Represented. Cognition and Instruction, 32(3), 290-312. https://doi.org/10.1080/07370008.2014.919298

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Secondary statistical analysis of relations among the four narrative characteristics in the 38 narratives. The article reports identification dissociation occurred in 14 of 38 narratives (37%), χ²(1, N = 38) = 2.545, p = .11.

> "Further statistical analysis demonstrates that identiﬁcation is unrelated to the other three characteristics. The homogeneous historical subject, the simple account of the events, and the essentialist nation concept are all signiﬁcantly related among each other: Fisher’s p < .01 for each of the three relations between these characteristics"
