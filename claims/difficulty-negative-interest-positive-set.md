## Evidence

### Murray 2020

Murray, D.; Boothby, C.; Zhao, H.; Minik, V.; Bérubé, N.; Larivière, V.; Sugimoto, C. R. (2020). Exploring the personal and professional factors associated with student evaluations of tenure-track faculty. PLoS ONE 15(6): e0233515. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0233515

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Observational linear regression on the matched 18,946-faculty dataset; difficulty was the largest negative estimate, and the authors note median overall ratings of 3.6 for the matched dataset. Tag analysis (Fig 1C) confirmed the difficulty–rating relationship.

> "The rated difficulty of the course was largest negative estimate from the model; each additional point of difficulty was associated with 0.39 lower points of overall teaching quality (β = −0.39, 95% CI = [−0.40, −0.38])."

### Murray 2020 (2)

Murray, D.; Boothby, C.; Zhao, H.; Minik, V.; Bérubé, N.; Larivière, V.; Sugimoto, C. R. (2020). Exploring the personal and professional factors associated with student evaluations of tenure-track faculty. PLoS ONE 15(6): e0233515. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0233515

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Same exploratory regression; student interest was the second largest positive estimate. The authors caution that interest ratings may reflect a halo effect tied to opinion of the professor rather than the course material.

> "The student interest ratings of a faculty was the second largest positive estimate; each additional point in interest was associated with 0.33 points higher overall teaching quality (β = 0.33, 95% CI = [0.32, 0.34])."
