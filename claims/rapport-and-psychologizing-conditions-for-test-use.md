# Mental tests are useful for appraising an individual only when the examiner can psychologize about the results and establish rapport with the subject

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r?` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Tests appraise individuals only when the user can translate simple responses into terms of the mental process involved and avoid confusing mental content with mental process. [→ Lewis M. Terman and Clara Harbison Town 1917](#lewis-m-terman-and-clara-harbison-town-1917)
`q1 i?` Tests appraise only when rapport is established; if the subject is intimidated or repelled, a true estimate of ability cannot be made, so rapid examinations for diagnostic purposes are unjustifiable. [→ Lewis M. Terman and Clara Harbison Town 1917](#lewis-m-terman-and-clara-harbison-town-1917)

## Evidence

### Lewis M. Terman and Clara Harbison Town 1917

Lewis M. Terman and Clara Harbison Town. (1917). Communications and Discussions, The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://www.jstor.org

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r?`

An expert-theoretical communication stating two conditions of use: the examiner must make or watch the procedure because a response 'can be fairly judged only as a part of the whole circumstance which produced it,' and rapport must be established. No empirical data are reported.

> "Mental tests are of use in appraising an individual only when a rapport is established between the experimenter and the subject. If the subject is intimidated or repelled by the examiner or by the sur rounding conditions a true estimate of his ability can not be made."
