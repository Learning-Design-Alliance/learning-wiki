## Evidence

### Yusuf S 2026

Yusuf S, Eyup DI, Emre E, Necati E, Dursun B, Arda T. (2026). Operative time-based learning curve and surgical outcomes of laparoscopic radical prostatectomy in a surgeon with limited open surgery experience. BMC Surgery 26:184. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12893-026-03577-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Chi-square analysis across the three chronological groups showed lymph node dissection use rising significantly with experience (23.3% to 56.7%), which the authors interpret as growing surgical confidence and a more comprehensive oncological approach.

> "A significant increase was noted in the number of cases in which lymph node dissection was performed as surgical experience increased, with post hoc analysis revealing a significant difference between Group 1 and Group 3 (23.3% vs. 56.7%; p = 0.022)."

### Yusuf S 2026 (2)

Yusuf S, Eyup DI, Emre E, Necati E, Dursun B, Arda T. (2026). Operative time-based learning curve and surgical outcomes of laparoscopic radical prostatectomy in a surgeon with limited open surgery experience. BMC Surgery 26:184. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12893-026-03577-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Fisher's exact test found the PSM decline (13.3%/20.0%/3.3%; overall 12.2%) non-significant, which the authors attribute to the small number of positive cases; 72% of positive margins were apical.

> "The rates of positive surgical margins were 13.3%, 20.0%, and 3.3% in Groups 1, 2, and 3, respectively (overall 12.2%). Although a numerical decrease was observed in Group 3, the difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.140)."

### Yusuf S 2026 (3)

Yusuf S, Eyup DI, Emre E, Necati E, Dursun B, Arda T. (2026). Operative time-based learning curve and surgical outcomes of laparoscopic radical prostatectomy in a surgeon with limited open surgery experience. BMC Surgery 26:184. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12893-026-03577-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

One-year biochemical recurrence showed no significant group difference; the authors note a numerical increase in later phases possibly explained by operating on higher-risk patients as experience grew.

> "PSA recurrence rates did not differ significantly among groups (p = 0.383)."
