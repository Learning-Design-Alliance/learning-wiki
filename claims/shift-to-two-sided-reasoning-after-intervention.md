## Evidence

### Iordanou 2025

Iordanou, K., Fotiou, C., Manoli, A., & Zembylas, M. (2025). Examining myside bias on a controversial historical event after engagement in dialogic argumentation: Insights from a think aloud study. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102209

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative analysis of opinion-based comments in the think-aloud protocols, discussed in the article's interpretation of students' reflection on their own position when reading the other-side account. Counts are descriptive; no statistical test is reported for this shift.

> "the think-aloud comments against the “own-side” doubled, while statements supporting their “own-side” decreased by half in the final assessment compared to the initial assessment."

### Iordanou 2025 (2)

Iordanou, K., Fotiou, C., Manoli, A., & Zembylas, M. (2025). Examining myside bias on a controversial historical event after engagement in dialogic argumentation: Insights from a think aloud study. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102209

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Case-study and count-based qualitative analysis: five pupils expressed other-side-supporting opinions at posttest (only one had at pretest) and six expressed opinions against the Greek side at posttest (one at pretest), yielding "a total of 8 pupils" with evidence of a shift.

> "In other words, a total of 8 pupils show evidence that they have shifted from one- sided to two-sided reasoning after the intervention."
