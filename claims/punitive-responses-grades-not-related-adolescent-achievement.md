# Punitive responses to lower-than-expected grades are not related to later academic achievement among low-income African American adolescents

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Punitive responses to lower-than-expected grades were not related to age 16 academic achievement in either the base model or the model controlling prior achievement. [→ Hardaway 2020](#hardaway-2020)

## Evidence

### Hardaway 2020

Hardaway, C. R., Sterrett-Hong, E. M., De Genna, N. M., & Cornelius, M. D. (2020). The Role of Cognitive Stimulation in the Home and Maternal Responses to Low Grades in Low-Income African American Adolescents’ Academic Achievement. Journal of Youth and Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-020-01217-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Path analysis across Models 1 and 2 on 226 low-income African American mother–child pairs; punitive responses showed no significant relation to age 16 achievement in either model. No effect size was printed, and the measure of punitive responses was self-reported hypothetical intent.

> "Nonpunitive responses were no longer associated with age 16 academic achievement, and, as in Model 1, punitive responses were not related to age 16 academic achievement."
