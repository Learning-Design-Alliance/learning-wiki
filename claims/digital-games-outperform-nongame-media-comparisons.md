# Digital game conditions produce significantly better learning outcomes than nongame conditions (g = 0.33) in media comparisons

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Across 209 effect sizes from 57 studies, students in digital game conditions demonstrated significantly better learning outcomes than students in nongame comparison conditions (g = 0.33, 95% CI [0.19, 0.48]). [→ Clark 2016](#clark-2016)

## Evidence

### Clark 2016

Clark, D. B., Tanner-Smith, E. E., & Killingsworth, S. S. (2016). Digital Games, Design, and Learning: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Review of Educational Research, 86(1). https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315582065

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random-effects meta-regression with robust variance estimation across 209 pairwise media-comparison effect sizes from 57 studies of K–16 students. The review reports "students in digital game conditions demonstrated significantly better outcomes overall" with g = 0.33, CI [0.19, 0.48].

> "Table 3 shows that students in digital game conditions demonstrated significantly better outcomes overall relative to students in the nongame comparison conditions (g¯ = 0.33, 95% CI [0.19, 0.48], τ2 = 0.28)."
