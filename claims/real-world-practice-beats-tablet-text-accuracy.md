# Real-world practice yields higher instrument-identification accuracy than tablet-based simulation or text-based study, with all conditions declining after one week

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Instruments were identified with greater accuracy following real-world training compared to either tablet or text-based training, and accuracy declined from immediate to delayed testing for all conditions. [→ Kryklywy James H. 2021](#kryklywy-james-h-2021)

## Evidence

### Kryklywy James H. 2021

Kryklywy James H., Roach Victoria A., Todd Rebecca M. (2021). Assessing the efficacy of tablet-based simulations for learning pseudo-surgical instrumentation. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0245330

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVA (3 training-type x 2 time) on accuracy in a 36-subject within-subject experiment; main effects of training-type (F(2,70) = 6.375, p = 0.003) and time (F(1,35) = 122.699, p < 0.001), with "greater accuracy following real-world training" and no tablet-text difference.

> "Instruments were identified with greater accuracy following real-world training compared to either tablet (pMM = 0.049) or text-based (pMM = 0.006) training. No differences in accuracy were noted between tablet and text-based training (pMM = 1.00)"
