## Evidence

### Yama Brie A. 2018

Yama Brie A., Hodgins Michael, Boydell Katherine, Schwartz Sarah B. (2018). A qualitative exploration: questioning multisource feedback in residency education. BMC Medical Education 18. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1270-7

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

From the Results section on perceived areas of expertise: a senior resident gave the example that palliative care nurses would be better placed than a senior cardiologist to give feedback on breaking bad news; "the perception of expertise was grounded in the feedback provider’s de facto clinical practice".

> "The perception of expertise was grounded in the feedback provider’s de facto clinical practice, not the presumed skills attributed to their titles or credentials."

### Yama Brie A. 2018 (2)

Yama Brie A., Hodgins Michael, Boydell Katherine, Schwartz Sarah B. (2018). A qualitative exploration: questioning multisource feedback in residency education. BMC Medical Education 18. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1270-7

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

From the Discussion: the perception of expertise was not limited to specific professions; residents named scenarios where AHCP feedback was more valued, and AHCPs identified expertise in assessing communication, a competency difficult to evaluate.

> "While the senior physician is often the ‘expert’, residents identified AHCPs as more valued sources of feedback in certain scenarios. AHCPs supported this and further identified their expertise in the assessment of residents’ communication skills."
