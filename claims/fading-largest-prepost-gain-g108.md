## Evidence

### Miller‐Cotto 2026

Miller‐Cotto, D., & Medrano, J. (2026). Does working memory moderate the effect of fading on math performance? British Journal of Educational Psychology, 96, 108–126. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12781

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Within-condition pre-post change analysis of the randomized ASSISTments homework study, using Hedges' g on pooled imputed data; pre-post differences were significant in BAU (t = 3.19, p = .007), WE (t = 3.84, p = .001), and fading (t = 3.30, p = .004) but not FSE (t = 1.72, p = .11).

> "Hedge's g effect sizes (Table 3) show, however, that the change from pretest to posttest was most significant for the Fading condition, indicating a large effect (g = 1.08), followed by the fading with self‐explanation condition (g = .93), worked example condition (g = .32) and the BAU condition (g = .24)."
