# The potentially negative oral language effect of phonological awareness plus letter knowledge training is likely an artifact of the comparison condition, not the intervention

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The WWC attributes the negative oral language rating to the comparison condition's focus on vocabulary and language comprehension rather than to the intervention itself. [→ What Works Clearinghouse 2006](#what-works-clearinghouse-2006)

## Evidence

### What Works Clearinghouse 2006

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2006). WWC Intervention Report: Phonological Awareness Training plus Letter Knowledge Training. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/InterventionReport/376

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Authors' interpretation in the effectiveness findings section: the comparison condition was a language comprehension intervention focused on increasing children's vocabulary and language comprehension, which would be expected to have a greater impact on oral language than letter-rhyme training, which was not intended to increase those skills.

> "The finding of a statistically significant and negative effect for Phonological Awareness Training plus Letter Knowledge Training in this study was most likely due to the nature of the comparison condition used, rather than an effect of the Phonological Awareness Training plus Letter Knowledge Training intervention."
