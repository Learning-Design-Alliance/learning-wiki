# The PISG and PSGI classroom script sequences showed no statistically significant difference in high- and low-level grounding and testing processes, alongside a trend favoring PISG

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There was no significant difference between the PISG and PSGI script conditions on high-level grounding and high-level testing, and the effect sizes were small. [→ Esther Tan 2018](#esther-tan-2018)
`q3 i?` Results were not significant for low-level grounding and testing, though PISG students showed lower occurrences of low-level testing with close to medium-effect size. [→ Esther Tan 2018 (2)](#esther-tan-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Esther Tan 2018

Esther Tan (2018). Effects of two differently sequenced classroom scripts on common ground in collaborative inquiry learning. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-018-9460-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Independent-samples t tests on coded frequencies from 20 groups (10 PISG, n = 32; 10 PSGI, n = 29) found "no significant difference between the two script conditions on the high-level grounding and high-level testing processes."

> "However, there was no significant difference between the two script conditions on the high-level grounding and high-level testing processes and their effect sizes were small."

### Esther Tan 2018 (2)

Esther Tan (2018). Effects of two differently sequenced classroom scripts on common ground in collaborative inquiry learning. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-018-9460-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same t-test analysis of the 20 groups' low-level regulative processes was "not significant," with PISG students showing "lower occurrences of low-level testing with close to medium-effect size"; the printed effect size value is not stated in the results text.

> "Likewise, the results were not significant for low-level grounding and low-level testing in both script conditions though students in the PISG condition showed lower occurrences of low-level testing with close to medium-effect size."
