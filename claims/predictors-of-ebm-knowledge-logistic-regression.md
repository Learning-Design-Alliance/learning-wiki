# Healthcare work experience, student research, upper study year and EBM course participation independently predict better EBM term knowledge, with EBM course participation the strongest factor (OR = 3.32)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a multifactorial logistic regression on medical students, EBM course participation (OR = 3.32), student research (OR = 2.02), upper year (OR = 1.65) and healthcare work experience (OR = 1.59) significantly predicted better term knowledge. [→ Csertő Mónika 2019](#cserto-monika-2019)

## Evidence

### Csertő Mónika 2019

Csertő Mónika, Berényi Károly, Decsi Tamás, Lohner Szimonetta. (2019). Self-reported attitudes, knowledge and skills of using evidence-based medicine in daily health care practice: A national survey among students of medicine and health sciences in Hungary. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0225641

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multifactorial logistic regression on the mean term-knowledge score (higher or lower than 2.0) of medical students. Printed odds ratios with 95% CIs as quoted; EBM course participation had the largest OR.

> "Healthcare work experience (OR = 1.59; 95% CI = 1.01–2.52, p = 0.048), conducting student research as member of the Scientific Students' Associations (OR = 2.02; 95% CI = 1.45–2.82, p<0.001), upper year university students (OR = 1.65; 95% CI = 1.37–1.98, p<0.001) and participation in an EBM teaching course (OR = 3.32; 95% CI = 2.32–4.76, p<0.001) proved to have a significant positive influence"

### Csertő Mónika 2019 (2)

Csertő Mónika, Berényi Károly, Decsi Tamás, Lohner Szimonetta. (2019). Self-reported attitudes, knowledge and skills of using evidence-based medicine in daily health care practice: A national survey among students of medicine and health sciences in Hungary. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0225641

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same logistic regression in medical students: gender and family member in healthcare were not significant predictors. Odds ratios and p-values as printed in the article.

> "while gender (OR = 1.22; 95% CI = 0.93–1.61, p = 0.15) and having a close family member working in healthcare (OR = 0.96; 95% CI = 0.73–1.26, p = 0.75) had no significant effect."

### Csertő Mónika 2019 (3)

Csertő Mónika, Berényi Károly, Decsi Tamás, Lohner Szimonetta. (2019). Self-reported attitudes, knowledge and skills of using evidence-based medicine in daily health care practice: A national survey among students of medicine and health sciences in Hungary. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0225641

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Corresponding analysis among health sciences faculty students; only upper year and EBM course participation were reported as significant factors, with odds ratios as printed.

> "Upper year university students (OR = 1.47; 95% CI = 1.05–2.05, p<0.05) and participation in an EBM teaching course (OR = 1.69; 95% CI = 1.47–1.93, p<0.001) were factors that significantly influenced EBM-related knowledge."
