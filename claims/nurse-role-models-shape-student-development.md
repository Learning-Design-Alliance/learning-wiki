## Evidence

### Kılıç Gülşen 2026

Kılıç Gülşen, Işık Sevcan Avcı, Çevik Banu. (2026). Nursing students’ first clinical encounters with delirium: a qualitative study of experiential learning and professional development. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09980-4

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Students reported integrating observed behaviors into their own practice, including adopting communication strategies they "wouldn't have thought of" independently.

> "Positive role modeling directly guided students’ clinical behavior and communication: “By watching them, I was able to behave in a similar way. When they first went in, I saw how they talked to the patients, so I acted the same way."

### Kılıç Gülşen 2026 (2)

Kılıç Gülşen, Işık Sevcan Avcı, Çevik Banu. (2026). Nursing students’ first clinical encounters with delirium: a qualitative study of experiential learning and professional development. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09980-4

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Witnessing harsh or dismissive attitudes prompted students to reflect on "what kind of nurse they wanted to become" and reconsider their professional values.

> "Conversely, witnessing dismissive or abrasive attitudes caused significant moral disappointment: “The nurses didn’t explain anything to the patient, it was really bad… They didn’t even try to talk to the patient… they were very harsh… like ‘let’s just finish and get out.’ Honestly, it made me sad.”"
