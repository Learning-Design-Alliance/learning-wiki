## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Associations of screen use and sleep with academic performance were significant in crude analyses (p < 0.001) but attenuated to non-significant in fully adjusted models (e.g., screen use OR = 1.09, 1.09, 1.08). [→ Pagerols M 2022](#pagerols-m-2022)

## Evidence

### Pagerols M 2022

Pagerols M, Prat R, Rivas C, Español-Martín G, Puigbó J, Pagespetit È, Haro JM, Ramos-Quiroga JA, Casas M, Bosch R. (2022). The impact of psychopathology on academic performance in school-age children and adolescents. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-08242-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Model 3 regressions (Tables 2–4) show frequency of screen use ORs of 1.09 (0.99–1.20), 1.09 (0.99–1.20), and 1.08 (0.98–1.19) and sleep ORs near 1, all non-significant; the article reports the associations "attenuated to a non-significant level" after full adjustment.

> "those who were more frequently exposed to screens had higher risk for underachievement, although these associations attenuated to a non-significant level when the influence of psychopathology, sociodemographic factors, stressful events, and lifestyle variables was taken into account"
