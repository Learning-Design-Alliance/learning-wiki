# ACCESS produces a moderate improvement in self-reported daily functioning relative to delayed treatment among college students with ADHD

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` The rate of change in AIM-A Performance/Daily Functioning was significantly higher in the ACCESS group (d = .72), with the delayed condition showing no change. [→ Eddy et al. 2021](#eddy-et-al-2021)

## Evidence

### Eddy et al. 2021

Laura D. Eddy, Arthur D. Anastopoulos, Melissa R. Dvorsky, Paul J. Silvia, Jeffrey D. Labban & Joshua M. Langberg (2021). An RCT of a CBT Intervention for Emerging Adults with ADHD Attending College: Functional Outcomes. Journal of Clinical Child & Adolescent Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1080/15374416.2020.1867989

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Latent growth curve analysis of the AIM-A Performance/Daily Functioning scale in the RCT showed a moderate-to-large between-condition effect ("d = .72 [.46, .97]") favoring immediate ACCESS.

> "the immediate ACCESS (b = 18.01, SE = 2.01, p < .001) condition significantly increased in overall functioning over time, but the DTC (b = 2.30, SE = 1.92, p = .230) condition did not change. The rate of change was significantly higher in the ACCESS group, Wald (1) = 36.43, p < .001, d = .72 [.46, .97]."
