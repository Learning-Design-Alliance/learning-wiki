## Evidence

### Omar Alsaiari 2026

Omar Alsaiari, Nilufar Baghaei, Jason M. Lodge, Omid Noroozi, Dragan Gašević, Marie Boden, Hassan Khosravi. (2026). DIRECTIVE, METACOGNITIVE OR A BLEND OF BOTH? A COMPARISON OF AI-GENERATED FEEDBACK TYPES ON STUDENT ENGAGEMENT, CONFIDENCE, AND OUTCOMES. A PREPRINT. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1016/j.caeai.2026.100553](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeai.2026.100553)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Binomial GLMM (logit link) on binary revision outcomes in the RCT, with student ID as random intercept; the overall effect of feedback condition was significant, χ2(2) = 8.74, p=.013.

> "Students in the Metacognitive condition acted least often (12.1%), significantly less than those in both the Directive (21.1%) and Hybrid (27.5%) conditions, whereas rates did not differ significantly between the Directive and Hybrid conditions"

### Omar Alsaiari 2026 (2)

Omar Alsaiari, Nilufar Baghaei, Jason M. Lodge, Omid Noroozi, Dragan Gašević, Marie Boden, Hassan Khosravi. (2026). DIRECTIVE, METACOGNITIVE OR A BLEND OF BOTH? A COMPARISON OF AI-GENERATED FEEDBACK TYPES ON STUDENT ENGAGEMENT, CONFIDENCE, AND OUTCOMES. A PREPRINT. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1016/j.caeai.2026.100553](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeai.2026.100553)

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Tukey-adjusted pairwise contrasts from the binomial GLMM. The Hybrid group also showed significantly greater action on feedback than Metacognitive (about 4.3 times higher odds; 95% CI [1.82, 10.0], p< .001); Directive vs Hybrid was not significant (OR = 0.69, p=.484).

> "students in the Directive group were nearly three times as likely to revise compared to those in the Metacognitive group (OR = 2.93, 95% CI [1.25, 6.88], p=.009 )"

### Omar Alsaiari 2026 (3)

Omar Alsaiari, Nilufar Baghaei, Jason M. Lodge, Omid Noroozi, Dragan Gašević, Marie Boden, Hassan Khosravi. (2026). DIRECTIVE, METACOGNITIVE OR A BLEND OF BOTH? A COMPARISON OF AI-GENERATED FEEDBACK TYPES ON STUDENT ENGAGEMENT, CONFIDENCE, AND OUTCOMES. A PREPRINT. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1016/j.caeai.2026.100553](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeai.2026.100553)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

First-Order Markov Models of platform task-flow traces (Question, QuestionDetails, Options, SelfAssessment, Submission) per feedback group, with edges below probability .05 suppressed. The authors state the denser Hybrid editing loops align with its higher action on feedback.

> "16% of transitions from feedback led back to theQuestion and 10% to theOptions, compared with only 7% and 13% respectively in the Directive group, and 2% and 8% in the Metacognitive group."
