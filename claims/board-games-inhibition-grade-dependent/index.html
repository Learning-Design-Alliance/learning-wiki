Evidence¶

Nuria Vita-Barrull 2024¶

Nuria Vita-Barrull, Verónica Estrada-Plana, Jaume March-Llanes, Pablo Sotoca-Orgaz, Núria Guzmán, Rosa Ayesa, Jorge Moya-Higueras. (2024). Do you play in class? Board games to promote cognitive and educational development in primary school: A cluster randomized controlled trial. Learning and Instruction, 93. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.101946

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Mixed-model analysis of the first-grade subsample of the cluster-randomised trial. Play-group inhibition reaction time fell from 155.16 to 38.36 while the control rose from −15.46 to 179.71, a significant time-group effect.

"Also, inhibition time-group effects were found in this sample (F = 13.01; p = 0.00). The average reaction time reduced in the experimental group but increased in the control group."

Nuria Vita-Barrull 2024 (2)¶

Nuria Vita-Barrull, Verónica Estrada-Plana, Jaume March-Llanes, Pablo Sotoca-Orgaz, Núria Guzmán, Rosa Ayesa, Jorge Moya-Higueras. (2024). Do you play in class? Board games to promote cognitive and educational development in primary school: A cluster randomized controlled trial. Learning and Instruction, 93. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.101946

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Mixed-model analysis of the second-to-sixth-grade subsample of the cluster-randomised trial. Inhibition error and efficiency measures showed no effects, and flexibility efficiency improved in both the play and control groups, so no group advantage.

"No time, group or time-group effects were found on the percentage of errors or the inhibition efficiency. In the flexibility task, only a significant effect was seen on efficiency, which improved in both groups (F = 13.36; p = 0.00)."
