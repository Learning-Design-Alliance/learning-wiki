## Evidence

### Stefan Küchemann 2023

Stefan Küchemann, Steffen Steinert, Natalia Revenga, Matthias Schweinberger, Yavuz Dinc, Karina E. Avila, and Jochen Kuhn. (2023). Physics task development of prospective physics teachers using ChatGPT. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020128](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020128)

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r1`

Group comparison of rated task quality in the randomized controlled trial found a statistically significant clarity difference between the ChatGPT and textbook conditions, "with a small eﬀect size of Cohen’s d = 0.38"; the article reports p = 0.04 for this contrast.

> "Apart from that, we found signiﬁcant diﬀerences in task clarity (p=0.04) with a small eﬀect size of Cohen’s d = 0.38"

### Stefan Küchemann 2023 (2)

Stefan Küchemann, Steffen Steinert, Natalia Revenga, Matthias Schweinberger, Yavuz Dinc, Karina E. Avila, and Jochen Kuhn. (2023). Physics task development of prospective physics teachers using ChatGPT. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020128](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020128)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

The same group comparison found a large difference in how often tasks were embedded in meaningful context: "task context (p = 6 · 10−5) with a large eﬀect size of d = 1.04 between the two groups", and "In both cases, students who worked with the textbook achieved higher scores."

> "and task context (p = 6 · 10−5) with a large eﬀect size of d = 1.04 between the two groups. In both cases, students who worked with the textbook achieved higher scores."
