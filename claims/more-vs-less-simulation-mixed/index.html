Evidence¶

Prothero 2026¶

Prothero, L., Abdulmohdi, N., Shaw, S., Edmonds, M., and Meads, C. (2026). The Effectiveness of Simulation‐Based Versus Patient‐Based Clinical Education in Nursing: A Systematic Review and Meta‐Analysis. Nursing Open 13, no. 9: e70689. https://doi.org/10.1002/nop2.70689

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Three-arm RCT embedded in the review comparing 50%, 25% and 10% simulation across a full course: the 50% group scored highest on the Global Assessment of Clinical Competency and Readiness for Practice (8.23 vs 7.78 and 7.83, p = 0.001).

"50% (n = 154) versus 25% (165) versus 10% (153) Global Assessment of Clinical Competency and Readiness for Practice (mean [SD]) 8.23 (1.1) 7.78 (1.14) 7.83 (1.3) 50% > 25% and 10%, p = 0.001"

Prothero 2026 (2)¶

Prothero, L., Abdulmohdi, N., Shaw, S., Edmonds, M., and Meads, C. (2026). The Effectiveness of Simulation‐Based Versus Patient‐Based Clinical Education in Nursing: A Systematic Review and Meta‐Analysis. Nursing Open 13, no. 9: e70689. https://doi.org/10.1002/nop2.70689

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Same trial, different measure: on Fundamentals of Nursing Final CCEI Clinical Ratings the 25% and 10% groups outperformed the 50% group (97.1 and 96.4 vs 93.9, p = 0.001); NCLEX first-time pass rates were reported as NS.

"50% (n = 230) versus 25% (268) versus 10% (216) Fundamentals of Nursing Final CCEI Clinical Ratings (mean [SD]) 93.9 (14.4) 97.1 (6.2) 96.4 (7.6) 25% + 10% > 50%, p = 0.001"
