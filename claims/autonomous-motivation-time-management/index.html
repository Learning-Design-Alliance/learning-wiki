Evidence¶

Christopher A. Wolters 2025¶

Christopher A. Wolters, Anna C. Brady, Hyun Ji Lee. (2025). Time Management and Achievement Motivation: A Review of What We Know and Directions for Where to Go. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10032-4

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports de Bilde et al.'s study of Flemish high school and college students (N = 275) using the LASSI, finding "moderately positive correlations of time management with intrinsic (r = 0.41) and identified (r = 0.36) motivation." Similar positive correlations appear in Turkish, Chinese, and other samples.

"Findings indicated moderately positive correlations of time management with intrinsic (r = 0.41) and identified (r = 0.36) motivation for studying, a negative correlation with external motivation for studying (r = −0.22), and no relation with introjected regulation."

Christopher A. Wolters 2025 (2)¶

Christopher A. Wolters, Anna C. Brady, Hyun Ji Lee. (2025). Time Management and Achievement Motivation: A Review of What We Know and Directions for Where to Go. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10032-4

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports Vansteenkiste et al.'s two-sample study of Chinese students in Belgium, in which autonomous motivation positively predicted time management (β = 0.22, 0.52) and controlled motivation negatively (β = −0.45, −0.22), with autonomous motivation fully mediating parental autonomy support effects.

"These authors also reported that students' autonomous (vs. controlled) study motivation fully mediated the positive prediction of parents' autonomy support (vs. psychologically controlling parenting) for students' learning attitudes, including time management."
