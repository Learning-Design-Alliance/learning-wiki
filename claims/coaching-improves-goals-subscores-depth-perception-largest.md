# Depth perception, bimanual dexterity, tissue handling, and efficiency scores rose from native to video-teaching sequences in one group measured before and after, with the largest rise in depth perception and the smallest in efficiency

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Scores across skills rose from native to video-teaching sequences by 0.28 to 0.62 in one group measured before and after, with the highest rise in depth of perception (0.62, 95%CI: 0.47–0.77, P < 0.001) and the lowest rise in efficiency (0.28, 95%CI: 0.08–0.48, P = 0.006). ## Evidence

### Chatziisaak D 2024

Chatziisaak D, Sparn M, Krstic D, Bauci G, Warschkow R, Brunner W, Schmied B, Hahnloser D, Bischofberger S. (2024). Be prepared! Impact of structured video-assisted coaching on performance in a simulated bleeding exercise during laparoscopic surgery. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-024-11173-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Mixed-effects model comparing coaching sequences (3 and 4) against native sequences (1 and 2) in the 54-video Davos Course dataset; the analysis shows effects "from 0.28 to 0.62" across the five subscales with printed confidence intervals and P values.

> "This positive effect ranges from 0.28 to 0.62, with the highest positive impact observed in depth of perception (0.62 95%CI: 0.47–0.77, P < 0.001) and the lowest impact in efficiency (0.28 95%CI: 0.08–0.48, P = 0.006)"

### Chatziisaak D 2024 (2)

Table 1 mixed-effects analysis of mean (SD) GOALS subscores across sequences 1–4; depth of perception rose from 2.56 (0.97) native to 3.37 (0.80), with P values adjusted for false discovery rate. All subscales except autonomy improved.
