## Evidence

### Zeng Zhaolan 2026

Zeng Zhaolan, Shi Zeyao, Hou Shulin, Yan Jing, Yang Ru, Li Xiaowen. (2026). Comprehensive effects and dose–response relationship of screen exposure on university students' physical and mental health and academic performance in the digital age: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-026-27025-9

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 9 studies assessing physical pain via self-report; after excluding 3 studies contributing substantial heterogeneity (I2 = 41.70%), the association remained significant (OR = 1.70; 95%CI: 1.31–2.20).

> "The meta-analysis on physical pain (9 studies, 4654 university students) showed that the risk of physical pain increased with longer daily screen time (OR = 1.60; 95%CI: 1.19–2.15; I2 = 84.50%)."

### Zeng Zhaolan 2026 (2)

Zeng Zhaolan, Shi Zeyao, Hou Shulin, Yan Jing, Yang Ru, Li Xiaowen. (2026). Comprehensive effects and dose–response relationship of screen exposure on university students' physical and mental health and academic performance in the digital age: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-026-27025-9

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Restricted cubic spline dose–response analysis of seven studies (3,544 students) showing a U-shaped curve; risk decreased slowly below 1.6 h/day and rose continuously above it. No standardized effect size is printed for this finding.

> "Notably, beyond 3 h of daily screen time, the risk of physical pain increased significantly, with each additional hour of screen exposure associated with a more than 5% increase in the risk of physical pain."
