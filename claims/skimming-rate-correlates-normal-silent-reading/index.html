Whipple 1914¶

Whipple, G. M., & Curtis, J. N. (1914). Preliminary Investigation of Skimming in Reading. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=preliminary+investigation+of+skimming+in+reading

q2 · i3 · causal · r1

Auxiliary Experiment 3 computed Pearson correlations for reading speed across the five methods (Table 11 lists normal silent and skimming r = .71, P.E. .16). The article reports the greatest correlation was between skimming rate and normal silent reading rate.

"Preferred rate in skimming is fairly closely correlated with natural rate in ordinary reading—the coefficient of correlation between normal silent and skimming speeds is +0.71."

  • Individual differences in skimming speed are large even among college-trained readers, with one observer skimming nearly three times the pace of another — related

  • Instructed slow reading ran about 40% below normal rate and rapid reading about 16% below normal time, fairly uniformly across groups except seventh graders — related
