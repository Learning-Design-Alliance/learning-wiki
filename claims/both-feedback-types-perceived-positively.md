# Both instructor and AI-generated feedback were perceived positively, with instructor feedback rated higher descriptively on all four perception dimensions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Descriptive results showed both feedback types were perceived positively, with the instructor-feedback group rating feedback higher on fairness (M = 4.59 vs 4.25), usefulness (4.39 vs 3.77), development (4.17 vs 3.99) and encouragement (4.53 vs 4.22). [→ Er 2024](#er-2024)

## Evidence

### Er 2024

Er, Erkan; Akçapınar, Gökhan; Bayazıt, Alper; Noroozi, Omid; Banihashem, Seyyed Kazem. (2024). Assessing student perceptions and use of instructor versus AI-generated feedback. British Journal of Educational Technology. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13558

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive statistics from the perception survey (17 instructor, 16 AI students). All mean ratings were above the scale midpoint for both groups; instructor-feedback means were higher on every dimension, though these descriptive differences were not all statistically tested as significant.

> "According to descriptive results, both instructor and AI feedback were perceived positively by students. Students who received instructor feedback (ie, group 1) consistently considered feedback more fair ( M = 4.59, SD = 0.46), useful ( M = 4.39, SD = 0.79), developmental ( M = 4.17, SD = 0.84) and encouraging ( M = 4.53, SD = 0.65) compared to students who received AI feedback"
