## Evidence

### Al Otaiba 2006

Al Otaiba, S., & Fuchs, D. (2006). Who Are the Young Children for Whom Best Practices in Reading Are Ineffective? An Experimental and Longitudinal Study. Journal of Learning Disabilities, 39(5), 414-431. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00222194060390050401

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

MANOVA of 104 children classified as nonresponsive (n = 34), sometimes responsive (n = 26), or always responsive (n = 44) on nine pretreatment and posttreatment measures. The "multivariate main effect for respon- siveness status" was significant, with no main effect for intervention and no interaction.

> "The Wilks’ Λ test statistic indicated a statistically significant multivariate main effect for respon- siveness status, F(9, 164) = 5.29 p < .001; absence of a main effect for interven- tion, F(27, 240.13) = .76, ns; and absence of an interaction between student re- sponsiveness status and intervention, F(54, 422.71) = 1.00, ns."
