Evidence¶

Financial Incentives for Teen Parents to Stay in School 2006¶

Financial Incentives for Teen Parents to Stay in School. (2006). What Works Clearinghouse Intervention Report, U.S. Department of Education. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Randomized controlled trial of Cal-Learn (met standards with reservations due to attrition); the WWC-calculated effect size for this dropout contrast was 0.19, statistically significant, improvement index +7. The report states "44.7% of Cal-Learn students dropped out compared with 52.3% of control group students."

"The Cal-Learn study reported that for teens 18 years of age and older, 44.7% of Cal-Learn students dropped out compared with 52.3% of control group students. The WWC confirmed that this difference was statistically significant."

Financial Incentives for Teen Parents to Stay in School 2006 (2)¶

Financial Incentives for Teen Parents to Stay in School. (2006). What Works Clearinghouse Intervention Report, U.S. Department of Education. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Randomized controlled trial of Ohio's LEAP program meeting WWC standards; survey of 913 respondents three years after random assignment. The dropout effect size was 0.12, not significant, improvement index +5; equivalence was not tested.

"The LEAP study reported that three years after random assignment, 48.4% of LEAP teens dropped out compared with 53.5% of control group teens, but the difference was not statistically significant."
