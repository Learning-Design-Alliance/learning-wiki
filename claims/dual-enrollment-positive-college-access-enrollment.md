# Dual enrollment programs have positive effects on college access and enrollment, with an average improvement index of +15 percentile points

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Four studies meeting WWC group design standards found statistically significant positive effects on college enrollment rates and plans to attend a 4-year college. [→ What Works Clearinghouse 2017](#what-works-clearinghouse-2017)

## Evidence

### What Works Clearinghouse 2017

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2017). Dual Enrollment Programs. WWC Intervention Report, Transition to College. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/671

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Systematic review synthesis of four studies including 67,474 students. Table 1 reports an average improvement index of +15 percentile points (range +12 to +19) for college access and enrollment, based on enrollment rates and plans to attend a 4-year college; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Thus, for the college access and enrollment domain, four studies found statistically significant positive effects. Dual enrollment programs have a positive effect on college access and enrollment with a medium to large extent of evidence."
