# In the Forward temporal model, initial enjoyment predicted later engagement, while initial boredom did not and emotions were unrelated to later raw performance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Enjoyment at T1 predicted engagement at T6 (β = .26), but boredom at T1 did not predict later engagement (p = .45), and emotions at T1 were unrelated to later raw performance (ps > .22). [→ Chevalère 2023](#chevalere-2023)

## Evidence

### Chevalère 2023

Chevalère, J., Yun, H. S., Henke, A., Pinkwart, N., Hafner, V., & Lazarides, R. (2023). A sequence of learning processes in an intelligent tutoring system from topic-related appraisals to learning gains. Learning and Instruction, 87, 101799. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2023.101799

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Forward path model on first-session data (T1 to T6, N = 140), assuming temporal precedence of emotions; it fit well (CFI = .992) and reproduced the sequence interest → enjoyment T1 → engagement T6 → final performance → learning gains.

> "Students’ initial enjoyment (T1) predicted their later engagement after sixty minutes (T6), β = .26, SE = .09, t = 2.92, p = .003 [.08; .42], but t he initial boredom (T1) did not predict later engagement (T6), p = .45"
