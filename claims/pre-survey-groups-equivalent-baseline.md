# Simulation and control groups were comparable on prior learning experiences and motivation before the intervention

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The groups did not differ significantly on watching the educational movie (p = .86), previous role-playing experience (p = .90), encountering an MSK patient (p = .50), motivation for physical examination (p = .095), or motivation for clinical reasoning (p = .079). ## Evidence

### Yamauchi Kazuyo 2021

Yamauchi Kazuyo, Hagiwara Yoko, Iwakura Nahoko, Kubo Saori, Sato Azusa, Ohtsuru Tadahiko, Okazaki Ken, Okubo Yumiko. (2021). Using peer role-playing to improve students’ clinical skills for musculoskeletal physical examinations. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-021-02742-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre-survey of prior learning status (Pearson's chi-square test) and motivation (Fisher's exact test, scales as printed in Tables 1 and 2) compared across simulation (N = 64) and control (N = 26) groups; the article states "The pre-learning status and motivation were similar between the simulation group and the control group."

> "There were no significant differences between the simulation group and the control group for watching the educational movie (p > .05, p = .86), previous role-playing experience (p > .05, p = .90), encountering an MSK patient (p > .05, p = .50)."
