Related Claims¶

  • Computerized cognitive training produces a small but statistically significant improvement in cognitive performance in healthy older adults — related
  • Multitasking training transferred to untrained working memory and sustained attention tasks in older adults, unlike control training — reports the opposite
  • Training benefits did not extend to processing speed tasks, indicating specificity to cognitive control processes — related
  • Multitasking training on adaptive NeuroRacer reduced older adults' multitasking costs beyond active and no-contact controls, exceeding untrained 20-year-olds, with gains persisting 6 months — reports the opposite
  • Casual games show weak to no cognitive benefits compared to other interventions, except for elderly adults and people with specific cognitive impairments — related
  • Cognitive benefits of balance training do not differ by age in adults aged 19–65 — related
  • Speed-of-processing training based on UFOV tasks reduces minimum duration thresholds in older adults, with benefits persisting up to two years — related
  • Speed-of-processing training transfers to health and functional outcomes including reduced crash risk, improved driving, faster daily-task completion, slower health-related quality-of-life decline, and reduced depression risk — related
  • Explicit transfer instructions, in which trainees discuss how trained strategies could apply to a new task, produced reliable transfer of imagery and sentence-generation training to unpracticed text learning in older adults — related
  • Video-game play and mind-brain training literatures suggest the possibility of training selective attention — related
  • Working memory training improvement decomposes into an early task-specific process accounting for 44% of the total increase and a slower capacity improvement — related
  • Training effects are selective: active control improved processing efficiency but not executive function, and uni/multimodal interventions improved executive function but not processing efficiency — related
  • Multitasking training benefits do not transfer to untrained action control tasks (PRP, Stroop, AB, single response selection, Go-Nogo, Flanker) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Classic cognitive training tasks improved reaction time, processing speed, working memory, executive function, memory, visual spatial ability, and attention in older adults — reports the opposite
  • Computerized cognitive training interventions improve cognitive performance in cognitively healthy older adults across three program types — related
  • Video games improved reaction time, processing speed, executive function, and global cognition in older adults, with largest effects on reaction time and processing speed — related
  • Working memory training in older adults improved trained tasks and transferred to non-trained working memory measures — related
