# In mixed-gender pairs in an introductory physics lab, female students on average handle the equipment no more or less than an equal share of the time (40±6%, not statistically different from 50%)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A one-sample t-test found the average fraction of time female students used the equipment (40% ± 6%) was not statistically different from 50%. [→ Natasha G. Holmes 2019](#natasha-g-holmes-2019)

## Evidence

### Natasha G. Holmes 2019

Natasha G. Holmes, Ido Roll, and Douglas A. Bonn. (2019). Participating in the Physics Lab: Does Gender Matter? Physics in Canada 70(2). Originally published 2014. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1905.03331](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1905.03331)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Observational study of 37 mixed-gender pairs in a first-year honours physics lab during a mass-on-a-spring experiment; a researcher swept the room every five minutes coding which member handled the equipment. The one-sample t-test showed "not statistically different from 50%".

> "on average, the female students handled the equipment 40% + 6% of the time, which was not statistically different from 50% through a one-sample t-test: t(36) = —1.66, p = 0.106."
