# A brief training intervention increases law students' self-reported use of an LLM in an issue-spotting examination (41.38% vs 26.32%)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among participants with identical LLM access, those who received training reported using DeepSeek at a significantly higher rate than untrained participants (41.38% vs 26.32%, p = 0.044). [→ Benjamin M. Chen and Hong Bao 2026](#benjamin-m-chen-and-hong-bao-2026)

## Evidence

### Benjamin M. Chen and Hong Bao 2026

Benjamin M. Chen and Hong Bao. (2026). Training for Technology: Adoption and Productive Use of Generative AI in Legal Analysis. [doi:10.2139/ssrn.6217459](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6217459)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized between-subjects mock examination with 164 law students; self-reported use was a binary post-examination questionnaire item. The trained group's adoption rate of 41.38% exceeded the untrained group's 26.32%, a "difference of 15.06% points is statistically significant (p = 0.044*, one-tailed z-test)". No effect size printed.

> "Of the 57 participants in Group 2, 15 declared that they used DeepSeek during the examination, giving an adoption rate of 26.32%. By comparison, 24 of 58 participants in Group 3 declared such use, giving an adoption rate of 41.38%."
