# In a single-group pre-post study, self-rated self-directed learning ability scores rose significantly among BA pragmatists and theorists, while no significant pre-post rise was reported among BA activists and reflectors

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the BA group, self-rated self-directed learning ability scores rose significantly from pre-test to post-test among pragmatist and theorist learning styles at the .01 level, while no significant pre-post rise was reported among activist and reflector learning styles, in a single-group pre-post study. [→ Orawiwatnakul 2011](#orawiwatnakul-2011)

## Evidence

### Orawiwatnakul 2011

Orawiwatnakul, W., & Wichadee, S. (2011). A Comparison Of Students' Outcomes In Two Classes: Business Administration Students VS Communication Arts Students Based On Self-Directed Learning Activities. Contemporary Issues In Education Research, 4(5). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1072831

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Learning-style subgroup t-tests of Guglielmino SDL questionnaire scores for the BA group (pragmatist t=3.63, theorist t=3.92, both p=.00; activist p=.07, reflector p=.09, non-significant). The article reports "only two learning styles namely pragmatist and theorist in the group of BA students developed their self-directed ability"; no effect sizes are printed.

> "It was found that only two learning styles namely pragmatist and theorist in the group of BA students developed their self-directed ability at a significance level of .01 while the two learning styles namely activist and reflector did not improve their self-directed learning ability."
