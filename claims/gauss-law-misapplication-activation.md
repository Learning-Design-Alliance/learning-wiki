# Students frequently misapplied Gauss's Law when direct integration was required, and requiring a sketch of the charge distribution was associated with no Gauss's Law attempts

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Wilcox 2013

Wilcox, B. R., Caballero, M. D., Rehn, D. A., & Pollock, S. J. (2013). ACER: An Analytic Framework for Students' Use of Mathematics in Upper-Division Physics. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.9.020119](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.9.020119)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Exam analysis (N=172) of element CA3, which eliminates Gauss's Law as a valid approach. The article reports "11% of 172" attempted Gauss's Law, an inappropriate use consistent with previous junior-level research.

> "Approximately a tenth of our students (11% of 172) attempted to employ Gauss' Law to solve for E and then to calculate V by taking line integral."

### Wilcox 2013 (2)

Wilcox, B. R., Caballero, M. D., Rehn, D. A., & Pollock, S. J. (2013). ACER: An Analytic Framework for Students' Use of Mathematics in Upper-Division Physics. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.9.020119](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.9.020119)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cross-semester comparison within the exam corpus: the one semester (N=25) whose prompt asked for a sketch rather than total charge produced no Gauss's Law attempts. The article suggests calculation of total charge likely activated resources associated with Gauss's Law; this is an observational association, not a tested manipulation.

> "Interestingly, none of the students in the single semester (N=25) that were asked to sketch the charge distribution rather than to calculate total charge attempted to use Gauss's Law."

### Wilcox 2013 (3)

Wilcox, B. R., Caballero, M. D., Rehn, D. A., & Pollock, S. J. (2013). ACER: An Analytic Framework for Students' Use of Mathematics in Upper-Division Physics. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.9.020119](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.9.020119)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Think-aloud interview set (N=10 across two protocols) probing Activation. Misapplication of Gauss's Law was the primary issue observed; two students in the second set explicitly considered Coulomb's Law but rejected it in favor of Gauss's Law or the point-charge field expression.

> "The misapplication of Gauss's Law was also the primary issue observed in the interviews. Even when the students were explicitly prompted to use direct integra- tion, one of ﬁve students still attempted to use Gauss's Law."
