## Evidence

### Knox S 2015

Knox S, Cullen W, Dunne CP. (2015). A national study of Continuous Professional Competence (CPC) amongst pre-hospital practitioners. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-015-1197-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey ratings of a pre-determined list of CPC activities (Table 2). Practical training scenarios were rated relevant by 94% of Paramedics/APs and 83% of EMTs; simulation manikin training by 88% and 93% respectively, and e-learning followed by related practice by 92% and 91%.

> "Most respondents considered practical type learning relevant to their roles (Figure 5 and Table 2): practical training scenarios, EMT 83 % (266), Paramedic/Advanced Paramedic 94 % (582)"

### Knox S 2015 (2)

Knox S, Cullen W, Dunne CP. (2015). A national study of Continuous Professional Competence (CPC) amongst pre-hospital practitioners. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-015-1197-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey results on least-relevant CPC activities. "e-learning modules only and no related practice" drew the highest 'not relevant' responses (36% Paramedics/APs, 32% EMTs), followed by project work and appraisal of journal publications (24% and 20%).

> "The activities that received the highest ‘not relevant’ responses were: ‘e-learning modules only and no related practice EMT 32 % (101), Paramedics/APs 36 % (210); project work EMT16% (50), Paramedics/APs 27 % (166)"
