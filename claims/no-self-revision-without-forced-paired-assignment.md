# Students did not show evidence of self-revising when asked to write short first drafts without a forced paired revision assignment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When students were asked to write a 125-word essay as a first draft rather than a revision, the resulting essays showed little coherence, indicating students do not revise unless explicitly forced to by a paired assignment. [→ Hermsen 2006](#hermsen-2006)

## Evidence

### Hermsen 2006

Hermsen, L. M., & Franklin, S. V. (2006). Realizing a New Research Agenda for Writing-to-Learn: Embedding Process in Context. Manuscript submission to Educational Researcher. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0609070](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0609070)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

A follow-up task in the revision-based assessment: students wrote a 125-word first-draft essay on a density prediction prompt with a more specific prompt. The sample essay (Fig. 7) showed no transitions building coherence, relying on simplistic repetition. This is a qualitative observation with no printed statistics.

> "Our students, therefore, did not show evidence of learning how to self-revise and write reflectively without an assignment that forced them to."
