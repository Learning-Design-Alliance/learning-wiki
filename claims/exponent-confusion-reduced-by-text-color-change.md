# A presentation change — rendering the randomized exponent on the interaction constant in black rather than WebAssign's default red — reduced exponent-confusion errors from 30.5% to 9.1% between assignment versions.

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After the exponent's text color was changed to black in Version 3, the frequency of students misreading the length-unit exponent on k as scientific notation dropped from 30.5% to 9.1%. [→ Caballero 2012](#caballero-2012)

## Evidence

### Caballero 2012

Caballero, M. D., Schatz, M. F., and Kohlmyer, M. A. (2012). Implementing and assessing computational modeling in introductory mechanics. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1107.5216](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1107.5216)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Cross-version observational comparison of erroneous programs: in Version 2 the randomized exponent on the length unit of k was colored red (WebAssign's default for random values), leading students to misread 0.1 Nm3 as 0.1E3 = 100; recoloring the text in Version 3 coincided with the drop from 30.5% to 9.1%.

> "In Version 3 of the assignment, we changed the exponent's text color to black like the rest of the non-random text. The overall frequency of this mistake dropped from 30.5% to 9.1%."
