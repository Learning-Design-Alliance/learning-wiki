# Same-construct memory task correlations exceed different-construct correlations

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Correlations among tasks thought to measure the same memory construct (STM or WM) were larger than correlations between tasks measuring different constructs. [→ Robert Kail and Lynda K. Hall 2001](#robert-kail-and-lynda-k-hall-2001)

## Evidence

### Robert Kail and Lynda K. Hall 2001

Robert Kail and Lynda K. Hall. (2001). Distinguishing short-term memory from working memory. Memory & Cognition, 29(1), 1-9. https://link.springer.com/journal/13421/29/1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of the correlation matrices (Table 1) from Study 1 (N = 155) and Study 2 (N = 142) showed all measures correlated positively, with same-construct rs of ".36 to .47, with a median of .45" versus different-construct rs with a median of .26. All Study 1 rs were significant at p < .05.

> "the 5 rs from tasks involving the same memory construct ranged from .36 to .47, with a median of .45; the 10 rs from tasks involving different memory con- structs ranged from .19 to .32, with a median of .26."
