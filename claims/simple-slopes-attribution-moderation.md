# Simple slope analyses show negative life events predict more problem behaviors when the relevant external attribution tendency is higher

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2025 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` At higher high-achievement external attribution, negative life events led to more internalizing behaviors (b = 0.710 vs b = 0.683 at lower levels). [→ Yi Da 2025](#yi-da-2025)
`q2 i?` At higher low-achievement external attribution, negative life events led to more externalizing behaviors (b = 0.511 vs b = 0.457 at lower levels). [→ Yi Da 2025 (2)](#yi-da-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Yi Da 2025

Yi Da, Xu Xing, Zhang Qingqi, Ma Ai, Liu Xiaoqian, Lian Cheng. (2025). The moderating role of academic achievement attribution in shaping the impact of negative life events on adolescent problem behaviors and school dropout tendency. BMC Psychology 13. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-025-03650-8

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Pick-a-point simple slope analysis at mean ± 1 SD of the moderator, shown in Fig. 4: the slope was "b = 0.710" at high moderator levels versus b = 0.683 at low levels, both p < 0.001.

> "when an individual’s high academic achievement with an external attribution tendency is higher, negative life events lead to more internalizing problem behaviors (b = 0.710, p < 0.001)"

### Yi Da 2025 (2)

Yi Da, Xu Xing, Zhang Qingqi, Ma Ai, Liu Xiaoqian, Lian Cheng. (2025). The moderating role of academic achievement attribution in shaping the impact of negative life events on adolescent problem behaviors and school dropout tendency. BMC Psychology 13. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-025-03650-8

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Simple slope analysis shown in Fig. 5: the slope was "b = 0.511" at high moderator levels versus b = 0.457 at low levels, both p < 0.001.

> "when an individual’s low academic achievement with an external attribution tendency is higher, negative life events result in more externalizing problem behaviors (b = 0.511, p < 0.001)"
