`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Qualitative finding from the second DBR cycle, based on trainee interviews, mentor accounts and observed telementoring sessions. Trainees reported that mediastinal POCUS positioning was harder — "You think you're positioning the probe correctly, but the image doesn't come out as easily" — and the setup required the child lying down with the head tilted back, whereas lung POCUS could be done with the child seated on a caregiver's lap.

> "Over time, the participants encountered a ceiling to what their collaborative scaffolding could achieve through telementoring alone. Remote support for lung POCUS was described as “a productive struggle” (C2FNt), where challenges were overcome and deepened learning. In contrast, competency development for mediastinal POCUS proved less responsive to telementored feedback, primarily due to higher psychomotor demands"
