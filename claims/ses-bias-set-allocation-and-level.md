## Evidence

### Doyle 2023

Doyle, L., Easterbrook, M. J., & Harris, P. R. (2023). Roles of socioeconomic status, ethnicity and teacher beliefs in academic grading. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 93, 91–112. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12541

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

In the same 2×2 experiment, set allocation scores were reverse-coded so higher numbers indicate a more advanced ability grouping. The quote reports the significant SES main effect on set allocation; the ethnicity effect was not significant (p = .562).

> "students of lower SES were allocated to lower sets than were those from higher SES backgrounds (p < .001; η2 = .03; Figure 2)"

### Doyle 2023 (2)

Doyle, L., Easterbrook, M. J., & Harris, P. R. (2023). Roles of socioeconomic status, ethnicity and teacher beliefs in academic grading. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 93, 91–112. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12541

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Perceived level (below/at/above expected standard) showed a significant SES main effect in the same experiment. A multinomial logistic regression confirmed the odds of being perceived at or above the expected standard were significantly greater for higher-SES students (OR = .58, p = .021; OR = .18, p < .001).

> "teachers perceived students of lower SES to be working at an inferior level to those from higher SES backgrounds (p < .001; η2 = .06; Figure 3)"
