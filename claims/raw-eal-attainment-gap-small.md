## Evidence

### Whiteside 2017

Whiteside, Katie E.; Gooch, Debbie; Norbury, Courtenay F. (2017). English Language Proficiency and Early School Attainment Among Children Learning English as an Additional Language. Child Development, 88(3), 812-827. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.12615

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Chi-square comparisons on national assessment outcomes (Table 2): good level of development 59% vs 45% (χ2(1) = 54.46, φ = .09); on target Year 2 86% vs 82% (φ = .03); above target 31% vs 23% (φ = .05). The article notes "all effects were small."

> "children with EAL were significantly less likely than monolingual children to achieve a good level of development in reception year and perform on target, or above target, in Year 2 assessments. However, all effects were small."
