## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For matched students, the average shift in BEMA score from post-Physics II to post-Phys 301 was 0±2%, statistically indistinguishable from zero, and the shift from Phys 301 to Phys 302 was -0.3±2 points, also indistinguishable from zero. [→ S. J. Pollock 2009](#s-j-pollock-2009)

## Evidence

### S. J. Pollock 2009

S. J. Pollock. (2009). A Longitudinal Study of Student Conceptual Understanding in Electricity and Magnetism. Physics Education Research Conference proceedings. https://www.compadre.org/PER/ [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0907.5597](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0907.5597)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Longitudinal matched-score analysis: N=38 students with matched post-Physics II and post-Phys 301 BEMA data (Fig 2a), and N=41 students who took the BEMA after both 301 and 302 (Fig 2b). Both average shifts were statistically indistinguishable from zero, though some individuals shifted significantly.

> "The average of this distribution of shifts in score is 0 ± 2%, statistically indistinguishable from zero. Fig b (right) shows a histogram of the shift in individual's scores across upper-division courses, (student scores after Phys 302) - (same students' score after Phys 301). The average shift is -0.3±2 points (s.d.=11%), statistically indistinguishable from zero."
