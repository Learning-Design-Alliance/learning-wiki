# Orthographic detectability of derivational affixes is low and highly variable, with pseudo-affixation rare overall but concentrated in some affixes

> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · theoretical `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A reader relying on spelling alone cannot detect the derivational morphemes in approximately half of genuinely prefixed words and about a third of genuinely suffixed words. [→ Korochkina 2025](#korochkina-2025)
`q2 i?` Detectability varies widely across affixes, from over 93% for un- and -ly to 6% for co- and 22% for -ate. [→ Korochkina 2025 (2)](#korochkina-2025-2)
`q2 i?` Pseudo-affixation is uncommon overall (no more than 3% unprefixed and 5% unsuffixed words), but false alarms outnumber hits about fourfold for co-. [→ Korochkina 2025 (3)](#korochkina-2025-3)

## Evidence

### Korochkina 2025

Korochkina, M. & Rastle, K. (2025). Morphology in children's books, and what it means for learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00313-6

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

RegEx algorithm analysis simulating a reader with limited etymological knowledge across the entire CYP-LEX corpus of 1200 books. The simulation showed the reader "will be unable to detect the derivational morphemes in approximately half of all genuinely preﬁxed words" and about a third of genuinely suffixed words, largely because many complex words have bound stems.

> "Figures 4 and 5 reveal that this reader will be unable to detect the derivational morphemes in approximately half of all genuinely preﬁxed words and about a third of all genuinely sufﬁxed words."

### Korochkina 2025 (2)

Korochkina, M. & Rastle, K. (2025). Morphology in children's books, and what it means for learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00313-6

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Same RegEx detectability analysis, per-affix results (Figs. 4 and 5): the algorithm detected "over 93% of all complex words containing the preﬁx un- and the sufﬁx -ly" but only 6% of co- words and no more than 22% of -ate words, because many of their stems do not occur in isolation in modern English.

> "For instance, our algorithm was able to detect over 93% of all complex words containing the preﬁx un- and the sufﬁx -ly; however, it could identify only 6% of words with the pre ﬁx co- and no more than 22% the sufﬁx -ate."

### Korochkina 2025 (3)

Korochkina, M. & Rastle, K. (2025). Morphology in children's books, and what it means for learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00313-6

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Same RegEx analysis of pseudo-affixed words (potential false alarms) in CYP-LEX: "no more than 3% of unpreﬁxed and no more than 5% of unsufﬁxed words" were flagged, consistent with pseudo-affixed words being exceptional in English orthography, though for co- the number of false alarms was about four times the number of hits.

> "no more than 3% of unpreﬁxed and no more than 5% of unsufﬁxed words (based on etymological information) were identiﬁed as containing preﬁxes or sufﬁxes."
