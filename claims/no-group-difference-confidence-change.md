## Evidence

### Huh Yeon-Ju 2026

Huh Yeon-Ju, Kim Ju Whi, Yoon Narae, Jeong Seoi, Kong Hyoun-Joong, Myung Sun Jung. (2026). Immersive Virtual Reality Training to Improve Novice Physicians’ Emergency Response Skills: Randomized Controlled Trial. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/71455

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Between-group analysis of the randomized trial (VR n=79, HFS n=85) tested the group-by-time interaction for desaturation confidence via repeated-measures ANOVA. The article reports no significant difference detected; no effect size for this interaction is printed, so equivalence was not tested.

> "The training method did not significantly affect confidence (F 1, 162=3.28; P=.07), indicating that we did not detect a statistically significant difference between groups in confidence change within the precision of this study"
