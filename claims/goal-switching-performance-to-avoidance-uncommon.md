## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Senko 2011

Senko, C., Hulleman, C. S., & Harackiewicz, J. M. (2011). Achievement Goal Theory at the Crossroads: Old Controversies, Current Challenges, and New Directions. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2011.538646

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review's narrative synthesis of goal stability studies reports test-retest correlations "ranging from .40 to .70", suggesting goals remain stable during the academic period. These are correlations of goal stability, not effect sizes of an intervention.

> "The test-retest correlations for mastery goals and performance goals tend to be similar and high, ranging from .40 to .70, with values typically stronger for goal measures taken in the same semester than over successive semesters"

### Senko 2011 (2)

Senko, C., Hulleman, C. S., & Harackiewicz, J. M. (2011). Achievement Goal Theory at the Crossroads: Old Controversies, Current Challenges, and New Directions. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2011.538646

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Muis and Edwards' (2009) student-centered analyses: "only 4% of students in Study 1 and 0% in Study 2" switched from performance to performance-avoidance goals. Percentages are not effect sizes.

> "They found that only 4% of students in Study 1 and 0% in Study 2 actually switched from performance to performance-avoidance goals."
