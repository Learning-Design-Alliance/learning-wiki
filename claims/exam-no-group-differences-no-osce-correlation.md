## Evidence

### Herchenröther Julia 2021

Herchenröther Julia, Tiedemann Elena, Vogel Heiner, Simmenroth Anne. (2021). Which teaching method is more effective in a communication course – role-playing versus simulated patients, taught by tutors or faculty staff? A randomized trial. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001450](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001450)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Voluntary electronic exam-question analysis (N=79 complete questionnaire-OSCE-exam datasets): "No significant differences in performance on the exam questions were seen" between any compared groups. Only a median and p-values are printed, no effect size.

> "No significant differences in performance on the exam questions were seen between the SP-Lecturer group and SP-Tutor group or between the SP-Tutor group and the RP-Tutor group (Mdn=4, p>.359, N=79)."

### Herchenröther Julia 2021 (2)

Herchenröther Julia, Tiedemann Elena, Vogel Heiner, Simmenroth Anne. (2021). Which teaching method is more effective in a communication course – role-playing versus simulated patients, taught by tutors or faculty staff? A randomized trial. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001450](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001450)

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Spearman rank correlation between OSCE and exam performance in the randomized trial: "OSCE performance does not correlate with the performance on the exam questions (r=.03, p=.783)". No correlation with learning success or self-assessed knowledge was found either.

> "OSCE performance does not correlate with the performance on the exam questions (r=.03, p=.783)."
