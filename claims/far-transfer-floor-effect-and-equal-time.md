# All learners, regardless of condition, showed a floor effect on far-transfer problems, and instructional time did not differ between treatment groups

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` All treatment groups encountered difficulties on far-transfer problems, indicating a floor effect on that measure. [→ Reisslein 2004](#reisslein-2004)
`q2 i?` An ANOVA on instructional time found no statistically significant differences between treatment groups. [→ Reisslein 2004 (2)](#reisslein-2004-2)

## Evidence

### Reisslein 2004

Reisslein, J., Atkinson, R. K., & Reisslein, M. (2004). Exploring the Presentation and Format of Help in a Computer-Based Electrical Engineering Learning Environment. Arizona State University. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED484994

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the 51-participant experiment, far-transfer posttest means were low in all four conditions (e.g., 1.04 and 1.00 of 12); the authors state "we encountered a floor effect on this measure".

> "The results suggest that all the learners, regardless of the treatment condition, encountered difficulties when attempting to solve the far -transfer problems. Essentially, we encountered a floor effect on this measure."

### Reisslein 2004 (2)

Reisslein, J., Atkinson, R. K., & Reisslein, M. (2004). Exploring the Presentation and Format of Help in a Computer-Based Electrical Engineering Learning Environment. Arizona State University. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED484994

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

An ANOVA on time spent in paper-based training and computer-based learning found "No statistically significant differences between the different treatment groups were found", ruling out time as an explanation for format effects.

> "an analysis of variance (ANOVA) was conducted on these times. No statistically significant differences between the different treatment groups were found."
