`q3 i?` Fifth graders' initial pass studying rate was significantly faster for videos than for illustrated texts, with studying rates of the same child almost unrelated across modalities. [→ Mikko Haavisto 2026](#mikko-haavisto-2026)

## Evidence

### Mikko Haavisto 2026

Mikko Haavisto, Tomi Jaakkola. (2026). Fifth graders study videos faster than illustrated texts: Studying rate, review strategies, and learning implications. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09817-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mixed-effects analysis of frame-by-frame coded screen recordings from a within-subjects classroom experiment in which 52 fifth graders studied both modalities. The article reports a moderate to strong modality effect on initial pass duration, with "t(49.2) = 4.17, p < .001". No standardized effect size was printed.

> "A mixed-effects analysis revealed a moderate to strong effect of modality on the initial pass duration, (t(49.2) = 4.17, p < .001, B = 0.204 (SE = 0.049), R2mar = 0.148)."
