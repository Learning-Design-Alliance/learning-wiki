# Experts show superior recall of realistic circuit diagrams, but the advantage diminishes or disappears with randomly arranged elements (chunking)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Experts' superior recall of realistic circuit diagrams reflects chunking of interconnected elements, and vanishes when the logical substructure is absent. [→ Gerace 2007](#gerace-2007)

## Evidence

### Gerace 2007

Gerace, W. J. (2007). Contributions from Cognitive Research to Mathematics and Science Education. In D. Grayson (Ed.), Proceedings of the Workshop on Research in Science and Mathematics Education, Pietermaritzburg, South Africa. Originally published 1992. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0708.2496](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0708.2496)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Memory-recall study in electronics: experts and novices briefly studied circuit diagrams and then recalled them. The article reports experts' "significantly superior ability to recall the circuit" for realistic circuits, attributed to chunking; no effect size is printed.

> "The result is that experts have a significantly superior ability to recall the circuit when the circuit represents a realistic situation, but their advantage is diminished, or even disappears, when the circuit is comprised of randomly arranged electronic elements."
