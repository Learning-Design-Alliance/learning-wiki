`q3 i?` Nursing students receiving VP training in addition to standard training improved significantly more in confidence in asking about IPV than controls (between-group difference +1.26, 95% CI 0.40–2.15, p = 0.010). [→ Sörman K 2025](#sorman-k-2025)

## Evidence

### Sörman K 2025

Sörman K, El Alaoui S, Mårtensson S, Larsson M, Knez R, Ljudvåg M, Mac R, Dahlström K, Nowak YE, Fors U, Eckerström J. (2025). Virtual patient simulation strengthens confidence in clinical conversations among undergraduated nursing students: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08413-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial of 54 nursing students (29 VP, 25 control) analyzed with adjusted linear mixed-effects models. The VP group gained 2.07 points on the 10-point confidence VAS versus 0.81 for controls, a "between-group difference: +1.26 points". No standardized effect size was printed.

> "the VP group showed significantly greater improvement in confidence in IPV inquiry compared with controls (between-group difference: +1.26 points, 95% CI 0.40–2.15, p = 0.010)"
