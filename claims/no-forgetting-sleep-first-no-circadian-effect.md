## Evidence

### Talamini and Takashima 2008

Lucia M. Talamini, Ingrid L.C. Nieuwenhuis, Atsuko Takashima, and Ole Jensen. (2008). Sleep directly following learning benefits consolidation of spatial associative memory. Learning & Memory 15:233–237. https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.771608

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-group one-sample tests of forgetting in each of the four conditions of the experiment; the sleep-first groups showed no significant forgetting, consistent with sleep directly after learning stabilizing the trace.

> "Forgetting was significantly different from zero in the 12-h wake group (t = 2.93, df = 18, P = 0.009) and in the 24-h wake–sleep group (t = 5.94, df = 19, P < 0.0005), but not in the 12-h sleep (t = ⳮ0.57, df = 19, P = 0.58) and 24-h sleep–wake (t = 1.57, df = 17, P = 0.14) groups."

### Talamini and Takashima 2008 (2)

Lucia M. Talamini, Ingrid L.C. Nieuwenhuis, Atsuko Takashima, and Ole Jensen. (2008). Sleep directly following learning benefits consolidation of spatial associative memory. Learning & Memory 15:233–237. https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.771608

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA on immediate retrieval data with time of learning (morning/evening) as between-subject factor, a control analysis addressing circadian confounds; no significant effect was found.

> "No significant effect was found, that is, the immediate recall performance was similar in the morning and in the evening. Hence, confounds from time-of-learning effects on the interpretation of the retention data are unlikely."
