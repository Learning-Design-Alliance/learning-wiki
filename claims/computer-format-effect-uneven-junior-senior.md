## Evidence

### Chang Yu-Che 2016

Chang Yu-Che, Lee Ching-Hsing, Chen Chien-Kuang, Liao Chien-Hung, Ng Chip-Jin, Chen Jih-Chang, Chaou Chung-Hsien. (2016). Exploring the influence of gender, seniority and specialty on paper and computer-based feedback provision during mini-CEX assessments in a busy emergency department. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-016-9682-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Subgroup analysis of EPs using computer-format mini-CEXs, split at a seniority cut-off of 10 years (junior N = 418, senior N = 186). Junior EPs' suggestions for development (69.1% vs. 42.5%) and agreed action plan use (40.7% vs. 24.7%) were also significantly higher. Percentages and p-value printed; no effect size.

> "Junior EPs also provided a significantly higher portion of all three aspects of feedback (37.1 %) than did seniors (8.60 %, p < 0.001) when using the computer format."
