# The effect of the written-question intervention cannot be attributed to question writing versus plenary prioritization because the intervention was dual in nature

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The intervention contained two components — individual written question generation and plenary prioritization by group discussion — and the randomized design could not determine which component caused the observed effect. [→ Olde Bekkink M 2015](#olde-bekkink-m-2015)

## Evidence

### Olde Bekkink M 2015

Olde Bekkink M, Donders ART, Kooloos JG, de Waal RMW, Ruiter DJ. (2015). Challenging students to formulate written questions: a randomized controlled trial to assess learning effects. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0336-z

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' stated limitation of their own trial: the intervention combined individual question writing with "prioritization of questions during a plenary discussion", and the design could not separate their contributions. Cited work by Bobby et al. suggests effects differ by achievement level, but stratification data were unavailable in this trial.

> "Another limitation was the dual nature of the intervention, as it contains a part where individual students generate a question and formulate it in writing, and a second part consisting of prioritization of questions during a plenary discussion in the small group."
