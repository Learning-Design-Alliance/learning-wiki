# The distribution of highest-performing teachers across schools is statistically unlikely to be random, with significance for middle school teachers on both poverty and achievement groupings but for elementary teachers only on achievement groupings

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Chi-square tests of independence reject a uniform distribution of highest-performing teachers across school quintiles for middle school math and ELA on both poverty and achievement groupings, and for elementary teachers only when schools are grouped by achievement quintile. [→ Steven Glazerman and Jeffrey Max 2011](#steven-glazerman-and-jeffrey-max-2011)

## Evidence

### Steven Glazerman and Jeffrey Max 2011

Steven Glazerman and Jeffrey Max. (2011). Do Low-Income Students Have Equal Access to the Highest-Performing Teachers? NCEE Technical Appendix. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee/pubs/20114016/pdf/20114016.pdf

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Chi-square independence tests of teacher status by school quintile using district administrative data from ten districts, with all-district combined p-values shown in Table A.2 (elementary poverty .176; middle school math and ELA .000 on both groupings). The article reports that "The distribution of highest -performing middle school teachers differed significantly from a uniform distribution".

> "The distribution of highest -performing middle school teachers differed significantly from a uniform distribution when comparing schools by achievement and poverty. However, the results fo r elementary teachers are only significant when the schools were grouped by school achievement quintile."
