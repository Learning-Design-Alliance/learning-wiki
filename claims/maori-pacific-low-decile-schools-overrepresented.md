## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the 2002–2012 University of Auckland health professional cohort, 27% of Māori and 33% of Pacific students attended low decile schools compared with 5% of nMnP students (p < 0.0001). [→ Wikaire Erena 2016](#wikaire-erena-2016)

## Evidence

### Wikaire Erena 2016

Wikaire Erena, Curtis Elana, Cormack Donna, Jiang Yannan, McMillan Louise, Loto Rob, Reid Papaarangi. (2016). Patterns of privilege: A total cohort analysis of admission and academic outcomes for Māori, Pacific and non-Māori non-Pacific health professional students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0782-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of secondary administrative data for 2686 students (150 Māori, 257 Pacific, 2279 nMnP) enrolled in BHSc, BNurs or BPharm at the University of Auckland 2002–2013. School decile was used as a socioeconomic proxy; the chi-square comparison was significant (p < 0.0001), with "low proportion of non-Māori non-Pacific students from low decile (red) schools (5 %)".

> "Of particular note is the low proportion of non-Māori non-Pacific students from low decile (red) schools (5 %) compared to much larger proportions of low decile (red) for Māori (27 %) and Pacific (33 %) student groups (Table 2)."
