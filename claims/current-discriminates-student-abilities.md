## Evidence

### Ryan 2014

Ryan, Q. X., Astolfi, C., Baily, C., & Pollock, S. J. (2014). Validation of a Conceptual Assessment Tool in E&M II. 2014 Physics Education Research Conference Proceedings. [doi:10.1119/perc.2014.pr.054](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.054)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Test discrimination analysis of CURrENT scores from 271 students, using two adaptations of Ferguson's delta for the open-ended format: treating points as items, and dichotomizing scoring elements. Both values exceed the traditional δ > 0.9 threshold.

> "We obtained δ =0.99 with the first method, and 0.98 with the second method. The possible range of δ values is [0,1]. Traditionally, δ > 0.9 is considered good discrimination and thus the CURrENT has substantial discrimination power in differentiating students with different abilities."

### Ryan 2014 (2)

Ryan, Q. X., Astolfi, C., Baily, C., & Pollock, S. J. (2014). Validation of a Conceptual Assessment Tool in E&M II. 2014 Physics Education Research Conference Proceedings. [doi:10.1119/perc.2014.pr.054](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.054)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Item-test correlation analysis using Pearson correlation of each question with the rest of the test (item excluded), since point-biserial correlation applies only to dichotomous variables. All six questions exceeded the ~0.2 minimum.

> "The correlation coefficient for each of the six questions ranged from 0.40 and 0.49. Minimum acceptable correlation coefficients are generally considered to be around 0.2 [14]."
