# Children and adults engage a common network of occipito-temporal, precentral, and superior parietal cortices during numerical judgments of both Arabic numerals and dot arrays

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A conjunction analysis revealed regions significantly active during both notations of numerical comparison in children and adults: occipito-temporal, precentral, and superior parietal cortices. [→ Cantlon Jessica F. 2009](#cantlon-jessica-f-2009)

## Evidence

### Cantlon Jessica F. 2009

Cantlon Jessica F., Libertus Melissa E., Pinel Philippe, Dehaene Stanislas, Brannon Elizabeth M., Pelphrey Kevin A. (2009). The Neural Development of an Abstract Concept of Number. Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn.2008.21159

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Random-effects conjunction analysis (p < .003, cluster ≥ 8 voxels) of Arabic numeral and dot array contrasts in 14 children and 14 adults. For children the network additionally included inferior frontal gyrus (BA 44) and thalamus (Table 1).

> "Regions that exhibited a significant increase in activity during both notations of numerical comparison for children and adults were occipito-temporal (OTC), precentral, and superior parietal cortices."
