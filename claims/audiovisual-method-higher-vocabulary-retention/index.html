Evidence¶

Bozavlı 2017¶

Bozavlı, E. (2017). Vocabulary Teaching in Foreign Language via Audiovisual Method Technique of Listening and Following Writing Scripts. International Education Studies, 10(5). https://doi.org/10.5539/ies.v10n5p129

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Comparison of retention one week after instruction between two groups of 14 French-language-teaching students that the article describes as experimental research with groups "randomly established" (the allocation procedure is not described); each group was taught as one class at a different time, and results were analysed by percentage calculations. The discussion states "Retention rate is just above 70% with audiovisual method, while it is about 25% in conventional method"; the results tables show week-two rates of 60–83.33% (experimental) and 16.66–43.33% (control). No inferential statistics were reported.

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Vocabulary knowledge measured after audiovisual instruction remained stable between week one and week two, with changes restricted to one or two words — related
  • Learners taught French vocabulary with an audiovisual method (listening to video pronunciation while following the written script) achieved higher learning success rates in week one than learners taught with the conventional method — related
  • Control-group participants showed declining retention success between week one and week two, with 13 of 14 participants declining — related
  • Second-semester French learners who received four weeks of daily audiovisual-media listening activities scored 14.18% higher than the control group on an immediate listening posttest, a medium-to-large effect (d = −0.68) that did not reach statistical significance — related
