Evidence¶

Copur-Gencturk 2026¶

Copur-Gencturk, Y., Moreno, K., Chu, Y., Li, H., & Tang, J. (2026). Automated coding of content and pedagogical content knowledge of mathematics using a multi-agent large language model. ZDM – Mathematics Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11858-026-01796-2

q2 · i? · design · r3

Secondary analysis of agreed-on human codes from 268 U.S. middle school mathematics teachers' responses to nine open-ended PCK items, evaluated on a held-out 20% test set. The article reports "Cohen's κ = .68" overall and QWK = .79 for GradeOpt, with exact agreement ranging from 73% to 89%.

"Across the automated coding models, GradeOpt demonstrated higher performance on each indicator of success, exact agreement, Cohen's κ, and QWK, at the overall PCK level, across subdomains, and for all items except one, knowledge of mathematical tasks, Item 2."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Comparison models (RoBERTa, SBERT, naïve GPT-4o prompting) failed to reach acceptable agreement on PCK items, with Cohen's κ at or below .55 — related
  • On CK items, GradeOpt showed near-perfect alignment with human codes (κ = .88 and .85; QWK = .89 and .94), comparable to human–human agreement — related
  • All automated models, including GradeOpt, were most challenged by knowledge of teaching mathematics items (GradeOpt κ = .59) and least challenged by knowledge of students' mathematical thinking (κ = .79) — related
  • Gaps between Cohen's κ and QWK indicate GradeOpt often assigned codes adjacent to human codes rather than entirely discrepant ones — related
  • Prior non-LLM automated coding approaches yielded variable and often lower agreement with human coders on teacher knowledge measures, especially PCK — related
  • In an AI-guided PD program, rural teachers engaged with mathematical creativity, content knowledge, and PCK as interconnected forms of reasoning rather than isolated domains — related
