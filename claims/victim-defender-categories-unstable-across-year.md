## Evidence

### Laninga-Wijnen 2023

Laninga-Wijnen, L., van den Berg, Y. H. M., Garandeau, C. F., Mulder, S., & de Castro, B. O. (2023). Does Being Defended Relate to Decreases in Victimization and Improved Psychosocial Adjustment Among Victims? Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000712

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive stability analysis (Tables 2 and 3) of victimization/defending categories across the school year, reported separately for intervention and control schools. The article calls the stability "surprisingly low"; percentages are as printed.

> "Only 32 nondefended victims (16.8%) remained nondefended across the two time points, whereas 45.0% of the initially nondefended victims became defended victims at T2 and 38.2% were no longer victi- mized. Of the defended victims at T1, 61.0% still reported being defended as victim at T2"
