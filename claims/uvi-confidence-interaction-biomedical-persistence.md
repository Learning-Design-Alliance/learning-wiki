# A utility-value intervention interacted with students' confidence to predict enrollment in the second biology course and biomedical major two years post-intervention

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a double-blind randomized controlled trial, the UVI x confidence interaction significantly predicted both enrollment in the second biology course (z = 2.08, b = 0.18, p = .04) and biomedical major two years post-intervention (z = 2.62, b = 0.21, p = .01). [→ Hecht 2019](#hecht-2019)
`q3 i?` Within the UVI condition, confidence significantly positively predicted enrollment (z = 2.75, b = .31, p < .01) and biomedical major (z = 2.67, b = .28, p < .01), but confidence did not significantly predict persistence in the control condition. [→ Hecht 2019 (2)](#hecht-2019-2)
`q3 i?` The UVI x confidence effect on biomedical major was partially mediated by enrollment in the second course; students who enrolled were 58.22% more likely to major in biomedical fields than students who did not enroll. [→ Hecht 2019 (3)](#hecht-2019-3)

## Evidence

### Hecht 2019

Hecht, C. A., Harackiewicz, J. M., Priniski, S. J., Canning, E. A., Tibbetts, Y., & Hyde, J. S. (2019). Promoting Persistence in the Biological and Medical Sciences: An Expectancy-Value Approach to Intervention. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000356

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Path models with logit link on binary persistence outcomes from the randomized trial (1,039 students; 579 UVI, 460 control). The article reports the significant UVI x confidence interaction on "enrollment in the second biology course" and on "biomedical major two years post-intervention" with the printed coefficients.

> "However, there was a significant UVI x confidence interaction on both enrollment in the second biology course (z = 2.08, b = 0.18, p = .04) and biomedical major two years post-intervention (z = 2.62, b = 0.21, p = .01; see Figure 2)."

### Hecht 2019 (2)

Hecht, C. A., Harackiewicz, J. M., Priniski, S. J., Canning, E. A., Tibbetts, Y., & Hyde, J. S. (2019). Promoting Persistence in the Biological and Medical Sciences: An Expectancy-Value Approach to Intervention. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000356

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Simple-effects probes of the interaction in the same path models. In the control condition confidence did not significantly affect enrollment (z = -.34, p = .74) or major (z = -1.17, p = .24), while in the UVI condition "there was a significant positive effect of confidence" on both outcomes.

> "but in the UVI condition, there was a significant positive effect of confidence on enrollment (z = 2.75, b = .31, p < .01) and major (z = 2.67, b = .28, p < .01)."

### Hecht 2019 (3)

Hecht, C. A., Harackiewicz, J. M., Priniski, S. J., Canning, E. A., Tibbetts, Y., & Hyde, J. S. (2019). Promoting Persistence in the Biological and Medical Sciences: An Expectancy-Value Approach to Intervention. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000356

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

A second path model added enrollment in the second course as a predictor of biomedical major (z = 13.96, b = 2.88, p < .001). The article reports that the UVI x confidence effect on major was partially mediated by enrollment (z = 2.06, p = .04).

> "Students who enrolled in the second course were 58.22% more likely to major in biomedical fields than students who did not enroll in the second course."
