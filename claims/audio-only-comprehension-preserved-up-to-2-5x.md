# Comprehension of audio-only podcast-style clips is not significantly impaired at playback speeds up to 2.5x, though performance numerically declines with speed

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a four-group experiment, one-way ANOVA found no significant effect of playback speed (1x, 1.5x, 2x, 2.5x) on comprehension test performance for audio-only clips, and Tukey post-hoc comparison of 1x vs 2.5x approached but did not reach significance. [→ Ashley Chen 2024](#ashley-chen-2024)

## Evidence

### Ashley Chen 2024

Ashley Chen, Suchita E. Kumar, Rhea Varkhedi, Dillon H. Murphy. (2024). The Effect of Playback Speed and Distractions on the Comprehension of Audio and Audio-Visual Materials. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09917-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Experiment 1 randomly assigned 200 undergraduates to listen to two Science Podcast audio clips at 1x, 1.5x, 2x, or 2.5x speed before a 30-item multiple-choice test per topic. The ANOVA showed "no significant effect of speed" and the 1x vs 2.5x Tukey comparison was ptukey = 0.051, with BF01 = 4.83 favoring the null.

> "There was no significant effect of speed, F(3, 219) = 2.40, p = 0.069, BF01 = 4.83, and Tukey’s post-hoc anal-ysis indicated no differences between the 1x and 2.5x speed groups’ performance, t(396) = 2.57, ptukey = 0.051, BF01 = 0.29."
