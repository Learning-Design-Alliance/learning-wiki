## Evidence

### Martin E. Horn 2003

Martin E. Horn, Helmut F. Mikelskis. (2003). Student Conceptions of Holography. German Physical Society Spring Conference Bremen 2001. Originally published 2001. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0311108](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0311108)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Post-lesson interviews asked students explicitly about their conceptions of light; students lacking meta-conceptual capabilities were "not able to explicitly recognize these switches", unlike capable students who switch consciously and justify the change.

> "Students without this level of expertise also switch between models, yet are not able to explicitly recognize these switches and demonstrate a certain unreflected arbitrariness in their methods of argumentation."

### Martin E. Horn 2003 (2)

Martin E. Horn, Helmut F. Mikelskis. (2003). Student Conceptions of Holography. German Physical Society Spring Conference Bremen 2001. Originally published 2001. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0311108](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0311108)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

In a focal-point drawing task at the concave mirror (Figure 4), one student merged model properties so that "light particles follow along the sinus-shaped light path"; the article calls the resulting model separation non-existent.

> "After being asked to possibly explain using the particle model, the student eventually modifies his drawing in such a way (figure 4 c) so that the light particles follow along the sinus-shaped light path and are steered into a focus."
