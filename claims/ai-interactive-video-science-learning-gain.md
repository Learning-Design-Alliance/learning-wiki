# Children who watched AI-powered interactive videos with contingent feedback scored 0.32 standard deviations higher on a science post-test than children who watched the non-interactive broadcast version

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` In a randomized controlled trial, children in the interactive condition outperformed children in the non-interactive condition on the science post-test by 0.32 standard deviations (p < .001), controlling for age, baseline science knowledge, and language proficiency. ## Evidence

### Ying Xu 2023

Ying Xu, Kunlei He, Julian Levine, Daniel Ritchie, Zexuan Pan, Andres Bustamante, and Mark Warschauer. (2023). Artificial Intelligence Enhances Children's Science Learning from Television Shows. https://osf.io/j7upd

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Preregistered between-subjects RCT; regression analyses covaried age, baseline science knowledge, and language proficiency. The printed standardized effect size is 0.32 standard deviations: "children in the interactive condition scored 0.32 standard deviations higher than those in the non-interactive condition (p < .001)". Descriptive accuracy was 63.13% interactive vs 55.69% non-interactive.
