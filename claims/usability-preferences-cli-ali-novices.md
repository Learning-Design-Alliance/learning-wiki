## Evidence

### Uysal Daniel 2020

Uysal Daniel, Gasch Claudia, Behnisch Rouven, Nickel Felix, Müller-Stich Beat Peter, Hohenfellner Markus, Teber Dogu. (2020). Evaluation of new motorized articulating laparoscopic instruments by laparoscopic novices using a standardized laparoscopic skills curriculum. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-020-08086-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Questionnaire administered after each run in the cross-over study: "participants preferred CLI over the new motorized ALI due to its lighter weight and easier handling". Opening and closing of ALI caused slightly more discomfort for a minority.

> "In general, participants preferred CLI over the new motorized ALI due to its lighter weight and easier handling."

### Uysal Daniel 2020 (2)

Uysal Daniel, Gasch Claudia, Behnisch Rouven, Nickel Felix, Müller-Stich Beat Peter, Hohenfellner Markus, Teber Dogu. (2020). Evaluation of new motorized articulating laparoscopic instruments by laparoscopic novices using a standardized laparoscopic skills curriculum. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-020-08086-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Study-specific digital questionnaire results: "88% agreed stating that the biggest advantage of ALI over CLI could be seen within this exercise". Additionally, 61% believed ALI's degrees of freedom eased knot tying and suturing, while 29% disagreed, citing weight and complexity.

> "When asked if the possibility to angulate the tip of the scissors facilitated the cutting a circle task, 88% agreed stating that the biggest advantage of ALI over CLI could be seen within this exercise."
