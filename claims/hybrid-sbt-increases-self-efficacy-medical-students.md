# Medical students' self-rated self-efficacy scores rose significantly from pretest to posttest after hybrid simulation-based training, in one uncontrolled pre-post study

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` After hybrid SBT, students' self-efficacy medians rose significantly from pretest to posttest, with a rank-biserial correlation of rpb = 0.461. [→ Tam 2025](#tam-2025)

## Evidence

### Tam 2025

Tam, C. Y. W., Dizon, J. I. W. T., Sin, S. W. C., Tang, G. K. Y., Lai, V. Y. W., Wong, Y. Y., Ganotice, F. A., & Yeung, N. P. (2025). Implementation of hybrid simulation training to promote collective and self-efficacy in medical students: a two-pronged approach. Annals of Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1080/07853890.2025.2527362

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on paired, non-normally distributed SE scores from 94 students (pretest median 24, 23–32 vs posttest 32, 28–34, p < 0.001). The article reports "A medium to moderate effect size (rpb = 0.461)".

> "A similar trend was observed in the SE of students, where their self-perceived SE at baseline was significantly lower, but would subsequently be improved after undergoing hybrid SBT as evidenced by the median of their pretest vs posttest SE scores (24, 23–32, vs 32, 28–34, p < 0.001). A medium to moderate effect size (rpb = 0.461) suggests that differences were practical and substantial after undergoing hybrid SBT."
