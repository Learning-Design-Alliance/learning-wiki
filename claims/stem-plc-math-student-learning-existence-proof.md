# A small set of mathematics-only studies gives existence proof that teacher participation in a mathematics PLC can enhance student learning, but evidence is insufficient to link formal assessment results to PLC participation

## Evidence

### Fulton 2010

Fulton, K., Doerr, H., & Britton, T. (2010). STEM Teachers in Professional Learning Communities: A Knowledge Synthesis. National Commission on Teaching & America's Future / WestEd. https://www.nctaf.org/resources/events/STEMWingspreadConference.htm

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Knowledge synthesis finding on student learning impacts, from the Overview of Key Findings. The review reports that "Onlya few studies were available, and all of them investigated student impacts in mathematics", attributing this partly to greater assessment data availability in mathematics.

> "Onlya few studies were available, and all of them investigated student impacts in mathematics; this is likely, in part, because there commonly are more assessment data available in mathematics."

### Fulton 2010 (2)

Fulton, K., Doerr, H., & Britton, T. (2010). STEM Teachers in Professional Learning Communities: A Knowledge Synthesis. National Commission on Teaching & America's Future / WestEd. https://www.nctaf.org/resources/events/STEMWingspreadConference.htm

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The synthesis's overall judgment on the few mathematics studies: they give "some existence proof that teacher participation in a mathematics PLC can lead to an enhancement in what their students learn". No effect sizes are printed.

> "Overall, this small set of studies gives some existence proof that teacher participation in a mathematics PLC can lead to an enhancement in what their students learn."
