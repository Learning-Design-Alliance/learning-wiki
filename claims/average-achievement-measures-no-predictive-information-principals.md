# Average achievement and adjusted average achievement measures provide no information for predicting principals' contributions to student achievement in the following year

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Measures that do not account for students' past achievement (average achievement and adjusted average achievement) provide no information for predicting principals' contributions to student achievement in the following year. [→ Hanley Chiang 2016](#hanley-chiang-2016)

## Evidence

### Hanley Chiang 2016

Hanley Chiang, Moira McCullough, Stephen Lipscomb, Brian Gill. (2016). Can Student Test Scores Provide Useful Measures of School Principals' Performance? Washington, DC: U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED570000

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Key finding from the report's simulation of each measure's accuracy for predicting principals' future contributions, based on Pennsylvania statewide data on students and principals from 2007/08 to 2013/14. The report states these measures "provide no information for predicting principals’ contributions to student achievement in the following year."

> "Average achievement and adjusted average achievement—the measures that do not account for students’ past achievement—provide no information for predicting principals’ contributions to student achievement in the following year."
