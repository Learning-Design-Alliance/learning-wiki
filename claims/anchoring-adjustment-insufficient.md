# Anchoring-and-adjusting perspective taking saves effort but adjustments systematically fall short

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` When perceivers anchor on their own experience to read others, their adjustments systematically fail to go far enough, and they stop adjusting once they reach a plausible conclusion. [→ Gehlbach 2018](#gehlbach-2018)

## Evidence

### Gehlbach 2018

Gehlbach, H., & Vriesema, C. C. (2018). Meta‐bias: A practical theory of motivated thinking. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9454-6

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article reports, citing Epley and Gilovich and Epley et al., that adjustments from a self-anchor are chronically insufficient because perceivers stop at a good-enough conclusion rather than exhaustively searching for the most accurate inference. The article presents this as prior research, not its own study.

> "A key characteristic of this process is that these adjustments systematically fail to go far enough (Epley & Gilovich, 2006; Epley et al., 2004)."
