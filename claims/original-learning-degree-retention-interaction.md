## Evidence

### Semb 1994

Semb, G. B., & Ellis, J. A. (1994). Knowledge Taught in School: What Is Remembered? Review of Educational Research, 64(2), 253-286. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543064002253

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Semb, Ellis, and Araujo (1993) compared student proctors with the students they proctored in a PSI child development course, tested at semester end and 4 months later; the authors hypothesized teaching the content provided additional learning opportunities.

> "Both proctors and students scored near 90% (this score is at ceiling for the test) at the end of the semester, but after 4 months proctors' scores (79%) were significantly higher than their former students' scores (73%)."

### Semb 1994 (2)

Semb, G. B., & Ellis, J. A. (1994). Knowledge Taught in School: What Is Remembered? Review of Educational Research, 64(2), 253-286. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543064002253

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review of Bahrick (1984) and Bahrick and Hall (1991) very long-term retention studies in Spanish and mathematics; the reviewers report an interaction in which the rate of forgetting decreases as degree of original learning increases.

> "Students who took the fewest Spanish courses and presumably had a low degree of original learning forgot most of what they had learned during the first 6 to 9 years of retention."
