## Evidence

### Yu Jihye 2026

Yu Jihye, Lee Sukyung, Kim Mira, Lee Janghoon, Jung Yun Jung. (2026). Evaluating medical students’ engagement and confidence across three simulation-based education methods: standardized patient, high fidelity simulator, and virtual reality. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08634-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-hoc follow-up (Tukey HSD, Table 5) of the confidence ANOVAs in the 43-student survey study; the article reports the SP group "scored significantly higher than the HFS and VR simulation groups" in the three patient-facing areas. No effect sizes are printed.

> "The SP simulation group scored significantly higher than the HFS and VR simulation groups in the areas of history-taking, physical examination, and interacting and communicating with patients."

### Yu Jihye 2026 (2)

Yu Jihye, Lee Sukyung, Kim Mira, Lee Janghoon, Jung Yun Jung. (2026). Evaluating medical students’ engagement and confidence across three simulation-based education methods: standardized patient, high fidelity simulator, and virtual reality. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08634-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Univariate ANOVAs on post-simulation confidence ratings (10-point scale) from the same 43 students; the article reports F = 8.569, p < .001 for history-taking, F = 6.110, p = .003 for physical examination, and F = 6.866, p = .001 for interacting and communicating with patients.

> "There were significant differences based on the simulation method for history-taking (F = 8.569, p < .001), physical examination (F = 6.110, p = .003), and interacting and communicating with patients (F = 6.866, p = .001)."

### Yu Jihye 2026 (3)

> "Conversely, there were no significant differences between the HFS and VR simulation groups in these areas."
