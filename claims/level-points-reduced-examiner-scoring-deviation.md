# The “Level-Points” online training model reduced surgical examiners' overall scoring deviation from 19.34 to 15.52 points, a large effect (d = -1.09)

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` After the two-week online training, examiners' mean overall scoring deviation decreased by 3.82 points (19.34 to 15.52), significant before and after FDR correction, with a large effect size. [→ Zhang Lina 2026](#zhang-lina-2026)

## Evidence

### Zhang Lina 2026

Zhang Lina, Yan Mengling, Qiu Yiwen, Cai Haolei, Yu Yunxian. (2026). Effectiveness of the “Level-Points” online training model and influencing factors in enhancing scoring accuracy among surgical examiners. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-10033-z

`q3 · i3` · `design · r2`

Paired-sample t-tests compared pre- and post-training deviation scores for 670 surgical examiners in the 2024 national training program. The mean deviation "declined from 19.34 ± 3.60 to 15.52 ± 3.42", Cohen's d = -1.09 (95% CI -1.17 to -1.00), remaining significant after FDR correction.

> "The overall mean scoring deviation declined from 19.34 ± 3.60 to 15.52 ± 3.42 (P < 0.001, adjusted P < 0.001), with a large effect size (Cohen’s d = -1.09, 95% CI: -1.17 to -1.00)."
