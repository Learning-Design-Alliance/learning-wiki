Evidence¶

Juan-Carlos Pérez-González 2022¶

Juan-Carlos Pérez-González, Gemma Filella, Anna Soldevila, Yasmine Faiad, Maria-Jose Sanchez-Ruiz. (2022). Integrating self-regulated learning and individual differences in the prediction of university academic achievement across a three-year-long degree. Metacognition and Learning (2022) 17:1141–1165. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09315-w

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Hierarchical regression (Step 1) on first-year GPA in a sample of 386 Spanish undergraduates: the three non-cognitive variables were significant predictors, with "adjusted R2 = 0.25". Comparable Step 1 models for years 2 and 3 yielded adjusted R² of 0.22 and 0.17.

"Using first year GPA as the criterion variable, at step 1, the three non-cognitive variables were significant predictors ( F(4, 184) = 16.94, p < .001, adjusted R2 = 0.25)"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Academic engagement and regulation of behavior and context show incremental validity over cognitive predictors of university GPA
  • A latent global non-cognitive factor predicts a latent three-year GPA factor, explaining 27–29% of its variance
  • Previous academic performance is the strongest predictor of university GPA, and adding cognitive predictors raises explained variance to about 44–50%
  • Trait emotional intelligence showed no significant association with university GPA, despite correlating with engagement, SRL strategies and self-efficacy
  • Cognitive ability (inductive reasoning) showed no significant association with university GPA in any analysis — related
  • Canfield's learning style variables predicted academic success where cognitive style did not — related
