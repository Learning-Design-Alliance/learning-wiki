# Need for cognition correlates moderately with mathematics achievement (r = .25)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r3` · `q3` · `i2` medium · n=30 meta-analyses, 66 correlations from 13,853 effect sizes (~4.66 million participants)

Need for cognition, the tendency to engage in and enjoy effortful thinking, correlates r = .25 with mathematics achievement (Liu & Nesbit 2024, k = 12, n = 2,115). These are correlations compiled across meta-analyses: they describe how strongly the characteristic goes with mathematics achievement, not what causes it.

## Evidence

### Breit et al. 2025

Breit, M., Schneider, M., & Preckel, F. (2025). Mathematics achievement and learner characteristics: A systematic review of meta-analyses. *Learning and Individual Differences, 118*, 102621. [doi:10.1016/j.lindif.2024.102621](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2024.102621)

`q3 · systematic review of meta-analyses` · `i2 · medium effect, r = .25 (k = 12)` · `n=30 meta-analyses, 66 correlations from 13,853 effect sizes (~4.66 million participants)` · `review · r3`

The review compiles zero-order correlations from published meta-analyses (each with at least ten effect sizes, the largest one kept where several covered a variable), found by a systematic PsycINFO search in July 2024. It pools nothing further and controls for nothing: many of these characteristics correlate with one another, so a correlation here says how strongly the characteristic goes with mathematics achievement, not that it causes it. One meta-analytic estimate, r = .25 (Liu & Nesbit 2024, 12 effect sizes, 2,115 school and university students).

## Discussion

The authors relate it to engagement with cognitively demanding material; need for cognition also correlates with intelligence, academic self-concept and intrinsic motivation, so the estimate is not unique to it. It rests on twelve effect sizes.
