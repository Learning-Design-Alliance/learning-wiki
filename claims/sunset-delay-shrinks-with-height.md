# Calculated sunset delays are generally under a minute between heights 200 m apart and shrink with altitude, so a team of observers at multiple heights is needed

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For the example conditions (latitude 40°, declination 23.5°, R = 6378 km), sunset-time differences between points 200 m apart in altitude are generally less than a minute and shrink going upward. [→ P.K.Aravind 2005](#pkaravind-2005)

## Evidence

### P.K.Aravind 2005

P.K.Aravind. (2005). Sunsets, tall buildings, and the Earth’s radius. http://units.aps.org/units/fed/newsletters/summer2005/wyp.html

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Numerical evaluation of Eq.(1) for heights 200–1000 m, tabulated for declinations 23.5° and 15°, giving delays from 166 s to 372 s and 151 s to 338 s respectively. The article finds "less than a minute’s difference" between points 200 m apart, shrinking upward.

> "It is seen (at least for the above conditions) that  there is generally less than a minute’s difference  between sunsets observed from points 200m apart in altitude, and that this interval shrinks as one goes upward."
