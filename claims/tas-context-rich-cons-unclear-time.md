## Evidence

### Melanie Good 2018

Melanie Good, Emily Marshman, Edit Yerushalmi, and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Physics teaching assistants' views of different types of introductory problems: Challenge of perceiving the instructional benefits of context-rich and multiple-choice problems. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020120](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020120)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Open-coded cons for the context-rich problem type from worksheets and interviews; the con unclear "appears to be one major reason why the TAs thought that the context-rich problem type was highly challenging."

> "the con “unclear” appears to be one major reason why the TAs thought that the context-rich problem type was highly challenging and may have contributed to the TAs’ reluctance to use this problem type"

### Melanie Good 2018 (2)

Melanie Good, Emily Marshman, Edit Yerushalmi, and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Physics teaching assistants' views of different types of introductory problems: Challenge of perceiving the instructional benefits of context-rich and multiple-choice problems. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020120](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020120)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Worksheet cons among TAs ranking context-rich problems lowest for use; "the majority listed “time” as a con," reflecting perceived time costs of reading and interpreting the problem.

> "Of the TAs who ranked the context-rich problem type the lowest in terms of “use”, the majority listed “time” as a con"
