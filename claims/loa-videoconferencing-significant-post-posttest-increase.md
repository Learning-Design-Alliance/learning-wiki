# Reported videoconferencing adoption rose significantly after practitioners returned to the workplace (p<0.005), with mean level increasing from 2.00 to 4.53

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For videoconferencing, a technology directly taught in the course, reported level of use increased significantly (p<0.005) between the posttest and the post-posttest after participants returned to their schools. [→ Orr 2009](#orr-2009)

## Evidence

### Orr 2009

Orr, D., & Mrazek, R. (2009). Developing the level of adoption survey to inform collaborative discussion regarding educational innovation. Canadian Journal of Learning and Technology, V35(2). https://www.cjlt.ca

`q2 · i?`

Descriptive analysis of the 15 respondents completing all three surveys showed videoconferencing means rising from 2.00 (pretest) to 3.00 (posttest) to 4.53 (post-posttest). The article reports a "significant (p<0.005) increase in reported level of use" at the post-posttest, and nine respondents reported routine use or higher.

> "It is important to note the significant (p<0.005) increase in reported level of use as these students (practicing educational professionals) returned to the workplace and had the opportunity to access and apply these technologies within their schools (mean=4.53, median=5.00)."
