# Whether the learner is a friend or a relative shows no significant main effect on estimated success at helping them learn Facebook

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Acquaintance type (friend vs. relative) showed no significant main effect on estimated success, contrary to kin selection predictions. [→ Harvey J. GINSBURG 2016](#harvey-j-ginsburg-2016)

## Evidence

### Harvey J. GINSBURG 2016

Harvey J. GINSBURG, Rebecca CAMERON, Roque V. MENDEZ & Michael WESTHOFF. (2016). Helping others use social media: age stereotypes when estimating learner's success. Psychology, Society, & Education, 8(1), 1-12. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1123456

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within the same repeated measures 2x3 ANOVA of 212 undergraduates, the friend-versus-relative contrast was not significant: "No significant main effect was shown for acquaintance type, F(1,211) = 1.71, p= .193". Equivalence was not tested; the printed partial η2 = .008 is very small.

> "No significant main effect was shown for acquaintance type, F(1,211) = 1.71, p= .193; forage, partial η2 =  .059 and for acquaintances, partial η2 = .008."
