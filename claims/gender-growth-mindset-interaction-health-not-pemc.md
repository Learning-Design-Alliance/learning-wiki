# Growth mindset interacts with gender for health major choice — men's chances decline 3.9 percentage points per unit increase — but no gender difference appears in how ability beliefs influence PEMC major choice

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` One significant interaction emerged, between gender and growth mindset in choice of health majors: men's chances of majoring in health fields decline by 3.9 percentage points for every one-unit increase in growth mindset, while growth mindset has no significant effect on women's health major chances. [→ Perez-Felkner L 2017](#perez-felkner-l-2017)
`q3 i?` No evidence was found that boys and girls differ in how any mathematics ability belief measure influences choice of PEMC major; 12th grade perceived ability under challenge and gender each remained significant predictors of PEMC majoring even in the weaker interaction model (pmale < 0.000, pmath12 < 0.009). [→ Perez-Felkner L 2017 (2)](#perez-felkner-l-2017-2)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Interaction terms in the full multinomial models assessed gender differences in how ability beliefs influence major selection. The article notes growth mindset is based on a single item, so a one-unit increase aligns with students' response categories.

> "While growth mindset does not have a signiﬁcant eﬀect on women’s chances of majoring in health ﬁelds, men’s chances of majoring in health ﬁelds decline by 3. 9% points for every one-unit increase in growth mindset"

### Perez-Felkner L 2017 (2)

Perez-Felkner L, Nix S and Thomas K (2017) Gendered Pathways: How Mathematics Ability Beliefs Shape Secondary and Postsecondary Course and Degree Field Choices. Front. Psychol. 8:386. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00386

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same interaction-effects models tested gender moderation for PEMC major choice and found none, though the main effects of 12th grade perceived ability under challenge and gender remained significant in this otherwise weaker model.

> "We do not however ﬁnd evidence indicating boys and girls diﬀer in how any of our mathematics ability belief measures (growth mindset and perceived ability under challenge in 10th and 12th grades) inﬂuence choice of PEMC major."
