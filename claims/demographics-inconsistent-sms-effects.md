# Demographic variables show no consistent effects on tapping performance, except a single female advantage in one condition

## Evidence

### Yunhee Kim 2026

Yunhee Kim, Doyeon Chae, Jaehwan Jang, Kyeong Taek Oh, Byeong-Sun Park & Min-gu Kim. (2026). Does tapping in sync reflect cognitive control? Links between sensorimotor synchronization, social context, and executive function in early childhood. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00443-5

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Preliminary independent samples t-tests of age (4 vs. 5 years) and gender on tapping performance in 140 children; the only significant effect favored females with printed effect size d = –0.36 and was "not observed elsewhere".

> "Although a single significant gender difference favoring females emerged during the right-hand trial at 150 bpm in the solo tapping condition, t (138) = –2.12, p = .036, d = –0.36, this effect was not observed elsewhere."
