# Percent-scale grading did not simply inflate grades: percent-scale students earned about 20% more A's yet also failed more, with only a small average grade shift and a narrower 4-point distribution

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students in percent scale classes received more A's (about 20% more than peers in 4-point classes) while also failing more, contradicting a simple grade-inflation account. [→ Webb 2020](#webb-2020)

## Evidence

### Webb 2020

Webb, D. J., Paul, C. A., & Chessey, M. K. (2020). Relative impacts of diﬀerent grade-scales on student success in introductory physics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020114](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020114)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of the complete course grade distribution for both scales over 2003-2012 (Fig. 3). The authors conclude the 4-point distribution was narrower rather than uniformly shifted higher, so fewer students fail under it.

> "we find that students in percent scale classes are the ones receiving more A’s (about 20% more than their peers in 4-point classes)."

### Webb 2020 (2)

Webb, D. J., Paul, C. A., & Chessey, M. K. (2020). Relative impacts of diﬀerent grade-scales on student success in introductory physics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020114](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020114)

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Average course grades computed on a GPA-like mapping with printed effect size d = 0.086: 2.852 (SD = 0.89) under percent scale and 2.918 (SD = 0.67) under 4-point, a shift of 0.066. CLASP A showed no significant difference (t = 1.1, P = 0.288) while CLASP B did (t = 11.3, P < 0.001), which the authors call conflicting evidence for grade inflation.

> "The effect size, using Cohen’s d, of this grade shift is about 0.086 so it is a small effect."
