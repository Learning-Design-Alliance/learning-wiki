# Adolescents with a morning chronotype report higher initial in-depth exploration than those with intermediate or evening chronotypes

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Morning chronotype adolescents showed significantly higher initial in-depth exploration than intermediate (Wald = 7.228, p < 0.01) and evening (Wald = 13.189, p < 0.01) chronotype peers, and intermediate exceeded evening (Wald = 4.453, p < 0.05). [→ Bacaro 2025](#bacaro-2025)

## Evidence

### Bacaro 2025

Bacaro, V., De Lise, F., Natale, V., Tonetti, L., & Crocetti, E. (2025). Night Owls and Early Birds: The Role of Adolescents' Chronotype on Educational Identity Trajectories. International Journal of Psychology, 60(5), e70108. https://doi.org/10.1002/ijop.70108

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multigroup latent growth curve analysis comparing intercepts of in-depth exploration across chronotype groups in the same longitudinal sample. Morning > intermediate > evening ordering was supported by Wald tests ("significantly higher initial levels of in‐depth exploration"). No standardized effect size was reported.

> "adolescents with a morning chronotype reported significantly higher initial levels of in‐depth exploration compared to those with intermediate (Wald = 7.228, p < 0.01) and evening (Wald = 13.189, p < 0.01) chronotypes"
