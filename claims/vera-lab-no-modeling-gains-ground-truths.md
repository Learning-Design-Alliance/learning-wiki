# Lab experiment showed no gains on modeling and simulation questions, with students viewing models as ground truths rather than abstractions

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the lab experiment, modeling and simulation question scores decreased from pre-test (73.21) to post-test (66.07), with no significant gain. [→ An 2022](#an-2022)
`q2 i?` Students' incorrect answers suggest they understood simulation models as "ground truths" rather than abstractions of the world for explaining and making predictions. [→ An 2022 (2)](#an-2022-2)

## Evidence

### An 2022

An, S., Bates, R., Rugaber, S., Hammock, J., Weigel, E., & Goel, A. K. (2022). Cognitive Assistance for Inquiry-Based Modeling. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2209.02576](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2209.02576)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Controlled lab experiment (N=14 paired pre/post). The article reports modeling and simulation scores decreased from "pre-test=73.21" to "post-test=66.07"; no significance test or effect size is printed for this comparison.

> "We did not observe similar gains on questions pertaining to conceptual and simulation modeling as parts of the scientiﬁc process (pre-test=73.21; post-test=66.07)."

### An 2022 (2)

An, S., Bates, R., Rugaber, S., Hammock, J., Weigel, E., & Goel, A. K. (2022). Cognitive Assistance for Inquiry-Based Modeling. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2209.02576](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2209.02576)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Qualitative analysis of incorrect post-test answers in the lab experiment. The most popular incorrect answer on Question 8 held that simulation models "determine the exact outcome of an ecological scenario with set inputs", which the authors read as students treating models as ground truths rather than abstractions.

> "in Question 8, the most popular incorrect answer was that the simulation models can be incorporated into the scientiﬁc model “because they determine the exact outcome of an ecological scenario with set inputs.”"
