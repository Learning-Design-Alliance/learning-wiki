Evidence¶

Goetz 2006¶

Goetz, T., Frenzel, A. C., Pekrun, R., Hall, N. C., & Lüdtke, O. (2006). Between- and Within-Domain Relations of Students' Academic Emotions. https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/edu

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Structural equation modeling of multi-item scales (4 items per emotion per domain) in a correlational study of 542 German high school students. "all coefficients were below .35" in Grade 8 and below .20 in Grade 11, with mathematics–physics relations .38–.61 and .48–.64; coefficients were disattenuated latent-level estimates.

"all coefficients were below .35 in the Grade 8 sample and below .20 in the Grade 11 sample. The relations between emotions in mathematics and physics ranged from .38 to .61 in the Grade 8 sample and from .48 to .64 in the Grade 11 sample"

Related Claims¶

  • Within-domain emotion structure only partially matches the valence–activation model: anger relates more strongly to boredom than to anxiety, and enjoyment–boredom and anger–boredom correlations are conspicuously strong — related
  • The six achievement emotion regulation strategies are empirically separable constructs, and corresponding strategies across emotions are sufficiently distinct to warrant emotion-specific measures — related
  • Emotional volatility shows small marginally significant incremental positive associations with cognitive activation, classroom management, and teacher enthusiasm, and the general anxiety factor is weakly negatively associated with classroom management — related
  • Between-domain relations of academic emotions are weaker in Grade 11 than Grade 8, indicating increasing domain differentiation with age — related
  • The strength of within-domain relations among academic emotions differs considerably across school subjects, strongest in mathematics and weakest in German — related
  • Within-domain relations of academic emotions are stable across grade levels, with no significant differences between Grade 8 and Grade 11 — related
