## Evidence

### Liu Zhenhua 2022

Liu Zhenhua, Chen Qiong, Wu Jing, Li Xinhua, He Yuchen, Yu Qiao. (2022). Simulation-based training in asthma exacerbation for medical students: effect of prior exposure to simulation training on performance. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-022-03300-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Within-group Wilcoxon comparisons of 10-question multiple-choice quizzes before and after simulation plus debriefing in the 203-student study. The article reports "Knowledge improved after the simulation" in both groups with printed means and p < 0.001.

> "Knowledge improved after the simulation, as demonstrated by post-simulation scores that were higher than pre-simulation scores in the unexposed (77.35 ± 12.50 vs. 88.33 ± 9.16, p < 0.001) and pre-exposure (77.03 ± 12.45 vs. 86.83 ± 8.80, p < 0.001) groups."

### Liu Zhenhua 2022 (2)

Liu Zhenhua, Chen Qiong, Wu Jing, Li Xinhua, He Yuchen, Yu Qiao. (2022). Simulation-based training in asthma exacerbation for medical students: effect of prior exposure to simulation training on performance. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-022-03300-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Mann–Whitney between-group comparisons of quiz scores in the same study. No statistically significant difference between the two named groups; equivalence was not tested.

> "There were no differences in average scores between the unexposed and pre-exposure groups in pre-simulation test scores (77.35 ± 12.50 vs. 77.03 ± 12.45, p = 0.911) and post-simulation scores (88.33 ± 9.16 vs. 86.83 ± 8.80, p = 0.218)."
