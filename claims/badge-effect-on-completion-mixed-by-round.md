## Evidence

### Nevin Christa R 2014

Nevin Christa R, Westfall Andrew O, Rodriguez J Martin, Dempsey Donald M, Cherrington Andrea, Roy Brita, Patel Mukesh, Willig James H. (2014). Gamification as a tool for enhancing graduate medical education. Postgraduate Medical Journal. https://doi.org/10.1136/postgradmedj-2013-132486

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum comparison of the proportion of remaining questions completed after 25 questions in round 2. Badge earners were "found to be more likely to answer a greater proportion of remaining questions" with p=0.02 and Hodges–Lehmann shift estimate 5.9 (0.0 to 39.0).

> "In round 2, after 25 questions, those who had earned badges (n=12, median (IQR) of 100.0 (100.0 to 100.0)) compared with those who had not (n=64, median (IQR) of 77.1 (32.2 to 100.0)) were found to be more likely to answer a greater proportion of remaining questions (p=0.02, Hodges–Lehmann estimate of shift=5.9 (0.0 to 39.0))."

### Nevin Christa R 2014 (2)

Nevin Christa R, Westfall Andrew O, Rodriguez J Martin, Dempsey Donald M, Cherrington Andrea, Roy Brita, Patel Mukesh, Willig James H. (2014). Gamification as a tool for enhancing graduate medical education. Postgraduate Medical Journal. https://doi.org/10.1136/postgradmedj-2013-132486

`q3 · i0` · `design · r2`

Wilcoxon rank-sum comparison at the round-1 badge checkpoint (after 25 questions). "There was no statistically significant difference in the proportion of subsequent questions completed"; equivalence was not tested. Round 3 similarly showed no significant difference (p=0.15).

> "In round 1, after 25 questions, there was no statistically significant difference in the proportion of subsequent questions completed for those who had earned at least one badge (n=38; median (IQR) of 98.2 (51.2 to 100.0)) versus those who had not earned at least one badge (n=40, median (IQR) of 90.9 (51.2 to 100.0), p=0.36, Hodges–Lehmann estimate of shift=0.0 (0.0 to 7.3))."
