Evidence¶

Almasloukh 2023¶

Almasloukh, K. B., Miner, M., Phillips, K., & Evans, M. (2023). Educational strategies and nursing theory to prepare leaners for NCLEX‐RN. Nursing Open, 10, 7144–7153. https://doi.org/10.1002/nop2.1998

q2 · i? · review · r2

Scoping review following Arksey and O'Malley's framework and PRISMA-ScR, searching CINAHL, ERIC, PsychINFO and Web of Science (2015–2022); 25 studies were charted. The review reports "there was not a consensus on a best practice" and counts active learning as the most frequent strategy (n = 11).

"Studies (N = 25) revealed that multiple educational strategies are used to prepare students for the NCLEX‐RN exam; however, there was not a consensus on a best practice."

Related Claims¶

  • Computer-adaptive quizzing software (PassPoint, PrepU) showed a statistically significant positive relationship with NCLEX-RN success (Pence & Wood, 2018) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Concept-based curriculum correlates with critical thinking, which predicts first-time NCLEX-RN pass probability (Harrison, 2018) — related
  • No reviewed article focused on students' quality of life during NCLEX-RN preparation; only 36% of NCLEX-RN success was attributed to academic factors (Sanderson et al., 2022) — related
  • Failing two nursing courses is associated with twice the likelihood of failing the first NCLEX-RN attempt compared with failing one or none (Sanderson et al., 2022) — related
  • A 6-week NCLEX Coaching Model showed no significant first-time pass-rate improvement but significantly improved confidence/readiness scores (Schlairet & Rubenstein, 2019) — related
  • Active learning conditions more often include quizzes, homework, and learning/exam preparation than lecture conditions — related
