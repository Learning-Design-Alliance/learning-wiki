# Late-evening local television news viewing increases with age and its consistency and parent-child similarity also rise

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Exposure to local late evening news shows a clear age-related increase, largest between ages 10 and 13, with over-time consistency rising from .16 (ages 10-12) to .55 (ages 16-17). [→ Tims 1983](#tims-1983)

## Evidence

### Tims 1983

Tims, Albert R., Jr. (1983). Development of Public Affairs Media Use. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED238044

`q2 · i1`

Age-group ANOVAs of late-evening local news viewing frequency at both waves; unlike network news there was no winter-to-fall decline. Consistency correlations rose with age (.16 to .55), reaching adult levels by about age 14.

> "Second, there is a clear age-related increase in exposure (winter:F = 2.53, d.f. = 7, 492, 2 < .02; fall:F = 3.98, d.f. = 7, 492, E < .001)."
