# Metapragmatic written feedback outperformed direct feedback on recognizing appropriate email requests, while both treatments beat control on production

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students receiving metapragmatic feedback significantly outperformed those receiving direct feedback and the control group on the recognition task for email requests. [→ Yousefi 2021](#yousefi-2021)
`q2 i?` Both treatment groups performed significantly better than the control group on the production task, with no significant difference between the two treatment groups. [→ Yousefi 2021 (2)](#yousefi-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Yousefi 2021

Yousefi, M., & Nassaji, H. (2021). Corrective Feedback in Second Language Pragmatics: A Review of Research. Teaching English as a Second Language Electronic Journal (TESL-EJ), 25(1). https://tesl-ej.org/pdf/ej97/a6.pdf

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Nguyen et al.'s (2015) study of written corrective feedback on email requests, comparing direct-feedback, metapragmatic-feedback, and control groups with pretest, immediate and delayed posttests; on the recognition task "students who received metapragmatic feedback significantly outperformed those receiving direct feedback and the control group." No effect size is printed.

> "students who received metapragmatic feedback significantly outperformed those receiving direct feedback and the control group in the recognition task."

### Yousefi 2021 (2)

Yousefi, M., & Nassaji, H. (2021). Corrective Feedback in Second Language Pragmatics: A Review of Research. Teaching English as a Second Language Electronic Journal (TESL-EJ), 25(1). https://tesl-ej.org/pdf/ej97/a6.pdf

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

In the same study, on the production task both treatment groups beat the control group while the two feedback types did not differ significantly; equivalence between them was not tested. No effect size is printed.

> "The results indicated that the treatment groups performed significantly better than the control group in the production task, but there was no significant difference between the two treatment groups."
