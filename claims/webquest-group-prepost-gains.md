# The WebQuest experimental group improved significantly from pretest to posttest in planning, implementation and evaluation teaching skills

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Paired-samples t-tests showed statistically reliable pre-post gains for the experimental group in planning (M=21.93 to M=40.79, t=30.92), implementation (M=65.43 to M=126.64, t=42.91) and evaluation (M=12.21 to M=18.79, t=14.12), all p=0.000. ## Evidence

### Haythem Abdel Mageed Mohamed & Rasha Nageh Ali Abd El Rheem 2008

Haythem Abdel Mageed Mohamed & Rasha Nageh Ali Abd El Rheem. (2008). The Web Quest: Its Impact on Developing Teaching Skills of Physical Education Student Teachers. Journal of Research, volume 5, issue 1. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=The+Web+Quest+Its+Impact+on+Developing+Teaching+Skills+of+Physical+Education+Student+Teachers

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-group pre-post analysis of the experimental WebQuest group (n=14), whose peer teaching was videotaped and rated by three external judges on the 70-item observation sheet. All three paired t-tests were reported at p=0.000; no effect size was printed.

> "A paired samples t test revealed a statistically reliable difference between the mean scores for the experimental group in the pre-performance in the planning, implementation and evaluation skills (M=21.93; M=65.43; M=12.21) and in the post-performance on the observation sheet (M=40.79, M=126.64; M=18.79), t=30.92, p=0.000; t=42.91, p=0.000; t=14.12, p=0.000, respectively"
