## Evidence

### Shioiri Toshiki 2024

Shioiri Toshiki, Nakashima Michiyo, Tsunekawa Koji. (2024). When can we identify the students at risk of failure in the national medical licensure examination in Japan using the predictive pass rate? BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05948-4

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Simulation of movement between high (PPR < 95%) and low (PPR ≥ 95%) risk groups for all 637 students across 6 years, based on the five logistic regression PPR formulae. The article reports the high-risk group "decreased by 46.1% at the end of 6th grade (from 193 to 104)".

> "Although approximately 30% (193 of 637) students were determined “high-risk group” at admission, the percentage had decreased by 46.1% at the end of 6th grade (from 193 to 104)."

### Shioiri Toshiki 2024 (2)

Shioiri Toshiki, Nakashima Michiyo, Tsunekawa Koji. (2024). When can we identify the students at risk of failure in the national medical licensure examination in Japan using the predictive pass rate? BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05948-4

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Actual NMLE outcomes compared between PPR-defined risk groups at the end of 6th grade in the same cohort simulation. The article prints a pass rate of "64.4% (67/104)" for the high-risk group versus "99.2% (529/533)" for the low-risk group, and notes some swings between groups.

> "Among 104 students in the “high-risk group” at the end of 6th grade, the pass rate for NMLE was 64.4% (67/104) compared to 99.2% (529/533) in the “low-risk group.”"
