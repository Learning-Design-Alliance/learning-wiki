## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Among low prior knowledge learners, learning gains were significantly higher in the EDT condition than in the CS condition (r = .16). [→ Siswa van Riesen 2018](#siswa-van-riesen-2018)

## Evidence

### Siswa van Riesen 2018

Siswa van Riesen, Hannie Gijlers, Anjo Anjewierden, Ton de Jong. (2018). Supporting learners' experiment design. Education Tech Research Dev (2018) 66:475–491. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-017-9568-4

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Subgroup analysis of 63 learners classified as low prior knowledge (maximum two of nine correct on the Archimedes' principle pre-test part). Mann–Whitney follow-ups showed "significantly higher learning gains for low prior knowledge learners in the EDT condition compared to low prior knowledge learners in the CS condition" (r = .16).

> "An independent samples Kruskal–Wallis test showed a significant difference between the conditions, H(2) = 6.54, p = .038. Follow-up Mann–Whitney analyses showed significantly higher learning gains for low prior knowledge learners in the EDT condition compared to low prior knowledge learners in the CS condition, U = 115.50, z = 2.438, p = .015, r = .16."
