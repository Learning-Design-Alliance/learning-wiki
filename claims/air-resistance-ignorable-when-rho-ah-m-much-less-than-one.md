# Air resistance can be ignored for a falling full bucket but not an empty one, because the criterion ratio Fa/Fg ≈ ρAh/m must be much smaller than unity

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The dimensionless ratio ρAh/m (air resistance force over gravitational force) determines whether air resistance matters; for an estimated empty bucket the ratio reaches unity while a water-filled bucket stays safely small. [→ David W. Hogg 2007](#david-w-hogg-2007)

## Evidence

### David W. Hogg 2007

David W. Hogg. (2007). Real-world ballistics: A dropped bucket. https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.0107 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0709.0107](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0709.0107)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Order-of-magnitude estimation in the lecture: with ρ ≈ 1 kg m−3, A ≈ 0.1 m2, h ≈ 10 m, and 1 < m < 30 kg depending on fullness, "The empty bucket gets a ratio ( Fa/Fg) of unity" while the full bucket is fine, matching intuition that a heavy bucket falls straight down.

> "Uh oh! We can ’t ignore air resistance if the bucket is empty! The empty bucket gets a ratio ( Fa/Fg) of unity. The full bucket is ﬁne (or ﬁne enough for our precision today)."
