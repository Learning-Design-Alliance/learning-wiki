# MPOIS-coded student essays show speculative and motivational sentences declining after feedback while inference statements do not increase

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In coded physics journal essays, speculative sentences fell from 35% of all writing to about 25% after feedback and remained at that level, and motivational statements fell by about 10%. [→ Hermsen 2006](#hermsen-2006)
`q2 i?` Inference statements were the hardest to impact; despite repeated definitions and examples, students never incorporated them well into the essays. [→ Hermsen 2006](#hermsen-2006)

## Evidence

### Hermsen 2006

Hermsen, L. M., & Franklin, S. V. (2006). Realizing a New Research Agenda for Writing-to-Learn: Embedding Process in Context. Manuscript submission to Educational Researcher. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0609070](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0609070)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Analysis of approximately 600 student writing samples from weekly cumulative journals in the Explorations in Physics course during the 2003-2004 academic year, coded with the MPOIS rubric and displayed in Fig. 2. The article reports that speculative sentences "drop after feedback to about 25%" and that motivation statements "also fell by about 10%"; inference frequency showed no significant increase. Percentages are descriptive; no effect size or test statistic is printed.

> "Speculative sentences, which initially make up 35% of all student writing drop after feedback to about 25% and remain at that level for the rest of the quarter."
