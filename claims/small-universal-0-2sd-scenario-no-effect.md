## Evidence

### Chittleborough 2014

Chittleborough, C. R., Mittinty, M. N., Lawlor, D. A., & Lynch, J. W. (2014). Effects of Simulated Interventions to Improve School Entry Academic Skills on Socioeconomic Inequalities in Educational Achievement. Child Development. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.12309

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Simulation using the marginal structural model on the imputed ALSPAC sample (n = 11,764): a 0.2 SD universal improvement (mean score 12.8 to 13.5) was not large enough to move any individual across the categorical mediator thresholds, so predicted risk stayed at 49.3 per 100 overall and excess risks were unchanged (0.0% reduction).

> "In the universal intervention Scenario 1, where school entry scores of all children are increased, the predicted proportion of children with poor educational achievement was not changed."
