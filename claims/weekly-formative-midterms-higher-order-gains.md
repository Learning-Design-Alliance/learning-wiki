## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Final exam performance improved significantly with weekly midterm exams compared with the original two-midterm format, with gains of 9% on understand items and 17% on apply/evaluate items but no statistically significant gain on remember items. [→ Bailey E. G. 2017](#bailey-e-g-2017)

## Evidence

### Bailey E. G. 2017

Bailey E. G., Jensen J., Nelson J., Wiberg H. K., Bell J. D. (2017). Weekly Formative Exams and Creative Grading Enhance Student Learning in an Introductory Biology Course. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-02-0104

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of common final exam items (45 items across remember, understand, apply/evaluate) between two original-format semesters (2005) and three formative-format semesters (2011–2013), analyzed by two-way ANOVA. The article reports "understand, 9% gain" and "application/evaluation (17%" gains, with format main effect p = 0.015.

> "In contrast, benefits of the new format were observed for items assessing comprehension (understand, 9% gain) and particularly for those requiring application/evaluation (17%, see Supplemental Figures S1–S5 for example test items)."
