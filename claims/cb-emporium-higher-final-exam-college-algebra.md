# Computer-based emporium students outperform traditional lecture students on a common multiple-choice final exam in college algebra

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Students in computer-based sections scored significantly higher on the common final exam than face-to-face students (r=.182). [→ Krupa 2013](#krupa-2013)

## Evidence

### Krupa 2013

Krupa, E., Webel, C., & McManus, J. (2013). Evaluating the impact of computer-based and traditional learning environments on students' knowledge of algebra. In Martinez, M. & Castro Superfine, A (Eds.), Proceedings of the 35th annual meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education. Chicago, IL: University of Illinois at Chicago. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED584492

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Level 1 t-test comparing all students with an exam score (nCB=134, nF2F=192) across twelve sections of Intermediate Algebra. CB students averaged "µ=70.75" versus "µ =65.49" for F2F, a "small effect size (r=.182)".

> "On average, student achievement on the final exam was higher for students in the CB (µ=70.75, σ =15.29) than for students in the F2F sections (µ =65.49, σ =13.12). This difference was significant (t=-3.33, p" .001), which represented a small effect size (r=.182)"
