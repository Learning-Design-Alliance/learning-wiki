# Assigning schools to use RM-CC5 as their core Grade 5 mathematics curriculum for a full year did not significantly change state assessment achievement compared with business-as-usual schools

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a 46-school RCT, the main effect of assignment to the RM-CC5 condition on Grade 5 WVGSA mathematics scores was not statistically significant; equivalence was not tested. [→ Nicole Shechtman 2019](#nicole-shechtman-2019)

## Evidence

### Nicole Shechtman 2019

Nicole Shechtman, Jeremy Roschelle, Mingyu Feng, Corinne Singleton. (2019). An Efficacy Study of a Digital Core Curriculum for Grade 5 Mathematics. AERA Open. https://doi.org/10.1177/2332858419850482

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Confirmatory RQ1 analysis of the MCIS randomized controlled trial: two-level hierarchical linear models on 1,919 Grade 5 students in 23 treatment and 23 control West Virginia schools, controlling for Grade 4 WVGSA. Table 2 reports an effect size of −0.06 (coefficient −5.0, t = −0.94, p = .348). The article reports "no overall difference in outcomes between the treatment and control groups."

> "a two-level MLM model controlling for Grade 4 WVGSA indicated that the main effect of assignment to condition did not result in a statistically significant difference on the Grade 5 WVGSA: There was no overall difference in outcomes between the treatment and control groups."
