Evidence¶

De Wever 2007¶

De Wever, B., Van Keer, H., Schellens, T., & Valcke, M. (2007). Applying multilevel modelling to content analysis data: Methodological issues in the study of role assignment in asynchronous discussion groups. Learning and Instruction, 17, 436-447. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2007.04.001

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Four-level model C adding the five ASIMeC dimensions as explanatory variables (better fit than model B, χ² = 1464.001, df = 10, p < .001); message characteristics explained 11.4% of between-message variance (R1²).

"Concerning theory , both mentioning and discussing theory leads to a significantly higher mean level of knowledge construction (mean = 1.321 + 0.223 = 1.544, χ² = 33.950, df = 1, p < .001 and mean = 1.321 + 0.238 = 1.559, χ² = 24.764, df = 1, p < .001 respectively)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • In knowledge-construction content analysis data from discussion groups, the largest share of variance lies at the message level, with only small between-group and between-student variance. — related
  • Messages merely mentioning sources, containing organisational moderating, or introducing new points reflect significantly lower levels of knowledge construction, while discussing sources shows no significant difference. — related
  • Students assigned the summariser role post messages reflecting a significantly higher level of knowledge construction than students without roles, while starter, moderator, theoretician, and source searcher roles do not differ significantly. — related
  • Knowledge construction does not increase gradually across successive discussion themes: messages in theme 2 reflect significantly lower levels, theme 3 significantly higher, and theme 4 no significant difference from theme 1. — related
