Evidence¶

Bonczar 1983¶

Bonczar, Thomas P., & Easton, John Q. (1983). The Effect of Mastery Learning on Student Achievement. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED238505

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Retrospective observational analysis of institutional records from the City Colleges of Chicago's computer-based student recordkeeping system, comparing earned credit rates of full-time Fall 1981 students in mastery versus non-mastery sections of the same courses. The report states that "in eight of nine comparisons, mastery learning sections had higher ECR's than comparable non-mastery learning sections." No effect size is printed.

Bonczar 1983 (2)¶

Bonczar, Thomas P., & Easton, John Q. (1983). The Effect of Mastery Learning on Student Achievement. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED238505

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Observational comparison of earned credit rates grouped by number of mastery learning classes taken. The report prints "an overall ECR of 64%" for no ML classes, 59% for one, and 66% for two. The authors argue course-selection effects explain the one-class anomaly and that controlling for course would favor ML sections.

"students who took no mastery learning classes had an overall ECR of 64%, while the ECR of those taking one mastery learning class was 59% and that of students taking two mastery learning classes was 66%"
