## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The authors attribute the convergence between simulated-patient and expert evaluations partly to the comprehensive training simulated patients receive, and call for studies comparing trained and untrained simulated patients. [→ Carrard Valerie 2026](#carrard-valerie-2026)

## Evidence

### Carrard Valerie 2026

Carrard Valerie, Bourquin Céline, Félix Sylvie, Viret Francine, Takeuchi Yusuke L., Berney Alexandre. (2026). Simulated-patient evaluations during formative interviews predict exam performance better than self-reported empathy and the ability to recognize others’ emotions. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09934-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

This is the authors' interpretation in the Discussion, not a tested result. They note the Lausanne simulated-patient training program includes "theoretical introductions, role-plays, feedback giving skills, debriefing, and regular evaluations" and suggest training may enable evaluations congruent with expert assessments.

> "Further studies comparing trained and untrained simulated patients are needed to clearly estimate the impact of training quality on the congruence between simulated-patient and expert evaluations."
