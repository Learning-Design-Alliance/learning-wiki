## Evidence

### Giusti Laura 2021

Giusti Laura, Mammarella Silvia, Salza Anna, Del Vecchio Sasha, Ussorio Donatella, Casacchia Massimo, Roncone Rita. (2021). Predictors of academic performance during the covid-19 outbreak: impact of distance education on mental health, social cognition and memory abilities in an Italian university student sample. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-021-00649-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Chi-square test of depression severity levels by gender in the 203-student survey (Table 2); 27.1% of women vs 12.5% of men were in the severe category. No statistically significant gender difference was found in BDI-II total scores.

> "Specifically, concerning the severity levels, the analyses carried out with Chi-square showed a statistically significant difference by gender, with a high proportion of women having a severe level of depression (chi-square: 8.813, d.f. 3; p = 0.032)"

### Giusti Laura 2021 (2)

Giusti Laura, Mammarella Silvia, Salza Anna, Del Vecchio Sasha, Ussorio Donatella, Casacchia Massimo, Roncone Rita. (2021). Predictors of academic performance during the covid-19 outbreak: impact of distance education on mental health, social cognition and memory abilities in an Italian university student sample. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-021-00649-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

One-way ANOVA on the five-vignette memory and attention task in the 203-student sample (Table 3): women's mean was 1.19 (SD 1.34) vs men's 0.77 (SD 0.9). No significant gender difference appeared on the Eyes Task.

> "The analyses carried out with one-way ANOVA showed women more skilled than men in temporarily retaining non-verbal information (F = 3.964; p = 0.048) (Table 3)."
