# In an introductory calculus-based physics course, the percentage of correctly solved online homework problems is highest in the semester with the lowest number of allowed tries (10) and lowest with essentially unlimited tries (99)

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Gerd Kortemeyer 2014

Gerd Kortemeyer. (2014). An Empirical Study of the Eﬀect of Granting Multiple Tries for Online Homework. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1407.2276](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1407.2276)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of online homework logs from four semesters of a large enrollment introductory physics course, comparing semesters with 10, 12, 20, and 99 maximum tries (Table I). The article reports "the percentage of correctly solved problems is the highest for the course with the lowest number of allowed tries"; the 99-tries semester solved only about 92%.

> "In fact, quite counter-intuitively, the percentage of correctly solved problems is the highest for the course with the lowest number of allowed tries."
