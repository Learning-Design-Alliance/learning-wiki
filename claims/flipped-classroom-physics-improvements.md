## Evidence

### Tolks D 2016

Tolks D, Schäfer C, Raupach T, Kruse L, Sarikas A, Gerhardt-Szép S, Kllauer G, Lemos M, Fischer MR, Eichner B, Sostmann K, Hege I. (2016). An Introduction to the Inverted/Flipped Classroom Model in Education and Advanced Training in Medicine and in the Healthcare Professions. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001045](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001045)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review attributing this finding to the Deslauriers, Schelew and Wieman study in a large-enrollment physics class. The review reports that flipped classroom use "can lead to significant learning improvements among physics pupils"; no effect size is printed.

> "In a study by Deslauriers and colleagues, it was shown that the use of the flipped classroom method can lead to significant learning improvements among physics pupils"

### Tolks D 2016 (2)

Tolks D, Schäfer C, Raupach T, Kruse L, Sarikas A, Gerhardt-Szép S, Kllauer G, Lemos M, Fischer MR, Eichner B, Sostmann K, Hege I. (2016). An Introduction to the Inverted/Flipped Classroom Model in Education and Advanced Training in Medicine and in the Healthcare Professions. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001045](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001045)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review of studies, chiefly those summarised by Fischer and Spannagel and the Lage, Platt and Treglia work, on student reception of the ICM. The review reports higher classroom activity versus traditional lectures and that "Most students report preferring the ICM to traditional instruction"; no effect sizes are printed.

> "They generally display higher activity levels in the classroom phase in comparison to traditional lecture situations. Lecturers have observed a more active participation among students in discussions. Most students report preferring the ICM to traditional instruction."
