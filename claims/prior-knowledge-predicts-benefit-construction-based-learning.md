## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students with high prior knowledge benefit from practices in which they construct understanding themselves, whereas students with little prior knowledge often struggle. [→ Garvin Brod 2021](#garvin-brod-2021)

## Evidence

### Garvin Brod 2021

Garvin Brod. (2021). How Can We Make Active Learning Work in K–12 Education? Considering Prerequisites for a Successful Construction of Understanding. Psychological Science in the Public Interest. https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100621997376

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Narrative synthesis in the commentary, citing Kirschner et al. (2006), reports that prior knowledge predicts benefit from construction activities: "students with little prior knowledge often struggle," putting younger children at a disadvantage (with exceptions, see Chi, 1978).

> "Levels of prior knowledge are a strong predictor of how much students will benefit from learning activities in which they have to construct understanding themselves, as is the case in many active-learning practices; whereas students with high levels of prior knowledge can benefit, students with little prior knowledge often struggle"
