## Evidence

### Walensky Rochelle P. 2018

Walensky Rochelle P., Kim Younji, Chang Yuchiao, Porneala Bianca C., Bristol Mirar N., Armstrong Katrina, Campbell Eric G. (2018). The impact of active mentorship: results from a survey of faculty in the Department of Medicine at Massachusetts General Hospital. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1191-5

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional faculty survey (553 respondents, 56.0% response rate) analyzed with multivariate logistic regression. The article reports that having a mentor oneself was associated with "a 3.5-times increased odds of mentoring others". No effect size beyond the OR is printed.

> "Further, those who reported having a mentor themselves had a 3.5-times increased odds of mentoring others (OR 3.50, 95% CI: 1.84–6.67, p < 0.001)."
