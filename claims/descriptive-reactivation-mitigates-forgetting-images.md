# Descriptive reactivations significantly improve free recall of images compared with distractor and no-reactivation controls

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants given three descriptive reactivations remembered significantly more images than those given distractor reactivations or no reactivation. [→ MacLeod 2018](#macleod-2018)

## Evidence

### MacLeod 2018

MacLeod, S., Reynolds, M. G. & Lehmann, H. (2018). The mitigating effect of repeated memory reactivations on forgetting. npj Science of Learning 3:9. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0025-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the 3×4 between-subject ANOVA (n = 167 analyzed after exclusions and trimming) of correctly recalled images, the reactivation main effect was significant, F(3, 155) = 5.50, p = 0.001. Pairwise comparisons showed the Descriptive group beat both controls, "demonstrating mitigated forgetting".

> "the participants in the Descriptive group remembered signi ﬁcantly more images than the participants in the Distractor group ( p < 0.001) and the no reactivation Control group (p < 0.05), demonstrating mitigated forgetting."
