# Students report that distributed pair programming groups completed tasks without meaningful peer discussion

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r1` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2023 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Qualitative interview data reveal that students in distributed pair programming groups followed teacher demonstrations and completed weekly tasks without substantive peer discussion, and conflicting proposals between pair and larger team reduced engagement. [→ Tsai Chia-Wen 2023](#tsai-chia-wen-2023)

## Evidence

### Tsai Chia-Wen 2023

Tsai Chia-Wen, Lin Michael Yu-Ching, Cheng Yih-Ping, Lee Lan-Yu, Chyr Wen-Li, Lin Chih-Hsien, Lin Jian-Wei, Tsai Meng-Chuan. (2023). The effects of online peer-facilitated learning and distributed pair programming on students’ learning. Computers & Education, 203, 104849. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2023.104849

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Semi-structured interviews with 20 students (5 per group) in week 17, analyzed via content analysis. A student from the distributed pair programming condition described minimal peer interaction and conflicts with the larger team's proposal.

> "We usually followed the teacher's teaching and operations in the paired group and completed our weekly tasks without peer discussion"
