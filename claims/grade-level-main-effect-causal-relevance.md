# Seventh graders outperformed 5th graders on causal relevance of differences and scenario explanations across both phenomena

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Grade level main effects favoured 7th graders for causal relevance of alignable differences and the hot-milk explanation (heat flow) and for causal relevance of the hot-knife explanation (change of state). [→ Mason 2016](#mason-2016)

## Evidence

### Mason 2016

Lucia Mason & Maria Caterina Tornatora (2016). Analogical encoding with and without instructions for case comparison of scientific phenomena. Educational Psychology, 36(2), 391-412. https://doi.org/10.1080/01443410.2014.953038

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

MANOVA grade-level main effect for heat flow scenarios favoured 7th graders on causal relevance (g2p = .03) and explanation of the hot milk scenario (g2p = .02). For change of state, 7th graders outperformed 5th graders on the hot knife explanation, F(1, 327) = 11.71, p = .001, g2p = .03.

> "For the main effect of grade level, univariate tests showed signiﬁcant differences regarding the causal relevance of the alignable differences, F(1, 327) = 9.99, MSE = 1.14, p = .002, g2p = .03, and the explanation of the hot milk scenario, F(1, 327) = 4.18, MSE = .89, p = .042, g2p = .02, in favour of 7th graders."
