## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The interaction between group (between subjects) and test occasion (within subjects) on self-motivated learning strategies was statistically significant, indicating the change over time was not identical across the traditional, cooperative, and self-regulated groups. ## Evidence

### Hatami 2015

Ali Hatami. (2015). The effect of collaborative learning and self-assessment on self-regulation. Educational Research and Reviews. https://doi.org/10.5897/ERR2015.2349

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Repeated-measures ANOVA on the three-group quasi-experiment; Table 1 prints the interaction term with F = 32.09 and significance level 0. The article states the effect of teaching method on motivation scores "is not statistically identical" across groups.

> "The significant level for interactions within and between groups is equal to zero and less than .05. That is, the effect of learning teaching method on the motivation scores of students in traditional, self-regulated, and cooperative groups is not statistically identical."
