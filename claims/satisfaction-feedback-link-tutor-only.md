# Satisfaction with course leadership correlates with perceived benefit from instructor feedback only in tutor-taught groups, not in the lecturer-taught group

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` The satisfaction–feedback-benefit correlation held for tutor-taught groups (r=.61 and r=.59) but not for the lecturer-taught group (r=.02), and the two correlations differed significantly. [→ Herchenröther Julia 2021](#herchenrother-julia-2021)

## Evidence

### Herchenröther Julia 2021

Herchenröther Julia, Tiedemann Elena, Vogel Heiner, Simmenroth Anne. (2021). Which teaching method is more effective in a communication course – role-playing versus simulated patients, taught by tutors or faculty staff? A randomized trial. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001450](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001450)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Course-evaluation correlation analysis in the randomized trial: satisfaction with course leadership correlated with benefiting from instructor feedback "only for the small groups taught by a tutor", with the lecturer-group correlation near zero and the difference between correlations significant.

> "this correlation held only for the small groups taught by a tutor (SP-Tutor: r=.61, p=.001; RP-Tutor: r= .59, p<.001), but not for the lecturer-taught group, r=.02, p=.919, whereby the correlations SP-Lecturer and SP-Tutor significantly differed, z=-2.38, p<.017"
