## Evidence

### S. B. McKagan 2008

S. B. McKagan, K. K. Perkins, M. Dubson, C. Malley, S. Reid, R. LeMaster, and C. E. Wieman. (2008). Developing and Researching PhET simulations for Teaching Quantum Mechanics. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.4503 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0709.4503](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0709.4503)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

End-of-term perception survey in the reformed modern physics course with N = 173 respondents. The quote reports the printed average ranking of 4.0 and the scale; other course aspects ranged from 3.2 (textbook) to 4.3 (posted lecture notes). No effect size is printed.

> "On the end-of-term survey for our modern physics course ( N = 173), the average student ranking of the usefulness of the simulations for their learning on a scale of 1 (not useful) to 5 (a great deal) was 4.0, close to the highest ranked aspect of the course."

### S. B. McKagan 2008 (2)

S. B. McKagan, K. K. Perkins, M. Dubson, C. Malley, S. Reid, R. LeMaster, and C. E. Wieman. (2008). Developing and Researching PhET simulations for Teaching Quantum Mechanics. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.4503 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0709.4503](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0709.4503)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Comment analysis from the same end-of-term survey: 35% of respondents (N = 61) volunteered comments, of which 80% were positive about usefulness. Remaining comments concerned need for guidance (13%), incorrectness or uselessness (8%), technical problems (7%), and simulations not being experiments (3%).

> "35% (N = 61) chose to do so. Of these comments, 80% were positive comments about the usefulness of the simulations"
