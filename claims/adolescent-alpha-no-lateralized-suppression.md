# Adolescent anticipatory alpha-band activity lacks the adult-like lateralized suppression pattern, instead showing higher right-parieto-occipital alpha and stronger modulation for cue-left than cue-right

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Alpha power was higher over the right than left parieto-occipital ROI in both cue conditions, and significantly higher for cue-left than cue-right over the right ROI. [→ Banerjee Snigdha 2015](#banerjee-snigdha-2015)

## Evidence

### Banerjee Snigdha 2015

Banerjee Snigdha, Frey Hans-Peter, Molholm Sophie, Foxe John J. (2015). Interests shape how adolescents pay attention: The interaction of motivation and top-down attentional processes in biasing sensory activations to anticipated events. The European Journal of Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1111/ejn.12810

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Anticipatory alpha (TSE) ANOVA in 24 adolescents: cue × ROI interaction F(1,23) = 6.45, p = .018; main effect of ROI F(1,23) = 13.02, p = .001. Paired t-tests showed "significantly higher alpha power for the cue left versus cue right condition over the right parieto-occipital ROI, t(23) = 2.30, p = .031."

> "Instead, there was higher alpha power over the right vs left ROI for both cue left and cue right conditions, although this difference was less pronounced for the cue right condition"
