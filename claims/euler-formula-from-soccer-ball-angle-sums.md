# The chapter derives Euler's formula from angle sums over a soccer-ball polygon covering and shows the derivation is general to any spherical polygon covering

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` V + F − E = 2 is obtained by summing each face's angle excess, setting the total equal to the sphere's area 4π, and counting each edge twice when summing over faces. [→ Marissa L. Weichman 2018](#marissa-l-weichman-2018)
`q2 i?` The derivation uses no property specific to the soccer ball, so it applies to any polygonal covering of the sphere. [→ Marissa L. Weichman 2018 (2)](#marissa-l-weichman-2018-2)

In the chapter's closing derivation, Robert verifies the formula on the soccer ball by counting "twelve pentagons and twenty hexagons, and I count sixty vertices, thirty-two faces and ninety edges," confirming 60 + 32 − 90 = 2, and the angle-sum argument is then walked through step by step.

### Marissa L. Weichman 2018 (2)

Marissa L. Weichman. (2018). The Forgotten Night: The Number Devil Explores Spherical Geometry. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.3342

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

After the derivation, the Number Devil generalizes: the argument is purely combinatorial over vertices, faces and edges, so it holds for any polygonal covering, and the covering may be distorted into pyramids, cubes, or the icosahedron underlying the soccer ball without changing the formula.

> "you will see that none of our steps actually used the fact that we were dealing with a soccer ball, so this for-mula works for any polygonal covering of the sphere."
