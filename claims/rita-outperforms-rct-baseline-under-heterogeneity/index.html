In multi-period simulations across four worlds, RITA achieves better average cumulative outcome gains than a baseline model assigning individuals based on RCT-derived ATEs in Worlds 2–4 (where heterogeneous treatment effects exist), but not in World 1 (no heterogeneity). → Ilja Cornelisz and Chris van Klaveren 2022

Evidence¶

Ilja Cornelisz and Chris van Klaveren 2022¶

Ilja Cornelisz and Chris van Klaveren. (2022). Recurrent individual treatment assignment: a treatment policy approach to account for heterogeneous treatment effects. npj Science of Learning 7:3. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00117-4

q2 · i? · design · r2

Multi-period simulation (60 periods, 1000 individuals) comparing RITA to an RCT-derived ATE baseline across four treatment-effect worlds. Simulation results show RITA performs better than the baseline model in all worlds except World 1, which lacks heterogeneity.

"Figure 4 visualizes the average cumulative outcome gains over periods for both assignment strategies. These results indicate that RITA performs better than the baseline model in all worlds, except in World 1."

Related Claims¶

  • The baseline RCT-derived ATE model outperforms RITA when no heterogeneity exists and one intervention is uniformly more effective — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • RITA produces an asymmetry: small losses for individuals not benefiting from exploration are offset by large gains for those who do — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • RITA can account for unobserved heterogeneity in treatment effects, while existing frequentist and bandit approaches can only address observed heterogeneity — a broader claim this one bears on
  • RITA's advantage is largest when two interventions have equal ATEs but large unobserved individual differences in relative effectiveness (World 4) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Taking individual treatment effects into account yields better intervention choices than a single uniform assignment — related
