## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Students in generative-instruction classes spent a significantly greater proportion of class time on questions requiring higher-order cognitive skills (Hedge's g = 2.7) than students in active-instruction classes. [→ Andrews Tessa C. 2019](#andrews-tessa-c-2019)

## Evidence

### Andrews Tessa C. 2019

Andrews Tessa C., Auerbach Anna Jo J., Grant Emily F. (2019). Exploring the Relationship between Teacher Knowledge and Active-Learning Implementation in Large College Biology Courses. CBE—Life Sciences Education 18(4). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-01-0010

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Welch's t test comparing proportion of class time on HOC questions between five generative and eight active instructors. Mean proportions were 0.32 (SD 0.07) for generative versus 0.09 (SD 0.08) for active instructors; the article calls g = 2.7 "a very large effect".

> "Students in classes using generative instruction also spent a significantly greater proportion of class time on questions that required HOCs (t = 5.03, p = 0.001). The effect size for this comparison was 2.7 (95% confidence interval: 1.0–4.4), which is also considered a very large effect (Maher et al., 2013)."
