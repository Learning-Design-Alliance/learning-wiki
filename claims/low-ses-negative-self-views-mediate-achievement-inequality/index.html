Evidence¶

Brummelman 2024¶

Brummelman, E., van Atteveldt, N., Wolf, S. & Sierksma, J. (2024). Using social and behavioral science to address achievement inequality. npj Science of Learning, 9, 44. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00259-1

q2 · i? · associational · r2

The editorial reports, citing Hofer et al., a secondary analysis of PISA 2018 data from 520,729 students in 70 countries: low-SES students "had more negative self-views", which predicted poorer achievement and partially mediated the SES-achievement association. No effect size is printed.

"Using data from the 2018 Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA), including 520,729 15-year-old students from 70 countries, Hofer et al. demonstrated that those from low-SES backgrounds indeed had more negative self-views"
