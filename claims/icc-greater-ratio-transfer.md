# Inventing with contrasting cases before being told produces more spontaneous transfer to semantically unrelated ratio phenomena than being told first

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` In Experiment 1, ICC students used ratio to describe surface pressure more often than T&P students on the immediate transfer task (d = .27). [→ Schwartz 2011](#schwartz-2011)
`q3 i1` In Experiment 1, ICC students transferred more on the delayed spring-constant task three weeks after instruction (d = .33). [→ Schwartz 2011 (2)](#schwartz-2011-2)
`q3 i1` In Experiment 2, ICC students showed greater transfer on the combined posttest (d = .31). [→ Schwartz 2011 (3)](#schwartz-2011-3)

## Evidence

### Schwartz 2011

Schwartz, D. L., Chase, C. C., Oppezzo, M. A., & Chin, D. B. (2011). Practicing versus inventing with contrasting cases: The effects of telling first on learning and transfer. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://aaalab.stanford.edu/transfer.html. [doi:10.1037/a0025140](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0025140)

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Immediate transfer task on aerosol-can plate pressure given at the start of Day 8, before ICC students received the ratio lecture; data from one class were excluded due to an implementation error.

> "The means in Table 1 indicate that the T&P students used ratio to describe surface pressure significantly less often than the ICC students, who had not yet been told about the formulas or the importance of ratio; F(1,94) = 6.8, p = .011, d = .27."

### Schwartz 2011 (2)

Schwartz, D. L., Chase, C. C., Oppezzo, M. A., & Chin, D. B. (2011). Practicing versus inventing with contrasting cases: The effects of telling first on learning and transfer. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://aaalab.stanford.edu/transfer.html. [doi:10.1037/a0025140](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0025140)

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Delayed transfer on the trampoline spring-constant problem, embedded in a biology test 21 days after instruction ended and after both groups had received the ratio lecture.

> "Table 1 indicates that the ICC students performed significantly better than the T&P students on the delayed transfer test; F(1,124) = 13.6; p < .001, d = .33."

### Schwartz 2011 (3)

Schwartz, D. L., Chase, C. C., Oppezzo, M. A., & Chin, D. B. (2011). Practicing versus inventing with contrasting cases: The effects of telling first on learning and transfer. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://aaalab.stanford.edu/transfer.html. [doi:10.1037/a0025140](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0025140)

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r3`

Experiment 2 combined the word-problem and delayed transfer tests into a single posttest one week after instruction, with blind instructors and random assignment to one- or four-case transfer problems.

> "With respect to posttest performance, the ICC students exhibited greater transfer; F(1, 111) = 10.6, MSE = 0.20, p = .002, d = .31; there was an effect of achievement on transfer; F(1, 111) = 9.2, p = .003; but there was no treatment by achievement interaction; F(1, 111) = 0.18, p = .67."
