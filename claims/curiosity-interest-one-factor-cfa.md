# State epistemic curiosity and situational interest items load on a single latent factor in confirmatory factor analysis

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A one-factor CFA solution including both epistemic curiosity and situational interest items provided the best model fit, with a two-factor solution only fitting equally well when the latent correlation was .99. [→ Henk G. Schmidt & Jerome I. Rotgans 2021](#henk-g-schmidt-jerome-i-rotgans-2021)

## Evidence

### Henk G. Schmidt & Jerome I. Rotgans 2021

Henk G. Schmidt & Jerome I. Rotgans. (2021). Epistemic Curiosity and Situational Interest: Distant Cousins or Identical Twins?. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09539-9

`q3 · i?`

Confirmatory factor analysis of 158 secondary-school students' responses to both measures. The one-factor model fit best ("χ2 (35, N = 158) = 32.65, p = .58, CFI = 1.00, RMSEA = .00"), and the two-factor model required a latent correlation of .99 to fit comparably.

> "The model fit indices of the four models suggest that the one-factor solution in which all situational interest and epistemic curiosity items load on a single factor, resulted in the best model fit: χ2 (35, N = 158) = 32.65, p = .58, CFI = 1.00, RMSEA = .00"
