Ouhao Chen, Fred Paas, John Sweller. (2022). Reply to Sana et al.'s (2022) Commentary on Rest-from-Deliberate-Learning as a Mechanism for the Spacing Effect. Educational Psychology Review, 34, 1851–1858. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-022-09678-1

q2 · i1 · theoretical · r2

Quoted from Sana et al.'s commentary as reported in this article: in Ostrow et al. (2015), middle school students practicing angles, surface area, and probability problems showed no overall interleaving benefit (Hedge's g = 0.22), but "a large interleaving benefit for low-skilled students, Hedge's g = 0.60".

"participants who practiced angles, surface area, and probability problems in an interleaved manner did not perform better on a final test than did those who practiced the problems in a blocked manner, Hedge's g = 0.22. What CPS omit, however, is that there was in fact a large interleaving benefit for low-skilled students, Hedge's g = 0.60."

Related Claims¶

  • Interleaving category examples improves inductive category learning for visual and mathematical materials, but not for expository texts or word categories — related
  • Interleaved practice produces higher mathematics test scores than blocked practice in a classroom experiment — related
  • The interleaving benefit is not caused by spacing, and the benefit extends to delayed tests — related
  • Evidence on working memory capacity as a moderator of the interleaving effect is mixed, but WM does not appear to be a major barrier to classroom use — related
  • Interleaving benefits are small for mathematical tasks and absent or reversed for expository texts and words — related
