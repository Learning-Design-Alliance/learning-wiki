# Simulation studies show standard IEV factor analysis yields well-fitting solutions bearing no resemblance to participants' idiosyncratic personal factor models

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In simulated populations where each participant obeys a different personal factor model, standard q-factor solutions (q = 1 or q = 2) fit satisfactorily yet bear no resemblance to the individual models. [→ Molenaar 2004](#molenaar-2004)
`q2 i?` Likelihood ratio tests fail to reject one- or two-common-factor solutions even with substantial sample sizes, and nothing in the solutions indicates the heterogeneity present. [→ Molenaar 2004](#molenaar-2004)

## Evidence

### Molenaar 2004

Molenaar, P. C. M. (2004). A Manifesto on Psychology as Idiographic Science: Bringing the Person Back Into Scientific Psychology, This Time Forever. Measurement, 2(4), 201-218. https://www.fmg.uva.nl/ml/publicaties_ml.cfm

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Converging simulation studies (Molenaar, 1997; Molenaar, 1999; Molenaar, Huizenga, & Nesselroade, 2002) using the same scenario: cross-sectional, longitudinal, and simulated twin data sampled from behavior spaces in which each participant obeys a different personal factor model. The article reports that "likelihood ratio tests are unable to reject solutions with one or two common factors, even with substantial sample sizes."

> "a standard q-factor solution, with q = 1 or q = 2, yields satisfactorily fitting solutions that bear no resemblance to the idiosyncratic factor models de- scribing the life history of each individual participant in the sample."
