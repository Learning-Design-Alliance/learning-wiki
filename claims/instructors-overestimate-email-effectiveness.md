`q1 i?` Two instructors generally correctly predicted which email condition was better, but both predicted a 10% action-rate difference when the observed aggregate effect was under 5% for attempt rate and under 2 hours for start time. [→ Angela Zavaleta-Bernuy 2022](#angela-zavaleta-bernuy-2022)

## Evidence

### Angela Zavaleta-Bernuy 2022

Angela Zavaleta-Bernuy, Ziwen Han, Hammad Shaikh, Qi Yin Zheng, Lisa-Angelique Lim, Anna Rafferty, Andrew Petersen, and Joseph Jay Williams. 2022. How can Email Interventions Increase Students’ Completion of Online Homework? A Case Study Using A/B Comparisons. In LAK22: 12th International Learning Analytics and Knowledge Conference (LAK22), March 21–25, 2022, Online, USA. ACM, New York, NY, USA. https://doi.org/10.1145/3506860.3506874

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Survey of two computer science instructors with 10 years of experience, who predicted condition effectiveness without seeing data. The article reports instructors "generally correctly predict the better condition" but overestimated effectiveness: observed attempt-rate effect was <5% and start-time effect <2 hours, versus predicted 10% action-rate differences. No effect size is printed.

> "the overall effectiveness of emails in Table 2 was <5% for attempt rate (statistically significant) and <2 hour increase for start time (non-statistically significant), but both instructors predicted a 10% action ate difference."
