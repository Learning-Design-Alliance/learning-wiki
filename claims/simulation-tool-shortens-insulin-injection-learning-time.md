## Evidence

### Liang K 2021

Liang K, Xie Q, Nie J, Deng J. (2021). Study on the effect of education for insulin injection in diabetic patients with new simulation tools. Medicine. 2021;100:14(e25424). https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000025424

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Results of a 2-arm randomized quasi-experimental study of 120 insulin-naive diabetic patients; the simulation group's detailed teaching time was shorter, with the article reporting "8.0 minutes, 6/11 vs10.0 minutes, 8/13, P = .001". No effect size printed.

> "the time taken to complete the detailed teaching stage was shorter for the test group compared to that of the control group (median, 25/75-percentile 8.0 minutes, 6/11 vs10.0 minutes, 8/13, P = .001)"

### Liang K 2021 (2)

Liang K, Xie Q, Nie J, Deng J. (2021). Study on the effect of education for insulin injection in diabetic patients with new simulation tools. Medicine. 2021;100:14(e25424). https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000025424

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same 120-patient two-group study; mastery time favored the simulation group, with medians "10.0 minutes, 8/13 vs 16.0 minutes, 13/18, P < .001". Simple teaching stage time did not differ (P = .377).

> "the time taken to master the technique in the trial group was shorter than that in the traditional group (median, 25/75-percentile 10.0 minutes, 8/13 vs 16.0 minutes, 13/18, P < .001)"
