# Closed-note students outperform open-note students on Bloom's level 3 questions by a small margin the authors attribute to noise

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` On Bloom's level 3 (prediction) questions, closed-note students performed better than open-note students, a small difference of 0.16 SD the authors speculate may be noise. [→ Sato 2015](#sato-2015)

## Evidence

### Sato 2015

Sato, B. K., He, W., Warschauer, M., & Kadandale, P. (2015). The Grass Isn't Always Greener: Perceptions of and Performance on Open-Note Exams. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-08-0121

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within the same randomized comparison of paper quiz performance, Bloom's 3 questions were the one exception: "closed-note students performed better." Table 2 shows the open-note coefficient −0.089 (p = 0.016). No standardized effect size is printed for this contrast beyond the SD difference reported separately.

> "there was no significant difference between the two groups with the exception of questions of Bloom's level 3, for which closed-note students performed better."

### Sato 2015 (2)

Sato, B. K., He, W., Warschauer, M., & Kadandale, P. (2015). The Grass Isn't Always Greener: Perceptions of and Performance on Open-Note Exams. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-08-0121

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The authors' own caveat on the same regression analysis: the small magnitude "(a difference of 0.16 SD)" and large sample may make the Bloom's 3 result "merely noise in the data."

> "We speculate that the Bloom's 3 result may be merely noise in the data, both because of the large sample size and the small magnitude of this result (a difference of 0.16 SD)."
