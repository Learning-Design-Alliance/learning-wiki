# A single feedback trial on the number 150 shifted most children from logarithmic to linear estimation abruptly

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Feedback on the single number 150 increased the percentage of children best fit by the linear function from 0% on the pretest to 85% after one feedback problem. [→ Opfer 2007](#opfer-2007)

## Evidence

### Opfer 2007

Opfer, J. E., & Siegler, R. S. (2007). Representational change and children's numerical estimation. Cognitive Psychology 55 (2007) 169–195. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2006.09.002

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Trial-block analysis in the microgenetic study of 61 logarithmic-pattern second graders. On trial block 1 the 150-feedback group exceeded the no-feedback (p < .001), 5-feedback (p < .005), and 725-feedback (p < .05) groups; only a p-value is printed for the 0% to 85% shift.

> "Providing feedback on the single number 150 increased the percentage of children for whom the linear function provided the better Wt from 0% on the pretest to 85% on the test phase after that one feedback problem (p < .001)"
