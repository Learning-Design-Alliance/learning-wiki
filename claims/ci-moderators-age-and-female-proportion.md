# Age moderates both CI effects and female proportion moderates the acute CI effect, while other tested variables do not

## Evidence

### Achraf Ammar 2024

Achraf Ammar, Khaled Trabelsi, Mohamed Ali Boujelbane, Atef Salem, Omar Boukhris, Jordan M. Glenn, Piotr Zmijewski, Haitham A. Jahrami, Hamdi Chtourou, Wolfgang I. Schöllhorn. (2024). The Effects of Contextual Interference Learning on the Acquisition and Relatively Permanent Gains in Skilled Performance: A Critical Systematic Review with Multilevel Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09892-z

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Multivariate subgroup analyses and meta-regression across the 36 studies tested 10 moderator variables (age groups, experience level, sports/skills, testing protocol, sex proportion, intervention duration, sessions, trials, between-test duration). Only age (p < 0.0001) and female proportion (p = 0.009) reached significance; no effect sizes are printed.

> "Out of 10 tested variables, only the age significantly moderated both CI effects (p  < 0.0001 for both Δ pre-post and Δ pre-retention) and the female proportion significantly moderated only the first CI effect (p  = 0.009 for Δ pre-post)."
