Evidence¶

Amogh Sirnoorkar and Omkar Mamidpalliwar 2026¶

Amogh Sirnoorkar and Omkar Mamidpalliwar. (2026). Students' Epistemological Beliefs and their Chatbot Preferences in AI-mediated Physics Learning. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29385 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.29385

q2 · i0 · associational · r2

Kruskal-Wallis tests (α = 0.05, scores not normally distributed) on 1048 overlapping EBAPS/activity responses. Table III prints effect sizes of 0.004 (Total), 0.007 (Axis 4), and 0.006 (Axis 5); the article states "the small effect sizes indicate that chatbot preference accounted for only a modest proportion of the variability".

"The results indicate that at least one pair of chatbot preference groups (Inquiry, Answer, and Combination) differed significantly in their overall EBAPS scores and in their scores on Axis 4 (balance between extremes of absolutism and relativism) and Axis 5 (success in science through innate ability versus effort)."

Amogh Sirnoorkar and Omkar Mamidpalliwar 2026 (2)¶

Amogh Sirnoorkar and Omkar Mamidpalliwar. (2026). Students' Epistemological Beliefs and their Chatbot Preferences in AI-mediated Physics Learning. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29385

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Robustness check applying a Bonferroni-adjusted significance level (α=0.0083) across the six omnibus Kruskal-Wallis tests; the printed nominal results did not survive the correction.

"Under this more conservative criterion, the omnibus differences observed for the total score and Axes 4 and 5 no longer highlighted statistical significance."
