## Subclaims
`q2 i?` At posttest, treatment participants perceived that an app might threaten smartwatch data (mean 4.8) while controls lacked this knowledge (mean 3.4). [→ Meredydd Williams 2019](#meredydd-williams-2019)
`q2 i?` Treatment users reported greater confidence in their own understanding of smartwatch privacy than controls (mean 4.6 vs 3.8). [→ Meredydd Williams 2019 (2)](#meredydd-williams-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Meredydd Williams 2019

Meredydd Williams, Jason R C Nurse, Sadie Creese. (2019). Smartwatch Games: Encouraging Privacy-Protective Behaviour in a Longitudinal Study. [doi:10.1016/j.chb.2019.04.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2019.04.026)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Posttest questionnaire responses from the 10 participants after the 18-day posttest phase. The article reports group means only and states that the sample size impedes significance testing, so these are descriptive comparisons.

> "Whereas the treatment group perceived a threat ( ¯x = 4.8), controls appeared to lack this knowledge ( ¯x = 3.4)."

### Meredydd Williams 2019 (2)

Meredydd Williams, Jason R C Nurse, Sadie Creese. (2019). Smartwatch Games: Encouraging Privacy-Protective Behaviour in a Longitudinal Study. [doi:10.1016/j.chb.2019.04.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2019.04.026)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Posttest confidence question in the identical posttest survey; the article reports descriptive means (4.6 vs 3.8) with no significance test applied to concern or opinion scores.

> "As expected, treatment users appeared to have greater self-efﬁcacy than the other group ( ¯x = 4.6 vs 3.8)."
