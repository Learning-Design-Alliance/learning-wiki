Evidence¶

Lefèvre 2024¶

Lefèvre, E., Law, J. M., Prado, J., Anders, R., & Cavalli, E. (2024). Reading Comprehension Resiliency in Adolescents with and without Dyslexia relates to Vocabulary, Listening Comprehension and Socioeconomic Status. https://osf.io/fzyeh/?view_only=84fc51248a0d48ba9a505835e525efdc

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Second mediation model within the path analysis on the merged sample: SES predicted vocabulary, and the SES-to-listening-comprehension association operated indirectly through vocabulary (A*B significant, p = .03).

"Vocabulary itself was associated with SES (A: β = 0.34, SE = 0.09, Z = 3.52, p < .001). The direct effect of SES on listening comprehension (C’: β = 0.16, SE = 0.10, Z = 1.56, p = .12) was fully mediated by vocabulary and the indirect effect was significant (A*B: β = 0.09, SE = 0.04, Z = 2.13, p = .03)."

Lefèvre 2024 (2)¶

Lefèvre, E., Law, J. M., Prado, J., Anders, R., & Cavalli, E. (2024). Reading Comprehension Resiliency in Adolescents with and without Dyslexia relates to Vocabulary, Listening Comprehension and Socioeconomic Status. https://osf.io/fzyeh/?view_only=84fc51248a0d48ba9a505835e525efdc

q2 · i? · associational · r1

The path analysis printed a significant total indirect effect of SES on the RCR index (β = 0.12, p = .012), which the authors interpret, following Hayes (2022), as an indirect association between SES and RCR.

"the total indirect effect ((ABD)+(CD)+(AE)), representing the pathway from SES to RCR via all possible indirect paths (i.e., mediations with vocabulary and listening comprehension variables), was significant (β = 0.12, SE = 0.05, Z = 2.53, p = .012)."
