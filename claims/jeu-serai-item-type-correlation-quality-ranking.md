# In JEU SERAI, questionnaire-like in-game items (diary ordering, mini game ranking, narrative questionnaires) correlated best with RIASEC questionnaire results, while other item types correlated poorly

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Mini game diary ordering achieved 78% correct correlations with the right RIASEC type, the best of all item types. [→ Guardiola 2015](#guardiola-2015)
`q2 i?` All other item types achieved only 10 to 22% correct correlations. [→ Guardiola 2015 (2)](#guardiola-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Guardiola 2015

Guardiola, E., & Natkin, S. (2015). A Game Design Methodology for Generating a Psychological Profile of Players. In Serious Games Analytics, Methodologies for Performance Measurement, Assessment, and Improvement. Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-05834-4_16

`q2 · i?`

Correlation analysis of the first JEU SERAI test session, comparing item aggregations to the IRMR3 reference test using the Bravais-Pearson coefficient. The authors report that through psychometric protocols the method "successfully ranked item type quality", with diary ordering at "78% success".

> "“Mini game diary ordering” tops the chart with 78% success. In terms of how they are created , they are close to a classical psychometric questionnaire."

### Guardiola 2015 (2)

Guardiola, E., & Natkin, S. (2015). A Game Design Methodology for Generating a Psychological Profile of Players. In Serious Games Analytics, Methodologies for Performance Measurement, Assessment, and Improvement. Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-05834-4_16

`q2 · i?`

The same correlation analysis of JEU SERAI test data found that besides diary ordering, mini game ranking and narrative questionnaires, remaining item types showed only "10 to 22% correct correlations" with the right RIASEC type.

> "All the other types of items get very bad results: 10 to 22% correct correlations. They help us to reveal new questions."
