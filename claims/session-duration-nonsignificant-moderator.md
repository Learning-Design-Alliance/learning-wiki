# Duration of an average PLL session (50–180 minutes) did not significantly moderate the effect on cognitive achievement; the descriptive 60-versus-120-minute advantage did not generalise across durations

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` No statistically significant relationship was found between session duration and the effect size (coefficient −0.001, p = 0.31); shorter sessions did not have significantly better effects than longer sessions. [→ Zha 2019](#zha-2019)

## Evidence

### Zha 2019

Zha, Shenghua; Estes, Michele D.; and Xu, Ling. (2019). A meta-analysis on the effect of duration, task, and training in peer-led learning. Journal of Peer Learning, 12. https://ro.uow.edu.au/ajpl/vol12/iss1/2

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Conditional meta-regression of session duration (50 to 180 minutes, k = 28): printed coefficient −0.001 (p = 0.31, 95% CI −0.003 to 0.001). The article states that across all durations, shorter sessions did not have significantly better effects than longer sessions.

> "No statistical significance was found regarding variations in the duration of an average PLL session (Table 3 ). Among 28 studies, a PLL session was typically hosted for 60 or 120 minutes."

### Zha 2019 (2)

Zha, Shenghua; Estes, Michele D.; and Xu, Ling. (2019). A meta-analysis on the effect of duration, task, and training in peer-led learning. Journal of Peer Learning, 12. https://ro.uow.edu.au/ajpl/vol12/iss1/2

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Descriptive contrast between the two most common duration types in Table 3 (60 minutes, 9 studies; 120 minutes, 10 studies): 60-minute sessions outperformed 120-minute sessions by 0.2 standard deviation units, but no effect size for this specific contrast was printed, and the article notes it occurred only between those two duration types.

> "Groups in 60-minute sessions outperformed groups in 120-minute sessions by 0.2 standard deviation units. However, when examined across all durations, shorter sessions did not have significantly better ES than longer sessions."
