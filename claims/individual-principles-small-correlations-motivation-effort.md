# Individual instructional design principles show small correlations with self-efficacy, mental effort, and transfer performance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q2` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Applying the example-first-principle positively correlated with average self-efficacy in the learning phase (r = .183), while applying the lowest-level-first-principle negatively correlated with self-efficacy (r = −.257; r = −.268 after learning), perceived competence (r = −.219), and procedural transfer performance (r = −.235). [→ Milou van Harsel 2022](#milou-van-harsel-2022)

## Evidence

### Milou van Harsel 2022

Milou van Harsel, Vincent Hoogerheide, Eva Janssen, Peter Verkoeijen, Tamara van Gog. (2022). How do higher education students regulate their learning with video modeling examples, worked examples, and practice problems? Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-022-09589-2

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Pearson correlation analysis between individual principle scores and cognitive/motivational variables in the same sample. The article also reports lowest-level-first-principle correlations of r = −.257, r = −.268, r = −.219, and r = −.235, and simple-to-complex-principle correlations with mental effort of r = −.180 and r = −.183.
