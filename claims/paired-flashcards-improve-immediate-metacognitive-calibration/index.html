Evidence¶

Imundo 2024¶

Imundo, M. N., Zung, I., Whatley, M. C., & Pan, S. C. (2024). When Two Learners Are Better Than One: Using Flashcards with a Partner Improves Metacognitive Accuracy. Metacognition and Learning. https://link.springer.com/journal/11409/articles

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Experiment 1, a preregistered between-subjects experiment with 152 undergraduates learning GRE word-definition pairs, compared metacognitive calibration (gJOL minus actual test performance). Individual learners were "overconfident (M = .20, SD = .22)" while paired learners were near-perfectly calibrated; the difference was significant at p < .001.

"Individual learners were overconfident (M = .20, SD = .22), whereas Paired learners were relatively accurate (M = .00, SD = .22), t (149) = 5.66, p < .001, 95% CI [.13, .28]."

Imundo 2024 (2)¶

Imundo, M. N., Zung, I., Whatley, M. C., & Pan, S. C. (2024). When Two Learners Are Better Than One: Using Flashcards with a Partner Improves Metacognitive Accuracy. Metacognition and Learning. https://link.springer.com/journal/11409/articles

q3 · i1 · causal · r3

Experiment 2 (141 participants, dropping prohibited, initial study phase added) replicated the immediate-test calibration difference with a smaller effect, d = 0.36, p = .034, confirming the miscalibration "cannot simply be attributed to a lack of exposure to dropped items."

"Again replicating the results of Experiment 1, Individual learners (M = .18, SD = .27) were more overconfident than Paired learners (M = .08, SD = .26), t (139) = 2.14, p = .034, d = 0.36, 95% CI [.007, .19]."
