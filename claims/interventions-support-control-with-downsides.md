## Evidence

### Ulrik Lyngs 2020

Ulrik Lyngs, Kai Lukoff, Petr Slovak, William Seymour, Helena Webb, Marina Jirotka, Jun Zhao, Max Van Kleek, Nigel Shadbolt. (2020). 'I Just Want to Hack Myself to Not Get Distracted': Evaluating Design Interventions for Self-Control on Facebook. CHI '20, April 25–30, 2020, Honolulu, HI, USA. https://doi.org/10.1145/3313831.3376672

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Thematic analysis of free-text survey responses and post-study interviews (RQ3): interventions "supported control" by helping participants stay on task, but goal reminders were "annoying/frustrating" and newsfeed removal caused "fear of missing out"; a survey item showed participants divided about the experimental conditions' impact on control.

> "The qualitative data suggested that C goal and C no-feed sup- ported control in the sense of helping participants avoid un- intended use and staying on task, but at the cost of being annoying/frustrating (Cgoal) or leading to fear of missing out (Cno-feed)."
