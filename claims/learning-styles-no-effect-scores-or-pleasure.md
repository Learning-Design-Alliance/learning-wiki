### Lyra 2016

Lyra, K. T., Isotani, S., Reis, R. C. D., Marques, L. B., Pedro, L. Z., Jaques, P. A., & Bitencourt, I. I. (2016). Infographics or Graphics+Text: Which Material is Best for Robust Learning?. Proceedings of the IEEE International Conference on Advanced Learning Technologies (ICALT), to appear. [doi:10.1109/icalt.2016.83](https://doi.org/10.1109/icalt.2016.83)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

New-ILS questionnaire (visual/verbal classification) crossed with material type in the randomized study; the authors note this follows researchers who discuss the lack of evidence that learning styles benefit learning.

> "in our data analys is we did not find evidence that learning styles have an e ffect on the number of correct answers"

### Lyra 2016 (2)

Lyra, K. T., Isotani, S., Reis, R. C. D., Marques, L. B., Pedro, L. Z., Jaques, P. A., & Bitencourt, I. I. (2016). Infographics or Graphics+Text: Which Material is Best for Robust Learning?. Proceedings of the IEEE International Conference on Advanced Learning Technologies (ICALT), to appear. [doi:10.1109/icalt.2016.83](https://doi.org/10.1109/icalt.2016.83)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Table II pleasure means: infographic visual 5.2143 / verbal 6.4286; graphic+text visual 5.7679 / verbal 4.4000. Random assignment left unequal visual/verbal counts per group (10/4 and 8/5), which the authors flag as a limitation.

> "Despite the difference between the averages, it is not possible to say that visual learners enjoyed more using graphic+text than infographics, since t-test did not reveal a significant difference"
