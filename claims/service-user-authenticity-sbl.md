## Evidence

### Dairo Yetunde M. 2024

Dairo Yetunde M., Hunter Kirsty, Ishaku Timothy. (2024). The impact of simulation-based learning on the knowledge, attitude and performance of physiotherapy students on practice placement. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05718-2

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview findings from the 'Working with Service Users' theme: participants reported increased confidence communicating with unfamiliar service users and gathering detailed subjective findings; one noted that with service users "you can find out more about them than you can from, maybe, a case study."

> "Interacting with service users improved the simulation placement by allowing participants to practice transferable skills such as communication and clinical reasoning, which are essential for their practice placements. Participants reported increased confidence in communicating with service users, as they were unfamiliar to them, requiring them to think on their feet more readily."
