## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A significant sex-by-increase interaction was observed: self-rated self-efficacy scores rose from pre-test to post-test for both men and women, with a larger rise among men than women, in one uncontrolled pre-post study. [→ Meurling Lisbet 2013](#meurling-lisbet-2013)

## Evidence

### Meurling Lisbet 2013

Meurling Lisbet, Hedman Leif, Sandahl Christer, Felländer-Tsai Li, Wallin Carl-Johan. (2013). Systematic simulation-based team training in a Swedish intensive care unit: a diverse response among critical care professions. BMJ quality & safety 22(6):485-494. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjqs-2012-000994

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Three-way repeated measures ANOVA on matched self-efficacy questionnaires (n=102), performed because of uneven sex distribution across professions. The article reports the interaction "(p=0.022)" and simple effects for women (p=0.016, effect 0.21, 95% CI 0.039 to 0.371) and men (p<0.001, effect 0.59, 95% CI 0.308 to 0.876).

> "There was a significant interaction between increase and sex (p=0.022), displaying a larger effect for men."
