# Base strength predicted outcome improvement better than growth in aggregate, and pooled regression estimates exceeded school fixed effects estimates

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Most outcome improvement was, in aggregate, better predicted by strength in a measure coming into the school year than by growth during the year. [→ Hart 2020](#hart-2020)
`q2 i?` Measures generally predicted larger outcome changes in the pooled regression model than in the more conservative school fixed effects model. [→ Hart 2020 (2)](#hart-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Hart 2020

Hart, H., Young, C., Chen, A., Zou, A., & Allensworth, E.M. (2020). Supporting school improvement: Early findings from reexamination of the 5Essentials survey. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/supporting-school-improvement

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Results overview comparing base strength and growth predictors across outcomes. The report gives the example that "base strength in Student-Teacher Trust predicted improvement in college enrollment better than growth in Student-Teacher Trust."

> "We also observed that most outcome improvement was, in aggregate, better predicted by having strength in a measure coming into the school year, rather than by growing over the school year in question."

### Hart 2020 (2)

Hart, H., Young, C., Chen, A., Zou, A., & Allensworth, E.M. (2020). Supporting school improvement: Early findings from reexamination of the 5Essentials survey. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/supporting-school-improvement

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Comparison of the two statistical models. The report explains the pooled regression is a more liberal test while the fixed effect model is more conservative, since schools with consistent measure scores and outcome growth contribute no within-school variation to estimate.

> "In general, measures predicted a larger change in outcomes in the pooled regression model than the fixed effects model."
