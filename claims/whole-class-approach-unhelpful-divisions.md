## Evidence

### Genovesi Elisa 2024

Genovesi Elisa, Gaches Akhina, McKenzie Judith, Hanlon Charlotte, Hoekstra Rosa A. (2024). Inclusive Strategies for Children With Developmental Disabilities in Mainstream Classrooms in African Countries: A Systematic Review of Stakeholder Experiences, Attitudes, and Perspectives. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543241288247

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Thematic synthesis of qualitative study reports. Strategies such as teaching social skills were applied to everyone, and adaptations were applied to each learner differently without clear distinctions between children with and without disabilities, framed as valuable in collectivistic African cultures.

> "the evidence base for inclusive education recommends that strategies are implemented with a whole-class approach (e.g. Norwich & Lewis, 2013), to support effective inclusion by targeting the needs of all children"

### Genovesi Elisa 2024 (2)

Genovesi Elisa, Gaches Akhina, McKenzie Judith, Hanlon Charlotte, Hoekstra Rosa A. (2024). Inclusive Strategies for Children With Developmental Disabilities in Mainstream Classrooms in African Countries: A Systematic Review of Stakeholder Experiences, Attitudes, and Perspectives. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543241288247

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Thematic synthesis across studies in Zimbabwe, South Africa, Uganda, and Botswana. Examples include extra schoolwork assigned to children with ADHD to keep them busy, described as "highly questionable", remedial classes misused for strategies that should benefit all, and fixed ability groups that learners experienced as stigmatizing.

> "highlighted how some of the strategies used for task/curriculum differentiation and to give extra attention to children with learning and behavioral difficulties could foster discrimination of the same learners, when they were applied segregating children with DD, rather than addressing each learner’s needs."
