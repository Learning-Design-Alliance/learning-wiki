## Evidence

### Lockwood 2017

Lockwood, J., & Mooney, A. (2017). Computational Thinking in Education: Where does it fit? A systematic literary review. Maynooth University, Ireland. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1703.07659](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1703.07659)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes this to Davies's study in an introductory programming course using structured pseudocode "designed to highlight and facilitate algorithmic construction", with language knowledge compared in a final exam.

> "The formal programming language (C++) was only introduced in the final three  weeks of the course and it was found that their knowledge of the language was equal to those who were taught C++ for 13 weeks, as examined in a final exam."

### Lockwood 2017 (2)

Lockwood, J., & Mooney, A. (2017). Computational Thinking in Education: Where does it fit? A systematic literary review. Maynooth University, Ireland. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1703.07659](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1703.07659)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Davies's survey-based finding that the pseudocode-first approach may especially benefit women's attitudes and confidence in programming; the review presents this as an author interpretation.

> "They also found that women especially might benefit from this method of teaching, particularly in terms of their attitudes and confidence of programming."
