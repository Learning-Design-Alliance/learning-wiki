## Evidence

### Salomon F. Itza-Ortiz 2003

Salomon F. Itza-Ortiz, N. Sanjay Rebello and Dean Zollman. (2003). Students' Models of Newton's Second Law in Mechanics and Electromagnetism. Pre-print, Kansas State University. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/25/1/011](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/25/1/011)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

In Phase II, after instruction on electric fields, students answered a modified CSEM question about a charged sphere in a uniform electric field plus two Phase I mechanics problems. "Seven of the students used a pure Newtonian model on the first interview of Phase II".

> "Students transferred their models to the second semester.  Seven of the students used a pure Newtonian model on the first interview of Phase II."

### Salomon F. Itza-Ortiz 2003 (2)

Salomon F. Itza-Ortiz, N. Sanjay Rebello and Dean Zollman. (2003). Students' Models of Newton's Second Law in Mechanics and Electromagnetism. Pre-print, Kansas State University. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/25/1/011](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/25/1/011)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The authors' interpretation of the Phase II interviews is that in abstract electromagnetism contexts students "are more likely to base their responses on instruction and not  on intuitive reasoning" compared with mechanics contexts.

> "It is also important to note that in electromagnetism contexts, when students are faced w ith abstract contexts, they are more likely to base their responses on instruction and not  on intuitive reasoning as they might do in mechanics contexts."
