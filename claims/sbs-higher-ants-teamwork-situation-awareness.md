## Evidence

### Yang Xiuli 2026

Yang Xiuli, Jiang Hui, Wang Huan, Wang Lixia, Chen Lijian. (2026). Effect of scenario-based simulation on students’ learning outcomes and satisfaction in perioperative anaphylaxis: a pilot randomised controlled trial. BMJ Open Quality. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjoq-2025-003713

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Non-technical skills were rated with the ANTS system (range 15–60), contextually adapted for perioperative anaphylaxis, by two blinded trained tutors. The SBS group's total of "47.5 (44.0–51.0) vs 43.0 (42.0–48.0), p=0.002" was higher.

> "Besides, the ANTS total score was also higher in the SBS group (47.5 (44.0–51.0) vs 43.0 (42.0–48.0), p=0.002)."

### Yang Xiuli 2026 (2)

Yang Xiuli, Jiang Hui, Wang Huan, Wang Lixia, Chen Lijian. (2026). Effect of scenario-based simulation on students’ learning outcomes and satisfaction in perioperative anaphylaxis: a pilot randomised controlled trial. BMJ Open Quality. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjoq-2025-003713

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Domain-level ANTS analysis in the same scenario test showed only teamwork and situation awareness favoured SBS ("15.0 (13.0–17.0) vs 13.0 (12.7–15.0), p=0.002; 10.0 (10.0–11.0) vs 9.0 (8.0–10.0), p<0.001"), while task management and decision-making showed no significant differences.

> "For specific items of ANTS, only teamwork and situation awareness were higher in the SBS group than in the VBT group (15.0 (13.0–17.0) vs 13.0 (12.7–15.0), p=0.002; 10.0 (10.0–11.0) vs 9.0 (8.0–10.0), p<0.001, respectively)."
