### Pérez-Fuentes María del Carmen 2026

Pérez-Fuentes María del Carmen, Molero Jurado María del Mar, Romanos-Rodríguez Armando, Martos Martínez África, Gómez-Gómez Francisco J., Aguado-Campos Javier. (2026). Self-efficacy and Communication in Health Personnel After Simulation Training in Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation: A Quasi-experimental Study. Inquiry. https://doi.org/10.1177/00469580251411471

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Spearman correlational analysis of the nurses' subsample (n = 55), pre- and post-test (Table 2). The article interprets this as training helping nurses dissociate efficacy from a cold, authoritarian communicative approach.

> "a negative association is observed in the pretest between Verbal aggressiveness and self-efficacy, with a large effect size. However, this association is not observed in the posttest measure."

### Pérez-Fuentes María del Carmen 2026 (2)

Pérez-Fuentes María del Carmen, Molero Jurado María del Mar, Romanos-Rodríguez Armando, Martos Martínez África, Gómez-Gómez Francisco J., Aguado-Campos Javier. (2026). Self-efficacy and Communication in Health Personnel After Simulation Training in Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation: A Quasi-experimental Study. Inquiry. https://doi.org/10.1177/00469580251411471

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-intervention Spearman correlations in the total sample: self-efficacy with impression manipulativeness (rho = 0.32) and questioningness (rho = 0.21); the article reports no correlations with years of experience at pre-test.

> "positive associations could be observed between self-efficacy and the communicative styles: Impression manipulativeness (Spearman's rho = 0.32, sig. < .01, P = .56, 1 − β = .99) and Questioningness (Spearman's rho = 0.21, sig. < .05, P = .45, 1 − β = .99)"
