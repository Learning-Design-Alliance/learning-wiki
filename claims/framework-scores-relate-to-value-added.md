## Evidence

### Elias Walsh 2013

Elias Walsh, Stephen Lipscomb. (2013). Classroom Observations from Phase 2 of the Pennsylvania Teacher Evaluation Pilot: Assessing Internal Consistency, Score Variation, and Relationships with Value Added. Cambridge, MA: Mathematica Policy Research. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/classroom-observations-from-phase-2-of-the-pennsylvania-teacher-evaluation-pilot-assessing-internal-consistency-score-variation-and-relationships-with-value-added

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

The report's summary of Phase 2 findings states that "across nearly all components, teachers with higher scores on the rubric tended to make larger contributions to student achievement" as measured by value added. No effect size or test statistic is printed in the available text.

> "across nearly all components, teachers with higher scores on the rubric tended to make larger contributions to student achievement than did teachers with lower scores, as measured by value added"

### Sartain 2011

Sartain, L., Stoelinga, S. R., & Brown, E. R., with Luppescu, S., Kapadia Matsko, K., Miller, F. K., Durwood, C. E., Jiang, J. Y., & Glazer, D. (2011). Rethinking Teacher Evaluation in Chicago: Lessons Learned from Classroom Observations, Principal-Teacher Conferences, and District Implementation. Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/rethinking-teacher-evaluation-chicago-lessons-learned-classroom-observations-principal

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Validity analysis comparing principal Danielson ratings to CPS value-added indicators for 955 principal observations of 501 grade 4-8 reading and math teachers. The article reports the pattern that "teachers with the lowest observation ratings also have the lowest value-added measures" and states the rating-value-added relationship was statistically significant for all components.

> "Across almost all of the Framework components, teachers with the lowest observation ratings also have the lowest value-added measures"

### Sartain 2011 (2)

Sartain, L., Stoelinga, S. R., & Brown, E. R., with Luppescu, S., Kapadia Matsko, K., Miller, F. K., Durwood, C. E., Jiang, J. Y., & Glazer, D. (2011). Rethinking Teacher Evaluation in Chicago: Lessons Learned from Classroom Observations, Principal-Teacher Conferences, and District Implementation. Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/rethinking-teacher-evaluation-chicago-lessons-learned-classroom-observations-principal

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

The validity sample comprised principal ratings linked to value-added indicators: "a sample of 955 principal observations of 501 teachers." Figure 1 shows average standardized value-added rising monotonically across Unsatisfactory, Basic, Proficient, and Distinguished ratings for both reading and math.

> "This leaves us with a sample of 955 principal observations of 501 teachers."
