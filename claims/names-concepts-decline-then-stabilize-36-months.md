# Recognition and cued recall of proper names and concepts decline over the first 36-48 months of retention and then stabilize at above-chance levels, with names declining more rapidly than concepts.

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Recognition and cued recall of names and concepts declined over the first 36-48 months and then leveled out above chance across the remaining retention intervals. [→ Conway 1991](#conway-1991)

## Evidence

### Conway 1991

Conway, M. A., Cohen, G., & Stanhope, N. (1991). On the Very Long-Term Retention of Knowledge Acquired Through Formal Education: Twelve Years of Cognitive Psychology. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 120(4), 395-409. https://psycnet.apa.org/record/xge120040395

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional study of 373 former Open University cognitive psychology students tested at 12 retention intervals (3-125 months). ANOVAs on recognition d-primes and cued recall showed significant RI effects; "retention rapidly declines over the first 36 to 48 months of retention and then levels out". Performance stayed reliably above chance.

> "It can be seen from Figure 1 that retention rapidly declines over the first 36 to 48 months of retention and then levels out and stays at the same level throughout the remaining 8 years."

### Conway 1991 (2)

Conway, M. A., Cohen, G., & Stanhope, N. (1991). On the Very Long-Term Retention of Knowledge Acquired Through Formal Education: Twelve Years of Cognitive Psychology. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 120(4), 395-409. https://psycnet.apa.org/record/xge120040395

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Simple main effects on the recognition ANOVA (F(11,361) = 3.32 for the RI by test-type interaction, p < .001) confirmed that "the decline in retention is more rapid for names than concepts"; the 27- and 39-month comparisons reached significance (p < .033 and p < .016).

> "It is also clear from Figure 1 that the decline in retention is more rapid for names than concepts. To explore this further, simple main effects analyses were conducted."
