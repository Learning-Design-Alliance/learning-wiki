# Greater intake of less healthful food groups (sweet snacks, salty snacks, sugar-sweetened beverages) is associated with lower math and English standardized test scores in lower-income schoolchildren

`q2 i1` In ordered logistic regression models (n = 430 fourth graders), less healthful food intake was associated with lower math (OR = 0.91, 95% CI 0.84–0.98) and English (OR = 0.87, 95% CI 0.80–0.94) MCAS scores. [→ Bleiweiss-Sande R. 2019](#bleiweiss-sande-r-2019)

## Evidence

### Bleiweiss-Sande R. 2019

Bleiweiss-Sande R., Chui K., Wright C., Amin S., Anzman-Frasca S., Sacheck J.M. (2019). Associations between Food Group Intake, Cognition, and Academic Achievement in Elementary Schoolchildren. Nutrients 11(11):2722. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu11112722

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional ordered logistic regression analysis of 430 fourth graders in the FLEX study baseline, using FLEX FFQ food-group servings and MCAS achievement categories. The less healthful food group showed "lower math (OR = 0.91, 95% CI = 0.84, 0.98)" and lower English scores, strongest for English. Models adjusted for sex, ELL status, IEP enrollment, parent education, and BMI z-score.

> "Less healthful food intake was associated with lower math (OR = 0.91, 95% CI = 0.84, 0.98) and English (OR = 0.87, 95% CI = 0.80, 0.94) scores, with the greatest effect seen for English score (Table 5)."
