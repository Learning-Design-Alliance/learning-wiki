# Adding a self-explanation feature to a base game improved transfer performance

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students who played the base version of The Circuit Game plus prompts to self-explain each major decision performed better on solving transfer problems than students who played the base version alone. [→ Mayer 2014](#mayer-2014)

## Evidence

### Mayer 2014

Mayer, Richard E. (2014). Computer Games for Learning: An Evidence-Based Approach. The MIT Press. http://direct.mit.edu/books/monograph/4028/bookpreview-pdf/2362507

`q2 · i?`

Value-added study conducted in the author's UCSB lab comparing students who played the base version of The Circuit Game with those who played it with self-explanation prompts. The "base plus self-explanation group performed better than the base group" on transfer problems. No effect size is printed in the text.

> "The base plus self-explanation group performed better than the base group on solving transfer problems on a embedded test, indicating that adding an instructional feature based on self-explanation improved student learning."
