Evidence¶

Raffael Schmitt 2023¶

Raffael Schmitt, Martin Meyer and Nathalie Giroud. (2023). Improvements in naturalistic speech-in-noise comprehension in middle-aged and older adults after 3 weeks of computer-based speechreading training. npj Science of Learning 8:32. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00179-6

q3 · i? · causal · r1

GLMM analysis of single-trial data from 32 speechreading-training and 30 active-control participants on a naturalistic speech-in-noise task. The article reports "a signi ﬁcant training effect" with the ST group improving in the AV condition, supported by the three-way interaction ΔΧ2(4) = 13.62, p = 0.009 (condition [AV]:session [post]:group [ST] OR = 1.07, p = 0.047).

"Using generalized linear mixed-effects models (GLMMs) on single-trial data, we found a signi ﬁcant training effect, with the ST group improving signi ﬁcantly from pre- to post-training in the audiovisual (AV) condition."

Raffael Schmitt 2023 (2)¶

Raffael Schmitt, Martin Meyer and Nathalie Giroud. (2023). Improvements in naturalistic speech-in-noise comprehension in middle-aged and older adults after 3 weeks of computer-based speechreading training. npj Science of Learning 8:32. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00179-6

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Supplementary GLMM re-run with treatment coding comparing speechreading training and active control groups at pre-training in the audiovisual condition. The groups "did not signi ﬁcantly differ at pre-training" (OR = 0.75, 95% CI[0.45, 1.25], p = 0.271). The authors take this as suggesting baseline differences did not confound the effect; it shows no significant difference was detected, not that the groups, which were not randomly assigned, were equivalent.

"Indeed, the two groups did not signi ﬁcantly differ at pre-training (OR = 0.75, 95% CI[0.45, 1.25], p = 0.271) (Fig. 5 and Supplementary Table 3) suggesting that baseline differences in AV did not pose a potentially confounding factor."

Related Claims¶

  • Participants who improved on audiovisual speech-in-noise comprehension had significantly lower pre-training scores than non-learners, reflecting the inherent correlation between baseline and change scores — related
  • Lexical overlap between speechreading training material and test sentences did not significantly account for the observed training effect — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Speechreading training does not significantly improve subjectively perceived everyday listening effort or hearing abilities — related
  • Visual enhancement is not significantly associated with hearing level, age, visual working memory or processing speed, and learning rate in speechreading training is not significantly associated with those or with hearing aid use or training intensity — related
  • Older adults show larger multisensory gains for simple stimuli but multisensory deficits for complex speech stimuli — related
  • Speech-in-noise perception improves significantly after a short-term immersive auditory-cognitive training, but not after an audio-only training session — related
