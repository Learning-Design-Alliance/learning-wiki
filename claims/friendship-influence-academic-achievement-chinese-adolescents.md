## Evidence

### Qin Xingna 2026

Qin Xingna, Li Simeng, Sjögren Björn, Ren Ping, Thornberg Robert. (2026). The Role of Parental Influences in Chinese Adolescents’ Academic Achievement through Shaping Friendship Network Dynamics. Journal of Youth and Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-025-02306-5

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

RSiena stochastic actor-oriented modeling of four-wave friendship and achievement data from about 1,419 students in 26 classrooms (20 converged), meta-analyzed across classrooms. The article reports the "average alter" effect was significant (β = 0.20, p < .001).

> "Chinese adolescents’ academic achievement was influenced to be similar to their friends’ achievement (average alter, β = 0.20, p < .001)."

### Qin Xingna 2026 (2)

Qin Xingna, Li Simeng, Sjögren Björn, Ren Ping, Thornberg Robert. (2026). The Role of Parental Influences in Chinese Adolescents’ Academic Achievement through Shaping Friendship Network Dynamics. Journal of Youth and Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-025-02306-5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Ego-alter influence table analysis derived from the RSiena model (Fig. 1) showed that "friends with lower academic achievement exerted a stronger negative influence" than higher-achieving friends; low-achieving students were strongly influenced while high achievers were more independent.

> "The further ego-alter influence table analysis examined the direction of friendship influence in academic achievement. It showed that friends with lower academic achievement exerted a stronger negative influence on adolescents’ achievement than the positive influence exerted by higher-achieving friends."
