## Evidence

### Tamar Kricheli-Katz and Tali Regev 2021

Tamar Kricheli-Katz and Tali Regev. (2021). The effect of language on performance: do gendered languages fail women in maths? npj Science of Learning 6:9. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00087-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

After the main experiment, participants rated agreement (1–7) that science is for men; feminine address produced lower agreement (p = 0.007). No significant differences appeared for arts-and-humanities ratings or between female and male participants.

> "Whereas when addressed in the masculine, the average response of participants was 5.03; when addressed in the feminine, the mean was 4.84 ( t-test, p = 0.007)."

### Tamar Kricheli-Katz and Tali Regev 2021 (2)

Tamar Kricheli-Katz and Tali Regev. (2021). The effect of language on performance: do gendered languages fail women in maths? npj Science of Learning 6:9. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00087-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A null result: the gender-science Implicit Association Test did not differ by address condition. The article attributes this to implicit associations being immediate, unconscious, and difficult to change. Equivalence was not tested.

> "no statistically signiﬁcant differences were found between the results of the Implicit Association Tests of participants who were addressed in the masculine and the responses of those who were addressed in the feminine."
