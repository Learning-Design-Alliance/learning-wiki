# Youth's intentions to have sexual intercourse declined marginally from entry to exit (3.2 to 3.0, p = .08, effect size = -0.16), with no change in condom or birth control intentions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2` · `q2` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Intentions to engage in sexual intercourse in the next year declined from 3.2 to 3.0 on a 1-to-4 scale, a marginally significant difference (p = .08) equivalent to an effect size of -0.16. [→ Nakamoto 2025](#nakamoto-2025)
`q2 i?` Youth showed essentially no changes in intentions to use condoms (p = .68) or other forms of birth control (p = .72). [→ Nakamoto 2025 (2)](#nakamoto-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Nakamoto 2025

Nakamoto, J., Carter, C., & Russo, S. (2025). Implementation and impact of the Project With curriculum in juvenile justice facilities and group homes. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED673580

`q2 · i1` · `design · r2`

Paired samples t-tests comparing entry and exit survey responses of youth who remained at their sites (item sample sizes 105 to 118). The decline in intentions to have sexual intercourse was "marginally significant (p = .08)" with "an effect size of -0.16, which is considered a small impact."

> "The participating youth declined from an average of 3.2 at the entry survey to 3.0 at the exit survey on the 1 (no, definitely not) to 4 (yes, definitely) scale. This difference was marginally significant (p = .08) and was equivalent to an effect size of -0.16, which is considered a small impact."

### Nakamoto 2025 (2)

Nakamoto, J., Carter, C., & Russo, S. (2025). Implementation and impact of the Project With curriculum in juvenile justice facilities and group homes. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED673580

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Paired samples t-tests on condom-use and birth-control intention items (sample sizes 105 to 118) found no statistically significant differences: condoms p = .68, other birth control p = .72. The report concludes Project With did not have an impact on these two outcomes; equivalence was not tested.

> "The participating youth showed essentially no changes between the entry and exit surveys in their intentions to use condoms (p = .68) and other forms of birth control (p = .72)."
