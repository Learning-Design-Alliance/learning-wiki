Evidence¶

DeSchryver 2015¶

DeSchryver, David A., with Deirdre Dlugoleski. (2015). Accelerating Change: How education technology developers can jump-start a new adult education market. Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/29f39d7d-87f2-4e1e-920e-0c1ce86035cc

q2 · i? · theoretical · r2

The Opportunity section, citing OECD figures, sizes the demand–service gap: about "36 million “low-skilled” adults in the US" against an estimated 4.1 million served by $10 billion in annual investment. These are descriptive market figures, not experimental findings.

"According to the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD), there are about 36 million “low-skilled” adults in the US, yet only an estimated 4.1 million adults benefit from the $10 billion we invest each year in adult learning services."

DeSchryver 2015 (2)¶

DeSchryver, David A., with Deirdre Dlugoleski. (2015). Accelerating Change: How education technology developers can jump-start a new adult education market. Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/29f39d7d-87f2-4e1e-920e-0c1ce86035cc

q2 · i? · theoretical · r1

The Opportunity section reports, citing Tyton Partners estimates, that of about $800 million of adult education spending on instructional supplies and materials, "only twenty-five percent... is currently spent on digital materials." The figures are descriptive estimates of market investment, with no outcome test.

"Estimates suggest that only twenty-five percent of that $800 million is currently spent on digital materials."
