## Evidence

### Eleanor C. Sayre 2016

Eleanor C. Sayre, Claudia Fracchiolla, Ben Van Dusen. (2016). iPad Screencasts as Evidence of Accountable Disciplinary Knowledge. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1605.08105](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1605.08105)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Results from a previous study in which screencasts and notebooks were scored for correctness and completeness. The authors cite these percentages as corroborating their claim that students detail steps and procedures more when teaching peers than when solving for a teacher.

> "In this paper results show that when solving problems in screen- casts students solutions were 21% more complete then when solving problems in their notebooks. At the same time the students answers in the screencasts were 33% more likely to be correct than when solving the notebook problems."
