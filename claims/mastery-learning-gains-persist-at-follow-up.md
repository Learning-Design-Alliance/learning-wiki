## Evidence

### Kulik et al. 1990

Kulik, C.-L. C., Kulik, J. A., & Bangert-Drowns, R. L. (1990). Effectiveness of Mastery Learning Programs: A Meta-Analysis. *Review of Educational Research, 60*(2), 265–299. [doi:10.3102/00346543060002265](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543060002265)

`q4 · meta-analysis (11 studies with follow-up examinations)` · `i2 · medium-to-large effect, mean ES = 0.71 SD at follow-up` · `n=11 studies` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Eleven of the 108 evaluations in this meta-analysis gave a follow-up or retention examination, on average about eight weeks after instruction ended. Mastery students' advantage was 0.71 SD at follow-up, against 0.60 SD at the end of instruction in the same 11 studies; the authors note the increase is not significant. They conclude the benefits are relatively enduring rather than short-term.

> "The average follow-up interval was about 8 weeks in these studies, and the average effect size was 0.71, a strong positive effect."

## Discussion

Eight weeks is a short horizon for a course-level design, and only 11 of 108 studies measured it. The finding rules out a quick fade, not decay over a year or more.
