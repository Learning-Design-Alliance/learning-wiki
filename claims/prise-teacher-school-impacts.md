## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Thematic analysis of teacher responses identified eight distinct areas of positive impact, with negative impact rare (one teacher reporting a small amount of extra stress). [→ Martin O. Archer and Jennifer DeWitt 2021](#martin-o-archer-and-jennifer-dewitt-2021)
`q2 i?` 70 ± 10% of teachers and schools returned for multiple years of PRiSE projects, solidifying the impacts and changing how they interact with universities. [→ Martin O. Archer and Jennifer DeWitt 2021 (2)](#martin-o-archer-and-jennifer-dewitt-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Martin O. Archer and Jennifer DeWitt 2021

Martin O. Archer and Jennifer DeWitt. (2021). “Thanks for helping me ﬁnd my enthusiasm for physics!” The lasting impacts ‘research in schools’ projects can have on students, teachers, and schools. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2104.04431](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2104.04431)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of open-ended responses from 21 teachers (2015–2018), with codes applied across all years (45 teachers): new physics knowledge, confidence discussing research, lesson implementation, mentoring confidence, skills, sharing project work, raised physics/STEM profile, and university relationships; a ninth theme on teachers' preconceptions of student ability emerged later.

> "we identiﬁed eight distinct areas (indicated in bold) in which teachers and schools seem to have been positively affected by their involvement in PRiSE."

### Martin O. Archer and Jennifer DeWitt 2021 (2)

Martin O. Archer and Jennifer DeWitt. (2021). “Thanks for helping me ﬁnd my enthusiasm for physics!” The lasting impacts ‘research in schools’ projects can have on students, teachers, and schools. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2104.04431](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2104.04431)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Reported repeat participation rate among teachers and schools completing projects, cited by the authors as backing up the relationship theme from the qualitative analysis.

> "This is further backed-up by the signiﬁcant repeated buy-in of teachers and schools after completing the project, with70±10% returning for multiple years of PRiSE projects"
