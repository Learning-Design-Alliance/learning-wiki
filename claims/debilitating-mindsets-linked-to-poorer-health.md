## Evidence

### Jenkins Anna 2021

Jenkins Anna, Weeks Molly S., Hard Bridgette Martin. (2021). General and specific stress mindsets: Links with college student health and academic performance. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0256351

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Zero-order and partial correlations (Table 2) in the 498-participant cross-sectional study, controlling sample and gender. No effect size is printed for this association.

> "Also consistent with previous research [4, 11], stress-is-debilitating mindsets were related to higher levels of perceived distress and to poorer health."

### Jenkins Anna 2021 (2)

Jenkins Anna, Weeks Molly S., Hard Bridgette Martin. (2021). General and specific stress mindsets: Links with college student health and academic performance. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0256351

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical multiple regressions (Table 4) predicting three health measures; no significant main effects emerged for general, acute controllable, acute uncontrollable, or chronic uncontrollable mindsets. No effect size is printed.

> "The only mindset for which there were significant main effects was the chronic controllable mindset, such that viewing stress as more enhancing in that situation was related to fewer days on which health interfered with normal activities and lower levels of mental health symptoms."
