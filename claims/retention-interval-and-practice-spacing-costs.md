## Evidence

### Philip I. Pavlik Jr. 2005

Philip I. Pavlik Jr., John R. Anderson. (2005). Practice and Forgetting Effects on Vocabulary Memory: An Activation-Based Model of the Spacing Effect. Cognitive Science 29. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog0000_14

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated measures ANOVA of S2 data in the vocabulary experiment; mean S2 correctness was 0.64 for the 1-day and 0.52 for the 7-day retention interval, with a strong spacing main effect "F(2, 76) = 58.2, p < .001".

> "First, this analysis showed that people forgot more when the retention interval went from 1 to 7 days, F(1, 38) = 4.26, p < .05. It also revealed a strong main effect of spacing, F(2, 76) = 58.2, p < .001."

### Philip I. Pavlik Jr. 2005 (2)

Philip I. Pavlik Jr., John R. Anderson. (2005). Practice and Forgetting Effects on Vocabulary Memory: An Activation-Based Model of the Spacing Effect. Cognitive Science 29. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog0000_14

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated measures ANOVA on the first four S1 trials aggregated by practice condition, spacing, and retention group; the authors attribute the wider-spacing cost to the overall longer retention intervals for those trials.

> "The results of this analysis confirmed that performance improved across the first four trials of S1, F(3, 114) = 412, p < .001, and that there was significantly lower performance with wider spacing, F(2, 76) = 240, p < .001."
