# Non-users of the service declined 19 percentage points in Mathematics attainment from Grade 9 to Grade 10 versus 11.5 points for regular/extensive users (d = 0.45)

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Across 1,950 learners, mean attainment decline was significantly smaller for regular/extensive users (151+ questions) than for non-users (zero questions), with a small-to-medium effect size (d = 0.45). [→ Roberts N 2015](#roberts-n-2015)

## Evidence

### Roberts N 2015

Roberts N, Spencer-Smith G, Vänskä R, Eskelinen S. (2015). From challenging assumptions to measuring effect: Researching the Nokia Mobile Mathematics Service in South Africa. South African Journal of Education, 35(2). https://doi.org/10.15700/saje.v35n2a1045

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Independent t-test on school-assessment shifts for the quasi-experimental groups (same classes, same teachers, non-randomised) within the cleaned dataset of 1,950 learners; the printed result is "t (1344) = 8.0, p = 2.2 x 10 -15" with "d = 0.45".

> "There was a significant difference in the percentage point shifts between the group of non-users (zero questions) (M = -19.0, SD = 17.1) compared to the group of regular/ extensive users (151+ questions) (M = -11.5, SD = 16.7); t (1344) = 8.0, p = 2.2 x 10 -15. The magnitude of Cohen's effect size (d = 0.45) indicates that the effect of the service here was small to medium."
