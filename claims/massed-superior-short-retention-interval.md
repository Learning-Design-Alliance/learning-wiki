# At short retention intervals massed presentation is superior to spaced presentation, reversing the spacing effect

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the Rumelhart (1967) data, with a short retention interval, massed presentation yielded higher recall than spaced presentation (0.967 vs. 0.943 on Trial 4), and the model predicted this reversal (0.942 vs. 0.916). [→ Raaijmakers 2003](#raaijmakers-2003)

## Evidence

### Raaijmakers 2003

Raaijmakers, J.G.W. (2003). Spacing and repetition effects in human memory: application of the SAM model. Cognitive Science 27 (2003) 431–452. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0364-0213(03)00007-7

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Observed proportions from the Rumelhart (1967) experiment as fitted in the article: the massed condition 1-1-1-1-10 scored 0.967 versus 0.943 for the spaced condition 10-10-1-1-10 at a short retention interval, confirming "massed presentation is superior to spaced presentation" there.

> "However, with a short retention interval massed presentation is superior to spaced presentation. For example, comparing again performance on Trial 4 for the conditions 1-1-1-1-10 and 10-10-1-1-10, the performance for the massed condition is 0.967 while the performance in the spaced condition is 0.943 (predicted values: 0.942 vs. 0.916)."
