# A 6-hour activity-based powers-of-ten module raised average test scores from 18% to 68% across three trials with students aged 7-13

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across three school trials, average pre-test scores of 18% rose to 68% on post-tests covering estimation, comparison and calculation of extreme numbers. [→ Lonshakova 2023](#lonshakova-2023)

## Evidence

### Lonshakova 2023

Lonshakova, A., Blair, D. G., Treagust, D. F., & Zadnik, M. (2023). Powers of the Universe: Empowering primary school students with the powers of ten notation. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2311.18192](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2311.18192)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre/post questionnaire analysis across three trials (27, 19 and 20 students in Years 3-7) of the Powers of the Universe module. The authors report "The pre-test averaged at 18%, while the post-test reached 68%", with no statistical testing performed because samples were small and heterogeneous.

> "The pre-test averaged at 18%, while the post-test reached 68%. Independently of age and knowledge before the trials, all the students had positive dynamics learning advanced mathematical concepts."
