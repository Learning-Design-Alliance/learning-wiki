## Subclaims
`q2 i?` American Government posters were more likely to gather upvotes from people with shared ideology, an effect not apparent in Saving Schools. [→ Yeomans 2018](#yeomans-2018)
`q2 i?` Ideological sorting in upvoting was stronger among conservatives, who upvoted conservative posts more than liberal posts. [→ Yeomans 2018](#yeomans-2018)

## Evidence

### Yeomans 2018

Yeomans, M., Stewart, B. M., Mavon, K., Kindel, A., Tingley, D., & Reich, J. (2018). The Civic Mission of MOOCs: Engagement across Political Differences in Online Forums. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education, 28, 553–589. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-017-0161-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Rank-order correlation analysis of 400 reply-upvote pairs in course-team-generated content threads. "rank-order correlation: rτ = .121" significant in American Government (p < .05) but not in Saving Schools (rτ = −.019, p = .669), a non-replication across the two courses.

> "American Government posters were more likely to gather upvotes from people with shared ideology than not (rank-order correlation: rτ = .121, z(213) = 2.0,p < .05), though this relationship was not apparent in Saving Schools (rτ = −.019, z(155) = 0.43, p = .669)."
