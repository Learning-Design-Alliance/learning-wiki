# Emotional engagement is the strongest predictor of motivation, especially need-based motivations

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the motivation meta-analysis, emotional engagement (M Fisher's z = 0.64) predicted pooled motivation significantly more strongly than cognitive, behavioral, and agentic engagement. [→ Johnmarshall Reeve 2025](#johnmarshall-reeve-2025)
`q3 i?` For need-based motivations, emotional engagement (z = 0.71) was significantly stronger than all three other types, confirming hypothesis 3a. [→ Johnmarshall Reeve 2025 (2)](#johnmarshall-reeve-2025-2)
`q3 i?` For cognition-based motivations, cognitive engagement (z = 0.48) was significantly stronger than only agentic engagement, providing only minimal support for hypothesis 3b. [→ Johnmarshall Reeve 2025 (3)](#johnmarshall-reeve-2025-3)

## Evidence

### Johnmarshall Reeve 2025

Johnmarshall Reeve, Geetanjali Basarkod, Hye-Ryen Jang, Rafael Gargurevich, Hyungshim Jang, Sung Hyeon Cheon. (2025). Specialized Purpose of Each Type of Student Engagement: A Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-09989-z

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analysis #3 pooled 300 effect sizes from 41 studies. Engagement type significantly moderated the motivation relation, χ2(3) = 50.20, p < 0.001; emotional engagement reached M Fisher's z = 0.64 versus agentic z = 0.45. The post-hoc tests found emotional engagement's "predictive power ... significantly greater" than all three other types. No standardized effect size was printed for the pairwise differences.

> "the predictive power of emotional engagement was significantly greater than it was for cognitive engagement (p < 0.001), behavioral engagement (p < 0.001), and agentic engagement (p < 0.001)"

### Johnmarshall Reeve 2025 (2)

Johnmarshall Reeve, Geetanjali Basarkod, Hye-Ryen Jang, Rafael Gargurevich, Hyungshim Jang, Sung Hyeon Cheon. (2025). Specialized Purpose of Each Type of Student Engagement: A Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-09989-z

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

The need-based motivations sub-analysis included 184 effect sizes from 31 studies; emotional engagement correlated at z = 0.71 versus agentic z = 0.48, behavioral z = 0.54, cognitive z = 0.52, with engagement type a significant moderator, χ2(3) = 63.05, p < 0.001. The article states this result confirmed H3a. No standardized effect size was printed for the pairwise differences.

> "the predictive power of emotional engagement was significantly greater than it was for agentic engagement (p < 0.001), behavioral engagement (p < 0.001), and cognitive engagement (p < 0.001), thereby confirming H3a"

### Johnmarshall Reeve 2025 (3)

Johnmarshall Reeve, Geetanjali Basarkod, Hye-Ryen Jang, Rafael Gargurevich, Hyungshim Jang, Sung Hyeon Cheon. (2025). Specialized Purpose of Each Type of Student Engagement: A Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-09989-z

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

The cognition-based motivations sub-analysis included 116 effect sizes from 16 studies; cognitive engagement correlated at z = 0.48, tied with emotional engagement at z = 0.48, with moderation χ2(3) = 10.35, p = 0.016. The article reports "only minimal support for H3b" because cognitive engagement did not beat behavioral or emotional engagement. No standardized effect size was printed.

> "the predictive power of cognitive engagement was significantly greater than it was for agentic engagement (p = 0.004) but not for behavioral engagement (p = 0.300) or emotional engagement (p = 0.940), thereby providing only minimal support for H3b"
