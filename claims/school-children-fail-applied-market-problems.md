## Evidence

### Banerjee 2025

Banerjee, A. V., Bhattacharjee, S., Chattopadhyay, R., Duflo, E., Ganimian, A. J., Rajah, K. & Spelke, E. S. (2025). Children's arithmetic skills do not transfer between applied and academic mathematics. Nature. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08502-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Study 3 comparison on the Vishal market word problem, designed to be equally familiar to both groups, with pen and paper disallowed. The article reports "36% of working children correctly answered the question compared with 1% of non-working children" (β = 0.35, 95% CI 0.30-0.40, P < 0.001).

> "At the first attempt, 36% of working children correctly answered the question compared with 1% of non-working children (β = 0.35, s.e.m. = 0.03, 95% CI = 0.30–0.40, P < 0.001; Fig. 4, centre)"

### Banerjee 2025 (2)

Banerjee, A. V., Bhattacharjee, S., Chattopadhyay, R., Duflo, E., Ganimian, A. J., Rajah, K. & Spelke, E. S. (2025). Children's arithmetic skills do not transfer between applied and academic mathematics. Nature. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08502-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Study 3 (835 working-child surveys over two waves; 271 non-working children). The pretend market imposed a time limit, no pen and paper, and difficulty matched to real market tasks; the article reports only 10% of non-working children gave correct change while "85% cal- culated the correct amount due and change in the market transactions".

> "Overall, 85% cal- culated the correct amount due and change in the market transactions (Supplementary Table 15), whereas non-working children struggled in a pretend market situation"

### Banerjee 2025 (3)

Banerjee, A. V., Bhattacharjee, S., Chattopadhyay, R., Duflo, E., Ganimian, A. J., Rajah, K. & Spelke, E. S. (2025). Children's arithmetic skills do not transfer between applied and academic mathematics. Nature. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08502-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Study 3 oral abstract subtraction and division problems given to both groups with pen and paper allowed. The article reports "59% of non-working children correctly solved these problems compared with 45% of working children" (β = -0.14, 95% CI -0.20 to -0.08, P < 0.001), replicating studies 1 and 2.

> "Overall, 59% of non-working children correctly solved these problems compared with 45% of working children (β = –0.14, s.e.m. = 0.03, 95% CI = –0.20 to –0.08, P < 0.001; Fig. 4, left)"

### Banerjee 2025 (4)

Banerjee, A. V., Bhattacharjee, S., Chattopadhyay, R., Duflo, E., Ganimian, A. J., Rajah, K. & Spelke, E. S. (2025). Children's arithmetic skills do not transfer between applied and academic mathematics. Nature. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08502-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Examination of the untimed written work of the 200 Delhi non-working school children across the exercises. The article reports "a non-working child wrote 102 numbers and 22 operations", 46% repeated the same operation, and 17% used tally marks and serial counting, so answers arrived too slowly to be useful.

> "On average, a non-working child wrote 102 numbers and 22 operations on their sheets of paper (Fig. 3b and Supplementary Table 13). Nearly half (46%) of these children repeated the same operation and 6% wrote the same operation in two different ways"
