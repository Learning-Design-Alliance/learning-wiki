# Higher OSCE scores are strongly associated with better final M1 grades, and students scoring below 80% seldom reach a good or very good M1

> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students scoring less than 80% in the OSCE seldom reach a good or very good M1 grade; with a near-perfect OSCE, NC students have an at least 90% probability of the best M1 grade. [→ Tsikas 2023](#tsikas-2023)
`q2 i?` The association between an excellent OSCE and a very good M1 is distinctly stronger for internal medicine and neurology stations than for medical skills and communication stations. [→ Tsikas 2023 (2)](#tsikas-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Tsikas 2023

Tsikas, Stefanos A.; Afshar, Kambiz. (2023). Clinical experience can compensate for inferior academic achievements in an undergraduate objective structured clinical examination. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04082-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Ordered logistic regression (Fig. 3, model 2 in Table B of the Online Appendix) relating OSCE percentage to M1 grade for students who had passed all prior courses. Students "who score less than 80% in the clinical examination seldom reach a good or very good" M1 grade.

> "Those students who score less than 80% in the clinical examination seldom reach a good or very good (equivalent to an A or B) M1 grade."

### Tsikas 2023 (2)

Tsikas, Stefanos A.; Afshar, Kambiz. (2023). Clinical experience can compensate for inferior academic achievements in an undergraduate objective structured clinical examination. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04082-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Ordered logistic regressions repeated with OSCE subpart scores as dependent variables (Figure A, Online Appendix). The association with a very good M1 is "distinctly stronger for the parts internal medicine and neurology, compared with medical skills and communication."

> "the association between an excellent OSCE and a very good M1 is distinctly stronger for the parts internal medicine and neurology, compared with medical skills and communication."
