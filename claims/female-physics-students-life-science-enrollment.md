## Evidence

### Steven Turnbull 2017

Steven Turnbull, Dion R. J. O'Neale, Frederique Vanholsbeeck, S. Earl Irving, Thomas Lumley. (2017). A Leaky Pipe Dream? A Study of Gender Differences in Undergraduate Physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1702.06249](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1702.06249)

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r3`

Odds-ratio analysis of second-year enrollment (Table II) for first-year physics students at the University of Auckland: the printed odds ratios were 2.6 for biosci, 2.5 for medsci and 1.9 for chemistry, i.e. female students were "around 2.6 times more likely to take biological science (biosci)" than male students.

> "Female first year physics students were around 2.6 times more likely to take biological science (biosci), 2.5 times more likely to take medical science (medsci) and 1.9 times more likely to take chemistry in their second year than male students."

### Steven Turnbull 2017 (2)

Steven Turnbull, Dion R. J. O'Neale, Frederique Vanholsbeeck, S. Earl Irving, Thomas Lumley. (2017). A Leaky Pipe Dream? A Study of Gender Differences in Undergraduate Physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1702.06249](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1702.06249)

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Same odds-ratio analysis of second-year subject enrollment (Table II), across the overall physics population: the printed odds ratio was OR = 5, i.e. male students were "around 5 times more likely to take computer science in their second year compared to female students."

> "Overall, male students were around 5 times more likely to take computer science in their second year compared to female students."
