## Evidence

### Stephan E. Vogel and Bert De Smedt 2021

Stephan E. Vogel and Bert De Smedt. (2021). Developmental brain dynamics of numerical and arithmetic abilities. npj Science of Learning (2021) 6:22. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00099-3

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Review attribution to the few studies of basic numerical abilities in children with dyscalculia: "significant differences, often deactivation" in regions associated with nonsymbolic and symbolic number processing, especially the IPS; no effect sizes are printed.

> "Findings from this research suggest signi ﬁcant differences, often deactivation, in the func- tional organization of the brain regions associated with nonsym- bolic and symbolic number processing, in particular the IPS"

### Stephan E. Vogel and Bert De Smedt 2021 (2)

Stephan E. Vogel and Bert De Smedt. (2021). Developmental brain dynamics of numerical and arithmetic abilities. npj Science of Learning (2021) 6:22. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00099-3

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports greater activation during number processing in left IPS, frontal cortex and visual areas in children with dyscalculia, "probably indicating" compensatory engagement of additional cognitive control resources; this is the authors' interpretation and no effect sizes are printed.

> "Studies have also found greater activation during number processing in the left IPS, the frontal cortex, and in visual areas in children with dyscalculia,"

### Stephan E. Vogel and Bert De Smedt 2021 (3)

Stephan E. Vogel and Bert De Smedt. (2021). Developmental brain dynamics of numerical and arithmetic abilities. npj Science of Learning (2021) 6:22. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00099-3

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

On arithmetic activity in dyscalculia, the review reports both increases (parietal, prefrontal, occipitotemporal during addition and subtraction in one study) and decreases (prefrontal, right posterior parietal, occipitotemporal in another); it states the body of data "is simply too small to draw reliable conclusions".

> "The existing body of the data is simply too small to draw reliable conclusions on what these activation and deactivation differences mean."
