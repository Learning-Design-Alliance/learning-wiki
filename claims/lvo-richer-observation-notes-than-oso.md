## Evidence

### Pickering 2011

Pickering, L. E., & Walsh, E. J. (2011). Using Videoconferencing Technology to Enhance Classroom Observation Methodology for the Instruction of Preservice Early Childhood Professionals. Journal of Digital Learning in Teacher Education, 27(3). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ918901

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Content analysis in a quasi-experiment comparing two sections of a child development course (Wave 1: OSO-only section n = 20; LVO-only section n = 21), counting OBDs, interpretations, and words as proxies for observational richness. Table 1 reports, e.g., Quantity of OBDs t = -4.08, p = .000, favoring LVO.

> "LVO students produced significantly more OBDs and developmental interpretations than OSO students. In addition, LVO students were found to provide fuller descriptions and interpretations of their observations than OSO students, as they used significantly more words in both."
