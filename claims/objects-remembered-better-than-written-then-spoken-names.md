# In immediate recall by school pupils, objects shown are remembered better than written words, and written words better than spoken words

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` School pupils in all grades immediately recalled more shown objects (average 8.28 of 10) than written words (6.92) or spoken words (6.85). [→ Patterson 1909](#patterson-1909)

## Evidence

### Patterson 1909

Patterson, T. L. (1909). Pedagogical Suggestions from Memory Tests. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://books.google.com

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

A classroom memory test in a school and college, all grades from third primary up, presented three ten-word lists as spoken, written-on-board, and shown objects, with immediate written recall. The printed averages show "objects were remembered better than the written names" and written better than spoken. Only descriptive means are printed.

> "The following were the averages for the first three lists of words, namely,—6.85, 6.92 and 8.28 respectively, showing that objects were remembered better than the written names, nd the latter better than the spoken names."
