## Evidence

### Alex Liu 2026

Alex Liu, Min Sun, Lief Esbenshade, Victor Tian, Zachary Zhang, and Kevin He. (2026). Teacher-Authored Prompts for Configuring StudentAI Dialogue: K12 Classroom Implementation. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16738 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2604.16738](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2604.16738)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Descriptive analysis of 94 teacher-authored prompts coded with LLM assistance and 100% human validation in the Spring 2025 pilot. The article reports "80.9% of prompts provided detailed, concrete task descriptions" against the three-level task specificity rubric.

> "Teachers predominantly authored highly specific tasks. 80.9% of prompts provided detailed, concrete task descriptions, with only 16.0% using moderately specific prompts and a single prompt (1.1%) minimally specified."

### Alex Liu 2026 (2)

Alex Liu, Min Sun, Lief Esbenshade, Victor Tian, Zachary Zhang, and Kevin He. (2026). Teacher-Authored Prompts for Configuring StudentAI Dialogue: K12 Classroom Implementation. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16738

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

DOK coding of the same 94 prompts under Webb's framework with full human validation. The article reports 48.9% at DOK 3, 42.6% at DOK 2, and 5.3% at DOK 1; a single prompt (1.1%) targeted DOK 4.

> "Nearly half of prompts (48.9%) targeted DOK 3 (strategic thinking), requiring students to engage in reasoning, analysis, or problem-solving that goes beyond routine procedures. An additional 42.6% targeted DOK 2 (skills and concepts), while only 5.3% targeted DOK 1 (recall/reproduction)."
