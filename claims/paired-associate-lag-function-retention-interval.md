# In paired-associate paradigms, the lag function is decreasing at very short retention intervals, inverted U-shaped at slightly longer intervals, and monotonically increasing at long intervals

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The shape of the paired-associate lag effect depends on retention interval: decreasing at very short retention intervals, inverted U-shaped at slightly longer intervals, and monotonically increasing at long intervals. [→ Glenberg 1979](#glenberg-1979)

## Evidence

### Glenberg 1979

Glenberg, A. M. (1979). Component-level theory of the effects of spacing of repetitions on recall and recognition. Memory & Cognition, 7(2), 95-112. https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03197590

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

The review attributes these paired-associate lag-function shapes to Peterson, Hillner, and Saltzman (1962), Peterson et al. (1963), and Glenberg (1976), interpreting distributed practice effects in this paradigm as based on descriptive components. No effect sizes are printed.

> "At very short retention intervals, the lag funetion is deereasing (Peterson, Hillner, & Saltzman, 1962). With slightly longer retention intervals, the lag function is an inverted U-shaped function (Peterson et al., 1963). Finally, with long retention intervals, the funetion is monotonically inereasing (Glenberg, 1976)."
