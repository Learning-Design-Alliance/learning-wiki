## Evidence

### Schwartz 2011

Schwartz, D. L., Chase, C. C., Oppezzo, M. A., & Chin, D. B. (2011). Practicing versus inventing with contrasting cases: The effects of telling first on learning and transfer. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://aaalab.stanford.edu/transfer.html. [doi:10.1037/a0025140](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0025140)

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 randomly assigned half of each condition to the four-case trampoline problem and half to a single-trampoline version; the absent interaction indicates, in the authors' words, that "the ICC students transferred their understanding of ratio."

> "Figure 7 indicates a consistent advantage for the ICC condition for both 1- and 4-case problems; F(1,112) = 10.8, p = .001, d = .31. Although the 4-case problem was solved more frequently than the 1-case problem, the difference was not significant; F(1, 112)=2.7, p = .105. Importantly, there was no treatment by number-of-cases interaction; F(1,112) = 0.5, p = .481, which indicates that the ICC students transferred their understanding of ratio."
