## Evidence

### Ortiz Martín I 2025

Ortiz Martín I, Del Espino Pérez Á, García Luque E, Viguera Mínguez E. (2025). Using active learning methodologies to teach sequence analysis and molecular phylogeny. Biochemistry and Molecular Biology Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/bmb.21861

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Cohort assessment of reports from 244 students who completed the activity across 2020/21 and 2021/22, scored on the weighted Likert-type rubric. The article reports "64.3% (60.3% for 2020/21 and 68.6% for 2021/22)" met standards; no effect size is printed.

> "In this sense, this didactic proposal proved to be effective in most cases, with 64.3% (60.3% for 2020/21 and 68.6% for 2021/22) of students demonstrating knowledge and skills that met the set standards, compared to 35.7% (39.7% for 2020/21 and 31.4% for 2021/22) who did not meet the minimum requirements and needed significant support (Figure 7a)."

### Ortiz Martín I 2025 (2)

Ortiz Martín I, Del Espino Pérez Á, García Luque E, Viguera Mínguez E. (2025). Using active learning methodologies to teach sequence analysis and molecular phylogeny. Biochemistry and Molecular Biology Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/bmb.21861

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Chi-squared test of association between proficiency levels and academic years performed on the rubric assessment results. The printed test "p‐value of 0.1137" exceeded 0.05; only a test statistic is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "The results showed a chi‐squared value of χ2 ≈ 5.96 with 3 ° of freedom, and a p‐value of 0.1137. Since the p‐value is greater than the significance level of 0.05, there were no significant differences between the qualifications of the different academic years."
