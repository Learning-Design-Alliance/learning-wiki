## Evidence

### Mirofora Pilakouta and John Sinatkas 2020

Mirofora Pilakouta and John Sinatkas. (2020). Surveying Undrergraduare Greek Student's Knowledge on Some Issues of Radiations and Nuclear Energy. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2006.03549](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2006.03549)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Survey question B1 (multiple answers allowed) on which radiations could cause genetic mutation in high dose. The article reports 29% correct (exclusively x and γ rays) versus 30% attributing mutation to cell phone radiation and 18% to radio waves, indicating confusion about ionizing and non-ionizing radiation.

> "only 29%  of the participant students , table 2,  indicate the correct answer that is exclusively   x and γ rays. Moreover the fact that 30% of student thinks that cell phone radiation and radio waves 18% or eve n the visible light may cause genetic mutatio"

### Mirofora Pilakouta and John Sinatkas 2020 (2)

Mirofora Pilakouta and John Sinatkas. (2020). Surveying Undrergraduare Greek Student's Knowledge on Some Issues of Radiations and Nuclear Energy. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2006.03549](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2006.03549)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Survey question B2 on which radiations are emitted by radioactive nucleus. Only 28% answered exclusively γ rays; 10% thought cell phone radiation and 16% radio waves are emitted by unstable nucleus, which the authors read as knowledge that is "not only poor but is also very confused."

> "Overall 64% of them indicated gamma rays together with other radiations, 36% indicated X Rays and other radiations, but only 28% of the participants indicated the correct answer that is exclusively γ rays."
