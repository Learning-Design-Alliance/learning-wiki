## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Point–biserial discrimination indices (leave-one-out adjusted) were moderate or higher (≳ 0.30) for most questions, peaking at Q10 (0.59), and the joint difficulty–discrimination pattern favored questions in the roughly 0.5–0.7 difficulty band. [→ Yong Oh Lee 2026](#yong-oh-lee-2026)

## Evidence

### Yong Oh Lee 2026

Yong Oh Lee, Byeonghun Bang, Joohyun Lee, Sejun Oh. (2026). Personalized Auto-Grading and Feedback System for Constructive Geometry Tasks Using Large Language Models on an Online Math Platform. Preprint. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1109/access.2026.3657726](https://doi.org/10.1109/access.2026.3657726)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Psychometric analysis of first-attempt data from the field study: difficulty indices ranged roughly 0.42–0.91, and LOO-adjusted discrimination peaked for Q10 (0.59), Q4 (0.53), Q2 (0.54), and Q13 (0.51), with low values for very easy items like Q8 (0.14).

> "Questions in the mid –difficulty band (roughly 0.5–0.7) tended to discriminate best, whereas very easy questions (high difficulty index) showed attenuated discrimination, as expected."
