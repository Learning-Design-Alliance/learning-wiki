# California LEAs reported sufficient fully qualified TK lead teachers in 2022–23 but high second-adult vacancies, and projected needing 1,489 more TK lead teachers and 962 more aides in 2023–24

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Almost all LEAs (92%) indicated having enough fully qualified staff to accommodate TK classrooms, and only 1% of TK lead teacher positions were vacant at the beginning and middle of 2022–23. [→ Wang 2024](#wang-2024)
`q2 i?` The vacancy rate for second classroom adult positions was 12% at the beginning of 2022–23 and 8% mid-year, higher than the 2–4% vacancy rates for CSPP or Head Start teaching positions. [→ Wang 2024 (2)](#wang-2024-2)
`q2 i?` For 2023–24, LEAs predicted needing to fill 1,489 more TK lead teacher and 962 more classroom aide positions than in 2022–23, when they reported needing 7,740 TK teachers and 7,513 second adults. [→ Wang 2024 (3)](#wang-2024-3)

## Evidence

### Wang 2024

Wang, V., Leung-Gagné, M., Melnick, H., & Wechsler, M. E. (2024). Universal prekindergarten expansion in California: Progress and opportunities. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/597.103

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive survey results on TK staffing (Figure 3). The report notes it is unclear whether respondents interpreted "fully qualified" against 2022–23 requirements (credential plus 1:12 adult-to-student ratio) or the stricter 2025–26 requirements, and that recruiting qualified staff was the second most cited challenge.

> "Almost all LEAs (92%) indicated having enough fully qualified staff to accommodate Tk classrooms."

### Wang 2024 (2)

Wang, V., Leung-Gagné, M., Melnick, H., & Wechsler, M. E. (2024). Universal prekindergarten expansion in California: Progress and opportunities. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/597.103

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey finding on paraprofessional vacancies, contrasted with only 1% of TK lead teacher positions and 2–4% of CSPP or Head Start positions vacant. The report states these high vacancy rates indicate a need to invest in recruiting, training, and retaining the paraprofessional workforce.

> "The vacancy rate for the second classroom adult positions was higher, at 12% at the beginning of the 2022–23 school year and 8% in the middle of the school year."

### Wang 2024 (3)

Wang, V., Leung-Gagné, M., Melnick, H., & Wechsler, M. E. (2024). Universal prekindergarten expansion in California: Progress and opportunities. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/597.103

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey projection based on projected 2023–24 enrollment (Figure 4); LEAs anticipated similar numbers of CSPP and Head Start teachers as 2022–23. In 2022–23 LEAs reported needing 7,740 TK teachers, 3,010 CSPP teachers, 745 Head Start teachers, and 7,513 second adults.

> "for Tk classrooms, however, LEAs predicted needing to fill 1,489 more Tk lead teacher and 962 more classroom aide positions in 2023–24 than in the previous year."
