Evidence¶

Ramirez 2013¶

Ramirez, H., & Jones, D. (2013). Effects of reading strategies and the writing process with written recasts on second language achievement. Administrative Issues Journal: Education, Practice, and Research, 3(1). https://doi.org/10.5929/2013.3.1.7

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Paired-sample (repeated-measures) t tests within each group across the three test administrations in the 116-student study; gains were "highly statistically significant (p > .00) for both groups", with effect sizes the article calls of very large practical importance.

"The difference between the posttest – pretest, and the delayed posttest – pretest means for both groups was highly statistically significant (p > .00) for both groups."

Ramirez 2013 (2)¶

Ramirez, H., & Jones, D. (2013). Effects of reading strategies and the writing process with written recasts on second language achievement. Administrative Issues Journal: Education, Practice, and Research, 3(1). https://doi.org/10.5929/2013.3.1.7

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Repeated-measures comparison of posttest to delayed posttest within each group; the article reports the differences were "not statistically significant", indicating both groups maintained performance over the two-week interval. No effect size is printed for this contrast.

"The delayed posttest – posttest differences, although not statistically significant, show that both groups maintained their performance over that time period."
