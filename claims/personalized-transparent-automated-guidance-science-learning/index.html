Evidence¶

Passonneau 2017¶

Passonneau, R. J., McNamara, D., Muresan, S., & Perin, D. (2017). Preface: Special Issue on Multidisciplinary Approaches to AI and Education for Reading and Writing. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-017-0158-8

q2 · i? · causal · r?

The editors report, citing Tansomboon et al., a study of adaptive guidance for short answers in a web-based science inquiry curriculum; "The effect was significant, but only for a school that comprised more students with low prior knowledge". No effect size is printed.

"The first of two studies found that students demonstrated better learning if automated guidance was personalized (e.g., with use of students' names) and transparent (i.e., students were provided age-appropriate explanations of how the computer selected feedback statements)."

Passonneau 2017 (2)¶

Passonneau, R. J., McNamara, D., Muresan, S., & Perin, D. (2017). Preface: Special Issue on Multidisciplinary Approaches to AI and Education for Reading and Writing. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-017-0158-8

q2 · i? · causal · r?

The editors report the second Tansomboon et al. study comparing revisit-ideas guidance with revision-planning prompts; "found similar learning gains". No effect size is printed.

"The second study compared two kinds of specific guidance, asking students to revisit the ideas and prompting them on ways to plan a revision, and found similar learning gains."
