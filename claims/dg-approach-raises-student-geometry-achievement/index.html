Evidence¶

Jiang et al. 2015¶

Zhonghong Jiang, Edwin Dickey, Alexander White, Ewelina McBroom, M. Alejandra Sorto, & Alana Rosenwasser. (2015). A Dynamic Geometry-Centered Teacher Professional Development Program and Its Impact. Proceedings of the 37th annual meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education, East Lansing, MI: Michigan State University. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED584322

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

Two-level hierarchical linear modeling of student Exiting Geometry Test (XGT) post-test scores with Entering Geometry Test (ENT) pre-test scores as covariate, across Regular, Pre-AP, and Middle School class levels. The article reports "the DG effect was strongly significant (p = .002)" with effect size .45.

"The results of the model indicated that the DG effect was strongly significant (p = .002). Comparing the means, the DG group was higher than the control group in each level of Geometry and the effect size (.45) was substantially larger at the Re gular Geometry level."
