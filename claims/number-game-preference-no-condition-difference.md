## Evidence

### James-Brabham 2024

James-Brabham, E., Jay, T., & Sella, F. (2024). No evidence that playing a linear number board game improves numerical skills beyond teaching as usual: a randomized controlled trial in four- to five-year-old primary school children. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000857

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Pre-test game-preference question (pointing to number or letter pictures) analyzed with a chi-square test in the RCT; printed proportions were 61%, 54.9%, and 61.2% preferring the number game across the three conditions.

> "Children preferred playing a number game and such preference did not significantly vary among conditions (Χ2(2)=0.87, p=0.65; Number game – Forward: 61%; Number Game – Bidirectional: 54.9%; Alphabet Lotto: 61.2%)."
