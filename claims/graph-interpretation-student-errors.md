## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Four error types documented in STEM education research and seen by the author in introductory physics create barriers to building the blend: treating a graph as a picture, confusing slope and height, confusing an interval and a point, and reading positive and negative areas under a curve. ## Evidence

### Edward F. Redish 2023

Edward F. Redish. (2023). Using math in physics: 6. Reading the physics in a graph. The Physics Teacher (series paper, dated 3/14/23). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2303.09589](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2303.09589)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article attributes these errors to extensive STEM education research (citing Beichner, Leinhardt et al., Glazer, Susac et al.) and reports the author has seen them in his own introductory physics students. For example, a student may read a rising velocity graph as meaning the object is moving up, a one-step-thinking response. No new empirical data are reported here.

> "Here are 4 errors documented in this literature that I have seen from my students in introductory physics classes that create barriers to their building the blend."
