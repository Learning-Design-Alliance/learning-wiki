## Evidence

### Gupta R 2021

Gupta R, DeSandro S, Doherty NA, Gardner AK, Pillow MT. (2021). Medical and Physician Assistant Student Competence in Basic Life Support: Opportunities to Improve Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation Training. Western Journal of Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2020.11.48536

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cross-sectional manikin assessment of 80 students using t-tests and one-way ANOVA to relate prior experience to CPR metrics. The study reports these three experience types "were associated with improved performance."

> "Past BLS course attendance, prior healthcare certification, and previous provision of real-life CPR were associated with improved performance."

### Gupta R 2021 (2)

Gupta R, DeSandro S, Doherty NA, Gardner AK, Pillow MT. (2021). Medical and Physician Assistant Student Competence in Basic Life Support: Opportunities to Improve Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation Training. Western Journal of Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2020.11.48536

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same manikin study comparing students who had administered real-life compressions with those who had not. The study reports "significant increases in mean compression depth (56.4 mm ±6.0 vs 51.2 mm ±7.3; P < 0.001)" and no correlation for observation alone.

> "Previous observation of CPR did not correlate with performance; however, provision of real-life CPR did. Specifically, participation in CPR was associated with significant increases in mean compression depth (56.4 mm ±6.0 vs 51.2 mm ±7.3; P < 0.001)"
