## Evidence

### Wolf Julian 2021

Wolf Julian, Wolfer Viviane, Halbe Maximilian, Maisano Francesco, Lohmeyer Quentin, Meboldt Mirko. (2021). Comparing the effectiveness of augmented reality-based and conventional instructions during single ECMO cannulation training. International Journal of Computer Assisted Radiology and Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11548-021-02408-y

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Counterbalanced simulator study with 21 medical students, each performing two procedures with one AR and one conventional instruction mode. For the more complex P2, the article reports significantly lower error counts with AR instructions and only half the variance in training times, quoting "significantly lower error counts than conventional instructions".

> "For P2, AR instructions were associated with slightly higher training times, but with only half the variance. AR instructions also resulted in significantly lower error counts than conventional instructions (p<0.05, Mann–Whitney U test)."
