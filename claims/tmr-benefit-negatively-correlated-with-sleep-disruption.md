## Evidence

### Whitmore 2022

Whitmore, N. W., Bassard, A. M. & Paller, K. A. (2022). Targeted memory reactivation of face-name learning depends on ample and undisturbed slow-wave sleep. npj Science of Learning, 7:1. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00119-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Correlational analysis in the same nap experiment using a newly developed Sleep Disruption Index summing absolute EEG power change at Cz from 0.38 to 20.35 Hz during the 5 seconds after cue onset relative to the 5 prior seconds.

> "cue-related sleep disruption, as assessed by the Sleep Disruption Index, was negatively correlated with the cuing effect on Δrecall [χ² (1,N = 24) = 3.98, p = 0.046]"

### Whitmore 2022 (2)

Whitmore, N. W., Bassard, A. M. & Paller, K. A. (2022). Targeted memory reactivation of face-name learning depends on ample and undisturbed slow-wave sleep. npj Science of Learning, 7:1. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00119-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary frequency-band analysis dividing the 0.38–20.35 Hz spectrum into six 3.33-Hz-wide bands and correlating cue-evoked power in each band with the recall cuing effect. Marginal negative correlations appeared in lower alpha (p=0.05) and lower beta (p=0.06) bands.

> "Cue-evoked power in the highest beta band (17.03 –20.35 Hz) was negatively correlated with the cuing effect; a greater cuing effect was seen with less beta power after a cue [ χ² (1,N = 24) = 10.1, p = 0.0015"
