# Outcome differences between CHSRI new-start and redesigned schools were not statistically significant after accounting for incoming student differences

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Although new-start schools served students with stronger backgrounds and showed stronger raw outcomes, differences in absences, on-track rates, core GPA, and ACT scores between similar students in new-start versus redesigned CHSRI schools were not large enough to be statistically significant. [→ Susan Sporte 2010](#susan-sporte-2010)

## Evidence

### Susan Sporte 2010

Susan Sporte, Marisa de la Torre. (2010). Chicago High School Redesign Initiative: Schools, Students, and Outcomes. Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago, Urban Education Institute. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/chicago-high-school-redesign-initiative-schools-students-and-outcomes

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Comparative analysis adjusting for special education status, age relative to grade, prior mobility, race/ethnicity, and prior achievement. Raw outcomes differed substantially (e.g., freshman absences of 6.0-7.4 days at new-starts versus 21.7-22.6 at redesigned schools in 2004-06), but adjusted differences were not significant.

> "while students in the new-start schools did slightly better than similar students in the redesigned schools, these outcome differences were not large enough to be statistically significant"
