Evidence¶

Maximilian Seitz 2025¶

Maximilian Seitz, Anna Hawrot, Kathrin Lockl. (2025). Performance judgment in mathematical and reading competence in adults. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09416-2

q3 · i1 · associational · r3

Weighted linear regression on the mathematics subsample controlling for education showed "a significant age effect" on bias (β = 0.14, p < 0.01); the model explained R² = 0.06 and the generalized additive model showed the same positive age pattern.

"Regarding performance judgment bias in mathematical competence, there was a significant age effect in the linear model (Fig. 1), suggesting that older age groups overestimated their mathematical competence (β = 0.14, p < 0.01)."

Maximilian Seitz 2025 (2)¶

Maximilian Seitz, Anna Hawrot, Kathrin Lockl. (2025). Performance judgment in mathematical and reading competence in adults. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09416-2

q3 · i1 · associational · r3

Weighted linear regression on the reading subsample controlling for education showed a positive age effect on bias (β = 0.11, p < 0.01); the model explained R² = 0.04 and the generalized additive model replicated the pattern.

"Finally, the linear model predicting performance judgment bias in reading competence indicated a positive age effect (β = 0.11, p < 0.01), namely that older age groups showed more overestimation."

Maximilian Seitz 2025 (3)¶

Maximilian Seitz, Anna Hawrot, Kathrin Lockl. (2025). Performance judgment in mathematical and reading competence in adults. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09416-2

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Sensitivity analysis restricted to cases within 1/8 SD of the mean mathematics score (subsample n = 420): age was not a significant predictor of judgment or bias; the parallel reading analysis (n = 521) showed the same null result.

"In both models predicting performance judgment and performance judgment bias, age was not a significant predictor (Table 3S, 4S)."
