# Staff-tutored tutorial groups achieve slightly but significantly higher marks than peer-tutored groups in problem-based learning

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students guided by staff tutors received somewhat higher course marks than students guided by peer tutors; the difference was statistically significant but small in practical terms. [→ Schmidt 1995](#schmidt-1995)

## Evidence

### Schmidt 1995

Schmidt, H., van der Arend, A., Kokx, I. & Boon, L. (1995). Peer versus staff tutoring in problem-based learning. Instructional Science 22: 279-285. https://doi.org/10.1007/bf00891781

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Field experiment aggregating achievement data at the tutorial-group level across 98 problem-based health sciences courses; ANOVA showed a small significant advantage for staff tutoring, with average scores of 6.79 versus 6.64 on a ten-point scale, "statistically significant, F(1,733) -- 9.42, p < 0.002".

> "The effect of staff versus peer tutoring was small - average score in the staff tutoring condition: 6.79; peer tutoring: 6.64. This difference, however, is statistically significant, F(1,733) -- 9.42, p < 0.002."
