## Evidence

### Pontifex 2016

Pontifex, M. B., Gwizdala, K. L., Parks, A. C., Pfeiffer, K. A. & Fenn, K. M. (2016). The Association between Physical Activity During the Day and Long-Term Memory Stability. Scientific Reports 6, 38148. https://doi.org/10.1038/srep38148

`q3 · i?`

Data-driven sliding 20-minute-window regression across 9 hours post-encoding, controlling for Age, Sex, and Aerobic Fitness, identified one critical sedentary window; more sedentary time there predicted "greater long-term memory" (β = 0.27, p < 0.001).

> "Individuals who spent more time sedentary 0:56:20 to 1:51:20 following the first session exhibited greater long-term memory as indexed by a greater proportion of items maintained between session 1 and session 2 (β = 0.27, t (87) = 3.5, p < 0.001; see Fig. 2, Table 3)"
