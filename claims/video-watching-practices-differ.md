## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Specific rating disagreements (e.g., L3P1#1 vs L3P2#1, both rated "poor" by instructors but with very different student distributions) suggest a difference between the video features instructors and students attend to. [→ Scott S. Douglas 2014](#scott-s-douglas-2014)

## Evidence

### Scott S. Douglas 2014

Scott S. Douglas, Shih-Yin Lin, John M. Aiken, Brian D. Thoms, Edwin F. Greco, Marcos D. Caballero and Michael F. Schatz. (2014). Peer Evaluation of Video Lab Reports in a Blended Introductory Physics Course. [doi:10.1119/perc.2014.pr.015](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.015)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Qualitative interpretation of individual rating cases in the Discussion; instructors rated L3P1#1 "poor" because the introduction was "simply [a] reading [of] the problem statement" but L3P2#1 for containing "no intro at all", with correspondingly different student agreement. This is the authors' posited explanation, not a tested result.

> "These ratings suggest a difference in the actual content of those videos and a difference between the video features to which instructors and students attend when evaluating that rubric item (i.e., our posited difference in video-watching practices)."
