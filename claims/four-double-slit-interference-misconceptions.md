# Students commonly hold four misconceptions about double-slit interference, the most damaging being that constructive interference requires both pathlengths to be integer multiples of the wavelength

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Instructors observe that students commonly believe constructive interference requires both pathlengths to be integer multiples of the wavelength rather than only the pathlength difference, and are confused about the validity limits of the d sin θ and sin θ = y/D approximations. ### Beth Parks and Hans Benze 2022

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

Instructors' observational report of misconceptions seen in their own teaching, listing four common errors including the belief "that constructive interference requires both pathlengths to be integer multiples of the wavelength". No effect size is reported.

> "Student misconceptions of the double-slit experiment (Fig. 1) are abundant. The most common ones that we observe include: (1) belief that constructive interference requires both pathlengths to be integer multiples of the wavelength"
