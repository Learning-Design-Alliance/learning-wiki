## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Preliminary results suggest multiple choice questions can approximate certain aspects of free-response questions, especially when incorrect answers reflect common student mistakes and partial credit levels are assigned to incorrect answers. [→ Connor Gorman 2018](#connor-gorman-2018)

## Evidence

### Connor Gorman 2018

Connor Gorman, David Webb, Kevin Gee. (2018). Hierarchical Linear Modeling Approach to Measuring the Effects of Class Size and Other Classroom Characteristics on Student Learning in an Active-Learning Based Introductory Physics Course. University of California, Davis. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1809.00218](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1809.00218)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The article reports, citing preliminary results from prior work, that multiple choice questions may mimic free-response counterparts when incorrect answers conform to common free-response mistakes and partial credit is given similarly. The article notes the two formats will never be equivalent and no effect size is printed.

> "While the two will never be equivalent there are some preliminary results suggesting that it is possible for multiple choice questions to mimic their free-response counterparts under the right circumstances [15]."
