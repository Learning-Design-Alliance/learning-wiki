Evidence¶

McKinney 2026¶

McKinney, D., Walters, K., Powell, K., Sabalburo, E., & Morton, C. (2026). The impact of NBA Math Hoops on students' math achievement: A randomized controlled trial. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED681343

q4 · i1 · causal · r2

A 2-year RCT randomly assigned rising 4th-6th graders to NBA Math Hoops or business-as-usual STEM enrichment in a summer camp. Treatment students scored "0.19 standard deviations higher" than controls (β = 0.19, p < 0.05), "equivalent to a student in the 50th percentile moving to the 58th percentile."

"Regression estimates indicate that there was a significant difference between the composite math score of students in the treatment condition as compared to the control condition (𝛽 = 0.19, 𝑝 < 0.05), controlling for pre-math composite score, pre-SEL composite score, gender, and individualized education program (IEP) status."

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

Exploratory subscore analyses from the same RCT found the largest contributors were Numbers and Operations (β = 0.17, p < 0.10) and Probability and Data (β = 0.15, p > 0.10), with positive average impacts on other subscores.

"Exploratory analyses indicated that the largest contributors to the significant difference in the composite math score were Numbers and Operations (𝛽 = 0.17, 𝑝 < 0.10) and Probability and Data (𝛽 = 0.15, 𝑝 > 0.10). The average impacts on the other math subscores were also positive (Figure 2)."
