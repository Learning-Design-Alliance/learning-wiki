## Evidence

### Paine A. R. 2025

Paine A. R., Wilton M., Solanki S. M., Ellefson M., Ferguson J. E., Lo S. M., Sato B. K. (2025). Reimagining the role of teaching-focused faculty in research-intensive universities: The evolution of scholarly expectations and departmental influence. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0334895

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Self-reported formal training (Table 5); Assistant PoTs slightly more likely to have education-research postdoctoral experience (10.4% vs 2.8%; p=0.07, not significant at α=.05).

> "only about 10% of PoTs reported earning an undergraduate or graduate degree in an education research field and 5% with postdoctoral experience"

### Paine A. R. 2025 (2)

Paine A. R., Wilton M., Solanki S. M., Ellefson M., Ferguson J. E., Lo S. M., Sato B. K. (2025). Reimagining the role of teaching-focused faculty in research-intensive universities: The evolution of scholarly expectations and departmental influence. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0334895

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey resource items (Table 6): Assistant PoTs received higher start-up funds ($45,400 vs $29,000, p<.01); of those needing lab space, only 37% received it. Open-ended responses noted technically available resources were limited in practice.

> "60% of PoTs reported that these resources were inadequate (no significant difference between non-tenured and tenured faculty responses). In comparison, only 7.5% of PoTs reported that campus opportunities to improve their teaching were inadequate."
