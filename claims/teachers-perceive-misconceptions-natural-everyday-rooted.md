## Evidence

### Mutmainna 2025

Mutmainna, Istiyono, E., Haryanto, Retnawati, & Setiawan, C. (2025). Physics Teachers' Perceptions about Diagnostic Assessment of Students' Physics Misconceptions: A Phenomenological Study. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2501.10422](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2501.10422)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview finding from the phenomenological study (theme 2, RQ1): teachers such as Aswar stated misconceptions at the start of learning are "natural due to the complexity of the concepts being taught" and stem from daily-experience intuitions conflicting with physics principles.

> "Participants generally regard students' misconceptions of Physics as a natural occurrence, believing it to be rooted in their everyday experiences."

### Mutmainna 2025 (2)

Mutmainna, Istiyono, E., Haryanto, Retnawati, & Setiawan, C. (2025). Physics Teachers' Perceptions about Diagnostic Assessment of Students' Physics Misconceptions: A Phenomenological Study. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2501.10422](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2501.10422)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Same interview study: participant Budi reported students "find it very difficult to accept" teacher explanations because they struggle to move away from everyday terms; the authors link this to literature that misconceptions persist after instruction.

> "Budi further emphasized that misconceptions in Physics are particularly difficult to change, especially when they stem from students' everyday lives."
