## Evidence

### Bitar Anas 2024

Bitar Anas, Barakat Fady, Hawat Angie, Alsaid Bayan. (2024). Dietary and smoking habits during the exam period and their effect on the academic achievement among Syrian medical students. BMC Medical Education 24:60. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04950-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The Spearman analysis found "no significant correlation was found for snacks (rs = -0.031, p = 0.45), or weekly breakfast consumption (rs = 0.033, p = 0.41), or weekly fruit consumption (rs = -0.026, p = 0.52)". The article notes breakfast findings are consistent with one prior study but contrast with others; equivalence was not tested.

> "no significant correlation was found for snacks (rs = -0.031, p = 0.45), or weekly breakfast consumption (rs = 0.033, p = 0.41), or weekly fruit consumption (rs = -0.026, p = 0.52)"

### Bitar Anas 2024 (2)

Bitar Anas, Barakat Fady, Hawat Angie, Alsaid Bayan. (2024). Dietary and smoking habits during the exam period and their effect on the academic achievement among Syrian medical students. BMC Medical Education 24:60. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04950-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

An independent samples t-test comparing students with and without scheduled eating (breakfast, lunch, dinner) found "no significant effect was observed (t = -0.356, p = 0.72)". The authors limit this by noting the survey did not capture meal components and timing.

> "no significant effect was observed (t = -0.356, p = 0.72)"
