Evidence¶

Armah 2018¶

Armah, R.B., Cofie, P.O., & Okpoti, C.A. (2018). Investigating the effect of van Hiele Phase-based instruction on pre-service teachers' geometric thinking. International Journal of Research in Education and Science (IJRES), 4(1), 314-330. https://doi.org/10.21890/ijres.383201

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Pre-test bar-chart analysis (Figure 3) of van Hiele levels for both groups; the article adds that 36% of each group attained VHL 2 and "no PT attained VHL 4 and 5", indicating low entry levels of geometric thinking.

"As shown in Figure 3, 24% and 22.67% of PTs attained van Hiele Level (VHL) 0 (i.e. the Pre -recognition Level or Level for those who have not yet attained any van Hiele Level) in the control and experimental groups respect ively."

Armah 2018 (2)¶

Armah, R.B., Cofie, P.O., & Okpoti, C.A. (2018). Investigating the effect of van Hiele Phase-based instruction on pre-service teachers' geometric thinking. International Journal of Research in Education and Science (IJRES), 4(1), 314-330. https://doi.org/10.21890/ijres.383201

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Authors' interpretation of the post-test level distribution: the low percentages at Level 4 and the absence of Level 5 indicate the pre-service teachers had difficulties understanding the proof of theorems despite the experimental group's significant gains.

"Key to this study is the finding that none of the PTs attained van Hiele Level 5 in both pretest and posttest. These findings show that the cohorts of PTs were not prepared for formal geometric proofs in college of education."
