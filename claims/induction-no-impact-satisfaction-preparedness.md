## Evidence

### Glazerman 2010

Glazerman, S., E. Isenberg, S. Dolfin, M. Bleeker, A. Johnson, M. Grider, and M. Jacobus. (2010). Impacts of Comprehensive Teacher Induction: Final Results From a Randomized Controlled Study (NCEE 2010-4027). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Teacher surveys measuring attitudes administered six times over four years to the full sample of 1,009 teachers. Neither one-year nor two-year exposure produced "more satisfied or feeling more prepared" reports relative to controls at any time point.

> "Treatment teachers did not report being more satisfied or feeling more prepared to teach than control teachers at any of the six time points over the four school years in which we collected data."
