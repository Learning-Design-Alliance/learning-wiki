## Evidence

### Gilmore C 2013

Gilmore C, Attridge N, Clayton S, Cragg L, Johnson S, Marlow N, Simms V, Inglis M. (2013). Individual Differences in Inhibitory Control, Not Non-Verbal Number Acuity, Correlate with Mathematics Achievement. PLoS ONE 8(6): e67374. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0067374

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Experiment 1 correlational analysis treating the RT difference between trial types as an index of inhibition skill. Children with "good inhibition skills" showed higher Woodcock-Johnson III calculation scores (r = -.27, p = .015).

> "Children with a smaller RT difference between correctly answered incongruent and congruent trials, and thus good inhibition skills, scored higher on the mathematics achievement test than children with a larger RT difference (r = -.27, p = .015)."
