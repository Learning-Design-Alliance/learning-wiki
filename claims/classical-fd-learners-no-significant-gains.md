# Classical field-dependent learners show no significant knowledge or competence gains after M-TEL, unlike classical field-independent learners

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Increases in MCQ and MST scores did not reach statistical significance in the 5 classical field-dependent learners (P=.14 and P=.85) but were significant in the 55 classical field-independent learners (P<.001 and P=.003). [→ Lee Li-Ang 2018](#lee-li-ang-2018)

## Evidence

### Lee Li-Ang 2018

Lee Li-Ang, Chao Yi-Ping, Huang Chung-Guei, Fang Ji-Tseng, Wang Shu-Ling, Chuang Cheng-Keng, Kang Chung-Jan, Hsin Li-Jen, Lin Wan-Ni, Fang Tuan-Jen, Li Hsueh-Yu. (2018). Cognitive Style and Mobile E-Learning in Emergent Otorhinolaryngology-Head and Neck Surgery Disorders for Millennial Undergraduate Medical Students: Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.8987

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-group comparisons after classical GEFT classification (FD: score ≤12, n=5; FI: score >12, n=55). The article reports FD learners' increases "did not reach statistical significance" while FI learners' scores were "significantly increased"; no effect sizes are printed.

> "After 100 min of M-TEL, increases in MCQ and MST scores in the classical FD learners did not reach statistical significance (P=.14 and P=.85, respectively), whereas they were significantly increased in the classical FI learners (P<.001 and P=.003, respectively)."

### Lee Li-Ang 2018 (2)

Lee Li-Ang, Chao Yi-Ping, Huang Chung-Guei, Fang Ji-Tseng, Wang Shu-Ling, Chuang Cheng-Keng, Kang Chung-Jan, Hsin Li-Jen, Lin Wan-Ni, Fang Tuan-Jen, Li Hsueh-Yu. (2018). Cognitive Style and Mobile E-Learning in Emergent Otorhinolaryngology-Head and Neck Surgery Disorders for Millennial Undergraduate Medical Students: Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.8987

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Between-group comparison of percentage changes between the classical FD and FI groups using the printed P values; the article notes the classical FD learners had a lower GSS than FI learners even though that difference was not statistically significant.

> "However, the percentage changes in MCQ and MST scores were not significantly different between the classical FD and FI groups (P=.90 and P=.68, respectively)."
