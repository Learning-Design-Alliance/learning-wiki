# Individuals improve more from day 1 to day 4 than dyads, with the learning effect dominating group effects

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The proportional hazards interaction model showed group differences in improvement for time to diagnosis (p = 0.02) and time to TP indication (p < 0.001), with individuals improving more (bradycardia HR = 5.1 vs 3.1; pacing indication HR = 19.0 vs 8.1), though the day effect was greatest. [→ Abbott Eduardo F. 2021](#abbott-eduardo-f-2021)

## Evidence

### Abbott Eduardo F. 2021

Abbott Eduardo F., Laack Torrey A., Licatino Lauren K., Wood-Wentz Christina M., Warner Paul A., Torsher Laurence C., Newman James S., Rieck Katie M. (2021). Comparison of dyad versus individual simulation-based training on stress, anxiety, cognitive load, and performance: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-021-02786-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Three-parameter interaction model (day, treatment, day*treatment) in the randomized trial; individuals "had a greater improvement in their time to task completion relative to dyads" because they performed more poorly initially.

> "The proportional hazards model for the differential training effect between the randomized groups indicates the groups differed in their time to task completion for the time to correct diagnosis of bradycardia (p = 0.02), time to TP indication (p < 0.001), but in both cases the learning effect (day 1 versus day 4) has the greatest effect."
