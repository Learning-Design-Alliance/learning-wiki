Evidence¶

Thomas M. Cavanagh 2023¶

Thomas M. Cavanagh, Christa Kiersch. (2023). Using commonly-available technologies to create online multimedia lessons through the application of the Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-022-10181-1

q1 · i? · theoretical · r3

This is the authors' theoretical argument, not a tested result: applying CTML in authentic online settings raises unanswered questions, including that "CTML needs to clarify the relationship between working memory capacity and working memory duration" and retrieval's status.

"we suggest that CTML needs to clarify the relationship between working memory capacity and working memory duration, the importance of retrieval as a learning process, and the relative impact of learning principles on selection and organization processes"

Thomas M. Cavanagh 2023 (2)¶

Thomas M. Cavanagh, Christa Kiersch. (2023). Using commonly-available technologies to create online multimedia lessons through the application of the Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-022-10181-1

q1 · i? · review · r2

The authors argue the coherence principle may trade learning against satisfaction: seductive details harm learning yet "are more likely to be perceived as enjoyable by learners", so future research should explore such double-edged principles.

"Yet such attention-grabbing material is more likely to be perceived as enjoyable by learners and may contribute to their overall affective reactions to the lesson (Sung & Mayer, 2012)"
