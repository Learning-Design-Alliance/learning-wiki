## Evidence

### Bartley 2019

Bartley, J. E., Riedel, M. C., Salo, T., Boeving, E. R., Bottenhorn, K. L., Bravo, E. I., Odean, R., Nazareth, A., Laird, R. W., Sutherland, M. T., Pruden, S. M., Brewe, E. & Laird, A. R. (2019). Brain activity links performance in science reasoning with conceptual approach. npj Science of Learning 4:20. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0059-8

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Whole-brain one-way ANOVA on FCI > Control activity across the three normative groups, with post hoc pairwise tests (P < 0.001 CDT, P < 0.05 CET FWE corrected). No significant differences were observed between Groups A and B. The authors suggest less coherent reasoners engage more visual search for salient problem features.

> "Overall, the student who answered by using more coherent physics conceptions, even if incorrect, showed increased reliance on a lOFC-V5/MT + network, whereas students who held less consistent ideas involving multiple conceptual approaches showed increased primary visual and SN activity."
