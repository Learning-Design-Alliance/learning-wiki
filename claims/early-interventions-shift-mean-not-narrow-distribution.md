## Evidence

### H. Moriah Sokolowski and Daniel Ansari 2018

H. Moriah Sokolowski and Daniel Ansari. (2018). Understanding the effects of education through the lens of biology. npj Science of Learning 3:17. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0032-y

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Follow-up study of the Carolina Abecedarian Project reporting Woodcock–Johnson age-referenced standard scores: experimental group scored 5–7 points higher than controls, yet "little difference in variance of scores" between groups, so the high–low gap persisted.

> "standard scores for reading, math, written language, and knowledge were greater in the experimental group than in the control group by 5 – 7 points. However, there was little difference in variance of scores in the intervention group compared to the control group."

### H. Moriah Sokolowski and Daniel Ansari 2018 (2)

H. Moriah Sokolowski and Daniel Ansari. (2018). Understanding the effects of education through the lens of biology. npj Science of Learning 3:17. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0032-y

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, from empirical reading and mathematics intervention research, that "approximately 5% of the children were labeled as “treatment resisters”" because they did not respond, indicating large inter-individual differences in response to interventions.

> "In empirical research on both reading and mathematics interventions, approximately 5% of the children were labeled as “treatment resisters”, because they did not respond to interventions."
