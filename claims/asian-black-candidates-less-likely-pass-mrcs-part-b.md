## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After adjusting for prior academic attainment and all sociodemographic factors, Asian candidates (OR 0.49, 95% CI 0.35–0.69) and Black candidates (OR 0.41, 95% CI 0.18–0.92) had lower odds of passing MRCS Part B at the first attempt than White candidates. ## Evidence

### Ricky Ellis 2022

Ricky Ellis, Peter A Brennan, Amanda J Lee, Duncan SG Scrimgeour, Jennifer Cleland. (2022). Differential attainment at MRCS according to gender, ethnicity, age and socioeconomic factors: a retrospective cohort study. Journal of the Royal Society of Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1177/01410768221079018

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multivariate logistic regression in the Part B cohort (final model N = 1125) adjusting for EPM decile and all sociodemographic factors, with White candidates as reference. The article reports "Asian (OR 0.49, 95% CI 0.35–0.69)" and Black candidates (OR 0.41) as significantly less likely to pass; no standardised effect size is printed.

> "In addition, Asian (OR 0.49, 95% CI 0.35–0.69) and Black candidates (OR 0.41, 95% 0.18–0.92) were significantly less likely to pass MRCS Part B at the first attempt in comparison to their white colleagues."
