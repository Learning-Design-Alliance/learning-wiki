# The plateau visual form of the moment-by-moment learning curve is significantly negatively associated with all four measures of robust learning in a college genetics tutor

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Students with a higher proportion of plateau curve forms scored significantly lower on problem-solving, transfer, PFL, and retention tests, with the strongest association on retention (r = -.52). [→ Baker et al. 2013](#baker-et-al-2013)

## Evidence

### Baker et al. 2013

Ryan S. Baker, Arnon Hershkovitz, Lisa M. Rossi, Adam B. Goldstein & Sujith M. Gowda (2013). Predicting Robust Learning With the Visual Form of the Moment-by-Moment Learning Curve. Journal of the Learning Sciences, 22:4, 639-666. https://doi.org/10.1080/10508406.2013.836653

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis of 72 undergraduates using the Genetics Cognitive Tutor three-factor cross module: average occurrence of each curve-form tag per student was correlated with four posttests, controlling false discovery rate with q values. The plateau form was negatively associated with all four tests, with printed correlations r = -.38 (posttest), r = -.28 (transfer), r = -.27 (PFL), and r = -.52 (retention).

> "The plateau curve form, corresponding to considerable learning occurring over consecutive steps, was statistically significantly negatively associated with the problem-solving posttest, r = –.38, F(1, 71) = 11.79, q < .01; the transfer test, r = –.28, F(1, 71) = 5.85, q = .04; the PFL test, r = –.27, F(1, 71) = 5.66, q = .04; and the problem-solving retention test, r = –.52, F(1, 71) = 25.65, q < .01."
