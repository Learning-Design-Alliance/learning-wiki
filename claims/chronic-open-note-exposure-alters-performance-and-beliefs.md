# Chronic exposure to open-note testing produces a significant open/closed difference on Bloom's 1–2 questions and shifts student beliefs about which format is advantageous

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students with chronic (quarter-long) open-note exposure showed a significant open- versus closed-note difference on Bloom's 1 and 2 questions and a larger, non-significant difference on overall exam score. [→ Sato 2015](#sato-2015)
`q3 i?` Chronic open-note testers taking the quiz open note were more likely than the onetime group to believe closed-note testing was advantageous (p = 0.02). [→ Sato 2015 (2)](#sato-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Sato 2015

Sato, B. K., He, W., Warschauer, M., & Kadandale, P. (2015). The Grass Isn't Always Greener: Perceptions of and Performance on Open-Note Exams. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-08-0121

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Comparison of molecular biology lab students (chronic: all regular exams open note) versus microbiology and biochemistry lab students (onetime). Table 2 shows the open onetime interaction on Bloom's 1–2 of −0.150 (p = 0.041). No effect size printed.

> "Examining scores in the context of chronic versus onetime open-note testing uncovered a significant difference in performance on questions at the Bloom's 1 and 2 levels for chronic students and a larger, albeit not statistically significant, difference on overall exam score"

### Sato 2015 (2)

Sato, B. K., He, W., Warschauer, M., & Kadandale, P. (2015). The Grass Isn't Always Greener: Perceptions of and Performance on Open-Note Exams. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-08-0121

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Perception survey item on which section would earn the highest score, analyzed across chronic versus onetime exposure groups (Figure 4B); the chronic open-note group shifted toward seeing closed-note testing as advantageous (p = 0.02). Reported as a p-value only.

> "Chronic open-note testers who were allowed to take the paper quiz open note as well were more likely to believe that closed-note testing was advantageous compared with the views of the onetime group (p = 0.02)."
