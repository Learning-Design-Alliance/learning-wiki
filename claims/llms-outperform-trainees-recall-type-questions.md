## Evidence

### Kim Seonghwan 2026

Kim Seonghwan, Ji Man, Kim Chung Young, Yun Young In, Lee Gahye, Yoon Chang Ho, Kim Mee Kum. (2026). Comparative performance of large language models in answering cornea and cataract surgery questions for resident training. BMC Ophthalmology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12886-026-04767-z

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Comparison on the 27 recall-type questions between the four LLMs and six human trainees. The article interprets this as LLMs effectively retrieving textbook-based factual knowledge, supporting their role as supplementary tools for reinforcing foundational learning.

> "In recall-type questions, ChatGPT-5 achieved 92.59% accuracy and the other LLMs achieved 85.19%, all outperforming residents (71.60 ± 17.50%) and fellows (82.72 ± 4.28%) (Table 1)."

### Kim Seonghwan 2026 (2)

Kim Seonghwan, Ji Man, Kim Chung Young, Yun Young In, Lee Gahye, Yoon Chang Ho, Kim Mee Kum. (2026). Comparative performance of large language models in answering cornea and cataract surgery questions for resident training. BMC Ophthalmology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12886-026-04767-z

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

One-sample Wilcoxon signed-rank test on recall-type scores versus all trainees; only ChatGPT-5 reached significance (p = 0.031).

> "In recall-type questions, ChatGPT-5 significantly outperformed all trainees (p = 0.031), while ChatGPT-4o, Gemini 3.0 Pro, and Claude Sonnet 4.5 did not show significant differences compared with all trainees (Table 1)."
