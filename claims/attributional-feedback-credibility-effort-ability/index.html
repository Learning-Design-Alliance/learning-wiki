Evidence¶

Schunk 1995¶

Schunk, D. H. (1995). Self-efficacy, motivation, and performance. Journal of Applied Sport Psychology, 7(2), 112-137. http://www.taylorandfrancis.com/

q3 · i? · review · r2

Experiment during a subtraction instruction program in which children periodically received feedback linking performance with ability, effort, or both. All three conditions raised motivation equally; ability-plus-effort subjects may have discounted ability information.

"Ability feedback enhanced post-instructional self-efficacy and performance better than effort feedback or ability-plus-effort feedback."

Schunk 1995 (2)¶

Schunk, D. H. (1995). Self-efficacy, motivation, and performance. Journal of Applied Sport Psychology, 7(2), 112-137. http://www.taylorandfrancis.com/

q3 · i? · review · r2

Study of children with severe reading deficiencies during reading comprehension instruction; comprehension success was limited early, so ability feedback for early successes may not have been credible.

"Schunk and Rice (1986) found during reading comprehension instruction that later ability feedback exerted better effects on ability attributions and efficacy than did early ability feedback."
