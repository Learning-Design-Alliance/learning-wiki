# Grade 6 learners show no significant difference in mathematics gains whether using a customised workbook or a conventional textbook

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a 44-school RCT, gains did not differ significantly between the workbook (PM) and textbook (CM) groups, each improving by over 8 percentage points. [→ Brahm Fleisch 2011](#brahm-fleisch-2011)

## Evidence

### Brahm Fleisch 2011

Brahm Fleisch, Nick Taylor, Roelien Herholdt and Ingrid Sapire. (2011). Evaluation of Back to Basics mathematics workbooks: a randomised control trial of the Primary Mathematics Research Project. South African Journal of Education, Vol 31:488-504. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1136612

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomised control trial in 44 Gauteng primary schools (22 per group), Grade 6, 14 weeks, with 1374 CM and 1141 PM learners tested pre and post. The difference in gains between CM and PM was "not statistically significant (p value of 0.29)"; equivalence was not tested.

> "Despite the differences in both form (workbook vs. textbook) and internal logic of these materials, there was no significant difference in the gains made by the two groups, each group achieving a mean improvement of over 8 percentage points"
