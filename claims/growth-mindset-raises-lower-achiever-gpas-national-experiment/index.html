q4 · i1 · causal · r3

Intention-to-treat analysis of the National Study of Learning Mindsets, a nationally representative randomized experiment in 65 regular US public high schools. Treated lower-achieving students showed "standardized mean difference effect size of 0.11" on end-of-year core-course GPA versus control.

"lower-achieving adolescents earned higher GPAs in core classes at the end of the ninth grade when assigned to the growth mindset intervention, B = 0.10 grade points (95% confidence interval = 0.04, 0.16), s.e. = 0.03, n = 6,320, k = 65, t = 3.51, P = 0.001, standardized mean difference effect size of 0.11"

q2 · i? · causal · r3

Blinded Bayesian machine-learning robustness analysis (Bayesian causal forest) run by statisticians blind to study hypotheses corroborated the positive average GPA treatment effect, with "PPATE > 0 ≥ 0.999".

"The BCF analysis assigned a near-certain posterior probability that the population-average treatment effect (PATE) among lower-achieving students was positive and greater than zero, PPATE > 0 ≥ 0.999"

  • GPA treatment effects among lower-achieving students were smaller in higher-achieving schools (moderation by school achievement level) — related
  • The growth mindset intervention increased tenth-grade advanced mathematics course enrolment overall, with larger gains in the highest-achieving schools — related
  • Cross-school variability in the GPA treatment effect was significant while variability in the mindset treatment effect was not — related
  • GPA treatment effects among lower-achieving students were larger in schools whose peer norms supported challenge-seeking — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
