# A 4-hour WHO-based PFA training program significantly increases community nurses' PFA knowledge immediately after training, with a smaller but sustained advantage at 2 months

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Intervention-group nurses scored significantly higher on PFA knowledge than controls immediately after training (r = 0.42). [→ He Yazhou 2026](#he-yazhou-2026)

## Evidence

### He Yazhou 2026

He Yazhou, Ding Yulan, Yan Jiaqin, Xiong Xiaoning, Huang Shan, Li Sijian. (2026). Evaluation of the Effectiveness of Psychological First Aid Training for Community Nurses in China: A Quasi‐Experimental Study on Knowledge and Skills Enhancement. Nursing Open. https://doi.org/10.1002/nop2.70497

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental between-group Mann–Whitney comparison of 48 intervention vs 50 control nurses at T1 using a 27-item questionnaire. The intervention group's advantage was significant, "p < 0.001, r = 0.42", with a median difference of 3.00.

> "immediately after the intervention (T1), the intervention group showed significantly higher PFA knowledge scores compared to the control group (Z = 4.205, p < 0.001, r = 0.42, median difference = 3.00, 95% CI: 2.00–4.00)"

### He Yazhou 2026 (2)

He Yazhou, Ding Yulan, Yan Jiaqin, Xiong Xiaoning, Huang Shan, Li Sijian. (2026). Evaluation of the Effectiveness of Psychological First Aid Training for Community Nurses in China: A Quasi‐Experimental Study on Knowledge and Skills Enhancement. Nursing Open. https://doi.org/10.1002/nop2.70497

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Between-group comparison at the 2-month follow-up in the same quasi-experiment. The knowledge advantage persisted at "p = 0.033, r = 0.22", a smaller effect than at T1, with median difference 1.00.

> "Two months after the intervention (T2), the intervention group continued to demonstrate significantly higher PFA knowledge scores than the control group (Z = 2.129, p = 0.033, r = 0.22, median difference = 1.00, 95% CI: 1.00–2.00)"
