# Perceived power distance correlates with expert and authority roles, collaboration with facilitator and delegator roles, and competition correlates with all five teacher roles

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teachers perceiving larger power distance more likely take up expert and authority roles; higher collaboration perception associates with facilitator and delegator roles; competition perception is positively associated with all five roles. [→ Chang Zhu 2010](#chang-zhu-2010)

## Evidence

### Chang Zhu 2010

Chang Zhu, Martin Valcke and Tammy Schellens. (2010). A cross-cultural study of teacher perspectives on teacher roles and adoption of online collaborative learning in higher education. European Journal of Teacher Education. https://doi.org/10.1080/02619761003631849

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis (Table 7, Pearson correlations) across the combined Chinese and Flemish samples linking Cultural Environment Survey subscales to the five Teaching Style Survey role scales; printed coefficients include r = .312 (power distance-expert) and r = .582 (collaboration-delegator).

> "The correlation analysis results show that teachers who perceive power-distance between teachers and students to a larger extent are more likely to take up the roles as an expert and an authority. Teachers who score higher on student collaboration perceive to a larger extent the roles as a facilitator and a delegator. The perception of competition is positively associated with the perceptions of the five roles."
