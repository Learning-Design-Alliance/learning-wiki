# Lower linguistic quality of physics explanations is associated with higher odds that AI-based scoring underestimates conceptual understanding, across all eleven examined AI-based scoring approaches

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` A one-unit decrease in expert-rated linguistic quality was associated with approximately 1.3- to 3.9-times higher odds of underestimation of conceptual understanding by AI-based scoring, with the regression coefficient negative for every approach and statistically significant (p < 0.05) in five of eleven. ## Evidence

### Markus S. Feser & Paul L. Tschisgale 2026

Markus S. Feser & Paul L. Tschisgale. (2026). AI-based scoring systematically underestimates conceptual understanding of linguistically weak students' explanations in physics. Physics Education, Leibniz Institute for Science and Mathematics Education, Kiel. https://orcid.org/0000-0001-8503-0951

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Multinomial logistic regression analysis of scoring deviations (AI score minus expert score) for 116 Grade 9 physics explanations, with expert-assigned conceptual understanding as covariate. The article reports odds ratios of "1.30 ≤ 1/OR ≤ 3.85" (a medium-to-large range) and that the coefficient "was negative for every scoring approach" with p < 0.05 in five of eleven approaches.

> "Overall, a one -unit decrease in linguistic quality was associated with approximately 1.3 - to 3.9 -times higher odds of underestimating conceptual understanding (1.30 ≤ 1/OR ≤ 3.85) across all scoring approaches."
