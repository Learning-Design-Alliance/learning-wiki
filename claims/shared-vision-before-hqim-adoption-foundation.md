# District leaders report that building a shared instructional vision before curriculum adoption lays the foundation for successful IM implementation

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Hannan 2026

Hannan, M., Rainier, S., & Perry, R. (2026). Beyond curriculum adoption: What district leaders are saying about using Illustrative Mathematics. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED681318

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Cross-case thematic analysis of interviews with district administrators from seven Massachusetts districts implementing IM. Five of seven endorsed curriculum at district level with teacher voice in selection; one leader's account showed inclusive process did not prevent teacher resistance.

> "Most respondents (five out of seven) endorsed the curriculum at the district level and talked about teacher involvement, voice, and review in the selection process, emphasizing the importance of including teachers’ perspectives."

### Hannan 2026 (2)

Hannan, M., Rainier, S., & Perry, R. (2026). Beyond curriculum adoption: What district leaders are saying about using Illustrative Mathematics. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED681318

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Contrasting spotlight cases within the cross-case study: Oak's methodical K–6 adoption with shared vision-building was followed by consistent, deep grade-6 use, whereas the upper-grades adoption "wasn't as methodical" and teachers gravitated toward supplemental resources.

> "Without the foundation of a long-term plan that accounted for where teachers were starting from and what support they would need over time, implementation of IM did not take hold in the same way."
