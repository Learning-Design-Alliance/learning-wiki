## Evidence

### Alhroub Wasef 2026

Alhroub Wasef, Jabareen Maaweya, Fasfoos Ahmad, Bseiso Omair, Deeik Joana, Ahmoud Ahmad Hasan, Al-Manasrah Amro K., Thalji Ibraheem Mohammed, Arish Ahmad Basil Abu, Adawi Iyad Moayad, Alqawasmeh Mustafa Murad, Hroub Monther M., Hroub Musbah, Ahmad Jamal. (2026). Lifestyle behaviors during exam periods and their association with academic performance among Palestinian medical students: a cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09591-z

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Spearman rank correlation analysis in a cross-sectional study of 403 Palestinian medical students found a minor negative correlation between traditional coffee consumption during exams and self-reported average grades; the article reports "a minor negative correlation with traditional coffee consumption (ρ = -0.137, p = 0.006)". Tea was borderline significant.

> "Results of correlation analysis showed that during the exam period, average grades had a minor negative correlation with traditional coffee consumption (ρ = -0.137, p = 0.006). Tea was borderline significant (ρ = -0.097, p = 0.051)"

### Alhroub Wasef 2026 (2)

Alhroub Wasef, Jabareen Maaweya, Fasfoos Ahmad, Bseiso Omair, Deeik Joana, Ahmoud Ahmad Hasan, Al-Manasrah Amro K., Thalji Ibraheem Mohammed, Arish Ahmad Basil Abu, Adawi Iyad Moayad, Alqawasmeh Mustafa Murad, Hroub Monther M., Hroub Musbah, Ahmad Jamal. (2026). Lifestyle behaviors during exam periods and their association with academic performance among Palestinian medical students: a cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09591-z

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple linear regression with robust estimators on the same 403-student sample, adjusted for sex, traditional smoking and waterpipe use, found "each additional cup of traditional coffee and tea per day was associated with slightly lower average grades"; the printed B values are unstandardized, so no standardized effect size is available.

> "each additional cup of traditional coffee and tea per day was associated with slightly lower average grades (traditional coffee: B = -0.46 in Model 1 and − 0.40 in Model 2; tea: B = -0.54 in Model 1 and − 0.52 in Model 2; all 95% CIs excluded 0)"
