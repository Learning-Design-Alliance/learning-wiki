## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students from CALD backgrounds report increased pre-simulation anxiety, mental fatigue from extra language preparation, and post-simulation self-doubt, alongside recognised growth in confidence and self-efficacy. [→ Zhang 2025](#zhang-2025)
`q2 i?` Stress responses vary with simulation scenario demands and assigned roles, with students more at ease delivering prepared recommendations than in spontaneous clinical conversations. [→ Zhang 2025](#zhang-2025)

## Evidence

### Zhang 2025

Zhang, L., Forbes, R., Patterson, F., & Penman, A. (2025). Becoming through simulation‐based learning: Lived experiences of occupational therapy students from culturally and linguistically diverse backgrounds. Australian Occupational Therapy Journal, 72(4), e70044. https://doi.org/10.1111/1440-1630.70044

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Semi-structured interviews (45–60 minutes) with 15 CALD-background occupational therapy students across nine Australian universities, analysed with reflexive thematic analysis. Participants reported "increased pre‐simulation anxiety and pressure", extra preparation time due to language barriers, and self-doubt, but also increased self-efficacy after successful simulations.

> "The participants described a range of emotional and psychological changes throughout SBL, including increased pre‐simulation anxiety and pressure, particularly during their first simulation experience"
