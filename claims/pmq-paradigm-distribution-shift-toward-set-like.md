## Evidence

### Benjamin Pollard 2017

Benjamin Pollard, Robert Hobbs, Jacob T. Stanley, Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer, and H. J. Lewandowski. (2017). Impact of an introductory lab on students’ understanding of measurement uncertainty. arXiv:1707.01979v1 [physics.ed-ph]. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1707.01979](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1707.01979)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre/post comparison of 525 students in a large-enrollment stand-alone introductory lab course at CU, where the PMQ was administered electronically at the beginning and end of the course. The authors report "a signiﬁcant change in paradigm distribution after instruction ( p ≪ 0.01)" with an increase in set-like students and decreases in point-like and mixed students.

> "We see a signiﬁcant change in paradigm distribution after instruction ( p ≪ 0.01), with an increase in the percentage of students coded as set-like and a decrease in the percentage of those coded as point-like and mixed."
