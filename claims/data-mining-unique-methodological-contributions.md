# Data mining is viewed as offering a distinct methodological contribution: fewer statistical assumptions, less reliance on theory, and efficient analysis of non-traditional data forms

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r2`–`r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Unlike traditional statistics designed for small samples, data mining is designed to efficiently analyze very large datasets and requires fewer statistical assumptions. [→ Iwatani 2018](#iwatani-2018)
`q2 i?` Data mining can analyze non-traditional data on text, location, audio, images, interactions, and social relations, expanding the analytic scope of qualitative sub-fields. [→ Iwatani 2018 (2)](#iwatani-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Iwatani 2018

Iwatani, Emi. (2018). Overview of Data Mining's Potential Benefits and Limitations in Education Research. Practical Assessment, Research & Evaluation, 23(15). http://pareonline.net/getvn.asp?v=23&n=15

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports this contrast citing Grover and Mehra (2008), and notes data mining "requires fewer statistical assumptions," e.g., decision trees without parametric assumptions of linearity, normality, and homogeneity of variance.

> "In contrast to traditional statistical approaches, which were designed to analyze small samples, data mining is designed to efficiently analyze very large datasets (Grover & Mehra, 2008)."

### Iwatani 2018 (2)

Iwatani, Emi. (2018). Overview of Data Mining's Potential Benefits and Limitations in Education Research. Practical Assessment, Research & Evaluation, 23(15). http://pareonline.net/getvn.asp?v=23&n=15

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes this benefit to Grover and Mehra (2008) and Papamitsiou and Economides (2014), citing Lang and Baehr (2012) using text-mining to analyze larger quantities of writing composition data with more confidence.

> "Data mining can be applied to data on text, location, audio, images, interactions, and social relations (Grover & Mehra, 2008; Papamitsiou & Economides, 2014)."
