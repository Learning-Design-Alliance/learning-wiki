# Quasi-experimental Korean simulation-based IPE studies show mixed effectiveness: positive effects in Jeon (2022), environment-dependent effects in Jung et al. (2020), and null changes in Park and Park (2021)

## Evidence

### Lee Sunmin 2024

Lee Sunmin, Kim Myung Chun, Kim Jongyoon. (2024). The status of interprofessional education for healthcare students in South Korea: a scoping review focusing on simulation-based education. Korean Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.3946/kjme.2024.304

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports on a non-equivalent control group pre-test/post-test study (intervention n=20, control n=19) with no statistical differences in baseline characteristics; "positive effects in the intervention group compared to the control group" on five indicators.

> "the study by Jeon [17] in 2022 found positive effects in the intervention group compared to the control group before and after the program implementation for a total of five indicators measured, including readiness, knowledge in specific fields, self-efficacy, team awareness, and problem-solving ability."

### Lee Sunmin 2024 (2)

Lee Sunmin, Kim Myung Chun, Kim Jongyoon. (2024). The status of interprofessional education for healthcare students in South Korea: a scoping review focusing on simulation-based education. Korean Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.3946/kjme.2024.304

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports this randomized study (random drawing) included medical, nursing, and pharmacy students; homogeneity lacked between groups and analysis of variance was used. The review reports the effect "differ depending on the research environment".

> "In the study by Jung et al. [26] in 2020, the effect was clearly shown to differ depending on the research environment, such as self-efficacy for interprofessional experience (p=0.000)."

### Lee Sunmin 2024 (3)

Lee Sunmin, Kim Myung Chun, Kim Jongyoon. (2024). The status of interprofessional education for healthcare students in South Korea: a scoping review focusing on simulation-based education. Korean Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.3946/kjme.2024.304

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports this semi-randomized split study found age differed between groups (p-value <0.001) and yielded "no significant change in potential interest"; equivalence was not tested.

> "Park and Park [29] in 2021 reported no significant change in potential interest both before and after the IPE implementation, and no statistically significant difference in the satisfaction survey of medical studies."
