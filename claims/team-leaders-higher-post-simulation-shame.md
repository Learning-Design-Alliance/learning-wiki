## Evidence

### Grewal 2026

Grewal, K., Azher, S., Moreno, M., Pekrun, R., Wiseman, J., Flake, J., Lajoie, S., Sun, N.‐Z., Fried, G. M., Khalil, E., & Harley, J. M. (2026). Medical trainees' emotions and their effects on perceptions of performance and team mood in team‐based simulations. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 96, 306–336. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.70017

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Multi-level model analysis of 173 datapoints from 85 residents in team-based simulations, with simulation role as predictor and presimulation emotion, PGY and specialty as covariates, found "team leaders reported higher levels of shame after a simulation as compared to team members" (b = .38, p = .001, η² = .04).

> "The post‐simulation shame model indicated significant differences between team leaders and team members. Specifically, team leaders reported higher levels of shame after a simulation as compared to team members (b = .38, p = .001, η 2 = .04, power = .99)."
