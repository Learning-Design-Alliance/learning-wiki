## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The article argues that expanding robot-trainer availability to all medical students is constrained by cost, since dedicated trainers are priced as full robotic systems and cheaper alternatives cannot simulate the bedside assist role. [→ Greenberg AL 2022](#greenberg-al-2022)

## Evidence

### Greenberg AL 2022

Greenberg AL, Syed SM, Alseidi A, O'Sullivan PS, Chern H. (2022). Robotic training for medical students: feasibility of a pilot simulation curriculum. Journal of Robotic Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11701-022-01508-4

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Authors' interpretive discussion of implementation costs in their discussion section, based on their pilot's reliance on a loaner robot and semi-annual access windows. They conclude that incorporating robotic training into medical student bootcamps "may be more feasible than holding such a training for all medical students." No empirical comparison was conducted.

> "However, a trainer dedicated to robotic simulation carries the price tag of a complete robotic system and, although in place at select surgical simulation centers [17], is cost prohibitive in many settings [24, 25]."
