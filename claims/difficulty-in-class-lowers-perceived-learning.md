# The author proposes that students in flipped classes perceive less learning because they experience difficulty during class-time problem solving, whereas lecture students perceive understanding while following lectures

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Students judge their learning by how much they feel they understand in class; flipped students recall in-class difficulty on problem work, so they report lower learning despite achieving more. [→ Jenna Van Sickle 2016](#jenna-van-sickle-2016)

## Evidence

### Jenna Van Sickle 2016

Jenna Van Sickle. (2016). Discrepancies between Student Perception and Achievement of Learning Outcomes in a Flipped Classroom. Journal of the Scholarship of Teaching and Learning, Vol. 16, No. 2, April 2016, pp. 29-38. https://doi.org/10.14434/josotl.v16i2.19216

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The author's interpretive explanation (not a tested result) for the perception-achievement discrepancy: lecture students "feel they understood because they understood the lectures," while flipped students experience problem-solving difficulty in class and recall it at evaluation time. The article offers this as reasoning drawn from prior research, not as a measured finding.

> "Looking back at the class, they remember the difficulty they had during class, and they do not feel they understood because they did not understand all of the problems."
