# More experienced teachers earned additional certificates in shortage areas (47 percent) than in nonshortage areas (25 percent), with special education the most common shortage area (27 percent)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` 47 percent of additional certificate holders earned certificates in shortage certification areas versus 25 percent in nonshortage areas; special education (27 percent) exceeded every other shortage area (fewer than 6 percent each). [→ Camille Lemieux 2021](#camille-lemieux-2021)
`q3 i?` Administration, a nonteaching certification area, was the single certification area with the highest percentage of teachers earning additional certificates (36 percent). [→ Camille Lemieux 2021 (2)](#camille-lemieux-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Camille Lemieux 2021

Camille Lemieux, Jacqueline Zweig, Karen Shakman, Rebecca Schillaci, and Laura O'Dwyer. (2021). Additional Certification for Teachers in New York State: Teachers' Experience and Employment Location, Certification Pathways, and Certification Areas. Regional Educational Laboratory Northeast & Islands, REL 2022–110. https://go.usa.gov/xecdz

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive analysis of additional certification areas (figure 1 and appendix table B2) among the 9,865 additional certificate holders; percentages sum above 100 because some teachers earned multiple certificates.

> "A higher percentage of experienced teachers earned additional certificates in shortage areas (47 percent) than in nonshortage areas (25 percent; figure 1), a substantive difference."

### Camille Lemieux 2021 (2)

Camille Lemieux, Jacqueline Zweig, Karen Shakman, Rebecca Schillaci, and Laura O'Dwyer. (2021). Additional Certification for Teachers in New York State: Teachers' Experience and Employment Location, Certification Pathways, and Certification Areas. Regional Educational Laboratory Northeast & Islands, REL 2022–110. https://go.usa.gov/xecdz

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive analysis (appendix table B2) shows administration at 36 percent, followed by special education at 27 percent and English to speakers of other languages at 10 percent.

> "However, the nonshortage area of administration, which is a nonteaching certification area, was the certification area with the highest percentage of teachers earning additional certificates (36 percent; see table B2 in appendix B)."
