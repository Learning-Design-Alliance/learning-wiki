# Poor readers show moderately elevated anxiety compared to typical readers (d = 0.41)

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Poor readers experience greater problems with anxiety than typical readers, with a moderate pooled effect size of d = 0.41 and moderate heterogeneity. [→ Francis 2019](#francis-2019)

## Evidence

### Francis 2019

Francis, D. A.; Caruana, N.; Hudson, J. L.; McArthur, G. M. (2019). The association between poor reading and internalising problems: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Clinical Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2018.09.002

`q3 · i2`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 22 studies comparing poor reading and control groups on anxiety measures (n poor reading groups = 22, sample size = 1732). The pooled effect was "moderate, positive, and statistically significant (d= 0.41)" with moderate heterogeneity (I2 = 68.39%).

> "The overall effect was moderate, positive, and statistically significant (d= 0.41), 95% CI [0.26, 0.55], p ≤ 0.001, and there was also evidence for moderate heterogeneity, I 2 = 68.39%, Q (21) = 66.43, p < 0.001."
