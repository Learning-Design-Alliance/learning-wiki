## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Contrary to Hypotheses 1 and 2, the googling-only and thinking-before-googling groups did not differ significantly in cognitive self-esteem, performance estimation, or study time estimation after searching. [→ Ju Ae Kim 2026](#ju-ae-kim-2026)

## Evidence

### Ju Ae Kim 2026

Ju Ae Kim, Jiwon Kim, Sung-il Kim, Mimi Bong. (2026). How mindful and mindless online searching affects curiosity and information recall. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10570-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Univariate ANCOVAs following a significant MANCOVA (F(4,95) = 2.78, p = .031, ηp² = .10) in the randomized experiment found no significant condition effects on the three metacognitive judgments; equivalence was not tested. The googling-only group's higher cognitive self-esteem (M = 4.71 vs 4.49) was non-significant.

> "No significant differences emerged in the estimations of performance and study time or cognitive self-esteem between the two groups. Whereas Hypotheses 1 and 2 did not receive support, the results supported Hypothesis 4."
