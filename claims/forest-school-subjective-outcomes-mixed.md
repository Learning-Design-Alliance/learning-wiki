### Enrico Sella · Monica Bolognesi · Emma Bergamini · Lucia Mason · Francesca Pazzaglia 2023

Enrico Sella · Monica Bolognesi · Emma Bergamini · Lucia Mason · Francesca Pazzaglia. (2023). Psychological Benefits of Attending Forest School for Preschool Children: a Systematic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09750-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Review's account of Choi et al. (2014), a quasi-experimental pre-post study with parent-reported Children's Sleep Habits Questionnaire data from 18 forest-kindergarten and 19 regular-kindergarten children.

> "After 8 months, children in the forest kindergarten showed significant improvements in overall subjective sleep quality (i.e., fewer sleep disturbances); in particular, fewer children experienced sleep disordered breathing (p = 0.04) and daytime sleepiness levels (p < 0.001) compared to children attending the regular kindergarten."

### Enrico Sella · Monica Bolognesi · Emma Bergamini · Lucia Mason · Francesca Pazzaglia 2023 (2)

Enrico Sella · Monica Bolognesi · Emma Bergamini · Lucia Mason · Francesca Pazzaglia. (2023). Psychological Benefits of Attending Forest School for Preschool Children: a Systematic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09750-4

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Review's account of Frenkel et al. (2019), an 11–15-week illness/injury log in 71 forest-preschool and 70 indoor-preschool children; no overall difference, though girls in nature preschool had higher minor-injury incidence. Cordiano et al. (2019) also found no readiness differences (p > 0.05).

> "There were no significant differences between nature and indoor preschools (IRR: 0.93, 95% CI  = [0.64, 1.34]). The only difference was that girls at nature pre -school had a significantly higher incidence of minor injury compared to those in indoor preschool (IRR: 5.91, 95% CI = [1.98, 17.7], p < 0.01)."
