# Using a blocks-based visual language instead of a text-based language has no significant overall effect on GUI-programming practical exam success

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2017 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a 6-week GUI-programming course, students taught with the blocks-based language and students taught with the text-based language did not differ significantly in practical exam scores. [→ Mihci 2017](#mihci-2017)

## Evidence

### Mihci 2017

Mihci, C. & Ozdener Donmez, N. (2017). Teaching GUI-programming concepts to prospective K12 teachers: MIT App Inventor as an alternative to text based languages. International Journal of Research in Education and Science (IJRES), 3(2), 543-559. https://doi.org/10.21890/ijres.327912

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Posttest control group quasi-experiment: Experimental Group 1 (App Inventor, n=54, M=37.96, SD=18.23) versus Control Group 1 (.NET C#, n=47, M=41.06, SD=25.38) after 6 weeks; independent samples t-test found "no significant effect upon success in a practical exam".

> "the programming language that is used has no significant effect upon success in a practical exam, which measures GUI programming skills (t(99)= .71, p>.05)."
