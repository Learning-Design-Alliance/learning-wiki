## Evidence

### Bukumiric Zoran 2022

Bukumiric Zoran, Ilic Aleksandra, Pajcin Mirjana, Srebro Dragana, Milicevic Sasa, Spaic Dragan, Markovic Nenad, Corac Aleksandar. (2022). Effects of problem-based learning modules within blended learning courses in medical statistics – A randomized controlled pilot study. PLoS ONE 17(1): e0263015. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0263015

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary regression analysis in the same trial; the authors interpret device modality as a significant predictor of problem solving score, attributed to EZR software use via PC, with no printed effect size.

> "The results of our study suggest that the modality used to access the course is a significant predictor of the problem solving score (if it is via PC/laptop or Tablet/Smartphone). Students who accessed the course via PC/laptop had better scores, which was expected because the problems are solved in the EZR software via PC"

### Bukumiric Zoran 2022 (2)

Bukumiric Zoran, Ilic Aleksandra, Pajcin Mirjana, Srebro Dragana, Milicevic Sasa, Spaic Dragan, Markovic Nenad, Corac Aleksandar. (2022). Effects of problem-based learning modules within blended learning courses in medical statistics – A randomized controlled pilot study. PLoS ONE 17(1): e0263015. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0263015

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same regression analyses, access modality did not significantly predict the total medical statistics score; the article reports this null finding without an effect size, so equivalence was not tested.

> "However, the method of access is not a statistically significant predictor of total medical statistics score."
