## Evidence

### Mollick 2023

Mollick, E., & Mollick, L. (2023). Assigning AI: Seven Approaches for Students with Prompts. Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania & Wharton Interactive. [doi:10.2139/ssrn.4475995](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4475995)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Definitional risk statement in the LLMs: Prompts and Risks section. The paper adds that error rates differ across models and that "GPT-4 and Bing have the lowest error rates" as of its writing.

> "Large Language Models are prone to producing incorrect, but plausible facts, a phenomenon known as confabulation or hallucination. These errors can be deeply woven into the outputs of the AI, and can be hard to detect."

### Mollick 2023 (2)

Mollick, E., & Mollick, L. (2023). Assigning AI: Seven Approaches for Students with Prompts. Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania & Wharton Interactive. [doi:10.2139/ssrn.4475995](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4475995)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The paper's own comparative ranking of confabulation concern across its seven use cases, with its recommendation that students bear responsibility for factual accuracy. This is an authorial judgment, not a measured comparison.

> "We discuss confabulation risks in each use case, noting where the concern is highest (AI as Tutor) and lowest (AI as Student). We strongly recommend making students responsible for getting the facts correct in their AI output."
