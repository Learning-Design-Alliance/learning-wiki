# AI multimodal feedback is rated higher than educator feedback on specificity, clarity, simplicity, motivation, satisfaction, and lower mental effort, with comparable correctness, trust, and acceptance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants rated AI feedback significantly higher on specificity, clarity, and simplicity (all p < 0.05). [→ Chloe Qianhui Zhao 2026](#chloe-qianhui-zhao-2026)
`q3 i?` Participants reported significantly lower mental effort and higher satisfaction and motivation with AI feedback. [→ Chloe Qianhui Zhao 2026 (2)](#chloe-qianhui-zhao-2026-2)
`q3 i?` No significant differences were observed for correctness (p = 0.5378), trust (p = 0.1456), or acceptance (p = 0.2393). [→ Chloe Qianhui Zhao 2026 (3)](#chloe-qianhui-zhao-2026-3)

## Evidence

### Chloe Qianhui Zhao 2026

Chloe Qianhui Zhao, Jie Cao, Jionghao Lin, and Kenneth R. Koedinger. (2026). LLM-based Multimodal Feedback Produces Equivalent Learning and Better Student Perceptions than Educator Feedback. LAK 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15280

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mann–Whitney U tests of post-survey Likert ratings (81 baseline vs. 99 AI valid responses) in RQ3. Table 2 reports specificity U = 2638.00, p = 0.0001; clarity U = 3346.50, p = 0.0199; simplicity U = 3246.00, p = 0.0207; no effect sizes printed.

> "AI feedback received significantly higher ratings for specificity, clarity, and simplicity (all𝑝< 0.05), indicat- ing that participants found the AI-facilitated multimodal feedback to be clearer, more understandable, more concise, and more fo- cused than original educator feedback."

### Chloe Qianhui Zhao 2026 (2)

Chloe Qianhui Zhao, Jie Cao, Jionghao Lin, and Kenneth R. Koedinger. (2026). LLM-based Multimodal Feedback Produces Equivalent Learning and Better Student Perceptions than Educator Feedback. LAK 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15280

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-survey Mann–Whitney tests in RQ3. Mental effort (rated 1–10) was lower for AI feedback and satisfaction (5-point Likert) higher; motivation was also higher (U = 3174.50, p = 0.0146). No effect sizes printed.

> "Participants reported significantly lower mental effort when interacting with the AI Multimodal Feedback (𝑈= 5190.50,𝑝= 0.0006***), while their satisfaction scores were also significantly higher for AI feedback compared to original edu- cator feedback (𝑈=3310.50,𝑝=0.0240*)."

### Chloe Qianhui Zhao 2026 (3)

Chloe Qianhui Zhao, Jie Cao, Jionghao Lin, and Kenneth R. Koedinger. (2026). LLM-based Multimodal Feedback Produces Equivalent Learning and Better Student Perceptions than Educator Feedback. LAK 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15280

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-survey comparisons in RQ3; trust (p = 0.1456) and acceptance (p = 0.2393) also showed no significant differences, which the authors note contrasts with prior studies reporting much lower trust in AI feedback.

> "No significant difference was observed for correctness (𝑝= 0.5378), suggesting that learners perceived the factual accuracy of AI and original educator feedback to be similar."
