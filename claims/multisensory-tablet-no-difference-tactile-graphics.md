# A multisensory tablet with tonal and vibration feedback produced no significant difference in graphics-comprehension accuracy compared with tactile graphics

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among 22 BVI students asked questions about graphics, there was no significant difference in the number of correct answers between those using a tablet with tonal and vibration feedback and those using tactile representations. [→ Steinbach 2022](#steinbach-2022)

## Evidence

### Steinbach 2022

Steinbach, S. (2022). Looking beyond vision: Supports for students who are blind or visually impaired in mathematics. NWEA. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/looking-beyond-vision-supports-for-students-who-are-blind-or-visually-impaired-in-mathematics/

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Hahn et al. (2019), a study of 22 BVIs comparing a Samsung multimodal tablet with tonal and vibration feedback against tactile representations of the same graphics; "there was no significant difference in the number of correct answers in either group." No effect size printed; equivalence was not tested.

> "Hahn et al. (2019) used a Samsung multimodal tablet in combination with Google Classroom to ask 22 BVIs questions about graphics. Some students were provided with the tablet, which had tonal and vibration feedback, while others were given tactile representations of the same graphics. The authors found that there was no significant difference in the number of correct answers in either group."
