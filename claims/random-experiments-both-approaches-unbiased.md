# In random experiments, both the ANCOVA and the CHANGE approach estimate the instruction effect without bias

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Given successful randomization, instruction is independent of the outcome at T1 and of the change-score, so both approaches return unbiased instruction effects. [→ Köhler 2021](#kohler-2021)

## Evidence

### Köhler 2021

Köhler, C., Hartig, J., & Naumann, A. (2021). Detecting Instruction Effects—Deciding Between Covariance Analytical and Change-Score Approach. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09590-6

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical deduction within the article's hypothetical data-generating framework for the random-experiment scenario (Fig. 3). The article states that with successful randomization, neither stable nor time-specific components are associated with instruction, so "the instruction effect can be estimated without bias under both approaches".

> "In a random experiment, instruction is independent of the outcome at T1 and it is also independent of the change-score, and the instruction effect can be estimated without bias under both approaches."
