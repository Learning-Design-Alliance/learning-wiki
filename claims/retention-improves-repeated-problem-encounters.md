## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across nearly 10,000 repeated-encounter instances, median time to solution drops by a factor of two for the second and third occurrences and stays just under 20 seconds thereafter, while correct responses rise from 52% to above 90%. [→ Evrard 2013](#evrard-2013)

## Evidence

### Evrard 2013

Evrard, A. E., Mills, M., Winn, D., Jones, K., Tritz, J., and McKay, T. A. (2013). Problem Roulette: Studying Introductory Physics in the Cloud. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1309.7678](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1309.7678)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Log analysis from the PR Responses table (Fig. 9): nearly 10,000 instances of a student repeating a problem, a few hundred of six encounters. Descriptive percentages only; the article notes the largest gains are concentrated in the first few occurrences.

> "The center panel shows a marked drop in the median time to solution, by a factor of two for the second and third occurrences, constant thereafter at just under 20 seconds. The correct response fraction grows in turn, climbing from 52% on the ﬁrst view to above 90% for the fourth and higher occurrences."
