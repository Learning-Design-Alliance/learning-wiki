### Nizamani 2026

Nizamani, S. B., Lee, Y., Donekal Chandrashekar, N., Ellis, M., & Ramakrishnan, N. (2026). Understanding Student Use of Large Language Models Across Computer Science Subfields. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01158 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2610.01158](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2610.01158)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive analysis of non-verification among LLM users across the seven assignments. The article reports users "(93%–100%) reported using at least one verification method" and non-verification rates varied significantly (χ 2 = 13.95, df = 6, p = 0.030) but remained low (0% to 8%).

> "Across all assignments, the LLM users (93%–100%) reported using at least one verification method, indicating that students did verify LLM outputs in most cases. Non-verification rates varied significantly across assignments (χ 2 = 13.95, df = 6, p = 0.030), though all rates remained low."

### Nizamani 2026 (2)

Nizamani, S. B., Lee, Y., Donekal Chandrashekar, N., Ellis, M., & Ramakrishnan, N. (2026). Understanding Student Use of Large Language Models Across Computer Science Subfields. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01158

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive analysis of the number of verification strategies used by LLM users (Fig. 3). The article reports "49.7% used exactly two methods, 31.6% used a single method, and 15.6% used three methods"; the most common combination was manual reasoning plus tests (27.5%).

> "Most students used multiple strategies: 49.7% used exactly two methods, 31.6% used a single method, and 15.6% used three methods (a further 3.1% reported no verification method)."
