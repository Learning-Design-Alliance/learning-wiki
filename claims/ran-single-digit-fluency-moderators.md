## Evidence

### Koponen 2017

Koponen, T., Georgiou, G., Salmi, P., Leskinen, M., & Aro, M. (2017). A Meta-Analysis of the Relation Between RAN and Mathematics. Journal of Educational Psychology, 109(7), 977-992. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000182

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-regression of dependent effects clustered into 33 unique samples; the β = .14 is a moderator regression coefficient, no effect size printed. The article explains multi-digit calculation additionally requires "knowledge of place value and mastery of arithmetic procedures."

> "the type of calculation performed (single-digit vs. multi-digit calculations) was a significant moderator (β = .14, p < .01). The correlations were higher for the single-digit calculations."

### Koponen 2017 (2)

Koponen, T., Georgiou, G., Salmi, P., Leskinen, M., & Aro, M. (2017). A Meta-Analysis of the Relation Between RAN and Mathematics. Journal of Educational Psychology, 109(7), 977-992. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000182

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Same meta-regression analysis; β = –.13 is a moderator coefficient, no effect size printed. The article links this to RAN being a speeded measure, mirroring the fluency effect reported for the RAN–reading relationship in prior meta-analyses.

> "The math outcome measure (fluency vs. accuracy) was also a significant moderator associated with the between-study variation (β = –.13, p < .05). Fluency math outcomes produced higher correlations than math accuracy outcomes."
