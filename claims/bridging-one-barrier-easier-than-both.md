# Bridging either the physics/biology or the microscopic/macroscopic barrier appears easier for a student than bridging both at once

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The interview data contain no example of a student connecting microscopic biological phenomena to macroscopic non-biological phenomena, suggesting crossing both barriers simultaneously is harder. [→ Benjamin W. Dreyfus 2011](#benjamin-w-dreyfus-2011)

## Evidence

### Benjamin W. Dreyfus 2011

Benjamin W. Dreyfus, Edward F. Redish, and Jessica Watkins. (2011). Students' Views of Macroscopic and Microscopic Energy in Physics and Biology. PERC 2011. [doi:10.1063/1.3680024](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3680024)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Across the two interviews, the student bridged physics/biology via analogy and microscopic/macroscopic in non-biological contexts, but never connected microscopic biological to macroscopic non-biological phenomena. The authors caution this absence "does not prove anything." No effect size is reported.

> "Absent from the data, however, is an example of connecting microscopic biological phenomena to macroscopic non-biological phenomena."
