## Evidence

### Liang 2023

Liang, M., Lim, C. P., Park, J., & Mendoza, N. B. (2023). A review of ICT-enabled learning for schoolgirls in Asia and its impacts on education equity. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-022-10178-w

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Theme synthesized from the reviewed studies; examples include improved perceived value of ICT after 3D printing (Ali et al., 2019) and increased girls' self-efficacy with mobile devices (Aktas & Çan, 2019).

> "Improved participation and performance may increase girls' interest in and motivation to use ICT to support their own learning. Such increase in interest and motivation we found in most studies incorporated four different ICT-enabled learning approaches in this review may stem from the changes in their perceptions of ICT and in girls' capacity."

### Liang 2023 (2)

Liang, M., Lim, C. P., Park, J., & Mendoza, N. B. (2023). A review of ICT-enabled learning for schoolgirls in Asia and its impacts on education equity. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-022-10178-w

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review synthesizes that girls may have different gaming experiences and motivations, spatial abilities, and reading strategies, and that differences associated with ICT self-efficacy may be reduced by granting more ICT-enabled learning opportunities (Tam et al., 2020).

> "The studies reveal to the educators and researchers that girls' learning gain could be equal to boys', while their learning style or experience may be different from boys'."
