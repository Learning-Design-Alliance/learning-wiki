# Congruence between perceived parent and teacher mastery goals is linearly associated with higher mathematics performance, and discrepancy in mastery goals is associated with lower mathematics performance

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among Chinese Grade 3–8 students, higher congruent levels of perceived parent and teacher mastery goals predict higher mathematics scores, and greater discrepancy predicts lower scores. [→ Zhao 2025](#zhao-2025)

## Evidence

### Zhao 2025

Zhao, N., Dou, D., Chen, X., Chen, F., Luo, R., Zhu, X., & Xiang, G.-X. (2025). Exploring the congruence between perceived parent–teacher achievement goals and student academic outcomes: A study using polynomial regression with response surface analysis. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 95, 603–626. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12742

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Polynomial regression with response surface analysis of survey data from 4944 students in Grades 3–8. A significant a1 with a nonsignificant a2 indicates "a linear prediction from the similarity of perceived parental mastery goals on students' math performance"; the significant negative a4 indicates discrepancy matters.

> "Response surface parameters were estimated through linear combinations of polynomial regression coefficients: a 1 = 0.16 (SE = 0.025, p < .001), a 2 = −0.04 (SE = 0.021, p = .089), a 3 = 0.073 (SE = 0.030, p = .013) and a 4 = −0.14 (SE = 0.030, p < .001)."
