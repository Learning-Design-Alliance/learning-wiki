# Parents' own incremental theories did not predict their process praise use or their children's motivational frameworks, and counter-intuitively correlated with more person praise

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Parents' incremental theory scores correlated with more frequent person praise, r(44) = .29, p < .05, but not with process praise (r = −.12) or children's frameworks (r = −.06). [→ Gunderson Elizabeth A. 2013](#gunderson-elizabeth-a-2013)

## Evidence

### Gunderson Elizabeth A. 2013

Gunderson Elizabeth A., Gripshover Sarah J., Romero Carissa, Dweck Carol S., Goldin-Meadow Susan, Levine Susan C. (2013). Parent Praise to 1-3 Year-Olds Predicts Children's Motivational Frameworks 5 Years Later. Child development. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.12064

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of the 46 dyads with parent implicit-theory questionnaire data (8-item scale, α = .92). The printed effect size was r = .29 ("r(44) = .29, p < .05"); parents' theories were unrelated to children's frameworks, r(44) = −.06, p = .70, and to process praise, r(44) = −.12, p = .41.

> "Counter-intuitively, parents' higher incremental theory scores were correlated with more frequent use of person praise, as a percent of total praise, r(44) = .29, p < .05"
