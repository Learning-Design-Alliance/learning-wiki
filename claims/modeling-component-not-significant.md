# Modeling as a training component shows no statistically significant effect on DTT implementation fidelity

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` The moderation effect of modeling was 1.35 percentage points and not statistically significant (p = .36): the meta-analysis found no evidence of a difference in fidelity between those who did and did not receive modeling, though it did not test equivalence. ## Evidence

### Joelle Fingerhut and Mariola Moeyaert 2022

Joelle Fingerhut and Mariola Moeyaert. (2022). Training Individuals to Implement Discrete Trials with Fidelity: A Meta-Analysis. Focus on Autism and Other Developmental Disabilities, 37(4), 239–250. https://doi.org/10.1177/10883576221081076

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

HLM moderator analysis (Model 2) in the 46-study meta-analysis tested modeling as a moderator of the intervention effect. The article reports "1.35%, γ̂200 = 1.35, t(3692) = 0.91, p =.36", a non-significant result; equivalence was not tested.

> "For Model 2 (modeling as the moderator), the moderation effect of modeling was 1.35%, γ̂200 = 1.35, t(3692) = 0.91, p =.36. Participants who received modeling had an average DTT implementation score of 90.59%."
