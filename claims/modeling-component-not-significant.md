# Modeling as a training component shows no statistically significant effect on DTT implementation fidelity

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` The moderation effect of modeling was 1.35% and not statistically significant (p = .36), indicating no difference between those who did and did not receive modeling. [→ Joelle Fingerhut and Mariola Moeyaert 2022](#joelle-fingerhut-and-mariola-moeyaert-2022)

`q2 i?` The moderation effect of modeling was 1.35 percentage points and not statistically significant (p = .36). [→ Joelle Fingerhut 2022](#joelle-fingerhut-and-mariola-moeyaert-2022)

## Evidence

### Joelle Fingerhut and Mariola Moeyaert 2022

Joelle Fingerhut and Mariola Moeyaert. (2022). Training Individuals to Implement Discrete Trials with Fidelity: A Meta-Analysis. Focus on Autism and Other Developmental Disabilities. https://doi.org/10.1177/10883576221081076

`q3 · i?`

HLM moderator analysis (Model 2) in the 46-study meta-analysis tested modeling as a moderator of the intervention effect. The article reports "1.35%, γ̂200 = 1.35, t(3692) = 0.91, p =.36", a non-significant result; equivalence was not tested.

> "For Model 2 (modeling as the moderator), the moderation effect of modeling was 1.35%, γ̂200 = 1.35, t(3692) = 0.91, p =.36. Participants who received modeling had an average DTT implementation score of 90.59%."
