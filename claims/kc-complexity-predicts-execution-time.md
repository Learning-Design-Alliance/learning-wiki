# Knowledge component complexity predicts execution time: variable-condition and variable-response KCs take longer to perform than constant-constant KCs

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2012 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Learning-curve data from computer tutors show that more complex KCs (variable conditions/responses) have slower correct execution times than simpler constant-constant KCs. [→ Koedinger 2012](#koedinger-2012)

## Evidence

### Koedinger 2012

Koedinger, K.R., Corbett, A.C., & Perfetti, C. (2012). The Knowledge-Learning-Instruction (KLI) Framework: Bridging the Science-Practice Chasm to Enhance Robust Student Learning. Cognitive Science, 36(5), 757-798. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1551-6709.2012.01245.x

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Learning-curve analysis of tutor log data (Fig. 2) from Chinese vocabulary, English articles, and geometry units. The article reports correct performance times of about 3 to 6 seconds for constant-constant KCs, 6 to 10 seconds for variable-constant, and 10 to 14 seconds for variable-variable, noting "the variable condition or action KCs with longer descriptions correspond with slower execution times."

> "These examples illustrate the possibility of supporting theoretically derived hypotheses about KC complexity (e.g., from KC category or description length) with empirical results (e.g., time to execute the KC) – the variable condition or action KCs with longer descriptions correspond with slower execution times."
