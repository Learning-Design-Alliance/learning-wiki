# Expert teachers watching AVT interactions could reliably predict when a learner understood material well, but not what the learner did not know—a one-sided signal of understanding

`q1 i?` In a small investigation, two expert teachers predicted exam mistakes from midterm AVT interactions and were correct in practically all cases, but could much more easily predict when a learner understood something well than make strong claims about what the learner didn't know. ## Evidence

### Ali Malik 2024

Ali Malik, Juliette Woodrow, and Chris Piech. 2024. Learners Teaching Novices: An Uplifting Alternative Assessment. In Proceedings of the 55th ACM Technical Symposium on Computer Science Education V. 1 (SIGCSE 2024). https://doi.org/10.1145/3626252.3630887

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A small investigation in which two expert teachers watched midterm AVT interactions and predicted what mistakes the learner would or would not make on the exam; a validation found the predictions correct in practically all cases. The article describes this as a one-sidedness relative to traditional exams.

> "the experts could easily predict when a learner understood something well and likely wouldn’t make a mistake, but it was much harder to make strong claims about what the learner didn’t know."
