# Tutees' Reading Self-Concept gains may be hindered because tutees either play down their improvement or attribute their achievement to external factors such as their tutor's help

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Tutee self-reports suggest some tutees attribute progress to their tutor's effort rather than their own, which may hinder the tutees' Reading Self-Concept. [→ Flores 2013](#flores-2013)

## Evidence

### Flores 2013

Flores, M., & Duran, D. (2013). Effects of Peer Tutoring on Reading Self-Concept. International Journal of Educational Psychology, 2(3), 297-324. https://doi.org/10.4471/ijep.2013.29

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Qualitative analysis of tutee free-writing and focus-group comments in the third segment (after reading), where around 20% of tutees read the text correctly whereas just under 80% still make some mistakes. Findings include one verbatim fragment: tutees "attribute their achievement to external factors".

> "the development of the tutees’ Reading Self-Concept may be hindered, since the tutees seem to either play down their improvement or attribute their achievement to external factors"
