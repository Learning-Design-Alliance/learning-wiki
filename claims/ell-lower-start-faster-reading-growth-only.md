# ELLs start middle school roughly .6 SD lower in math and .65 SD lower in reading, with faster reading growth (.13 SD) but no faster math or self-efficacy growth

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Being an ELL is associated with reading growth between 5th and 8th grade that is .13 standard deviations higher than among peers, while initial reading scores are .65 standard deviations lower; these are standardized SEM coefficients, so no effect-size impact is coded. [→ Soland 2019](#soland-2019)
`q2 i?` ELLs' initial self-efficacy is .23 standard deviations lower and ELL status is not significantly associated with the self-efficacy slope in either the math or reading model. [→ Soland 2019 (2)](#soland-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Soland 2019

Soland, J. (2019). English Language Learners, Self-efficacy, and the Achievement Gap: Understanding the Relationship between Academic and Social-emotional Growth (The Collaborative for Student Growth at NWEA Working Paper). https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/english-language-learners-self-efficacy-and-the-achievement-gap-understanding-the-relationship-between-academic-and-social-emotional-growth/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Reading model of the same latent curve SEM (Question 2, Figure 3; RMSEA = .045, CFI = .992). The article attributes the faster reading growth to ELLs mastering English and catching up over time, citing Cook et al. and Umansky & Reardon; the .65 and .13 values are standardized coefficients, so impact is null.

> "Turning to reading, ELLs have initial achievement scores that are .65 standard deviations below non-ELLs. However, being an ELL is associated with growth in reading between 5th and 8th grade that is .13 standard deviations higher than among their peers."

### Soland 2019 (2)

Soland, J. (2019). English Language Learners, Self-efficacy, and the Achievement Gap: Understanding the Relationship between Academic and Social-emotional Growth (The Collaborative for Student Growth at NWEA Working Paper). https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/english-language-learners-self-efficacy-and-the-achievement-gap-understanding-the-relationship-between-academic-and-social-emotional-growth/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Self-efficacy growth results from the same SEMs (Question 2, Figure 3). The article concludes ELLs start much lower on self-efficacy and those scores do not grow more over time relative to non-ELLs; the reading-model self-efficacy slope coefficient is negative but indistinguishable from zero. No effect size statistic is printed, so impact is null.

> "For self-efficacy, ELLs have initial scores that are .23 standard deviations lower, but ELL status is not significantly associated with the slope on self-efficacy."
