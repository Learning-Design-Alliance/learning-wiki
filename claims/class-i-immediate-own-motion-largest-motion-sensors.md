## Evidence

### Carolien Duijzer 2019

Carolien Duijzer, Marja Van den Heuvel-Panhuizen, Michiel Veldhuis, Michiel Doorman, & Paul Leseman. (2019). Embodied Learning Environments for Graphing Motion: a Systematic Literature Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09471-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Classification of the 62 learning environments on bodily involvement and immediacy shows "Class I — Immediate Own Motion was the largest (34 learning environments)"; the review further reports Class III was smallest with 4, and the other two classes contained 12 environments each.

> "Class I — Immediate Own Motion was the largest (34 learning environments)."

### Carolien Duijzer 2019 (2)

Carolien Duijzer, Marja Van den Heuvel-Panhuizen, Michiel Veldhuis, Michiel Doorman, & Paul Leseman. (2019). Embodied Learning Environments for Graphing Motion: a Systematic Literature Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09471-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Descriptive analysis of Class I environments found that in "30 out of the 34" motion sensor technology was used, allowing immediate representation of a student's motion as a graph, for example walking in front of a motion sensor with the graph shown on a graphing calculator screen.

> "In 30 out of the 34 learning environments that belonged to Class I — Immediate Own Motion, motion sensor technology was used"
