## Evidence

### Borovnik Lesjak Vesna 2019

Borovnik Lesjak Vesna, Šorgo Andrej, Strnad Matej. (2019). Development, validation and assessment of the test on knowledge about basic life support and use of automated external defibrillator among schoolchildren. Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13049-019-0683-6

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Educometric analysis of the Study 1 pilot test (N = 172). The question on what kind of breathing is NOT a sign of life was too easy (difficulty "0,99") with near-zero discrimination ("0,02") and was dropped from the revised instrument.

> "Only one question had to be eliminated due to inadequate difficulty and discriminating index (0,99 and 0,02, respectively)."

### Borovnik Lesjak Vesna 2019 (2)

Borovnik Lesjak Vesna, Šorgo Andrej, Strnad Matej. (2019). Development, validation and assessment of the test on knowledge about basic life support and use of automated external defibrillator among schoolchildren. Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13049-019-0683-6

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Confirmatory Study 2 (N = 611) item analysis of the revised test, with discriminating indices computed from top 20% (n = 134) and bottom 20% (n = 163) of scorers. "No item needed revision incumbently or had to be eliminated."

> "All questions had excellent difficulty indices except no. 9, which had an as good as acceptable index (Table 2). The ability to discriminate between top and bottom scoring students were satisfactory for 6 out of 10 questions (Table 2)."
