## Evidence

### Consiglio P 2026

Consiglio P, Fokkens-Bruinsma M, Opdenakker M-C, Jansen EPWA, Fleer J. (2026). Dual Pathways From Self-Compassion and Self-Criticism to Academic Achievement: The Roles of Engagement and Exhaustion. Perspectives on Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5334/pme.1821

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bright-path PP-LGCM covariate effect in 117 medical students: nationality predicted initial engagement, with international students higher than Dutch students (β = –0.48, p < .01); unstandardized coefficient, no effect size printed.

> "In addition, nationality emerged as a significant covariate in the bright path. International students reported higher initial levels of engagement compared to Dutch students (intercept: β = –0.48, p < .01), suggesting that international students begin their studies with greater academic engagement."

### Consiglio P 2026 (2)

Consiglio P, Fokkens-Bruinsma M, Opdenakker M-C, Jansen EPWA, Fleer J. (2026). Dual Pathways From Self-Compassion and Self-Criticism to Academic Achievement: The Roles of Engagement and Exhaustion. Perspectives on Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5334/pme.1821

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Dark-path PP-LGCM covariate effect: gender predicted the self-criticism slope, with males increasing more steeply than females (β = 0.85, p < .05); unstandardized coefficient, no effect size printed.

> "gender predicted the rate of change in self-criticism: males showed a steeper increase in self-criticism compared to females (slope: β = 0.85, p < .05)."

### Consiglio P 2026 (3)

Consiglio P, Fokkens-Bruinsma M, Opdenakker M-C, Jansen EPWA, Fleer J. (2026). Dual Pathways From Self-Compassion and Self-Criticism to Academic Achievement: The Roles of Engagement and Exhaustion. Perspectives on Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5334/pme.1821

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Dark-path PP-LGCM covariate effect: nationality predicted the exhaustion slope, with Dutch students declining while international students increased (β = –2.18, p < .01); unstandardized coefficient, no effect size printed.

> "nationality predicted the trajectory of exhaustion: Dutch students experienced a decline in exhaustion over time, whereas international students experienced an increase (slope: β = –2.18, p < .01)."
