## Evidence

### Chen 2025

Chen, Z.; Singh, C. (2025). Opportunities and Challenges in Harnessing Digital Technology for Effective Teaching and Learning. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.3390/higheredu4010006](https://doi.org/10.3390/higheredu4010006)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Analysis of student performance on an online tutorial implemented as learning modules, plotted in Figure 3B as fraction correct within the first three attempts. The reported correct rate on the Tutorial Post attempt was 'close to 80%', while the Example Pre attempt 'dropped back to between 20 and 30%', which the authors take to indicate limited transfer despite the same underlying physics principles.

> "while on the “Tutorial” module, the “Post” attempt correct rate is close to 80%, on the “Example Pre” attempt , the correct rate dropped back to between 20 and 30%."

### Chen 2025 (2)

Chen, Z.; Singh, C. (2025). Opportunities and Challenges in Harnessing Digital Technology for Effective Teaching and Learning. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.3390/higheredu4010006](https://doi.org/10.3390/higheredu4010006)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r1`

The next semester the tutorial sequence gained an on-ramp module with simpler problems and an Example 2 practice module. Student performance on the updated sequence is plotted in red in Figure 3B; the article reports 'signiﬁcant performance improvements' on Tutorial Pre, Example 1 Pre, and Example 1 Post, with the largest gain on Example 1 Pre, suggesting that on-ramp preparation of basic skills increased transfer to similar new problems.

> "As seen in the ﬁgure, signiﬁcant performance improvements are observed for “Tutorial Pre”, “Example 1 Pre”, and “Example 1 Post”. Notably, the largest improvement was observed for the “Example 1 Pre” attempt"
