# Knowing an argument was written by an expert mathematician increases reported persuasion for a heuristic argument among both students and researchers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants who knew Gowers wrote the heuristic argument rated it as more persuasive than those who did not, with a mean difference of 8%. [→ Matthew Inglis 2008](#matthew-inglis-2008)
`q3 i?` The authority effect on the heuristic argument held when the analysis was restricted to researchers only. [→ Matthew Inglis 2008](#matthew-inglis-2008)

## Evidence

### Matthew Inglis 2008

Matthew Inglis, Juan Pablo Mejia-Ramos. (2008). The Effect of Authority on the Persuasiveness of Mathematical Arguments. https://doi.org/10.1080/07370000802584513

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Web-based Experiment 1 with 384 participants (190 research-active mathematicians, 194 undergraduates) randomly assigned to named or anonymous conditions. The ANOVA showed those who knew the author was Gowers "rated the argument as more persuasive than those who did not"; no effect size printed.

> "there was also a significant main effect for condition, F(1,370)=5.45, MSE=928.5, p=0.020, showing that those participants who knew the argument was written by Gowers rated the argument as more persuasive than those who did not (a mean difference of 8%)."
