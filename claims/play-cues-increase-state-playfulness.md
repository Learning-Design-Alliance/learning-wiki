# Play cues significantly increase state playfulness in workplace meetings with a moderate effect size

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

> **Currency** · newest study 2016 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Play-cued participants showed a statistically significant increase in playfulness (median 8 to 10, r = .37) while control playfulness did not change significantly. [→ West 2016](#west-2016)

## Evidence

### West 2016

West, Samuel E., Eva Hoff, and Ingegerd Carlsson. (2016). Play and Productivity: Enhancing the Creative Climate at Workplace Meetings with Play Cues. American Journal of Play, 9(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1123863

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures field experiment measuring playfulness as a state with a two-item seven-point scale. The play-cued group increased "from a median of 8 to 10" with "a moderate effect size (r  = .37)"; the control group's change was not significant.

> "The intervention group showed a statistically significant increase in playfulness from a median of 8 to 10 [z = -6.76, p = <.001], with a moderate effect size (r  = .37). The control group's playfulness did not change significantly [z = -1.60, p = .11]."
