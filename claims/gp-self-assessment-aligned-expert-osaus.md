BMC Medical Education 25:733. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07356-8

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Paper questionnaire self-assessment collected from the 18 GPs at follow-up, with expert assessors blinded to the questionnaire; the proportion rating themselves able to scan unsupervised "increased for all applications (Figure 2)".

> "At follow-up, the proportion of participants who rated themselves as being able to perform a specific POCUS application unsupervised in general practice (to a very high degree or to a high degree) increased for all applications (Figure 2)."

### Andersen Camilla Aakjær 2025 (2)

Andersen Camilla Aakjær, Lundbye-Christensen Søren, Riis Allan, Mehnert Ulrike, Løkkegaard Thomas, Mengel-Jørgensen Troels, Rasmussen Liv Dyre, Soll Nicolai, Stork Bo, Andersen Søren Kæseler, Pil Louise, Stjernebjerg Christian, Graumann Ole, Jensen Martin Bach. BMC Medical Education 25:733. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07356-8

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion comparing the self-assessment secondary outcome with the blinded expert OSAUS ratings; they report that self-assessment "aligned nicely with the assessment done by the experts".

> "The GPs’ self-assessment of competence was included as secondary outcomes despite being prone to subjective bias. This was done to evaluate whether insight into own abilities was obtained, and we found that self-assessment of scanning competence (Figure 2), aligned nicely with the assessment done by the experts."
