## Subclaims
`q2 i?` All interviewed experimental-group TTs reported being observed three or four times, while in the control group eight of 18 TTs reported never being observed. [→ Gürsoy 2016](#gursoy-2016)
`q2 i?` All experimental-group interviewees reported receiving feedback during planning and were satisfied with post-lesson feedback quality, whereas 12 control-group TTs reported never receiving planning feedback and eight reported receiving no feedback at all. [→ Gürsoy 2016 (2)](#gursoy-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Gürsoy 2016

Gürsoy, E., Kesner, J. E., & Salihoglu, U. M. (2016). Clinical Supervision Model in Teaching Practice: Does it Make a Difference in Supervisors' Performance?. Australian Journal of Teacher Education, 41(11). https://doi.org/10.14221/ajte.2016v41n11.5

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Interview study with 39 TTs (20 experimental, 18 control) conducted in fall 2013 and spring 2014 about observation frequency. The experimental group's uniform three-observation schedule contrasts with the control group's variable, often absent, observations.

> "All of the participants (n=20) in the experimental group claimed that they were observed three times throughout the semester and one said that s/he was observed four times, whereas, the number of observations varied in the control group. Eight (out of 18) TTs stated that they were never observed during practicum."

### Gürsoy 2016 (2)

Gürsoy, E., Kesner, J. E., & Salihoglu, U. M. (2016). Clinical Supervision Model in Teaching Practice: Does it Make a Difference in Supervisors' Performance?. Australian Journal of Teacher Education, 41(11). https://doi.org/10.14221/ajte.2016v41n11.5

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Interview data on feedback during lesson planning: all experimental-group participants received pre-conference or Facebook feedback and 19 of 20 found it adequate, while in the control group 12 TTs indicated they never received feedback while planning their lessons.

> "All of the participants in the experimental group agreed that they had feedback during pre-conferences and/or via Facebook. The majority (n=19) stated that they benefitted from the feedback and that it was adequate."
