# Nursing students report significantly greater satisfaction and motivation after serious-game simulation than after traditional teaching

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Global satisfaction with the training course was significantly higher in the SG group (8.5 ± 1.6) than the TT group (7.6 ± 1.7). [→ Blanié Antonia 2020](#blanie-antonia-2020)
`q3 i?` Perceived motivation to learn was significantly higher in the SG group (8.7 ± 1.9) than the TT group (7.7 ± 2.0). [→ Blanié Antonia 2020 (2)](#blanie-antonia-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Blanié Antonia 2020

Blanié Antonia, Amorim Michel-Ange, Benhamou Dan. (2020). Comparative value of a simulation by gaming and a traditional teaching method to improve clinical reasoning skills necessary to detect patient deterioration: a randomized study in nursing students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-1939-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

End-of-session questionnaire in the randomized trial (Kirkpatrick level 1); Table 4 prints global satisfaction of 8.5 ± 1.6 in SG versus 7.6 ± 1.7 in TT, with the Bonferroni criterion set at alpha/5 = 0.01.

> "Students in the SG group significantly expressed significantly more satisfaction toward the training session than those in the TT group (p = 0.001) (Table 4)."

### Blanié Antonia 2020 (2)

Blanié Antonia, Amorim Michel-Ange, Benhamou Dan. (2020). Comparative value of a simulation by gaming and a traditional teaching method to improve clinical reasoning skills necessary to detect patient deterioration: a randomized study in nursing students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-1939-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary outcome from the same trial's end-of-session Likert questionnaire; the printed motivation item scored 8.7 ± 1.9 in SG versus 7.7 ± 2.0 in TT, significant at the article's stated 0.01 threshold.

> "Students of the SG group perceived the training session as more engaging, as reflected by their significantly increased motivation (p = 0.003)."
