Evidence¶

Reid Smith 2021¶

Reid Smith, Pamela Snow, Tanya Serry & Lorraine Hammond (2021) The Role of Background Knowledge in Reading Comprehension: A Critical Review, Reading Psychology, 42:3, 214-240, https://doi.org/10.1080/02702711.2021.1888348

q2 · i? · review · r2

Review reports Lipson (1982): children relied more heavily on prior knowledge than on the text when information contradicted prior knowledge, with low-skill readers substituting misconceptions in recall.

"When asked to recall the contents of the article, low skill readers were much more likely to omit contradictory information from the text and replace with their misconception."

Reid Smith 2021 (2)¶

Reid Smith, Pamela Snow, Tanya Serry & Lorraine Hammond (2021) The Role of Background Knowledge in Reading Comprehension: A Critical Review, Reading Psychology, 42:3, 214-240, https://doi.org/10.1080/02702711.2021.1888348

q2 · i? · review · r2

Review reports Holmes (1983) replication with above/below-average readers, showing differential ability to detect and resolve text-knowledge inconsistencies.

"She observed similar effects to Lipson, noting that above-average readers were more likely to identify contradictions between prior knowledge and information in the text. Below-average readers were more reliant on their (incorrect) knowledge and struggled to resolve inconsistencies between the text and prior knowledge (Holmes, 1983)."
