## Evidence

### Cao W 2021

Cao W, Hu L, Li X, Li X, Chen C, Zhang Q, Cao S. (2021). Massive Open Online Courses-based blended versus face-to-face classroom teaching methods for fundamental nursing course. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000024829

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Cluster randomized controlled trial measuring critical thinking with the Chinese Critical Thinking Disposition Inventory (70 items, 6-point Likert). Mean changes on the named subdomains favored the blended group; the article prints medians and P values but no effect size.

> "the items forming critical thinking ability, including truth seeking (P < .001), analytical ability (P < 0.001), critical thinking self-confidence (P < 0.001), inquisitiveness (P < .001), and cognitive maturity (P < 0.001), following the intervention in the MOOC-based blended group were significantly improved than those in face-to-face classroom teaching group."

### Cao W 2021 (2)

Cao W, Hu L, Li X, Li X, Chen C, Zhang Q, Cao S. (2021). Massive Open Online Courses-based blended versus face-to-face classroom teaching methods for fundamental nursing course. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000024829

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same trial, Table 4 mean-change comparisons: open mindedness and systemic capacity changes favored the face-to-face group (both P < .001), while cognitive maturity change did not differ (P = .473). No effect sizes are printed.

> "However, the improvement in open mindedness (P < .001), and systemic capacity (P < .001) in MOOC-based blended group were inferior than those in face-to-face classroom teaching group. Finally, there was no significant difference between MOOC-based blended and face-to-face classroom teaching groups for the improvement in cognitive maturity (P = .473)."
