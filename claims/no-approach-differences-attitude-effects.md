# No significant differences in attitude effects between the five types of innovative teaching approach

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Meta-regression found no evidence that context-based, inquiry-based, ICT-rich, collaborative, or extracurricular approaches differ in their effects on overall attitude. [→ Savelsbergh 2016](#savelsbergh-2016)

## Evidence

### Savelsbergh 2016

Savelsbergh, E. R., Prins, G. T., Rietbergen, C., Fechner, S., Vaessen, B. E., Draijer, J. M., & Bakker, A. (2016). Effects of innovative science and mathematics teaching on student attitudes and achievement: A meta-analytic study. Educational Research Review, 19, 158-172. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2016.07.003

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Meta-regression of overall attitude effects on teaching-approach type, domain, timeframe, grade level, and teacher training, excluding Akçay et al. experiments. The authors report "no evidence for one approach being more effective than the others"; all approach coefficients were nonsignificant (e.g., Inquiry B = 0.13, p = 0.59).

> "The data provide no evidence for one approach being more effective than the others, no evidence for differences between domains, and also teacher training and timeframe do not explain signiﬁcant amounts of variance."
