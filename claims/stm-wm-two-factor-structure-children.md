# Simple span and complex span tasks define two distinct but correlated memory factors in 7- to 13-year-olds

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A two-factor model separating STM tasks from WM tasks fits children's memory-task correlations significantly better than a one-factor model in both studies. [→ Robert Kail and Lynda K. Hall 2001](#robert-kail-and-lynda-k-hall-2001)

## Evidence

### Robert Kail and Lynda K. Hall 2001

Robert Kail and Lynda K. Hall. (2001). Distinguishing short-term memory from working memory. Memory & Cognition, 29(1), 1-9. https://link.springer.com/journal/13421/29/1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Confirmatory factor analyses (structural equation modeling with EzPath, maximum likelihood) compared one- and two-factor models of the memory tasks in Study 1 (N = 155) and Study 2 (N = 142). The two-factor model fit significantly better, with "c2s(1) = 15.66 and 8.65, respectively, p , .01". The two factors were correlated, not independent.

> "The difference in fit was significant in Studies 1 and 2 [c2s(1) = 15.66 and 8.65, respectively, p , .01]."
