Evidence¶

Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante 2023¶

Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante, Andrea P. Goldin, Mariano Sigman and María Juliana Leone. (2023). A better alignment between chronotype and school timing is associated with lower grade retention in adolescents. npj Science of Learning 8:21. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00171-0

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Logistic regression on n = 407 first-year students tracking who reached 5th year: "lower 1st year grades are associated with higher grade retention" for both math (η2 = 0.130) and language (η2 = 0.046).

"The selected model shows a main effect of both math (F1406 = 51.156, P < 0.0001, partial η2 = 0.130, 90% CI = 0.083–0.182) and language grades ( F1406 = 18.006, P < 0.0001, partial η2 = 0.046, 90% CI = 0.018–0.084), which means that lower 1st year grades are associated with higher grade retention"

Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante, Andrea P. Goldin, Mariano Sigman and María Juliana Leone. (2023). A better alignment between chronotype and school timing is associated with lower grade retention in adolescents. npj Science of Learning 8:21. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00171-0

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

Odds ratios from the selected retention model: at average chronotype, 1-point-lower math grades give "1.616 signi ﬁcant higher odds of repeating a grade" (OR = 1.616, a small effect); not significant for a 1h-earlier chronotype and stronger (OR = 2.278) for a 1h-later one.

"a student with 1 point-lower grade and an average chronotype (i.e. 06:10) would have 1.616 signi ﬁcant higher odds of repeating a grade (Table 2d)."

Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante 2023 (3)¶

Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante, Andrea P. Goldin, Mariano Sigman and María Juliana Leone. (2023). A better alignment between chronotype and school timing is associated with lower grade retention in adolescents. npj Science of Learning 8:21. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00171-0

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

Lower language grades increase retention odds in all timings but "stronger for morning than for evening-attending students": OR = 3.106 in the morning versus OR = 1.553 in the evening, small-to-moderate odds-ratio effects.

"in morning school timing a 1-point-lower language grade is associated with 3.106 higher odds of grade retention while in the evening the odds are 1.553 higher (Table 2e)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Chronotype predicts grade retention through its interaction with school timing (synchrony effect), not through a main chronotype effect: morning-attending late chronotypes with low math grades show higher retention odds than their evening peers — related
  • The association between later chronotype and lower math grades is strongest in the morning timing, indicating a synchrony effect that partially counteracts the chronotype effect — related
  • The propensity-for-retention measure discriminates retained from not-retained students, with first-grade log-odds of retention more than three times greater for actually retained students — related
  • Later chronotypes obtain lower academic performance (chronotype effect), moderated by age, school subject and school timing — related
