Evidence¶

Yang Gao and Barbara E. Hanna 2016¶

Yang Gao and Barbara E. Hanna. (2016). Exploring optimal pronunciation teaching: Integrating instructional software into intermediate-level EFL classes in China. CALICO Journal. https://doi.org/10.1558/cj.v33i2.26054

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Planned contrasts on pronunciation score changes among the three groups (N = 60): Group 3 vs Group 2 "t(57) = 2.14, p < 0.05 (one-tailed)"; Group 3 vs Group 1 t(57) = 0.09, p > 0.05. Group 3's mean change was 2.38 (SD = 0.83), Group 2's 1.78 (SD = 0.95).

"planned contrasts (Table 18) revealed that combined instruction significantly increased students’ pronunciation performance compared to instructional software, t(57) = 2.14, p < 0.05 (one-tailed), while the effectiveness of combined instruction was similar to that of human-aided instruction, t (57) = 0.09, p > 0.05 (one-tailed)."

Yang Gao and Barbara E. Hanna 2016 (2)¶

Yang Gao and Barbara E. Hanna. (2016). Exploring optimal pronunciation teaching: Integrating instructional software into intermediate-level EFL classes in China. CALICO Journal. https://doi.org/10.1558/cj.v33i2.26054

q2 · i? · causal · r1

One-way ANOVA on score changes across the three instruction groups: "F(2, 57) = 2.937, p > 0.05, w = 0.26"; Levene's test showed equal group variances (p > 0.05). The omnibus effect was not significant despite the significant pairwise contrast.

"There was a non-significant effect of teaching method on differences of students’ pronunciation improvements with a medium effect size, F(2, 57) = 2.937, p > 0.05, w = 0.26."
