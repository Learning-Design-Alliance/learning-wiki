## Evidence

### S. B. McKagan 2008

S. B. McKagan, K. K. Perkins, and C. E. Wieman. (2008). A Deeper Look at Student Learning of Quantum Mechanics: the Case of Tunneling. https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.3194 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0802.3194](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0802.3194)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Final exam question in the transformed courses asking students to draw the real part of the wave function for an electron tunneling through a square barrier. The article reports "18% of students drew the wave function with an oﬀset" and 23% a shorter wavelength, showing the documented difficulties persist though in smaller numbers than in traditional courses.

> "18% of students drew the wave function with an oﬀset as in Figure 2a, and 23% drew a shorter wavelength on the right than on the left as in Figure 2b."

### S. B. McKagan 2008 (2)

S. B. McKagan, K. K. Perkins, and C. E. Wieman. (2008). A Deeper Look at Student Learning of Quantum Mechanics: the Case of Tunneling. https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.3194 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0802.3194](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0802.3194)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Comparison across the eight courses using QMCS responses to the Figure 6 question (Table II). Transformed engineering students answered correctly at "37 − 41%", which the article states was higher than the traditional engineering course and comparable to traditional physics courses. No effect size is printed.

> "37 − 41% of students in the transformed course for engineering majors answered correctly (higher than the scores in the traditional course for engineering majors and comparable to the scores in the traditional course for physics majors)"
