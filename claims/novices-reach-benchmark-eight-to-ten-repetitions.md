## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Using 1-sample t tests against a trimmed-mean expert benchmark, novice mean scores became statistically comparable to the benchmark between the eighth and tenth repetition for each of the four construct-valid tasks. [→ Sharma Mitesh MS 2012](#sharma-mitesh-ms-2012)

## Evidence

### Sharma Mitesh MS 2012

Sharma Mitesh MS, Macafee David MA MD, Pranesh Nagarajan MD, Horgan Alan F. MD. (2012). Construct Validity of Fresh Frozen Human Cadaver as a Training Model in Minimal Access Surgery. JSLS : Journal of the Society of Laparoendoscopic Surgeons. https://doi.org/10.4293/108680812X13462882735818

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

1-sample t test comparisons of each of 10 novice repetition scores against task-specific benchmark scores (e.g., 14.66 for peg transfer, 17.83 for appendicectomy). Per-task tables show P values becoming nonsignificant on repetition 9 (peg transfer, appendicectomy), repetition 10 (intracorporeal knot), and repetition 8 (extracorporeal knot); no effect sizes are printed.

> "The mean scores for the these tasks performed by novices became nonsignificant (or comparable to the benchmark scores) between the eighth to tenth repetitions for each task."
