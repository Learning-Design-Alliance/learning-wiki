# Final-year medical students rate university education as more sufficient for cardiovascular diseases than for infectious diseases

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Australian final-year medical students were significantly more likely to rate their university education as sufficient or more than sufficient for cardiovascular diseases (91.3%) than for infectious diseases (72.5%). [→ Weier N 2017](#weier-n-2017)

## Evidence

### Weier N 2017

Weier N, Thursky K, Zaidi STR. (2017). Antimicrobial knowledge and confidence amongst final year medical students in Australia. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0182460

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cross-sectional online survey of Australian final-year medical students (163 responses analysed). In the sufficiency-of-education comparison, more students rated cardiology education sufficient; the article reports the difference was "significantly different at p<0.05" with no effect size printed.

> "Relatively more students reported their education at university in cardiology to be ‘sufficient’ or ‘more than sufficient’ compared to infectious diseases (Table 1). These results were significantly different at p<0.05."
