## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Linguistically disadvantaged students had significantly larger writing improvement than their peers across all indicators, with differential improvement growing from 0.044 SD on average in Phase 1 to 0.088 SD on average in Phase 2. [→ Renzhe Yu 2024](#renzhe-yu-2024)
`q2 i?` Because disadvantaged groups wrote 0.043 to 0.209 SD less proficiently pre-LLM, the improvement patterns suggest LLM tools helped close or even reverse language gaps. [→ Renzhe Yu 2024 (2)](#renzhe-yu-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Renzhe Yu 2024

Renzhe Yu, Zhen Xu, Sky CH-Wang, Richard Arum. (2024). Whose ChatGPT? Unveiling Real-World Educational Inequalities Introduced by Large Language Models. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2410.22282](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.22282)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression analysis with interaction terms between linguistic-disadvantage indicators and phase indicators, controlling for course and year-of-study fixed effects. Table S2 reports Phase 2*LingDis coefficients up to 0.298 for international students on readability.

> "linguistically disadvantaged students (from non-English-speaking countries or families, or with lower entering writing scores) had significantly larger improvement than their peers across all indicators of writing language proficiency"

### Renzhe Yu 2024 (2)

Renzhe Yu, Zhen Xu, Sky CH-Wang, Richard Arum. (2024). Whose ChatGPT? Unveiling Real-World Educational Inequalities Introduced by Large Language Models. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2410.22282](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.22282)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same observational study; the authors compare pre-LLM baseline gaps (LingDis coefficients in Table S2, e.g., -0.209) with the post-LLM differential improvements to argue gap closure or reversal.

> "Given that linguistically disadvantaged student groups wrote 0.043 to 0.209 standard deviation less proficiently than their peers in the pre-LLM period, the estimated improvement patterns suggest that the emerging LLM tools helped close or even reverse language gaps in academic writing."
