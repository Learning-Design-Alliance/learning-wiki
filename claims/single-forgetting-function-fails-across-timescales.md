# A single time-invariant ACT-R forgetting function cannot simultaneously capture recall accuracy on short and long between-session intervals

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When an ACT-R model is fitted to short intervals it under-predicts retention at longer intervals, and when fitted to longer intervals it over-predicts accuracy on short intervals. [→ Maarten van der Velde 2026](#maarten-van-der-velde-2026)

## Evidence

### Maarten van der Velde 2026

Maarten van der Velde, Florian Sense, Jelmer P. Borst & Hedderik van Rijn. (2026). Explaining forgetting at different timescales requires a time-variant forgetting function. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00447-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Model-fitting analysis of naturalistic recall data from an adaptive fact-learning system. The standard ACT-R model fitted to intervals up to 10 minutes became "far too pessimistic" at longer intervals, and "the same model ﬁtted to longer intervals overestimates accuracy on short intervals (Fig.1c)." No effect size is printed.
