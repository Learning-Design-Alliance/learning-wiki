## Evidence

### Corey McInerney 2026

Corey McInerney. (2026). A Thematic Analysis of A-level Physics Examiner Reports on Gravity. arXiv. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/ae806d](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/ae806d)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Quantitative coding of 137 examination questions by topic across six UK exam boards. The results state that "Orbital motion constitutes the largest proportion of questions (31%)", which the author reads as a curriculum emphasis on gravity as a dynamical and spatial interaction.

> "Orbital motion constitutes the largest proportion of questions (31%), followed by gravitational energy and potential (24%). Gravitational fields (19%), Keplerian relationships (12%) and force (11%) make up a smaller part of the sample."

### Corey McInerney 2026 (2)

Corey McInerney. (2026). A Thematic Analysis of A-level Physics Examiner Reports on Gravity. arXiv. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/ae806d](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/ae806d)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Coding of the same 137 questions by type/style. The results report that "Calculation-type questions were the most prevalent across all exam boards (55%)".

> "Calculation-type questions were the most prevalent across all exam boards (55%), followed by explanation of a statement, phenomenon or data (26%)."

### Corey McInerney 2026 (3)

Corey McInerney. (2026). A Thematic Analysis of A-level Physics Examiner Reports on Gravity. arXiv. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/ae806d](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/ae806d)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Sentiment coding of examiner comments across the dataset. The results state reports are "mostly positive (60%)", which the author interprets as most candidates achieving most of the available marks, while approximately one third of comments were negative.

> "when examiner reports are assessed based on their overall sentiment (Table 7 provides a breakdown of question topic against report sentiment), it is found that they are mostly positive (60%)."
