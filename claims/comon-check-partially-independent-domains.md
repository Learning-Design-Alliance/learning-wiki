# ComOn check items A1 and A2 are uncorrelated with other subscales, while domains B, C, E, and item D correlate small to large, strongest between C and E (rho = 0.59)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r3` · `q2` · `i3` large
> **Currency** · newest study 2017 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` The scale measures partially independent skills: initiating a conversation and patient's perception items did not correlate with other domains, whereas structure, emotions, communication skills, and end-of-conversation showed small-to-large associations. [→ Radziej K 2017](#radziej-k-2017)

## Evidence

### Radziej K 2017

Radziej K, Loechner J, Engerer C, Niglio de Figueiredo M, Freund J, Sattel H, Bachmann C, Berberat PO, Dinkel A, Wuensch A. (2017). How to assess communication skills? Development of the rating scale ComOn Check. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2017.1392823

`q2 · i3` · `design · r3`

Spearman correlation matrix across subscales B, C, E and items A1, A2, D from the 135-consultation evaluation. The correlation between Patient's emotions and Communication skills was ρ = 0.59 (p < 0.001; r = .59, large by the article's own criterion), about 35% shared variance; B–D was 0.44, B–E 0.30, C–D 0.35, D–F 0.28 (all significant).

> "Whereas the items A1 and A2 did not correlate with any other item or subscale, associations between the domains B, C, E, and item D were small to large. The strongest association could be found between subscales C and E (ρ = 0.59, p < 0.001), indicating that their proportion of shared variance is about 35%."
