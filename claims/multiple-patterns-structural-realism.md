## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Models designed to reproduce multiple observed patterns are more likely to be structurally realistic and can make independent, testable secondary predictions. [→ Grimm 2005](#grimm-2005)

## Evidence

### Grimm 2005

Grimm, V., Revilla, E., Berger, U., Jeltsch, F., Mooij, W. M., Railsback, S. F., Thulke, H.-H., Weiner, J., Wiegand, T., & DeAngelis, D. L. (2005). Pattern-Oriented Modeling of Agent-Based Complex Systems: Lessons from Ecology. Science. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1116681

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Case study of the beech forest model of central Europe, whose structure was determined by multiple characteristic patterns (mosaic of successional stages, vertical structure, canopy gaps). The model delivered independent predictions not considered during development, which were in "good agreement with observations".

> "Predictions of age structure in the canopy and the spatial distribution of very old ''giant'' trees were in good agreement with observations, considerably increasing the model's credibility and justifying a completely new application: tracking woody debris."
