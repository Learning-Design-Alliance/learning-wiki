## Evidence

### Stegers-Jager K. M. 2016

Stegers-Jager K. M., Brommet F. N., Themmen A. P. N. (2016). Ethnic and social disparities in different types of examinations in undergraduate pre-clinical training. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-016-9676-7

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal prospective cohort study of students entering a 3-year Dutch Bachelor of Medicine course 2008–2013; logistic regression on first-attempt pass/fail. The results section reports Dutch students more likely to pass the first-year CPST (74 %) with unadjusted ORs of 0.52, 0.46 and 0.46 for the three non-Western groups; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Dutch students were more likely to pass the first-year CPST (74 %) compared with Turkish/Moroccan/African students (60 %; unadjusted OR 0.52), Surinamese/Antillean students (57 %; unadjusted OR 0.46) and Asian students (57 %; unadjusted OR 0.46"

### Stegers-Jager K. M. 2016 (2)

Stegers-Jager K. M., Brommet F. N., Themmen A. P. N. (2016). Ethnic and social disparities in different types of examinations in undergraduate pre-clinical training. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-016-9676-7

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Same cohort study, clinical skills examination outcome. The results report Dutch students more likely to pass the second-year OSCE (71 %) than each of the three non-Western minority groups, with unadjusted ORs 0.55, 0.57 and 0.61; confounders and socio-demographic characteristics failed to explain these differences. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "Dutch students were more likely to pass the second-year OSCE (71 %) than Turkish/Moroccan/African students (58 %; unadjusted OR 0.55), Surinamese/Antillean students (58 %; unadjusted OR 0.57) and Asian students (57 %; unadjusted OR 0.61"

### Stegers-Jager K. M. 2016 (3)

Stegers-Jager K. M., Brommet F. N., Themmen A. P. N. (2016). Ethnic and social disparities in different types of examinations in undergraduate pre-clinical training. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-016-9676-7

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cohort study, language skills outcome. The results report that the percentage of Dutch students passing the language skills test (56 %) was significantly higher than for all other ethnic subgroups, with pass rates ranging from 28 to 48 %. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "The percentage of Dutch students that passed the language skills test (56 %) was significantly higher than for all other ethnic subgroups (ranging from 28 to 48 %; Table 2)."
