# Asking teachers first about teacher perception surveys increases their support for student perception surveys (pre-registered split-ballot experiment)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Teachers randomly assigned to first answer a teacher perception survey scale reported greater support for student perception surveys than control teachers, controlling for national teaching recognition. [→ Robinson 2016](#robinson-2016)

## Evidence

### Robinson 2016

Robinson, C.; Finefter-Rosenblum, I.; Benshoof, C.; Gehlbach, H. (2016). Getting Teachers Excited about Student Feedback: It's all in the Ask. SREE Spring 2016 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED567735

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre-registered randomized split-ballot online survey (Qualtrics); 279 of 407 K-12 teachers completed the full survey. OLS regression with NNSTOY covariate showed the treatment effect: "𝛽 = 0.14; 𝑝 = 0.018" with 95% CI 0.04–0.42. The article reports no standardized effect size (d or r) for this contrast, so impact is null.

> "On average, teachers in the treatment condition report greater support for student perception surveys than teachers in the control condition when controlling for whether teachers have received national recognition for their teaching (𝐵 = 0.23, 𝑆𝑆 = 0.10, 𝐶𝐶: 0.04, 0.42; 𝛽 = 0.14; 𝑝 = 0.018)."
