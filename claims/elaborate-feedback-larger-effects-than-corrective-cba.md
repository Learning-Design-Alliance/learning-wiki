## Evidence

### Hepburn CY 2026

Hepburn CY. (2026). Evidence to support integrating feedback best practice for computer‐based assessment in pharmacology education. Br J Clin Pharmacol. https://doi.org/10.1002/bcp.70302

`q2 · i2` · `review · r2`

The review reports a meta-analysis of quantitative achievement improvement for three styles of item-based feedback in computer-based assessment, primarily in college, university or adult education samples. It states elaborate feedback "produces larger effect sizes (g = 0.49)" than corrective feedback forms.

> "elaborate feedback, which includes some form of explanation, for example hints or extra reading material, produces larger effect sizes (g = 0.49; 90% confidence interval [CI] [0.36–0.62]) as compared with corrective feedback of any form"
