## Evidence

### Nichols 2016

Nichols, A. H., Eberle-Sudré, K., & Welch, M. (2016). Rising Tide II: Do Black Students Benefit as Grad Rates Increase? The Education Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED566672

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive IPEDS analysis (Table 2, Figure 6) of the 27 widening-gap institutions. At Missouri–Kansas City the black rate fell from 45.5 percent to 31.2 percent while the white rate rose to 53.9 percent, so "the gap between white and black student graduation rates has grown to 22.7 percentage points."

> "Despite having increases in its overall graduation rate of approximately 10 percentage points over the last 10 years, the gap between white and black student graduation rates has grown to 22.7 percentage points."
