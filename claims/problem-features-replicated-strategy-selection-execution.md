# Collecting metacognitive judgments did not disrupt typical strategy selection and execution: both age groups selected the better strategy more on rounding-up homogeneous problems and executed rounding-down faster on homogeneous problems

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants selected the better strategy more when it was rounding-up and problems were homogeneous, and executed rounding-down faster on homogeneous problems, in both age groups. [→ Marie Geurten & Patrick Lemaire 2017](#marie-geurten-patrick-lemaire-2017)

## Evidence

### Marie Geurten & Patrick Lemaire 2017

Marie Geurten & Patrick Lemaire. (2017). Metacognition for Strategy Selection during Arithmetic Problem Solving in Young and Older Adults. https://www.google.com/search?q=Metacognition+for+Strategy+Selection+during+Arithmetic+Problem+Solving+in+Young+and+Older+Adults

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Supplemental analyses (S1) of strategy selection and execution in the 66-participant experiment replicated prior problem-feature effects: participants "selected the better strategy on each problem when this better strategy was the rounding-up strategy and the problems were homogeneous problems". No effect sizes printed in the main text.

> "They selected the better strategy on each problem when this better strategy was the rounding-up strategy and the problems were homogeneous problems. They executed the rounding-down strategy more quickly on homogeneous problems."
