# Task type strongly determines trust in educational robots, with highest trust for procedural instruction, moderate trust for teaching, and lowest trust when robots request personal information

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In a repeated-measures ANOVA, TASK had a significant large main effect on trust ratings (η² = .439). [→ Anna-Maria Velentza 2026](#anna-maria-velentza-2026)
`q3 i?` Post hoc comparisons showed instruction tasks produced significantly higher trust than teaching and personal-information tasks, and teaching higher than personal information. [→ Anna-Maria Velentza 2026 (2)](#anna-maria-velentza-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Anna-Maria Velentza 2026

Anna-Maria Velentza, Konstantina Nikou, Anne-Gwenn Bosser, Nikolaos Fachantidis. (2026). What Robots Do Matters More Than What They Look Like: Task Context Shapes Trust in Educational HRI. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22What+Robots+Do+Matters+More+Than+What+They+Look+Like%22 [doi:10.1109/ro-man72286.2026.11714558](https://doi.org/10.1109/ro-man72286.2026.11714558)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Two-way repeated-measures ANOVA with ROBOT and TASK as within-subject factors in the N = 81 video-based experiment. The article reports a significant TASK main effect, "F (1.88, 148.18) = 61.88, p < .001, η 2 = .439".

> "Results revealed a significant main effect of TASK, F (1.88, 148.18) = 61.88, p < .001, η 2 = .439, indicating that trust levels differed significantly across activities."

### Anna-Maria Velentza 2026 (2)

Anna-Maria Velentza, Konstantina Nikou, Anne-Gwenn Bosser, Nikolaos Fachantidis. (2026). What Robots Do Matters More Than What They Look Like: Task Context Shapes Trust in Educational HRI. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22What+Robots+Do+Matters+More+Than+What+They+Look+Like%22

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Bonferroni-adjusted pairwise comparisons in the same repeated-measures ANOVA. Estimated marginal means were highest for Instructions (T2, M = 3.43), followed by Teaching (T1, M = 3.14), and lowest for Personal Information (T3, M = 2.88); only test statistics with p < .001 are printed, no effect size.

> "Post hoc comparisons for TASK (see Fig. 6) showed that T2 (Instructions) produced significantly higher trust scores than both T1 (Teaching) and T3 (Personal Information), all p < .001."
