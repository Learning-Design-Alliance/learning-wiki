# The L1-L2 distinction in incidental vocabulary acquisition from reading lies in the rate, not the ultimate outcome, of the acquisition process

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` L1 readers showed significant repetition effects earlier (from the first encounter in some measures) than L2 readers (third to fourth encounter), but there were no significant differences between L1 and L2 gains in reading times. [→ Pellicer-Sánchez 2016](#pellicer-sanchez-2016)
`q3 i?` There was no significant difference between L1 and L2 participants in acquisition of form recognition, meaning recognition, or meaning recall. [→ Pellicer-Sánchez 2016](#pellicer-sanchez-2016)

## Evidence

### Pellicer-Sánchez 2016

Pellicer-Sánchez, Ana. (2016). Incidental L2 Vocabulary Acquisition From and While Reading: An Eye-Tracking Study. Studies in Second Language Acquisition, 38(1), 97-130. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263115000224

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mann-Whitney tests comparing gain scores of 25 L1 and 23 L2 readers across the eight encounters; no significant differences in gains for targets or controls. The article states the only L1-L2 difference "was not in the magnitude of the observed effect but in its rate."

> "Results showed that there were no significant differences between their gains, both for targets (gains first fixation duration: Z = –.59, p = .556; gains gaze duration: Z = –.96, p = .337; gains number of fixations: Z = –1.08, p = .282; gains total reading time: Z = –3.30, p = .741)"
