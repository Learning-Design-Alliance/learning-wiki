# Using rubrics has a positive, moderate effect on students' academic performance (corrected g = 0.45; standardized mean change = 0.38)

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Panadero 2023

Panadero, Ernesto; Jonsson, Anders; Pinedo, Leire; Fernández-Castilla, Belén. (2023). Effects of Rubrics on Academic Performance, Self-Regulated Learning, and self-Efficacy: a Meta-analytic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09823-4

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Three-level meta-analysis of 54 standardized mean differences from 21 studies comparing rubric users to controls, with a pooled effect of g = 0.57, described as "a large overall effect"; after selection-model correction, "The corrected overall effect was 0.45 for moderate bias, and 0.33 for severe bias."

> "The overall Hedges’ g  was 0.57 (SE = 0.125, t  = 4.59, p-value < 0.001, 95% CI [0.312, 0.831], 95% PI [-0.558, 1.701]), which is a large overall effect."

### Panadero 2023 (2)

Panadero, Ernesto; Jonsson, Anders; Pinedo, Leire; Fernández-Castilla, Belén. (2023). Effects of Rubrics on Academic Performance, Self-Regulated Learning, and self-Efficacy: a Meta-analytic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09823-4

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Second meta-analysis of 30 standardized mean changes from 12 studies reporting pre- and post-measures for both intervention and control groups, with a standardized mean change of 0.38, described as "a medium overall effect" favoring rubrics.

> "The overall standardized mean change was 0.38 (SE = 0.16, t = 2.34, p-value = 0.042, 95% CI [0.02, 0.75], 95% PI [-0.75, 1.51]), which is a medium overall effect."
