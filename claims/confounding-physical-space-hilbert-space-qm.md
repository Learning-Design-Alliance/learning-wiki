## Evidence

### Singh 2016

Singh, C. (2016). Student Difficulties with Quantum Mechanics Formalism. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.075](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.075)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Written surveys with reasoning explanations and semi-structured think-aloud interviews with advanced undergraduates and graduate students. The article reports that it is "diﬃcult for many students to distinguish between vectors in the 3D laboratory space and Hilbert space", with such difficulties frequently observed in surveys and interviews.

> "It is diﬃcult for many students to distinguish between vectors in the 3D laboratory space and Hilbert space."

### Singh 2016 (2)

Singh, C. (2016). Student Difficulties with Quantum Mechanics Formalism. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.075](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.075)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey responses to the two-part Stern-Gerlach question show confusion about the direction of beam splitting in physical space; "Thirteen percent of the students" drew the splitting along the wrong axis across the two question parts.

> "Thirteen percent of the students (including questions (a) and (b)) drew the splitting of the beam in the wrong direction (along the x axis in (a) and along the z axis in (b))."
