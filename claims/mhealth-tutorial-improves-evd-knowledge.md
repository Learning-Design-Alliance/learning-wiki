## Evidence

### Otu Akaninyene 2016

Otu Akaninyene, Ebenso Bassey, Okuzu Okey, Osifo-Dawodu Egbe. (2016). Using a mHealth tutorial application to change knowledge and attitude of frontline health workers to Ebola virus disease in Nigeria: a before-and-after study. Human Resources for Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12960-016-0100-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Before-and-after cross-sectional surveys of 203 frontline health workers in 14 Ondo State facilities; paired sample Z test on pre- and post-tutorial knowledge scores. The article reports "an 11% improvement in average knowledge levels between pre- and post-intervention scores" with significant differences on transmission, symptoms and preventability questions.

> "Further analysis showed an 11% improvement in average knowledge levels between pre- and post-intervention scores with statistically significant differences (P < 0.05) recorded for questions concerning the transmission of the Ebola virus among humans, common symptoms of Ebola fever and whether Ebola fever was preventable."

### Otu Akaninyene 2016 (2)

Otu Akaninyene, Ebenso Bassey, Okuzu Okey, Osifo-Dawodu Egbe. (2016). Using a mHealth tutorial application to change knowledge and attitude of frontline health workers to Ebola virus disease in Nigeria: a before-and-after study. Human Resources for Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12960-016-0100-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same before-and-after survey analysis; Table 3 prints P = 0.001 for the transmission, symptoms and preventability questions, while other knowledge questions showed no significant change.

> "Further analysis showed an 11% improvement in average knowledge levels between pre- and post-intervention scores with statistically significant differences (P < 0.05) recorded for questions concerning the transmission of the Ebola virus among humans, common symptoms of Ebola fever and whether Ebola fever was preventable."
