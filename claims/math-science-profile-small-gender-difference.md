# Gender differences in the math–science achievement profile of top-performing math students are small, with both genders tilted toward mathematics

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · quant-synthesis `r2`–`r3` · `q4`

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` Math–science profile distributions of male and female top performers show only 18% nonoverlap, a small gender difference by the article's benchmarks. [→ Keller 2021](#keller-2021)
`q4 i?` 69% of male and 65% of female top performers score higher in mathematics than in science. [→ Keller 2021 (2)](#keller-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Keller 2021

Keller, L., Preckel, F., Eccles, J. S., & Brunner, M. (2021). Top-Performing Math Students in 82 Countries: An Integrative Data Analysis of Gender Differences in Achievement, Achievement Profiles, and Achievement Motivation. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/edu0000685

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analytic synthesis of country-specific nonoverlap for the math–science profile. The distributions "had a nonoverlap of 18%", which the article classifies as a small gender difference; no standardized effect size was printed.

> "Female and male students' math–science profile distributions had a nonoverlap of 18% (Table 5; see Figure 5B for the distribution of effect sizes), and thus, gender differences in the math–science profile were small."

### Keller 2021 (2)

Keller, L., Preckel, F., Eccles, J. S., & Brunner, M. (2021). Top-Performing Math Students in 82 Countries: An Integrative Data Analysis of Gender Differences in Achievement, Achievement Profiles, and Achievement Motivation. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/edu0000685

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analytic integration of tilt percentages. "69% demonstrated a math tiltM-S" among male top performers and 65% of female top performers scored higher in mathematics than in science, showing both groups tilted toward mathematics.

> "For 69% of all male students, mathematics was their strongest skill compared with science (i.e., 69% demonstrated a math tiltM-S). Similarly, 65% of all female students scored higher in mathematics than in science"
