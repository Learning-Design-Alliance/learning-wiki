## Evidence

### Westphal 2024

Westphal, A., Richter, E., Lazarides, R., & Huang, Y. (2024). More I-talk in student teachers’ written reflections indicates higher stress during VR teaching. Computers & Education, 212, 104987. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2024.104987

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional regressions found male student teachers used significantly more subjective (β = 0.28, p = .020) and objective (β = 0.23, p = .040) pronouns than female student teachers. However, the authors note that "gender differences in the use of subjective and objective first-person singular pronouns disappeared" when controlling for physiological stress.

> "Gender showed a statistically significant association with subjective and objective pronoun use, indicating that student teachers who identified as male used subjective and objective first-person singular pronouns more frequently than student teachers who identified as female."
