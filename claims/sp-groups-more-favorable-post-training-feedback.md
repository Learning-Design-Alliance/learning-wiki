# Trainees in both SP groups reported more favorable post-training feedback than TM trainees across multiple perceived outcomes

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` SSP-TCM and OSP-TCM trainees reported greater improvements than TM trainees in medical record quality, TCM thinking, communication skills, confidence, satisfaction, interest in TCM, and inquiry skills, with no significant SSP versus OSP differences on these items. [→ Zeng Jinhao 2023](#zeng-jinhao-2023)

## Evidence

### Zeng Jinhao 2023

Zeng Jinhao, Liang Shuang, Fu Xiaoxu, Guo Jing, Bai Yaolin, Zhou Shan, Du Quanyu, Wang Zhenxing, Zhang Xiyu, Peng Sihan, Wen Lijuan, Li Wenyuan, Li Bin, Yang Han, Zhang Yi. (2023). Student standardized patients versus occupational standardized patients for improving clinical competency among TCM medical students: a 3-year prospective randomized study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04198-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Anonymous post-training questionnaire (15 items, 4- or 5-point Likert) analyzed by chi-square across the three groups (n = 147). Significant Pa and Pb contrasts favored both SP groups on communication skills, confidence, satisfaction, interest, and inquiry skills; Pc values on these items were non-significant.

> "Trainees in both SSP-TCM and OSP-TCM groups reported that they achieved greater improvements relative to the TM group in “the quality of medical record” (χ2 = 10.584, Pa=0.032; χ2 = 10.04, Pb=0.04), “TCM thinking ability” (χ2 = 10.781, Pa=0.032; χ2 = 10.585, Pb=0.029)"
