# Students hesitated to share learning data except with their own teachers, while teachers insisted on data access for instruction and feared selective sharing by low-performing students would undermine the system's usefulness

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students wanted their data kept private but were willing to let their own teachers access it, and wanted transparency about what data are shared, with whom, and how they are used. [→ data-students](#data-students)
`q2 i?` Teachers wanted access to student data for effective instruction and did not want students deciding on data sharing, since only willing sharers would leave an overview. [→ data-teachers](#data-teachers)

## Evidence

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Student quote (S7, age 13, Data Control) from the Hesitation in Data Sharing theme; the article reports students had "a strong desire for keeping their own data private" yet recognized benefits of teacher access.

> "If you leave everything to yourself, then no one can help you [. . . ], but if you just share the biggest part with the teacher, then the teacher can also help you"

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Teacher quote (T5, 15 years of teaching, Data Control) from the data-sharing theme; teachers argued that with communication and transparency students would understand why sharing is essential.

> "I strongly assume that the low-performing students in particular don't want to share their data and that is not an overview for me if only the good ones share it or those who want to share it"
