## Evidence

### Janaka 2025

Janaka, N., Zhao, S., Ram, A., Sun, R., Tan, S. T. J. W., Li, D., & Hsu, D. (2025). Progressive Sentences: Combining the Benefits of Word and Sentence Learning. MobileHCI '25 Adjunct. [doi:10.1145/3737821.3749564](https://doi.org/10.1145/3737821.3749564)

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Study 1 (N=12) 2×2 within-subject design (Mobility × Word-Gap). ART-based repeated-measures ANOVA showed significant main and interaction effects of Word-Gap on Free-Recall; post-hoc Gap outperformed NoGap with d = 0.44.

> "Free-Recall also showed significant main and interaction effects of Word-Gap (𝐹1,33 = 5.62,𝑝 = 0.02;𝐹1,33 = 5.96,𝑝 = 0.02). Post-hoc results (Figure 2) revealed Gap (𝑀 = 4.9, 𝑆𝐷 = 2.0) outperformed NoGap (𝑀 = 4.3,𝑆𝐷 = 1.7;𝑝𝑏𝑜𝑛𝑓 < 0.05,𝑑 = 0.44)."

### Janaka 2025 (2)

Janaka, N., Zhao, S., Ram, A., Sun, R., Tan, S. T. J. W., Li, D., & Hsu, D. (2025). Progressive Sentences: Combining the Benefits of Word and Sentence Learning. MobileHCI '25 Adjunct. [doi:10.1145/3737821.3749564](https://doi.org/10.1145/3737821.3749564)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Study 1 (N=12) post-hoc comparison: during Walking, Gap (M = 4.7, SD = 2.2) significantly exceeded NoGap (M = 3.1, SD = 1.8); no such effect was found in Sitting. The gap benefit is specific to mobile multitasking.

> "In Walking, Gap (𝑀 = 4.7, 𝑆𝐷 = 2.2) significantly exceeded NoGap (𝑀 = 3.1, 𝑆𝐷 = 1.8); no such effect was found in Sitting."
