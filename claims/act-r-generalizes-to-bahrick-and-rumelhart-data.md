## Evidence

### Philip I. Pavlik Jr. 2005

Philip I. Pavlik Jr., John R. Anderson. (2005). Practice and Forgetting Effects on Vocabulary Memory: An Activation-Based Model of the Spacing Effect. Cognitive Science 29. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog0000_14

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Numerical fit of the extended ACT–R model to Bahrick's (1979; 1987 with Phelps) Spanish–English vocabulary data; the model captured the 30-day crossover, though it underestimated one anomalous six-session 30-day condition.

> "More important, the crossover interaction across the final 30-day retention period was captured by the model. The r2 was .926 with an adjusted RMSD of 0.060."

### Philip I. Pavlik Jr. 2005 (2)

Philip I. Pavlik Jr., John R. Anderson. (2005). Practice and Forgetting Effects on Vocabulary Memory: An Activation-Based Model of the Spacing Effect. Cognitive Science 29. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog0000_14

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Numerical fit to Rumelhart (1967, Experiment 1) continuous paired-associate data with 66 items, estimating only the two decay parameters; the authors take the good fit as support for the model across stimuli and timescales.

> "This fit captured the important effects in Fig. 5 (adjusted RMSD = 0.021, r2 = .927)."
