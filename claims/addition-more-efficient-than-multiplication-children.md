# Retrieval is faster and procedural execution more efficient on addition than on multiplication problems, and these operation effects persist across the primary school years

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Retrieval latencies were faster on addition than multiplication problems, F(1, 57) = 6.2, with no grade × operation interaction, and procedural efficiencies were higher on addition, F(1, 57) = 52.5. [→ Ineke Imbo · André Vandierendonck 2008](#ineke-imbo-andre-vandierendonck-2008)
`q3 i?` Procedural strategies were executed faster on addition than on multiplication problems in all grades, with the efficiency increase larger for multiplication between second and fourth grade. [→ Ineke Imbo · André Vandierendonck 2008 (2)](#ineke-imbo-andre-vandierendonck-2008-2)

## Evidence

### Ineke Imbo · André Vandierendonck 2008

Ineke Imbo · André Vandierendonck. (2008). Effects of problem size, operation, and working-memory span on simple-arithmetic strategies: differences between children and adults? Psychological Research (2008) 72:331–346. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00426-007-0112-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The repeated-measures ANOVA on correct retrieval RTs in 60 children showed the operation effect on retrieval efficiency did not change across age, indicating parallel development of addition and multiplication retrieval speed.

> "Retrieval was faster on addition problems than on multiplication problems, F(1, 57) = 6.2, MSe = 448058, but operation did not interact with grade. The age-relate d increase in retrieval eYciency thus runs parallel for addition and multiplication."

### Ineke Imbo · André Vandierendonck 2008 (2)

Ineke Imbo · André Vandierendonck. (2008). Effects of problem size, operation, and working-memory span on simple-arithmetic strategies: differences between children and adults? Psychological Research (2008) 72:331–346. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00426-007-0112-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The ANOVA on correct procedural RTs showed higher procedural efficiency on addition in all age groups; the increase in procedural efficiency was larger for multiplication than addition between second and fourth grade, F(1, 57) = 5.3.

> "The main eVect of operation was signiWcant, with higher procedural eYciencies on addition problems than on multiplication problems, F(1, 57) = 52.5, MSe = 3820986. Operation also interacted with grade, F(2, 57) = 3.0."
