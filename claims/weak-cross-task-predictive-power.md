## Evidence

### Inmaculada Márquez & Mario Treviño 2025

Inmaculada Márquez & Mario Treviño. (2025). Dissociable sensory, motor, and visuomotor predictive functions. npj Science of Learning 10:85. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00377-4

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Within-subject multivariate linear regression in 54 participants who completed all three tasks, predicting eight adaptation indices from eight predictors, with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction across 72 predictor–outcome combinations. Only one significant intercept emerged; the result "suggests weak cross-task pre- dictive power".

> "However, all predictor coefﬁcients were non-signiﬁcant (white bars), indicating that individual differences in detection performance and motor variability did not reliably explain var- iation in visuomotor adaptation."

### Inmaculada Márquez & Mario Treviño 2025 (2)

Inmaculada Márquez & Mario Treviño. (2025). Dissociable sensory, motor, and visuomotor predictive functions. npj Science of Learning 10:85. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00377-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pairwise correlations between matching detection and visuomotor discrepancy types in the same participants revealed "weak associations"; motor variability showed negligible correlation with visuomotor performance (average Pearson's r < ±0.01).

> "These analyses revealed weak associations, with average Pearson correlation coefﬁcients below ±0.15 (Supplementary Table 2)."
