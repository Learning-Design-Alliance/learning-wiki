# Neither content-specific nor generic support during feedback reception shows significant effects on feedback reception quality or subject-matter-related knowledge, but the evidence base is too small to interpret

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r?` · `q4`

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` Content-specific and generic support during feedback reception showed no significant effects on the quality of feedback reception or subject-matter-related knowledge, but degrees of freedom below four make these analyses non-interpretable. [→ Hornstein 2025](#hornstein-2025)

## Evidence

### Hornstein 2025

Hornstein, J., Keller, M. V., Greisel, M., Dresel, M., & Kollar, I. (2025). Enhancing the Peer-Feedback Process Through Instructional Support: A Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10017-3

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Meta-analysis of feedback reception support: on reception quality, 2 studies (k = 8), g = 0.14, p = 0.70; on knowledge, 2 studies (k = 6), g = 0.18, p = 0.60 (Table 4). The article states the number of studies "was too small to reasonably conduct a meta-analysis".

> "For RQ4c, we found no significant effects neither of content-specific support nor of generic support during feedback reception on the quality of feedback reception and on subject-matter-related knowledge."
