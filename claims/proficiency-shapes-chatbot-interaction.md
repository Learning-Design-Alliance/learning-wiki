### Steve Woollaston 2026

Preprint, accepted at the 1st Workshop on Transition Network Analysis (TNA) at LAK '26. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14575 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.14575](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2607.14575)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Chi-squared test of independence on behavioural state frequencies by proficiency group (High n = 37,722 events; Low n = 25,606), visualised in a mosaic plot of standardised residuals; the test "yielded a 𝜒2 value of 25.4 (𝑝 < .003)".

> "High-proficiency learners displayed a distinct over-representation in conversational states. Specifically, user_chat showed a positive association (residual between 2 and 4), indicating these learners engaged in significantly more dialogue than expected."

### Steve Woollaston 2026 (2)

Steve Woollaston, Brendan Flanagan, Yuko Toyokawa and Hiroaki Ogata. (2026). Penny: Transition Network Analysis of Learner-Chatbot Interactions in Scaffolded EFL Writing. Preprint, accepted at the 1st Workshop on Transition Network Analysis (TNA) at LAK '26. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14575

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same chi-squared mosaic-plot analysis, low-proficiency group pattern: significant over-representation of check_button and under-representation of user_chat (residual between −2 and −4), described as "a higher dependency on general feedback".

> "This group showed a statistically significant over-representation for check_button (residual between 2 and 4), indicating a higher dependency on general feedback."
