Evidence¶

Christopher Nikulin 2019¶

Christopher Nikulin, Gabriela Lopez, Eduardo Piñonez, Luis Gonzalez, Pia Zapata. (2019). NASA-TLX for predictability and measurability of instructional design models: case study in design methods. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-019-09657-4

q2 · i? · design · r2

Quasi-experimental mixed-methods case study in which four young designers completed an 11-module design process; NASA-TLX questionnaires were administered per activity, and the authors report "The overall average of the perceived workload was between 50 and 75%". No effect size is printed.

"The overall average of the perceived workload was between 50 and 75% (normal to high workload; see Fig. 2)."

Related Claims¶

  • Module VI (conceptual design) showed the highest perceived workload, attributed to sketch-based conceptualization with extensive trial and error — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • NASA-TLX dimensions varied by design phase: frustration and mental demand rose considerably in the initial modules, and mental demand was lower during the final manufacturing steps — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • NASA-TLX workload dimensions were significantly intercorrelated, including mental demand with effort (r = 0.768) and performance negatively with frustration (r = −0.443) — related
  • The Bloom classification UI shows low perceived workload and high usability in a 50-participant user study — related
  • AI assistance reduces subjective mental effort across all tasks even when it does not reduce completion time, dissociating time and effort — related
  • The AI+Robot condition is associated with lower perceived workload than AI-Only on effort, frustration, temporal demand, physical demand, and Raw TLX — related
  • Teacher perceived workload did not differ significantly across conditions on any NASA-TLX subscale, though the experimental condition was descriptively lower on most subscales — related
  • Personalized paths reduced self-reported cognitive load on all six adapted NASA-TLX dimensions with large effect sizes — related
