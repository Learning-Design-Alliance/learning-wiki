The review reports, citing Roberts et al. (2013), that high-fidelity team simulations "significantly improved both team performance and patient outcomes in critical care settings". No effect size or test statistic is printed.

> "Roberts et al. (2013) found that high-fidelity team simulations significantly improved both team performance and patient outcomes in critical care settings"

### Shahrezaei Aidin; Sohani Maryam; Taherkhani Soroush; Zarghami Seyed Yahya 2024 (2)

Shahrezaei Aidin; Sohani Maryam; Taherkhani Soroush; Zarghami Seyed Yahya. (2024). The impact of surgical simulation and training technologies on general surgery education. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06299-w

> "Current evidence predominantly highlights intermediate outcomes—such as error reduction, procedural efficiency, and improved technical skills—but further research, particularly large-scale and longitudinal studies, is required"
