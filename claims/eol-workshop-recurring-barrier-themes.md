## Evidence

### Brezis Mayer 2017

Brezis Mayer, Lahat Yael, Frankel Meir, Rubinov Alan, Bohm Davina, Cohen Matan J, Koslowsky Meni, Shalomson Orit, Sprung Charles L, Perry-Mezare Henia, Yahalom Rina, Ziv Amitai. (2017). What can we learn from simulation-based training to improve skills for end-of-life care? Insights from a national project in Israel. Israel Journal of Health Policy Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13584-017-0169-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative thematic analysis of group discussions following simulations and of pre/post questionnaires across 100 workshops, analyzed with a "connecting strategy" for deliberative-dialogue data under a participatory inquiry paradigm. Themes included lack of training, lack of knowledge about the law and palliative care, perceived overtreatment, dilemmas and uncertainty, lack of time, lack of hope, and poor teamwork culture. No effect size applies.

> "The following themes recurred throughout discussions and questionnaires at every workshop."
