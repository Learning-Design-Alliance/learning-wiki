## Evidence

### Claussen AH 2018

Claussen AH, Bitsko RH, Holbrook JR, Bloomfield J, Giordano K. (2018). Impact of Tourette Syndrome on School Measures in a Nationally Representative Sample. Journal of Developmental & Behavioral Pediatrics. https://doi.org/10.1097/DBP.0000000000000550

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Weighted prevalence-ratio analyses of the pooled nationally representative survey sample compared children currently with TS to children who never had TS on school measures. Parent contact about school problems (fully adjusted PR 1.4, p = 0.07) and homework non-completion (PR 1.0, ns) did not remain significant after adjusting for co-occurring MEB or learning/language conditions.

> "After adjusting for demographics, compared with children without TS, children currently with TS were more likely to have an IEP, have a parent contacted about school problems, and not complete homework. After further adjusting for co-occurring conditions, only IEP status remained statistically significant."
