# Students are significantly worse at translating two-step algebra story problems than matched one-step problems, implicating a hidden integrative knowledge component

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students were significantly worse at translating two-step algebra story problems (like 800-40x) than closely matched one-step problems, and instruction targeting the hypothesized integrative KC improved performance. [→ Kenneth R. Koedinger 2010](#kenneth-r-koedinger-2010)

## Evidence

### Kenneth R. Koedinger 2010

Kenneth R. Koedinger, Albert T. Corbett, Charles Perfetti. (2010). The Knowledge-Learning-Instruction (KLI) Framework: Toward Bridging the Science-Practice Chasm to Enhance Robust Student Learning. Carnegie Mellon University Technical Report. http://learnlab.org/research/wiki

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article reports, citing Heffernan and Koedinger (1997), a performance difference between two-step and matched one-step algebra translation tasks, attributed to a missing integrative KC (an algebra grammar rule for composing expressions). It adds that instruction targeting this hidden KC 'significantly improved student performance' (Koedinger & McLaughlin, in press).

> "Heffernan and Koedinger (1997) found students were significantly worse at translating two-step algebra story problems into expressions (like 800-40x) than they were at translating two closely matched one-step problems"
