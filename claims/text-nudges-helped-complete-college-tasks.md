# Most surveyed text-message recipients reported the nudges helped them complete college tasks

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among survey respondents, majorities reported the messages got them to complete a task they hadn't yet done (86%), informed them of needed tasks (86%), helped them manage time better (75%), and made summer tasks less overwhelming (70%). [→ Arnold 2015](#arnold-2015)

## Evidence

### Arnold 2015

Arnold, Karen D.; Chewning, Alexandra; Castleman, Benjamin; Page, Lindsay C. (2015). Advisor and Student Experiences of Summer Support for College-Intending, Low-Income High School Graduates. Journal of College Access. http://scholarworks.wmich.edu/jca/vol1/iss1/3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

A mobile-phone survey eight months after the campaign asked treatment students about message effects; 41 of 150 sampled students responded. Percentages are self-reported agreement, not tested effects.

> "Got me to complete a task I hadn't yet done (86%); Informed me about something that I hadn't realized I needed to do (86%) Helped me manage my time better, like planning better or meeting my college deadlines, or not procrastinating as much (75%)"
