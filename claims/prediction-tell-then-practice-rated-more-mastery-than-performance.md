# Students rate prediction and tell-then-practice activities as more helpful for mastery than for exam performance, while worked examples are rated similarly for both

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Prediction activities were rated more helpful for mastery than performance (OR = 1.54), as were tell-then-practice activities (OR = 1.82); rating type did not predict worked-example helpfulness (p = 0.120). [→ Walck-Shannon 2025](#walck-shannon-2025)

## Evidence

### Walck-Shannon 2025

Walck-Shannon, E. M., Barton, H. D., Rowell, S. F., Chalker, D. L., & Fink, A. (2025). Students Don't Learn the Way They Think They Do in a Large, Active-Learning Genetics Course. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.24-10-0251

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Three post-hoc ordinal regressions comparing mastery and performance ratings within each activity type. "the odds of rating the activities as more helpful for mastery was 1.54 times the odds of rating them as more helpful for performance" for prediction; tell-then-practice OR = 1.82 (χ²(1) = 16.76, p < 0.001); worked examples showed no significant difference (χ²(1) = 2.42, p = 0.120).

> "the odds of rating the activities as more helpful for mastery was 1.54 times the odds of rating them as more helpful for performance, 95% CI [1.14–2.07]. Similarly, the odds of rating tell-then-practice activities as more helpful for mastery was 1.82 times the odds of rating them as more helpful for performance"
