## Evidence

### Michael Vignal 2023

Michael Vignal, Gayle Geschwind, Benjamin Pollard, Rachel Henderson, Marcos D. Caballero, H. J. Lewandowski. (2023). Survey of physics reasoning on uncertainty concepts in experiments: an assessment of measurement uncertainty for introductory physics labs. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020139](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020139)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Beta 2 was administered to students in one course, with items presented primarily in open-response format specifically to verify the reasonableness of planned MC distractors. The authors report the beta "strongly indicated that our previously identified distractors covered the most frequent incorrect answers provided by students."

> "the responses gathered strongly indicated that our previously identified distractors covered the most frequent incorrect answers provided by students, with only a few new distractors being identified through this beta."

### Michael Vignal 2023 (2)

Michael Vignal, Gayle Geschwind, Benjamin Pollard, Rachel Henderson, Marcos D. Caballero, H. J. Lewandowski. (2023). Survey of physics reasoning on uncertainty concepts in experiments: an assessment of measurement uncertainty for introductory physics labs. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020139](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020139)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Beta 1 collected responses from students in eight courses at eight different institutions. Item 4.4 was presented in NOR format despite being designed as MC. The histogram of responses (Fig. 3) showed "peaks at values that corresponded to our planned answer options" with no unanticipated attractive distractors, informing beta 2's design.

> "The distribution of student responses had peaks at values that corresponded to our planned answer options and, critically, there were no unexpected peaks indicating an attractive distractor that we had not anticipated."
