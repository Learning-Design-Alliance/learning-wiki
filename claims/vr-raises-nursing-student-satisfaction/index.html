Evidence¶

Liu Kai 2023¶

Liu Kai, Zhang Weiwei, Li Wei, Wang Ting, Zheng Yanxue. (2023). Effectiveness of virtual reality in nursing education: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04662-x

q3 · i3 · quant-synthesis · r2

Fixed-effects meta-analysis of four randomized controlled trials measuring learning satisfaction; heterogeneity was low (P = 0.36, I2 = 7%). The pooled "SMD = 1.14" favoured VR over the control teaching modality.

"The combined results showed a significant difference in the improvement of satisfaction with nursing education with VR technology compared to the control teaching modality (SMD = 1.14, 95% CI [0.85, 1.43], P < 0.001, Fig. 7)."

Related Claims¶

  • VR instruction shows no statistically significant advantage for nursing students' critical thinking (SMD = 0.79, p = 0.07) — related
  • VR instruction shows a statistically significant difference in nursing students' practical skills versus traditional methods, though the pooled estimate is small (SMD = 0.15) — related
  • Review syntheses: VR/AR most often improved academic achievement and motivation, but sometimes performed no better than conventional technologies — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Digital clinical skills teaching produces higher student satisfaction and engagement across multiple modalities, despite mixed efficacy for skills — related
  • VR instruction improves nursing students' theoretical knowledge more than traditional teaching methods (SMD = 0.97) — related
