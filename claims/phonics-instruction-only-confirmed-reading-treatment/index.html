Evidence¶

Galuschka Katharina 2014¶

Galuschka Katharina, Ise Elena, Krick Kathrin, Schulte-Körne Gerd. (2014). Effectiveness of Treatment Approaches for Children and Adolescents with Reading Disabilities: A Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0089900

q3 · i1 · quant-synthesis · r2

Random-effects meta-analysis of 29 phonics instruction comparisons from randomized controlled trials in children and adolescents with reading disabilities. The pooled Hedges g effect size was g = 0.322 with a 95% CI of [0.177, 0.467] and zero heterogeneity, described as "a small but statistically significant effect".

"The mean effect size for phonics instruction was g' = 0.322 (95% CI [0.177, 0.467]; I 2 = 0). This suggests a small but statistically significant effect of phonics instructions on reading performance."

Related Claims¶

  • Phonics instruction has a small but statistically confirmed effect for children with reading disabilities, while Orton-Gillingham structured-literacy programs show positive but non-significant effects — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • After correcting for publication bias, the effect of phonics instruction on reading performance remains small but statistically significant — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Prior reviews report positive word-reading intervention effects for adolescents but with a wide range (0-1.31) due to broad outcome conceptualization — related
  • No significant differences emerged between word-reading intervention approaches, though whole word recognition showed trends toward null effects — related
  • Children and adolescents with mild reading disabilities show more improvement in literacy skills than more severely impaired participants — related
