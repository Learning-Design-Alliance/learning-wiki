# DaisyQuest matched teacher-delivered phonological awareness instruction but beat other instructional software in low-progress readers

## Evidence

### DaisyQuest 2006

DaisyQuest. (2006). WWC Intervention Report: DaisyQuest. What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Randomized trial of 69 low-progress readers in three conditions. The report states "no statistical differences on the total test score" versus teacher-led instruction; WWC computed a negative, non-significant but substantively important effect favoring the teacher group.

> "In the comparison between the DaisyQuest group and the teacher-delivered phonological awareness group, the authors found no statistical differences on the total test score."

### DaisyQuest 2006 (2)

DaisyQuest. (2006). WWC Intervention Report: DaisyQuest. What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same trial's second comparison against mathematics and drawing software controls. Statistically significant positive effects on the Phonological Awareness Test total score favoring DaisyQuest were found by authors and confirmed by WWC.

> "In the comparison of DaisyQuest students and stu- dents using the other instructional technology, the study authors found, and the WWC conﬁrmed, statistically signiﬁcant positive effects on the total test score for the DaisyQuest group."
