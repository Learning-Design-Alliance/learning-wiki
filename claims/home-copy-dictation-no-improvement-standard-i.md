# Twelve weeks of copy-and-home-study spelling practice with next-day dictation produced no improvement in Standard I boys' ability to acquire new spellings

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The class average on 30-word dictations was 17.0 at the start of the twelve-week series and 17.0 at the end, with 30 per cent of the class showing no improvement anywhere along the series. [→ Winch 1913](#winch-1913)

## Evidence

### Winch 1913

Winch, W. H. (1913). Experimental researches on learning to spell. Part II. *Journal of Educational Psychology, 4*(10), 579–592. [doi:10.1037/h0076021](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0076021)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Subsidiary experiment with Standard I boys (average age 7): six words copied from the blackboard daily for home study, dictated next morning, for twelve weeks (August 26th to November 15th), about seventy minutes of school time weekly. The author reports "no improvement at all" in the class as a whole and no transfer to learning later sets more readily.

> "Of the first 30 words, dictated on September 2nd, an average of 17.0 was spelled correctly; of the last 30 words, dictated on November 18th, the average spelt correctly was 17.0."
