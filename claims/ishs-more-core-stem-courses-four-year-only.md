# ISHS attendance is associated with more Core STEM courses completed at four-year colleges but not with STEM GPA or total courses, and shows no course-taking advantage at two-year colleges

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Four-year college entrants from ISHSs completed more Core STEM courses between fall 2014 and spring 2016 (fixed effect 2.59, p = .004), with no significant effect on total courses or Core STEM GPA. [→ Means 2018](#means-2018)
`q2 i?` Two-year college entrants showed no significant ISHS difference in Core STEM courses, total courses, or STEM GPA. [→ Means 2018](#means-2018)

## Evidence

### Means 2018

Means, Wang, Wei, Iwatani, & Peters. (2018). Broadening Participation in STEM College Majors: Effects of Attending a STEM-Focused High School. AERA Open. https://doi.org/10.1177/2332858418806305

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Propensity score-weighted HLM of Texas course-taking records for 317 ISHS and 565 comparison four-year enrollees and 276/703 two-year enrollees. The printed Core STEM course fixed effect is 2.59 (p = .004); GPA p = .065 and total courses p = .099 are not significant, and no effect sizes are printed.

> "Among students who go to four-year colleges, there is a positive advantage related to ISHS attendance in terms of the number of Core STEm courses completed between fall 2014 and spring 2016. There was no significant ISHS effect on total number of courses completed in four-year colleges or on grades in Core STEm courses."
