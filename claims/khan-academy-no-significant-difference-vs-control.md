# Khan Academy use as supplemental mathematics instruction produced no statistically significant difference in post-assessment scores compared to a combined control group

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Seventh graders who used Khan Academy for a minimum of 30 minutes per class over four weeks scored no differently on a 21-item standardized assessment than students in combined math and ELA enrichment control classes. [→ Daniel P. Kelly and Teomara Rutherford 2017](#daniel-p-kelly-and-teomara-rutherford-2017)

## Evidence

### Daniel P. Kelly and Teomara Rutherford 2017

Daniel P. Kelly and Teomara Rutherford. (2017). Khan Academy as Supplemental Instruction: A Controlled Study of a Computer-Based Mathematics Intervention. International Review of Research in Open and Distributed Learning, 18(4). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1146230

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-test only control-group quasi-experiment in a large suburban North Carolina charter school with 114 seventh graders; treatment (n=39) used Khan Academy, controls (n=75) attended math or ELA enrichment. The article reports "unremarkable differences" with treatment M = 73.75 versus control M = 72.22; the reported p = .596 indicates no significant difference.

> "Analysis revealed unremarkable differences in mean post-assessment scores between the combined math and ELA supplement control (M = 72.22, SD = 14.750) and treatment (M = 73.75, SD = 14.280) groups."
