# Relearning an old response pattern generally beats learning a new one in the Post phase, except under severe 25% punishment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Experiment 1 (50% fixed win rate), Post phase win rates were significantly higher for relearning old than learning new. [→ Zhang 2026](#zhang-2026)
`q3 i?` In Experiment 2 (25% fixed win rate), Post phase win rates did not differ between relearning-old and learning-new conditions, eliminating the relearning-old advantage. [→ Zhang 2026 (2)](#zhang-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Zhang 2026

Zhang, Y., & Dyson, B. J. (2026). Recover or discover: How outcome feedback shapes response patterns within game spaces. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00414-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-participants t-test in Experiment 1 (n = 76 analysed) comparing Post phase win rates after a 50% fixed-win-rate During phase. Relearning old (66.16%) exceeded learning new (61.39%), "t(75) = 3.25, p = .002"; no effect size printed.

> "However, in the Post phase, relearning old win rates (66.16%) were significantly higher than learning new win rates (61.39%) as assessed via a within-participants t-test (t(75) = 3.25, p = .002)."

### Zhang 2026 (2)

Zhang, Y., & Dyson, B. J. (2026). Recover or discover: How outcome feedback shapes response patterns within game spaces. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00414-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 (n = 72 analysed) with the During win rate fixed at 25%: the Post phase relearning-old advantage was "eliminated in Experiment 2 as a result of increasing the punishment rate to 25%"; equivalence was not tested.

> "Post phase win rates did not differ between relearning-old and learning-new conditions (66.05% vs. 68.25%, respectively: t[71] = -1.032, p = .306)."
