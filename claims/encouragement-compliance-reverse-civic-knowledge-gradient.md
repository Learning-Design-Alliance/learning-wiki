## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Both verbal and monetary encouragement increased the probability of conducting a topic-related search, with significant negative interactions with civic knowledge across all topics but no significant interactions with educational attainment. [→ Ulloa 2026](#ulloa-2026)

## Evidence

### Ulloa 2026

Ulloa, Roberto; Leonard Tiedemann; Selb, Peter; Kacperski, Celina. (2026). The Knowledge Gap in a High-Choice Media Environment: Experimental Evidence from Online Search. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2605.21019](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.21019)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Logistic regressions of binary information search on treatment and moderators (Table 3, tracked participants). Main treatment coefficients were positive and significant (e.g., verbal encouragement 1.86*** for child support); civic-knowledge interactions were negative across all topics.

> "We find statistically significant interactions of both conditions with civic knowledge across all policy topics, but not with educational attainment. All the significant coefficients are negative, i.e., the treatment effect is higher for individuals with lower civic knowledge"
