## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Less-experienced trainees performed significantly better after studying worked examples than after problem solving at Stage 2 (F(1, 22) = 5.85, p < .05); at Stage 1 the means favoured worked examples but the difference was not significant. [→ Kalyuga 2001](#kalyuga-2001)

## Evidence

### Kalyuga 2001

Kalyuga, S., Chandler, P., Tuovinen, J., & Sweller, J. (2001). When Problem Solving Is Superior to Studying Worked Examples. Journal of Educational Psychology, 93(3), 579-588. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.93.3.579

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1, with separate 2 (instructional procedure) x 3 (stages) ANOVAs for each measure, had 24 trade apprentices randomly allocated to worked examples or problem solving groups of 12. Simple effects showed "the worked examples group performed significantly better than the problem solving group" at Stage 2.

> "there was a significant effect for Stage 2, F(1, 22) 5.85, MSE 13.79, p < .05 (the worked examples group performed significantly better than the problem solving group)"
