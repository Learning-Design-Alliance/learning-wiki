## Evidence

### Hettiarachchi Manjula 2014

Hettiarachchi Manjula, Lakmal Fonseka Chathuranga, Gunasekara Priyanka, Jayasinghe Prasanjanie, Maduranga Dasun. (2014). How does the quality of life and the underlying biochemical indicators correlate with the performance in academic examinations in a group of medical students of Sri Lanka? Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.3402/meo.v19.22772

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis of serum biochemical parameters against QoL score in the 78-student cohort before examination (Table 2, one-tailed p values). After the exam only TSH remained marginally significant (r=−0.190; p=0.05); cortisol correlations were non-significant at both times.

> "Both serum TSH (r=−0.194; p=0.04) and cortisol (r=−0.012; p=0.46) had negative correlations before examination. Further, SF (r= 0.212; p=0.03) had a significant correlation with baseline QoL."
