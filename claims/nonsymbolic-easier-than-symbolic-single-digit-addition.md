## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Addition performance showed a significant main effect of stimulus type, with nonsymbolic conditions outperforming symbolic ones. [→ Xenidou-Dervou 2014](#xenidou-dervou-2014)

## Evidence

### Xenidou-Dervou 2014

Xenidou-Dervou, I., van der Schoot, M., & van Lieshout, E. C. D. M. (2015). Working memory and number line representations in single-digit addition: Approximate versus exact, nonsymbolic versus symbolic. *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology, 68*(6), 1148–1167. [doi:10.1080/17470218.2014.977303](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470218.2014.977303)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

A 2 x 2 repeated measures ANOVA with 103 kindergarteners (Mage = 5.98) compared four addition formats. The stimulus-type main effect was significant, "F(1, 102) = 20.30, p <.001, partial η2 = .17"; nonsymbolic M = 59.93% vs symbolic M = 49.55%.

> "Results showed a significant main effect for stimulus type, F(1, 102) = 20.30, p <.001, partial η2 = .17, on addition performance."
