# Radiologists in training achieve substantially higher diagnostic accuracy than laypeople on both short-presentation (2s) and long-presentation (10s) radiograph trials

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Radiologists in training scored significantly higher than laypeople on diagnostic accuracy in both trial lengths, with a large expertise main effect. [→ Kok Ellen M. 2021](#kok-ellen-m-2021)

## Evidence

### Kok Ellen M. 2021

Kok Ellen M., Sorger Bettina, van Geel Koos, Gegenfurtner Andreas, van Merriënboer Jeroen J. G., Robben Simon G. F., de Bruin Anique B. H. (2021). Holistic processing only? The role of the right fusiform face area in radiological expertise. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0256849

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Mixed 2×2 ANOVA on diagnostic performance (percentage correct) in an fMRI study of 11 laypeople and 17 radiologists in training diagnosing 66 radiographs. The expertise main effect was large: "F(1,25) = 142.12, p <. 0001, η2p = 0.85". Laypeople scored near chance (53.8% short, 57.3% long); residents 80.5% and 84.2%.

> "A main effect of expertise level was found, with all radiologists in training scoring higher than all laypeople, F(1,25) = 142.12, p <. 0001, η2p = 0.85."
