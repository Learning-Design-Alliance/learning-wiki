# Recurring movements between professional (in-role) and educational (out-of-role) contexts during simulation are important for simulator training to fulfil its pedagogical potential

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Conversations during simulations have multiple layers of meaning because of roleplay, and shifts between the professional realm and the educational context are central to simulation pedagogy, as when instructors shift from roleplaying a captain to clarifying corrections as the course instructor. Authors' analytical conclusion (type e) drawn from the episode analyses, noting that in-scenario corrections are delivered as roleplay first and then clarified through explicit instructor role shifts when students show no displays of understanding the correction.

> "A conclusion from the analysis is that the recurring movements between these activity contexts—the professional (in-role) and the educational (out-of-role)—is important for simulation training to fulfill its pedagogical potential of training students how to think, perform, and act as professionals."
