Evidence¶

Ana M. Giménez Gualdo 2014¶

Ana M. Giménez Gualdo, Simon C. Hunter, Kevin Durkin, Pilar Arnaiz, and Javier J. Maquilón. (2014). The emotional impact of cyberbullying: Differences in perceptions and experiences as a function of role. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2014.11.013

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive analysis of individual impact items (Table 2) from the survey, rated on five-point scales from "1 = never" to "5 = always"; the article reports cyberbullies' top expected emotion was "scared" while cybervictims' most intense reported emotion was "sad".

"the most common emotional reaction that was expected of victims by cyberbullies was “scared”, followed by “offended”. In contrast, cybervictims actually reported that the most intense emotion was “sad”, followed by “rejected.”"
