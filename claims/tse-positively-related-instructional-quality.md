# Teachers with higher self-efficacy are rated by students as showing better classroom management, more cognitive activation, and greater support

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` At the teacher (class) level, teacher self-efficacy is positively associated with student-rated classroom management, cognitive activation, and supportive climate. [→ Burić 2020](#buric-2020)

## Evidence

### Burić 2020

Burić, Irena and Kim, Lisa (2020) Teacher self-efficacy, instructional quality, and student motivational beliefs: An analysis using multilevel structural equation modeling. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2019.101302

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Doubly latent multilevel SEM at the teacher level with 94 teachers and their 2087 students; standardized effects of teacher self-reported TSE on the three student-rated instructional quality dimensions were β = .255, .261, and .234, all significant. The study reports teachers "rated by students as managing classrooms better" with higher TSE.

> "teachers with higher levels of TSE were, on average, rated by students as managing classrooms better (β = .255, p = .041), providing more cognitive activation (β = .261, p = .033), and showing greater support (β = .234, p = .035)."
