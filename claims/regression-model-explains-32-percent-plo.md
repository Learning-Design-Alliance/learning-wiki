## Evidence

### Torbergsen HKS 2025

Torbergsen HKS, Paulsby TE, Haugan G, Utvær BK. (2025). Students’ motivation, study-effort and perceptions of teachers’ goals when engaging in a learning design within the flipped classroom. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07729-z

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Stepwise linear regression on data from 351 nursing students, with activities entered in design order plus PTG, study-effort, gender and study year. The article reports "the regression model explains 32% of the variation in students’ perceived learning outcomes"; no standardized effect size is printed for the model.

> "In sum, the regression model explains 32% of the variation in students’ perceived learning outcomes. The factors that are significantly related to perceived learning outcomes throughout the model are the pre-class activity multiple-choice questions, the in-class activity simulation, perceptions of teachers’ goals, the study-effort, and the students’ year of study."

### Torbergsen HKS 2025 (2)

Torbergsen HKS, Paulsby TE, Haugan G, Utvær BK. (2025). Students’ motivation, study-effort and perceptions of teachers’ goals when engaging in a learning design within the flipped classroom. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07729-z

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The article reports that perceptions of teachers’ goals and study-effort showed "the strongest associations (β = 27 and β = 21, respectively)" in the model explaining 32% of variance; the beta values are reported without confidence intervals or effect-size interpretation.

> "The regression model explained 32% of the variance in perceived learning outcomes, with students’ perceptions of teachers’ goals and study-effort showing the strongest associations (β = 27 and β = 21, respectively)."
