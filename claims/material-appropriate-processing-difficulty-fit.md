# Whether a difficulty benefits memory depends on whether it exercises processes not already supported by the material (material-appropriate processing)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Per McDaniel and Einstein's material-appropriate processing framework, a difficulty's impact on comprehension and memory depends on whether it exercises beneficial processes not already supported by the material. [→ Bjork 2016](#bjork-2016)

## Evidence

### Bjork 2016

Bjork, R. A., & Yue, C. L. (2016). Commentary: Is disfluency desirable? Metacognition Learning, 11, 133–137. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-016-9156-8

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The commentary reports, citing McDaniel and Einstein (2005), the material-appropriate processing framework. It gives the example that re-arranging sentences enhances performance for expository text but not for material with familiar organization, whereas letter deletion shows the opposite pattern.

> "how a certain difficulty impacts comprehension and later memory performance can depend on whether that difficulty exercises beneficial processes that are not already supported by characteristics of the material to be learned."
