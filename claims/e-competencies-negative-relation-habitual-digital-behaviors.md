## Evidence

### Ana Cebollero-Salinas 2022

Cebollero-Salinas, A., Cano-Escoriaza, J., & Orejudo, S. (2022). Are emotional e-competencies a protective factor against habitual digital behaviors (media multitasking, cybergossip, phubbing) in Spanish students of secondary education? *Computers & Education, 181*, 104464. [doi:10.1016/j.compedu.2022.104464](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2022.104464)

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis of cross-sectional survey data from n = 776 secondary students (12–16), comparing the five e-competency scores against the habitual digital behaviors factor. Printed correlations (Table 3) include e-self-control r = -.556 (boys) and -.532 (girls); social e-competency is r = .178 (boys), .111 (girls, not significant), .148 and .155 (age groups); emotional e-awareness ranges from -.115 to .051, none significant. The authors note that the transversal design does not allow causal conclusions, and describe "social e-competency, which is associated with a greater prevalence of habitual digital behaviors".

> "All of these relations are negative, i.e., the greater the degree of competency, the less habitual digital behaviors appear, except in the case of social e-competency, which is associated with a greater prevalence of habitual digital behaviors."

### Ana Cebollero-Salinas 2022 (2)

Cebollero-Salinas, A., Cano-Escoriaza, J., & Orejudo, S. (2022). Are emotional e-competencies a protective factor against habitual digital behaviors (media multitasking, cybergossip, phubbing) in Spanish students of secondary education? *Computers & Education, 181*, 104464. [doi:10.1016/j.compedu.2022.104464](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2022.104464)

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Group comparisons using Fisher's Z transformation of correlations, n = 776. The gender difference in the e-regulation correlation is significant (Z = 13.72, p < .001), as is the age difference for e-independence (Z = 2.57, p = .01), with "emotional e-independence" more pronounced in older students.

> "Thus the correlation between emotional e-regulation and “habitual digital behaviors” is lower in boys (r = −0.332) than in the case of girls (r = −0.566), and the relation between emotional e-independence and “habitual digital behaviors” is more pronounced in older students (r = −0.485) than in younger ones (r = −0.331)."

The minus signs are as printed in Table 3 (they are lost in the extracted text of the running prose). All four correlations are negative, so "lower in boys" means a weaker negative association.
