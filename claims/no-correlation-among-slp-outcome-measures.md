# Checklist scores, task completion speed, and confidence change show no significant bivariate correlations in a simulated lumbar puncture workshop cohort

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` None of the pairwise correlations between checklist score, speed, and confidence change were statistically significant for the cohort, the Peyton's subgroup, or the "see one, do one" subgroup. [→ Dugan L 2025](#dugan-l-2025)

## Evidence

### Dugan L 2025

Dugan L, Rei KM, Ueno A, Mahutga J, Varma M. (2025). Assessing Best Practices in a Simulated Lumbar Puncture Workshop With Medical Students. The Permanente Journal. https://doi.org/10.7812/TPP/24.155

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pearson correlation analysis across the full cohort (N = 35) and both teaching-method subgroups. The quoted sentence reports the checklist–speed correlations; the article similarly reports no significant confidence–checklist or confidence–speed correlations (largest r = 0.41, P = .082).

> "Checklist scores were not correlated with speed for the cohort (r = −0.13, P = .473), the Peyton's subgroup (r = 0.02, P = .945), or the "see one, do one" subgroup (r = −0.10, P = .689)."
