Evidence¶

Bamberg 2025¶

Bamberg, C., Weigelt, S. & Hagelweide, K. (2025). Reversal learning is influenced by cognitive flexibility and develops throughout early adolescence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00308-3

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Cross-sectional regression in the analysed sample of N = 142 (ages 10–22) shows the age effect: "For an increase in age by one year, the estimated improvement in RL performance is 0.01 units"; a 17-year-old is estimated 0.07 units higher than a 10-year-old.

"For an increase in age by one year, the estimated improvement in RL performance is 0.01 units (SE = 0.003, 95% CI = [0.01, 0.02], p < 0.001; Fig. 1)."

Bamberg 2025 (2)¶

Bamberg, C., Weigelt, S. & Hagelweide, K. (2025). Reversal learning is influenced by cognitive flexibility and develops throughout early adolescence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00308-3

q2 · i? · associational · r2

A regression spline model with knots at 14 and 18 years fitted the age-RL relation; the early-adolescent slope "is 0.05", indicating the relationship is strongest between 10 and 14 years.

"For early-adolescents (between the y-intercept and the ﬁrst knot at 14 years), the estimated slope is 0.05 (see Supplementary Table 3); for mid-adolescence it is −0.002; and for adults 0.01."
