## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students in a reformed undergraduate biology course reported expecting knowledge to be narrowly stated facts from authority that need not be understood in a larger context, and this expectation influenced how they approached exams and studying. [→ Hall 2011](#hall-2011)

## Evidence

### Hall 2011

Hall, K. L., Watkins, J. E., Coffey, J. E., Cooke, T. J., & Redish, E. F. (2011). Examining the Impact of Student Expectations on Undergraduate Biology Education Reform. AERA 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1105.6349](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1105.6349)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative analysis of videotaped interviews with students enrolled in the reformed Organismal Biology course; vignettes from Patrick, Joe, and London illustrate the authority-based expectation. Students reported accepting professor claims as "true" and hunting for "key words" on exams rather than reasoning about the biology.

> "A number of students in our interview data corpus made statements indicating that they thought the class was about particular facts, and some reported explicitly on ways that this expectation affected their behavior in the class."
