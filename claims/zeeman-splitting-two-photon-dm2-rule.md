# Applying a magnetic field to the rubidium cell produces Zeeman splitting of the two-photon transitions — unsplit through Fe = 1, two lines through Fe = 2, three lines through Fe = 3 — illustrating the Δm = 2 selection rule

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` With a permanent magnet near the cell, the 87Rb Fg = 1 → 5D5/2 transition through Fe = 1 is not split, through Fe = 2 splits into two lines, and through Fe = 3 splits into three lines, illustrating Δm = 2. [→ V Jacques 2009](#v-jacques-2009)

## Evidence

### V Jacques 2009

V Jacques, B Hingant, A Allafort, M Pigeard and J F Roch. (2009). Non-linear spectroscopy of rubidium: An undergraduate experiment. https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3115 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0902.3115](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0902.3115)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

Final undergraduate experiment: a permanent magnet is placed close to the vapor cell and the Zeeman-split two-photon fluorescence spectrum of 87Rb is recorded (figure 8). The splitting pattern of transitions A–F illustrates the Δm = 2 selection rule.

> "The transition through Fe = 1 is not split, the one through Fe = 2 is split into two lines and the one through Fe = 3 is split into three lines. Such a result is an illustration of the transition selection rule ∆ m = 2 (see Fig. 8)."
