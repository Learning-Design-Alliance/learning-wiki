## Evidence

### Abasi-amefon O. Affia 2022

Abasi-amefon O. Affia, Alexander Nolte, and Raimundas Matulevičius. (2022). Integrating Hackathons into an Online Cybersecurity Course. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1145/3510456.3514151](https://doi.org/10.1145/3510456.3514151)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Questionnaire analysis of the lecture intervention's perceived usefulness per grade group (Fig 5); all teams rated it above average. Students reported that without the lectures and reading resources completing the hackathon tasks would have been unfeasible. No effect size was printed.

> "High grade teams (𝑀 = 4.00,𝐼𝑄𝑅 = 0.00, see Lecture Outcome in Fig 5) however, reported higher values of the lecture intervention than other teams followed by low grade teams (𝑀 = 4.00,𝐼𝑄𝑅 = 0.00) then middle grade teams (𝑀 = 3.50,𝐼𝑄𝑅 = 0.25)."

### Abasi-amefon O. Affia 2022 (2)

Abasi-amefon O. Affia, Alexander Nolte, and Raimundas Matulevičius. (2022). Integrating Hackathons into an Online Cybersecurity Course. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1145/3510456.3514151](https://doi.org/10.1145/3510456.3514151)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Discussion of open-ended questionnaire responses about the lecture intervention; students reported having too much content that got confusing at times, and one student noted the team had difficulties correctly completing hackathon tasks due to misunderstandings of the lecture.

> "Additionally, in the bid to provide as much information as required for learning, there were some complaints of the lectures having too much content, which can be confusing and affect the students’ learning process."
