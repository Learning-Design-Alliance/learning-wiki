# Training effects interact with participants' political orientation: conservative participants were less accurate and biased sentence labeling effects were absent in politically neutral participants

## Evidence

### Spinde 2025

Spinde, T., Wu, F., Gaissmaier, W., Demartini, G., & Giese, H. (2025). Enhancing Media Literacy: The Effectiveness of (Human) Annotations and Bias Visualizations on Bias Detection. [doi:10.1016/j.ipm.2025.104244](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ipm.2025.104244)

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r3`

Study 2 GLM adding political orientation (scaled −50 to +50) and article orientations: conservative participants were less accurate with a printed effect size of η2part = 0.007, and the biased sentence × political affiliation interaction (η2part = 0.01) left no significant sentence-label effect in politically neutral participants (η2part = 0.002).

> "Still, more conservative participants were generally less accurate (F(798,1) = 6.48, p = .011, η2part = 0.007) and also decreased the effects of biased sentence labeling (biased sentence × political affiliation: F(798,2) = 5.43, p = .005, η2part = 0.01), so that the effects were not significantly present in politically neutral participants (biased sentence F(798,2) = 1.04, p = .353, η2part = 0.002)."
