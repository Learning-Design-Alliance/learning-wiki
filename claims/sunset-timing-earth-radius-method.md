## Evidence

### P.K.Aravind 2005

P.K.Aravind. (2005). Sunsets, tall buildings, and the Earth’s radius. http://units.aps.org/units/fed/newsletters/summer2005/wyp.html

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

The article proposes this measurement procedure, extending Rawlins' method to a tall building. It states the experiment consists of "observing sunset repeatedly from a succession of floors" and using times and heights to deduce the Earth's radius.

> "The experiment consists of going up the building and observing sunset repeatedly from a succession of floors, and then using the times of the sunsets and the heights of the floors above sea level to deduce the Earth’s radius."

### P.K.Aravind 2005 (2)

P.K.Aravind. (2005). Sunsets, tall buildings, and the Earth’s radius. http://units.aps.org/units/fed/newsletters/summer2005/wyp.html

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

Analytical derivation (Appendix 1) of the formula linking Earth radius R to the sunset delay dt for a height h, involving latitude, solar declination and the rotational period T. The derivation shows "a straight line whose slope can be used to determine the Earth’s radius."

> "One sees that plotting the quantity on the left side of (1) as a function of h yields a straight line whose slope can be used to determine the Earth’s radius."
