## Evidence

### Gopnik 2012

Gopnik, A., & Wellman, H. M. (2012). Reconstructing constructivism: Causal models, Bayesian learning mechanisms and the theory theory. Psychological Bulletin, 138(6), 1085-1108. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028044

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports Kushnir, Xu and Wellman (2010) as a particularly striking demonstration that statistical Bayesian causal reasoning operates in the psychological domain by 20 months, not only for physical causation.

> "Kushnir, Xu & Wellman (2010), found that children as young as 20 months old would use statistical Bayesian reasoning to infer the desires of another person."

### Gopnik 2012 (2)

Gopnik, A., & Wellman, H. M. (2012). Reconstructing constructivism: Causal models, Bayesian learning mechanisms and the theory theory. Psychological Bulletin, 138(6), 1085-1108. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028044

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review attributes to Schulz and Gopnik (2004) the finding that covariation-based causal learning generalizes across physical, psychological and biological domains in 4-year-olds.

> "Schulz and Gopnik (2004) found that 4-year-old children used covariation to infer psychological and biological causes as well as physical ones."
