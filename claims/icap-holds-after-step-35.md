# In Procedural ABICAP simulations, the ICAP ordering (interactive > constructive > active > passive) holds after enough steps

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Rismanchian 2024

Rismanchian, S., & Doroudi, S. (2024). Reconciling Different Theories of Learning with an Agent-based Model of Procedural Learning. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2408.13364](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2408.13364)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Simulation of 50 learners per engagement mode on a 20-node Watts-Strogatz graph run for 60 steps, reporting average nodes mastered where mastery exceeded 0.8; the figure shows the ICAP ordering emerging after enough steps. Simulation result only, no human data.

> "As shown in Figure 2, after enough steps, the ICAP hypothesis does indeed hold (i.e., I > C > A > P). However, what is more interesting is that this is not always the case."
