# Maps in which learners gave up and requested the system's diagnosis contained fewer nodes, lower scores, fewer summary statements and lower confidence than maps solved by learners themselves

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Maps in group S (solution requested from system) had significantly fewer summary statements, lower scores in all four categories, fewer nodes and connections, and lower learner confidence than groups C and W. [→ Hege I 2018](#hege-i-2018)
`q2 i?` Of 1,393 completed maps, 59.0% were solved correctly on the first attempt, 13.1% after multiple attempts, and 27.9% ended with the learner giving up and requesting the solution. [→ Hege I 2018 (2)](#hege-i-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Hege I 2018

Hege I, Kononowicz AA, Kiesewetter J, Foster-Johnson L. (2018). Uncovering the relation between clinical reasoning and diagnostic accuracy – An analysis of learner's clinical reasoning processes in virtual patients. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0204900

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational log analysis of 1,393 completed concept maps from 317 medical students working with virtual patients, analyzed with linear mixed modeling and GEE over seven months. The study reports group S maps "were scored lower in all categories" and learners were "less confident with their final diagnosis decision"; group S also had fewer problems, diagnoses, tests, treatments and connections.

> "maps in group S had significantly fewer summary statements, were scored lower in all categories, and learners in this group were less confident with their final diagnosis decision."

### Hege I 2018 (2)

Hege I, Kononowicz AA, Kiesewetter J, Foster-Johnson L. (2018). Uncovering the relation between clinical reasoning and diagnostic accuracy – An analysis of learner's clinical reasoning processes in virtual patients. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0204900

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive analysis of all completed maps in the same log dataset. The article reports 59.0% (n = 822) of maps were solved on the first attempt, 13.1% (n = 183) after multiple attempts, and in 27.9% (n = 388) "learners gave up and requested the correct solution from the system". Percentages are descriptive; no effect sizes are printed.

> "We found that in 59.0% (n = 822) of the maps the correct final diagnosis was provided on the first attempt (Group C)."
