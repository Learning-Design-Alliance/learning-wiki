## Evidence

### Mayrand Kelsey P. 2015

Mayrand Kelsey P., Fischer Eric J., Ten Eyck Raymond P. (2015). A Simulation-based Randomized Controlled Study of Factors Influencing Chest Compression Depth. Western Journal of Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2015.9.28167

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Spearman correlation in the 55-subject randomized mannequin simulation between estimated weight group (<150lbs, 150–200lbs, >200lbs) and mean compression depth, printing "Spearman Rho correlation coefficient −r=0.499, p=0.0001" — a large positive correlation.

> "The weight category and compression depth are strongly, positively correlated (Spearman Rho correlation coefficient −r=0.499, p=0.0001). As weight increases by category, compression depth also increases."

### Mayrand Kelsey P. 2015 (2)

Mayrand Kelsey P., Fischer Eric J., Ten Eyck Raymond P. (2015). A Simulation-based Randomized Controlled Study of Factors Influencing Chest Compression Depth. Western Journal of Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2015.9.28167

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Point biserial correlation in the same 55-subject simulation study (35 female, 20 male) between gender and mean compression depth, printing "point biserial correlation −r=0.499, p=0.0001", with males achieving greater mean depth.

> "Gender and compression depth are strongly correlated (point biserial correlation −r=0.499, p=0.0001). Mean compression depth for males is greater than that for females."
