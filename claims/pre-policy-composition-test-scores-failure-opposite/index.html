Evidence¶

Takako Nomi 2010¶

Takako Nomi, Elaine M. Allensworth. (2010). The Effects of Tracking with Supports on Instructional Climate and Student Outcomes in High School Algebra. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/effects-tracking-supports-instructional-climate-and-student-outcomes-high-school

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Pre-policy cohort analyses of classroom composition and outcomes, with interaction terms between own ability and classroom average ability. High-ability students were more affected by class ability levels than low-ability students.

"students with average incoming ability have average algebra scores that are.43 points higher if in a high-ability classroom, compared to a low-ability classroom, but students with entering achievement one standard deviation above the mean would benefit by .89 points."

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Pre-policy bottom panel of Table 3. The article links this pattern to a frog pond effect: teachers assign higher grades to students who look better relative to peers, and more demanding high-ability classes may reduce assignment completion.

"At the same time, all students were more likely to fail in classes with more high-ability peers."
