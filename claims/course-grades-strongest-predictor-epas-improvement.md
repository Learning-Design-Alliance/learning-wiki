# The grade students receive in the corresponding subject course is the strongest predictor of improvement from one EPAS test to the next

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Regardless of starting scores, students with higher grades in English, math, or science courses show larger improvements on the corresponding EPAS subject test. [→ Elaine Allensworth 2008](#elaine-allensworth-2008)

## Evidence

### Elaine Allensworth 2008

Elaine Allensworth, Macarena Correa, Steve Ponisciak. (2008). From High School to the Future: ACT Preparation–Too Much, Too Late. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/high-school-future-act-preparation-too-much-too-late

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Quantitative analysis of CPS transcript grades and EPAS scores finds students who receive higher grades in a subject course show higher improvements on that subject's test, whether they start the year with low or high scores.

> "The strongest predictor of improvements from one Educational Planning and Assessment System (EPAS) test to another is the grade students receive in the corresponding subject course."
