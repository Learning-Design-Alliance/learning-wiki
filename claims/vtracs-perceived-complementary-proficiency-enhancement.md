## Evidence

### Komolafe AO et al 2026

Komolafe AO et al. (2026). Virtual Reality in Training and Assessment Among Clinical Students and Lecturers at a Nigerian University: A Phenomenological Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/75021

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Phenomenological qualitative study of 24 clinical students and 8 lecturers using 4 FGDs and 8 IDIs analyzed in NVivo. In the results, participants described time flexibility: "the students don't always have to be in the hospital settings...it's flexible."

> "The clinical students adjudged VTRACS as an educational supplement with a feeling of unlimited learning access, enhancing clinical abilities while positively impacting their confidence and reducing clinical errors."
