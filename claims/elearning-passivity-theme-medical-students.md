## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students described a lack of compulsion and accountability in elearning, constructing themselves as passive recipients whose 'completion' of sections did not involve active learning. [→ Reid Helen J. 2016](#reid-helen-j-2016)

## Evidence

### Reid Helen J. 2016

Reid Helen J., Thomson Clare, McGlade Kieran J. (2016). Content and discontent: a qualitative exploration of obstacles to elearning engagement in medical students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0710-5

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Focus-group thematic analysis found students viewed elearning as something to be 'got through'; lacking a formal timetable, they deferred it, worked alone at a computer, and at times disengaged, e.g. leaving it 'up there in a different window' while browsing.

> "Although they could ‘tick off’ that they had ‘completed’ a particular section of the elearning, ‘completion’ of these sections didn’t mean that they were engaged in any active learning process."
