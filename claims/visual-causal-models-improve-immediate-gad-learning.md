# Visual causal model (dual-mode) presentations yield larger immediate knowledge gains than auditory-only presentations of GAD explanations

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants learned significantly more immediately after a visual causal model presentation than after an auditory-only presentation of the same explanation. [→ Kim 2013](#kim-2013)

## Evidence

### Kim 2013

Kim, N. S., Khalife, D., Judge, K. A., Paulus, D. J., Jordan, J. T., & Yopchick, J. E. (2013). Visual Causal Models Enhance Clinical Explanations of Treatments for Generalized Anxiety Disorder. Journal of Health Communication, 18(Suppl 1), 103-117. https://doi.org/10.1080/10810730.2013.829136

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized experiment with 190 analyzed participants (86 mood-disorder patients, 104 lay people) assigned to auditory-only or dual-mode conditions. The critical Assessment × Presentation interaction was significant (F[1,186] = 4.3; p < .04), and the net gain comparison quoted here reached p = .05.

> "the net gain in knowledge following the visual causal model presentation (M = 8.7, SE = 0.6) was larger than the net gain in knowledge following the auditory-only presentation (M = 7.0; SE = 0.6; t[188] = 1.9; p = .05)"
