## Evidence

### Divoll 2010

Divoll, Kent and Browning, Sandra (2010) "The "Classroom Ticket" to Concept Retention," International Journal for the Scholarship of Teaching and Learning: Vol. 4: No. 2, Article 10. https://doi.org/10.20429/ijsotl.2010.040210

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Authors' analysis of their own field notes across two semesters of implementing the TtR. They attributed the absence of incorrect reinforcement to students using notes, accountability from collected sheets, and discussion with two people, and witnessed students revise answers after meeting a partner.

> "Finally, we were concerned that the TtR strategy has the potential for students to reinforce incorrect information by writing, repeating their answers, and hearing others share incorrect answers. Yet, our field notes, the student scores, and the class discussion did not yield evidence of this."
