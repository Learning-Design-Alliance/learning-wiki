## Evidence

### Katrina E. Black and Michael C. Wittmann 2008

Katrina E. Black and Michael C. Wittmann. (2008). Visualizing Changes in Student Responses Using Consistency Plots. https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.3136 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0812.3136](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0812.3136)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Analysis of the 2006/08 consistency plot (figure 8): seven students ended in the (other, other) region on the final, while most midterm solutions used separation of variables and three contained only math errors. The authors state "most of the students who performed badly on the final gave little forecast of this eventuality on the midterm."

> "We call such a region an attractor. In our idealized plot, no responses left the region; in this real world example, three leave, giving a net ﬂow of four students."
