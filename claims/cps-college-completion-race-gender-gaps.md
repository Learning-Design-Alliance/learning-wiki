# College completion among immediate enrollees in the CPS class of 2015 shows large race/ethnicity and gender gaps, with fewer than a third of Black and Latino young men completing a bachelor's degree

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among Black and Latino young men in the CPS graduating class of 2015 who immediately enrolled in college, fewer than a third completed a bachelor's degree within six years, and young men were less likely to complete a college degree or certificate than young women of the same race/ethnicity. ## Evidence

### Usher 2023

Usher, A., Mahaffie, S., & Nagaoka, J. (2023). The educational attainment of Chicago Public Schools students: 2022. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://toandthrough.uchicago.edu/tool/cps/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive table of six-year college completion rates among immediate enrollees in the CPS graduating class of 2015, disaggregated by race/ethnicity and gender. Total completion ranged from 29.7% for Black young men to 79.5% for Asian/Pacific Islander young women, against an all-students rate of 48.7%.

> "Among Black and Latino young men in the CPS graduating class of 2015, fewer than a third of students who immediately enrolled in college completed a bachelor’s degree within six years."
