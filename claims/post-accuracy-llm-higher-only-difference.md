## Evidence

### Divekar 2025

Divekar, R. R., Guerra, S., Gonzalez, L., Boos, N. (2025). Exploring The Interaction-Outcome Paradox: Seemingly Richer and More Self-Aware Interactions with LLMs May Not Yet Lead to Better Learning. arXiv:2511.09458. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2511.09458](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2511.09458)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-learning essay scores compared across tools by Repeated Measures MANOVA (N=20 within-subjects): no significant overall difference, with "only a statistical significance in Accuracy (LLM: 4.08±0.86, Search: 3.6±0.99)" favoring the LLM condition, reported with a large effect.

> "However, as seen in Table 7, the post scores compared between two tools do not significantly differ from each other. Additional univariate analysis showed only a statistical significance in Accuracy (LLM: 4.08±0.86, Search: 3.6±0.99) with large effect."
