The review reports the Steinberger et al drive-simulator study with two groups of teens (control and intervention), collecting driver data and subjective experience data; it found "gamified intervention may reduce unsafe driving by reducing driving boredom" while visual cues raised workload.

> "Results showed that gamified intervention may reduce unsafe driving by reducing driving boredom. However, visual cues can increase cognitive workload and thus cause slower reaction times to driving events."
