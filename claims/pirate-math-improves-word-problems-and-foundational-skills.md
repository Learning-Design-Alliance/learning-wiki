## Evidence

### Fuchs 2009

Fuchs, L. S., Powell, S. R., Seethaler, P. M., Cirino, P. T., Fletcher, J. M., Fuchs, D., Hamlett, C. L., & Zumeta, R. O. (2009). Remediating Number Combination and Word Problem Deficits Among Students With Mathematics Difficulties: A Randomized Control Trial. Journal of Educational Psychology, 101(3), 561-576. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0014701

`q3 · i2 · medium effect` · `n=133` · `causal · r2`

A randomized control trial with 133 third graders with mathematics difficulties in Houston and Nashville: 42 received Pirate Math word-problem tutoring (schema-broadening instruction with embedded number-combination, calculation and algebra practice), 44 received number-combination tutoring, and 47 were a no-tutoring control, over 16 weeks. Against the control group, word-problem tutoring improved number-combination fluency (d = 0.62), procedural calculations (d = 0.53), Find X algebra (d = 0.36), Number Sentences (d = 0.74), Vanderbilt Story Problems (d = 0.79) and KeyMath Problem Solving (d = 0.28); on the most distal measure, the Iowa test, there were no significant effects. Effects were largest on measures most closely aligned with the tutoring.

> "Pirate Math enhanced word problem skill as well as fluency with NCs, procedural computation, and algebra."
