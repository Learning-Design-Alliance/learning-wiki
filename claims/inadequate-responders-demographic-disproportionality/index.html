Evidence¶

Cho Eunsoo 2015¶

Cho Eunsoo, Roberts Garrett J., Capin Philip, Roberts Greg, Miciak Jeremy, Vaughn Sharon. (2015). Cognitive Attributes, Attention, and Self-Efficacy of Adequate and Inadequate Responders in a Fourth Grade Reading Intervention. Learning Disabilities Research & Practice. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4793275/

q3 · i? · associational · r1

Post-hoc analysis of demographic differences across three reader groups (TR vs. AR vs. IR) using adjusted standardized residuals. The analysis found "inadequate responders were more likely to have an identified learning disability or other health impairment and also more likely to be labeled as limited English proficiency than adequate responders and typical readers". Omnibus chi-squares were significant for gender, special education, LEP, and ethnicity.

"After adjusting alpha levels for multiple comparisons (using the Sidak method; see Beasley & Schumacker, 1995; Haberman, 1973), we found that inadequate responders were more likely to have an identified learning disability or other health impairment and also more likely to be labeled as limited English proficiency than adequate responders and typical readers."
