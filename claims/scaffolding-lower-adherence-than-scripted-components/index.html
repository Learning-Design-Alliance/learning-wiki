Evidence¶

Zucker 2021¶

Zucker, T. A., Jacbos, E., & Cabell, S. Q. (2021). Exploring Barriers to Early Childhood Teachers' Implementation of a Supplemental Academic Language Curriculum. Early Education and Development, 32(8), 1194-1219. https://doi.org/10.1080/10409289.2020.1839288

q2 · i? · qualitative · r?

Observation-based fidelity data summarized in Table 4 across three Developing Talkers studies. Adherence to scaffolding was 73.71% in Study 1, 76.00% in Study 2 and 61.46% in Study 3 (the text gives the low end as 62.46%); adherence to the scripted vocabulary and inferential-question components ranged from 72.50% to 95.76%. Scaffolding trailed both scripted components in Studies 2 and 3, but in Study 1 it was slightly above both (vocabulary 72.50%, inferential questions 72.69%), so the gap is not consistent across all three studies. The article attributes the difficulty to scaffolding being less amenable to scripting because it requires teacher skill and practice to respond to each child's verbalization. Descriptive percentages only; no test of the difference is reported.

"teachers struggled to implement the scaffolding approach (62.46% to 76.00% adherence) more than other focal evidence-based practice."
