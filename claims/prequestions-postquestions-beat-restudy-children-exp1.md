## Evidence

### Lima 2020

Lima, N. K. d., & Jaeger, A. (2020). The Effects of Prequestions versus Postquestions on Memory Retention in Children. Journal of Applied Research in Memory and Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jarmac.2020.08.005

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Within-subjects experiment with 27 fifth graders reading a 303-word text about rockets, with 10 target-words per condition counterbalanced, tested after 7 days. Both question conditions beat restudy on cued recall with large effects ("t(26) = 9.44, p < 0.001, d = 2.09" for prequestions; d = 3.05 for postquestions).

> "The follow-up t-tests also revealed that both prequestions and postquestions yielded greater cued recall performance than restudy, preques- tion vs. restudy, t(26) = 9.44, p < 0.001, d = 2.09; postquestion vs. restudy, t(26) = 11.74, p < 0.001, d = 3.05"
