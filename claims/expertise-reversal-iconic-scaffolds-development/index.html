Evidence¶

Slava Kalyuga 2010¶

Slava Kalyuga, Alexander Renkl. (2010). Expertise reversal effect and its instructional implications: introduction to the special issue. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-009-9102-0

q3 · i? · review · r2

A study of computer-based narrated chemistry visualizations with middle- and high-school students of differing prior knowledge. Visual scaffolds improved outcomes for low-prior-knowledge but not higher-prior-knowledge high-school students, and cognitive developmental level moderated the expertise reversal effect.

"For younger middle-school children who presumably lacked more general symbolic competencies (even if they had content knowledge), the effect was eliminated: the icons helped the younger children, regardless of their level of prior knowledge."
