# Simulation-group residents value the program and PGY-II residents as teaching staff more than lecture-group residents

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2019 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The simulation group rated the training as more meaningful for all three cases, more strongly endorsed continuing the program (10 vs 8, p = 0.001), expected more stress relief in clinical situations (9 vs 8, p = 0.001), and rated PGY-II residents as more appropriate teaching staff (10 vs 9, p = 0.02). [→ Yamamoto Akira 2019](#yamamoto-akira-2019)

## Evidence

### Yamamoto Akira 2019

Yamamoto Akira, Obika Mikako, Mandai Yasuhiro, Murakami Taku, Miyoshi Tomoko, Ino Hideo, Kataoka Hitomi, Otsuka Fumio. (2019). Effects on postgraduate-year-I residents of simulation-based learning compared to traditional lecture-style education led by postgraduate-year-II residents: a pilot study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1509-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Program-evaluation questionnaires in the randomized comparison. "The simulation group felt the training they participated in was more meaningful than did the lecture group" for all cases; continuation, stress-relief and teaching-staff ratings also favoured simulation with printed p-values.

> "The simulation group felt the training they participated in was more meaningful than did the lecture group (Case, simulation group median points (IQR) - lecture group median points (IQR), p value: Case.1, 9.5 (1) - 8 (2), 0.01; Case.2, 10 (1) - 9 (2), 0.01; Case.3, 9.5 (2) - 9 (2), 0.03)."
