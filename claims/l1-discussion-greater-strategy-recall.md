## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants who discussed texts in their L1 mentioned 57% of identified reading comprehension strategies and constituent elements on average, compared with 41% for the L2 discussion group and 27% for the control group. [→ Turnbull 2017](#turnbull-2017)

## Evidence

### Turnbull 2017

Turnbull, B., & Sweetnam Evans, M. (2017). The effects of L1 and L2 group discussions on L2 reading comprehension. Reading in a Foreign Language, 29(1), 133–154. http://nflrc.hawaii.edu/rfl

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Small quasi-experimental study of fifteen Japanese intermediate English students in three groups of five; recalls coded for conventional reading strategies. Group 3 (L1 discussion) averaged 57% versus 41% (L2) and 27% (control), "suggesting the greatest engagement with all four texts as a whole".

> "Overall, participants in group 1 (the control group) mentioned 27% of the reading comprehension strategies and relevant constituent elements identified by the researchers across all four texts in their written recalls, with group 2 slightly higher at 41%, and group 3 the highest at 57%"
