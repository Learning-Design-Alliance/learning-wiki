# A feature-level analysis found Valence associated with perceptions only, while an instructional composite was associated with both perceptions and uptake, most strongly with uptake

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Entering Valence and the instructional composite alongside provider reduced provider coefficients for fairness and usefulness to non-significance (all ps ≥ .124); Valence was associated with fairness (b = 0.29, p = .024) and usefulness (b = 0.31, p = .029), and the instructional composite with fairness (b = 0.33, p = .029), usefulness (b = 0.54, p = .002) and uptake (b = 0.71, p < .001). ## Evidence

### Jacobsen 2025

Jacobsen, L. J., Mertens, U., Jansen, T., & Weber, K. E. (2025). AI, Expert or Peer? Provider Biases and Feedback Uptake among Pre-Service Teachers. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2507.16013](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2507.16013)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Feature-level regression analysis (supplementary Table S6) entering the coded Valence category and an instructional composite alongside provider. The article concludes the two kinds of content "act on different outcomes."

> "Valence was associated with fairness (b = 0.29, SE = 0.13, p = .024) and usefulness (b = 0.31, SE = 0.14, p = .029), and the instructional composite was associated with fairness (b = 0.33, SE = 0.15, p = .029), usefulness (b = 0.54, SE = 0.17, p = .002) and, most strongly, with uptake (b = 0.71, SE = 0.19, p < .001)."
