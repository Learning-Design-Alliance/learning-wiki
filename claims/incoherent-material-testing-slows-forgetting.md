## Evidence

### de Jonge 2015

de Jonge, M., Tabbers, H. K., & Rikers, R. M. J. P. (2015). The Effect of Testing on the Retention of Coherent and Incoherent Text Material. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-015-9300-z

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2, same 2×2 between-subjects design with 64 psychology students learning scrambled facts about black holes. The interaction had "ηp2=.06"; the restudy group forgot 31% while the testing group showed no apparent decline.

> "Importantly, however, there was a significant learning condition×retention interval interaction, F(1, 64)=4.13, p<.05, ηp2=.06. As can be seen in Fig. 2, the restudy group showed a substantial amount of forgetting (31 %)."

### de Jonge 2015 (2)

de Jonge, M., Tabbers, H. K., & Rikers, R. M. J. P. (2015). The Effect of Testing on the Retention of Coherent and Incoherent Text Material. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-015-9300-z

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Follow-up t-tests within Experiment 2 conditions: the restudy condition showed a significant interval effect with "d=1.18", while the testing condition showed no significant decline (t<1).

> "Accordingly, follow-up analysis revealed that the effect of retention interval was significant for the restudy condition, t(30)=3.33, p<.001 ,d=1.18, but not for the testing condition (t<1)."
