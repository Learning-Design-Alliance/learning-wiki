# Without formal training, peer teachers develop their teaching role through informal strategies, which give unequal opportunities across students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Peer teachers supported their teaching role by using peer learners' feedback, observing and copying teachers or experienced peers as role models, and drawing on their own experiences of being a student. [→ George SS 2025](#george-ss-2025)
`q2 i?` Informal and extracurricular training opportunities, being voluntary, did not give equal possibilities for all students to develop teaching skills. [→ George SS 2025 (2)](#george-ss-2025-2)

## Evidence

### George SS 2025

George SS, Bonnevier A, Stenfors T, Kenne E. (2025). Learning to teach by teaching your peers: exploring students’ needs for training in the undergraduate medical education curriculum. BMC Medical Education 25(1):414. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07022-z

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Results section on finding support for the teaching role: PTs asked peer learners about sessions, copied appreciated behaviours of more experienced PTs and supervisors, and used their own experience of being a student to guide teaching.

> "For example, PTs used the feedback they received from PLs. They received feedback primarily by asking the PLs what they thought about the session and wanted to learn more about."

### George SS 2025 (2)

George SS, Bonnevier A, Stenfors T, Kenne E. (2025). Learning to teach by teaching your peers: exploring students’ needs for training in the undergraduate medical education curriculum. BMC Medical Education 25(1):414. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07022-z

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Results section: beyond the formal curriculum, students described informal opportunities such as mock OSCEs, mentoring, and extracurricular roles (e.g. sports coaching), but noted these "did not give equal possibilities" for all students.

> "However, students emphasized that these, often voluntary, training opportunities did not give equal possibilities to develop teaching skills for all students."
