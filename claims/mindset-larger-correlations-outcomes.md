## Evidence

### Limeri Lisa B. 2023

Limeri Lisa B., Carter Nathan T., Lyra Franchesca, Martin Joel, Mastronardo Halle, Patel Jay, Dolan Erin L. (2023). Undergraduate Lay Theories of Abilities: Mindset, universality, and brilliance beliefs uniquely predict undergraduate educational outcomes. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.22-12-0250

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis of phase-4 survey data (n = 1396) tested hypothesized relationships between ULTrA dimensions and belonging, evaluative concern, goal orientation, self-handicapping, persistence intent, and grades. The authors report mindset "exhibited larger magnitude correlations with the psychosocial and academic outcomes than universality and brilliance."

> "Overall, growth and fixed mindset exhibited larger magnitude correlations with the psychosocial and academic outcomes than universality and brilliance."

### Limeri Lisa B. 2023 (2)

Limeri Lisa B., Carter Nathan T., Lyra Franchesca, Martin Joel, Mastronardo Halle, Patel Jay, Dolan Erin L. (2023). Undergraduate Lay Theories of Abilities: Mindset, universality, and brilliance beliefs uniquely predict undergraduate educational outcomes. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.22-12-0250

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the same phase-4 correlation analysis, the growth mindset–performance-avoid goal relationship was the single hypothesized relationship not observed as predicted; the authors describe it as "nonsignificant and near-zero." No equivalence test was reported.

> "the only observed relationship that did not match predictions was the nonsignificant and near-zero correlation between growth mindset and performance-avoid goals."
