# Nature exposure relieves stress in learners, including experimentally demonstrated reductions in heart rate and self-reported stress

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 1 causal `r?`, 1 review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A window view of vegetation from a high school classroom yields systematic decreases in heart rate and self-reported stress, whereas built views do not. [→ Kuo M 2019](#kuo-m-2019)

## Evidence

### Kuo M 2019

Kuo M, Barnes M and Jordan C (2019) Do Experiences With Nature Promote Learning? Converging Evidence of a Cause-and-Effect Relationship. Front. Psychol. 10:305. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00305

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports an experimental study comparing vegetation versus built window views in a high school classroom, finding "systematic decreases in heart rate and self-reported stress" only for the vegetation view. Attributed to Li and Sullivan (2016).

> "a window view of vegetation from a high school classroom yields systematic decreases in heart rate and self-reported stress, whereas built views do not (Li and Sullivan, 2016)"

### Kuo M 2019 (2)

Kuo M, Barnes M and Jordan C (2019) Do Experiences With Nature Promote Learning? Converging Evidence of a Cause-and-Effect Relationship. Front. Psychol. 10:305. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00305

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

The review reports a study comparing outdoor forest schooling one day a week with indoor classes, in which cortisol dropped over the school day in the forest setting but not the classroom; the review notes the effects "could not be attributed to the physical activity associated with learning outdoors". Attributed to Dettweiler et al. (2017).

> "students learning in a forest setting one day a week showed healthier diurnal rhythms in cortisol in that setting than a comparison group that learned indoors"
