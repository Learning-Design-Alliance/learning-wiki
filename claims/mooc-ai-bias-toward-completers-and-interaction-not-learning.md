## Evidence

### Yu 2017

Yu, H., Miao, C., Leung, C. and White, T. J. (2017). Towards AI-powered personalization in MOOC learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-017-0016-3

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The authors' analytical argument in the personalization section: because available behaviour data come mostly from completers, trained AI models may be biased towards high performing learners; they state the extent of this bias "requires more studies to be fully understood."

> "as the available learning behaviour data are mostly from people who managed to complete the MOOCs, it is possible that the AI models trained from such data may be biased towards high performing learners."

### Yu 2017 (2)

Yu, H., Miao, C., Leung, C. and White, T. J. (2017). Towards AI-powered personalization in MOOC learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-017-0016-3

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The authors argue from existing MOOC analytics literature that, using data derived from interaction mechanisms currently available in MOOC platforms, learner assessment "mainly re ﬂect how students interact with, rather than learn from, the courses".

> "machine learning- based learner assessment techniques mainly re ﬂect how students interact with, rather than learn from, the courses."
