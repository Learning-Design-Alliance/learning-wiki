# Flipped-format students did not perceive learning more but ranked the course better overall, and many first-offering flipped students wanted more in-class time

## Evidence

### Gross D 2015

Gross D, Pietri ES, Anderson G, Moyano-Camihort K, Graham MJ. (2015). Increased Preclass Preparation Underlies Student Outcome Improvement in the Flipped Classroom. CBE—Life Sciences Education 14(4). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-02-0040

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of anonymous standardized end-of-course evaluation responses between standard- and flipped-course instances, on perceived learning and course ranking. The article reports students "did not feel that they learned more" in the flipped version, yet "did rank their course version better overall"; no effect size is printed.

> "Overall, students did not feel that they learned more in the flipped-course version compared with the standard course (despite having better exam scores); however, flipped-format students did rank their course version better overall than did students in the standard-course version"

### Gross D 2015 (2)

Gross D, Pietri ES, Anderson G, Moyano-Camihort K, Graham MJ. (2015). Increased Preclass Preparation Underlies Student Outcome Improvement in the Flipped Classroom. CBE—Life Sciences Education 14(4). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-02-0040

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Postcourse survey of the two flipped-course instances asking students to select three of 13 options to improve the course. The article reports 45% of the first instance chose "Increase in-class time" versus 19% in the second; no effect size is printed.

> "45% of students from the first flipped-course instance identified "Increase in-class time" as a desired change. Surprisingly, only 19% of the students from the second flipped offering held this view."
