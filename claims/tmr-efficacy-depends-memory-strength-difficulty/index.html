Evidence¶

Shin 2025¶

Shin, G.-H., Kweon, Y.-S., Oh, S., & Lee, S.-W. (2025). Personalized targeted memory reactivation enhances consolidation of challenging memories via slow wave and spindle dynamics. npj Science of Learning 10:47. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00340-3

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Two-way ANOVA on L1 accuracy: a significant group effect but no time or interaction effects, and no significant pre-to-post change in any group; equivalence was not formally tested, only non-significance reported.

"For the L1 condition, a signiﬁcant group effect was observed (F2,66 = 3.61, p = 0.033), while effects of time (F1,66 = 1.85, p =0 . 1 7 9 )a n d interaction (F2,66 = 0.63, p = 0.538) were not signiﬁcant. Post-hoc pairedt-tests revealed no signiﬁcant changes from pre- to post-sleep in any group."

Shin 2025 (2)¶

Shin, G.-H., Kweon, Y.-S., Oh, S., & Lee, S.-W. (2025). Personalized targeted memory reactivation enhances consolidation of challenging memories via slow wave and spindle dynamics. npj Science of Learning 10:47. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00340-3

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Spearman rank correlations between prospective subjective difficulty ratings and retrieval accuracy across all three groups and both sessions validated the difficulty-level classification used to drive the personalized cueing schedule.

"Strong negative correlations were consistently observed across all groups and sessions (rho = -0.878 to -0.948; allp < 0.001), indicating that higher difﬁculty ratings were reliably associated with lower recall performance."

Related Claims¶

  • Personalized TMR improves recall accuracy for the most challenging (L3) word pairs, outperforming both uniform TMR and no-stimulation control in the post-sleep session — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Sleep-stage durations, sleep fragmentation, and pre- and post-cue EEG band power were not significantly correlated with forgetting of cued items or the TMR-SD effect — related
  • The TMR-SD effect did not differ for objects with low versus high pre-nap spatial error, ruling out memory strength as the explanation — reports the opposite
  • In the personalized TMR group, memory improvement for challenging items correlates positively with SW-spindle coupling (r = 0.70), but no such correlation appears in the All condition or other groups — related
  • Personalized TMR improves error correction (higher incorrect-to-correct transitions) and reduces persistence of incorrect responses, mainly for challenging items — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Lower pre-sleep performance predicts larger TMR memory benefit in night 3 — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Hippocampal sharp-wave ripples during SWS and waking support memory consolidation and spatial task performance — related
