## Evidence

### Zhongzhou Chen and Tom Zhang 2021

Zhongzhou Chen and Tom Zhang. (2021). Analyzing the Heterogeneous Impact of Remote Learning on Students' Ability to Stay on Track During the Pandemic. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2108.00601](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2108.00601)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Mann-Whitney U comparison of early submission fractions (Table IV): first-generation vs non-first-generation significant for all FA20 modules (p = 0.0016) and first 41 FA20 modules (p = 0.00), not significant for the first 41 modules in SP20 (p = 0.36). Figure 5(a): 50% of non-first-generation versus about 25% of first-generation students submitted more than 50% of modules early. No effect size printed.

> "Comparison between demographic groups revealed that ﬁrst generation students in FA20 had signiﬁcantly lower fraction of early submission compared to non-ﬁrst generation stu- dents, as listed in Table IV."

### Zhongzhou Chen and Tom Zhang 2021 (2)

Zhongzhou Chen and Tom Zhang. (2021). Analyzing the Heterogeneous Impact of Remote Learning on Students' Ability to Stay on Track During the Pandemic. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2108.00601](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2108.00601)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Within-group between-semester comparison of the first 41 modules (Table III, Early column): first-generation students p = 0.06 (smaller sample, not significant at α < 0.05), non-first-generation students significant at p = 0.031. The gap magnitude was greater for first-generation students. No effect size printed.

> "ﬁrst generation students had lower early submission frequencies during FA20 when compared to SP20 , while non-ﬁrst generation students had signiﬁcantly higher early submission frequencies (Figure 6(a))."
