Evidence¶

Pilnick Alison 2018¶

Pilnick Alison, Trusson Diane, Beeke Suzanne, O'Brien Rebecca, Goldberg Sarah, Harwood Rowan H. (2018). Using conversation analysis to inform role play and simulated interaction in communications skills training for healthcare professionals: identifying avenues for further development through a scoping review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1381-1

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports Stokoe's CA comparison of audio recordings of role-played assessment police interviews with real police interviews, demonstrating "substantial differences between real life interviews and those using role play". Qualitative interactional analysis; no effect sizes.

"Using CA to examine the interactions in both cases demonstrated substantial differences between real life interviews and those using role play [51], casting doubt on the assumptions that role play is an accurate representation of what happens in real life situations."

Pilnick Alison 2018 (2)¶

Pilnick Alison, Trusson Diane, Beeke Suzanne, O'Brien Rebecca, Goldberg Sarah, Harwood Rowan H. (2018). Using conversation analysis to inform role play and simulated interaction in communications skills training for healthcare professionals: identifying avenues for further development through a scoping review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1381-1

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports a discourse-analytic study of student HCP and SP interactions finding SPs "generally more dominant in the conversation than real patients would be" and initiating closings; a related CA study found SPs presented symptoms as strange and unrecognizable with no referral context.

"SPs were identified as generally more dominant in the conversation than real patients would be, and also closings were initiated by the SP rather than the HCP."
