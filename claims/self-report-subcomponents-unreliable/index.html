Evidence¶

Craig 2020¶

Craig, K., Hale, D., Grainger, C., & Stewart, M. E. (2020). Evaluating metacognitive self-reports: systematic reviews of the value of self-report in metacognitive research. Metacognition and Learning, 15, 155-213. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-020-09222-y

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Systematic review of 22 factor-structure studies. Component analysis "varied widely both across the measures as well as on repeated assessments of the same measure," with "Structures with two, three, four, five, six, eight, and nine components were found (see Table 1)" and no discernable pattern by age.

"Structures with two, three, four, five, six, eight, and nine components were found (see Table 1). Just in the MAI, four, five, six, and eight subcomponents were found."

Related Claims¶

  • Self-report factors correlate weakly with metacognitive behavior while within-factor subcomponent correlations are larger, per studies reviewed for the meta-analysis
  • Self-report measures of metacognition support a two-factor structure separating metacognitive knowledge from metacognitive regulation — reports the opposite
  • Schellings' questionnaire-thinking-aloud convergent correlation of .51 rests on a small sample and is questionable against other studies — related
  • LLM-human factor-structure similarity is consistently lower than human-human similarity on both assessments — related
  • The MAKQ dimensions show acceptable internal consistency across both studies (alphas 0.77-0.93) — related
