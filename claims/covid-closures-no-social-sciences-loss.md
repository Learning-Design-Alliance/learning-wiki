# Social sciences scores showed no significant decrease after the 2020 school closures

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In social sciences, the COVID-19 related school closures did not lead to a significant decrease in test scores; the effect was negative but small and not significant without controls or with grade 4 scores. [→ Joana Elisa Maldonado and Kristof De Witte 2020](#joana-elisa-maldonado-and-kristof-de-witte-2020)

## Evidence

### Joana Elisa Maldonado and Kristof De Witte 2020

Joana Elisa Maldonado and Kristof De Witte. (2020). The Effect of School Closures on Standardised Student Test Outcomes. KU Leuven Faculty of Economics and Business Discussion Paper Series DPS20.17. https://www.kuleuven.be/dps20.17

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Difference-in-differences estimation for social sciences on the 2019-2020 and 2017-2020 sub-samples; the uncontrolled effect was "negative, but small and not signiﬁcant", and significant only in intermediate specifications without grade 4 scores, with smaller effect sizes than Dutch and mathematics.

> "Therefore, it can be concluded that, in social sciences, the COVID-19 related school closures did not lead to a signiﬁcant decrease in test scores."
