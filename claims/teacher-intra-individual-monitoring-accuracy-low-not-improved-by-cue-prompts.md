# Teachers' intra-individual monitoring accuracy of students' text-by-text learning is not better than chance and is not improved by students' keywords or summaries (Experiment 1).

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Teachers' monitoring accuracy (gamma between T-JOLs and test scores) had a grand mean of −.016 and did not differ across no-cue, keyword, and summary conditions. [→ Jan A. A. Engelen 2018](#jan-a-a-engelen-2018)

## Evidence

### Jan A. A. Engelen 2018

Jan A. A. Engelen, Gino Camp, Janneke van de Pol, Anique B. H. de Bruin. (2018). Teachers' monitoring of students' text comprehension: can students' keywords and summaries improve teachers' judgment accuracy? Metacognition and Learning 13:287–307. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-018-9187-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1 with 255 sixth-graders and 14 teachers: teachers gave text-by-text JOLs, seeing each student's keywords or summaries when present; monitoring accuracy was the intra-individual gamma correlation. The grand mean was "−.016, 95% CI [ −.113 .082]", and cue prompt had no significant effect, F(2, 26) = 2.586, p = .095.

> "The grand mean of the model was −.016, 95% CI [ −.113 .082], suggesting that teachers' monitoring accuracy was not better than chance. The effect of cue prompt was not significant, F(2, 26) = 2.586,p = .095, suggesting that teachers' monitoring accuracy did not differ between cue-prompt conditions."
