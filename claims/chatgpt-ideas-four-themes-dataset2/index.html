Analysis of 35 written ideas submitted by K12-to-higher-education educators in a public social-media competition, coded inductively; "the ideas presented by educators followed four themes" organized in Table 2 (comparison, role play, brainstorming, lesson planning, among other categories).

Related Claims¶

  • Educators' ideas on AI and VR in teaching were classified into three main categories: AI acting as a character, emerging technology to assist students' learning, and emerging technology to help the teacher — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Topic modeling of open-ended responses revealed five themes centered on ChatGPT's simplicity, speed, and role as a support tool — related
  • Teachers viewed ChatGPT as a source of inspiration rather than a generator of genuinely innovative lesson content — related
  • Students' main AI challenges are content reliability and depth, and most expect AI to serve as tutor, creative partner, and critical thinking partner rather than mere content generator — related
  • Teachers perceive their role shifting from knowledge transmitters to facilitators, co-investigators, and ethical supervisors of AI use — related
  • The empirical evidence for claimed pedagogical benefits of virtual characters is weak and inconclusive — related
