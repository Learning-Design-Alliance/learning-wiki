# Expert teachers show expertise-related eye movement patterns in some classroom tasks but not others, indicating task and context specificity

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Expert teachers tended to make more fixations on uninterested, underestimated, and struggling students than novices when evaluating student profiles, though the statistical evidence is weak. [→ Kaakinen 2021](#kaakinen-2021)
`q2 i?` Expert teachers made more fixations on the disrupted student during a critical incident than novices, and experts who reported noticing the incident fixated it more and longer than those who did not. [→ Kaakinen 2021 (2)](#kaakinen-2021-2)
`q2 i?` In a mobile eye tracking math instruction study, expertise effects in eye gaze behavior were not found; both experts and novices engaged in 'checking in' gaze shifts, but during different instruction types. [→ Kaakinen 2021 (3)](#kaakinen-2021-3)

## Evidence

### Kaakinen 2021

Kaakinen, J. K. (2021). What Can Eye Movements Tell us about Visual Perception Processes in Classroom Contexts? Commentary on a Special Issue. Educational Psychology Review, 33, 169-179. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09573-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The commentary reports, citing Seidel et al. in the Special Issue, that experts' eye movement pattern while evaluating student profiles differed from novices, with more fixations on students needing attention. The author states "the statistical evidence is rather weak." No effect size is printed.

> "Seidel at al. (this issue) report that expert teachers show a different pattern of eye movements while evaluating students ' profiles on a video than novices: they tend to make more fixations on uninterested, underestimated, and struggling students. Unfortunately the statistical evidence is rather weak."

### Kaakinen 2021 (2)

Kaakinen, J. K. (2021). What Can Eye Movements Tell us about Visual Perception Processes in Classroom Contexts? Commentary on a Special Issue. Educational Psychology Review, 33, 169-179. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09573-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The commentary reports, citing Wyss et al. in the Special Issue, that expert teachers viewing a classroom video with a critical incident fixated the disrupted student more than novices, and that the six experts who reported noticing the incident looked longer at the student. No effect size is printed.

> "A more detailed analysis revealed that the six expert teachers who reported noticing the critical incident made more fixations and spent longer time looking at the student during the incident than participants who did not report noticing the incident."

### Kaakinen 2021 (3)

Kaakinen, J. K. (2021). What Can Eye Movements Tell us about Visual Perception Processes in Classroom Contexts? Commentary on a Special Issue. Educational Psychology Review, 33, 169-179. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09573-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The commentary reports, citing Pouta et al. in the Special Issue, a mobile eye tracking study of expert and novice teachers during math instruction. Both groups made 'checking in' gaze shifts from materials to student, but experts did so during supporting and novices during non-supporting instructions. No effect size is printed.

> "In contrast, Pouta and colleagues (this issue) did not find expertise effects in eye gaze behavior."
