Evidence¶

Foorman 2017¶

Foorman, B., Herrera, S., Dombek, J., Schatschneider, C., & Petscher, Y. (2017). The relative effectiveness of two approaches to early literacy intervention in grades K–2 (REL 2017–251). Washington, DC: U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Regional Educational Laboratory Southeast. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Descriptive pre-post percentile-rank comparison from table 1 of the two-cohort cluster-randomized trial; the report states "the average difference between baseline and outcome percentile ranks on FRA reading measures was 13–25 percentile points across grades", with parallel language gains of 6–25 points, and cautions the gains may reflect regression to the mean or normative growth.

"The average difference between baseline and outcome percentile ranks on FRA reading measures was 13–25 percentile points across grades."

Related Claims¶

  • A standalone tier 2 early literacy intervention did not significantly improve reading or language outcomes relative to an embedded intervention in grades K–2, except for grade 2 spelling — related
  • In kindergarten, English learner students benefited more from the embedded intervention in phonological awareness while non–English learner students benefited more from the standalone intervention in word reading — related
  • The grade 2 spelling advantage of the standalone intervention was larger among students with low baseline spelling scores (0.27 standard deviation) — related
  • The standalone intervention significantly improved grade 2 FRA Sentence Comprehension only among cohort 1 students with a low vocabulary baseline (0.38 standard deviation) — related
  • Enhanced Proactive Reading shows no discernible effects on English language development — related
  • Relative effects of the standalone versus embedded intervention were inconsistent across cohorts and baseline scores, favoring first one intervention and then the other — related
