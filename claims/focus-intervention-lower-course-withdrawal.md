## Subclaims
`q2 i3` In a quasi-experimental comparison, FOCUS Intervention students withdrew from College Algebra at lower rates than baseline comparison students, with a large odds-ratio effect (0.22). [→ Mireles 2014](#mireles-2014)

## Evidence

### Mireles 2014

Mireles, S. V., Acee, T. W., & Gerber, L. N. (2014). FOCUS: Sustainable Mathematics Successes. Journal of Developmental Education, 38(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1071436

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Between-subjects quasi-experimental design comparing FOCUS students (N = 127) with a baseline comparison group (N = 1994) of prior-year developmental-placed students. Binary logistic regression with bootstrapping (2,000 samples) controlling for age, gender, and ethnicity found 6.3% versus 16.4% withdrawal and an intervention odds ratio of 0.22, which the authors call large.

> "Because the intervention group was less likely to withdraw from the course, the odds ratio for the intervention effect (0.22) is less than 1. This odds ratio is also large; a rule of thumb for interpreting the size of odds ratios is: when less than one (small = .67, medium = .40, large = .23)"
