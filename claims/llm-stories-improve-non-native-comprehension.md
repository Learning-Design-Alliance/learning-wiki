# LLM-generated stories improve comprehension accuracy for non-native English speakers on prediction and limitation questions compared to definitions alone

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2024 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among non-native English speakers, the treatment group reading definitions plus stories achieved statistically significant higher accuracy than the control group on prediction questions (p = .038) and limitation questions (p < .001). [→ Hang Jiang 2024](#hang-jiang-2024)

## Evidence

### Hang Jiang 2024

Hang Jiang, Xiajie Zhang, Robert Mahari, Daniel Kessler, Eric Ma, Tal August, Irene Li, Alex 'Sandy' Pentland, Yoon Kim, Deb Roy, Jad Kabbara. (2024). Leveraging Large Language Models for Learning Complex Legal Concepts through Storytelling. [doi:10.18653/v1/2024.acl-long.388](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.acl-long.388)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

RCT with 136 participants (71 non-native speakers) recruited through Prolific, comparing definition-only control to definition-plus-story treatment. Chi-squared tests show "statistical significance is achieved for both prediction and limitation questions" for non-native speakers.

> "For the non-native speaker group, statistical significance is achieved for both prediction and limitation questions (concept question: X 2(1, N= 355) = 0 .20, p = .653; prediction question: X 2(1, N = 355) = 4 .29, p = .038; limitation question: X 2(1, N = 355) = 11 .77, p < .001)."
