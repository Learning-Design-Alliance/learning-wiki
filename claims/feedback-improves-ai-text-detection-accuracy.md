# Immediate per-trial feedback improves accuracy in distinguishing human-written from AI-generated texts

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants receiving immediate feedback after each trial identified AI-generated texts more accurately (65.1%) than those receiving no feedback (55.4%). [→ Milička J 2025](#milicka-j-2025)

## Evidence

### Milička J 2025

Milička J, Marklová A, Drobil O, Pospíšilová E. (2025). Humans can learn to detect AI-generated texts, or at least learn when they can't. Preprint. Manuscript submitted to PLOS One. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2505.01877](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.01877)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Online experiment with 254 Czech native speakers randomly assigned to feedback and no-feedback conditions, judging side-by-side pairs of human and GPT-4o texts. The article reports a "substantial and statistically significant difference between feedback and no feedback groups" with the printed rates and CIs.

> "The no feedback group showed considerably lower performance, with an average correctness rate of 55.4% (95% CI: 52.0%–58.7%), while the feedback group achieved a higher average success rate of 65.1% (95% CI: 62.4%–67.8%)."
