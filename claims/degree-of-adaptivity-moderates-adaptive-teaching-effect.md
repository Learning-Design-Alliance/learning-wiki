# Degree of adaptivity moderates the adaptive teaching effect: only highly adaptive units outperform control, and boost self-efficacy

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Units with more than 18 adaptive elements significantly outperformed control at both posttests (β = 0.37 immediate; β = 0.44 delayed), while low-adaptivity units showed no differences. [→ Sibley 2025](#sibley-2025)
`q3 i2` Students in highly adaptive units showed higher self-efficacy in using technology than control students at immediate posttest (β = 0.34). [→ Sibley 2025 (2)](#sibley-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Sibley 2025

Sibley, L., Fabian, A., Plicht, C., Pagano, L., Ehrhardt, N., Wellert, L., Bohl, T., & Lachner, A. (2025). Adaptive teaching with technology enhances lasting learning. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102141

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Subsample analyses split at the mean degree of adaptivity (M = 18.06) in the same study. Low-adaptivity units showed no differences (immediate β = 0.21, p = .120; delayed β = 0.15, p = .286).

> "teaching units with high degree of adaptivity demonstrated a significant benefit regarding students' learning compared to the control condition (immediate posttest: β = 0.37, p = .004, small effect; delayed posttest: β = 0.44, p = .001, small effect)."

### Sibley 2025 (2)

Sibley, L., Fabian, A., Plicht, C., Pagano, L., Ehrhardt, N., Wellert, L., Bohl, T., & Lachner, A. (2025). Adaptive teaching with technology enhances lasting learning. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102141

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Additional moderation analysis in the same study; the self-efficacy benefit appeared only in the immediate posttest subsample of highly adaptive units.

> "Students in highly adaptive technology-enhanced teaching units showed higher self-efficacy compared to students in the control condition in the immediate posttest (β = 0.34, p = .014, small effect)."
