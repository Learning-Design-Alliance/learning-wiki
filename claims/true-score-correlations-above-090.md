# True-score-model correlations between term scores exceed 0.90 for almost all course-specific tests

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Pearson correlation coefficients computed from true score model estimates are above 0.90 for almost all tests, indicating strong correlations between fall, winter, and spring scores; these corrected coefficients are higher than those from observed scores. [→ Wei He 2022](#wei-he-2022)

## Evidence

### Wei He 2022

Wei He. (2022). Achievement and Growth Norms for Course-Specific MAP® Growth™ Tests. NWEA Research Report. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/achievement-and-growth-norms-for-course-specific-map-growth-tests/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis reported in Section 3.3 and Table 3.2, comparing model-based correlations (corrected for attenuation) with observed-score correlations for each subject across fall–winter, fall–spring, and winter–spring pairs. The report notes the corrected coefficients "are therefore higher than those from the observed scores," e.g., Algebra 1 fall–winter r of 0.92 versus 0.85 observed.

> "the Pearson correlation coefficients computed based on the estimates from the true score models are above 0.90 for almost all tests, suggesting that scores from each administration were strongly correlated"
