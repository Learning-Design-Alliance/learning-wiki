# Coupling interactive simulations with real-time open-format formative assessment produced statistically significant learning gains across all five simulations in an engineering physics course

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students achieved significant learning gains on all five simulation-based topics, with paired two-tailed t-tests yielding p values all less than 0.001. [→ F.V. Kowalski and S.E. Kowalski 2013](#fv-kowalski-and-se-kowalski-2013)

## Evidence

### F.V. Kowalski and S.E. Kowalski 2013

F.V. Kowalski and S.E. Kowalski. (2013). The Effect of Student Learning Styles on the Learning Gains Achieved When Interactive Simulations Are Coupled with Real-Time Formative Assessment via Pen-Enabled Mobile Technology. [doi:10.1109/fie.2012.6462281](https://doi.org/10.1109/fie.2012.6462281)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Classroom study of two sections (total 41 students) in an undergraduate engineering physics course using pre-test and summative assessments with rubric-scored responses; learning gains were average normalized gains. Every p < 0.001, and the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test reached "the same conclusion (every p < 0.001)". No effect size is printed.

> "The p values for paired two tailed t-test results with unequal variance, all less than 0 .001, showed signiﬁcant learning gains which were most likely not due to chance."
