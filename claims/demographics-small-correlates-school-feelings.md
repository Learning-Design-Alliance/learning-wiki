# Demographic characteristics (gender, age, SES, school type, race/ethnicity) were only small correlates of high school students' school feelings, explaining less than 9% of variance in positive and less than 6% in negative feelings

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Positive feelings were higher for male, older, higher-SES, private-school, and ethnic-minority students, but all effects were small, with SES the largest (β = .209**). [→ Moeller 2017](#moeller-2017)
`q2 i?` Negative feelings were higher for female, younger, lower-SES, and some ethnic-minority students, with SES the largest coefficient (β = -.157**). [→ Moeller 2017 (2)](#moeller-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Moeller 2017

Moeller, J., Brackett, M. A., Ivcevic, Z., & White, A. E. (2017). High school students' feelings: Discoveries from a large national survey and an experience sampling study. Preprint / self-archived. https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=High+school+students%E2%80%99+feelings+Moeller+Brackett+Ivcevic+White

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression analyses in the national survey (N = 21,678) predicting positive feelings from gender, age, SES, school type, and race/ethnicity. The article reports "the regression coefficient for SES (β = .209**)" as the largest of small effects, with under 9% of variance explained; no standardized effect size beyond the regression coefficient is printed.

> "However, all effect sizes were small, with the largest being the regression coefficient for SES (β = .209**). Combined, these demographic variables explained less than 9% of variance in positive feelings."
