`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Trend synthesis (RQ3) of studies varying reviewer count. Supporting instances the review reports include Kulkarni et al., where raising raters from 5 to 11 led to 7.4% more grades within 10% of the staff grade, and Rodgers, where moving from one to two reviews improved alignment with expert grading alongside student-reported educational value.

> "Caragiannis et al. [41] showed that accuracy improved from 83% to 93% when the number of reviews increased from 4 to 10."

### Uchswas Paul 2026 (2)

Uchswas Paul, Shail Shah, Sri Vaishnavi Mylavarapu, M. Parvez Rashid, and Edward Gehringer. (2026). Optimizing Peer Grading: A Systematic Literature Review of Reviewer Assignment Strategies and Quantity of Reviewers. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1007/978-3-032-15743-0_27](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-15743-0_27)

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Synthesis of studies characterizing the accuracy–reviewer-count curve. Nakayama et al. reported substantial accuracy improvement up to 5 reviewers, moderate gains from 5 to 8, slight gains between 8 and 19, and little to no improvement thereafter; Raman et al. found benefits plateaued beyond around fifteen reviewers.

> "Capuano et al. [7] found that RMSE dropped sharply when increasing the number of reviewers from 1 to 4, showed moderate improvement between 4 and 6, and yielded only marginal gains beyond that point."
