## Evidence

### Gartland Nicola 2022

Gartland Nicola, Aljofi Halah E., Dienes Kimberly, Munford Luke Aaron, Theakston Anna L., van Tongeren Martie. (2022). The Effects of Traffic Air Pollution in and around Schools on Executive Function and Academic Performance in Children: A Rapid Review. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph19020749

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review's synthesis of attention findings across six publications. It notes variation in how attention was operationalised hampered comparability, and that all four studies measuring inattention were based on the BREATHE Barcelona data. No effect sizes are printed for this synthesis.

> "No studies reported an effect of NO2 or BC on attentional outcomes. There is evidence to support the suggestion that PAHs, EC, and UFP affect attentional outcomes and mixed findings for the effect of PM2.5."

### Gartland Nicola 2022 (2)

Gartland Nicola, Aljofi Halah E., Dienes Kimberly, Munford Luke Aaron, Theakston Anna L., van Tongeren Martie. (2022). The Effects of Traffic Air Pollution in and around Schools on Executive Function and Academic Performance in Children: A Rapid Review. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph19020749

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The Saenen et al. Belgian study (n = 310, ages 8–11, cohort analysed cross-sectionally) measured indoor classroom PM2.5 and PM10 directly. The review reports IQR increments associated with 42.7 ms and 50.2 ms longer reaction times on selective attention, with no effect on sustained attention; only p-values and latencies are printed.

> "an inter-quartile range increment in PM2.5 was associated with 42.7 ms longer reaction times, while an inter-quartile range increment in PM10 was associated with 50.2 ms longer reaction times. However, they found no effect on sustained attention."
