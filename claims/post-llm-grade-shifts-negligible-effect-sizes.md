## Evidence

### Potasznik 2026

Potasznik, Amanda, and Daniel Haehn. (2026). Measuring Changes in Instructor Class Design and Student Learning After the Release of Large Language Models (LLMs). 19th International Conference on e-Learning & Innovative Pedagogies. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2605.16284](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.16284)

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r1`

Retrospective analysis of up to ten years of registrar grade data across approximately fifteen courses, comparing pre- and post-LLM periods (Spring 2023 onward). GPA change was "statistically significant (Welch t-test, p = 0.0078)" but practically minimal.

> "Mean student GPA in individual courses increased slightly from 2.96 (pre-LLM) to 3.00 (post-LLM). Although this difference was statistically significant (Welch t-test, p = 0.0078), the effect size was negligible (Cohen’s d = 0.04)"

### Potasznik 2026 (2)

Potasznik, Amanda, and Daniel Haehn. (2026). Measuring Changes in Instructor Class Design and Student Learning After the Release of Large Language Models (LLMs). 19th International Conference on e-Learning & Innovative Pedagogies. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2605.16284](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.16284)

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r1`

Same retrospective registrar grade analysis. Withdrawal rates fell from 4.95% to 4.17%; the difference was significant with a negligible effect size of "Cohen’s d = −0.037".

> "Withdrawal rates decreased slightly from 4.95% in the pre-LLM period to 4.17% post-LLM. Although the difference was statistically significant (Welch t-test, p = 0.00055), the effect size was negligible (Cohen’s d = −0.037)."

### Potasznik 2026 (3)

Potasznik, Amanda, and Daniel Haehn. (2026). Measuring Changes in Instructor Class Design and Student Learning After the Release of Large Language Models (LLMs). 19th International Conference on e-Learning & Innovative Pedagogies. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2605.16284](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.16284)

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Same retrospective registrar grade analysis. Failure rates fell from 3.96% to 2.57%; significant at "p < 0.001" with a small effect size of "Cohen’s d = −0.08".

> "Failure rates decreased from 3.96% in the pre-LLM period to 2.57% post-LLM. Although the difference was statistically significant (Welch t-test, p < 0.001), the effect size was small (Cohen’s d = −0.08)."
