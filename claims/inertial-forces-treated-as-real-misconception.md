## Evidence

### S. Küchemann 2020

S. Küchemann, P. Klein, H. Fouckhardt, S. Gröber, and J. Kuhn. (2020). Improving students' understanding of rotating frames of reference using videos from different perspectives. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010112](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010112)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Interview question Q2 on a water glass moving uniformly in the stationary frame but on a curved path in the rotating frame; the most common incorrect answer (23.8%) was that water sloshes opposite to the sum vector of Coriolis and centrifugal force, as would hold for real forces.

> "about one out of four physics students (23.8 %) do not include the ﬁctitious character of inertial forces in their arguments and rather argue that they have the same eﬀect on objects as real forces."
