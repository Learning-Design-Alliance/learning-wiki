## Subclaims
`q1 i?` People's epistemic beliefs may be tacit rather than explicit, inferable from regularities in their practices even when they cannot verbalize them. [→ CHINN et al. 2011](#chinn-et-al-2011)

## Evidence

### CHINN et al. 2011

Clark A. Chinn, Luke A. Buckland & Ala Samarapungavan (2011). Expanding the Dimensions of Epistemic Cognition: Arguments From Philosophy and Psychology. Educational Psychologist, 46(3), 141-167. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2011.587722

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument in the article's framework section: epistemic beliefs may be tacit, and the authors suggest the term epistemic commitment for such unarticulated tendencies, using the terms interchangeably. The framework is intended to work for scholars who reject explicit epistemic beliefs. No empirical data are reported.

> "A tacit belief is a belief that people may be unable to verbally express but that could potentially be inferred from their actions."
