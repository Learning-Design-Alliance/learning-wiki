# Grade level moderates multigrade cognitive effects, with a diminishing positive effect from lower to higher grades

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3` · `i0` negligible

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` Grade level significantly predicted variance in multigrade cognitive effects: a small positive effect in grades K-2 (d = .06), essentially zero in grades 3-4, and a small negative effect in grades 5-6 (d = -.08). [→ Simon Veenman 1996](#simon-veenman-1996)

## Evidence

### Simon Veenman 1996

Simon Veenman. (1996). Effects of multigrade and multi-age classes reconsidered. Review of Educational Research, 66(3), 323-340. https://doi.org/10.2307/1170526

`q3 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Between-study homogeneity analysis of the multigrade cognitive subset (Table 3): lower grades K-2 d = .06 (k = 9), intermediate grades 3-4 d = .01 (k = 20), higher grades 5-6 d = -.08 (k = 5); Q(2) = 18.9, p < .01.

> "A significant amount of variance in effects was found for grade level, Q(2, N = 34) = l8.9,p <  .01. The average weighted d-indexes across these studies showed a diminishing positive effect of multigrade grouping."
