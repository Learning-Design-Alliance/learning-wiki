# Students predominantly evaluated team success as fast gameplay, equating efficiency and victory with good teamwork despite prior instructions

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students interpreted team purpose and success predominantly in terms of fast gameplay, quick problem solving, and victory over other teams, overriding collaborative learning. [→ Elina Riivari 2021](#elina-riivari-2021)

## Evidence

### Elina Riivari 2021

Elina Riivari, Marke Kivijärvi, Anna-Maija Lämsä. (2021). Learning teamwork through a computer game: for the sake of performance or collaborative learning? Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-021-10009-4

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative analysis of student feedback in the HRM course case; many students described their main task as solving problems as quickly as possible, and the discussion notes students "measured their team's efficiency mainly by the time it took to complete the game" despite being told speed was not the objective.

> "For many students, the main task was, therefore, to “solve the problems as quickly as possible and to avoid making any mistakes,” or as another student phrased it, “to pass the game as efficiently and as quickly as possible.”"
