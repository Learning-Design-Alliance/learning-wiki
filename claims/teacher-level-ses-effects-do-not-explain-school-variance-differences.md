# Variation in teacher effectiveness as a function of student SES does not explain differences in teacher-effect variance across schools

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2004 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Teacher-effect variance components are virtually identical whether the student-SES effect is fixed or random, so variation in teacher effectiveness by student SES cannot explain the larger teacher-effect variance in low-SES schools. [→ Nye 2004](#nye-2004)

## Evidence

### Nye 2004

Nye, B., Konstantopoulos, S., & Hedges, L. V. (2004). How Large Are Teacher Effects? Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis, 26(3), 237-257. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=How+Large+Are+Teacher+Effects

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

HLM analysis of achievement gains introducing a random effect for the student-SES coefficient at level 2, estimating the degree to which teacher effects differ for low-SES versus high-SES students within classrooms. This null finding is reported alongside the Tables 7-8 decomposition; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "The teacher effect variance components are virtually identical whether the SES effect is fixed or random."
