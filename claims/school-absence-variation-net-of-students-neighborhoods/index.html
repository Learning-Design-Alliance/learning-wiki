Evidence¶

Allensworth 2026¶

Allensworth, E.M., Antony, M., Delgado, W., & de la Torre, M. (2026). Connection, trust, and learning: Student attendance in the middle and high school grades following the COVID-19 pandemic. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/connection-trust-and-learning

q2 · i? · associational · r3

Cross-nested HLMs with students nested in schools and census tracts, using CPS data for grades 6-11. High school 75th vs. 25th percentile differences were 8.7 points post-pandemic with neighborhood adjustment versus 9.2 without.

"School variation did not change much when we took into account the neighborhood where students resided, either before or after the pandemic. This suggests the school students attended affected their attendance much more than the neighborhood in which they lived."

Allensworth 2026 (2)¶

Allensworth, E.M., Antony, M., Delgado, W., & de la Torre, M. (2026). Connection, trust, and learning: Student attendance in the middle and high school grades following the COVID-19 pandemic. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/connection-trust-and-learning

q2 · i? · associational · r3

Cross-nested HLMs controlling demographics, prior attendance and test scores (grades 3-5 or 6-8). The brief notes this likely underestimates school effects in middle grades because K-8 schools' earlier-grade strategies are controlled out.

"At the high school level, schools at the 75th percentile had absence rates that were 5.9 points higher than schools at the 25th percentile, after controlling for their students’ absences in prior years and backgrounds. That’s about 10 days of school."
