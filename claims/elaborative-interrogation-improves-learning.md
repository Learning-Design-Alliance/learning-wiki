# Elaborative Interrogation Improves Learning

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · none recorded yet

Elaborative interrogation — prompting learners to answer "why is this true?" or "why does this make sense?" about factual statements — supports learning by connecting new material to prior knowledge.

## Discussion

Elaborative interrogation works through elaborative processing: generating an answer to a "why" question forces the learner to search prior knowledge for a causal or explanatory link, which integrates the new fact into existing schemas. This mechanism places it in the same family as [self-explanation](../elements/self-explanation.md) and [analogical reasoning](analogical-reasoning-improves-transfer.md), and it presupposes that relevant prior knowledge is available — see [Activation improves learning](activation-improves-learning.md).

Expected moderators, to be confirmed once evidence entries are added: the technique appears most effective for learners with sufficient domain knowledge to generate plausible answers, and for fact-dense material where individual statements can be interrogated one at a time. Learners with sparse prior knowledge may produce weak or incorrect explanations, and the technique is less suited to procedural skills or complex tasks where the unit of analysis is a whole procedure rather than an isolated fact. Generating explanations also consumes working-memory resources, so it should be considered alongside [cognitive load theory](../theories/cognitive-load-theory.md) and [chunking](chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) when material is high in element interactivity.

Boundary conditions for design practice: prompts should be attached to specific, discrete factual claims rather than to entire passages, since a vague "why does this matter?" prompt invites generic rather than integrative responses. Where learners cannot generate a plausible answer, providing the explanatory link after the prompt (a feedback-supported variant) is likely safer than leaving the learner with an unresolved or misconceived explanation — a design tension shared with [advance organizers improve learning](advance-organizers-improve-learning.md), where the organizer must actually connect to what the learner knows.

A further design consideration is response quality: because learners generate their own answers, incorrect or superficial explanations can be encoded as confidently as correct ones. Pairing interrogation prompts with verification (checking the answer against the text or receiving corrective feedback) mitigates this risk, and distinguishes productive elaborative interrogation from unguided "think about why" instructions that learners may satisfy with low-effort responses.

Open questions for future enrichment: effect sizes relative to other learning techniques, durability of gains over delay, and whether prompted interrogation transfers to spontaneous questioning during self-study. Until evidence entries are added, this page should be treated as a mechanism-level claim awaiting empirical grounding.
