# Gender moderates the direct effect of parental beliefs and interests on children's social–emotional competence but not the direct effect of structural family characteristics

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The interaction of parental beliefs and interests with gender significantly predicted social–emotional competence (β = 0.26, p < 0.01). [→ Li S 2023](#li-s-2023)
`q3 i?` The interaction of structural family characteristics with gender did not predict social–emotional competence (β = 0.13, p > 0.5). [→ Li S 2023 (2)](#li-s-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Li S 2023

Li S, Tang Y and Zheng Y (2023) How the home learning environment contributes to children's social–emotional competence: A moderated mediation model. Front. Psychol. 14:1065978. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1065978

`q3 · i?`

PROCESS Model 59 analysis for parental beliefs and interests. The article reports gender moderated "the direct effect of parental briefs and interests on children's social–emotional competence"; simple slopes showed the direct effect significant for girls (βsimple = 0.57) and much weaker for boys (βsimple = 0.18).

> "gender was also found to moderate the direct effect of parental briefs and interests on children's social–emotional competence ( β = 0.26, p < 0.01)"

### Li S 2023 (2)

Li S, Tang Y and Zheng Y (2023) How the home learning environment contributes to children's social–emotional competence: A moderated mediation model. Front. Psychol. 14:1065978. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1065978

`q3 · i?`

PROCESS Model 59 analysis for structural family characteristics. The article reports the interaction "did not predict children's social–emotional competence", so gender moderated this pathway only in the second stage of the mediation; Hypothesis 3b was partially supported.

> "the interaction effect of structural family characteristics and gender did not predict children's social–emotional competence ( β = 0.13, p > 0.5)"
