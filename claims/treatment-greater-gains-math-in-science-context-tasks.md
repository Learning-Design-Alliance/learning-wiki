# Treatment-group students increased significantly more than Delayed Treatment students on math-in-science-context tasks from pretest to midtest and pretest to posttest

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students whose teachers taught GC/CoV-rich units first showed significantly greater pre-to-mid and pre-to-post improvement on math-in-science-context assessment tasks than students who taught those units later. [→ McElhaney 2025](#mcelhaney-2025)

## Evidence

### McElhaney 2025

McElhaney, K. W., Hodkowski, N. M., McMahon, L. H., Jacobs, J., Penuel, W. R., Anton, G., Henson, K., Urban, R., & Parker, D. A. (2025). Promoting Mathematical Reasoning in OpenSciEd Middle School Curriculum Units. Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/4ea4b3c5-c663-4484-8727-258bff346d2d

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A delayed-treatment quasi-experiment in which teachers taught GC/CoV-rich or GC/CoV-lean OpenSciEd units in different orders, with a student mathematics assessment at three time points. The report states that "Treatment students increased significantly more from pre-to-mid and from pre-to-post than Delayed Treatment students." No effect size is printed.

> "on the math-in-science-context tasks, Treatment students increased significantly more from pre-to-mid and from pre-to-post than Delayed Treatment students (Figure 3)."
