## Evidence

### Bitzenbauer 2023

Bitzenbauer, P., Teußner, T., Veith, J., & Kulgemeyer, C. (2023). (How) Do pre-service teachers use YouTube features in the selection of instructional videos for physics teaching? arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1007/s11165-023-10148-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11165-023-10148-z)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Eye-tracking of N = 24 pre-service physics teachers in phase 1, selecting one of eight videos from an image chart. Descriptive statistics show the thumbnail AOI dominated attention: "With a mean percentage of 35.39% of the total fixation duration, the Thumbnail was by far the most compelling AOI".

> "With a mean percentage of 35.39% of the total fixation duration, the Thumbnail was by far the most compelling AOI and the only one with a share above 10%."

### Bitzenbauer 2023 (2)

Bitzenbauer, P., Teußner, T., Veith, J., & Kulgemeyer, C. (2023). (How) Do pre-service teachers use YouTube features in the selection of instructional videos for physics teaching? arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1007/s11165-023-10148-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11165-023-10148-z)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Fixation-count analysis of the same phase 1 eye-tracking data confirms the pattern: "with a mean percentage of 45.59% of the total number of fixations, there is a predominant focus on the AOI Thumbnail".

> "Again, with a mean percentage of 45.59% of the total number of fixations, there is a predominant focus on the AOI Thumbnail, with Title (11.60%) and Channel (7.12%) being second and third, respectively."
