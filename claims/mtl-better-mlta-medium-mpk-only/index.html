Evidence¶

Aminah 2018¶

Aminah, M., Kusumah, Y. S., Suryadi, D., & Sumarmo, U. (2018). The Effect of Metacognitive Teaching and Mathematical Prior Knowledge on Mathematical Logical Thinking Ability and Self-Regulated Learning. International Journal of Instruction, 11(3), 45-62. https://doi.org/10.12973/iji.2018.1134a

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Quasi-experiment (Nonequivalent pretest-posttest control-group design) with 36 MTL and 34 conventional tenth-graders; Table 4 reports Med-Med post-test Sig. 0.041 and N-Gain Sig. 0.023, both interpreted as "Different".

"Table 2 point s out that on students of high and medium MPK, the attainment of MLTA and its N -gain of students who were taught by MTL are better than students taught who were by conventional teaching."

Aminah 2018 (2)¶

Aminah, M., Kusumah, Y. S., Suryadi, D., & Sumarmo, U. (2018). The Effect of Metacognitive Teaching and Mathematical Prior Knowledge on Mathematical Logical Thinking Ability and Self-Regulated Learning. International Journal of Instruction, 11(3), 45-62. https://doi.org/10.12973/iji.2018.1134a

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Same quasi-experiment; Table 4 reports Low-Low post-test Sig. 0.661 and N-Gain Sig. 0.543, and Total-Total post-test Sig. 0.364 and N-Gain Sig. 0.190, all interpreted as "No difference".

"However, there is no difference of MLTA and their N-gain of students of low MPK and of total."

Related Claims¶

  • The SSML advantage in MPSA enhancement is significant for middle-MPK students but not for upper- and low-MPK students — related
