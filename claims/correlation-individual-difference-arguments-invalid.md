# Correlation-based arguments for individual differences in far transfer are statistically incorrect

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Positive correlations between training-task gains and transfer-task gains arise whenever within-session covariances exceed between-session covariances and do not evidence a training effect. [→ Gobet Fernand 2022](#gobet-fernand-2022)
`q2 i?` Negative correlations between pretest scores and gains are likely artifacts of regression to the mean, not compensatory effects. [→ Gobet Fernand 2022 (2)](#gobet-fernand-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Gobet Fernand 2022

Gobet Fernand, Sala Giovanni. (2022). Cognitive Training: A Field in Search of a Phenomenon. Perspectives on Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916221091830

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Statistical critique of Jaeggi et al.'s (2011) gain-correlation argument, drawing on Tidwell et al. (2014); the correlation pattern is a mathematical consequence of covariance structure, not evidence of transfer.

> "Positive correlations between gains occur every time within-sessions (i.e., same time point) covariances are bigger than between-sessions covariances. However, there is no good reason why this should be considered as evidence in favor of a training effect"

### Gobet Fernand 2022 (2)

Gobet Fernand, Sala Giovanni. (2022). Cognitive Training: A Field in Search of a Phenomenon. Perspectives on Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916221091830

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Critique of compensatory-effect arguments (e.g., Karbach et al., 2015): baseline-gain correlations are confounded by regression to the mean, and only structural models with control groups can establish true compensatory effects.

> "However, such negative correlations are likely to be, at least in part, statistical artifacts due to regression to the mean (Smoleń et al., 2018). Therefore, correlations between pretest/posttest gains and pretest scores alone cannot be considered as evidence for true individual differences in training-induced transfer effects."
