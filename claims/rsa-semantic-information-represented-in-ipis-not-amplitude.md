# Semantically associated words induce similar distributions of inter-peak intervals: semantic structure is represented in IPIs, not in oscillation amplitude, and not explained by visual word similarity

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Noguchi 2024

Noguchi, Y. (2024). Harmonic memory signals in the human cerebral cortex induced by semantic relatedness of words. npj Science of Learning, 9:6. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00221-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Representational similarity analysis of Experiment 2 animacy-judgment EEG (n = 27, accuracy 99.28 ± 0.28%): correlating fastText semantic RDM with IPI-histogram neural RDM yielded "signiﬁcant p-values after the FDR corrections" at six electrodes, showing semantically associated words induced similar IPI distributions.

> "Six electrodes with signiﬁcant p-values after the FDR corrections were as fol- lows; CP5 (p = 0.009), C3 (p = 0.007), CP1 (p =0 . 0 0 8 ), C z(p = 0.005), FC6 (p = 0.003), T6 (p = 0.004)."

### Noguchi 2024 (2)

Noguchi, Y. (2024). Harmonic memory signals in the human cerebral cortex induced by semantic relatedness of words. npj Science of Learning, 9:6. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00221-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Control analyses in Experiment 2: no significant RSI for the amplitude-based neural RDM (uncorrected p > 0.106 for all) or for the visual RDM ("uncorrected p > 0.046 for all"), leading the author to conclude semantic information was represented in IPIs, not amplitude. Equivalence was not formally tested.

> "Non-signiﬁcant RSI in this analysis (uncorrected p > 0.046 for all, right panel) indicated that visual factors had no effect on the distribution of alpha- to-beta IPIs at 300– 1000 ms."
