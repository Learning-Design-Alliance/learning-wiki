## Evidence

### Charlotte E. Wolff 2016

Charlotte E. Wolff, Halszka Jarodzka, Niek van den Bogert, Henny P. A. Boshuizen. (2016). Teacher vision: expert and novice teachers' perception of problematic classroom management scenes. Instr Sci 44:243-265. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-016-9367-z

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Chi-square comparison of semantic-prime lemma frequencies in verbatim think-aloud transcripts, analysed with TXM, found experience associated with higher frequency of both mental/perceptual and action/event word categories, suggesting "more complex thinking, perceptual processing, and attention to classroom events on the part of experts".

> "There was a signiﬁcant association between experience and word usage, v2 (8, N = 937) = 30.45, p \ 0.001."

### Charlotte E. Wolff 2016 (2)

Charlotte E. Wolff, Halszka Jarodzka, Niek van den Bogert, Henny P. A. Boshuizen. (2016). Teacher vision: expert and novice teachers' perception of problematic classroom management scenes. Instr Sci 44:243-265. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-016-9367-z

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Follow-up adjusted standardized residuals on individual primes showed experts overused 'happen' (N = 58 vs 43.8 expected) while novices underused it (N = 16 vs 30.2 expected); the article concludes experts expressed more words related to action/event processing.

> "Expert teachers used the action/event word ‘happen’ signiﬁcantly more, ( N = 58, versus 43.8 expected, z = 2.1, p \ 0.001), whereas novices used it less ( N = 16, vs. 30.2 expected, z =- 2.6, p \ 0.001)."
