# Addressing women in the feminine rather than the masculine in Hebrew improves their maths test scores and reduces the maths gender gap

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Women addressed in the feminine scored higher on an SAT-type maths test than women addressed in the masculine (54.5 vs. 59.5), while men scored lower when addressed in the feminine (70.1 vs. 64.0). [→ Tamar Kricheli-Katz and Tali Regev 2021](#tamar-kricheli-katz-and-tali-regev-2021)
`q3 i?` When women were addressed in the feminine and men in the masculine, the gender gap in maths scores shrank to 9.9 percentage points; the gap became statistically nonsignificant when both were addressed in the feminine. [→ Tamar Kricheli-Katz and Tali Regev 2021 (2)](#tamar-kricheli-katz-and-tali-regev-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Tamar Kricheli-Katz and Tali Regev 2021

Tamar Kricheli-Katz and Tali Regev. (2021). The effect of language on performance: do gendered languages fail women in maths? npj Science of Learning 6:9. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00087-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the main online experiment with native Hebrew speakers randomly assigned to masculine or feminine address forms, women addressed in the feminine scored higher; the article also reports men's scores fell from 70.1 to 64.0 when addressed in the feminine. Descriptive t-tests are reported, no standardized effect size.

> "Whereas the average score of women who were addressed in the masculine was 54.5, the average score of women who were addressed in the feminine was 59.5 ( t-test, p = 0.059, N = 383)."

### Tamar Kricheli-Katz and Tali Regev 2021 (2)

Tamar Kricheli-Katz and Tali Regev. (2021). The effect of language on performance: do gendered languages fail women in maths? npj Science of Learning 6:9. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00087-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

OLS regression (Model 1) on native Hebrew speakers born in Israel shows the male-female gap narrows to 9.9 percentage points under feminine address for women, versus a 15-point gap under masculine address; when both genders were addressed in the feminine the gap became statistically nonsignificant.

> "When women were addressed in the feminine and men in the masculine, women received scores that are lower only by 9.9 percentage points on average than men’s (Model 1; F(1,755) = 13.90, p < 0.001, N = 759)."
