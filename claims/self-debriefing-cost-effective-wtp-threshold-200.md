# Self-debriefing is cost-effective when willingness-to-pay is ≤Can$200, while instructor debriefing is preferred when WTP >Can$300

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` With WTP ≤Can$200 the incremental net benefit of self-debriefing was positive; at WTP ≥Can$300 it was negative. [→ Isaranuwatchai W 2016](#isaranuwatchai-w-2016)

## Evidence

### Isaranuwatchai W 2016

Isaranuwatchai W, Alam F, Hoch J, Boet S. (2016). A cost-effectiveness analysis of self-debriefing versus instructor debriefing for simulated crises in perioperative medicine in Canada. J Educ Eval Health Prof. https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2016.13.44

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Net benefit regression analysis of RCT data from 50 anesthesia residents, reported in the Discussion. The article reports that with WTP ≤Can$200 "self-debriefing was cost-effective (INB >0)" and at ≥Can$300 the INB became negative.

> "The results showed that with a WTP ≤Can$200, self-debriefing was cost-effective (INB >0). As the decision makers’ WTP increased to ≥Can$300, the INB became negative, which means that self-debriefing was not cost-effective compared to the traditional instructor debriefing."
