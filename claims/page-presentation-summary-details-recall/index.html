Evidence¶

Annie Piolat 1997¶

Annie Piolat, Jean-Yves Roussey and Olivier Thunin. (1997). Effects of screen presentation on text reading and revising. International Journal of Human-Computer Studies. https://doi.org/10.1006/ijhc.1997.0145

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Experiment 1 summarizing task: judge-graded details in summaries written by no-page-break participants. Page readers included more details (3.61 vs. 5.33), F(1, 34) = 3.907, p = 0.0562, just above the .05 threshold.

"they did so significantly more often in the Page condition than in the Scroll condition, which validates Prediction 6 (3.61vs. 5.33;F(1, 34) = 3.907; p = 0.0562)."

Annie Piolat 1997 (2)¶

Annie Piolat, Jean-Yves Roussey and Olivier Thunin. (1997). Effects of screen presentation on text reading and revising. International Journal of Human-Computer Studies. https://doi.org/10.1006/ijhc.1997.0145

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Experiment 1 summarizing task, important-ideas grades: Page 2.61 vs. Scroll 1.83, F(1, 34) = 1.865, p = 0.1811; the article reports this difference as not statistically significant.

"the mean grade for the Page summaries was higher than for the Scroll summaries. However, this difference was not statistically significant (2.61vs. 1.83;F(l, 34) = 1.865; p = 0.1811)."
