# First-semester dietetic students show no statistical difference between 2-D and 3-D digital food viewing skills in food identification or calorie quantification

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2022 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a first-semester laboratory exam (n = 65), food identification accuracy was statistically similar across the two formats (2-D: 89% vs. 3-D: 85%) and quantification accuracy within ±10% of calories was nearly identical (2-D: 19.4% vs. 3-D: 19.3%). [→ Ho Dang Khanh Ngan 2022](#ho-dang-khanh-ngan-2022)

## Evidence

### Ho Dang Khanh Ngan 2022

Ho Dang Khanh Ngan, Lee Yu-Chieh, Chiu Wan-Chun, Shen Yi-Ta, Yao Chih-Yuan, Chu Hung-Kuo, Chu Wei-Ta, Le Nguyen Quoc Khanh, Nguyen Hung Trong, Su Hsiu-Yueh, Chang Jung-Su. (2022). `q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

First-semester kitchen-laboratory exam of the NP course in which each student viewed standardized 2-D images (45° and 90°) and an interactive 3-D model of five food sets and estimated ingredients, weight, exchanges and calories. The study reports "no statistical difference between the 2-D and 3-D food viewing skills" with the printed accuracies above.

> "Table 1 shows no statistical difference between the 2-D and 3-D food viewing skills of food identification (2-D: 89% vs. 3-D: 85%) and quantification (within ±10% difference of total calorie) (2-D: 19.4% vs. 3-D: 19.3%)."
