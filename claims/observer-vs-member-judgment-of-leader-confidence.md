# Team members' judgments of the leader's confidence correlate with their own confidence, while observers' judgments do not

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Active team members' ratings of the leader's confidence were partly predicted by their own confidence (r = 0.61, p < 0.001), whereas passive observers' judgments were independent of their own confidence (r = 0.188, p = 0.32). [→ Stroben Fabian 2016](#stroben-fabian-2016)

## Evidence

### Stroben Fabian 2016

Stroben Fabian, Schröder Therese, Dannenberg Katja A., Thomas Anke, Exadaktylos Aristomenis, Hautz Wolf E. (2016). A simulated night shift in the emergency room increases students' self-efficacy independent of role taking over during simulation. BMC Medical Education 16:177. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0699-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pearson correlations from the simulation pilot comparing how members versus observers judge the team leader's confidence: members' judgments correlate with their own confidence (r = 0.61, p < 0.001), observers' do not (r = 0.188, p = 0.32).

> "How an active team member judges the team leader's confidence is in part predicted by his or her own confidence (r = 0.61; p < 0.001) while the confidence of the team leader judged by the passive observers does not correlate to the observer's personal confidence (r = 0.188; p = 0.32)."
