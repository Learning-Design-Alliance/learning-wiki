## Evidence

### Terregino Carol A. 2019

Terregino Carol A., Copeland H. Liesel, Sarfaty Suzanne C., Lantz-Gefroh Valeri, Hoffmann-Longtin Krista. (2019). Development of an empathy and clarity rating scale to measure the effect of medical improv on end-of-first-year OCSE performance: a pilot study. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2019.1666537

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of archived end-of-year OSCE videos at RWJMS: enhanced improv (11 volunteers), standard improv (12 randomly selected videos), and no improv (12 videos of 2014–2015 students), compared by ANOVA. The enhanced group scored significantly higher (p = .015).

> "The students who had the enhanced medical improv scored significantly higher than those with no medical improv as assessed by the LSD posthoc test (p = .015)."

### Terregino Carol A. 2019 (2)

Terregino Carol A., Copeland H. Liesel, Sarfaty Suzanne C., Lantz-Gefroh Valeri, Hoffmann-Longtin Krista. (2019). Development of an empathy and clarity rating scale to measure the effect of medical improv on end-of-first-year OCSE performance: a pilot study. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2019.1666537

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Omnibus ANOVA across the three groups (standard, enhanced, no medical improv) on ECRS mean scores was significant (F = 3.51, p = .042); the standard-versus-none post hoc contrast was not significant.

> "There was a statistically significant difference between the three groups on average ECRS scores (F = 3.51, p = .042)."
