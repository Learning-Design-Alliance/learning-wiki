# PGR students rejected GenAI generation of research ideas, original arguments, or whole papers as violating authorship norms, while accepting surface-level support

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students consistently rejected GenAI generating research ideas, original arguments, or whole papers, viewing such use as delegating core scholarly work to AI and violating authorship and originality norms, while accepting mechanical support such as language polishing. [→ Wei Dai and Cecilia Ka Yuk Chan 2026](#wei-dai-and-cecilia-ka-yuk-chan-2026)

## Evidence

### Wei Dai and Cecilia Ka Yuk Chan 2026

Wei Dai and Cecilia Ka Yuk Chan. (2026). Shaping responsible GenAI use in research through AI literacy-oriented guidelines: insights from postgraduate students. International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00609-6

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

RQ2 thematic analysis found ethical acceptability framed around whether GenAI use altered the locus of intellectual contribution; the quote names the rejected practices. Surface-level support such as language polishing was seen as enhancing efficiency without altering substantive intellectual contribution.

> "generating research ideas, producing original academic arguments, or even writing the whole paper in place of the researcher were consistently rejected by students, as these practices delegate core scholarly work to AI and thereby violate fundamental norms of authorship and originality."
