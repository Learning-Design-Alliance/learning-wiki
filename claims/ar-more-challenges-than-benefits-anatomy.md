## Evidence

### Williams A 2026

Williams A, Sun Z, Vaccarezza M. (2026). Augmented reality for teaching undergraduate human anatomy: An educators' perspective. Anatomical Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.70214

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of short-answer responses from 10 educators (Survey Part 2.3). The study reports that "Educators often felt like they might drop the iPad and found it difficult to achieve stable interactions with the models." No effect size is printed.

> "The challenging aspects of AR were centered on the limited usability of hardware and software. Educators often felt like they might drop the iPad and found it difficult to achieve stable interactions with the models."

### Williams A 2026 (2)

Williams A, Sun Z, Vaccarezza M. (2026). Augmented reality for teaching undergraduate human anatomy: An educators' perspective. Anatomical Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.70214

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of educators' qualitative feedback identifying cadaveric study as a comparative standard. The study reports that "stylised anatomical models were considered inadequate in comparison to cadaveric specimens" and that without haptic feedback educators did not perceive sufficient value in AR.

> "stylised anatomical models were considered inadequate in comparison to cadaveric specimens. Without haptic feedback or the ability to replicate real‐life interactions, educators did not perceive sufficient value in AR."
