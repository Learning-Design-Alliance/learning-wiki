## Evidence

### L. N. Simpfendoerfer 2024

L. N. Simpfendoerfer, Meagan Sundstrom, Matthew Dew, and N. G. Holmes. (2024). What topics of peer interactions correlate with student performance in physics courses?. arXiv. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ad358b](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ad358b)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

ERGM analysis of directed peer-interaction networks from survey data in six introductory physics courses at Cornell. The article reports "a significant, positive correlation between students' degree in the interaction network and their final course grade" in the lab and engineering-lecture courses; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Controlling for other structural features of the network, we find that there is a significant, positive correlation between students' degree in the interaction network and their final course grade in both offerings of the lab course and both offerings of the lecture course for engineers"

### L. N. Simpfendoerfer 2024 (2)

L. N. Simpfendoerfer, Meagan Sundstrom, Matthew Dew, and N. G. Holmes. (2024). What topics of peer interactions correlate with student performance in physics courses?. arXiv. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ad358b](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ad358b)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same ERGM analysis, physics-majors lecture course (45 and 36 students in fall and spring). The article reports no significant correlation; equivalence was not tested, and the authors discuss possible range restriction in grades (fall grades ranged only from B to A+).

> "In both offerings of the lecture course for physics majors, however, we see no significant correlation between students' grades and their interaction network degree."
