# New graduate loan limits fall below median program costs, creating an affordability gap

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Median 2020 costs for master's, doctoral, and professional degrees each exceed the new annual borrowing limits, so many students cannot cover costs with federal loans alone. [→ Victoria Jackson 2026](#victoria-jackson-2026)

## Evidence

### Victoria Jackson 2026

Victoria Jackson. (2026). New Loan Limits Threaten Graduate Education Access and Affordability. EdTrust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED682424

`q2 · i?` · `review · r1`

Policy analysis of 2020 cost data compared with the new OBBBA caps. The brief reports the median master's cost was "18% more than the $20,500 annual borrowing limit," and similarly reports doctoral and professional medians above their caps.

> "In 2020, the median cost for a master's degree was $24,250, which is 18% more than the $20,500 annual borrowing limit for graduate students."
