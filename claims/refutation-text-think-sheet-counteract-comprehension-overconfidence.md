# Learners with more misconceptions made more overconfident comprehension predictions after a standard text with a misconception list, an association not seen with refutation texts or think sheets

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the control group (a standard text with a list of the targeted misconceptions), learners with more misconceptions were more overconfident in their comprehension predictions; this association was not significant in the refutation-text or think-sheet groups. Misconceptions were measured, not manipulated, so the association does not show that they cause overconfidence. [→ Anja Prinz 2021](#anja-prinz-2021)
`q3 i?` The overall interaction between misconceptions and instructional method on bias of comprehension predictions was marginally significant. [→ Anja Prinz 2021 (2)](#anja-prinz-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Anja Prinz 2021

Anja Prinz, Stefanie Golke, Jörg Wittwer. (2021). Counteracting detrimental effects of misconceptions on learning and metacomprehension accuracy: The utility of refutation texts and think sheets. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-021-09535-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Conditional effect on prediction bias (signed difference between predicted and actual questions correct) in the randomized experiment, where instructional method was randomized and misconceptions were measured beforehand: in the control group, which read the standard text together with a list of the targeted misconceptions, more misconceptions went with greater overconfidence; the conditional effects in the other groups were not significant. Because misconceptions were not manipulated, this is an association within the control condition, not evidence that misconceptions cause overconfidence.

> "In the control group, the effect of misconceptions on bias was sig- nificant and positive, b = 0.55, SE = 0.22, t(91) = 2.56, p = .012, indicating that more misconceptions resulted in greater overconfidence."

### Anja Prinz 2021 (2)

Anja Prinz, Stefanie Golke, Jörg Wittwer. (2021). Counteracting detrimental effects of misconceptions on learning and metacomprehension accuracy: The utility of refutation texts and think sheets. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-021-09535-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Omnibus moderation test on comprehension-prediction bias in the same experiment: "∆R2 = .06, F(2, 86) = 2.76, p = .069". The think-sheet/control interaction was significant (b = −0.68, p = .031) and the refutation-text/control interaction marginal. The control condition was the standard text plus a list of the targeted misconceptions, and misconceptions were measured rather than manipulated, so the moderation describes how the misconception–overconfidence association differed by condition.

> "The analysis on bias of comprehension predictions revealed that there was a marginally significant overall interaction between misconceptions and instructional method, ∆R2 = .06, F(2, 86) = 2.76, p = .069."
