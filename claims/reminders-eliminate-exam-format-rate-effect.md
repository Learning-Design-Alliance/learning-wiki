## Subclaims
`q4 i?` In Study 3, after students received academic integrity reminders, neither the gender effect nor the exam type effect on cheating rate was significant. [→ Li Zhao 2024](#li-zhao-2024)

## Evidence

### Li Zhao 2024

Li Zhao, Junjie Peng, Shiqi Ke, Kang Lee. (2024). Effectiveness of Unproctored vs. Teacher-Proctored Exams in Reducing Students’ Cheating: A Double-Blind Randomized Controlled Field Experimental Study. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09965-z

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Study 3 (N = 191, Foundations of Psychology) added academic integrity reminders on the exam booklet cover page in both conditions of the same double-blind randomized design. Cheating rates were 9.4% unproctored vs. 16.8% proctored, but "the exam type effect was significant" was not supported: the difference was not statistically significant and equivalence was not tested.

> "Neither the gender effect ( p = 0.349, BF01 = 8.98) nor the exam type effect was significant ( p = 0.131, BF01 = 37.99)."
