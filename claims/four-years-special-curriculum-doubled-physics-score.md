## Evidence

### G.S. Djordjevic 2011

G.S. Djordjevic, D. Pavlovic-Babic, J. Stankovic. (2011). Evaluation of High School Programme for Gifted Pupils in Physics and Sciences in Serbia - Experience in Regional Cooperation - SEENET-MTP Network. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1110.5072](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1110.5072)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Two independent spring 2007 batteries for 4th graders (born 1988): an external Ministry-nominated evaluators' test (Table 2: physicists 85.68% total vs standard class 34.47%) and the Nis evaluators' test (Table 3: physicists 37.93% vs mathematicians 19.81%, standard 14.32%), showing "significantly better than “mathematicians’”" performance.

> "it is obvious  that “physicists” after four years spend in the special class with the new curricula have excellent results, and that their abilities and skills in physics are significantly better than “mathematicians’”, who were selected at the beginning of high school study ."

### G.S. Djordjevic 2011 (2)

G.S. Djordjevic, D. Pavlovic-Babic, J. Stankovic. (2011). Evaluation of High School Programme for Gifted Pupils in Physics and Sciences in Serbia - Experience in Regional Cooperation - SEENET-MTP Network. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1110.5072](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1110.5072)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparison of the same first-generation classes at the very first testing (Table 4: physicists 21.69%, mathematicians 27.83%, standard 21.34%) with their 4th-grade results; the article states physicists "has doubled their score after 4 years in comparison to ``mathematicians``".

> "Even on the first glance it is visible that ``physicists`` has doubled their score after 4 years in comparison to ``mathematicians`` and that results in respect to the ``standard class`` a re even better."
