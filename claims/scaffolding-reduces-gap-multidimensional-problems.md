# Scaffolding may reduce the gender gap on specific multi-dimensional problems: a low-scaffolding 2D projectile question shows a significant male advantage while a high-scaffolding counterpart shows none

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On a pair of 2D projectile motion questions, male students significantly outperformed female students on the low-scaffolding question L (difference 7.8, p = 0.013) but performance was equal on the high-scaffolding question H (difference 3.3, p = 0.46). ## Evidence

### Hillary Dawkins 2017

Hillary Dawkins, Holly Hedgeland, and Sally Jordan. (2017). The impact of scaffolding and question structure on the gender gap. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.020117](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.020117)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of two exam questions of different scaffolding levels, both 2-dimensional projectile motion. Table VI reports male 64.5 vs female 56.6 on L (difference 7.8, p = 0.013) and male 73.3 vs female 70.0 on H (difference 3.3, p = 0.46); scaffolding gains were comparable to those in the Cambridge study.

> "Male students very signiﬁcantly outperform female students on L, but performance is equal across gender on H."
