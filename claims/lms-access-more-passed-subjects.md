# Students who ever log into the LMS pass significantly more subjects than students who never connect, in every educational stage

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q2` quasi-experiment
> **Currency** · newest study 2019 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across all stages and within each stage separately, students who ever logged into the LMS had significantly more passed subjects than students who never logged in (Mann-Whitney, p = 0.0). [→ Fernández-Soriano Francisco Luis 2019](#fernandez-soriano-francisco-luis-2019)
`q2 i?` Descriptively, students who ever logged on averaged 9.27 passed and 1.1 failed subjects, versus lower passed and higher failed means for students who never logged on. [→ Fernández-Soriano Francisco Luis 2019 (2)](#fernandez-soriano-francisco-luis-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Fernández-Soriano Francisco Luis 2019

Fernández-Soriano Francisco Luis, López Belén, Martínez-España Raquel, Muñoz Andrés, Cantabella Magdalena. (2019). Use of Computing Devices as Sensors to Measure Their Impact on Primary and Secondary Students' Performance. Sensors (Basel, Switzerland). https://doi.org/10.3390/s19143226

`q2 · i?`

Inferential Mann-Whitney analysis comparing students who ever logged into the LMS with those who never logged in, across all stages and each stage separately; all four tests returned p = 0.0, the article concluding there are "significant differences with a 95% confidence level in the number of subjects passed".

> "The p-value obtained for the four tests is 0.0, concluding that there are significant differences with a 95% confidence level in the number of subjects passed between students who have ever logged into the LMS and those who have not."

### Fernández-Soriano Francisco Luis 2019 (2)

Fernández-Soriano Francisco Luis, López Belén, Martínez-España Raquel, Muñoz Andrés, Cantabella Magdalena. (2019). Use of Computing Devices as Sensors to Measure Their Impact on Primary and Secondary Students' Performance. Sensors (Basel, Switzerland). https://doi.org/10.3390/s19143226

`q2 · i?`

Descriptive analysis of the whole sample (Table 3) reports connectors "have an average of 9.27 passed subjects and 1.1 failed subjects" (SD 2.26 and 1.75), while students who never logged on show lower passed and higher failed means.

> "This Table 3 shows that students who have ever logged on have an average of 9.27 passed subjects and 1.1 failed subjects with a standard deviation of 2.26 and 1.75, respectively."
