## Evidence

### Hogg H. D. Jeffry 2020

Hogg H. D. Jeffry, Pereira Michael, Purdy Julian, Frearson Richard J. R., Lau Gordon B. (2020). A non-randomised trial of video and written educational adjuncts in undergraduate ophthalmology. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1923-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

As-treated comparison based on materials students actually used, on an OSCE testing visual acuity assessment with a simulated patient. The intention-to-treat OSCE comparison was not significant (p = 0.53).

> "With an as-treated analysis, students that went on to use video materials had a mean OSCE score of 90.4% (95% CI: 86.4–90.4%) compared to 83.6% (95% CI: 80.1–87.1%) for students who went on to use written materials (p = 0.03)."

### Hogg H. D. Jeffry 2020 (2)

Hogg H. D. Jeffry, Pereira Michael, Purdy Julian, Frearson Richard J. R., Lau Gordon B. (2020). A non-randomised trial of video and written educational adjuncts in undergraduate ophthalmology. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1923-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Fifty-three students (54%) completed ten-point Likert questionnaires; paired sign tests found no significant difference for knowledge acquisition (p = 0.12) but video rated higher for skill (6.6 vs 4.8, p < 0.001).

> "However, video materials were considered significantly better for skill acquisition (Table 4)."
