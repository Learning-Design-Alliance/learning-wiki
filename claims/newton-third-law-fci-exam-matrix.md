## Evidence

### Steinberg and Sabella 1997

Richard N. Steinberg and Mel S. Sabella. (1997). Performance on Multiple-Choice Diagnostics and Complementary Exam Problems. The Physics Teacher 35, 150–155. https://doi.org/10.1119/1.2344625

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Histogram analysis (Fig. 6) of 128 students in three fall 1995 classes relating the number of the four FCI Newton's Third Law questions answered correctly to success on exam problem 2. The article prints the graded percentages from 67% down to 14%.

> "the fraction of students who were able to identify and compare the N3 forces on the exam was 67% for students who answered all four FCI questions correctly, 65% for three FCI questions answered correctly, 53% for two FCI questions answered correctly, 29% for one FCI question answered correctly, and 14% for none of the FCI questions answered correctly."

### Steinberg and Sabella 1997 (2)

Richard N. Steinberg and Mel S. Sabella. (1997). Performance on Multiple-Choice Diagnostics and Complementary Exam Problems. The Physics Teacher 35, 150–155. https://doi.org/10.1119/1.2344625

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Matrix (Table I) of response combinations for FCI question 13 versus exam problem 2, the two most similar N3 items, in the same 128-student sample. The article reports "only about half the students fell on the diagonal."

> "If there were perfect correlation between the way students answered, only the diagonal elements would be non-zero. However, only about half the students fell on the diagonal, indicating that many answered the FCI question and the similar exam problem inconsistently."
