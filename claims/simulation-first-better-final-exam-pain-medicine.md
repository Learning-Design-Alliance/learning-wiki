# Students trained with simulated patients before bedside teaching scored significantly better on the final practical examination than students trained in reverse order

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Group A (simulation first) achieved a significantly better grade in the second examination than Group B (bedside teaching first). [→ Kurz Sandra 2021](#kurz-sandra-2021)

## Evidence

### Kurz Sandra 2021

Kurz Sandra, Lohse Jana, Buggenhagen Holger, Schmidtmann Irene, Laufenberg-Feldmann Rita, Engelhard Kristin. (2021). Improving competence and safety in pain medicine: a practical clinical teaching strategy for students combining simulation and bedside teaching. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-021-02554-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Prospective randomized study of 35 medical students in an elective pain-medicine course; Group A (n = 18) received simulation first, Group B (n = 17) bedside teaching first. The reported examination grades were "1.7 vs. 2.2 out of 6 points, p < 0.05"; Table 1 prints the t-test p value as 0.0043.

> "The statistical analysis of the trial showed that students of Group A were significantly better in the final examination (1.7 vs. 2.2 out of 6 points, p < 0.05)."
