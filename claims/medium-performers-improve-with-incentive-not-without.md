## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the comparison group, medium-pretest students were as likely to improve as to deteriorate on the posttest; in the incentivized group almost all improved, and about half saturated the boundary for maximal improvement. ## Evidence

### Benjamin R. Brown and Chandralekha Singh 2015

Benjamin R. Brown and Chandralekha Singh, Andrew Mason. (2015). The effect of giving explicit incentives to correct mistakes on subsequent problem solving in quantum mechanics. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.012](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.012)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Per-student gain-versus-pretest analysis shown in Figure 1 and Table II for the four repeated problems. Medium-category incentivized students gained +21.3% on average versus +1.6% for comparison students, as printed in Table II.

> "In the comparison group, students in the Medium performance category on the pretest were as likely to improve on the posttest (above the horizontal axis in Figure 1A) as they were to deteriorate (below the horizontal axis). In contrast, in the incentivized group, almost all of the students in the Medium category on the pretest improved on the posttest (see Figure 1B)."
