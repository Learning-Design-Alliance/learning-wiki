## Evidence

### Zeng Zhaolan 2026

Zeng Zhaolan, Shi Zeyao, Hou Shulin, Yan Jing, Yang Ru, Li Xiaowen. (2026). Comprehensive effects and dose–response relationship of screen exposure on university students' physical and mental health and academic performance in the digital age: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-026-27025-9

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Restricted cubic spline dose–response analysis of 17 studies (11,593 students) showing a U-shaped curve; risk rose continuously above 1.7 h/day and the rate of increase slowed above 5 h/day. No standardized effect size is printed for this finding.

> "Notably, beyond 2.5 h of daily screen time, the risk of digital eye strain increased significantly, with each additional hour of screen exposure associated with a more than 15% increase in the risk of digital eye strain."

### Zeng Zhaolan 2026 (2)

Zeng Zhaolan, Shi Zeyao, Hou Shulin, Yan Jing, Yang Ru, Li Xiaowen. (2026). Comprehensive effects and dose–response relationship of screen exposure on university students' physical and mental health and academic performance in the digital age: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-026-27025-9

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Subgroup analysis by screen type and population; by population, the association was significant in the Asia group (OR = 2.36; 95%CI: 1.81–3.07) but not in the Africa, South America, or Europe and America groups.

> "Subgroup analysis stratified by screen type showed significant associations between screen time and digital eye strain in the Personal Computer group (OR = 2.39; 95%CI: 1.64–3.48), and the Multiple Devices group (OR = 2.01; 95%CI: 1.58–2.54), whereas no significant association was observed in the Smartphone group (OR = 1.89; 95%CI: 0.50–7.15)."
