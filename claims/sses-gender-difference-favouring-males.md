## Evidence

### Batdi 2020

Batdi, V. & Elaldi, S. (2020). Effects of drama method on social communication skills: A comparative analysis. International Journal of Research in Education and Science (IJRES), 6(3), 435-457. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1258528

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive survey of 386 preservice teachers at two state universities using the 15-item Social Skills Evaluation Scale, analyzed with SPSS 18.0 t-tests and ANOVA. Item 7 mean was 3.44 (SD = 1.12); the gender difference "favouring male preservice teachers" was significant.

> "Additionally, there is a significant difference between the views of the participants in terms of gender variable ( t=2.000; p<0.05) favouring male preservice teachers. According to the results, male preservice teachers ( Mean=3.65), are more likely to give support to their friends in need than do their female counterparts (Mean=3.38)."

### Batdi 2020 (2)

Batdi, V. & Elaldi, S. (2020). Effects of drama method on social communication skills: A comparative analysis. International Journal of Research in Education and Science (IJRES), 6(3), 435-457. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1258528

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same descriptive survey (N = 386): item 10, "I take the first step in making friends", had a mean of 3.26 (SD = 1.10), at the Neither Agree nor Disagree level, with a significant gender difference favouring male participants.

> "Additionally, regarding this item, there is a significant gender difference ( t= 2.338; p<0.05) favouring the male participants. The results reveal that the male participants ( Mean= 3.49) are better than their female counterparts ( Mean= 3.19) regarding making friends easily."
