Evidence¶

Stefan A. Schreder 2025¶

Stefan A. Schreder, Helen Jossberger, Hans Gruber. (2025). Mountain rescuers' experiences with video-assisted and verbal debriefings: a qualitative study. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09716-9

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Qualitative focus group interviews after three one-day simulation trainings in the Bavarian mountain rescue organisation; "the majority (20 participants) preferred high-tech V AD" for audio and video quality, two camera angles, and timestamps making debriefings more focused. Remaining preferences are reported descriptively with no effect size.

"Of all 42 participants, the majority (20 participants) preferred high-tech V AD. In their opinion, high-tech V AD provided better audio and video quality compared to lower-tech V AD."

Related Claims¶

  • Implementing VAD raised facilitator cognitive demand, debriefing time, and attention competition with the video, with segment-search time greater in lower-tech VAD — related
  • High-tech VAD equipment made some participants feel under observation and stressed, and viewing recorded mistakes before teammates was seen as critical in both VAD forms, a potential threat to psychological safety — related
  • The outside perspective of video-assisted debriefing helped participants observe teammates and see how individual actions related to team performance, though most participants focused mainly on themselves — related
  • All interviewed facilitators preferred video-assisted debriefing over verbal debriefing because video allowed precise, objective feedback whose credibility was not restricted by facilitator hierarchy — related
  • Video evidence in VAD increased participants' acceptance of feedback and its credibility, reducing disputes over whether critical observations were correct — related
  • Participants reported preferring learning from videos over learning from digital texts — related
  • Equipment and display choices affect educational TV quality: black-on-white for fine-line charts, lapel microphone, two cameras, and a 16-inch minimum screen for 20 viewers — related
  • Low facilitator-to-student ratios (roughly 1:6 to 1:16) are preferred in CBD- and simulation-based IPE and were associated with more interaction and feedback opportunities — related
