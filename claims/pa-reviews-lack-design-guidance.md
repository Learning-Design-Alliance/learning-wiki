# Existing reviews and meta-studies of pedagogical agents provide neither a comprehensive design framework nor a consistent picture of contested design aspects

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Prior meta-studies and reviews of pedagogical agents fall short as design guidance: some ask only whether agents help, others isolate single design aspects, and none yields a consistent account of which aspects are context-dependent, under-researched, or controversial. ## Evidence

### Mateusz Dolata 2023

Mateusz Dolata, Dzmitry Katsiuba, Natalie Wellnhammer, and Gerhard Schwabe (2023) Learning with Digital Agents: An Analysis based on the Activity Theory, Journal of Management Information Systems, 40:1, 56-95, https://doi.org/10.1080/07421222.2023.2172775

`q1 · i?` · `review · r3`

Theoretical argument in the introduction motivating the systematic review: the authors argue that "the existing reviews and meta-studies neither provide a comprehensive theoretical framework for designers" because many ask only whether agents help, focus on single aspects like gesturing, or cover only subsets of studies by domain or agent type.

> "the existing reviews and meta-studies neither provide a comprehensive theoretical framework for designers when creating PA or to inform comprehensive analysis of learning with PA, nor do they yield a consistent picture of which design aspects might require careful context-dependent design or further research, or are controversial."
