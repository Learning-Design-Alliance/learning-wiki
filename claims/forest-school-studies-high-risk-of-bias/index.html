Evidence¶

Enrico Sella · Monica Bolognesi · Emma Bergamini · Lucia Mason · Francesca Pazzaglia 2023¶

Enrico Sella · Monica Bolognesi · Emma Bergamini · Lucia Mason · Francesca Pazzaglia. (2023). Psychological Benefits of Attending Forest School for Preschool Children: a Systematic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09750-4

q3 · i? · review · r3

The review's own risk-of-bias assessment of all 16 included studies using the Joanna Briggs Institute critical appraisal checklist for quasi-experimental designs, rated by two authors with a third resolving disagreements.

"Scores obtained with the JBI Critical Appraisal checklist ranged from 0 to 9 (mean 4). The overall quality of the studies was poor. Nine stud -ies (53%; mean 3.22) were judged to be at high risk of bias, and the other 7 (47%; mean 5) were judged to be at moderate risk."

Enrico Sella · Monica Bolognesi · Emma Bergamini · Lucia Mason · Francesca Pazzaglia 2023 (2)¶

Enrico Sella · Monica Bolognesi · Emma Bergamini · Lucia Mason · Francesca Pazzaglia. (2023). Psychological Benefits of Attending Forest School for Preschool Children: a Systematic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09750-4

q3 · i? · review · r3

The review's bias synthesis: only 37.5% of studies had both pre- and posttest assessment, none had follow-up, and in 87.5% the statistical procedure was inadequate (no power analysis, effect sizes, or post hoc adjustments).

"Only 37.5% paid attention to both pre- and posttest assessment, and no studies included a follow-up beyond the posttest assessment to evaluate how long the effects of forest exposure persisted."
