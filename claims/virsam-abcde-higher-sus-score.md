## Evidence

### Berg Helen 2020

Berg Helen, Steinsbekk Aslak. (2020). Is individual practice in an immersive and interactive virtual reality application non-inferior to practicing with traditional equipment in learning systematic clinical observation? A randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02030-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Usability secondary outcome from the randomized controlled trial questionnaire. The VR application "favored VR with a mean SUS score of 79.7" versus 73.3 in TP, absolute difference 6.4% points, 95% CI 2.8 to 10.1, p < 0.001; no standardized effect size printed. The authors note the A- rating corresponds to the 85-89 percentile.

> "the outcome on the SUS favored VR with a mean SUS score of 79.7, corresponding to Grade A-, and 73.3, Grade B-, in TP (absolute difference 6.4% points, 95% CI 2.8 to 10.1)"
