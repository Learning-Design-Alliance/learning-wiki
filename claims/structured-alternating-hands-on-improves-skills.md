## Evidence

### Grundgeiger 2023

Grundgeiger, T., Ertle, F., Diethei, D., Mengelkamp, C., & Held, V. (2023). Improving procedural skills acquisition of students during medical device training: experiments on e-Learning vs. e-Learning with hands-on. Advances in Health Sciences Education, 28(1), 127-146. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-022-10148-0

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 with 56 nursing and social science students randomly assigned; the structured alternating procedure produced a significant skills advantage, "p = 0.008, ηp2 = 0.124", with the learning method × test interaction also significant, p = 0.007, ηp2 = 0.128.

> "The e-Learning + hands-on group (M = 0.77, SD = 0.16) solved significantly more tasks in the skills test than the e-Learning only group (M = 0.67, SD = 0.18), p = 0.008, ηp2 = 0.124 (Fig. 4)."

### Grundgeiger 2023 (2)

Grundgeiger, T., Ertle, F., Diethei, D., Mengelkamp, C., & Held, V. (2023). Improving procedural skills acquisition of students during medical device training: experiments on e-Learning vs. e-Learning with hands-on. Advances in Health Sciences Education, 28(1), 127-146. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-022-10148-0

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Follow-up decomposition of the Experiment 2 interaction: the advantage was "significant benefits in the immediate session, p < 0.001, d = 1.01", whereas the follow-up session showed no significant difference, p = 0.327.

> "Bonferroni-corrected t-tests (alpha = 0.025) showed that the better performance of the e-Learning + hands-on group (M = 0.80, SD = 0.13) compared to the e-Learning only group (M = 0.63, SD = 0.20) is mostly due to significant benefits in the immediate session, p < 0.001, d = 1.01."
