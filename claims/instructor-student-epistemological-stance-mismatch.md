# Physics instructors and biology students have different go-to epistemological resources, creating instructional conflict

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Physics professors explained an atom-pair force question by starting from the equation F = -dU/dr, while biology students responded better to a ball-on-a-hill physical analogy, reflecting different epistemological stances. [→ Edward F. Redish 2015](#edward-f-redish-2015)

## Evidence

### Edward F. Redish 2015

Edward F. Redish. (2015). Analysing the Competency of Mathematical Modelling in Physics. [doi:10.1007/978-3-319-44887-9_3](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-44887-9_3)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Observation from a clicker question in NEXUS/Physics lectures facilitated by the author in two classes, where two professors defaulted to equation-based explanation while the author elicited responses with a physical analogy. The article reports no effect size.

> "For physicists, math is often the “go to” epistemological resource – the one activated first and the one brought in to support intuitions and results developed in other ways."
