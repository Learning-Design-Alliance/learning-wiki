# Summed scoring and IRT pattern scoring produce very similar scale score means and failing rates within any equating procedure

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across 45 administrations, score means and percentages of failing examinees were very similar between summed and pattern scoring regardless of whether chain equating, simultaneous equating, or concurrent calibration was used. [→ Jungnam Kim 2017](#jungnam-kim-2017)

## Evidence

### Jungnam Kim 2017

Jungnam Kim, Furong Gao, & Dong-In Kim. (2017). Robust IRT Scaling: Considerations in constructing item bank from tests across years. Paper presented at the annual meeting of the National Council on Measurement in Education, San Antonio, TX. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/robust-irt-scaling-considerations-in-constructing-item-bank-from-tests-across-years/

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Comparative analysis of scale score means (Figure 4) and failing percentages (Figure 5) for all 45 tests from 1992F to 2014F under six equating-by-scoring combinations; the authors report the score means were "very similar between the two scoring methods regardless of equating procedure."

> "The comparison of the solid and dotted lines of a same color indicated that the score means were very similar between the two scoring methods regardless of equating procedure."
