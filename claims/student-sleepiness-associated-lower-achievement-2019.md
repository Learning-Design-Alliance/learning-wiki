## Evidence

### Vik Frøydis N. 2022

Vik Frøydis N., Nilsen Trude, Øverby Nina C. (2022). Associations between sleep deficit and academic achievement - triangulation across time and subject domains among students and teachers in TIMSS in Norway. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-022-14161-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Two-step regression analysis of TIMSS 2019 student questionnaire data in Mplus; the effects of sleepiness on mathematics and science achievements were significant, with feeling less sleepy associated with "an increase of about 8 score points in mathematics achievement".

> "Before controlling for SES, gender, and minority status, the effects of sleepiness on mathematics and science achievements are significant. Being less sleepy is associated with an increase of about 8 score points in mathematics achievement (unstandardized regression coefficient)."

### Vik Frøydis N. 2022 (2)

Vik Frøydis N., Nilsen Trude, Øverby Nina C. (2022). Associations between sleep deficit and academic achievement - triangulation across time and subject domains among students and teachers in TIMSS in Norway. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-022-14161-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Full regression model on the 2019 student data adjusting for SES, gender, and minority status; the sleepiness coefficient "remains at about 8" for mathematics and was about 5 for science, which the authors note is a little less than half a year of schooling.

> "When controlling for SES, gender, and minority status, the regression coefficient remains at about 8. For achievements in science, the regression coefficient was about 5 after taking the control variables into account."
