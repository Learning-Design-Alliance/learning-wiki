# CLCI posttest and follow-up scores correlate more strongly with students' physics grades (r = –.42) than pretest scores (r = –.24)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Posttest and follow-up CLCI scores correlate with average physics grades at r = –.42 (p < .0001), which the authors interpret as good criterion validity. [→ Wörner 2022](#worner-2022)
`q3 i?` Pretest scores correlate with physics grades more weakly, at r = –.24 (p < .0001), plausibly due to students' lack of prior knowledge. [→ Wörner 2022 (2)](#worner-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Wörner 2022

Wörner, S., Becker, S., Küchemann, S., Scheiter, K., & Kuhn, J. (2022). Development and validation of the Converging Lenses Concept Inventory for middle school physics education. Receipt Date: 19.05.2022. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020131)

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pearson correlation of CLCI scores (15-item version, excluding item Q2) with students' average physics grades on the German 1–6 scale; the article prints r = –.42, p < .0001 for posttest and follow-up test performance, the minus sign arising because lower grades indicate better performance.

> "We calculated Pearson’s correlation coefficient for students’ pretest performance with their physics grades yielding r = –.24, p < .0001, and for each posttest and follow-up test performance with their physics grades r = –.42, p < .0001"

### Wörner 2022 (2)

Wörner, S., Becker, S., Küchemann, S., Scheiter, K., & Kuhn, J. (2022). Development and validation of the Converging Lenses Concept Inventory for middle school physics education. Receipt Date: 19.05.2022. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020131)

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same correlational analysis; the pretest correlation r = –.24 is smaller than the post/follow-up correlation, which the article calls "plausible, given the students’ lack of prior knowledge of the topic of the test".

> "We calculated Pearson’s correlation coefficient for students’ pretest performance with their physics grades yielding r = –.24, p < .0001, and for each posttest and follow-up test performance with their physics grades r = –.42, p < .0001"
