# Peer assessment systems can generate explanatory feedback for students by having graders assess multiple labeled aspects of an answer, not just an overall grade.

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Having peer graders assess multiple labeled aspects of an answer (e.g., quality of composition, specific competencies) in addition to an overall grade enables the system to return feedback explaining the overall grade. [→ Luaces 2018](#luaces-2018)

## Evidence

### Luaces 2018

Luaces, O., Díez, J., & Bahamonde, A. (2018). A peer assessment method to provide feedback, consistent grading and reduce students’ burden in massive teaching settings. Computers & Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2018.07.016

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The method asks peer graders to score a set of instructor-defined labels (annotations) about each answer, alongside the overall grade; these labels are then used to construct feedback explaining the final grade to the answer's author.

> "The proposal requires graders to assess a number of annotations or labels about the answer that they are assessing. The overall grade is another label in this context."
