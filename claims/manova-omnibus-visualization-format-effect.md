# Visualization format has a large multivariate and univariate effect on identifying motions and spatial arrangements

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` A MANOVA showed the three groups differed significantly on the multivariate level (Wilks lambda = 0.35, F = 28.84, p = 0.000, η2 = 0.407) and on both univariate dependent variables (motions: η2 = 0.552; arrangements: η2 = 0.208). [→ Rolf Ploetzner 2021](#rolf-ploetzner-2021)

## Evidence

### Rolf Ploetzner 2021

Rolf Ploetzner, Sandra Berney, Mireille Bétrancourt. (2021). When learning from animations is more successful than learning from static pictures: learning the specifics of change. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-021-09541-w

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Multivariate analysis of variance (Table 3) on the two dependent measures across the Picture, Four Pictures, and Animation groups: multivariate Wilks lambda = 0.35, F = 28.84, p = 0.000, η2 = 0.407; univariate η2 = 0.552 for motions and η2 = 0.208 for arrangements, both p = 0.000. Mechanical and spatial ability were not entered as covariates because group differences and correlations were nonsignificant.

> "On the multivariate level, the investigated groups differ significantly. The effect size is large (cf. Cohen, 1988; Rosenthal, 1994). On the univariate level, the groups differ significantly with respect to the identification of motions as well as with respect to the identification of arrangements. The effect sizes are large"
