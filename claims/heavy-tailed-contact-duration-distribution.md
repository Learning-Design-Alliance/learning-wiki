# Face-to-face contact durations at scientific events follow a heavy-tailed distribution, with 20-second contacts most common

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Cumulated face-to-face contact durations measured by RFID badges were heavily skewed, with 20-second contacts most frequent and contact duration not normally distributed. [→ Eberle 2021](#eberle-2021)

## Evidence

### Eberle 2021

Eberle, J., Stegmann, K., Barrat, A., Fischer, F., & Lund, K. (2021). Initiating scientific collaborations across career levels and disciplines – a network analysis on behavioral data. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-021-09345-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive analysis of 1,436 RFID-captured face-to-face contacts across workshops; the article notes this pattern "has been found for face-to-face contact duration measured with RFID badges in previous studies". Printed medians ranged from 1.00 to 7.83 minutes across workshops.

> "The cumulated duration of face-to-face contacts follows a heavy-tailed distribution and is neither normally nor exponentially distributed. Instead, shorter face-to-face contacts are more common with 20 second durations being the most observed face-to-face contact durations, and longer contacts being increasingly rarer."
