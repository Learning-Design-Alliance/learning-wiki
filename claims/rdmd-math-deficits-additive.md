## Evidence

### Nurit Viesel-Nordmeyer 2023

Nurit Viesel-Nordmeyer, Julia Reuber, Jörg-Tobias Kuhn, Kristina Moll, Heinz Holling, Christian Dobel. (2023). Cognitive Profiles of Children with Isolated and Comorbid Learning Difficulties in Reading and Math: a Meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09735-3

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Three-level meta-analysis of 74 studies of children aged 6–12 with and without learning difficulties. The pooled Hedges' g = 0.84 for C-RDMD in math was "comprised of C-RD (0.27) and C-MD (0.64)", consistent with additivity.

> "Overall effect sizes of mathematical outcomes (Table 4) already point to an additive effect for C-RDMD (0.84) , comprised of C-RD (0.27) and C-MD (0.64) ."

### Nurit Viesel-Nordmeyer 2023 (2)

Nurit Viesel-Nordmeyer, Julia Reuber, Jörg-Tobias Kuhn, Kristina Moll, Heinz Holling, Christian Dobel. (2023). Cognitive Profiles of Children with Isolated and Comorbid Learning Difficulties in Reading and Math: a Meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09735-3

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Equivalence testing (Lakens, 2017) with a smallest effect size of interest of |gAdd| < 0.20 checked whether additivity held practically. All three domains' 90% CIs fell "within the acceptable SESOI range for equivalence".

> "We found 90% confidence intervals of gAdd for math [− 0.08, 0.16], reading [− 0.10, 0.13], and EF [0.01, 0.19] all within the acceptable SESOI range for equivalence, supporting the notion that at the overall level, additivity was present in math, reading, and EF."
