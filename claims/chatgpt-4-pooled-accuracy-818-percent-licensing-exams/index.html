q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Meta-analysis of 53 studies using a random-effects inverse-variance model found ChatGPT-4.0 "achieving a pooled accuracy of 81.8%, compared to ChatGPT-3.5's 60.8%" in medical licensing exams. No standardized effect size is printed for this contrast, so impact is null.

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • GPT-4 achieves the highest pooled accuracy among LLMs (81%), significantly outperforming GPT-3.5 (60%) — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Large language models achieve a pooled accuracy of 72% on medical licensing examination questions across 36 studies — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Subgroup analyses show significant between-model differences, with Copilot (75.25%) and ChatGPT-4 (72.97%) achieving the highest pooled accuracies — related
  • In in-training residency exams, ChatGPT-4.0 achieved 72.2% accuracy compared to 57.7% for ChatGPT-3.5 — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Pooled LLM accuracy differs minimally by question source (71% for internal exams vs. 73% for public databases) — related
  • GPT-4 achieves passing accuracy on USMLE Steps 1–3 (mean 86%), significantly exceeding GPT-3.5 (mean 56%) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • ChatGPT performed at or near the passing threshold on the United States Medical Licensing Exam without domain fine-tuning (reported from Kung et al.) — related
  • Significant heterogeneity (I2=87.25%) exists among studies of ChatGPT-4.0 accuracy in medical licensing exams — related
  • LLMs perform worse on dental licensing examinations than on medical licensing examinations — related
