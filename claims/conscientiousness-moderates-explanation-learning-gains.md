# Conscientiousness moderates the effect of explanations on learning gains: low-Conscientiousness users tended to learn better with explanations

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` ANCOVA revealed a significant interaction between Conscientiousness and condition (explanation vs. control) on percentage learning gains, ηp²=.11, with a trend that users low in Conscientiousness learned better with explanations than without. [→ Cristina Conati 2019](#cristina-conati-2019)

## Evidence

### Cristina Conati 2019

Cristina Conati, Oswald Barral, Vanessa Putnam, Lea Rieger. (2019). Toward Personalized XAI: A Case Study in Intelligent Tutoring Systems. Preprint submitted to the JAI – Special Issue on Explainable Artificial Intelligence. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1912.04464](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1912.04464)

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA on the controlled ACSP study (24 explanation users vs. 17 control) with Conscientiousness as covariate and PLG as dependent variable found a significant interaction, F(1,37)=4.76, p=.035, ηp²=.11. The article reports "a trend of users with low conscientiousness learning better with explanations than without".

> "We found a significant interaction effect for Conscientiousness (F(1,37)=4.76, p=.035, ηp²=.11), which is illustrated in Figure 17 by binarizing users into high and low test scores for Conscientiousness, based on a median split."
