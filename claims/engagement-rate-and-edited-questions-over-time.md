## Evidence

### Binisha Marahatta and Matthew D. Sievert 2026

Binisha Marahatta and Matthew D. Sievert. (2026). Adapting Public Exams for Introductory Physics: A Mixed-Methods Investigation of Student Outcomes. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2610.03847](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.03847)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Counts of consenting students receiving extra credit for engaging with the Public Exam revision process on each of the three exams (Fig. 16). The authors note the non-monotonic pattern mirrors students' net opinions about Public Exams, with engagement measured before each exam and opinions after.

> "By this measure, the level of engagement with the Public Exams was extremely high across the three exams (100%, 81% and 95%, respectively) and fairly consistent over time."

### Binisha Marahatta and Matthew D. Sievert 2026 (2)

Binisha Marahatta and Matthew D. Sievert. (2026). Adapting Public Exams for Introductory Physics: A Mixed-Methods Investigation of Student Outcomes. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2610.03847](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.03847)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Comparison of Public Exam versions to identify which problems changed during revision, across the three exams in the 42-participant sample (11 total edited questions). The authors suggest the doubling and further increase indicates growth in deep, authentic engagement.

> "In contrast, the number of edited questions increased monotonically over time, from 2 questions edited on the first midterm, to 4 on the second midterm, to 5 on the final exam."
