# Theory-based EBP teaching produced significant knowledge gains in two nursing studies and one medical-study summative assessment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Small to moderate significant increases in EBP knowledge scores were reported in two nursing studies and one medical-study summative assessment. [→ Ramis Mary-Anne 2019](#ramis-mary-anne-2019)

## Evidence

### Ramis Mary-Anne 2019

Ramis Mary-Anne, Chang Anne, Conway Aaron, Lim David, Munday Judy, Nissen Lisa. (2019). Theory-based strategies for teaching evidence-based practice to undergraduate health students: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1698-4

`q2 · i?`

Review synthesis of knowledge outcomes measured at completion of the intervention in two quasi-experimental nursing studies; the review reports "mean difference = 0.25; p = 0.001" for Kim et al. and a post-intervention group difference for Ashktorab et al. (p < 0.0001).

> "Small to moderate significant increases in EBP knowledge scores were reported in two studies of nursing students by Kim et al. [46] (mean difference = 0.25; p = 0.001) and Ashktorab et al. [45] (intervention group mean score 45.2, SD = 3.89; control group mean score 31, SD = 7.05; paired t-test, p < 0.0001)."
