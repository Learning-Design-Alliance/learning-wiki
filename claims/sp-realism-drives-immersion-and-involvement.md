## Evidence

### García Carpintero-Blas E 2025

García Carpintero-Blas E, Del Pozo-Herce P, Hernández-Cediel MDC, Rodríguez-García M, Navas-Echazarreta N, Chover-Sierra E, Martínez-Sabater A, Juárez-Vela R, Tovar-Reinoso A. (2025). First Contact With Care Through Clinical Simulation in Nursing Students: Qualitative Study. JMIR Nursing. https://doi.org/10.2196/81617

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Theme 1 (impact of realism) of the qualitative thematic analysis. Students described authenticity of setting and clinical elements as producing total immersion; one participant said "For a moment I thought I was in the hospital."

> "This realism generated a deep emotional response, which contributed to the real experience of the simulation."

### García Carpintero-Blas E 2025 (2)

García Carpintero-Blas E, Del Pozo-Herce P, Hernández-Cediel MDC, Rodríguez-García M, Navas-Echazarreta N, Chover-Sierra E, Martínez-Sabater A, Juárez-Vela R, Tovar-Reinoso A. (2025). First Contact With Care Through Clinical Simulation in Nursing Students: Qualitative Study. JMIR Nursing. https://doi.org/10.2196/81617

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Same qualitative study, Theme 1. Participants reported that the standardized patient's performance created a meaningful connection with the simulated situation; one said "having it be an actor helps me get into the role."

> "Many noted that interacting with an actor, rather than an inert mannequin or simulator, facilitated emotional involvement and the development of more realistic relational skills."
