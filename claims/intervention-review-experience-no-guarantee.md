## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Prior experience with intervention reviews led participants to make wrong assumptions about DTAR design, most commonly misinterpreting the reference standard as a comparator. [→ Zhelev Zhivko 2013](#zhelev-zhivko-2013)

## Evidence

### Zhelev Zhivko 2013

Zhelev Zhivko, Garside Ruth, Hyde Christopher. (2013). A qualitative study into the difficulties experienced by healthcare decision makers when reading a Cochrane diagnostic test accuracy review. Systematic Reviews. https://doi.org/10.1186/2046-4053-2-32

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of think-aloud interviews found participants drew on intervention-review knowledge and "mistakenly assumed that the index test was compared to the reference standard in order to establish which of the two technologies was performing better".

> "The most common error was to misinterpret the role of the reference standard as if it was a comparator. A number of participants (GP1, GP2, R1, HE3) acknowledged that they did not know the meaning of the terms 'index test' and 'reference standard'."
