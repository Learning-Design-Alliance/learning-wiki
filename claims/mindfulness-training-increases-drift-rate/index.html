Evidence¶

Kou 2025¶

Kou, H., Luo, W., Li, X. et al. (2025). Mindfulness training enhances face working memory: evidence from the drift-diffusion model. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00389-0

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Bayesian hierarchical drift-diffusion modeling (dockerHDDM, 10000 samples, 5000 burn-ins) of DMTS responses and RTs in the randomized experiment. T2-T1 drift-rate improvements were "significantly larger improvements for the training group than the control group" for happy (Cohen's d=.56), sad (d=.79), and angry (d=.57) faces; neutral was marginal (p=.010 uncorrected) and fearful nonsignificant (p=.051).

Kou 2025 (2)¶

Kou, H., Luo, W., Li, X. et al. (2025). Mindfulness training enhances face working memory: evidence from the drift-diffusion model. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00389-0

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

T3-T1 drift-rate improvements from the same DDM analysis showed "significantly larger improvements for the training group than the control group" for happy (Cohen's d=.64) and sad (d=.55) faces, with marginal uncorrected effects for angry (d=.38), fearful (d=.38), and neutral (d=.39) faces, indicating the processing-efficiency benefit persisted one month.

Related Claims¶

  • Training-induced accuracy improvements correlate strongly with drift-rate improvements within each emotion category but weakly across categories — related
  • Five-week mindfulness training improves delayed match-to-sample accuracy for happy, sad, angry, and neutral faces, but not fearful faces, with effects persisting one month — related
  • Mindfulness training does not change reaction times, decision boundary, or response bias, with only emotion-specific nondecision-time increases — related
  • Five-week mindfulness training increases dispositional mindfulness (FFMQ) relative to a lecture-only control, persisting one month — related
