## Evidence

### Chan Enoch 2021

Chan Enoch, Botelho Michael George, Wong Gordon Tin Chun. (2021). A flipped classroom, same-level peer-assisted learning approach to clinical skill teaching for medical students. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0258926

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-class questionnaire (Cronbach's alpha 0.844) completed by 131–164 respondents; Table 5 prints Q11 mean 4.2 ± 0.7 (N = 159) on the much-worse-to-much-better scale. Perceptions are self-reported; no effect size is printed.

> "As shown in Fig 2, when asked to compare this new approach of clinical skills teaching with the existing approach, the majority of students thought the new approach was better."

### Chan Enoch 2021 (2)

Chan Enoch, Botelho Michael George, Wong Gordon Tin Chun. (2021). A flipped classroom, same-level peer-assisted learning approach to clinical skill teaching for medical students. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0258926

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Kendall's tau-b correlations between Q11 and Q6–Q8 in the post-class questionnaire, all above 0.4 and significant at p < 0.01 as printed. These are correlational, not causal, findings.

> "there were statistically significant positive correlations (tau-b > 0.4; p < 0.01) between students’ perceived superiority of this new approach over traditional approach (Q11) and the perceived usefulness of being a “coach” (Q6), being a “monitor” (Q7) and rotating between different roles within a triad (Q8)."
