# Male physics students show significant anxiety–tripartite-network connectivity correlations at pre- and post-instruction, while female students show none

## Evidence

### Ariel A. Gonzalez 2019

Ariel A. Gonzalez, Katherine L. Bottenhorn, Jessica E. Bartley, Timothy Hayes, Michael C. Riedel, Taylor Salo, Elsa I. Bravo, Rosalie Odean, Alina Nazareth, Robert W. Laird, Matthew T. Sutherland, Eric Brewe, Shannon M. Pruden and Angela R. Laird. (2019). Sex differences in brain correlates of STEM anxiety. npj Science of Learning 4:18. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0058-9

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Pearson correlations between inter-network rs-fMRI edge weights and anxiety scores, FDR-controlled at 0.25, computed separately by sex. Male students showed significant positive correlations (e.g., r = 0.311 for science anxiety and DMN–SN); the only negative correlation was clinical anxiety with SN–CEN (r = −0.343).

> "At pre-instruction, among female students, there were no signi ﬁcant correlations between any of the anxiety scores and inter-network connectivity. In contrast, male students at pre-instruction exhibited signi ﬁcant correlations between science anxiety and CEN –DMN connectivity r(55) = 0.275, P = 0.042, αFDR = 0.13), science anxiety and DMN –SN ( r = 0.311, P = 0.021, αFDR = 0.10)"

### Ariel A. Gonzalez 2019 (2)

Ariel A. Gonzalez, Katherine L. Bottenhorn, Jessica E. Bartley, Timothy Hayes, Michael C. Riedel, Taylor Salo, Elsa I. Bravo, Rosalie Odean, Alina Nazareth, Robert W. Laird, Matthew T. Sutherland, Eric Brewe, Shannon M. Pruden and Angela R. Laird. (2019). Sex differences in brain correlates of STEM anxiety. npj Science of Learning 4:18. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0058-9

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

The same FDR-controlled correlation analysis at post-instruction found no significant anxiety–connectivity correlations for female students, while male students showed significant positive correlations, e.g., spatial anxiety with DMN–SN (r = 0.435).

> "At post-instruction, no signi ﬁcant correlations were observed between anxiety scores and inter-network connectivity for female students. Male students at post-instruction exhibited signi ﬁcant correlations between spatial anxiety and CEN –DMN (r(55) = 0.381, P = 0.004, αFDR = 0.04), spatial anxiety and DMN –SN ( r = 0.435, P = 0.001, αFDR = 0.02)"
