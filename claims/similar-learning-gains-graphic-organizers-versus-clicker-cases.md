# Students in both a graphic-organizer/worksheet section and a clicker-case-study section of introductory biology showed significant conceptual learning gains, with no significant between-section difference

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Within both active-learning sections, students exhibited statistically significant IMCA learning gains with large effect sizes (Cohen's d = 0.43 and 0.85), while the between-section difference in normalized gains was not statistically significant (p = 0.077). ## Evidence

### Cleveland Lacy M. 2017

Cleveland Lacy M., Olimpo Jeffrey T., DeChenne-Peters Sue Ellen. (2017). Investigating the Relationship between Instructors’ Use of Active-Learning Strategies and Students’ Conceptual Understanding and Affective Changes in Introductory Biology: A Comparison of Two Active-Learning Environments. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-06-0181

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental matched comparison of two large-lecture introductory biology sections (ntotal = 132; 66 per section) using pre–post IMCA scores and Hake normalized gains. The between-section t test was not significant; within-group gains were significant with printed Cohen's d of 0.43 (Matthew) and 0.85 (Jennifer).

> "This analysis revealed no statistically significant difference between the student learning gains observed in Jennifer’s section of the course (M = 16.07%; SEM = 3.73%) and those observed in Matthew’s section of the course (M = 7.11%; SEM = 3.38%) (t(130) = −1.78; p = 0.077)."
