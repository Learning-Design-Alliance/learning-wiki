Evidence¶

Foster 1910¶

Foster, W. S. (1910). The Effect of Practice upon Visualizing and upon the Reproduction of Visual Impressions. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0075685

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Three-observer practice study comparing material classes. The results section reports the average final nonsense gain of 28% against a sense gain of 10%, and gives a threefold explanation including repeated elements and verbal tricks.

"gains wiui sense-material are relatively small; with nonsense material, relatively great. The average final nonsense gain is 28%; the average final sense gain, 10%."

  • Delayed-recall errors greatly exceeded immediate-recall errors, more so under the orderly procedure (4.33 times) than the random procedure (2.6 times) — related
  • In immediate recall, an orderly (fixed spatial-temporal) procedure for spelling word groups produced fewer errors than a random procedure, by 9.4% — related
