## Evidence

### Wilkes Michael S. 2017

Wilkes Michael S., Day Frank C., Fancher Tonya L., McDermott Haley, Lehman Erik, Bell Robert A., Green Michael J. (2017). Increasing confidence and changing behaviors in primary care providers engaged in genetic counselling. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0982-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Transcript analysis of standardized patient visits found both groups fell short on ELSI counseling: "only half of the participants ever talked with the SP about medical record confidentiality, insurance discrimination, and federal legal protections" and none explored social stigma. The abstract and conclusion state there was no difference between groups in ELSI discussion behavior change.

> "However, intervention doctors were more likely to engage in key shared decision-making behaviors, such as suggesting the choice of testing was up to the patient (control doctors were more likely to recommend the test), encouraging a discussion with a genetic counsellor, and asking about prostate and ovarian cancer [22]."

### Wilkes Michael S. 2017 (2)

Wilkes Michael S., Day Frank C., Fancher Tonya L., McDermott Haley, Lehman Erik, Bell Robert A., Green Michael J. (2017). Increasing confidence and changing behaviors in primary care providers engaged in genetic counselling. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0982-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Six objective structured video exercises scored 0–40 total via linear mixed effects model; total change was 3.0 (intervention) vs 2.6 (control), between p = 0.69. Both groups improved significantly within-group; equivalence was not formally tested.

> "The OSVE assessment showed that overall the intervention and Control Group both improved significantly from pre to post OSVE; neither group improved more than the other (Table 4)."
