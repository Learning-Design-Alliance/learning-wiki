## Evidence

### Joseph David 2014

Joseph David, Jehl Jean-Philippe, Maureira Pablo, Perrenot Cyril, Miller Neal, Bravetti Pierre, Ambrosini Pascal, Tran Nguyen. (2014). Relative Contribution of Haptic Technology to Assessment and Training in Implantology. BioMed Research International. https://doi.org/10.1155/2014/413951

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of the same 20 simulator-trained students across eight trials; the article reports gradual angulation improvement during guided trials and that "accuracy stopped progressing" once the virtual pointer was withdrawn. Only t and P statistics are printed.

> "Indeed, the difference in angulation was of 9.44 ± 1.16° initially and was gradually reduced to a value of 5.9 ± 0.67° after the fourth attempt (t = 2.66, P = 0.0121). When angulation virtual pointer was no longer available, accuracy stopped progressing."
