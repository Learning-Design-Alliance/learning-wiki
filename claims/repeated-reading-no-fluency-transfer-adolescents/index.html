Evidence¶

Still 2012¶

Still, K. L., & Flynt, C. A. (2012). Does Repeated Reading Improve Reading Fluency and Comprehension for Struggling Adolescent Readers? JAASEP, Winter 2012. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1135781

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Quasi-experimental 12-week study of 12 treatment students with learning disabilities in small-group English; AIMSweb pre-post percentages as printed. The article reports "17% of the participants’ wpm increased, 75% decreased"; no effect size or significance test is printed.

"when analyzing the data from AIMSweb pre- and posttest that was given, 17% of the participants’ wpm increased, 75% decreased, and there was no change with 8% of the treatment group participants."

Still 2012 (2)¶

Still, K. L., & Flynt, C. A. (2012). Does Repeated Reading Improve Reading Fluency and Comprehension for Struggling Adolescent Readers? JAASEP, Winter 2012. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1135781

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Control group of 12 special education students in a co-teach English class receiving no repeated reading treatment; AIMSweb pre-post percentages as printed. The article reports "42% of the participants’ wpm increase and 58% decrease"; no effect size printed.

"In contrast, the control group had 42% of the participants’ wpm increase and 58% decrease from the pre- and post-AIMSweb assessment that was given"
