## Evidence

### Yılmaz 2022

Yılmaz, E., Yel, S., & Griffiths, M. D. (2022). Comparison of children's social problem-solving skills who play videogames and traditional games: A cross-cultural study. Computers & Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2022.104548

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-cultural survey of 523 children aged 9-11 (255 British, 268 Turkish) comparing self-reported daily game time. The article reports "British children time spent significantly more daily time playing videogames than Turkish children (χ2=42.19, p<0.001)"; Turkish children also played traditional games significantly more (χ2=13.67, p<0.05).
