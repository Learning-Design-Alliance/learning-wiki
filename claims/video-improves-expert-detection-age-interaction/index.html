Evidence¶

Chuey 2021¶

Chuey, A., McCarthy, A., Lockhart, K., Trouche, E., Sheskin, M. & Keil, F. (2021). No guts, no glory: underestimating the benefits of providing children with mechanistic details. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00108-5

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Expert-detection task (six pairs of informant statements) analyzed with linear regression; children picked which of two informants was a real car-engine expert. Immediate (M = 4.07) > delayed (M = 3.5) > control (M = 2.67). Age interactions: t(174) = 4.37, p < 0.001 and t(174) = 2.69, p = .008.

"Children in the immediate test condition (M = 4.07, SD = 1.21) were better at detecting car-engine experts relative to those in the delayed test condition (M = 3.5, SD = 1.11), t(174) = 2.43, p = 0.048, who in turn were better than those in the control condition (M = 2.67, SD = 1.31), t(174) = 6.50, p < 0.001."
