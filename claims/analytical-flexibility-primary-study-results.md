# Analytical degrees of freedom in the primary study (prior-knowledge filtering and control-variable selection) affected its results: removing prior knowledge as a control rendered the comprehension interaction nonsignificant

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the primary study's data, the comprehension interaction p value changed from 0.025 to 0.075 when prior knowledge was removed as a control variable, and the disfluent-condition WMC effect changed from p = 0.05 to p = 0.13. [→ Sophia C. Weissgerber 2024](#sophia-c-weissgerber-2024)

## Evidence

### Sophia C. Weissgerber 2024

Sophia C. Weissgerber, Denia Indah Permatasari Terhorst, Ralf Rummer. (2024). Is the perceptual disfluency effect moderated by working memory capacity? Direct replication of Lehmann et al. (2016). Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-023-09366-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Re-analysis of the primary study's reported results by the replication authors, who identify subjective analytical decisions (filtering item solution probabilities between 10% and 90%, selecting participants with medium prior knowledge between 25% and 75%, choosing control variables) as degrees of freedom. "For example, the interaction effect for comprehension is rendered nonsignificant when removing previous knowledge as a control variable in the model. The p value of the interaction changes from 0.025 to 0.075." The retention interaction and the transfer null were robust to this choice.
