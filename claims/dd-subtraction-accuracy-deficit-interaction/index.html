Evidence¶

Rosenberg-Lee 2014¶

Rosenberg-Lee, M., Ashkenazi, S., Chen, T., Young, C. B., Geary, D. C., & Menon, V. (2014). Brain hyper-connectivity and operation-specific deficits during arithmetic problem solving in children with developmental dyscalculia. Developmental Science. https://doi.org/10.1111/desc.12216

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Two-way ANOVA on accuracy (Operation within-participants, Group between-participants) in 16 DD and 20 TD children. The interaction "was qualified by a significant Group × Operation interaction (F(34, 1) = 5.37, p = .027)", driven by subtraction impairment (DD 49.7% vs TD 73.9%, t(34) = −3.62, p = .001) versus addition (DD 65.6% vs TD 76.4%, t(34) = 1.84, p = .074).

"The significant main effects for Operation (F(34, 1) = 10.10, p = .003) and Group (F(34, 1) = 9.87, p = .003) were qualified by a significant Group × Operation interaction (F(34, 1) = 5.37, p = .027)"

Related Claims¶

  • Children with DD show greater brain activation than TD children in parietal, occipito-temporal and prefrontal regions during both addition and subtraction — related
  • DD children show operation-specific hyperactivation: greater IPS, superior parietal lobule and fusiform activity during subtraction but not addition — related
  • Children with DD show slower reaction times than TD children during arithmetic problem solving, with no operation-specific RT effect — related
  • Direct subtraction items were solved more accurately and faster than indirect addition items — related
  • Arithmetic problem-solving performance improves over 1.2 years in children, with larger gains on standard addition than control problems — related
