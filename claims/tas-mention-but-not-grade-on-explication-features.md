## Evidence

### Yerushalmi 2014

Yerushalmi, E., Marshman, E., Maries, A., Henderson, C., & Singh, C. (2014). Grading Practices and Considerations of Graduate Students at the Beginning of their Teaching Assignment. 2014 PERC Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.068

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Coding of the features TAs listed and weighed on the grading worksheet found TAs 'mentioned features from clusters which promote prescribed PS strategies' far more often than they graded on them; no effect size is printed.

> "We found a significant gap between the percentage of TAs who mentioned features from clusters which promote prescribed PS strategies and the percentage of TAs who graded on these features."

### Yerushalmi 2014 (2)

Yerushalmi, E., Marshman, E., Maries, A., Henderson, C., & Singh, C. (2014). Grading Practices and Considerations of Graduate Students at the Beginning of their Teaching Assignment. 2014 PERC Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.068

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cluster-level analysis of the worksheet data: for problem description and evaluation, '20% or less of the TAs stated that they grade on these features in both SSE and SSD', while many more mentioned the cluster without weighing it.

> "Regarding cluster C1, (problem description and evaluation), 20% or less of the TAs stated that they grade on these features in both SSE and SSD."
