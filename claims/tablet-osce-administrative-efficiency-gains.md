## Evidence

### Monteiro S 2018

Monteiro S, Sibbald D, Coetzee K. (2018). i-Assess: Evaluating the impact of electronic data capture for OSCE. Perspectives on Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40037-018-0410-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Process-related findings reported from Touchstone Institute's operational experience after the 2015 tablet introduction. The article also reports that scanning and cleaning previously delayed candidate scores by up to 3 weeks, now available immediately post-exam.

> "Typically, the manufacturing, production and quality assurance process for scan test sheets prior to an administration took approximately 3–4 weeks’ time. The tablet OSCE system reduced this amount of time to 1 week."

### Monteiro S 2018 (2)

Monteiro S, Sibbald D, Coetzee K. (2018). i-Assess: Evaluating the impact of electronic data capture for OSCE. Perspectives on Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40037-018-0410-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Operational comparison reported by the assessment centre: the tablet interface will not allow an incomplete rating form to be submitted, eliminating the track staff position previously needed to check scan sheets and reducing the margin of error.

> "In previous administrations, an error rate of between 2 to 5% was expected due to missing scores or errors in data input or scanning. For candidates assessed using tablets, there were no missing scores."
