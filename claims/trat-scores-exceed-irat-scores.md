# Students' IRAT scores were consistently poorer than their TRAT scores, suggesting team collaboration benefits team test performance

## Subclaims

`q1 i?` IRAT scores were consistently poorer than TRAT scores, which the author interprets as evidence of the beneficial nature of team collaboration on team test performance. [→ Bell 2022](#bell-2022)

`q2 i?` Across three course modules, mean t-RAT scores were significantly higher than mean i-RAT scores, with the overall average rising from 8.17 to 9.68. [→ Ngoc Lan Duong Thi 2025](#ngoc-lan-duong-thi-2025)

## Evidence

### Bell 2022

Bell, E. C. (2022). Individual Vs. team based readiness assurance testing in pharmacy calculations. International Journal of Instruction, 15(1), 733-746. https://doi.org/10.29333/iji.2022.15142a

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The author's interpretation of score patterns across the 69-student cohort, offered in the discussion rather than as a tested comparison; no effect size or test statistic is printed for this contrast. The article states IRAT scores were "consistently poorer than TRAT scores."

> "Students’ IRAT scores were consistently poorer than TRAT scores, alluding to the benefi cial nature of team collaboration to TRAT performance."

### Ngoc Lan Duong Thi 2025

Ngoc Lan Duong Thi, Quynh Anh Nguyen Ngoc, Hoang Thi Phuong Thao. (2025). A pre – post quasi-experimental study of team-based learning effectiveness for Vietnamese nursing students. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0323656

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Single-group pre-post quasi-experiment with 186 fourth-year nursing students; paired t-tests compared i-RAT and t-RAT means across three modules. The abstract reports "increasing from 8.17 to 9.68" with t = -19.507, p < 0.001; per-module t values ranged from -13.414 to -15.573 (all p < 0.001).

> "The mean t-RAT scores significantly exceeded i-RAT scores, increasing from 8.17 to 9.68 (t = -19.507, p < 0.001), indicating improved group performance."
