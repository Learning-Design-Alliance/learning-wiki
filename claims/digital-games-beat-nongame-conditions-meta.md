# Digital game conditions significantly enhance K–16 student learning relative to nongame comparison conditions (g = 0.33)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Across 209 effect sizes from 57 studies, digital game conditions showed significantly better learning outcomes than nongame conditions (g = 0.33). [→ Clark Douglas B. 2016](#clark-douglas-b-2016)
`q3 i1` The beneficial effect was similar for cognitive (g = 0.35) and intrapersonal (g = 0.35) learning outcomes. [→ Clark Douglas B. 2016 (2)](#clark-douglas-b-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Clark Douglas B. 2016

Clark Douglas B., Tanner-Smith Emily E., Killingsworth Stephen S. (2016). Digital Games, Design, and Learning: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315582065

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random-effects meta-regression with robust variance estimation of 209 pairwise media-comparison effect sizes from 57 studies of K–16 students, using pretest-adjusted posttest standardized mean differences. The printed effect size is g = 0.33 (95% CI [0.19, 0.48]), a small-to-moderate benefit.

> "Table 3 shows that students in digital game conditions demonstrated significantly better outcomes overall relative to students in the nongame comparison conditions (g¯ = 0.33, 95% CI [0.19, 0.48], τ2 = 0.28)."

### Clark Douglas B. 2016 (2)

Clark Douglas B., Tanner-Smith Emily E., Killingsworth Stephen S. (2016). Digital Games, Design, and Learning: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315582065

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Domain-specific moderator results from the same meta-regression: 173 cognitive effect sizes from 55 studies yielded a printed effect size of g = 0.35 (95% CI [0.20, 0.51]); intrapersonal outcomes (35 effect sizes, 14 studies) also averaged g = 0.35, with no significant difference between domains (b = 0.03).

> "The mean effect size for cognitive competencies was 0.35, indicating a significant beneficial effect of digital games on cognitive learning outcomes, relative to comparison conditions (95% CI [0.20, 0.51], τ2 = 0.29)."
