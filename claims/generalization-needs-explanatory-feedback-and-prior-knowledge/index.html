Evidence¶

Michael W. Asher & Paulo F. Carvalho 2026¶

Michael W. Asher & Paulo F. Carvalho. (2026). Conditions for Effective Learning Without Upfront Instruction: How Practice with Feedback Supports Memory, Generalization, Motivation, and Metacognition. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10103-6

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Study 1 preregistered regression on generalization scores found a "significant feedback type × baseline confidence interaction, b = −.08, t(291) = −2.22, p =.027"; participants one SD above the mean in confidence scored 44%, 13 points (.45 SD) above equally confident correct-response peers. Main effects of condition on generalization were not significant.

"However, there was a significant feedback type × baseline confidence interaction, b = −.08, t(291) = −2.22, p =.027 (Fig. 3), indicating that whereas students in the correct-response condition performed poorly regardless of confidence, those with higher confidence benefited from explanatory feedback."

Michael W. Asher & Paulo F. Carvalho 2026 (2)¶

Michael W. Asher & Paulo F. Carvalho. (2026). Conditions for Effective Learning Without Upfront Instruction: How Practice with Feedback Supports Memory, Generalization, Motivation, and Metacognition. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10103-6

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Study 2, with a four-item statistical reasoning pretest, found the "Lecture × Pretest interaction was significant, b =.11, t(294) = −2.35, p =.019"; the corresponding Correct-Response × Pretest interaction was not significant (b = −0.06, p =.202) though in the predicted direction. Explanatory feedback exceeded correct-response feedback on generalization by 9 points (.26 SD, p =.050).

"In addition, the Lecture × Pretest interaction was significant, b =.11, t(294) = −2.35, p =.019, further suggesting that participants with higher prior knowl- edge benefited more from instruction that included conceptual explanations provided in the lecture, compared to correct-response feedback (Fig. 6A)."

Related Claims¶

  • Correct-answer feedback alone was insufficient for generalization: significantly worse than lecture in Study 1, with the difference not significant in Study 2 — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Learners judge explanatory feedback as more effective preparation than correct-response feedback, in both studies — related
  • Practice with feedback—either correct-answer or explanatory—produces better memory than lecture, in both multiple-choice and open-ended formats — related
