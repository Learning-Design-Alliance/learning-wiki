# Academic performance in adolescents is significantly affected by the interaction of school timing, age and school subject, with better performance in later timings as students age

## Evidence

### Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante 2023

Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante, Andrea P. Goldin, Mariano Sigman and María Juliana Leone. (2023). A better alignment between chronotype and school timing is associated with lower grade retention in adolescents. npj Science of Learning 8:21. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00171-0

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Linear mixed-effect model on grades of students randomly assigned to morning, afternoon or evening school timing, assessed in 1st and 5th year. The model found "academic performance was significantly affected by school timing interact- ing with both age and school subject" (partial η2 = 0.004, a negligible effect size).

> "academic performance was significantly affected by school timing interact- ing with both age and school subject ( F1257 = 17.967, P < 0.0001, partial η2 = 0.004, 90% CI = 0.003–0.006"

### Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante 2023 (2)

Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante, Andrea P. Goldin, Mariano Sigman and María Juliana Leone. (2023). A better alignment between chronotype and school timing is associated with lower grade retention in adolescents. npj Science of Learning 8:21. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00171-0

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc pairwise comparison in the 1st year (younger adolescents) of the mixed model: "better math performance in afternoon-attending students than in their evening-attending peers", Cohen's d = 0.411.

> "we observed better math performance in afternoon-attending students than in their evening-attending peers ( t = 3.018, P = 0.007, Cohen 's d = 0.411, 95% CI = [0.144–0.677])."

### Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante 2023 (3)

Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante, Andrea P. Goldin, Mariano Sigman and María Juliana Leone. (2023). A better alignment between chronotype and school timing is associated with lower grade retention in adolescents. npj Science of Learning 8:21. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00171-0

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

In 5th year (older adolescents), post-hoc comparisons showed "evening-attending students presented better math perfor- mance than their morning- and afternoon-attending peers", d = −0.468 vs morning and d = −0.396 vs afternoon; no differences in language.

> "evening-attending students presented better math perfor- mance than their morning- and afternoon-attending peers (t = −3.432, P = 0.002, Cohen 's d = −0.468, 95% CI = [−0.736 to −0.201]"
