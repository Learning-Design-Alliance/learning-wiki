## Evidence

### Tsarava 2022

Tsarava, K., Moeller, K., Román-González, M., Golle, J., Leifheit, L., Butz, M. V., & Ninaus, M. (2022). A Cognitive Definition of Computational Thinking in Primary Education. Computers & Education, 179, 104425. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2021.104425

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple linear regression (ENTER method) in the 192-child sample with six cognitive predictors. The model "significantly predicted CTt performance and explained about 24% of variance"; verbal reasoning (β = .265), problem completion (β = .214), and visuospatial reasoning (β = .178) were significant predictors.

> "The multiple regression model significantly predicted CTt performance and explained about 24% of variance (F (6, 179) = 9.635; p < .001, R2 = .244, adj. R2 = .219)."

### Tsarava 2022 (2)

Tsarava, K., Moeller, K., Román-González, M., Golle, J., Leifheit, L., Butz, M. V., & Ninaus, M. (2022). A Cognitive Definition of Computational Thinking in Primary Education. Computers & Education, 179, 104425. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2021.104425

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Null regression findings from the same model: calculation abilities and multiplication skills were not significant predictors of CTt performance; no effect size was printed.

> "Calculation abilities (CALC; β = -.073, p = .482) and multiplication (MUL; β = .101, p = .296) skills were no significant predictors of CTt performance."
