## Evidence

### Bayley Julie E. and Brown Katherine E 2015

Bayley Julie E. and Brown Katherine E. (2015). Translating group programmes into online formats: establishing the acceptability of a parents’ sex and relationships communication serious game. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-015-2545-0

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

During translation of the WSWTTC group programme, character options and backgrounds designed by the technical team, including realistic and cartoon-style images, were reviewed by 17 parents in two parenting groups. A clear preference emerged for first person role play, a home setting and realistic characters.

> "These were reviewed by 17 parents within two parenting groups, and a clear preference emerged for first person role play (n = 16), a home setting (n = 14) with realistic characters (n = 16)."

### Bayley Julie E. and Brown Katherine E 2015 (2)

Bayley Julie E. and Brown Katherine E. (2015). Translating group programmes into online formats: establishing the acceptability of a parents’ sex and relationships communication serious game. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-015-2545-0

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

An online survey with 62 parents explored preferences for framing of feedback messages in terms of positive/negative reinforcement, numeric feedback style and extent of evaluation statements. Positive framing was preferred by 100%, percentages by 45.9% and statement plus targeted questions by 35.2%.

> "Results (see Table 2 for feedback options) showed an overwhelming preference for positively framed questions (100 %), percentages (45.9 %) and evaluation statements with targeted questions (35.2 %)."
