## Evidence

### Seyed Parsa Neshaei 2026

Seyed Parsa Neshaei, Richard Lee Davis, and Tanja Käser. (2026). Moving Beyond Review: Applying Language Models to Planning and Translation in Reflection. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1007/978-3-032-29763-1_6](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-29763-1_6)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Behavioral analysis of learner messages during the planning phase, comparing word counts in first versus last three messages with Benjamini-Hochberg corrected t-tests. Mean response length did not differ between groups (t=0.505, p=.615).

> "In TG, responses decreased from24.50words on average (SD =18.58) in the first three messages to8.47words (SD =7.23) in the last three (t= 8.205;p < .0001)"

### Seyed Parsa Neshaei 2026 (2)

Seyed Parsa Neshaei, Richard Lee Davis, and Tanja Käser. (2026). Moving Beyond Review: Applying Language Models to Planning and Translation in Reflection. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1007/978-3-032-29763-1_6](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-29763-1_6)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Content analysis of all user and CA messages in the treatment group. The article reports most users (93%) interacted with the CA similarly to how control students used static prompts, and the CA consistently ended responses with a question.

> "only three users asked a single follow-up question each, all of which were clarification questions about terms mentioned by the bot"
