# Scaffolding behaviour (pointing or demonstration) did not reduce dogs' perseveration errors in an A-not-B detour task

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Neilands P. 2021

Neilands P., Kingsley-Smith O., Taylor A. H. (2021). Dogs’ insensitivity to scaffolding behaviour in an A-not-B task provides support for the theory of natural pedagogy. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-79557-8

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

A between-subjects experiment with 30 pet dogs (10 per condition) in an A-not-B detour task. Bayesian contingency tests found no difference in perseveration errors between scaffolding conditions and control: "Pointing vs Control Bayesian contingency test: BF = 0.299".

> "Dogs in either the Pointing (Pointing vs Control Bayesian contingency test: BF = 0.299) or Demonstration conditions (Demonstration vs Control Bayesian contingency test: BF = 0.176) were no less likely to commit the perseveration error than dogs in the Control condition."
