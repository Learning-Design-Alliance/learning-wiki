## Evidence

### Turgut 2015

Turgut, F., & Kayaoğlu, M. N. (2015). Using rubrics as an instructional tool in EFL writing courses. Journal of Language and Linguistic Studies, 11(1), 47-58. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1105202

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experiment with 38 intermediate-level university EFL students: treatment group (N=16) taught with the rubric, control group (N=22) without, same instructor. Post-treatment essays were scored by three independent raters; the article reports "experimental group mean (M=74), control group mean (M = 58)" as a statistically significant difference. The printed p-value reads p>.05 despite the reported significance.

> "The results showed that  there was statistically significant difference between two groups in the sense that the experimental group distinctively performed better than the control group in writi ng composition papers; (experimental group mean (M=74), control group mean (M = 58), t(df2)=: 9.987, p>.05."
