# Grow use is consistently greater in predominantly African-American schools across Chicago

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Mapping and HLM analyses of school-average Grow scores show greater use in predominantly African-American areas and lower use in white, integrated, and Latino areas. [→ John Q. Easton 2004](#john-q-easton-2004)

## Evidence

### John Q. Easton 2004

John Q. Easton, Stuart Luppescu. (2004). Teacher and Principal Responses to the Grow Network: A Preliminary Evaluation of Use in Chicago Public Elementary Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/teacher-and-principal-responses-grow-network-preliminary-evaluation-use-chicago-public

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Descriptive mapping of school-average Teacher Use of and Belief in Grow scores (top and bottom quartiles) across Chicago elementary schools, followed by hierarchical linear modeling of teachers within schools that, per the brief, confirmed the geographic pattern. No effect size is printed for this contrast.

> "There are many more high Grow users on the Far South Side of the city, the mid-South Side near the lakefront, and the Far West Side. These are predominantly African-American areas. There is lower Grow use on the North Side in white, racially integrated, and Latino areas. Our statistical analyses confirm this eyeball inspection and show consistently greater Grow use in predominantly African-American schools."
