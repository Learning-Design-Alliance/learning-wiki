## Evidence

### Karpicke 2017

Karpicke, J. D. (2017). Retrieval-Based Learning: A Decade of Progress. Learning and Memory: A Comprehensive Reference, 2nd edition, Volume 2. https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-809324-5.21055-9

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The chapter reports Roediger and Marsh (2005), who varied the number of alternatives: more alternatives raised initial errors, and "students tended to produce the incorrect responses that they had selected on the initial multiple-choice test".

> "Importantly, on a subsequent cued recall test, students tended to produce the incorrect responses that they had selected on the initial multiple-choice test."

### Karpicke 2017 (2)

Karpicke, J. D. (2017). Retrieval-Based Learning: A Decade of Progress. Learning and Memory: A Comprehensive Reference, 2nd edition, Volume 2. https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-809324-5.21055-9

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The chapter reports Marsh et al. (2009), with college students answering SAT II questions and a final test 5 min later: there was an overall benefit, but multiple-choice "increased the likelihood that students would intrude incorrect items".

> "However, taking the initial multiple-choice tests increased the likelihood that students would intrude incorrect items on the ﬁnal short-answer test, relative to the no-test control condition."
