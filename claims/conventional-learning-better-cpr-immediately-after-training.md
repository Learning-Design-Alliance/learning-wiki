# Conventional learning produced faster first chest compression and more total compressions than flipped learning immediately after training

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

> **Currency** · newest study 2017 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Immediately after training, time to the first chest compression was significantly shorter in the conventional learning group than in the flipped learning group (29.5 vs 34.0 seconds, p=0.01). [→ Nakanishi Taizo 2017](#nakanishi-taizo-2017)
`q3 i?` Immediately after training, total chest compressions were significantly greater in the conventional learning group than in the flipped learning group (120.0 vs 101.0, p=0.005). [→ Nakanishi Taizo 2017 (2)](#nakanishi-taizo-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Nakanishi Taizo 2017

Nakanishi Taizo, Goto Tadahiro, Kobuchi Taketsune, Kimura Tetsuya, Hayashi Hiroyuki, Tokuda Yasuharu. (2017). The effects of flipped learning for bystander cardiopulmonary resuscitation on undergraduate medical students. International Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.5a2b.ae56

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary outcome from the randomized trial's immediate post-training assessment (n=54 per group, blinded examiners, mannequin-based). The quote reports medians 29.5 vs 34.0 seconds with "U=1864.0, p=0.01"; no effect size printed.

> "the time to the first chest compression immediately after training was significantly shorter in the conventional learning group than in the flipped learning group (29.5 vs 34.0, U=1864.0, p=0.01)."

### Nakanishi Taizo 2017 (2)

Nakanishi Taizo, Goto Tadahiro, Kobuchi Taketsune, Kimura Tetsuya, Hayashi Hiroyuki, Tokuda Yasuharu. (2017). The effects of flipped learning for bystander cardiopulmonary resuscitation on undergraduate medical students. International Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.5a2b.ae56

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary outcome from the same immediate post-training assessment. The quote reports medians 120.0 vs 101.0 compressions with "U=998.5, p=0.005"; no effect size printed.

> "Another secondary outcome, the number of total chest compressions performed during the 2-minute test period immediately after training, was significantly greater in the conventional learning group than in the flipped learning group (120.0 vs 101.0, U=998.5, p=0.005)."
