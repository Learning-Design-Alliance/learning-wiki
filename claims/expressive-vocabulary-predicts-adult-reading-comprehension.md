# Expressive vocabulary uniquely explains variance in reading comprehension of adult struggling readers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In hierarchical regression, expressive vocabulary entered at the final step significantly explained 16.4% of unique variance in reading comprehension beyond nonword, general, and exception word reading. [→ Hall 2012](#hall-2012)

## Evidence

### Hall 2012

Hall, R., Greenberg, D., Laures-Gore, J., & Pae, H. K. (2012). The Relationship Between Expressive Vocabulary Knowledge and Reading Skills for Adult Struggling Readers. Journal of Research in Reading. [doi:10.1111/j.1467-9817.2012.01537.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9817.2012.01537.x)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical regression predicting WJ-III Passage Comprehension (N = 232): nonword reading, general word reading, and exception word reading together explained 10.7%; expressive vocabulary added a significant 16.4% unique variance at the final step (Table 4).

> "When expressive vocabulary was added to the model in the final step, it significantly explained 16.4% of the unique variance."
