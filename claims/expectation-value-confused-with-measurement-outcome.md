# Many students mistake the expectation value of energy for a possible measurement outcome and cannot distinguish individual measurements from expectation values

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a junior-senior quantum mechanics class, only 6 of 15 students correctly identified that measuring energy of the state (psi1+psi2)/sqrt(2) yields either E1 or E2; the most common incorrect choice, selected by 27%, was (E1+E2)/2, the expectation value. [→ Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#guangtian-zhu-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)
`q2 i?` In a survey of 202 graduate students from seven universities, 67% correctly listed possible energy values and probabilities, but only 39% correctly computed the expectation value of energy. [→ Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)](#guangtian-zhu-and-chandralekha-singh-2016-2)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Multiple-choice question on possible energy measurement outcomes for a superposition state, given to 15 students; 40% chose the correct answer C and "27% of the 15 students was B" representing the expectation value.

> "The most common incorrect choice selected by 27% of the 15 students was B ( (E1 +E2)/2 ) which actually represents the expectation value of energy."
