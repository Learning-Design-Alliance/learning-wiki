# Working with a low-competency pedagogical agent raises learners' self-efficacy in the task (d = 0.49)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Students who worked with the low-competency PAL showed significantly higher self-efficacy in the task than students with the high-competency PAL (Cohen's d = 0.49). [→ Kim 2006](#kim-2006)

## Evidence

### Kim 2006

Kim, Y., Baylor, A. L., & PALS Group. (2006). Pedagogical Agents as Learning Companions: The Role of Agent Competency and Type of Interaction. Educational Technology Research and Development, 54(3), 223-243. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-006-8805-z

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

In the same 2x2 experiment, ANCOVA with prior self-efficacy as covariate showed the competency main effect on self-efficacy. Self-efficacy was measured with one item before and after the intervention; posttest means are quoted from the Results section.

> "Students in the low-competency condition ( M = 3.00, SD = 1.18) showed significantly higher self-efficacy about the task than students in the high-competency condition ( M = 2.47, SD = 0.98). The stan dardized effect size for this difference was Cohen's d = 0.49"
