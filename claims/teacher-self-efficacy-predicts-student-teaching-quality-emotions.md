## Evidence

### Daumiller 2021

Daumiller, M., Janke, S., Hein, J., Rinas, R., Dickhäuser, O., & Dresel, M. (2021). Do teachers' achievement goals and self-efficacy beliefs matter for students' learning experiences? Evidence from two studies on perceived teaching quality and emotional experiences. Learning and Instruction. Advanced online publication. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2021.101458

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Study 1 prospective design with 166 teachers and 2,106 students; latent two-level modeling with teacher motivations at semester start and student assessments at semester end. The article reports that self-efficacy "positively predicted students' end -of-course assessments" on all three positive outcomes and negatively predicted boredom; no effect size is printed.

> "teachers' self-efficacy beliefs measured at the beginning of the semester positively predicted students' end -of-course assessments of overall rating, learning, and joy, and were negatively associated with their boredom experienced in the courses of these teachers."

### Daumiller 2021 (2)

Daumiller, M., Janke, S., Hein, J., Rinas, R., Dickhäuser, O., & Dresel, M. (2021). Do teachers' achievement goals and self-efficacy beliefs matter for students' learning experiences? Evidence from two studies on perceived teaching quality and emotional experiences. Learning and Instruction. Advanced online publication. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2021.101458

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Descriptive multilevel analysis in Study 1 showing teacher-level clustering of student ratings; the article characterizes ICC1 = .17–.31 as "a medium to large effect size following LeBreton & Senter, 2008".

> "Inspection of the intra -class-correlations of the student ratings (i.e., how strongly the student a ssessments were similar for the individual teachers) revealed that this variance could be attributed to differences be-tween different teachers (ICC1 = .17 –.31;"
