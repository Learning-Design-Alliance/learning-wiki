# Students with disabilities cost 50.5 percent more to educate to comparable academic growth than peers without disabilities

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Ensuring students with disabilities achieve the same academic growth as peers without disabilities required, on average, 50.5 percent more funding on top of the general education base. [→ Doutre 2021](#doutre-2021)

## Evidence

### Doutre 2021

Doutre, S. M., Willis, J., Barrett, T., Ripma, T., Caparas, R., & Krausen, K. (2021). California State Special Education Funding System Study, Part 2: Findings, Implications, and Considerations for Improving Special Education Funding in California. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED616106

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Estimates from the study's education cost function model of California statewide data (2016/17–2018/19) found "50.5 percent more" spending was needed for comparable growth. The authors note this excludes nonacademic IEP goals, school-size variation, regional cost differences, and NPS placements.

> "It cost, on average, 50.5 percent more to ensure that students with disabilities achieved the same academic growth as their peers without disabilities."
