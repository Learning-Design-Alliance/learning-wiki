## Evidence

### Lilja Helmiina 2024

Lilja Helmiina, Talvisara Maria, Eskola Vesa, Heikkilä Paula, Sievänen Harri, Palmu Sauli. (2024). Novice providers' success in performing lumbar puncture: a randomized controlled phantom study between a conventional spinal needle and a novel bioimpedance needle. BMC Medical Education 24:520. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05505-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

After the phantom LP, all 60 participants rated seven SUS-derived statements on a 5-point Likert scale; five usability statements were compared by Mann‒Whitney U test and "consistently favour[ed] the bioimpedance needle (p < 0.001)".

> "Regarding the statements on regular use, ease of use, need for support from an experienced user, learning to use, and cumbersomeness, the responses differed significantly between groups, consistently favouring the bioimpedance needle (p < 0.001)."

### Lilja Helmiina 2024 (2)

Lilja Helmiina, Talvisara Maria, Eskola Vesa, Heikkilä Paula, Sievänen Harri, Palmu Sauli. (2024). Novice providers' success in performing lumbar puncture: a randomized controlled phantom study between a conventional spinal needle and a novel bioimpedance needle. BMC Medical Education 24:520. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05505-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same SUS-based usability questionnaire administered after the phantom LP, the confidence statement's responses "significantly favoured the bioimpedance needle (p = 0.012)" between the two randomized groups.

> "Regarding the feeling of confidence in use, the responses significantly favoured the bioimpedance needle (p = 0.012)."
