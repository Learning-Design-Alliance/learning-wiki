## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Medical students who published in the NZMSJ were significantly more likely to have PubMed-indexed publications following graduation than gender-, university- and graduation-year-matched controls (OR 3.09). [→ Al-Busaidi Ibrahim S. 2019](#al-busaidi-ibrahim-s-2019)

## Evidence

### Al-Busaidi Ibrahim S. 2019

Al-Busaidi Ibrahim S., Wells Cameron I., Wilkinson Tim J. (2019). Publication in a medical student journal predicts short- and long-term academic success: a matched-cohort study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1704-x

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Retrospective matched-cohort study of 49 NZMSJ student authors and 98 matched controls; outcomes drawn from graduate databases, medical registers and PubMed searches (October 2017). Post-graduation publication occurred in "61.2% of NZMSJ authors versus 33.7% of controls", with a printed effect size of OR = 3.09 (p = 0.001).

> "Following graduation, this increased to 61.2% of NZMSJ authors versus 33.7% of controls (Univariate OR 3.09, 95% CI 1.52–6.26, p = 0.001)."
