# California's LEA-based FAFSA/CADAA requirement for the class of 2023 coincided with a 7.8% rise in submissions and 6.7% rise in completions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Following implementation of Assembly Bill No. 469, which tasks local education agencies rather than students with ensuring completion, California's FAFSA submissions rose 7.8% and completions 6.7% among the class of 2023. [→ Sayda Martinez-Alvarado 2025](#sayda-martinez-alvarado-2025)

## Evidence

### Sayda Martinez-Alvarado 2025

Sayda Martinez-Alvarado. (2025). A Scan of State Universal FAFSA Policies. EdTrust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED672497

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

EdTrust's analysis of Federal Student Aid data for California, where the requirement falls on LEAs and students' graduation is not affected by noncompletion: "FAFSA submissions rose by 7.8%, while completions increased by 6.7%." Descriptive percentages only.

> "Following the implementation of the policy, California experienced a significant increase in FAFSA submissions and completions among the class of 2023, despite it not being a strict graduation requirement. FAFSA submissions rose by 7.8%, while completions increased by 6.7%."
