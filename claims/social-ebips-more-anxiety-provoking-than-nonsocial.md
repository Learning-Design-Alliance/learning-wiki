# Students rate instructional practices with a social component as more anxiety provoking than non-social practices

## Evidence

### Hood 2021

Hood, S., Barrickman, N., Djerdjian, N., Farr, M., Magner, S., Roychowdhury, H., Gerrits, R., Lawford, H., Ott, B., Ross, K., Paige, O., Stowe, S., Jensen, M., & Hull, K. (2021). “I Like and Prefer to Work Alone”: Social Anxiety, Academic Self-Efficacy, and Students’ Perceptions of Active Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-12-0271

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Survey of community college anatomy and physiology students (N = 227) rating 12 teaching practices on a 1–5 anxiety scale at the start of term. Social EBIPs (e.g., group quizzes, cold calling) were rated "significantly higher (2.77 ± 0.96" than non-social EBIPs, with "Cohen’s d = 0.79".

> "The mean anxiety rating of social EBIPs at the beginning of the semester was significantly higher (2.77 ± 0.96, 95% CI [2.59, 2.96]) compared with non-social EBIPs (2.07 ± 0.91, 95% CI [1.89, 2.24]) (paired-sample t test, t (105) = 8.15, p < 0.001, Cohen’s d = 0.79)."
