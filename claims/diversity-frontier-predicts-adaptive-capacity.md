# Workers closest to the diversity frontier show the strongest adaptive outcomes, while frontier position is not associated with stronger wage sorting

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Workers who achieve the highest attainable diversity for their level of specialisation display the strongest skill acquisition, occupational mobility, and AI skill adoption. [→ Carpanelli 2026](#carpanelli-2026)
`q2 i?` Frontier position is not associated with stronger sorting into higher-wage occupations, distinguishing adaptive from productive capital. [→ Carpanelli 2026 (2)](#carpanelli-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Carpanelli 2026

Carpanelli, M., Duszynski, J. & Stephany, F. (2026). Navigating the skill diversity frontier: How skill complexity explains worker resilience. Working paper, Oxford Internet Institute. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02102 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2608.02102](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2608.02102)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression analysis comparing standardised specialisation, diversity, and frontier-position coefficients across six outcomes on the LinkedIn panel. The study reports frontier workers "display the strongest adaptive outcomes, particularly skill acquisition, occupational mobility, and AI skill adoption". No effect sizes are printed.

> "Workers closer to the diversity frontier, that is, those who achieve the highest attainable diversity for their level of specialisation, display the strongest adaptive outcomes, particularly skill acquisition, occupational mobility, and AI skill adoption."

### Carpanelli 2026 (2)

Carpanelli, M., Duszynski, J. & Stephany, F. (2026). Navigating the skill diversity frontier: How skill complexity explains worker resilience. Working paper, Oxford Internet Institute. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02102. [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2608.02102](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2608.02102)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same regression framework, reward outcome. The study reports frontier position "is not associated with stronger sorting into higher wage occupations"; equivalence was not tested, and no effect size is printed.

> "Frontier position is not associated with stronger sorting into higher wage occupations, reinforcing our argument that productive and adaptive capital capture distinct dimensions of human capital."
