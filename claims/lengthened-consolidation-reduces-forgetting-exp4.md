# Lengthening consolidation time in the simultaneous condition reduces forgetting to a rate comparable to sequential presentation (Experiment 4)

## Evidence

### Ricker Timothy J. 2014

Ricker Timothy J., Cowan Nelson. (2014). Differences between Presentation Methods in Working Memory Procedures: A Matter of Working Memory Consolidation. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0034301

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 4 with 30 college students replicated Experiment 1 but presented the full array three times for 250 ms with 500-ms unmasked gaps, matching sequential timing. Only the Duration main effect "F(2,56)=8.81, p<.001, ηp2=0.24" was significant; all other effects were p>.1.

> "This was the main effect of Retention-Interval Duration, F(2,56)=8.81, p<.001, ηp2=0.24 (means; 1s=.84, 6s=.81, 12s=.78), indicating that performance was better with shorter retention intervals"

### Ricker Timothy J. 2014 (2)

Ricker Timothy J., Cowan Nelson. (2014). Differences between Presentation Methods in Working Memory Procedures: A Matter of Working Memory Consolidation. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0034301

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Visual inspection of Figure 8 means in Experiment 4 showed comparable forgetting across methods; Cowan's k estimates were 0.22 items forgotten with sequential and 0.31 with simultaneous presentation between 1 and 12 s.

> "It is noteworthy that the rate of forgetting here appears to be roughly half the rate of forgetting as in Experiment 3"
