# PLAN-to-ACT science score gains were larger under both non-traditional contexts (4.1 and 4.3 points) than under the traditional context (2.8 points)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Treatment 1 and Treatment 2 students gained more than 4 points on average from PLAN to ACT science, versus 2.8 points for the traditional baseline group. [→ Dye 2013](#dye-2013)

## Evidence

### Dye 2013

Dye, J., Cheatham, T., Rowell, G. H., Barlow, A. T., & Carlton, R. (2013). The Impact of Modeling Instruction within the Inverted Curriculum on Student Achievement in Science. Electronic Journal of Science Education, 17(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1188400

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive gain analysis (ACT minus PLAN science) for the same 808 students grouped by instructional context; no inferential test or effect size is reported for the gains, so the differences are descriptive. The article calls the improvement modest but important.

> "Students from the traditional instructional context gained only 2.8 points over their 9th grade PLAN science subscore. Students in Treatment 1 and Treatment 2 gained more than 4 points, on average, from the PLAN science subscore to the ACT science subscore (Mdiff = 4.1and 4.3, respectively)."
