## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Concept mapping outperformed conventional teaching with a post-test mean score difference of 6.56 (p = 0.000), and cooperative learning outperformed conventional teaching with a difference of 3.57 (p = 0.001). [→ Bot & Eze 2016](#bot-eze-2016)
`q2 i?` Concept mapping and cooperative learning also differed significantly from each other, with a post-test score difference of 2.83 (p = 0.012). [→ Bot & Eze 2016](#bot-eze-2016)

## Evidence

### Bot & Eze 2016

Bot, T. D., & Eze, J. E. (2016). Comparative effects of concept mapping and cooperative learning strategies on senior secondary school students' achievement in mathematics-trigonometry in Kano State, Nigeria. European Journal of Science and Mathematics Education, 4(1), 56-66. https://doi.org/10.30935/scimath/9453

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Scheffe's post-hoc analysis (Table 3) of the three-group quasi-experiment. The article reports differences of 2.83 (p = 0.012) between the two strategies, 3.57 (p = 0.001) and 6.56 (p = 0.000) versus conventional teaching; "there is a significant mean difference in the application of the two strategies".

> "the post‐test achievement mean score based on the use of concept mapping and cooperative learning strategies is 2.83 with a p‐value of 0.012 is less than α=0.05. This means there is a significant mean difference in the application of the two strategies"
