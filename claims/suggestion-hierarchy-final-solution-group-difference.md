## Evidence

### Wood 1914

Wood, B. D., & Bell, J. C. (1914). Solution of problems in geometry. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Wood+Bell+Solution+of+Problems+in+Geometry

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Final-correct-solution counts in Table I across four exercises for the two six-student groups. The authors state "in every case the good group reached a final correct solution" versus three of eighteen for the poor group, validating the Stockard-Bell selection.

> "The most striking difference revealed by the table is that in every case the good group reached a final correct solution, while in only three cases out of eighteen was this true of the poor group."

### Wood 1914 (2)

Wood, B. D., & Bell, J. C. (1914). Solution of problems in geometry. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Wood+Bell+Solution+of+Problems+in+Geometry

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Analysis of the Effective Suggestions rubric in Results. The authors attribute the poor group's reliance on objective suggestions to faulty memory from weak concentration and inability to fasten attention on essential elements of the problem.

> "The poor group were not helped by the Pure Reason Suggestions nor by the Concrete Imagery, because they could not remember what was said."
