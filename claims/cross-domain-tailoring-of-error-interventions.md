# Error-induced quitting effects in the addition game can be used to tailor error interventions in the subtraction game

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Because individual error-induced quitting effects are stable across addition and subtraction, a user's estimated effect in one domain can inform individualized intervention in the other. [→ Johansson 2024](#johansson-2024)

## Evidence

### Johansson 2024

Johansson, A. M., Savi, A. O., van der Maas, H. L. J., & Hofman, A. D. (2024). Three Strikes and Who is Out? Individual Differences in Error-Induced Quitting. https://github.com/ann1ejohansson/three-strikes

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The authors' interpretive recommendation in the Discussion, resting on the observed cross-domain stability of random slopes (r = .64). They propose using an effect estimated in the addition game to tailor interventions in the subtraction game.

> "Second, our findings that individual differences are stable across both addition and subtraction games imply that a user’s individual effect of sequential errors on quitting in the addition game can be used to tailor their individual error intervention in the subtraction game."
