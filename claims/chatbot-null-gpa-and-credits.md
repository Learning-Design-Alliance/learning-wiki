# Chatbot outreach showed no statistically significant effect on credits enrolled, earned, accumulated, or on term and cumulative GPA

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Enrollment intensity, measured by units enrolled in and earned each semester, was not significantly affected by treatment. [→ Mata 2026](#mata-2026)
`q3 i?` Neither term-level nor cumulative GPA showed a statistically significant treatment effect, consistent across cohorts and subgroups. [→ Mata 2026 (2)](#mata-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Mata 2026

Mata, Catherine, Emily Russell, and Lindsay C. Page. (2026). Sustaining AI-Enabled Student Support: A Four-Year Implementation and Impact Study. (EdWorkingPaper: 26-1409). Annenberg Institute at Brown University. https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1409

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Semester-level enrollment intensity analysis from the four-year RCT, using administrative records on credits attempted and earned from Fall 2018 to Spring 2023 for both cohorts. The article reports no significant impact on credits enrolled in, earned, or accumulated; no effect size is printed.

> "Specifically, there was no significant impact on the number of credits students enrolled in, earned, or accumulated compared to the control group."

### Mata 2026 (2)

Mata, Catherine, Emily Russell, and Lindsay C. Page. (2026). Sustaining AI-Enabled Student Support: A Four-Year Implementation and Impact Study. (EdWorkingPaper: 26-1409). Annenberg Institute at Brown University. https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1409

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Term-level and cumulative GPA analysis from the same RCT, drawing on term and cumulative GPA from CSUN's Office of Institutional Records. The article reports no statistically significant effect on either GPA measure, consistent across cohorts and the four named subgroups; no effect size is printed.

> "We observe no statistically significant effect of the text-based outreach on either measure (Table 6). These results remained consistent across individual cohorts and student subgroups, including female, Hispanic, Pell-eligible, and first-generation students (results available upon request)."
