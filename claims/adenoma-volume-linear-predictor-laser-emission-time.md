# Adenoma volume, not accumulating surgical experience, linearly predicts ThuLEP laser emission time

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Multivariate analysis found a positive linear correlation between prostatic adenoma volume and laser emission time (correlation index 0.856; R2 adjusted = 72.74%). [→ Saredi Giovanni 2015](#saredi-giovanni-2015)
`q2 i?` The number of total operations by an operator (experience) was not a statistically significant predictor of laser emission time normalized by prostate size. [→ Saredi Giovanni 2015 (2)](#saredi-giovanni-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Saredi Giovanni 2015

Saredi Giovanni, Pirola Giacomo Maria, Pacchetti Andrea, Lovisolo Jon Alexander, Borroni Giacomo, Sembenini Federico, Marconi Alberto Mario. (2015). Evaluation of the learning curve for thulium laser enucleation of the prostate with the aid of a simulator tool but without tutoring: comparison of two surgeons with different levels of endoscopic experience. BMC Urology 15. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12894-015-0045-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Statistical multivariate analysis of the 100-patient series comparing laser emission time and adenoma volume; at each 1-mL increment in adenoma volume, laser emission time increased by 0.3232 min, confirmed by Student's t-test and Shapiro-Wilk test.

> "There was a positive linear correlation between these two variables, with a correlation index of 0.856. This model explains the 73 % (R2 adjusted = 72.74 %) variability in laser emission time relative to the prostatic adenoma volume."

### Saredi Giovanni 2015 (2)

Saredi Giovanni, Pirola Giacomo Maria, Pacchetti Andrea, Lovisolo Jon Alexander, Borroni Giacomo, Sembenini Federico, Marconi Alberto Mario. (2015). Evaluation of the learning curve for thulium laser enucleation of the prostate with the aid of a simulator tool but without tutoring: comparison of two surgeons with different levels of endoscopic experience. BMC Urology 15. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12894-015-0045-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Second part of the statistical analysis on the same series: linear regression of normalized laser emission time on cumulative operator experience found no statistically significant effect; laser emission time did not change significantly across the 100-patient series.

> "Analyzing the effect of experience (calculated as the number of total operations by an operator) on the laser emission time normalized by prostate size, the linear regression showed that this variable is not statistically significant."
