# Presentation format in formative exams reverses the format effect: students score higher on text items than video items regardless of learning format

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2018 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across both formative exams, students achieved higher scores on text-presented items than video-presented items (exit exam: 78.2 ± 18.5% vs. 70.4 ± 18.9%, p < 0.001; retention test: 72.2 ± 18.4% vs. 63.4 ± 21.2%, p < 0.001). [→ Ludwig Sascha 2018](#ludwig-sascha-2018)

## Evidence

### Ludwig Sascha 2018

Ludwig Sascha, Schuelper Nikolai, Brown Jamie, Anders Sven, Raupach Tobias. (2018). How can we teach medical students to choose wisely? A randomised controlled cross-over study of video- versus text-based case scenarios. BMC Medicine 16:107. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-018-1090-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary analysis of format alignment in the cross-over study: the Friedman test across the four item types was significant for both exams (p < 0.001), and Wilcoxon tests showed "higher scores in text items than in video items" in both formative exams.

> "students achieved higher scores in text items than in video items (exit exam: 78.2 ± 18.5% vs. 70.4 ± 18.9%, T = 5.485; df = 92; p < 0.001; retention test: 72.2 ± 18.4% vs. 63.4 ± 21.2%, T = 5.845; df = 92; p < 0.001)"
