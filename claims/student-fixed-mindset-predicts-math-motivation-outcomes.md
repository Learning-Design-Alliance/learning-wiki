# Students' fixed mindsets in math positively predict test anxiety and negatively predict self-efficacy, persistence and achievement

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` At the student level, students' fixed mindset positively predicted their math test anxiety and negatively predicted their math self-efficacy, persistence and achievement. [→ Lee 2025](#lee-2025)

## Evidence

### Lee 2025

Lee, H. J., Kim, S., & Bong, M. (2025). Social antecedents of children's mindsets, motivation and achievement in math: Investigating parental beliefs and perceived teacher beliefs. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 95, 849–870. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12764

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Student-level paths from the multilevel path analysis of 507 students in 28 classrooms: "Students' fixed mindset, in turn, linked positively to their test anxiety" with β = .40 (i = 3) and negatively to the other three outcomes, with the printed standardized βs.

> "Students' fixed mindset, in turn, linked positively to their test anxiety (β = .40, p < .001) and negatively to their own math self‐efficacy (β = −.33, p < .001), persistence (β = −.11, p = .008) and achievement (β = −.12, p = .014)."
