## Evidence

### Wile Rachel K. 2023

Wile Rachel K., Riley Brian, Rodriguez Natalie, Chern Hueylan, Cruff Jason, O'Sullivan Patricia S. (2023). Home practice for robotic surgery: a randomized controlled trial of a low-cost simulation model. Journal of Robotic Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11701-023-01688-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Qualitative analysis: semi-structured debriefing interviews with all 29 students, inductively coded by two readers with consensus codebook and double-coding. The study identified "three main themes of student experience" including perceived kit impacts on simulator experience.

> "Through the participant interviews, we identified three main themes of student experience: (1) Novices encounter initial challenges with the robotic simulator that improve with exposure, (2) Practice with the home simulation kit impacts the robotic simulator experience, and (3) The fidelity of the home simulation kit could be improved (Table 3)."

### Wile Rachel K. 2023 (2)

Wile Rachel K., Riley Brian, Rodriguez Natalie, Chern Hueylan, Cruff Jason, O'Sullivan Patricia S. (2023). Home practice for robotic surgery: a randomized controlled trial of a low-cost simulation model. Journal of Robotic Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11701-023-01688-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same interview study; students described orientation and rule understanding difficulties, body positioning, instrument control including clutching and needle handling, and depth perception. Perceived transfer included improved needle control and ambidexterity.

> "Students experienced challenges upon initial exposure to the robotic simulator in three areas: (1) familiarity, (2) technical skills, and (3) visuospatial skills."
