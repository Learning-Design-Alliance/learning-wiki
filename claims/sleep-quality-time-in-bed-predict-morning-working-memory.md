# Previous night's sleep quality and time in bed predict children's morning working memory performance fluctuations

## Evidence

### Könen 2015

Könen, T., Dirk, J., & Schmiedek, F. (2015). Cognitive benefits of last night's sleep: daily variations in children's sleep behavior are related to working memory fluctuations. Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry, 56(2), 171–182. https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12296

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel two-level models (N = 110, average about 22–24 occasions per child) predicting daily working memory updating from sleep quality, tiredness, and time in bed, controlling individual daily trends. Sleep-related predictors explained 8% of morning performance fluctuations.

> "In the morning, all the dimensions of sleep behavior accounted for 8% of the children's daily performance fluctuations. Time in bed and sleep quality reliably predicted performance over and above daily trends."

### Könen 2015 (2)

Könen, T., Dirk, J., & Schmiedek, F. (2015). Cognitive benefits of last night's sleep: daily variations in children's sleep behavior are related to working memory fluctuations. Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry, 56(2), 171–182. https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12296

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel analysis of morning working memory updating as a function of the previous night's time in bed, plotted in Figure 2 centered at the person mean. The relationship was robust to outliers when tested within the 2.5 and 97.5 percentiles of the time in bed data.

> "Time in bed had a signiﬁcant positive linear and a negative quadratic ﬁxed effect, which indicates an asymmetric inver- ted-U shaped relationship (Figure 2). Performance after too little and after too much sleep was lowered."
