## Evidence

### B. Slof 2012

B. Slof, G. Erkens, P. A. Kirschner, J. Janssen, J. G. M. Jaspers. (2012). Successfully carrying out complex learning-tasks through guiding teams' qualitative and quantitative reasoning. Instr Sci 40:623–643. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-011-9185-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Planned orthogonal contrast on team performance scores between the two single-tool conditions; the test was not significant and equivalence was not tested.

> "No signiﬁcant difference was found between teams in the causal-only and simulation-only conditions, t(30) = 1.50, P[ 0.05, r = 0.26."

### B. Slof 2012 (2)

B. Slof, G. Erkens, P. A. Kirschner, J. Janssen, J. G. M. Jaspers. (2012). Successfully carrying out complex learning-tasks through guiding teams' qualitative and quantitative reasoning. Instr Sci 40:623–643. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-011-9185-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Games-Howell post-hoc tests comparing the mismatched condition to each partly matched condition on team performance; neither comparison was significant and equivalence was not tested.

> "No signiﬁcant differences were found ( t(16) = 1.01, P[ 0.05, r = 0.24, and t(16) = 1.36, P[ 0.05, r = 0.32 respectively), indicating that learning-task performance in the mismatched condition did not differ from performance in both partly matched conditions."
