## Evidence

### Brendel Elaina B. K. 2025

Brendel Elaina B. K., Alzubi Ala, Rai Shrujan, Mariathasan Christine, Snook Laelie A., Monk Jennifer M. (2025). Using a flipped classroom teaching and learning approach to promote scientific literacy skill development and retention. FEBS Open Bio. https://doi.org/10.1002/2211-5463.13938

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre-post analysis of TOSLS subcategory scores in the flipped classroom cohort; Table 2 prints significant gains such as +22.0% for research design understanding and +19.9% for justifying quantitative inferences, with individual gains "ranged from 12.1% to 22.0%" per the discussion.

> "Specifically, these skills included significantly increased capabilities in identifying a scientific argument, the validity and use (or misuse) of scientific information, understanding of research study designs and data interpretation (including graphical representations of data), comprehension and interpretation of statistics, and interpreting and applying quantitative data."

### Brendel Elaina B. K. 2025 (2)

Brendel Elaina B. K., Alzubi Ala, Rai Shrujan, Mariathasan Christine, Snook Laelie A., Monk Jennifer M. (2025). Using a flipped classroom teaching and learning approach to promote scientific literacy skill development and retention. FEBS Open Bio. https://doi.org/10.1002/2211-5463.13938

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The same pre-post analysis reports non-significant changes for creating graphs (44.8% to 58.6%, P = 0.059) and quantitative problem-solving (63.2% to 71.8%, P = 0.054), which the authors attribute to possible knowledge gaps requiring more practice.

> "Interestingly, despite increased skill competency in students' ability to create graphical representations of data and solve problems using quantitative skills, the outcome did not reach statistical significance and could reflect knowledge gaps where additional practice and instructional emphasis are required."
