Evidence¶

Spatariu 2010¶

Spatariu, A., & Winsor, D. L. (2010). A Qualitative Perspective: Pre-Service Teachers Discuss Educational Technology Online. i-manager's Journal of School Educational Technology, 6(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1102777

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Directed content analysis of 272 discussion transcripts coded in Atlas-Ti with inter-rater reliability of .90. The article reports "5 groups that had very similar trends in their rank orders" across perspective of dilemma evaluation, nature of dilemma evaluation, and support of position.

"Out of the six treatment groups the researchers found 5 groups that had very similar trends in their rank orders in three of the categories"

Spatariu 2010 (2)¶

Spatariu, A., & Winsor, D. L. (2010). A Qualitative Perspective: Pre-Service Teachers Discuss Educational Technology Online. i-manager's Journal of School Educational Technology, 6(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1102777

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Qualitative content analysis of control group transcripts found "little continuity between the groups"; if the leader used a personal experience, others followed. The authors interpret this as a lack of individual thinking and critical perspective-taking.

"The control groups varied drastically in this category, there was little continuity between the groups and typically each of the control groups took on the perspective of their leader"
