# TAs consistently overestimated how novice-like introductory students' thinking would be, expecting more novice-like errors than students actually made

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On multiple EMCS questions (e.g., Q3, Q5, Q16, Q23), sizeable fractions of TAs selected incorrect answers implying more novice-like student reasoning than students actually exhibited. [→ Liam Doyle 2026](#liam-doyle-2026)

## Evidence

### Liam Doyle 2026

Liam Doyle, Alexandru Maries, Chandralekha Singh. (2026). Exploring pedagogical content knowledge of physics teaching assistants using the Energy and Momentum Conceptual Survey. [doi:10.1103/fgqc-dfbz](https://doi.org/10.1103/fgqc-dfbz)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Question-level comparison of TA predictions with introductory student response percentages on the EMCS (Figure 2). On Q5, 23% of TAs selected an option chosen by only 7% of students; similar patterns appeared on Q3, Q16, and Q23, where TAs expected students to focus on missing numerical values.

> "it appears that on Q5 as well, the TAs may expect introductory physics students to be more novice-like than they actually are."
