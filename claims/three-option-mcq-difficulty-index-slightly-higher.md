# Reducing MCQs from five to three options slightly raises item difficulty index without a statistically significant difference

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Mean difficulty index was 0.75 for three-option items versus 0.73 for five-option items, not significant (p = 0.57), and mean test scores did not differ significantly (p = 0.54). [→ Al-lawama Manar 2023](#al-lawama-manar-2023)

## Evidence

### Al-lawama Manar 2023

Al-lawama Manar, Kumwenda Ben. (2023). Decreasing the options’ number in multiple choice questions in the assessment of senior medical students and its effect on exam psychometrics and distractors’ function. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04206-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Item-analysis comparison of the same 50 items in both formats; the DF difference was not statistically significant. Mean test scores were 37.74 ± 7.85 versus 36.53 ± 6.44, p = 0.54, also not significant; no effect sizes are printed.

> "The three-option test was easier, as the mean DF for all items was higher for three-option format: 0.75 ± 0.18 and 0.73 ± 0.17, while for the five-option test, the p-value was 0.57."
