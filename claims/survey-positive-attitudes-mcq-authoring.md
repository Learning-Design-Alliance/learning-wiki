## Evidence

### Enrique Abad 2015

Enrique Abad, Pilar Suárez, Julia Gil. (2015). A game-based educational method relying on student-generated questions. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1501.06439](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1501.06439)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Anonymous satisfaction survey conducted among the 11 students of the experimental group. The survey reports "45 % of the students" positive and 55% very positive, and all found the method useful or very useful (36% and 64%); these are self-reported percentages with no inferential test.

> "45 % of the students had a positive attitude towards the use of the method, whereas the remaining 55% had a very positive attitude"

### Enrique Abad 2015 (2)

Enrique Abad, Pilar Suárez, Julia Gil. (2015). A game-based educational method relying on student-generated questions. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1501.06439](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1501.06439)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Self-reported perceived benefits from the same anonymous survey of the 11 experimental-group students, reported in the student-satisfaction section. Most students also said the two question-upload deadlines helped them keep up-to-date with course contents; no effect sizes are printed.

> "73% think that the method helped them to improve their level of understanding of the course contents. Interestingly, almost 82% found that the method also helped them to solve problems better"
