# Both VR training paradigms improved real-task performance, with significant improvement across trial runs but no significant main effect of training group on completion times

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Completion times improved significantly across the five real-task trials (main effect of trial run, η² = 0.37), consistent with successful learning, while the main effects of group and group×trial were not significant. [→ Cooper Natalia 2021](#cooper-natalia-2021)

## Evidence

### Cooper Natalia 2021

Cooper Natalia, Millela Ferdinando, Cant Iain, White Mark D., Meyer Georg. (2021). Transfer of training—Virtual reality training with augmented multisensory cues improves user experience during training and task performance in the real world. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0248225

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Mixed ANOVA on the real tyre-change task with 42 participants randomly allocated to three matched groups (14 each), trial runs 1–5 as within-subject factor and training group as between-subject factor. The article reports a "significant main effect of trial run" with η² = 0.37, but non-significant group and interaction effects.

> "Our analysis revealed a significant main effect of trial run (F (8,156) = 22.674, p < 0.001, n2 = 0.37). The interaction between the training groups and trial was not significant (p = 0.081). The main effect of group was also found to be not significant (p = 0.284)."
