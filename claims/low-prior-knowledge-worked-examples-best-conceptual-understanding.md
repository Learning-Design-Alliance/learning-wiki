# Low prior knowledge students learned most conceptual understanding from worked-out examples, and least from questions plus arguments

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Among low prior knowledge students, studying worked-out examples yielded better conceptual understanding than the combination of open-ended questions and arguments, t(25) = 2.415, Cohen's d = 0.96. [→ Leppink 2012](#leppink-2012)

## Evidence

### Leppink 2012

Leppink, J., Broers, N. J., Imbos, T., van der Vleuten, C. P. M., & Berger, M. P. F. (2012). Self-explanation in the domain of statistics: an expertise reversal effect. Higher Education, 63, 771-785. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-011-9476-1

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Pairwise comparison within the low prior knowledge group of the randomized between-subjects experiment on the conceptual understanding test (scale 0-10). The comparison of the worked-out examples condition against the questions-plus-arguments condition shows the latter "is least effective" for this group, d = 0.96.

> "Low prior knowledge students learn most from studying worked-out examples, while the combination of open-ended questions and arguments is least effective, t(25) = 2.415, p\ .023 (Cohen'sd = 0.96)."
