# Most ESA statutes require data collection and public reporting, and many states publish annual report data beyond statutory requirements

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` ESA statutes generally mandate some form of data collection and public reporting, though the data released varies significantly across states. [→ Berry 2024](#berry-2024)
`q2 i?` In states' annual or quarterly reports, published data frequently surpassed what state statutes required, covering achievement, student characteristics, and finances. [→ Berry 2024 (2)](#berry-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Berry 2024

Berry, W. & Chait, R. (2024). Education savings accounts and accountability: A landscape analysis across states. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED663763

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Analysis of the 15 statutes finds most mandate data collection and public reporting on aspects such as assessment results, graduation rates, expenditures, and services provided, with significant variation in what states release publicly.

> "ESA statutes generally mandate some form of data collection and public reporting to monitor ESA funds."

### Berry 2024 (2)

Berry, W. & Chait, R. (2024). Education savings accounts and accountability: A landscape analysis across states. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED663763

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

WestEd reviewed annual reports from Arizona, Florida, Indiana, New Hampshire, North Carolina, Tennessee (ESA Pilot), and West Virginia, finding reports frequently exceed statutory requirements; Tables 3a-3c map reported content on test scores, demographics (grade level, gender, race/ethnicity, disability type, location), and spending.

> "Frequently, these annual reports published data that surpassed what was required by their state statutes."
