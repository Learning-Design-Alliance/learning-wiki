Evidence¶

Nathan Dwyer & Daniel D. Suthers 2006¶

Nathan Dwyer & Daniel D. Suthers. (2006). Consistent practices in artifact-mediated collaboration. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning 1: 481–511. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-006-9001-1

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Video analysis of the six sessions; the authors observe that partners "frequently contributed to the workspace simultaneously," with pairs using separate cards or sheet areas for concurrent threads.

"Unlike the alternating exchange of contributions postulated by models of face-to-face conversation (Gerbner, 1956; Jakobson, 1960), partners frequently contributed to the workspace simultaneously."

Nathan Dwyer & Daniel D. Suthers 2006 (2)¶

Nathan Dwyer & Daniel D. Suthers. (2006). Consistent practices in artifact-mediated collaboration. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning 1: 481–511. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-006-9001-1

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

In the same observational analysis, the S2 and S6 pairs exploited the speed asymmetry of reading versus writing: they "would frequently read their partner ’s partially completed contribution and then start writing a reply."

"Since reading the contribution took much less time than writing it, the S2 and S6 participants would frequently read their partner ’s partially completed contribution and then start writing a reply."
