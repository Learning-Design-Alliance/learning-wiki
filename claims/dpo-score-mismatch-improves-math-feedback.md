## Evidence

### Alexander Scarlatos 2024

Alexander Scarlatos, Digory Smith, Simon Woodhead, and Andrew Lan. (2024). Improving the Validity of Automatically Generated Feedback via Reinforcement Learning. Published in AIED 2024. [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-64302-6_20](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-64302-6_20)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Quantitative evaluation in §4.3 of feedback generated by Llama 2 7B variants on the Eedi test set, scored by GPT-4 on the rubric. The article reports a "45% increase compared to Zero-Shot and SFT" for DPO (Score + Mismatch); no standardized effect size is printed.

> "We see that DPO (Score + M ismatch) sig- niﬁcantly improves the feedback scores compared to baselin es (a 45% increase compared to Zero-Shot and SFT), showing that our data augmen tation and training setup is highly eﬀective at improving the quality o f feedback generation with Llama 2."

### Alexander Scarlatos 2024 (2)

Alexander Scarlatos, Digory Smith, Simon Woodhead, and Andrew Lan. (2024). Improving the Validity of Automatically Generated Feedback via Reinforcement Learning. Published in AIED 2024. [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-64302-6_20](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-64302-6_20)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Comparison of DPO (Score) versus DPO (Score + Mismatch) in the same GPT-4-scored test-set evaluation; the article reports including "mismatched feedback messages substantially increases the correctness" of generated feedback. No effect size is printed.

> "We additionally observe that including the mis matched feedback messages substantially increases the correctness of gener ated feedback, conﬁrm- ing their eﬀectiveness as hard negative examples."
