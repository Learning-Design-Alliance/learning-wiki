# Roundtable debriefing in the emergency department produced no statistically significant reduction in patient fall rates

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Although fall rates declined somewhat postintervention, there were no statistically significant differences in assisted falls (P = 0.17), unassisted falls (P = 0.28), or falls per 1000 patient encounters (P = 0.28). [→ Costar 2020](#costar-2020)

## Evidence

### Costar 2020

Costar, D. M., & Hall, K. K. (2020). Improving Team Performance and Patient Safety on the Job Through Team Training and Performance Support Tools: A Systematic Review. Journal of Patient Safety. https://doi.org/10.1097/PTS.0000000000000746

`q2 · i?`

The review reports a quality-improvement study of roundtable debriefing on emergency department fall rates: "no statistically significant differences" on assisted falls, unassisted falls, or falls per 1000 patient encounters. Only p-values are printed; equivalence was not tested.

> "Although the patient fall rates did decline somewhat in the postintervention period, there were no statistically significant differences in the number of assisted falls (P = 0.17), unassisted falls (P = 0.28), and the rate of falls per 1000 patient encounters (P = 0.28) as compared with the preintervention period."
