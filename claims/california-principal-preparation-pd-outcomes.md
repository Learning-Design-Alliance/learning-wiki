# In a large California study, principals' preparation quality and professional development access predicted teacher retention and student achievement gains

## Evidence

### Darling-Hammond 2026

Darling-Hammond, L., Fitz, J., Giani, M. V., Gordon, M. F., & Wechsler, M. (2026). The principal effect: How investing in school leaders is key to solving education's challenges. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/644.197

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

A large-scale California study reported in the review found principals with high-quality preparation had better teacher retention than peers with low-quality preparation, controlling for teacher traits and school conditions: "was 89%" versus "dropped to 78%."

> "The probability of a teacher remaining through the following year in a school served by a well-prepared principal was 89%, whereas the likelihood of remaining in a school served by a poorly prepared principal dropped to 78%."

### Darling-Hammond 2026 (2)

Darling-Hammond, L., Fitz, J., Giani, M. V., Gordon, M. F., & Wechsler, M. (2026). The principal effect: How investing in school leaders is key to solving education's challenges. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/644.197

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

In the California study, students of color from historically underserved communities experienced the largest gains, especially with principals having substantial instructional-leadership professional development; the differential was "equivalent to approximately 4 months of instruction."

> "The differentials in mathematics learning for historically underserved students who had a principal with little versus extensive training in this area, for example, were equivalent to approximately 4 months of instruction."

### Darling-Hammond 2026 (3)

Darling-Hammond, L., Fitz, J., Giani, M. V., Gordon, M. F., & Wechsler, M. (2026). The principal effect: How investing in school leaders is key to solving education's challenges. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/644.197

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The California study found early-career principals with extensive professional development produced mathematics gains "greater than those in schools led by more senior principals with similar access to professional development," suggesting intensive PD can help close the gap for new principals.

> "students in schools led by early-career principals who experienced extensive professional development—including on instructional leadership, teaching diverse learners, and managing change—demonstrated achievement gains in mathematics greater than those in schools led by more senior principals with similar access to professional development."
