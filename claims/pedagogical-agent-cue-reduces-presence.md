## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The pedagogical agent cue lowered self-presence relative to both the no-cue and light-cue conditions, and lowered physical presence relative to no cueing; social presence was unaffected. [→ Daniela Decker 2025](#daniela-decker-2025)

## Evidence

### Daniela Decker 2025

Daniela Decker, Martin Merkt. (2025). Look at me! Can a pedagogical agent facilitate orientation and support learning in VR? Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10531-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Presence was measured with the Makransky et al. (2017) scale in the 91-participant VR experiment. Physical presence showed a significant cueing effect, F(2,88) = 4.74, p =.011, ηp2 = 0.10, with the agent condition lower than no cueing; self-presence showed F(2, 88) = 4.47, p =.014, ηp2 =.09, with "lower self-presence for the pedagogical agent cueing condition" versus both other conditions.

> "Bonferroni-adjusted post-hoc tests also showed lower self-presence for the pedagogical agent cueing condition (M = 2.60, SD = 0.77) compared to the condition without cueing (M = 3.21, SD = 1.00), p =.039, and the light cueing condition (M = 3.24, SD = 0.98), p =.027."
