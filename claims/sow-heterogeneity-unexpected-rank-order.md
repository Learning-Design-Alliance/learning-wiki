## Evidence

### Buchan 2020

Buchan, L., Hejmadi, M., Abrahams, L. & Hurst, L. D. (2020). A RCT for assessment of active human-centred learning finds teacher-centric non-human teaching of evolution optimal. npj Science of Learning 5:19. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-020-00078-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Kruskal–Wallis rank-sum tests on LOESS residual scores with Dunn post-hoc and Bonferroni correction across the four schemes in both tranches. The significant heterogeneity shows "the four SoW do not have the same effectiveness".

> "There is significant heterogeneity in the relative effectiveness of the four SoW, seen in both tranches of data (tranche 1: χ2 = 37.53, P = 3.56 × 10−8; tranche 2: χ2 = 40.91, P = 6.84 × 10−9, Kruskal–Wallis rank-sum test on residuals with Dunn post-hoc and Bonferroni correction) (Table 6; Fig. 4)."

### Buchan 2020 (2)

Buchan, L., Hejmadi, M., Abrahams, L. & Hurst, L. D. (2020). A RCT for assessment of active human-centred learning finds teacher-centric non-human teaching of evolution optimal. npj Science of Learning 5:19. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-020-00078-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Dunn post-hoc pairwise comparisons (Table 6) revealed the observed rank orders. SoW 3 (teacher-centred PowerPoint, trilobites) outperformed expectations, while SoW 1 (student-centred, non-human) was significantly least effective relative to other schemes in both tranches.

> "In tranche 1, the order of relative effectiveness of the schemes is 1 < 4 < 2 < 3 and in tranche 2, the order was 1 < 4 < 3 < 2 (Table 6). The most striking insight here is that the scheme we expected to be the least effective (SoW 3) was either the most effective or the next most effective."
