# Most praise functions as a verbal reward, creating dependence on others' approval rather than self-generated criteria

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Most praise is tantamount to verbal reward: it makes approval conditional, can create dependence on securing someone else's approval, and devalues learning as a prerequisite for teacher approval. [→ Kohn 1994](#kohn-1994)

The digest argues, citing Kohn (1993), that "most praise is tantamount to verbal reward," making positive responses conditional on adult demands and devaluing learning. This is the author's interpretive argument, not a tested result; no data are offered.

> "most praise is tantamount to verbal reward. Rather than helping children to develop their own criteria for successful learning or desirable behavior, praise can create a growing dependence on securing someone else's approval."
