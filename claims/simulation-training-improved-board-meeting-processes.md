# Simulation-based training improved health service board members' perceptions of board meeting processes at 3 months (d=0.54)

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In a cluster randomised trial of 12 health service boards, board meeting processes (as perceived by members) improved more in the intervention arm than in the wait list control arm at 3 months, d=0.54, one-sided p=0.005. [→ Faulkner Nicholas 2020](#faulkner-nicholas-2020)

## Evidence

### Faulkner Nicholas 2020

Faulkner Nicholas, Wright Breanna, Lennox Alyse, Bismark Marie, Boag Jane, Boffa Sophie, Waxman Bruce, Watson-Kruse Janet, Paine Geoff, Bragge Peter. (2020). Simulation-based training for increasing health service board members' effectiveness: a cluster randomised controlled trial. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2019-034994

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Cluster randomised controlled trial with 12 boards and 57 members analysed, GEE controlling for time 1 scores and region. The GEE analysis found the intervention significantly improved board meeting processes (b=0.40, 90% CI (0.14 to 0.65), p=0.005), described as a "'medium' effect on board meeting processes (d=0.54)".

> "Board meeting processes were also improved after training (control marginal mean=4.97, intervention marginal mean=5.37, mean difference=0.40, 90% CI (0.14 to 0.65), one-sided p=0.005, d=0.54)."
