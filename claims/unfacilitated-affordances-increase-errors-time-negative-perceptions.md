## Evidence

### Murano 2011

Murano, P. & Sethi, T. (2011). Anthropomorphic User Interface Feedback in a Sewing Context and Affordances. International Journal of Advanced Computer Science and Applications, Vol. 2, No. 4. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1208.3323](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1208.3323)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' conclusion drawn from the MANOVA results of the 40-participant sewing experiment, in which the non-anthropomorphic condition was deliberately designed to subtly violate affordances. The perception results (tables VII–XX) show the non-anthropomorphic feedback rated more negatively, with users feeling less satisfied, less stimulated, less confident, and perceiving less understanding.

> "When particularly the cognitive affordances and sensory affordances are not facilitated, the number of errors and the time taken to complete a task are significantly increased, particularly when the task has a degree of difficulty."
