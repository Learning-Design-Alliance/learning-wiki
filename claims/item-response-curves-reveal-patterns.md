## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith 2020

Trevor I. Smith, Philip Eaton, Suzanne White Brahmia, Alexis Olsho, Charlotte Zimmerman, Andrew Boudreaux. (2020). Toward a valid instrument for measuring physics quantitative literacy. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.smith_t](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.smith_t)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Item Response Curves for three PIQL v2.2 items (Fig. 3) show fraction choosing each answer by total score. Item 14 shows even fairly high-scoring students persist in choosing incorrect answer F; on item 17 fewer than 20% of above-average scorers chose either incorrect response.

> "Item 18 is particularly interesting in that all responses are chosen by 20%–60% of students in the middle score range (8-12). This supports the results from Fig. 2 that students are likely to choose both a correct and an incorrect response to Q18."
