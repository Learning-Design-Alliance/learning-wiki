# Perceptual metacognitive efficiency declines with age in healthy adults aged 18–84, even when task performance is controlled

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Perceptual metacognitive efficiency (meta-d′/d′) shows a significant negative correlation with age (r = −0.38, p = 0.005) despite staircase-controlled task performance. [→ Palmer E.C. 2014](#palmer-ec-2014)

## Evidence

### Palmer E.C. 2014

Palmer E.C., David A.S., Fleming S.M. (2014). Effects of age on metacognitive efficiency. Consciousness and Cognition. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4154452/

`q3 · i3`

Cross-sectional correlational analysis of 53 adults aged 18–84 performing a staircase-controlled perceptual task. The article reports "a significant negative relationship between perceptual metacognitive efficiency (meta-d′/d′) and age (r = −0.38, p = 0.005; n = 53)". The correlation r = −0.38 corresponds to a medium effect.

> "We found a significant negative relationship between perceptual metacognitive efficiency (meta-d′/d′) and age (r = −0.38, p = 0.005; n = 53; see Fig. 2b)."
