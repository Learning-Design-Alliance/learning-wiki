## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Basic operative laparoscopic skills improved by 34% and cognitive knowledge by 14%, both statistically significant. [→ Tang B 2020](#tang-b-2020)

## Evidence

### Tang B 2020

Tang B, Zhang L, Alijani A. (2020). Evidence to support the early introduction of laparoscopic suturing skills into the surgical training curriculum. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-1986-z

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre/post comparison in the same 57 senior trainees with previous laparoscopic experience, assessed with OSCE and OCHRA checklists. Both outcome sets "improved significantly", at rates of 34% and 14% respectively; no standardised effect size is printed.

> "The total scores of the other operative laparoscopic skills including port insertion, tissue dissection, using of diathermy hook, clipping, and application of endoloop (127.5 ± 10.1 vs 95.5 ± 16.8, P < 0.001) and cognitive knowledge (66.5 ± 16.2 vs 58.8 ± 10.3, P < 0.001) also improved significantly in trainees with previous laparoscopic experience"
