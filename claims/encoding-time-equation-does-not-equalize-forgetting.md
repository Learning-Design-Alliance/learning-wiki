## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Even when encoding time per character was equated across methods, the Presentation Method x Retention-Interval Duration interaction remained significant, with greater forgetting under simultaneous presentation. [→ Ricker Timothy J. 2014](#ricker-timothy-j-2014)

## Evidence

### Ricker Timothy J. 2014

Ricker Timothy J., Cowan Nelson. (2014). Differences between Presentation Methods in Working Memory Procedures: A Matter of Working Memory Consolidation. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0034301

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 with 36 college students equated encoding time per character (250+83 ms) across methods by masking 83 ms after each sequential item. The significant interaction "F(2,68)=7.11, p<.005, ηp2=0.17" persisted; Cowan's k showed 0.67 items forgotten with simultaneous versus 0.22 with sequential presentation.

> "there was also a significant interaction of Presentation Method with Retention-Interval Duration, F(2,68)=7.11, p<.005, ηp2=0.17 (means: sequential 1s=.79, 6s=.74, 12s=.74; simultaneous 1s=.81, 6s=.71, 12s=.68), indicating that the rate of time-based forgetting was different for sequential and simultaneous presentation methods"
