## Evidence

### Lai 2025

Lai, S., Choi, J., Kim, J., Kunievsky, N., Potter, Y., & Evans, J. (2025). Biased AI improves human performance but reduces perceived helpfulness. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2508.09297](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2508.09297)

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Pre-registered investment-experiment priming condition (N = 300 analytic) in which participants were falsely told a default assistant had a steered risk preference. No performance difference emerged (all p ≥ 0.480); no effect size was printed, so the null indicates the study did not detect an effect.

> "Labelling a default assistant as risk-averse or risk-seeking, without actually altering its risk preference, left performance unchanged (all p ≥ 0.480; Fig. 2e)."
