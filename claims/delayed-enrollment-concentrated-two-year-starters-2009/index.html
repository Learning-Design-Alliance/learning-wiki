Sector breakdown of delayed entry in the same class-of-2009 tracking data. The report contrasts this with four-year starters, of whom almost nine out of ten enrolled immediately (37 percent immediate plus 5 percent delayed).

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Among the CPS class of 2022, 60.8% immediately enrolled in college; four-year enrollment recovered to pre-pandemic levels while two-year enrollment only began recovering in 2022 — related
  • Few CPS graduates who delay college entry or first enroll in a two-year college go on to earn a four-year degree
  • Delaying college enrollment was associated with low completion: only 8 percent of CPS students who delayed completed a degree or credential within six years
  • Six-year college completion among the CPS class of 2015 varied sharply by enrollment pathway: 55.8% for immediate four-year enrollees, 32.5% for immediate two-year enrollees, and 7.8% for delayed/non-enrollees
  • Immediate four-year enrollees were far more likely than immediate two-year enrollees to complete a four-year degree within six years (48 percent vs 7 percent)
  • Four-year college graduation rates remained flat at about 49 percent for direct four-year enrollees, with much lower rates for two-year (8 percent) and delayed enrollees (4 percent)
  • CPS alumni college completion (48%) remains below the national average (64%), with 56% of immediate four-year and 32% of immediate two-year enrollees completing within six years
  • Six-year college completion rates for immediate four-year enrollees rose from 54 to 56 percent for the CPS class of 2013, double the rate for immediate two-year enrollees
  • College Track completers enrolled in college at higher rates than similar students, and were much more likely to enroll in four-year colleges — related
