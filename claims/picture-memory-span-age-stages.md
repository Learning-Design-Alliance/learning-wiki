## Evidence

### Squire 1912

Squire, C. R. (1912). Graded Mental Tests. Part I. Attention, Perception, Comprehension and Memory. The Journal of Educational Psychology, III(7). https://www.jstor.org/journal/jeducpsych

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

In the picture memory test, a single card of twenty unrelated objects was exposed for thirty seconds and children recalled object names. The author reports children of six and seven "do not seem able to retain much more than six objects".

> "Children of six and seven do not seem able to retain much more than six objects, though occasionally a child will recall more."

### Squire 1912 (2)

Squire, C. R. (1912). Graded Mental Tests. Part I. Attention, Perception, Comprehension and Memory. The Journal of Educational Psychology, III(7). https://www.jstor.org/journal/jeducpsych

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The same picture-recall study shows averages "between nine and ten objects" for ages eight to ten, with exceptional recalls of fifteen; the eleven-year group graded with the twelve- and thirteen-year groups rather than with younger children.

> "The average number retained by children of eight, nine and ten years seems to fall between nine and ten objects, though the exceptional child may recall as many as fifteen."
