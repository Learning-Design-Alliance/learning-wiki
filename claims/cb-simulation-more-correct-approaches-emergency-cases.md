## Evidence

### Karakuş Akan 2014

Karakuş Akan, Duran Latif, Yavuz Yücel, Altintop Levent, Çalişkan Fatih. (2014). Computer-based simulation training in emergency medicine designed in the light of malpractice cases. BMC Medical Education, 14, 155. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-14-155

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Controlled comparison of 24 simulation-trained and 24 traditionally trained final-year students examined on 5 randomized simulation cases, with accuracy judged by emergency medicine and forensic medicine faculty. The study reports "an average of 3.9 correct medical approaches" versus 2.8 (t = 3.90, p < 0.005).

> "In 5 cases, an average of 3.9 correct medical approaches carried out by computer-based simulation trained students, an average of 2.8 correct medical approaches carried out by traditionally trained group (t = 3.90, p < 0.005)."
