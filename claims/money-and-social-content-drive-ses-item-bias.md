# The disadvantage from ecologically relevant content is driven by money and social-interaction items in both grades, but food items show the effect only in grade 4

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Separate regressions show significant SES × content interactions for money and social interaction in both grades, and for food in grade 4 only; in grade 8 the food interaction runs in the opposite direction (Exp(B) = 1.15) and is not significant. ## Evidence

### Marjolein Muskens 2024

Marjolein Muskens, Willem E. Frankenhuiss & Lex Borghans. (2024). Math items about real-world content lower test-scores of students from families with low socioeconomic status. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00228-8

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Student-level mixed logistic regressions run separately for money, food, and social interaction dummies (Table 1). The article prints Exp(B) = 0.78 for food × SES in grade 4 versus Exp(B) = 1.15 [1.04, 1.28] in grade 8, the latter unmarked for significance. The food result is thus mixed across grades.

> "These results indicate that the interaction we found depends speciﬁcally on content about money and social interaction in both grade 4 and 8, and on content about food in grade 4 (but not in grade 8)."
