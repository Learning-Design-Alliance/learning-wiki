# Frequently used word stems such as "can", "want", "thing" and "feel" carry no discriminative value between curiosity and interest definitions

## Evidence

### Donnellan 2021

Donnellan, E., Aslan, S., Fastrich, G. M., & Murayama, K. (2021). How Are Curiosity and Interest Different? Naïve Bayes Classification of People's Beliefs. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09622-9

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of word stems appearing in more than 10% of main-data definitions but not selected during feature selection; the article states these stems "have no discriminative value despite frequent occurrence" and labels them common features of both definition types.

> "The most common word stems (except derivatives of "curios", "curious" and "interest") were "can", "want", "thing" and "feel". These have no discriminative value despite frequent occurrence; they can be considered "common features" of curiosity and interest definitions."
