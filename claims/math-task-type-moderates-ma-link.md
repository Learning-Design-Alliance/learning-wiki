# Type of math task moderates the math anxiety–performance link: basic numerical knowledge shows the weakest correlation (r = −.22) and applied math among the strongest (r = −.31)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q4 i2` Math type significantly moderates the math anxiety–performance relationship; basic knowledge tasks show the smallest correlation (r = −.22) while other task types range from −.25 to −.31. [→ Caviola 2022](#caviola-2022)

## Evidence

### Caviola 2022

Caviola, S., Toffalini, E., Giofrè, D., Mercader Ruiz, J., Szűcs, D., & Mammarella, I. C. (2022). Math Performance and Academic Anxiety Forms, from Sociodemographic to Cognitive Aspects: a Meta-analysis on 906,311 Participants. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09618-5

`q4 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Moderation analysis (Table 2: χ2 = 36.42, p < .001) of math type across 78 applied-math, 43 arithmetic, 38 grade, and 10 basic-knowledge studies. Basic knowledge showed "the smallest relationship with math anxiety" at r = −.22; applied math reached r = −.31.

> "The latter showed the smallest relationship with math anxiety,r = − .22 [− .34, − .09; p < .001], while all other correlations with math anxiety were moderate and ranged from− .25 [− .28, − .22; p < .001] to − .31 [− .34, − .28; p < .001]."
