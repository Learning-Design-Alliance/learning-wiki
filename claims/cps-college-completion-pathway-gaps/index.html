Evidence¶

Mahaffie 2022¶

Mahaffie, S., Malone, M., Usher, A., Mukherjee, D., & Nagaoka, J. (2022). The educational attainment of Chicago Public Schools students: 2021. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://toandthrough.uchicago.edu/tool/cps/

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Six-year completion tracking of CPS graduating classes of 2008–2014 (Figures 9–11). For the class of 2014: 51.3% of immediate four-year enrollees earned bachelor's degrees; 7.6% of immediate two-year enrollees earned bachelor's degrees plus 19.3% associate/certificate; only 2.4% of delayed/non-enrollees earned bachelor's degrees.

"Among high school graduates who immediately enrolled in a two-year college, completion rates have remained relatively steady since 2008."

  • Four-year college graduation rates remained flat at about 49 percent for direct four-year enrollees, with much lower rates for two-year (8 percent) and delayed enrollees (4 percent) — related

  • Ten percent of CPS graduates who did not enroll in college within six years had an average high school GPA above 3.0, a missed opportunity for high-achieving students — related

  • Four-year enrollees had stronger academic qualifications than two-year enrollees and non-enrollees, yet many non-enrollees and two-year entrants had credentials sufficient for four-year access — related

  • A record high 7,368 students from the CPS class of 2018 graduated from college with a degree or certificate — related

  • Few CPS graduates who delay college entry or first enroll in a two-year college go on to earn a four-year degree — related
  • CPS four-year completion rates rose 7 percentage points over ten years while enrollees nearly doubled, but remained far below six-year rates — related
  • Students meeting the continuous four-term persistence definition completed college at higher rates than students meeting the third-semester definition, for both bachelor's and community college enrollees — related
  • A majority of NFTE alumni respondents were attending or had finished college, and 91 percent of college finishers completed a four-year degree or higher — related
