## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Reconstructed counts from the published figures show the fraction of admitted students below the 10th percentile fell from about 10% to 3.9% under the old system, with a one-sided Fisher exact p-value below 0.001 for the increase. [→ Weissman 2023](#weissman-2023)

## Evidence

### Weissman 2023

Weissman, M. B. (2023). Comment on "Rubric-based holistic review represents a change from traditional graduate admissions approaches in physics". Department of Physics, University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.028001](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.028001)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

A re-analysis reconstructing low-score counts from Figures 5-7 of the original study, comparing old-system and rubric-system admitted applicants below the 10th-percentile cutoff. The author reports "3.9% of the total rather than 10%" with Fisher exact p < 0.001.

> "the total number of these low old-system points for the three metrics shown was 29, i.e. 3.9% of the total rather than 10%. The one-sided Fisher exact p-value for the increase (i.e. the probability that an increase at least that big would happen by pure chance) is less than 0.001."

### Weissman 2023 (2)

Weissman, M. B. (2023). Comment on "Rubric-based holistic review represents a change from traditional graduate admissions approaches in physics". Department of Physics, University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.028001](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.028001)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The same figure-based re-analysis, testing each metric separately. The comment notes the Physics GRE change "would not be large enough to meet the conventional alpha=0.05 significance standard."

> "For the Quantitative GRE, Physics GRE, and UGPA taken separately the one-sided Fisher exact p-values were less than 0.001, 0.10, and 0.04, respectively."
