# Students incorrectly focus on the degeneracy in the weaker perturbation instead of the degeneracy in the stronger Hamiltonian when determining a good basis

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r?` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2020 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person · **overdue for a recheck since 2025**: baselines and conditions change

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In both steps of the two-step approximation, students incorrectly examined the degenerate subspaces of H′ (e.g., equal diagonal elements of H′fs) rather than the degenerate subspaces of H0strong to decide whether DPT applies and whether a basis is good. [→ Emily Marshman 2020](#emily-marshman-2020)

## Evidence

### Emily Marshman 2020

Emily Marshman, Christof Keebaugh, and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). Student difﬁculties with ﬁnding the corrections to the energy spectrum of the hydrogen atom for the strong and weak ﬁeld Zeeman effects using degenerate perturbation theory. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2006.07144](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2006.07144)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Written and interview data; in step 2 of the strong-field case students were given the H0Z matrix and H′fs matrix for n = 2 and asked whether the uncoupled representation is a good basis. Students focused on "the degeneracy in ˆH ′strong" instead of degenerate subspaces of H0strong, though the degeneracy of the weaker perturbation is not relevant.

> "some students incorrectly focused on the degeneracy in ˆH ′strong to deter- mine whether DPT should be used and whether the basis provided was good."
