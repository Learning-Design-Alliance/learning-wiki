# Pilot testing showed that game frames lacking a well-defined objective leave students stuck because they do not know when to proceed

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In the pilot test, three of the original six frames in DiffGame failed their purpose because they lacked a well-defined objective and students did not know when to proceed. [→ Pedersen 2016](#pedersen-2016)

## Evidence

### Pedersen 2016

Pedersen, M. K., Svenningsen, A., Dohn, N. B., Lieberoth, A., & Sherson, J. (2016). DiffGame: Game-based mathematics learning for physics. [doi:10.1016/j.sbspro.2016.07.047](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sbspro.2016.07.047)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r3`

Pilot test with 51 Grade 11 students from two Danish high schools in two-hour sessions; observations and a focus-group interview revealed that in three of six frames "the students got stuck in these, because they did not know when to proceed", leading to their removal.

> "The lack of a well -defined objective was common for the three frames, and during the pilot the students got stuck in these, because they did not know when to proceed."
