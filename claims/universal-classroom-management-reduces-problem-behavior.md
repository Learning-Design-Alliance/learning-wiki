# Teachers' universal classroom management practices significantly reduce problem classroom behavior (mean classroom ES = 0.80, ICC=.05)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Participants in classroom management intervention conditions exhibited significantly less problem classroom behavior after intervention, with a random-effects mean classroom effect size of 0.80 (SE = 0.15, z = 5.44, p = .000) using ICC=.05 and .71 (SE = .13, z = 5.53, p = 0.00) using ICC=.10. ## Evidence

### Oliver 2011

Oliver, R. M., Wehby, J. H., & Reschly, D. J. (2011). Teacher Classroom Management Practices: Effects on Disruptive or Aggressive Student Behavior. 2011 SREE Conference. https://www.sree.org. [doi:10.4073/csr.2011.4](https://doi.org/10.4073/csr.2011.4)

`q3 · i2`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 12 studies of universal, whole-classroom management interventions in K-12 settings, using standardized mean differences with Hedges small-sample correction and ICC adjustments. The review reports "a statistically significant mean classroom effect size of 0.80" for ICC=.05 and .71 for ICC=.10, with effect sizes ranging from -0.05 to 1.74 (ICC=.05).

> "The random effects analysis on the 12 effect sizes produced a statistically significant mean classroom effect size of 0.80 (SE = 0.15, z = 5.44, p = .000) for ICC=.05 and a statistically significant mean classroom effect size of .71 (SE = .13, z = 5.53, p = 0.00) for ICC=.10"
