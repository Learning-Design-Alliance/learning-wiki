# Intensive three-day simulator training significantly improves single surgical knot performance across time, movement economy, smoothness, acceleration, instrument activity and overall score

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among course participants, paired comparisons of simulator-analyzed Single Surgical Knot performance showed significant improvement from day 1 to day 3 in time, economy of movement, distance, smoothness, acceleration, jolt, activity and overall score (e.g., overall score mean 4.4 to 6.0, p < 0.001). [→ Borselle D. 2025](#borselle-d-2025)

## Evidence

### Borselle D. 2025

Borselle D., Więckowski A., Patkowski D. (2025). Evaluating suturing skill improvement for pediatric minimally invasive esophageal anastomosis model: an observational cohort study based on simulator training. Scientific Reports 15, 7692. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-91307-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Observational cohort analysis of 55 course participants (N=54, 43, 51 assessed on days 1–3) using automatic simulator motion analysis with paired tests; Table 1 reports overall score means of 4.4 (SD 1.4) on day 1 rising to 6.0 (SD 1.4) on day 3 (p < 0.001), and "significant improvements in various performance metrics for the Single Surgical Knot task".

> "These results indicate significant improvements in various performance metrics for the Single Surgical Knot task, highlighting the effectiveness of the training course (Table 1, Fig. 5)."
