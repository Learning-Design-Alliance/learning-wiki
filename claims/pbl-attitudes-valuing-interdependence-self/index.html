Evidence¶

Murray-Harvey 2013¶

Murray-Harvey, R., Pourshafie, T., & Santos Reyes, W. (2013). What teacher education students learn about collaboration from problem-based learning. Journal of Problem Based Learning in Higher Education, 1(1), 114-134. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1108330

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Qualitative analysis of written reflections (journals, two assignments) from 122 Australian teacher education students who studied two PBL cases over one semester. Open coding in NVivo classified statements into ASK dimensions; the study reports that "Attitudes identified as necessary for collaborative learning were valuing others' perspectives, interdependence, and learning about self."

Related Claims¶

  • Students characterised the skills needed for collaborative group work as interpersonal (social), problem solving, and group skills — related
  • Students described knowledge gained through PBL group work in terms of generation, application, and dissemination of knowledge, plus pedagogical knowledge for future teaching — related
  • No significant relationship between attitudes toward group work and achievement goal orientation profiles, but attitudes correlate with perceived discussion and active listening behaviors — related
  • Research on PBL covers flexible knowledge, problem-solving, and SDL skills but little on collaboration and motivation — related
  • Students in PBL experimental groups reported positive views of problem-based learning in qualitative follow-up interviews — related
  • Students made minimal reference to higher-level cognitive or metacognitive processing benefits of group work, departing from prior PBL research — related
  • Children exhibited creative and communicative ways of thinking and working in collaborative tasks — related
  • In one experiment with undergraduate business majors in asynchronous online small groups, reward interdependence raised agreement that group work was beneficial and generated better ideas — related
  • Participants reported that diverse perspectives raised new questions and affirmed educator contributions — related
  • Students enact agency toward groups by fostering relational awareness, building interpersonal relationships, holding dialogue, and seeing from other perspectives — related
