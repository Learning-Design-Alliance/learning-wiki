## Evidence

### Josephine C. Meyer 2022

Josephine C. Meyer, Gina Passante, Steven J. Pollock, and Bethany R. Wilcox. (2022). The interdisciplinary quantum information classroom: Themes from a survey of quantum information science instructors. arXiv:2202.05944. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2202.05944](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2202.05944)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic coding of free-response survey answers on observed student difficulties, from the N = 27 instructors who had previously taught their course; difficulties were coded by topic into math, quantum mechanics, computer science/coding, and other categories, with "Fully 14 instructors reported stu- dent difﬁculties with linear algebra."

> "student dif- ﬁculties with mathematics were by far the most frequently reported category of student difﬁculty, being reported by 17 of 27 instructors who had previously taught at least one it- eration of their course. Fully 14 instructors reported stu- dent difﬁculties with linear algebra."

### Josephine C. Meyer 2022 (2)

Josephine C. Meyer, Gina Passante, Steven J. Pollock, and Bethany R. Wilcox. (2022). The interdisciplinary quantum information classroom: Themes from a survey of quantum information science instructors. arXiv:2202.05944. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2202.05944](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2202.05944)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Follow-up focus interview with Franz, a CS-department instructor at a baccalaureate institution, explaining that his students handle concrete matrix multiplication but struggle with tensor-product and bra-ket notation as "a level of abstraction."

> "They start to get a problem when things are not written out that way, but are instead written out as like tensor products of states, right? Because that then is like a level of abstraction."
