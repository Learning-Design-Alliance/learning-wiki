## Evidence

### Van Der Maas et al. 2006

Han L. J. van der Maas, Conor V. Dolan, Raoul P. P. P. Grasman, Jelte M. Wicherts, Hilde M. Huizenga, and Maartje E. J. Raijmakers. (2006). A Dynamical Model of General Intelligence: The Positive Manifold of Intelligence by Mutualism. Psychological Review. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.113.4.842

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical property of the mutualism model's stable states, used to explain Bayley's (1949) finding of essentially no correlation between test performance in the first 3 years and at 18 years. The article states this "implies a low or even zero correlation between initial and later performance". No effect size is printed.

> "do not depend on the growth parameters and the initial values (see equation for the stable states in the appendix). This implies a low or even zero correlation between initial and later performance."

### Van Der Maas et al. 2006 (2)

Han L. J. van der Maas, Conor V. Dolan, Raoul P. P. P. Grasman, Jelte M. Wicherts, Hilde M. Huizenga, and Maartje E. J. Raijmakers. (2006). A Dynamical Model of General Intelligence: The Positive Manifold of Intelligence by Mutualism. Psychological Review. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.113.4.842

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Simulation results shown in Figure 2b and 2c, where growth of the mean of 20 subjects' processes indexes general intelligence. The article reports that "the correlation between test performance and asymptotic performance increases over time". No effect size is printed.

> "Figure 2c supports this. The correlation between test performance and asymptotic performance increases over time. Note that this is a property of the logistic equation."
