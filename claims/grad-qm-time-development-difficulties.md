# Beginning graduate students show pervasive difficulties with time-development of the wavefunction: 31% wrote a common phase factor for a superposition state and 9% believed the wavefunction has no time dependence

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` 31% of students applied a single common phase factor to both terms of a superposition of stationary states, indicating difficulty distinguishing time-dependence of stationary and non-stationary states. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016)
`q2 i?` 9% of students believed the wavefunction should have no time dependence at all, some justifying this via the time-independent Schrödinger equation. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)](#chandralekha-singh-2016-2)

`q2 i?` On a superposition-state time-evolution question, 43% answered correctly, 31% wrote a common phase factor for both terms, and 9% wrote responses with no time dependence. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016)

## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Student understanding of quantum mechanics at the beginning of graduate instruction. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06660](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06660)

`q2 · i?`

Survey Question 2(a) asked for the wavefunction at time t for a superposition of the ground and first excited states in an infinite square well. "31% of students wrote common phase factors f or both terms"; interviews suggest difficulty distinguishing stationary from non-stationary states.

> "As shown in Table I, 31% of students wrote common phase factors f or both terms, for example, Ψ(x,t ) = Ψ(x, 0)e−iEt/¯h."
