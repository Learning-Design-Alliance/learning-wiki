## Evidence

### Alexis Olsho 2021

Alexis Olsho, Suzanne White Brahmia, Andrew Boudreaux, and Trevor Smith. (2021). When negative is not “less than zero”: Electric charge as a signed quantity. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1119/10.0004149](https://doi.org/10.1119/10.0004149)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Online ungraded pretest in the second quarter of calculus-based introductory physics at a large public research university, with question version randomly assigned by section. In this version, "93% of students (171 of N = 183) included choice C"; the binomial test gave p < 0.001.

> "On version 2, however, 93% of students (171 of N = 183) included choice C, that the two spheres have equal amounts of unbalanced charge. (A binomial test indicates that this difference is statistically significant, with p < 0.001.)"

### Alexis Olsho 2021 (2)

Alexis Olsho, Suzanne White Brahmia, Andrew Boudreaux, and Trevor Smith. (2021). When negative is not “less than zero”: Electric charge as a signed quantity. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1119/10.0004149](https://doi.org/10.1119/10.0004149)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same pretest, version 1 with “net charge” wording: "67% of students (105 of N = 158) included answer choice C". The authors report no significant differences between sections' average midterm or final exam scores.

> "On version 1, 67% of students (105 of N = 158) included answer choice C, that the net charge on sphere A is neither greater than nor less than the net charge on sphere B."
