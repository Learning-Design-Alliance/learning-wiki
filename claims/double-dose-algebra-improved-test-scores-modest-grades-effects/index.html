Evidence¶

Double-dose algebra policy evaluation in the Chicago Public Schools 2003¶

Double-dose algebra policy evaluation in the Chicago Public Schools. (2003). Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness. https://www.tandfonline.com/journals/ujree20

q3 · i? · causal · r?

Evaluation of the Chicago 2003 double-period algebra policy using regression discontinuity combined with interrupted time series designs and instrumental variable models, examining 9th-grade algebra outcomes for students below the national median. The article reports that providing support courses improved algebra test scores for the target population.

"Providing support courses improved algebra test scores for the target population but only modestly affected grades and failure rates."

Takako Nomi and Elaine M. Allensworth 2008¶

Takako Nomi and Elaine M. Allensworth. (2008). “Double-dose” Algebra as an Alternative Strategy to Remediation: Effects on Students’ Academic Outcomes. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. http://www.informaworld.com/smpp/content~db=all?content=10.1080/19345740802676739

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Cohort comparison across percentile rank groups; the article explains grades depend more on student behaviors than incoming skills, so skill gains need not translate into grade or failure improvements. No effect size printed.

"For students targeted by the policy, including students far from the cut-off, there were only negligible changes in grades or failure rates with the policy (GPAs increased by .01 points and failure rates increased by two percentage points)."

Related Claims¶

  • Double-dose algebra improved test scores but did not reduce algebra failure rates — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Algebra failure rates rose post-policy for above-norm students (~3 percentage points) but fell for below-norm students (~4 points), largely explained by classroom environment and peer ability — related
  • The double-dose algebra policy adversely affected non-targeted above-norm students: their algebra grades declined and failure rates increased post-policy — related
  • Below-norm students' algebra test scores improved despite having lower-skill peers post-policy, consistent with additional supports offsetting compositional decline — related
  • Evidence on the policy's effects on 10th-grade geometry outcomes was inconsistent across groups and years — related
  • Fish pond effects: above-norm students became more likely to fail algebra when their skills were low relative to classroom peers after sorting — related
  • Failure rates increased and grades declined for higher-skill students in single-period algebra after the policy — related
  • The algebra-for-all policy increased algebra access for low-skill students previously in remedial math — related
  • Chicago's double-dose algebra policy raised algebra test scores for both low-skill and high-skill students — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Double-dose algebra was least effective for students with the weakest incoming skills, most of whom received special education services — related
