# Fathers' initial growth mindset predicted girls' later growth mindset but not boys', a gender-moderated path

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In multiple-group analyses, the path from fathers' growth mindset intercept to children's intercept differed by child gender (χ2(1) = 4.154, p < 0.042): significant for girls (β = 0.253, p = 0.014) but not boys (β = −0.038, p = 0.709). [→ Jiao Chen and Chunhui Liu 2023](#jiao-chen-and-chunhui-liu-2023)

## Evidence

`q3 · i?`

Multiple-group analysis by child gender with Wald tests of eight paths; only the father-intercept-to-child-intercept path differed by gender. The effect held for daughters but not sons, contrary to same-gender modeling expectations the article cites.

> "paternal growth mindset in the first test predicted girls’ growth mindset in the fifth test (β = 0.253, p = 0.014) but not boys’ growth mindset (β = −0.038, p = 0.709)"
