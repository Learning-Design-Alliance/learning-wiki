## Evidence

### Connolly Murray 2026

Connolly Murray, Iohom Gabriella, O’Brien Niall, Volz James, O’Muircheartaigh Aogán, Serchan Paschalitsa, Shorten George. (2026). Delivering remote procedural training on peripheral intravenous cannulation to medical students using the Microsoft Hololens 2: a mixed-methods evaluation. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08648-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Randomised block-allocated trial with 8 students per group; video-recorded competency assessments scored by two trained tutors (mean inter-rater reliability 0.953). Both groups improved significantly, "p < 0.001 in both groups", with large Cohen's d values of 3.51 (HL) and 3.87 (IP).

> "The HL and IP groups showed statistically significant improvements in their post-feedback assessment scores, (26.69, 1.85) and (27.94, 1.37) respectively, p < 0.001 in both groups. Cohen’s d was 3.51 and 3.87 for the HL and IP groups respectively"

### Connolly Murray 2026 (2)

Connolly Murray, Iohom Gabriella, O’Brien Niall, Volz James, O’Muircheartaigh Aogán, Serchan Paschalitsa, Shorten George. (2026). Delivering remote procedural training on peripheral intravenous cannulation to medical students using the Microsoft Hololens 2: a mixed-methods evaluation. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08648-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Between-group comparison of post-feedback scores in the same randomised trial found no significant difference (p = 0.11); the between-group Cohen's d was −0.77 and post-hoc power was only 0.41, so equivalence was not established.

> "The HL and IP groups were simlar in terms of performance in the post-feedback assessment, p = 0.11. Cohen’s d comparing the HL and IP groups, with HL as the intervention group was − 0.77, with post-hoc analysis displaying a power of 0.41"
