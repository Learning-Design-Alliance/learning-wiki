# The CCT treatment effect on comprehension was statistically significant only for students with pretest ORF up to 0.20 SD above the sample mean (64.0% of the sample), and not for higher-fluency students

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Regions-of-significance analysis showed the treatment effect was significant for students from the bottom of the ORF distribution through 0.20 SD above the mean (64.0% of the sample) and not significant for students 0.20 to 2.68 SD above the mean (36.0%). [→ Nathan H. Clemens 2019](#nathan-h-clemens-2019)

## Evidence

### Nathan H. Clemens 2019

Nathan H. Clemens, Eric Oslund, Oi-man Kwok, Melissa Fogarty, Deborah Simmons, and John L. Davis. (2019). Skill Moderators of the Effects of a Reading Comprehension Intervention. Exceptional Children, 85(2), 197–211. https://doi.org/10.1177/0014402918787339

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Regions-of-significance procedures in Mplus conditioned the treatment effect on the continuous ORF moderator. For students 0.20 through 2.68 SD above the mean (36.0% of the sample), "there was no statistically significant effect of the CCT treatment".

> "for students with pretest ORF scores from the bottom of the dis- tribution through 0.20 standard deviations above the mean of the sample (64.0% of the sample), students in the CCT intervention out- performed students in the comparison condi- tion on posttest comprehension on a statistically significant basis"
