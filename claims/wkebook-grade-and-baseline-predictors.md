## Evidence

### Stephanie L. Day 2024

Stephanie L. Day, Jin K. Hwang, Tracy Arner, Danielle McNamara, Carol M. Connor. (2024). Choose Your Own Adventure: Interactive E-Books to Improve Word Knowledge and Comprehension Skills. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2403.02496](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2403.02496)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Grade level was entered as a teacher/classroom-level covariate centered at fourth grade in the Dragon's Lair HLM; Table 2 shows γ01 = 0.562, p = 0.026. In the Hurricane! model the grade coefficient was γ01 = 0.581, p = 0.051, marginal and not reported as significant.

> "We also found evidence of a grade effect such that on average, students in fourth and fifth grade tended to perform better than the third-grade students on the posttest (Table 2)."

### Stephanie L. Day 2024 (2)

Stephanie L. Day, Jin K. Hwang, Tracy Arner, Danielle McNamara, Carol M. Connor. (2024). Choose Your Own Adventure: Interactive E-Books to Improve Word Knowledge and Comprehension Skills. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2403.02496](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2403.02496)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

WMG and L2M growth scaled scores were included as student-level covariates in the Dragon's Lair HLM; Table 2 shows L2M γ20 = 0.021, p < .001, WMG γ10 = 0.025, p = 0.050, and pretest γ30 = 0.559, p < .001. For Hurricane!, only L2M (γ20 = 0.007, p = 0.015) was significant. These are unstandardized HLM coefficients, not effect sizes.

> "Overall, higher pretest and L2M (decoding and comprehension) scores were associated with higher scores on the Word Knowledge posttest."
