# Performance aims are ambiguously aligned, tied nearly equally to autonomous and controlled reasons

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In a single unanchored study, performance goals correlated almost equally with autonomous reasons (r = 0.30) and controlled reasons (r = 0.39), unlike mastery aims in the same dataset (r = 0.62 with autonomous reasons). [→ Janke 2024](#janke-2024)

## Evidence

### Janke 2024

Janke, S. (2024). Dynamic or Static Goal Regulation: Implications of Weak and Strong Bonds Between Autonomous/Controlled Reasons and Aims for Achievement Goal Striving. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09968-w

`q3 · i3` · `theoretical · r3`

Study 4 of Sommet and Elliot (2017), reviewed here, measured generalized reasons detached from aims in a university sample. Performance goals showed near-equivalent correlations with both reason types, while mastery aims in the same dataset were tightly tied to autonomous reasons (r = 0.62) and weakly to controlled reasons (r = 0.19).

> "In contrast, performance goals were almost equally strong tied to autonomous reasons ( r = 0.30, p < 0.001) as to controlled reasons (r = 0.39, p < 0.001)."

### Janke 2024 (2)

Janke, S. (2024). Dynamic or Static Goal Regulation: Implications of Weak and Strong Bonds Between Autonomous/Controlled Reasons and Aims for Achievement Goal Striving. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09968-w

`q3 · i2` · `review · r2`

Meta-analytic pooling of ten anchored studies (n = 2213) for performance aims and controlled reasons, printed as "r = 0.35", compared with r = 0.17 for mastery aims in the same analysis tradition.

> "compared to r = 0.35 (95% CI [0.27, 0.43], k = 10, n = 2213, Q(9) = 44.00, p < 0.001, I2 = 80.03%) for performance aims"
