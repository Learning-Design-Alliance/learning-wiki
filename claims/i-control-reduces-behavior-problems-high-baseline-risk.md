# I Control reduced externalizing and internalizing behavior problems, with significant effects concentrated among students with higher baseline risk

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teacher-reported externalizing problems (TRF) differed significantly from controls at high baseline risk (ES = −.63). [→ Smith et al. 2017](#smith-et-al-2017)
`q2 i?` Student-reported externalizing (ES = −.70) and internalizing (ES = −.57 at high risk) problems on the YSR favored I Control at higher baseline risk. [→ Smith et al. 2017 (2)](#smith-et-al-2017-2)
`q2 i?` SSiS Problem Behavior effects appeared only for students with the most significant pretest risk (ES = −.50), and no significant findings emerged for the SSiS Social Skills domain. [→ Smith et al. 2017 (3)](#smith-et-al-2017-3)

## Evidence

### Smith et al. 2017

Stephen W. Smith, Ann P. Daunic, James Algina, Donna L. Pitts, Kristen L. Merrill, Michelle M. Cumming, and Courtney Allen. (2017). Self-Regulation for Students With Emotional and Behavioral Disorders: Preliminary Effects of the I Control Curriculum. Journal of Emotional and Behavioral Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1177/1063426616661702

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Teacher-report outcome from the Mplus pre–post pilot analysis of 152 students; the TRF Externalizing Covariate × Treatment interaction was significant, with the simple effect significant only at high baseline risk.

> "For Externalizing Problems measured by the TRF, the effect of I Control was larger for participants with higher (worse) covariate scores, and the simple effect was significant at the high covariate score (z = −3.17 p = .001, ES = −.63)."

### Smith et al. 2017 (2)

Stephen W. Smith, Ann P. Daunic, James Algina, Donna L. Pitts, Kristen L. Merrill, Michelle M. Cumming, and Courtney Allen. (2017). Self-Regulation for Students With Emotional and Behavioral Disorders: Preliminary Effects of the I Control Curriculum. Journal of Emotional and Behavioral Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1177/1063426616661702

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Student self-report YSR Externalizing in the same pilot analysis: the effect favored I Control only at high baseline risk; at the grand covariate mean and low covariate values the simple effects were not significant.

> "For Externalizing, the simple effect test was significant when the covariate score was high (z = −2.58, p = .005, ES = −.70) but not when the covariate score was equal to the GCM (ES = −.17) or was low (ES = .37)."

### Smith et al. 2017 (3)

Stephen W. Smith, Ann P. Daunic, James Algina, Donna L. Pitts, Kristen L. Merrill, Michelle M. Cumming, and Courtney Allen. (2017). Self-Regulation for Students With Emotional and Behavioral Disorders: Preliminary Effects of the I Control Curriculum. Journal of Emotional and Behavioral Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1177/1063426616661702

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Teacher-reported SSiS Problem Behavior in the same pilot analysis: the effect was significant only at high baseline risk. The article reports no significant findings for the SSiS Social Skills domain.

> "For Problem Behavior measured by the SSiS, there was little difference between conditional means for I Control and control participants (ES = −.08) at the GCM; the treatment effect was larger for participants with higher (worse) covariate scores and significant at the high value of the covariate only (z = −1.89, p = .030, ES = −.50)."
