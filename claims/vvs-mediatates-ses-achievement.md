## Evidence

### Rosen Maya L. 2021

Rosen Maya L., Lurie Lucy A., Sambrook Kelly A., Meltzoff Andrew N., McLaughlin Katie A. (2021). Neural mechanisms underlying the income-achievement gap: The role of the ventral visual stream. Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2021.101025

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bootstrapped mediation analysis in the 62-child sample tested VVS activation as mediator of the SES–achievement link, controlling for age, sex, and violence exposure. The article reports "a significant indirect effect of INR on academic achievement through activation in the right VVS during cued attention (95% CI: [.088, 2.852]"; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "We found a significant indirect effect of INR on academic achievement through activation in the right VVS during cued attention (95% CI: [.088, 2.852], Table 4, Fig. 9C). We additionally found a marginal indirect effect of INR on academic achievement through activation in left VVS during cued attention (90% CI: [.041, 2.250])."

### Rosen Maya L. 2021 (2)

Rosen Maya L., Lurie Lucy A., Sambrook Kelly A., Meltzoff Andrew N., McLaughlin Katie A. (2021). Neural mechanisms underlying the income-achievement gap: The role of the ventral visual stream. Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2021.101025

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Multiple regression in the same sample, controlling for age and sex, tested log income-to-needs against the Woodcock-Johnson IV Academic Skills Cluster. The article reports "INR was positively associated with academic achievement (β = 0.258, p = .043"; the a-path needed for the mediation model.

> "Consistent with previous work, INR was positively associated with academic achievement (β = 0.258, p = .043)."
