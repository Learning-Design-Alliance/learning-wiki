## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Compared with untrained students, EBM-trained medical students were more likely to name online journals and professional guidelines, and less likely to name printed books, as their main information-retrieval source. [→ Csertő Mónika 2019](#cserto-monika-2019)

## Evidence

### Csertő Mónika 2019

Csertő Mónika, Berényi Károly, Decsi Tamás, Lohner Szimonetta. (2019). Self-reported attitudes, knowledge and skills of using evidence-based medicine in daily health care practice: A national survey among students of medicine and health sciences in Hungary. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0225641

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional survey comparing source preferences of EBM-trained vs non-trained medical students (Fig 1; abstract figures: printed books 33.6% vs 52.6%, p<0.001; guidelines 15.4% vs 6.1%, p<0.001). Trained students favoured "online journals and professional guidelines".

> "Medical students not yet trained in EBM were significantly more likely to choose printed books as the main source of healthcare information retrieval, while medical students who already participated in an EBM course choose online journals and professional guidelines to a significantly higher extent compared tothose who did not participate in EBM course."

### Csertő Mónika 2019 (2)

Csertő Mónika, Berényi Károly, Decsi Tamás, Lohner Szimonetta. (2019). Self-reported attitudes, knowledge and skills of using evidence-based medicine in daily health care practice: A national survey among students of medicine and health sciences in Hungary. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0225641

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Health sciences faculty students (n = 911) in the same survey: printed books 28.8% vs 37.8% (p = 0.01), guidelines 10.2% vs 3.6% (p<0.001). The article states "No other significant difference was seen between the two groups."

> "trained in EBM choose printed books to a significantly lower extent (28.8% vs 37.8%; p = 0.01) and professional guidelines to significantly higher extent (10.2% vs 3.6%; p<0.001) than those students who did not participate in EBM course. No other significant difference was seen between the two groups."
