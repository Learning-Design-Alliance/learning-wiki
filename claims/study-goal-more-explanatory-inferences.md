## Subclaims
`q3 i?` College students instructed to read expository text for study produced more explanatory inferences than students reading for entertainment. [→ P. van den Broek 2001](#p-van-den-broek-2001)

## Evidence

### P. van den Broek 2001

P. van den Broek, R. F. Lorch, Jr., T. Linderholm, & M. Gustafson. (2001). The effects of readers' goals on inference generation and memory for texts. Memory & Cognition, 29(8), 1081-1087. https://link.springer.com/journal/13414

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Controlled experiment in which 71 analyzed college students were randomly assigned to read four Scientific American-derived expository texts for study or entertainment while thinking aloud. The ANOVA against participant and item variability confirmed "more explanatory inferences were made in the study condition than in the entertainment condition."

> "First, more explanatory inferences were made in the study condition than in the entertainment condition [ F1(1,55) = 13.86, MSe = .0098; F2(1,135) = 41.61, MSe = .0255]."
