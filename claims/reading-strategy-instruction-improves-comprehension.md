# Reading Strategy Instruction Improves Comprehension

Explicit teaching of reading strategies — planning, monitoring, and evaluating one's understanding of text — improves learners' comprehension beyond what general reading practice produces [+S].

## Discussion

**Mechanism.** Strategy instruction is typically explained through self-regulated learning: skilled readers actively plan, monitor, and repair their understanding, while struggling readers often lack these procedures rather than the underlying language skill. Teaching strategies makes these otherwise invisible processes explicit and practicable, connecting to [Self-regulated learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) [+M].

**What "instruction" means matters.** The claim is strongest for *explicit, teacher-modeled* instruction — the teacher names the strategy, demonstrates its use, guides practice, and gradually releases responsibility [+S]. Simply telling learners that strategies exist, or assigning strategy-labelled activities without modeling, produces much weaker effects [~M]. Strategies such as summarizing, questioning, predicting, and clarifying are the most consistently studied.

**Transfer and maintenance.** A recurring boundary condition is whether strategy use persists after instruction ends. Effects are strongest on trained texts and near-transfer tasks [~M]; durable, independent use requires extended practice across varied texts, not a single instructional unit.

**Interaction with decoding.** Strategy instruction cannot compensate for weak word recognition [~S]. If decoding is effortful, working-memory resources are consumed before comprehension strategies can operate — see [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension](automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) and [Cognitive overload degrades learning](cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md). Strategy instruction is most effective once basic decoding is reasonably automatic.

**Expertise reversal.** For already-skilled readers, extensive strategy scaffolding can become redundant or even counterproductive [~M], mirroring the [Expertise reversal effect](../theories/expertise-reversal-effect.md). Instruction should fade as readers internalize the strategies and begin deploying them flexibly and independently.

**Open questions.** The relative effectiveness of single-strategy versus multiple-strategy (packages) instruction, optimal timing across grade levels, and how much explicit modeling is needed before independent practice remain actively debated.
