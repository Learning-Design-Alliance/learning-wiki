## Evidence

### Bailey E. G. 2018

Bailey E. G., Baek D., Meiling J., Morris C., Nelson N., Rice N. S., Rose S., Stockdale P. (2018). Learning Gains from a Recurring “Teach and Question” Homework Assignment in a General Biology Course: Using Reciprocal Peer Tutoring Outside Class. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-12-0259

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

End-of-course survey asking students to rank six at-home activities with 6 most helpful; distributions distinguishable by Mann-Whitney U-test (p = 0.015, n = 33–35). No effect size printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "The TQW and RW assignments were ranked the highest of any at-home activity for both sections, but the TQW assignment was ranked slightly higher than the RW assignment"

### Bailey E. G. 2018 (2)

Bailey E. G., Baek D., Meiling J., Morris C., Nelson N., Rice N. S., Rose S., Stockdale P. (2018). Learning Gains from a Recurring “Teach and Question” Homework Assignment in a General Biology Course: Using Reciprocal Peer Tutoring Outside Class. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-12-0259

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Survey item asking which portion of the assignment was most helpful; distributions compared by Mann-Whitney U-test (p = 0.0012, n = 40–43). Percentages as printed; no standardized effect size, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "Most of the students in the TQ section (87.5%) reported that the TQ portion of the assignment was more helpful than the written portion, while only 51.7% of the RW section preferred the R portion over the written portion."
