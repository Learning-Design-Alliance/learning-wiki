# Lower-GPA students show greater improvement than higher-GPA students in the flipped format on homework attempts, accuracy, and exam scores

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A significant GPA × class type interaction showed students in the lower two GPA quartiles improved most in homework attempts and accuracy before exams in the flipped course. [→ Gross D 2015](#gross-d-2015)

## Evidence

### Gross D 2015

Gross D, Pietri ES, Anderson G, Moyano-Camihort K, Graham MJ. (2015). Increased Preclass Preparation Underlies Student Outcome Improvement in the Flipped Classroom. CBE—Life Sciences Education 14(4). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-02-0040

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Regression-based moderation analysis treating GPA as continuous, examining attempts and accuracy of online homework in the 2 weeks before the exam. The article reports a "significant interaction between GPA and class type" for attempts, t(444) = −2.68, p < 0.01, and accuracy, t(443) = −2.17, p < 0.04; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "This was indicated by a significant interaction between GPA and class type predicting both attempts (t(444) = −2.68, p < 0.01) and accuracy (t(443) = −2.17, p < 0.04), with students in the lower two GPA quartiles showing the greatest improvement in attempts and accuracy (p values < 0.01)."
