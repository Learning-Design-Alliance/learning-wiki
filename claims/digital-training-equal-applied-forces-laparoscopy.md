# Applied instrument forces during the final laparoscopic assessment show no significant differences between digitally and in-person trained novices

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Mean non-zero force and peak force did not differ significantly between the digital and in-person groups across the four exercises (e.g., mean non-zero force: peg 0.69 vs. 0.68 N, p = 0.064; suturing 0.92 vs. 0.95 N, p = 0.684). [→ Geissler Mark Enrik 2024](#geissler-mark-enrik-2024)

## Evidence

### Geissler Mark Enrik 2024

Geissler Mark Enrik, Bereuter Jean-Paul, Geissler Rona Berit, Bökkerink Guus Mattheus Johannes, Egen Luisa, Kowalewski Karl-Friedrich, Haney Caelan. (2024). Comparison of distance versus in-person laparoscopy training using a low-cost laparoscopy simulator—a randomized controlled multi-center trial. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-024-11069-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ForceSense sensor-based force measurements in the final assessment of the randomized trial (in-person n=21, digital n=19). All four exercises showed non-significant mean non-zero force comparisons; the article states there were no significant differences in applied force between groups.

> "The mean non-zero force showed no significant differences between the groups (Peg: control: 0.69 N vs. intervention: 0.68 N, p value = 0.064; circle cutting: control: 0.75 N vs. intervention: 0.72 N, p value = 0.416; balloon resection: control: 1.15 N vs. intervention: 0.95 N, p value = 0.078; suturing: control: 0.92 N vs. intervention: 0.95 N, p value = 0.684)"
