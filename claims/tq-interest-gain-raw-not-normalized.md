## Evidence

### Bailey E. G. 2018

Bailey E. G., Baek D., Meiling J., Morris C., Nelson N., Rice N. S., Rose S., Stockdale P. (2018). Learning Gains from a Recurring “Teach and Question” Homework Assignment in a General Biology Course: Using Reciprocal Peer Tutoring Outside Class. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-12-0259

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Attitudinal survey at course end (n = 40–43) with self-reported interest on a five-point Likert scale before and after the course; raw change compared by Mann-Whitney U-test. No effect size printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "The TQ section increased more than the R section (Mann-Whitney U-test, p = 0.017)."

### Bailey E. G. 2018 (2)

Bailey E. G., Baek D., Meiling J., Morris C., Nelson N., Rice N. S., Rose S., Stockdale P. (2018). Learning Gains from a Recurring “Teach and Question” Homework Assignment in a General Biology Course: Using Reciprocal Peer Tutoring Outside Class. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-12-0259

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Because the TQ section started slightly less interested, the authors compared normalized gains; the difference was no longer statistically distinguishable (p = 0.06), so the raw interest result is qualified.

> "we also compared normalized interest gains, and course sections were no longer distinguishable statistically (Figure 4C; p = 0.06)."
