# Learners partly use example length as a cue when evaluating their self-generated examples

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r3` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Learners tended to give higher quality judgments to lengthier examples (G = .22), and their examples were shorter than the expert examples. [→ Linda Froese 2022](#linda-froese-2022)

## Evidence

### Linda Froese 2022

Linda Froese, Julian Roelle. (2022). Expert example standards but not idea unit standards help learners accurately evaluate the quality of self-generated examples. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09293-z

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r3`

Exploratory analysis in the N = 131 experiment correlated learners' ratings with example word count (G = .22) and found learner examples contained fewer words than expert examples for all eight concepts (all ps < .001).

> "We found a gamma correlation of G = .22, which indicates that learners tended to give higher judgments to lengthier examples and thus, at least in part, might have used the length of their examples as cue."
