## Evidence

### Sapey-Triomphe 2023

Sapey-Triomphe, L.-A., Sanchez, G., Hénaff, M.-A., Sonié, S., Schmitz, C., & Mattout, J. (2023). Disentangling sensory precision and prior expectation of change in autism during tactile discrimination. npj Science of Learning, 8:54. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00207-5

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment I measured 30 Hz tactile detection thresholds with a method of limits in NT and autistic adults without intellectual disability. The article reports "did not differ between the neurotypical (NT) group" and the ASD group, with t(61) = 0.3, p = 0.79 and Bayesian evidence favoring no difference.

> "the average intensity detection threshold of 30 Hz stimulations did not differ between the neurotypical (NT) group (0.6 mA ± 0.2) and the ASD group (0.6 mA ± 0.4) ( t(61) = 0.3, p = 0.79, d = 0.68)"

### Sapey-Triomphe 2023 (2)

Sapey-Triomphe, L.-A., Sanchez, G., Hénaff, M.-A., Sonié, S., Schmitz, C., & Mattout, J. (2023). Disentangling sensory precision and prior expectation of change in autism during tactile discrimination. npj Science of Learning, 8:54. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00207-5

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Two-alternative forced-choice context-setting blocks of Exp. II showed comparable accuracy across groups (81% vs 79%); "did not differ between groups" with only anecdotal Bayesian evidence (BF = 0.43). Exp. III similarly found no group difference.

> "In Exp. II, the percentages of correct answers were 81% (± 8) in NT and 79% (± 7) in ASD and did not differ between groups (t(31) = 0.8, p = 0.40, d = 0.29, BF = 0.43)"
