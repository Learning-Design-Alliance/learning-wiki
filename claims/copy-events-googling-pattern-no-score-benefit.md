## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2020

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2020). Student behavior and test security in online conceptual assessment. arXiv:1912.05698v1 [physics.ed-ph]. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/perc.2019.pr.wilcox](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2019.pr.wilcox)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Behavioral log analysis of copy and focus events among 207 respondents. The study reports 95 copy events by 18 students, and that 53 of 95 copy events (56%) were followed within 5 seconds by a sustained hidden focus event, a pattern consistent with attempting to Google item prompts.

> "Copy events were more common in the data set than print events, with a total of 95 copy events made by 18 studets (9% of the 207)."

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2020 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2020). Student behavior and test security in online conceptual assessment. arXiv:1912.05698v1 [physics.ed-ph]. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/perc.2019.pr.wilcox](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2019.pr.wilcox)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Score comparison in the only two courses with more than one copy-event student. Copying students "scored lower than the rest of the class by close to 10%", but the difference lacked statistical power and was not significant in either course.

> "In both cases, stu - dents with copy events scored lower than the rest of the class by close to 10%, though small-N in the “copy" category did not allow for sufﬁcient statistical power for this differen ce to be statistically signiﬁcant in either case."
