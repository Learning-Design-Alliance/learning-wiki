## Evidence

### Thomaschewski M 2024

Thomaschewski M, Kist M, Zimmermann M, Benecke C, Kalff JC, Krüger CM, Mann B, Türler A, Keck T, Hummel R. (2024). Conception and prospective multicentric validation of a Robotic Surgery Training Curriculum (RoSTraC) for surgical residents: from simulation via laboratory training to integration into the operation room. Journal of Robotic Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11701-023-01813-6

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Prospective multicenter validation study; seven residents recruited, videos evaluable for "5 out of 7 participants" due to recording limitations, scored blindly by three RAS experts using the GEARS score. Paired t-test gave "p < 0.001" for the GE I to GE II change.

> "Blinded evaluation of the robotic gastroenterostomy before (pre-test/GE I) and after (GE II) step 2 of RoSTraC revealed a mean GEARS score of 17.1 (±5.8) points and 23.1 (±4.9) points, respectively"

### Thomaschewski M 2024 (2)

Thomaschewski M, Kist M, Zimmermann M, Benecke C, Kalff JC, Krüger CM, Mann B, Türler A, Keck T, Hummel R. (2024). Conception and prospective multicentric validation of a Robotic Surgery Training Curriculum (RoSTraC) for surgical residents: from simulation via laboratory training to integration into the operation room. Journal of Robotic Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11701-023-01813-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same validation study, results section: all six GEARS domains improved significantly after step 2 laboratory training; the article prints no per-domain effect sizes, so no magnitudes are reported.

> "surgical participants showed a significant improvement in the RAS performance in all domains of the GEARS score including depth perception, bimanual dexterity, efficiency, force sensitivity, robotic control, and autonomy"
