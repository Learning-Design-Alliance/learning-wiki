## Evidence

### John A. Milsom 2021

John A. Milsom. (2021). The Brachistochrone: An excellent problem for all levels of physics students. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2106.07726](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2106.07726)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Coarse 1-3 grading of six kinematics graphs drawn by all four groups; the author reports "Only the 𝑥(𝑡) graph was drawn well by all groups," with many students treating an acceleration component as constant, illustrated by a Group III student attributing the y-acceleration to gravity alone.

> "Only the 𝑥(𝑡) graph was drawn well by all groups. Many students think 𝑎𝑥 and/or 𝑎𝑦 is constant. For example, one Group III student wrote "The acceleration is g since gravity is the only force in the y-direction.""

### John A. Milsom 2021 (2)

John A. Milsom. (2021). The Brachistochrone: An excellent problem for all levels of physics students. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2106.07726](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2106.07726)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Graph-grading data for Group IV, the seven graduate facilitators; all seven "had 𝑎𝑦 = 0 at the very bottom," and six who realized the sign flip still ended the graph at zero, which the author calls disappointing.

> "All seven Group IV graduate students had 𝑎𝑦 = 0 at the very bottom. While six of them realized that 𝑎𝑦 flipped sign during the descent they had it end at zero."

### John A. Milsom 2021 (3)

John A. Milsom. (2021). The Brachistochrone: An excellent problem for all levels of physics students. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2106.07726](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2106.07726)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Graph-grading across the groups; among the junior physics/astronomy majors in Group III, only one student realized that 𝑎𝑦 "had to flip sign" during the descent.

> "Only one Group III student realized that 𝑎𝑦 had to flip sign."
