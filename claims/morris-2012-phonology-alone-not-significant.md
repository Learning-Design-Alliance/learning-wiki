# In Morris et al. (2012), the phonological (phonics-including) intervention alone showed no significant reading benefits, while conditions adding orthographic and semantic instruction did

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The phonological intervention did not significantly improve any reading outcomes versus a no-instruction control (positive but nonsignificant g = 0.06 to 0.32). [→ Jeffrey S. Bowers 2021](#jeffrey-s-bowers-2021)
`q2 i?` The two interventions adding orthographic and semantic instruction showed similar, significant benefits over the phonology condition across multiple outcome measures. [→ Jeffrey S. Bowers 2021 (2)](#jeffrey-s-bowers-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Jeffrey S. Bowers 2021

Jeffrey S. Bowers. (2021). Yes Children Need to Learn Their GPCs but There Really Is Little or No Evidence that Systematic or Explicit Phonics Is Effective: a response to Fletcher, Savage, and Sharon (2020). Educational Psychology Review (2021) 33:1965–1979. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09602-z

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Morris et al. (2012) intervention study for struggling readers in grades 2–3, described in this commentary: 70 × 30-min phonological lessons including phonics produced no significant improvement on word reading accuracy, spelling, fluency, or comprehension versus no-instruction control (g = 0.06 to 0.32).

> "The authors found that the phonological intervention did not signif- icantly improve any reading outcomes (including word reading accuracy, spelling, fluency, or comprehension) compared to a control condition that included no reading instruction, although the effect sizes were positive, ranging from g = 0.06 to 0.32"

### Jeffrey S. Bowers 2021 (2)

Jeffrey S. Bowers. (2021). Educational Psychology Review (2021) 33:1965–1979. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09602-z

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

In the same Morris et al. (2012) study, the two conditions adding 30 min of orthographic and semantic (including morphological) instruction to each phonological lesson significantly outperformed the phonology-only condition across multiple outcome measures.

> "By contrast, the two interventions that added orthographic and semantic instruction showed similar and significant benefits compared to the phonology condition across multiple outcome measures."
