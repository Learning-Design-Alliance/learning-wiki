# EIR® had a positive, substantively important but statistically non-significant effect on first-grade reading comprehension

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Early Intervention in Reading® 2008

Early Intervention in Reading®. (2008). WWC Intervention Report, What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. http://www.earlyinterventioninreading.com

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

In the Taylor et al. (1991) randomized controlled trial, comprehension was measured with the Gates-MacGinitie reading test. The report notes the effect was "not statistically significant" but met the WWC substantively-important criterion of an effect size of at least 0.25.

> "The study authors did not find statistically significant effects of EIR®, but the effect was positive and large enough to be considered substantively important according to WWC criteria (that is, an effect size greater than or equal to 0.25)."

### Early Intervention in Reading® 2008 (2)

Early Intervention in Reading®. (2008). WWC Intervention Report, What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. http://www.earlyinterventioninreading.com

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

WWC calculations for the Taylor et al. (1991) randomized controlled trial (Appendix A3.2) report a Gates-MacGinitie mean difference of 3.62 favoring EIR®, an "effect size" of 0.47, marked ns (not statistically significant), with an improvement index of +18.

> "Gates-MacGinitie Reading Test Grade 1 12/57 20.76 (8.03) 17.14 (6.97) 3.62 0.47 ns +18"
