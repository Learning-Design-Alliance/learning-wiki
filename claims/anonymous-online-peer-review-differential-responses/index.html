Evidence¶

Gerry Stahl & Friedrich Hesse 2011¶

Gerry Stahl & Friedrich Hesse. (2011). CSCL in Asia. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning 6:1–7. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-011-9112-1

q2 · i? · review · r2

Zi-Gang Ge's experiment with engineering students at a university in Beijing classified students by English writing ability; asynchronous anonymous online exchanges were hypothesized to lessen the Chinese cultural aversion to criticizing face-to-face.

"those with lower skills sensed that they were being reviewed by stronger students and often felt inferior, which sometimes increased their determination to improve."

Gerry Stahl & Friedrich Hesse 2011 (2)¶

Gerry Stahl & Friedrich Hesse. (2011). CSCL in Asia. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning 6:1–7. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-011-9112-1

q2 · i? · review · r1

The same Beijing peer-review experiment found generally positive student responses, but stronger writers reported receiving lower-quality advice than they provided; the editorial compares this to Western engineering students resisting collaboration.

"The better writers, on the other hand, felt that they were not learning as much from the process because the advice they received was not as good as what they gave."

Related Claims¶

  • Anonymity in computer-mediated collaboration can produce a stronger group identity than non-anonymous face-to-face situations — related
  • In two secondary-school writing studies, peer reviewers' own performance predicted how helpful their feedback was but not how accurately it identified problems — related
  • Effortful, fluent-feeling experiences can create an illusion of learning: felt sense of learning is a poor gauge of actual learning — related
  • In one experiment with undergraduate business majors in asynchronous online small groups, reward interdependence raised agreement that group work was beneficial and generated better ideas — related
  • Peer assessment of writing yields outcomes at least as good as teacher assessment and sometimes better — related
