Evidence¶

Joly Louise 2026¶

Joly Louise, Nunes de Sousa Alexandra, Bardiau Marjorie, Dory Valérie, Bayot Marie, Lenoir Anne-Laure. (2026). Impact of emotional competence on clinical reasoning: a scoping review. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2025-113483

q2 · i? · review · r3

Narrative synthesis of the review's patient-outcome studies (33 of 98 articles); the review reports these associations from mostly non-interventional literature with no printed effect size.

"When patients were treated by physicians with higher global scores of EC or empathy, they were more satisfied with care and displayed fewer symptoms of anxiety and depression."

Joly Louise 2026 (2)¶

Joly Louise, Nunes de Sousa Alexandra, Bardiau Marjorie, Dory Valérie, Bayot Marie, Lenoir Anne-Laure. (2026). Impact of emotional competence on clinical reasoning: a scoping review. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2025-113483

q2 · i? · review · r3

Narrative synthesis reporting the mixed obstetric findings and, separately, that mindfulness-based emotional regulation showed no conclusive effect on medical errors. The review identifies patient outcomes as a major evidence gap.

"Obstetricians exhibiting greater emotional regulation (eg, lower anxiety and more reflective coping) were associated on one hand with fewer operative deliveries, a trend towards more spontaneous births and on the other hand with increased risks of postpartum haemorrhage and chorioamnionitis."
