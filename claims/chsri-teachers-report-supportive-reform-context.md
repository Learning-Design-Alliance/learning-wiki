# Teachers in CHSRI small schools report significantly more supportive contexts for reform than similar teachers in other Chicago high schools

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` CHSRI teachers scored significantly higher on teacher-teacher trust (effect size = 0.37), collective responsibility (0.47), commitment to innovation (0.52), and teacher influence (0.83). [→ Kahne 2006](#kahne-2006)

## Evidence

### Kahne 2006

Kahne, J. E., Sporte, S., de la Torre, M., with Easton, J. Q. (2006). Small High Schools on a Larger Scale: The First Three Years of the Chicago High School Redesign Initiative. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/small-schools-larger-scale-first-three-years-chicago-high-school-redesign-initiative

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Survey analysis using three-level hierarchical linear models of 190 teachers in 10 CHSRI schools and 3,083 teachers in 42 non-CHSRI schools (2005 Consortium survey), controlling for individual and school characteristics. Reported effect sizes: trust "effect size = 0.37", collective responsibility 0.47, innovation 0.52, teacher influence "a 'substantial' difference, with an effect size of 0.83".

> "once we controlled for the academic and demographic qualities of students, teachers, and their schools, we found that CHSRI teachers had significantly higher scores on all of the indicators of Box 6, Teacher Context for Reform, than similar teachers at other schools."
