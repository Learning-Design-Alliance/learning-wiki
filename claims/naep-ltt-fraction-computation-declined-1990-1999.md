## Evidence

### Hartman 2016

Hartman, JudithAnn R., and Eric A. Nelson. (2016). Automaticity in Computation and Student Success in Introductory Physical Science Courses. Originally published 2015. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1608.05006](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1608.05006)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Item analysis of fraction questions on the calculator-free NAEP Long-Term Trend test, which kept the same structure from 1971 to 1999. Scores “declined from a national average of 76% correct in 1990 to 56% correct in 1999.”

> "In 2004, Loveless and Coughlan reported that on LTT questions about fractions, scores for 17 year olds declined from a national average of 76% correct in 1990 to 56% correct in 1999 (Figure 4)."

### Hartman 2016 (2)

Hartman, JudithAnn R., and Eric A. Nelson. (2016). Automaticity in Computation and Student Success in Introductory Physical Science Courses. Originally published 2015. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1608.05006](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1608.05006)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Comparison of gains on the two NAEP mathematics assessments between 1990 and 1999 reported by Loveless and Coughlan: main NAEP gains “were 10 times larger than gains on the LTT during the same period.”

> "As one outcome, the main NAEP showed gains between 1990 and 1999 that were 10 times larger than gains on the LTT during the same period (Loveless and Coughlan 2004)."
