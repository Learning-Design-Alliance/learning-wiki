Evidence¶

Jan Cvengros and Gerd Kortemeyer 2026¶

Jan Cvengros and Gerd Kortemeyer. (2026). Assisting the grading of a handwritten general chemistry exam with artificial intelligence. Discover Artificial Intelligence. https://doi.org/10.1007/s44163-026-01606-4

q2 · i? · design · r2

Descriptive linear-fit comparison of AI-assigned versus TA-assigned total scores for 296 consenting students in the ETH Zurich general chemistry final exam. The article reports that "the aggregate total score shown in the top-left panel of Fig. 11 achieved an R2 of 0.91", attributing the gain over per-problem fits to partial cancellation of unsystematic item-level misgrading.

"the aggregate total score shown in the top-left panel of Fig. 11 achieved an R2 of 0.91"

Jan Cvengros and Gerd Kortemeyer 2026 (2)¶

Jan Cvengros and Gerd Kortemeyer. (2026). Assisting the grading of a handwritten general chemistry exam with artificial intelligence. Discover Artificial Intelligence. https://doi.org/10.1007/s44163-026-01606-4

q2 · i? · design · r2

Per-problem scatterplots and linear regressions of AI-assigned versus TA-assigned points (Fig. 10), one data point per student. The article reports R2 "ranging from 0.61 to 0.85" and states this per-problem reliability "is low and not suitable for grading without additional measures".

"for the individual six problems, the AI-derived scores correlated with human scores at coefficients of determination ( R2) ranging from 0.61 to 0.85"

Related Claims¶

  • LLM grading performance relative to human graders is mixed: comparable in 12 reviewed cases, worse in 11, with the strongest alignment on short, straightforward tasks — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it
  • AI grading of the exam is highly stable across five independent runs at the total-score level (ICC(A,1) = 0.967), with lower but still strong cell-level stability (ICC(A,1) = 0.836) — related
  • AI total scores correlate strongly with official human scores on handwritten physics assessments (r = 0.91–0.97 in Round I; 0.93–0.96 in Round II) — related
  • Round II raised exact question-part agreement from about 63% to about 70% and cut parts differing by more than one point from about 13% to 7%, while exact partial-credit scoring remained hardest — related
  • LLM-based rubric scores agreed with expert ratings at the total-score level and on cognitive-process dimensions, but agreement was low for more subjective dimensions — related
  • A page-by-page, rubric-guided multimodal LLM workflow graded an authentic handwritten exam in about three hours at roughly $100 in token costs, versus about $3,500 in TA time — related
  • The revised Round II grading workflow reduced AI over-awarding and total-score MAD, most clearly for OE1 (7.1% to 4.8%) and QM (9.4% to 3.8%) — related
  • AI grading errors concentrate in graphical tasks and include both false positives on incorrect equations and false negatives from misread sketches and labels — related
