## Evidence

### Killingsworth 2015

Killingsworth, S. S., Clark, D. B., & Adams, D. M. (2015). Self-Explanation and Explanatory Feedback in Games: Individual Differences, Gameplay, and Learning. International Journal of Education in Mathematics, Science and Technology, 3(3), 162-186. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1066360

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Regression models controlling for pre-test performance and highest level completed, with separate models for near- and far-transfer questions. The advantage for self-explanation was observed specifically for near-transfer items and was described as small but significant.

> "For the model of near -transfer question post -test performance, game condition was a significant predictor of post -test scores, with students scoring higher i n the self -explanation condition than in the explanatory feedback condition, b = 11.129, SEb = 4.518, p = .016, 95% CI (2.141, 20.118). For the model of far -transfer question post -test performance, game condition was not a significant predictor, b = -.465, SEb = 5.149, p = .928, 95% CI (-10.711, 9.780)."
