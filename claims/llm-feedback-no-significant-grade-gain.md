## Evidence

### Letteri 2025

Letteri, I. & Vittorini, P. (2025). Enhancing Student Feedback in Data Science Education: Harnessing the Power of AI-Generated Approaches. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-025-00492-8

`q2 · quasi-experiment (two academic-year cohorts, not randomised)` · `i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of final grades between two consecutive, non-randomised cohorts: AY 2022/23 (139 enrolled, schematic feedback from traditional AI) and AY 2023/24 (136 enrolled, combined feedback with LLMs), using independent-samples t-tests. Grades were collected only from students who attended the first exam session, so the analysed numbers are smaller than the enrolled cohorts (106 graded students in the 2023/24 cohort's exercise analysis; the analysed n for each year in the cohort comparison is given only in Table 4). The article reports the difference in average grade as not statistically significant.

> "The comparison between the two A Ys 2022/23 and 2023/24 (when we introduced the feedback based on LLMs) shows an improvement in the average ﬁnal grades (from 27.6/30 of the A Y 2022/23 to 28.2/30 of the A Y 2023/23), even if this difference is not statistically signiﬁcant (see bottom of Table 4)."
