# In one single-group pre-post study, VSD and RVOT patch appliance times fell significantly from the first to third simulation surgery among inexperienced cardiothoracic surgeons on a 3D-printed TOF model

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Six attendees' median VSD patch appliance time fell from 34.4 to 17.3 minutes and RVOT patch time from 21.4 to 13.6 minutes between the first and third simulation surgeries (p = 0.03 and p = 0.01). [→ Nam Ju Gang 2021](#nam-ju-gang-2021)

## Evidence

### Nam Ju Gang 2021

Nam Ju Gang, Lee Whal, Jeong Baren, Park Eun-Ah, Lim Ji Yeon, Kwak Yujin, Lim Hong-Gook. (2021). Three-Dimensional Printing of Congenital Heart Disease Models for Cardiac Surgery Simulation: Evaluation of Surgical Skill Improvement among Inexperienced Cardiothoracic Surgeons. Korean Journal of Radiology. https://doi.org/10.3348/kjr.2020.0682

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental repeated-measures study: six attendees (two junior residents, two senior residents, two clinical fellows) each performed three simulation surgeries at approximately 1-week intervals in an operating room. Median patch times, quoted as "significantly reduced to 17.3 (16.2–29.5) and 13.6 (10.3–30.0) minutes", with Wilcoxon signed-rank or paired t tests; no effect size printed.

> "six surgeons required a median of 34.4 (range 32.5–43.5) and 21.4 (17.9–192.7) minutes to apply the ventricular septal defect (VSD) and right ventricular outflow tract (RVOT) patches, respectively, on their first simulation surgery. These times had significantly reduced to 17.3 (16.2–29.5) and 13.6 (10.3–30.0) minutes, respectively, in the third simulation surgery (p = 0.03 and p = 0.01, respectively)"
