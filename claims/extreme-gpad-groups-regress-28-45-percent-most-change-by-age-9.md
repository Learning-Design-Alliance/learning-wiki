## Evidence

### Kaito Kawakami 2024

Kaito Kawakami, Francesca Procopio, Kaili Rimfeld, Margherita Malanchini, Sophie von Stumm, Kathryn Asbury, Robert Plomin. (2024). Exploring the genetic prediction of academic underachievement and overachievement. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00251-9

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Group trajectory analysis of relative and absolute GPAΔ extremes in TEDS (Fig. 4). Absolute overachievers regressed to 45.3%, relative overachievers 38.9%, relative underachievers 28.4%, absolute underachievers 30.6%; "most of the change occurs by age 9."

> "both absolute and relative groups returned to 28-45% of their predicted levels (Fig. 4). It is noteworthy that most of the change occurs by age 9, suggesting the importance of the early school years."

### Kaito Kawakami 2024 (2)

Kaito Kawakami, Francesca Procopio, Kaili Rimfeld, Margherita Malanchini, Sophie von Stumm, Kathryn Asbury, Robert Plomin. (2024). Exploring the genetic prediction of academic underachievement and overachievement. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00251-9

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Decile analysis (N = 1981) of children whose age-7 achievement was within 0.5 SD of the mean, stratified by GPAΔ decile. Lowest-decile underachievers improved and highest-decile overachievers declined by almost half a standard deviation by age 16.

> "the lowest decile of GPA Δ underachievers at age 7 improved by nearly half a standard deviation by age 16 (top line in Fig.5). Conversely, the achievement of the highest decile of GPAΔ overachievers at age 7 declined by almost half a standard deviation by age 16."
