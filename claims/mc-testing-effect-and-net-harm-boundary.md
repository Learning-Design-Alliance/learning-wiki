## Evidence

### Andrew C. Butler and Henry L. Roediger III 2008

Andrew C. Butler and Henry L. Roediger III. (2008). Feedback enhances the positive effects and reduces the negative effects of multiple-choice testing. Memory & Cognition, 36(3), 604-616. https://doi.org/10.3758/MC.36.3.604

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Comparison of the tested (no-feedback) condition against the no-test control on forced-report cued recall, d = .97. The article calls this the basic testing effect, generally quite robust.

> "an additional t test revealed that the no-feedback condition (which received a test ) produced a si gnificantly greater proportion of correct responses than did the no-test condi-tion [t(71)  9.11, SEM  0.018, d  .97, prep  1.00], showing the basic testing effect."
