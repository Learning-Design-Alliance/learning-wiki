# Lecturer-generated summaries spliced into online video lectures improve student recall of lecture content on quizzes

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Fanguy 2017

Fanguy, M., Costley, J., & Baldwin, M. (2017). Pinch Hitter: The Effectiveness of Content Summaries Delivered by a Guest Lecturer in Online Course Videos. International Review of Research in Open and Distributed Learning, 18(7). http://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl/article/view/3210

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of 118 graduate students across seven sections of a flipped Scientific Writing course at KAIST, with groups assigned by section. The Scheffe post-hoc test showed the summaries-only group scored .998 points higher than control (p < .05); no effect size was printed.

> "Table 5 shows that in the lecture with summary group, the quiz mean was .998 higher than that of the basic lecture group."

### Fanguy 2017 (2)

Fanguy, M., Costley, J., & Baldwin, M. (2017). Pinch Hitter: The Effectiveness of Content Summaries Delivered by a Guest Lecturer in Online Course Videos. International Review of Research in Open and Distributed Learning, 18(7). http://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl/article/view/3210

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

One-way ANOVA on quiz scores across the three conditions (control, summaries only, summaries with guest lecturer) found a significant omnibus difference, F = 3.633, p = .005, supporting the presence of a condition effect before pairwise testing.

> "Table 4 shows that there was a statistically significant difference between the three groups that were a part of this study (p = <.01)."
