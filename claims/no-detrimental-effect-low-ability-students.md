# Rank feedback did not produce the detrimental effects on low-ability students reported in some prior relative-rewarding studies

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Contrary to laboratory and field findings that relative performance information lowers performance of low-performing students, this experiment found feedback reduced dropout and raised low performers' scores instead. [→ Kajitani Shinya 2020](#kajitani-shinya-2020)

## Evidence

### Kajitani Shinya 2020

Kajitani Shinya, Morimoto Keiichi, Suzuki Shiba. (2020). Information feedback in relative grading: Evidence from a field experiment. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0231548

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' discussion of their own RCT results against prior literature (Eriksson et al.; Freeman and Gelber; Ashraf et al.) reporting negative effects on low performers. The article offers this as an interpretation of its own null-detriment finding.

> "However, we did not find such detrimental effects. Instead, we find that relative performance information feedback reduces the number of students who drop out before the final examination."
