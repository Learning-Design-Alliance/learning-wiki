# AI-generated and prebuilt simulations produced higher immediate conceptual-assessment scores than physical laboratory work, but the advantage did not persist in later examinations

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Ben-Zion et al. (2026) found higher immediate conceptual-assessment scores in AI-generated and prebuilt simulation conditions than in the physical-laboratory condition, with no persistence in midterm and final examinations. [→ Malikova Z 2026](#malikova-z-2026)

`q2 i?` Median midterm performance did not differ significantly across lab types (H(2)=0.837, p=.658), nor did final exam performance (H(2)=3.302, p=.192). [→ Yossi Ben-Zion 2026](#yossi-ben-zion-2026)

`q2 i?` The lab-induced differences on conceptual questions vanished on subsequent exams, which the authors attribute to continued lecture, homework, and interactive instruction after the lab. [→ Yossi Ben-Zion 2026 (2)](#yossi-ben-zion-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Malikova Z 2026

Malikova Z, Ualikhanova B, Turmambekov T, Rakhashev B and Berdaliyev D (2026). Artificial intelligence in university physics education: a systematic review of empirical studies. Front. Educ. 11:1964629. https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2026.1964629

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports this quasi-experimental study (initial enrollment 187; three laboratory conditions) in Section 3.4; Table 5 lists η2 = 0.359 for the immediate conceptual-learning comparison.

> "Ben-Zion et al. (2026) similarly reported higher immediate conceptual-assessment scores in the AI-generated and prebuilt simulation conditions than in the physical-laboratory condition. However, these differences did not persist in the midterm and final examinations"

### Yossi Ben-Zion 2026

Yossi Ben-Zion, Turhan K. Carroll, Colin G. West, Jesse Wong, and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2026). Leveraging generative artificial intelligence for simulation-based physics experiments: A new approach to virtual learning about the real world. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1103/s8dy-kqy5](https://doi.org/10.1103/s8dy-kqy5)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Kruskal-Wallis tests on midterm and final exam totals across the three lab groups found "no significant difference in median MQ performance across lab type"; equivalence was not tested, and final exam differences were also not significant (H(2)=3.302, p=.192).

> "We found that there was no significant difference in median MQ performance across lab type, HMQTot(2) = 0.837, p = .658"

### Yossi Ben-Zion 2026 (2)

Yossi Ben-Zion, Turhan K. Carroll, Colin G. West, Jesse Wong, and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2026). Leveraging generative artificial intelligence for simulation-based physics experiments: A new approach to virtual learning about the real world. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1103/s8dy-kqy5](https://doi.org/10.1103/s8dy-kqy5)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The authors contrast this non-persistence with earlier simulation studies where learning differences persisted through the term, explaining that in this course the lab was followed by continued discussion in lectures, homework, and exam reviews using interactive methods; the differences "vanished on the subsequent midterm and final exam questions".

> "the differences in conceptual performance difference among the treatment groups that showed up following the lab vanished on the subsequent midterm and final exam questions."
