# Conceptions of motivation to learn relate positively to self-regulation and external regulation and negatively to lack of regulation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1`–`r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the ILS model, motivation to learn had a negative relationship with lack of regulation (standardized estimate = -.31) and positive relationships with self-regulation (.28) and external regulation (.34). [→ Loyens 2008](#loyens-2008)
`q3 i?` In the MSLQ model, motivation to learn had a strong relationship with self-regulation (standardized estimate = .59). [→ Loyens 2008 (2)](#loyens-2008-2)

## Evidence

### Loyens 2008

Loyens, S. M. M., Rikers, R. M. J. P., & Schmidt, H. G. (2008). Relationships between students' conceptions of constructivist learning and their regulation and processing strategies. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-008-9065-6

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

SEM path analysis of the ILS model (N = 98); Table 3 shows significant standardized estimates of .28 (self-regulation), .34 (external regulation), and -.31 (lack of regulation), all p < .01, for paths from motivation to learn.

> "Conceptions of motivation to learn had a negative relationship with lack of regulation and positive relationships with self-regulation as well as external regulation."

### Loyens 2008 (2)

Loyens, S. M. M., Rikers, R. M. J. P., & Schmidt, H. G. (2008). Relationships between students' conceptions of constructivist learning and their regulation and processing strategies. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-008-9065-6

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

SEM path analysis of the MSLQ model (N = 98); Table 4 reports a standardized estimate of .59 (p < .01) for motivation to learn on MSLQ self-regulation, one of only two significant paths in that model.

> "On the conceptual level, motivation to learn had a strong relationship with self-regulation."
