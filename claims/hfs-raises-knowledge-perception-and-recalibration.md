## Evidence

### Cortegiani A 2015

Cortegiani A, Russotto V, Montalto F, Iozzo P, Palmeri C, Raineri SM, Giarratano A. (2015). Effect of High-Fidelity Simulation on Medical Students' Knowledge about Advanced Life Support: A Randomized Study. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0125685

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Secondary outcome from the randomized study: Likert-scale personal perception of ALS knowledge compared between group S and group C with Mann-Whitney U-test, as quoted with "p = 0.0074, U = 756.50, Z = 2.68".

> "Students in group S showed a significantly higher overall score in the post-test subjective section (8.0, IQR 7.0–9.0) than students in group C (6.5, IQR 6.0–8.0), p = 0.0074, U = 756.50, Z = 2.68."

### Cortegiani A 2015 (2)

Cortegiani A, Russotto V, Montalto F, Iozzo P, Palmeri C, Raineri SM, Giarratano A. (2015). Effect of High-Fidelity Simulation on Medical Students' Knowledge about Advanced Life Support: A Randomized Study. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0125685

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary analysis of the perception-confirmation question in the same randomized study; the quote prints the proportions 60.4% (group C) versus 32.6% (group S) and p = 0.0079. Among non-confirming group S students, 74.2% (23/31) attributed the change to HFS.

> "The proportion of students confirming their previously given perception of knowledge in ALS was significantly different among the two groups (p = 0.0079), with 60.4% of subjects in group C (31/48) confirming their previously reported perception compared to 32.6% in group S (15/46)."
