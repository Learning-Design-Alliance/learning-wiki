# Adding cross-lagged effects significantly improves model fit over autoregressive models for every model tested

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Achievement and self-concept influence each other over time beyond their initial correlation, as shown by significant fit improvements from adding cross-lagged paths. [→ Helmke 1995](#helmke-1995)

## Evidence

### Helmke 1995

Helmke, A., & van Aken, M. A. G. (1995). The causal ordering of academic achievement and self-concept of ability during elementary school: A longitudinal study. Journal of Educational Psychology, 87(4), 624-637. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.87.4.624

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Across all tested LISREL models (Models 1, 2a, 2b, 3 variants) on the 697-student panel, autoregressive-only counterparts were tested against models with cross-lagged effects; the improvement was "significant (p < .001)" in every case. No effect size printed.

> "In all cases, the addition of cross-lagged effects led to significant (p < .001) improvements in the fit of the models."
