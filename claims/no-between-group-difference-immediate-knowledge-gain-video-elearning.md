# Interspersed short answer questions or logic puzzles during video viewing produced no significant difference in immediate knowledge gain compared with uninterrupted viewing among pediatric residents

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among pediatric residents watching an HFOV video, the change in pre- to post-test scores did not differ significantly across the full video, logic puzzles, and short answer questions groups. [→ Lisa A DelSignore 2016](#lisa-a-delsignore-2016)

## Evidence

### Lisa A DelSignore 2016

Lisa A DelSignore, Traci A Wolbrink, David Zurakowski, Jeffrey P Burns. (2016). Test-Enhanced E-Learning Strategies in Postgraduate Medical Education: A Randomized Cohort Study. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.6199

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized cohort study of 49 pediatric residents at 3 Boston academic medical centers, randomized to full video (n=17), logic puzzles (n=16), or short answer questions (n=16). One-way ANOVA of pre-to-post change found "comparable mean improvement" across groups (F2,46=0.35, P=.71).

> "One-way ANOVA revealed comparable mean improvement in the change in the test score from pre- to post-test in all 3 groups during the initial testing session (F2,46=0.35, P=.71)."
