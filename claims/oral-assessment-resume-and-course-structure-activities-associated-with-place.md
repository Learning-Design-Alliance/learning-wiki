## Evidence

### Laurence Caroline O 2013

Laurence Caroline O, Zajac Ian T, Lorimer Michelle, Turnbull Deborah A, Sumner Karen E. (2013). The impact of preparatory activities on medical school selection outcomes: a cross-sectional survey of applicants to the university of Adelaide medical school in 2007. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-159

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression on the 359 applicants who received an oral assessment interview in the 2007 Adelaide survey. The most common activities were learning about course structure (65%), discussing the process with previous applicants (59%), preparing answers (51%), and practice interviews with family and friends (49%); only two activities remained significant after correction.

> "These activities were refining and learning a personal resume (OR 9.73, 95% CI 2.97 - 31.88; P < 0.001 corrected) and learning the course structure (OR 2.05, 95% CI 1.29 - 3.26; P = 0.022 corrected)."
