## Evidence

### Stephen Akinyemi Omotayo & Joshua Oluwatoyin Adeleke 2017

Stephen Akinyemi Omotayo & Joshua Oluwatoyin Adeleke. (2017). The 5E Instructional Model: A Constructivist Approach for Enhancing Students' Learning Outcomes in Mathematics. JISTE, Vol. 21, No. 2, 2017. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1176946

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pretest comparison in the same quasi-experiment, one week before treatment began. The independent t-test found "no significant difference" between groups on the Mathematics Achievement Test; equivalence was not formally tested beyond this non-significant result.

> "there was no significant difference between the mean achievement scores of students in the 5E instructional group (M = 7.52, SD = 2.29) and those in the control group (M= 7.76, SD = 2.64), t(170) = .64, p > 0.05."

### Stephen Akinyemi Omotayo & Joshua Oluwatoyin Adeleke 2017 (2)

Stephen Akinyemi Omotayo & Joshua Oluwatoyin Adeleke. (2017). The 5E Instructional Model: A Constructivist Approach for Enhancing Students' Learning Outcomes in Mathematics. JISTE, Vol. 21, No. 2, 2017. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1176946

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pretest interest comparison on the Mathematics Interest Inventory in the same study. The t-test found "no significant difference" between groups before treatment; no effect size is printed.

> "there was no significant difference between the mean interest scores of students in the 5E group (M = 44.06, SD = 9.28) and those in the traditional instruction (control) group (M = 43.33, SD = 9.43), t(170) = .51 p > 0.05."
