Evidence¶

Garvin Brod 2021¶

Garvin Brod. (2021). Toward an understanding of when prior knowledge helps or hinders learning. npj Science of Learning 6:24. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00103-w

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

Review-reported research on the Baker–baker paradox: surname associations lack relevant knowledge networks, whereas a common profession activates a large knowledge network participants reported using to link the profession to the face.

"The paradox describes the ﬁnding that remembering a face –name association (i.e., that a person ’s surname is Baker) is disproportionately harder than remembering face –profession associations (i.e., that a person’s profession is baker)."

Garvin Brod 2021 (2)¶

Garvin Brod. (2021). Toward an understanding of when prior knowledge helps or hinders learning. npj Science of Learning 6:24. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00103-w

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Review-reported study (Castel et al.): football experts who studied animal names that were also football-team names later falsely recalled many non-presented team names, a pattern absent in a body-parts control task, illustrating perceptual biases and intrusions from irrelevant knowledge.

"This resulted in a higher number of falsely recalled words in experts than in non-experts, a pattern that was absent in a control task in which participants had to study body parts"
