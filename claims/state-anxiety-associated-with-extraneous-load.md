## Evidence

### Fredericks Salim 2021

Fredericks Salim, ElSayed Mostafa, Hammad Mustafa, Abumiddain Omneya, Istwani Leila, Rabeea Abdulla, Rashid-Doubell Fiza, Bella Abdelhaleem M.E. (2021). Anxiety is associated with extraneous cognitive load during teaching using high-fidelity clinical simulation. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2021.1994691

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Observational analysis in a cross-sectional study: cognitive load measured with the Leppink CLS after scenarios. The study found post-scenario STAI-S and ECL "positively correlated (r = 0.356, p < 0.010)" and the high- versus low-state-anxiety contrast significant by Mann-Whitney U test.

> "Post-scenario STAI-S scores and ECL rating were found to be positively correlated (r = 0.356, p < 0.010). Students with high state-anxiety had higher ECL scores (Mdn = 2.0) than students with low state-anxiety (Mdn = 0.9) at the post scenario time point, as tested using a Mann-Whitney U test (U = 220.0, p = 0.034)"

### Fredericks Salim 2021 (2)

Fredericks Salim, ElSayed Mostafa, Hammad Mustafa, Abumiddain Omneya, Istwani Leila, Rabeea Abdulla, Rashid-Doubell Fiza, Bella Abdelhaleem M.E. (2021). Anxiety is associated with extraneous cognitive load during teaching using high-fidelity clinical simulation. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2021.1994691

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same cross-sectional study: Pearson correlations between STAI scores and the ICL and SPL subscales found no significant relations; equivalence was not tested. Table 2 Mann-Whitney comparisons for ICL and SPL across anxiety groups were all non-significant.

> "No significant correlations were found for either ICL or SPL with any of the STAI scores."
