## Evidence

### McKenna K. D. 2015

McKenna K. D., Carhart E., Bercher D., Spain A., Todaro J., Freel J. (2015). Simulation Use in Paramedic Education Research (SUPER): A Descriptive Study. Prehospital Emergency Care. [doi:10.3109/10903127.2014.995845](https://doi.org/10.3109/10903127.2014.995845)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional census survey of accredited paramedic programs with 389 valid responses (61% response rate), analyzed with descriptive statistics. Respondents reported the availability percentages above, and use rates lagging availability, e.g., advanced manikins used by 71% and virtual reality by 4%.

> "Paramedic programs reported they have or have access to a wide range of simulation resources (task trainers [100%], simple manikins [100%], intermediate manikins [99%], advanced/fully programmable manikins [91%], live simulated patients [83%], computer-based [71%], and virtual reality [19%]); however, they do not consistently use them"
