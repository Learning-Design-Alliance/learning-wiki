# Within-group gains on the multiple-choice achievement test were significant only for the Roundhouse Diagram group, not the control group

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2014 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The experimental group's pretest-posttest gain on the Force and Motion Subject Achievement Test was statistically significant (t=3.053, p=0.005), while the control group's increase was not statistically significant (t=0.255, p=0.802). [→ Kocakaya and Gönen 2014](#kocakaya-and-gonen-2014)

## Evidence

### Kocakaya and Gönen 2014

F. Kocakaya, S. Gönen. (2014). Influence of Computer-Assisted Roundhouse Diagrams on High School 9th Grade Students’ Understanding the Subjects of “Force and Motion”. Science Education International, 25(3), 283-311. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1044458

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Paired sample t-tests of pretest-posttest achievement test scores: experimental group rose from 4.77 to 6.40 (t=3.053, p=0.005, df=29); control group from 4.29 to 4.41 (t=0.255, p=0.802, df=16). No effect size printed for these paired comparisons.

> "the experimental group students’ levels of achievement increased significantly (P<0.05). In addition, the results of the paired sample t -test analysis conducted revealed an increase in the control group students’ achievement, yet it was not statistically significant (P>0.05)."
