# Blended-learning and didactic EBM courses produced no difference in EBM competency measured with the Berlin tool in graduate medical students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Second-year graduate medical students taught EBM via blended learning did not differ from didactic-taught peers on the validated Berlin tool (p = 0.29). [→ Ilic Dragan 2013](#ilic-dragan-2013)

## Evidence

### Ilic Dragan 2013

Ilic Dragan, Hart William, Fiddes Patrick, Misso Marie, Villanueva Elmer. (2013). Adopting a blended learning approach to teaching evidence based medicine: a mixed methods study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-169

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Controlled trial (not randomised) with 61 of 71 eligible second-year graduate MBBS students; BL (n = 34) versus DID (n = 27) on the 15-question Berlin tool. The article reports "No statistical difference in EBM competency" with p = 0.29; means were 6.08 (BL) versus 6.77 (DID). Equivalence was not tested.

> "No statistical difference in EBM competency was identified between students allocated to the DID group versus the BL group when using the Berlin tool (p = 0.29)"
