## Evidence

### M.M.L. Muijselaar 2017

M.M.L. Muijselaar, N.M. Swart, E.G. Steenbeek-Planting, M. Droop, L. Verhoeven, P.F. de Jong. (2017). The dimensions of reading comprehension in Dutch children: Is differentiation by text and question type necessary? Journal of Educational Psychology, 109(1), 70-83. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000120

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis of factor scores from the multitrait, multimethod models fitted to items completed by 996 fourth-graders. The article reports the specific-factor scores "were not reliable" and shows no effect size for this null pattern.

> "Factor scores that were supposed to reflect specific comprehension factors were not reliable and were hardly related to word reading speed, vocabulary, and working memory."

### M.M.L. Muijselaar 2017 (2)

M.M.L. Muijselaar, N.M. Swart, E.G. Steenbeek-Planting, M. Droop, L. Verhoeven, P.F. de Jong. (2017). The dimensions of reading comprehension in Dutch children: Is differentiation by text and question type necessary? Journal of Educational Psychology, 109(1), 70-83. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000120

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The study examined correlations between the different comprehension factor measures and three cognitive abilities — "word reading speed, vocabulary, and working memory" — in the same sample; specific factors showed hardly any relation (per the companion finding).

> "In addition, the correlations of factor scores, reflecting the different measures of reading comprehension, with word reading speed, vocabulary, and working memory were examined."
