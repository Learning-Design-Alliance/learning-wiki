## Evidence

### Wei Yingying 2025

Wei Yingying, Deng Na, Zheng Hongmei, Guo Haijun, Zhang Huiling, Li Bing, Wu Daiqin, Li Wei, Si Xiaoyun. (2025). Comparison of teacher-standardized patients and peer role-play in assessing medical students' history-taking skills. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07758-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized group comparison of history-taking completeness during consultations with real patients (Table 4: F = 6.516, p = 0.020), with scores "in descending order for the spRP, non-spRP, and TSP groups."

> "The difference in students'scores of completeness of history taking was statistically significant (p < 0.05), and the scores were in descending order for the spRP, non-spRP, and TSP groups."

### Wei Yingying 2025 (2)

Wei Yingying, Deng Na, Zheng Hongmei, Guo Haijun, Zhang Huiling, Li Bing, Wu Daiqin, Li Wei, Si Xiaoyun. (2025). Comparison of teacher-standardized patients and peer role-play in assessing medical students' history-taking skills. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07758-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within present-illness subitems of the same assessment, only causes/triggers (F = 7.144, p = 0.001), main-symptom characteristics (F = 3.63, p = 0.030), and accompanying symptoms (F = 5.443, p = 0.005) differed significantly; onset, progression, diagnosis/treatment, and general condition did not (all p > 0.05).

> "the differences in the scores of causes and triggers, characteristics of the main symptoms, and concomitant symptoms were statistically significant (all p < 0.05), with highest to lowest scores in the given order: spRP group, non-spRP group, and TSP group."
