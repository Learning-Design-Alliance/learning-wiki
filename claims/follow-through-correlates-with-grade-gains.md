# Greater follow-through with planned study strategies correlates with larger quiz grade gains (R² = 0.17)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Percent follow-through (rereporting planned strategies across sequential quiz pairs) was positively associated with change in quiz grade, with R² = 0.17, described by the article as a small to medium effect size. [→ Hazlett 2025](#hazlett-2025)
`q3 i?` Students who rereported a mix of active, metacognitive, and passive strategies gained an average of 19.9% between quiz pairs, significantly more than other categories; students rereporting only passive strategies or no strategies showed average grade decreases. ## Evidence

### Hazlett 2025

Hazlett, Z. S., Jimenez, P. C., & Knight, J. K. (2025). Self-Testing and Follow-Through of Learning Strategies Supports Student Success. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.24-04-0128

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis comparing percent follow-through between post-quiz survey pairs (quiz 1 to 3, quiz 3 to 5) with grade change for N = 102 students; the article prints R² = 0.17, which it calls a small to medium effect size. No standardized effect size (d, r, η²) is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "This analysis demonstrated a positive association between percent follow-through and change in quiz grade (Figure 2A; R2 = 0.17 coefficient of determination, a small to medium effect size, N = 102; Sullivan and Feinn, 2012)."

### Hazlett 2025 (2)

Hazlett, Z. S., Jimenez, P. C., & Knight, J. K. (2025). Self-Testing and Follow-Through of Learning Strategies Supports Student Success. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.24-04-0128

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Kruskal–Wallis with Dunn's post-hoc test comparing grade change across rereporting categories (Figure 2B): the mixed active/metacognitive/passive group averaged a 19.9% gain, the only-active/metacognitive group 9.5%, while only-passive and no-follow-through groups decreased (−8.4% and −1.2%). No effect size is printed.

> "students who rereported a mix of active, metacognitive, and passive strategies experienced, on average, a 19.9% quiz grade change between the sequential pairs of quizzes (Q1-to-Q3 or Q3-to-Q5), significantly higher than any of the other categories (p < 0.05; Kruskal–Wallis, Dunn's post-hoc test)."
