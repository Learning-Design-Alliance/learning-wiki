Evidence¶

George 2021¶

George, J., & Darling-Hammond, L. (2021). Advancing integration and equity through magnet schools. Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/magnet-schools

q2 · i? · review · r2

Descriptive trend reported in the Segregation Today section and plotted in Figure 1 (Percentage of Intensely Segregated Schools, 1988–2013), sourced to NCES Common Core of Data and Orfield and colleagues. The report also prints that 74% of Black students attend majority non-white schools.

"Since 1988, the high point of integration, the share of intensely segregated non-white schools (defined as those schools with only 0–10% white students) more than tripled, increasing from about 6% to 19% of all public schools."

George 2021 (2)¶

George, J., & Darling-Hammond, L. (2021). Advancing integration and equity through magnet schools. Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/magnet-schools

q2 · i? · review · r2

The report states this finding to explain why segregation is most often between school districts, citing contributing factors including district boundary drawing, secessions, annexations, and white flight. No effect sizes are printed.

"Research demonstrates that, as a result, racial composition differences across district boundary lines contribute more to segregation than differences within them."
