# Schematic visual games show larger learning effects than realistic games, which show no significant benefit over nongame conditions

## Subclaims

`q3 i2` Schematic games showed a significant positive effect (g = 0.48) while realistic games showed no significant effect (g = −0.01), consistent with the prediction that increased visual realism would be associated with smaller learning outcomes. [→ Clark 2016](#clark-2016)

`q3 i2` Schematic games averaged g = 0.48 while realistic games averaged g = −0.01, a significant moderator difference. [→ Clark Douglas B. 2016](#clark-2016)

## Evidence

Clark, D. B., Tanner-Smith, E. E., & Killingsworth, S. S. (2016). Digital Games, Design, and Learning: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Review of Educational Research, 86(1), 79–122. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315582065

The review attributes this finding to Wouters et al. (2013), one of the prior meta-analyses it synthesizes as background; the present study's Table 3 shows schematic g = 0.48, a medium effect, versus realistic g = −0.01 in media comparisons. The review reports the Wouters finding second-hand, so quality is capped at 2.

> "In terms of visual realism, schematic serious games were significantly more effective than cartoon-like or realistic games."
