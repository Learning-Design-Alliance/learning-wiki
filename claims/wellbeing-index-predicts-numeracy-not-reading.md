## Evidence

### Cárdenas 2022

Cárdenas, D., Lattimore, F., Steinberg, D., & Reynolds, K. J. (2022). Youth well-being predicts later academic success. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-05780-0

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal observational analysis of survey and administrative data from N = 3368–3414 students in 19 high schools, using machine learning causal models with 40 control variables (141 after transformation). Table 1 reports two-stage ridge β* = 0.0332 (0.0052, 0.0612), p = .0202 — a small standardised effect; a one-SD well-being gain accounts for "approximately 2.2 points", ~5% of the 42.52-point average grade-7-to-9 Numeracy difference.

> "The estimated effect for well-being on Numeracy is statistically significant for all three models (Bayesian ridge, two-stage ridge and DML) at a level-of-significance of α = .05, with greater well-being predicting better Numeracy scores."

### Cárdenas 2022 (2)

Cárdenas, D., Lattimore, F., Steinberg, D., & Reynolds, K. J. (2022). Youth well-being predicts later academic success. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-05780-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same longitudinal ML analysis; Table 1 shows Reading β* = 0.0139–0.0201 with p values .124–.2271 across the three models, none significant. The effect held for Numeracy but not Reading, so evidence is mixed across outcomes.

> "However, the well-being index did not significantly predict Reading scores in any of the models."
