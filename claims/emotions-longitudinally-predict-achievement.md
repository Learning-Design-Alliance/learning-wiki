# Emotions measured early in the semester longitudinally predict cumulative grades and exam scores, with negative deactivating emotions (hopelessness, boredom) showing stronger coefficients than activating negative emotions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` University students' emotions measured early in the semester predicted cumulative grades and final course exam scores; positive emotions predicted high achievement and negative emotions low achievement, except relief. [→ Reinhard Pekrun 2002](#reinhard-pekrun-2002)

## Evidence

### Reinhard Pekrun 2002

Reinhard Pekrun, Thomas Goetz, Wolfram Titz, & Raymond P. Perry. (2002). Academic Emotions in Students' Self-Regulated Learning and Achievement: A Program of Qualitative and Quantitative Research. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep3702_4

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal studies using AEQ trait or state scales with university students, with grades and written exam performance as achievement indicators. Positive emotions such as enjoyment, hope, and pride predicted high achievement.

> "Moving beyond cross-sectional correlations, uni-versity students' emotions measured early in the semester predicted cumulative grades as well as final course exam scores at the end of the semester (Pekrun et al., 2000)."
