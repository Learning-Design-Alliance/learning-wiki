## Evidence

### Lesselroth Blake; Bruthans Jan; Hidki Asmaa; Yoon Soo-Hyuk; Oh Seok Kyeong; Lim Byung Gun; Lee Ho-Jin 2024

Lesselroth Blake; Bruthans Jan; Hidki Asmaa; Yoon Soo-Hyuk; Oh Seok Kyeong; Lim Byung Gun; Lee Ho-Jin. (2024). Performance of ChatGPT in the In-Training Examination for Anesthesiology and Pain Medicine Residents in South Korea: Observational Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/56859

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Chi-square comparison of model performance by question logical level across the 398-question set, classified as first-order or higher-order following a prior neurosurgery study design. "GPT-4 showed a significantly higher performance for higher-order questions"; no effect size printed.

> "in terms of the level of logical thinking, GPT-3.5 and CLOVA X showed no significant difference, whereas GPT-4 showed a significantly higher performance for higher-order questions than for first-order questions (77% vs 64.2%; P=.02)."

### Lesselroth Blake; Bruthans Jan; Hidki Asmaa; Yoon Soo-Hyuk; Oh Seok Kyeong; Lim Byung Gun; Lee Ho-Jin 2024 (2)

Lesselroth Blake; Bruthans Jan; Hidki Asmaa; Yoon Soo-Hyuk; Oh Seok Kyeong; Lim Byung Gun; Lee Ho-Jin. (2024). `q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Chi-square comparison of performance on questions classified as containing versus not containing a clinical case (185 vs 213 questions); "no significant performance differences when clinical cases were included". Equivalence was not tested.

> "The models exhibited no significant performance differences when clinical cases were included."
