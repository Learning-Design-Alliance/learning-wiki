# Stronger teacher-rated school ethos is associated with higher average academic achievement at the school level, beyond student- and school-level sociodemographic controls

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Schools where teachers rate the ethos more strongly have higher average student achievement in core subjects, controlling for student gender, migration background, parental education, family structure and school composition profile. ## Evidence

### Granvik Saminathen Maria; Plenty Stephanie; Modin Bitte 2020

Granvik Saminathen Maria; Plenty Stephanie; Modin Bitte. (2020). The Role of Academic Achievement in the Relationship between School Ethos and Adolescent Distress and Aggression: A Study of Ninth Grade Students in the Segregated School Landscape of Stockholm. Journal of Youth and Adolescence. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8116286/

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Structural ML-SEM analysis of combined teacher and student survey data from 9776 ninth-graders in 150 Stockholm school units. The article reports that "stronger ratings of school ethos were significantly related to higher average academic achievement" after sociodemographic controls; no standardised coefficient is printed for this path.

> "As expected, stronger ratings of school ethos were significantly related to higher average academic achievement among students, beyond the effects of the student- and school-level sociodemographic control variables."
