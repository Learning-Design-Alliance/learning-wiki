# Classroom prior achievement moderates the treatment-implementation relationship: implementation gaps between high- and low-achieving classrooms appear only in the CS condition

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Classroom prior achievement significantly moderated the effect of treatment condition on implementation (β = .47, p = .067). [→ Desimone and Hill 2017](#desimone-and-hill-2017)
`q3 i?` In the CS condition, teachers with high-achieving classes had substantially higher implementation than teachers with low-achieving classes (−0.10 vs. 0.42), while control teachers had roughly equivalent low levels regardless of prior achievement (−0.49 vs. −0.39). [→ Desimone and Hill 2017 (2)](#desimone-and-hill-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Desimone and Hill 2017

Laura M. Desimone, Kirsten Lee Hill. (2017). Inside the Black Box: Examining Mediators and Moderators of a Middle School Science Intervention. Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis. https://doi.org/10.3102/0162373717697842

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

SEM results for Research Question 2: the moderator coefficient β = .47 (p = .067), printed in Table 3 as 0.465*, significant at the .1 level.

> "Classroom prior achievement was not signifi- cantly related to implementation but was a sig- nificant moderator of the relationship between treatment condition and implementation (β = .47, p = .067)"

### Desimone and Hill 2017 (2)

Laura M. Desimone, Kirsten Lee Hill. (2017). Inside the Black Box: Examining Mediators and Moderators of a Middle School Science Intervention. Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis. https://doi.org/10.3102/0162373717697842

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Figure 4 moderation results, with high and low achievement defined as one standard deviation above and below the mean. Control-group teachers showed roughly equivalent low implementation (−0.49 vs. −0.39); only descriptive means are printed, no effect size.

> "However, in the CS condition, teachers with high-achieving classes had substan- tially higher levels of implementation than teach- ers with low-achieving classes (−0.10 vs. 0.42)"
