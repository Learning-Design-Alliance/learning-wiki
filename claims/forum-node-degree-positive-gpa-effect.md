## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Node degree has a significant and positive effect on students' final GPA for first-year students and third-year students enrolled in 2018, beyond and above the number of read and written posts. [→ Cristian Candia 2022](#cristian-candia-2022)

## Evidence

### Cristian Candia 2022

Cristian Candia, Alejandra Maldonado-Trapp, Karla Lobos, Fernando Peña, and Carola Bruna. (2022). Disadvantaged students increase their academic performance through collective intelligence exposure in emergency remote learning due to COVID 19. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2203.05621](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2203.05621)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical regression models on university records and CANVAS LMS logs for 7,528 students in three groups during emergency remote teaching. The article reports the node degree "has a signiﬁcant and positive eﬀect" on final GPA in Groups I and II; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "the node degree, Ki, has a signiﬁcant and positive eﬀect on students’ ﬁnal GPA for ﬁrst-year students and third-year students enrolled in 2018 beyond and above controlling for the number of read and written posts."

### Cristian Candia 2022 (2)

Cristian Candia, Alejandra Maldonado-Trapp, Karla Lobos, Fernando Peña, and Carola Bruna. (2022). Disadvantaged students increase their academic performance through collective intelligence exposure in emergency remote learning due to COVID 19. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2203.05621](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2203.05621)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Robustness checks in the Results section replicate the hierarchical models using PageRank centrality and the national exam (PSU) score as an alternative ability proxy; the article reports "obtaining the same results", with no effect size printed.

> "We replicate our analysis by replacing the node degree with the PageRank centrality (Table 2), obtaining the same results (Fig. S13)."
