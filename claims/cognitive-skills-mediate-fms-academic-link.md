# MASEM suggests cognitive skills partially mediate the link between FMS and academic skills, which drops from r = .334 to .200

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In a two-stage MASEM, the FMS–academic path dropped from r = .334 to .200 after including cognitive skills and executive functions, with all pathways statistically significant (p < .0001). [→ Sebastian P. Suggate 2025](#sebastian-p-suggate-2025)

## Evidence

### Sebastian P. Suggate 2025

Keep the hands in mind: A meta-analysis of correlations between fine motor skills and reading, writing, mathematics, and cognitive development in children and adolescents. Educational Research Review 49 (2025) 100748. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2025.100748

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Two-stage MASEM (18 studies with academic–intelligence correlations, 27 with executive-function–academic correlations; academic skills collapsed into one score). The article reports the FMS–academic path "dropped from r = .334 to .200, gives an indication of the extent of the mediation".

> "Comparing the relationship between FMS and academic skills in the fixed-effects correlations in Table 4 with the MASEM, which dropped from r = .334 to .200, gives an indication of the extent of the mediation."
