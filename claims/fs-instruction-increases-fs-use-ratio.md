# FS treatment group significantly increased its ratio of formulaic syllables to total syllables (d = 1.2); between-group difference not statistical (p = .07, d = 1.0)

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` The treatment group used significantly more formulaic sequences posttest (within-group d = 1.2) while the control group did not change; the between-group gain comparison was not statistical (p = .07) but showed a medium-size effect (d = 1.0). [→ Michael McGuire & Jenifer Larson-Hall 2017](#michael-mcguire-jenifer-larson-hall-2017)

## Evidence

### Michael McGuire & Jenifer Larson-Hall 2017

Michael McGuire & Jenifer Larson-Hall. (2017). Teaching Formulaic Sequences in the Classroom: Effects on Spoken Fluency. TESL Canada Journal/Revue TESL du Canada, 34(3), 1–25. http://dx.doi.org/10.18806/tesl.v34i3.1271

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

FS/S ratio (formulaic syllables over total syllables) was coded by the two authors with 95% agreement using five criteria weighted toward phonological coherence. The treatment group's increase was statistical with "d = 1.2"; the between-group gain test was not statistical (p = .07, d = 1.0).

> "A paired-samples t-test for the treatment group found a statistical difference between performance on the pretest and posttest (t10 = -4.6, p = .0009, 95% CI: -0.15, -0.05, d = 1.2)."
