# Standardised and non-standardised VSWM measures perform equally in relating VSWM to mathematics performance

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Mean effect sizes did not differ significantly between studies using standardised (mean = 0.40) versus non-standardised (mean = 0.38) VSWM measures. [→ Katie Allen & Steve Higgins & John Adams 2019](#katie-allen-steve-higgins-john-adams-2019)

## Evidence

### Katie Allen & Steve Higgins & John Adams 2019

Katie Allen & Steve Higgins & John Adams. (2019). The Relationship between Visuospatial Working Memory and Mathematical Performance in School-Aged Children: a Systematic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09470-8

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Subsection analysis comparing standardised versus non-standardised VSWM measures across the 35 studies; the difference was non-significant, and no significant relationship with sample size was found either.

> "The mean effect size for studies using a standardised measure was higher, but not statistically significantly so, than that found for those using non-standardised measures (mean = 0.40 and mean = 0.38, respectively; t(33) = 0.212, p = 0.833)."
