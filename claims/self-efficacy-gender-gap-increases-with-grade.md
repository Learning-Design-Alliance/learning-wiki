## Evidence

### Christopher J. Fischer 2021

Christopher J. Fischer, Jennifer Delgado, Sarah LeGresley. (2021). Interplay between physics self-efficacy, calculus transfer ability, and gender. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2110.07364](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2110.07364)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Self-efficacy scores fractionated by course grade (Table 4 and Figure 2); the article concludes "the gender imbalance in physics self-efficacy increased with improved course grade". Men have higher self-efficacy within each grade cohort.

> "although women consistently exhibited a lower physics self-efficacy than that of men in all grade cohorts (Table 3), the differences in physics self-efficacy between men and women were much smaller in the cohort of students earning a ‘B’ and the cohort earning a ”C, D, or F” than in the cohort of students earning an ‘A ’."
