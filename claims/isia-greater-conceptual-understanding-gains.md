# ISIA students gained significantly more conceptual understanding of forces and motion than conventional-instruction peers

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Grade 10 physics students taught with the ISIA simulation-supported inquiry sequence scored significantly higher on a conceptual understanding post-test than students taught with conventional instruction, with a large effect size. [→ Fan et al. 2018](#fan-et-al-2018)

## Evidence

### Fan et al. 2018

Xinxin Fan, David Geelan and Robyn Gillies. (2018). Evaluating a Novel Instructional Sequence for Conceptual Change in Physics Using Interactive Simulations. Education Sciences. https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci8010029

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental comparison of two pre-existing classes taught with ISIA (n = 55) and two with conventional instruction (n = 62) over eight weeks; one-way ANOVA on post-test conceptual understanding gave "F (1, 115) = 25.11, p = 0.000, η2 = 0.181".

> "There was a statistically signiﬁcant difference at the p < 0.05 level in scores for the two groups: F (1, 115) = 25.11, p = 0.000, η2 = 0.181 which, according to generally accepted criteria [54], is considered a large effect size."
