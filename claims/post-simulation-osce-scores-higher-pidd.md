# Second-year medical students taking a PIDD OSCE question after simulation scored modestly but statistically significantly higher than students taking it before simulation

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who completed the OSCE post-encounter question after the simulation achieved a modest, statistically significant improvement in overall mean question scores compared to those who had not yet participated. [→ Cavuoto Petrizzo Marie 2019](#cavuoto-petrizzo-marie-2019)

## Evidence

### Cavuoto Petrizzo Marie 2019

Cavuoto Petrizzo Marie, Barilla-LaBarca Maria-Louise, Lim Youn Seon, Jongco Artemio M., Cassara Michael, Anglim James, Stern Joel N.H. (2019). Utilization of high-fidelity simulation to address challenges with the basic science immunology education of preclinical medical students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1786-5

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of pre- and post-simulation cohort scores on a summative faculty-created OSCE post-encounter question (Max = 12) within the immunology/rheumatology course; the class (N = 102) was randomly assigned across three OSCE sessions. The t-test showed a "modest increase in total score when tested after the simulation experience". No effect size is printed.

> "The results of a two-tailed independent-sample t-test showed a modest increase in total score when tested after the simulation experience (t (100) = − 2.45, P = 0 .01)"
