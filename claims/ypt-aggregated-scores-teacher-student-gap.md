# Aggregated scores show teachers rate YPT's development of high-level hard and soft skills at about twice the students' rate, and both groups see no significant role for solving close-ended problems

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teachers scored YPT's development of high-level hard skills and soft skills versus regular classes at 1.6 ± 0.1 and 1.4 ± 0.1, while students scored 0.9 ± 0.1 and 0.7 ± 0.1, about half the teachers' score. [→ Sergej Faletič 2025](#sergej-faletic-2025)
`q2 i?` Assessment of YPT's role for mastering the high-school curriculum is generally neutral (-0.1 ± 0.1), and the only skill where neither group sees a significant role of YPT is solving close-ended problems. [→ Sergej Faletič 2025](#sergej-faletic-2025)

Aggregated results summarized in Fig. 3, on a scale where 4 is the maximum difference. The conclusions section also reports the neutral curriculum assessment of -0.1 ± 0.1 and states YPT does not compromise curriculum goals while developing other skills much more than regular classes.

> "the teachers scored this development at 1.6 ± 0.1 and 1.4 ± 0.1, respectively. Students scored this development a little lower at 0.9 ± 0.1 and 0.7 ± 0.1, respectively, so at about half the score of the teachers."
