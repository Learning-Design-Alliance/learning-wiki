## Evidence

### Geneviève Allaire-Duquette 2021

Geneviève Allaire-Duquette, Lorie-Marlène Brault Foisy, Patrice Potvin, Martin Riopel, Marilyne Larose, Steve Masson. (2021). An fMRI study of scientists with a Ph.D. in physics confronted with naive ideas in science. npj Science of Learning 6:11. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00091-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Whole-brain random-effects full-factorial fMRI analysis of 25 Ph.D. physicists; the main effect of CONGRUENCY at pFWE-corrected < 0.05 showed the three frontal clusters, each with "Higher blood oxygen level ‐dependent (BOLD) response ... for incongruent statements".

> "The analysis identi ﬁed a set of areas in the left IFG, bilateral superior frontal gyrus, and bilateral ACC. Higher blood oxygen level ‐dependent (BOLD) response in all three clusters was found for incongruent statements compared to congruent statements."

### Geneviève Allaire-Duquette 2021 (2)

Geneviève Allaire-Duquette, Lorie-Marlène Brault Foisy, Patrice Potvin, Martin Riopel, Marilyne Larose, Steve Masson. (2021). An fMRI study of scientists with a Ph.D. in physics confronted with naive ideas in science. npj Science of Learning 6:11. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00091-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc t-contrasts (p uncorrected < 0.005) in the same 25-participant whole-brain analysis, reported in the congruency-dependent BOLD modulation section.

> "incongruent statements, more than congruent ones, activated also the bilateral cerebellum and the MFG, while conversely the congruent statements activated the bilateral middle occipital gyrus more than incongruent ones."
