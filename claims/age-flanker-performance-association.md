## Evidence

### Beckmann 2021

Beckmann, J.; Lang, C.; du Randt, R.; Gresse, A.; Long, K.Z.; Ludyga, S.; Müller, I.; Nqweniso, S.; Pühse, U.; Utzinger, J.; Walter, C.; Gerber, M. (2021). Prevalence of Stunting and Relationship between Stunting and Associated Risk Factors with Academic Achievement and Cognitive Function: A Cross-Sectional Study with South African Primary School Children. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 18(8), 4218. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18084218

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Mixed regression models for boys (Table 4); age β = 0.31 (congruent accuracy) and −0.51 (congruent reaction time), both p = 0.000. The quoted sentence states the direction of the age association.

> "Among boys, age was associated with better congruent and incongruent stimuli (p < 0.001). Thus, older children achieved higher accuracy scores and faster reaction times in the Flanker task."

### Beckmann 2021 (2)

Beckmann, J.; Lang, C.; du Randt, R.; Gresse, A.; Long, K.Z.; Ludyga, S.; Müller, I.; Nqweniso, S.; Pühse, U.; Utzinger, J.; Walter, C.; Gerber, M. (2021). Prevalence of Stunting and Relationship between Stunting and Associated Risk Factors with Academic Achievement and Cognitive Function: A Cross-Sectional Study with South African Primary School Children. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 18(8), 4218. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18084218

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Mixed regression models for girls (Table 5); age β = 0.22 (congruent accuracy) and −0.44 (congruent reaction time), both p = 0.000. The quoted sentence states the direction of the age association.

> "Among girls, age was also associated with better congruent and incongruent stimuli (p < 0.001) (see Table 5). Again, older girls provided more accurate and faster responses to congruent and incongruent stimuli."

### Gerber Markus 2021

Gerber Markus, Lang Christin, Beckmann Johanna, du Randt Rosa, Gall Stefanie, Seelig Harald, Long Kurt Z., Ludyga Sebastian, Müller Ivan, Nienaber Madeleine, Nqweniso Siphesihle, Pühse Uwe, Steinmann Peter, Utzinger Jürg, Walter Cheryl. (2021). How are academic achievement and inhibitory control associated with physical fitness, soil-transmitted helminth infections, food insecurity and stunting among South African primary schoolchildren? BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-021-10779-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional mixed linear regression analyses of Flanker task results in boys (n = 664) and girls (n = 613); age estimates were the largest across all four outcome indices (e.g., 0.28 for congruent accuracy in boys). The article reports "age was the most important explanatory factor."

> "Across all Flanker task performance indicators, age was the most important explanatory factor. Thus, higher age was associated with better information processing (higher accuracy and lower reaction time on congruent trials) and inhibitory control of attention (higher accuracy and lower reaction time on incongruent trials)."
