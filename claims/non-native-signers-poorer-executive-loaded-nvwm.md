# Deaf non-native signers scored lower than hearing children on executive-loaded non-verbal working memory tasks, while deaf native signers did not differ from hearing children

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` On the two executive-loaded NVWM tasks (Spatial Span Backward and Odd One Out), non-native signers scored significantly more poorly than hearing children, with native signers not differing from hearing children. [→ Marshall C 2015](#marshall-c-2015)

## Evidence

### Marshall C 2015

Marshall C, Jones A, Denmark T, Mason K, Atkinson J, Botting N and Morgan G (2015) Deaf children's non-verbal working memory is impacted by their language experience. Front. Psychol. 6:527. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00527

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

ANCOVAs controlling for age and WASI matrix reasoning compared deaf native signers (N = 8), deaf non-native signers (N = 19), and hearing children (N = 28) on NVWM tasks. For the Odd One Out task, "the only group diﬀerence being between the non-native signers and the hearing group, p = 0.020."

> "Likewise, for the Odd One Out task, there was a signiﬁcant eﬀect of group, F(2, 54) = 4.187, p = 0.021, with the only group diﬀerence being between the non-native signers and the hearing group, p = 0.020."
