## Evidence

### Tolonen M 2024

Tolonen M, Arvonen M, Renko M, Paakkonen H, Piippo-Savolainen E. (2024). The effect of promoting factors on learning by four different delivery modes. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05864-7

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Multivariate linear regression in the total randomized cohort of 325 medical and nursing students, controlling for gender, field of study and prior education. The article reports the high-concentration group’s mean scores were "1.928 points higher (p = 0.010)" short-term and "1.649 (p = 0.106)" long-term; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "In the total cohort, the high-concentration group’s mean scores were 1.928 points higher (p = 0.010) in the short-term test and 1.649 (p = 0.106) points higher in the long-term test compared to the low-concentration group, regardless of the teaching method."

### Tolonen M 2024 (2)

Tolonen M, Arvonen M, Renko M, Paakkonen H, Piippo-Savolainen E. (2024). The effect of promoting factors on learning by four different delivery modes. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05864-7

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive comparison of test-score ranges across the four randomized teaching groups in the same cohort. The article reports that "the average test scores ranged from 21.9 to 23.4" for high concentration versus "18.3–20.0" for low concentration; these are descriptive means with no printed effect size.

> "Among those with high concentration, the average test scores ranged from 21.9 to 23.4, while the range for low concentration was 18.3–20.0."
