## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Subject was a significant moderator (Qb(2) = 16,298, p < .05): the effect size was 0,897 for science and technology and 1,095 for other subjects, both significant, while mathematics showed a non-significant effect size of -0,285. [→ Toraman 2016](#toraman-2016)

## Evidence

### Toraman 2016

Toraman, C., & Demir, E. (2016). The effect of constructivism on attitudes towards lessons: A meta–analysis study. Eurasian Journal of Educational Research, 62, 115-142. http://dx.doi.org/10.14689/ejer.2016.62.8

`q3 · i3`

Moderator analysis by subject across the 43 studies (Table 4): science and technology (26 studies, ES = 0,897), mathematics (8 studies, ES = -0,285, p = 0,290), other subjects (9 studies, ES = 1,095). The article reports "the effect size is not significant" for mathematics.

> "On examining Table 4, we find that attitudinal studies demonstrate heterogenity in accordance with  the subject moderating factor (Q (b) (2)  = 16,298,  p<.05).  In light of the meta– analysis conducted in connection with the effect of the constructivist approach on mathematics lessons, we can conclude that the effect size is not significant."
