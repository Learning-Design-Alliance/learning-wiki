# Eleven-month-old infants who see a core knowledge violation preferentially explore the violating object over a novel distractor

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After seeing either a solidity or support violation, infants reversed the standard novelty preference and spent most exploration time on the object that had violated expectations. [→ Stahl Aimee E. 2019](#stahl-aimee-e-2019)

## Evidence

### Stahl Aimee E. 2019

Stahl Aimee E., Feigenson Lisa. (2019). Violations of Core Knowledge Shape Early Learning. Topics in Cognitive Science. https://doi.org/10.1111/tops.12389

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Controlled lab experiment with 11-month-old infants who saw a single expected or surprising solidity or support event, then chose between the event object and a distractor. Infants seeing surprising outcomes "spending most of their time exploring the toy that had just violated their expectations" (Stahl & Feigenson, 2015).

> "infants who had just seen the surprising outcome of either event (ball passed through wall, or car hovered in mid-air) reversed this preference, spending most of their time exploring the toy that had just violated their expectations"
