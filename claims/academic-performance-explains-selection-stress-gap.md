## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Broks VMA 2023

Broks VMA, Stegers-Jager KM, Fikrat-Wevers S, Van den Broek WW, Woltman AM. (2023). The association between how medical students were selected and their perceived stress levels in Year-1 of medical school. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04411-0

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Post-hoc multilevel Model 3 added a binary academic-performance variable (passing all courses with exams up to the PSS-14 administration) for the 650-student sample; optimal performers showed lower stress with a medium effect size of 0.5.

> "Students with optimal academic performance had statistically significantly lower stress perception levels than students with non-optimal academic performance (B=-4.38 [-5.64 – -3.12, ES = 0.5 (medium), p < .001,)."

### Broks VMA 2023 (2)

Broks VMA, Stegers-Jager KM, Fikrat-Wevers S, Van den Broek WW, Woltman AM. (2023). The association between how medical students were selected and their perceived stress levels in Year-1 of medical school. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04411-0

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

In Model 3, adding academic performance closed the assessment-versus-high-grades gap and reduced the lottery-versus-high-grades coefficient from 3.95 to 2.45, a small effect size of 0.3 that remained statistically significant at p < .05.

> "the difference between students selected by assessment and high grades was no longer statistically significant, and the difference between weighted lottery and grades decreased from 3.95 (ES = medium) to 2.45 (B = 2.45 [0.03–4.88], ES = 0.3 (small), p < .05, Table 2 – Model 3)"
