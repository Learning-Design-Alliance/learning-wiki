# Attending a game session rather than a lecture increased all epistemic emotions except boredom among university students in a Diplomacy-based international relations course.

## Evidence

### Ketonen 2026

Ketonen, E. E., Kruskopf, M., Lonka, K., & Mattlin, M. (2026). Dynamics of university students' epistemic emotions during game-based learning in an international relations course. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09771-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Within-person DSEM analysis of 212 session-level surveys nested in 22 students, controlling lagged effects. Standardised session-type coefficients ranged from 0.18 (curiosity) to 0.44 (surprise); boredom was −0.28. The article reports " increased all other epistemic emotions except boredom ".

> "Attending a game session (rather than a lecture) increased all other epistemic emotions except boredom (see Table 2), which again was less often reported in game sessions compared to lectures"
