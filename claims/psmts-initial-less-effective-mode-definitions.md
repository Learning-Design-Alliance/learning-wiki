# Preservice mathematics teachers may initially operate with less-effective definitions of mode, such as mode as 'the most' frequent value

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` A preservice mathematics teacher initially defined mode as 'the most' and applied an algorithmic sorting scheme, which misidentified the mode of a dataset with no repeated values. [→ Md Amiruzzaman 2020](#md-amiruzzaman-2020)

## Evidence

### Md Amiruzzaman 2020

Md Amiruzzaman, Karl W. Kosko, Stefanie R. Amiruzzaman. (2020). Preservice Mathematics Teachers Understanding of Mode. Proceedings of the 39th annual meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2004.10304](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2004.10304)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Individual teaching experiment, episode 4 with participant Alex. Given the dataset 1, 3, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, Alex sorted the data and identified 3 as mode, saying "[after sorting] …now I see two 3s…so 3 is mode." Asked for 1, 2, 3, 4, he said all values are mode as all occurred most, consistent with a definition of mode as "the most."

> "Alex knew that the mode is a repeated number and his algorithmic scheme helped him to find the mode"
