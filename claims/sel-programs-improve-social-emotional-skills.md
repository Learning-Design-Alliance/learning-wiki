# SEL programs improve social emotional skills

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

Structured social-emotional learning (SEL) programs — sequenced curricula that explicitly teach self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, and responsible decision-making — improve students' social-emotional competencies.

## Discussion

**Scope of the claim.** This claim concerns gains in social-emotional *skills* specifically, not downstream academic or behavioral outcomes, which are treated separately in the literature. Skill gains are the most consistently reported proximal outcome of SEL programs [+S]; effects on achievement and conduct are typically smaller and more variable [~M], plausibly because they are mediated by the skill gains claimed here.

**Program quality as a moderator.** The literature consistently attributes larger effects to programs that are sequenced, active (role-play, rehearsal, feedback rather than lecture), focused, and explicit — the SAFE features [+M] — and to programs implemented with fidelity over multiple years [+M]. One-off assemblies, poster campaigns, or loosely structured "awareness" activities should not be expected to produce the skill gains that structured curricula do [-M]. The "active" feature aligns with broader findings that [active learning](../principles/active-learning.md) outperforms passive reception, and the "explicit, sequenced" feature parallels the value of [clear structure](../principles/clear-structure.md) in other domains.

**Implementation and culture.** Skill gains depend on opportunities to practice skills in authentic social contexts, not just learn about them. Programs embedded in a supportive classroom climate and reinforced through everyday routines (see [Check-ins](../elements/check-in.md) and [Building empathy](../principles/building-empathy.md)) are better positioned to produce durable gains than pull-out lessons alone [~M]. Role-play and rehearsal formats (see [Acting and role-play](../strategies/acting-role-play.md)) give learners the guided practice that skill acquisition requires.

**Implementation as a constraint.** Effects shrink or vanish when programs are delivered without training or buy-in from teachers [-M], when lesson time is crowded out by competing demands [-M], or when the program conflicts with the school's existing disciplinary culture [-M]. Because SEL skills are practiced in live social interaction, a classroom climate that contradicts the taught skills (e.g., public shaming alongside empathy lessons) can undermine transfer entirely [-M].

**Open questions.** Effects for adolescents appear weaker than for elementary students in much of the literature [~M], and long-term maintenance of skill gains after program end is under-studied. Cultural fit and linguistic accessibility of curricula are also open design questions; see [Accessible vocabulary and syntax](../principles/accessible-vocabulary-syntax.md) for the broader accessibility concern.
