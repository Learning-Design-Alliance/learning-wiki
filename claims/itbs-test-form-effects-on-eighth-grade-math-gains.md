# ITBS test form progression systematically influences eighth-grade math gain scores, with Form L-to-M years showing the largest gains and Form K-to-L years the smallest

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The two largest eighth-grade math gains (1997 and 1999) share the Form L to Form M progression and the three lowest gains (1994, 1996, and 2001) share the Form K to Form L progression, a pattern the authors say is consistent with test form effects. [→ Todd Rosenkranz 2002](#todd-rosenkranz-2002)

## Evidence

### Todd Rosenkranz 2002

Todd Rosenkranz. (2002). 2001 CPS Test Trend Review: Iowa Tests of Basic Skills. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/2001-cps-trend-review-iowa-tests-basic-skills

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of eighth-grade math gain scores by test-form transition, 1994-2001, reported in the gain-trends discussion and Figure 4. Comparing only same form-to-form transitions is offered as a control for form difficulty differences.

> "the two largest gains (1997 and 1999) both happen to have the same form progression (Form L to Form M); and the three lowest gains (1994, 1996, and 2001) all share the same form progression as well (Form K to Form L). This pattern is consistent with test form effects."
