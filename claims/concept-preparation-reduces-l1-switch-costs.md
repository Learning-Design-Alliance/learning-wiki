# Concept preparation reduces L1 switch costs, indicating language control can initiate at the concept level

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When concepts could be prepared, smaller L1 switch costs were observed than when concepts could not be prepared, with a much smaller effect on L2 switch costs. [→ Mathieu Declerck & Andrea M. Philipp 2015](#mathieu-declerck-andrea-m-philipp-2015)

## Evidence

### Mathieu Declerck & Andrea M. Philipp 2015

Mathieu Declerck & Andrea M. Philipp. (2015). A review of control processes and their locus in language switching. Psychon Bull Rev. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-015-0836-1

`q2 · i?`

The review reports, citing Declerck et al. (2015a) with German-English bilinguals who could prepare language, concept, or both, that "smaller L1 switch costs were ob- served than when concepts could not be prepared." The asymmetry was explained by stronger concept-to-L1-lemma connections. No effect sizes are printed.

> "When con- cepts could be prepared, smaller L1 switch costs were ob- served than when concepts could not be prepared, whereas the effect of concept preparation on L2 switch costs was much smaller."
