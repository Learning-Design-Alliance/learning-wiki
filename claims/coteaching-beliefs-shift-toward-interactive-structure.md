# Co-teaching shifted a new instructor's beliefs about teaching from skepticism toward appreciation of the interactive in-class problem structure

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The new instructor moved from initial skepticism about students doing problems throughout class to appreciating in-class problems as a good method. [→ Charles Henderson 2006](#charles-henderson-2006)
`q2 i?` The belief shift was driven by observed student engagement and an end-of-semester survey in which students most liked the in-class work. [→ Charles Henderson 2006 (2)](#charles-henderson-2006-2)

## Evidence

### Charles Henderson 2006

Charles Henderson, Andrea Beach, and Michael Famiano. (2006). Promoting Instructional Change via Co-Teaching. Manuscript submitted to American Journal of Physics (Physics Education Research Section). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0608294](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0608294)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Mid-semester interview (MF2) in the instrumental case study: MF reported coming to appreciate in-class problems after initial skepticism 'about having students do nothing but problems in class'.

> "I have really come to appreciate the use of in-class problems. It’s surprising to know, because when I first came I was skeptical about having students do nothing but problems in class"

### Charles Henderson 2006 (2)

Charles Henderson, Andrea Beach, and Michael Famiano. (2006). Promoting Instructional Change via Co-Teaching. Manuscript submitted to American Journal of Physics (Physics Education Research Section). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0608294](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0608294)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Mid-semester interview data: MF described being convinced by student engagement and by an end-of-semester survey in which 'the thing they liked the most was the in-class work.'

> "at the end of the semester when we gave them the survey, the thing they liked the most was the in-class work. Very strangely surprisingly to me was that they liked doing this"
