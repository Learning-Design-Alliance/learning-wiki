# Colonization-introduced formal education creates greater discontinuities with informal education than evolved systems

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In countries where the formal education system and a new written language were introduced through colonization, the discontinuities between formal and informal education are far greater and more complex than in systems that evolved directly from informal education, and this is the root cause of the formal system's irrelevance to people's lives. [→ Doronila 1997](#doronila-1997)

## Evidence

### Doronila 1997

Doronila, Maria Luisa C. (1997). A Research and Development Approach to the Delivery of Comprehensive Functional Education and Literacy in the Philippines. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED418217

`q2 · i?`

Theoretical-analytical argument in Part I of the conference paper, contrasting countries whose formal education evolved directly from informal education with countries like the Philippines where formal education arrived through colonization. The author states this is "the root cause of the problem of irrelevance of our formal education system to the lives and aspirations of our people." No empirical test is reported; this is the author's analytical framing.

> "In countries like the Philippines where the introduction of the formal education system as well as of a new written language(s) and the knowledge encoded in it were part of the process of colonization, the discontinuities are far greater and more complex"
