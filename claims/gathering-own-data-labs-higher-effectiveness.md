## Evidence

### P. Klein 2021

P. Klein, L. Ivanjek, M.N. Dahlkemper, K. Jeličić, M.-A. Geyer, S. Küchemann, and A. Susac. (2021). Studying physics during the COVID-19 pandemic: Student assessments of learning achievement, perceived effectiveness of online recitations, and online laboratories. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010117](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010117)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

ANOVA among the 220 students enrolled in physics laboratories, grouped by data source (simulated data, real data gathered by students themselves, real data gathered by someone else). The article reports the data type "had a signiﬁcant impact on both scales"; no effect size is printed.

> "Second, the type of data that was analyzed by the students had a signiﬁcant impact on both scales, the acquisition of experimental skills [F (2, 198) = 14 .2,p < 0.001)] and the reinforcement of content [ F (2, 216) = 3 .50,p = 0 .03]."

### P. Klein 2021 (2)

P. Klein, L. Ivanjek, M.N. Dahlkemper, K. Jeličić, M.-A. Geyer, S. Küchemann, and A. Susac. (2021). Studying physics during the COVID-19 pandemic: Student assessments of learning achievement, perceived effectiveness of online recitations, and online laboratories. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010117](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010117)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bonferroni-corrected post hoc comparisons on the same lab-course ANOVAs, shown in Fig. 4. The article reports students "assessed signiﬁcant higher scores when they gathered the data by themselves" than with simulated or handed-out data; no effect size is printed.

> "Bonferroni-corrected post-hoc comparisons revealed that students assessed signiﬁcant higher scores when they gathered the data by themselves (e.g. from a video) as compared to simulated data or data that was handed out, see Fig. 4."
