# All chronotypes exhibit significant academic improvement in later classes, with evening class grades at least 0.27 points higher than morning class grades

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` All chronotype categories show significant academic improvement across the day, with evening class grade points at least 0.27 points higher than morning class grade points. [→ Smarr 2018](#smarr-2018)

## Evidence

### Smarr 2018

Smarr, B. L., & Schirmer, A. E. (2018). 3.4 million real-world learning management system logins reveal the majority of students experience social jet lag correlated with decreased performance. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-23044-8

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

N-way ANOVA of class grades by chronotype and class start time on raw (z-scored) grades. "All chronotypes exhibited significant academic improvement across the day", with the effect of time F = 273, p = 1 × 10−118, and evening grades at least 0.27 points higher than morning grades.

> "All chronotypes exhibited significant academic improvement across the day (Fig. 4D; effect of time: F = 273, p = 1 × 10−118; larks: χ2 = 153, p = 7 × 10−34; finches: χ2 = 483, p = 1 × 10−105; owls: χ2 = 150, p = 2 × 10−33), with the average evening class grade points being at least 0.27 points higher than the average morning class grade points."
