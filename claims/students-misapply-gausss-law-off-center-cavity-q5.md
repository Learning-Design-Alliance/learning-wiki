## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2015

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2015). Validation and analysis of the coupled multiple response Colorado upper-division electrostatics (CUE) diagnostic. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020130](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020130)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Analysis of method selections on Q5 (a charged sphere with an off-center cavity), N=421 (Fig. 5a). The remaining overwhelming majority (42%, N=176 of 421) selected Gauss's Law.

> "Almost half of the students (45%, N=191 of 421) correctly selected Superposition as the easiest method, and only a small number (6%, N=24 of 421) selected the more diﬃcult method, Integration."

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2015 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2015). Validation and analysis of the coupled multiple response Colorado upper-division electrostatics (CUE) diagnostic. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020130](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020130)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Analysis of reasoning selections among the 176 students who chose Gauss's Law (Fig. 5b). The most common reasoning elements 'd' and 'f' are consistent with the incorrect reasoning that the cavity's location does not matter; the authors state this is consistent with previous research that students often misapply Gauss's law.

> "of the students who selected Gauss's law, only a tenth (11%, N=20 of 176) did not select one or both of reasoning elements 'd' or 'f'."
