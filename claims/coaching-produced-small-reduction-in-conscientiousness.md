# One in-person coaching intervention produced a small significant reduction in conscientiousness while the control group showed no change

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a quasi-experiment, coaching produced significant reductions in conscientiousness between pre- and post-intervention (0.06 points on a 1–5 scale), with no significant change in the control group. [→ Melanie Turner 2025](#melanie-turner-2025)

## Evidence

### Melanie Turner 2025

Melanie Turner, Flaviu A. Hodis. (2025). Strengthening Conscientiousness by Means of Interventions: A Systematic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10016-4

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Quasi-experiment with business-as-usual control of 321 UK undergraduates receiving four months of in-person coaching informed by the GROW model and the Demands Affordances and Transactional model. The review reports the small decrease and the null control-group change.

> "Coaching produced significant reductions in conscientiousness between pre-intervention and post-intervention. This decrease was small (i.e., 0.06 points on a 1–5 scale). For the control group, there was no significant change in trait conscientiousness between the same two time points"
