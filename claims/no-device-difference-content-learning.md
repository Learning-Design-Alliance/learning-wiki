## Evidence

### Karen B. Schloss 2021

Karen B. Schloss, Melissa A. Schoenlein, Ross Tredinnick, Simon Smith, Nathaniel Miller, Chris Racey, Christian Castro, and Bas Rokers. (2021). The UW Virtual Brain Project: An immersive approach to teaching functional neuroanatomy. Preprint. [doi:10.1037/tps0000281](https://doi.org/10.1037/tps0000281)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the powered Experiment 1B (120 undergraduates, within-subjects device comparison), "no significant difference between devices" was found (d = .15); the full ANOVA also showed no significant device effect (F(1, 116) = 2.95, p = .089).

> "The t-test comparing devices averaged over all other factors again showed no significant difference between devices (t(119) = 1.62, p = .109, d = .15)."

### Karen B. Schloss 2021 (2)

Karen B. Schloss, Melissa A. Schoenlein, Ross Tredinnick, Simon Smith, Nathaniel Miller, Chris Racey, Christian Castro, and Bas Rokers. (2021). The UW Virtual Brain Project: An immersive approach to teaching functional neuroanatomy. Preprint. [doi:10.1037/tps0000281](https://doi.org/10.1037/tps0000281)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A mixed-design ANOVA over Experiments 1 and 2 (lesson device within-subjects, testing device between-subjects) found no effect of lesson device, testing device, or their interaction, so the null device result was not due to 2D/3D testing misalignment.

> "There were no significant effects of lesson device ( F < 1) or testing device (F(1, 226) = 1.30, p = .254, η2p = .006), and no interaction (F(1, 226) = 2.86, p = .092, η2p = .013)."
