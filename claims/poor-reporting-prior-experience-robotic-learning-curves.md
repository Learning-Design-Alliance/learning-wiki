# Reporting standards in robot-assisted learning curve studies are poor, with only 35 per cent quantifying surgeons' previous experience

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Only 17 of 49 studies (35 per cent) quantified previous experience of robot-assisted, laparoscopic or open operations, and only 4 of 49 (8 per cent) indicated whether simulation, dry lab or cadaver training had been completed before patient enrolment. [→ Soomro N. A. 2020](#soomro-n-a-2020)

## Evidence

### Soomro N. A. 2020

Soomro N. A., Hashimoto D. A., Porteous A. J., Ridley C. J. A., Marsh W. J., Ditto R. and Roy S. (2020). Systematic review of learning curves in robot‐assisted surgery. BJS Open. https://doi.org/10.1002/bjs5.50235

`q2 · i?`

Review assessment of reporting standards across the 49 included studies. Although 34 of 49 (69 per cent) acknowledged previous surgeon experience in some form, only "17 of 49 studies (35 per cent)" quantified it, and pre-study simulation training was indicated in only four studies.

> "only 17 of 49 studies (35 per cent) quantifying previous experience of robot‐assisted (8 of 49, 16 per cent), laparoscopic (13 of 49, 27 per cent) or open (6 of 49, 12 per cent) operations completed. Only four of 49 studies (8 per cent) indicated whether simulation, dry lab or cadaver training had been completed before patient enrolment."
