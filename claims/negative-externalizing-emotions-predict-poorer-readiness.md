# Children showing high levels of negative externalizing emotions during the challenge task are rated as less socially competent, more hyperactive, and with poorer interpersonal and self-regulatory skills

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Children high in anger, frustration, or showing hostility were rated less socially competent, more hyperactive, and with poorer interpersonal and self-regulatory skills than peers. [→ Berhenke 2011](#berhenke-2011)

## Evidence

### Berhenke 2011

Berhenke, Amanda; Miller, Alison L.; Brown, Eleanor; Seifer, Ronald; Dickstein, Susan. (2011). Observed Emotional and Behavioral Indicators of Motivation Predict School Readiness in Head Start Graduates. Early childhood research quarterly. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecresq.2011.04.001

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Follow-up t-test comparison of N = 43 children in the top quartile for anger or frustration or showing hostility versus N = 74 peers; "Children who showed high levels of negative externalizing emotion expressions were rated by their teachers as less socially competent" and more hyperactive.

> "Children who showed high levels of negative externalizing emotion expressions were rated by their teachers as less socially competent (t (114) = −2.08, p < 0.05), more hyperactive (t (115) = 2.40, p < 0.05)"
