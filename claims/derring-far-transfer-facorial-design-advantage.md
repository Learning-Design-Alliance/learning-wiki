# Deliberately designing and correcting one's own invalid experiments improves far transfer to a structurally more complex factorial design, whereas spotting and correcting others' errors does not outperform errorless practice on far transfer

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Sarah Shi Hui Wong 2026

Sarah Shi Hui Wong. (2026). Deliberate erring improves scientific reasoning: Learning experimentation skills by intentionally designing invalid experiments. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09819-x

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r3`

In the same randomized experiment (N = 276), the far transfer test required designing a 2 × 2 factorial study inferred from a teacher-dialogue scenario, more complex than practiced. The omnibus test was significant, F(2, 273) = 7.58, p = .001, ŋ² = .05; the pairwise results above show the deliberate-erring advantage persisted while the advantage of "observing and correcting others' invalid designs" did not.

> "The derr-explain-fix group (M = 7.27, SD = 2.56) outperformed both the errorless practice ( M = 5.73, SD = 2.93) and spot-explain-fix (M = 6.01, SD = 3.07) groups, p <.001 and p =.003, d = 0.56 and 0.45, respectively, whereas the latter two groups did not differ, p =.50, d = 0.09."
