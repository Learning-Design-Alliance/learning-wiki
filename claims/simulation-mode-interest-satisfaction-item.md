## Evidence

### Yu Jiafeng 2026

Yu Jiafeng, Wang Qian, Yin Ling, Yuan Fang, Kan Yufei, Wang Zengkun, Yu Yonghao, Li Qing, Yu Yang. (2026). Prospective randomized controlled trial of computer simulation versus high-fidelity mannequin simulation in advanced cardiovascular life support (ACLS) training for anesthesiology novice clinicians. BMC Anesthesiology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12871-026-04080-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Satisfaction questionnaire administered at T1 with a 100% response rate: the interest item scored 4.56 ± 0.53 (Group M) and 4.53 ± 0.56 (Group SM) versus 4.02 ± 0.48 (Group C), P < 0.05.

> "For the item “This mode stimulates my interest,” the scores in Group M (4.56 ± 0.53) and Group SM (4.53 ± 0.56) were significantly higher than those in Group C (4.02 ± 0.48) (P < 0.05)"

### Yu Jiafeng 2026 (2)

Yu Jiafeng, Wang Qian, Yin Ling, Yuan Fang, Kan Yufei, Wang Zengkun, Yu Yonghao, Li Qing, Yu Yang. (2026). Prospective randomized controlled trial of computer simulation versus high-fidelity mannequin simulation in advanced cardiovascular life support (ACLS) training for anesthesiology novice clinicians. BMC Anesthesiology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12871-026-04080-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

On the remaining four satisfaction and self-confidence items, the trial found no significant differences among the three groups (all P > 0.05); the article notes limited clinical significance given 58% power for this outcome.

> "Across the four items—“This mode is suitable for ACLS training courses,” “This mode enables rapid mastery of CPR-related knowledge,” “This mode improves analytical and problem-solving abilities,” and “This mode increased my confidence”—no significant differences were found among the three groups (all P > 0.05)."
