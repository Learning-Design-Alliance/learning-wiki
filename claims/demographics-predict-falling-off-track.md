# Among students initially on-track, being male, Black, or Hispanic and attending higher-FRPL schools predict higher odds of falling off track in math

## Evidence

### Angela Johnson 2020

Angela Johnson, Megan Kuhfeld, and Greg King. (2020). Catching up or Falling Behind: Measuring Middle School Achievement Trajectories for College Readiness. EdWorkingPaper No. 20-233, Annenberg Institute at Brown University. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/catching-up-or-falling-behind-measuring-middle-school-achievement-trajectories-for-college-readiness/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical generalized linear models predicting status change among students on-track in fall of 6th grade, with students nested within schools (Table 2, Panel A). The quote prints odds ratios of 1.381, 1.398, 1.171, and 1.139 for the demographic predictors.

> "Being male, Black, Hispanic, or a member of another race/ethnicity group is associated with significantly higher odds of falling off track in math (odds ratios of 1.381, 1.398, 1.171, and 1.139, respectively). Changing schools between grades is also associated with higher odds of falling off track (odds ratios of 1.352 and 1.665)."
