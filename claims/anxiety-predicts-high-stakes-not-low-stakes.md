## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Alysa Malespina 2024

Alysa Malespina, Fargol Seifollahi, Chandralekha Singh. (2024). Bioscience students in physics courses with higher test anxiety have lower grades on high-stakes assessments and women report more test anxiety than men. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.3390/educsci14101092](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci14101092)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple regression models predicting average homework grades in both courses; the only significant predictor was high school GPA and gender was not significant, confirming the hypothesis that anxiety would not predict low-stakes scores.

> "For both Physics 1 and Physics 2, low-stakes assessment scores are not predicted by pre-self-efficacy, average self-efficacy, pre-test anxiety, or average test anxiety. Also, the models that include self-efficacy and test anxiety do not explain any more variance than the models that exclude them."

### Alysa Malespina 2024 (2)

Alysa Malespina, Fargol Seifollahi, Chandralekha Singh. (2024). Bioscience students in physics courses with higher test anxiety have lower grades on high-stakes assessments and women report more test anxiety than men. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.3390/educsci14101092](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci14101092)

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Regression models predicting weighted Physics 1 high-stakes scores (75% midterm, 25% final); "high-stakes assessment scores were predicted by average test anxiety but not self-efficacy", with Average Model 1 adjusted R² = 0.45 and β = -0.26*** for test anxiety.

> "in Average Model 1, a one-standard-deviation increase in test anxiety decreases the Physics 1 high-stakes assessment score by 0.26 standard deviations, when keeping all other variables constant."

### Alysa Malespina 2024 (3)

Alysa Malespina, Fargol Seifollahi, Chandralekha Singh. (2024). Bioscience students in physics courses with higher test anxiety have lower grades on high-stakes assessments and women report more test anxiety than men. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.3390/educsci14101092](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci14101092)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Physics 2 regressions (N = 78 in average models): self-efficacy β = 0.24* in Model 3 and test anxiety β = -0.28** in Model 4 were significant, but neither was significant when both were included, which the authors attribute to the constructs being inversely correlated.

> "test anxiety and self-efficacy each become statistically significant predictors of the high-stakes assessment score in Model 3 and Model 4."
