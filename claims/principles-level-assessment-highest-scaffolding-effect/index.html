Evidence¶

Belland 2017¶

Belland, B. R., Walker, A. E., Kim, N. J., & Lefler, M. (2017). Synthesizing Results From Empirical Research on Computer-Based Scaffolding in STEM Education: A Meta-Analysis. Review of Educational Research, 87(2), 309-344. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316670999

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Moderator analysis using Sugrue's (1995) assessment framework (concept, principles, application) across the 333-outcome corpus, reported with Figure 6. Discussion reports effect sizes of 0.40, 0.51, and 0.44 for concept, principles, and application levels respectively.

"Hedges' ḡ estimates were significantly greater than zero across all assessment levels; thus, scaffolding positively influences learning for a variety of assessment types (see Figure 6). Scaffolding's influence was greater when measured at the principles level than when measured at the concept level, z = 2.17, p < .05."
