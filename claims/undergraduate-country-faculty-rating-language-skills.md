## Evidence

### Burmeister Jay 2014

Burmeister Jay, McSpadden Erin, Rakowski Joseph, Nalichowski Adrian, Yudelev Mark, Snyder Michael. (2014). Correlation of admissions statistics to graduate student success in medical physics. Journal of Applied Clinical Medical Physics 15(1):375-385. https://doi.org/10.1120/jacmp.v15i1.4451

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Retrospective cohort analysis of 108 students; faculty rating dichotomized at the median. "The only other statistically significant correlation for faculty rating was with undergraduate country (p=0.006), with students from the US and Canada rated higher than those from other countries."

> "The only other statistically significant correlation for faculty rating was with undergraduate country (p=0.006), with students from the US and Canada rated higher than those from other countries."

### Burmeister Jay 2014 (2)

Burmeister Jay, McSpadden Erin, Rakowski Joseph, Nalichowski Adrian, Yudelev Mark, Snyder Michael. (2014). Correlation of admissions statistics to graduate student success in medical physics. Journal of Applied Clinical Medical Physics 15(1):375-385. https://doi.org/10.1120/jacmp.v15i1.4451

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same cohort analysis across the remaining performance metrics. "none of the other three performance metrics were correlated with undergraduate country (p>0.395)" - that is, course average, first-year GGPA, and exit exam showed no country correlation.

> "While faculty rating was strongly correlated with undergraduate country (p=0.006), none of the other three performance metrics were correlated with undergraduate country (p>0.395)."

### Burmeister Jay 2014 (3)

Burmeister Jay, McSpadden Erin, Rakowski Joseph, Nalichowski Adrian, Yudelev Mark, Snyder Michael. (2014). Correlation of admissions statistics to graduate student success in medical physics. Journal of Applied Clinical Medical Physics 15(1):375-385. https://doi.org/10.1120/jacmp.v15i1.4451
