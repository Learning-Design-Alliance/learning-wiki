Evidence¶

Thibault Asselborn 2021¶

Thibault Asselborn, Wafa Johal, Bolat Tleubayev, Zhanel Zhexenova, Pierre Dillenbourg, Catherine McBride and Anara Sandygulova. (2021). The transferability of handwriting skills: from the Cyrillic to the Latin alphabet. npj Science of Learning 6:6. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00084-w

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Wilcoxon tests per feature and grade on tablet-copied text from 190 children; the result was judged abnormal because these features positively indicate proficiency, so the constant difference was attributed to intrinsic alphabet differences.

"The Mean Speed of Pressure Change (#12) and Max Speed of Pressure Change (#13) were found to be signi ﬁcantly higher when children were writing in the Latin alphabet compared to when they were writing in the Cyrillic alphabet across all grades (see Fig. 2B and Supplementary Table S1)."

Thibault Asselborn 2021 (2)¶

Thibault Asselborn, Wafa Johal, Bolat Tleubayev, Zhanel Zhexenova, Pierre Dillenbourg, Catherine McBride and Anara Sandygulova. (2021). The transferability of handwriting skills: from the Cyrillic to the Latin alphabet. npj Science of Learning 6:6. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00084-w

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Wilcoxon comparisons across grades for tilt features; the authors interpret the constant abnormal-direction difference as stemming from the alphabets requiring different pen manipulation styles during writing.

"The Std. of Tilt-X (#16) and the Std. of Speed of Tilt-X change (#17) were always different between the two alphabets. Once again, children writing with the Cyrillic alphabet exhibited a higher standard deviation, which appears to be abnormal."

Related Claims¶

  • Fine motor handwriting skills transfer positively from Cyrillic to Latin: Latin-script writing quality improved with grade despite zero Latin experience — related
  • Children's handwriting specificity transfers across scripts: 90% of Cyrillic–Latin feature rank correlations were significant and correlation levels increased with grade — related
  • For features such as Mean Velocity and Handwriting Density, Cyrillic improves faster than Latin, producing an increasing gap that indicates incomplete transfer — related
  • Transfer between scripts depends on the level of handwriting automation: Cyrillic–Latin differences in shakiness decreased with grade and were nearly non-existent by grade 4 — reports the opposite
  • Low-level dynamic handwriting features may be transferable to more languages, paving the way for a cross-lingual model for detecting handwriting difficulties — reports the opposite
