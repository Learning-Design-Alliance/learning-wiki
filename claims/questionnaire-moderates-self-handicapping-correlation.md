# The self-handicapping questionnaire used moderates the correlation: the SHS yields weaker correlations than the ASHS and MES self-sabotage scale

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Studies using the Self-Handicapping Scale report lower correlations (r = -.11) than studies using the Academic Self-Handicapping Scale or the MES Self-Sabotage subscale (both r = -.25). [→ Schwinger 2014](#schwinger-2014)

## Evidence

### Schwinger 2014

Schwinger, M., Wirthwein, L., Lemmer, G., & Steinmayr, R. (2014, February 10). Academic Self-Handicapping and Achievement: A Meta-Analysis. Journal of Educational Psychology. Advance online publication. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/a0035832

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Mixed-effects moderator analysis of questionnaire type across the 49 effect sizes (QB = 24.74, p < .01). The SHS category showed r = -.11, lower than the ASHS (r = -.25) and MES (r = -.25) categories, whose intervals did not overlap with the SHS interval.

> "Lower correlations were found for the "SHS" (r ⫽ ⫺.11; 95% CI: ⫺.17 ⱕ r ⱕ ⫺.06) compared to the categories "Academic SHS" (r ⫽ ⫺.25; 95% CI: ⫺.29 ⱕ r ⱕ ⫺.23) and "MES" (r ⫽ ⫺.25; 95% CI: ⫺.31 ⱕ r ⱕ ⫺.20)."
