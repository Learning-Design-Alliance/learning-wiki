# Within the e-learning cohort, each percentage increase in online engagement was associated with a 0.05% increase in SNQ grade after adjusting for prior performance, with no significant relationship for MCQ grade

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Regression in the 2017/2018 cohort adjusting for previous exam performance found each percentage increase in engagement raised SNQ score by 0.05% (95% CI: 0.02 to 0.08%, P = 0.002). [→ Stevens Niall T. 2019](#stevens-niall-t-2019)
`q3 i?` There was no evidence of a significant relationship between engagement and MCQ performance (0.03, 95% CI: −0.04 to 0.07%, P = 0.179); equivalence was not tested. [→ Stevens Niall T. 2019 (2)](#stevens-niall-t-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Stevens Niall T. 2019

Stevens Niall T., Holmes Killian, Grainger Rachel J., Connolly Roisín, Prior Anna-Rose, Fitzpatrick Fidelma, O'Neill Eoghan, Boland Fiona, Pawlikowska Teresa, Humphreys Hilary. (2019). Can e-learning improve the performance of undergraduate medical students in Clinical Microbiology examinations? BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1843-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Regression analysis using the 2017/2018 cohort only, with engagement as a continuous variable and previous semester grade as covariate. "For every percentage increase in online engagement, the SNQ score increased on average by 0.05%" (P = 0.002).

> "For every percentage increase in online engagement, the SNQ score increased on average by 0.05% (95% CI: 0.02 to 0.08%, P = 0.002). There was no evidence of significant relationship between engagement with the online material and performance in the MCQ examination"
