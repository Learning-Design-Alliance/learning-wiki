## Evidence

### Nour 2025

Nour, G., Singh, H., Longbottom, E., Hayes, J., Farnell, D., & Dutta, A. (2025). Assessment of Case Complexity of Root Canal Treatments Using Contemporary Complexity Grading Systems: A Clinical Service Evaluation. International Endodontic Journal. [doi:10.1111/iej.70039](https://doi.org/10.1111/iej.70039)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Agreement analysis of the same 201 case records scored under a common level 1–3 grading scheme. The results report "substantial levels of agreement between the two (κ = 0.647)" for E-CAT vs ECS; the corresponding fair kappas for EA pairs (0.290, 0.385) are printed in Table 2, all p < 0.001.

> "The scores for ECS and E‐CAT coincided perfectly in 78.6% of the cases, with E‐CAT scoring higher in 5 cases and substantial levels of agreement between the two (κ = 0.647, 95% CI: 0.517 to 0.776, p < 0.001) (Table 2)."

### Nour 2025 (2)

Nour, G., Singh, H., Longbottom, E., Hayes, J., Farnell, D., & Dutta, A. (2025). Assessment of Case Complexity of Root Canal Treatments Using Contemporary Complexity Grading Systems: A Clinical Service Evaluation. International Endodontic Journal. [doi:10.1111/iej.70039](https://doi.org/10.1111/iej.70039)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Reliability substudy in which 20 records were scored independently by a postgraduate trainee and a dental core trainee, plus 60 records re-scored after 2 months. The results state "Inter‐examiner and intra‐examiner agreement was statistically significant (p < 0.001) and generally high"; only p-values are printed in the supplied text, so no effect size is coded.

> "Inter‐examiner and intra‐examiner agreement was statistically significant (p < 0.001) and generally high for all complexity grading systems (Table S1)."
