## Evidence

### Kamp Rachelle J. A. 2012

Kamp Rachelle J. A., Dolmans Diana H. J. M., Van Berkel Henk J. M., Schmidt Henk G. (2012). The effect of midterm peer feedback on student functioning in problem-based tutorials. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-012-9364-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Qualitative focus group with seven students, one per tutorial group, conducted after the course in the explanatory second phase of the mixed-methods design. Students attributed increased awareness to the distinction between cognitive, collaborative and motivational contribution types.

> "Students indicated that giving feedback made them more aware of what was considered ‘appropriate tutorial behavior’ and how individual contributions influence the functioning of the group."

### Kamp Rachelle J. A. 2012 (2)

Kamp Rachelle J. A., Dolmans Diana H. J. M., Van Berkel Henk J. M., Schmidt Henk G. (2012). The effect of midterm peer feedback on student functioning in problem-based tutorials. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-012-9364-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Six-item evaluation questionnaire filled in by 68 of the 74 feedback recipients in week 7. Two items scored below 3.0: useful improvement goals (M = 2.65) and tutor stimulation of reflection (M = 2.84).

> "Firstly, students reported that they did not formulate useful improvement goals (M = 2.65, SD = 1.10). In addition, students perceived that the tutors did not sufficiently stimulate them to reflect on the received feedback (M = 2.84, SD = 1.03)."
