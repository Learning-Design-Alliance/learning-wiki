# Flipped and nonflipped active classrooms perform equally on a comprehensive final exam, on both low-level and high-level items

## Evidence

### Jensen Jamie L. 2015

Jensen Jamie L., Kummer Tyler A., Godoy Patricia D. d. M. (2015). Improvements from a Flipped Classroom May Simply Be the Fruits of Active Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-08-0129

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA on an identical 104-question comprehensive final exam administered to both sections found no difference (F = 0.37, p = 0.55); ANCOVA with pre-LCTSR and BKA covariates confirmed it (p = 0.66).

> "The overall total score on the final exam was evaluated using ANOVA between the nonflipped and flipped conditions. Results indicated that students learning under both conditions performed equally (Mnonflipped = 66.6%, Mflipped = 67.9%; see Figure 3 and Table 5)."

### Jensen Jamie L. 2015 (2)

Jensen Jamie L., Kummer Tyler A., Godoy Patricia D. d. M. (2015). Improvements from a Flipped Classroom May Simply Be the Fruits of Active Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-08-0129

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA on the 40 high-level items found no difference (p = 0.48; ANCOVA p = 0.86); low-level items were likewise equal (61.1% vs 60.9%, p = 0.93; ANCOVA p = 0.40).

> "An ANOVA was conducted to evaluate whether the condition affected student performance on high-level Bloom's items (i.e., "apply" and above). Results indicated that students learning under both conditions performed equally (Mnonflipped = 57.2%, Mflipped = 58.9%; see ­Figure 3 and Table 5)."
