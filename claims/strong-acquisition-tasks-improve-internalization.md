# Strong acquisition tasks explicating the organization produce better internalization than weak tasks asking only for a structured summary

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Subjects in strong treatments, whose acquisition tasks included marking organizational charts and arranging items into a chart, significantly outperformed weak-treatment subjects who wrote a structured summary. [→ Eylon 1979](#eylon-1979)

## Evidence

### Eylon 1979

Eylon, Bat-Sheva; Reif, F. (1979). Effects of Internal Knowledge Organization on Task Performance. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED171804

`q2 · i1`

Experiment 2 two-way ANOVA (input version x acquisition tasks) on the sum of composite scores of all type-a and type-b tasks showed a significant acquisition-task effect [F(1,12)=5.96, p<0.05], despite all subjects studying the same well-organized materials for equal time.
