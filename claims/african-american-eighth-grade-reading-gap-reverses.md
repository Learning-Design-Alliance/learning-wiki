# The eighth-grade reading gap between Chicago and rest-of-state African-American students disappears and reverses in Chicago's favor

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Chicago African-American students trail rest-of-state counterparts by 0.27 SDs in third-grade reading but score 0.06 SDs higher by eighth grade. [→ John Q. Easton 2007](#john-q-easton-2007)
`q2 i?` In math, the African-American gap shrinks from 10 points in third grade to slightly over two points in eighth grade. [→ John Q. Easton 2007 (2)](#john-q-easton-2007-2)

## Evidence

### John Q. Easton 2007

John Q. Easton, Stuart Luppescu, Todd Rosenkranz. (2007). 2006 ISAT Reading and Math Scores in Chicago and the Rest of the State. Research Brief, Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/2006-isat-reading-and-math-scores-chicago-and-rest-state

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of 2006 ISAT reading scores for African-American students in CPS versus the rest of Illinois; the printed effect sizes are d = 0.27 ("0.27 standard deviation units lower" at grade three) and d = 0.06 ("0.06 standard deviations units higher" at grade eight).

> "By eighth grade, that gap has not only disappeared but reversed, with Chicago African-American students outscoring their counterparts in the rest of Illinois by one point."

### John Q. Easton 2007 (2)

John Q. Easton, Stuart Luppescu, Todd Rosenkranz. (2007). 2006 ISAT Reading and Math Scores in Chicago and the Rest of the State. Research Brief, Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/2006-isat-reading-and-math-scores-chicago-and-rest-state

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of 2006 ISAT math scores for African-American students; the gap narrows across grades but does not reverse as it does in reading. No standardized effect size is printed for this math contrast.

> "In math, there is a 10 point gap in third grade between African-American students in CPS compared to those in the rest of the state. By eighth grade, that gap is reduced to slightly over two points."
