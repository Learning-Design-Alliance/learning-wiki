## Evidence

### van Duijvenvoorde 2022

van Duijvenvoorde, A. C. K., Whitmore, L. B., Westhoff, B. & Mills, K. L. (2022). A methodological perspective on learning in the developing brain. npj Science of Learning 7:12. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00127-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The review reports a study (attributed to Siugzdaite et al.) comparing subgrouping on behavioral measures (literacy, numeracy, working memory) and structural brain measures in 479 children and adolescents, partly struggling learners; behavioral clustering produced six cognitive profiles, and "the same neural profile could be associated with different cognitive impairments in different children".

> "A similar profile mapping based on structural brain measures indicated that neural and cognitive mappings for individuals were not one-to-one. That is, the same neural profile could be associated with different cognitive impairments in different children 46"

### van Duijvenvoorde 2022 (2)

van Duijvenvoorde, A. C. K., Whitmore, L. B., Westhoff, B. & Mills, K. L. (2022). A methodological perspective on learning in the developing brain. npj Science of Learning 7:12. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00127-w

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

A subsequent analysis in the same reviewed study found the hub-like structure of individuals' white-matter connectome related to children's cognitive abilities, indicating the brain–cognition relationship is moderated by network organization. The review presents this as evidence against single neuro-anatomical substrates for learning difficulties.

> "the authors observed that an individual's whole-brain network (i.e., the connectome) of white-matter tracts was more strongly related to the cognitive profiles of struggling learners"
