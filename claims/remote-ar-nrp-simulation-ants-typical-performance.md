## Evidence

### Rojas Marcos 2026

Rojas Marcos, Yun Romy, Rama Asheen, Plane Jaime, Arancibia Claudia, Paredes Pamela, Penna Antonello, Zhao Yiling, Caruso Thomas J. (2026). Non-technical skill performance during remote, international, augmented-reality neonatal resuscitation protocol simulations: a feasibility study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08611-2

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Prospective single-arm feasibility study of 34 pediatric and anesthesiology residents in Chile, each acting as team leader in a remote, Stanford-facilitated AR neonatal resuscitation scenario; "Participants demonstrated typical performance across all four ANTS domains, with mean scores ranging from 2.5 to 2.9 on the 1–4 scale".

> "Participants demonstrated typical performance across all four ANTS domains, with mean scores ranging from 2.5 to 2.9 on the 1–4 scale (Table 2)."

### Rojas Marcos 2026 (2)

Rojas Marcos, Yun Romy, Rama Asheen, Plane Jaime, Arancibia Claudia, Paredes Pamela, Penna Antonello, Zhao Yiling, Caruso Thomas J. (2026). Non-technical skill performance during remote, international, augmented-reality neonatal resuscitation protocol simulations: a feasibility study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08611-2

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Descriptive ANTS element-level results from the same feasibility study (Table 2): teamwork elements "coordinating activities and exchanging information (mean 2.9, SD 0.9 for both)" were highest; the lowest element was anticipating at mean 2.5 (SD 0.9). No inferential tests were reported.

> "teamwork scored highest overall, particularly in coordinating activities and exchanging information (mean 2.9, SD 0.9 for both). Situational awareness showed greater variability, with the lowest scores observed for anticipating (mean 2.5, SD 0.9)."
