# Physiologically activating group states are rare but co-occur with visible emotional expressions more often than deactivating states

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Activating affective states were rare (6.6% of segments) compared with deactivating states, and activating segments contained significantly more observable emotional expressions than deactivating segments. [→ Törmänen 2021](#tormanen-2021)

## Evidence

### Törmänen 2021

Törmänen, T., Järvenoja, H., & Mänty, K. (2021). Exploring groups’ affective states during collaborative learning: what triggers activating affect on a group level? Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-021-10037-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Chi-square test of independence across 1922 30-s video/EDA segments from 10 groups of 12–13-year-olds; the article reports "χ2 (3) = 9.579, V = 0.071" with p < 0.05, and activating segments in only 6.6% of the time.

> "The results showed that, in physiologically activating segments, group members also indicated significantly more emotional expressions (the interaction had emotional valence) in the video than in physiologically deactivating situations (χ2 (3) = 9.579, V = 0.071, f = 1922, p < 0.05)."
