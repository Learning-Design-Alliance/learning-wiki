## Evidence

### Inmaculada Márquez & Mario Treviño 2025

Inmaculada Márquez & Mario Treviño. (2025). Dissociable sensory, motor, and visuomotor predictive functions. npj Science of Learning 10:85. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00377-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Comparison of trial-wise within-subject variance against between-subject variance across the visuomotor, detection, and motor variability tasks, using paired t-tests. The motor task showed the largest effect, with endpoint dispersion variability "roughly 24 times greater than between-s ubject variability".

> "On average, within-subject variability was ~12 times larger than between-subject variability, revealing the dynamic and highly variable nature ofindividual response proﬁles."

### Inmaculada Márquez & Mario Treviño 2025 (2)

Inmaculada Márquez & Mario Treviño. (2025). Dissociable sensory, motor, and visuomotor predictive functions. npj Science of Learning 10:85. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00377-4

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis relating within-subject variability measures (vU*, IDD*, GTD, GUD) to interception performance; no significant associations were found except var(GTD), positive after FDR correction (q < 0.0001).

> "Within-subject variability in the visuomotor task did not reliably predict interception performance, except for GTD variability, which was positively associated with performance"
