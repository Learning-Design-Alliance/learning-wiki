# The article attributes the discrepancy with inspection-time studies to methodology, arguing standardized measures are sensitive enough to detect processing speed deficits in ASD

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors interpret the conflicting literature as methodological: inspection-time tasks may produce ceiling effects, and standardized measures better detect clinically-related deficits. [→ Haigh Sarah M. 2018](#haigh-sarah-m-2018)

## Evidence

### Haigh Sarah M. 2018

Haigh Sarah M., Walsh Jennifer A., Mazefsky Carla A., Minshew Nancy J., Eack Shaun M. (2018). Processing Speed is Impaired in Adults with Autism Spectrum Disorder, and Relates to Social Communication Abilities. Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-018-3515-z

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion, not a tested result: they attribute the conflict with inspection-time studies (Scheuffgen et al. 2000; Wallace et al. 2009; Barbeau et al. 2013) to possible ceiling effects in inspection tasks, and advocate standardized processing speed measures.

> "The discrepancy between the current findings and the inspection time findings could be due to the inspection tasks producing ceiling effects, and thus it may not have been sensitive enough to detect differences in processing speed in ASD."
