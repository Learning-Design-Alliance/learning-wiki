Evidence¶

Sascha Schneider 2021¶

Sascha Schneider, Maik Beege, Steve Nebel, Lenka Schnaubert, Günter Daniel Rey. (2021). The Cognitive-Affective-Social Theory of Learning in digital Environments (CASTLE). Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09626-5

q1 · i? · theoretical · r3

Theoretical hypothesis formulated in the review from work on social comparison and schema development (e.g., Garcia and Tor 2009; Fischer et al. 2013). The article states the hypothesis; no effect size is printed.

"Schema Influence Hypothesis: How strong and fast social schemata are activated depends on the strength and number of the social cue as well as the degree of development of the social schemata."

Sascha Schneider 2021 (2)¶

Sascha Schneider, Maik Beege, Steve Nebel, Lenka Schnaubert, Günter Daniel Rey. (2021). The Cognitive-Affective-Social Theory of Learning in digital Environments (CASTLE). Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09626-5

q1 · i? · theoretical · r3

Second hypothesis of the same claim, derived from social presence theory: text-based communication is low in social presence while face-to-face and synchronous video-mediated materials are high. The article presents this as a theoretical moderation statement without effect sizes.

"Social Cue Strength Hypothesis: The strength of social cues to activate social schemata is moderated by the modality of the learning material, the temporal changeability of information, as well as the degree of interactivity of a learning material."
