# Frequency and type of physical activity show no statistically significant association with mean GPA among physically active students

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` One-way ANOVA found no statistically significant correlation between frequency of physical activity (p = 0.7) or type of physical activity (p = 0.2) and mean GPA. [→ Raja Saud Mohammed 2025](#raja-saud-mohammed-2025)

## Evidence

### Raja Saud Mohammed 2025

Raja Saud Mohammed, Elfaki Mohammed Ahmed, Ibrahim Alaa, Maroof Hibaallah, Abdelrahim Abrar. (2025). The relationship between physical activity and academic achievement in medical students: a single-institution cross-sectional study in Sudan. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07985-z

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

One-way ANOVA among the 166 physically active students compared GPA across activity frequencies (daily, weekly, mixed) and types (walking, running, football, other). Table 3 prints p-values of 0.7 and 0.2; no effect size is reported.

> "Other variables such as the frequency and type of physical activity of the students were assessed for association with the mean GPA using one-way ANOVA (Table 3). However, no statistically significant correlation was observed."
