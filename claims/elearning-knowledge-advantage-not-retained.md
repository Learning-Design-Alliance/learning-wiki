# Knowledge advantage of e-learning at 4 weeks is not significant after multiple-testing adjustment, and no difference remains at 12 weeks

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Knowledge 4 weeks after the course favoured e-learning descriptively (mean difference 1.0, p = .043) but was not significant after adjustment for multiple testing. [→ Andersson ML 2023](#andersson-ml-2023)

## Evidence

### Andersson ML 2023

Andersson ML, Duch P, Bessmann EL, Lundstrøm LH, Ekelund K. (2023). Preparing for obstetric anaesthesia—An educational randomised controlled trial comparing e‐learning to written course material. Acta Anaesthesiol Scand. https://doi.org/10.1111/aas.14148

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary outcome of the RCT with 42 participants analysed at 4 weeks; knowledge scaled 0–35, means 30.2 vs 29.2. The article states the difference "was not significant" after adjusting for multiple testing.

> "Knowledge 4 weeks after the course was increased in favour of e-learning (mean difference 1.0; 95% CI 0.03–2.0; p = .043). However, after adjusting for multiple testing, this difference was not significant."

### Andersson ML 2023 (2)

Andersson ML, Duch P, Bessmann EL, Lundstrøm LH, Ekelund K. (2023). Preparing for obstetric anaesthesia—An educational randomised controlled trial comparing e‐learning to written course material. Acta Anaesthesiol Scand. https://doi.org/10.1111/aas.14148

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary outcome with 41 participants analysed at 12 weeks; means 29.7 vs 29.2 on the 0–35 knowledge scale. The article reports "no significant difference in knowledge 12 weeks after the course".

> "Likewise, there was no significant difference in knowledge 12 weeks after the course (mean difference 0.5; 95% CI −0.6 to 1.6; p = .371)"
