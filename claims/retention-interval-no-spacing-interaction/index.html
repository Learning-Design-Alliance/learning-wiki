Paulo F. Carvalho 2020¶

Paulo F. Carvalho, Faria Sana and Veronica X. Yan. (2020). Self-regulated spacing in a massive open online course is related to better learning. npj Science of Learning 5:2. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-020-0061-1

From the same mixed regression on MOOC log data: "shorter retention intervals being associated with higher quiz grades, β = −0.07", while the spacing-by-retention-interval interaction was not significant (β = 0.02, p > 0.20).

"although there was also a significant main effect of retention interval with shorter retention intervals being associated with higher quiz grades, β = −0.07, SE = 0.01, t(6664.91) = −5.19, p < 0.001, there was not a significant spacing by retention interval effect, β = 0.02, SE = 0.01, t(6512) = 1.28, p > 0.20."

Related Claims¶

  • The spacing benefit holds even at very short retention intervals, contradicting the Peterson paradox — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Retention interval moderates the testing effect: effects are larger after a day or more than at shorter intervals, where individual studies vary from reliable to null or reversed — related
  • Recall of words repeated across lists spaced up to 1 week follows a proportionality rule: recall is better after short spacing at short retention intervals but better after long spacing at long retention intervals — reports the opposite
  • Spaced Practice Improves Retention — a broader claim this one bears on
  • ISI effects on recall are nonmonotonic and depend jointly on retention interval — reports the opposite
  • Expanding and uniform spaced practice better for long retention intervals; contracting better for short intervals — related
  • In a self-regulated MOOC, spacing study across more sessions predicts higher unit quiz performance within participants — related
  • Students who spaced their study more also performed better on the MOOC final exam — related
