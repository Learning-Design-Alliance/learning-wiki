## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Go/No-Go reaction time improved more in B-E than F-E from start to mid-morning (p = 0.019); from mid- to end-of-morning F-E improved more (p = 0.021), and by the end of the morning reaction times were similar across groups. ## Evidence

### Kawabata Masato 2021

Kawabata Masato, Lee Kerry, Choo Hui-Cheng, Burns Stephen F. (2021). Breakfast and Exercise Improve Academic and Cognitive Performance in Adolescents. Nutrients 13(4). https://doi.org/10.3390/nu13041278

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Go/No-Go attention task in the randomized trial: B-E reaction times were faster than F-E at test-series2 (335 vs 362 ms). From mid- to end-of-morning F-E improved more than B-E, and the authors report that by test-series3 reaction times were similar in B-E and all other conditions (332 vs 347 ms against F-E); the B-E advantage faded rather than reversed. No group differences in error rates emerged over the morning.

> "Reaction time improved to a greater extent in B-E from the start to mid-morning compared with F-E (β13 = 33.99, p = 0.019) but this finding for F-E was reversed from the middle to end of the morning (β23 = −15.29, p = 0.021)."
