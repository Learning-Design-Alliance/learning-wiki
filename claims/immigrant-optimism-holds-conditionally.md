# The immigrant optimism hypothesis holds in some cultural contexts but not in others, depending on cultural congruence, cooperative learning, and family educational investment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` High future expectations of minority parents and youth predict educational attainment, but the effect is conditional on classroom cultural congruence and family investment. [→ Phalet 2004](#phalet-2004)
`q2 i?` High parental aspirations may fail to produce school results when perceived discrimination or unequal opportunities limit their motivational impact. [→ Phalet 2004 (2)](#phalet-2004-2)

## Evidence

### Phalet 2004

Phalet, K., Andriessen, I., & Lens, W. (2004). How Future Goals Enhance Motivation and Learning in Multicultural Classrooms. Educational Psychology Review, Vol. 16, No. 1. https://hdl.handle.net/1874/23520

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's own synthesis of cross-cultural studies in the United States and Europe, including longitudinal evidence that parental aspirations predict educational progress after controlling for social and family background. It states the hypothesis "holds in some cultural contexts but not in others."

> "To conclude, the immigrant optimism hypothesis holds in some cultural contexts but not in others; speciﬁcally, it holds when teaching prac- tices and interactions in multicultural classrooms support cultural congru- ence and cooperative learning, and/or when shared cultural values and be- liefs in minority families and communities promote educational investment."

### Phalet 2004 (2)

Phalet, K., Andriessen, I., & Lens, W. (2004). How Future Goals Enhance Motivation and Learning in Multicultural Classrooms. Educational Psychology Review, Vol. 16, No. 1. https://hdl.handle.net/1874/23520

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Pels (1998) and Phalet and Schönpflug (2001b), that high parental aspirations do not always translate into achievement under perceived discrimination or unequal opportunities. The hypothesis also excludes so-called involuntary minorities in the U.S. context.

> "On a cautionary note, some ﬁndings suggest that there are limits to the mo- tivational impact of high parental aspirations in the presence of perceived discrimination or unequal opportunities (Pels, 1998; Phalet and Sch ¨onpﬂug, 2001b)."
