# Children with ADHD on methylphenidate were more likely than unmedicated children with ADHD to attend higher levels of secondary education

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` The odds of attending the highest level of secondary education were lower for the ADHD group with medication than the no-ADHD group (OR = 0.33) but higher than the ADHD group without medication (OR = 1.72, p = 0.013). [→ de Zeeuw Eveline L. 2017](#de-zeeuw-eveline-l-2017)

## Evidence

### de Zeeuw Eveline L. 2017

de Zeeuw Eveline L., van Beijsterveldt Catharina E. M., Ehli Erik A., de Geus Eco J. C., Boomsma Dorret I. (2017). Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder Symptoms and Low Educational Achievement: Evidence Supporting A Causal Hypothesis. Behavior Genetics. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10519-017-9836-4

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Ordinal logistic generalized linear models on self-reported secondary education level at ages 14 and/or 16, with family membership as a random effect. The article reports the medicated ADHD group had "higher compared to the ADHD group without medication (OR = 1.72, p = 0.013)" odds of the highest level.

> "The probability for attending the highest level of education compared to the lower levels was lower for the children in the ADHD group with medication compared to the no-ADHD group (OR = 0.33, p < 0.001), but higher compared to the ADHD group without medication (OR = 1.72, p = 0.013)."
