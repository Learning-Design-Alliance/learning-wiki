Evidence¶

Sawyer 2025¶

Sawyer, G., Fraser, A., Lawlor, D. A., Sharp, G. C. & Howe, L. D. (2025). Associations of adolescent menstrual symptoms with school absences and educational attainment: analysis of a prospective cohort study. npj Science of Learning 10:54. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00338-x

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Confounder-adjusted linear regression of GCSE point score on menstrual pain in ALSPAC (N = 2698); the confidence interval includes zero, so the article reports less evidence rather than a significant difference.

"There was less evidence that menstrual pain ( −3.14; 95% CI: −7.46, 1.17) was associated with a difference in GCSE score."

Sawyer 2025 (2)¶

Sawyer, G., Fraser, A., Lawlor, D. A., Sharp, G. C. & Howe, L. D. (2025). Associations of adolescent menstrual symptoms with school absences and educational attainment: analysis of a prospective cohort study. npj Science of Learning 10:54. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00338-x

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Logistic regression for the binary attainment outcome in the same cohort. The discussion notes the discrepancy between the binary and continuous attainment results is of unclear reason.

"menstrual pain (OR 0.84; 95 CI: 0.81, 0.86: absolute risk difference −2.2%) were associated with a reduced likelihood of achieving ﬁve A*-C GCSEs including Maths and English"
