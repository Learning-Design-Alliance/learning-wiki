## Evidence

### Federer Meghan Rector 2016

Federer Meghan Rector, Nehm Ross H., Pearl Dennis K. (2016). Examining Gender Differences in Written Assessment Tasks in Biology: A Case Study of Evolutionary Explanations. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-01-0018

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

A subset of participants completed oral interviews with four isomorphic ACORNS items after the written task; written and oral KC/NI scores were compared by gender (Table 8). The article prints "Cohen’s d = −0.40" for oral animal-taxa KC scores.

> "A significant difference and moderate effect size in favor of males was found for KC scores of oral interview items about animal taxa (p < 0.05, Cohen’s d = −0.40), with males using more KCs in their explanations for these items"

### Federer Meghan Rector 2016 (2)

Federer Meghan Rector, Nehm Ross H., Pearl Dennis K. (2016). Examining Gender Differences in Written Assessment Tasks in Biology: A Case Study of Evolutionary Explanations. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-01-0018

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

DIF analysis of the eight ACORNS items used across written and oral assessments (Table 9) found the majority of items did not function differently; the Opossum item, prompting trait loss in familiar taxa, showed "Cohen’s d = 0.67" at p < 0.001.

> "Item difficulty measures indicated that females had significantly more difficulty (i.e., included fewer KCs) with the Opossum item than males (p < 0.001, Cohen’s d = 0.67)."
