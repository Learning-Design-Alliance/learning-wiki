## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Across 43 cross-sectional and longitudinal studies (211 effect sizes), muscular fitness showed a positive but very small association with combined cognition and academic achievement (SMD 0.13, 95% CI 0.10–0.16). [→ Robinson Katie 2023](#robinson-katie-2023)

## Evidence

### Robinson Katie 2023

Robinson Katie, Riley Nicholas, Owen Katherine, Drew Ryan, Mavilidi Myrto F., Hillman Charles H., Faigenbaum Avery D., Garcia-Hermoso Antonio, Lubans David Revalds. (2023). Effects of Resistance Training on Academic Outcomes in School-Aged Youth: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Sports Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-023-01881-6

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Multilevel meta-analysis of cross-sectional (k = 35) and longitudinal (k = 8) observational studies including 1,916,424 participants; printed effect size SMD = 0.13 (95% CI 0.10–0.16), heterogeneity negligible (I2 = 17% within, 5% between studies). GRADE certainty rated moderate. This is an association, not a causal effect.

> "An additional 43 studies (including 211 effect sizes) examined the association between muscular fitness and cognition or academic achievement, also yielding a positive relationship (SMD 0.13, 95% CI 0.10–0.16)."
