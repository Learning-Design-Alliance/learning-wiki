# Students' JoL ratings became more consistent over time and evolved from under-rating to over-rating to accurate judgement

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1`–`r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Variance among a student's subsequent JoLs decreased over the intervention, indicating increased consistency of self-rating. [→ Graf 2026](#graf-2026)
`q2 i?` Early JoLs primarily under-rated mastery, mid-study JoLs tended to over-rate, and late JoLs were mostly accurate; students who rated low tended to underestimate. [→ Graf 2026 (2)](#graf-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Graf 2026

Graf, L., Bassner, P., Anzinger, M., Dietrich, F., Krusche, S., & Poquet, O. (2026). Interactive learning dashboards: rethinking learning visualisations as engagement tools. Education and Information Technologies. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10639-026-14082-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Process analysis of 482 submitted JoLs: variance of each three subsequent JoL ratings per student was averaged over students for beginning, middle and end time windows, showing a calibration process over time.

> "As shown in Fig. 3a, the variance among subse-quent JoLs submitted by a student decreased over time, meaning consistency in how the students rated themselves increased."

### Graf 2026 (2)

Graf, L., Bassner, P., Anzinger, M., Dietrich, F., Krusche, S., & Poquet, O. (2026). Interactive learning dashboards: rethinking learning visualisations as engagement tools. Education and Information Technologies. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10639-026-14082-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Figure 3b and Table 1 distribution analysis of JoL-minus-system-rating discrepancies by competency; the article adds "Table 1 shows that students who rated their learning low, tended to under-estimate their learning".

> "For the first two competencies, i.e. the JoLs made early in the study, students primarily under-rate their mastery. For the last two competencies, i.e. JoLs in the later study period, most of the ratings were accurate."
