Duckworth 2013¶

Duckworth, A. L., Kim, B., and Tsukayama, E. (2013). Life stress impairs self-control in early adolescence. Frontiers in Psychology. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00608

Descriptive stability statistics from Study 2's summary correlations; Study 1 reported r=0.70 across grades 4 and 6 and Study 3 r=0.81. The article notes "despite substantial rank-order stability in self-control across development... situational influences can play an important role."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Negative life events predict rank-order decreases in self-control in early adolescents over time, controlling for baseline self-control and demographics — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • The rank ordering of math and reading skills is highly stable over time, while four SEL domains are more strongly influenced by contextual factors — related
  • The authors hypothesize a possible vicious cycle in which stress-induced self-control losses generate further stressful life events — related
  • Perceived stress mediates the effect of negative life events on self-control decreases when measured at temporally separated occasions (Study 3) — related
  • Learning patterns are moderately stable over months but changeable, and more stable in constant than in changing educational contexts — related
