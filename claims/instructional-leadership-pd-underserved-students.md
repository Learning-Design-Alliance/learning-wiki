# As principals' professional development in instructional leadership increases, mathematics gains are significantly more pronounced for historically underserved students of color than for white and Asian students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Gains in mathematics are significantly more pronounced for historically underserved students of color (relative coefficient 1.654*) as principals' instructional leadership professional development increases, with no statistically significant ELA differences across racial/ethnic groups. [→ Campoli 2022](#campoli-2022)

## Evidence

### Campoli 2022

Campoli, A. K., & Darling-Hammond, L. (with Podolsky, A., & Levin, S.). (2022). Principal learning opportunities and school outcomes: Evidence from California. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/438.376

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression analysis (Table 6) of mathematics gains by student racial/ethnic group, with Asian students as reference. The leading instruction coefficient for historically underserved students of color is 1.654*. Figure 6 forecasts an 11.3-point difference (about 3.5 months of instructional time) between low and high access for historically underserved students, versus a non-significant 5.3-point difference for white students. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "as principals' experience of professional development in instructional leadership increases, gains in mathematics are significantly more pronounced for historically underserved students of color and, to a somewhat lesser extent, for students identified as "Multiracial & Other" than for white and Asian students."
