## Evidence

### Janzing Paul 2023

Janzing Paul, Nourkami-Tutdibi Nasenien, Tutdibi Erol, Freundt Paula, von Ostrowski Thomas, Langer Martin, Zemlin Michael, Steinhard Johannes. (2023). Controlled prospective study on ultrasound simulation training in fetal echocardiography: FESIM II. Archives of Gynecology and Obstetrics. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00404-023-07133-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Within-student longitudinal comparison across the three biweekly standardized tests in the FESIM II study. Table 3 reports mean TTC falling from 847.09 s (2 weeks) to 565.91 s (4 weeks) to 289.27 s (6 weeks); only the p-value is printed, so no effect size is available.

> "Friedman- and Wilcoxon-tests demonstrate a significant stepwise decrease in the students' TTC in each test after 2, 4, and 6 weeks (p < 0.05)."

### Janzing Paul 2023 (2)

Janzing Paul, Nourkami-Tutdibi Nasenien, Tutdibi Erol, Freundt Paula, von Ostrowski Thomas, Langer Martin, Zemlin Michael, Steinhard Johannes. (2023). Controlled prospective study on ultrasound simulation training in fetal echocardiography: FESIM II. Archives of Gynecology and Obstetrics. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00404-023-07133-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Between-group comparison (Mann–Whitney U) after 6 weeks among students (n = 11), 10 Ob/Gyn physicians (median 750 prior scans; mean TTC 951.3 s), and 10 fetal echocardiography experts (median 15,000 prior scans; mean TTC 269.2 s). Only p-values are printed, so no effect size is available.

> "The Mann–Whitney-U-test showed that students became significantly faster than the physician group after 6 weeks (p < 0.001) and were no longer significantly slower than the expert group (p = 0.944)."
