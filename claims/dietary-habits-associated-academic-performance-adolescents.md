# Healthier dietary habits are associated with better academic performance in middle school students, while chips, sweets and soft drink consumption are associated with worse performance

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Eating breakfast, and consuming fruit, vegetables, fish and milk are positively correlated with academic performance, whereas chips, sweets and soft drinks are negatively correlated (Kendall tau from 0.079 to −0.205). [→ Sánchez-Hernando 2021](#sanchez-hernando-2021)

## Evidence

### Sánchez-Hernando 2021

Sánchez-Hernando, B.; Antón-Solanas, I.; Juárez-Vela, R.; Gea-Caballero, V.; Carboneres-Tafaner, M.I.; Ferrer-Gracia, E.; Gállego-Diéguez, J.; Santolalla-Arnedo, I.; Gasch-Gallén, Á. (2021). Healthy Lifestyle and Academic Performance in Middle School Students from the Region of Aragón (Spain). Int. J. Environ. Res. Public Health 18(16): 8624. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18168624

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional survey of 1745 Spanish 7th–8th grade students using a validated HBSC-adapted questionnaire; Table 1 Kendall correlations ranged from tau = 0.079 (fish) to tau = −0.205 (soft drinks), all p < 0.001; the largest printed effect size is tau = −0.205 for soft drinks. The authors report "better academic results were observed when breakfast was eaten during the week".

> "In the inferential study, better academic results were observed when breakfast was eaten during the week, fruit and vegetables were consumed at least once a day and fish and milk were consumed 2–6 times a week. In addition, the higher the consumption of chips, sweets, and soft drinks, the lower the academic performance (p < 0.001)."
