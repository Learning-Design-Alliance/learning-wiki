# Learning number symbols from multiple concrete representations carries a cost compared with consistent single representations, whether concrete or abstract

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across three experiments, children learning from multiple concrete representations performed worse on symbolic comparison than those learning from a single consistent representation. [→ Amy Bennett 2018](#amy-bennett-2018)

## Evidence

### Amy Bennett 2018

Amy Bennett, Matthew Inglis and Camilla Gilmore. (2018). The Cost of Multiple Representations: Learning Number Symbols with Abstract and Concrete Representations. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000318

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The general discussion synthesises the three experiments: multiple concrete representations, the dominant format in children's number books, produced lower symbolic comparison accuracy than either consistent concrete or consistent abstract representations in each experiment. No summary effect size is printed.

> "Overall, we found that there is a cost to multiple representations. Learning number symbols from multiple concrete representations – the approach adopted by the majority of children's number books (Ward et al., 2016) – seems to be less effective than learning from a consistent concrete representation or a consistent abstract representation"
