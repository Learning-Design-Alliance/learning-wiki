## Evidence

### Nicholas Mattei 2014

Nicholas Mattei, Thomas Dodson, Joshua T. Guerin, Judy Goldsmith, Joan M. Mazur. (2014). Lessons Learned from Development of a Software Tool to Support Academic Advising. [doi:10.1109/aseezone1.2014.6820659](https://doi.org/10.1109/aseezone1.2014.6820659)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

ESS predicted-usage question: 31 of 44 (70%) CS and 95 of 121 (78%) PSY respondents said they would use the tool at home, with multiple selections allowed. The authors call this "the intended use pattern".

> "Students were allowed to select multiple responces, and overall 84% in the CS group and 87% in the PSY group responded that they would use the tool either at home or while talking to an advisor—the intended use pattern."

### Nicholas Mattei 2014 (2)

Nicholas Mattei, Thomas Dodson, Joshua T. Guerin, Judy Goldsmith, Joan M. Mazur. (2014). Lessons Learned from Development of a Software Tool to Support Academic Advising. [doi:10.1109/aseezone1.2014.6820659](https://doi.org/10.1109/aseezone1.2014.6820659)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same ESS survey. The authors note the low percentage choosing fully automated advising is encouraging and link it to students who view advising as a scheduling chore.

> "There was a very small group of students, 7 of 44 (15%) for CS and 27 of 165 (16%) for PSY , that said they would use our system instead of talking to an advisor."
