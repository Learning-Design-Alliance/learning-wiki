# Prior comparative studies of live versus video lectures report contradictory results, mostly on restricted curriculum content

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Most prior medical-education comparisons used few lectures and few test questions, with mixed findings: several found no difference, while Ramlogan et al. found live lectures significantly better and Fernandes et al. found better live results. [→ Brockfeld 2018](#brockfeld-2018)

## Evidence

### Brockfeld 2018

Brockfeld, T., Müller, B., & de Laffolie, J. (2018). Video versus live lecture courses: a comparative evaluation of lecture types and results. Medical Education Online, 23(1). https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2018.1555434

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The article's narrative review of prior work (Introduction and Discussion): Paegle, Schreiber, Spickard, Solomon and Davis found no significant difference; Ramlogan found a 6.3 percentage-point live advantage (p = 0.049); Fernandes found better live results. The present study's novelty is covering "the complete subject matter of the clinical section".

> "Most of the studies that compared live and video lectures in the medical field focused on a quite restricted part of the medical curriculum and use only a small number of test questions for the comparison of live and video lessons."
