## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Interviewed Water Module students expressed a transition away from the traditional view of engineering toward one requiring a broader set of knowledge and skills, including people skills and business acumen. [→ Singer 2015](#singer-2015)

## Evidence

### Singer 2015

Singer, K. P., Foutz, T., Navarro, M., & Thompson, S. (2015). Investigating The Extent That An Integrative Learning Module Broadens The Perception Of First-Year Students About The Engineering Profession. American Journal of Engineering Education, 6(2), 99-110. https://www.cluteinstitute.com/ojs/index.php/AJEE/article/view/9603

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Interview study of three volunteering Water Module students interviewed at beginning and end of semester. The article reports one engineering major's transition away from the traditional view and a non-engineering major who "indicated an interest in engineering as a career choice" after the course.

> "in the follow-up interview, this student expressed a broader view of engineering indicating that “…it’s not just if you’re good at math and science, then you’ll be a good engineer.” This student’s response indicated a transition away from the traditional view of engineering to one where the engineer needs a broader set of knowledge and skills."
