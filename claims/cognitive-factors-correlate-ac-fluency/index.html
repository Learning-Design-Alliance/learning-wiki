Evidence¶

Agaliotis and Teli 2016¶

Agaliotis, I., & Teli, A. (2016). Teaching Arithmetic Combinations of Multiplication and Division to Students with Learning Disabilities or Mild Intellectual Disability: The Impact of Alternative Fact Grouping and the Role of Cognitive and Learning Factors. Journal of Education and Learning, 5(4). https://doi.org/10.5539/jel.v5n4p90

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Spearman's rho correlations (Table 6) between cognitive factors and post-intervention fluency; processing speed reached significance for LD in both operations (rho .496 and .469) and for MID multiplication (rho .671), with MID division fluency correlated significantly with none of the cognitive factors (language, working memory, phonological short-term memory, processing speed), though it did correlate with the two learning factors, counting skills and properties of operations (see the second entry). These are correlations and cannot show cause.

"it was found that in the case of students with LD information processing speed had a significant correlation with the final fluency in the use of ACs of both operations, while phonological short-term memory was correlated only with the fluency of division ACs."

Agaliotis and Teli 2016 (2)¶

Agaliotis, I., & Teli, A. (2016). Teaching Arithmetic Combinations of Multiplication and Division to Students with Learning Disabilities or Mild Intellectual Disability: The Impact of Alternative Fact Grouping and the Role of Cognitive and Learning Factors. Journal of Education and Learning, 5(4). https://doi.org/10.5539/jel.v5n4p90

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Spearman's rho correlations between the two learning factors and post-intervention fluency in Table 6 (LD n = 39, MID n = 14); counting skills and properties of operations correlated significantly with fluency of both operations for both LD and MID students. The article's text gives p < .01 for all of them, but Table 6 marks three only at p < .05: counting skills with division fluency for LD (rho = .379) and MID (rho = .590), and properties of operations with division fluency for MID (rho = .569); the rest are at p < .01 (rho .465 to .753). These are correlations after the intervention and cannot show that the factors cause fluency.

"Regarding the two learning factors, counting skills and properties of operations, results showed a significant correlation with the fluency of ACs of both operations (multiplication and division) for students with LD and students with MID (p < .01)"
