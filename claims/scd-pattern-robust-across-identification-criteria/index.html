Evidence¶

Spencer 2019¶

Spencer, M., Wagner, R. K., & Petscher, Y. (2019). The Reading Comprehension and Vocabulary Knowledge of Children with Poor Reading Comprehension despite Adequate Decoding: Evidence from a Regression-Based Matching Approach. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000274

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Multilevel moderation model for the SCD-2540 subsample reported in the Results section. The cohort × group interaction was non-significant (0.01, p = .09) with 0% explained variance; the SCD-540 interaction was likewise non-significant (0.04, p = .21).

"the cohort and group interaction was neither statistically significant (0.01, p = .09), nor did it explain additional variance at any of the clustering level (i.e., 0%)."

Spencer 2019 (2)¶

Spencer, M., Wagner, R. K., & Petscher, Y. (2019). The Reading Comprehension and Vocabulary Knowledge of Children with Poor Reading Comprehension despite Adequate Decoding: Evidence from a Regression-Based Matching Approach. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000274

q2 · i? · associational · r3

Introduction-level attribution to Ronberg and Petersen (2015), reported as background motivation. The article attributes the 0.8%–22% range to that review; the present study addresses this by comparing lenient (25th) and stringent (5th) percentile criteria.

"examined the influence of variable identification criteria for children with SCD and noted that different criteria identified anywhere from 0.8% to 22% of children."
