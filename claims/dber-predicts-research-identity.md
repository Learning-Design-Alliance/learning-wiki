# Assistant PoTs identify significantly more as researchers than tenured PoTs, with similar instructor identity, and DBER engagement predicts research identity

## Evidence

### Paine A. R. 2025

Paine A. R., Wilton M., Solanki S. M., Ellefson M., Ferguson J. E., Lo S. M., Sato B. K. (2025). Reimagining the role of teaching-focused faculty in research-intensive universities: The evolution of scholarly expectations and departmental influence. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0334895

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

7-point identity-overlap Likert items (Table 4): researcher alignment means 4.00 (±1.65) Assistant vs 3.28 (±1.59) tenured; instructor alignment nearly identical (5.84 vs 5.83). Only p-value printed, no effect size.

> "when asked about their identity as researchers, Assistant PoTs identify significantly more as researchers than their more senior colleagues (p = 0.006)."

### Paine A. R. 2025 (2)

Paine A. R., Wilton M., Solanki S. M., Ellefson M., Ferguson J. E., Lo S. M., Sato B. K. (2025). Reimagining the role of teaching-focused faculty in research-intensive universities: The evolution of scholarly expectations and departmental influence. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0334895

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple regression on research identity with scholarly-activity and demographic predictors (S2 Table); a Type III ANOVA confirmed "DBER contributed significant unique variance to the model (F(1,122) = 7.57, p = 0.007)".

> "Engaging in DBER was a significant predictor of research identity (β = 0.88, p = 0.01), but engagement in other scholarly activities did not predict their research identity"
