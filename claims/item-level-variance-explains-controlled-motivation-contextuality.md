# Item-level shared variance, not contextual variance, explains why controlled motivation appears less situational

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Contextual variance did not increase continuously along the motivational continuum; item-level shared variance was 10–16% for autonomous but 22–50% for controlled motivation. [→ Chanal J 2020](#chanal-j-2020)

## Evidence

### Chanal J 2020

Chanal J, Paumier D. (2020). The school-subject-specificity hypothesis: Implication in the relationship with grades. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0230103

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The new CTCM-1 modeling disentangled contextual and item-level shared variance for the first time; the article reports that "autonomous motivation shares only between 10 to 16% of variance at the item level" while controlled motivation fluctuates between 22 and 50%.

> "Indeed, if autonomous motivation shares only between 10 to 16% of variance at the item level, for controlled motivation, percentage variance at the item level fluctuates between 22 to 50%."
