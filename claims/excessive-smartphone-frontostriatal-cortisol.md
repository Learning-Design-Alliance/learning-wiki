# Excessive smartphone use in adolescents is associated with reduced frontostriatal connectivity and greater cortisol-related withdrawal symptoms

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Excessive smartphone users had diminished functional connectivity between the right OFC and NAcc and between the left OFC and the MCC, and withdrawal symptoms were related to greater cortisol concentrations. [→ Marciano Laura 2021](#marciano-laura-2021)

## Evidence

### Marciano Laura 2021

Marciano Laura, Camerini Anne-Linda, Morese Rosalba. (2021). The Developing Brain in the Digital Era: A Scoping Review of Structural and Functional Correlates of Screen Time in Adolescence. Frontiers in Psychology. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.671817

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

fMRI and cortisol study of 38 15-year-old participants with excessive smartphone use or Internet addiction and an equal number of controls; the review reports damaged PFC-ventral striatum connectivity associated with "impaired top-down executive control ability." No effect size printed.

> "excessive smartphone users had diminished functional connectivity between the right OFC and NAcc and between the left OFC and the medial cingulate gyrus (MCC), which are regions related to cognitive control in the PFC and ventral striatum."
