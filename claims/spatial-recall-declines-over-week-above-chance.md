## Evidence

### Nathan W. Whitmore 2024

Nathan W. Whitmore, Erika M. Yamazaki & Ken A. Paller. (2024). Targeted memory reactivation with sleep disruption does not weaken week-old memories. npj Science of Learning 9, 64. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00276-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

One-way repeated-measures ANOVA over the three tests in the main experiment (n = 18 analyzed); post-hoc corrected comparisons showed significant decline from the initial test, and pre-nap versus post-nap error did not differ (p = 0.53).

> "Comparing the initial, pre-nap, and post-nap memory tests revealed a signi ﬁcant test effect [ F(2,51) = 25.8, p < 0.001]."

### Nathan W. Whitmore 2024 (2)

Nathan W. Whitmore, Erika M. Yamazaki & Ken A. Paller. (2024). Targeted memory reactivation with sleep disruption does not weaken week-old memories. npj Science of Learning 9, 64. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00276-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of observed pre-nap error against 1000 simulated participants placing objects at uniformly random locations; participants retained knowledge of object locations after the 7-day delay.

> "recall error on the pre-nap test was signi ﬁcantly less than that expected from uniform random placement [uniform random mean error ± SEM = 19.4 cm ± 1.1, t(1017) = 29.1, p < 0.001"
