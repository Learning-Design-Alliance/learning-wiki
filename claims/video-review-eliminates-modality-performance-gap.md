# Video review enhances practical skill performance in both modalities and eliminates the hands-on versus virtual simulation difference

## Evidence

### Zhanyong Qi 2026

Zhanyong Qi, Jiayi Wang, Yue Du, Xinyue Zhang. (2026). The effect of blended learning approaches on vocational school students' practical skills performance and learning behaviors: virtual simulations or hands-on activities while accessing instructional videos. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09793-4

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA of the 2x2 experiment (n = 130, pre-test covariate) on post-test skill performance: video review main effect F(1,125) = 17.1, ηp2 = 0.120; operation type ηp2 = 0.070; interaction ηp2 = 0.047, all significant.

> "ANCOV A revealed significant main effects on both types of operation ( F [1, 125] = 9.36, p = 0.003, ηp2 = 0.070) and video reviewing (F [1, 125] = 17.1, p < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.120) on learning performance, along with a significant interaction effect (F [1, 125] = 6.18, p = 0.014, ηp2 = 0.047)."

### Zhanyong Qi 2026 (2)

Zhanyong Qi, Jiayi Wang, Yue Du, Xinyue Zhang. (2026). The effect of blended learning approaches on vocational school students' practical skills performance and learning behaviors: virtual simulations or hands-on activities while accessing instructional videos. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09793-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Follow-up pairwise analysis of the same ANCOVA: the modality difference was significant only "in the absence of video review"; the article reports no significant difference when video reviewing was provided, and equivalence was not tested.

> "Additional analysis indicated that, in the absence of video review, the hands-on activity group demonstrated superior performance in detecting circuit faults compared to the virtual simulation group (MD = 2.42, P < 0.001; see Fig. 6)."
