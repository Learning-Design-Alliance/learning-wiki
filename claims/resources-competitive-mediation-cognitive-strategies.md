# The linear effect of task difficulty on cognitive strategy use is competitively mediated by learner resources: difficulty decreases resources, which lowers strategy use, while a positive direct effect remains

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Available resources mediated a negative indirect linear effect of task difficulty on cognitive strategy use, competing with a positive direct effect (competitive mediation). [→ Seufert 2024](#seufert-2024)

## Evidence

### Seufert 2024

Seufert, T., Hamm, V., Vogt, A., & Riemer, V. (2024). The Interplay of Cognitive Load, Learners' Resources and Self-Regulation. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09890-1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Mediation analysis (Sobel test) in the hierarchical models of the 67-student within-subject study: higher difficulty reduced the aggregated resource scale (prior knowledge, interest, self-efficacy, etc.), which lowered cognitive strategy use; the article names this a competitive mediation.

> "we revealed a significant indirect linear effect of perceived task difficulty on cognitive strategy use, mediated by available resources (a × b = -0.18, z =—4.90, SE = 0.04, p < 0.001; see Table 3). With increasing perceived task difficulty, fewer resources were available, which resulted in lower cognitive strategy use."
