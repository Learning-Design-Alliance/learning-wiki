## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Workshop participants showed a large, statistically significant greater reduction in PABS-PT biomedical subscale scores than iBeST participants (MD −7.43, 95% CI −10.97, −3.89; effect size −1.62). [→ Richmond Helen 2016](#richmond-helen-2016)

## Evidence

### Richmond Helen 2016

Richmond Helen, Hall Amanda M., Hansen Zara, Williamson Esther, Davies David, Lamb Sarah E. (2016). Using mixed methods evaluation to assess the feasibility of online clinical training in evidence based interventions: a case study of cognitive behavioural treatment for low back pain. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0683-4

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA-adjusted change in PABS-PT biomedical subscale (baseline-adjusted) in the exploratory RCT (n=16 workshop, n=14 iBeST with data); "large and statistically significant between group difference ... in favour of the workshop", printed effect size −1.62 (95% CI −2.46, −0.78).

> "There was a large and statistically significant between group difference observed in the PABS-PT biomedical subscale in favour of the workshop (MD −7.43, 95 % CI −10.97, −3.89), indicating that workshop participants held less of an orientation towards a biomedical treatment approach after training."

### Richmond Helen 2016 (2)

Richmond Helen, Hall Amanda M., Hansen Zara, Williamson Esther, Davies David, Lamb Sarah E. (2016). Using mixed methods evaluation to assess the feasibility of online clinical training in evidence based interventions: a case study of cognitive behavioural treatment for low back pain. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0683-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same RCT, PABS-PT psychosocial subscale change: "a small between group difference in favour of the workshop" that "was not statistically significant" (MD 3.35, 95% CI −0.19, 6.89; printed effect size −0.68).

> "We observed a small between group difference in favour of the workshop on the PABS-PT psychosocial subscale, suggesting a greater psychosocial orientation after training, although this difference was not statistically significant (MD: 3.35, 95 % CI: −0.19, 6.89)."
