# Staying motivated was the most pervasive challenge of learning remotely, and students missed instructor feedback, peer collaboration, and hands-on experiences

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Seventy-nine percent of students cited staying motivated as a problem (42 percent major, 37 percent minor); other common challenges were finding a quiet place (55 percent), home/family responsibilities (54 percent), and not knowing where to get help (54 percent). [→ Means 2020](#means-2020)
`q3 i?` Open-ended responses most often cited self-motivation, lost immediate instructor feedback and peer discussion, and lost hands-on experiences. [→ Means 2020 (2)](#means-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Means 2020

Means, B., and Neisler, J., with Langer Research Associates. (2020). Suddenly Online: A National Survey of Undergraduates During the COVID-19 Pandemic. San Mateo, CA: Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/32aee7c4-5363-4d50-bc9c-a338e7dfac7e

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Structured-response items asked students to rate a set of specific problems with continuing their courses online. Feeling too unwell was cited by 45 percent and fitting the course around work by 31 percent.

> "the most pervasive problem reported by students was staying motivated to do well in the course after it went online: 79 percent of students cited this as a problem (42 percent said it was a major problem, and another 37 percent said it was a minor problem)"

### Means 2020 (2)

Means, B., and Neisler, J., with Langer Research Associates. (2020). Suddenly Online: A National Survey of Undergraduates During the COVID-19 Pandemic. San Mateo, CA: Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/32aee7c4-5363-4d50-bc9c-a338e7dfac7e

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Thematic analysis of open-ended greatest-challenge responses found motivation, missed instructor and peer presence (immediate feedback, discussing content), and loss of authentic hands-on experiences such as labs as recurring themes.

> "Many responses to this open-ended question focused on staying motivated when they did not have the structure of regular class routines and were subject to many kinds of distractions."
