## Evidence

### Banerjee Snigdha 2015

Banerjee Snigdha, Frey Hans-Peter, Molholm Sophie, Foxe John J. (2015). Interests shape how adolescents pay attention: The interaction of motivation and top-down attentional processes in biasing sensory activations to anticipated events. The European Journal of Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1111/ejn.12810

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Behavioral ANOVA in the cued spatial attention task of 24 male adolescents (mean age 15.07). The authors report these findings were "driven by higher d-prime scores for high versus low interest stimuli, and higher d-prime scores in the right hemifield." No effect size printed.

> "For d-prime, there was a significant main effect of motivation (F(1,23) = 12.27, p = .002), a significant main effect of stimulus location (F(1,23) = 14.26, p = .001), and a significant motivation by stimulus location interaction (F(1,23) = 17.63, p < .001)."
