# Literacy demands and institutional support correlate with perceived negative consequences of dyslexia but do not uniquely contribute to them

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Literacy demands correlated with all three perceived consequence types but did not uniquely contribute to any; better support of the institution correlated negatively but did not independently contribute. [→ Bazen 2022](#bazen-2022)

## Evidence

### Bazen 2022

Bazen, Loes L.; de Bree, Elise H.; van den Boer, Madelon; de Jong, Peter F. (2022). Perceived negative consequences of dyslexia: the influence of person and environmental factors. Annals of Dyslexia. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11881-022-00274-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Regression analysis in the 123-student sample: literacy demands betas were .169 (academic, p=.056), .106 (anxiety, p=.216), .163 (depression, p=.034, above the .017 threshold); support of institution betas were -.206 (academic, p=.020), -.068 (anxiety, p=.425), -.145 (depression, p=.085). "literacy demands did not uniquely contribute to any type of perceived consequences."

> "As for the environmental factors, literacy demands did not uniquely contribute to any type of perceived consequences, despite its correlation with all three types of perceived consequences."
