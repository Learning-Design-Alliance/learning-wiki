# On a novel laparoscopic task, participants tested in 3D complete it significantly faster than those tested in 2D, regardless of training mode

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Groups tested in 3D took significantly less time on the novel Navigating in Space task than groups tested in 2D (η2 = 0.56); all other NIS comparisons were non-significant. [→ Beattie 2021](#beattie-2021)

## Evidence

### Beattie 2021

Beattie, K. L., Hill, A., Horswill, M. S., Grove, P. M., & Stevenson, A. R. L. (2021). Laparoscopic skills training: the effects of viewing mode (2D vs. 3D) on skill acquisition and transfer. Surgical Endoscopy, 35(8), 4332–4344. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-020-07923-8

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Testing-phase analysis of the novel Navigating in Space task (two timed test attempts, 10-min limit each). Main effect of group significant (F = 23.44, p < 0.001, η2 = 0.56), a large effect; "different training, same testing" comparisons non-significant (p = 0.623, 0.610).

> "Specifically, those tested in 3D (the 3D → 3D and 2D → 3D groups) took significantly less time to complete the novel task than those tested in 2D (the 2D → 2D and 3D → 2D groups). All other NIS comparisons were non-significant"
