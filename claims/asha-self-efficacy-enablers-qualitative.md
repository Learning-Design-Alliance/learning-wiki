## Evidence

### Sarin Enisha 2017

Sarin Enisha, Lunsford Sarah Smith. (2017). How female community health workers navigate work challenges and why there are still gaps in their performance: a look at female community health workers in maternal and child health in two Indian districts through a reciprocal determinism framework. Human Resources for Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12960-017-0222-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative semi-structured interviews with 49 ASHAs and 49 family members in Mewat and Gurdaspur districts. The Results section reports that work-related resources "increased an ASHA's self-efficacy to successfully perform her duties", with ASHA narratives citing health knowledge, confidence, and family support with household chores.

> "By joining the workforce, ASHAs began accruing resources including financial incentives, technical knowledge, and social capital such as respect and support from family and community. These, in turn, increased an ASHA's self-efficacy to successfully perform her duties."
