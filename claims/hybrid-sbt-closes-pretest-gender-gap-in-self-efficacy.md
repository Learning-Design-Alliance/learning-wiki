## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Male students scored significantly higher than females on pretest SE (rank-biserial rpb = 0.3492), but posttest SE did not differ significantly by gender. [→ Tam 2025](#tam-2025)

## Evidence

### Tam 2025

Tam, C. Y. W., Dizon, J. I. W. T., Sin, S. W. C., Tang, G. K. Y., Lai, V. Y. W., Wong, Y. Y., Ganotice, F. A., & Yeung, N. P. (2025). Implementation of hybrid simulation training to promote collective and self-efficacy in medical students: a two-pronged approach. Annals of Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1080/07853890.2025.2527362

`q3 · i2` · `design · r2`

Mann-Whitney U subgroup analysis of SE by sex (n = 91 after dropping two 'prefer not to say' participants); pretest males 25.5 (24–32) vs females 24 (20–25), p < 0.001. The article reports the printed effect size "rpb = 0.3492, t(89)= 3.5157, p < 0.001". Posttest medians did not differ (p = 0.385).

> "To determine the practical significance of the relationship of gender differences and pretest SE scores, rank biserial correlation was employed to evaluate the effect size, revealing a positive correlation between higher pre-SE scores and male participants (rpb = 0.3492, t(89)= 3.5157, p < 0.001)"
