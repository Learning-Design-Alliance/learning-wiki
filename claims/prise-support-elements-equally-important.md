# All PRiSE support elements are rated positively and appear almost equally important

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2021 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students (n = 68) and teachers (n = 23) rated the usefulness of each support element above neutral, with overall means of 2.62 ± 0.04 (teachers) and 2.23 ± 0.03 (students), and no element differed significantly from its group's overall rating, suggesting all elements are almost equally important. [→ Martin O. Archer 2021](#martin-o-archer-2021)

## Evidence

### Martin O. Archer 2021

Martin O. Archer, Jennifer DeWitt, Charlotte Thorley, and Olivia Keenan. (2021). Evaluating participants' experience of extended interaction with cutting-edge physics research through the PRiSE 'research in schools' programme. [doi:10.5194/gc-4-147-2021](https://doi.org/10.5194/gc-4-147-2021)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Ratings of the framework's support elements (assignment, kick-off, visits, webinars, ad hoc support, comments, conference) on an unimportant/helpful/essential scale. Overall "means were 2.62 ± 0.04 for teachers and 2.23 ± 0.03 for students"; each element's mean exceeded 2 to high confidence (p < 0.002 teachers; p < 0.023 students, except kick-off at 2.15 ± 0.08).

> "coding the responses to values of 1 (negative) to 3 (essential) the overall means were 2.62 ± 0.04 for teachers and 2.23 ± 0.03 for students"

### Martin O. Archer 2021 (2)

Martin O. Archer, Jennifer DeWitt, Charlotte Thorley, and Olivia Keenan. (2021). Evaluating participants' experience of extended interaction with cutting-edge physics research through the PRiSE 'research in schools' programme. [doi:10.5194/gc-4-147-2021](https://doi.org/10.5194/gc-4-147-2021)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Because between-element scoring differences were slight and not statistically significant — and no students and only 3 teachers gave the same answer in every category — the authors conclude the elements are "almost equally important and necessary"; the intensive support is deemed necessary (Abstract).

> "This therefore suggests that all of the elements of support provided as part of PRiSE are almost equally important and necessary."
