## Subclaims

`q3 i1` Performance goals correlate positively with achievement when items emphasize normative comparisons (r = +.14) and negatively when they emphasize competence demonstration (r = -.14). [→ Senko 2011](#senko-2011)

`q3 i1` Among performance-approach goal scales, a majority of normatively referenced items yields a positive correlation with performance outcomes (r = .14), whereas a majority of appearance and evaluative items yields a negative relationship (r = -.14). [→ Hulleman 2010](#hulleman-2010)

## Evidence

### Senko 2011

Senko, C., Hulleman, C. S., & Harackiewicz, J. M. (2011). Achievement Goal Theory at the Crossroads: Old Controversies, Current Challenges, and New Directions. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2011.538646

`q3 · i1` · `review · r3`

The review describes Hulleman et al.'s (2010) coding of 98 studies of performance goal measures, finding "r = +.14" for normative items and "r = -.14" for competence-demonstration items, both p < .01.

> "The average correlation between performance goals and academic achievement was positive when the majority of performance goal items emphasized normative comparisons (r = +.14, p \< .01) but negative when they emphasized competence demonstration (r = -.14, p \< .01)."

### Hulleman 2010

Hulleman, C. S., Schrager, S. M., Bodmann, S. M., & Harackiewicz, J. M. (2010). A Meta-Analytic Review of Achievement Goal Measures: Different Labels for the Same Constructs or Different Constructs With Similar Labels? Psychological Bulletin. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018947

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Meta-analysis of 243 correlational studies of self-reported achievement goals (91,087 participants), moderator analysis of item content within performance-approach scales. Scales with normative items correlated with performance at r = .14; appearance/evaluative scales showed "a negative relationship" at r = -.14.

> "performance-approach goal scales coded as having a majority of normatively referenced items had a positive correlation with performance outcomes (r ⫽ .14), whereas scales with a majority of appearance and evaluative items had a negative relationship (r ⫽ ⫺.14)"
