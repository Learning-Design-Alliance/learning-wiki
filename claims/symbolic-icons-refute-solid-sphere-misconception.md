# Particle Dobble's arbitrary, non-literal symbols are designed to dispel the Newtonian misconception that elementary particles are solid spheres

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Replacing literal particle images with arbitrary, whimsical icons acts as a refutational prompt against the conception of particles as tiny solid objects, without requiring formal quantum mechanics. [→ Mientus 2026](#mientus-2026)

## Evidence

### Mientus 2026

Mientus, L., Ruechel, A., Kalke, K., & Borowski, A. (2026). Particle Dobble – Exploring the Particle Zoo. The Physics Teacher (accepted manuscript). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2602.23955](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.23955)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

The authors' design rationale for the game: arbitrary icon-to-particle mappings act as what they call a visual constructivism refutational prompt, so that "a particle cannot be a tiny object with everyday attributes in the Newtonian sense". No empirical evaluation of the game is reported in the article.

> "Each icon-to-particle mapping is arbitrary by design. Students see that an electron or a gluon can be “this symbol” only because we agree it is and not because particles have fixed shapes."
