# Reading proficiency level moderates incidental vocabulary gains: higher-proficiency readers gained more, consistent with i+1

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Intermediate, upper-intermediate and advanced EFL readers of 'The Golden Fleece' showed significant lexical gains on post-test, with gains proportionate to comprehensibility of the text and current vocabulary knowledge (i+1). [→ Ponniah 2011](#ponniah-2011)

## Evidence

### Ponniah 2011

Ponniah, R. J. (2011). The Effectiveness of the Comprehension Hypothesis: A Review on the Current Research on Incidental Vocabulary Acquisition. i-manager's Journal on English Language Teaching, 1(2), April-June 2011. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1071042

`q2 · i?` · `review · r1`

Review reports Tekmen and Daloglu (2006): intermediate, upper-intermediate and advanced students read 'The Golden Fleece' (50 min, audio first reading, silent rereading, no dictionary); 30 target words tested at pre, post, and delayed post-test one week later. Gains: intermediate 3.12, upper-intermediate 4.00, advanced 5.34.

> "Analysis of the data revealed that there was significant lexical gains on the raw scores for the subjects after the post test. The results of the delayed posttest showed that the subjects were able to retain the incidentally acquired words without much attrition."
