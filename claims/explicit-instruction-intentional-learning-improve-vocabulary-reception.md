## Evidence

### Vincy 2018

Vincy, R. I. (2018). The impact of explicit instruction and intentional learning in the reception of vocabulary: An experimental study. *i-manager's Journal on English Language Teaching, 8*(2). [doi:10.26634/jelt.8.2.14155](https://doi.org/10.26634/jelt.8.2.14155)

`q3 · two-group experiment, groups formed by random selection` · `i?` · `n=30` · `causal · r2`

Two-group experiment: 30 fifth-grade learners at one Tamil Nadu government school were divided "by random sampling" into two groups of 15; "Fifteen students were randomly selected among the total 30 students for the control group" and the remaining 15 formed the experimental group. The control group was taught the Unit 2 lesson by an English teacher unaware of the study; the experimental group was taught the same lesson by the researcher with explicit vocabulary instruction in an intentional learning environment. The paper does not describe how the control teacher handled vocabulary (its "partial embedded" label describes the class teacher's Unit 1 lesson observed the day before), and teacher and condition are not separated. Receptive knowledge was a self-report Vocabulary Knowledge Scale taken straight after the lesson, analysed with an independent two-sample t-test in Excel (alpha 0.05): the experimental group "outperformed the control group" (means 28.4 vs 14.133, SD 7.872 vs 6.895; t = 5.279, df = 28, p two-tail 0.00001; 95% CI of the difference 8.73 to 19.80).

> "The mean of the VKS test scores of the participants of the control group was 14.133 and that of the experimental group was 28.4. The mean of the experimental group when compared with the mean of the control group again indicated the improvement in the reception of the target vocabulary."
