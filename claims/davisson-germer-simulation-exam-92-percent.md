## Subclaims

## Evidence

### S. B. McKagan 2008

S. B. McKagan, K. K. Perkins, M. Dubson, C. Malley, S. Reid, R. LeMaster, and C. E. Wieman. (2008). Developing and Researching PhET simulations for Teaching Quantum Mechanics. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.4503 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0709.4503](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0709.4503)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Reading-quiz study in a modern physics course for physics majors (N=59, all students; N=38 who reported doing the reading). Students answered before instruction; the quote reports the printed distribution of response types from Fig. 7a. No effect size is printed.

> "As shown in Fig. 7a, only about a third of students gave this type of answer. Another third said that electrons are waves but did not explain this result in terms of interference, as in the second sample response."

### S. B. McKagan 2008 (2)

S. B. McKagan, K. K. Perkins, M. Dubson, C. Malley, S. Reid, R. LeMaster, and C. E. Wieman. (2008). Developing and Researching PhET simulations for Teaching Quantum Mechanics. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.4503 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0709.4503](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0709.4503)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Midterm exam result in the same physics-majors course after a reading quiz, an interactive lecture using the Davisson Germer: Electron Diffraction simulation in one section, and simulation-based homework. The quote reports the printed 92% figure; no effect size or test statistic is printed.

> "On the exam question, 92% of students correctly explained that there was an interference pattern that illustrated the wave nature of electrons."
