## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The writing post-test difference between the experimental group (backward design) and control group (forward design) was not statistically significant after controlling for pre-test scores. [→ Hosseini 2019](#hosseini-2019)

## Evidence

### Hosseini 2019

Hosseini, H., Chalak, A., & Biria, R. (2019). Impact of Backward Design on Improving Iranian Advanced Learners’ Writing Ability: Teachers’ Practices and Beliefs. International Journal of Instruction, 12(2), 33-50. https://doi.org/10.29333/iji.2019.1223a

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANCOVA comparing writing post-test scores of the experimental group (M = 5.09) and control group (M = 4.99), each n = 50, controlling for pre-test scores. The Groups effect was not significant (p = .10, partial eta squared = .02); the article reports "not of statistical significance".

> "The p value corresponding to th e Groups row of the above table equalled .10, which is greater than the alpha level of significance (i.e., .10 > .05). This indicates that the difference between the writing post-test mean scores of the two groups of EG (M = 5.09) and CG ( M = 4.99) was not  of statistical significance."
