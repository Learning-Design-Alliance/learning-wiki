# Problems whose per cents solved do not increase monotonically across grades are eliminated as not measuring grade-to-grade progress

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Problems showing a constant grade-to-grade increase in per cents solving them are best for scaling, while erratic problems are regarded with suspicion and were eliminated from earlier editions of the test. [→ Herring 1919](#herring-1919)

## Evidence

### Herring 1919

Herring, J. P. (1919). Derivation of a scale to measure abilities in scientific thinking. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://psycnet.apa.org/journal/edu

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Methodological criterion stated in the section on locating each problem relative to grade medians: problems behaving erratically across grades were discovered and eliminated in earlier editions of the Scientific Method Test. No effect size is printed.

> "if the sixth grade scores lower upon a given problem than the fifth, that problem does not measure progress from fifth to sixth grade ability."
