## Evidence

### Çevikbaş Pınar 2026

Çevikbaş Pınar, Açıksarı Kurtuluş, Karahaliloğlu Cihad Ünsal. (2026). PHTLS training increases knowledge but paradoxically decreases self-efficacy in emergency healthcare professionals: a cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09149-z

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Subgroup analysis by profession and training status (Table 3) in the cross-sectional study; paramedic stratum n = 32. The authors describe this as "compatible with descriptions of the Dunning-Kruger effect". Nurses/EMT stratum was underpowered (small sample size limits statistical power).

> "Non-trained paramedics had moderate knowledge scores (54.8 ± 12.1) with high self-confidence (66.7%). However, trained paramedics achieved the highest knowledge scores of all groups (72.5 ± 15.8) while reporting the lowest self-confidence (17.6%)."
