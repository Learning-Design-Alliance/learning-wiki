# Students identify ten design principles for Virtual Patients that foster learning of clinical reasoning, and perceive well-designed VPs as preparing them better than any previously experienced method

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Five student focus groups revealed ten principles of VP design, including relevance, appropriate difficulty, interactivity, specific feedback, appropriate media, focus on and recapitulation of relevant learning points, authenticity, and questions and explanations enhancing clinical reasoning. [→ Huwendiek S 2019](#huwendiek-s-2019)
`q2 i?` After exposure to well-designed VPs, students felt very well prepared for reasoning in real patients and better prepared than with any other previously experienced method. [→ Huwendiek S 2019](#huwendiek-s-2019)

## Evidence

### Huwendiek S 2019

Huwendiek S. (2019). Design and implementation of virtual patients for learning of clinical reasoning. GMS Journal for Medical Education 36(4):Doc33. [doi:10.3205/zma001241](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001241)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Focus-group study with fifth-year clerkship students (n=104) exposed to at least eight VPs varying in design. The article reports "Five student focus groups revealed 10 principles of VP design" and that students perceived these principles as conducive to their learning.

> "Five student focus groups revealed 10 principles of VP design. To facilitate learning, Virtual Patients should be relevant, encompass an appropriate level of difficulty, be interactive, provide specific feedback, utilize different, appropriate media"
