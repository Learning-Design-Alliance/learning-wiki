Evidence¶

Arthur I. Gates 1920¶

Arthur I. Gates, Teachers College, Columbia University. (1920). The True-False Test as a Measure of Achievement in College Courses. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=true-false+test+as+a+measure+of+achievement+in+college+courses+gates

q2 · i3 · associational · r1

Correlational analysis against a criterion weighting essays 1.0, true-false tests 1.0, written work 0.5, and recitations/conferences 0.5. The printed correlations (effect sizes) are r = .65 for true-false with criterion (Table IX, N = 39) versus r = .56 for essays (Table VII, N = 17), difference .09 ± .052.

"That the average correlation + 0.65 of True-False with criterion is greater than + 0.56 of essay with criterion is evidenced by the a (tr-obt. avs.). The difference is .09 ± a (tr.-obt. Diff.) .052."
