# After adjusting for selection bias with beginning-of-term GPA, regular PR users show a grade point improvement of 0.22, significant above the random noise level of 0.04, with replication at 0.32 in Winter 2013.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2013 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Partitioned into beginning-of-term GPA bins, regular users are offset about 0.22 grade points above light/non-users at all ability levels, roughly a 3σ offset in all but the lowest GPA bin. [→ Evrard 2013](#evrard-2013)
`q2 i?` A randomization null test (10,000 realizations of 229 students drawn from 518) gives mean 2.88 and dispersion 0.04; the regular users' mean of 3.10 is displaced 5σ upward. A GPA-matching analysis reproduces a 0.22 grade point boost, and the effect was 0.32 grade points in Winter term 2013. [→ Evrard 2013 (2)](#evrard-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Evrard 2013

Evrard, A. E., Mills, M., Winn, D., Jones, K., Tritz, J., and McKay, T. A. (2013). Problem Roulette: Studying Introductory Physics in the Cloud. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1309.7678](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1309.7678)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

GPA-bin comparison of Fall 2012 Phys 240 final grades (Fig. 8), regular users versus complement, points plotted at mean GPA and mean grade with linear fits. The article prints a 0.22 grade point offset and ~3σ language, no standardized effect size.

> "Students of all abilities beneﬁt from regular PR usage. The mean oﬀset of the linear ﬁts is 0.22 grade points, a boost of approximately a 3σ in all but the lowest GPA bin for which statistics are poor."

### Evrard 2013 (2)

Evrard, A. E., Mills, M., Winn, D., Jones, K., Tritz, J., and McKay, T. A. (2013). Problem Roulette: Studying Introductory Physics in the Cloud. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1309.7678](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1309.7678)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Randomization null test drawing regular-user-sized cohorts from the 518 graded students. The article also reports a Winter 2013 replication effect of 0.32 grade points with noise 0.06 (a 5σ effect) and a GPA-matching analysis consistent at 0.22.

> "Generating 10,000 random realizations, we ﬁnd a mean grade point of 2 .88 and dispersion of 0 .04. The mean grade point of the regular PR users is 3 .10, displaced 5 σ upward from the random mean."
