## Evidence

### Goff Eric E. 2017

Goff Eric E., Reindl Katie M., Johnson Christina, McClean Phillip, Offerdahl Erika G., Schroeder Noah L., White Alan R. (2017). Efficacy of a Meiosis Learning Module Developed for the Virtual Cell Animation Collection. CBE—Life Sciences Education 16(1):ar9. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-03-0141

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Multiple linear regression on normalized gain scores (model R2 = 0.20, F = 33.6) showed "a significant contribution of treatment condition (t(412) = 3.28, p = 0.001, d = 0.32)"; the parsimonious model retained pretest score and SAT total score.

> "Regression analysis again shows a significant contribution of treatment condition (t(412) = 3.28, p = 0.001, d = 0.32) to student achievement (Table 1)."

### Goff Eric E. 2017 (2)

Goff Eric E., Reindl Katie M., Johnson Christina, McClean Phillip, Offerdahl Erika G., Schroeder Noah L., White Alan R. (2017). Efficacy of a Meiosis Learning Module Developed for the Virtual Cell Animation Collection. CBE—Life Sciences Education 16(1):ar9. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-03-0141

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the initial model, "no significant explanatory effects from the student demographic variables of multimedia preference, gender, year in school, or ethnicity" were found; these low-correlation variables (r = 0.03, 0.002, 0.03, 0.02) were removed in the parsimonious model.

> "Linear regression modeling resulted in no significant explanatory effects from the student demographic variables of multimedia preference, gender, year in school, or ethnicity."
