Christina Luke 2016¶

Christina Luke, Christa M. Simone, Aubrey T. Francisco. (2016). Rapid Cycle Pilots: Summer Ed-Tech Programs. Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/8017846c-4099-4af3-92e4-462cd5d6262d doi:10.51388/20.500.12265/26

q1 · i? · design · r2

Authors' interpretive conclusion drawn from usage data across the three pilots; it is their stated interpretation rather than a tested result, since the pilots did not manipulate implementation format.

"The limited time students spent using their assigned ed-tech tools suggests that the program’s implementation, and not necessarily the tools themselves, led to largely stagnant results for student learning."

Related Claims¶

  • U.S. technology spending per learner is far lower in adult education (about $50) than in K-12 (about $145), signaling stagnant ed-tech innovation in adult learning — related
  • US federal adult education funding structures (AEFLA and Title I) are fragmented and lack purchasing leverage, making scale difficult for ed-tech entrepreneurs — related
  • A matched-comparison analysis found no effect of the TenMarks summer program on NWEA MAP math scores — related
  • Students used their assigned ed-tech tools far below recommended levels, and usage declined over the summer — related
  • Adult education programs implement technology along a spectrum of blended learning models, often multiple models within one program — related
  • When refined prompts failed, novices overgeneralized and concluded LLMs could not detect anaphors, attributing failure to the model rather than to their prompting — related
  • Educator involvement in choosing a pilot tool is associated with higher engagement and implementation fidelity, while lack of autonomy contributed to low usage and negative feelings — related
