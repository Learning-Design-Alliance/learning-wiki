## Evidence

### Gomez ED 2024

Gomez ED, Husin HM, Dumon KR, Williams NN, Kuchenbecker KJ. (2024). Simulation training with haptic feedback of instrument vibrations reduces resident workload during live robot-assisted sleeve gastrectomy. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-024-11459-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Mixed-model ANCOVA on 60 simulated RAGB/RASG cases (Haptic = 30, Control = 30) from 12 residents, adjusted for PGY level and sleep, found "a significant interaction between feedback group and procedure number" and a significant main effect of feedback group.

> "We found a significant interaction between feedback group and procedure number (F(4) = 3.389, p = 0.01) and a significant main effect of feedback group (F(1) = 6.201, p = 0.042)."

### Gomez ED 2024 (2)

Gomez ED, Husin HM, Dumon KR, Williams NN, Kuchenbecker KJ. (2024). Simulation training with haptic feedback of instrument vibrations reduces resident workload during live robot-assisted sleeve gastrectomy. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-024-11459-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Bonferroni-Holm-corrected pairwise comparisons from the same mixed ANCOVA showed the haptic group lower than controls only at procedures 4 and 5; no significant difference was found in the first three procedures (p > 0.4).

> "The haptic group showed a significantly lower NASA-TLXoverall than the control group in both the fourth and fifth procedures (Procedure 4: p(Holm) = 0.030, Procedure 5: p(Holm) = 0.040, Table 4, Fig. 4a)."
