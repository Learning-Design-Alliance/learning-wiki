`q2 i?` One interviewed student gave three separate ways of thinking about interacting wavepulses (bouncing, peak-point addition, and correct superposition for a limited case), switching between them when prompted. [→ Michael C. Wittmann 2002](#michael-c-wittmann-2002)

## Evidence

### Michael C. Wittmann 2002

Michael C. Wittmann. (2002). The object coordination class applied to wavepulses: Analysing student reasoning in wave physics. International Journal of Science Education 24:1, 97-118. [doi:10.1080/09500690110066944](https://doi.org/10.1080/09500690110066944)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Case evidence from one interview (David) covering the propagation, wave-math, and superposition questions. He first said wavepulses would bounce off each other, then sketched peak-only addition, and described superposition correctly only for overlapping amplitude peak points.

> "Where an expert physicist would think of these problems in only one way, David had three separate ways of thinking about the physics."
