## Evidence

### Brame CJ 2015

Brame CJ, Biel R. (2015). Test-Enhanced Learning: The Potential for Testing to Promote Greater Learning in Undergraduate Science Courses. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-11-0208

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Butler and Roediger (2008), a laboratory experiment in which undergraduates studied 12 historical passages, took multiple-choice tests with no, immediate, or delayed feedback, and returned 1 week later for a cued-recall test. Feedback added benefit beyond testing alone.

> "Interestingly, delayed feedback resulted in better final performance than did immediate feedback, although both conditions showed benefit over no feedback."

### Brame CJ 2015 (2)

Brame CJ, Biel R. (2015). Test-Enhanced Learning: The Potential for Testing to Promote Greater Learning in Undergraduate Science Courses. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-11-0208

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Butler et al. (2008), a laboratory study with confidence-rated general-knowledge multiple-choice items and a cued-recall test after 5 min. The benefit was "most pronounced for low-confidence correct answers."

> "While a testing effect was observed even in the absence of feedback, feedback strongly improved final performance, approximately doubling student performance over testing without feedback."
