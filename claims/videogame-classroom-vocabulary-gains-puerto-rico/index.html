Evidence¶

Vélez-Agosto 2018¶

Vélez-Agosto, N. M. & Rivas-Vélez, A. (2018). Benefits and meanings of educating with videogames in a Puerto Rican sixth grade public school classroom. International Journal of Educational Technology, 5(1), 9-19. http://educationaltechnology.net/ijet/

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Small-scale pre-post quasi-experiment with purposive sampling; EG students (n = 25, one missing post-test) played the game twenty minutes daily for four weeks. Paired-samples t-test showed a significant difference in words known, t(23) = 4.346, p = 0.000. The internal control group (n = 22; same school and same English teacher, no game) also grew significantly, t(16) = 4.877, p = 0.000, and the article describes "significant and extensive growth of words known for the EG and ICG", which the authors say could be explained by a Hawthorne effect (both groups shared the same English teacher). Groups were compared only within themselves, pre to post; no between-group test is reported.

"In the EG, there was a significant difference in words known at T1 (M = 1664; SD = 827.1) and words known at T2 (M = 2513; SD = 880.9); t(23) = 4.346, p = 0.000."

Vélez-Agosto, N. M. & Rivas-Vélez, A. (2018). Benefits and meanings of educating with videogames in a Puerto Rican sixth grade public school classroom. International Journal of Educational Technology, 5(1), 9-19. http://educationaltechnology.net/ijet/

q2 · i? · causal · r1

External control group (n = 30) in a different school without the tool was tested on the same Vocabulary Size Test at both time points; the paired-samples t-test was not significant, t(24) = 1.102, p = 0.281; equivalence was not tested.
