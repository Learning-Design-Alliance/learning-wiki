Kraft 2018¶

Kraft, M.A., Blazar, D., Hogan, D. (2018). The effect of teacher coaching on instruction and achievement: A meta-analysis of the causal evidence. Review of Educational Research, 88(4), 547-588. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654318759268

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Exploratory meta-analytic regression (Table 4) across the 60 causal studies; the authors stress these regressions are descriptive and do not capture the causal effect of a program feature.

"we find that pairing coaching with group trainings is associated with 0.31 SD larger effect size on instruction and 0.12 SD larger effect size on achievement."

Kraft 2018 (2)¶

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Same exploratory meta-analytic regression of design features on pooled effect sizes; coefficients are associations from between-study comparisons, not experimental contrasts.

"pairing coaching with instructional resources and materials (e.g., curriculum) also is associated with greater gains (0.21 SD larger), while providing teachers with a video library is associated with more limited benefits (-0.27 SD smaller)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

- Teacher coaching programs improve teachers' instructional practice by a pooled 0.49 SD across 43 causal studies — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it¶

  • A practitioner review reports a meta-analysis of causal coaching studies as finding positive effects of coaching on teachers' instructional practice and student achievement, though one contrasting study found coached teachers no more effective — related
  • Coaching program effects shrink substantially at scale: effectiveness trials of larger programs show only a fraction of efficacy-trial effects — related
  • No specific coaching dosage is established as necessary; coaching outcomes appear more sensitive to coaching quality than dosage, with sustained duration favoring comprehensive coaching — related
  • Coaching dosage shows precisely estimated null effects: more coaching hours are not associated with larger program effects — related
  • Effective skill training combines theory presentation, demonstration, practice with feedback, and coaching for transfer (review attribution) — related
  • Coaching time focused on instructional delivery improves coached teachers' practices, while time focused on behavioral management is associated with a significant decrease in practice quality — related
  • In grade 2, 1-1 with coaching shows the largest effect (LATE = 0.8 SD) and 1-group is also significant, while 1-group with coaching is insignificant — related
  • Training combining modeling and performance feedback is associated with larger implementation fidelity effects (β = .77, p = .04) — related
  • Training combining educational and practical elements improves more delivery-quality outcomes (52%) than education-only training (23%) — related
  • The EGRS RCT found that the structured pedagogy model combining on-site coaching, quality materials, and scripted lesson plans was the most effective of the tested models for improving early grade reading outcomes — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
