## Evidence

### Fonseca 2025

Fonseca, Braz, Vieira, de Souza Ferreira, Damian. (2025). Do CBCT and Clinical Experience Impact Decision‐Making in Endodontic Diagnosis and Treatment Planning? A Before‐After Study. Australian Endodontic Journal. https://doi.org/10.1111/aej.12966

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

In this single-center before-after study, five final-year dental students assessed 28 cases with periapical radiographs then CBCT 30 days later. The article reports "32.9% of diagnostic decisions" altered, significant by McNemar's test (p = 0.000), with OR = 11.98 (Table 1).

> "Among undergraduate students, 32.9% of diagnostic decisions and 36.4% of treatment plans were altered during the second assessment compared to the first. In both outcomes, the proportions of change were statistically significant (p = 0.000)."

### Fonseca 2025 (2)

Fonseca, Braz, Vieira, de Souza Ferreira, Damian. (2025). Do CBCT and Clinical Experience Impact Decision‐Making in Endodontic Diagnosis and Treatment Planning? A Before‐After Study. Australian Endodontic Journal. https://doi.org/10.1111/aej.12966

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Five experienced endodontists (minimum 5 years' experience) evaluated the same 28 cases in both stages. The article reports 45.7% of diagnostic decisions revised with "an odds ratio of approximately 17 (p = 0.000)"; Table 1 gives OR = 16.85.

> "Among professionals, the rate of change was even higher, with 45.7% of diagnostic decisions and 47.1% of treatment plans revised, and an odds ratio of approximately 17 (p = 0.000), reflecting a markedly increased likelihood of revising clinical decisions in light of the additional information provided by CBCT"
