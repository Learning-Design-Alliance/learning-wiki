# Rereading significantly improves metacomprehension monitoring accuracy relative to reading once (Experiment 1)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Experiment 1, relative monitoring accuracy was significantly better when participants reread each text than when they read each text once. [→ Griffin 2008](#griffin-2008)

## Evidence

### Griffin 2008

Griffin, T. D., Wiley, J., & Thiede, K. W. (2008). Individual differences, rereading, and self-explanation: Concurrent processing and cue validity as constraints on metacomprehension accuracy. Memory & Cognition, 36(1), 93-103. https://doi.org/10.3758/MC.36.1.93

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized experiment with 75 introductory psychology students (three dropped), reading five explanatory texts with inference verification tests. Relative accuracy was computed as Pearson correlations of judgments with test performances, regressed on condition, ability, and their interaction; the article reports "a main effect of rereading" with the quoted means.

> "There was a main effect of rereading, so that monitoring accuracy for the reread condition (M  .46, SEM  .06) was significantly better than that for the read-once condition (M  .22, SEM  .10)."
