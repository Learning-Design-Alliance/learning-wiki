# SAIL-10 discriminates between medical and pharmacy students and shows adequate internal consistency and stable reliability

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Total SAIL-10 scores differ significantly between medical and pharmacy students in the validation cohort, supporting discriminative validity. [→ Chua Siew Siang 2019](#chua-siew-siang-2019)

## Evidence

### Chua Siew Siang 2019

Chua Siew Siang, Lai Pauline Siew Mei, Sim Si Mui, Tan Choo Hock, Foong Chan Choong. (2019). Acceptance of interprofessional learning between medical and pharmacy students in a prescribing skills training workshop: pre-post intervention study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1525-y

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Validation-phase Mann-Whitney U-test on 115 medical and 59 pharmacy students; Table 2 reports median total score 84 (IQR 20) vs 80 (IQR 18), z = −2.079, p = 0.038, indicating "SAIL-10 was able to discriminate between medical and pharmacy students".

> "The total SAIL-10 score between medical and pharmacy students were significantly different (p = 0.038)."

### Chua Siew Siang 2019 (2)

Chua Siew Siang, Lai Pauline Siew Mei, Sim Si Mui, Tan Choo Hock, Foong Chan Choong. (2019). Acceptance of interprofessional learning between medical and pharmacy students in a prescribing skills training workshop: pre-post intervention study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1525-y

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Reliability analysis in Phase 1 (n = 174 returned questionnaires) with domain alphas of 0.899 and 0.671; test-retest with 157 respondents gave total-score Spearman rho = 0.550 (p < 0.001), reported as "stable reliability".

> "The Cronbach's alpha value of the overall SAIL-10 instrument was 0.870."
