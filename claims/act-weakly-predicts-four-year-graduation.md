# For four-year (rather than six-year) graduation, ACT scores are slightly predictive but their coefficient is one-sixth the HSGPA coefficient

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` ACT is slightly predictive of graduating in four years controlling for HSGPA and college characteristics, but the HSGPA relationship mirrors six-year graduation with odds doubling per SD. [→ Allensworth 2019](#allensworth-2019)

## Evidence

### Allensworth 2019

Allensworth, E. M., & Clark, K. (2019). High School GPAs and ACT Scores as Predictors of College Completion: Examining Assumptions about Consistency across High Schools. University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/high-school-gpas-and-act-scores-as-predictors-of-college-completion

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Four-year graduation models in Table 5 for the CPS sample; only about half as many students graduate in four years as in six. Variation in ACT slopes across high schools is not significant, but the standard deviation of ACT slopes is larger than the average ACT slope.

> "ACT scores are slightly predictive of graduating in four years in the full model, even controlling for HSGPA and college characteristics. However, the ACT coefficient is one-sixth the size of the HSGPA coefficient"
