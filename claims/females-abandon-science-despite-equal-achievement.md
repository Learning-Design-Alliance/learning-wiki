## Evidence

### Dickie 2006

Dickie, L. O., Dedic, H., Rosenfield, S., Rosenfield, E., & Simon, R. A. (2006). Success and Persistence in Science: The Influence of Classroom Climate. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0610243](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0610243)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cohort study of N = 1425 academically able CEGEP students (765 females, 660 males) using academic records. The article reports no performance differences but that "female students were more likely to abandon science" at every career decision stage (Figure 3).

> "nor were there differences in their performances in the first semester in CEGEP (83.6% vs. 82.2%). However at every stage of decision making about future careers in science, female students were more likely to abandon science"
