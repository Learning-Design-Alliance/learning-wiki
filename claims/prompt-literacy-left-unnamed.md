## Evidence

### Shahin Hossain 2026

Shahin Hossain, Sima Ahmadi, Leqi Li, Idowu David Awoyemi, Wei Huang, Chenxi Zhou, Jujia Li, Samaa Haniya, Shapla Khanam, Tasbirun Mashreka Subaha. (2026). Rethinking Generative AI Literacy: An Integrative, Developmental, and Dialectical Framework for K–12 Teacher Education. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01705 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2608.01705](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2608.01705)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Conceptual analysis of existing AI literacy frameworks (Long & Magerko 2020; Ng et al. 2021; Allen & Kendeou 2024): the authors argue that although these frameworks could in principle house prompting, "none of these frameworks explicitly names prompt construction as a competency."

> "In practice, however, none of these frameworks explicitly names prompt construction as a competency, operationalizes how teachers should develop it, or distinguishes it from general AI tool use."

### Shahin Hossain 2026 (2)

Shahin Hossain, Sima Ahmadi, Leqi Li, Idowu David Awoyemi, Wei Huang, Chenxi Zhou, Jujia Li, Samaa Haniya, Shapla Khanam, Tasbirun Mashreka Subaha. (2026). Rethinking Generative AI Literacy: An Integrative, Developmental, and Dialectical Framework for K–12 Teacher Education. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01705

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review attributes to Yang and Appleget (2025) that most pre-service teacher prompts were broad and unstructured, and reports that "pre-service teachers who received structured prompt training demonstrated significantly higher self-efficacy and more purposeful AI use" (Barbieri & Nguyen 2025); underlying designs are not described here.

> "Yang and Appleget (2025), for instance, identified three levels of pre-service-teacher prompting and found that most prompts were broad and not purposefully structured."
