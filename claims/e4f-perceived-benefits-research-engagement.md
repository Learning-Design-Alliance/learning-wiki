# Teachers and senior leaders reported that using E4F improved their schools' approach to using research evidence and gave opportunities to discuss research, though around a fifth reported no evidence of pupil learning benefits

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Users reported benefits particularly for research discussion opportunities, interest and enthusiasm for research evidence, and their schools' use of research evidence, with improvements to classroom practice and pupils' learning reported to a slightly lesser extent. [→ Lord 2017](#lord-2017)
`q2 i?` A large minority (up to a fifth) of respondents did not feel there was evidence of benefits to pupils' learning, which interviews attributed to it being too early to see practice changes translate into pupil outcomes. [→ Lord 2017](#lord-2017)

## Evidence

### Lord 2017

Lord, P., Sims, D., White, R., & Roy, P. (2017). Evidence for the Frontline: Evaluation report and executive summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED581162

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Perceived-outcome findings from the pilot's baseline and follow-up staff surveys and in-depth interviews in seven schools. The report notes "a large minority (around a fifth) of respondents did not report evidence of pupil learning improvements" because it was too early to observe outcome changes.

> "Senior leaders and teachers were most positive in reporting that using E4F helped to improve their schools' approach to using research evidence and that it gave them opportunities to discuss research evidence with others."
