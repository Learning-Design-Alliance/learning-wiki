# Students' cognitive style (adaptive vs. innovative) did not significantly affect content knowledge scores

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There was no statistically significant difference in content knowledge between students classified as more adaptive and those classified as more innovative on Kirton's Adaption-Innovation Inventory. [→ Blackburn 2015](#blackburn-2015)

## Evidence

### Blackburn 2015

Blackburn, J. J., Robinson, J. S., & Kacal, A. (2015). Determining the effects of reflection type and cognitive style on students' content knowledge. Journal of Agricultural Education, 56(3), 195-209. https://doi.org/10.5032/jae.2015.03195

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same two-way ANOVA tested the main effect of cognitive style (more adaptive vs. more innovative, classified via KAI scores of 95 or below vs. 96 or higher) on biofuels content knowledge. The article reports no statistical significance for the main effect of cognitive style (F(1, 44) = 0.05, p = 0.83).

> "Further, there was no statistical significance of the main effect of cognitive style, F(1, 44) = 0.05, p = 0.83. Therefore, the researchers failed to reject the third null hypothesis."
