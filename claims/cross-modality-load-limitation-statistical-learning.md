# The absence of a load effect may reflect cross-modality design: visual load may spare auditory resources, and load was not sustained to full capacity

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The authors propose that taxing visual rather than auditory resources, and possibly not exhausting working memory capacity, may conceal cross-modal interference on statistical learning. [→ Marta Isabel Garrido 2016](#marta-isabel-garrido-2016)

## Evidence

### Marta Isabel Garrido 2016

Marta Isabel Garrido, Chee Leong James Teng, Jeremy Alexander Taylor, Elise Genevieve Rowe and Jason Brett Mattingley. (2016). Surprise responses in the human brain demonstrate statistical learning under high concurrent cognitive demand. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/npjscilearn.2016.6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Stated limitation in the Discussion: because load was manipulated in the visual modality while learning was probed auditorily, modalities may allocate resources independently; alternatively, spare capacity may have concealed cross-modal interference, which might appear with a higher load (N>2-back) or a verbal working memory task.

> "In other words, taxing visual resources may have no impact on auditory resources or learning. An alternative explanation is that our task manipulation did not push the working memory system to its full capacity, which may have left spare capacity available for both implicit auditory learning and the visual task"
