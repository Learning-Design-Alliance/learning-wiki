## Evidence

### Rebecca Y. Bayeck 2026

Rebecca Y. Bayeck. (2026). Exploring learning in classic African board games: a review of recent literature. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10733-9

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Abah (2018), that I'tche of the Orokam people in Nigeria engages players in "basic arithmetic operations" plus problem-solving, critical thinking, anticipation, and spatial perception that aid mathematics.

> "As a strategic game, playing the game engaged players in basic arithmetic operations such as addition, subtraction, and multiplication."

### Rebecca Y. Bayeck 2026 (2)

Rebecca Y. Bayeck. (2026). Exploring learning in classic African board games: a review of recent literature. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10733-9

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes to Magonziwa (2017), an empirical dissertation on Tsoro in Zimbabwe, the finding that counting, numeracy, addition, matrices, subtraction, and angles are embedded in the game and that play can increase children's performance.

> "Playing Tsoro can increase children's performance, and teachers need to integrate this game into the teaching and learning of mathematics (Magonziwa, 2017)."
