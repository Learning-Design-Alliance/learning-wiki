# ECG diagnostic accuracy scores rose from Test 1 to Test 2 with case vignettes in one uncontrolled pre-post study: residents (d = 0.59) more than senior (d = 0.38) or junior students (d = 0.29)

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` ECG diagnostic accuracy scores rose from Test 1 to Test 2 with case vignettes, with the change more pronounced amongst residents than senior or junior students, in a pre-post study without a comparison group. [→ Viljoen Charle André 2021](#viljoen-charle-andre-2021)

## Evidence

### Viljoen Charle André 2021

Viljoen Charle André, Millar Rob Scott, Manning Kathryn, Hoevelmann Julian, Burch Vanessa Celeste. (2021). Clinically contextualised ECG interpretation: the impact of prior clinical exposure and case vignettes on ECG diagnostic accuracy. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-021-02854-x

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Within-group Test 1 to Test 2 comparisons in the same cross-sectional study of 176 trainees. The Results section reports effect sizes for each cohort: residents d = 0.59, senior students d = 0.38, junior students d = 0.29, all p < 0.001, with the vignette benefit "more pronounced amongst the residents".

> "The gains in accuracy were more pronounced amongst the residents (Cohen’s d = 0.59, p < 0.001), than amongst senior (Cohen’s d = 0.38, p < 0.001) or junior students (Cohen’s d = 0.29, p < 0.001)."
