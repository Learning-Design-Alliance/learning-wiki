Evidence¶

Garvin Brod and Jasmin Breitwieser 2019¶

Garvin Brod and Jasmin Breitwieser. (2019). Lighting the wick in the candle of learning: generating a prediction stimulates curiosity. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0056-y

q2 · i1 · causal · r2

Pupillometry analysis (exploratory time-window definition, preregistered contrasts) of the 29-participant experiment during a 3-s anticipation phase. The curiosity effect on pupil dilation appeared in the prediction condition and was marginally significant in the example condition.

"high-curiosity facts induced greater pupil dilation than low-curiosity facts in the prediction condition, t(28) = 1.93, p = 0.032, and d = 0.357. This effect was marginally signiﬁcant in the example condition as well, t(28) = 1.66, p = 0.054, and d = 0.308"
