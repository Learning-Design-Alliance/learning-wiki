# In a randomized experiment in Russia, doubling the amount of EdTech use did not double test-score performance, indicating a diminishing marginal rate of substitution for traditional learning

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among students ages 9–11 given no EdTech, a base level of about 45 minutes per week, or double that level, doubling EdTech use did not double test-score performance, so EdTech substitutes for traditional learning only to a limited extent. [→ Robert Fairlie and Prashant Loyalka 2020](#robert-fairlie-and-prashant-loyalka-2020)

## Evidence

### Robert Fairlie and Prashant Loyalka 2020

Robert Fairlie and Prashant Loyalka. (2020). Schooling and Covid-19: lessons from recent research on EdTech. npj Science of Learning 5:13. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-020-00072-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

A randomized experiment in Russia with primary school students ages 9–11 compared no EdTech, a base level of ~45 minutes per week of EdTech, and a doubled level of EdTech on standardized test scores and grades. The article reports "a diminishing marginal rate of substitution for traditional learning"; no effect size or test statistic is printed.

> "There is a diminishing marginal rate of substitution for traditional learning from doubling the amount of EdTech use (that is, when we double the amount of EdTech used we do not find that test scores performance doubles)."
