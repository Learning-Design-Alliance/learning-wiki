# Children in Tier 4 and Tier 5 preschool programs gained more learning and development from fall to spring than children in Tier 3 programs

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Compared to children in Tier 3 programs, children in Tier 4 programs gained an additional 1.2–1.7 months of learning and development, and those in Tier 5 programs gained an additional 2.2–2.5 months, with effect sizes of 0.08 to 0.16 standard deviations. [→ Sussman 2022](#sussman-2022)

## Evidence

### Sussman 2022

Sussman, J., Melnick, H., Newton, E., Kriener-Althen, K., Draney, K., Mangione, P., & Gochyyev, P. (2022). Preschool quality and child development: How are learning gains related to program ratings? Learning Policy Institute.

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Regression analysis of fall and spring DRDP scores for approximately 70,000 children in 1,700 QCC-rated programs, controlling for fall scores and demographics. The article reports effect sizes "ranging from 0.08 to 0.16 standard deviations" as "modest but educationally meaningful gains."

> "From fall to spring of the given study year, compared to children in Tier 3 programs, children in Tier 4 programs gained an additional 1.2–1.7 months of learning and development, and those in Tier 5 programs gained an additional 2.2–2.5 months."
