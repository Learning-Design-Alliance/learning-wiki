# Reasoning ability moderated the word-problem intervention's effect, which did not compensate for very low reasoning: the WP advantage was nonsignificant 1.81 SDs below the sample mean

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Reasoning was the only significant moderator of the WP-versus-EXP effect on word problems; effects favoring WP were not significant for students with very low reasoning, transitioning to nonsignificance at 1.81 SDs below the at-risk sample mean. [→ Fuchs 2015](#fuchs-2015)

## Evidence

### Fuchs 2015

Fuchs, L. S., Malone, A., Schumacher, R. F., Namkung, J., Hamlett, C. L., Jordan, N. C., Siegler, R., Gersten, R., & Changas, P. (2015). Supported Self-Explaining during Fraction Intervention. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED557580

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Path-analytic moderation on the word-problem outcome; the reasoning model had R2 = .51, F(7,134) = 20.20, p < .001, and reasoning was the only significant moderator. The article concludes the intervention "did not adequately compensate for the word-problem demands."

> "The effect favoring WP transitioned to nonsignificance at 1.81 SDs below the at-risk sample mean on reasoning. Thus, the intervention did not adequately compensate for the word-problem demands."
