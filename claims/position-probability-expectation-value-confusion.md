## Evidence

### Singh 2016

Singh, C. (2016). Student Difficulties with Quantum Mechanics Formalism. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.075](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.075)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Written survey question in which students, told that an energy measurement yielded the first excited state, were asked to describe possible position measurement outcomes and their probabilities; even with partial credit, "Only 38% of the students provided the correct response".

> "Only 38% of the students provided the correct response. Partial responses were considered correct for tallying purposes if students wrote anything that was correct related to the above wavefunction"

### Singh 2016 (2)

Singh, C. (2016). Student Difficulties with Quantum Mechanics Formalism. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.075](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.075)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey responses to the same position-probability question; "Eleven percent of the students tried to ﬁnd the ex- pectation value of position ⟨x⟩", writing integrals involving the wavefunction while believing they were computing position probabilities.

> "Eleven percent of the students tried to ﬁnd the ex- pectation value of position ⟨x⟩ instead of the probability of ﬁnding the electron at a given position."
