## Evidence

### Rotthoff Thomas 2014

Rotthoff Thomas, Ostapczuk Martin S., Kröncke Klaus D., Zimmerhofer Alexander, Decking Ulrich, Schneider Matthias, Ritz-Timme Stefanie. (2014). Criterion validity of a competency-based assessment center in medical education – a 4-year follow-up study. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.3402/meo.v19.25254

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Pearson correlations within the n=30 AC sample between the three AC elements and the overall AC score. The role play was the best predictor (r=0.77), but the differences between elements were not statistically significant.

> "The best predictor of overall performance was the role play, which correlated best with overall performance [r=0.77, p<0.001 (one-tailed)], followed by group discussion [r=0.65, p<0.001 (one-tailed)] and the interview [r=0.57, p<0.001 (one-tailed)]. However, the three correlations did not differ significantly (all p's>0.05)."

### Rotthoff Thomas 2014 (2)

Rotthoff Thomas, Ostapczuk Martin S., Kröncke Klaus D., Zimmerhofer Alexander, Decking Ulrich, Schneider Matthias, Ritz-Timme Stefanie. (2014). Criterion validity of a competency-based assessment center in medical education – a 4-year follow-up study. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.3402/meo.v19.25254

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Construct validity analysis correlating corresponding competencies between the different AC elements within the same n=30 sample. Only social-ethical competency showed a significant cross-element correlation, which the authors interpret as limited construct validity.

> "The resulting correlations were rather low and insignificant (r's=−0.29 to 0.21), except for the moderate correlation between social-ethical competency as assessed by group discussion and social-ethical competency as assessed by interview [r=0.32, p=0.04 (one-tailed)]."
