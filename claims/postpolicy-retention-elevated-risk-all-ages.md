# Prepolicy retained students dropped out earlier, but postpolicy retained students showed elevated dropout risk throughout their remaining years rather than at early ages

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1`–`r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Prepolicy retained students were much more likely than other students to drop out and to drop out early. [→ Allensworth 2004](#allensworth-2004)
`q2 i?` Postpolicy, eighth-grade retention was not associated with a greater hazard of dropping out at ages 15 or 16 than at 17; risk was elevated throughout remaining school years. [→ Allensworth 2004 (2)](#allensworth-2004-2)

## Evidence

### Allensworth 2004

Allensworth, E. (2004). Ending Social Promotion: Dropout Rates in Chicago after Implementation of the Eighth-Grade Promotion Gate. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/ending-social-promotion-dropout-rates-chicago-after-implementation-eighth-grade

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Hazard analysis predicting dropout at each age 15-17, adjusted for achievement, demographics, and school enrollment (Figure 5). Prepolicy retained students dropped out early despite being a very select group.

> "Consistent with studies of teacher-initiated retention, prepolicy students who were retained in eighth grade were not only much more likely to drop out of school than other students, but they were also more likely to drop out early."

### Allensworth 2004 (2)

Allensworth, E. (2004). Ending Social Promotion: Dropout Rates in Chicago after Implementation of the Eighth-Grade Promotion Gate. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/ending-social-promotion-dropout-rates-chicago-after-implementation-eighth-grade

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Postpolicy hazard models show retained students about 3 to 4 percent more likely to drop out at each age than similar postpolicy students. The report notes they did drop out at earlier grades because high school entry was delayed.

> "In contrast, postpolicy, eighth-grade retention was not associated with a greater likelihood of dropping out at age 15 or 16 than it was at age 17. Instead, postpolicy students’ like- lihood of dropping out was el- evated throughout their remaining years in school."
