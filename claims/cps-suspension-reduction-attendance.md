## Evidence

### Rebecca Hinze-Pifer and Lauren Sartain 2015

Rebecca Hinze-Pifer and Lauren Sartain. (2015). Rethinking Universal Suspension for Severe Student Behavior. Stanford University and University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Schoolwide observational analysis of attendance for all CPS high school students during the reform period, net of suspension days. The article reports "roughly one additional day per year, per student"; no effect size is printed.

> "Attendance: On average, all students came to school more often—roughly one additional day per year, per student—net of any days missed due to suspension."

### Rebecca Hinze-Pifer and Lauren Sartain 2015 (2)

Rebecca Hinze-Pifer and Lauren Sartain. (2015). Rethinking Universal Suspension for Severe Student Behavior. Stanford University and University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Subgroup analysis by school racial composition within the same student-fixed-effect framework. In predominantly Black schools attendance rose "an average of three days of school per year, per student"; no effect size or significance test is printed for this contrast.

> "Attendance increases were concentrated in schools serving predominantly Black students, where attendance increased an average of three days of school per year, per student."
