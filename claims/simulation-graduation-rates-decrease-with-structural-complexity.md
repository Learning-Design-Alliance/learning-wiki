## Evidence

### Gregory L. Heileman 2018

Gregory L. Heileman, Chaouki T. Abdallah, Ahmad Slim, Michael Hickman. (2018). Curricular Analytics: A Framework for Quantifying the Impact of Curricular Reforms and Pedagogical Innovations. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1811.09676](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1811.09676)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Simulation experiment over all balanced four-course curricula offered over two terms, with all course pass rates fixed at 50% and only structure varying. Table 1 reports simulated graduation rates at 100%, 150% and 200% time; the article states the rates "all decrease monotonically" from curriculum (a) to (g).

> "Notice that as we allow more time, the graduation rates in each curriculum increase, as we would expect. In addition, as we move from curriculum (a) to (g), the 100%, 150% and 200% graduation rates all decrease monotonically."
