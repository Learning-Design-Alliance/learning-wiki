## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Class-averaged normalized change in self-efficacy was positive (0.09) with a small effect size (Cohen's d = 0.27), but the change was not statistically significant. [→ Meg Foster 2023](#meg-foster-2023)

## Evidence

### Meg Foster 2023

Meg Foster, Philip von Doetinchem, and Sandra von Doetinchem. (2023). Virtual physics laboratory courses: An evaluation of students' self-efficacy and intelligence mindset. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2301.02699](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2301.02699)

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r1`

Pre/post modified CLASS survey of a remote introductory physics laboratory; post-test average self-efficacy was 9.3 ± 0.4 (n = 110) versus pretest 8.3 ± 0.5 (n = 65), a 5% increase in percent favorable. The increase "corresponded to a class-averaged normalized change⟨c⟩ of 0.09 and an eﬀect size of 0.27" (Cohen's d = 0.27, a small effect) but was not statistically significant.
