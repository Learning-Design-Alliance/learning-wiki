# Neither blood circulation nor diffusion has an inherent correct ontology; predicates from both direct and emergent process categories apply to each

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Ayush Gupta 2008

Ayush Gupta, David Hammer, Edward F. Redish. (2008). The Case for Dynamic Models of Learners' Ontologies in Physics. Journal of the Learning Sciences. https://www.tandfonline.com/journals/hlns20

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Addressing the SO framework's flagship contrast between blood flow (direct process) and diffusion (emergent process), the authors show experts model blood as a non-linear fluid governed by Navier-Stokes equations and argue predicates cross both categories. They conclude "neither blood circulation nor diffusion has an inherent ‘correct’ ontology".

> "In sum, neither blood circulation nor diffusion has an inherent ‘correct’ ontology. Like plasmas, electrons, waves, and vibrations, expert understanding of circulation or diffusion cannot be captured by a single ontological description or described by a single category of ontological predicates."
