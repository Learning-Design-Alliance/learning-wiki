## Evidence

### Ana Cebollero-Salinas 2022

Cebollero-Salinas, A., Cano-Escoriaza, J., & Orejudo, S. (2022). Are emotional e-competencies a protective factor against habitual digital behaviors (media multitasking, cybergossip, phubbing) in Spanish students of secondary education? *Computers & Education, 181*, 104464. [doi:10.1016/j.compedu.2022.104464](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2022.104464)

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Multi-group SEM on cross-sectional survey data from n = 776 secondary students (12–16). Unlike the regulatory competencies, the social e-competency path is positive in both gender and age models; its zero-order correlations are small (r = .111 to .178, not significant for girls). The authors state that the transversal design does not allow causal relations to be established. The authors attribute this to virtual environments where distant, anonymous relations favor prosocial online behaviors that reinforce commenting and sharing conducts.

> "Social e-competency serves as a relevant, invariant predictor among genders (β = 0.18) and age groups (β = 0.22)."
