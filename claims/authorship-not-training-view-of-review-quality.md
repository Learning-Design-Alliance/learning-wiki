## Evidence

### Glonti K 2019

Glonti K, Boutron I, Moher D, Hren D. (2019). Journal editors' perspectives on the roles and tasks of peer reviewers in biomedical journals: a qualitative study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2019-033421

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview finding from the 56-editor qualitative study: editors described learning to review "by just doing it" and did not consider training courses a prerequisite, though opinions divided on course usefulness for junior researchers.

> "All interviewees stated that they learnt to peer review manuscripts 'by just doing it', without having had previous training, and suggested that this was also the case for the majority of the peer reviewers in biomedical journals."

### Glonti K 2019 (2)

Glonti K, Boutron I, Moher D, Hren D. (2019). Journal editors' perspectives on the roles and tasks of peer reviewers in biomedical journals: a qualitative study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2019-033421

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Authors' discussion of the external evidence base: they note the only substantial study could not predict reviewer performance from experience or qualifications, and that highest-rated reviewers tended to be young, while acknowledging that evidence base is limited.

> "However, there is no evidence to support this perspective; evidence linking authorship experience and academic qualifications to high-quality reviews is very limited."
