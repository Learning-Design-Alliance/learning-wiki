## Evidence

### Jérôme Brender 2026

Jérôme Brender, Laila El-Hamamsy, Kim Uittenhove, Aitor Perez, Patrick Jermann, Francesco Mondada, and Engin Bumbacher. (2026). Reflective Dialogue or Prompt Refinement? Effects of Tutor Scaffolding on Students' Independent LLM Use for Programming. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1007/978-3-032-29763-1_37](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-29763-1_37)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of session-3 prompting-pattern clusters against practice lab task performance found no significant cluster differences, indicating "comparable task-level outcomes across different ways of interacting with the LLM."

> "A Kruskal–Wallis test, complemented by regression analy- ses, revealed no significant differences in task performance between prompting- pattern clusters."

### Jérôme Brender 2026 (2)

Jérôme Brender, Laila El-Hamamsy, Kim Uittenhove, Aitor Perez, Patrick Jermann, Francesco Mondada, and Engin Bumbacher. (2026). Reflective Dialogue or Prompt Refinement? Effects of Tutor Scaffolding on Students' Independent LLM Use for Programming. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1007/978-3-032-29763-1_37](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-29763-1_37)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures mixed-effects regression of test scores on time, cluster, and their interaction (130 observations, 65 students) found a significant time-by-cluster interaction only for the Understanding-driven prompters with high-quality prompts cluster (β = 0.72, p = 0.044); no other cluster was significant.

> "Students in this cluster showed significantly greater learning gains than the grand mean across clusters (β= 0.72,p= 0.044), see Figure 3."
