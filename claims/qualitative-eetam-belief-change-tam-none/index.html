Evidence¶

Kızkapan 2021¶

Kızkapan, O., & Bektaş, O. (2021). Improving 7th Grade Students’ Epistemological Beliefs by Epistemologically Enriched Argumentation Model. Pedagogical Research, 6(3), em0095. https://doi.org/10.29333/pr/10949

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Content analysis of semi-structured interviews with 12 students (six per experimental group), selected by maximum variation sampling. EETAM interviews yielded "certain knowledge, omniscient authority and innate ability" categories; e.g., students emphasized tentativeness of knowledge in books and the role of experiment and observation.

"Based on the opinions of the students in EETAM group, findings related to certain knowledge, omniscient authority and innate ability dimensions of epistemological beliefs were obtained."

Kızkapan 2021 (2)¶

Kızkapan, O., & Bektaş, O. (2021). Improving 7th Grade Students’ Epistemological Beliefs by Epistemologically Enriched Argumentation Model. Pedagogical Research, 6(3), em0095. https://doi.org/10.29333/pr/10949

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Content analysis of the six TAM-group interviews found perceived effects on achievement, peer learning and participation, but "no findings were found regarding the effect of TAM on epistemological beliefs". This null qualitative result parallels the TAM group's non-significant quantitative difference.

"In the interviews conducted with students in the TAM group, it was concluded that the model had an effect on some factors such as academic achievement, peer learning, active participation in the lesson, but no findings were found regarding the effect of TAM on epistemological beliefs."
