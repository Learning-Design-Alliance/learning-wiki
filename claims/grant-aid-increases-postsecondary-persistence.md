# Grant aid increases postsecondary persistence, raising within-year persistence by 3.2 and year-to-year persistence by 1.8 percentage points in U.S. causal studies

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` Grant aid increases within-year persistence by 3.2 percentage points (statistically significant at the 5% level). [→ Nguyen 2018](#nguyen-2018)
`q4 i?` Grant aid increases year-to-year persistence by 1.8 percentage points, highly statistically significant. [→ Nguyen 2018 (2)](#nguyen-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Nguyen 2018

Nguyen, T.D., Kramer, J.W., & Evans, B.J. (2018). The Effects of Grant Aid on Student Persistence and Degree Attainment: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of the Causal Evidence (CEPA Working Paper No. 18-04). Stanford Center for Education Policy Analysis. http://cepa.stanford.edu/wp18-04

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random-effects meta-analysis of nine U.S. studies providing within-year persistence estimates; the pooled estimate indicates grant aid "increases within year persistence by 3.2 percentage points," significant at the 5% level despite a sizable standard error. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "In terms of within year persistence, the summary estimate from nine studies indicates that grant aid increases within year persistence by 3.2 percentage points."

### Nguyen 2018 (2)

Nguyen, T.D., Kramer, J.W., & Evans, B.J. (2018). The Effects of Grant Aid on Student Persistence and Degree Attainment: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of the Causal Evidence (CEPA Working Paper No. 18-04). Stanford Center for Education Policy Analysis. http://cepa.stanford.edu/wp18-04

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 25 U.S. year-to-year persistence estimates from causal studies; the pooled effect is "a modest 1.8 percentage points" with standard error .005 and a 95% CI of about one to three percentage points. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "Next we find grant aid increases year-to-year persistence by a modest 1.8 percentage points with a fairly precise standard error of .005, which is highly statistically significant; the 95% confidence interval ranges from about one to three percentage points."
