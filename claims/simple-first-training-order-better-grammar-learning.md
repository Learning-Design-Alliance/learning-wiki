## Evidence

### Antoniou Mark 2016

Antoniou Mark, Ettlinger Marc, Wong Patrick C. M. (2016). Complexity, Training Paradigm Design, and the Contribution of Memory Subsystems to Grammar Learning. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0158812

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 3 assigned 36 learners to simple-complex or complex-simple exposure-based training blocks before a feedback test. The ANOVA showed "the simple-complex training order (75.4%) resulted in better grammar learning than the complex-simple order (54.7%)", with a simple-rule advantage overall (70.3% vs 58.6%).

> "A main effect of training order, F(1, 34) = 8.2, p = .007, ηp2 = .195, revealed that the simple-complex training order (75.4%) resulted in better grammar learning than the complex-simple order (54.7%)."

### Antoniou Mark 2016 (2)

Antoniou Mark, Ettlinger Marc, Wong Patrick C. M. (2016). Complexity, Training Paradigm Design, and the Contribution of Memory Subsystems to Grammar Learning. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0158812

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Bonferroni-adjusted post-hoc tests following the significant interaction (F(1, 34) = 10.0, p = .003, ηp2 = .227) showed "subjects in the simple-complex training order (86.0%) outperformed those in the complex-simple order (56.2%) for the simple grammar"; the orders did not differ on the complex grammar. Memory correlations also differed by order.

> "subjects in the simple-complex training order (86.0%) outperformed those in the complex-simple order (56.2%) for the simple grammar, t(25.3) = 4.0, p < .001, whereas the two training orders did not differ on the complex grammar (64.7% vs. 53.2%), p = .141"
