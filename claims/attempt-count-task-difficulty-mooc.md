# Average number of attempts before reaching the correct answer served as the task-difficulty measure, with hints preventing bottleneck tasks in the MOOC

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The authors used the average number of attempts a student makes before reaching the correct answer to measure task difficulty, and added hints so no task became a significant bottleneck. [→ Juha Reinikainen 2024](#juha-reinikainen-2024)

## Evidence

### Juha Reinikainen 2024

Juha Reinikainen, Vlad Stirbu, Teiko Heinosaari, Vesa Lappalainen, Tommi Mikkonen. (2024). Quantum Computing for All: Online Courses Built Around Interactive Visual Quantum Circuit Simulator. [doi:10.1109/mcg.2024.3444952](https://doi.org/10.1109/mcg.2024.3444952)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Design observation from the first MOOC cohort: because attempts do not affect grades, students were motivated to tackle challenges "without the pressure of exam failure or low grades"; the authors report this as the MOOC format functioning as intended, with no statistics printed.

> "To measure the difficulty of a task, we have used the average number of attempts made by the student before reaching the correct answer. Since the number of attempts does not impact the course grade"
