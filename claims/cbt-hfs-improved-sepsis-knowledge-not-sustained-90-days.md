# CBT plus high-fidelity simulation significantly improved nurses' sepsis knowledge immediately after the intervention, but the improvement was not sustained at 90 days

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Marsack 2023

Marsack, W. J., De Gagne, J. C., Reid, J., Pakieser‐Reed, K., Francisco, M. A., & Kang, H. S. (2023). Evaluation of computer‐based training and high‐fidelity simulation to improve early recognition of sepsis on the adult general ward. Nursing Open, 10, 4880–4887. https://doi.org/10.1002/nop2.1718

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

One-group pretest-posttest QI study with 36 nurses at pretest, 29 at posttest and 21 at 90-day follow-up on one medical-surgical ward. Repeated-measures ANOVA showed a "statistically significant difference" with ηp² = 0.30; Tukey post hoc showed the gain held only from Time 1 to Time 2.

> "There was a statistically significant difference in the mean percentage scores for knowledge of sepsis between the three timepoints, F (2,83) = 18.14, p < 0.001, η p 2 = 0.30. Post hoc testing using Tukey's test revealed statistically significant differences between Time 1 and Time 2 (p < 0.001) but not between Time 1 and Time 3 (p = 0.125)"
