Evaluating the impact of simulation on patient outcomes in pediatric and neonatal critical care: a systematic review and critical appraisal of the evidence. European Journal of Pediatrics 185(5):302. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00431-026-06925-3

q3 · i? · review · r3

Systematic quality appraisal with RoB 2 for RCTs and ROBINS-I-V2 for nonrandomized studies; interrater agreement was reported as Cohen's kappa 0.91 (95% CI: 0.86–0.96). Seven of 13 nonrandomized studies had serious, six critical risk of bias.

"For nonrandomized and before–after studies, the ROBINS-I-V2 tool revealed that seven out of 13 studies had a serious RoB, while the remaining six were judged to have a critical RoB"

Zanin A 2026 (2)¶

Zanin A, Poletto E, Mencia S, Gawronski O, Jourdain G. (2026). Evaluating the impact of simulation on patient outcomes in pediatric and neonatal critical care: a systematic review and critical appraisal of the evidence. European Journal of Pediatrics 185(5):302. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00431-026-06925-3

q3 · i? · review · r3

The review's discussion identifies the scarcity of rigorous Level 4 evidence as its principal finding: from 2481 screened articles, 16 studies met inclusion criteria, nearly all at high or critical risk of bias.

"Despite the global adoption of simulation over the past 15 years, our analysis identified only 16 studies meeting our criteria, nearly all of which were limited by a high or critical risk of bias"
