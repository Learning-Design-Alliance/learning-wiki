# In calculus-based mechanics, male students scored significantly higher on the first, third, and final exams in pooled data, with small-to-weak effect sizes and no grade difference

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Male students scored significantly higher on Exams 1, 3, and 4 of calculus-based mechanics with small (d = 0.240) and weak (d = 0.066, d = 0.105) effect sizes. [→ Matthew Dew 2021](#matthew-dew-2021)

## Evidence

### Matthew Dew 2021

Matthew Dew, Jonathan Perry, Lewis Ford, William Bassichis, and Tatiana Erukhimova. (2021). Gendered Performance Diﬀerences in Introductory Physics: A Study from a Large Land-Grant University. arXiv:2009.09536. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010106](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010106)

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

T-tests on z-score-transformed exam data pooled across 14 instructors and 49 lecture sections in calculus-based mechanics; the article reports "small (d = 0.240) for the ﬁrst exam" and weak effects for Exams 3 and 4, with no significant grade difference.

> "As a combined sample, male students scored higher to a signiﬁcant degree on the ﬁrst, third, and ﬁnal exams of the course. The eﬀect size of these diﬀerences is small (d = 0.240) for the ﬁrst exam, and weak for the third (d = 0.066) and ﬁnal exams (d = 0.105)."
