# Gender differences in high-stakes exam scores appear in Physics 1 but not Physics 2, with no gender differences in low-stakes homework in either course

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Alysa Malespina 2024

Alysa Malespina, Fargol Seifollahi, Chandralekha Singh. (2024). Bioscience students in physics courses with higher test anxiety have lower grades on high-stakes assessments and women report more test anxiety than men. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.3390/educsci14101092](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci14101092)

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Unpaired t-tests of assessment grades in Physics 1 (153 women, 77 men for exam comparisons); midterm exams d = -0.40, p = 0.004, final exam d = -0.27, p = 0.052, with negative d indicating "female students had lower scores than male students".

> "In Physics 1, on average, men had higher exam scores. The gender differences were statistically significant with a medium effect size for the midterm exams and marginally significant for the final exam."

### Alysa Malespina 2024 (2)

Alysa Malespina, Fargol Seifollahi, Chandralekha Singh. (2024). Bioscience students in physics courses with higher test anxiety have lower grades on high-stakes assessments and women report more test anxiety than men. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.3390/educsci14101092](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci14101092)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

T-tests of homework and Physics 2 exam grades; Physics 2 exams showed "no statistically significant gender differences in exam scores for Physics 2" (midterm d = -0.09, final d = -0.03); homework differences were small and non-significant.

> "female students had higher average homework scores than male students in Physics 1 (Table 2) and Physics 2 (Table 3). However, both effect sizes were small (d ∼ 0.2), and gender differences were not statistically significant."
