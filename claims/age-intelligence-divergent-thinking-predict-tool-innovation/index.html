Evidence¶

Guo 2025¶

Guo, J.; Bai, H.; Long, X.; Su, X.; Pang, W. (2025). The process of tool innovation in young children: An attempt to bridge the Gestalt and the perception-action theories. Learning and Instruction, 99, article 102182. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102182

q3 · i? · design · r2

Multiple linear regressions (N = 139) using CFA factor scores as outcomes; the authors report age, PPVT-R general intelligence, and TCAM divergent thinking were significant positive predictors of both performance measures.

"children's age and their measures of general intelligence and divergent thinking all significantly and positively predicted both children's task success and ideal manipulation."

Guo 2025 (2)¶

Guo, J.; Bai, H.; Long, X.; Su, X.; Pang, W. (2025). The process of tool innovation in young children: An attempt to bridge the Gestalt and the perception-action theories. Learning and Instruction, 99, article 102182. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102182

q3 · i? · associational · r1

In the same regressions, Simon Says inhibition positively and DCCS cognitive flexibility negatively predicted ideal manipulation, while no EF measure predicted task success; the success–ideal manipulation factor correlation was 0.88 (p < 0.001).

"None of children's EF measures were a significant predictor of their task success; however, the EF measures of inhibition and cognitive flexibility were significant predictors of ideal manipulation, with positive and negative effects, respectively."
