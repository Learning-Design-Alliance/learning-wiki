## Evidence

### Mallick R. 2020

Mallick R., Henderson T., Lahue B. J., Kafal A., Bassett P., Scalchunes C. (2020). Subcutaneous immunoglobulin in primary immunodeficiency – impact of training and infusion characteristics on patient-reported outcomes. BMC Immunology. [doi:10.1186/s12865-020-00371-y](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12865-020-00371-y)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariate logistic regression on the survey cohort evaluated predictors of efficient infusions. "Shorter infusion and preparation duration were more likely with training conducted in the doctor’s office rather than at home" (p = 0.006); Table 6 shows OR = 3.05 for doctor's office vs home.

> "Shorter infusion and preparation duration were more likely with training conducted in the doctor’s office rather than at home (p = 0.006)."

### Mallick R. 2020 (2)

Mallick R., Henderson T., Lahue B. J., Kafal A., Bassett P., Scalchunes C. (2020). Subcutaneous immunoglobulin in primary immunodeficiency – impact of training and infusion characteristics on patient-reported outcomes. BMC Immunology. [doi:10.1186/s12865-020-00371-y](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12865-020-00371-y)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same analysis of infusion predictors found "The odds of completing an infusion in ≤2 h were 60% higher with a 20% concentration product" (OR = 1.6, p = 0.05), indicating product concentration relates to infusion efficiency.

> "The odds of completing an infusion in ≤2 h were 60% higher with a 20% concentration product, relative to a 10% concentration (Table 6)."
