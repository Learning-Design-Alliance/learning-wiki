# Instructional approaches that include inquiry learning are more effective than direct instruction for acquiring conceptual knowledge, per the review's synthesis of meta-analyses

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across research syntheses, instructional approaches including inquiry learning show more positive effects on conceptual knowledge than direct instruction. [→ de Jong 2023](#de-jong-2023)

## Evidence

### de Jong 2023

de Jong, T., Lazonder, A. W., Chinn, C. A., Fischer, F., Gobert, J., Hmelo-Silver, C. H., Koedinger, K. R., Krajcik, J. S., Kyza, E. A., Linn, M. C., Pedaste, M., Scheiter, K., & Zacharia, Z. C. (2023). Let's talk evidence – The case for combining inquiry-based and direct instruction. Educational Research Review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2023.100536

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports that the Minner et al. (2010) synthesis of 138 studies found "51% of the studies indicated a positive effect of inquiry-based instruction" on conceptual knowledge, with only 2% showing negative impact; 42 studies involved direct controlled comparisons.

> "Minner, Levy, and Century (2010) analyzed the findings of 138 studies that included some level of inquiry and found that 51% of the studies indicated a positive effect of inquiry-based instruction on the acquisition of conceptual knowledge."

### de Jong 2023 (2)

de Jong, T., Lazonder, A. W., Chinn, C. A., Fischer, F., Gobert, J., Hmelo-Silver, C. H., Koedinger, K. R., Krajcik, J. S., Kyza, E. A., Linn, M. C., Pedaste, M., Scheiter, K., & Zacharia, Z. C. (2023). Let's talk evidence – The case for combining inquiry-based and direct instruction. Educational Research Review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2023.100536

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes to the Alfieri et al. (2011) meta-analysis of 164 primary studies the conclusion that "assisted inquiry is more effective than explicit instruction" while unguided discovery is less effective, indicating guidance is needed for inquiry to pay off.

> "Alfieri, Brooks, Aldrich, and Tenenbaum (2011) concluded that unassisted (or unguided) discovery is less effective than explicit instruction, but that assisted inquiry is more effective than explicit instruction"
