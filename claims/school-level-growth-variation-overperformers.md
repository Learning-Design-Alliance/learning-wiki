# Over a quarter of variance in student growth lies at the school level, and 12-16% of schools significantly overperform average growth

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Substantial variation in linear growth rate was attributable to schools (27-30% of random variance) in all three grade levels. [→ Hiroyuki Yamada 2024](#hiroyuki-yamada-2024)
`q2 i?` Schools classified as overperformers ranged from 12% to 16% across grade levels, with Grade 3 showing a wider distribution (16% overperformers, 15% underperformers). [→ Hiroyuki Yamada 2024 (2)](#hiroyuki-yamada-2024-2)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

School-level random effects from the three-level growth model (Table 1: %Sch = 29%, 27%, 30% for Grades 1-3) for 4,928-4,931 schools per grade, using school-level growth estimates relative to the average.

> "there was substantial variation in the linear growth rate at the school level (27% to 30% in Table 1)."

### Hiroyuki Yamada 2024 (2)

Hiroyuki Yamada. (2024). New Normal in Early Elementary Mathematics Learning: Part III - Learning from Variation in Grade 3. E4 Center, Northwestern University. https://e4.northwestern.edu/2024/12/19/new-normal-in-early-elementary-mathematics-learning-part-iii-learning-from-variation-in-grade-3/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Schools were classified as overperformer, average, or underperformer by non-overlapping confidence intervals of school-level versus average growth estimates; Table 5 prints overperformer shares of 13%, 12%, and 16% for Grades 1-3.

> "The proportion of schools classified as overperformers ranged from 12% to 16% across the grade levels. The distribution of schools in Grade 3 was wider than the distribution in other grade levels."
