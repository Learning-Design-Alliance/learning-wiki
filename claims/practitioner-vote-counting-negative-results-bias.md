# Practitioners showed vote-counting-like behavior, rating evidence more positively when a large non-significant summary effect had no negative individual studies than when a large significant one included one

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Practitioners were more likely to rate the evidence as positive when the summary effect was large (d = 0.2) and non-significant (condition c) than when it was large and significant (condition bc), apparently because condition bc included one study with a negative effect. [→ Kaitlyn G. Fitzgerald and Elizabeth Tipton 2022](#kaitlyn-g-fitzgerald-and-elizabeth-tipton-2022)

## Evidence

### Kaitlyn G. Fitzgerald and Elizabeth Tipton 2022

Kaitlyn G. Fitzgerald and Elizabeth Tipton. (2022). The Meta-Analytic Rain Cloud Plot: A New Approach to Visualizing Clearinghouse Data. Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness. https://doi.org/10.1080/19345747.2022.2031366

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Exploratory analysis of practitioner effectiveness ratings (Q9): "practitioners were curiously more likely to rate the evidence as “positive”" under condition c than bc; the authors attribute this to condition bc containing one negative-effect study, suggesting a vote-counting variation attending to any negative results.

> "practitioners were curiously more likely to rate the evidence as “positive” (the highest rating on the 5-point scale) when the summary effect was large ( d¼ 0:2) and non-significant (experimental condition c) than they were when the summary effect was large and significant (experimental condition bc)."
