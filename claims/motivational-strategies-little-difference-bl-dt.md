## Evidence

### Balakrishnan Athira 2021

Balakrishnan Athira, Nair Sreedharan, Kunhikatta Vijayanarayana, Rashid Muhammed, Unnikrishnan M. K., Jagannatha P. S., Chandran Viji P., Khera Kanav, Thunga Girish. (2021). Effectiveness of blended learning in pharmacy education: An experimental study using clinical research modules. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0256814

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

MSLQ motivational scales compared across the three trial arms by Kruskal-Wallis test (Table 4). Pairwise comparisons showed no significant DT-BL differences in intrinsic goal orientation, task value or control of learning belief (p>0.05); BL scored higher on extrinsic goal orientation (p = 0.015) and self-efficacy (p<0.001).

> "But there were no statistically significant difference of motivational orientations between didactic and blended learning group except strategies of extrinsic goal orientation and self-efficacy."

### Balakrishnan Athira 2021 (2)

Balakrishnan Athira, Nair Sreedharan, Kunhikatta Vijayanarayana, Rashid Muhammed, Unnikrishnan M. K., Jagannatha P. S., Chandran Viji P., Khera Kanav, Thunga Girish. (2021). Effectiveness of blended learning in pharmacy education: An experimental study using clinical research modules. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0256814

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Null findings from the same MSLQ analysis (Table 4): test anxiety p = 0.059, time p = 0.077, effort regulation p = 0.585 across the three groups; equivalence was not tested.

> "There was no statistically significant difference in test anxiety, time management and effort regulation between groups (p>0.05)."
