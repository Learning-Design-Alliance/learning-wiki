Evidence¶

Lea Fröscher 2022¶

Lea Fröscher, Ann‑Kathrin Friedrich, Max Berentelg, Curtis Widmer, Sam J. Gilbert and Frank Papenmeier. (2022). Framing cognitive offloading in terms of gains or losses: achieving a more optimal use of reminders. Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-022-00416-3

q3 · i2 · causal · r3

Experiment 1 preregistered one-tailed paired t tests (OIPs vs AIPs) showed the loss-condition reminder bias remained significant, t(77) = 3.60, p < 0.001, dz = 0.41 (printed effect size dz = 0.41, a medium effect); the article states "participants still offloaded more than optimal also under loss framing".

"the reminder bias was still significant both in the gain, t(62) = 6.49, p < 0.001, dz = 0.82, and loss condition, t(77) = 3.60, p < 0.001, dz = 0.41."

Related Claims¶

  • Framing reminders as losses reduces, but does not eliminate or reverse, the reminder bias in both between- and within-participants designs — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Participants show a significant reminder bias (overuse of reminders relative to the optimal strategy) when reminders are framed as gains — related
  • The more underconfident participants are about their memory abilities, the larger their reminder bias, regardless of framing — related
  • Individuals systematically overuse reminders relative to the optimal offloading level, and reminder bias correlates with confidence and metacognitive bias — a broader claim this one bears on
