# Course context and environment, rather than instructional format (remote, hybrid, in-person), appear to determine whether student collaboration correlates with performance

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Course format (in-person, remote, or hybrid) does not appear to dictate whether a correlation between student collaboration and performance arises; the Mines environment preserved the connection while fully remote, whereas hybrid CU Boulder courses did not consistently produce it. ## Evidence

### Nathan Crossette 2023

Nathan Crossette, Lincoln D. Carr, and Bethany R. Wilcox. (2023). Correlations between student connectivity and academic performance: a pandemic follow-up. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010106](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010106)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cross-course comparison in the conclusions of an observational three-course study: the fully remote Mines course replicated pre-pandemic correlation patterns while the hybrid CU Boulder courses showed weaker or nearly no correlations. The article states this as its overall interpretive conclusion; no effect size is printed.

> "Overall, these results show that the format of the course (whether in-person, remote, or hybrid), does not appear to dictate whether there will be a correlation between student collaboration and performance."

### Nathan Crossette 2023 (2)

Nathan Crossette, Lincoln D. Carr, and Bethany R. Wilcox. (2023). Correlations between student connectivity and academic performance: a pandemic follow-up. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010106](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010106)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Supporting interpretation from the results section: the pandemic-era Mines network reproduced the pre-pandemic centrality–performance patterns, which the authors read as "the environment at Mines was largely able to preserve the connection" despite full remoteness.

> "This replication of the patterns from the pre-pandemic courses indicates that the environment at Mines was largely able to preserve the connection between collaboration and performance during the pandemic despite the course being fully remote."
