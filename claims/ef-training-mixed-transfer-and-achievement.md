# Training executive function shows mixed effects: near transfer in children without far transfer, near and far transfer in adults, and no causal evidence that EF training improves academic achievement

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Most meta-analyses found EF training leads only to weak, non-lasting effects on cognitive control; one meta-analysis found near transfer in children but no far transfer. [→ Damien S. Fleur 2021](#damien-s-fleur-2021)
`q3 i?` A meta-analysis in adults suggests EF training in general and working memory training specifically may lead to significant near- and far-transfer effects. [→ Damien S. Fleur 2021](#damien-s-fleur-2021)
`q3 i?` A meta-analysis did not show evidence for a causal relationship between EF and academic achievement, instead suggesting a bidirectional relationship. [→ Damien S. Fleur 2021](#damien-s-fleur-2021)

## Evidence

### Damien S. Fleur 2021

Damien S. Fleur, Bert Bredeweg and Wouter van den Bos. (2021). Metacognition: ideas and insights from neuro- and educational sciences. npj Science of Learning 6:13. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00089-5

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Meta-analyses of EF training reviewed in the training section show "near-transfer following EF training in children" but no far transfer, with training improving only the trained component; in adults, one meta-analysis suggests near- and far-transfer; a meta-analysis found no evidence for a causal EF–achievement relationship, suggesting instead a bidirectional, mutually supportive relation.

> "One meta-analysis did ﬁnd evidence of near-transfer following EF training in children (in particular working memory, inhibitory control and cognitive ﬂexibility), but found no evidence of far-transfer"
