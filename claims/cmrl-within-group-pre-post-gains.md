# Within the CMRL group, diagnosis, comprehensive analysis, and prescribing medical orders scores rose and incorrect operations fell from before to after teaching

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` CMRL group ability scores after teaching were higher than before teaching for diagnosis (78.75 ± 17.83% vs. 67.08 ± 24.10%), comprehensive analysis (88.27 ± 10.95% vs. 69.56 ± 13.11%), and prescribing medical orders (90.03 ± 9.63% vs. 78.31 ± 10.28%), all p < 0.05. [→ Li Min 2025](#li-min-2025)
`q3 i?` Incorrect operations in the CMRL group decreased from 4.22 ± 1.17 to 2.64 ± 1.50 events (p < 0.05). [→ Li Min 2025](#li-min-2025)

## Evidence

### Li Min 2025

Li Min, Liu Tao, Wen Yiqiong, Zhang Min, Zhu Wenye, Wang Xuming, Hou Yunjiao, Li Lin, Zu Anju, Sun Shibo. (2025). Combination of mentor-based and resident-based learning may benefit to improving the ability of clinical practice in resident training. BMC Medical Education 25:197. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06798-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-group pre-post comparison for the 36 CMRL residents over four weeks of teaching, from Table 3. All three ability measures improved with p < 0.05, and incorrect operations decreased from 4.22 ± 1.17 to 2.64 ± 1.50 events; no effect sizes printed.

> "scores of CMRL group in the ability of diagnosis, comprehensive analysis, and prescribing medical orders after training were higher than those before training (ability of diagnosis, 78.75 ± 17.83% vs. 67.08 ± 24.10%, p < 0.05; ability of comprehensive analysis 88.27 ± 10.95% vs. 69.56 ± 13.11%, p < 0.05; ability of prescribing medical orders, 90.03 ± 9.63% vs. 78.31 ± 10.28%, p < 0.05; respectively)"
