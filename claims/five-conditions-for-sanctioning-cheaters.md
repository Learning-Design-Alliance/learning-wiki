## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Punishing cheaters on the grounds that cheating hinders learning requires, among other conditions, that the assignment teaches something worthwhile, that cheating actually interfered with learning, and that the sanction does not hamper learning more than the cheating did. [→ Mathieu Bouville 2009](#mathieu-bouville-2009)

### Mathieu Bouville 2009

Mathieu Bouville. (2009). Why is cheating wrong?. [doi:10.1007/s11217-009-9148-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11217-009-9148-0)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument (section 3.2) enumerating necessary conditions for sanctioning cheaters on learning grounds. The article notes the best students may learn nothing from homework, that forbidden collaboration is often seen by students as furthering learning, and that not doing homework at all hampers learning as much as copying it. Philosophical analysis, no data.

> "For cheaters to be punished because cheating hinders learning, the following five conditions are necessary (the last of them —that sanctioning cheaters must actually have a positive consequence— will be addressed in the next section)."
