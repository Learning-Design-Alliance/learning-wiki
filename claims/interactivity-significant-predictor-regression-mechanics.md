## Evidence

### Rudolph 2012

Rudolph, A. L., Lamine, B., Joyce, M., Vignolles, H., & Consiglio, D. (2012). Introduction of interactive learning into French university physics classrooms. Physical Review Special Topics – Physics Education Review. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1311.3622](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1311.3622)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Regression Model 3 for FCI Newton's Third Law Gain (N=102) at UPMC, controlling for gender, parents' education, pre-score, first-semester final exam, and hours studied. Table III reports interactivity coefficient 0.701 (standardized 0.231), p < 0.01.

> "The third model introduces level of course interactivity as an independent variable, which is found to be highly statistically significant at the p < 0.01 level. The adjusted R-squared of this final model continues to increase to 0.269"

### Rudolph 2012 (2)

Rudolph, A. L., Lamine, B., Joyce, M., Vignolles, H., & Consiglio, D. (2012). Introduction of interactive learning into French university physics classrooms. Physical Review Special Topics – Physics Education Review. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1311.3622](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1311.3622)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Regression Model 3 for CSEM Gain (N=154) across the four E&M classes, with interactivity coded 0, 1, 2 for low, medium, high. Table IV reports interactivity coefficient 2.535 (standardized 0.313), p < 0.01; adjusted R-squared rose from 0.120 to 0.208.

> "The level of interactivity is highly statistically significant (p < 0.01), and the R-squared jumps 73% from the addition of this single variable, to 0.208, suggesting that interactive learning was strongly related to student learning of the material in the CSEM."
