# Proactive tutoring increases learners' use of metacognitive help tools compared with reactive tutoring

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Learners in the pro-active tutoring condition activated the metacognitive help significantly more than learners in the reactive condition (F=92.33, p=0.000). [→ Bruno De Lièvre 2006](#bruno-de-lievre-2006)

## Evidence

### Bruno De Lièvre 2006

Bruno De Lièvre, Christian Depover & Pierre Dillenbourg. (2006). The relationship between tutoring mode and learners' use of help tools in distance education. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-005-6076-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of variance in a randomised experimental study of 120 distance learners assigned to four tutoring conditions. Mean activations of metacognitive help were 3.05 (pro-active CT) and 3.42 (pro-active HT) versus 1.55 and 1.71 under reactive tutoring; the ANOVA showed "a signiﬁcant VVS eﬀect at 0.000 in favour of the pro-active mode."

> "The analysis of the variance (Table 5) shows a signiﬁcant VVS eﬀect at 0.000 in favour of the pro-active mode."
