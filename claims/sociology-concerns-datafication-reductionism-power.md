# Sociology-of-science concerns: data-reliance in education risks tunnel-vision, unhealthy reductionism, and inequitable power dynamics

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Increased data-reliance may lead researchers to treat complex social and educational problems as solvable statistical problems and miss nuances, context, causal factors, and counter-narratives. [→ Iwatani 2018](#iwatani-2018)
`q2 i?` Data mining may lure researchers toward unhealthy reductionism of teaching and learning into easily operationalized attributes, and concentrates power in those who can analyze data. [→ Iwatani 2018 (2)](#iwatani-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Iwatani 2018

Iwatani, Emi. (2018). Overview of Data Mining's Potential Benefits and Limitations in Education Research. Practical Assessment, Research & Evaluation, 23(15). http://pareonline.net/getvn.asp?v=23&n=15

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Selwyn's (2015) concerns about the "datafication" of education, citing Ruppert (2012) and Savage (2009) that digital data supports little more than 'surface' understandings of sociological entities.

> "Focusing too much on available data may prevent education researchers from considering important and relevant nuances, contextual factors, causal factors, and counter-narratives."

### Iwatani 2018 (2)

Iwatani, Emi. (2018). Overview of Data Mining's Potential Benefits and Limitations in Education Research. Practical Assessment, Research & Evaluation, 23(15). http://pareonline.net/getvn.asp?v=23&n=15

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Drawing on Lupton (2013), Manovich (2012), and Ruppert (2012), the review reports concerns that machine learning specialists could gain disproportionate power in education decisions, alongside worries about neglecting "deep data" on few cases for "surface data" about many.

> "suspect that data, and the ability to use data, is a form of power that has the potential to be distributed inequitably and misused."
