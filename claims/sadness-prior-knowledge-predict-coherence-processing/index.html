Evidence¶

Iordanou 2020¶

Iordanou, K., Kendeou, P., & Zembylas, M. (2020). Examining my-side bias during and after reading controversial historical accounts. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-020-09240-w

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Standard multiple regression with coherence-based processing during other-side text reading as the dependent variable and think-aloud sadness and prior knowledge as predictors. Both were significant predictors; more coherence-based processing was exhibited by those expressing more sadness and having more prior knowledge.

Related Claims¶

  • Cognitive processing differs by text type: participants engaged in more epistemic processing, meta-comprehension, and coherence-based processing when reading the other-side text than the own-side text — related
  • Emotions reported during think-aloud reading were mostly negative (anger, sadness) for both texts, in contrast to positive questionnaire-reported emotions toward the other ethnic group — related
  • Epistemic beliefs, prior knowledge, and the think-aloud emotion of anger jointly predict low-epistemic processing during reading of the other-side text — related
  • Greater anger, hopelessness, and negative emotions overall during reading are associated with poorer text comprehension, while positive emotions show no relation — related
  • The frequency of associations produced during think-aloud reading predicts sentence reading times during silent reading — related
  • Participants' prior knowledge about the historical event was entirely consistent with their own ethnic group's narrative, with none providing an alternative interpretation — related
