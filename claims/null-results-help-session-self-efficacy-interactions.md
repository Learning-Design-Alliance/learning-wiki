## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A supplementary weekly help session at HSWC 2018 showed no significant impact on final exam score, and no two-way interactions between demographics and other factors were significant. [→ Shima Salehi 2019](#shima-salehi-2019)
`q2 i?` Composite SAT/ACT had less predictive power than math SAT/ACT alone (AIC 837 vs 820), and CLASS self-efficacy scores were negligible predictors. [→ Shima Salehi 2019 (2)](#shima-salehi-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Shima Salehi 2019

Shima Salehi, Eric Burkholder, Peter Lepage, Steven Pollock, and Carl Wieman. (2019). The impact of incoming preparation and demographics on performance in Physics I: a multi-institution comparison. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1905.00389](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1905.00389)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Secondary regression analyses at subset institutions testing supplementary variables. The article reports the help-session null quoted here and that no two-way interactions (e.g. help session disproportionately helping URM students) were significant, so higher-order interactions were assumed negligible.

> "We also examined the effect of a supplementary weekly help session (HSWC 2018) and found no significant impact on final exam score."

### Shima Salehi 2019 (2)

Shima Salehi, Eric Burkholder, Peter Lepage, Steven Pollock, and Carl Wieman. (2019). The impact of incoming preparation and demographics on performance in Physics I: a multi-institution comparison. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1905.00389](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1905.00389)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Model comparison at HSEC using AIC for non-nested models. The article reports the AIC values quoted here and that adding composite SAT/ACT to the math-plus-CI model did not change R-squared (0.30 for both). CLASS self-efficacy pre- and post-course scores were also negligible predictors.

> "The composite SAT/ACT score had less predictive power than math SAT/ACT: the AIC of the model with only math SAT/ACT as predictor was lower than the model with only composite SAT/ACT as a predictor (820 as opposed to 837)."
