## Evidence

### Patel Mandeep K. 2026

Patel Mandeep K., Timko‐Swaim Lynn, Conklin Perrilynn, Lu Wei‐Hsin. (2026). Curriculum Integration of an Interactive Blood Pressure Measurement Module to Improve Healthcare Students' Knowledge, Confidence, and Skills. The Journal of Clinical Hypertension. https://doi.org/10.1111/jch.70234

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Formative 5-minute BPM OSCE with standardized patients two months after training, at the Clinical Simulation Center (133 medical, 69 PA students). Descriptive checklist data show high overall performance: 78% and 89% correct BP measurement.

> "The overall mean scores for all of the categories were high with 78% (medical students) and 89% (PA students) performing the BP measurement correctly on standardized patients."

### Patel Mandeep K. 2026 (2)

Patel Mandeep K., Timko‐Swaim Lynn, Conklin Perrilynn, Lu Wei‐Hsin. (2026). Curriculum Integration of an Interactive Blood Pressure Measurement Module to Improve Healthcare Students' Knowledge, Confidence, and Skills. The Journal of Clinical Hypertension. https://doi.org/10.1111/jch.70234

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

OSCE checklist comparison between cohorts. PA students scored higher on all three patient-preparation items (50% vs. 82% section means) and on "performed BP by palpation" (45% vs. 96%), suggesting gaps in medical students' procedural familiarity.

> "A difference was noted between the medical and PA student scores, with the PA students scoring higher in all three assessments in the patient preparation category (mean scores 50% vs. 82%)."
