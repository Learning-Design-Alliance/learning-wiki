# Pre-instruction testing enhances memory for directly tested text content relative to non-testing controls, at both immediate and one-week delayed posttests

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants who attempted practice questions before reading a text passage outperformed controls on posttest questions targeting that content, both immediately and after one week. [→ Steven C. Pan 2023](#steven-c-pan-2023)

## Evidence

### Steven C. Pan 2023

Steven C. Pan, Shana K. Carpenter. (2023). Prequestioning and Pretesting Effects: a Review of Empirical Research, Theoretical Perspectives, and Implications for Educational Practice. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09814-5

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Narrative review of Richland et al. (2009), in which participants attempted five practice questions on a cerebral achromatopsia passage before reading; the review reports that "participants in the experimental group consistently outperformed the control group" on immediate and one-week delayed posttests.

> "When the test questions were re-administered on an immediate posttest (experiments 1–3) or on a one-week delayed posttest (experiment 4), however, participants in the experimental group consistently outperformed the control group"
