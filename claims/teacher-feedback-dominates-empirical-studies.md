# Empirical feedback studies predominantly examine teacher feedback, at odds with theoretical advocacy of peer feedback

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The large majority of feedback studies examine teacher-provided feedback, a distribution the authors say was at odds with theoretical discussions advocating peer feedback. [→ Douglas Biber 2011](#douglas-biber-2011)

## Evidence

### Douglas Biber 2011

Douglas Biber, Tatiana Nekrasova, and Brad Horn. (2011). The Effectiveness of Feedback for L1-English and L2-Writing Development: A Meta-Analysis. ETS Research Report RR-11-05. https://www.ets.org/toefl

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

The survey's coding of feedback source across the 306 studies (Table 5 lists 119 teacher-source studies, 34 peer, 18 computer) found "the large majority of these studies focus on feedback given by the teacher." The authors interpret this as reflecting typical classroom practice.

> "As Table 5 shows, the large majority of these studies focus on feedback given by the teacher. In this regard, feedback studies probably reflected typical classroom practice, but they were at odds with many theoretical discussions that advocated the utility of peer feedback."
