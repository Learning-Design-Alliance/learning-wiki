Evidence¶

Federico Nardi 2025¶

Federico Nardi, A. Aldo Faisal & Shlomi Haar. (2025). Motor learning mechanisms are not modified by feedback manipulations in a real-world task. npj Science of Learning 10:73. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00373-8

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Reward-dependent motor variability analysis of trial-to-trial shot-angle change after success vs failure in the EVR billiards task, N = 32. Within-condition comparisons were significant in both conditions and both rounds (e.g., Round 1: WErr = 0, p < 0.001; WRew = 23, p = 0.02).

"participantsmade greater corrections after a failed shot rather than after a successful (rewarded) one (Fig. 5a-ﬁrst panel), which led to increased exploration when using reinforcement-based learning. This difference was presen t in both conditions, supporting the hypothesis that reward-based lea rning was also occurring in the error feedback condition."

Federico Nardi 2025 (2)¶

Federico Nardi, A. Aldo Faisal & Shlomi Haar. (2025). Motor learning mechanisms are not modified by feedback manipulations in a real-world task. npj Science of Learning 10:73. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00373-8

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Mann–Whitney comparisons between conditions on reward-dependent motor variability, N = 32, split by session round. The error condition showed reward-like learning in Round 2 only ("U = 189, p = 0.02" for mean absolute change); Round 1 comparisons were non-significant.

"For the condition comparison we found here as well that the error condition showed a“reward-like” learning in the second round only, where the learning was comparable, in both mean absolute change (Round 1: U = 168,p = 0.14;Round 2: U = 189,p = 0.02) and standard deviation of change (Round 1: U = 167,p = 0.15;Round 2: U = 182, p = 0.04)."

Related Claims¶

  • Feedback manipulations were insufficient to isolate learning mechanisms: participants used both error-based and reward-based learning regardless of the feedback provided — a broader claim this one bears on
