# School and district factors relate to risk identification: low income concentration correlates with repeated risk identification, and segregation modestly increases it, though some schools outperform model predictions

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` School-level factors like higher concentrations of students from low income backgrounds correlated with increased likelihood of repeated risk identification, and historically underserved students in more racially segregated districts were slightly more likely to be identified as significantly below benchmark multiple times. ## Evidence

### Lemke 2025

Lemke, M., Faulkner-Bond, M., LeBeau, B., Soo Ping Chow, A., & Quesen, S. (2025). Early literacy performance in Massachusetts: Results of ongoing analysis of literacy screening assessments (2023/24 data). WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED677571

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

New district-level analysis using Segregation Tracking Project measures of poverty and racial/ethnic concentration, related to repeated risk identification. The report describes a slight increase in likelihood in more racially segregated contexts; correlational, no effect size printed.

> "Zooming out to the district level this year— through an analysis of district-level school segregation measures— showed that historically underserved students in more racially segregated contexts were slightly more likely to be identified as significantly below benchmark multiple times."
