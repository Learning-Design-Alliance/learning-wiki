## Evidence

### Hamid Marashi 2013

Hamid Marashi, Mojgan Khaksar. (2013). Dictogloss or Dicto-Phrase: Which Works Better for Listening Comprehension? li-manager's Journal of English Language Teaching, Vol. 3 No. 1, January-March. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1068862

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experimental comparison of two randomly assigned groups of 30 Iranian female pre-intermediate teenage EFL learners each. Posttest means were 18.10 (dictogloss) and 16.23 (dicto-phrase); the t-test showed "a significant difference between the mean scores of the two groups at the posttest". No effect size is printed for this test statistic itself.

> "Based on Table 4 (t = 2.060, df = 58, p = 0.044 < 0.05, two-tailed), there was a significant difference between the mean scores of the two groups at the posttest."

### Hamid Marashi 2013 (2)

Hamid Marashi, Mojgan Khaksar. (2013). Dictogloss or Dicto-Phrase: Which Works Better for Listening Comprehension? li-manager's Journal of English Language Teaching, Vol. 3 No. 1, January-March. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1068862

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

The authors estimated the effect size for the posttest difference between the dictogloss and dicto-phrase groups; the article prints the effect size as 0.53, which it characterizes as a moderate effect size per Cohen (1988), allowing moderate generalization of the findings.

> "To determine the strength of the findings of the research, that is, to evaluate the stability of the research findings across samples, effect size was also estimated to be 0.53."
