# Frequency-based content analysis shows little variation in how CPS was actualized across groups and tasks, with Problem Reanalysis the most frequent sub-element in both task designs

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Cumulative frequency accounts did not indicate large variation between groups or tasks in the appearance of CPS elements. [→ Johanna Pöysä-Tarhonen 2022](#johanna-poysa-tarhonen-2022)
`q2 i?` Problem Reanalysis was the most frequent CPS sub-element in both the technology-enhanced task and the physical-objects task; cognitive elements were more identifiable from interaction data than social ones. [→ Johanna Pöysä-Tarhonen 2022 (2)](#johanna-poysa-tarhonen-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Johanna Pöysä-Tarhonen 2022

Johanna Pöysä-Tarhonen, Päivi Häkkinen, Pasi Tarhonen, Piia Näykki, Sanna Järvelä. (2022). “Anything taking shape?” Capturing various layers of small group collaborative problem solving in an experiential geometry course in initial teacher education. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-021-09562-5

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Directed content analysis (Phase 1) of 26 hours of video from three groups, coded three times in Atlas.ti, summarized with descriptive counts. The article states "the low SD implies a more or less equal appearance of CPS elements in the different groups and task designs".

> "In general, the cumulative accounts did not indicate a large variation between the differ - ent groups in either task design."

### Johanna Pöysä-Tarhonen 2022 (2)

Johanna Pöysä-Tarhonen, Päivi Häkkinen, Pasi Tarhonen, Piia Näykki, Sanna Järvelä. (2022). “Anything taking shape?” Capturing various layers of small group collaborative problem solving in an experiential geometry course in initial teacher education. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-021-09562-5

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Descriptive statistics across Tables 7 and 8 show proportions such as Problem Reanalysis at 49–59% (Task 1). The article reports "that cognitive elements were more identifiable from the interaction data but with slightly different task-related emphases".

> "Problem Reanalysis from the cognitive set formed the most frequent sub-element in both the technology-enhanced task (see Table 7) and the task utilizing physical objects (see Table 8)."
