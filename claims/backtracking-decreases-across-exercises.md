# Student backtracking before setting up the correct induction decreased across exercises in both deployments, indicating a learning curve

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Backtracking before setting up the correct induction fell from 94% to 50% of students across exercises in the first deployment and from 33% to 0% in the second. [→ Matthew Keenan 2026](#matthew-keenan-2026)

## Evidence

### Matthew Keenan 2026

Matthew Keenan, Nishant Kheterpal, Jean-Baptiste Jeannin, Cyrus Omar. (2026). Hazel Prover: A Classroom Proof Assistant for Learning Structural Induction. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23309

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Fine-grained analysis of replayed usage logs (Table 1(a)) from 18 analyzed first-deployment submissions and 14 second-deployment submissions, splitting proofs into milestones. The printed percentages are descriptive; no effect size is reported.

> "In the first deployment these went from 94% of students backtracking the first exercise to 50% of students backtracking in the last exercise. In the second deployment, it went from 33% to 0%."
