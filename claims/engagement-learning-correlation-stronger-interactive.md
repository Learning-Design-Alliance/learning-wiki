## Evidence

### Ying Xu 2023

Ying Xu, Kunlei He, Julian Levine, Daniel Ritchie, Zexuan Pan, Andres Bustamante, and Mark Warschauer. (2023). Artificial Intelligence Enhances Children's Science Learning from Television Shows. https://osf.io/j7upd

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r3`

Exploratory correlational analysis of four verbal-engagement metrics against post-test scores among the 156 children receiving questions (Figure 5, Table 5). Printed effect sizes: r = 0.485 (interactive) vs r = 0.193 (pseudo-interactive); "The difference in correlation between these two groups was statistically significant (p = .005)".

> "For the interactive group, the correlation between response rate and science learning outcomes was 0.485 (p < .001), compared to a correlation of 0.193 (p = .09) for the pseudo-interactive group. The difference in correlation between these two groups was statistically significant (p = .005)"

### Ying Xu 2023 (2)

Ying Xu, Kunlei He, Julian Levine, Daniel Ritchie, Zexuan Pan, Andres Bustamante, and Mark Warschauer. (2023). Artificial Intelligence Enhances Children's Science Learning from Television Shows. https://osf.io/j7upd

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r3`

Same exploratory correlational analysis. Printed effect sizes: r = .585 (interactive) vs r = .269 (pseudo-interactive) for relevance–learning, "a statistically significant difference (p = .022)"; the accuracy correlation (r = .540 vs r = .377) did not differ significantly (p = .09).

> "in the interactive condition, there was a higher correlation between relevance and science learning outcomes (r = .585, p < .001) compared to the pseudo-interactive condition (r = .269)—also a statistically significant difference (p = .022)"
