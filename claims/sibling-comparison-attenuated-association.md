# In differentially exposed sibling comparisons the association is weakened but remains statistically significant (z = 0.04), significant only for exposure at ages 14–16, not 11–13.

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The sibling fixed-effect model yields a much weaker but significant association (z = 0.04), significant for siblings exposed at 14–16 (β = −0.03; 95% CI: −0.05, −0.01) but not for exposure at 11–13. [→ Nordmo 2023](#nordmo-2023)
`q3 i?` No significant difference was found between siblings exposed at age 11–13 versus the 17–19 comparison group. [→ Nordmo 2023 (2)](#nordmo-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Nordmo 2023

Nordmo, M., Kleppestø, T., Sunde, H. F., Flatø, M., Demange, P. & Torvik, F. A. (2023). The association between parental internalizing disorders and child school performance. npj Science of Learning 8:34. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00182-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Fixed-effect sibling comparison model (n = 73,740 siblings with unique exposures), adjusted for birth order and birth year, siblings exposed at 17–19 as comparison group. The article reports β = −0.03 for the 14–16 exposure group.

> "However, we do ﬁnd a signiﬁcant difference between siblings exposed at age 14 – 16 versus age 17 – 19 (β = −0.03; 95% CI: −0.05, −0.01)."

### Nordmo 2023 (2)

Nordmo, M., Kleppestø, T., Sunde, H. F., Flatø, M., Demange, P. & Torvik, F. A. (2023). The association between parental internalizing disorders and child school performance. npj Science of Learning 8:34. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00182-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Same fixed-effect sibling model: the early-exposure (11–13) contrast against siblings exposed at 17–19 shows no statistically significant difference; equivalence was not tested.

> "The results from the sibling comparison model suggests no signiﬁcant difference between siblings exposed at age 11 – 13 versus age 17 – 19 (early exposure)."
