## Evidence

### Kamimura Go 2025

Kamimura Go, Maeda Koki, Aoki Masaya, Imamura Satomi, Morizono Shoichiro, Tokunaga Takuya, Umehara Tadashi, Harada-Takeda Aya, Nagata Toshiyuki, Ueda Kazuhiro. (2025). Importance of future motivation for skill acquisition among medical students undergoing endoscopic forceps training. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07048-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Analysis of covariance comparing rate of skill acquisition across 98 students grouped by sex, preferred department, and club activity; surgical aspiration was "the only factor that impacted the rate of skill acquisition".

> "However, we found that the aspiration to become a surgeon was the only factor that impacted the rate of skill acquisition. The aspiring surgeon group progressed faster. (p = 0.007)."

### Kamimura Go 2025 (2)

Kamimura Go, Maeda Koki, Aoki Masaya, Imamura Satomi, Morizono Shoichiro, Tokunaga Takuya, Umehara Tadashi, Harada-Takeda Aya, Nagata Toshiyuki, Ueda Kazuhiro. (2025). Importance of future motivation for skill acquisition among medical students undergoing endoscopic forceps training. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07048-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Baseline comparison of first-half time-trial means between the aspiring and non-aspiring surgeon groups in the same cohort; the article states the groups were "comparable" with p = 0.94.

> "Interestingly, the baseline performance of the aspiring surgeons (71.4 ± 48.5 s, mean ± 2SD) and non-aspiring surgeons (70.5 ± 67.4 s, mean ± 2SD, p = 0.94) groups was comparable, indicating that the aspiring surgeon group had no inherent advantage before training."

### Kamimura Go 2025 (3)

Kamimura Go, Maeda Koki, Aoki Masaya, Imamura Satomi, Morizono Shoichiro, Tokunaga Takuya, Umehara Tadashi, Harada-Takeda Aya, Nagata Toshiyuki, Ueda Kazuhiro. (2025). Importance of future motivation for skill acquisition among medical students undergoing endoscopic forceps training. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07048-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Second-week time-trial comparison from the Discussion section of the same cohort: the aspiring surgeon group "performed the time trial significantly faster than the non-aspiring surgeon group".

> "However, by the second week, the aspiring surgeon group performed the time trial significantly faster than the non-aspiring surgeon group (41.5 ± 26.4 s vs. 48.9 ± 43.6 s, p = 0.03)."
