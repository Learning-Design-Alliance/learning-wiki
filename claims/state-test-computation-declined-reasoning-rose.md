## Evidence

### Hartman 2016

Hartman, JudithAnn R., and Eric A. Nelson. (2016). Automaticity in Computation and Student Success in Introductory Physical Science Courses. Originally published 2015. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1608.05006](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1608.05006)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Analysis of Iowa Test of Basic Skills math subtest scores for 8th graders, 1978–2001, compared to fixed 1965 national norms. After 1991 “computation” scores “declined every year, falling in 2001 to their lowest level in 23 years.”

> "After 1991, “non-computation” was relatively level, but “computation” scores declined every year, falling in 2001 to their lowest level in 23 years [Figure 1]."

### Hartman 2016 (2)

Hartman, JudithAnn R., and Eric A. Nelson. (2016). Automaticity in Computation and Student Success in Introductory Physical Science Courses. Originally published 2015. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1608.05006](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1608.05006)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of Stanford 9 percentile ranks against fixed 1995 norms for nearly all Virginia 9th graders, 1998–2002. Computation scores “were below the national average and went lower every year” while math reasoning scores were higher every year.

> "In math reasoning from 1998 to 2002, Virginia 9th graders scored above the national average and were higher every year, while in computation, scores were below the national average and went lower every year."
