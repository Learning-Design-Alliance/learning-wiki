Evidence¶

Livingston et al. 2018¶

Livingston, S., Toce, A., Casey, C., Montoya, F., Hart, B. R., & O'Flaherty, C. (2018). Effect of X-Word Grammar and Traditional Grammar Instruction on Grammatical Accuracy. English Language Teaching, 11(3). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v11n3p119

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Two-group comparison of pre-college L2 students (N=47) placed in the traditional (N=25) or X-Word (N=22) group by the odd or even last digit of their student identification numbers over a 12-week immersion term, with error percentages computed on first drafts of essays at September and November data points. The X-Word group improved 5.25 percentage points (14.21% to 8.93%) versus 0.12 points for the traditional group, and the article reports the t-test p-value as "0.056 which is borderline significant".

"For the verb construct category, the t-test shows that the p-value is 0.056 which is borderline significant. In this category, the evidence is strong enough to believe that X-Word Grammar allowed students to show greater gain."
