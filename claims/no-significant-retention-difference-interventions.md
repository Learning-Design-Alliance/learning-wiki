# Fall-to-spring and fall-to-fall retention rates did not differ significantly between intervention and control undecided students

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Fall-to-spring retention: FYS 84.0% vs. control 84.4% (p<0.9513); FYS/LC 92.1% vs. control 84.4% (p<0.0599); neither significant. [→ Tampke 2013](#tampke-2013)
`q3 i?` Fall-to-fall retention: FYS 71.0% vs. control 71.6% (p<0.93); FYS/LC 78.8% vs. control 71.6% (p<0.17); neither significant. [→ Tampke 2013 (2)](#tampke-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Tampke 2013

Tampke, D. R., & Durodoye, R. (2013). Improving Academic Success for Undecided Students: A First-Year Seminar/Learning Community Approach. Learning Communities Research and Practice, 1(2), Article 3. http://washingtoncenter.evergreen.edu/lcrpjournal/vol1/iss2/3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Two-sample t-tests of fall-to-spring retention for FYS (n=69), FYS/LC (n=165), and control (n=109) undecided FTIC students. The article reports "Fall-to-spring retention rates did not differ significantly across the sub-groups" with t=-0.0612, p<0.9513 (FYS) and t=1.8934, p<0.0599 (FYS/LC).

> "Fall-to-spring retention rates did not differ significantly across the sub-groups, although it is worth noting that the FYS/LC intervention produced the highest numerical retention rates of all of the groups, a rate that exceeded the overall retention rate for all FTICs on the subject campus."

### Tampke 2013 (2)

Tampke, D. R., & Durodoye, R. (2013). Improving Academic Success for Undecided Students: A First-Year Seminar/Learning Community Approach. Learning Communities Research and Practice, 1(2), Article 3. http://washingtoncenter.evergreen.edu/lcrpjournal/vol1/iss2/3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Two-sample t-tests of fall-to-fall retention for FYS (n=69), FYS/LC (n=165), and control (n=109) undecided FTIC students. FYS: 71.0% vs. 71.6% (t=0.08, p<0.93); FYS/LC: 78.8% vs. 71.6% (t=-1.37, p<0.17).

> "While the outcomes are similar to those indicated for the previous semester, statistically significant differences vanish."
