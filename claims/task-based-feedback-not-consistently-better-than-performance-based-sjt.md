# Task-based (formative) feedback was not consistently more effective than performance-based (summative) feedback across motivational, cognitive, perception and benefit outcomes in an online SJT

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across planned contrasts of four task-based feedback groups against the performance-based group, task-based feedback was superior on some outcomes (e.g. perceived competence, usefulness) but not on others (e.g. interest, willingness to improve). [→ Reiser 2026](#reiser-2026)

## Evidence

### Reiser 2026

Reiser, S., Schick, K., Pittroff, S. I. D., Janssen, L., Berberat, P. O., Gartmeier, M., & Bauer, J. (2026). Integrating formative and summative feedback in online situational judgement tests: effects of feedback design on medical students’ motivational and cognitive learning factors. Medical Education Online, 31(1). https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2026.2639198

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Planned-contrast analyses in an RCT (N = 269 medical students, five randomly assigned feedback groups) comparing each task-based version against the Summative Test Profile. The results section states the findings "were only partially in line with H1", and contrasts in Table 4 are significant for some outcomes only (e.g. perceived competence p < .001 for PR and PE; interest p = .109–.943).

> "In summary, the findings were only partially in line with H1. Descriptively, this is because performance-based feedback turned out to score relatively high on the dependent variables."
