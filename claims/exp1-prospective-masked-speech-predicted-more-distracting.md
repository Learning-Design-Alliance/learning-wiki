# In Experiment 1, participants predicted masked speech (speech in pink noise) to be more distracting than pure speech, even predicting pure speech to be slightly helpful

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Participants in the prospective-judgment group predicted masked speech to be significantly more distracting than pure speech (η²p = .63), and relative to the scale midpoint predicted pure speech to be helpful (d = 0.21). [→ Gesa Fee Komar 2025](#gesa-fee-komar-2025)

## Evidence

### Gesa Fee Komar 2025

Gesa Fee Komar, Axel Buchner, Laura Mieth, Raoul Bell. (2025). Partially masking task-irrelevant speech has opposite effects on metacognitive judgments of distraction and objective distraction effects. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09436-y

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Online experiment with 309 participants randomly assigned to a prospective-judgment group (n = 168) or control group; the prospective group rated each sound on a −6 to +6 metacognition scale before the serial-recall task. The article reports "masked speech was predicted to be distracting" with η²p = .63 and d = 0.21 for pure speech.

> "Participants in the prospective-judgment group predicted masked speech to be significantly more distracting than pure speech, F(1, 167) = 283.20, p < .001, η2 p = .63. Relative to the neutral midpoint of the metacognition scale, masked speech was predicted to be distracting, t(167) = − 31.02, p < .001, d = − 2.39, while pure speech was predicted to be helpful, t(167) = 2.79, p = .006, d = 0.21."
