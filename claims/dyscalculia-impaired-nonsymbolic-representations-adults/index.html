Evidence¶

Bulthé Jessica 2019¶

Bulthé Jessica, Prinsen Jellina, Vanderauwera Jolijn, Duyck Stefanie, Daniels Nicky, Gillebert Céline R., Mantini Dante, Op de Beeck Hans P., De Smedt Bert. (2019). Multi-method brain imaging reveals impaired representations of number as well as altered connectivity in adults with dyscalculia. Neuroimage, 190, 289-302. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2018.06.012

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Whole-brain MVPA searchlight analysis of fMRI data from 24 adults with dyscalculia and 24 matched controls during number comparison. The group comparison showed "significantly less distinct non-symbolic number representations" in dyscalculia in anterior parietal, frontal and temporal regions (FWE-corrected p < 0.05).

"A group comparison for non-symbolic numbers (Fig. 3C–D) clearly demonstrated significantly less distinct non-symbolic number representations in the anterior parietal and frontal lobes, as well as in a small spot in the temporal lobe in individuals with dyscalculia compared to controls."

q3 · i? · associational · r2

ROI-based decoding with SVM classifiers in 17 hierarchically organized ROIs confirmed the searchlight findings; e.g. All Regions non-symbolic decoding differed between groups (t46 = 2.16, p = 0.04) and IPS (pFDR = 0.048), with more distinct patterns in controls.

"For non-symbolic numbers, we found significantly less distinguishable neural representations in adults with dyscalculia compared with matched controls in the All Regions, the parietal lobe (including the IPS and right anterior IPS), the temporal lobe, and the frontal lobe (including superior and inferior frontal gyrus)."
