Evidence¶

Webb 2016¶

Webb, M., & Doman, E. (2016). Does the Flipped Classroom Lead to Increased Gains on Learning Outcomes in ESL/EFL Contexts? The CATESOL Journal, 28(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1111606

q2 · i2 · causal · r1

Mixed factorial ANOVA on grammar test scores from 39 experimental and 25 control students in Macau and the US. Table 2 prints F = 7.66(1, 62), p = .007, partial η² = .11 for the flipped/nonflipped effect. The article reports "all three hypotheses were statistically significant."

"The ANOVA results in Table 2 and descriptive statistics in Table 3 reveal that all three hypotheses were statistically significant. Students in the experimental group scored higher compared to the students in the control group."

Webb 2016 (2)¶

Webb, M., & Doman, E. (2016). Does the Flipped Classroom Lead to Increased Gains on Learning Outcomes in ESL/EFL Contexts? The CATESOL Journal, 28(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1111606

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Post hoc comparison across the four pretest/posttest by group conditions in the same quasi-experiment; only the posttest experimental group differed significantly. Descriptive means rose from 65.85 to 71.77 in the experimental group while control means stayed near 62.7. No effect size is printed for the post hoc contrast.

"Post hoc comparison of the four conditions, (a) pretest control group, (b) pretest experimental group, (c) posttest control group, and (d) posttest experimental group, revealed that the posttest experimental group had a significantly higher mean compared to the rest."
