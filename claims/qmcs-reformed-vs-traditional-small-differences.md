## Evidence

### McKagan 2010

McKagan, S. B., Perkins, K. K., & Wieman, C. E. (2010). The Design and Validation of the Quantum Mechanics Conceptual Survey. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.6.020121](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.6.020121)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Comparison of posttest scores in Fig. 2 between traditional (solid bars) and reformed (hatched) courses. Early results had suggested reformed instruction leads to higher QMCS scores, but "After collecting more data, it is less clear that this is the case."

> "As can be seen in Fig. 2, the scores for all reformed courses are higher than the scores for all traditional courses of the same type, but on average these diﬀerences are small."

### McKagan 2010 (2)

McKagan, S. B., Perkins, K. K., & Wieman, C. E. (2010). The Design and Validation of the Quantum Mechanics Conceptual Survey. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.6.020121](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.6.020121)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Per-item comparison in reformed versus traditional modern physics courses. Ref. 50 reportedly shows much more dramatic reformed-versus-traditional differences at the University of British Columbia.

> "Students in reformed courses do score much higher than students in traditional classes on some questions, with the largest diﬀerences on questions 4, 9, and 12."
