## Evidence

### Lautrette Alexandre et al 2020

Lautrette Alexandre et al. (2020). Impact of take-home messages written into slide presentations delivered during lectures on the retention of messages and the residents' knowledge: a randomized controlled study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02092-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multivariable generalized linear mixed modeling of 853 lecture-level knowledge scores from the randomized controlled study, with lecturers and residents as crossed random effects. The article reports "better knowledge was associated with notetaking (aOR = 1.88, 95% CI 1.41–2.51)" and THM retention; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "In the multivariable analysis adjusted for randomization group and gender, better knowledge was associated with notetaking (aOR = 1.88, 95% CI 1.41–2.51; p < 0.001) and a higher rate of THMs retention (aOR = 2.17, 95% CI 1.54–3.04; p < 0.001)."

### Lautrette Alexandre et al 2020 (2)

Lautrette Alexandre et al. (2020). Impact of take-home messages written into slide presentations delivered during lectures on the retention of messages and the residents' knowledge: a randomized controlled study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02092-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive gradient across the study's three performance groups (<50, 50–80, >80 points out of 100), n = 250, 359 and 244. Retention means were "10.9 ± 16.1%, 13.0 ± 18.5% and 22.3 ± 25.2%", a significant relationship with no printed effect size.

> "The relationship between THMs retention and residents' knowledge was significant (P < 0.001): the THMs retention was 10.9 ± 16.1%, 13.0 ± 18.5% and 22.3 ± 25.2% in the low-, medium-, and high-performance groups, respectively."
