# Teacher feedback produced larger first-task score gains that dropped 32.8% by the second task, while AI-assisted peer feedback gains remained stable (a learning-curve effect)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Both modes produced significant within-group score gains in each task, but the teacher-feedback gain fell from 4.06 to 2.73 points while the AI-assisted peer feedback gain stayed stable at 3.87 then 3.89 points. [→ Tang J 2026](#tang-j-2026)

## Evidence

### Tang J 2026

Tang J, Li P, Luo R (2026) Teacher feedback VS AI-assisted peer feedback in L2 writing: A quasi-experimental study in a Chinese University. PLoS One 21(6): e0345976. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0345976

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures score data from the 8-week quasi-experiment (control n = 31, experimental n = 30) on iWrite 2.0 scores. Between-group gain comparisons were non-significant (first task d = −0.037; second task d = 0.294); the experimental class ultimately "surpassing the average score of the control class" in task 2.

> "After the first writing task, the score improvement for the teacher feedback class(4.06 points) was slightly higher than that of the AI-assisted peer feedback class (3.87 points). However, in the second task, the improvement for the teacher feedback class decreased significantly (a drop of 32.8%)"
