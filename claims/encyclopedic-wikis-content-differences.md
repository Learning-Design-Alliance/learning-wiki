# Articles from encyclopedic wikis had more videos, pictures, and external resources, whereas non-encyclopedic wikis had more posology details and better readability

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among 116 sampled articles, pictures, videos, and external resources were more frequent in articles from encyclopedic wikis, while posology details were more frequent in non-encyclopedic wikis (P<.01). [→ Brulet A 2015](#brulet-a-2015)

## Evidence

### Brulet A 2015

Brulet A, Llorca G, Letrilliart L. (2015). Medical Wikis Dedicated to Clinical Practice: A Systematic Review. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4392552/

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Comparison of 116 sampled articles (58 most revised, 58 most finalized) between encyclopedic (n=55) and non-encyclopedic (n=61) wikis using Fisher's exact test and Wilcoxon rank test. The review reports: "Posology details were more frequent in articles from non-encyclopedic wikis (P<.01)."

> "Pictures, videos, and external resources were more frequent in articles from encyclopedic wikis. Posology details were more frequent in articles from non-encyclopedic wikis (P<.01)."

### Brulet A 2015 (2)

Brulet A, Llorca G, Letrilliart L. (2015). Medical Wikis Dedicated to Clinical Practice: A Systematic Review. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4392552/

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Flesch reading ease test adapted to each language, applied to sampled articles. Encyclopedic wikis median 26.1 (college graduate level) versus non-encyclopedic 33.9 (college level), P=.041 per Table 7. The review states: "The Flesch reading ease scores were lower in encyclopedic wikis."

> "The Flesch reading ease scores were lower in encyclopedic wikis (Table 7)."
