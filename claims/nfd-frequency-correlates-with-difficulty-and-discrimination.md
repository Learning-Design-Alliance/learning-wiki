# More non-functioning distractors per item correlate with easier items and lower discrimination in both MCQ formats

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` NFD frequency per item correlated positively with item difficulty (r = 0.71 three-option, r = 0.81 five-option) and negatively with discrimination (r = -0.44 and r = -0.38), p < 0.01 both. [→ Al-lawama Manar 2023](#al-lawama-manar-2023)

## Evidence

### Al-lawama Manar 2023

Al-lawama Manar, Kumwenda Ben. (2023). Decreasing the options’ number in multiple choice questions in the assessment of senior medical students and its effect on exam psychometrics and distractors’ function. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04206-3

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Pearson correlation analysis of NFD frequency against item DF and DS in both test versions; the printed correlations r = 0.71 and r = 0.81 are strong, and the discrimination correlations were r = -0.44 and r = -0.38, p < 0.01.

> "There was a positive correlation, r = 0.71 and 0.81, for the three and five option tests, respectively, p-value < 0.01, for both versions."
