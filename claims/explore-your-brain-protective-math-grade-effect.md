## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Intervention students' math grades declined less steeply than controls' from pre to 1-year follow-up (.42 points higher math grade, d = .36). [→ Janssen 2025](#janssen-2025)
`q3 i?` GPA was also higher (.14 points, d = .22), but the GPA effect disappeared when math was excluded, indicating the effect was math-specific. [→ Janssen 2025 (2)](#janssen-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Janssen 2025

Janssen, T. W. P., & van Atteveldt, N. (2025). Explore your brain: A randomized controlled trial into the effectiveness of a growth mindset intervention with psychosocial and psychophysiological components. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 95, 280–302. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12572

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r3`

Linear mixed model on math grades in the cluster RCT (Time × Condition: F(1, 450.28) = 9.26, p = .002). The printed slope was β = .42 with effect size d = .36. Figure 3 shows the effect reflects "a less steep decline in grades, rather than a larger increase in grades for the growth mindset intervention".

> "For math grades, the positive slope, β = .42, 95% CI [.15, .69], indicates that the growth mindset intervention resulted in .42 points higher math grade from pre (T0) to 1‐year follow‐up (T2) than the control condition. This is a small‐medium effect size (d = .36)."

### Janssen 2025 (2)

Janssen, T. W. P., & van Atteveldt, N. (2025). Explore your brain: A randomized controlled trial into the effectiveness of a growth mindset intervention with psychosocial and psychophysiological components. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 95, 280–302. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12572

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Sensitivity re-analysis of GPA without math in the same LMM models; the effect was no longer significant, supporting the article's conclusion of "a selective, relatively large (compared to the literature) protective effect of the growth mindset intervention on math grades (less steep decline)".

> "To test whether the GPA effects were actually driven by the more pronounced effects of math grades, we re‐analysed GPA based on five instead of six subjects (without math). This seemed to be the case, as the Time × Intervention interaction for the adapted GPA without math was no longer significant, F(1, 453.05) = .38, p = .537."
