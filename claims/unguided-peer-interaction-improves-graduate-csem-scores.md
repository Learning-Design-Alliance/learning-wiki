## Evidence

### Ghimire 2024

Ghimire, A. and Singh, C. (2024). How often does unguided peer interaction lead to correct response consensus? An example from Conceptual Survey of Electricity and Magnetism. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ad336c](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ad336c)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

In-class study in a graduate TA professional development course: 42 first-year graduate students answered the CSEM individually for 50 minutes, then 20 self-chosen groups (two groups of three) retook it without instructor guidance or feedback. The article reports "the average individual score was (77  ± 3)%" versus (91 ± 2)% in groups; no overall effect size is printed.

> "Out of the 32 items of the CSEM survey, 19 items had an individual score less than 85%, which decreased to 6 items after peer interaction. As a measure of improvement, along with the standard errors, the average individual score was (77  ± 3)% and the average group score was (91  ± 2)% ."
