# In a classroom field trial, 7th-grade students sequenced by MAPLE achieved higher post-test grades than students sequenced by the Ascending or YBKT algorithms

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students assigned to the MAPLE condition achieved higher post test results and larger pre- to post-test grade gains than students in the Ascending or YBKT conditions. [→ Avi Segal 2018](#avi-segal-2018)

## Evidence

### Avi Segal 2018

Avi Segal, Yossi Ben David, Joseph Jay Williams, Kobi Gal, Yaar Shalom. (2018). Combining Difficulty Ranking with Multi-Armed Bandits to Sequence Educational Content. [doi:10.1007/978-3-319-93846-2_59](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-93846-2_59)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Field study in one school with 7th-grade math students randomly divided into three cohorts (MAPLE, YBKT, Ascending) over 35 days; 92 students took the pretest and 28 completed the post test. MAPLE's post-test average grade was 71.28 versus 43.76 (Ascending) and 67.08 (YBKT); no statistical significance test is reported for grades.

> "As measured by post test grades, students assigned to the MAPLE condition achieved higher post test results than students assigned to the Ascending condition or to the YBKT condition. This effect is also evident when observing the pre- to post- test change in grades."
