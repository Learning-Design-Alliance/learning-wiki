# High-fidelity simulation methods (simulators, 3D models, cadaver-based practice) are associated with superior Acquisition retention, though some studies report contradictory findings

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Advanced methodologies such as simulators, three-dimensional models, or cadaver-based practice were associated with superior Acquisition retention compared to traditional hands-on approaches. [→ Wang L‐W 2025](#wang-lw-2025)
`q2 i?` Several studies found the opposite: e.g., simulator-based TEE training decayed more (−17.3%) than video-based instruction (−8.2%), and supplementary simulator training showed greater decline (−8.1%) than standard training (−3.6%). [→ Wang L‐W 2025 (2)](#wang-lw-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Wang L‐W 2025

Wang L‐W, Liu C‐H, Li W‐Y, Chiang W‐C, Chiu Y‐L, Ma M H‐M, Chen H‐L, Yang C‐W. (2025). Is ultrasound training sustainable? A systematic review of competency retention in healthcare trainees. Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.15751

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Review-level comparison across included studies (Appendix S4). Supporting examples cited include Kule et al (2022) reporting "a 36.5–68% improvement" with simulation-based mastery learning and Schott et al (2021) reporting minimal decay of 2.6% over 8 months.

> "participants exhibited superior retention outcomes in the Acquisition domain when advanced methodologies, such as simulators, three‐dimensional models, or cadaver‐based practice, were incorporated, compared to traditional hands‐on approaches"

### Wang L‐W 2025 (2)

Wang L‐W, Liu C‐H, Li W‐Y, Chiang W‐C, Chiu Y‐L, Ma M H‐M, Chen H‐L, Yang C‐W. (2025). Is ultrasound training sustainable? A systematic review of competency retention in healthcare trainees. Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.15751

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

The review reports contradictory individual findings: Zhao et al (2021), Ochoa et al (2019) (−35.6% vs −24.3%), Thomas et al (2013) (−19.1%), and Edrich et al (2014) (−8.1% vs −3.6%) all found simulation or 3D-model approaches did not improve retention.

> "Zhao et al (2021) found that simulator‐based training for TEE interpretations led to greater skill decay (−17.3%) compared to video‐based instruction (−8.2%) at the 1‐month follow‐up"
