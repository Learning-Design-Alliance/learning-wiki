Evidence¶

Andreeva et al. 2018¶

Nadezhda M. Andreeva, Ivan P. Artyukhov, Elena G. Myagkova, Nikolay I. Pak, Zhamilya K. Akkasynova. (2018). Organizing Blended Learning for Students on the Basis of Learning Roadmaps. Journal of Social Studies Education Research, 9(2), 47-64. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1181973

q2 · i? · design · r2

Experience report of the course "History of computer science" for 4th-year bachelor's students of the physics and mathematics profile at KSPU and KazNPU, with online-broadcast lectures and individual learning roadmaps. The article reports descriptively that 90% overcame the 100-point limit two weeks before the deadline; no inferential test or effect size is printed.

"Two weeks ahead of the credit deadline, it was already 90% of students who overcame the required limit of 100 points."

Andreeva et al. 2018 (2)¶

Nadezhda M. Andreeva, Ivan P. Artyukhov, Elena G. Myagkova, Nikolay I. Pak, Zhamilya K. Akkasynova. (2018). Organizing Blended Learning for Students on the Basis of Learning Roadmaps. Journal of Social Studies Education Research, 9(2), 47-64. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1181973

q2 · i? · causal · r1

In the same course, roadmaps differed in scope: the majority opted for the teacher's expert variant of attending lectures and seminars with low-value backup measures, while the article reports "It was only 5% o f the students who planned the creation of a website". Student feedback described the strategy as fairly effective.

"It was only 5% o f the students who planned the creation of a website which ensures getting the credit."
