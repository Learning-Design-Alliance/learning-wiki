Evidence¶

Gonny Schellings 2011¶

Gonny Schellings. (2011). Applying learning strategy questionnaires: problems and possibilities. Metacognition and Learning, 6, 91–109. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-011-9069-5

q1 · i? · associational · r1

Theoretical argument in the article's review section: questionnaires aimed at 'orienting', 'structuring' and 'evaluating' may be compared with protocols coded for 'monitoring', 'guiding' and 'reflecting', and general 'task-exceeding' questionnaires should be expected to correlate lowly with task-bound think-aloud measures. The article offers this as an argument, not a tested result.

"explaining the low correlations may involve two other aspects: the learning strategies measured and the learning task to which the measurement is related."

Related Claims¶

  • Schellings' questionnaire-thinking-aloud convergent correlation of .51 rests on a small sample and is questionable against other studies — related
  • A task-specific questionnaire built item-by-item from a think-aloud coding taxonomy correlated r=.51 with think-aloud protocols, higher than the -.07 to .42 regularly reported — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • When SRL is measured at a coarse grain size (global self-regulation), students are relatively well able to report on their strategy use — related
  • At scale level, the questionnaire's Elaboration & evaluation scale correlated significantly with the same think-aloud scale (r=.60) while the Orientation & planning scale did not (r=.24) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • In the case study, students answering the questionnaire while thinking aloud showed misunderstanding of items, general rather than task-specific reference perspectives, middle-category 'neutral' answering, and probable social desirability — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
