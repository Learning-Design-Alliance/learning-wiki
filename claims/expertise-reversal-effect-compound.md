# Expertise reversal: cognitive load effects for novices decrease, disappear, and can reverse as expertise increases

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Instructional procedures benefiting novices processing high element interactivity information become counterproductive with increasing expertise as elements become embedded in long-term memory structures. [→ Sweller 2019](#sweller-2019)

## Evidence

### Sweller 2019

Sweller, J., van Merriënboer, J. J. G., & Paas, F. (2019). Cognitive Architecture and Instructional Design: 20 Years Later. Educational Psychology Review, 31, 261–292. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09465-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

This narrative review attributes the expertise reversal effect to Kalyuga et al. (2003, 2012): pre-1998 CLT effects obtained with novices shrink, vanish, or reverse as expertise rises because element interactivity falls via the environmental organising and linking principle.

> "As a consequence, with increasing expertise, the above effects first decrease in size, then disappear, and can eventually reverse (Kalyuga et al. 2003, 2012)."
