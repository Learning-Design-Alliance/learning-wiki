# Blended learning is no more effective than didactic learning at increasing medical students' EBM competency as measured by the Berlin Questionnaire

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Medical students taught EBM via a blended learning approach did not differ significantly in EBM competency from students taught via didactic learning on the Berlin Questionnaire. [→ Ilic Dragan 2015](#ilic-dragan-2015)

## Evidence

### Ilic Dragan 2015

Ilic Dragan, Nordin Rusli Bin, Glasziou Paul, Tilson Julie K, Villanueva Elmer. (2015). A randomised controlled trial of a blended learning education intervention for teaching evidence-based medicine. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0321-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Cluster-randomised controlled trial in which 147 third-year medical students completed outcome assessments one month post-teaching, assessed by a blinded assessor. The Berlin Questionnaire comparison showed "MD=−0.68, (95% CI–1.71, 0.34), p=0.19", a non-significant difference; equivalence was not tested.

> "No significant difference in EBM competency was observed between students undertaking the EBM course using the BL approach compared to students undertaking the DL approach. This outcome was consistent regardless of whether outcomes were assessed via the Berlin Questionnaire [MD=−0.68, (95% CI–1.71, 0.34), p=0.19]"
