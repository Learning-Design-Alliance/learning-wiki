## Evidence

### Lee Hyun Jik 2020

Lee Hyun Jik, Cho Chang Min, Heo Jun, Jung Min Kyu, Kim Tae Nyeun, Kim Kook Hyun, Kim Hyunsoo, Cho Kwang Bum, Kim Ho Gak, Han Jimin, Lee Dong Wook, Lee Yoon Suk. (2020). Impact of Hospital Volume and the Experience of Endoscopist on Adverse Events Related to Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography: A Prospective Observational Study. Gut and Liver 14(2). https://doi.org/10.5009/gnl18537

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the same prospective multicenter observational study, PEP occurred in 95 of 1,061 patients (9.0%) after excluding 130 with pancreatitis at enrollment. Rates were "statistically higher rates of PEP compared with experts (12.0% vs 6.8%, p=0.004)", while PEP did not differ by center volume (9.4% vs 8.0%, p=0.560).

> "However, in terms of endoscopist's experience, less experienced operators showed statistically higher rates of PEP compared with experts (12.0% vs 6.8%, p=0.004). This result was attributed to higher rates of difficult cannulation among less experienced operators than among experts (215/433 [49.7%] vs 234/628 [37.3%], p<0.001)."
