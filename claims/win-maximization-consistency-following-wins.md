# In win maximization, performance is more consistent following wins than losses in both repetition- and alternation-exploitable opponents

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Against repetition opponents under win maximization, stay actions following wins correlate more strongly with win rate than shift actions following losses (z = 2.347, p = 0.019). [→ Yajing Zhang 2023](#yajing-zhang-2023)
`q2 i?` Against alternation opponents under win maximization, actions following wins again have a significantly stronger correlation with win rate than actions following losses (z = 5.019, p < 0.001). [→ Yajing Zhang 2023 (2)](#yajing-zhang-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Yajing Zhang 2023

Yajing Zhang, Thi Kim Truc Huynh and Benjamin James Dyson. (2023). Deliberately making miskates: Behavioural consistency under win maximization and loss maximization conditions. npj Science of Learning 8:55. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00206-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1 correlation analysis of win rate against degree of win-stay (r = 0.708, p < 0.001) and lose-shift (r = 0.423, p < 0.001); a two-tailed z-test showed that "stay actions following wins had a signi ﬁcantly stronger correlation than shift actions following losses".

> "we found that stay actions following wins had a signi ﬁcantly stronger correlation than shift actions following losses (z = 2.347, p = 0.019)"

### Yajing Zhang 2023 (2)

Yajing Zhang, Thi Kim Truc Huynh and Benjamin James Dyson. (2023). Deliberately making miskates: Behavioural consistency under win maximization and loss maximization conditions. npj Science of Learning 8:55. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00206-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1, alternation opponent: win-stay (r = -0.874) and lose-shift (r = -0.446) were negatively correlated with win rate, consistent with win-shift/lose-stay requirements; "Actions following wins once again had a signi ﬁcantly stronger correlation than actions following losses".

> "Actions following wins once again had a signi ﬁcantly stronger correlation than actions following losses (z = 5.019, p < 0.001)"
