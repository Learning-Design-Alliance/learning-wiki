## Evidence

### Kim Kyong-Jee 2019

Kim Kyong-Jee, Kim Giwoon. (2019). Development of e-learning in medical education: 10 years’ experience of Korean medical schools. Korean Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.3946/kjme.2019.131

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive analysis of 7 years of cumulative per-resource hit data (October 2011–October 2018) from the e-MedEdu database, stratified by resource type (Table 2). Videos drew "90% of all website hits"; clinical cases drew 5.8%. No inferential test is reported.

> "The contents that users visited mostly frequently were online videos, which accounted for 90% of all website hits. This was followed by clinical cases (5.8%) and clinical images (2.7%)."

### Kim Kyong-Jee 2019 (2)

Kim Kyong-Jee, Kim Giwoon. (2019). Development of e-learning in medical education: 10 years’ experience of Korean medical schools. Korean Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.3946/kjme.2019.131

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The same 7-year cumulative hits dataset stratified by access device. The study prints Web hits at 72% (n=636,027) and mobile hits at 28% (n=243,499) for online videos. This is a descriptive proportion; no effect size applies.

> "Of all hits on online videos over the past 7 years, 72% (n=636,027) were from the Web and 28% (n= 243,499) were from mobile devices."
