# Initial study trials show subsequent memory effects in left-hemisphere ROIs, but restudy trials after initial study show none

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` During initial study, significant subsequent memory effects occurred in left PFC, left PPC, and left HPC. [→ Liu 2016](#liu-2016)

## Evidence

### Liu 2016

Liu, X. L., & Reder, L. M. (2016). fMRI exploration of pedagogical benefits of repeated testing: when more is not always better. Brain and Behavior. https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.476

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Subsequent memory analysis of initial study trials in the within-subject fMRI experiment (20 subjects, one excluded from fMRI analyses), back-sorted on later recall. Significant effects appeared in "left PFC (t 18 = 5.28, P < 0.001, d = 1.21), left PPC (t 18 = 2.23, P = 0.039, d = 0.51), and left HPC"; no other regions were significant.

> "For the initial study phase (Phase 1), we obtained significant subsequent memory effects in left PFC (t 18 = 5.28, P < 0.001, d = 1.21), left PPC (t 18 = 2.23, P = 0.039, d = 0.51), and left HPC (t 18 = 2.58, P = 0.019, d = 0.59)."

### Liu 2016 (2)

Liu, X. L., & Reder, L. M. (2016). fMRI exploration of pedagogical benefits of repeated testing: when more is not always better. Brain and Behavior. https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.476

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same fMRI experiment, restudy trials back-sorted on subsequent recall. The article reports that "no significant subsequent memory effects were found for any ROIs in the restudy conditions", in contrast to the initial study trials.

> "Unlike the initial study trials, no significant subsequent memory effects were found for any ROIs in the restudy conditions."
