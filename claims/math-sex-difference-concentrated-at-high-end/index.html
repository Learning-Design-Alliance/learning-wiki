Evidence¶

Stoet G 2013¶

Stoet G, Geary DC (2013) Sex Differences in Mathematics and Reading Achievement Are Inversely Related: Within- and Across-Nation Assessment of 10 Years of PISA Data. PLoS ONE 8(3): e57988. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0057988

q3 · i? · associational · r2

PISA percentile analysis of mathematics sex differences across the four assessments. The bottom 5% showed differences of "a 1.9 point difference (2003), favoring girls, to a 2.4 point difference (2006), favoring boys", versus 19.3–21.7 points favoring boys at the high end.

"Comparing the bottom 5% of boys (relative to all other boys) to the bottom 5% of girls (relative to all other girls), the difference in mathematics achievement ranged from a 1.9 point difference (2003), favoring girls, to a 2.4 point difference (2006), favoring boys. In contrast, boys scored from 19.3 (2006) to 21.7 points (2003) higher than girls at the high end of performance."

Stoet G 2013 (2)¶

Stoet G, Geary DC (2013) Sex Differences in Mathematics and Reading Achievement Are Inversely Related: Within- and Across-Nation Assessment of 10 Years of PISA Data. PLoS ONE 8(3): e57988. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0057988

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Boys-to-girls ratio analysis (Table 1) for the 33 countries in all four assessments: "a ratio of 1.7–1.9:1" above the 95th percentile and "a 2.3–2.7:1 ratio" above the 99th percentile. The article links high-end ratios to overrepresentation of men in STEM.

"For the 33 countries that participated in all four of the PISA assessments (i.e., 2000, 2003, 2006, and 2009), a ratio of 1.7–1.9:1 was found for students achieving above the 95th percentile, and a 2.3–2.7:1 ratio for students scoring above the 99th percentile."
