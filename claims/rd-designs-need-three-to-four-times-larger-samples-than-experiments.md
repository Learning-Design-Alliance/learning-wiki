# Clustered regression discontinuity designs typically require three to four times larger samples than clustered experimental designs to produce impact estimates with the same level of statistical precision in education evaluations

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Under clustered RD designs, three to four times larger samples are typically required compared to experimental clustered designs to achieve the same level of statistical precision for impact estimates on student test scores. [→ Peter Z. Schochet 2008](#peter-z-schochet-2008)

## Evidence

### Peter Z. Schochet 2008

Peter Z. Schochet. (2008). Technical Methods Report: Statistical Power for Regression Discontinuity Designs in Education Evaluations. Washington, DC: U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/technical-methods-report-statistical-power-for-regression-discontinuity-designs-in-education-evaluations

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r?`

The report's stated main conclusion from its theoretical and empirical examination of statistical power under clustered RD designs, grounded in "the causal inference and HLM modeling literature" and focused on education research testing intervention effects on student test scores.

> "The main conclusion is that three to four times larger samples are typically required under RD than experimental clustered designs to produce impacts with the same level of statistical precision."
