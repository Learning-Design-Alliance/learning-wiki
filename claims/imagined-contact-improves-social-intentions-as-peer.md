## Evidence

### Eleni Fleva 2014

Eleni Fleva. (2014). Imagined Contact Improves Intentions towards a Hypothetical Peer with Asperger Syndrome but not Attitudes towards Peers with Asperger Syndrome in General. World Journal of Education, 5(1). https://doi.org/10.5430/wje.v5n1p1

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

A 2x2x2 ANOVA on SAQ social scores in a classroom experiment with 416 adolescents (M age = 15.2) found "a significant main effect of imagined activity", with imagined contact participants reporting greater intentions than controls (η2= .009).

> "The analysis of the SAQ social scores revealed a significant main effect of imagined activity with participants in the imagined contact condition reporting greater intentions than control participants [ M= 19.3 vs. M= 18.7, F (1, 400) = 3.76, p= .05, η2= .009]"
