# During-treatment procedure use: Strategies plus Definitions significantly outperformed Definitions-only in applying definitions (an early second-session boost, partial η² = .24), while strategy use did not distinguish the two strategy-trained groups

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` The Strategies plus Definitions group significantly outperformed the Definitions group in applying explicit definitions to explain word meanings in sentences, with the advantage evident only during the second treatment session (partial η² = .24 for the treatment effect). [→ Craigo L. 2017](#craigo-l-2017)
`q3 i?` Use of the figuring-out strategy did not statistically distinguish the Strategies plus Definitions group from the Strategies group; the difference fell short of significance (p = .07). [→ Craigo L. 2017 (2)](#craigo-l-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Craigo L. 2017

Craigo L., Ehri, L. C., & Hart, M. (2017). Teaching community college students strategies for learning unknown words as they read expository text. Higher Learning Research Communications, 7(1). https://doi.org/10.18870/hlrc.v7i1.350

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA comparing definition use in the two definition-trained groups (Table 4: F = 5.68, p = .03, partial η² = .24, S+D > D); the interaction arose because the S+D mean rose to 75% on the second passage while the Definitions group mean declined to 39%, and the article attributes the early boost to prior context-cue analysis reducing practice needed.

> "In contrast, the Strategies plus Definitions group significantly outperformed the Definitions group in applying explicit definitions of the target words to explain their meanings in sentences."
