# College enrollment and persistence did not improve after the science requirement, and college-going declined in later years

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Despite large increases in college-preparatory coursework, college enrollment did not increase, students who attended college were no more likely to persist two years, and college-going actually declined in later years. [→ Montgomery 2010](#montgomery-2010)

## Evidence

### Montgomery 2010

Montgomery, N., & Allensworth, E. M., with Correa, M. (2010). Passing Through Science: The Effects of Raising Graduation Requirements in Science on Course-Taking and Academic Achievement in Chicago. Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/passing-through-science-effects-raising-graduation-requirements-science-course-taking

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Cohort comparison using National Student Clearinghouse data on four-year college entry and two-year persistence, across cohorts before and after the 1997 policy. No improvements in either enrollment or persistence were found.

> "While students were more likely to graduate with college-preparatory credits in science, and in other subjects as well, college enrollment did not increase; moreover, those students who attended college were no more likely to stay in college for at least two years than students were prior to the policy change. In later years, college-going actually declined."
