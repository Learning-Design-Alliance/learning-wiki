# Self Explanation Prompts Improve Learning From Worked Examples

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 studies · `q2`–`q4` · `i2` medium

Prompting learners to explain worked-example steps to themselves — why each step was taken, what principle it applies — improves learning outcomes compared with studying examples without such prompts.

## Subclaims

`q4 i2` Across 69 effect sizes from 64 reports, prompting learners to self-explain while studying or solving problems improved learning outcomes by a moderate average amount (g = .55), across task types, subject areas and levels of education. [→ Bisra et al. 2018](#bisra-et-al-2018)

`q3 i?` In mathematics, prompted self-explanation gives a small-to-moderate immediate improvement in procedural knowledge, conceptual knowledge and procedural transfer, but evidence for gains in classroom settings or after a delay is much more limited. [→ Rittle-Johnson et al. 2017](#rittle-johnson-et-al-2017)

`q2 i?` Eighth-grade students prompted to self-explain each line of a text on the circulatory system gained more from pretest to posttest than students who read it twice, and those who explained most built the most accurate mental models. [→ Chi et al. 1994](#chi-et-al-1994)

## Evidence

### Bisra et al. 2018

Bisra, K., Liu, Q., Nesbit, J. C., Salimi, F., & Winne, P. H. (2018). Inducing Self-Explanation: a Meta-Analysis. *Educational Psychology Review, 30*(3), 703–725. [doi:10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x)

`q4 · meta-analysis (random effects)` · `i2 · medium effect, g=.55` · `n=69 effect sizes from 64 research reports`

A systematic search and meta-analysis of studies in which participants received self-explanation prompts while studying or solving problems, compared with learners who did not. The random-effects weighted mean effect was g = .55. The authors coded 20 moderators, including the type of task (solving problems, studying [worked examples](../elements/demonstration.md), studying text), subject area, level of education, type of prompt and treatment duration, and conclude that self-explanation prompts are a potentially powerful intervention across a range of instructional conditions. Read from the abstract only; moderator results are not reported here.

### Rittle-Johnson et al. 2017

Rittle-Johnson, B., Loehr, A. M., & Durkin, K. (2017). Promoting self-explanation to improve mathematics learning: A meta-analysis and instructional design principles. *ZDM, 49*(4), 599–611. [doi:10.1007/s11858-017-0834-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11858-017-0834-z)

`q3 · meta-analysis` · `i? · described as small to moderate; no effect size in the abstract read` · `n=unreported (in the abstract)`

A meta-analysis of studies that prompted self-explanation during mathematics learning. Prompted self-explanation produced a small-to-moderate improvement in procedural knowledge, conceptual knowledge and procedural transfer on immediate tests. Evidence that it helps in real classrooms, or that the gains are retained after a delay, is much more limited. The effect on immediate outcomes was stronger when learners were scaffolded toward high-quality explanations, and did not depend on whether time on task was equated. This qualifies the claim: the evidence is strongest for immediate, lab-style outcomes.

### Chi et al. 1994

Chi, M. T. H., De Leeuw, N., Chiu, M.-H., & Lavancher, C. (1994). Eliciting Self-Explanations Improves Understanding. *Cognitive Science, 18*(3), 439–477. [doi:10.1207/s15516709cog1803_3](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1803_3)

`q2 · small controlled experiment, two groups` · `i? · no effect size in the abstract` · `n=24 students`

Fourteen eighth-grade students were asked, with no extensive training, to self-explain after reading each line of an expository passage on the human circulatory system; ten control students read the same text twice without prompts. All were tested before and after. The prompted group gained more from pretest to posttest, and within it the students who generated many self-explanations learned with greater understanding than those who generated few. All high explainers reached the correct mental model of the circulatory system, while many unprompted students and low explainers did not.

## Discussion

**Mechanism.** Self-explanation prompts are thought to work by directing attention to the underlying principles and rationale of each solution step, helping learners build schemas that connect the example to domain knowledge — consistent with [Cognitive Load Theory](../theories/cognitive-load-theory.md) accounts of worked-example learning. Without prompts, learners often passively read examples and fail to engage in the inferential processing that produces transferable schemas; prompts convert example study from reading into an active, generative act, in line with [Activation](../principles/activation.md) of relevant prior knowledge.

**Moderators and boundary conditions.** The benefit of self-explanation prompts is widely understood to interact with learner expertise: for novices, prompts scaffold productive processing, but for more advanced learners the same prompts can impose extraneous load or become redundant — the expertise reversal pattern described in [Worked examples can become redundant or counterproductive for advanced learners.](worked-examples-expertise-reversal.md) and formalized in the [Expertise Reversal Effect](../theories/expertise-reversal-effect.md). Prompt design also matters: open-ended "why" prompts can be demanding, and learners who generate poor or shallow explanations may gain little; the added processing can itself be a source of [cognitive overload](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) when examples are complex or working-memory resources are stretched. Designers should consider providing partial explanations, fading prompts over time, and pairing prompts with [worked examples](../elements/demonstration.md) interleaved with [practice](../elements/practice.md) rather than examples alone. Prompt timing is another open design variable: prompts attached to every step may disrupt the smooth reading of a solution, whereas prompts placed after complete solution steps tend to preserve the example's coherence while still eliciting explanation.

**Open questions.** Optimal prompt formats and the durability of gains across domains are not settled by the evidence recorded above, which concerns self-explanation in general rather than worked examples specifically.
