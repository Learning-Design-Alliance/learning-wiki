## Evidence

### Sirajudeen N. 2024

Sirajudeen N., Boal M., Anastasiou D., Xu J., Stoyanov D., Kelly J., Collins J. W., Sridhar A., Mazomenos E., Francis N. K. (2024). Deep learning prediction of error and skill in robotic prostatectomy suturing. Surgical Endoscopy 38(12). https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-024-11341-5

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Descriptive error analysis of 2507 annotated errors across 54 RARP videos, presented in Table 1. The article reports "the greatest number of errors (n = 726)" for pulling the needle out and n = 686 for poor instrument control.

> "The 'Pulling the needle out of the tissue' gesture contained the greatest number of errors (n = 726) (Table 1). 'Tying the Knot' was the next most erroneous gesture (n = 696), whilst 'Cutting the Suture' gesture contained the least [19]. Error category 'Incorrect/poor instrument control' occurred the most frequently (n = 686)"

### Sirajudeen N. 2024 (2)

Sirajudeen N., Boal M., Anastasiou D., Xu J., Stoyanov D., Kelly J., Collins J. W., Sridhar A., Mazomenos E., Francis N. K. (2024). Deep learning prediction of error and skill in robotic prostatectomy suturing. Surgical Endoscopy 38(12). https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-024-11341-5

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Inferential analysis of error distribution across gestures in the annotated RARP dataset. The article reports "χ2 = 1945.15" and "F-statistic = 74.7987" with the printed p values; no effect size is given.

> "Analysis showed a statistically significant difference in the frequency of each error type (χ2 = 1945.15, p < 2.2e-16), some errors were more common in certain gestures (p = 0.0004998). The repeated samples ANOVA test found a statistically significant difference in the number of errors performed between the different gestures (F-statistic = 74.7987, p = 0)."
