# Among top-performing math students, male students show strongly math-oriented math–reading achievement profiles whereas female students' profiles are more balanced

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Keller 2021

Keller, L., Preckel, F., Eccles, J. S., & Brunner, M. (2021). Top-Performing Math Students in 82 Countries: An Integrative Data Analysis of Gender Differences in Achievement, Achievement Profiles, and Achievement Motivation. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/edu0000685

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analytic synthesis of country-specific profile nonoverlap statistics from the PISA integrative data analysis. The math–reading distributions showed "a nonoverlap of 44%, representing a large effect" by the article's nonoverlap benchmarks; no standardized effect size was printed.

> "Female and male students' math–reading profile distributions had, on average, a nonoverlap of 44%, representing a large effect (Table 5; see Figure 5B for the distribution of effect sizes)."

### Keller 2021 (2)

Keller, L., Preckel, F., Eccles, J. S., & Brunner, M. (2021). Top-Performing Math Students in 82 Countries: An Integrative Data Analysis of Gender Differences in Achievement, Achievement Profiles, and Achievement Motivation. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/edu0000685

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Country-specific mean profile scores integrated meta-analytically. Male top performers' math exceeded reading "by, on average, 57.65 points"; the companion result reports female students' math exceeded reading by 22.71 points, a less pronounced difference.

> "mathematically top-performing male students' math achievement clearly exceeded their reading achievement by, on average, 57.65 points (see Table 5)."

### Keller 2021 (3)

Keller, L., Preckel, F., Eccles, J. S., & Brunner, M. (2021). Top-Performing Math Students in 82 Countries: An Integrative Data Analysis of Gender Differences in Achievement, Achievement Profiles, and Achievement Motivation. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/edu0000685

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analytic synthesis of tilt percentages across countries. "87% demonstrated a math tiltM-R" among male top performers versus 66% of female top performers, both majorities but with a gender difference; no standardized effect size was printed.

> "Of all male students, 87% scored higher in mathematics than in reading (i.e., 87% demonstrated a math tiltM-R), whereas 66% of all female students showed stronger achievement tilts in mathematics than in reading"
