Evidence¶

Alharbi 2024¶

Alharbi, A., Nurfianti, A., Mullen, R. F., McClure, J. D., & Miller, W. H. (2024). The effectiveness of simulation-based learning (SBL) on students’ knowledge and skills in nursing programs: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education, 24. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06080-z

q3 · i? · review · r2

Systematic review narrative synthesis of 33 studies (15 RCTs, 18 quasi-experimental; 3,670 participants) on CPR/life-support, critical care and decision-making skills. The review reports "a steady and credible agreement" that SBL enhances knowledge and skills, with effects observed in both randomised and non-randomised designs.

"Despite the diversity in simulation environments, methodologies, and outcomes which encompassed a notable level of heterogeneity among the studies included, there was a steady and credible agreement suggesting that SBL is a potent method for enhancing both knowledge and skills of nursing students."
