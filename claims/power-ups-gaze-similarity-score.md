# Power-up use in the game correlates positively with gaze similarity and with team score

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Number of power ups used correlates with gaze similarity (r = 0.53) and with score (r = 0.47), but not with any individual power-up type. [→ Sharma Kshitij 2020](#sharma-kshitij-2020)

## Evidence

### Sharma Kshitij 2020

Sharma Kshitij, Leftheriotis Ioannis, Giannakos Michail. (2020). Utilizing Interactive Surfaces to Enhance Learning, Collaboration and Engagement: Insights from Learners’ Gaze and Speech. Sensors (Basel). https://doi.org/10.3390/s20071964

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive/correlational analysis of game logs from 18 dyads; printed effect sizes are "r (16) = 0.53, p = .02" for gaze similarity and "r (16) = 0.47, p = .04" for score. Most power ups were used towards the end of the game.

> "We observe a positive correlation with gaze similarity (r (16) = 0.53, p = .02, Figure 14a) and a positive correlation with score (r (16) = 0.47, p = .04, Figure 14b). However, we did not observe any relation with the individual type of power up"
