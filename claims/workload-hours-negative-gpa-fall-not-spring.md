# Workload hours are negatively associated with GPA significantly in the fall semester but only marginally in the spring semester

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Work hours per week were negatively associated with GPA through all four models in fall, but the negative association was attenuated to marginal significance in spring Models III and IV. [→ Okogbaa John 2020](#okogbaa-john-2020)

## Evidence

### Okogbaa John 2020

Okogbaa John, Allen Rondall E., Sarpong Daniel F. (2020). Time Spent at Work and Its Impact on the Academic Performance of Pharmacy Students. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17020496

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Stratified regression analysis of the 384-student survey cohort. Fall work-hours coefficients ranged from −0.016 ± 0.005 (p = 0.0016) in Model I to −0.011 ± 0.005 (p = 0.0236) in Model IV; spring Model IV was −0.010 ± 0.005 (p = 0.0522), consistent with the association being "attenuated (marginally) for Models III and IV".

> "For the fall semester, workload was negatively associated with GPA. This was through Model I to the fully adjusted model (Model IV). However, for the spring semester, the negative association between workload and GPA was attenuated (marginally) for Models III and IV."
