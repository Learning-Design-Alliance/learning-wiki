## Evidence

### Spagnulo R 2025

Spagnulo R, Marzola F, Corso F, Distefano G, Pescio M, Hayashi K, Barontini F, Dagnino G, Althoefer K, Siciliano B, Ourselin S, Arezzo A. (2025). From precision to strength: computer vision for suture quality assessment—an ex vivo pilot study. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-025-12441-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Subjective assessment after each of the 45 procedures using the validated 10-item System Usability Scale (0–100). ANOVA/Kruskal–Wallis found "Flex received significantly lower usability ratings compared to both da Vinci Research Kit (dVRK) and conventional laparoscopy"; experience level did not affect SUS (p = 0.069).

> "A significant difference in perceived usability was observed across platforms (p = 0.003). Post hoc analysis indicated that Flex received significantly lower usability ratings compared to both da Vinci Research Kit (dVRK) and conventional laparoscopy"

### Spagnulo R 2025 (2)

Spagnulo R, Marzola F, Corso F, Distefano G, Pescio M, Hayashi K, Barontini F, Dagnino G, Althoefer K, Siciliano B, Ourselin S, Arezzo A. (2025). From precision to strength: computer vision for suture quality assessment—an ex vivo pilot study. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-025-12441-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

NASA-TLX workload ratings (six descriptors on 10-point scales) collected after each procedure. Significant platform-related differences (p < 0.05) showed "Higher levels of frustration and mental demand were observed when using the Flex system compared to the other platforms".

> "Higher levels of frustration and mental demand were observed when using the Flex system compared to the other platforms, while comparisons involving laparoscopy and dVRK yielded no significant differences"
