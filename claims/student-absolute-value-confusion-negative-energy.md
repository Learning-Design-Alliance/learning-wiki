# A student avoided negative energy by taking absolute values, confusing zero-point translation with absolute value, a move that does not preserve conservation of energy

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In a group problem-solving task using energy bar charts, Phillip's group drew all bars as positive, and Phillip conflated translating all potential energies by a constant (which preserves conservation) with taking the absolute value (which does not). [→ Benjamin W. Dreyfus 2013](#benjamin-w-dreyfus-2013)

## Evidence

### Benjamin W. Dreyfus 2013

Benjamin W. Dreyfus, Benjamin D. Geller, Julia Gouvea, Vashti Sawtelle, Chandra Turpen, and Edward F. Redish. (2013). Negative Energy: Why Interdisciplinary Physics Requires Multiple Ontologies. [doi:10.1119/perc.2013.pr.020](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2013.pr.020)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Video data from the NEXUS/Physics course: Phillip's group drew all bars, including chemical energy bars, as positive, saying "We said absolute value, like the magnitude of the energy." The authors note this avoidance of negative energy is a valid move under some circumstances, but he confuses the two methods of making negative quantities positive.

> "While the former method preserves conservation of energy, the latter does not."
