# Parents want more academic interaction with high schools, but schools focus communication on rules and problems

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · qualitative `r1`–`r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Over three-quarters of interviewed parents felt they got enough information on rules and regulations, but far fewer got enough information on day-to-day academics or help. [→ Roderick 1998](#roderick-1998)
`q2 i?` More than 85 percent of parents were very interested in programs where teachers tell parents what students need to do well. [→ Roderick 1998 (2)](#roderick-1998-2)

## Evidence

### Roderick 1998

Roderick, M., Stone, S., with Arney, M., Chiong, J., DaCosta, K., & Waxman, E. (1998). Changing Standards, Changing Relationships: Building Family-School Relationships to Promote Achievement in High Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://ccsr.uchicago.edu/publications/changing-standards-changing-relationships-building-family-school-relationships-promote

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

1996 parent interviews in three Chicago high schools (n=73). While 77% felt informed about rules, only 38% got enough information about what their child was doing in class, 36% about when the child did well, and 40% about finding a tutor (Figure 5).

> "Over three-quarters of the parents we interviewed felt that they got enough in-formation on rules and regulations"

### Roderick 1998 (2)

Roderick, M., Stone, S., with Arney, M., Chiong, J., DaCosta, K., & Waxman, E. (1998). Changing Standards, Changing Relationships: Building Family-School Relationships to Promote Achievement in High Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://ccsr.uchicago.edu/publications/changing-standards-changing-relationships-building-family-school-relationships-promote

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Same parent interviews (Figure 6). Parents expressed strongest interest in programs building their skills to support academic work and in college preparation; over 95% of African-American and over 75% of Hispanic parents wanted college-preparation programs.

> "More than 85 per-cent of the parents we interviewed said that they were very interested in programs in which teachers let parents know what students need to do well. Nearly three-quarters also expressed interest in finding out how to do more to help with their child’s work in school"
