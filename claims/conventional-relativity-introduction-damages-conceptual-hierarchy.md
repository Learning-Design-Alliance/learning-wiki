# Deriving the Lorentz transformations directly from Einstein's postulates damages students' conceptual hierarchy of space and time without replacing it

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In the usual derivation of the Lorentz transformations from Einstein's postulates, peculiarities of time are derived from the behavior of light velocity, damaging the student's existing conceptual hierarchy without fully deconstructing Galilean space and absolute time, so both frameworks coexist in students' minds. [→ Gürel 2011](#gurel-2011)

## Evidence

### Gürel 2011

Gürel, A. and Gürel, Z. (2011). On the Lorentzian route to relativity. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1107.4748](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1107.4748)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical-pedagogical argument in the section on problems of the conventional approach (type e). The authors argue that deriving Lorentz transformations from Einstein's postulates leaves the student with a confused conceptual hierarchy, and cite educational studies indicating that "both frameworks (Galilean space and time versus relativistic space-time) seem to coexist in students mind".

> "This approach trie s to construct the new conceptual hierarchy without actually first fully deconstructing the fundaments of the former one namely Galilean space and absolute time."
