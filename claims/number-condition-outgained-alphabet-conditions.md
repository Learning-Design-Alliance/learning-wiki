## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Children in the Number condition learned significantly more numbers than children in either alphabet condition, with differences surviving multiple-comparison correction. [→ Piasta 2010](#piasta-2010)

## Evidence

### Piasta 2010

Piasta, S. B., Purpura, D. J., & Wagner, R. K. (2010). Fostering Alphabet Knowledge Development: A Comparison of Two Instructional Approaches. Reading and Writing, 23(6), 607-626. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11145-009-9174-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the randomized experiment's ANOVA interaction contrasts, Number-condition children outgained LNLS (d = 0.22) and LS (d = 0.71) children on number identification; "these differences remained significant after application of the linear step up procedure".

> "children in the Number condition learned significantly more numbers than children in either the LNLS or LS conditions, F(1, 52) = 7.682, p = .004, d = 0.22 and F(1, 52) = 16.123, p < .001, d = 0.71, respectively"
