# Decoding ability predicted retrieval, transfer, and retention, and interacted with modality so that video retention benefits were largest for children with weaker decoding

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Haavisto 2025

Haavisto, M., Lepola, J., & Jaakkola, T. (2025). The “simple” view of learning from illustrated texts and videos. Learning and Instruction, 100, 102200. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102200

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mixed-effects models with decoding as a fixed effect in the 109-child classroom experiment showed "decoding improved retrieval from materials, transfer, and retention with medium to large effect sizes (ΔR2mar = .169, .086, and .098, respectively)"; total variance explained rose minimally (ΔR2con < .011).

> "When controlling for modality with a fixed effect, decoding improved retrieval from materials, transfer, and retention with medium to large effect sizes (ΔR2mar = .169, .086, and .098, respectively)."

### Haavisto 2025 (2)

Haavisto, M., Lepola, J., & Jaakkola, T. (2025). The “simple” view of learning from illustrated texts and videos. Learning and Instruction, 100, 102200. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102200

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the modality-by-decoding mixed model for delayed retention, the interaction term was significant (B = .230, p = .026); children in the lowest 25% of decoding showed substantially higher retention in the video condition, while the difference was minimal for the highest 25%.

> "Table 3 shows that the interaction predicted retention with a small effect size (ΔR2mar = .013): while videos improved retention overall, they were particularly beneficial for children with lower decoding abilities."
