Evidence¶

Gankin 2026¶

Gankin, K., Venkatesh, R., Khokhlovich, E., & Vyshedskiy, A. (2026). Receptive and expressive language phenotyping in over 62,000 autistic individuals. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00445-3

q2 · i? · associational · r2

UHCA and PCA of 11 caregiver-reported expressive items. Dendrogram and PCA (Figure 3) separated Single-Word, Single-Sentence, and Multi-Sentence clusters; the authors report the solution "stable across multiple seeds" and across linkage methods and time points.

"Consequently, the three-cluster solution is superior to both two- and four-cluster solutions based on the average inter-cluster distance of UHCA, stability, and also PCA."

Gankin 2026 (2)¶

Gankin, K., Venkatesh, R., Khokhlovich, E., & Vyshedskiy, A. (2026). Receptive and expressive language phenotyping in over 62,000 autistic individuals. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00445-3

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Chi-square test on Table 7 phenotype-by-severity counts for the four expressive phenotypes (Multi-Sentence 13.2%, Single-Sentence 24.1%, Nonspeaking 28.4%, Single-Word 34.4%). Significant per the printed statistic; no effect size is printed.

"A Chi-square test of independence revealed a significant association between expressive language phenotypes and ASD severity distribution, χ2 (6, N = 62,414) = 8202.30, p < 0.0001."
