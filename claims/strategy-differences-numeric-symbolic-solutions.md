## Evidence

### Eugene T. Torigoe 2011

Eugene T. Torigoe. (2011). How numbers help students solve physics problems. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1112.3229](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1112.3229)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Written-work analysis of final-exam booklets from the large-population study (Table II: 58 numeric, 46 symbolic correct solutions analyzed). On the numeric version 69% used the v = P/m strategy versus 28% symbolic. Percentages are descriptive; no test statistic is printed for this contrast.

> "In general, solutions that began with ﬁnding an unknown in terms of known quantities was found in 86% of the numeric solutions compared to in only 50% of the symbolic solutions."

### Eugene T. Torigoe 2011 (2)

Eugene T. Torigoe. (2011). How numbers help students solve physics problems. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1112.3229](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1112.3229)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Same written-work analysis: 15% of symbolic solutions started with a = F/m, replacing one pair of unknowns with another in a lateral step. The article calls these strategies not very efficient. Descriptive percentages only.

> "On the other hand, strategies that began with equations with multiple unknowns was seen in 32% of the symbolic solutions, compared to only 2% of numeric solutions."
