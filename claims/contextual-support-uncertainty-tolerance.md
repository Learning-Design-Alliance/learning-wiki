# Contextual support—including supervisors performing uncertainty and allowing residents to be uncertain—mediated residents' tolerance of uncertainty and adaptive practices

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Residents were more adaptive when allowed to be uncertain without their authority being questioned, and supervisors performing uncertainty in clinical settings supported this. [→ Gamborg 2023](#gamborg-2023)
`q2 i?` When a supervising physician was not available, residents experienced uncertainty and resorted to ordering more X-rays to confirm hypotheses. [→ Gamborg 2023 (2)](#gamborg-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Gamborg 2023

Gamborg, M. L., Mylopoulos, M., Mehlsen, M., Paltved, C., & Musaeus, P. (2023). Exploring adaptive expertise in residency: the (missed) opportunity of uncertainty. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-023-10241-y

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Discussion of the cognitive ethnographic findings: supervisors performing uncertainty and a culture accepting uncertainty fostered adaptive practices among residents. Qualitative finding, no effect size reported.

> "This study demonstrated the importance of supervisors performing uncertainty in the clinical settings, supporting earlier studies that also stress the importance of fostering a culture of acceptance of uncertainty to improve patient safety (Cristancho et al., 2013; Lingard et al., 2003; Ott et al., 2018). Residents were more adaptive when they were allowed to be uncertain"

### Gamborg 2023 (2)

Gamborg, M. L., Mylopoulos, M., Mehlsen, M., Paltved, C., & Musaeus, P. (2023). Exploring adaptive expertise in residency: the (missed) opportunity of uncertainty. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-023-10241-y

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Observed case excerpt (Sarah's case) from the ethnography: unable to locate a more experienced physician, the resident ordered additional X-rays to confirm her hypothesis, illustrating uncertainty when supervisory support was unavailable. Qualitative observation, no effect size.

> "When she cannot find one, she orders more X-rays to confirm her hypothesis before deciding on a treatment."
