# After group work, cold-calling an individual and asking the group for volunteers produced no significant difference in unique responder rates, though GTAs preferred volunteering

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Becker Erin A. 2017

Becker Erin A., Easlon Erin J., Potter Sarah C., Guzman-Alvarez Alberto, Spear Jensen M., Facciotti Marc T., Igo Michele M., Singer Mitchell, Pagliarulo Christopher. (2017). The Effects of Practice-Based Training on Graduate Teaching Assistants' Classroom Practices. CBE—Life Sciences Education 16(4):ar58. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.16-05-0162

`q3 · i0` · `design · r2`

Comparison of the two post-group-work response strategies across 45 sections: "no significant difference in unique responder rate between the two strategies" with a negligible printed effect size (Cliff's d = −0.02); equivalence was not tested. GTAs preferred asking for volunteers (Cliff's d = 0.57).

> "We found no significant difference in unique responder rate between the two strategies (Cliff's d = −0.02, 95% CI [−0.26, 0.23], p = 0.89), although GTAs were more likely to ask for volunteers than to cold-call students after group work (Cliff's d = 0.57, 95% CI [0.35, 0.74], p = 2.6 × 10−6)."
