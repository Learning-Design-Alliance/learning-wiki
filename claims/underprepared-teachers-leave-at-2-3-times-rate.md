# Teachers with little or no pedagogical preparation are 2 to 3 times more likely to leave teaching than comprehensively prepared teachers

## Evidence

### Espinoza 2018

Espinoza, D., Saunders, R., Kini, T., & Darling-Hammond, L. (2018). Taking the long view: State efforts to solve teacher shortages by strengthening the profession. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/long-view

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The report's synthesis of studies on teacher preparation and turnover finds a preparation continuum associated with attrition: "educators with little to no pedagogical preparation are 2 to 3 times more likely to leave the profession" than comprehensively prepared teachers. No effect size is printed.

> "Studies of the relationship between teacher preparation and teacher turnover suggest educators with little to no pedagogical preparation are 2 to 3 times more likely to leave the profession than those with the most comprehensive preparation"

### Espinoza 2018 (2)

Espinoza, D., Saunders, R., Kini, T., & Darling-Hammond, L. (2018). Taking the long view: State efforts to solve teacher shortages by strengthening the profession. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/long-view

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The report cites a large-scale controlled analysis finding alternative-certification entrants "25% more likely to leave teaching than other teachers" after controlling for school, teacher, subject, workplace, and salary factors. The report notes alternative routes vary in rigor.

> "a recent large-scale analysis that controlled for school and teacher characteristics, subject area, workplace conditions, and district salaries found that teachers who enter the profession through alternative certification pathways are 25% more likely to leave teaching than other teachers"
