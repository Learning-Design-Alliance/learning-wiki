# Teachers with little or no pedagogical preparation are 2 to 3 times more likely to leave teaching than comprehensively prepared teachers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 studies (5 entries) · 3 review `r2`, 1 associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Teachers with little to no pedagogical preparation are 2 to 3 times more likely to leave the profession than those with the most comprehensive preparation. [→ Espinoza 2018](#espinoza-2018)
`q2 i?` In a large-scale controlled analysis, teachers entering through alternative certification pathways were 25% more likely to leave teaching than other teachers. [→ Espinoza 2018 (2)](#espinoza-2018-2)

`q2 i?` Attrition rates are two to three times higher for teachers entering without full preparation, including those without certification, than for comprehensively prepared teachers. [→ Podolsky 2016](#podolsky-2016)

`q2 i?` Teachers with little preparation exit at rates two to three times as high as those with comprehensive preparation before entry. [→ Sutcher 2016](#sutcher-2016)

`q2 i?` One study found teachers with little to no pedagogical preparation were 2 to 3 times more likely to leave the profession than those with the most comprehensive preparation. [→ Saunders 2024](#saunders-2024)

## Evidence

### Espinoza 2018

Espinoza, D., Saunders, R., Kini, T., & Darling-Hammond, L. (2018). Taking the long view: State efforts to solve teacher shortages by strengthening the profession. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/long-view

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The report's synthesis of studies on teacher preparation and turnover finds a preparation continuum associated with attrition: "educators with little to no pedagogical preparation are 2 to 3 times more likely to leave the profession" than comprehensively prepared teachers. No effect size is printed.

> "Studies of the relationship between teacher preparation and teacher turnover suggest educators with little to no pedagogical preparation are 2 to 3 times more likely to leave the profession than those with the most comprehensive preparation"

### Espinoza 2018 (2)

Espinoza, D., Saunders, R., Kini, T., & Darling-Hammond, L. (2018). Taking the long view: State efforts to solve teacher shortages by strengthening the profession. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/long-view

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The report cites a large-scale controlled analysis finding alternative-certification entrants "25% more likely to leave teaching than other teachers" after controlling for school, teacher, subject, workplace, and salary factors. The report notes alternative routes vary in rigor.

> "a recent large-scale analysis that controlled for school and teacher characteristics, subject area, workplace conditions, and district salaries found that teachers who enter the profession through alternative certification pathways are 25% more likely to leave teaching than other teachers"

### Podolsky 2016

Podolsky, A., Kini, T., Bishop, J., & Darling-Hammond, L. (2016). Solving the Teacher Shortage: How to Attract and Retain Excellent Educators. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/solving-teacher-shortage. [doi:10.54300/262.960](https://doi.org/10.54300/262.960)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The report's preparation section synthesizes multiple studies of teacher preparation and attrition, reporting that "attrition rates are found to be two to three times higher" for those without full preparation. No single effect size or sample is printed; the range varies by study.

> "Depending on the study, attrition rates are found to be two to three times higher for those who enter the profession without full preparation, including those without certification, than for those who are comprehensively prepared."

### Sutcher 2016

Sutcher, L., Darling-Hammond, L., & Carver-Thomas, D. (2016). A coming crisis in teaching? Teacher supply, demand, and shortages in the U.S. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/coming-crisis-teaching. [doi:10.54300/247.242](https://doi.org/10.54300/247.242)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In its synthesis of attrition research (Section V), the report states "Teachers with little preparation tend to leave at rates two to three times as high as those who have had a comprehensive preparation before they enter."

> "Teachers with little preparation tend to leave at rates two to three times as high as those who have had a comprehensive preparation before they enter."

### Saunders 2024

Saunders, R., Fitz, J., DiNapoli, M. A., Jr., & Kini, T. (2024). Teacher residencies: State and federal policy to support comprehensive teacher preparation. Learning Policy Institute & EdPrepLab. https://doi.org/10.54300/358.825

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Cited in the report's introduction as background on turnover: "teachers with little to no pedagogical preparation were 2 to 3 times more likely to leave the profession than those with the most comprehensive preparation." The report cites several studies finding higher turnover for entrants without adequate preparation.

> "One study found that teachers with little to no pedagogical preparation were 2 to 3 times more likely to leave the profession than those with the most comprehensive preparation, which includes student teaching, formal feedback on their teaching, and multiple courses in student learning and teaching."
