# The benefits of rubrics on self-assessment accuracy do not depend on the inferential level of the assessment criteria

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` No significant condition × inferential-level interaction emerged for absolute accuracy or bias, so Hypothesis 3 was rejected. [→ Rebecca Krebs 2022](#rebecca-krebs-2022)

## Evidence

### Rebecca Krebs 2022

Rebecca Krebs, Björn Rothstein, Julian Roelle. (2022). Rubrics enhance accuracy and reduce cognitive load in self-assessment. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09302-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mixed repeated-measures ANOVA with condition as between-subjects factor and inferential level (low vs. high) as within-subjects factor. "no statistically significant interaction between condition and inferential level"; the same held for bias.

> "there was also no statistically significant interaction between condition and inferential level, F(1, 90) = 2.41, p = 0.124, ηp2 = 0.02, which indicates that the effects on absolute accuracy did not (substantially) depend on the inferential level of the criteria."
