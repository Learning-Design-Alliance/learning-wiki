## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For students performing an examination more than 10 times, males dominate across main system areas with odds ratios of about 2 compared to females (e.g., cardiovascular OR 2.07, thyroid OR 3.06). [→ Farnaz S 2020](#farnaz-s-2020)

## Evidence

### Farnaz S 2020

Farnaz S, Zoghoul S, Alahmad M, Al Qudah H. (2020). The influence of gender on clinical examination skills of medical students in Jordan: a cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02002-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cross-sectional survey of 188 final-year medical students (99 males, 89 females) in Northern Jordan comparing examination frequency by chi-square/OR analysis. The study reports "the odds of a male doing more than 10 cardiovascular examinations compared to a female student is 2.07" and OR 3.06 for thyroid examination.

> "the odds of a male doing more than 10 cardiovascular examinations compared to a female student is 2.07 (1.13–3.79) and as high as 3.06 (1.52–6.18) for a thyroid examination"
