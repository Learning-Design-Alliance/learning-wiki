Evidence¶

Paul Ginns & Jimmie Leppink 2019¶

Paul Ginns & Jimmie Leppink. (2019). Special Issue on Cognitive Load Theory: Editorial. Educational Psychology Review, 31, 255–259. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09474-4

q2 · i? · review · r2

Re-analyses of data from four recently published studies, each including repeated measures of self-reported mental effort along with workload, time on task, or self-rated certainty of correct task performance. The editorial reports "In three of the four studies, mental effort was more suitably modeled as a non-linear rather than linear predictor of outcomes."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Repeated measures of invested mental effort, perceived task difficulty, interest, and self-assessed understanding differ significantly across measurement points within complex learning tasks and are not adequately represented by their mean — related
  • The relationship between invested mental effort and self-assessed understanding is weak and inconsistent, with most correlations non-significant and mixed directions — related
  • Prior knowledge and intervention condition predict repeated cognitive load and interest measures: prior knowledge lowers effort and difficulty and raises interest and understanding, and prompting groups perceive tasks as more difficult — related
  • Invested mental effort and interest are only weakly and inconsistently positively correlated across measurement points, with some correlations non-significant — related
  • Pairwise relationships between invested mental effort, perceived task difficulty, interest, and self-assessed understanding fluctuate across measurement points, with most correlations stronger when more content knowledge is presented — related
  • Perceived task difficulty correlates negatively with self-assessed understanding and with interest at essentially all measurement points, while interest and self-assessed understanding correlate positively throughout — related
  • Model-based posterior-prediction correlations between the four variables show distinct dynamic patterns across the 21 time points and four intervention groups — related
  • The predictive value of cognitive load ratings for problem performance was mixed: mental effort predicted performance in Experiment 1, perceived difficulty in Experiment 2, but never both at once — related
  • Mental effort relates to self-rated certainty in a cubic (declining–flat–declining) rather than linear pattern — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
