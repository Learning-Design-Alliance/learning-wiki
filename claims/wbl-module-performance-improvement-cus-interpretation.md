# After studying the module, trainees' incorrect neonatal CUS interpretations fell from 49% of answer sheets to 8%, and complete answers rose from 26% to 63%

> **Currency** · newest study 2020 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On pre/post performance tests of CUS interpretation, incorrect diagnoses dropped from 99/200 (49%) to 17/200 (8%) answer sheets (p < 0.001). [→ Ben Fadel Nadya; McAleer Sean 2020](#ben-fadel-nadya-mcaleer-sean-2020)
`q2 i?` Complete answers rose from 52 (26%) pre-test to 126 (63%) post-test (p < 0.001). [→ Ben Fadel Nadya; McAleer Sean 2020 (2)](#ben-fadel-nadya-mcaleer-sean-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Ben Fadel Nadya; McAleer Sean 2020

Ben Fadel Nadya; McAleer Sean. (2020). Impact of a web-based module on trainees’ ability to interpret neonatal cranial ultrasound. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02400-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Performance assessment in which 20 trainees who completed both tests interpreted two sets of ten PACS-derived CUS studies on a radiologist-style reporting template, scored against the formal radiologist report as model answer key. No effect size is printed.

> "While 99 out of 200 answers were incorrect in the pre-test (49%), only 17 of 200 had an incorrect diagnosis in the post-test (8%) (p < 0.001)"

### Ben Fadel Nadya; McAleer Sean 2020 (2)

Ben Fadel Nadya; McAleer Sean. (2020). Impact of a web-based module on trainees’ ability to interpret neonatal cranial ultrasound. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02400-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same performance-test analysis of 200 pre- and 200 post-test answer sheets from 20 trainees, scored complete, incomplete, or wrong interpretation by two trained neonatologists against the radiologist report. No effect size is printed.

> "There were only 52 (26%) complete answers in the pre-test compared to 126 (63%) in the post-test (p < 0.001)"
