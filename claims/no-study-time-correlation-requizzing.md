## Evidence

### Walck-Shannon 2019

Walck-Shannon, E. M., Cahill, M. J., McDaniel, M. A., & Frey, R. F. (2019). Participation in Voluntary Re-quizzing Is Predictive of Increased Performance on Cumulative Assessments in Introductory Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education, 18(2). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.18-08-0163

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of voluntary self-reported weekly study hours: participation "was not correlated with study times" in either week type, with printed correlations below r = .10 (r(237) = 0.0274; r(229) = −0.035), suggesting the re-quizzing–performance link is not explained by more total study time.

> "We observed that delayed re-quizzing participation was not correlated with study times in exam weeks (pairwise r(237) = 0.0274, p = 0.675) or non–exam weeks (pairwise r(229) = −0.035, p = 0.602)."

### Walck-Shannon 2019 (2)

Walck-Shannon, E. M., Cahill, M. J., McDaniel, M. A., & Frey, R. F. (2019). Participation in Voluntary Re-quizzing Is Predictive of Increased Performance on Cumulative Assessments in Introductory Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education, 18(2). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.18-08-0163

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Objective attempt-log analysis of re-quiz durations: "this summed duration over the course of the semester was about 1 hour", with the average attempt close to 5 minutes, indicating a small time commitment.

> "On average, for students who re-quizzed, this summed duration over the course of the semester was about 1 hour (Table 4)."
