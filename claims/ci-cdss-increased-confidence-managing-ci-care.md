## Evidence

### JaKa Meghan M. 2025

JaKa Meghan M., Rossom Rebecca C., Crouse Bethany, Crain A Lauren, Gustafson Sally K., Ekstrom Heidi L., Zibley Laura J., Borson Soo, O'Connor Patrick J., Werner Ann M., Appana Deepa, von Sternberg Thomas L., Hanson Leah R. (2025). Exposure to clinical decision support and training increases primary care clinician confidence in managing cognitive impairment care but not confidence to diagnose. BMC Primary Care. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-025-03053-z

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Pre-post clinician survey within a pragmatic clinic-randomized trial in 38 clinics (CI-CDSS vs usual care), analyzed with GLMM/GEE adjusting for clinicians clustered in clinics. The quoted result "AME = 16.8; OR = 2.24, 95% CI: 1.05, 4.80" reached significance for overall care-management confidence.

> "Clinicians in CI-CDSS clinics did report significantly more confidence in managing CI care from baseline to follow-up compared to clinicians in UC clinics (AME = 16.8; OR = 2.24, 95% CI: 1.05, 4.80, see Fig. 2)."
