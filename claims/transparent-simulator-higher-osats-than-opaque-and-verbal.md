## Subclaims
`q3 i?` First-year medical students trained on the transparent simulator scored significantly higher on the OSATS global rating scale than students trained on the non-transparent simulator and students receiving verbal instruction only. [→ Hu Wen-Gang 2015](#hu-wen-gang-2015)
`q3 i?` Both simulator groups scored significantly higher than the verbal-instruction-only group on the performance assessment. [→ Hu Wen-Gang 2015](#hu-wen-gang-2015)

## Evidence

### Hu Wen-Gang 2015

Hu Wen-Gang, Feng Jia-Yu, Wang Jin, Song Ya-Jun, Xu Xiao-Ting, Zhou Hong, Huang Chi-Bing. (2015). Ureteroscopy and cystoscopy training: comparison between transparent and non-transparent simulators. BMC Medical Education 15:93. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0380-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial in which 36 first-year medical students were assigned to transparent simulator training (Group 1), non-transparent simulator training (Group 2) or verbal instruction only (Group 3), each with 12 one-hour sessions. The Results section reports the printed OSATS means and the pairwise comparisons: "the mean score of Group 1 was significantly higher than that of Group 2 (p = 0.022)" and Group 2 exceeded Group 3 (p = 0.043).

> "In the performance quality assessment, Groups 1, 2 and 3 achieved mean scores of 21.83 ± 3.64, 18.50 ± 4.03 and 15.58 ± 2.23 points, respectively (p = 0.001). Multiple comparison analysis showed that the mean score of Group 1 was significantly higher than that of Group 2 (p = 0.022), and that the mean score of Group 2 was significantly higher than that of Group 3 (p = 0.043)."
