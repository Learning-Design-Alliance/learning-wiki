Poull 1921¶

Poull, L. E. (1921). Constancy of the I.Q. in mental defectives according to the Stanford revision of Binet tests. Journal of Educational Psychology, 12(6), 323–324. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0071851

Interpretation of the retest change distribution from the 126-case institutional sample: the article states "A large percentage of the cases shows variations which operate to change the classification"; no exact percentage or effect size is printed.

"The injustice of disposing of persons under suspicion of mental defect on the basis of a single test is clearly demonstrated. A large percentage of the cases shows variations which operate to change the classification"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • The distribution of IQ changes among mental defectives does not differ from Terman's curve for unselected school children, indicating mental defectives are not more variable than normal subjects — related
  • On retest, institutionalized mental defectives as a group show no IQ deterioration, with a mean change of +1.28 points and a standard deviation of 5.83 — related
  • The study's conclusions rest on only sixty-five individually tested unretarded cases, limiting generalization — related
  • Children whose low first IQs reflected linguistic or environmental deficiency showed substantial IQ gains after gaining command of English or schooling — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Clinic cases retested within less than a month to 15 months show a median IQ difference of 3.5 points, with only four cases above 5 points — reports the opposite
  • The question of the constancy of IQ is not settled; further studies with regular intervals and repeated tests of the same cases over a number of years are needed — a broader claim this one bears on
  • One-year Binet retest gains average one year of mental rank, with defectives gaining about half a year (Berry) — related
