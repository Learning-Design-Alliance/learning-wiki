## Evidence

### Van Kelsey 2025

Van Kelsey, Tasawar Sana, Brendel Elaina B. K., Law Camille, Mahajan Anisha, Brownell‐Riddell Carissa, Diamond Natalia, Ritchie Kerry, Monk Jennifer M. (2025). Using a 'Students as Partners' model to develop an authentic assessment promoting employability skills in undergraduate life science education. FEBS Open Bio 15(3): 506–522. https://doi.org/10.1002/2211-5463.13941

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pearson correlation analysis of Perceived Stress Scale scores against final course grade among survey respondents (Fig. 3C). PSS scores were "negatively associated with students' final grade in the course (P < 0.05)"; average PSS was 28.7 out of 40, in the highest perceived-stress category.

> "Conversely, PSS scores were negatively associated with students' final grade in the course (P < 0.05; Fig. 3C), indicating that students experiencing higher stress levels had lower overall academic achievement in the course."

### Van Kelsey 2025 (2)

Van Kelsey, Tasawar Sana, Brendel Elaina B. K., Law Camille, Mahajan Anisha, Brownell‐Riddell Carissa, Diamond Natalia, Ritchie Kerry, Monk Jennifer M. (2025). Using a 'Students as Partners' model to develop an authentic assessment promoting employability skills in undergraduate life science education. FEBS Open Bio 15(3): 506–522. https://doi.org/10.1002/2211-5463.13941

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pearson correlations of PSS scores with both self-assessed and instructor-assessed Data Extraction Assignment grades (Fig. 3A,B) found "no relationship" (P > 0.05); equivalence was not tested.

> "There was no relationship between students' PSS scores and either student‐assessed assignment grade or the instructor‐assessed assignment grade (P > 0.05; Fig. 3A,B)."
