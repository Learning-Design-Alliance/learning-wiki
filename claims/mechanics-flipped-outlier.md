## Evidence

### Chad T. Kishimoto 2018

Chad T. Kishimoto, Michael G. Anderson, and Joe P. Salamon. (2018). Flipping the Large-Enrollment Introductory Physics Classroom. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1807.03850](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1807.03850)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-hoc archived-score comparison of identical questions (Figure 1 analysis); the three points most below the 45-degree line were mechanics questions, which the authors note represent roughly 20% of the total mechanics questions on the graph. No effect size printed.

> "In Figure 1, the identical questions where the ﬂipped class scores were most signiﬁcantly less than those in the traditional class were from mechanics courses."

### Chad T. Kishimoto 2018 (2)

Chad T. Kishimoto, Michael G. Anderson, and Joe P. Salamon. (2018). Flipping the Large-Enrollment Introductory Physics Classroom. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1807.03850](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1807.03850)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Gender-gap parameter comparison across flipped and traditional courses (Figure 3 analysis); E&M and Waves/Modern showed significant reduction while mechanics did not. The authors attribute this speculatively to student preconceptions and varied prior preparation, without further data.

> "the mechanics course results are generally consistent with no overall improvement, and one course reported a worsening of the gender gap."
