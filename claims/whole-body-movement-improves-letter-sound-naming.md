# Whole-body movement teaching improves 5–6-year-olds' letter-sound naming more than non-motor teaching, immediately and at 17–22-week retention

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` The whole-body movement group improved letter-sound naming significantly more than the control group from T1 to T2 (p < 0.001, d = 2.8) and from T1 to T3 (p < 0.05, d = 0.7). [→ Linn Damsgaard 2022](#linn-damsgaard-2022)

## Evidence

### Linn Damsgaard 2022

Linn Damsgaard, Anne-Mette Veber Nielsen, Anne Kær Gejl, Anne Sofie Bøgh Malling, Søren Kildahl Jensen, Jacob Wienecke. (2022). Effects of 8 Weeks with Embodied Learning on 5–6-Year-Old Danish Children's Pre-reading Skills and Word Reading Skills: the PLAYMORE Project, DK. Educational Psychology Review (2022) 34:1709–1737. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-022-09671-8

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Three-armed RCT (74 CON, 40 HM, 35 WM) of 5–6-year-olds in an 8-week phonics intervention; linear mixed models with group-time interactions. WM beat CON on naming letter-sounds with "p < 0.001, d = 2.8" post and "p < 0.05, d = 0.7" at retention.

> "A between-group analysis showed that WM improved their ability to name letter-sounds correctly significantly more than CON both from T1 to T2 (CONT1–T2 = 82% versus WM T1–T2 = 167%, p < 0.001, d = 2.8) and from T1 to T3 (CONT1–T3 = 120% versus WM T1–T3 = 183% p < 0.05, d = 0.7)"
