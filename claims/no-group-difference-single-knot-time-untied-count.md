# No significant difference between mono and bimanual training groups in time to tie one knot or number of knots that became untied

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There was no statistically significant difference between groups in time to tie one complete intracorporeal knot (p = 0.334); equivalence was not tested. [→ Asencio FA 2018](#asencio-fa-2018)
`q3 i?` There was no statistically significant difference between groups in the number of knots that became untied (p = 0.448). [→ Asencio FA 2018 (2)](#asencio-fa-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Asencio FA 2018

Asencio FA, Ribeiro HASA, Romeo A, Wattiez A, Ribeiro PAGA. (2018). The Effect on Performance Time and Quality of the Knots after Mono or Bimanual Training of Laparoscopic Intracorporeal Knot Tying according to the Gladiator Rule Technique. Rev Bras Ginecol Obstet. https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0038-1649494

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Group comparison in the 41-resident prospective observational training study; Table 2 reports mean times of 107.0 s (group A) and 118.3 s (group B) with p = 0.334, no effect size printed.

> "A comparative analysis of the groups showed that there was no statistically significant difference between the groups when the time to tie one knot was compared."

### Asencio FA 2018 (2)

Asencio FA, Ribeiro HASA, Romeo A, Wattiez A, Ribeiro PAGA. (2018). The Effect on Performance Time and Quality of the Knots after Mono or Bimanual Training of Laparoscopic Intracorporeal Knot Tying according to the Gladiator Rule Technique. Rev Bras Ginecol Obstet. https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0038-1649494

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same group comparison; Table 2 reports knots-untied means of 1.43 (group A) and 1.15 (group B) with p = 0.448, no effect size printed. The authors still note 53 of 184 knots (28.8%) became untied overall.

> "but there was no statistically significant difference between both groups regarding the number of knots that became untied."
