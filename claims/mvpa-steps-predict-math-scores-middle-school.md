# MVPA and average daily steps positively predict mathematics scores in both male and female middle school students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In boys, step count and MVPA positively predicted mathematics scores, with sustained attention, in a model explaining 47.7% of variance. [→ Li Lingshu; Zhang Li 2022](#li-lingshu-zhang-li-2022)

## Evidence

### Li Lingshu; Zhang Li 2022

Li Lingshu; Zhang Li. (2022). The Relationship between Physical Activity and Academic Achievement in Multimodal Environment Using Computational Analysis. Computational Intelligence and Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/9418004

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Forward stepwise multiple regression on data from 176 grade 7-8 students, dependent variable mathematics score. The boys' final equation included MVPA, steps and sustained attention and "could explain 47.7% of the total variation of the dependent variable"; Table 4 prints step beta 0.535 and MVPA beta 0.254.

> "MVPA and steps played a common role among the seventh and eighth-grade students and always had a positive effect on mathematics scores. In males, sustained attention had an effect on mathematics scores; selective attention 1, selective attention 2, alternating attention, and BMI were eliminated in the equation."

### Li Lingshu; Zhang Li 2022 (2)

Li Lingshu; Zhang Li. (2022). The Relationship between Physical Activity and Academic Achievement in Multimodal Environment Using Computational Analysis. Computational Intelligence and Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/9418004

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Stepwise multiple regression for girls in the same cross-sectional sample; the final equation included only MVPA and steps and "could explain 60.1% of the total variation of the dependent variable" (Table 5 prints step beta 0.594, MVPA beta 0.308).

> "Therefore, MVPA and steps could predict mathematics score to a certain extent, and MVPA and steps had positive effects on the improvement of mathematics scores in females."
