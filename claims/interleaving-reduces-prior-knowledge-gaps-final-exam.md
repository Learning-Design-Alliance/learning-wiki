# On the final exam, interleaving reduced gaps for URM students, first-generation students, students with lower mother's education, and students with weaker high school mathematics preparation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (4 entries) · causal `r2` · `q2` · `i1`–`i2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` The Homework Type × URM Status interaction was significant on the final exam (β = 0.22, p < 0.05), with URM students going from below average in the blocked condition to above average in the interleaved condition. [→ Steve Bennoun 2026](#steve-bennoun-2026)
`q2 i2` The Homework Type × First-generation status interaction was significant (β = 0.26, p < 0.05), reversing the gap so first-generation students outperformed continuing-generation students under interleaving. [→ Steve Bennoun 2026 (2)](#steve-bennoun-2026-2)
`q2 i1` The Homework Type × Mother Education interaction was significant (β = −0.11, p < 0.01), with interleaving disproportionately helping students whose mothers have lower education. [→ Steve Bennoun 2026 (3)](#steve-bennoun-2026-3)
`q2 i2` The Homework Type × High school math interaction was significant (β = −0.29, p < 0.001), with interleaving particularly supporting students with weaker high school mathematics preparation. [→ Steve Bennoun 2026 (4)](#steve-bennoun-2026-4)
