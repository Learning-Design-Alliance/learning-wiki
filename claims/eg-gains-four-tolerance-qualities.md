# Experimental-group students improved on all four measured prosocial-tolerance qualities, with the largest gain in courage in upholding position (~20%) and the smallest in independence (~10%)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the experimental group, statistically significant differences were obtained in all measured characteristics (responsibility, intolerance to shortcomings, courage in upholding positions, independence); the greatest dynamics was in upholding one's position (almost 1 point, approximately 20%) and the least in independence (0.5 points, 10%). ## Evidence

### Nagovitsyn et al. 2018

Roman S. Nagovitsyn, Dana K. Bartosh, Aleksandr Y. Ratsimor, Yuri G. Maksimov. (2018). Formation of Social Tolerance Among Future Teachers. European Journal of Contemporary Education, 7(4). https://doi.org/10.13187/ejced.2018.4.754

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Five-point scale ratings of four qualities (Table 4) in the 72-student experiment; the article attributes the largest gain to the case method, in which students "had to defend its position" in discussions; Wilcoxon tests significant at 0.01 or 0.05.

> "The greatest dynamics was obtained in such a quality as upholding one's position (almost by 1 point, which is  approximately 20 %). This was greatly influenced by the case method in the course, which in the discussions had to defend its position."
