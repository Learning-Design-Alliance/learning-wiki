## Subclaims
`q2 i?` An independent t-test of accuracy/rate showed the control group's average words per minute exceeded the treatment group's after the five-week iPad use, and the pre-existing rate gap became more significant at the study's conclusion. [→ Roser 2017](#roser-2017)

## Evidence

### Roser 2017

Roser, M. (2017). iPadegogy of reading: Do iPads influence studens' reading? Teachers and Curriculum, 17(2), 25-32. http://dx.doi.org/10.15663/tandc.v17i2.176

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Independent t-test result reported in the Results section of the quasi-experimental study: the control group showed a greater average reading rate in words per minute than the treatment group after five weeks. The article states the treatment group was "significantly slower in their reading rate ability" at the conclusion; no effect size is printed.

> "there was a greater difference in the average reading of words per minute from the control group, compared to the treatment group, once the iPads had been used as an e-reader and application for the five -week duration."
