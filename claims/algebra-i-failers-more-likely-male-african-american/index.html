Evidence¶

Rickles 2016¶

Rickles, J., Heppen, J., Taylor, S., Allensworth, E., Michelman, V., Sorensen, N., Walters, K., & Clements, P. (2016). Getting Back on Track: Who Needs to Recover Algebra Credit After Ninth Grade? (Research Brief 3). American Institutes for Research & University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://www.air.org/CreditRecovery

q2 · i? · associational · r?

Descriptive comparison of CPS administrative records for first-time ninth graders enrolled in Algebra I in 2010-11 or 2011-12. The brief reports that students who failed were "about as likely to be English learners, or to have identified disabilities" as students who passed, with the male difference at 58% vs. 47%.

"Students who failed Algebra did not come from communities with higher rates of poverty than students who passed, on average. They also were about as likely to be English learners, or to have identified disabilities."
