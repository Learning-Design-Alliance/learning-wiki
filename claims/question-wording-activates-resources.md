## Evidence

### Savannah L. Mitchem 2016

Savannah L. Mitchem, Dina Zohrabi Alaee, and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2016). Student understanding of electric and magnetic fields in materials. [doi:10.1119/1.4991376](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4991376)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Authors' interpretation from comparing the identically worded E-field and B-field exam questions and student responses. They argue the question itself can activate certain resources, and that although wording was identical for both parts, it favored saturation as opposed to breakdown.

> "The phrase “cannot become more magnetized” in the question is essentially the definition of saturation, and this may have activated resources related to saturation."

### Savannah L. Mitchem 2016 (2)

Savannah L. Mitchem, Dina Zohrabi Alaee, and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2016). Student understanding of electric and magnetic fields in materials. [doi:10.1119/1.4991376](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4991376)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

From reviewing 2015 magnetization lecture recordings and student responses: students used over twenty different words for the aligning 'thing,' including correct terms like 'magnetic moment' and incomplete ones like 'little pieces.' The authors link this to Borges and Gilbert's causal-agent finding, where students fill in resources to bridge logical gaps.

> "Over twenty diﬀerent words were used for this “thing,” and most of the pictures drawn were arrows lining up with each other, indicating a super-atomic model of magnetization."
