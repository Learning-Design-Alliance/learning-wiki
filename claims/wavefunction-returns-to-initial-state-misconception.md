## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On a multiple-choice energy-measurement question given to 76 students, 36% claimed the wavefunction evolves back to the initial state a long time after the measurement, and 20% thought it would remain in the original state. [→ Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#guangtian-zhu-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)
`q2 i?` On a position-measurement question, only 26% of the 76 students answered correctly by choosing both statements (1) and (3), while 41% believed the wavefunction returns to the pre-measurement state after a long time. ## Evidence

### Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Improving Students' Understanding of Quantum Measurement. Originally published 2008. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06926](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06926)

Multiple-choice question Q1, given to 76 students about the state of the system long after an energy measurement in a 1D infinite square well. "36% of the students claimed that the wavefunction will collapse upon the energy measurement but evolve back to the initial state long time after the measurement."
