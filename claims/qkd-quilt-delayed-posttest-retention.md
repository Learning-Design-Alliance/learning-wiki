# Gains from the QKD QuILT persisted on a delayed posttest administered roughly two months later

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` On matched posttest-to-delayed-posttest questions, performance remained substantially above pretest levels roughly two months after the QuILT, with printed Cohen's d of 0.36 and 0.63 on the two retained questions. [→ DeVore 2016](#devore-2016)
`q3 i?` On one retained question, slightly over 15% of students answered incorrectly on the delayed posttest, compared with roughly half on the pretest. [→ DeVore 2016 (2)](#devore-2016-2)

## Evidence

### DeVore 2016

DeVore, S. and Singh, C. (2016). An Interactive Learning Tutorial on Quantum Key Distribution. Originally published 2013. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1601.01301](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1601.01301)

`q3 · i1` · `design · r3`

Delayed posttest given to 24 students in one of the three classes as part of the final exam, with Wilcoxon ranked-sum tests on matched posttest/delayed data. The table rows for the two retained questions print Cohen's d of 0.36 and 0.63 with W values of 552 and 540.

> "The delayed posttest results are based on answers from 24 students (with the associated Wilcoxon ranked-sum test and Cohen’s d calculations performed with matched posttest and delayed posttest data)"

### DeVore 2016 (2)

DeVore, S. and Singh, C. (2016). An Interactive Learning Tutorial on Quantum Key Distribution. Originally published 2013. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1601.01301](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1601.01301)

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Question 5, which tests whether the photon's polarization can be determined only when the detector clicks, retained high performance: "slightly over 15% of students answered this question incorrectly" two months later, versus roughly half on the pretest.

> "Table 2 shows that a fter two months slightly over 15% of students answered this question incorrectly"
