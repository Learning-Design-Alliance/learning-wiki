# Structured note-taking and MCQ creation as post-seminar homework produced no statistically significant difference in first-year physiotherapy students' summative exam performance

## Evidence

### Wrzesińska Magdalena A. 2025

Wrzesińska Magdalena A., Rakoczy Jarosław, Binder-Olibrowska Katarzyna W., Kostyła Magdalena, Allott Vincent E.S., Harris Samuel R.L., Walsh Jason L., Handa Ashok, Harris Benjamin H.L. (2025). Comparing structured note-taking and multiple-choice question writing as learning strategies among physiotherapy students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08272-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Cluster-randomised controlled crossover trial of 173 first-year physiotherapy students (168 completing the exam) in a psychology course; standardised T1/T2 scores compared between arms via Mann-Whitney U and within arms via Wilcoxon signed-rank. The inter-group comparisons showed "no significant differences detected" and "no significant variance between the two arms"; intra-group comparisons were also non-significant (p = 0.142; p = 0.111).

> "In the inter-group assessment of T1, distinguishing between Arm A and Arm B, there were no significant differences detected (p = 0.161; r = 0.125, 95% CI [− 0.052, 0.299]). Likewise, the inter-group analysis for T2 also demonstrated no significant variance between the two arms (p = 0.296; r = − 0.094, 95% CI [− 0.263, 0.079])"
