## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Both HF and LF groups improved theoretical knowledge significantly from pre- to post-test, with no significant intergroup difference. [→ Massoth C 2019](#massoth-c-2019)

## Evidence

### Massoth C 2019

Massoth C, Röder H, Ohlenburg H, Hessler M, Zarbock A, Pöpping DM, Wenk M. (2019). High-fidelity is not superior to low-fidelity simulation but leads to overconfidence in medical students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1464-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized trial of 135 fourth-year medical students allocated to HF (n=67) or LF (n=68) ALS training; 20-item multiple-choice test administered pre and post. Mean scores rose from 12 ± 2.5 (HF) and 11.5 ± 2.1 (LF) to 16.5 ± 2.0 (HF) and 16 ± 2.6 (LF); "no significant intergroup differences were detected".

> "Both groups improved their theoretical knowledge significantly (p < 0.001), however, no significant intergroup differences were detected (p > 0.05)"
