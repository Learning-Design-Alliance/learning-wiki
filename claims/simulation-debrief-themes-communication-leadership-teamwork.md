## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Watters Colm 2015

Watters Colm, Reedy Gabriel, Ross Alastair, Morgan Nicola J, Handslip Rhodri, Jaye Peter. (2015). Does interprofessional simulation increase self-efficacy: a comparative study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2014-005472

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Thematic analysis of open-ended responses (11 initial categories revised to a final framework of teamwork, communication and leadership, with multiple researchers coding to minimise bias). The quote names communication as "The most common theme to emerge from the data".

> "The most common theme to emerge from the data was the value placed on communication. Learners reported (A) the importance of being able to practice communicating with colleagues in a ‘mock’ clinical setting and (B) enhanced understanding of the link between communication skills and clinical outcomes."

### Watters Colm 2015 (2)

Watters Colm, Reedy Gabriel, Ross Alastair, Morgan Nicola J, Handslip Rhodri, Jaye Peter. (2015). Does interprofessional simulation increase self-efficacy: a comparative study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2014-005472

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Leadership theme from the same thematic analysis: participants described enabling others to lead, nominating a leader, and juniors sometimes taking "to take [a] leadership role".

> "Learners said that they had increased awareness of the need to identify who was leading clinical scenarios so that they could adjust their behaviour appropriately."

### Watters Colm 2015 (3)

Watters Colm, Reedy Gabriel, Ross Alastair, Morgan Nicola J, Handslip Rhodri, Jaye Peter. (2015). Does interprofessional simulation increase self-efficacy: a comparative study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2014-005472

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Teamwork theme from the same analysis; participants described communication as sharing the cognitive burden of emergencies, e.g. "through communication my team helped to work out [the] problems".

> "Learners were quick to realise that by communicating with the team the cognitive and psychological burden of the clinical emergency could be shared"
