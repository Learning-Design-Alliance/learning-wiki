# Synchronous online discussions produce higher behavioral, emotional, and cognitive engagement than asynchronous online discussions in online math courses

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Students in synchronous online discussion conditions showed significantly higher behavioral, emotional, and cognitive engagement than students in asynchronous online discussion conditions. [→ Yorganci 2025](#yorganci-2025)

## Evidence

### Yorganci 2025

Yorganci, S. (2025). The impact of synchronous online discussions and online flipped learning on student engagement and self-regulation among preliminary undergraduates in a basic math course. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10459-0

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

MANCOVA on post-test engagement scores (pretest as covariate) among 121 undergraduates found a significant multivariate main effect of discussion type, Wilks' Λ = .889, p < .01, "η 2 = .111". Univariate effects on behavioral, emotional, and cognitive engagement were each significant at p < .025 (Bonferroni).

> "The results also revealed that the significant main effect of online discussion, Wilks’ /u1D706 = .889, F (3, 114) = 4.74, p < .01, η 2 = .111. The effect of online discussions was significant for behavioral engagement (F (1, 116) = 8.725, p < .25, η 2 = .070), for emotional engagement (F (1, 116) = 7.584, p < .25, η 2 = .061), and for cognitive engagement (F (1, 116) = 6.461, p < .25, η2 = .053)"
