## Evidence

### Cepeda et al. 2008

Nicholas J. Cepeda, Edward Vul, Doug Rohrer, John T. Wixted, and Harold Pashler. (2008). Spacing effects in learning: A temporal ridgeline of optimal retention. Psychological Science, 19(11). https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Internet-based experiment with 1354 subjects learning 32 obscure facts in two sessions separated by variable gaps, tested after retention intervals of 7 to 350 days across 26 gap-by-RI conditions. The article reports "a 64% increase, d = 1.1, in final recall" at the optimal gap versus a zero-day gap.

> "For each retention interval, final performance always rose initially with increasing gap and then fell as gap was further increased. The effects of gap were very large in magnitude: For a fixed amount of study time, the optimal gap provided a 64% increase, d = 1.1, in final recall, and a 26% increase, d = 1.5, in final recognition, as compared to a zero-day gap"
