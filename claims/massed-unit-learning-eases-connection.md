# Short maze units learned under massed conditions connect into a serial whole with great economy, whereas units learned under distributed conditions connect only with extreme difficulty

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Rats that learned the four short sections under massed conditions all effected the serial connections, several without a single error. [→ Pechstein 1921](#pechstein-1921)
`q2 i?` When units are learned under distributed conditions the complex act of connection is extremely difficult; under massed conditions it becomes extremely simple. [→ Pechstein 1921](#pechstein-1921)

## Evidence

### Pechstein 1921

Pechstein, L. A. (1921). Massed vs. Distributed Effort in Learning. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0070137

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

In the rat experiment, after learning Sections I-IV under massed conditions on successive days, each rat was set the problem of connecting the units into serial order the following day; all succeeded, several errorlessly. The author calls this "the most striking feature of the entire situation".

> "Not only did each member of the group effect the perfect connections under massed conditions, but several made the complex step without securing a single error."
