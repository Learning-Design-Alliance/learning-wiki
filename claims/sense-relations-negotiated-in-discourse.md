# Sense relations such as synonymy are negotiated in discourse rather than pre-existing

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Declarative knowledge of sense relations like synonymy is a provisional meaning bank drawn on in discourse; equivalences are negotiated locally and do not pre-exist interaction. [→ Robinson 1988](#robinson-1988)

## Evidence

### Robinson 1988

Robinson, Peter J. (1988). Procedural and Declarative Knowledge in Vocabulary Learning: Communication and the Language Learner's Lexicon. Paper presented at the 22nd Annual TESOL Convention, Chicago. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED296585

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument drawing on McCarthy's (1987) pragmatic relabelling of synonymy as 'equivalence' and Brazil's prominence analysis: whether 'dying to' counts as equivalent to 'want to' depends on the speakers' local choices, which a componential grid cannot capture.

> "The declarative knowledge we have of sense relations like synonymy, antonymy etc. is a 'provisional', 'meaning bank'( Robin3on 1988a)- or base competence- to be drawn on in discourse. Such relations are subjectto negotiation, they do 1.1t pre-exist it."
