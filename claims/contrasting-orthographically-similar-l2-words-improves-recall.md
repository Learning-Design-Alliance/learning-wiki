# Contrasting orthographically similar L2 words during multiple-choice learning improves their subsequent recall in the productive direction

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Orthographically similar L2 words were produced significantly more accurately in recall when they had been contrasted during learning than when they had not, an advantage not found for semantically similar words. [→ Baxter 2022](#baxter-2022)

## Evidence

### Baxter 2022

Baxter, P.A.; Bekkering, H.; Dijkstra, A.F.J.; Droop, W.; Hurk, M.M. van den; Léoné, F.T.M. (2022). Contrasting orthographically similar words facilitates adult second language vocabulary learning. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2022.101582

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1 recall posttest with 56 analyzed adults learning Dutch–pseudoword pairs in a mixed-effects regression; the sorted (contrasting) versus shuffled condition interaction with similarity was significant, with contrasted orthographically similar words recalled at "Mprop_cor = 0.24" versus 0.20 uncontrasted.

> "the orthographically similar words were produced significantly more accurately when they had been contrasted (Mprop_cor = 0.24, SD = 0.43) than when they had not (Mprop_cor = 0.20, SD = 0.40), which was not the case for the semantically similar words."
