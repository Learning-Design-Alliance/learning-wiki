Evidence¶

Fronius 2019¶

Fronius, Darling-Hammond, Persson, Guckenburg, Hurley, & Petrosino. (2019). Restorative Justice in U.S. Schools: An Updated Research Review. WestEd Justice & Prevention Research Center. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED595733

q1 · i? · review · r2

The review reports Rubio's (2018) in-depth interviews with 10 research participants involved in the first three years of RJ implementation at various school sites; "nine of the research participants" endorsed district-provided professional development as necessary.

"nine of the research participants stated that "having district-provided professional development and support was necessary for effective implementation of restorative practices.""

Fronius 2019 (2)¶

Fronius, Darling-Hammond, Persson, Guckenburg, Hurley, & Petrosino. (2019). Restorative Justice in U.S. Schools: An Updated Research Review. WestEd Justice & Prevention Research Center. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED595733

q1 · i? · review · r2

The same qualitative investigation of 10 participants (principals, counselors, specialists, and facilitators) in California found "8 of the 10 participants" cited adequate preparation and implementation time as a significant factor.

"Rubio found that 8 of the 10 participants "indicated that adequate time to prepare for and implement RJ practices was a significant factor to consider when looking at implementing restorative practices.""
