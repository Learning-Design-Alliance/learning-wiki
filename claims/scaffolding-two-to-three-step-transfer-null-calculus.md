# In calculus-based introductory physics, providing a solved isomorphic two-step problem with scaffolding does not significantly improve performance on a three-step isomorphic quiz problem

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Calculus-based students given the solved snowboard problem and scaffolding (interventions 1-3) scored 6.4, 7.3, and 6.8 out of 10 on the putty quiz problem versus 6.3 for the comparison group, and none of the differences were statistically significant. [→ Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2012](#shih-yin-lin-and-chandralekha-singh-2012)

## Evidence

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2012

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2012). Using an Isomorphic Problem Pair to Learn Introductory Physics: Transferring from a Two-step Problem to a Three-step Problem. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.9.020114](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.9.020114)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Recitation-quiz study in which calculus-based students were randomly divided into a comparison group and three scaffolding intervention groups; ANOVA of rubric scores found no statistically different performance from that of the comparison group (comparison mean 6.3/10). No effect size is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "In the calculus-based course, the comparison group students who solved the quiz problem on their own received an average score of 6.3 out of 10. The average scores of the three intervention groups were similar. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) indicates that none of the intervention groups in the calculus-based course show a statistically different performance from that of the comparison group."
