## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Higher-skill students who continued in single-period algebra experienced increased failure rates and declining average grades after the policy. [→ Claire Durwood 2010](#claire-durwood-2010)

## Evidence

### Claire Durwood 2010

Claire Durwood, Emily Krone, and Christopher Mazzeo. (2010). Are Two Algebra Classes Better Than One? The Effects of Double-Dose Instruction in Chicago. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. http://ccsr.uchicago.edu/content/page.php?cat=3&content_id=34

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the CCSR cohort study, students with slightly above-average skills "were now the weaker students in their classes and their course performance suffered accordingly," even though they learned more algebra than similar students in prior cohorts.

> "Failure rates actually increased for higher-skill students who continued to take single-period algebra, and their average grades declined."
