Evidence¶

Julia J. Yi 2026¶

Julia J. Yi, A. Angelique Aitken, Emily Sharp, Adiba Nusrat, Micheal Sandbank. (2026). What Works for Teaching Word Reading to Adolescents? A Meta-Analytic Investigation. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10124-9

q3 · i1 · quant-synthesis · r?

RVE meta-analysis (correlated and hierarchical effects working model) of 200 effects nested in 34 studies of word-reading interventions for middle and high school students. The printed effect size is g = 0.30: the summary effect "was 0.30 (SE = 0.11, p = .009)"; half the sample were identified as having a learning disability.

"The summary effect of word-reading interventions on all adolescent literacy outcomes, estimated from 200 effects across 34 studies, was 0.30 (SE = 0.11, p = .009)."

Related Claims¶

  • The MSDS curriculum showed no statistically significant effects on word recognition, spelling, fluency, or comprehension outcomes — related
  • SpellRead™ shows potentially positive effects on alphabetics for adolescent readers, with an average improvement index of +23 across two studies — related
  • Word-reading interventions significantly improve decoding, nonword decoding, reading comprehension, and spelling (g = 0.29-0.45) but not reading fluency — related
  • Prior reviews report positive word-reading intervention effects for adolescents but with a wide range (0-1.31) due to broad outcome conceptualization — related
  • SpellRead™ shows potentially positive effects on comprehension for adolescent readers, with an average improvement index of +11 — related
  • In the Rashotte, MacPhee, and Torgesen (2001) RCT, SpellRead™ produced statistically significant positive effects on alphabetics and reading fluency for fifth- and sixth-grade struggling readers after eight weeks — related
