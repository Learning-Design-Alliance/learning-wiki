# Students' short-term recall knowledge scores rose from pre-test to post-test in both virtual flipped and virtual traditional formats, based on within-group pre-test/post-test comparisons

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Wilcoxon signed-rank tests showed that scores rose significantly from pre-test to post-test within both instructional methods, with large effect sizes (r > 0.5). [→ Alharbi F 2022](#alharbi-f-2022)

## Evidence

### Alharbi F 2022

Alharbi F, Alwadei SH, Alwadei A, Asiri S, Alwadei F, Alqerban A, Almuzian M. (2022). Comparison between two asynchronous teaching methods in an undergraduate dental course: a pilot study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-022-03557-7

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Within-group pre/post comparisons in the crossover pilot (16 per group per period). Effect sizes were "great (r > 0.5)": r = 0.61 and 0.59 for group 1 under VTL and VFL, r = 0.58 for group 2 under both formats.

> "Results showed significant improvement in post-test scores across the two instructional methods (P < 0.01). This indicates that, regardless of the instructional approach, both instructional methods effectively improved students' short-term recall knowledge"
