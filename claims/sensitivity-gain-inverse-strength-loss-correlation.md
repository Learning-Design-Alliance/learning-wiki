`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Spearman correlation analysis of T0–T1 change scores in the same single-group cohort, with no control group. Table 3 is a Spearman's rho correlation matrix: dominant-hand dynamometer change correlates rho = −0.396 with medial ring finger and −0.394 with ulnar ring finger sensitivity change in the dominant hand. The authors' text labels these "r2" and cites 0.632 for the non-dominant hand, but in Table 3 0.632 is the correlation between the non-dominant ulnar ring finger and little finger sensitivity changes; the non-dominant dynamometer change correlates −0.448 and −0.356 with the dominant-hand ring finger measures. The authors note no studies have explicitly documented this relationship in a young healthy population and propose a neuromuscular-control readjustment as a possible explanation.

> "An increase in the sensitivity of the medial ring finger (r2 = 0.396*) and ulnar ring finger (r2 = 0.394*) in the dominant hand is associated with a loss of strength in the dominant hand, while in the non-dominant hand, the association is in the same direction, but in the meniscus (r2 = 0.632**)."
