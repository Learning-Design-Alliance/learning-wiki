Janssen M 2014¶

Janssen M, Toussaint HM, van Mechelen W, Verhagen EALM. (2014). Effects of acute bouts of physical activity on children's attention: a systematic review of the literature. SpringerPlus. https://doi.org/10.1186/2193-1801-3-410

q2 · i? · review · r2

Review's synthesis of the six laboratory studies (treadmill or ergometer bouts around 60% HRmax, flanker or visual attention tasks). Reports "Five of the six la- boratory studies found a significant effect on cognitive control of attention."

"Five of the six la- boratory studies found a significant effect on cognitive control of attention. One laboratory study (Cereatti et al. 2009) found a significant improvement in reaction time, one study showed a maintenance of accuracy of task (Drollette et al. 2012) and three studies showed a signifi- cant improvement on accuracy on the task and not on reaction time"

Related Claims¶

  • Evidence for effects of acute bouts of physical activity on children's attention is weak and inconclusive — related
  • Type of PA may moderate acute effects: coordinative exercises outperformed a normal PE lesson of the same intensity — related
  • Age may moderate acute PA effects on attention: effects appeared for 9–10 year olds but not 7–8 year olds — related
  • Duration and intensity of PA bouts may moderate attention effects, consistent with an inverted-U hypothesis — related
  • Acute moderate aerobic exercise speeds children's reaction times without improving accuracy, with greater speed gains on choice than simple reaction time tasks — reports the opposite
  • Exercise intensity differentially moderates acute exercise cognitive benefits, with moderate intensity favoring executive functions and high intensity favoring information processing — related
  • School-setting studies show no overall significant difference between active and classroom lessons, but subgroup effects emerge — related
  • Null Flanker effects under ecologically realistic exercise constrain claims of broad, generalized enhancement of executive control — reports the opposite
