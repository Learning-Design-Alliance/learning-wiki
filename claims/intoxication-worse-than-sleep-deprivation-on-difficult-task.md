## Subclaims
`q3 i3` For pegboard exchange, participants took less time but performed significantly worse when intoxicated compared with sleep deprived; contrasts between task levels showed large effect sizes (ranging from 0.51 to 0.69) for all variables except time. ## Evidence

### Mohtashami Fariba MD 2014

Mohtashami Fariba MD, Thiele Allison MD, PhD, Karreman Erwin PhD, Thiel John MD. (2014). Comparing Technical Dexterity of Sleep-Deprived Versus Intoxicated Surgeons. JSLS : Journal of the Society of Laparoendoscopic Surgeons. https://doi.org/10.4293/JSLS.2014.00142

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Interaction contrasts from the repeated-measures ANOVA (Table 1): overall interaction effects were not significant, but individual contrasts such as task 2 vs task 3 bimanual dexterity (P = .010, r = 0.76) and overall performance (P = .043, r = 0.65) were significant with large effect sizes.

> "when tasks 2 and 3 were compared, effect sizes for all variables except time indicated that large effects were present (ranging from 0.51 to 0.69, while the effect size for time is considered small at 0.15)."
