# Junior high math teachers were more controlling and less supportive than elementary teachers, and classrooms with the strongest such changes showed the largest declines in math motivation

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Junior high math teachers were more controlling, less trusting, and used more social-comparative grading than elementary teachers; where these changes were strongest, students showed larger declines in math expectancies, ability self-concepts, and task values. Classroom-level analyses from the transition longitudinal study reported via Midgley et al. (1988, 1989a, b): junior high teachers were "more controlling, less trusting of their students" than elementary teachers, and were seen as less supportive and caring.

> "Some key findings were that junior high math teachers were more controlling, less trusting of their students, and used more social comparative grading strategies than did elementary school teachers."
