## Evidence

### Kissel KA 2025

Kissel KA, Krewulak KD, Poulin TG, Parhar KKS, Niven DJ, Doiron VM, Fiest KM. (2025). Understanding ICU Nursing Knowledge, Perceived Barriers, and Facilitators of Sepsis Recognition and Management: A Cross-Sectional Study. Critical Care Explorations. https://doi.org/10.1097/CCE.0000000000001200

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Open-ended survey responses on sepsis recognition, coded by TDF domain. Barriers included “confounding pictures” (Skills), perceived confidence or competence (Beliefs about Capabilities), a post-pandemic shift in care goals, lack of screening reminders, and laboratory blood collection delays.

> "approximately half of the respondents shared several barriers (57.4%, n = 58) and facilitators (62.4%, n = 63) that they perceived to impact sepsis recognition"

### Kissel KA 2025 (2)

Kissel KA, Krewulak KD, Poulin TG, Parhar KKS, Niven DJ, Doiron VM, Fiest KM. (2025). Understanding ICU Nursing Knowledge, Perceived Barriers, and Facilitators of Sepsis Recognition and Management: A Cross-Sectional Study. Critical Care Explorations. https://doi.org/10.1097/CCE.0000000000001200

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Open-ended survey responses on sepsis management, coded by TDF domain. Barriers included challenges navigating patient orders, delayed access to equipment or medications including antibiotics, patient-specific factors such as limited IV access, and electronic health record challenges such as pre-set medication administration times.

> "many participants shared perceived barriers (72.3%, n = 73) or facilitators (59.4%, n = 60) to the implementation of priority sepsis treatment interventions"
