# Graduate students often ignore whether the initial state is an eigenstate of the Hamiltonian when reasoning about time dependence of spin expectation values

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On paired spin-1/2 questions differing only in whether the initial state is an eigenstate of Sx or Sz (a stationary state), 51% answered the first correctly but only 33% the second, and six of nine interviewees correct on the first were incorrect on the second. [→ Emily Marshman and Chandralekha Singh 2013](#emily-marshman-and-chandralekha-singh-2013)

## Evidence

### Emily Marshman and Chandralekha Singh 2013

Emily Marshman and Chandralekha Singh. (2013). Investigating Student Difficulties with Time dependence of Expectation Values in Quantum Mechanics. 2013 PERC Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2013.pr.049

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Spin-1/2 questions 3 and 4 in a uniform magnetic field, answered by the 39 graduate students; on question 4, 33% chose the correct answer A while 51% chose incorrect choice E, indicating students did not account for the stationary-state condition. The article also reports six of nine interviewees correct on question 3 answered question 4 incorrectly.

> "Table 1 also shows that for question 4, 33% of the students selected the correct answer choice A. Moreover, 51% of the students selected the incorrect answer choice E for question 4 suggesting they did not realize that, since the particle is initially in an eigenstate of the Hamiltonian operator, the expectation values of all observables will be time independent."
