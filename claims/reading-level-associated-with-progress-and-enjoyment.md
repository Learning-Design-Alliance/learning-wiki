## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Players' reading level was statistically significantly associated with how many levels they played (χ²(df=2)=102.44, p<.0001); a higher reported reading level (Above Average > Average > Below Average) was associated with a higher maximum level reached. [→ Gagnon 2022](#gagnon-2022)

## Evidence

### Gagnon 2022

Gagnon, D. J., Baker, R. S., Gagnon, S., Swanson, L., Spevacek, N., Andres, J., Harpstead, E., Scianna, J., Slater, S., San Pedro, M. O.C.Z. (2022). Exploring players' experience of humor and snark in a grade 3-6 history practices game. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2210.09906](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2210.09906)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Analysis of self-reported reading skill against in-game progress in the randomized experiment. A higher reported reading level (Above Average > Average > Below Average) was associated with a higher maximum level reached by the player.

> "In addition, players’ reading level was statistically significantly associated with how many levels they played within the game, χ 2 (df = 2) = 102.44, p < .0001."

### Gagnon 2022 (2)

Gagnon, D. J., Baker, R. S., Gagnon, S., Swanson, L., Spevacek, N., Andres, J., Harpstead, E., Scianna, J., Slater, S., San Pedro, M. O.C.Z. (2022). Exploring players' experience of humor and snark in a grade 3-6 history practices game. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2210.09906](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2210.09906)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of self-reported reading skill against enjoyment of the game in the same randomized experiment. Players who reported higher reading levels also "reported enjoying the game more"; only the test statistic is printed, with no effect size.

> "Finally, players who reported higher reading levels also reported enjoying the game more, χ 2 (df = 2) = 209.42, p < .0001."
