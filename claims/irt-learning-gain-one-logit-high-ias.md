# Students in high-IAS classes show IRT-estimated learning gains almost an entire logit greater than students in low-IAS classes (Cohen's d = 1.2)

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Average Δθ for high-IAS students (1.4 logits) exceeds that of low-IAS students (0.49 logits) by nearly 1 logit, a statistically significant difference with a very large effect size (Cohen's d = 1.2). [→ Colin S. Wallace 2017](#colin-s-wallace-2017)

## Evidence

### Colin S. Wallace 2017

Colin S. Wallace, Timothy G. Chambers, Edward E. Prather. (2017). An item response theory evaluation of the Light and Spectroscopy Concept Inventory national data set. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1709.05255](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1709.05255)

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of IRT-estimated learning gains (Δθ) for the 2178 students in high-IAS classes (average 1.4 logits, SD 0.90) and the 363 students in low-IAS classes (average 0.49 logits, SD 0.66). A one-tailed t-test found the difference significant (p < 0.0001) with "a very large eﬀect size (Cohen's d = 1.2)".

> "Surprisingly, students in high-IAS classes averaged a pre-post improvement in their abilities that was almost an entire logit greater than the average pre-post ability improvement of students in low-IAS classes."
