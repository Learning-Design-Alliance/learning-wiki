# Item response theory analysis supports the direction of greater content gains in the Extensive section but the section effect is not statistically significant

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the IRT-based regression, the section coefficient of 0.18 favored the Extensive section but was not statistically significant (p = 0.11), which the authors attribute to reduced statistical power from IRT estimation. [→ Connell 2016](#connell-2016)

## Evidence

### Connell 2016

Connell, G. L., Donovan, D. A., & Chambers, T. G. (2016). Increasing the Use of Student-Centered Pedagogies from Moderate to High Improves Student Learning and Attitudes about Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-03-0062

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

IRT analysis (unidimensional three-parameter logistic and polytomous models, n = 316) of pre/post content assessment data, with a multiple regression on postinstruction ability; the section coefficient of 0.18 was not significant. Histograms showed a greater fraction of Extensive-section students with ability gains in the range 0.5 < Δθ < 2.0.

> "The regression coefficient of 0.18 for the section variable indicates that students in the Extensive section had higher postinstruction abilities than students in the Moderate section, all other factors being held equal, but this effect was not large enough to be statistically significant given the power of our analysis."
