# High levels of induced social presence during non-interactive teaching can impair learning relative to restudy (a double-edged sword)

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Teaching a remote real person via webcam (high social presence) impaired transfer performance relative to restudy, while teaching a fictitious person did not differ from restudy. [→ Lachner 2022](#lachner-2022)

## Evidence

### Lachner 2022

Lachner, A., Hoogerheide, V., van Gog, T., & Renkl, A. (2022). Learning-by-Teaching Without Audience Presence or Interaction: When and Why Does it Work? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09643-4

`q2 · i2` · `review · r2`

Table 1 entry for Hoogerheide, Renkl, et al. (2020), Experiment 1 with university students teaching worked examples on electric circuits: restudy beat teaching a real remote student on transfer, d = 0.70, while fictitious-audience teaching equaled restudy. The review concludes "high levels of social presence impaired rather than contributed to learning."

> "Transfer: restudy > non-interactive teaching to real student (d = 0.70); non-interactive teaching fictitious student = non-interactive teaching real student; non-interactive teaching fictitious student = restudy"
