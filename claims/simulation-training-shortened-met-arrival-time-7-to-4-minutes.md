# On-site simulation training shortened MET arrival time from 7 minutes (2017 pre-project simulation) to 5 minutes in the first session and 4 minutes in the second session

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` MET arrival time at the site decreased across successive simulation sessions, from 7 minutes before the project to 5 minutes after the first session and 4 minutes after the second unannounced session. [→ Kawamura Hidemasa 2022](#kawamura-hidemasa-2022)

## Evidence

### Kawamura Hidemasa 2022

Kawamura Hidemasa, Komatsu Yasuhiro, Tanaka Kazumi, Kanamoto Masafumi, Tobe Masaru, Usami Chisato, Hinohara Hiroshi, Oshima Kiyohiro, Kitada Yoko, Tsuda Kazuhisa, Ogano Tomoaki, Shimada Hirofumi, Ohno Tatsuya. (2022). Implementation of a rapid response system at an isolated radiotherapy facility through simulation training. BMJ Open Quality. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjoq-2021-001578

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Single-institution quality improvement project at the Gunma University Heavy Ion Medical Center comparing MET arrival times across a 2017 pre-project simulation and two on-site simulation sessions. The article reports the reduction from "7 min" to "4 min" but no effect size or test statistic.

> "The time for the MET to arrive at the site during the 2017 simulation prior to the present project was 7 min; the time to arrive in the first session was shortened to 5 min, and this was shortened further to 4 min in the second session, despite the lack of prior explanation of the situation."
