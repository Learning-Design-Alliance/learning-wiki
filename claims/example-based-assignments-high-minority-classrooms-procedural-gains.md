## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students in high-minority classrooms assigned to the example-based condition showed higher posttest procedural scores than controls, with a trend toward a condition × minority-percentage interaction (β=.17, p=.08). [→ Paré-Blagoev 2013](#pare-blagoev-2013)

## Evidence

### Paré-Blagoev 2013

Paré-Blagoev, J., Booth, J., Elliot, A., & Koedinger, K. (2013). Using Worked Examples Assignments in Classroom Instruction. SREE Fall 2013 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED564068

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

HLM analysis of a randomized classroom study (395 students nested in 28 classrooms) found a marginal condition × minority-percentage interaction on procedural posttest scores ("β= .17, F(26, 389) = 1.75, p = .08").

> "in model 2proc there was a trend toward a positive association with the interaction between the slope of Condition and the percentage of minority students in the classroom (β= .17, F(26, 389) = 1.75, p = .08)"

### Paré-Blagoev 2013 (2)

Paré-Blagoev, J., Booth, J., Elliot, A., & Koedinger, K. (2013). Using Worked Examples Assignments in Classroom Instruction. SREE Fall 2013 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED564068

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Simple-slopes follow-up in the same HLM models showed the minority-percentage effect on procedural learning in the control group ("t = -2.50, p = .01") but not in the example-based group ("t = -0.68, p = .50").

> "Follow-up examination of simple slopes indicated that there was an effect of minority percentage in the control group (higher minority classes improved less than lower minority classes; t = -2.50, p = .01), but in the example-based group, students in both high and low-minority classes learned equally (t = -0.68, p = .50)"
