Evidence¶

Stichter 2018¶

Stichter, J. P., Herzog, M. J., Kilgus, S. P., & Schoemann, A. M. (2018). Exploring the Moderating Effects of Cognitive Abilities on Social Competence Intervention Outcomes. Behavior Modification, 42(1), 84-107 (published online 2017). https://doi.org/10.1177/0145445517698654

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Multilevel moderation analysis of the cluster randomized trial (SCI-A n = 146, BAU n = 123 students), probing simple slopes at FSIQ 85 and 115. The FSIQ × condition interaction was significant only for Communication (p = .037); for the other four SRS-2 subscales it was not significant, and the authors report that they "demonstrated the same intervention effect pattern across values of FSIQ". Simple-slope effect sizes at low FSIQ were d = −0.27 (motivation) to −0.46 (communication) on awareness, communication and motivation, favouring SCI-A; at high FSIQ they were positive (d = 0.40 to 0.93), favouring BAU. Only the communication pattern rests on a significant moderation.

"For groups characterized by lower FSIQ, students in SCI-A had lower T2 SRS-2 scores relative to BAU students, indicating more positive social behavior within the SCI-A group, on social awareness (d = −0.40), communication (d = −0.46), and motivation (d = −0.27)."
