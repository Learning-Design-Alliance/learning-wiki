# Many graduate students incorrectly describe a stationary state as an eigenstate of any operator corresponding to an observable rather than an eigenstate of the Hamiltonian

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In think-aloud interviews, many graduate students described a stationary state as an eigenstate of any operator corresponding to an observable, not recognizing it as a Hamiltonian eigenstate in which all expectation values are time independent. ## Evidence

### Emily Marshman and Chandralekha Singh 2013

Emily Marshman and Chandralekha Singh. (2013). Investigating Student Difficulties with Time dependence of Expectation Values in Quantum Mechanics. 2013 PERC Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2013.pr.049

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Think-aloud interviews with graduate students who had completed two first-year graduate quantum mechanics courses; the authors report that students "often described a stationary state as an eigenstate of any operator corresponding to an observable."

> "Additionally, during interviews, many students struggled to describe what a stationary state of a given quantum system is.  They often described a stationary state as an eigenstate of any operator corresponding to an observable."
