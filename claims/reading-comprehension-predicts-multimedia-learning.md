# Reading comprehension predicted retrieval, transfer, and retention more strongly than decoding, with a weaker modality interaction favoring lower-comprehension children in retention

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Reading comprehension improved retrieval, transfer, and retention with larger effect sizes than decoding (ΔR2mar = .222, .115, .276; p < .004). [→ Haavisto 2025](#haavisto-2025)

## Evidence

### Haavisto 2025

Haavisto, M., Lepola, J., & Jaakkola, T. (2025). The “simple” view of learning from illustrated texts and videos. Learning and Instruction, 100, 102200. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102200

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mixed-effects models reanalyzed with reading comprehension replacing decoding showed "improved retrieval from materials, transfer, and retention with medium to large effect sizes (ΔR2mar = .222, .115, and .276, respectively; p-values <.004)", repeating decoding's patterns with greater magnitudes.

> "After controlling for modality, reading compre- hension improved retrieval from materials, transfer, and retention with medium to large effect sizes (ΔR2mar = .222, .115, and .276, respectively; p-values <.004)."

### Haavisto 2025 (2)

Haavisto, M., Lepola, J., & Jaakkola, T. (2025). The “simple” view of learning from illustrated texts and videos. Learning and Instruction, 100, 102200. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102200

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the modality-by-reading-comprehension model for delayed retention, the interaction was significant but weaker than for decoding; Fig. 5 shows videos especially benefited children in the lowest 25% of reading comprehension, with smaller differences at higher levels.

> "Like decoding, reading comprehension interacted with modality, though the interaction was weaker: the retention benefits of video were more pronounced among children with lower reading comprehension (ΔR2mar = .009, p = .036)."
