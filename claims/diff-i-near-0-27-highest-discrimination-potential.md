## Evidence

### Joshi 2019

Joshi, P.K., Jain, Y., Khunyakari, R. and Basu, S. (2019). A novel alternative to analyzing multiple choice questions via discrimination index. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1906.07941](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1906.07941)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

From the theoretical limit relating DI to Diff-I, derived in three phases around the 0.27 and 0.73 boundaries, the authors conclude that questions with Diff-I close to 0.27 attaining maximum DI "have highest discrimination power with minimum number of participants correctly answering the questions."

> "Those questions which have Diﬀ-I close to 0.27 and tend to have maximum value of DI are ideally the best questions. These are the questions which have highest discrimination power with minimum number of participants correctly answering the questions."
