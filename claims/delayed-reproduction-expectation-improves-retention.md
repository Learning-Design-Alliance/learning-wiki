## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Stories and other memory materials are retained better when subjects expect to reproduce them after a considerable interval (four weeks) than when they expect reproduction within a short time (the next day). [→ Peters 1912](#peters-1912)

## Evidence

### Peters 1912

Peters, W. (1912). Pedagogy at the Berlin Psychological Congress. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 3, 452-457. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Pedagogy+at+the+Berlin+Psychological+Congress

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Experimental series reported at the congress by Aall of Christiania: in one series subjects expected questioning the next day, in another after four weeks; both were tested at four weeks, and "The material learned with the expectation of delayed reproduction was rendered more accurately."

> "Aall of Christiania found that stories and other memory materials are retained better if the subject is told beforehand that he will be required after the lapse of a considerable interval to reproduce what he has learned than if he is told that the reproduction will be called for within a short time."
