## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Binary mediation analyses showed confidence in the model's forecasts, but not confidence in the human's forecasts, mediated the effect of seeing the model perform on choosing the model. [→ Berkeley J. Dietvorst 2014](#berkeley-j-dietvorst-2014)

## Evidence

### Berkeley J. Dietvorst 2014

Berkeley J. Dietvorst, Joseph P. Simmons, and Cade Massey. (2014). Algorithm Aversion: People Erroneously Avoid Algorithms After Seeing Them Err. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General. https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000033

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Preacher and Hayes bootstrapping analyses in each study yielded significant confidence intervals for confidence in the model's forecasts (e.g., Study 1, 95% CI [–.165, –.070]) and nonsignificant intervals for confidence in the human's forecasts.

> "In all cases, confidence in the model’s forecasts significantly mediated the effect, whereas confidence in the human did not."
