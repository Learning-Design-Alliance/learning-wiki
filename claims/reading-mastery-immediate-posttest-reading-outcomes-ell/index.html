WWC Intervention Report: Reading Mastery SRA/McGraw-Hill. (2006). What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc

q3 · i2 · causal · r?

WWC calculations for the Gunn et al. (2000) randomized controlled trial ELL subsample (n=16-17 per measure) show effect sizes of 1.03 for Oral Reading Fluency, 0.55 for Letter-Word Identification, and 0.70 for Word Attack, each marked 'ns' (not statistically significant) at α = 0.05. Reading Vocabulary and Passage Comprehension were excluded from the rating due to severe attrition.

"Read Mastery had substantively important effects for four of the five measures immediately after implementation of the program (oral reading fluency, letter/word identification, word attack, and reading vocabulary but not passage comprehension)."

Related Claims¶

  • One year after the intervention, Reading Mastery showed a statistically significant effect on word attack for English language learners, with positive but non-significant differences on other reading measures — related
  • In the Vaughn, Cirino, et al. (2006) trial, no reading measure reached statistical significance, but five of seven effect sizes were substantively important — related
  • Students in the Readable English intervention group showed significant improvements in reading fluency and comprehension compared to the control group — related
  • TRI shows an average effect size of +0.51 on Woodcock reading outcomes across two studies — related
