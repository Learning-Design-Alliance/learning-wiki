# In Experiment 1, general self-assessment feedback during a learning phase led to less accurate self-assessment in a subsequent no-feedback test phase than receiving no feedback at all

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Adolescents who received general self-assessment feedback made significantly less accurate self-assessments in the test phase than the no-feedback control condition (ηp² = .076). [→ Steven F. Raaijmakers 2019](#steven-f-raaijmakers-2019)

## Evidence

### Steven F. Raaijmakers 2019

Steven F. Raaijmakers, Martine Baars, Fred Paas, Jeroen J. G. van Merriënboer, Tamara van Gog. (2019). Effects of self-assessment feedback on self-assessment and task-selection accuracy. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-019-09189-5

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Randomized experiment with 108 third-year Dutch secondary students assigned to no-feedback, general, or specific self-assessment feedback conditions; test-phase self-assessment accuracy favored the control, with a main effect of condition of ηp² = .076: "the general self-assessment feedback condition was significantly less accurate than the no feedback control condition (p = .015)".

> "An ANOV A on self-assessment accuracy during the test phase showed a main effect of condition, F(2, 105) = 4.29, p =. 0 1 6 ,η2 p =. 0 7 6 . Tukey’s post-hoc tests showed that, in contrast to our hypotheses, the general self-assessment feedback condition was significantly less accurate than the no feedback control condition (p = .015)."
