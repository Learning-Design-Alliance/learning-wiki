# Children's storybook knowledge (book-cover recognition) mediates the relationship between home literacy environment and literacy outcomes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` When book-cover recognition was entered, parent print exposure stopped predicting vocabulary, indicating mediation. [→ Davidse 2010](#davidse-2010)
`q3 i?` When book-cover recognition was entered, both home literacy indicators stopped predicting letter knowledge, indicating mediation. [→ Davidse 2010 (2)](#davidse-2010-2)

## Evidence

### Davidse 2010

Davidse, N. J., de Jong, M. T., Bus, A. G., Huijbregts, S. C. J., & Swaab, H. (2010). Cognitive and environmental predictors of early literacy skills. Read Writ 24, 395–412. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11145-010-9233-3

`q3 · i?`

Hierarchical regression Model 2 on vocabulary (N = 174): entering book-cover recognition made parent print exposure non-significant; "book-cover recognition was a strong predictor ( b = .32)".

> "the parent print exposure checklist test stopped being signiﬁcant, but book-cover recognition was a strong predictor ( b = .32). This indicates that the child’s book knowledge mediates HLE."

### Davidse 2010 (2)

Davidse, N. J., de Jong, M. T., Bus, A. G., Huijbregts, S. C. J., & Swaab, H. (2010). Cognitive and environmental predictors of early literacy skills. Read Writ 24, 395–412. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11145-010-9233-3

`q3 · i?`

Hierarchical regression Model 2 on letter knowledge (N = 174): book-cover recognition β = .29 and both home literacy indicators became non-significant, supporting mediation.

> "the book-cover test was a signiﬁcant predictor ( b = .29) and both home literacy indicators stopped being signiﬁcant. The child’s book knowledge evidently mediates the relationship between home literacy environment and letter knowledge."
