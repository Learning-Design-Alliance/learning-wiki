## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Applicants from the largest undergraduate physics programs are admitted at about 0.28 versus 0.20 for smaller programs, and applicants from selective institutions at about 0.31 versus 0.18 for less selective ones. [→ Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero 2020](#nicholas-t-young-and-marcos-d-caballero-2020)

## Evidence

### Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero 2020

Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero. (2020). The Physics GRE does not help applicants “stand out”. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2008.10712](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2008.10712)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Probability-of-admission analysis split by undergraduate institution size (AIP bachelor's-degree quartiles) and selectivity (Barron's Index via Chetty et al. groupings). Overall admission fractions favor "applicants from largest undergraduate programs" (0.28 vs 0.20) and selective institutions (0.31 vs 0.18).

> "Overall, by looking at the bin in the ‘Any’ row and ‘Any’ column of Fig. 4 and Fig. 5, we see that applicants from largest undergraduate programs are nearly 40% more likely to be admitted (0.28 to 0.20) while applicants from selective institutions are nearly 70% more likely to be admitted (0.31 to 0.18)."
