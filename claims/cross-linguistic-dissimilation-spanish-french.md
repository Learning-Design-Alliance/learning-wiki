# Spanish and French show the same dissimilation pattern for reflexive-type verbs as English

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Spanish and French counterparts of English walk-type verbs behave in much the same way, using idiomatic pro-verbs (dar, faire) whose meanings in dissimilated constructions differ from their lexical meanings. [→ Babcock 1967](#babcock-1967)

## Evidence

### Babcock 1967

Babcock, Sandra Scharff. (1967). Syntactic Dissimilation. ERIC. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED024020

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Comparative linguistic analysis. Spanish 'Juan daba un paseo por el parque' and French 'Jean fait une promenade tous les jours' use dar and faire idiomatically as pro-verbs for 'walk,' parallel to English take, with meanings "not even remotely similar to their lexical meanings as mainverbs."

> "By way of illustrationthat English is notunique in its treatment of such verbs aswalk, we note thatthe Spanish andFrench counterparts behave inmuch the same way."
