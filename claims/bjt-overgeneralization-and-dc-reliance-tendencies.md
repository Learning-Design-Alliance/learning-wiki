## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Kevin L. Van De Bogart and MacKenzie R. Stetzer 2018

Kevin L. Van De Bogart and MacKenzie R. Stetzer. (2018). Investigating student understanding of bipolar junction transistor circuits. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1806.00404](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1806.00404)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Authors' cross-task interpretation (Discussion section): students "used resistors as a proxy for voltages (or voltage drops) or always treat[ed] the voltage drop across a transistor junction as finite and constant," reflecting failure to apply constraints on element behavior; no effect size is reported.

> "Across all tasks, students tended to make comparisons based on an overgeneralization of some property of the circuit elements."

### Kevin L. Van De Bogart and MacKenzie R. Stetzer 2018 (2)

Kevin L. Van De Bogart and MacKenzie R. Stetzer. (2018). Investigating student understanding of bipolar junction transistor circuits. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1806.00404](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1806.00404)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Authors' cross-task summary of the tendency to rely on dc analysis over small-signal analysis, drawn from the five tasks' response patterns; this is an interpretation, not a single tested result.

> "In instances in which students were not explicitly prompted to consider both dc and small-signal analyses of a circuit, students frequently used inappropriate strategies to reason"
