# Providing a rubric reduces perceived difficulty of self-assessment but not invested mental effort

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Rebecca Krebs 2022

Rebecca Krebs, Björn Rothstein, Julian Roelle. (2022). Rubrics enhance accuracy and reduce cognitive load in self-assessment. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09302-1

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Subjective cognitive load rated on 7-point mental effort and perceived difficulty scales after self-assessment in the N = 93 experiment. "The rubric group perceived forming the self-assessments as easier than the control group"; mental effort did not differ.

> "In terms of perceived difficulty, a t test indicated a statistically significant effect, t(90) = -2.45, p = 0.016, d = 0.52. The rubric group perceived forming the self-assessments as easier than the control group. By contrast, in terms of mental effort, we did not find a statistically significant difference between the two groups, t(90) = -0.77, p = 0.444, d = 0.16."
