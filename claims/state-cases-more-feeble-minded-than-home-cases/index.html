Pintner 1917¶

Pintner, R. (1917). The Mentality of the Dependent Child, Together with a Plan for a Mental Survey of an Institution. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0075825

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Cross-group comparison of the two observational testing samples reported in the feeble-mindedness section, both diagnosed by the conservative method. The article explains the State Board receives children who are "problems" and difficult to manage, a selected group.

"A comparison of the State Charity cases with the County Home cases shows a much larger percentage of feeble-minded among the former than among the latter, 19.5 per cent, as contrasted with 5.7 per cent, (taking the most conservative estimate in each case)."

Related Claims¶

  • In the unselected county children's home population, the C. M. A. standard yielded 20.8% feeble-minded while the Three Per Cent. Hypothesis yielded 5.7%, with most children classified backward — related
  • Among 82 state charity cases of dependent children, the most conservative estimate put 19.5% feeble-minded and only about 37% testing up to normal — related
  • In Wallin's St. Louis psycho-educational clinic, of 430 referred cases, 25.9% of 320 boys and 40% of 110 girls were found feeble-minded, with almost twice as many backward and retarded as feeble-minded. — related
  • Applying the Stanford revision to selected children in four California cities, Williams classified 32% feeble-minded, 20% borderline, 22% dull normal, and 26% normal or superior. — related
