# Chicago's 1997 three-year college-preparatory science graduation requirement more than doubled the percentage of graduates completing three or more years of science

## Evidence

### Montgomery 2010

Montgomery, N., & Allensworth, E. M., with Correa, M. (2010). Passing Through Science: The Effects of Raising Graduation Requirements in Science on Course-Taking and Academic Achievement in Chicago. Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/passing-through-science-effects-raising-graduation-requirements-science-course-taking

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre/post cohort comparison of transcript data from CPS administrative records, controlling for changes in student characteristics over time. Completion among graduates "more than doubled, to 86 percent," then reached 90 percent. No effect size is printed.

> "Among CPS students entering high school in 1993 through 1995, 35 percent of students who went on to graduate, completed three or more years of science. In the first year of the policy (1997), the percent of graduates completing three or more years of science courses more than doubled, to 86 percent, and then reached 90 percent the next year."
