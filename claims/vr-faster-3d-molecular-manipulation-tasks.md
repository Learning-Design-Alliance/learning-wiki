## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For the nanotube-threading and knot-tying tasks, cohorts of 32 users with little or no prior VR experience accomplished the tasks faster in VR than on mouse or touchscreen platforms, with rates distinguished by non-overlapping Poisson error bars. ### Michael O'Connor 2018

Michael O'Connor, Helen M. Deeks, Edward Dawn, Oussama Metatla, Anne Roudaut, Matthew Sutton, Becca Rose Glowacki, Rebecca Sage, Philip Tew, Mark Wonnacott, Phil Bates, Adrian J. Mulholland, David R. Glowacki. (2018). Sampling molecular conformations and dynamics in a multi-user virtual reality framework. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1801.02884](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1801.02884)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Controlled user study (n = 32, Latin-square rotation across VR, mouse, touchscreen platforms, same renderer and simulation engine) comparing task accomplishment rates. The article reports that "VR provides a clear acceleration benefit" for tasks A and C, with Poisson error estimates; no effect size is printed.

> "For tasks A and C, Fig 3 indicates that VR provides a clear acceleration benefit. The more inherently 3d the task, the greater the benefit"
