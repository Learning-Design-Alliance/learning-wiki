## Evidence

### Mohebi Meysam 2025

Mohebi Meysam, Moosavi Soolmaz, Hasandoost Fateme, Forouzanfar Mohammad Mehdi. (2025). Impact of an advanced trauma life support (ATLS) simulation program on nursing students’ clinical decision-making in trauma care: a quasi-experimental study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08210-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated measures ANOVA in a quasi-experimental pretest-posttest control group study of 66 nursing students (intervention n = 31, control n = 35) found the "interaction effect was statistically significant (P = 0.006)". The article calls this between-group comparison the strongest evidence of the program's impact. No effect size is printed.

> "Most importantly, the interaction effect was statistically significant (P = 0.006), indicating that the change in scores over time was significantly greater in the intervention group compared to the control group."

### Mohebi Meysam 2025 (2)

Mohebi Meysam, Moosavi Soolmaz, Hasandoost Fateme, Forouzanfar Mohammad Mehdi. (2025). Impact of an advanced trauma life support (ATLS) simulation program on nursing students’ clinical decision-making in trauma care: a quasi-experimental study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08210-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Results section of the same repeated measures ANOVA: "both time and group had significant main effects on clinical decision-making scores". Table 4 prints Time F = 601.08, p < 0.001 and Group F = 47.26, p < 0.001; no effect size is reported.

> "The results showed that both time and group had significant main effects on clinical decision-making scores."
