# Classroom views of greenery improve children's concentration compared with built views or windowless classrooms

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students randomly assigned to classrooms with views of greenery perform better on concentration tests than students assigned to built views or windowless classrooms. [→ Kuo M 2019](#kuo-m-2019)

## Evidence

### Kuo M 2019

Kuo M, Barnes M and Jordan C (2019) Do Experiences With Nature Promote Learning? Converging Evidence of a Cause-and-Effect Relationship. Front. Psychol. 10:305. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00305

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports an experimental study in which random assignment to classroom views was used; students with "views of greenery perform better on concentration tests" than those with built views or no windows. Attributed to Li and Sullivan (2016).

> "Students randomly assigned to classrooms with views of greenery perform better on concentration tests than those assigned to purely "built" views or windowless classrooms (Li and Sullivan, 2016)."
