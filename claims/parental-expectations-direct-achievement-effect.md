## Evidence

### Qin Xingna 2026

Qin Xingna, Li Simeng, Sjögren Björn, Ren Ping, Thornberg Robert. (2026). The Role of Parental Influences in Chinese Adolescents’ Academic Achievement through Shaping Friendship Network Dynamics. Journal of Youth and Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-025-02306-5

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

RSiena Model 1 effect-from parameter for parental influences on achievement. The article reports expectations β = 0.09 (p < .001), while autonomy support (β = 0.05, p = .15), behavioral control (β = 0.03, p = .27) and psychological control (β = −0.04, p = .18) were non-significant.

> "only parental expectations significantly predicted higher achievement (β = 0.09, p < .001) in model 1, consistent with Hypothesis 2c."

### Qin Xingna 2026 (2)

Qin Xingna, Li Simeng, Sjögren Björn, Ren Ping, Thornberg Robert. (2026). The Role of Parental Influences in Chinese Adolescents’ Academic Achievement through Shaping Friendship Network Dynamics. Journal of Youth and Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-025-02306-5

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r3`

Descriptive correlational analysis (Table 1) of T1 parental variables with achievement at T1 and T4. Expectations correlated r = 0.40–0.43 with achievement; the article notes this pattern supports the centrality of expectations in the Chinese sociocultural setting.

> "correlations between parental expectations and academic achievement were relatively strong (0.40–0.43), whereas associations with autonomy support and behavioral control were modest (0.11–0.18), and negative for psychological control (–0.15 to –0.18)"
