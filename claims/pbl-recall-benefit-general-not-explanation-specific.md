# The recall benefit of prior problem-based discussion applies to both explanatory and descriptive propositions, with no interaction between treatment and proposition type

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The number of explanatory and descriptive propositions in recall did not differ significantly, and there was no significant treatment-by-type interaction. [→ W.S. de Grave 2001](#ws-de-grave-2001)
`q3 i?` Explanatory propositions were recalled significantly more by the 'Stinging incident' group than by the control group. [→ W.S. de Grave 2001 (2)](#ws-de-grave-2001-2)
`q3 i?` Descriptive propositions were also recalled significantly more by the 'Stinging incident' group than by the control group. [→ W.S. de Grave 2001 (3)](#ws-de-grave-2001-3)

## Evidence

### W.S. de Grave 2001

W.S. de Grave, H.G. Schmidt & H.P.A. Boshuizen. (2001). Effects of problem-based discussion on studying a subsequent text: A randomized trial among first year medical students. Instructional Science 29: 33-44. https://link.springer.com/journal/11251

`q3 · i?`

ANOVA of the same randomized recall study shows the predicted interaction was absent; the article says the hypothesis of a relatively greater number of explanatory propositions "is not conﬁrmed by the data".

> "the number of explanatory and descriptive propositions in the recall did not differ signiﬁcantly, F(1.46) = 0.03, MSe = 29.28, p< 0.86. Neither is there any signiﬁcant interaction between treatment and type of proposition, F(1.46) = 0.01, MSe = 29.28, p < 0.98"

### W.S. de Grave 2001 (2)

W.S. de Grave, H.G. Schmidt & H.P.A. Boshuizen. (2001). Effects of problem-based discussion on studying a subsequent text: A randomized trial among first year medical students. Instructional Science 29: 33-44. https://link.springer.com/journal/11251

`q3 · i?`

ANOVA on accurate explanatory propositions in the recall study: the experimental group (mean 25.0) recalled more than the control group (mean 20.1), a significant difference.

> "The average number of explanatory propositions accurately produced by the experimental group, however, differs signiﬁcantly from the average number of accurate declarative propositions produced by the control group F(1,46) = 6.24, MSe = 44.44, p< 0.05"

### W.S. de Grave 2001 (3)

W.S. de Grave, H.G. Schmidt & H.P.A. Boshuizen. (2001). Effects of problem-based discussion on studying a subsequent text: A randomized trial among first year medical students. Instructional Science 29: 33-44. https://link.springer.com/journal/11251

`q3 · i?`

ANOVA on accurate descriptive propositions: experimental mean 25.1 versus control mean 20.3; the authors conclude problem analysis improves memory for both proposition kinds.

> "The number of descriptive propositions during recall of the 'Stinging incident' problem group also turns out to be signiﬁcantly higher than of the control group F(1,46) = 6.54, MSe = 41.53, p< 0.05. Apparently, both kinds of propositions are remembered better due to problem analysis"
