## Evidence

### Mahdiyeh Seyed Beheshti Nasab & Seyyed Fariborz Pishdadi Motlagh 2015

Mahdiyeh Seyed Beheshti Nasab & Seyyed Fariborz Pishdadi Motlagh. (2015). A Complete Review for Metacognitive, Cognitive, and Social/Affective Strategies as Essential Components of Learning strategies and their relationships with EFL Learners' Reading Comprehension Promotion. Advances in Language and Literary Studies. https://doi.org/10.7575/aiac.alls.v.6n.3p.166

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Pearson correlation of age with reading scores in the cognitive group of the quasi-experiment (N=30). The article reports "r=0.5" with p=0.04, interpreting that the higher learners' age the higher their reading scores.

> "according to Pearson correlations the significant level is p=0.04 and it is less than 0.05 and r=0.5. Consequently, null hypothesis is rejected and alternative hypothesis is approved and there is significant meaningful level between two variables, so it certifies that the higher learners' age the higher reading scores in cognitive group will be."
