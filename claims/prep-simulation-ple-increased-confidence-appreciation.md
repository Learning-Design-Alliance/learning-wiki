## Evidence

### M.-L. Tremblay and C. Fournier 2025

M.-L. Tremblay and C. Fournier. (2025). Involving Patients Throughout Sensitive Simulation. The Clinical Teacher. https://doi.org/10.1111/tct.70163

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Post-simulation online survey in a mixed-methods sequential explanatory study of a mandatory third-year pharmacy course; of 142 eligible students, 109 responded (77%). The article reports a statistically significant increase in confidence with no printed effect size.

> "A Wilcoxon test showed significantly increased confidence in providing PrEP services postsimulation (p < 0.001)."

### M.-L. Tremblay and C. Fournier 2025 (2)

M.-L. Tremblay and C. Fournier. (2025). Involving Patients Throughout Sensitive Simulation. The Clinical Teacher. https://doi.org/10.1111/tct.70163

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same 10-item Likert-type survey (N = 109, expert-reviewed and pilot-tested) administered immediately after the simulation and debriefing; percentages are descriptive response proportions, not effect sizes.

> "After the simulation, 83% strongly agreed it improved their awareness of PrEP‐related issues, and 93% credited the PLE's presence."
