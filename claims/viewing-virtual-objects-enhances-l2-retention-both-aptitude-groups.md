# Simply viewing virtual referent objects during L2 learning enhances retention relative to audiovisual-only learning, for both aptitude groups

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` AVO learning significantly enhanced vocabulary test scores relative to AV learning (d = 1.06), and AVOG did not significantly exceed AVO (t = 0.56, p = .84). [→ Manuela Macedonia 2023](#manuela-macedonia-2023)

## Evidence

### Manuela Macedonia 2023

Manuela Macedonia, Brian Mathias, Astrid E. Lehner, Susanne M. Reiterer, Claudia Repetto. (2023). Grasping Virtual Objects Benefits Lower Aptitude Learners' Acquisition of Foreign Language Vocabulary. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09835-0

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Same mixed-effects model of vocabulary scores in the VR-cave training study. The article reports "b = 0.89; p < .001, d = 1.06" for AVO versus AV; post-hoc tests showed AVOG did not significantly exceed AVO (t = 0.56, p = .84).

> "A VO learning also significantly enhanced vocabulary test scores relative to learning in the A V condition (b = 0.89; p < .001, d = 1.06)."

### Manuela Macedonia 2023 (2)

Manuela Macedonia, Brian Mathias, Astrid E. Lehner, Susanne M. Reiterer, Claudia Repetto. (2023). Grasping Virtual Objects Benefits Lower Aptitude Learners' Acquisition of Foreign Language Vocabulary. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09835-0

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc comparisons by aptitude group in the same study. The picture benefit held for both groups: "LLA learners: t = 3.57, p = .007, d = 1.12; HLA learners: t = 3.47, p = .01, d = 1.00".

> "Both LLA and HLA learners significantly benefited from simply observing objects in the A VO learning condition relative to the A V learning condition (LLA learners: t = 3.57, p = .007, d = 1.12; HLA learners: t = 3.47, p = .01, d = 1.00)."
