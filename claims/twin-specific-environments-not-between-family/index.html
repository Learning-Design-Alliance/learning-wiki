Wolfram 2023¶

Wolfram, T. & Morris, D. (2023). Conventional twin studies overestimate the environmental differences between families relevant to educational attainment. npj Science of Learning 8, 24. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00173-y

q2 · i? · associational · r2

NTFD decomposition of the shared environment in the TwinLife sample, contrasting DZ twin with non-twin sibling correlations (r = 0.6 vs 0.45) to identify twin-specific effects. The best-fitting model attributes only "just 10% of the variance in EA" to between-family environmental differences.

"Taking both assortative mating and twin-speciﬁc shared environments into account, our best- ﬁtting model indicated just 10% of the variance in EA could be attributed to between-family environmental differences."

Related Claims¶

  • Adjusting published twin-study ACE estimates for assortative mating raises mean heritability by about 17 points and lowers shared environmental estimates by about 16 points — related
  • A Classical Twin Design model of educational attainment in the TwinLife sample estimates shared environmental influence at 43% and additive genetic influence at 34% — related
  • Dizygotic twin correlations for educational attainment are consistently higher than non-twin sibling correlations across countries, suggesting twin-specific shared environments are a general phenomenon — related
  • The best-fitting NTFD model estimates heritability of educational attainment at 51% and total shared environmental influence at 26%, disaggregated into twin-specific (16%) and sibling-shared (10%) components — related
  • Under phenotypic assortment NTFD models, parental education shows no environmental effect on offspring educational attainment once genetic influences are accounted for — related
  • Educational achievement is highly heritable, and the common environmental variance is attributable to SES effects — related
  • Genetic and environmental variance components of educational achievement decrease with increasing parental SES — related
  • Twin and adoption studies suggest that 50–80% of IQ variation is due to genetic differences, with the range reflecting population heritability — related
  • Within twin pairs, education–methylation associations persist but are attenuated, implicating shared environmental or genetic factors — related
  • GPAΔ is strikingly highly heritable (85%), exceeding the heritability of achievement itself (67%) — related
  • Educational achievement is highly heritable across school years and subjects, with twin heritability around 60% and SNP heritability around 30% — related
  • Genetic factors account for a substantial share of variation in educational outcomes and related behavioral traits — related
  • Age-to-age change in achievement is largely explained by non-shared environmental factors, while genetic and shared environmental influences are stable — related
  • Heritability estimates are population-specific parameters, and the heritability of cognitive skills rises gradually through childhood and adolescence — related
  • High heritability does not imply that environmental interventions to modify a trait are doomed to failure — related
  • Associations between the rearing environment and children's outcomes can be partly or fully due to genetic confounding rather than causal environmental transmission — related
