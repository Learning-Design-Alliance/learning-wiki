## Subclaims
`q4 i1` Across 56 between-participants effect sizes, paper-based reading yielded significantly better comprehension than digital-based reading (Hedges' g = -0.21). [→ Delgado 2018](#delgado-2018)

## Evidence

### Delgado 2018

Delgado, P., Vargas, C., Ackerman, R., & Salmerón, L. (2018). Don't throw away your printed books: A meta-analysis on the effects of reading media on reading comprehension. Educational Research Review, 25, 23-38. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2018.09.003

`q4 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 56 between-participants media comparisons (169,524 participants; 165,778 from four large-scale studies). The printed pooled effect size was Hedges' g = -0.21 (95% CI: -0.28, -0.14), a small effect favouring paper. Heterogeneity was medium-high (I2 = 72.24; Q = 208.96, p < .001).

> "The mean effect size of the sample was significant (Hedges' g = -0.21; 95% CI: -0.28, -0.14; k = 56), revealing an advantage of paper-based reading over digital-based reading."
