Evidence¶

Drijbooms 2015¶

Drijbooms, E., Groen, M. A., & Verhoeven, L. (2015). The contribution of executive functions to narrative writing in fourth grade children. Reading and Writing. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11145-015-9558-z

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Hierarchical regression on 102 fourth graders' written narratives; EF entered last. "EF explained 8 % of unique variance after controlling for transcription and language skills", with inhibition and updating as significant predictors (betas .22* and .21*).

"EF explained 8 % of unique variance after controlling for transcription and language skills. The variance is almost entirely explained by inhibition and updating."

Related Claims¶

  • Executive functions do not directly predict syntactic complexity or story content once transcription and language skills are controlled — related
  • In a cross-sectional path model of fourth graders, handwriting fluency statistically mediated the relation between inhibition/updating and all narrative outcomes (text length, syntactic complexity, story content) — related
  • Planning shows no contribution to any level of narrative composition in fourth graders — related
  • EF tasks consolidate into three factors: inhibition, updating, and planning, with shifting not dissociable — related
  • Sixth-grade syntactic complexity of narrative writing is predicted by fourth-grade oral grammar, inhibition, and planning in addition to initial syntactic complexity — related
