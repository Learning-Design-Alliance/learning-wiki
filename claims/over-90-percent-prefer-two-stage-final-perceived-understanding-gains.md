## Evidence

### Callaghan 2026

Callaghan, K., Milbourne, T., Klales, A., Kestin, G., Arguelles, C., McCarty, L., & Deslauriers, L. (2026). Two-Stage Final Exams: An Assessment Strategy for Enhanced Collaborative Learning and Reduced Student Stress. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1080/0047231x.2026.2620442](https://doi.org/10.1080/0047231x.2026.2620442)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Format-preference survey in two Institution 1 courses (Physical Sciences 3 in a later semester and Physics 15B), both using the "2 hr Individual + 1 hr Group" format. The article reports "Over 90% of students in both courses chose the ‘2 hr Individual + 1 hr Group’ format."

> "In both courses, a total of 159 students were surveyed and asked which format they preferred: ‘2 hr Individual + 1 hr Group’ or a ‘traditional individual final exam.’ Over 90% of students in both courses chose the ‘2 hr Individual + 1 hr Group’ format."

### Callaghan 2026 (2)

Callaghan, K., Milbourne, T., Klales, A., Kestin, G., Arguelles, C., McCarty, L., & Deslauriers, L. (2026). Two-Stage Final Exams: An Assessment Strategy for Enhanced Collaborative Learning and Reduced Student Stress. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1080/0047231x.2026.2620442](https://doi.org/10.1080/0047231x.2026.2620442)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Students in both courses were asked "How much of the exam do you now understand?" immediately after the individual final exam and again after the group exam. The reported values are self-perception percentages with standard errors, not tested learning gains.

> "For Physical Sciences 3, their self-perception of understanding increased from around 65% (±1.8%) after the individual exam to approximately 93% (±1.3%) after the group exam. For Physics 15B, it increased from around 72% (±6%) to 92% (±5%)."
