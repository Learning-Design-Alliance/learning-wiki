# Directive feedback is associated with higher cognitive load than Socratic feedback, with no agent-type effects on learning experiences

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Feedback style showed a significant main effect on cognitive load, with higher scores under directive feedback (moderate effect). [→ Han Xue 2026](#han-xue-2026)
`q3 i?` Agent type showed no significant main effects on perceived ease of use, usefulness, intention to use, engagement, critical thinking disposition, or cognitive load. [→ Han Xue 2026 (2)](#han-xue-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Han Xue 2026

Han Xue, Cao Jing, Wang Zi, Luo Heng. (2026). The Effects of Agent Type and Feedback Style on Self-Directed Learning: A Mixed-Methods Study. Behavioral Sciences. https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16071069

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r?`

Mixed-design ANOVA on Learning Experience Questionnaire responses collected after each task from 51 postgraduate students. Cognitive load scores were descriptively higher under directive feedback than under Socratic feedback across both agent types.

> "Feedback style showed a significant main effect only on cognitive load (η2p = 0.113), indicating a moderate effect."

### Han Xue 2026 (2)

Han Xue, Cao Jing, Wang Zi, Luo Heng. (2026). The Effects of Agent Type and Feedback Style on Self-Directed Learning: A Mixed-Methods Study. Behavioral Sciences. https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16071069

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same ANOVA series found no significant agent-type main effects across the nine learning-experience dimensions, and no significant agent type × feedback style interactions on these dimensions; descriptive differences were relatively small.

> "results showed no significant main effects of agent type on perceived ease of use, perceived usefulness, intention to use, cognitive engagement, behavioral engagement, emotional engagement, critical thinking openness, reflective skepticism, or cognitive load."
