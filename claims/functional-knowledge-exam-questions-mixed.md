## Evidence

### McKinlay JB 2025

McKinlay JB, Kearns K. (2025). Improving the learning experience in an undergraduate course on microbial metabolism by using an illustrated story. Biochem Mol Biol Educ. https://doi.org/10.1002/bmb.21869

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of traditionally low-scoring functional-knowledge final exam questions across pre-story (2015–2018) and story (2019–2023) years; "average performance was significantly higher after the implementation of the story" for the acetate ATP question (40 ± 16% vs. 62 ± 14%; p = 0.09) and the donor/acceptor question (65 ± 9% vs. 80 ± 8%; p = 0.05).

> "For both questions, average performance was significantly higher after the implementation of the story (Figure 10c average: 40 ± 16% SD vs. 62 ± 14% SD; t-test p = 0.09; Figure 10d average: 65 ± 9% SD vs. 80 ± 8% SD; t-test p = 0.05)."

### McKinlay JB 2025 (2)

McKinlay JB, Kearns K. (2025). Improving the learning experience in an undergraduate course on microbial metabolism by using an illustrated story. Biochem Mol Biol Educ. https://doi.org/10.1002/bmb.21869

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The multipart short-written-answer question on how E. coli could make ATP after nutrient exhaustion showed "no significant change in scores before versus after the implementation of the story" (57 ± 7% vs. 60 ± 8%; p = 0.54). Equivalence was not tested.

> "There was no significant change in scores before versus after the implementation of the story on this question (Figure 10b average: 57 ± 7% SD vs. 60 ± 8% SD; t-test p = 0.54)."
