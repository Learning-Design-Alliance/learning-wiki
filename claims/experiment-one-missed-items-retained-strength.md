# Experiment I: items missed on a first test trial retained associative strength, favoring incremental theory

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Experimental subjects recalled more shortened-list items than controls after covariance adjustment (t = 2.19, beyond the 5% level), suggesting subthreshold strength developed on the first study trial. [→ Underwood 1962](#underwood-1962)
`q2 i?` Item-based analysis gave t = 2.22 favoring Experimental recall, and first-test-trial item difficulty correlated .74 across subject subgroups. [→ Underwood 1962 (2)](#underwood-1962-2)

## Evidence

### Underwood 1962

Underwood, B. J., & Keppel, G. (1962). One-Trial Learning? Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior, 1, 1-13. https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/journal-of-verbal-learning-and-verbal-behavior

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Controlled experiment with 44 Experimental and 44 Control subjects run in pairs; Experimental subjects had one prior study trial on the shortened-list items. The unadjusted difference of .45 items (3.65 vs 3.10) was not significant; the covariance-adjusted t of 2.19 was.

> "The use of covariance to adjust for the difference in learning of the first list pro duced a t of 2.19 for recall of the second list, a value which is beyond the 5 % significance level."

### Underwood 1962 (2)

Underwood, B. J., & Keppel, G. (1962). One-Trial Learning? Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior, 1, 1-13. https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/journal-of-verbal-learning-and-verbal-behavior

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Item-level analysis of the same experiment: with the 22 items as entries, a direct-difference t of 2.22 showed greater recall by Experimental subjects, and item difficulty correlated .74 across two 44-subject subgroups, indicating consistent item-difficulty differences.

> "The product moment correlation between these two arrays of 22 scores was .74. This high relationship was attained in spite of the fact that some attempt was made to construct homogeneous pairs."
