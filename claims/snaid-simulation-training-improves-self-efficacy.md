## Evidence

### Segura-Fornieles 2026

Segura-Fornieles, A. J., Márquez-Hernández, V. V., García-Viola, A., Rodríguez-García, M. C., & Garrido-Molina, J. M. (2026). Improving operational preparedness for armored vehicle casualty rescue: effectiveness of simulation-based training with a novel extrication device. Frontiers in Public Health, 14. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1910210

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same one-group pretest-posttest study (N = 156); self-efficacy measured with a single-item 0–10 scale. The article reports statistically significant differences with no printed effect size.

> "The pretest self-efficacy score was 5.76 (SD = 2.58), while the post-intervention self-efficacy score was 7.72 (SD = 1.91), showing statistically significant differences between the two measurements (p < 0.001)."

### Segura-Fornieles 2026 (2)

Segura-Fornieles, A. J., Márquez-Hernández, V. V., García-Viola, A., Rodríguez-García, M. C., & Garrido-Molina, J. M. (2026). Improving operational preparedness for armored vehicle casualty rescue: effectiveness of simulation-based training with a novel extrication device. Frontiers in Public Health, 14. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1910210

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Secondary analysis in the same study found no statistically significant association between self-efficacy gains and sex or age; the marked sex imbalance (94.9% men) limits conclusions about sex-based differences.

> "No statistically significant differences were found when comparing self-efficacy scores with sex (p = 0.782) or age (rs = 0.001; p = 0.988)."
