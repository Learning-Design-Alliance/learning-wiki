# Higher course grades predict larger ACT gains regardless of starting PLAN score; students with Ds or Fs lose ground

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among students with an eleventh-grade Reading PLAN score of 17, ACT scores rose monotonically with English grades (As averaged 18.5, Bs 18.0, Cs 17.2), while students with Ds or Fs scored below their earlier PLAN scores. [→ John Q. Easton 2008](#john-q-easton-2008)

## Evidence

### John Q. Easton 2008

John Q. Easton, Stephen Ponisciak, Stuart Luppescu. (2008). From High School to the Future: The Pathway to 20. Research Report, Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/high-school-future-pathway-20

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Analysis from the CCSR ACT preparation study of test score growth from fall eleventh-grade PLAN to spring ACT across four subject areas, controlling for other factors. Figure 7 shows the same grade-achievement relationship for Math ACT, and the report states it holds for all four subjects regardless of starting PLAN score.

> "Students who got As in eleventh grade English averaged 18.5 on the ACT; students who got Bs averaged 18.0; C grades resulted in ACT scores of 17.2; and students with Ds or Fs in English actually received ACT scores lower than their earlier PLAN scores."
