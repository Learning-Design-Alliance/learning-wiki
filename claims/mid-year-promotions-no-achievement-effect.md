# Mid-year promotions after retention neither helped nor harmed students' tested achievement in basic skills

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students initially retained who rejoined age-appropriate classmates showed no evidence of harm or substantial benefit in tested achievement, though the authors advise caution. [→ Nagaoka 2004](#nagaoka-2004)

## Evidence

### Nagaoka 2004

Nagaoka, J., & Roderick, M. (2004). Ending Social Promotion: The Effects of Retention. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/ending-social-promotion-effects-retention

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Comparison of double-promoted students (over one-quarter of retained third graders and one-third of sixth graders in 1998-1999 rejoined their classmates) with summer-promoted peers found slightly higher third-grade gains and no sixth-grade differences; the authors call for cautious interpretation.

> "While our results should be interpreted with caution, there is no evidence that these students' tested achievement in basic skills was harmed by their short-term experience of retention and their resultant lack of instructional time in the subsequent grade. There is also no substantial evidence of any benefit."
