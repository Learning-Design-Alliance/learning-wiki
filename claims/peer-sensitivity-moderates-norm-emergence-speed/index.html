Numerical simulation results shown in Figure 2, varying β = 0.01, 0.1, and 0.5 with N = 100. The article links the tipping point to "norm cascades commonly observed in educational settings". No effect size is printed.

q2 · i? · theoretical · r2

Numerical simulation result from the same Figure 2 analysis across peer-sensitivity levels. The article associates this regime with learning environments where AI use is largely individualised and weakly shaped by social norms. No effect size is printed.

"When peer sensitivity is weak ( β = 0.01), responsible AI use increases only gradually with higher reflection rewards, remaining marginal even at high incentive levels."

Related Claims¶

  • In simulations, increasing the reflection reward produces a sharp threshold-driven transition around r ≈ 1.5, beyond which responsible AI use with meaningful reflection dominates and opportunistic AI use collapses — related
  • In simulations, responsible AI-use norms emerge only within a constrained design space: when reflection effort is high relative to its reward, opportunistic AI use persists, and near the boundary small changes in reward or effort produce large shifts in responsible-use frequency — related
  • In simulations, superficial reflection stays at consistently low frequency across all reflection-reward levels, and misuse remains negligible, indicating the main governance challenge is normalised opportunistic AI use rather than overt misconduct — related
