## Evidence

### Bosch 2017

Bosch, N., & D'Mello, S. (2017). The Affective Experience of Novice Computer Programmers. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0069-5

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Association rule (Lift) analysis of 1303 primary-secondary affect pairs from 46 students who made at least 10 secondary judgments; the article reports only "confusion + frustration and curiosity + engagement" exceeded chance co-occurrence.

> "Only the confusion + frustration and curiosity + engagement affective state pairs occurred at levels above what was expected by chance (Lift=1)."

### Bosch 2017 (2)

Bosch, N., & D'Mello, S. (2017). The Affective Experience of Novice Computer Programmers. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0069-5

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Paired-samples t-tests comparing confidence values for both orderings of the two co-occurring pairs (Table 4; e.g., frustration→confusion .672 vs confusion→frustration .419); the article reports asymmetric implication within each pair.

> "Specifically, confusion was more likely to imply frustration than vice versa (p<.001) and curiosity was more likely to imply engagement than vice versa ( p<.0 01)."
