Evidence¶

Laninga-Wijnen 2023¶

Laninga-Wijnen, L., van den Berg, Y. H. M., Garandeau, C. F., Mulder, S., & de Castro, B. O. (2023). Does Being Defended Relate to Decreases in Victimization and Improved Psychosocial Adjustment Among Victims? Journal of Educational Psychology, 115(2), 363–377. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000712

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Longitudinal observational study of 5,415 Dutch elementary students (238 classrooms, 68 schools) drawn from a national evaluation of antibullying programmes; nobody was assigned to be defended. The main preregistered multilevel model (Table 4) predicts T2 outcomes from T1 status (defended victim, nondefended victim, nonvictim), controlling for initial adjustment. Nondefended victims did not differ significantly from defended victims in self-esteem (B = .017, CI [-.093, .126], β = .004), depressive symptoms (B = .070, CI [-.006, .146], β = .032, p < .10) or severity of victimization (B = -.044, CI [-.265, .177], β = -.008). A nonsignificant difference is not evidence of no difference: no equivalence test was reported, and because the design is correlational it cannot show whether being defended causes, or fails to cause, any change. The article prints unstandardised B and standardised β for group dummies rather than a d-type effect size, so impact is left uncoded (i?). Two non-preregistered sensitivity analyses did find fewer depressive symptoms in defended than nondefended victims: a victims-only analysis (B = .081, SE = .040, p = .041) and a reanalysis handling missing data by multiple imputation instead of FIML, whose one exception to the main results was this difference (B = .078, SE = .037, p = .037).

"victims who were defended at T1 did not differ from nondefended victims in self-esteem, depressive symptoms, and severity of victimization at T2. Nonvictims at T1 had higher self-esteem, lower depressive symptoms, and lower victimization at T2 compared with defended victims."
