Evidence¶

Berhenke 2011¶

Berhenke, Amanda; Miller, Alison L.; Brown, Eleanor; Seifer, Ronald; Dickstein, Susan. (2011). Observed Emotional and Behavioral Indicators of Motivation Predict School Readiness in Head Start Graduates. Early childhood research quarterly. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecresq.2011.04.001

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Hierarchical OLS regression with 131 Head Start graduates in kindergarten; observed motivation variables predicted teacher-rated outcomes. The article reports "High persistence in the task predicted higher math and reading skills" (R2 = .17 reading; R2 = .20 math).

"High persistence in the task predicted higher math and reading skills; competence, pride, and help-seeking were not significantly related to academic skills."

Berhenke 2011 (2)¶

Berhenke, Amanda; Miller, Alison L.; Brown, Eleanor; Seifer, Ronald; Dickstein, Susan. (2011). Observed Emotional and Behavioral Indicators of Motivation Predict School Readiness in Head Start Graduates. Early childhood research quarterly. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecresq.2011.04.001

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Same regression models predicting teacher-rated learning-related behaviors; "Increased task persistence predicted greater social competence, interpersonal and work-related skills, self-regulation, and less hyperactivity" with R2 range .22-.30.

"Increased task persistence predicted greater social competence, interpersonal and work-related skills, self-regulation, and less hyperactivity."

Discussion¶

Learner Variables¶

  • Motivation — predictor: learners who differ on it differ in outcomes

Related Claims¶

  • Observed help-seeking during the challenge task is unrelated to teacher-rated learning-related behaviors and academic skills — related
  • Observed motivation indicators predict student-teacher conflict but not closeness: less persistence, greater task competence, and more interest/arousal predict greater conflict — related
  • Observed shame expressions during challenging tasks predict greater teacher-rated social competence, work-related skills, and academic skills — related
  • Persistence behaviors in the face of challenge differed significantly by age and gender — related
  • Observed interest/arousal during challenging tasks predicts poorer teacher-rated social competence and more hyperactivity: the top third was rated least socially competent, and the top and middle thirds more hyperactive than the bottom third — related
  • Observed anxiety expressions during challenging tasks predict lower teacher-rated work-related and self-regulation skills — related
  • Children showing high levels of negative externalizing emotions during the challenge task are rated as less socially competent, more hyperactive, and with poorer interpersonal and self-regulatory skills — related
  • Learners' task-specific self-efficacy beliefs predict whether they initiate, persist through, and recover from difficulty on academic tasks. — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Low self-regulation (inattention and impulsivity) at kindergarten entry predicts greater teacher-child conflict by kindergarten spring, and this effect persists into first grade — related
