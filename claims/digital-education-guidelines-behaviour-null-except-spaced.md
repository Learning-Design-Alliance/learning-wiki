# Digital education on clinical practice guidelines produces little or no change in healthcare professionals' behaviour, except email-delivered spaced education

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Of nine studies evaluating behaviour change, only the study comparing email-delivered spaced education to no intervention reported improvement (RR 0.75, 95% CI 0.69–0.82), sustained at 18 months. [→ Tudor Car Lorainne 2019](#tudor-car-lorainne-2019)
`q3 i?` Studies comparing digital education to traditional learning (three studies) and more to less interactive digital education (four studies) largely reported no difference in behaviour. [→ Tudor Car Lorainne 2019 (2)](#tudor-car-lorainne-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Tudor Car Lorainne 2019

Tudor Car Lorainne, Soong Aijia, Kyaw Bhone Myint, Chua Kee Leng, Low-Beer Naomi, Majeed Azeem. (2019). Health professions digital education on clinical practice guidelines: a systematic review by Digital Health Education collaboration. BMC Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-019-1370-1

`q3 · i?`

Narrative synthesis of behaviour outcomes across the review's comparisons. The spaced education RCT reported reduced inappropriate PSA screening (RR 0.75); the online module study reported no difference. Behaviour data could not be meta-analysed due to heterogeneity.

> "Study on the use of spaced education via emails reported improvement in healthcare professional's behaviour (RR = 0.75, 95% CI 0.69, 0.828) [32]. Conversely, the study on the use of online module and discussions reported no difference in healthcare professionals' behaviour [33]."

### Tudor Car Lorainne 2019 (2)

Tudor Car Lorainne, Soong Aijia, Kyaw Bhone Myint, Chua Kee Leng, Low-Beer Naomi, Majeed Azeem. (2019). Health professions digital education on clinical practice guidelines: a systematic review by Digital Health Education collaboration. BMC Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-019-1370-1

`q3 · i?`

Narrative synthesis: three RCTs comparing digital education with traditional learning found no behaviour difference; four studies comparing more to less interactive digital education also largely reported no difference (Fig. 4).

> "Three studies analysed the healthcare professionals' behaviour change and reported no difference between the groups (Additional file 1) [35, 37, 40]."
