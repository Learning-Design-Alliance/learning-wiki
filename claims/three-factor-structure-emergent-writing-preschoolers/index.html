Evidence¶

Puranik 2014¶

Puranik, C. S., & Lonigan, C. J. (2014). Emergent Writing in Preschoolers: Preliminary Evidence for a Theoretical Framework. Reading Research Quarterly, 49(4). https://doi.org/10.1002/rrq.79

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Confirmatory factor analysis in EQS 6.1 on age-standardized task scores from 372 preschool children compared one-, two-, and three-factor models. The three-factor model (CFI = .98, RMSEA = .05) fit best; all alternatives fit "significantly worse fit to the data than did the three-factor model" (χ2 difference tests, p < .001).

"Both the three-factor model and the two-factor model in which Conceptual Knowledge and Procedural Knowledge factors were combined into a single-factor provided adequate fits to the data; however, the χ2 difference test revealed that the two-factor model with the combined Conceptual Knowledge and Procedural Knowledge factor yielded a significantly worse fit to the data than did the three-factor model."

Puranik 2014 (2)¶

Puranik, C. S., & Lonigan, C. J. (2014). Emergent Writing in Preschoolers: Preliminary Evidence for a Theoretical Framework. Reading Research Quarterly, 49(4). https://doi.org/10.1002/rrq.79

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Parameter estimates from the three-factor CFA for the combined sample (N = 372) showed all paths significant at p < 0.001, with the "Procedural Knowledge factor accounting for between 41 and 71% of the variance in individual tasks" and Conceptual Knowledge 30–54%.

"Factors accounted for nontrivial amounts of the variance in children’s scores on the different emergent writing tasks, with the Conceptual Knowledge factor accounting for between 30 and 54% of the variance in individual tasks, the Procedural Knowledge factor accounting for between 41 and 71% of the variance in individual tasks"
