## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Students who attended all three prior sections had 24% higher odds of using GPT-4; students from the highest-HDI countries had 17% lower odds than those from the lowest; older and male students were more likely to use it. [→ Allen Nie 2024](#allen-nie-2024)

## Evidence

### Allen Nie 2024

Allen Nie, Yash Chandak, Miroslav Suzara, Ali Malik, Juliette Woodrow, Matt Peng, Mehran Sahami, Emma Brunskill, and Chris Piech. (2024). The GPT Surprise: Offering Large Language Model Chat in a Massive Coding Class Reduced Engagement but Increased Adopters' Exam Performances. [doi:10.31219/osf.io/qy8zd](https://doi.org/10.31219/osf.io/qy8zd)

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Logistic regression on experiment-group data predicting GPT-4 usage from prior covariates (Figure 6). Section attendance OR=1.24 and HDI, age, and gender effects were significant; "We did not find statistical significance in other characteristics, including English fluency."

> "If they were observed having attended all three sections in prior weeks, their odds of using GPT-4 would have increased by 24%. For HDI (the HDI is an index normalized to be between 0 and 1), students from the most wealthy country (HDI=1) will have 17% lower odds of using GPT-4 than students from the poorest country (HDI=0)."
