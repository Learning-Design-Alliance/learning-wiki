## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Human grading has no statistically significant effect on the probability of an unhelpful retrospective feedback evaluation; equivalence was not tested. [→ Heinrich Tobias 2025](#heinrich-tobias-2025)

## Evidence

### Heinrich Tobias 2025

Heinrich Tobias, Baily Spencer, Chen Kuan-Wu, DeOliveira Jack, Park Sanghoon, Wang Navida Chun-Han (2025). AI-assisted grading and personalized feedback in large political science classes: Results from randomized controlled trials. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0328041

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same test-level analysis with a binary any-unhelpful outcome; the coefficient was not statistically significant and equivalence was not tested. In the pooled sample 10.0% gave an unhelpful rating and 60.5% a helpful one.

> "Human grading has no statistically significant effect on unhelpful evaluations (mean: -0.30, SE: 0.50), but increases the probability of a helpful evaluation in a statistically significant but modest way (mean: 2.13, SE: 0.64)."
