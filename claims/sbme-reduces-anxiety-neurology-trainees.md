## Evidence

### Sela Y 2025

Sela Y, Cassuto M, Capua T, Dadbaeiv L, Molad J, Hallevi H, Gadoth A, Sadan O, Seyman E. (2025). Acute neurology simulation-based training boosts confidence and reduces anxiety in novice neurology residents and nursing staff. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07948-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Wilcoxon signed-rank analysis of pre-post questionnaires in the 79-participant SBME cohort; general VAS anxiety fell from "2.48 (SD0.84) pre vs. 2.06 (SD0.85) post-simulation". No effect size is printed.

> "An analysis using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test revealed that participants’ anxiety levels, as measured by both general anxiety (on a VAS scale of 1–4) and the STAI-6 questionnaire, significantly decreased post-intervention (z=-3.44, p = 0.001 and z=-5.13, p < 0.001, respectively)."

### Sela Y 2025 (2)

Sela Y, Cassuto M, Capua T, Dadbaeiv L, Molad J, Hallevi H, Gadoth A, Sadan O, Seyman E. (2025). Acute neurology simulation-based training boosts confidence and reduces anxiety in novice neurology residents and nursing staff. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07948-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Table 3 reports per-item STAI-6 medians pre and post simulation; the total score fell from 15 (IQR 16-12) to 11 (IQR 15-8), p < 0.001, though item 3 (I feel upset) was not significant (p = 0.16).

> "Furthemore, improvement was noted in all STAI-6 domains post simulations compared to pre-simulations as detailed in Table 3."
