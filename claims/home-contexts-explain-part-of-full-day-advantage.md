# Out-of-school contexts — home cognitive stimulation and household poverty — partly explain both the kindergarten-year advantage of full-day kindergarten and its later fade-out

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Adding home cognitive stimulation, poverty, and child-care measures reduced the program-type association with kindergarten-year math and reading growth by 26% and 19%, respectively. [→ Votruba-Drzal 2008](#votruba-drzal-2008)
`q3 i?` The same out-of-school measures reduced the part-day coefficient on the post-kindergarten slope by about 9%, suggesting convergence is partly explained by part-day children's lower poverty and more stimulating homes. [→ Votruba-Drzal 2008 (2)](#votruba-drzal-2008-2)

## Evidence

### Votruba-Drzal 2008

Votruba-Drzal, E., Li-Grining, C. P., & Maldonado-Carreño, C. (2008). A Developmental Perspective on Full- Versus Part-Day Kindergarten and Children's Academic Trajectories Through Fifth Grade. Child Development, 79(4), 957-978. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2008.01170.x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Model 2 of the piecewise HLM added home learning environment, poverty, and child-care covariates at Level 2. The article reports "a 26% and 19% reduction in the association between kindergarten program type and children's math and reading skills trajectories"; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "The inclusion of this important set of variables as predictors of the kindergarten slope term resulted in a 26% and 19% reduction in the association between kindergarten program type and children's math and reading skills trajectories, respectively, during the kindergarten year"

### Votruba-Drzal 2008 (2)

Votruba-Drzal, E., Li-Grining, C. P., & Maldonado-Carreño, C. (2008). A Developmental Perspective on Full- Versus Part-Day Kindergarten and Children's Academic Trajectories Through Fifth Grade. Child Development, 79(4), 957-978. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2008.01170.x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Model 2 of the ECLS-K piecewise HLM also predicted the post-kindergarten slope. The article reports the part-day coefficient "fell by about 9%" and attributes this to cognitive stimulation in the home and family poverty status; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "the coefficient on the part-day kindergarten indicator for the second slope of children's math and reading trajectories fell by about 9% with the introduction of theadditional measures into theanalysis"
