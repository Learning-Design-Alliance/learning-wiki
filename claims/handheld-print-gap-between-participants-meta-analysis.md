# Reading comprehension is slightly worse on handheld devices than in print in between-participants studies (g = -.113)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3` · `i0` negligible

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` Across primary studies using between-participants designs, comprehension is significantly lower when reading on handheld devices than in print, with a small pooled effect (k = 38, g = -.113). [→ Salmerón 2023](#salmeron-2023)

## Evidence

### Salmerón 2023

Salmerón, L., Altamura, L., Delgado, P., Karagiorgi, A., & Vargas, C. (2023). Reading comprehension on handheld devices vs. on paper: A narrative review and meta-analysis of the medium effect and its moderators. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://dx.doi.org/10.1037/edu0000830

`q3 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Multilevel random-effects meta-analysis of between-participants comparisons of handheld-device versus print reading, pooling k = 38 effect sizes with Hedges' g. The synthesis showed "a significant small size effect favoring print text comprehension" (g = -.113).

> "Results from the two multilevel random-effect meta-analyses, which included primary studies that used either between-participants (k = 38, g = -.113) or within-participants (k = 21, g = -.103) designs consistently showed a significant small size effect favoring print text comprehension."

### Salmerón 2023 (2)

Salmerón, L., Altamura, L., Delgado, P., Karagiorgi, A., & Vargas, C. (2023). Reading comprehension on handheld devices vs. on paper: A narrative review and meta-analysis of the medium effect and its moderators. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://dx.doi.org/10.1037/edu0000830

`q3 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

A second multilevel random-effects meta-analysis of within-participants comparisons (k = 21 effect sizes, 1,379 participants) using the standardized mean change index dc also showed "a significant small size effect favoring print text comprehension" (g = -.103).

> "Results from the two multilevel random-effect meta-analyses, which included primary studies that used either between-participants (k = 38, g = -.113) or within-participants (k = 21, g = -.103) designs consistently showed a significant small size effect favoring print text comprehension."
