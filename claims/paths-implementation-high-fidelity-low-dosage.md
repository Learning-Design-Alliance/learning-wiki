## Evidence

### Humphrey 2016

Humphrey, N., Barlow, A., Wigelsworth, M., Lendrum, A., Pert, K., Joyce, C., Stephens, E., Wo, L., Squires, G., Woods, K., Calam, R., & Turner, A. (2016). A cluster randomized controlled trial of the Promoting Alternative Thinking Strategies (PATHS) curriculum. Journal of School Psychology, 58, 73–89. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsp.2016.07.002

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Structured observations of PATHS lessons in treatment schools, rated on 0–10 indicators by three trained research assistants, found "Mean scores for fidelity (8.20), quality (8.48), participant responsiveness (7.34), and reach (91%) were all high", but dosage fell far below the recommended schedule.

> "Mean scores for fidelity (8.20), quality (8.48), participant responsiveness (7.34), and reach (91%) were all high. However, mean dosage scores indicated that classes were on course to deliver only 20 of the approximately 401 PATHS lessons in a given year."
