Evidence¶

McLay 2023¶

McLay, K. F., Thomasse, L., & Reyes, V. C., Jr. (2023). Embracing discomfort in active learning and technology-rich higher education settings: sensemaking through reflexive inquiry. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-023-10192-6

q1 · i? · qualitative · r2

Autobiographical snapshot (qualitative self-study): the first author reports developing "an internally persuasive discourse that allows me to \"navigate and negotiate\" SET data" through the team's Bakhtinian conversations. No effect size is printed.

"As we talked through the discomfort aroused by these data, I was able to synthesise my intellectual understanding that active learning can be unsettling and confusing (Dall' Alba & Bengtsen, 2019) with my lived experience. As a result, I developed an internally persuasive discourse that allows me to "navigate and negotiate" SET data"

McLay 2023 (2)¶

McLay, K. F., Thomasse, L., & Reyes, V. C., Jr. (2023). Embracing discomfort in active learning and technology-rich higher education settings: sensemaking through reflexive inquiry. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-023-10192-6

q1 · i? · qualitative · r2

Discussion-section interpretation: the authors suggest "'discomfort chronotopes' emerged" from dialogic engagement with SET data, an authors' interpretive claim rather than a tested result.

"Dialogic engagement with these data was supported through professional conversations that invoked chronotopic 'chunks of history' (Blommaert, 2010) and we suggest that 'discomfort chronotopes' emerged."
