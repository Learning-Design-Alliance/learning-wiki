# Action and role-playing videogames are most preferred and simulation and puzzle least preferred in both cultures, with culture-specific traditional game preferences

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In both cultures action and role-playing videogames were the most preferred and simulation and puzzle the least preferred genres; British children most preferred sports traditional games while Turkish children most preferred action traditional games. [→ Yılmaz 2022](#ylmaz-2022)

## Evidence

### Yılmaz 2022

Yılmaz, E., Yel, S., & Griffiths, M. D. (2022). Comparison of children's social problem-solving skills who play videogames and traditional games: A cross-cultural study. Computers & Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2022.104548

`q2 · i?`

Descriptive preference data from free-recall self-reports. British children most preferred action (65.5%), role-playing (61.6%) and sports (51%) videogames; for traditional games, British children most preferred sports games (50%) while Turkish children most preferred action games (45%).

> "Turkish children indicated that their most preferred videogame genres were action games (47.8%), and role-playing games (39.2%), and their least preferred videogame genres were simulation games (15.7%) and puzzle games (16.8%)."
