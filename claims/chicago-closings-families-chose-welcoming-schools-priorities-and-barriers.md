# Interviewed families enrolled in designated welcoming schools because the schools matched their priorities, because they believed enrollment there was mandatory, or because barriers limited other options

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Families choosing welcoming schools cited proximity to home, perceived strong academics, and personal connections such as transferred staff and friends. [→ de la Torre 2015](#de-la-torre-2015)

## Evidence

### de la Torre 2015

de la Torre, M., Gordon, M. F., Moore, P., & Cowhy, J. (2015). School Closings in Chicago: Understanding Families' Choices and Constraints for New School Enrollment. UChicago CCSR Research Report. https://www.cps.edu/qualityschools/Pages/Parents.aspx

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Qualitative interviews with 95 families directly affected by the closings. Families cited that the school was "close to home," perceived strong academics, and personal connections like friends and transferred staff as reasons for enrolling in the welcoming school.

> "Many families we interviewed chose to enroll their children into their designated welcoming school because the school matched their priorities and needs."

### de la Torre 2015 (2)

de la Torre, M., Gordon, M. F., Moore, P., & Cowhy, J. (2015). School Closings in Chicago: Understanding Families' Choices and Constraints for New School Enrollment. UChicago CCSR Research Report. https://www.cps.edu/qualityschools/Pages/Parents.aspx

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Interviews with affected families found some believed they had to enroll in the designated welcoming school because the district automatically enrolled students by mid-June; others cited practical constraints including neighborhood safety, transportation costs, missing IEP supports, and lack of time to explore options.

> "Many families believed that because of this automatic enrollment, they could not choose a different school."
