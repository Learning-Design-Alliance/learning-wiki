# Students incorrectly claim that a non-conducting charged object shields its interior from the electric field due to external charges

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students claim the field inside a hollow non-conducting charged sphere or cylinder is zero because external charges are shielded out. [→ Jing Li 2023](#jing-li-2023)

## Evidence

### Jing Li 2023

Jing Li, Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2023). Helping students apply superposition principle in problems involving spherical, cylindrical and planar charge distributions. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2308.02003](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2308.02003)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Difficulty observed on pre-test question (1) and again on post-test question (1) with a charged sphere near a point charge, probed in think-aloud interviews. Students claimed the interior is "shielded from charges outside" even when reminded the objects are non-conducting. Qualitative evidence; no effect size.

> "some students claimed that the inﬁnite line with uniform charge in Fig. 1 cannot produce a ﬁeld at point A inside the inﬁnite non-conducting hollow cylinder because point A is inside the cylinder and is shielded from charges outside"
