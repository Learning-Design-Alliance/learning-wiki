Evidence¶

Ge 2021¶

Ge, X. (2021). Emotion matters for academic success. Education Tech Research Dev, 69, 67–70. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-020-09925-8

q2 · i? · theoretical · r2

The commentary summarizes the Jarrell et al. (2017) study of learners' retrospective performance-outcome emotions and academic performance (efficiency and accuracy), reporting that "positive emotions were associated with higher performance while negative emotions were associated with poorer performance."

"The results revealed that positive emotions were associated with higher performance while negative emotions were associated with poorer performance, and the low intensity emotions were associated with performance between high and low levels."
