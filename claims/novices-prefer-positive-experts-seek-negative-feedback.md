## Evidence

### Gormally Cara 2014

Gormally Cara, Evans Mara, Brickman Peggy. (2014). Feedback about Teaching in Higher Ed: Neglected Opportunities to Promote Change. CBE—Life Sciences Education 13(2). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.13-12-0235

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The essay reports, citing Finkelstein and Fishbach (2012), that "Novices generally have lower self-esteem, and they indicate a preference for positive feedback," while experts seek negative feedback to learn how to correct errors. It concludes feedback providers should account for the recipient's confidence and experience level.

> "Novices generally have lower self-esteem, and they indicate a preference for positive feedback. For example, novice learners preferred language instructors who emphasized what students were doing well in the classroom rather than correcting mistakes (Finkelstein and Fishbach, 2012). Experts, however, will seek out negative feedback, indicating more interest in learning what they did wrong and how to correct it (Finkelstein and Fishbach, 2012)."
