# Reading for study results in slower reading than reading for general comprehension on both target and spill-over sentences

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Participants reading for study showed longer reading times than those reading for comprehension on target sentences (d = .23) and spill-over sentences (d = .24). [→ Moort 2022](#moort-2022)

## Evidence

### Moort 2022

Moort, M. L. van, Koornneef, A. W., Wilderjans, T. F., & Broek, P. W. van den. (2022). Validation processes and reading purpose: Is validation against knowledge and prior text influenced by reading goal? Journal of Educational Psychology, 114(7), 1533-1552. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000769

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

In a self-paced sentence-by-sentence reading experiment with 120 Dutch undergraduates (114 analyzed after exclusions), mixed-effects linear regression on log-transformed reading times showed that "participants that read for study showed longer reading times than participants that read for comprehension" on both target and spill-over sentences, with d = .23 and d = .24 respectively.

> "participants that read for study showed longer reading times than participants that read for comprehension, both on target (Mstudy = 2723 ms, SEstudy = 28; Mcomp = 2488 ms, SEcomp = 25, b = .10, SE = .04, t = 2.28, p = .023 d = .23) and spill-over sentences"
