# The two teacher groups did not differ on released-math tasks; both groups made pretest-to-posttest gains, suggesting math-in-science-context tasks better capture OpenSciEd math reasoning

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On released-math tasks, students in both groups made pretest-to-posttest gains with greater gains in fall, and the groups did not differ, unlike on math-in-science-context tasks. [→ McElhaney 2025](#mcelhaney-2025)

## Evidence

### McElhaney 2025

McElhaney, K. W., Hodkowski, N. M., McMahon, L. H., Jacobs, J., Penuel, W. R., Anton, G., Henson, K., Urban, R., & Parker, D. A. (2025). Promoting Mathematical Reasoning in OpenSciEd Middle School Curriculum Units. Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/4ea4b3c5-c663-4484-8727-258bff346d2d

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of student assessment scores across the two teacher groups and three administrations found group differences on math-in-science-context tasks "but not on released-math tasks." The report interprets this as the context-embedded tasks better capturing the reasoning OpenSciEd asks of students. No effect size is printed.

> "our finding that the Treatment and Delayed Treatment groups differed on math-in-science-context tasks but not on released-math tasks suggests that the math-in-science-context tasks better capture the kind of math reasoning students are asked to do in OpenSciEd."
