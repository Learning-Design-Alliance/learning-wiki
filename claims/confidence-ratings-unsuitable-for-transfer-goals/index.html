Evidence¶

Kit S. Double 2025¶

Kit S. Double, Micah B. Goldwater, Damian P. Birney. (2025). Reactivity to confidence ratings: evidence of impaired rule-learning. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09413-5

q1 · i? · causal · r3

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion, resting on the experimental finding of impaired rule-learning under confidence ratings and the argument that knowledge transfer is a central goal of education that is often difficult to generate. No separate educational outcome was measured in this study.

"Furthermore, this study provides evidence that confidence rat -ings are unlikely to be an effective educational tool, particularly if knowledge transfer is the ultimate goal."

Related Claims¶

  • Reactivity differs by rating scope: item-focused confidence ratings impaired rule learning while task-focused judgments of learning did not — related
  • Eliciting confidence ratings impairs rule-based category learning compared to both no-rating and judgment-of-learning conditions on ambiguous transfer items — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • The confidence-rating impairment on ambiguous transfer items persists among participants who met the learning criterion — related
  • Confidence ratings do not impede category learning per se: no condition differences in training accuracy or on unambiguous and trained transfer items — related
