Evidence¶

Treiman Rebecca 2013¶

Treiman Rebecca, Kessler Brett. (2013). Learning to Use an Alphabetic Writing System. Language Learning and Development. https://doi.org/10.1080/15475441.2013.812016

q2 · i? · review · r3

Review of studies of beginning spellers (Treiman, 1993, 1994): children bootstrap from letter-name knowledge to produce partial phonological spellings such as ‹R› and ‹KR› for car. The review frames this as use of the known to tackle the unknown.

"a U.S. child can write ‹R› for car if she detects the sequence /αɹ/ in the spoken word and if she writes down the letter that has this name."

Treiman Rebecca 2013 (2)¶

Treiman Rebecca, Kessler Brett. (2013). Learning to Use an Alphabetic Writing System. Language Learning and Development. https://doi.org/10.1080/15475441.2013.812016

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports cross-language evidence, citing Portuguese and Hebrew studies, that letter-name knowledge supports invented spellings beyond English.

"Further evidence that children use their knowledge of letter names when inventing spellings comes from learners of Portuguese (Pollo, Kessler, & Treiman, 2005, 2007) and Hebrew (Levin, Patel, Margalit, & Barad, 2002)."
