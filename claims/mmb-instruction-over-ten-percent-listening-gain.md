# Multimedia-based instruction yields a greater than 10% increase in L2 listening comprehension versus a 2.4% gain under regular instruction over a 10-week intervention

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After a ten-week intervention, the experimental group receiving MMB instruction showed more than 10% increase in listening comprehension, while the control group's mean score showed only 2.4% progress. [→ Sejdi Sejdiu 2017](#sejdi-sejdiu-2017)

## Evidence

### Sejdi Sejdiu 2017

Sejdi Sejdiu. (2017). Are Listening Skills Best Enhanced Through the Use of Multimedia Technology. Digital Education Review - Number 32, December 2017. http://greav.ub.edu/der/

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental study of 50 primary pupils aged 9–10 in Prishtina, Kosovo, with a control group taught by an ELT and an experimental group whose curriculum incorporated MMB instruction materials; the control group's post-test mean rose only "2.4% progress from the pre-test to the post-test assessment".

> "The mean score of the group showed only a 2.4% progress from the pre-test to the post-test assessment. This slight improvement in listening comprehension skills is a logical outcome of a systematic learning process targeting this skill."

### Sejdi Sejdiu 2017 (2)

Sejdi Sejdiu. (2017). Are Listening Skills Best Enhanced Through the Use of Multimedia Technology. Digital Education Review - Number 32, December 2017. http://greav.ub.edu/der/

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

In the same quasi-experiment, the experimental group receiving rich multimedia instruction showed "more than 10% increase in the listening comprehension" on pre- to post-test scores assessed with KET-based listening tasks; the article labels the result highly significant without printing a test statistic.

> "The experimental group, which received rich multimedia instruction, revealed more than 10% increase in the listening comprehension"
