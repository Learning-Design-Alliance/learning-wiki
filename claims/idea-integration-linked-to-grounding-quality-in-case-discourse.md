# In the case discourse, failure to integrate individuals' ideas with sound verification was linked to low-level inquiry outcomes, while successful high-level grounding enabled groups to integrate ideas and negotiate shared knowledge

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Where the group did not successfully integrate individuals' ideas or reached quick consensus without sound verification, forging common ground was hindered and low-level inquiry outcomes followed. [→ Esther Tan 2018](#esther-tan-2018)
`q1 i?` The PISG group leveraged one another's ideas and integrated them to negotiate shared knowledge, which the authors link to high-level grounding leading to high-level evidence generation. [→ Esther Tan 2018 (2)](#esther-tan-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Esther Tan 2018

Esther Tan (2018). Effects of two differently sequenced classroom scripts on common ground in collaborative inquiry learning. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-018-9460-6

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The authors' interpretive reading of the PISG case discourse: failed integration or "a quick consensus with no sound verification statements/questions" "hindered the process of forging common ground," leading to two GEL, two EEL and one DCL occurrences.

> "where the group did not successfully integrate individuals’ ideas and/or there was a quick consensus with no sound verification statements/questions to test new perspectives/knowledge, this hindered the process of forging common ground, which led to the occurrence of two low-level generation of evidence (GEL), two low-level evaluations of evidence (EEL) and one low-level drawing of a conclusion (DCL)"
