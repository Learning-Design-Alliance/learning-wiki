# On interpretation-type questions, ChatGPT-5, Gemini 3.0 Pro, and Claude Sonnet 4.5 achieved perfect scores (100%), significantly exceeding trainee performance, while ChatGPT-4o performed comparably to fellows

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` ChatGPT-5, Gemini 3.0 Pro, and Claude Sonnet 4.5 scored 27/27 (100%) on interpretation-type questions, exceeding residents (87.65 ± 4.28%) and fellows (92.59 ± 7.41%); ChatGPT-4o scored 92.59%, comparable to fellows. [→ Kim Seonghwan 2026](#kim-seonghwan-2026)

## Evidence

### Kim Seonghwan 2026

Kim Seonghwan, Ji Man, Kim Chung Young, Yun Young In, Lee Gahye, Yoon Chang Ho, Kim Mee Kum. (2026). Comparative performance of large language models in answering cornea and cataract surgery questions for resident training. BMC Ophthalmology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12886-026-04767-z

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Performance on the 27 interpretation-type questions. The article states ChatGPT-4o (92.59%) "performed comparable to fellows (92.59 ± 7.41%)" and better than residents (87.65 ± 4.28%).

> "In interpretation-type questions, ChatGPT-5, Gemini 3.0 Pro, and Claude Sonnet 4.5 achieved perfect scores (27/27; 100%), whereas ChatGPT-4o scored 25/27 (92.59%) (Fig. 2)."

### Kim Seonghwan 2026 (2)

Kim Seonghwan, Ji Man, Kim Chung Young, Yun Young In, Lee Gahye, Yoon Chang Ho, Kim Mee Kum. (2026). Comparative performance of large language models in answering cornea and cataract surgery questions for resident training. BMC Ophthalmology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12886-026-04767-z

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

One-sample Wilcoxon signed-rank test on interpretation-type scores versus all trainees; three models significant at p = 0.031, ChatGPT-4o not significant (p = 0.156).

> "In interpretation-type questions, ChatGPT-5 (p = 0.031), Gemini 3.0 Pro (p = 0.031), and Claude Sonnet 4.5 (p = 0.031) achieved significantly higher scores than all trainees, whereas ChatGPT-4o did not reach statistical significance (Table 1)."
