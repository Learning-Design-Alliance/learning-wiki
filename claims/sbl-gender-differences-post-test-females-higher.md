## Evidence

### Gomwe Howard 2026

Gomwe Howard, Phiri Lesego, Marange Chioneso Show, Kgongwana Thokozile Harriet, Zikalala-Mabope Lindi, Mothapo Rahab, Dyani Xolelwa. (2026). Simulation-based learning enhances interprofessional competencies in undergraduate health students: a retrospective pre-post study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09125-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Independent samples t-tests comparing males (N = 174) and females (N = 366) in the retrospective pre-post study; the article reports "Female participants scored significantly higher" on five post-test domains. Roles and responsibilities showed no significant difference (p = 0.057).

> "Female participants scored significantly higher in communication (t = −2.035, p = 0.042), collaboration (t = −2.625, p = 0.009), patient-centred approach (t = −2.922, p = 0.004), conflict management (t = −2.077, p = 0.039), and team functioning (t = −3.630, p < 0.001)."
