## Evidence

### Iwamoto 2017

Iwamoto, D. H., Hargis, J., Taitano, E. J., & Vuong, K. (2017). Analyzing the efficacy of the testing effect using Kahoot on student performance. Turkish Online Journal of Distance Education, 18(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1145220

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pilot quasi-experimental action research study with 49 undergraduate psychology students in two sections of one General Psychology course, one section assigned by coin flip to Kahoot. Both sections had the same lectures, videos and group discussions; the Kahoot section spent the last 10 minutes of some classes on no-stakes Kahoot quizzes while the control section continued the class. Before the exam the control section received a study guide and a 50-minute lecture-based review, and the Kahoot section a 50-minute session replaying the Kahoot quizzes; both had office hours. So the comparison is Kahoot quizzing against study-guide and lecture review, not against lecture and discussion alone. The Independent-Samples T-Test found significance "at the p<.05 level (F(1, 47) = 7.801, p = .008)"; the article prints neither group means nor an effect size, so the size of the difference, and its direction except as the authors describe it ("enhance and improve"), cannot be read from the reported results.

> "An Independent-Samples T -Test was conducted to compare the effects of the online application Kahoot™ on the mean test scores in the experimental and control groups. Significance was determined between the two groups at the p<.05 level (F(1, 47) = 7.801, p = .008)."
