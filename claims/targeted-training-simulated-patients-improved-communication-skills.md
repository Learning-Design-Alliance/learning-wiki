## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Comparing the first and third surveys, students rated their communication skills slightly better after the targeted training, but the difference was not significant (p > 0.05); they did report paying significantly more attention to asking open-ended questions (p = 0.0245) and that the course contributed to their skills (p = 0.0127). ## Evidence

### Bock Anna 2024

Bock Anna, Wagenknecht Nina, Winnand Philipp, Katz Marie Sophie, Ooms Mark, Heitzer Marius, Hölzle Frank, Modabber Ali. (2024). Improvement of students’ communication skills through targeted training and the use of simulated patients in dental education—a prospective cohort study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05818-z

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Prospective cohort study of dental students (n = 34 analyzed) in an oral and maxillofacial surgery internship; surveys at three time points using 10-point Likert scales. The abstract reports "a significant improvement in communication skills (p < 0.001)", but the results report the before-and-after comparison as not significant (p > 0.05); the p < 0.001 values are students' ratings of the earlier traditional internship. The before-and-after comparison is of self-ratings. Lecturers also rated students' encounters with simulated patients on a 9-point mini-CEX sheet (Table 4); between the first and second consultations those ratings did not differ significantly on any aspect (p > 0.05). No effect size is printed.

> "After targeted training followed by subsequent use in simulated patients, there was a significant improvement in communication skills (p < 0.001)."
