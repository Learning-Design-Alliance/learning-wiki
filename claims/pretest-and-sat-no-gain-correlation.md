# Class-average pre-test score and institutional SAT score do not correlate with normalized gain after correcting for test and teaching method

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` No correlation was found (p=0.47) between class-average pre-test scores and normalized gain after correcting for test and teaching method, and SAT scores correlated with pre-test scores but not with gains. [→ Von Korff 2016](#von-korff-2016)

## Evidence

### Von Korff 2016

Von Korff, J., Archibeque, B., Gomez, K.A., Heckendorf, T., McKagan, S.B., Sayre, E.C., Schenk, E.W., Shepherd, C., Sorell, L. (2016). Secondary Analysis of Teaching Methods in Introductory Physics: a 50k-Student Study. Originally published 2014. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1603.00516](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1603.00516)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis across the full secondary-analysis database, with about ten times as many institutions as the Coletta and Phillips study and more than three times as many as Hake and Coletta and Phillips combined. A second analysis found pre-test scores correlated with institutional 25th and 75th percentile SAT scores but gains did not, after correcting for SAT score changes and inflation.

> "We found no correlation (p=0.47) between class-average pre-test scores and normalized gain after correcting for test and teaching method."
