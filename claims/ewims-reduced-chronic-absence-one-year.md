## Subclaims
`q4 i?` In a randomized controlled trial, the percentage of students chronically absent (missing 10 percent or more of instructional time) was significantly lower in EWIMS schools (10 percent) than in control schools (14 percent). [→ Faria 2017](#faria-2017)

## Evidence

### Faria 2017

Faria, A.-M., Sorensen, N., Heppen, J., Bowdon, J., Taylor, S., Eisner, R., & Foster, S. (2017). Getting students on track for graduation: Impacts of the Early Warning Intervention and Monitoring System after one year (REL 2017–272). Washington, DC: U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Regional Educational Laboratory Midwest. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial of 73 high schools (37 EWIMS, 36 control) with 37,671 first- and second-year students; multilevel regression on extant student records. The model-adjusted chronic absence rate was "lower in EWIMS schools (10 percent) than in control schools (14 percent)".

> "The percentage of students who were chronically absent (that is, missed 10 percent or more of instructional time) was lower in EWIMS schools (10 percent) than in control schools (14 percent; see figure 3). This 4 percentage point difference was statistically significant."
