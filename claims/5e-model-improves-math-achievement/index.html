Evidence¶

Polanin 2024¶

Polanin, J. R., Austin, M., Taylor, J. A., Steingut, R. R., Rodgers, M. A., & Williams, R. (2024). Effects of the 5E Instructional Model: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. AERA Open. https://doi.org/10.1177/23328584241269866

q3 · i?

Unconditional random-effects meta-analysis of the math outcome domain, drawn from only k = 6 eligible studies per the article. The printed average was "g = 0.70, SE = 0.20, 95% CI[0.31, 1.10]".

"math (g = 0.70, SE = 0.20, 95% CI[0.31, 1.10])"

Polanin 2024 (2)¶

Polanin, J. R., Austin, M., Taylor, J. A., Steingut, R. R., Rodgers, M. A., & Williams, R. (2024). Effects of the 5E Instructional Model: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. AERA Open. https://doi.org/10.1177/23328584241269866

q3 · i?

Sensitivity analysis comparing studies with and without missing confirmatory moderator information in the math domain: g with missing = 1.09 versus g without missing = 0.38, a nearly threefold difference.

"For math, however, we found that studies with at least some missing information had effects nearly three times as large (g with missing = 1.09 vs. g without missing = 0.38)."
