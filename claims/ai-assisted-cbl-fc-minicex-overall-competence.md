# AI-assisted CBL+FC residents show superior overall clinical competence on Mini-CEX compared with lecture controls

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The AI-assisted CBL+FC group scored significantly higher on overall clinical competence in Mini-CEX assessments (6.36 vs. 5.12, P = 0.00009). [→ Wang 2026](#wang-2026)

## Evidence

### Wang 2026

Wang, Q., Hu, C., Li, Y., Zhang, T., & Zhang, S. (2026). AI-assisted case-based learning and flipped classroom to improve clinical decision-making: a randomized controlled trial in reproductive medicine. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2026.2670047

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Blinded two-examiner Mini-CEX assessment on a stable infertile patient case, completed within 40 minutes. The AI-assisted group scored 6.36 ± 1.11 versus 5.12 ± 0.93 for controls on overall clinical competence (P = 0.00009); no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Overall Clinical Competence	5.12 ± 0.93	6.36 ± 1.11	0.00009***"

### Wang 2026 (2)

Wang, Q., Hu, C., Li, Y., Zhang, T., & Zhang, S. (2026). AI-assisted case-based learning and flipped classroom to improve clinical decision-making: a randomized controlled trial in reproductive medicine. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2026.2670047

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The article reports significant between-group differences on medical interviewing skills (P = 0.0002), physical examination skills (P = 0.001), and humanistic qualities/professionalism (P = 0.0004), while clinical judgement (P = 0.281), counselling skills (P = 0.356), and organisation efficiency (P = 0.560) did not differ significantly.

> "Superior performance was observed in medical interviewing, physical examination, humanistic qualities, professionalism, and overall clinical competence (Table 2 and Figure 4B)."
