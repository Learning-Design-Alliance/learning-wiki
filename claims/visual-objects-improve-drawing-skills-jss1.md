# Teaching drawing with visual objects (2D or 3D) produces higher post-test drawing-skill scores than conventional teaching without visual objects

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` JSS1 students taught drawing with two- and three-dimensional visual objects scored significantly higher on post-test drawing achievement than students taught by conventional method (F = 14.163, p < 0.05). [→ Abass et al. 2014](#abass-et-al-2014)

## Evidence

### Abass et al. 2014

Bada Tayo Abass, Bello Isyakka, Ijisakin Yemi Olaolu & Fajuyigbe Michael Olusegun. (2014). Effects of Two and Three-Dimensional Visual Objects on the Acquisition of Drawing Skills among JSS1 Students in Osun State, Nigeria. World Journal of Education, 4(1). https://doi.org/10.5430/wje.v4n1p62

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pretest-posttest control-group experiment: 120 JSS1 students randomly assigned to a three-dimensional objects group, a two-dimensional objects group, and a conventional-method control (n = 40 each). ANCOVA on post-test VOAT scores yielded "an F-ratio of 14.163 which is significant at 0.05 level"; no effect size is printed.

> "As shown in Table 1.1, these values yielded an F-ratio of 14.163 which is significant at 0.05 level. This suggests that the post-test mean scores of subjects in the groups were statistically different."
