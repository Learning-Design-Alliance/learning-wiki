## Evidence

### Lehmann Ronny 2024

Lehmann Ronny, Klinke Petrowsky Michaela, Seitz Anke, Meyburg Jochen, Eppich Walter, Hoffmann Georg F., Tönshoff Burkhard, Huwendiek Sören. (2024). A novel blended and interprofessional approach to pediatric emergency training: self-assessment, perception, and perceived long-term effects. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06381-3

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Anonymous 10-month follow-up survey of all 112 training participants, returned by 57 (50.9%); the article reports 37 (64.9%) experienced real emergencies and, separately, that 91.7% of those agreed or totally agreed they benefited from the training in these situations.

> "Thirty-seven (64.9%) of the participants who returned follow-up survey forms (N = 57) experienced emergency situations at work during the 10 months after the training"

### Lehmann Ronny 2024 (2)

Lehmann Ronny, Klinke Petrowsky Michaela, Seitz Anke, Meyburg Jochen, Eppich Walter, Hoffmann Georg F., Tönshoff Burkhard, Huwendiek Sören. (2024). A novel blended and interprofessional approach to pediatric emergency training: self-assessment, perception, and perceived long-term effects. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06381-3

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same anonymous follow-up survey (50 responses to the beyond-emergencies item); the article reports 80% reported benefits in daily routine practice, with the house-specific guidelines broadly used and self-confidence reportedly improved.

> "Beyond emergencies situations, 80% of respondents also reported benefits from the training in daily routine practice."
