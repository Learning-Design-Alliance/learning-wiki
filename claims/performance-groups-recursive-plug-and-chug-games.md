## Evidence

### Andrew J. Mason 2016

Andrew J. Mason, Charles A. Bertram. (2016). Epistemic Impact on Group Problem Solving for Different Science Majors. US-China Education Review B. Originally published 2014. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.08666](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.08666)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Transcribed audiovisual data of 14 lab groups solving a context-rich rotational dynamics problem, categorized by group orientation (6 performance, 2 framework, 6 mixed; Table 3). Recursive Plug and Chug appeared in 5 performance and 5 mixed groups; "Performance and mixed gr oups both heavily used the Recursive Plug and Chug game."

> "Performance and mixed gr oups both heavily used the Recursive Plug and Chug game, while performance groups also focused strongly on Mapping Math(ematics) to Meaning in trying to find an equation that would relate the target to other problem concepts."
