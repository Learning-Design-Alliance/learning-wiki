Evidence¶

Langensee 2024¶

Langensee, L., Rumetshofer, T. & Mårtensson, J. (2024). Interplay of socioeconomic status, cognition, and school performance in the ABCD sample. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00233-x

q3 · i0 · associational · r2

Ordinal logistic regression models on ABCD data (N = 5001) at 2-year and 3-year follow-up, with self-reported grades as dependent variable. Tables 1–2 print parental education OR 1.29 (1.21, 1.38) and 1.34 (1.26, 1.43), ITN OR 1.37 and 1.36, and ADI OR 0.91 and 0.89, i.e. "the odds ratio for obtaining a higher grade at school increased with increasing levels of each of the three SES measures."

"For both models, the odds ratio for obtaining a higher grade at school increased with increasing levels of each of the three SES measures."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Cognitive ability interacts with parental education in predicting self-reported grades at both timepoints, but the authors judge the effect size negligible and not practically relevant — related
  • Neighborhood deprivation is no longer a statistically significant predictor of self-reported grades once cognitive ability is included in the models, and models including cognition fit better — reports the opposite
  • SES-by-timepoint interactions are not statistically significant for any of the three SES measures, although grade odds are higher at the 2-year than at the 3-year follow-up — related
  • Children reporting top grades come from systematically more advantaged socioeconomic circumstances than children reporting weaker grades, a pattern the authors relate to the Matthew effect — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • All SES subcomponents (parental education, family income, neighborhood quality) significantly predicted each intelligence domain, with education and income effects about twice neighborhood quality — related
