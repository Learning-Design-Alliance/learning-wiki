## Evidence

### Almomani Emad 2025

Almomani Emad, Tobin John, Fernandes Sonia, Sullivan Jacqueline, Saadeh Omar, Mustafa Emad, Pattison Natalie, Alinier Guillaume. (2025). A reflective learning conversation debriefing model for interprofessional simulation based education. BMC Medical Education, 25:1434. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07765-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Thematic analysis of four focus groups (two experimental, two control) conducted immediately after the final SBE session, using Braun and Clarke's six-step approach. This is Theme 2 from the experimental-group discussions, a qualitative finding with no effect sizes.

> "Participants reported that the learner-centered, incremental, and reflective nature of the RLC debriefing fostered deeper understanding and critical analysis of both technical and non-technical aspects of clinical scenarios."

### Almomani Emad 2025 (2)

Almomani Emad, Tobin John, Fernandes Sonia, Sullivan Jacqueline, Saadeh Omar, Mustafa Emad, Pattison Natalie, Alinier Guillaume. (2025). A reflective learning conversation debriefing model for interprofessional simulation based education. BMC Medical Education, 25:1434. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07765-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Qualitative finding from the experimental focus groups on multicultural group dynamics within the same thematic analysis; participants' perceptions, with no quantitative measures attached.

> "participants identified that working within multicultural, interprofessional groups posed challenges related to communication and engagement but expressed that the structured, inclusive nature of the RLC model helped facilitate equitable participation."
