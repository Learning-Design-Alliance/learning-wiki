# Revising the open-ended COVID-19 challenge (DC1 to DC3) significantly improved communication, data-visualization and technical grades, but the increase in scientific-quality grades was not significant

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Paired-sample t-tests show significant increases in mean grades from DC1 to DC3 in the communication, data-visualization and technical criteria, while the increase in the scientific-quality category was not significant. ## Evidence

### Marco Cosentino Lagomarsino 2022

Marco Cosentino Lagomarsino, Guglielmo Pacifico, Valerio Firmano, Edoardo Bella, Pietro Benzoni, Jacopo Grilli, Federico Bassetti, Fabrizio Capuani, Pietro Cicuta, and Marco Gherardi. (2022). Remote teaching data-driven physical modeling through a COVID-19 open-ended data challenge. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ac79e1](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ac79e1)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Paired-sample t-tests of student grades across the course's data challenges (Table III), treating each student's performance in different challenges as paired samples of the same criteria. Significant DC1→DC3 gains are reported in “communication (COM), data visualization (VIZ) and technical (TECH) performance”, while the “scientiﬁc quality (SCI) category was not signiﬁcant”.

> "The signiﬁcant changes from DC1 to DC3 suggest that students improved their communication (COM), data visualization (VIZ) and technical (TECH) performance in the revision of the COVID-19 open-ended challenge. The increase in the scientiﬁc quality (SCI) category was not signiﬁcant."
