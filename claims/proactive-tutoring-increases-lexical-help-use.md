# Proactive tutoring increases learners' use of lexical help tools compared with reactive tutoring

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Learners used the lexical help significantly more often in the pro-active mode (1.35 and 1.09) than in the reactive mode (0.70 and 0.85). [→ Bruno De Lièvre 2006](#bruno-de-lievre-2006)

## Evidence

### Bruno De Lièvre 2006

Bruno De Lièvre, Christian Depover & Pierre Dillenbourg. (2006). The relationship between tutoring mode and learners' use of help tools in distance education. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-005-6076-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

ANOVA on lexical help activations across the four tutoring conditions of the 120-learner experiment. Means were 1.35 (pro-active CT) and 1.09 (pro-active HT) versus 0.70 and 0.85 under reactive tutoring; "a signiﬁcant eﬀect due to the pro-active attitude of the tutor" with no HT or interaction effect.

> "The analysis of variance (Table 9) conﬁrms that there is a signiﬁcant eﬀect due to the pro-active attitude of the tutor but no eﬀect due to the presence of a human tutor or to interaction."
