## Evidence

### Shen Jun 2025

Shen Jun, Zhang Shaolin, Sun Dongdong, Ge Ruixiang, Chen Sansong, Fang Jincheng, An Qian. (2025). Comparison of face-to-face teaching and online teaching in neurosurgery education for medical students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06817-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Clinical practice abilities test (total 100 points) conducted offline for both groups and evaluated using standardized patients. The face-to-face group significantly outscored the online group on the total score.

> "In the face-to-face group, the total scores for clinical practice abilities were 72.31 ± 6.72, which were significantly higher than the 67.60 ± 7.21 scores in the online group (P < 0.01)."

### Shen Jun 2025 (2)

Shen Jun, Zhang Shaolin, Sun Dongdong, Ge Ruixiang, Chen Sansong, Fang Jincheng, An Qian. (2025). Comparison of face-to-face teaching and online teaching in neurosurgery education for medical students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06817-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The four standardized-patient components (medical history inquiry, physical examination, diagnosis, treatment; 25 points each) each favored the face-to-face group, with the printed P-values above; no effect sizes were reported.

> "Each item score for clinical practice abilities in the face-to-face group was notably higher than in the online group, with corresponding P-values of 0.013, 0.005, 0.031, and 0.039, respectively."
