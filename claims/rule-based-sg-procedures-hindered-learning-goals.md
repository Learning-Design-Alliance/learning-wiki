# Participants in the rule-based simulation game perceived it as demanding, and learning and applying its procedures and rules hindered investment in their learning goals

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants experienced the rule-based serious game as demanding, with its procedures and rules often hindering effort investment in learning goals; this extraneous cognitive load triggered more first-order responses from both participants and facilitators. ### Marieke de Wijse-van Heeswijk 2025

Marieke de Wijse-van Heeswijk, Etienne A. J. A. Rouwette, Sander V. Meijerink. (2025). The learning effects of first, second, and third order interventions in a rule-based and open simulation game. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09715-w

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Qualitative finding from the two-case study comparing an extreme rule-based SG and a positive open SG. The article reports participants "perceived the rule-based serious game as demanding" because learning and applying procedures and rules hindered effort on learning goals.

> "Participants perceived the rule-based serious game as demanding, as learning and applying its procedures and rules often hindered them from investing their efforts in achieving their learning goals."
