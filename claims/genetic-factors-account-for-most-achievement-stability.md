## Evidence

### Rimfeld 2018

Rimfeld, K., Malanchini, M., Krapohl, E., Hannigan, L. J., Dale, P. S. & Plomin, R. (2018). The stability of educational achievement across school years is largely explained by genetic factors. npj Science of Learning 3:16. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0030-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Bivariate twin analyses of each pair of assessment ages in TEDS; the printed range of genetic contribution to phenotypic correlations is "63 to 79%" (Fig. 2a). These are proportions of covariance, not standardised effect sizes.

> "In bivariate twin analyses comparing each pair of ages, genetic factors accounted for a substantial proportion of the covariance between ages, explaining from 63 to 79% of the phenotypic correlations (Fig. 2a)."

### Rimfeld 2018 (2)

Rimfeld, K., Malanchini, M., Krapohl, E., Hannigan, L. J., Dale, P. S. & Plomin, R. (2018). The stability of educational achievement across school years is largely explained by genetic factors. npj Science of Learning 3:16. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0030-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Genetic common pathway model across all four ages of assessment; the article prints "Seventy percent" genetic and "24%" shared environmental contributions to the latent achievement factor's stability. These are variance components, not effect sizes.

> "Seventy percent of the overall stability of core educational achievement across compulsory education (he ritability of the latent factor) was explained by genetic factors; 24% of the stability of educational achievement was explained by shared environ- mental factors"

### Rimfeld 2018 (3)

Rimfeld, K., Malanchini, M., Krapohl, E., Hannigan, L. J., Dale, P. S. & Plomin, R. (2018). The stability of educational achievement across school years is largely explained by genetic factors. npj Science of Learning 3:16. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0030-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Longitudinal simplex model of overall achievement across the four ages; the printed genetic transmission paths are 0.86, 0.84, and 0.86, with C stable but smaller and E age-specific. Path estimates are not standardised effect sizes.

> "the stability of core academic achievement was largely explained by additive genetic (A) factors — the genetic paths from age to age are 0.86, 0.84, and 0.86"
