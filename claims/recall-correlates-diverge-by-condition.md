## Evidence

### Ju Ae Kim 2026

Ju Ae Kim, Jiwon Kim, Sung-il Kim, Mimi Bong. (2026). How mindful and mindless online searching affects curiosity and information recall. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10570-2

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Zero-order correlation analysis (Table 2) of the whole sample in the randomized experiment; performance estimation also correlated with recall (r = .26, p < .01), while study time estimation and cognitive self-esteem did not.

> "Search performance (r =.42, p <.001) and prior knowledge (r =.32, p <.001) were the two strongest correlates of participants’ recall performance. Curiosity and interest also correlated positively with recall performance (.21 < rs <.29, all ps <.05)."

### Ju Ae Kim 2026 (2)

Ju Ae Kim, Jiwon Kim, Sung-il Kim, Mimi Bong. (2026). How mindful and mindless online searching affects curiosity and information recall. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10570-2

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r3`

Group-split correlation analysis (Table 3): post-search, recall correlated with performance estimation (r = .39), cognitive self-esteem (r = .34), and curiosity and interest (rs = .34 and .49) only in the googling-only group, with all thinking-before-googling post-search correlations non-significant.

> "Before the search, scores on the recall test cova-ried significantly with prior knowledge (r =.50, p <.001) and search performance (r =.59, p <.001) only in the thinking-before-googling condition and not in the googling-only con-dition (rs =.23 and.24, both ps >.05)."
