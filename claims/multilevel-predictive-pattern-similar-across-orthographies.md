# Multilevel analyses across the full sample showed largely similar predictive patterns across orthographies, with RAN's contribution to reading accuracy significantly heterogeneous (p = .008) because RAN was more important in English than in the other languages

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · associational `r2`–`r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Multilevel models nesting children within language supported a largely similar predictive pattern across orthographies once the same literacy skills were compared. [→ Moll 2014](#moll-2014)
`q2 i?` For reading accuracy, RAN showed clear heterogeneity between languages (p = .008), with pair-wise comparisons showing the English difference significant for all four other languages. [→ Moll 2014 (2)](#moll-2014-2)
`q2 i?` For spelling, the tendency toward a higher English RAN contribution was not significant in the direct multilevel language comparison. [→ Moll 2014 (3)](#moll-2014-3)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the multilevel models for reading accuracy, RAN showed "clear evidence for heterogeneity between languages (p = .008)"; pair-wise comparisons attributed this to RAN being more important in English, with the difference "significant for all four languages".

> "For reading accuracy, RAN showed clear evidence for heterogeneity between languages (p = .008), reflecting that RAN is more important in English than in the other languages. Pair-wise comparisons revealed that this difference was significant for all four languages."
