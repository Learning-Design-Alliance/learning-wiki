# Instructional design and preferences interacted in predicting knowledge: DI-PS assignment and DI-PS preference were negatively associated with conceptual knowledge gain

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The ANCOVA on conceptual knowledge showed a significant combined effect, with a negative beta-coefficient of .19 for DI-PS assignment and -.21 for preferences towards DI-PS. [→ Sabrina Ludwig 2026](#sabrina-ludwig-2026)
`q3 i?` For procedural knowledge, the design-by-preference interaction was significant (p = .01), with a positive beta-coefficient of .03 suggesting procedural knowledge acquisition increases when receiving the preferred design. [→ Sabrina Ludwig 2026 (2)](#sabrina-ludwig-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Sabrina Ludwig 2026

Sabrina Ludwig, Andreas Rausch, Michelle Taub. (2026). Effects of instructional design, instructional preferences, and cognitive load on problem solving and knowledge acquisition in a computer-based office simulation. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102255

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA on conceptual knowledge acquisition with instructional preferences as covariate (Table 6; n = 77). The design effect showed "a negative relationship" (beta .19, p = .09), preferences for DI-PS beta -.21 (p = .05), and a significant interaction (p = .04, beta .09).

> "The beta-coefficient of .19 indicates a negative relationship, meaning that the DI-PS group may be associated with a decrease in conceptual knowledge compared to the PS-DI group."

### Sabrina Ludwig 2026 (2)

Sabrina Ludwig, Andreas Rausch, Michelle Taub. (2026). Effects of instructional design, instructional preferences, and cognitive load on problem solving and knowledge acquisition in a computer-based office simulation. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102255

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA on procedural knowledge acquisition with instructional preferences as covariate (Table 7; n = 59). The design-by-preference interaction was significant at p = .01; the design main effect (p = .12) and preference main effect (p = .43) were not significant.

> "The positive beta-coefficient of .03 implies that procedural knowledge acquisition increases when receiving the preferred instructional design."
