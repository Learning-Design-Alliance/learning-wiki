# Novice and expert information seekers pursue different information goals, producing different outcomes on the same task

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a longitudinal comparison of a novice and an expert information worker, the novice sought the right answer while the expert sought to add value to the client's knowledge, yielding very different outcomes within the same assignment. [→ Kuhlthau 2005](#kuhlthau-2005)

## Evidence

### Kuhlthau 2005

Kuhlthau, C. C. (2005). Towards collaboration between information seeking and information retrieval. Information Research, 10(2) paper 225. http://InformationR.net/ir/10-2/paper225.html

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Longitudinal case study of one information worker: "The novice was looking for the right answer. The expert was seeking to add value to the client's knowledge." The expert explained his attention had shifted from getting the story right to adding value.

> "In a longitudinal study of an information worker comparing novice and expert approaches to work tasks, I found that the expert had quite different goals in information seeking than the novice"
