# In reading, declines in accuracy over the PISA test are steepest among low internet users and smallest among heavy users

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Heavy internet users show a smaller decline in reading accuracy across item position than moderate users. [→ Borgonovi Francesca 2025](#borgonovi-francesca-2025)
`q2 i3` Low internet users show the steepest decline, with odds about 78% lower at the end than at the start of the test. [→ Borgonovi Francesca 2025](#borgonovi-francesca-2025)

## Evidence

### Borgonovi Francesca 2025

Borgonovi Francesca, Andrieu Elodie. (2025). The Relationship Between Internet Use, Achievement, and Persistence in Digital Tasks. Journal of Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1002/jad.12503

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Observational GLMM analysis with internet-use × item-position interactions (Model 2a) on PISA 2018 reading items among 128,433 students, excluding 25,170 mis-routed students and controlling for item difficulty. The article prints "(OR = 0.36; B = −1.17 + 0.15; p ≤ 0.01)" for high users and "(OR = 0.22; B = −1.17 − 0.33; p ≤ 0.01)" for low users versus medium users.

> "students with high levels of internet use have odds of giving a correct answer that are about 36% lower when a question is at the end of the test than when the same question is at the start (OR = 0.36; B = −1.17 + 0.15; p ≤ 0.01) whereas among students who have low levels of use, the odds are about 78% lower (OR = 0.22; B = −1.17 − 0.33; p ≤ 0.01)."
