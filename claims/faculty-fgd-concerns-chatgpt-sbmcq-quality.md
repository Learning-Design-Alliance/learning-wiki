## Evidence

### Chauhan 2025

Chauhan, A., Khaliq, F., & Nayak, K. R. (2025). Assessing Quality of Scenario-Based Multiple-Choice Questions in Physiology: Faculty-Generated vs. ChatGPT-Generated Questions among Phase I Medical Students. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education, 35, 2315–2344. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-025-00471-z

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Thematic analysis of an in-person focus group discussion with five physiology faculty (mean duration 37.15 min), yielding two themes and four subthemes on ChatGPT SBMCQ quality. The reliability-challenges subtheme is quoted; qualitative work, no effect size.

> "Participants highlighted concerns regarding variations in question difficulty and identified errors or inaccuracies in ChatGPT-generated content, which could impact the credibility of assessment results."

### Chauhan 2025 (2)

Chauhan, A., Khaliq, F., & Nayak, K. R. (2025). Assessing Quality of Scenario-Based Multiple-Choice Questions in Physiology: Faculty-Generated vs. ChatGPT-Generated Questions among Phase I Medical Students. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education, 35, 2315–2344. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-025-00471-z

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Cultural-bias subtheme from the same FGD thematic analysis: participants judged ChatGPT items as disconnected from local realities despite region-specific prompting, which the authors attribute to predominantly Western training data. Qualitative finding, no effect size.

> "the ChatGPT-generated questions often fell short of authentically capturing these elements. Despite attempts to include regional context in the prompt used, the questions produced by ChatGPT appeared disconnected from local realities."
