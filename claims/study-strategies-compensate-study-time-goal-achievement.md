## Evidence

### Theobald 2025

Theobald, M. (2025). Study longer or study effectively? Better study strategies can compensate for less study time and predict goal achievement and lower negative affect. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 95, 405–420. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12725

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical linear modelling of 30-day ambulatory assessment data from 231 university students (k = 3411 daily observations, person-mean centred predictors) tested the interaction. The article reports "Study strategies significantly moderated the relation between study time and goal achievement", with simple slopes for planning, monitoring, concentration and procrastination all significant.

> "Study strategies significantly moderated the relation between study time and goal achievement (see Table 2, Model 3 & Figure 2). For example, the relation between perceived study time and goal achievement was stronger when planning was low (1 SD below personal mean, β = .22, 95% CI [.14, .29], p = .001) compared to when planning was high (1 SD above the personal mean; β = .33, 95% CI [.24, .41], p = .001)."
