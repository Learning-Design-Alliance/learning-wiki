Means 2020¶

Means, B., and Neisler, J., with Langer Research Associates. (2020). Suddenly Online: A National Survey of Undergraduates During the COVID-19 Pandemic. San Mateo, CA: Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/32aee7c4-5363-4d50-bc9c-a338e7dfac7e

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Survey respondents reported which of the eight recommended practices their online course used; satisfaction was compared between students whose courses did and did not include each practice. Personal messages, real-world examples, and reflective assignments showed the largest differences.

"Sixty-eight percent of those who received messages of this type were satisfied with the course overall, compared to 47 percent of those who did not."

Means 2020 (2)¶

Means, B., and Neisler, J., with Langer Research Associates. (2020). Suddenly Online: A National Survey of Undergraduates During the COVID-19 Pandemic. San Mateo, CA: Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/32aee7c4-5363-4d50-bc9c-a338e7dfac7e

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Survey analysis compared the prevalence of motivation as a major problem across courses with and without the personal-message practice. The report states motivation "was mitigated significantly by the practice of sending students personalized messages."

"53 percent of students in courses that did not include personal messages found staying motivated to be a major problem, compared to only 33 percent of students in courses that used personal messages"
