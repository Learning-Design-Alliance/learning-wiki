Cross-sectional regression analyses for girls (n = 613) with random intercepts for school classes; CRF estimates p = 0.031 (end-of-year), 0.028 (language), 0.044 (mathematics), 0.012 (life skills); stunting p = 0.038, 0.032. The article reports "higher CRF was associated with higher grades" consistently across domains.

> "A different picture emerged for girls (Table 2). Consistently across all academic domains, higher CRF was associated with higher grades. Furthermore, stunted growth was linked to poorer grades in language and mathematics."
