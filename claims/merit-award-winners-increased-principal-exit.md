# Merit award winners were nearly three times as likely to leave their school the following year (16.9% vs 5.9%), mostly leaving the district

Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/training-retaining-effective-school-leaders

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Regression discontinuity-style comparison of schools just above versus just below the merit award threshold. Award winners left at "16.9%" versus "5.9%" for near-miss principals; footnote reports 14.1/16.9 = 83% of departures were district exits, suggesting winners leveraged the award's labor-market signal.

> "Principals who received the award were nearly three times as likely to leave their school the year after they won the award (16.9%), compared to principals with similar effectiveness who just missed the merit award cut-off (5.9%)."

### Signaling effects of merit awards: Evidence from school principals in Chicago 2023

Signaling effects of merit awards: Evidence from school principals in Chicago. (2023). Economics of Education Review. https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/economics-of-education-review

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Regression discontinuity analysis of Chicago principals' merit awards compares principals just above and just below the award threshold. The study finds winners are "over twice as likely to exit their school the year after winning" than those just below the threshold.
