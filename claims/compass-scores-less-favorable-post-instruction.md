## Evidence

### Marcos Daniel Caballero 2011

Marcos Daniel Caballero. (2011). Evaluating and Extending a Novel Course Reform of Introductory Mechanics. Georgia Institute of Technology. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1112.5593](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1112.5593)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre/post COMPASS survey of non-honors Georgia Tech mechanics students (Table 14), scores with 95% confidence intervals: "Favorable post-instruction scores decreased on most dimensions" while three dimensions stayed the same within error. Unfavorable scores increased on all dimensions except Avoiding Rote.

> "Overall COMPASS scores for non-honors mechanics students were less favorable. Favorable post-instruction scores decreased on most dimensions but remained the same within error on Perceived Ability, Expert Behaviors and Avoiding Rote."

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

> "Overall COMPASS scores for E&M students were less favorable."

### Marcos Daniel Caballero 2011 (3)

Marcos Daniel Caballero. (2011). Evaluating and Extending a Novel Course Reform of Introductory Mechanics. Georgia Institute of Technology. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1112.5593](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1112.5593)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pre/post COMPASS survey at North Carolina State University (Table 16) reports "Overall COMPASS scores for NCSU mechanics students were less favorable," but with a different dimensional pattern from Georgia Tech: several favorable and unfavorable scores were lower post-instruction, and all other dimensional scores remained the same within error.

> "Overall COMPASS scores for NCSU mechanics students were less favorable. Favorable post-instruction scores were lower for Avoiding Novice Behavior."
