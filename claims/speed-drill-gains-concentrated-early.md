# Speed-drill gains in reading speed were concentrated in the early part of the training period

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Cases of relative gain in speed decreased from the first test period to the third while cases of relative loss increased, because gains were made largely early in training. [→ Peters 1916](#peters-1916)

## Evidence

### Peters 1916

Peters, C. C. (1916). The Influence of Speed Drills Upon the Rate and the Effectiveness of Silent Reading. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0073150

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Case-count analysis of relative gain and loss across the three test intervals, tabulated in Table II of the article; the author reports the pattern of decreasing gains and attributes it to early-period gains, noting intermediate fluctuations have little significance.

> "It will be noticed that the number of cases of relative gain decreases from the first period to the third while the number of cases of relative loss increases. This is due to the fact that the gains were made largely in the early- part of the training period."
