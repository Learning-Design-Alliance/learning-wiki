# The threat-of-scream manipulation successfully induced sustained anxiety, with equivalent subjective and startle responses in adolescents and adults

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Eye-blink startle responses were greater during threat than safe conditions, and anxiety ratings were higher during threat (means 3.98 vs 1.97), with no group differences. [→ Patel Nilam 2016](#patel-nilam-2016)

## Evidence

### Patel Nilam 2016

Patel Nilam, Vytal Katherine, Pavletic Nevia, Stoodley Catherine, Pine Daniel S., Grillon Christian, Ernst Monique. (2016). Interaction of Threat and Verbal Working Memory in Adolescents. Psychophysiology. https://doi.org/10.1111/psyp.12582

`q3 · i3`

Manipulation check in the same experiment: subjective "how anxious" ratings showed a Condition main effect (F(1,47) = 68.48, p < .001, ηp2 = .59; threat mean 3.98 vs safe 1.97), and "eye-blink startle responses were greater during the threat than safe condition (F(1,48) = 59.17, p < .001, ηp2 = .55)". Ratings did not decrease across runs.

> "Second, eye-blink startle responses were greater during the threat than safe condition (F(1,48) = 59.17, p < .001, ηp2 = .55). These results confirmed that the loud scream successfully induced anxiety."
