## Evidence

### Schiekirka S 2015

Schiekirka S, Feufel MA, Herrmann-Lingen C, Raupach T. (2015). Evaluation in medical education: A topical review of target parameters, data collection tools and confounding factors. GMS German Medical Science. [doi:10.3205/000219](https://doi.org/10.3205/000219)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review citing a recent analysis of school-specific assessment in German medical faculties. The review reports reliability analyses are performed on "less than 40% of summative exams" and that these exams often do not meet current quality standards.

> "Measuring the reliability of examinations is a prerequisite for using exam results for evaluation purposes. At German medical schools, however, these analyses are performed on less than 40% of summative exams [24]."

### Schiekirka S 2015 (2)

Schiekirka S, Feufel MA, Herrmann-Lingen C, Raupach T. (2015). Evaluation in medical education: A topical review of target parameters, data collection tools and confounding factors. GMS German Medical Science. [doi:10.3205/000219](https://doi.org/10.3205/000219)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Narrative review discussing outcome operationalisation. The review reports MC-based state examinations and Progress Tests "mainly assess factual knowledge" and are susceptible to memorisation and deliberate practice of MC-questions.

> "However, multiple-choice (MC) questions (such as those used in state examinations) mainly assess factual knowledge. By memorising the correct answer [21] or by deliberate practice of MC-questions [22], students may improve their exam results regardless of their actual knowledge."
