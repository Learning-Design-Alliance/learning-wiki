## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2014

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2014). Student Difficulties with the Dirac Delta Function. [doi:10.1119/perc.2014.pr.064](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.064)

`q3 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Exam coding of element E1: "27%, N=15 of 56" made significant mathematical errors executing the integral, with "73%, N=11 of 15" of those errors equating the delta function's integral with that of its argument. The same difficulty appeared implicitly in 32% (27 of 84) of CUE responses.

> "Roughly a quarter of the students (27%, N=15 of 56) made signiﬁcant math-ematical errors related to the delta function while execut-ing this integral (element E1). The most common error (73%, N=11 of 15) amounted to a variation of equating the integral of the delta function with the integral of its vector argument."

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2014 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2014). Student Difficulties with the Dirac Delta Function. [doi:10.1119/perc.2014.pr.064](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.064)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Second interview set (N=6) with context-free integrations: "Three of six interviewees also evaluated the r integral in part d) as if the delta function was not there", despite correct execution of parts a)-c), indicating limited transfer to more complex integrals.

> "Three of six interviewees also evaluated the r integral in part d) as if the delta function was not there (i.e., ∫δ (r −r′)r2dr = 13r′3), despite correctly executing parts a)-c). Their verbal explanations indicated that the issue was the delta function rather than the spherical integrals."
