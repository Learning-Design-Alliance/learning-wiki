# Evidence for a full-day kindergarten attendance advantage is small and tentative (d = 0.10 unadjusted)

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` FDK showed a small positive unadjusted association with student attendance (d = 0.10), but the single adjusted comparison was nonsignificant. [→ Harris Cooper 2010](#harris-cooper-2010)

## Evidence

### Harris Cooper 2010

Harris Cooper, Ashley Batts Allen, Erika A. Patall, and Amy L. Dent. (2010). Effects of Full-Day Kindergarten on Academic Achievement and Social Development. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654309359185

`q3 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r1`

Meta-analysis of eight reports (12 effect sizes, nine samples, 1980–2007, sample sizes 18–13,742), all unpublished. The one adjusted comparison revealed a "nonsignificant positive effect of FDK (d = 0.09, 95% CI = –0.32/0.50)".

> "Using fixed-effect assumptions, the weighted mean d-index for unadjusted effect sizes was d = 0.10 (95% CI = 0.08/0.13). Using a random-effects model, the weighted average d-index was also 0.10 (95% CI = 0.06/0.15)."
