# Chatbot response patterns on MCQ assessments show statistically significantly higher person-fit statistics than human respondents across instruments, chatbots, and PFS measures

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On two assessment instruments and three chatbots, all four PFS measures (G, G*, U3, ZU3) were statistically significantly higher for chatbot responses than for human responses, with the highest p-value below 0.00001. [→ Alona Strugatski and Giora Alexandron 2025](#alona-strugatski-and-giora-alexandron-2025)

## Evidence

### Alona Strugatski and Giora Alexandron 2025

Alona Strugatski and Giora Alexandron. (2025). Applying Item Response Theory to Distinguish Between Human and Generative AI Responses to Multiple-Choice Assessments. LAK'25 pre-print. https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02713

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Wilcoxon rank-sum comparisons of four PFS measures on 2x3 datasets combining human responses (931 chemistry; over 4,800 psychometric examinees) with 5% chatbot responses. The article reports the "highest p-value being less than 0.00001" across all 2x3x4 combinations; no effect size is printed.

> "Notably, the tests show that the PFS of the chatbots were statistically significantly higher than those of the human respondents, with the highest p-value being less than 0.00001."
