# Anxiety scores fell significantly from pre-test to post-test after high-fidelity simulation in medical students in both of two consecutive sessions, in a single-group pre-post study

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Pre-post paired comparisons showed anxiety total scores fell from M = 61.41 (SD = 12.01) to M = 45.41 (SD = 12.12) after the first simulation and from M = 56.86 (SD = 11.36) to M = 43.14 (SD = 12.04) after the second, with paired t-tests significant at p < 0.001 in both sessions. [→ Yu Ji Hye 2021](#yu-ji-hye-2021)

## Evidence

### Yu Ji Hye 2021

Yu Ji Hye, Chang Hye Jin, Kim Soon Sun, Park Ji Eun, Chung Wou Young, Lee Su Kyung, Kim Miran, Lee Jang Hoon, Jung Yun Jung. (2021). Effects of high-fidelity simulation education on medical students' anxiety and confidence. PLoS ONE 16(5): e0251078. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0251078

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Retrospective pre-post survey analysis of 37 fifth-year medical students who each completed two high-fidelity SimMan simulation sessions; anxiety was measured with a 5-item STAI short form before and after each session, and paired t-tests were significant (first session t = 5.39, p < 0.001; second t = 5.0, p < 0.001). The article reports "anxiety decreased after the simulation" with the printed means and SDs.

> "In both the first and second simulation, anxiety decreased after the simulation (M = 45.41, SD = 12.12; M = 43.14, SD = 12.04) compared to before (M = 61.41, SD = 12.01; M = 56.86, SD = 11.36)."
