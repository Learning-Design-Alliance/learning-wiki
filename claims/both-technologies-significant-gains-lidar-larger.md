## Evidence

### O'Brien 2023

O'Brien, D. J., Vieyra, R. E., Vieyra Cortés, C., Johnson-Glenberg, M. C., & Megowan-Romanowicz, C. (2023). Evaluating Learning of Motion Graphs with a LiDAR-Based Smartphone Application. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1119/perc.2023.pr.megowan-romanowicz](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2023.pr.megowan-romanowicz)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within the eight-section comparison, both technologies produced significant pre-to-post gains on the 15-point TUG-K/Diagnoser assessment: the LiDAR Motion group "increased by a statistically significant 1.32 points, t(56) = 4.12, p < 0.001" and the ultrasonic group by 0.53 points.

> "By post-test, the LiDAR Motion group increased by a statistically significant 1.32 points, t(56) = 4.12, p < 0.001, and the ultrasonic group improved by a statistically significant 0.53 points, t(48) = 2.65, p < 0.05."

### O'Brien 2023 (2)

O'Brien, D. J., Vieyra, R. E., Vieyra Cortés, C., Johnson-Glenberg, M. C., & Megowan-Romanowicz, C. (2023). Evaluating Learning of Motion Graphs with a LiDAR-Based Smartphone Application. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1119/perc.2023.pr.megowan-romanowicz](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2023.pr.megowan-romanowicz)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre-test equivalence check before the laboratory intervention: "The two groups (LiDAR and ultrasonic) were matched at pretest, t(104) = 1.33, p = 0.19," supporting the between-technology gain comparison.
