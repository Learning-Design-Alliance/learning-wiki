# Astro 101 students showed high normalized learning gains (<g> = 0.71) on interferometry after lecture, Think-Pair-Share, and the Lecture-Tutorial in both semesters

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · design `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2020 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Both semesters achieved a normalized learning gain of <g> = 0.71 (c = 0.64 fall 2016; c = 0.69 spring 2017) on the ten-item interferometry test. [→ Colin S. Wallace 2020 (2)](#colin-s-wallace-2020-2)
`q2 i?` Post-instruction score distributions showed substantially larger mean, median, and mode than pre-instruction distributions in both semesters. [→ Colin S. Wallace 2020 (3)](#colin-s-wallace-2020-3)
`q2 i?` The gain analysis used 121 matched pre/post responses from fall 2016 and 145 from spring 2017. [→ Colin S. Wallace 2020](#colin-s-wallace-2020)

## Evidence

### Colin S. Wallace 2020

Colin S. Wallace, Chase Hatcher, Timothy G. Chambers, Seth D. Hornstein, Julia Kamenetzky, and Edward E. Prather. (2020). A New Lecture-Tutorial for Teaching Interferometry to Astro 101 Students. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2001.10527](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2001.10527)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Test administration and data cleaning for the fall 2016 and spring 2017 Astro 101 assessments at UNC Chapel Hill; the article reports, 'This left us with a data set of 121 matched pre- and post-test responses for fall 2016 and 145 for the spring 2017.'

> "This left us with a data set of 121 matched pre- and post-test responses for fall 2016 and 145 for the spring 2017."

### Colin S. Wallace 2020 (2)

Colin S. Wallace, Chase Hatcher, Timothy G. Chambers, Seth D. Hornstein, Julia Kamenetzky, and Edward E. Prather. (2020). A New Lecture-Tutorial for Teaching Interferometry to Astro 101 Students. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2001.10527](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2001.10527)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre/post analysis of matched scores using 'Hake's normalized learning gain <g>' and normalized change, administered after a lecture, Think-Pair-Share questions, and the Lecture-Tutorial. 'For the fall 2016, <g> = 0.71 and c = 0.64. For the spring 2017, <g> = 0.71 and c = 0.69.'

> "For the fall 2016, <g> = 0.71 and c = 0.64. For the spring 2017, <g> = 0.71 and c = 0.69."

### Colin S. Wallace 2020 (3)

Colin S. Wallace, Chase Hatcher, Timothy G. Chambers, Seth D. Hornstein, Julia Kamenetzky, and Edward E. Prather. (2020). A New Lecture-Tutorial for Teaching Interferometry to Astro 101 Students. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2001.10527](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2001.10527)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Score-distribution comparison of pre- and post-instruction totals plotted in Figures 6 and 7 for both semesters; the post-instruction mean, median, and mode are 'all substantially larger than they are for the pre-instruction distribution, regardless of semester.'

> "The mean, median, and mode of the post-instruction distribution are all substantially larger than they are for the pre-instruction distribution, regardless of semester."
