# Executive function at 48 months moderates the preschool-to-kindergarten development of math ability on the ECLS-K Math battery but not the Applied Problems subtest

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r3` · `q3` · `i0` negligible

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` Executive function at 48 months significantly moderated the association between preschool and kindergarten math on the ECLS-K Math assessment (β = −0.14, p = .019), but not on Applied Problems. [→ Blair 2016 (2)](#blair-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Blair 2016

Blair, Clancy; McKinnon, Rachel D.; the Family Life Project Investigators. (2016). Moderating effects of executive functions and the teacher–child relationship on the development of mathematics ability in kindergarten. Learning and Instruction, 41, 85–93. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2015.10.001

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r3`

Multilevel regression (Model 2, Table 4) in the Family Life Project sample of 1005 children tested the Preschool Math × Executive Function interaction predicting kindergarten math. The printed interaction was β = −0.14, p = .019 for ECLS-K Math and non-significant for Applied Problems (β = 0.00).

> "Executive function scores at 48 months significantly moderated the association between preschool math ability and kindergarten math ability as assessed by the ECLS-K Math assessment, β = −0.14, b = −0.11, SE = 0.05, p = .019, but not the Applied Problems test."

### Blair 2016 (2)

Blair, Clancy; McKinnon, Rachel D.; the Family Life Project Investigators. (2016). Moderating effects of executive functions and the teacher–child relationship on the development of mathematics ability in kindergarten. Learning and Instruction, 41, 85–93. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2015.10.001

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r3`

The same multilevel model showed no EF × preschool math interaction for the Applied Problems subtest, so the moderation held for one measure only; the article attributes this to the ECLS-K battery's greater sensitivity to variation among young children.

> "Executive function scores at 48 months significantly moderated the association between preschool math ability and kindergarten math ability as assessed by the ECLS-K Math assessment, β = −0.14, b = −0.11, SE = 0.05, p = .019, but not the Applied Problems test."
