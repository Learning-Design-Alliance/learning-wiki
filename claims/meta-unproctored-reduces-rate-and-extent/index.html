Evidence¶

Li Zhao 2024¶

Li Zhao, Junjie Peng, Shiqi Ke, Kang Lee. (2024). Effectiveness of Unproctored vs. Teacher-Proctored Exams in Reducing Students’ Cheating: A Double-Blind Randomized Controlled Field Experimental Study. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09965-z

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Hierarchical meta-regression combining all four studies' cheating rate data, entering gender, course type, exam type, and reminders stepwise. The exam type step was significant (Q(df = 1) = 15.24, p < 0.001) with "b = –0.11"; gender and course type steps were not significant.

"The exam type significantly affected cheating rate, b = –0.11, SE = 0.03, 95% CI = –0.17 to –0.05, p < 0.001, BF10 = 8.96. It accounted for 59.62% of the variance."

Li Zhao 2024 (2)¶

Li Zhao, Junjie Peng, Shiqi Ke, Kang Lee. (2024). Effectiveness of Unproctored vs. Teacher-Proctored Exams in Reducing Students’ Cheating: A Double-Blind Randomized Controlled Field Experimental Study. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09965-z

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Meta-regression on cheating extent among cheaters across the four studies. The exam type step was significant (Q(df = 1) = 11.61, p = 0.001); the article attributes the significant effect, absent in Studies 1, 2, and 4 separately, to the combined data having greater power.

"The exam type significantly affected cheating extent, b = –1.19, SE = 0.36, 95% CI = –1.89 to –0.49, p = 0.001, BF10 = 6.97, explaining 58.61% of the variance."

Li Zhao 2024 (3)¶

Li Zhao, Junjie Peng, Shiqi Ke, Kang Lee. (2024). Effectiveness of Unproctored vs. Teacher-Proctored Exams in Reducing Students’ Cheating: A Double-Blind Randomized Controlled Field Experimental Study. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09965-z

q3 · i? · causal · r3

In the meta-regressions, no interactions were significant for cheating rate or extent, leading the authors to conclude the unproctored exam advantage holds regardless of student gender, course type, and reminder presence.

"However, no interactions were significant ( ps > 0.05), thus rejecting Hypotheses 4a and 5a, which predicted that academic integrity reminders would have differential effects on cheating rate based on exam format."

Related Claims¶

  • Unproctored exams produce significantly lower cheating rates than teacher-proctored exams in actual course exams without integrity reminders — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Study 4 replicated lower cheating rates in unproctored exams with reminders present, though with anecdotal Bayes evidence — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • In Study 3, among students who cheated, cheating extent was lower in unproctored exams, with a gender by exam type interaction driven by male students — reports the opposite
  • With academic integrity reminders present, the exam-format effect on cheating rate disappears (Study 3 null) — reports the opposite
