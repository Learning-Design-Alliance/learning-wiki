# Instruction with the Comprehensive Charts significantly improved EFL freshmen's tense-form recognition in the non-written context

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Experimental-group students taught with the Comprehensive Charts scored higher on a 12-item tense recognition test than control students, and the instruction-by-performance-level association was statistically significant. [→ Tsai 2017](#tsai-2017)

## Evidence

### Tsai 2017

Tsai, Min-hsiu. (2017). Reusing Comprehensive Charts of Tense Forms to Teach EFL Students in a University of Science and Technology. World Journal of Education, 7(3). https://doi.org/10.5430/wje.v7n3p115

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of 120 chart-instructed and 108 control freshmen at a Taiwanese national university. The experimental group mean was 7.5500 (62.92%) versus 6.3148 (52.62%) for controls; the Pearson Chi-Square was 10.408 (p = .015). The article reports "did significantly exist among groups" for recognition performance.

> "Table 10 shows the Pearson Chi-Square, two-sided asymmetrical significance , reaches the significant level (p ＜.05). That is, the instruction difference, teaching with or without the Comprehensive Charts (CC), did significantly exist among groups in relation to the participants' performance levels of tense form recognition."
