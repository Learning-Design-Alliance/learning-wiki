# Diagrams improve posttest performance on posterior probability problems for students who answered the pretest correctly, whether or not the diagram text is redundant

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large
> **Currency** · newest study 2013 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` For participants who answered the pretest correctly, diagrams produced higher posttest scores than text-only, with both diagram conditions benefiting (d = 1.01 and d = 1.26) and no difference between them. [→ Clinton 2013](#clinton-2013)

## Evidence

### Clinton 2013

Clinton, V., Alibali, M. W., & Nathan, M. J. (2013). The effects of diagrams and questioning-while-reading on learning from a statistics lesson. In Martinez, M. & Castro Superfine, A. (Eds.), Proceedings of the 35th annual meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education. Chicago, IL: University of Illinois at Chicago. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED584483

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA on posttest scores in the randomized experiment, pretest-correct subgroup (N = 47 per the Results section). Both diagram conditions beat text-only; the article reports "There was no difference between the two diagram conditions" and no effect of questioning.

> "For participants who answered the pretest correctly, diagrams had a positive effect on posttest scores F(2, 45) = 8.26, p = .001.  There was a benefit of diagrams both without and with redundant text compared to the text-only condition, t(28) = 3.04, p = .01,Cohen’s d = 1.01; t(28) = 3.98, p = .001, Cohen’s d = 1.26, respectively."
