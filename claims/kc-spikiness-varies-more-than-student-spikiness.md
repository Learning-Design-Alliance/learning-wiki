## Evidence

### Baker 2011

Baker, Ryan S.J.d., Goldstein, Adam B., Heffernan, Neil T. (2011). Detecting Learning Moment-by-Moment. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. In Press. https://doi.org/10.3233/JAI-2011-015

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive analysis of KC-level spikiness in Cognitive Tutor log data, restricted to KCs practiced at least 6 times and the first 20 opportunities; the reported range is {1.12, 113.52} with M=8.55, SD=14.62.

> "Within our data from the Cognitive Tutor, spikiness values range for KCs between {1.12, 113.52}, M=8.55, SD=14.62."

### Baker 2011 (2)

Baker, Ryan S.J.d., Goldstein, Adam B., Heffernan, Neil T. (2011). Detecting Learning Moment-by-Moment. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. In Press. https://doi.org/10.3233/JAI-2011-015

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Interpretive analysis of the maximum-spikiness KC 'ENTER-CORRECT-ANSWER-INTO-BOX', attempted 1488 times with 99.997% correct, showing its high maximum spikiness was due to rare outlier slips rather than genuine eureka learning.

> "Hence, it appears that some of the high spikiness seen in Cognitive Tutors comes from very easy KCs."
