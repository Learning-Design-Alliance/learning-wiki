## Evidence

### Carbonaro 2008

Carbonaro, M., Szafron, D., Cutumisu, M., & Schaeffer, J. (2008). Computer-game Construction: A Gender-neutral Attractor to Computing Science. Informing Science? No—venue not printed.

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

A two-sample t-test on rubric scores by gender found "There is a significant difference (p = 0.015)", with females (M = 15.92) scoring higher than males (M = 15.12).

> "The mean scores for interactive games for the males and females were 15.12 (SD = 1.12) and 15.92 (SD = 1.13) respectively. Given that the games produced by the females had a higher mean score, we performed a two-sample t-test to determine if the female scores were significantly higher than the male scores at a 95% confidence level. There is a significant difference (p = 0.015)."
