## Evidence

### Liu Zi-Jia 2017

Liu Zi-Jia, Yi Jie, Chen Wei-Yun, Zhang Xiu-Hua, Huang Yu-Guang, Martin Samantha. (2017). Comparison of learning performance of 2 intubating laryngeal mask airways in novice: A randomized crossover manikin study. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000006905

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subjective ease of insertion rated by participants on a 5-point numerical scale in the same crossover manikin study. Participants "reported i-gel was significantly easier to manipulate than Aura-i" at both initial (P = .001) and second evaluations (P < .001).

> "Comparing the 2 LMAs, the participants reported i-gel was significantly easier to manipulate than Aura-i for both initial (P = .001) and second evaluations (P < .001) (Table 3, Fig. 4)."

### Liu Zi-Jia 2017 (2)

Liu Zi-Jia, Yi Jie, Chen Wei-Yun, Zhang Xiu-Hua, Huang Yu-Guang, Martin Samantha. (2017). Comparison of learning performance of 2 intubating laryngeal mask airways in novice: A randomized crossover manikin study. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000006905

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-device change in subjective ease between the initial evaluation and the 90-day reassessment without retraining in the same study. Participants "reported that it was harder to insert the LMAs at reassessment" for both devices, consistent with the prolonged time to ventilation.

> "Regarding ease of insertion, participants reported that it was harder to insert the LMAs at reassessment (i-gel: P = .025; Aura-i: P < .001)."
