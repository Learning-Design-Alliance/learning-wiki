# Teacher-by-method interaction was significant on the standardized MPE posttest, with control-group performance dependent on the teacher

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The interaction between teacher and instructional method was significant on the MPE posttest, even when controlling for prior math ability, while control-group means varied widely across teachers. [→ Johnson 1992](#johnson-1992)

## Evidence

### Johnson 1992

Johnson, Scott D.; Fischbach, Rita McDonough. (1992). Teaching Problem Solving and Technical Mathematics Through Cognitive Apprenticeship at the Community College Level. National Center for Research in Vocational Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED352455

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Results-section analysis of the standardized MPE posttest in the two-teacher factorial quasi-experiment. The interaction was significant (p=.019; p=.047 with covariate); control means were teacher 1=11.29 and teacher 2=19.13 versus apprenticeship means of 16.44 and 16.73, indicating "the performance of students on the standardized test appeared to be dependent upon the teacher" under traditional instruction.

> "The interaction effect between teacher and the typ^ of instructional method was significant on the MPE posttest (p=.019), even when controlling for prior student math ability (p=.047). The cognitive apprenticeship group means on the MPE posttest differed little (teacher 1=16.44, teacher 2=16.73), unlike the control group means (teacher 1=11.29, teacher 2=19.13)."
