Evidence¶

Podolsky 2016¶

Podolsky, A., Kini, T., Bishop, J., & Darling-Hammond, L. (2016). Solving the Teacher Shortage: How to Attract and Retain Excellent Educators. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/solving-teacher-shortage

q2 · i? · review · r2

The report's induction section reviews research on induction program components (mentoring, coaching, feedback from same-subject mentors, observation, seminars, reduced loads). It reports retention "at rates more than twice those of teachers who lack these supports", while noting few teachers receive them. No effect size is printed.

"Teachers who receive this set of supports have been found to stay in teaching at rates more than twice those of teachers who lack these supports. However, only a small minority of teachers receive these supports."

  • New teachers who receive little mentoring are twice as likely to leave the classroom as well-mentored beginners — related
  • Strong mentoring is linked to novice elementary teachers being 25 percentage points more likely to plan to remain in the same school, with a similar but non-significant pattern for high school teachers — related
