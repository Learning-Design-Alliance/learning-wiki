# School growth status is changeable across Grades 1 and 2, and sustaining overperforming status is rare

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among schools observed in both grades (N = 6,274), 60% kept the same growth status, and only 22% of Grade 1 overperformers maintained that status in Grade 2, corresponding to 3% of all schools. [→ Yamada 2024](#yamada-2024)

## Evidence

### Yamada 2024

Yamada, H. (2024). New Normal in Early Elementary Mathematics Learning: Part II - School Level Variation in Growth in Grades 1 and 2. E4 Center, Northwestern University. https://e4.northwestern.edu/2024/06/25/new-normal-in-early-elementary-mathematics-learning-part-ii-school-level-variation-in-growth-in-grades-1-and-2/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal transition analysis of 6,274 schools present in both grade levels (Figure 1). Conditional maintenance rates were 22% for overperformers versus 12% (average) and 9% (underperformer); overperformer maintainers were "only 3% of the total number of schools".

> "Although schools classified as overperformers in Grade 1 were more likely to maintain the outperforming status (22%) than others (12% for the average and 9% for the underperformer), they corresponded to only 3% of the total number of schools in this sample."
