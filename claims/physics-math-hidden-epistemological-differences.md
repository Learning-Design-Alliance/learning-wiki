# Physics and mathematics communities hold different hidden epistemological assumptions about equations that can cause students difficulties

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Uncommunicated differences in how physics and math cultures use equations — units, parameters, and the derivative as a physical approximation — can cause students considerable difficulties. [→ Edward F. Redish 2010](#edward-f-redish-2010)

## Evidence

### Edward F. Redish 2010

Edward F. Redish. (2010). Introducing students to the culture of physics: Explicating elements of the hidden curriculum. PERC 2010. [doi:10.1063/1.3515245](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3515245)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Illustrative examples from a calculus final exam and standard texts: a trout-density expression mixing dimensioned objects, exams avoiding parameters entirely, and the physicist's treatment of dx/dt as a ratio of small physical changes rather than a limiting process. Argument by example from the author's own institution; no student data reported.

> "If we fail to be aware of these differences – and to communicate them clearly to our mathematical colleagues, they can cause our students considerable difficulties."
