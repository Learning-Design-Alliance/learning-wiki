## Evidence

### Stefan Küchemann 2023

Stefan Küchemann, Malte Ubben, David Dzsotjan, Sergey Mukhametov, Carrie A. Weidner, Carrie A. Weidner, Linda Qerimi, Jochen Kuhn, Stefan Heusler, and Jacob F. Sherson. (2023). The impact of an interactive visualization and simulation tool on learning quantum physics: Results of an eye-tracking study. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2302.06286](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2302.06286)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Relative dwell-time analysis in the eye-tracking study showed "the graph in both tasks received the most attention by novices (Task 1: 70.1%, Task 2: 74.4%) and by experts (Task 1: 66.3%, Task 2: 76.1%)", with a non-significant group difference; the discussion states nearly 80% of attention was on the graph.

> "It is noticeable that the graph in both tasks received the most attention by novices (Task 1: 70.1%, Task 2: 74.4%) and by experts (Task 1: 66.3%, Task 2: 76.1%) with a non-signiﬁcant diﬀerence between the two groups."

### Stefan Küchemann 2023 (2)

Stefan Küchemann, Malte Ubben, David Dzsotjan, Sergey Mukhametov, Carrie A. Weidner, Carrie A. Weidner, Linda Qerimi, Jochen Kuhn, Stefan Heusler, and Jacob F. Sherson. (2023). The impact of an interactive visualization and simulation tool on learning quantum physics: Results of an eye-tracking study. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2302.06286](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2302.06286)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The article reports that the numerical energy-difference value was redundant to the graph information, yet "among the 36 participants, no one used this number to solve Task 1 or Task 2"; the numerical values received so little attention that statistical comparison was not possible.

> "However, among the 36 participants, no one used this number to solve Task 1 or Task 2 even though it might be easier to extract because it is only a single number in comparison to the two visual energy levels in the graph that need to be compared for the solution."
