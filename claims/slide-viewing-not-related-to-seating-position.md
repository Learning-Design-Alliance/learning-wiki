## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Slide viewing does not correlate with front-to-back seating position (r(90) = -0.14, p = 0.17), so back-row students did not view slides more. [→ Day Alison J. 2021](#day-alison-j-2021)

## Evidence

### Day Alison J. 2021

Day Alison J., Fenn Kimberly M., Ravizza Susan M. (2021). Is it worth it? The costs and benefits of bringing a laptop to a university class. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0251792

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Secondary analysis using survey-reported seating position (map-based y-axis) against tracked slide-viewing duration; the article reports no correlation: "r(90) = -0.14, p = 0.17". Most participants (87.9%) reported sitting in their spot 90% or more of the time.

> "Slide viewing did not correlate with participants’ front-to-back position in the classroom, r(90) = -0.14, p = 0.17, suggesting that students who sat in the back of the lecture hall did not spend more time viewing their slides during lecture."
