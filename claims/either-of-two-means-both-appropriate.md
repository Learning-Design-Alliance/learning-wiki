## Evidence

### Micol Alemani 2026

Micol Alemani, Karel Kok, Eva Philippaki. (2026). Students’ reasoning in choosing measurement instruments in an introductory physics laboratory course. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2603.16638](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2603.16638)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Analysis of the multiple-choice decisions (Fig. 1) found this pronounced preference for committing to a single instrument; the article reports the underlying reason remains uncertain and calls for additional research.

> "in both the pre- and post-questionnaires, more than 90 % of participants selected one of the two available instruments, whereas fewer than 10 % opted for the alternative “Either of the two”"

### Micol Alemani 2026 (2)

Micol Alemani, Karel Kok, Eva Philippaki. (2026). Students’ reasoning in choosing measurement instruments in an introductory physics laboratory course. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2603.16638](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2603.16638)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Detailed analysis of “Either of the two” justifications (Table III) categorized them as both-appropriate, don’t-know, no justification, or uncodable; most cases were both-appropriate. No effect size is printed for this finding.

> "shows that in the majority of cases, students chose the answer ”Either of the two” when they think both instruments can be used (either because they are equivalent or because they have pro and contra) and not because they do not know what to use."
