## Subclaims
`q2 i1` In algebra-based mechanics, female students rated valuations of their contributions about a fourth of a point higher on average, with a small effect size (d = −0.271); no significant differences appeared in the other courses. [→ Matthew Dew 2021](#matthew-dew-2021)

## Evidence

Gendered Performance Diﬀerences in Introductory Physics: A Study from a Large Land-Grant University. arXiv:2009.09536. Originally published 2020. Fall 2019 questionnaire item on contribution valuation analyzed by t-test; the article reports a significant difference only in algebra-based mechanics with "a small eﬀect size ( d =−0.271)", corresponding to the only course where female students historically performed better on final letter grades.

> "When analyzing whether students felt that their contributions were valued, we found a statistically signiﬁcant diﬀerence in algebra-based mechanics (See Table XI). This had a small eﬀect size ( d =−0.271)."
