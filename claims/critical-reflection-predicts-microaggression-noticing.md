## Evidence

### Civitillo 2025

Civitillo, S., S. Glock, L. A. Naudszus, L. Venhoff, D. Lieck, and P. Jugert. (2025). Investigating Preservice Teachers' Competence to Notice Ethnic Microaggressions in the Classroom. Journal of Community Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1002/jcop.70056

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Study 2 is a cross-sectional association: preservice teachers rated how much microaggression they perceived in classroom vignettes, and their self-reported critical reflection predicted those ratings. No performance test of noticing ability was given. Multiple regression controlling for empathy, subtle prejudice, implicit prejudice, migration background, teaching experience, and gender. Critical reflection also positively predicted projected negative student sense of school belonging (β = 0.32, p < 0.001); that model accounted for 16% of variance.

> "Preservice teachers' degree of critical reflection positively predicted perceived microaggressions (β = 0.30, p < 0.001), indicating that those with higher levels of critical consciousness reported higher levels of awareness to microaggressions in the vignettes."
