Testimony of Phyllis Rosser at the 1987 hearing reporting 1985 SAT score averages by race and sex. The quote names the two-step comparison: minority women score "lower than the males in their ethnic group who, in turn, score lower than white males."

"Minority females are doubly penalized by the test. They all score lower than the males in their ethnic group who, in turn, score lower than white males. In 1985, black women scored 43 points lower than black men, and 264 points lower than white men."

Discussion¶

The entry reports one year of score averages for one group (Black test-takers) from a witness's testimony; the general statement about all minority females is the witness's, and no figures for other groups are recorded here. Score gaps alone do not show that the test, rather than unequal preparation or schooling, produces them.

Learner Variables¶

  • Gender — predictor: outcomes differ by group: a gap reflecting circumstances and treatment, not a cause
