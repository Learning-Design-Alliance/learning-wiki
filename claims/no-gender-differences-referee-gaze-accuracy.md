## Evidence

### Świdwa 2025

Świdwa, Jacek; Klatt, Stefanie; Kantanista, Adam. (2025). Gaze behavior and decision-making among handball referees: exploring gender and expertise differences. PeerJ. https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.19401

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Two-way ANOVAs (gender × game situation) on eye-tracking data from 11 female and 40 male referees viewing 31 match clips; all four gaze measures were non-significant, e.g. fixation counts "F(4,1561) = 0.591, p = 0.667". No effect sizes were printed.

> "No significant differences were found between male and female referees in fixation counts, F(4,1561) = 0.591, p = 0.667, average fixation duration, F(4,1561) = 0.473, p = 0.775, saccade count, F(4,1561) = 0.380, p = 0.823, or average saccade duration, F(4,1561) = 0.413, p = 0.799."

### Świdwa 2025 (2)

Świdwa, Jacek; Klatt, Stefanie; Kantanista, Adam. (2025). Gaze behavior and decision-making among handball referees: exploring gender and expertise differences. PeerJ. https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.19401

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Conclusions summarizing the overall gender contrast in decision accuracy (Table 4 reports p = 0.598 for the average/total comparison); no effect size was printed for this null finding.

> "The results indicated that higher-level referees exhibited greater decision-making accuracy compared to their lower-level counterparts, while no significant differences in gaze behavior or decision-making accuracy were found between male and female referees."
