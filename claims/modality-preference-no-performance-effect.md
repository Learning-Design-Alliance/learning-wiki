# Students strongly preferred physical specimens over VR and 2D, but modality preference did not affect exam performance

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` 62% of students preferred physical specimens and 54% least preferred VR, yet the preference–performance relationship was weak (p = 0.045, R² = 0.0449) with no significant Tukey differences, and two-way ANOVA with SSQ strata showed no relationship (p = 0.682). [→ Zhang Y 2026](#zhang-y-2026)

## Evidence

### Zhang Y 2026

Zhang Y, Sun J, Marshall K, Mitchell JP, Laprade J, Nederveen JP, Helli PB, Rebalka IA. (2026). A preliminary comparison of bellringer performance across three visual modalities for the assessment of anatomy knowledge. Anatomical Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.70145

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANOVA relating modality preference to bellringer test success, plus a two-way ANOVA stratifying by preference and SSQ score range (p = 0.682). The article reports that despite a significant p-value, "the effect size was weak (R 2 = 0.0449)" with no significant post-hoc differences.

> "Despite a significant p‐value (p = 0.045), the effect size was weak (R 2 = 0.0449). To further explore this phenomenon, no significant differences were observed in Tukey's post‐hoc analysis"
