## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Participants in the control, verbal scaffolding, and verbal+visual scaffolding groups each showed significant pre-to-post gains in conceptual understanding, with printed effect sizes d = 0.42, d = 0.52, and d = 0.43 respectively. [→ Caroline Wermann 2026](#caroline-wermann-2026)

## Evidence

### Caroline Wermann 2026

Caroline Wermann, Karina E. Avila, Sebastian André, Julia C. Draeger, Alvar Goetze, Jochen Kuhn, Maite Maurer, Sascha Mehlhase, Nikola Merkas, Fabian Schrodt, and Stefan Küchemann. (2026). AI-based Verbal and Visual Scaffolding in a Serious Game: Effects on Learning and Cognitive Load. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2602.08893](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.08893)

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Repeated measures ANOVA on pre/post multiple-choice concept test scores (16 items) in a randomized one-factor between-groups study with 152 school students, university students, and general public. The article reports "a significant gain in points achieved from pre- to post-test for all groups" with d = 0.42, d = 0.52, and d = 0.43.

> "Bonferroni post-hoc tests revealed a significant gain in points achieved from pre- to post-test for all groups: the control group (t(149) = −4.968, p < .0001, d = 0.42), the verbal scaffolding group (t(149) = −4.323, p < .0001, d = 0.52), and the verbal and visual scaffolding group ( t(149) = −4.009, p = .0001, d = 0.43)."
