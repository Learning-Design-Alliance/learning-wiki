Evidence¶

Gross Megan 2014¶

Gross Megan, Buac Milijana, Kaushanskaya Margarita. (2014). Conceptual scoring of receptive and expressive vocabulary measures in simultaneous and sequential bilingual children. American Journal of Speech-Language Pathology. https://doi.org/10.1044/2014_AJSLP-13-0026

q3 · i3 · causal · r1

Repeated-measures ANCOVA on the two bilingual groups (39 simultaneous, 19 sequential) with scoring method, modality, test language as within-subject factors, group as between-subjects, SES covariate; large scoring-method effect (ηp2 = .31), adjusted means rising from 90.5 to 102.4.

"There was a significant main effect of scoring method, F (1, 47) = 28.2, p < .001, ηp2 = .31. Overall, children received significantly higher scores with conceptual scoring (Madjusted marginal = 102.4, SE = 1.5) than with language-specific scoring (Madjusted marginal = 90.5, SE = 1.6)."

Gross Megan 2014 (2)¶

Gross Megan, Buac Milijana, Kaushanskaya Margarita. (2014). Conceptual scoring of receptive and expressive vocabulary measures in simultaneous and sequential bilingual children. American Journal of Speech-Language Pathology. https://doi.org/10.1044/2014_AJSLP-13-0026

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Same repeated-measures ANCOVA; all scoring-method interactions were non-significant (ps > .1). Equivalence was not tested; the null interactions indicate no detected modulation once SES was controlled.

"The interactions between scoring method and the other variables under consideration (modality, language, group) were not significant (all ps > .1), indicating that these variables did not significantly modulate the effects of conceptual scoring, once SES was taken into account."
