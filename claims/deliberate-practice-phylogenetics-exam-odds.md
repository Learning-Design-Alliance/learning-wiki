## Evidence

### Hobbs F. Collin 2013

Hobbs F. Collin, Johnson Daniel J., Kearns Katherine D. (2013). A Deliberate Practice Approach to Teaching Phylogenetic Analysis. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe-13-03-0046

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental comparison of final laboratory exam performance between the intervention group (2010–2011, n = 134) and comparison group (2007–2009, n = 124) using logistic regression. The intervention group was "1.56 times more likely to answer questions correctly about phylogenetic content" (p < 0.001).

> "Results of the logistic regression indicate that students in the intervention group were 1.56 times more likely to answer questions correctly about phylogenetic content than students in the comparison group."

### Hobbs F. Collin 2013 (2)

Hobbs F. Collin, Johnson Daniel J., Kearns Katherine D. (2013). A Deliberate Practice Approach to Teaching Phylogenetic Analysis. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe-13-03-0046

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Logistic regression on 25 unchanged nonphylogenetic exam questions found no significant difference between groups (odds ratio 0.96, p = 0.517), supporting the assignments as the factor behind the phylogenetic gain. Equivalence was not tested beyond this comparison.

> "For nonphylogenetic questions, the log odds between comparison and intervention groups were not significantly different (Table 2), indicating that our new assignments were the likely factor explaining the higher performance of the intervention group."
