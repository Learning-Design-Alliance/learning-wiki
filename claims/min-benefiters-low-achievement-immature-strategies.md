# A minority of adolescents were model-predicted to benefit greatly from min-counting yet used it significantly less often than peers and scored about one standard deviation below average in broad math achievement

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Seventeen adolescents (9 solution-time only, 8 dual benefiters) were predicted extreme min-counting benefits, yet used it less than peers (t(186) = −4.51, p < .001) and relied on immature sum, max, and finger counting (t(186) = 5.80, p < .001). [→ Rhodes 2018](#rhodes-2018)
`q3 i?` Min-benefiters scored significantly lower on broad math (mean standard score = 83.75; t(180) = 21.74, p < .001), about one standard deviation below average. [→ Rhodes 2018 (2)](#rhodes-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Rhodes 2018

Rhodes, K. T., Lukowski, S., Branum-Martin, L., Opfer, J., Geary, D. C., & Petrill, S. A. (2018). Individual Differences in Addition Strategy Choice: A Psychometric Evaluation. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11360214/

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Post hoc group comparison of the 17 model-predicted min-benefiters against peers in the 376-adolescent sample. The article attributes their benefit to reliance on "especially immature (given their age) sum and max counting strategies and even finger counting".

> "Surprisingly, those adolescents who were model predicted to benefit the most from min-counting used this strategy significantly less often than their peers (t(186) = −4.51, p < .001), suggesting maladaptive strategy choices"

### Rhodes 2018 (2)

Rhodes, K. T., Lukowski, S., Branum-Martin, L., Opfer, J., Geary, D. C., & Petrill, S. A. (2018). Individual Differences in Addition Strategy Choice: A Psychometric Evaluation. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11360214/

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of min-benefiters' Woodcock-Johnson III standard scores with peers. None had identified mathematical learning difficulties; the authors describe them as "garden variety low achievers" with continuously distributed EIRT parameters, not a distinct cluster.

> "These adolescents were about one standard deviation below average standard math achievement scores; calculations (mean standard score = 86.70; t(185) = 15.78, p < .001), applied problem-solving (mean standard score = 89.50; t(181) = 16.87, p < .001), math fluency (mean standard score = 85.12; t(186) = 18.93, p < .001), and broad mathematics (mean standard score = 83.75; t(180) = 21.74, p < .001)."
