# In a randomized experiment in China, EdTech and paper workbook exercises of equal time and content had the same effect on math test scores and grades

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among students ages 9–13 followed over an academic year, supplemental EdTech sessions produced the same effect on standardized math test scores and math grades as workbook sessions of equal time and content, making EdTech a perfect substitute for that traditional learning. ## Evidence

### Robert Fairlie and Prashant Loyalka 2020

Robert Fairlie and Prashant Loyalka. (2020). Schooling and Covid-19: lessons from recent research on EdTech. npj Science of Learning 5:13. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-020-00072-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A large-scale randomized experiment in China with ~10,000 primary school students ages 9–13 followed for several months over the academic year compared supplemental EdTech to paper-and-pencil workbook exercises of identical content outside school hours, controlling for time on task. The article reports that EdTech "had the same effect on standardized math test scores and grades in math classes" as the workbooks; no effect size is printed.

> "When we examined the impacts of each supplemental program we found that EdTech and workbook exercise sessions of equal time and content outside of school hours had the same effect on standardized math test scores and grades in math classes."
