# Mean-based knowledge estimates from DKT and DKVMN correlate better with an external posttest than BKT estimates

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

> **Currency** · newest study 2019 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person · **overdue for a recheck since 2022**: tool and model accuracy changes quickly

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For Ordering Decimals, mean-DKT (r=0.71) and mean-DKVMN (r=0.72) produced knowledge estimates closer to posttest scores than BKT (r=0.44), with the difference statistically significant after Benjamini-Hochberg control. [→ Scruggs 2019](#scruggs-2019)
`q3 i?` For Placement on Number Line, mean-DKT (r=0.64), mean-PFA (r=0.64), and mean-DKVMN (r=0.62) beat mean-BKT (r=0.52) and BKT (r=0.43). [→ Scruggs 2019](#scruggs-2019)

## Evidence

### Scruggs 2019

Scruggs, R., Baker, R. S., & McLaren, B. M. (2019). Extending Deep Knowledge Tracing: Inferring Interpretable Knowledge and Predicting Post-System Performance. Proceedings of the 28th International Conference on Computers in Education. Asia-Pacific Society for Computers in Education. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1910.12597](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1910.12597)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational comparison of six algorithm variants' knowledge estimates against posttest scores for 598 sixth-grade students, using a significance test for correlated-sample correlations with Benjamini-Hochberg control. The article reports "mean-DKT (r=0.71) and mean-DKVMN (r=0.72) produced the closest knowledge estimates to the posttest scores."

> "For Ordering Decimals, mean-DKT (r=0.71) and mean-DKVMN (r=0.72) produced the closest knowledge estimates to the posttest scores."
