## Evidence

### Hwang 2014

Hwang, GJ., Hung, CM. & Chen, NS. (2014). Improving learning achievements, motivations and problem-solving skills through a peer assessment-based game development approach. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-013-9320-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

An experiment in a science course at an elementary school compared an experimental group of 82 sixth graders using the peer assessment-based game development approach with a control group of 85 using the conventional approach. The abstract reports the design; no effect size is printed in the available text.

> "A total of 167 sixth graders participated in the experiment, 82 of whom were assigned to the experimental group and learned with the peer assessment-based game development approach, while 85 students were in the control group and learned with the conventional game development approach."
