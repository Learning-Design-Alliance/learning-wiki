# Blended MOOC-virtual simulation teaching produces higher post-course theoretical test scores than traditional face-to-face teaching in a general surgery experiment course

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Third-year clinical medicine students taught with the blended MOOC-virtual simulation model scored significantly higher on post-course theoretical tests than students taught traditionally (Cohen's d = 0.84). [→ Zhang Wang 2025](#zhang-wang-2025)

## Evidence

### Zhang Wang 2025

Zhang Wang, Li Zonghuan, Li Pengcheng, Liu Changhuan, Wang Zheng, Liu Yuping, Zhu Lekai, Shi Yulong, Fang Xue, Wang Xinghuan, Xie Zhe, Wang Xin. (2025). MOOC-virtual simulation integration enhances surgical clinical skills: a quasi-experimental study. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2025.2579396

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental comparison of 60 blended-model students vs. 79 traditionally taught controls at Wuhan University. The blended group's post-theoretical mean was 97.67 ± 6.47 vs. 91.27 ± 8.82, "t = 4.732, p < 0.001, Cohen’s d = 0.84"; pre-test scores did not differ (d = 0.09).

> "After intervention, the experimental group scored significantly higher in post–theoretical tests (97.67 ± 6.47 vs. 91.27 ± 8.82; t = 4.732, p < 0.001, Cohen’s d = 0.84)."
