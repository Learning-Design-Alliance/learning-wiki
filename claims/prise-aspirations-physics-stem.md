# PRiSE participation moderately increases physics aspirations and confirms or maintains already-high STEM aspirations, with 80 ± 8% of students reporting positive influence

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Physics aspirations showed a statistically significant moderate increase (mean change +0.46 ± 0.12, p = 8 ×10−4), while STEM aspirations were already very high and were typically confirmed. [→ Martin O. Archer and Jennifer DeWitt 2021](#martin-o-archer-and-jennifer-dewitt-2021)
`q2 i?` On the relative scale, 80 ± 8% of students indicated PRiSE positively affected their physics and/or STEM subject choices. [→ Martin O. Archer and Jennifer DeWitt 2021 (2)](#martin-o-archer-and-jennifer-dewitt-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Martin O. Archer and Jennifer DeWitt 2021

Martin O. Archer and Jennifer DeWitt. (2021). “Thanks for helping me ﬁnd my enthusiasm for physics!” The lasting impacts ‘research in schools’ projects can have on students, teachers, and schools. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2104.04431](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2104.04431)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

2020 quantitative retrospective questionnaire with 35 students rating likelihood of continuing physics on 5-point scales; paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test showed a statistically significant increase, with 40 ± 10% increasing on the absolute scale.

> "the mean change being +0.46 ± 0.12. While this indicates only moderate changes in students’ absolute physics aspirations, they are statistically signiﬁcant (p = 8 ×10−4 in a Wilcoxon signed-rank test)"

### Martin O. Archer and Jennifer DeWitt 2021 (2)

Martin O. Archer and Jennifer DeWitt. (2021). “Thanks for helping me ﬁnd my enthusiasm for physics!” The lasting impacts ‘research in schools’ projects can have on students, teachers, and schools. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2104.04431](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2104.04431)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same 2020 questionnaire: 69 ± 9% reported PRiSE made them more likely to continue physics or confirmed it, and 68 ± 9% indicated positive influence on STEM aspirations; STEM absolute-scale change (+0.18 ± 0.07) was also statistically significant (p = 0.031).

> "with 80 ± 8% of students indicating a positive effect in either or both."
