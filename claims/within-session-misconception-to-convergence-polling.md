## Evidence

### Haolin Feng 2026

Haolin Feng, Holly Barrett, Xinru Deng, Dimitrios G. Papageorgiou and Yiwei Sun. (2026). Detective scaffolding for within-session reasoning development: a three-phase framework evaluated in polymer engineering and pre-university outreach. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2606.07279](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2606.07279)

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Cross-sectional analysis of team-level Mentimeter responses across four staged polls in the undergraduate Polymer Detective session (n= 52, 32, 21, 9 across Q1–Q4). At Q1, 71% selected temperature and only 21% moisture; by Q4 "every remaining respondent selected humidity or moisture" (p < .001).

> "At Q1 (n= 52), temperature-based explanations dominated (71%), reflecting the strong prior association between temperature and polymer processing defects established in preceding lectures. Only 21% of respondents identified moisture as a contributing factor at this stage."

### Haolin Feng 2026 (2)

Haolin Feng, Holly Barrett, Xinru Deng, Dimitrios G. Papageorgiou and Yiwei Sun. (2026). Detective scaffolding for within-session reasoning development: a three-phase framework evaluated in polymer engineering and pre-university outreach. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2606.07279](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2606.07279)

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Intermediate poll results from the same undergraduate session: at Q2, 66% of 32 responding teams identified the morning peak window; at Q3 (n= 21), 52% selected step changes and 10% periodic/cyclic. Descriptive percentages, no test statistic printed for these stages.

> "In Q2 (n= 32), students examined time-resolved defect data and 66% correctly identified the 06:00–12:00 window as the peak-defect period."
