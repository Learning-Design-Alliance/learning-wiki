# Intensity of SES services and other provider characteristics are not significantly related to estimated impacts

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Deke 2012

Deke, John, Lisa Dragoset, Karen Bogen, and Brian Gill (2012). Impacts of Title I Supplemental Educational Services on Student Achievement (NCEE 2012-4053). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory correlational analysis linking provider-reported service intensity to district- and provider-level impact estimates. Average hours in math-focused services ranged from 0 to 27 and reading from 0 to 43 across providers, yet "the intensity of services is not significantly related to the estimated size of impacts."

> "However, the intensity of services is not significantly  related to the estimated size of impacts on ma th or reading achievement  ( for these eligible students near the cutoff for having been offered SES)."

### Deke 2012 (2)

Deke, John, Lisa Dragoset, Karen Bogen, and Brian Gill (2012). Impacts of Title I Supplemental Educational Services on Student Achievement (NCEE 2012-4053). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory analyses of provider characteristics (organization type, staff, group size, assessments) against impact estimates; the authors report none of the relationships "were stronger than what we would expect to see by random chance alone."

> "We also found no evidence that any other observed provider characteristics were significantly  associated with stronger impacts."
