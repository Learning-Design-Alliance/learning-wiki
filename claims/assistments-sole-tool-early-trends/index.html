Evidence¶

Feng 2025¶

Feng, M., Li, L., Brezack, N., & Schneider, M. (2025). Scaling teachers' professional development for ASSISTments companion report: Implementation analysis of the ASSISTments intervention. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED677784

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Exploratory comparison of 13 sole-tool teachers (721 students) versus multi-tool teachers (1,992 students); baseline scores were similar (p = 0.366). The article states these "trends should be interpreted with caution."

"teachers who used ASSISTments alone had marginally higher end-of-year scores (225.9 versus 223.4, p = 0.051, difference = 2.55 points; Figure 11), and a higher percentage of their students outperformed their VCG counterparts (average 63%, range 33–93%)"

Feng 2025 (2)¶

Feng, M., Li, L., Brezack, N., & Schneider, M. (2025). Scaling teachers' professional development for ASSISTments companion report: Implementation analysis of the ASSISTments intervention. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED677784

q2 · i? · causal · r2

System use data showing sole-tool classrooms also showed higher platform usage: more problems completed (536.9 versus 291.6), more assignments (51.9 versus 32.1), more days per week (2.1 versus 1.8), and higher vPLC engagement (3.77 versus 3.47).

"their students spent 517.1 minutes on ASSISTments across the school year compared with 310.6 minutes in classrooms with multiple tools"
