# PBL with high-fidelity simulation produces a significantly lower percentage of hands-off time during CPR, retained at six months

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` The intervention group showed significantly lower hands-off time at skill assessment (24.0% vs. 28.3%, g = 1.55) and this difference was reproduced at follow-up (23.7% vs. 31.0%, g = 1.88). [→ Berger C 2019](#berger-c-2019)

## Evidence

### Berger C 2019

Berger C, Brinkrolf P, Ertmer C, Becker J, Friederichs H, Wenk M, Van Aken H, Hahnenkamp K. (2019). Combination of problem-based learning with high-fidelity simulation in CPR training improves short and long-term CPR skills: a randomised single blinded trial. BMC Medical Education 19:180. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1626-7

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Manikin-pair video-recorded scenario analysis at two hours and at the six-month follow-up, with manikin data analysed for each pair of participants. The intervention group was "significantly lower" in interruption percentage at both measurements, with printed Hedges' g of 1.55 and 1.88.

> "The intervention group had a significantly lower percentage of interruptions during CPR (hands off time 24.0(23.0/26.7)% vs. 28.3(24.3/31.7)% p = 0.007, g = 1.55). This was reproduced during the follow-up (IFU: 23.7(19.3/26.6)% vs. CFU: 31.0(25.0/33.7)% p = 0.006, g = 1.88; Fig. 2a)."
