Evidence¶

Zawacki-Richter 2019¶

Zawacki-Richter, O., Marín, V. I., Bond, M., & Gouverneur, F. (2019). Systematic review of research on artificial intelligence applications in higher education – where are the educators? International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-019-0171-0

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r?

This is a meta-analytic finding cited within the review's ITS synthesis section. The review notes it was published over five years prior, calling for updated effectiveness research.

"Steenbergen-Hu and Cooper (2014) found that ITS had a moderate effect of students' learning, and that ITS were less effective that human tutoring, but ITS outperformed all other instruction methods (such as traditional classroom instruction, reading printed or digital text, or homework assignments)."

Related Claims¶

  • ITS significantly outperform large-group human instruction, individual computer-based instruction, and textbooks or workbooks, but not small-group or individual human tutoring — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Learning with intelligent tutoring systems yields a moderate, statistically significant overall advantage in achievement over non-ITS modes of instruction — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Intelligent tutoring systems improve achievement over non-individualised instruction — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • ChatGPT's effect on higher-order thinking is moderated by course type and by ChatGPT's role, with the intelligent-tutor role showing the largest effect — related
  • Meta-analytic evidence indicates intelligent tutoring systems yield positive learning outcomes compared with non-ITS conditions — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Intelligent tutoring systems are a mature, technologically sophisticated implementation of AI-driven personalized learning with mostly positive outcomes in programming, mathematics, and STEM — related
  • A narrative review of intelligent tutoring systems asserts, citing one source, that students in personalized learning environments outperform peers in standardized settings — related
  • ITS substitution: meta-analyses find no significant cognitive learning differences between ITS and human tutoring or small-group instruction — reports the opposite
  • Steenbergen-Hu and Cooper found smaller ITS effects in K-12 school settings where lower-achievers did worse, but more positive ITS effects for university students versus conventional instruction — related
  • A meta-analysis found that Intelligent Tutoring Systems outperformed, in aggregate, the other modes of instruction they were compared against, though publication bias may partly explain this — related
