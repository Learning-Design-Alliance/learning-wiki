# Problem Based Economics professional development raises grade 11–12 students' economics content knowledge on the Test of Economic Literacy by an average of 2.6 test items (effect size 0.32)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small
> **Currency** · newest study 2011 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` In a randomized controlled trial, students whose teachers received PBE professional development outscored control students on the 40-item Test of Economic Literacy (model-adjusted means 22.61 vs. 20.01; effect size 0.32). [→ Finkelstein 2011](#finkelstein-2011)

## Evidence

### Finkelstein 2011

Finkelstein, N., Hanson, T., Huang, C.-W., Hirschman, B., and Huang, M. (2011). Effects of Problem Based Economics on high school economics instruction (NCEE 2010-4002rev). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED533126

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Within-school randomized controlled trial with 4,350 students taught by 64 teachers (35 intervention, 29 control) in Arizona and California, analyzed with conditional multilevel regression models. The intervention group's model-adjusted TEL mean of 22.61 exceeded the control mean of 20.01, "an average of 2.6 test items (effect size = 0.32)".

> "A statistically significant finding that students whose teachers had received professional development and support in Problem Based Economics (model-adjusted mean score = 22.61) outscored their control group peers (model-adjusted mean score = 20.01) on the TEL by an average of 2.6 test items (effect size = 0.32)."
