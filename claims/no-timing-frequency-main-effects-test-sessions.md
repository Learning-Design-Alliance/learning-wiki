## Evidence

### Akizuki Kazunori 2019

Akizuki Kazunori, Yamamoto Ryohei, Yamaguchi Kazuto, Yabuki Jun, Ohashi Yukari. (2019). The effects of feedback timing and frequency on the acquisition of cardiopulmonary resuscitation skills of health sciences undergraduate students: A 2 x 2 factorial quasi randomized study. PLoS ONE 14(7): e0220004. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0220004

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Omnibus repeated-measures ANOVA on test-session overall scores in the 2x2 factorial study. The article reports no significant main effects or interaction; only the measurement sequence main effect was significant (p < 0.001). No effect sizes were printed for the timing, frequency, or interaction terms.

> "The ANOVA for the test sessions revealed that there were no significant main effects of feedback timing (p = 0.135) or frequency (p = 0.765), and no significant interaction between timing and frequency (p = 0.997)."
