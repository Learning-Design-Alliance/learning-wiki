# Praise for intelligence (person praise) induces a fixed mindset and helpless reactions compared with process praise

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across six studies, praise for children's intelligence (versus effort or outcome praise) was more likely to induce a fixed mindset, performance goals, low-ability attributions, and impaired performance after failure. [→ Dweck 2019](#dweck-2019)

## Evidence

### Dweck 2019

Dweck, C. S., & Yeager, D. S. (2019). Mindsets: A View From Two Eras. Perspectives on Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691618804166

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Mueller and Dweck (1998) conducted six studies with late grade-school children comparing person, process, and outcome praise after success; the article reports person praise "was more likely to induce a fixed mindset" with performance goals and helpless reactions. No effect sizes are printed in the quoted text.

> "Across the studies, we demonstrated that after a success, praise for intelligence (person praise), compared with praise for effort (process praise) or praise for outcome, was more likely to induce a fixed mindset, along with its old friends: performance goals, low-ability attributions for failure, and impaired performance (helpless reactions) following failure."
