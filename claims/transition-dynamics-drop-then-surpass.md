## Evidence

### Donner 2015

Donner, Y., & Hardy, J. L. (2015). Piecewise power laws in individual learning curves. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-015-0811-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Average curves centered at transition points, computed relative to position-matched control windows with shuffled piece order, showed "a sharp decrease followed by a slightly slower increase to a higher level than before the transition" across all four tasks.

> "Transitions were characterized across all tasks by a sharp decrease followed by a slightly slower increase to a higher level than before the transition. The posttransition level after the initial performance decrease exceeded the extrapolation of the pretransition curve."

### Donner 2015 (2)

Donner, Y., & Hardy, J. L. (2015). Piecewise power laws in individual learning curves. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-015-0811-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

For each transition the authors computed how many exercises the post-transition piece needed to exceed the pre-transition piece. "performance dropped in 78 % of the transitions", yet over 80% surpassed the prior piece within 50 exercises.

> "Immediately posttransition, performance dropped in 78 % of the transitions, but within 50 exercises, the posttransition PL exceeded the pretransition PL in over 80 % of the transitions."
