# Novices and experts benefit alike from problem analysis before reading; the treatment-by-expertise interaction is not significant

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The interaction between treatment and level of expertise on total recall was not significant, so neither the hypothesis that experts benefit more nor Anderson's discrepancy hypothesis that novices benefit more was supported. [→ Schmidt 1989](#schmidt-1989)

## Evidence

### Schmidt 1989

Schmidt, H. G., De Volder, M. L., De Grave, W. S., Moust, J. H. C., & Patel, V. L. (1989). Explanatory Models in the Processing of Science Text: The Role of Prior Knowledge Activation Through Small-Group Discussion. Journal of Educational Psychology, 81(4), 610-619. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.81.4.610

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 (n = 88) contrasted novices (ninth graders) and experts (tenth graders). Although the difference between experimental and control groups appeared greater for novices, the absence of an interaction means experts and novices alike seem to be influenced by the problem analysis.

> "The interaction effect between treatment and level of expertise was not significant, F( l, 82) = .01,/9 < .93."
