Adama 2026¶

Adama, V., Biddle, R., Arachchilage, N., & Lottridge, D. (2026). Confident yet Concerned: Inconsistencies in Computing Students' Attitudes on Cybersecurity. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18541

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Survey section on cybersecurity support networks among 236 computing students. The article reports "Most participants (61%) did not have trusted persons to whom to turn for cybersecurity help or advice", with only 13% reporting a go-to person with IT expertise and help-seeking generally infrequent.

"Most participants (61%) did not have trusted persons to whom to turn for cybersecurity help or advice. Only 19% indicated having someone they trust, and only 13% reported having a “Go-To” person with IT exper- tise/qualifications."

Adama 2026 (2)¶

Adama, V., Biddle, R., Arachchilage, N., & Lottridge, D. (2026). Confident yet Concerned: Inconsistencies in Computing Students' Attitudes on Cybersecurity. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18541

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Self-reported cybercrime experiences in the same survey. The article states "21% of participants disclosed having fallen to at least one cyber scam or fraud", with reported types including online marketplace fraud, malicious links, and bank or card compromise; 40% knew a victim.

"21% of participants disclosed having fallen to at least one cyber scam or fraud. 16% reported they were not sure if they had fallen victim. 40% of participants reported knowing someone who had fallen victim to a scam or fraud."
