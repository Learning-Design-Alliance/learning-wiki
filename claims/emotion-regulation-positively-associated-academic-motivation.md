# Emotion regulation is positively associated with academic motivation among university students

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Zhang Y 2026

Zhang Y, Shi Y and Lu J (2026). The relationship between AI anxiety and academic motivation among university students: the mediating role of emotion regulation and the moderating role of gender. Front. Psychol. 17:1918525. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1918525

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Bivariate Pearson correlation analysis (Table 1) in the cross-sectional survey of 1,484 Chinese university students. Emotion regulation was positively correlated with academic motivation (r = 0.457, p < 0.001).

> "AI anxiety was negatively correlated with both emotion regulation (r = −0.172, p < 0.001) and academic motivation (r = −0.175, p < 0.001), whereas emotion regulation was positively correlated with academic motivation (r = 0.457, p < 0.001)."

### Zhang Y 2026 (2)

Zhang Y, Shi Y and Lu J (2026). The relationship between AI anxiety and academic motivation among university students: the mediating role of emotion regulation and the moderating role of gender. Front. Psychol. 17:1918525. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1918525

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression path from PROCESS Model 4 (Table 2). With AI anxiety and daily AI use duration in the model, emotion regulation was positively associated with academic motivation (B = 0.439, p < 0.001). No standardized effect size is printed for this path.

> "When AI anxiety, emotion regulation, and daily AI use duration were entered simultaneously, emotion regulation was positively associated with academic motivation [B = 0.439, SE = 0.023, 95% CI (0.393, 0.485), p < 0.001]"
