# In the SEM model, gender has no direct effects on end-of-physics-2 self-efficacy, interest or identity; gender directly predicts only physics 1 self-efficacy (b = 0.28) and interest (b = 0.36)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Gender directly predicts only end-of-physics-1 self-efficacy (b = 0.28) and interest (b = 0.36), with no direct paths to any physics 2 outcome. [→ Sonja Cwik 2020](#sonja-cwik-2020)
`q3 i?` Multi-group SEM found no gender moderation: no group differences at weak or strong measurement invariance, including regression coefficients. [→ Sonja Cwik 2020 (2)](#sonja-cwik-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Sonja Cwik 2020

Sonja Cwik, Kyle Whitcomb, and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). How the learning environment predicts male and female students' motivational beliefs in algebra-based introductory physics courses. Physics Education Research Conference Proceedings. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.cwik](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.cwik)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Gender-mediation SEM on matched survey data controlling post physics 1 self-efficacy and interest; model fit CFI = 0.928, TLI = 0.918, RMSEA = 0.058, SRMR = 0.049. Gender had direct connections only to physics 1 self-efficacy (b = 0.28) and interest (b = 0.36); no standardized effect sizes beyond these path coefficients are printed.

> "Fig 1 shows that there is no direct effects from gender to any of the post student outcomes in physics 2, i.e., post self-efficacy, interest, and identity."

### Sonja Cwik 2020 (2)

Sonja Cwik, Kyle Whitcomb, and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). How the learning environment predicts male and female students' motivational beliefs in algebra-based introductory physics courses. Physics Education Research Conference Proceedings. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.cwik](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.cwik)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multi-group SEM moderation analysis comparing female and male students with likelihood ratio tests across levels of measurement invariance; the article reports no statistically significant moderation by gender, so mediation analysis was used instead.

> "There were no group differences at the level of weak and strong measurement invariance including no difference at the level of regression coefficients."
