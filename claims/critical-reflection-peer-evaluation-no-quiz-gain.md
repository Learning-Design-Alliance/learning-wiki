# Critical reflection during peer evaluation of peer presentations does not significantly improve quiz performance in a physics senior seminar

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who completed a critical-reflection peer evaluation form scored no better on end-of-class quizzes than students completing a control form. [→ Steven W. Tarr and Emily Alicea-Muñoz 2024](#steven-w-tarr-and-emily-alicea-munoz-2024)

## Evidence

### Steven W. Tarr and Emily Alicea-Muñoz 2024

Steven W. Tarr and Emily Alicea-Muñoz. (2024). Reflecting to learn in a physics multimedia communication course. [doi:10.1119/perc.2024.pr.tarr](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2024.pr.tarr)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized control trial across Fall 2023 (N=32) and Spring 2024 (N=17) seminar offerings: audience members were randomly assigned treatment (critical reflection) or control peer evaluation forms, then all took a short quiz. The study reports "Cohen's d = 0.003 ± 0.062, p = 0.47" for the reflection effect.

> "We did not observe a statistically significant effect due to the critical reflection activity (Cohen's d = 0.003 ± 0.062, p = 0.47)."
