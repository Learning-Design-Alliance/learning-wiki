# Private schooling is associated with a small positive average advantage in civic outcomes over public schooling (0.055 SD).

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across 531 effects from 57 studies, private schooling is associated with an average civic-outcome advantage of 0.055 SD, statistically significant at the 99% confidence interval. [→ Shakeel 2024](#shakeel-2024)

## Evidence

### Shakeel 2024

Shakeel, M. D., Wolf, P. J., Johnson, A. H., Harris, M. A., & Morris, S. R. (2024). The Public Purposes of Private Education: a Civic Outcomes Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09874-1

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Pre-registered RVE meta-analysis pooling 531 effect sizes from 57 qualified studies across 40 databases, comparing private with government-run public schooling on civic outcomes. The pooled estimate is "an increase of 0.055 standard deviations" significant at greater than 99% confidence; no standardized effect size beyond the pooled g is printed.

> "Using Robust Variance Estimation (RVE) regression and meta-regression, we determine that the average reported effect of private schooling on any of the civic outcomes is an increase of 0.055 standard deviations that is statistically significant with greater than 99% confidence."
