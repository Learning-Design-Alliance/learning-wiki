Evidence¶

Lichand 2024¶

Lichand, G., Christen, J., & Van Egeraat, E. (2024). Neglecting students’ socio-emotional skills magniﬁed learning losses during the pandemic. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00235-9

q3 · i1 · causal · r3

Cluster-randomized control trial in Goiás, Brazil (18,256 students, 87 schools), ITT OLS estimates on student-level test scores from an in-person Q2/2021 assessment. The printed effect size is 0.21 s.d. (90% CI: [0.06, 0.35]) on Portuguese test scores, a small standardized effect.

"Column 1 shows that nudges increased Portuguese standardized test scores by 0.21 s.d. (90% CI: [0.06, 0.35])."

Related Claims¶

  • Text-message nudges achieving impacts of the same order as remote remedial instruction indicates motivation itself carries much of remote-learning effectiveness — related
  • Nudges significantly decreased the share of schools with sub-proficient average test scores, from 17.1% to 8.4% — related
  • Nudges prevented 24.2% of learning losses in Portuguese and 7.5% in math at school level, though imprecisely estimated — related
  • Message content mattered: loss framing and avoiding social pressure produced significantly larger test-score effects, ruling out experimenter-demand explanations — related
  • The intervention increased student engagement with remote activities and motivation to return to in-person classes — related
  • Treatment effects were larger for previously higher-performing students and for schools with prior online activities, consistent with complementarity between curricular content and socio-emotional skills — related
  • Targeting students directly (versus caregivers) produced significantly larger nudge effects on test scores (0.31 vs. 0.08 s.d.) — related
  • Parents-only SMS/audio nudges produced statistically non-significant learning gains of d = 0.081 in rural Cote d'Ivoire — related
