## Evidence

### Mahoney B 2021

Mahoney B, Luebbert E. (2021). Updates on Simulation in Obstetrical Anesthesiology Through the COVID-19 Pandemic. Anesthesiology Clinics. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.anclin.2021.08.001

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

A randomized trial by Sorenson and colleagues compared simulation-based obstetric anesthesia training for emergency cesarean section and postpartum hemorrhage scenarios conducted in situ versus off-site. Outcomes were "similar individual and team outcomes" across measures; no effect sizes are printed.

> "They found similar individual and team outcomes in patient safety attitudes, stress, motivation, perceptions of the simulations, and team performance while those receiving in situ simulation training did find a greater degree of fidelity than those receiving remote training."

### Sørensen Jette Led 2017

Sørensen Jette Led, Østergaard Doris, LeBlanc Vicki, Ottesen Bent, Konge Lars, Dieckmann Peter, Van der Vleuten Cees. (2017). Design of simulation-based medical education and advantages and disadvantages of in situ simulation versus off-site simulation. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0838-3

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The authors report, citing their randomised educational trial comparing announced in situ with off-site in-house multiprofessional obstetric anaesthesia training with identical, standardised scenarios, that ISS participants "scored the authenticity of the simulation scenarios significantly higher than the OSS participants" while no other measured variable differed significantly. No effect size is printed.

> "The ISS participants scored the authenticity of the simulation scenarios significantly higher than the OSS participants, but the comparison of ISS versus OSS in-house did not reveal any significant differences regarding all other variables measured"
