# Post-testing after reading produces a large retention benefit over re-reading at a 7-day delay (d = 0.74 on trained questions)

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Reading then quizzing yielded significantly better final performance on trained questions than reading twice (post-testing effect, d = 0.74). [→ Latimier 2019](#latimier-2019)

## Evidence

### Latimier 2019

Latimier, A., Riegert, A., Peyre, H., Ly, S. T., Casati, R. & Ramus, F. (2019). Does pre-testing promote better retention than post-testing? npj Science of Learning 4:15. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0053-1

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Preregistered online between-subjects experiment (285 adults, three conditions, final test ~7 days later). Planned t-test comparison of reading-quiz vs. reading-reading on trained questions gave "d = 0.74 [0.44, 1.04]" with p < 0.0001 (Table 4).

> "Trained questions d = 0.74 [0.44, 1.04] d = 0.34 [0.05, 0.63] d = 0.35 [0.07, 0.64] p < 0.0001"
