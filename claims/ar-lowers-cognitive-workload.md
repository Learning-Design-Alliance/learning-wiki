## Evidence

### El Ashry M 2026

El Ashry M, El Ashry A, Khalique H, Abdalle Y, Yeung T. (2026). Augmented Reality in Surgical Training: Systematic Review of Its Impact on Technical Performance in Surgical Trainees. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/71572

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Crossover RCT (n=40) with Pupil Core eye-tracking; the review reports lower NASA-TLX (50 vs 56, P<.01) and a lower blink rate under AR telestration.

> "Felinska et al [4] reported significantly lower NASA Task Load Index scores during basic laparoscopic tasks with AR telestration compared with verbal instruction (mean 50 [SD 21] vs mean 56 [SD 22]; P<.01), alongside a lower objective blink rate."

### El Ashry M 2026 (2)

El Ashry M, El Ashry A, Khalique H, Abdalle Y, Yeung T. (2026). Augmented Reality in Surgical Training: Systematic Review of Its Impact on Technical Performance in Surgical Trainees. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/71572

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Crossover RCT (n=60 laparoscopic novices) using iSurgeon AR telestration versus verbal guidance; the review reports the NASA-TLX mental and physical demand contrasts with P<.001 and P=.002.

> "Wild et al [7] reported that participants found AR training significantly less mentally demanding (mean 33.3 [SD 14.8] vs mean 48.9 [SD 14.3]; P<.001) and less physically demanding (mean 35.1 [SD 13.8] vs mean 38.1 [SD 13.3]; P=.002)."
