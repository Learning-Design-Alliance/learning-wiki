# Student performance declines from freshman to sophomore year, with course failures rising in every subject and attendance declining sharply

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Course failure rates increased in every subject from freshman to sophomore year, most in math (5.0 to 7.5 percent), and the share of students with attendance below 80 percent grew from 9.5 to 13.7 percent. [→ Seeskin 2020](#seeskin-2020)
`q2 i?` Attendance declined more than grades: the proportion of students below 80 percent attendance rose four percentage points, while GPA below 1.5 rose only about one percentage point. [→ Seeskin 2020 (2)](#seeskin-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Seeskin 2020

Seeskin, A., Mahaffie, S., & Usher, A. (2020). The forgotten year: Applying lessons from Freshman success to Sophomore year. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/the-forgotten-year

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cohort comparison of course failure rates by subject (Figure 5, 2016-17 cohort) shows failures rose in all subjects; "increased most in math, from 5.0 percent for freshmen to 7.5 percent for sophomores." Non-core failures rose from 3.8 to 5.1 percent.

> "The course failure rate of the students in this study increased most in math, from 5.0 percent for freshmen to 7.5 percent for sophomores."

### Seeskin 2020 (2)

Seeskin, A., Mahaffie, S., & Usher, A. (2020). The forgotten year: Applying lessons from Freshman success to Sophomore year. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/the-forgotten-year

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cohort comparison of attendance distributions (Figure 6, 2014-15 cohort) found a four-percentage-point increase in students below 80 percent attendance: "9.5 percent of freshmen, compared to 13.7 percent of sophomores." GPA below 1.5 rose only marginally (13.3 to 14.4 percent).

> "9.5 percent of freshmen, compared to 13.7 percent of sophomores, had attendance rates below 80 percent"
