# An interactive workshop produced higher post-test knowledge scores than an e-learning course of equivalent content among nurses

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Both e-learning and interactive workshop formats improved diabetic foot care knowledge, but post-test scores were significantly higher in the interactive workshop group. [→ Al‐Me'ani Mohammad 2026](#almeani-mohammad-2026)

## Evidence

### Al‐Me'ani Mohammad 2026

Al‐Me'ani Mohammad, Alqaisi Omar, Dibas Mohammed, Al‐Aaraj Heyam, Rubbai Yousef. (2026). Effectiveness of Educational Programmes on Knowledge and Practice Related to Diabetic Foot Care Among Nurses: A Systematic Review. Endocrinology, Diabetes & Metabolism. https://doi.org/10.1002/edm2.70324

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Single quasi-experimental two-group pre-post study among 80 nurses (40 per format) comparing a five-module e-learning course with a face-to-face interactive workshop of equivalent content; the review attributes this to Aalaa et al. [19]. No effect size printed.

> "Both formats produced statistically significant post‐intervention improvements in diabetic foot care knowledge (p < 0.01); however, post‐test scores were higher in the interactive workshop group than in the e‐learning group"
