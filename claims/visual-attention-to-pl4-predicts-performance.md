## Evidence

### Panadero 2025

Panadero, E., Delgado, P., Zamorano, D., Pinedo, L., Fernández-Ortube, A., & Barrenetxea-Mínguez, L. (2025). Putting excellence first: How rubric performance level order and feedback type influence students' reading patterns and task performance. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102168

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

GLM analysis linking rubric reading behavior to written landscape analysis performance in the RCT. The study reports "the number of visits to PL4 significantly predicted better task outcomes" and fixation time on PL4 likewise; rubric order and its interactions with visits and fixation time were not significant.

> "the number of visits to PL4 significantly predicted better task outcomes (t = 2.11, p = .04), as did the average fixation time on PL4 (t = 2.56, p = .01). No such associations were observed for PL1, PL2, or PL3."

### Panadero 2025 (2)

Panadero, E., Delgado, P., Zamorano, D., Pinedo, L., Fernández-Ortube, A., & Barrenetxea-Mínguez, L. (2025). Putting excellence first: How rubric performance level order and feedback type influence students' reading patterns and task performance. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102168

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANOVA on 85 think-aloud protocols from Phases 1 and 2 referencing PLs; "A one-way ANOVA revealed no significant differences in task performance as a function of the referenced PL". The authors note the analysis does not fully meet the independence assumption.

> "A one-way ANOVA revealed no significant differences in task performance as a function of the referenced PL, F(3, 81) = 0.60, p = .62"
