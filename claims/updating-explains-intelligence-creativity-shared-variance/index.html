Benedek Mathias 2014¶

Benedek Mathias, Jauk Emanuel, Sommer Markus, Arendasy Martin, Neubauer Aljoscha C. (2014). Intelligence, creativity, and cognitive control: The common and differential involvement of executive functions in intelligence and creativity. Intelligence. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2014.05.007

Abstract-adjacent measurement model result in the same 230-participant SEM study; the latent Gf–creativity correlation was .45 (p < .001), later reduced by executive predictors as described in Section 3.3.

Related Claims¶

  • Creativity, crystallized intelligence, fluid intelligence and executive functions are all correlated in children aged 8 to 13 — related
  • Executive functions substantially account for the latent intelligence–creativity correlation, leaving a reduced but still positive residual of r = .34. — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Openness to experience predicts creativity but not fluid intelligence, and further reduces the residual intelligence–creativity correlation to a trend. — related
  • Creativity (divergent thinking ability) is predicted by both updating and inhibition, but not by shifting ability. — related
  • Updating ability strongly predicts fluid intelligence, while shifting and inhibition do not significantly predict it. — related
  • Fluid intelligence strongly predicts the creative quality of produced metaphors (β = .45) — related
  • In children, shifting is a more powerful predictor of creativity than fluid and crystallized intelligence — related
  • Men scored higher than women on rated divergent thinking creativity in this sample, with no sex differences in fluid intelligence or the executive functions. — related
  • Openness to experience predicts creative metaphor quality, and controlling for personality reduces Gc's creative-metaphor effect to zero — related
  • Intelligence and creativity share more variance than the traditional assumption of distinct abilities holds — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • A latent creativity factor (writing, drawing, openness) predicts paired-associate vocabulary learning, controlling for general intelligence — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
