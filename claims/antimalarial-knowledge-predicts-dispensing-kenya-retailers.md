## Evidence

### Rusk A 2012

Rusk A, Smith N, Menya D, Obala A, Simiyu C, Khwa-Otsyula B, O'Meara W. (2012). Does anti-malarial drug knowledge predict anti-malarial dispensing practice in drug outlets? A survey of medicine retailers in western Kenya. Malaria Journal. https://doi.org/10.1186/1475-2875-11-263

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional interviewer-administered survey of 115 medicine retailers in 97 outlets in Webuye, Kenya; unadjusted odds ratios from Table 4a show retailers who would "Recommend AL for uncomplicated malaria in adults" had over three times the odds of knowing AL as first-line therapy (OR = 3.55, 95% CI 1.49 - 8.47; p = 0.004).

### Rusk A 2012 (2)

Rusk A, Smith N, Menya D, Obala A, Simiyu C, Khwa-Otsyula B, O'Meara W. (2012). Does anti-malarial drug knowledge predict anti-malarial dispensing practice in drug outlets? A survey of medicine retailers in western Kenya. Malaria Journal. https://doi.org/10.1186/1475-2875-11-263

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey findings reported in the Discussion: among the 65% of retailers who identified AL correctly, "only 56% would recommend AL to adults, and only 43% would recommend it to children" — descriptive proportions, no effect size or significance test printed for this contrast.

> "Of those who identified the current first-line anti-malarial, only 56% would recommend AL to adults, and only 43% would recommend it to children."
