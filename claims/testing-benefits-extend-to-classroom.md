## Evidence

### Brame CJ 2015

Brame CJ, Biel R. (2015). Test-Enhanced Learning: The Potential for Testing to Promote Greater Learning in Undergraduate Science Courses. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-11-0208

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Leeming (2002), a classroom study of an exam-a-day approach in introductory psychology; daily-exam students also had higher average grades than unit-test students.

> "He found that students who completed an exam every day rather than exams that covered large blocks of material scored significantly higher on a retention test administered at the end of the semester."

### Brame CJ 2015 (2)

Brame CJ, Biel R. (2015). Test-Enhanced Learning: The Potential for Testing to Promote Greater Learning in Undergraduate Science Courses. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-11-0208

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Larsen et al. (2009), a randomized controlled trial in which medical residents were assigned to test or study a review sheet on two neurological topics and tested on both 6 months later.

> "The authors observed that the testing condition produced final test scores that averaged 13% higher than the study condition."

### Brame CJ 2015 (3)

Brame CJ, Biel R. (2015). Test-Enhanced Learning: The Potential for Testing to Promote Greater Learning in Undergraduate Science Courses. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-11-0208

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Lyle and Crawford (2011), a course comparison in which one statistics section spent the final 5–10 min of class answering two to four recall questions about the day's lecture (the PUREMEM method).

> "The students in this section of the course performed ∼8% higher on exams over the course of the semester than students in sections that did not use the retrieval-practice method, a statistically significant difference."

### Brame CJ 2015 (4)

Brame CJ, Biel R. (2015). Test-Enhanced Learning: The Potential for Testing to Promote Greater Learning in Undergraduate Science Courses. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-11-0208

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The review reports, citing Orr and Foster (2013), a study assigning 10 MasteringBiology quizzes in an introductory majors biology course and comparing students completing all versus none; the trend held for high-, middle-, and low-performing students.

> "Students of all abilities who completed all of the pre-exam quizzes had higher average exam scores than those who completed none."
