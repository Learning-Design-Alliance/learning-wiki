## Evidence

### Yanghee Kim · Michael Tscholl 2021

Yanghee Kim · Michael Tscholl. (2021). Young children's embodied interactions with a social robot. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-021-09978-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative theme of embodied collaboration in Study Two: asked "How big is a monkey?", a girl gestured a height, her partner countered with a shorter gesture, and she corrected hers to match. Connected embodiments occurred most frequently when representing mathematical properties or appearances.

> "While doing tasks together, children saw their partner's embodiment and built on and extended that embodiment to continue the tasks."

### Yanghee Kim · Michael Tscholl 2021 (2)

Yanghee Kim · Michael Tscholl. (2021). Young children's embodied interactions with a social robot. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-021-09978-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Observed during problem-solving sessions using a completion paradigm in which each child chose one half of an item on a shared tablet; withdrawal of a finger from the tablet triggered the partner's immediate selection.

> "we observed several instances of embodied turn-taking, that is, children used hands and bodily gestures to signal to the partner their intent to yield turns, such as gazes, elbow-nudging, and withdrawal postures"

### Yanghee Kim · Michael Tscholl 2021 (3)

Yanghee Kim · Michael Tscholl. (2021). Young children's embodied interactions with a social robot. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-021-09978-3

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Observed across both studies: all children commonly used celebratory gestures and hugging to express excitement and companionship, including thumbs-up, high-fives, and hugging the robot in response to remarks like "I like this!"

> "Another noteworthy pattern of embodiment was that children frequently used their bodies to express emotions towards the robot while playing and learning."
