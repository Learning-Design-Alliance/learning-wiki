# Diverse LLM explanations were associated with about 7.7% higher open-ended response accuracy than generic explanations, but the condition effect was not significant after Bonferroni adjustment

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` First-year computing students who received three diverse LLM-generated explanations scored consistently higher on open-ended comprehension questions (about 7.7–8.1% across three snippets) than students who received generic explanations, but the condition effect did not remain significant after Bonferroni adjustment. [→ Seth Bernstein 2026](#seth-bernstein-2026)

## Evidence

### Seth Bernstein 2026

Seth Bernstein, Paul Denny, Juho Leinonen, Kush Patel, Rayhona Nasimova, Matt Littlefield, and Stephen MacNeil. (2026). Exploring the Value of Diverse LLM Explanations in Introductory Programming. SIGCSE Virtual '26. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28882 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2606.28882](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2606.28882)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Between-subjects randomized study of 971 first-year computing students assigned to diverse or generic GPT-4o explanations; OE responses from 785 students were manually coded and compared with chi-square tests and logistic regression. The article reports "open-ended response accuracy was consistently about 7.7% higher" for diverse explanations.

> "Across participants, open-ended response accuracy was consistently about 7.7% higher when students received diverse explanations, with no difference in perceived cognitive load."
