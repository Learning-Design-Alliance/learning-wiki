## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Medical students receiving AI-assisted peer teaching scored significantly higher on postintervention knowledge than those receiving traditional peer teaching (d=0.49). [→ Brini Stefano 2026](#brini-stefano-2026)

## Evidence

### Brini Stefano 2026

Brini Stefano, Metcalf Mary, Li Ting, Fukuzawa Fumitoshi, Yu Chengcheng, Li Fangcai, Zhang Ning, Hu Hejia, Huang Hanxu, Wang Jingkai, Tao Yiqing, Wu Yinan. (2026). Effectiveness of Artificial Intelligence–Assisted Peer Teaching in Orthopedic Clinical Education: Historical Cohort Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/87959

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Historical cohort of 190 medical students (n=96 control, n=94 intervention) in an 8-week orthopedic rotation; independent samples t test showed "significantly higher postintervention knowledge scores" with "Cohen d=0.49".

> "the AI-assisted group demonstrated significantly higher postintervention knowledge scores (mean 79.69, SD 8.41 vs mean 75.33, SD 9.26; mean difference=4.36, 95% CI 1.84-6.87; P<.001; Cohen d=0.49)"
