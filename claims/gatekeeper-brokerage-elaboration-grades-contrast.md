## Evidence

### J. Pulgar 2019

J. Pulgar, Cristian Candia, P. Leonardi. (2019). Undergrad classroom cooperation and academic performance: Beneficial for real-world-like problems but detrimental for algebra-based problems. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1912.06923](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1912.06923)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

OLS regression of problem elaboration scores of student-generated physics problems on network predictors (Fig. 6B), coded from 26 generated problems with Cohen's Kappa of .92. The gatekeeper-by-section interaction was negative and significant for Mixed versus Traditional and less negative for Treatment.

> "Moreover, students seeking out information from peers from other groups, and sharing it with their team members (i.e., gatekeeper brokerage) is a positive predictor of problem elaboration above and beyond instructional differences."

### J. Pulgar 2019 (2)

J. Pulgar, Cristian Candia, P. Leonardi. (2019). Undergrad classroom cooperation and academic performance: Beneficial for real-world-like problems but detrimental for algebra-based problems. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1912.06923](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1912.06923)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The same OLS grade models show the gatekeeper coefficient was negative and significant for physics grades, contrasting with its positive sign for problem elaboration.

> "Consistent with our pre- vious results, connecting others outside one’s group for infor- mation seeking (Figure 4B) does not afford academic success in well-structured problems."
