# Both high- and low-prior-achieving students benefited from exposure to high-quality assignments

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 1 associational `r2`, 1 theoretical `r2` · `q2`–`q3`

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Students at both high and low prior achievement levels benefited from exposure to high-quality assignments. [→ Wenzel 2002](#wenzel-2002)

`q3 i?` Students with both high and low prior achievement benefit from intellectually demanding assignments, with high-achieving students benefiting more in reading and low-achieving students benefiting more in mathematics. [→ Smylie 2006](#smylie-2006)

## Evidence

### Wenzel 2002

Wenzel, S., Nagaoka, J., Morris, L., Billings, S., & Fendt, C. (2002). Documentation of the 1996-2002 Chicago Annenberg Research Project Strand on Authentic Intellectual Demand Exhibited in Assignments and Student Work: A Technical Process Manual. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/documentation-1996-2002-chicago-annenberg-research-project-authentic-intellectual

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The manual summarizes this finding from the Newmann, Bryk, and Nagaoka (2001) report, printing that "Both students with high and low prior achievement levels benefit from being exposed to high quality assignments." No effect size is given here.

> "Both students with high and low prior achievement levels benefit from being exposed to high quality assignments."

### Smylie 2006

Smylie, Mark A., and Stacy A. Wenzel. (2006). Promoting Instructional Improvement: A Strategic Human Resource Management Perspective. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/promoting-instructional-improvement-strategic-human-resource-management-perspective

`q3 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Newmann, Bryk, and Nagaoka examined how prior achievement related to benefits of demanding assignments. They found "students who received assignments that were relatively more intellectually demanding out-performed comparable students who received less demanding assignments" across prior-achievement levels.

> "Regardless of levels of prior achievement, students who received assignments that were relatively more intellectually demanding out-performed comparable students who received less demanding assignments."
