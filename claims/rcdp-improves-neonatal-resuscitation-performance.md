## Evidence

### Agrawal 2026

Agrawal, S., Rakshasbhuvankar, A., Rath, C., Brown, J. A., McEvoy, P. M., Rao, S., & Patole, S. (2026). Neonatal endotracheal intubation: development of a comprehensive educational program for procedural skills. Pediatric Research, 99, 484–489. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41390-025-04217-4

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

Study reported by the review comparing resident medical officers trained with RCDP — rapid cycling between deliberate practice and real-time feedback until mastery — against controls on neonatal resuscitation performance. The RCDP group "performed better than the control group for overall neonatal resuscitation performance (65% vs 87%, p = 0.004)"; no effect size is printed.

> "A recent study found that resident medical officers in the RCDP group performed better than the control group for overall neonatal resuscitation performance (65% vs 87%, p = 0.004), administering positive-pressure ventilation (63% vs 88%, p = 0.006)"

### Agrawal 2026 (2)

Agrawal, S., Rakshasbhuvankar, A., Rath, C., Brown, J. A., McEvoy, P. M., Rao, S., & Patole, S. (2026). Neonatal endotracheal intubation: development of a comprehensive educational program for procedural skills. Pediatric Research, 99, 484–489. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41390-025-04217-4

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

The review reports a systematic review commissioned by the ILCOR task force finding RCDP produced "a significantly shorter time to defibrillation and time to administration of epinephrine than controls".

> "A systematic review conducted on behalf of the International Liaison Committee on Resuscitation task force found that RCDP resulted in a significantly shorter time to defibrillation and time to administration of epinephrine than controls."
