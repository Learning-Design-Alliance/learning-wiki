## Evidence

### McManus IC 2013

McManus IC, Dewberry Chris, Nicholson Sandra, Dowell Jonathan S, Woolf Katherine, Potts Henry WW. (2013). Construct-level predictive validity of educational attainment and intellectual aptitude tests in medical student selection: meta-regression of six UK longitudinal studies. BMC Medicine. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3827328/

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r1`

Meta-regression of 24 postgraduate CLPVs (MRCP(UK) and Specialist Register) across the six cohorts. The article reports the omnibus test (chi-square = 9.57, P = .008) and pairwise tests: A-levels beat GCSEs/O-levels (chi-square = 5.535, P = .019) and aptitude tests (chi-square = 11.14, P < .001), while GCSEs/O-levels vs aptitude tests was non-significant (chi-square = .321, P = .571).

> "A-levels, GCSEs/O-levels and aptitude tests showed highly significant differences (chi-square = 9.57, 2df, P = .008), and Table 2 shows that A-levels had the highest construct-level predictive validity (mean = .556; CI: .426 to .663), followed by GCSEs/O-levels (mean = .316; CI: .148 to .466) and aptitude tests (mean = .243; CI: .090 to .385)."
