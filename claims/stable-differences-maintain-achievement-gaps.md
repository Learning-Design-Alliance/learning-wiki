## Evidence

### Scammacca Nancy 2020

Scammacca Nancy, Fall Anna-Mária, Capin Philip, Roberts Greg, Swanson Elizabeth (2020). Examining Factors Affecting Reading and Math Growth and Achievement Gaps in Grades 1–5: A Cohort-Sequential Longitudinal Approach. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000400

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Authors' interpretation of the cohort-sequential LGC results and Figure 3 trajectories: Quartile 1 students' end-of-Year-1 mean reading scores were below the beginning-of-year means of Quartile 4 students, and gaps worsened for older students.

> "the magnitude of the differences in reading scores between groups after one and two years of instruction suggested the predominant pattern was one of stable differences that maintained achievement gaps over time."

### Scammacca Nancy 2020 (2)

Scammacca Nancy, Fall Anna-Mária, Capin Philip, Roberts Greg, Swanson Elizabeth (2020). Examining Factors Affecting Reading and Math Growth and Achievement Gaps in Grades 1–5: A Cohort-Sequential Longitudinal Approach. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000400

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Math trajectories (Figure 4) showed Quartile 1 students in all grades ended Year 1 below Quartile 4 students' beginning-of-year scores, and after two years Quartile 1 students still scored below Quartile 4 students after one year.

> "As with reading, the pattern of growth in math showed that students with low initial achievement remained well below their classmates with higher initial achievement despite having faster rates of growth."
