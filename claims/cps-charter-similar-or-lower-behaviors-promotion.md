# On study habits, grit, and promotion to tenth grade, charter students performed similarly to or slightly below comparable non-charter students, with the promotion gap possibly reflecting charters' higher promotion requirements

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` On survey measures of study habits and grit, charter students scored at comparable levels to similar non-charter students. [→ Gwynne 2017](#gwynne-2017)
`q3 i?` Charter students' promotion rates to tenth grade within one year were around 2 percentage points lower than similar non-charter students, possibly due to charter schools' typically higher promotion requirements. [→ Gwynne 2017 (2)](#gwynne-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Gwynne 2017

Gwynne, J.A., & Moore, P.T. (2017). Chicago's charter high schools: Organizational features, enrollment, school transfers, and student performance. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. http://cps.edu/NewSchools/Pages/RFP2017.aspx

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Chapter 5 regression-controlled survey analysis in the primary sample of first-time ninth-graders (2010-13), finding no charter advantage on study habits and grit after controlling for incoming skills and background characteristics.

> "For example, on survey measures of study habits and grit, charter school students scored at comparable levels as similar students in non-charter schools."

### Gwynne 2017 (2)

Gwynne, J.A., & Moore, P.T. (2017). Chicago's charter high schools: Organizational features, enrollment, school transfers, and student performance. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. http://cps.edu/NewSchools/Pages/RFP2017.aspx

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Chapter 5 analysis of on-time promotion to tenth grade in the primary sample, showing charter students around 2 percentage points lower; the report attributes this possibly to charter schools' higher promotion requirements rather than weaker student performance.

> "Their promotion rates to tenth grade within one year of enter- ing high school—a proxy for whether students are likely to graduate from high school within four years—were around 2 percentage points lower than similar students in non-charter high schools; however, this difference may have been due to charter schools typically having more requirements for promotion than non-charter schools."
