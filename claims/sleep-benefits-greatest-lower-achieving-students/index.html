Evidence¶

Wang 2025¶

Wang, M., Chen, Z., Han, N., & Yao, H. (2025). Sleep duration and subject-specific academic performance among adolescents in China. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00361-y

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Quantile regression across the achievement distribution (Fig. 3), showing the marginal effect of sleep decreases as performance levels rise. The article attributes this to a possible marginal compensation mechanism of basic cognitive resources.

"in mathematics and foreign language, the positive effect of sleep duration is most pronounced among students in the 20th to 50th percentiles, suggesting that students with average or below-average performance are more likely to beneﬁt from adequate sleep"
