# First-year students rank lecture highly despite the authors' hypothesis that non-lecture methods would be preferred

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Contrary to the hypothesis that students prefer non-lecture methods, students ranked lecture highly, particularly first-year students (94.5% included it in their top 5). [→ Zinski Anne 2017](#zinski-anne-2017)

## Evidence

### Zinski Anne 2017

Zinski Anne, Blackwell Kristina T.C. Panizzi Woodley, Belue F. Mike, Brooks William S. (2017). Is lecture dead? A preliminary study of medical students' evaluation of teaching methods in the preclinical curriculum. International Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.59b9.5f40

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion of their survey findings, consistent with Tsang and Harris. Possible explanations offered include comfort with lectures from undergraduate training, efficient factual delivery, and flexibility of recorded online lectures.

> "While we hypothesized that lecture would be a less preferred instructional method, as it is categorized as passive learning28 and may offer lesser knowledge retention rates,28 students ranked lecture highly amongst the methods evaluated."
