# PAL produces no difference in academic outcomes compared with NAL, though teaching raised scores in both conditions

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Test scores did not differ between PAL and NAL at either post-intervention assessment point, while both conditions improved significantly from pre-intervention. [→ Bacon Paul 2021](#bacon-paul-2021)

## Evidence

### Bacon Paul 2021

Bacon Paul, Lord Rachel N. (2021). The impact of physically active learning during the school day on children's physical activity levels, time on task and learning behaviours and academic outcomes. Health Education Research. https://doi.org/10.1093/her/cyab020

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Repeated measures ANOVA on curricular multiplication assessments (immediate, applied and layered recall) taken before, at the end of, and one week after each condition week, with n = 29 after exclusions. Teaching raised scores in both conditions (P < 0.001); the article reports "No differences (P > 0.05)" between PAL and NAL post-test scores. No effect size printed.

> "Teaching had a significant effect (P < 0.001) in raising pupil outcomes across both learning conditions from the pre-intervention starting point, to the assessment at the end of the intervention, (pre PAL to Post-PAL 1, +2 marks; pre-NAL to post-NAL +3 marks, see Fig. 5)."
