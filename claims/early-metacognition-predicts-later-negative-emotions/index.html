Evidence¶

Palladino 2025¶

Palladino, P., Trotta, E., Bonvino, A., Carlucci, L., & Cottini, M. (2025). How do you feel during English class? Emotions and metacognition in primary school children learning English as a second language. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09414-4

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Pearson correlations among measures taken at T0 and T1 (not a formal cross-lagged panel model). Children better at discriminating correct from incorrect responses in the English vocabulary test at T0, and better at controlling their responses, were less bored and less anxious about learning English at T1 (T0 monitoring r = −0.31 with boredom, −0.33 with anxiety; T0 control r = −0.32 and −0.37). At T1, relative monitoring accuracy and metacognitive control were unrelated to the emotions, while T1 confidence judgments correlated weakly and negatively with T1 boredom and anxiety (r = −0.16 each). The study's prediction model predicted English vocabulary, not emotions, so these are associations, not evidence that metacognition reduces boredom or anxiety.

"In contrast, a better ability to monitor and control one's English vocabulary performance at T0 was negatively related to later feelings of boredom and anxiety at T1."
