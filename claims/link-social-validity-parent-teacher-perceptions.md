## Evidence

### Baker 2016

Baker, D. L., Mogna, V., Rodriguez, S., Farmer, D., & Yovanoff, P. (2016). Building the Oral Language of Young Hispanic Children Through Interactive Read Alouds and Vocabulary Games at Preschool and at Home. Journal of International Special Needs Education, 19(2), 81-94. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1195411

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive social validity survey in Study 1 completed by 6 parents and 5 teachers (two teachers and three assistants); results reported descriptively with no inferential test. Parent means were 1.25 for training helpfulness and 1.75 for perceived child improvement.

> "Overall results from the social validity survey of parents and teachers suggests that teachers thought the training was very helpful ( m¼ 1.4), that children's language in their classroom had improved (m ¼ 1.4), and that other teachers would also like to know more about the LINK interactive reading techniques ( m ¼ 1.2)."

### Baker 2016 (2)

Baker, D. L., Mogna, V., Rodriguez, S., Farmer, D., & Yovanoff, P. (2016). Building the Oral Language of Young Hispanic Children Through Interactive Read Alouds and Vocabulary Games at Preschool and at Home. Journal of International Special Needs Education, 19(2), 81-94. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1195411

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Qualitative interviews with 10 parents across both studies, conducted in Spanish at the library or in a home. The article reports all mothers said children now took the initiative to read and wanted to play the card games with siblings.

> "Two of the mothers, Gabriela and Maria, said that they, and their husbands, had noticed that their children had increased their level of engagement when the parents read books aloud to them, and they were impressed to see their children looking forward to reading and playing the vocabulary games over and over again ."
