## Evidence

### Rodrigo 2014

Rodrigo, V., Greenberg, D., & Segal, D. (2014). Changes in reading habits by low literate adults through extensive reading. Reading in a Foreign Language, 26(1), 73-91. http://nflrc.hawaii.edu/rfl

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-treatment Chi-square on bookstore-visit frequency; the article reports the difference as significant with "Eta = .176" but no standardized effect size in d, g, or r form, so no magnitude is coded. At follow-up the ER group still visited more (74% vs. 57%) but the difference was not significant (p > .05).

> "After treatment, 77% of the ER group had been to a bookstore to look at or buy books versus 60% of the no-ER group. Chi-square, however, revealed a significant difference (χ2 = 5.464, df = 1, n = 176, p =.019, Eta = .176) for bookstore visits."

### Rodrigo 2014 (2)

Rodrigo, V., Greenberg, D., & Segal, D. (2014). Changes in reading habits by low literate adults through extensive reading. Reading in a Foreign Language, 26(1), 73-91. http://nflrc.hawaii.edu/rfl

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Follow-up Chi-square on library visits; the article reports the difference as statistically significant with "Eta = .215" but no standardized effect size in d, g, or r form, so no magnitude is coded. The groups already differed at baseline (78% vs. 59%, p = .008), which the authors note as a pre-treatment difference.

> "However, 6 months after treatment, in the follow-up, the ER group appears to continue to visit a library more often (82% vs. 63%) while the no-ER group shows a decrease, and this difference is statistically significant (χ2 = 4.672, df = 1, n = 101, p = .031, Eta = .215)."
