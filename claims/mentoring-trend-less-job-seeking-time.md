## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a randomized longitudinal study, mentees reported fewer annual hours of job seeking (M = 126.17) than comparison students (M = 228.96), though the multivariate group effect was not significant. [→ Antonelli et al. 2018](#antonelli-et-al-2018)

## Evidence

### Antonelli et al. 2018

Karla Antonelli, Anne Steverson, and Jamie O'Mally. (2018). College Graduates with Visual Impairments: A Report on Seeking and Finding Employment. Journal of Visual Impairment & Blindness, January-February 2018. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1172174

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

MANOVA of quarterly-reported annual job search hours for 51 randomly assigned students with legal blindness. The group effect was not significant (Wilks' A = .882, p = .23), but comparison students reported more job-seeking hours than mentees, a descriptive trend the authors call "substantially more time on job seeking."

> "The multivariate main effect for the four variables was not significant by group, Wilks' A = .882, F (4, 44) = 1.477, p = .23; however, comparison group students reported spending substantially more time on job seeking (in annual hours; M = 228.96, SD = 284.17) than did mentees (M = 126.17, SD = 165.09)."
