## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Opportunistic item selection by item-criterion correlation produced a large odds ratio of 3.02 in the development sample that dropped to 1.67 in the validation sample, performing slightly worse than the Rasch-based unidimensional scale. [→ Hissbach Johanna C 2011](#hissbach-johanna-c-2011)

## Evidence

### Hissbach Johanna C 2011

Hissbach Johanna C, Klusmann Dietrich, Hampe Wolfgang. (2011). Dimensionality and predictive validity of the HAM-Nat, a test of natural sciences for medical school admission. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-11-83

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression comparison of the 33-item criterion-correlated scale against the Rasch-selected HAM-Nat-uni. The article concludes "a pragmatic (or opportunistic) stance, just demanding validity for a specific criterion, did not pay."

> "In development sample A, this scale produced a high odds ratio of 3.02, but transferred to validation sample B the value dropped to 1.67. This drop demonstrates the effect of overfitting caused by random associations exploited in the development sample"
