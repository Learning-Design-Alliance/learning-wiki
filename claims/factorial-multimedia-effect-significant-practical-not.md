# In the factorial analysis, multimedia-based training had a significant effect on ΔOSATS (4.6 vs 1.7, p = 0.001) while practical training did not (3.6 vs 2.8, p = 0.38)

## Evidence

### Pape-Koehler Carolina 2013

Pape-Koehler Carolina, Immenroth Marc, Sauerland Stefan, Lefering Rolf, Lindlohr Cornelia, Toaspern Jens, Heiss Markus. (2013). Multimedia-based training on Internet platforms improves surgical performance: a randomized controlled trial. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-012-2672-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Factorial between-group comparison in the RCT pooling the multimedia-based and combination groups (36 participants) against the practical and control groups (34 participants), analyzed per the pre-specified two-step ANOVA procedure. The printed means and p value are reported; no effect size is printed.

> "They reached a ΔOSATS score of 4.6, whereas the participants without multimedia-based training (practical training and control groups; n = 34) reached a ΔOSATS score of 1.7. With a p value of 0.001, the effect of multimedia-based training was significant."

### Pape-Koehler Carolina 2013 (2)

Pape-Koehler Carolina, Immenroth Marc, Sauerland Stefan, Lefering Rolf, Lindlohr Cornelia, Toaspern Jens, Heiss Markus. (2013). Multimedia-based training on Internet platforms improves surgical performance: a randomized controlled trial. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-012-2672-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The second pre-specified factorial comparison pooled the practical and combination groups against the multimedia-based and control groups. The article reports the printed ΔOSATS means and the non-significant p value; equivalence was not tested and no effect size is printed.

> "A total of 35 participants underwent practical training (practical training and combination training). They reached a ΔOSATS score of 3.6. The participants without practical training (multimedia-based training and control groups; n = 35) reached a ΔOSATS of score 2.8. Practical training did not have a significant effect on surgical performance (p = 0.38)."
