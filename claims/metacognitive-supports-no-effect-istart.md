# Neither metacognitive support (performance threshold or self-assessment) enhanced learning outcomes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There were no effects of the threshold or self-assessment features, in isolation or in tandem, on self-explanation scores, posttest comprehension, or transfer comprehension. [→ McCarthy 2017](#mccarthy-2017)

## Evidence

### McCarthy 2017

McCarthy, K. S., Jacovina, M. E., Snow, E. L., Guerrero, T. A., & McNamara, D. S. (2017). iSTART therefore I understand: But metacognitive supports did not enhance comprehension gains. Proceedings of the 18th International Conference on Artificial Intelligence in Education, Wuhan, China: Springer. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED577122

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within the iSTART condition (n = 118), a 2 (threshold) x 2 (self-assessment) between-subjects design tested both metacognitive supports. The article reports "all Fs < 1.00, ns" across all three outcome measures.

> "Analyses of the posttest data indicated no effects of the threshold or self-assessment features on self-explanation scores, posttest comprehension scores, or transfer test comprehension scores (all Fs < 1.00, ns)"
