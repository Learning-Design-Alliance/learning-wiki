# The estimated malaria effect accounts for roughly 40 to 50 percent of the English test-score gap between districts at the extremes of the 2000 PfPR distribution

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Applying the effect estimate to the difference in average birth-year PfPR between very high- and very low-prevalence districts implies malaria explains about 40–50% of their test-score gap. [→ Klejnstrup 2018](#klejnstrup-2018)

## Evidence

### Klejnstrup 2018

Klejnstrup, Buhl-Wiggers, Jones, & Rand. (2018). Early life malaria exposure and academic performance. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0199542

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' calculation in the Discussion comparing the gap between districts at or above the 95th and at or below the 5th percentile of 2000 PfPR (a 60.4 percentage-point PfPR difference and a 1.156-point score gap).

> "Thus, our effect estimate of -0.783 to -0.934 corresponds to around 40 to 50 percent of the test score gap between these districts. While this seems to be a very large effect magnitude, we need to bear in mind that a 107 to 128 percent decrease in malaria risk would be required for the test score of a child to increase by just one point"
