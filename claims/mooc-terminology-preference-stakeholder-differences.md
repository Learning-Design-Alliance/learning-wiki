# In autism-education MOOC discussion forums, self-advocates and family/friends showed a clear preference for identity-first language ('autistic'), while professionals spread their preferences across person-first, spectrum, identity-first and medical terms

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Self-advocates and family/friends in the MOOC forums preferred identity-first language ('autistic'), whereas professionals' preferences were more evenly distributed across 'person with autism', 'on the autism spectrum', 'autistic' and 'ASD'. [→ Lei Jiedi 2021](#lei-jiedi-2021)

## Evidence

### Lei Jiedi 2021

Lei Jiedi, Jones Lauren, Brosnan Mark. (2021). Exploring an e-learning community's response to the language and terminology use in autism from two massive open online courses on autism education and technology use. Autism. https://doi.org/10.1177/1362361320987963

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of 1203 comments from 803 learners (37 self-advocates, 250 family/friends, 443 professionals) on the 'Differences in terminology' forum across six MOOC runs; Table 3 reports counts, e.g. 'autistic' chosen by 20 self-advocates, 87 family/friends and 69 professionals. The study "showed a clear preference towards identity-first language" among self-advocates and family/friends.

> "Both self-advocates and family/friends showed a clear preference towards identity-first language by embracing the term autistic. Professionals showed a more evenly distributed terminology preference across person with autism, on the autism spectrum, autistic and ASD."
