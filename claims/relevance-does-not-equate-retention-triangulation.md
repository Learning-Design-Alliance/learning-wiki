## Evidence

### Alele Faith O. 2024

Alele Faith O., Albert Francis A., Anderson Emma, Seidu Abdul-Aziz, Mason Hannah, Heggarty Paula, Hollins Aaron, Sen Gupta Tarun, McArthur Lawrie, Hays Richard B., Malau-Aduli Bunmi S. (2024). The perceived relevance, utility and retention of basic sciences in general practice. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05750-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Integration of the quantitative and qualitative phases of the mixed-methods study. The authors report that participants "scored higher in biochemistry and lower in anatomy, indicating better retention of biochemistry" while perceiving anatomy and physiology as more relevant, and that "the AMGs outperformed the IMGs in most basic science disciplines", attributed to AMGs being younger and more recent graduates.

> "This suggests that perceived relevance doesn't equate to knowledge retention, possibly due to limited revision opportunities"
