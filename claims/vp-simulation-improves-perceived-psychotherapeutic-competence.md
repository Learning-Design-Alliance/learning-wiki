# AI-enabled virtual patient simulation training improves perceived psychotherapeutic competence over four sessions

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants completing four AI-enabled VP sessions showed significant pre-post improvements in global perceived psychotherapeutic competence and the counseling process and difficult client behavior subscales. [→ Cecil Julia 2026](#cecil-julia-2026)

## Evidence

### Cecil Julia 2026

Cecil Julia, Kokje Eesha, Kleine Anne-Kathrin, Schaffernak Insa, Vogel Lea, Angerer Selina, Lermer Eva, Gaube Susanne. (2026). The effect of AI-enabled virtual patient simulation on training outcomes and insecurities in psychotherapy education. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09917-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Preregistered randomized experiment with N = 87 psychology students and psychotherapy trainees; linear mixed-effects models on pre-post self-report (COSE). The VP group's within-group contrasts were significant for global competence and two subscales, e.g. "counselling process (estimate = 0.29".

> "Within-group contrasts analyses showed significant improvements in the VP group for global competence (estimate = 0.15, SE = 0.06, t(83) = 2.70, p = .009), counselling process (estimate = 0.29, SE = 0.08, t(83) = 3.39, p = .001) and dealing with difficult client behavior (estimate = 0.24, SE = 0.08, t(83) = 3.13, p = .002)."
