## Evidence

### Clemens Megan 2026

Clemens Megan, Boparai Josheil Kaur, Dawe Russell, Avery Susan. (2026). Problem-based and related learning approaches in family medicine residency: a scoping review of four countries. Canadian Medical Education Journal. https://doi.org/10.36834/cmej.81284

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports Colenda et al.'s quasi-experimental pre/post test after a PBL module on managed care: mean scores moved from 66.4% to 69.2% with "no statistically significant improvement"; the pass rate rose from 65% to 80%, also not significant. No effect size is printed.

> "One study objectively measured knowledge before and after PBL using a 10-item multiple-choice test but found no statistically significant improvement in the mean pre- vs. post-test (66.4% vs. 69.2% after PBL)."

### Clemens Megan 2026 (2)

Clemens Megan, Boparai Josheil Kaur, Dawe Russell, Avery Susan. (2026). Problem-based and related learning approaches in family medicine residency: a scoping review of four countries. Canadian Medical Education Journal. https://doi.org/10.36834/cmej.81284

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review's cross-study synthesis of objective knowledge comparisons (Benson et al.; Haidet et al.) found lecture and PBL comparable. One exception is noted: one study reported greater knowledge following PBL (p < 0.01). No effect sizes are printed.

> "Most studies have found no significant differences in knowledge gain or retention between lecture instruction and PBL when measured objectively.23,27"
