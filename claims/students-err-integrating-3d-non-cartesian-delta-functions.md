## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2015

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2015). Upper-division student difficulties with the Dirac delta function. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.010108](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.010108)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the single semester where the exam asked students to calculate the integral of a given three-point-charge density, "27%, N=15 of 56" made significant errors, 73% of them equating the delta-function integral with the integral of the zero point of its vector argument.

> "Roughly a quarter of the students (27%, N=15 of 56) made significant mathematical errors related to the δ-function while executing this integral (element E1)."

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2015 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2015). Upper-division student difficulties with the Dirac delta function. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.010108](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.010108)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

CUE multiple-choice question asked for the integral over all space of a two-point-mass density. "36%, N=53 of 146 at CU, 41%, N=66 of 162 at external institutions" selected an incorrect value, most commonly the vector position where the argument was zero.

> "more than a third of students (36%, N=53 of 146 at CU, 41%, N=66 of 162 at external institutions) selected an incorrect value for the integral of a point mass density in response to the CUE question."
