Evidence¶

Arslantas 2017¶

Arslantas, S. & Kurnaz, A. (2017). The effect of using self -monitoring strategies in social studies course on self -monitoring, self -regulation and academic achievement. International Journal of Research in Education and Science (IJRES), 3(2), 452-463. https://doi.org/10.21890/ijres.327905

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Quasi-experimental pretest-posttest study with 96 fifth graders in three groups (two experimental, one control); one-way ANOVA (Tukey) on self-monitoring scale post-test scores showed "the calculated F-value was found 14.67, while the resulting p-value was 0.00", with both experimental groups differing significantly from the control group. The article scores the self-monitoring scale relative to its ideal value (9.5), not as "more is better": post-test means were 1.04 and 1.39 in the experimental groups (down from 3.05 and 6.31) and 7.36 in the control group (up from 4.69), and the article describes the experimental groups as having "a mean score close to the ideal value (9,5) and a low standard deviation". It prints no effect size.

"the calculated F-value was found 14.67, while the resulting p-value was 0.00. The result is significant as p -value is less than the alpha value (p <0.05). It was observed that there was a significant difference both between the first experimental group and the control group, and between the second experimental group and the control group."
