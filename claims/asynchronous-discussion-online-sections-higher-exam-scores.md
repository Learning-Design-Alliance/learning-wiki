## Evidence

### Jorczak and Dupuis 2011

Robert L. Jorczak, Danielle N. Dupuis. (2011). Differences in Classroom Versus Online Exam Performance Due to Asynchronous Discussion. https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Differences+in+Classroom+Versus+Online+Exam+Performance+Due+to+Asynchronous+Discussion

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental in situ comparison of one classroom section (n = 35) and two online sections (n = 69) in an introductory psychology course with identical materials, schedule, and instructor. The article reports "t (102) = 3.56, p = .001" and the printed effect size "d = 0.66"; means were 0.67 (classroom) and 0.74 (online) proportion correct.

> "The online students did statistically significantly better on the exams, t (102) = 3.56, p = .001. The effect size was d = 0.66, which suggests that an average student who participates in online discussion will score 25 percentile points higher on course exams than an average student who participates in classroom discussion."
