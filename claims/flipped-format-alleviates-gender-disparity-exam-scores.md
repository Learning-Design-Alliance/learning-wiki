# The flipped format alleviates the gender disparity in exam scores: female students underperform males in the standard class but do not differ significantly on two of three exams in the flipped class

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the standard class, male students outperformed female students on all three exams; in the flipped class the difference was no longer significant on exams 1 and 3. [→ Gross D 2015](#gross-d-2015)

## Evidence

### Gross D 2015

Gross D, Pietri ES, Anderson G, Moyano-Camihort K, Graham MJ. (2015). Increased Preclass Preparation Underlies Student Outcome Improvement in the Flipped Classroom. CBE—Life Sciences Education 14(4). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-02-0040

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

2 (class type) × 2 (gender) × 3 (exam) mixed-model ANCOVA controlling for GPA across the five cohorts. The article reports a "consistent four to five percentage point difference" favoring males in the standard class (p values < 0.02), absent on flipped exams 1 (p = 0.09) and 3 (p = 0.32); no standardized effect size is printed.

> "In the standard class across all three exams there was a consistent four to five percentage point difference between female and male students (p values < 0.02). In the flipped class, however, female students did not differ significantly from their male counterparts on exam 1 (p = 0.09) and exam 3 (p = 0.32)."
