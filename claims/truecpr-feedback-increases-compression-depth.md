# Real-time feedback device use increases chest compression depth by trainee doctors in simulated CPR

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Trainee doctors performing simulated CPR compressed significantly deeper when using the TrueCPR feedback device (53 mm, IQR 51–55) than without it (45 mm, IQR 43–48). [→ Majer Jolanta 2019](#majer-jolanta-2019)

## Evidence

### Majer Jolanta 2019

Majer Jolanta, Jaguszewski Miłosz J., Frass Michael, Leskiewicz Marcin, Smereka Jacek, Ładny Jerzy R., Robak Oliver, Szarpak Łukasz. (2019). Does the use of cardiopulmonary resuscitation feedback devices improve the quality of chest compressions performed by doctors? A prospective, randomized, cross-over simulation study. Cardiology Journal. https://doi.org/10.5603/CJ.a2018.0091

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized cross-over simulation study of 65 trainee doctors performing 2-min uninterrupted chest compressions with and without TrueCPR on a Resusci Anne simulator; median depth was "53 mm (IQR 51–55) for the TrueCPR device" vs 45 mm manual, p < 0.001.

> "The depth of compressions varied in relation to the CC technique tested, with 45 mm (IQR 43–48) for manual CCs and 53 mm (IQR 51–55) for the TrueCPR device. This difference was statistically significant"
