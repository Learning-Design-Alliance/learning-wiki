Evidence¶

Artsi Yaara 2024¶

Artsi Yaara, Sorin Vera, Konen Eli, Glicksberg Benjamin S., Nadkarni Girish, Klang Eyal. (2024). Large language models for generating medical examinations: systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05239-y

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports Cheung et al., the only study comparing LLM to human MCQ writing, evaluated by five international medical experts with no post-generation modification allowed. Humans scored "30 (60%) eligible MCQs" versus the AI's 20 (40%).

"However, the questions written by humans were far better. Both in terms of quality and validity, outperforming the AI in a total score of 30 (60%) eligible MCQs"

Artsi Yaara 2024 (2)¶

Artsi Yaara, Sorin Vera, Konen Eli, Glicksberg Benjamin S., Nadkarni Girish, Klang Eyal. (2024). Large language models for generating medical examinations: systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05239-y

q2 · i? · review · r2

Same Cheung et al. comparison reported in the review: AI generation of 50 MCQs took "21 min", roughly 10% of the 211 minutes required for human writing.

"The overall time required for the AI to generate 50 MCQs was 21 min. This is about 10% of the total time human writing required (211 min)"
