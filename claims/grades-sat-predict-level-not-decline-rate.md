## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Bahrick 1991

Bahrick, H. P., & Hall, L. K. (1991). Lifetime maintenance of high school mathematics content. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 120(1), 20-33. https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.120.1.20

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical regression analysis of algebra retention in which interactions of predictor variables with the retention interval (Steps 7-9) identify variables affecting rate of decline. The article reports grades "continue to have a substantial effect on performance throughout the 50-year interval; however, compared with number of courses taken, they have a relatively small effect on the rate of loss."

> "Grades in courses and standardized test scores are the best predictors of test performance, but their influence on the rate of loss is trivial."

### Bahrick 1991 (2)

Bahrick, H. P., & Hall, L. K. (1991). Lifetime maintenance of high school mathematics content. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 120(1), 20-33. https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.120.1.20

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Separate hierarchical regression on the subsample of 284 individuals with available SAT/ACT scores. Standardized scores had the highest correlation with retention scores (r = .65) but the score-by-retention-interval interaction was not statistically significant, so equivalence in decline rates was not established beyond non-significance.

> "The addition of standardized scores increased the final multiple correlation to .85, but did not yield a statistically significant interaction between standardized scores and the retention interval, indicating that the scores per se do not significantly affect the rate of loss."
