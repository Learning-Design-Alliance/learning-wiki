## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Being female predicts better overall achievement in both school groups, with the coefficient doubling in magnitude in secondary school, but sex is not a significant predictor in the mathematics models of either group. [→ Flores Jerome 2022](#flores-jerome-2022)

## Evidence

### Flores Jerome 2022

Flores Jerome, Caqueo-Urízar Alejandra, López Verónica, Acevedo Daniel. (2022). Symptomatology of attention deficit, hyperactivity and defiant behavior as predictors of academic achievement. BMC Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-022-03714-8

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression analysis reported in the Discussion of results from both school groups. In primary school, sex was significant only in language (β = .127) and visual arts (β = .129) besides GPA; in secondary school it also appeared in natural and social sciences, "with female students obtaining higher achievements." No effect size is printed.

> "However, it is notable that, in the mathematical regression models of both groups, sex does not generate significant differences, which contradicts the results of a previous study that found that female students perform better in this area; we must keep in mind that, in this latter study, a mathematics-specific test was used"
