Evidence¶

Carver-Thomas 2024¶

Carver-Thomas, D., Leung-Gagné, M., & Jeannite, D. (2024). Tackling teacher shortages: What we know about California's teacher workforce investments. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/137.196

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Analysis of Higher Education Act Title II State Report Card System data (Figure 1). The report cautions the increases coincided with the COVID-19 pandemic, which also influenced workforce trends, so the investments' contribution is not isolated.

"however, California saw a modest uptick in the number of candidates completing traditional TPPs between 2019 and 2020, with a 1-year increase of about 10% (from 9,054 to 9,980 completers).11 2021 saw another sizable 1-year increase of 14% in completers (from 9,980 to 11,424)."

Carver-Thomas 2024 (2)¶

Carver-Thomas, D., Leung-Gagné, M., & Jeannite, D. (2024). Tackling teacher shortages: What we know about California's teacher workforce investments. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/137.196

q3 · i? · associational · r1

Enrollment analysis of the California State University system, which historically prepares about half of new teachers. The report notes this enrollment growth had not yet translated into licensed-teacher gains, with CSU credentials declining 13% between 2018–19 and 2022–23.

"The CSU saw a steady 6% increase in teacher preparation enrollments between 2019–20 and 2022–23 (from 17,490 to 18,470).13 Meanwhile, enrollments were either flat or declining in private institutions of higher education, University of California programs, or local education agencies (LEAs)."
