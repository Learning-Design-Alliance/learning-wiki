## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Over a semester, students using clickers and students using flashcards showed no significant differences in conceptual learning gains or traditional problem-solving skills. [→ N. Lasry 2007](#n-lasry-2007)

## Evidence

### N. Lasry 2007

N. Lasry. (2007). Peer Instruction: Comparing Clickers to Flashcards. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0702186](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0702186)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized comparison of two sections of an algebra-based mechanics course taught by the author, one responding to ConcepTests with clickers and one with flashcards; FCI normalized gain and comprehensive final exam showed no significant differences, with the printed p-values 0.745 and 0.630 and "no significant differences were found".

> "over the course of a semester no significant differences were found in conceptual learning  gains (p = 0.745) nor in  traditional problem solving skills (0.630)."
