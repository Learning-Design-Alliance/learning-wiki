# High school students report they learn best through interactive, hands-on projects, real-world applications, and discussions rather than lectures, note-taking, and worksheets

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students consistently report learning best or most effectively through interactive, hands-on projects, real-world applications, and discussions, and identify lectures, note-taking, and worksheets as their least favorite methods. [→ Viki M. Young 2026](#viki-m-young-2026)

## Evidence

### Viki M. Young 2026

Viki M. Young, Ph.D., Mai Chou Vang, Zareen Kasad, Isabelle Acosta, and Alicia Frausto with Cassie Graves. (2026). Reimagining High School: Student Experiences Today, Hopes for Tomorrow. Digital Promise, Center for Learner Pathway Innovations. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/22bd5fad-ec7b-4ec7-a86f-0bed0800ba30

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative synthesis of collaborative small focus groups with approximately 70 high school students and recent graduates from six districts, in the Learning and Engagement findings section. Students said hands-on learning helps retention: "I can remember what I did, but I won't remember what you told me or what I've heard."

> "Students consistently report that they learn best or most effectively through interactive, hands-on projects, real-world applications, and discussions, noting that their favorite classes incorporate at least some of these elements."
