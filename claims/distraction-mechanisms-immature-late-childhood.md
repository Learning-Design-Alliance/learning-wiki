# Not all neurophysiological aspects of auditory distraction mechanisms are mature in late childhood

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In an auditory-visual oddball task, 9-10-year-old children and adults showed similar behavioral distraction effects and similar early and late mismatch responses, but children's brain responses were delayed or differently distributed, indicating immature mechanisms. [→ Wetzel N 2014](#wetzel-n-2014)

## Evidence

### Wetzel N 2014

Wetzel N (2014) Development of control of attention from different perspectives. Front. Psychol. 5:1000. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01000

`q2 · i?`

The editorial reports, citing Ruhnau et al. (2013), electro- and magnetoencephalographic measurement of involuntary change detection, orienting of attention, and behavioral distraction in 9-10-year-old children and adults during an auditory-visual oddball task.

> "The authors observed similar behavioral distraction effects and an early and a late distractor-related mismatch response located at auditory cortex areas in children and adults. Children's brain responses were delayed or showed different distribution over the scalp."
