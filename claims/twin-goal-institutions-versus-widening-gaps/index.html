Evidence¶

Kimberlee Eberle-Sudré 2015¶

Kimberlee Eberle-Sudré, Meredith Welch, and Andrew Howard Nichols. (2015). Rising Tide: Do College Grad Rate Gains Benefit All Students? The Education Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED566656

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Identification of extreme performers among public four-year institutions using decade-long graduation-rate change (Tables 1 and 2). The report found 26 institutions meeting both gain and gap-narrowing criteria and 17 with declining URM rates and gaps widening by at least 7 points.

"There are 26 institutions that, over the past decade, have: • Increased the graduation rate for underrepresented stu-dents by 12 or more percentage points (i.e., two times the average increase for all institutions in the sample); and • Reduced the graduation rate gap between underrepresent-ed and white students. (Table 1)"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • White-Hispanic graduation-rate gaps narrowed modestly overall, and top gap-closers narrowed gaps by more than 7 percentage points — related
  • Institutions serving similar students can have divergent URM outcomes: NC State raised URM graduation rates 12 points and nearly halved its gap while demographically similar Auburn saw URM rates decline and its gap grow past 20 points — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Twenty-seven institutions improved overall graduation rates while black graduation rates declined and black-white gaps widened by at least 8 percentage points — related
  • At four-year public institutions that improved overall graduation rates, black students' rates rose 4.4 percentage points over a decade versus 5.6 points for white students, widening the gap — related
  • Graduation rates improved for all racial, ethnic, and economic groups, but racial and ethnic gaps increased while gender, poverty, and disability gaps declined — related
  • At exemplar institutions such as Ohio State, graduation rates for black students rose faster than for white students, narrowing gaps (black +31.1 vs white +20.5 points) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Institutional improvement was uneven for URM students: more than 1 in 5 of the 255 improvers showed no URM gain, and of those that did, roughly 45 percent narrowed gaps (average 4.6 points) while nearly 55 percent made no progress — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Despite URM gains outpacing white gains at improving publics, the white–URM completion gap narrowed by only 0.6 percentage points in a decade, leaving a 14-point gap — related
  • At the 255 improving public colleges with sizeable URM enrollments, underrepresented students' graduation rates rose slightly more than white students' rates (6.3 versus 5.7 percentage points) — related
