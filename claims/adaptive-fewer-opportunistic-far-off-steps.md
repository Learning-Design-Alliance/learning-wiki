# Adaptive students show fewer Opportunistic and Far Off training steps than Control, with no difference in Futile steps

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Adaptive students had significantly fewer quick inefficient Opportunistic steps (5 vs 7, p < .01) and Far Off steps (16 vs 25, p = .02) during training than Control students. [→ Maniktala 2020](#maniktala-2020)

## Evidence

### Maniktala 2020

Maniktala, M., Cody, C., Isvik, A., Lytle, N., Chi, M., & Barnes, T. (2020). Extending the Hint Factory for the Assistance Dilemma: A Novel, Data-driven Help-Need Predictor for Proactive Problem-solving Help. Journal of Educational Data Mining, Volume 12, No 4. https://jedm.educationaldatamining.org/index.php/JEDM/article/view/450

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Training-behavior comparison in the controlled Deep Thought study (66 Adaptive, 45 Control completers). The Adaptive condition showed significantly fewer Opportunistic and Far Off steps, while "no significant differences in Futile steps" were found; only p-values are printed, no effect sizes.

> "for both Opportunistic (Adaptive: 5, Control: 7, p < .01), and Far Off steps (Adaptive: 16, Control: 25, p = .02) but there were no signiﬁcant differences in Futile steps between the two conditions (Adaptive: 13, Control: 12, p = .45)"
