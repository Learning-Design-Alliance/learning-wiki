# Prior longitudinal studies of math and self-efficacy tend to fit models assuming the constructs lack unique developmental trajectories and do not examine within-person relations

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Existing longitudinal studies tend to fit models that assume math and self-efficacy do not have unique developmental trajectories and do not examine within-person relations. [→ James Soland 2019](#james-soland-2019)

## Evidence

### James Soland 2019

James Soland. (2019). Modeling academic achievement and self-efficacy as joint developmental processes: Evidence for education, counseling, and policy. Journal of Applied Developmental Psychology 65, 101076. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.appdev.2019.101076

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r?`

The article's framing of the research gap: prior studies of the longitudinal math–self-efficacy relationship "tend to fit models that assume the two constructs do not have unique developmental trajectories" and omit within-person analysis. This is the authors' characterization of prior literature, not a tested result.

> "While many studies consider the longitudinal relationship between math and self-efficacy scores, they tend to fit models that assume the two constructs do not have unique developmental trajectories (growth models), nor do they examine how the constructs relate within persons."
