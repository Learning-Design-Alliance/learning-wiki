## Evidence

### Zsifkovits Monika 2025

Zsifkovits Monika, Amplatz Lukas, Triebner Nina, Utz Janine, Kornhuber Johannes, Spitzer Philipp. (2025). Randomized controlled trial of asynchronous vs. synchronous online teaching formats: equal knowledge after training, greater acceptance and lower intrinsic motivation through asynchronous online learning. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07481-4

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

IMI pressure/tension subscale comparison between randomized groups; the webinar group reported more pressure, "Cohen's d = − 0.170, 95% CI [-0.292, − 0.048]". The authors attribute this to active participation demands and lack of breaks.

> "However, the level of tension and pressure (IMI; pressure/tension) was significantly greater during webinar training than during training through the learning platform (MLP = 1.44 ± 0.67; MW = 1.56 ± 0.66; t (1042) = -2.75; p =.003, p_adj = 0.015; Cohen's d = − 0.170, 95% CI [-0.292, − 0.048]; Fig. 4C)."

### Zsifkovits Monika 2025 (2)

Zsifkovits Monika, Amplatz Lukas, Triebner Nina, Utz Janine, Kornhuber Johannes, Spitzer Philipp. (2025). Randomized controlled trial of asynchronous vs. synchronous online teaching formats: equal knowledge after training, greater acceptance and lower intrinsic motivation through asynchronous online learning. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07481-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

IMI perceived competence subscale comparison between randomized groups; the formats were "equally effective in terms of perceived competence" (p =.420), and no effect size was printed. The authors suggest both formats presented equally challenging content.

> "The two learning formats were found to be equally effective in terms of perceived competence (MLP = 3.80 ± 0.68; MW = 3.81 ± 0.66; t (1042) = − 0.203, p =.420, p_adj > 0.999; Fig. 4D)."
