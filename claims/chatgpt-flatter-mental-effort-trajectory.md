# Self-reported mental effort increased less across tasks in the ChatGPT condition while perceived difficulty rose equally in both groups

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A significant tool×task interaction for self-reported mental effort (χ²(2)=8.88, pHolm=.047) showed mental effort rose further in the No AI group on Tasks 2 and 3. [→ Bergh 2026](#bergh-2026)
`q3 i?` There was no significant tool×task interaction for self-reported difficulty; both groups rated subsequent tasks as more difficult (main effect of task p<.001). [→ Bergh 2026 (2)](#bergh-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Bergh 2026

Bergh, C., Tag, B., Vassar, A., & Renzella, J. (2026). Your Programming Students'Cognition with ChatGPT: Higher Performance, Lower Retention, and Reduced Ownership. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21194

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Confirmatory likelihood-ratio test within a linear mixed model of Paas mental-effort ratings (task×tool, random intercept per participant), Holm-corrected across four cognitive-load indices. Follow-up showed mental effort increased further in the No AI group at Task 2 (beta = 1.31) and Task 3 (beta = 1.13).

> "There was a significant tool×task interaction for self-reported mental effort (𝜒 2(2)= 8.88, 𝑝=. 012, 𝑝Holm =. 047)"

### Bergh 2026 (2)

Bergh, C., Tag, B., Vassar, A., & Renzella, J. (2026). Your Programming Students'Cognition with ChatGPT: Higher Performance, Lower Retention, and Reduced Ownership. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21194

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same mixed-model confirmatory test applied to Paas difficulty ratings. The interaction was not significant, though follow-up showed a significant main effect of task (F(2, 106.0)=38.71, p<.001): both groups rated the tasks as getting harder.

> "There was no significant tool×task interaction for self-reported difficulty (𝜒 2(2)= 2.44,𝑝=. 296,𝑝Holm =. 591) and remains insignificant after Holm correction"
