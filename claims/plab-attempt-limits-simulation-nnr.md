# Simulated PLAB attempt restrictions would prevent only small absolute numbers of censures: the proposed four-attempt limit reduces risk by roughly two censures per 5 years, and restricting to first-attempt passes only would approximately halve the censure rate

`q2 i?` A retrospective simulation of attempt restrictions on 21,329 doctors observed for at least 5 years (176 censured) estimated the proposed four-attempt limit would avert only about two censures per 5 years, with the most favourable single-part restriction (three attempts at part 1, unlimited part 2) yielding a number needed to reject of 48. ## Evidence

### Tiffin Paul A. 2017

Tiffin Paul A., Paton Lewis W., Mwandigha Lazaro M., McLachlan John C., Illing Jan. (2017). Predicting fitness to practise events in international medical graduates who registered as UK doctors via the Professional and Linguistic Assessments Board (PLAB) system: a national cohort study. BMC Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-017-0829-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Retrospective simulation (Table 5) applying hypothetical attempt restrictions to the cohort, restricted to doctors observed at least 5 years (n = 21,329; 176 censured within a 5-year window). The number needed to reject concept was introduced in the authors' earlier selection study. The abstract estimates the proposed limit would reduce risk by only approximately two censures per 5 years.

> "the most favourable conditions would be to have unlimited attempts permitted at part 2 of the PLAB but restrict the number of sittings of part 1 of the exam to three (i.e. two resits would be permitted). This would provide a NNR of 48; i.e. 48 doctors who would not go on to be censured during a 5-year period would have to be excluded in order to prevent the registration of one doctor who was censured during that 5-year exposure period."
