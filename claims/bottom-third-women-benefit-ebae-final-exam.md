## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On the shared final exam, bottom 1/3 female students scored 54% in the EBAE course versus 47% in the LB course, while bottom 1/3 male students scored 50% versus 48%, indicating greater EBAE benefit for low-prior-achievement women. [→ Nafis I. Karim 2018](#nafis-i-karim-2018)

## Evidence

### Nafis I. Karim 2018

Nafis I. Karim, Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Do evidence-based active-engagement courses reduce the gender gap in introductory physics? https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.14.010103

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subgroup analysis of final exam scores (Table V(a)) in algebra-based EBAE and LB courses taught by the same instructor with the same homework and final exam, students divided into pretest-score thirds within gender. Percentages are descriptive means.

> "the bottom 1/3 of the female students benefited more from EBAE pedagogies than the bottom 1/3 of the male students (performance of bottom 1/3 of female students is 54% in the EBAE course and only 47% in the LB course, whereas the performance of the bottom 1/3 of male students is 50% in the EBAE course and 48% in the LB course)."
