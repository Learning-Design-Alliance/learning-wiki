# Reaction times differ significantly across all three cue conditions, with fastest RT for valid cues and slowest for no-cue stimuli

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Post hoc analyses with Bonferroni correction showed significant differences between all stimuli categories, with RT to invalid cues slower than valid cues and faster than no cue. [→ Wengaard E 2017](#wengaard-e-2017)

## Evidence

### Wengaard E 2017

Wengaard E, Kristoffersen M, Harris A and Gundersen H (2017) Cardiorespiratory Fitness Is Associated with Selective Attention in Healthy Male High-School Students. Front. Hum. Neurosci. 11:330. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2017.00330

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Post hoc Bonferroni-corrected comparisons of reaction times across the three stimuli categories in the Posner cue task (n = 54). The article reports "RT to stimuli following invalid cues were slower compared to valid cue (Mean differences = 59.94, SE = 2.81, p < 0.001) and faster compared to stimuli following no cue (Mean differences = −23.16, SE = 2.13, p < 0.001)".
