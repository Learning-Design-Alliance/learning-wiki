Evidence¶

Samuel Tobler 2025¶

Samuel Tobler, Tanmay Sinha, Katja Köhler, Manu Kapur. (2025). Teaching biology with narratives: examining the impact of affective and cognitive variables on undergraduate student learning. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09723-w

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Follow-up simple main effect analyses on learning mechanism ratings by educational background found that non-STEM students in the narrative condition descriptively reported higher ratings for all mechanisms and lower extraneous load, though most comparisons were nonsignificant.

"we found medium to large effects and strong Bayesian evidence for all learning mechanisms favoring the narrative instruction materials for those with a non-STEM background. Yet, only the comparison of effect size for germane cognitive load and self-efficacy reached statistical significance"

Samuel Tobler 2025 (2)¶

Samuel Tobler, Tanmay Sinha, Katja Köhler, Manu Kapur. (2025). Teaching biology with narratives: examining the impact of affective and cognitive variables on undergraduate student learning. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09723-w

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Comparing STEM vs. non-STEM students within the expository condition, effect sizes and Bayesian analyses suggested STEM students were more engaged and experienced greater germane load, interest, and self-efficacy, with only self-efficacy reaching significance; no subgroup effect sizes are printed for these comparisons.

"STEM-background students in the expository condition might have benefited more than non-STEM-background students in terms of cognitive engagement, germane cognitive load, interest, and self-efficacy. However, only the self-efficacy comparison reached statistical significance"
