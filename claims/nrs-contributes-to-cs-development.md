# Initial levels and growth in numerical relational skills contribute to counting skill development, suggesting NRS may precede CS

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A multivariate LGCM in which NRS growth factors predict CS growth factors fit better than models with correlations or reversed direction. [→ Verkerk 2026](#verkerk-2026)
`q3 i?` Higher initial NRS strongly contributed to higher initial CS, and stronger linear NRS growth contributed to stronger linear CS growth. [→ Verkerk 2026 (2)](#verkerk-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Verkerk 2026

Verkerk, J. C., van de Rijt, B., & van Viersen, S. (2026). Development of numerical relational and counting skills as components of early numeracy. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102313

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariate (cross-domain parallel process) LGCM on the population sample compared three structural models; AIC and BIC provided "strong to very strong evidence for Model 2", in which NRS contributes to CS, over the correlation model and the reversed-direction model.

> "When considering model parsimony, the BIC and AIC values provided strong to very strong evidence for Model 2 over Model 1 and Model 3."

### Verkerk 2026 (2)

Verkerk, J. C., van de Rijt, B., & van Viersen, S. (2026). Development of numerical relational and counting skills as components of early numeracy. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102313

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the final multivariate LGCM (Model 2), "higher initial NRS levels strongly contributed to higher initial CS levels"; the linear slope of NRS also positively predicted the linear slope of CS, and initial NRS contributed to more linear and less accelerating CS growth.

> "the intercept of NRS significantly predicts the intercept of CS, indicating that higher initial NRS levels strongly contributed to higher initial CS levels."
