# Students rated the VR-based brain death course significantly higher than the manikin-based course in a randomized pilot

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a randomized pilot comparison, the VR course received a significantly higher global rating (74.4 ± 22.2) than the manikin-based course (47.4 ± 28.9). [→ Junga A 2025](#junga-a-2025)

## Evidence

### Junga A 2025

Junga A, Kockwelp P, Valkov D, Schulze H, Bozdere P, Hätscher O, Ahrens H, Marschall B, Risse B, Holling M. (2025). Teach the Unteachable with a Virtual Reality (VR) Brain Death Scenario – 800 Students and 3 Years of Experience. Perspectives on Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5334/pme.1427

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized pilot in which 57 students took the VR course and 62 the manikin course, distributed alternately in groups of six; students rated their course format from 1–100. The VR group rated "74.4 (±22.2)" versus 47.4 (±28.9) for manikin, t(82) = 4.79, p < .001, n = 84.

> "The result shows a significantly better rating of 74.4 (±22.2) [max. 100] points for the VR course and 47.4 (±28.9) [max. 100] points for the manikin-based course (t(82) = 4.79, p < .001), n = 84) (Figure 3)."
