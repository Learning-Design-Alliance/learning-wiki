## Evidence

### Spinde 2025

Spinde, T., Wu, F., Gaissmaier, W., Demartini, G., & Giese, H. (2025). Enhancing Media Literacy: The Effectiveness of (Human) Annotations and Bias Visualizations on Bias Detection. [doi:10.1016/j.ipm.2025.104244](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ipm.2025.104244)

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r3`

Experiment 1, a 3-label (Human/AI/control) between × 2-phase within design with 470 Prolific participants; F1 accuracy against BABE human classifications was compared after Sidak-corrected post-hoc tests. Human-label training beat control with a printed effect size of d = 0.42, as participants "improved correct detection of media bias compared to the control."

> "both the Human label (t(467) = 4.55, p < .001, d = 0.42) as well as the AI label (t(467) = 2.49, p = .039, d = 0.23) improved correct detection of media bias compared to the control."

### Spinde 2025 (2)

Spinde, T., Wu, F., Gaissmaier, W., Demartini, G., & Giese, H. (2025). Enhancing Media Literacy: The Effectiveness of (Human) Annotations and Bias Visualizations on Bias Detection. [doi:10.1016/j.ipm.2025.104244](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ipm.2025.104244)

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r3`

Same Experiment 1 analysis: the AI label condition (BiasRoBERTa-generated sentence highlights) also beat control with a printed effect size of d = 0.23, smaller than the Human-label d = 0.42, so "Human labels demonstrate larger effect sizes and higher statistical significance."

> "both the Human label (t(467) = 4.55, p < .001, d = 0.42) as well as the AI label (t(467) = 2.49, p = .039, d = 0.23) improved correct detection of media bias compared to the control."
