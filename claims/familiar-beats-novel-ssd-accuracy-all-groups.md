## Evidence

### Kucinkas 2025

Kucinkas, A., Retsa, C., Meijer, P. B. L., Wallace, M. T., Gori, M. & Murray, M. M. (2025). Learning visual to auditory sensory substitution reveals flexibility in image to sound mapping. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00385-4

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Evaluation-phase mixed ANOVA with 60 sighted adults; "Participants achieved higher accuracy for familiar stimuli (M = 60.29%, SD = 19.31)" than novel stimuli, F(1,57) = 135.03, with printed effect size η²ₚ = 0.703 (partial eta-squared = 0.703), a large effect.

> "Participants achieved higher accuracy for familiar stimuli (M = 60.29%, SD = 19.31) than for novel stimuli (M = 29.83%, SD = 25.78)."

### Kucinkas 2025 (2)

Kucinkas, A., Retsa, C., Meijer, P. B. L., Wallace, M. T., Gori, M. & Murray, M. M. (2025). Learning visual to auditory sensory substitution reveals flexibility in image to sound mapping. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00385-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Interaction term from the same evaluation-phase ANOVA; "the accuracy difference between familiar and novel stimuli was consistent across groups", with F(2,57) = 0.883 reported; the printed effect size η²ₚ = 0.030 is negligible, so impact is 0-level and coded null here as the subclaim reports only non-significance.

> "The interaction between group and stimulus type was not significant (F(2,57) = 0.883, p = 0.419, η²ₚ = 0.030), indicating that the accuracy difference between familiar and novel stimuli was consistent across groups."
