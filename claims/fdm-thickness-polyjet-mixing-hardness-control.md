## Subclaims
`q2 i?` FDM materials could not be mixed and their hardness increased with printed thickness, whereas PolyJet materials could be mixed in 10 steps, with higher Vero (and lower Agilus) proportion producing higher hardness. [→ Hong Dayeong 2021](#hong-dayeong-2021)

## Evidence

### Hong Dayeong 2021

Hong Dayeong, Kim HaeKang, Kim Taehun, Kim Yong-Hee, Kim Namkug. (2021). Development of patient specific, realistic, and reusable video assisted thoracoscopic surgery simulator using 3D printing and pediatric computed tomography images. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-85738-w

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Material-property evaluation prior to phantom fabrication, measuring hardness of FDM KFLX28 filament by thickness and of PolyJet material mixtures across mixing ratios, with results shown in Fig. 4 against a human skin Shore A hardness range of 0 to 65.

> "the materials could not be mixed in the FDM printing process and the hardness of materials differed according to the thickness of the printed object. At this time, the greater the thickness, the higher the hardness. In the case of PolyJet printing, the materials could be mixed. Therefore, the material mixture was printed and measured in 10 steps. The higher the proportion of Vero material (or the lower the proportion of Agilus material), the higher the hardness"
