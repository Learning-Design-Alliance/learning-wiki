# Landscape design students’ CSES-Feelings empathy scores increased significantly from pre-test to post-test for both female and male participants

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Female landscape students’ CSES-Feelings median scores rose significantly from pre-test to post-test (Z = -1.99, p<0.05). [→ Donnelly S 2019](#donnelly-s-2019)

## Evidence

### Donnelly S 2019

Donnelly S, Dean S, Razavy S, Levett-Jones T. (2019). Measuring the impact of an interdisciplinary learning project on nursing, architecture and landscape design students’ empathy. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0215795

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Wilcoxon signed rank test comparing pre and post CSES-Feelings median scores by gender in the three-group pre-test post-test study. "female participants in the landscape group reported a significantly higher level" on positively worded items (Mdn 12.5 to 19).

> "female participants in the landscape group reported a significantly higher level of CSES-Feelings when median scores from the pre-test (Mdn = 12.5) were compared to the post-test (Mdn = 19) (Z = -1.99, p<0.05)"

### Donnelly S 2019 (2)

Donnelly S, Dean S, Razavy S, Levett-Jones T. (2019). Measuring the impact of an interdisciplinary learning project on nursing, architecture and landscape design students’ empathy. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0215795

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Wilcoxon signed rank test for male landscape students in the same study. "an increased level of CSES-Feelings" was significant from Mdn 14 to 21 (Z = -2.20, p<0.05).

> "A similar pattern was also observed in the male landscape participants with an increased level of CSES-Feelings when the pre-test score (Mdn = 14) was compared to the post-test score (Mdn = 21) (Z = -2.20, p<0.05)"
