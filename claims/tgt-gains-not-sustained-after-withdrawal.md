## Evidence

### Veloo 2016

Veloo, A., Md-Ali, R., & Chairany, S. (2016). Using Cooperative Teams-Game-Tournament in 11 Religious School to Improve Mathematics Understanding and Communication. Malaysian Journal of Learning and Instruction, 13(2), 97-123. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1134733

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-test 2 (without cooperative TGT) results from Table 3 of the randomized study: experimental group M = 11.13 (SD = 2.14) versus control group M = 11.13 (SD = 2.47). The authors attribute the similar scores to withdrawing TGT from the teaching.

> "However, when the use of cooperative TGT was stopped after Post-test 1, the MU score (11.13) for the experimental group was equal to the score for the control group (conventional approach)."

### Veloo 2016 (2)

Veloo, A., Md-Ali, R., & Chairany, S. (2016). Using Cooperative Teams-Game-Tournament in 11 Religious School to Improve Mathematics Understanding and Communication. Malaysian Journal of Learning and Instruction, 13(2), 97-123. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1134733

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-test 2 (without cooperative TGT) results from Table 6: experimental group M = 25.03 (SD = 4.06) versus control group M = 25.00 (SD = 3.97). The article attributes the almost similar scores to the withdrawal of cooperative TGT.

> "However, when the use of cooperative TGT was stopped after Post-test 1, the mathematics communication scores (25.03) for the experimental group is almost equal to the score for the control group (conventional approach) (25.00)."
