# Undergraduate students reported using relatively effective strategies (elaboration, organisation, spacing) more than less effective ones, with elaboration most endorsed and self-testing least endorsed

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Students endorsed elaboration far more than self-testing and rereading, with a large printed difference (Cohen's d = 1.03 for elaboration vs self-testing). [→ Rebecca Hey · Mark McDaniel · Flaviu A. Hodis 2024](#rebecca-hey-mark-mcdaniel-flaviu-a-hodis-2024)

## Evidence

### Rebecca Hey · Mark McDaniel · Flaviu A. Hodis 2024

Rebecca Hey · Mark McDaniel · Flaviu A. Hodis. (2024). How undergraduate students learn: uncovering interrelationships between factors that support self-regulated learning and strategy use. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-024-09389-8

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive survey analysis of 140 NZ undergraduates' strategy self-reports (Table 1): elaboration had the highest mean (M = 5.10) and self-testing the lowest (M = 3.27); the article reports "the Cohen's d for the difference between the means for elaboration and self-testing was 1.03" and d = 0.96 for elaboration versus rereading.

> "The Cohen's d for the difference between the means for elaboration and self-testing was 1.03. Furthermore, the Cohen's d value for the difference between the mean scores for elaboration and rereading was 0.96."
