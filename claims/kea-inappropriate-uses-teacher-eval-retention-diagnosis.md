# It is inappropriate to use KEA scores to evaluate teachers or preschool programs, hold children back from kindergarten, or diagnose learning disabilities

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The report identifies three misuses: KEA scores as measures of teacher or program effectiveness, KEA-based kindergarten retention, and KEA-based learning-disability diagnosis. [→ Yun 2021](#yun-2021)

## Evidence

### Yun 2021

Yun, C., Melnick, H., & Wechsler, M. (2021). High-quality early childhood assessment: Learning from states' use of kindergarten entry assessments. Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/high-quality-kea

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The report's Purposes of Kindergarten Entry Assessments section, an expert-consensus argument rather than an empirical test. It states KEA scores should not measure teacher or program effectiveness, that schools 'should not use KEAs to hold children back from kindergarten,' and that KEAs 'should not be used to diagnose a learning disability because they do not provide sufficient detail for this purpose.'

> "It is inappropriate to use KEA scores as a measure of teacher or preschool program effectiveness because children's scores are affected by a multitude of factors that schools cannot control."
