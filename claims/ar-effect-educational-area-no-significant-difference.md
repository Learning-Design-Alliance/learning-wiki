# AR effect sizes do not differ significantly between Natural Sciences and Social Sciences, though the descriptive Natural Sciences effect is higher (0.562 vs 0.409)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The difference in AR effect sizes between Natural Sciences (0.562) and Social Sciences (0.409) was not statistically significant (QB = 0.195, p = 0.659). [→ Ozdemir M. 2018](#ozdemir-m-2018)

## Evidence

### Ozdemir M. 2018

Ozdemir M., Sahin C., Arcagok S., & Demir M. K. (2018). The effect of augmented reality applications in learning process: A meta-analysis study. Eurasian Journal of Educational Research, 74, 165-186. https://doi.org/10.14689/ejer.2018.74.9

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Subgroup moderator analysis within the meta-analysis comparing effect sizes across educational areas under a random-effects model. The pooled ES was 0.562 for Natural Sciences (12 studies) and 0.409 for Social Sciences (4 studies); "there is not a significant difference among the groups" (QB = 0.195, p = 0.659).

> "In this respect, it can be stated that there is not a significant difference among the groups with regard to the effect sizes (QB = 0.195, p = 0.659). Therefore, it can be stated that the educational area does not affect AR applications."
