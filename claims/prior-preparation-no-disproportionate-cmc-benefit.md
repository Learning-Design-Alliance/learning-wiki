## Evidence

### Nicholas T. Young 2023

Nicholas T. Young, Mark Mills, Rebecca L. Matz, Eric F. Bell, and Caitlin Hayward. (2023). Complex multiple-choice questions are inequitable for low-income and domestic students (and difficult for everyone). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2306.11068](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2306.11068)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression model with GPAO as a continuous predictor. Higher GPAO was associated with increased odds of answering correctly, but the GPAO-by-question-type interaction was not significant; descriptively the gap was "around 8 percentage points" for both GPAO groups.

> "However, the interaction term was not statistically different from one, suggesting that there is no disproportionate difference in performance on CMC and non-CMC questions."

### Nicholas T. Young 2023 (2)

Nicholas T. Young, Mark Mills, Rebecca L. Matz, Eric F. Bell, and Caitlin Hayward. (2023). Complex multiple-choice questions are inequitable for low-income and domestic students (and difficult for everyone). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2306.11068](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2306.11068)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive analysis by ACT score quartile bands (Figure 4) plus a regression model with an ACT-by-question-type interaction. The authors state the interaction "can likely be explained" by the flat CMC performance across ACT scores.

> "while performance on non-CMC questions tends to increase with ACT score, performance on CMC questions is largely unchanged."
