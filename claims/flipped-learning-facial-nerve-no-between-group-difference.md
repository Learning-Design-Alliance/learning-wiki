# Both flipped learning and traditional teaching significantly improved facial nerve anatomy knowledge within groups, with no statistically significant difference between the groups

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Facial nerve anatomy knowledge scores improved significantly from pretest to posttest and follow-up in the experimental flipped group (Friedman p = 0.000), and in the control group (p = 0.022), but no statistically significant difference was found between groups (p > 0.05). ## Evidence

### Akbaytürk N 2026

Akbaytürk N, Aygün T. (2026). Investigation of the effect of flipped learning on neuroanatomy education in dentistry faculty students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09742-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental pretest-posttest-follow-up study with 59 second-year dental students (experimental n = 28, control n = 18... n = 31). Friedman test showed significant within-group gains; Mann-Whitney U showed no between-group difference, including one verbatim fragment of source_quote.

> "Although the post-test and follow-up test scores for facial nerve anatomy knowledge were numerically higher in the experimental group, no statistically significant difference was observed between the experimental and control groups in terms of pre-test, post-test, and follow-up test scores (p > 0.05)."
