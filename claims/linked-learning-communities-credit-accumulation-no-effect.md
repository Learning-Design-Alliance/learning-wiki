# Linked learning communities produced no statistically significant or substantively important effects on credit accumulation across six studies

## Evidence

### What Works Clearinghouse 2014

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2014). Linked Learning Communities. WWC Intervention Report. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/630

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Kingsborough RCT (Sommo et al., 2012; 1,534 students) reporting regular credits accumulated within two semesters postprogram; the WWC prints an average improvement index of +3 for this study's credit accumulation finding.

> "Although the intervention group had accumulated more credits on average (27.7 credits versus 26.2 credits), this difference was not statistically significant, and was not large enough to be considered substantively important."

### What Works Clearinghouse 2014 (2)

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2014). Linked Learning Communities. WWC Intervention Report. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/630

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

WWC synthesis of six RCTs (about 7,400 students). Site-level contrasts were small: Hillsborough 5.3 versus 4.9 credits, Houston 4.7 in both groups, Queensborough 12.4 versus 11.8, Baltimore 5.9 versus 6.0, Merced 4.9 versus 5.1.

> "Thus, for the credit accumulation domain, none of the six studies that met WWC group design standards showed either a statistically significant effect or an effect large enough to be considered substantively important."
