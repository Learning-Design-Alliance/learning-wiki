# Within biology majors, perceived relevance shows a large significant effect on CLASS posttest scores and normalized gains; for health science majors the posttest effect is significant but the gains effect is not

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Biology majors show a large effect size and statistical significance on CLASS posttest (d = 0.79, p < 0.01) and normalized gains (d = 0.58, p < 0.05), with novice-like shift for irrelevant-perceiving students. [→ Andrew J. Mason 2020](#andrew-j-mason-2020)

## Evidence

### Andrew J. Mason 2020

Andrew J. Mason. (2020). Learning goals and perceived irrelevance to major within life science majors in introductory physics. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/perc.2019.pr.mason](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2019.pr.mason)

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Table 4 comparison within biology majors (Irrelevant n = 16, Relevant n = 75): posttest 49.0% vs 61.7%, d = 0.79, p < 0.01; gains -0.11 vs +0.06, d = 0.58, p < 0.05. Within item clusters, biology majors perceiving relevance had statistically higher post-test scores and gains on PI, RWC, and SME clusters with large effect sizes (0.6 < d < 0.85 post-test, 0.5 < d < 0.8 gains).

> "There is also a large effect size and statistical significance for normalized gains within the biology majors, again with a novice -like shift for irrelevant -perceiving students and a slight expert -like shift for relevant-perceiving students."

### Andrew J. Mason 2020 (2)

Andrew J. Mason. (2020). Learning goals and perceived irrelevance to major within life science majors in introductory physics. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/perc.2019.pr.mason](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2019.pr.mason)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Table 4 comparison within health science majors (Irrelevant n = 19, Relevant n = 66): posttest 43.4% vs 52.5%, d = 0.52, p < 0.05; gains -0.09 vs +0.02, d = 0.39, p = 0.14. Pretest comparisons were not significant for either major (biology d = 0.39 p = 0.16; health d = 0.29 p = 0.27).

> "This pattern also appears for health science majors’ gains, but is not statistically significant in this case ."
