## Evidence

### Waters Peadar S 2014

Waters Peadar S, McVeigh Terri, Kelly Brian D, Flaherty Gerard T, Devitt Dara, Barry Kevin, Kerin Michael J. (2014). The acquisition and retention of urinary catheterisation skills using surgical simulator devices: teaching method or student traits. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0264-3

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Correlational analysis of all 42 students' beginner laparoscopic drill performance against catheter scores; printed correlations "r = 0.39, 0.51, 0.43". A significant negative correlation with time to completion was also reported (Figure 5b).

> "A significant correlation was observed between pathway efficiency, economy of movement and total laparoscopy score with overall catheter scores at index examination (p = 0.01, 0.001, 0.004, r = 0.39, 0.51, 0.43) respectively"

### Waters Peadar S 2014 (2)

Waters Peadar S, McVeigh Terri, Kelly Brian D, Flaherty Gerard T, Devitt Dara, Barry Kevin, Kerin Michael J. (2014). The acquisition and retention of urinary catheterisation skills using surgical simulator devices: teaching method or student traits. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0264-3

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Correlational analysis across the same cohort linking index catheter score to 4-week retention score, r = 0.91, p = 0.0001, significant within each teaching group; the authors interpret this as some students possessing innate skills.

> "A significant positive correlation was also observed between catheter score at index examination and scores at the retention test four weeks later (p = 0.0001, r = 0.91, Figure 5c). This correlation remained significant within each teaching group."
