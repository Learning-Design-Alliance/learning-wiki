# The evidence type produced by formative assessment prompts shapes what can be diagnosed: closed-ended prompts reveal pervasiveness of ideas, while open-ended prompts reveal diversity of student thinking

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Closed-ended prompts such as clicker questions reveal the pervasiveness of ideas within a class but may fail to reveal unanticipated student ideas, whereas open-ended prompts can reveal diversity in how students make sense of phenomena. [→ Offerdahl E. G. 2018](#offerdahl-e-g-2018)
`q2 i?` Overly open-ended prompts such as the muddiest point may yield information unrelated to the stated outcome or overwhelmingly broad ranges of ideas that are hard for instructors to summarize and respond to. [→ Offerdahl E. G. 2018](#offerdahl-e-g-2018)

## Evidence

### Offerdahl E. G. 2018

Offerdahl E. G., McConnell M., Boyer J. (2018). Can I Have Your Recipe? Using a Fidelity of Implementation (FOI) Framework to Identify the Key Ingredients of Formative Assessment for Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.18-02-0029

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article's analysis of the evidence-of-student-understanding critical component, drawing on the formative assessment literature. It reasons that clickers' response options are instructor-generated and "may fail to reveal unanticipated student ideas," while open-ended prompts like concept maps invite students to represent their own connections between ideas; the article states there is little empirical evidence on how such variations influence learning.

> "Large class sizes common to introductory undergraduate biology courses often favor the use of closed-ended prompts, such as forced-choice questions (e.g., clicker questions), due to their ease of application and their capacity to reveal the pervasiveness of ideas within the class"
