## Evidence

### Kwant Kelly J. 2015

Kwant Kelly J., Custers Eugene J. F. M., Jongen-Hermus Femke J., Kluijtmans Manon. (2015). Preparation by mandatory E-modules improves learning of practical skills: a quasi-experimental comparison of skill examination results. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0376-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Self-report data from student evaluations cited in the Discussion: not-preparing fell from 72% to 2%, and preparation time rose from an average of 40 min to 69 min. These are descriptive self-reports with no test statistics.

> "we saw a spectacular increase in the number of students who indicated in the student evaluations that they prepared well (from 72 % not preparing to 2 % not preparing), and also the time they spent for the preparation increased substantially (from an average of 40 min per student in case of text based preparation to 69 min when they used e-modules)."

### Kwant Kelly J. 2015 (2)

Kwant Kelly J., Custers Eugene J. F. M., Jongen-Hermus Femke J., Kluijtmans Manon. (2015). Preparation by mandatory E-modules improves learning of practical skills: a quasi-experimental comparison of skill examination results. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0376-4

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion: the intervention's three aspects (obligatory character, richer format and organization, changed trainer approach) cannot be distinguished, so the active ingredient is undetermined.

> "it remains an open question what caused the improvement, whether it was the content of the e-modules or some collateral factor, such as motivation or time on task."
