# Explicitly unfolding higher-order contributions into lower-order concept elements produces deeper conceptual learning opportunities than jumping directly to procedure

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

Across video-recorded whole-class discussions on formal volume calculation, teacher facilitation practices that explicitly grounded higher-order student contributions in lower-order concept elements (rows, layers, structured counting) produced extensively connected, deep conceptual learning opportunities, while practices that jumped straight to procedure or funneled toward an answer produced only shallow ones — and the same eight teachers, using identical curriculum materials, varied substantially in which they enacted.

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Of 48 identified teacher-navigation segments (from 62 total whole-class discussion segments), 19 (40%) were classified as revealing extensively connected, deep conceptual learning opportunities, and 29 (60%) as revealing only shallow ones. [→ Ademmer & Prediger 2025](#ademmer-prediger-2025)

`q2 i2` Three facilitation practices accounted for the deep-opportunity segments — refocusing to conceptually ground elements (10 segments, 5 teachers), unfolding knowledge elements to conceptually ground them (6 segments, 3 teachers), and connecting up and down by repeated leveraging and grounding (3 segments, 2 teachers) — and all three shared the feature of explicitly unfolding a higher-order contribution into its constituent lower-order elements before reconnecting it upward. [→ Ademmer & Prediger 2025](#ademmer-prediger-2025)

`q2 i2` Two facilitation practices accounted for the shallow-opportunity segments — hasty jumping to procedure elements (14 segments, 3 teachers) and funnel steering to unjustified knowledge elements (15 segments, 5 teachers) — both of which reached a correct formula or answer without explicitly grounding it in structured counting of rows, columns, or layers. [→ Ademmer & Prediger 2025](#ademmer-prediger-2025)

`q2 i2` Despite using the same curriculum materials and participating in the same three-year professional-development program, the eight teachers varied substantially: one teacher enacted only deep-opportunity practices across all observed segments, two teachers enacted only shallow-opportunity practices, and the remaining five enacted a mix of both. [→ Ademmer & Prediger 2025](#ademmer-prediger-2025)

`q2 · peer-reviewed qualitative video study with a theory-driven, empirically-refined coding framework (deductive-inductive, "empirically grounded building of ideal types"); segment classification reached by discussion to consensus between two authors rather than independent blind coding with a computed reliability statistic` `i2 · a clear, well-documented qualitative pattern with direct professional-development implications and an informative within-curriculum comparison across teachers, but frequencies should not be over-interpreted given only eight teachers observed (noted by the authors themselves)` `n=8 experienced mathematics teachers (12+ years), Grades 5-7, 549 minutes of video recorded, 280 minutes fully transcribed`

Eight experienced mathematics teachers using the same conceptually-focused volume-calculation curriculum were video-recorded across Grade 5-7 lessons with heterogeneous and high-support-needs classes. Sixty-two whole-class discussion segments were identified and analyzed against a two-dimensional "navigation space" mapping eight knowledge elements (from concrete structured counting through the formal volume formula) against three representations (material/iconic, verbal, symbolic). Five distinct facilitation practices emerged from comparing how teachers navigated this space when students' contributions deviated from the intended sequence; three practices that explicitly unfolded higher-order contributions into concrete structure were associated with deep conceptual learning opportunities, while two that skipped this step were associated with shallow ones. This directly operationalizes [Grounding and Unfolding Facilitation Moves](../patterns/grounding-and-unfolding-facilitation-moves.md).

## Discussion
"Learning opportunity" here describes what the discourse made available for students to construct, not a measured student learning outcome — the study does not include a pre/post assessment linking facilitation practice to actual student understanding. Segment classification was reached through discussion between the two authors rather than independent coding with a computed interrater-reliability statistic, which the authors note explicitly. The study is limited to one mathematical topic (volume of rectangular prisms) and one curriculum; whether the same five practices and their depth classification generalize to other topics with different underlying concept networks is untested here.
