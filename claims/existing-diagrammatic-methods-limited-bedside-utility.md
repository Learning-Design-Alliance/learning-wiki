`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article's comparison section reviews algorithms, concept maps, Ishikawa diagrams, Mind Maps and causal diagrams, concluding they are "rarely used at the bedside by clinicians and of limited utility at the bedside". This is the author's narrative assessment of the literature, not an empirical comparison.

> "a wide range of diagrammatic methods are used in health sciences to deepen our understanding of events or assist in learning, yet they are rarely used at the bedside by clinicians and of limited utility at the bedside."
