## Evidence

### Juliette Woodrow 2024

Juliette Woodrow, Ali Malik, and Chris Piech. 2024. AI Teaches the Art of Elegant Coding: Timely, Fair, and Helpful Style Feedback in a Global Course. In Proceedings of the 55th ACM Technical Symposium on Computer Science Education V. 1 (SIGCSE 2024). https://doi.org/10.1145/3626252.3630773

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized control trial in Code in Place with three assigned groups (Delay, Real Time, Real Time With Nudge) over five weeks of a six-week course. The results section reports that "if the feedback is not timely, almost nobody views it" and that nudging plus real-time delivery produced the most viewing (Figure 4). No effect size is printed.

> "We found that if the feedback is not timely, almost nobody views it. We also found that nudging people to view feedback in real time has the highest chance of getting students to look at their feedback."

### Juliette Woodrow 2024 (2)

Juliette Woodrow, Ali Malik, and Chris Piech. 2024. AI Teaches the Art of Elegant Coding: Timely, Fair, and Helpful Style Feedback in a Global Course. In Proceedings of the 55th ACM Technical Symposium on Computer Science Education V. 1 (SIGCSE 2024). https://doi.org/10.1145/3626252.3630773

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Rating behavior in the same RCT: about 30% of the real-time group provided a rating with 97% rating it helpful, while the delay group gave ratings at under 3%. The authors note definitive conclusions cannot be drawn due to the delay group's negligible ratings.

> "The real time group was much more likely to provide a rating for the feedback. About 30% of students in that group provided a rating with 97% rating it helpful. The delay group almost never gave a rating (<3%)."
