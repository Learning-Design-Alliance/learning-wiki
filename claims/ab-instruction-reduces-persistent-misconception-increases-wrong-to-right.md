## Evidence

### Emily M. Reiser and Mark E. Markes 2005

Emily M. Reiser and Mark E. Markes. (2005). An inferential analysis of the effect of activity based physics instruction on the persistent misconceptions of lecture students. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0506208](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0506208)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

FMCE pre/post testing of 87 LB and 64 AB students in six algebra-level sections (1995-1997); Table II reports mean ww probabilities of 0.658 (LB) versus 0.345 (AB) and wr probabilities of 0.127 (LB) versus 0.464 (AB), with the article reporting the differences as "significantly reducing" and "significantly increasing".

> "It can be seen that the most significant differences are found in the wrong to same wrong ( ww ) and the wrong to right ( wr ) categories with AB instruction significantly reducing the probability of a ww response and significantly increasing the probability of wr response."
