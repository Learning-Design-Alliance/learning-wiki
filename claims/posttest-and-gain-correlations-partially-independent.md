## Evidence

### Simonsmeier 2022

Simonsmeier, B. A., Flaig, M., Deiglmayr, A., Schalk, L., & Schneider, M. (2022). Domain-specific prior knowledge and learning: A meta-analysis. Educational Psychologist. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2021.1939700

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Comparison of meta-analytic means for posttest knowledge versus gain-based correlations across 476 vs. 69 studies; the printed effect sizes d = 1.26 (rP vs rNG) and d = 1.16 (rP vs rAG) increased when restricted to the 28 studies reporting all three effect-size types (d = 2.25 and d = 2.71).

> "The correlation rPþ found with posttest knowledge differed statistically significantly from the correlation rNGþ found with normalized knowledge gains, t(482) ¼ 13.80, p < .001 (d ¼ 1.26)."

### Simonsmeier 2022 (2)

Simonsmeier, B. A., Flaig, M., Deiglmayr, A., Schalk, L., & Schneider, M. (2022). Domain-specific prior knowledge and learning: A meta-analysis. Educational Psychologist. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2021.1939700

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Across-study analysis of 238 pairs of correlations computed from the same study, sample, measures, and measurement occasion. The printed effect size r = .216 (p = .001) shows the rP–rNG intercorrelation was weak, so gain correlations cannot be inferred from rP.

> "When the two correlations were Fisher Z-standardized to bring them to a linear scale, their intercorrelation was r ¼ .216, p ¼ .001."

### Simonsmeier 2022 (3)

Simonsmeier, B. A., Flaig, M., Deiglmayr, A., Schalk, L., & Schneider, M. (2022). Domain-specific prior knowledge and learning: A meta-analysis. Educational Psychologist. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2021.1939700

`q3 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Same-study-pairs analysis of the association between rP+ and rAG+ (Figure 5b). The printed effect size r = -.051 (p = .435) was below r = .10 and not statistically significant, so absolute-gain correlations also cannot be inferred from rP.

> "Their intercorrelation was r ¼ -.051, p ¼ .435."
