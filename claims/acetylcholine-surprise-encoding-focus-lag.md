# Acetylcholine gates memory encoding: surprising outcomes drive high release that encodes new memories distinct from prior ones, and focus lags alertness by several minutes

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When outcomes or information are surprising, high acetylcholine release causes the hippocampus to encode new memories based on how they differ from prior memories; unsurprising outcomes lead to retrieval of prior memories instead. [→ Lauren Margulieux 2025](#lauren-margulieux-2025)
`q2 i?` Because alertness must be generated before attention can be focused, there is a 5- to 10-minute period at the start of a task in which focusing attention is difficult, including after failure or uncertainty. [→ Lauren Margulieux 2025 (2)](#lauren-margulieux-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Lauren Margulieux 2025

Lauren Margulieux, James Prather, Masoumeh Rahimi. (2025). The Biological Benefits of Failure on Learning and Tools to Manage the Fallout. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10013-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Review narrative citing Decker and Duncan (2020) on acetylcholine as a gate for learning and pattern differentiation; no effect size printed. The review also cites pharmacological blockade causing memory deficits in young people and cholinergic stimulation improving memory in older people.

> "When outcomes or information are surprising, acetylcholine release is high, causing the hippocam- pus to encode new memories based on how they are distinct from prior memories (Decker & Duncan, 2020)."

### Lauren Margulieux 2025 (2)

Lauren Margulieux, James Prather, Masoumeh Rahimi. (2025). The Biological Benefits of Failure on Learning and Tools to Manage the Fallout. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10013-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Review narrative on the sequencing of (nor)epinephrine-driven alertness before acetylcholine-driven focus, citing Rajkowski et al. (1994) and Usher et al. (1999) on alertness making undifferentiated awareness and movement more likely. No effect size printed.

> "Because learners have to generate alertness before they can focus, there can be a 5- to 10-min period at the start of a task in which focusing attention is difficult."
