# Remembering an SLN or LD label in a student description increases teachers' belief that the diagnosis is correct

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teachers who remembered the SLN or LD label more frequently believed the fictitious student had the respective diagnosis than teachers who read the vignette without a label. [→ Kashikar 2025](#kashikar-2025)

## Evidence

### Kashikar 2025

Kashikar, L., Lüke, T., & Grosche, M. (2025). Effects of Diagnostic Labels for Students With Learning Problems on Teachers' Stereotypes and Performance Expectations. Journal of Learning Disabilities. https://doi.org/10.1177/00222194251315187

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Binomial logistic regression on 413 teachers randomly assigned to label groups; LG2 (remembered SLN, p = .030) and LG3 (remembered LD, p = .031) were significant predictors versus LG0, with "92.63% of the teachers in LG3 assumed the presence of an LD." Teaching profession was not significant.

> "Specifically, in LG0, 73.64% thought the fictitious student had SLN or an LD. In LG2, 89.36% of the teachers assumed the presence of SLN, and 92.63% of the teachers in LG3 assumed the presence of an LD."
