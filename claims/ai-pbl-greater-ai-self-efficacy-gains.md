# AI-supported PBL produced significantly greater gains in AI self-efficacy than traditional PBL, with a very large between-group effect (Cohen's d = 1.78)

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` The AI-PBL group increased AI self-efficacy scores (ΔX̄ = 19.8) while the traditional PBL group declined (ΔX̄ = –3.9), yielding a very large between-group effect (d = 1.78, Welch's t = 5.00, p = 0.00003). [→ Ergezen Sahin Gizem 2025](#ergezen-sahin-gizem-2025)

## Evidence

### Ergezen Sahin Gizem 2025

Ergezen Sahin Gizem, Aras Bayram Gulay, Sanchez Sierra Alberto, Akdemir Simay, Kurc Dogukan, Tarakci Devrim, Tunali Ayse Nur. (2025). Effects of artificial intelligence based physiotherapy educational approach in developing clinical reasoning skills: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07926-w

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Secondary-outcome analysis of the randomized trial using the 22-item AI Self-Efficacy Scale administered at two time points; only the AI-PBLG's within-group gain was significant (d = 1.70, p < 0.000001) while the PBLG's change was non-significant (d = –0.26).

> "The AI-PBLG exhibited a significant increase in self-efficacy scores (ΔX̄=19.8, SD = 11.7), whereas the PBLG group experienced a decrease (ΔX̄=–3.9, SD = 15.0). This contrast resulted in a very large between-group effect size (Cohen’s d = 1.78) and reached a high level of statistical significance (Welch’s t = 5.00 and p = 0.00003)."
