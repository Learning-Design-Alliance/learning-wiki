# Students who solved a symbolic problem incorrectly using conflicting meanings of the same symbol t solved the numeric version correctly, with plugging in numbers acting as a cue to specify symbol meaning

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Two interview subjects canceled the t's in a symbolic derivation, conflating two different times, but both correctly solved the numeric version. [→ Eugene T. Torigoe 2011](#eugene-t-torigoe-2011)

`q2 i?` Two interview subjects used the symbol t, defined as the time to reach the final velocity, as the time to reach half the final velocity, canceling the t's after combining two equations. [→ Eugene T. Torigoe 2015](#eugene-t-torigoe-2015)

`q2 i?` Both students who made the symbolic t-cancellation error were able to correctly solve the numeric version, and plugging in numbers appeared to cue them to specify the variable's meaning. [→ Eugene T. Torigoe 2015 (2)](#eugene-t-torigoe-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Eugene T. Torigoe 2011

Eugene T. Torigoe. (2011). How numbers help students solve physics problems. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1112.3229](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1112.3229)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Speak-aloud interview evidence: two subjects made the t-cancellation error on the symbolic Airliner question, then realized while plugging in numbers that they needed the time to reach 40 m/s (half the final speed). Qualitative, n not stated as an analyzed count beyond the two subjects.

> "Even though they made this error on the symbolic version, both were able to correctly solve the numeric version. The act of plugging in numbers seemed to act as a cue to specify the meaning of the variable they were replacing."

### Eugene T. Torigoe 2015

Eugene T. Torigoe. (2015). Unpacking Symbolic Equations in Introductory Physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1508.00535](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1508.00535)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview study using the Airliner question, an analogous numeric–symbolic pair from the earlier final exam study. Two subjects combined x = xi + vit + ½at2 with a = v/t into L = vt/2, incorrectly canceling t's that denoted two different times.

> "One of the observed errors for this problem was when the students used the symbol t, which is deﬁned as the time to reach the ﬁnal velocity, as the time to reach half the ﬁnal velocity instead. Two interview subjects made this speciﬁc error."

### Eugene T. Torigoe 2015 (2)

Eugene T. Torigoe. (2015). Unpacking Symbolic Equations in Introductory Physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1508.00535](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1508.00535)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Within the same interview study, the two error-making students then solved the numeric Airliner version correctly, realizing while plugging in a time value that they needed the time to reach 40 m/s, half the final speed.

> "Even though they made this error on the symbolic version, both were able to correctly solve the numeric version. The act of plugging in numbers seemed to act as a cue to specify the meaning of the variable they were replacing."
