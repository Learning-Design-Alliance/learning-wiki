# Many advanced students incorrectly predict no beam splitting in an SGE whose field gradient is orthogonal to the spin state, connecting physical-space field direction with Hilbert-space state vectors

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Many first-year graduate and advanced undergraduate students claim there can be no splitting of the beam when the magnetic field gradient is in the −x direction but the spin state is along z, apparently because they connect the field direction in physical space with the direction of state vectors in Hilbert space. [→ Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#guangtian-zhu-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)

## Evidence

### Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Students’ Understanding of Stern Gerlach Experiment. arXiv:1602.06374 [physics.ed-ph]. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06374](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06374)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Written surveys of more than two hundred physics graduate students and advanced undergraduates in quantum mechanics courses, plus think-aloud interviews with a subset, included an SGE question with a −x field gradient and |↑⟩z input. The authors report that "Many students claimed that there cannot be any splitting of the beam" and that responses and interviews "suggest that they were incorrectly connecting the direction of the magnetic ﬁeld in the physical space with the “direction" of state vectors in the Hilbert space."

> "Many students claimed that there cannot be any splitting of the beam since the magnetic ﬁeld gradient is in the −x direction but the spin state is along the z direction and they are orthogonal to each other"
