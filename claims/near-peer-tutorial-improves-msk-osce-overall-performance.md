# A single near-peer-led OSCE-style tutorial significantly improves overall OSCE performance in upper limb musculoskeletal examination among pre-clinical medical students

`q2 i?` Overall average OSCE performance improved significantly from 63.8 ± 15.8% pre-test to 76.3 ± 10.5% post-test (p = 0.01) following a single near-peer-led tutorial. [→ Qian Kevin 2026](#qian-kevin-2026)

## Evidence

### Qian Kevin 2026

Qian Kevin, Malau-Aduli Bunmi, Sundaraj Keran. (2026). Enhancing musculoskeletal examination skills through near-peer teaching: student outcomes and perspectives. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09235-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental pre-post study with 23 second-year medical students completing a pre-test OSCE, a near-peer-led tutorial, and a post-test OSCE three days later. Paired samples t-test showed a statistically significant overall improvement from "63.8 ± 15.8 vs. post-test: 76.3 ± 10.5, p = 0.01". No effect size was reported.

> "The overall average performance of the participant cohort also observed a statistically significant improvement (pre-test: 63.8 ± 15.8 vs. post-test: 76.3 ± 10.5, p = 0.01)."
