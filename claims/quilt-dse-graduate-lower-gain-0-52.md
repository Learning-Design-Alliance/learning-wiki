# First-year physics graduate students improved less than undergraduates (pre 44% to post 73%, normalized gain 0.52), possibly due to lack of grade incentives

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` First-year graduate students in a TA training course improved from 44% on the pre-test to 73% on the post-test (p<0.001) with a normalized gain of 0.52, less than the undergraduates. [→ Sayer 2015](#sayer-2015)

## Evidence

### Sayer 2015

Sayer, R., Maries, A., & Singh, C. (2015). Developing and evaluating a tutorial on the double-slit experiment. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.070](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.070)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Evaluation of the QuILT with first-year physics graduate students in a mandatory TA training course (N=45). Table III reports G pre 44%, post 73%, p<0.001, normalized gain 0.52. Graduate work was graded for completeness, not correctness (pass/fail course).

> "graduate students performed better than undergraduates in  the pre -test, while the undergraduates performed better on the post -test."

### Sayer 2015 (2)

Sayer, R., Maries, A., & Singh, C. (2015). Developing and evaluating a tutorial on the double-slit experiment. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.070](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.070)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r3`

The authors' interpretation of the graduate-undergraduate post-test discrepancy: "the undergraduates were given grade incentives" while graduates had "a pass/fail grading scheme". This is an author explanation, not a tested result.

> "one possible reason  for the post -test score discrepancy is that, as noted earlier, the undergraduates were given grade incentives to learn from the QuILT while the graduate students were given the QuILT in a TA training course with no final exam and a pass/fail grading scheme."
