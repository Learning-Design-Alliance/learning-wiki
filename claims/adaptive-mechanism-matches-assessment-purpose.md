# The choice of adaptive assessment mechanism should match the assessment's purpose: IRT for time-limited diagnostic tests, MAB for learning-gain formative assessment

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Which adaptive mechanism should be adopted depends on the purpose of the formative assessment: an IRT approach suits a time-limited diagnostic test, while a reinforcement learning algorithm such as MAB suits unit formative assessment where knowledge may change during the test. [→ Hongxin Yan 2021](#hongxin-yan-2021)

## Evidence

### Hongxin Yan 2021

Hongxin Yan, Fuhua Lin & Kinshuk. (2021). Including Learning Analytics in the Loop of Self-Paced Online Course Learning Design. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education (2021) 31:878–895. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-020-00225-z

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

The authors' design argument: for a time-limited diagnostic test students' knowledge will unlikely change, so IRT is appropriate; for unit formative assessment promoting learning gain, where knowledge could change during the test, a MAB-based algorithm is a better solution.

> "Which adaptive mechanism should be adopted will depend on the purposes of the formative assessment. For example, a diagnostic test at the beginning of the course can be used to quickly estimate students’prior-knowledge."
