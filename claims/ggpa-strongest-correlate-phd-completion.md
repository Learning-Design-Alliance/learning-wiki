## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among the bivariate correlations with final disposition, graduate GPA shows the strongest association with PhD completion. [→ Mike Verostek 2021](#mike-verostek-2021)

## Evidence

### Mike Verostek 2021

Mike Verostek, Casey Miller and Benjamin Zwickl. (2021). Analyzing admissions metrics as predictors of graduate GPA and whether graduate GPA mediates PhD completion. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020115](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020115)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bivariate point-biserial correlation analysis of US students' records (Table II). The article reports GGPA correlated with final disposition at "rpb = 0.43", significant at p < .001, stronger than UGPA, GRE-P, or GRE-Q correlations with completion.

> "Inspection of Table II reveals that GGPA is the predictor most strongly correlated with ﬁnal disposition (rpb = 0.43). This value is statistically signiﬁcant (p<. 001) and positive, meaning that students with higher GGPA are more likely to ﬁnish their PhD program successfully."
