Evidence¶

Condelli 2010¶

Condelli, L., Cronen, S., Bos, J., Tseng, F., and Altuna, J. (2010). The Impact of a Reading Intervention for Low-Literate Adult ESL Learners—Executive Summary (NCEE 2011-4004). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

Impact analysis of pre/post assessments (N = 580 Sam and Pat, 557 control) in the randomized evaluation. Gains were "equivalent to 1 to 2 months of growth on the reading assessments, and 5 to 6 months of growth on the English language assessments", though the report cautions months-of-growth interpretations due to norming population differences.

"On average, students participating in the study made statistically significant gains in reading and English language skills over the course of the term (effect sizes of 0.23 to 0.40)."

Condelli 2010 (2)¶

Condelli, L., Cronen, S., Bos, J., Tseng, F., and Altuna, J. (2010). The Impact of a Reading Intervention for Low-Literate Adult ESL Learners—Executive Summary (NCEE 2011-4004). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Randomized impact comparison of Sam and Pat versus control groups at end of term on seven reading and English language outcome measures; no impacts were statistically significant at the 0.05 level. Equivalence was not tested.

"However, there were no statistically significant impacts of Sam and Pat on the reading and English language outcomes measured for the overall sample (Figure ES.2). Effect sizes ranged from -0.06 to 0.01."

Related Claims¶

  • Sam and Pat produced no statistically significant impacts on reading or English language outcomes for the overall sample of low-literate adult ESL learners — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Low-literate adult ESL learners in the study made statistically significant gains in reading and English language skills over one term, with effect sizes of 0.23 to 0.40 — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Sam and Pat classrooms devoted far more observed time to reading development instruction and far less to English language acquisition instruction than control classrooms — related
  • Sam and Pat showed no statistically significant impacts for subgroups defined by native-language background or study cohort — related
  • About two-thirds of observed Sam and Pat classes met the study's instructional fidelity criteria for using the materials and delivering reading instruction — related
  • Control classrooms spent significantly more time on English language acquisition instruction than Sam and Pat classrooms (68 percent vs. 27 percent) — related
  • Exposure to English language instruction was positively and exposure to reading instruction negatively correlated with ROWPVT scores, with very small standardized coefficients — related
  • Sam and Pat showed no impacts for five of six subgroups, with a suggestive but non-significant positive effect on word attack decoding for lower-literacy students (effect size = 0.16) — related
