## Evidence

### Leung-Gagné 2022

Leung-Gagné, M., McCombs, J., Scott, C., & Losen, D. J. (2022). Pushed out: Trends and disparities in out-of-school suspension. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/235.277

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The report's review of prior research on suspension and school climate, citing controlled studies. It states that "both students and teachers tend to perceive school safety to be worse" in higher-suspending schools, and that such schools showed the largest decreases in school connectedness and meaningful participation.

> "In schools with higher suspension rates, both students and teachers tend to perceive school safety to be worse, after controlling for various school and student characteristics."

### Leung-Gagné 2022 (2)

Leung-Gagné, M., McCombs, J., Scott, C., & Losen, D. J. (2022). Pushed out: Trends and disparities in out-of-school suspension. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/235.277

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The report's review of research on spillover effects, reporting that one study found "a relationship between harsher exclusionary punishments and lower test scores among peers of suspended students." The report also cites a Texas study in which suspended students with minor violations were twice as likely to repeat a grade.

> "One study found a relationship between harsher exclusionary punishments and lower test scores among peers of suspended students."
