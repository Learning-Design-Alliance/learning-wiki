## Evidence

### Opitz Sebastian 2020

Opitz Sebastian, Harms Ute. (2020). Assessing High Performers in the Life Sciences: Characteristics of Exams Used at the International Biology Olympiad (IBO) and Their Implications for Life Science Education. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-10-0215

`q2 · i3` · `qualitative · r3`

Practice coding across six IBO cohorts with ANOVA; Analyzing and Using Data was "the practice most widely spread" (55% average) and became "approximately three times more prevalent" in theory items (F(5, 618) = 37.84, p < 0.001, η² = 0.23).

> "Analyzing and Using Data was the practice most widely spread among IBO theoretical and practical items (average: 55%; respectively 57% of items across IBOs), and it was also the one with the most pronounced change over time. Among theoretical items, this practice become approximately three times more prevalent from the 1990s to 2017 (93%, F(5, 618) = 37.84, p < 0.001, η² = 0.23)."

### Opitz Sebastian 2020 (2)

Opitz Sebastian, Harms Ute. (2020). Assessing High Performers in the Life Sciences: Characteristics of Exams Used at the International Biology Olympiad (IBO) and Their Implications for Life Science Education. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.19-10-0215

`q2 · i2` · `design · r3`

ANOVA of practice codes shows Developing and Using Models rose from <1% to ca. 20% (F(5, 618) = 11.3, p < 0.001, η² = 0.08), but items involved "students' application of provided models"; the article notes model development, critique, and revision were not addressed.

> "The practice Developing and Using Models appeared rarely, but with increasing frequency (theory exams 1990s: <1%; IBO 2017: ca. 20%, F(5, 618) = 11.3, p < 0.001, η² = 0.08). However, this practice was largely connected to theoretical exams and, more importantly, to students' application of provided models."
