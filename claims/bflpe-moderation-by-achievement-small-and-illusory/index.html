Evidence¶

Herbert W. Marsh 2026¶

Herbert W. Marsh. (2026). Four Decades After the Big-Fish–Little-Pond Effect: Critical Boundary Tests and Integrative Models of Mean-Context, Ordinal-Standing, and Distributional-Scaling in 46 Countries. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10200-0

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Multilevel Model 5 (L1, L2, L1², L1 × L2) in the TIMSS 2019 sample: the interaction was "small and slightly negative (β = −0.052, SE = 0.006)", providing little evidence that the contextual effect weakens at higher achievement levels.

"In the fully specified curvature-adjusted model (Model 5), the interaction was small and slightly negative (β = −0.052, SE = 0.006). Figure 1D shows that the contextual-effect profiles remained close to parallel across levels of individual achievement."

Herbert W. Marsh 2026 (2)¶

Herbert W. Marsh. (2026). Four Decades After the Big-Fish–Little-Pond Effect: Critical Boundary Tests and Integrative Models of Mean-Context, Ordinal-Standing, and Distributional-Scaling in 46 Countries. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10200-0

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Model 4 (without L1²) in the same sample produced "an apparently moderate positive interaction (β = 0.153, SE = 0.003)"; the article attributes this to illusory moderation arising from omitted nonlinear terms.

"The importance of modeling curvature is illustrated by Model 4, where omission of the quadratic term produced an apparently moderate positive interaction (β = 0.153, SE = 0.003) and the fan-shaped pattern visible in Fig. 1C. Once the quadratic term was included, however, the apparent moderation largely disappeared."
