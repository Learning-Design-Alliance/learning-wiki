# Response times in number comparison show group, notation, and ratio main effects, with a larger ratio effect for dot arrays than Arabic numerals

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Adults responded faster than children, Arabic numerals faster than dot arrays, and 0.5 ratio trials faster than 0.8 ratio trials. [→ Cantlon Jessica F. 2009](#cantlon-jessica-f-2009)

## Evidence

### Cantlon Jessica F. 2009

Cantlon Jessica F., Libertus Melissa E., Pinel Philippe, Dehaene Stanislas, Brannon Elizabeth M., Pelphrey Kevin A. (2009). The Neural Development of an Abstract Concept of Number. Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn.2008.21159

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Response-time ANOVA from the behavioral results of the same fMRI experiment. The article reports "Adults performed significantly faster than children (1041 vs. 723 msec)" and a dot arrays ratio effect of 112 msec versus 13 msec for Arabic numerals.

> "An ANOVA with factors of Group (children, adults) × Notation (Arabic, dots) × Ratio (0.5, 0.8) with a repeated measure of response time revealed main effects of group [F(1, 52) = 74.8, p < .001], notation [F(1, 52) = 9.01, p < .001], and ratio [F(1, 52) = 38.14, p < .001]."
