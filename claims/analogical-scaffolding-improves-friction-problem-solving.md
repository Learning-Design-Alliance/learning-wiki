## Evidence

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Using Analogical Problem Solving with Different Scaffolding Supports to Learn about Friction. Originally published 2008. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06390](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06390)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Recitation-quiz study in which calculus-based and algebra-based introductory physics students were divided into a comparison group and two intervention groups receiving different scaffolding supports around a solved isomorphic tension problem. The article reports that interventions 1 and 2 provided good scaffolding for calculus-based students and that Chi-square tests indicated the differences between intervention and comparison groups were significant.

> "Tables 1 and 2 show that interventions 1 and 2 provided good scaffolding in helping calculus-based students solve the friction problem correctly, while intervention 1 was best in the algebra-based course."
