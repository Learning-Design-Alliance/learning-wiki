# Content-retention scores rose from initial exam to final exam among students who initially failed to master microbiology course material and mastered it during oral examination retesting

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students who initially did not master content but mastered it during an oral examination (U to S group) significantly increased their scores on those questions from initial exam to final exam (P < 0.001). [→ St James Andrew R 2026](#st-james-andrew-r-2026)

## Evidence

### St James Andrew R 2026

St James Andrew R, Widener Camille. (2026). Oral examination as a tool for mastery learning in undergraduate microbiology. FEMS Microbiology Letters. https://doi.org/10.1093/femsle/fnag010

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Single-course quasi-experimental analysis of 20 students in an advanced undergraduate microbiology course, comparing initial exam and final exam scores on matched questions using paired t-tests. The U to S group "significantly increased (P < 0.001)"; no effect size is printed.

> "Averaged across four modules, scores on student answers to questions in the S group decreased (P < 0.001), whereas scores on student answers to questions in the U to S group significantly increased (P < 0.001)."
