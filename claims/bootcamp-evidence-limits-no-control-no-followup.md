# Evidence for bootcamp benefits is limited by absent control groups, self-reported subjective measures and lack of long-term follow-up

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The study lacked a control group, a small single-location sample, subjective self-reported outcomes, and no follow-up over time, limiting conclusions about clinical proficiency and long-term benefit. [→ Rai Amar 2024](#rai-amar-2024)

## Evidence

### Rai Amar 2024

Rai Amar, Shukla Shivani, Mehtani Nikita, Acharya Vikas, Tolley Neil. (2024). Does a junior doctor focused 'Bootcamp' improve the confidence and preparedness of newly appointed ENT registrars to perform their job roles? BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05691-w

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Authors' stated limitations: no control group; a "small sample size from a singular location"; formal performance assessment was avoided, so results are "subjective and self-reported"; and trainees were not followed up, preventing assessment of long-term improvements. No effect sizes are printed.

> "Limitations within the study design exist, for example the lack of a control group for the comparison of results which reduces internal validity."
