# Mothers' and fathers' education polygenic scores are unexpectedly negatively associated with maternal positive and literacy-focused parenting

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Mothers' PGSedu were negatively associated with maternal positive (β = −0.10, p < .001) and literacy-focused (β = −0.05, p < .001) parenting, contrary to theories that higher parental education encourages positive parenting. [→ Austerberry 2026](#austerberry-2026)

## Evidence

### Austerberry 2026

Austerberry, C., Zayats, T., Ronald, A., Corfield, E., Smajlagic, D., Havdahl, A., Andreassen, O. A., Magnus, P., Njølstad, P. R., Bekkhus, M., & Fearon, P. (2026). Evocative effects of children's education‐associated genetics on maternal parenting: results from the Norwegian mother, father and child cohort study. Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry, 67(2), 158–170. https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.70025

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Trio SEM (Figure 1C) in the MoBa sample; mothers' and fathers' PGSedu both negatively predicted maternal positive parenting. Negative associations run counter to prior studies (Wertz et al., 2023) reporting warmer, more stimulating parenting among higher-PGSedu parents.

> "parent PGSedu were significantly (unexpectedly, negatively) associated with maternal positive parenting (PGSmother: β = −0.10, 95% CI [−0.12, −0.07], p < .001; PGSfather: β = −0.04, 95% CI [−0.07, −0.01], p = .009)"
