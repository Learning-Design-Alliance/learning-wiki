Evidence¶

Lucia Patino Melo 2026¶

Lucia Patino Melo, Sebastian Wallot, Keerat Grewal, Matthew Moreno, Osamu Nomura, Jason M. Harley. (2026). Physiological synchrony amongst medical residents during crisis management simulation training and a video-based assessment of leaders' performance. Learning and Instruction 102 (2026) 102302. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102302

q2 · i3 · associational · r1

Wilcoxon signed-rank test comparing 9 teams' real pre-simulation %REC against randomized surrogate datasets from a field-based observational EDA study. Real teams showed "higher %REC than surrogate data" with "r = 0.89: large effect"; paired t-tests in supplemental material replicated the result.

"the Wilcoxon signed-rank test result of the pre-simulation window analysis revealed that there was a statistically significant difference between surrogate %REC and real data %REC PreSim values of all teams, with the real teams exhibiting higher %REC than surrogate data (Surrogate %REC and real %REC: W = 2.66, p = .008, r = 0.89: large effect)."

Lucia Patino Melo 2026 (2)¶

Physiological synchrony amongst medical residents during crisis management simulation training and a video-based assessment of leaders' performance. Learning and Instruction 102 (2026) 102302. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102302

q2 · i3 · associational · r2

Wilcoxon signed-rank test on during-simulation %REC for the same 9 teams against surrogate datasets. Real teams again showed "higher %REC than surrogate data" with "r = 0.89: large effect", supporting PS during the scenarios themselves.

"the Wilcoxon signed-rank test result of the simulation window analysis revealed that there was a statistically significant difference between surrogate and real data %REC Sim values of all teams, with the real teams exhibiting higher %REC than surrogate data (Surrogate %REC and real %REC: W = 2.66, p = .008, r = 0.89: large ef- fect)."
