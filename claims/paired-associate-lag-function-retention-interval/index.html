Evidence¶

Glenberg 1979¶

Glenberg, A. M. (1979). Component-levels theory of the effects of spacing of repetitions on recall and recognition. Memory & Cognition, 7(2), 95-112. https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03197590

q2 · i? · causal · r?

The review attributes these paired-associate lag-function shapes to Peterson, Hillner, and Saltzman (1962), Peterson et al. (1963), and Glenberg (1976), interpreting distributed practice effects in this paradigm as based on descriptive components. No effect sizes are printed.

"At very short retention intervals, the lag funetion is deereasing (Peterson, Hillner, & Saltzman, 1962). With slightly longer retention intervals, the lag function is an inverted U-shaped function (Peterson et al., 1963). Finally, with long retention intervals, the funetion is monotonically inereasing (Glenberg, 1976)."
