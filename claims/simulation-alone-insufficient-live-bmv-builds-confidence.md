# Training in resuscitation skills in a simulated environment alone is not enough to change clinical practice; witnessing successful bag-and-mask ventilation in live resuscitation builds confidence and changes beliefs and behaviors

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Midwives reported that simulated mannikin practice alone teaches skill but does not build confidence, whereas witnessing live successful resuscitation changed beliefs and clinical practice. [→ Becker J 2022](#becker-j-2022)

## Evidence

### Becker J 2022

Becker J, Becker C, Oprescu F, Wu C-J, Moir J, Shimwela M, Gray M. (2022). Silent voices of the midwives: factors that influence midwives' achievement of successful neonatal resuscitation in sub-Saharan Africa: a narrative inquiry. BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12884-021-04339-7

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Theme analysis from the narrative interviews: midwives described live practice as superior to mannikin practice; one stated "the dummy doesn't give confidence. It teaches a skill." Qualitative findings only; no effect sizes printed.

> "Witnessing and/or partaking in successful resuscitations directly (hands-on with full peer support) built confidence, especially when midwives observed superior results and outcomes of BMV."
