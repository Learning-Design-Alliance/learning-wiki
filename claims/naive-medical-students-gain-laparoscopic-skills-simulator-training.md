# Medical students naive to laparoscopy had lower laparoscopic task times and higher scores from pre-test to post-test after a 150-minute simulator training program, in one single-group pre-post study

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In one group of 68 students, laparoscopic task times fell and scores rose from pre-test to post-test after training, with statistically significant pre-post differences. [→ Pereira CWL 2014](#pereira-cwl-2014)

## Evidence

### Pereira CWL 2014

Pereira CWL, Paggi CCM, Daniellson D, Filho CAM, Aimoré BE, de Paula LM, Salvalaggio P. (2014). Development of laparoscopic skills in Medical students naive to surgical training. Einstein (Sao Paulo). https://doi.org/10.1590/S1679-45082014AO3237

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Prospective longitudinal pre-post study of 68 first- and second-year medical students with no prior surgical exposure, trained 150 minutes on a box simulator in two sessions one week apart. The article reports "All students improved performance in score and time, after training (p<0,001)" using the Wilcoxon test.

> "Sixty-eight students participated of the study, with a mean age of 20.4 years, with a predominance of first-year students (62%). All students improved performance in score and time, after training (p<0,001)."

### Pereira CWL 2014 (2)

Pereira CWL, Paggi CCM, Daniellson D, Filho CAM, Aimoré BE, de Paula LM, Salvalaggio P. (2014). Development of laparoscopic skills in Medical students naive to surgical training. Einstein (Sao Paulo). https://doi.org/10.1590/S1679-45082014AO3237

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Conversion of pre-post times into FLS-based scores showed exercise-dependent gains; the article states "a variation in improvement from 294.1 to 823%, depending on the exercise", with every score significant at p<0,001. No effect sizes were printed.

> "There was a variation in improvement from 294.1 to 823%, depending on the exercise. Every score had a p<0,001."
