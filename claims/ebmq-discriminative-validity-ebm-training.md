# The EBMQ discriminated between doctors with and without EBM training for 24 of 42 items

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Doctors with EBM training scored significantly higher than those without training on knowledge, practice and barriers items, discriminating on 24 of 42 items. [→ Hisham Ranita 2018](#hisham-ranita-2018)

## Evidence

### Hisham Ranita 2018

Hisham Ranita, Ng Chirk Jenn, Liew Su May, Lai Pauline Siew Mei, Chia Yook Chin, Khoo Ee Ming, Hanafi Nik Sherina, Othman Sajaratulnisah, Lee Ping Yein, Abdullah Khatijah Lim, Chinna Karuthan. (2018). Development and validation of the Evidence Based Medicine Questionnaire (EBMQ) to assess doctors’ knowledge, practice and barriers regarding the implementation of evidence-based medicine in primary care. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-018-0779-5

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Chi-square comparisons of 222 doctors with EBM training against 98 without, using baseline EBMQ data. The article reports significant higher scores for trained doctors in the knowledge and practice domains, with the two groups named and compared item by item.

> "In the “knowledge” domain, doctors who had EBM training had significant higher scores in 13 out of 24 items compared to those without training. In the “practice” domain, doctors who had EBM training had significant higher scores in 5 out 8 items compared to those without training."

### Hisham Ranita 2018 (2)

Hisham Ranita, Ng Chirk Jenn, Liew Su May, Lai Pauline Siew Mei, Chia Yook Chin, Khoo Ee Ming, Hanafi Nik Sherina, Othman Sajaratulnisah, Lee Ping Yein, Abdullah Khatijah Lim, Chinna Karuthan. (2018). Development and validation of the Evidence Based Medicine Questionnaire (EBMQ) to assess doctors’ knowledge, practice and barriers regarding the implementation of evidence-based medicine in primary care. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-018-0779-5

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

In the same discriminative validity analysis, the knowledge subdomain on information sources showed no significant differences between the two named groups; the authors attribute this to untrained doctors still accessing online information resources.

> "No differences was found between those with and without EBM training in “information sources related to EBM” as those who did not attend EBM training could still access online information resources."
