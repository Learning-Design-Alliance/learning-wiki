# Both tutoring conditions improve procedural calculation relative to no tutoring, with no difference between them

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` NC tutoring improved procedural calculation versus control (d = 0.27), which the article treats as transfer from NC remediation. [→ Fuchs 2009](#fuchs-2009)
`q3 i2` WP tutoring improved procedural calculation versus control (d = 0.53), with no difference between the two tutoring groups. [→ Fuchs 2009 (2)](#fuchs-2009-2)

## Evidence

### Fuchs 2009

Fuchs, L. S., Powell, S. R., Seethaler, P. M., Cirino, P. T., Fletcher, J. M., Fuchs, D., Hamlett, C. L., & Zumeta, R. O. (2009). Remediating Number Combination and Word Problem Deficits Among Students With Mathematics Difficulties: A Randomized Control Trial. Journal of Educational Psychology, 101(3), 561-576. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0014701

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Posttest three-way analysis of covariance (treatment, difficulty status, site; pretest covariate) on the procedural calculation factor score in the randomized trial. "Students in NC tutoring (p < .007, d = 0.27)" outperformed control students.

> "Students in NC tutoring (p < .007, d = 0.27) and WP tutoring (p < .0001, d = 0.53) outperformed control students, but students in the two tutoring groups did not differ."
