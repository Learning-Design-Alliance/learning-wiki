# Changing the audio default from opt-in to opt-out raised audio availability from 72% to 99% of assignments and student audio use from 68% to 84%

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Changing the default so teachers could disable rather than had to enable audio increased availability from 72 percent to 99 percent of assignments and the proportion of students playing audio from 68 percent to 84 percent. [→ Tare 2019](#tare-2019)

## Evidence

### Tare 2019

Tare, M. & Shell, A. R. (2019). Designing for learner variability: Examining the impact of research-based edtech in the classroom. Washington, DC: Digital Promise Global. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/82839c44-adb1-400c-b422-9bfbbca65985

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Platform-level manipulation conducted with ReadWorks for users in the target district: the audio default was changed so teachers no longer had to actively enable it but could disable it. The report calls this its experimental study of audio supports and concludes that when audio was available to more students, more students used it.

> "This change in the default setting resulted in an increase in audio support availability from 72 percent to 99 percent of assignments. More students also made use of the feature, with the proportion of students who played audio in any of their assignments rising from 68 percent to 84 percent."
