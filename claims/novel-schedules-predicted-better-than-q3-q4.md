# Mathematical modeling predicts that three novel schedules eliminating extended shifts yield fewer hours of poor performance and alertness than traditional Q3 or Q4 schedules, especially during night-time work hours

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Simulation predictions show fewer hours with poor performance and alertness for all three novel schedules (RCR, XC-V1, XC-V2) than for traditional Q3 or Q4 schedules, especially during night-time work hours. [→ Klerman Elizabeth B. 2016](#klerman-elizabeth-b-2016)
`q2 i?` During night work hours, 10% of time was spent at performance values less than 40 for the Q3 and Q4 schedules but approximately 0% of the time for the three intervention schedules. [→ Klerman Elizabeth B. 2016 (2)](#klerman-elizabeth-b-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Klerman Elizabeth B. 2016

Klerman Elizabeth B., Beckett Scott A., Landrigan Christopher P. (2016). Applying mathematical models to predict resident physician performance and alertness on traditional and novel work schedules. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0751-9

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Numerical simulation using Circadian Performance Simulation Software compared two traditional (Q3, Q4) and three novel schedules over 28 days. The results section reports that "the three intervention schedules have much less time spent at these relatively low performance and alertness values" during night work.

> "The major difference in predicted performance and alertness between the schedules is during night work; all group schedules spend more time at lower values, but the three intervention schedules have much less time spent at these relatively low performance and alertness values"

### Klerman Elizabeth B. 2016 (2)

Klerman Elizabeth B., Beckett Scott A., Landrigan Christopher P. (2016). Applying mathematical models to predict resident physician performance and alertness on traditional and novel work schedules. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0751-9

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Simulation results quantify the night-work contrast: "10 % of time was spent at performance values less than 40 for the Q3 and Q4 schedules" versus approximately 0% for RCR, XC1 and XC2. No effect size statistic is printed.

> "10 % of time was spent at performance values less than 40 for the Q3 and Q4 schedules but ~0 % of the time was at performance values less than 40 for the three intervention schedules of RCR, XC1 and XC2"
