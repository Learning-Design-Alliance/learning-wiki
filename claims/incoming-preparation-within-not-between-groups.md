# Students' incoming FCI and calculus understanding predict within-group grade differences but do not explain between-group demographic gaps, making a student deficit model untenable for these data

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Correcting for FCI pre-scores and introductory math grades does not reduce the demographic gap to zero and may have enlarged it in the concepts-first class. [→ David J. Webb 2021](#david-j-webb-2021)

## Evidence

### David J. Webb 2021

David J. Webb. (2021). Addendum to Concepts First Paper: A Student Deﬁcit Model is Untenable in Understanding a Demographic Grade Gap. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2109.09240](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2109.09240)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

HLM analysis of 633 students' FCI pre-scores, normalized calculus grades and final exam scores. The article states the student-level variables are "excellent predictors" of within-group differences, yet controlling for them "does not explain (i.e. reduce to zero) the demographic gap."

> "Nevertheless, correcting for these student-level variables not only does not explain (i.e. reduce to zero) the demographic gap but may have enlarged the demographic gap in the concepts-ﬁrst class."

### David J. Webb 2021 (2)

David J. Webb. (2021). Addendum to Concepts First Paper: A Student Deﬁcit Model is Untenable in Understanding a Demographic Grade Gap. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2109.09240](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2109.09240)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The author's conclusion from the HLM fits reported in Results: the same student-level variables that work within groups fail as between-group predictors of the demographic grade gap in these data.

> "So using these particular student-level variables as between-group predictors is untenable for these data."
