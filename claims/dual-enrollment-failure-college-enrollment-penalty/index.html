Evidence¶

Terrell 2026¶

Terrell, J. H., Mathias, J., Blanchard, A., & Roldan-Rueda, D. (2026). The arc of dual enrollment: Access, struggle, and what makes success possible. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED681344

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Statistical matching of students who enrolled and did not pass against students who enrolled and passed, in the CALPADS statewide data. The authors state matching "cannot fully overcome the selection and omitted variable bias in this case," so the estimate is associational. Only the printed percentage range is reported; no effect size.

"11–12 percentage point reduction in college enrollment probability for students who do not pass dual enrollment, compared to students who pass, after controlling for prior academic preparation and demographics"

Terrell 2026 (2)¶

Terrell, J. H., Mathias, J., Blanchard, A., & Roldan-Rueda, D. (2026). The arc of dual enrollment: Access, struggle, and what makes success possible. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED681344

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Authors' subgroup analysis of CALPADS administrative data, cohorts 2015-16 through 2018-19, finding no statistically significant difference in the failure penalty between SED and non-SED students; equivalence was not formally tested and no effect size is printed.

"the penalty is consistent in magnitude across student groups—the difference between students from low-income and first-generation backgrounds and their peers is not statistically significant"
