## Evidence

### Zavlin D 2017

Zavlin D, Jubbal KT, Noé JG, Gansbacher B. (2017). A comparison of medical education in Germany and the United States: from applying to medical school to the beginnings of residency. GMS German Medical Science, Vol. 15, Doc15. [doi:10.3205/000256](https://doi.org/10.3205/000256)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review section on the US residency match. The review reports that most graduates pursue residency through the National Residency Matching Program, established in 1952, which "standardizes the entire application process across nearly all specialties"; urology and ophthalmology use separate matches (AUA and SF Match) while still using NRMP for preliminary years.

> "This program standardizes the entire application process across nearly all specialties and somewhat mitigates the bureaucratic difficulties for foreign medical graduates."

### Zavlin D 2017 (2)

Zavlin D, Jubbal KT, Noé JG, Gansbacher B. (2017). A comparison of medical education in Germany and the United States: from applying to medical school to the beginnings of residency. GMS German Medical Science, Vol. 15, Doc15. [doi:10.3205/000256](https://doi.org/10.3205/000256)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review section on German postgraduate training. The review reports that German residents "apply independently at various accredited institutions", that residencies "last a minimum of 48 to 72 months depending on specialty", and that a residency can commence at any time of year when a position is available.

> "Residencies in general last a minimum of 48 to 72 months depending on specialty."
