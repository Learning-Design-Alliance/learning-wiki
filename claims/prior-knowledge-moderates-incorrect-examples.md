## Evidence

### Barbieri 2016

Barbieri, C. & Booth, J.L. (2016). Support for struggling students in algebra: Contributions of incorrect worked examples. Learning and Individual Differences, 48, 36–44. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22Support+for+struggling+students+in+algebra%22+Barbieri+Booth+incorrect+worked+examples. [doi:10.1016/j.lindif.2016.04.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2016.04.001)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multiple regression predicting post-test scores from pre-test, demographics, Time 1 motivation, and condition dummies with centered pre-test interactions; the Pre-Overall x Incorrect interaction was significant, with ΔR² = .023 over the main effects model.

> "This moderation model revealed a significant Prior Knowledge by Incorrect condition interaction (β = -.182, p = .045)."

### Barbieri 2016 (2)

Barbieri, C. & Booth, J.L. (2016). Support for struggling students in algebra: Contributions of incorrect worked examples. Learning and Individual Differences, 48, 36–44. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22Support+for+struggling+students+in+algebra%22+Barbieri+Booth+incorrect+worked+examples. [doi:10.1016/j.lindif.2016.04.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2016.04.001)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Follow-up split regressions by condition controlling for demographics and Time 1 motivation showed prior knowledge predicted post-test scores in the control and correct examples groups but not in the incorrect examples group; the authors conclude low prior knowledge students benefited more from incorrect examples.

> "it did not predict post-test scores for the incorrect examples group (β = .039, p = .793)."
