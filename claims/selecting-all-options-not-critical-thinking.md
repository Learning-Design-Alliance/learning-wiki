# Selecting all available options on a multiple-response assessment does not represent critical thinking, but limiting selections forces prioritization

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Three students selected all options that resonated with them regardless of priority, behavior the authors say does not represent critical thinking; urging students to select no more than three options forced them to be critical and prioritize their answers. [→ Katherine N. Quinn 2018](#katherine-n-quinn-2018)

## Evidence

### Katherine N. Quinn 2018

Katherine N. Quinn, Carl Wieman, and N.G. Holmes. (2018). Interview validation of the Physics Lab Inventory of Critical thinking (PLIC). [doi:10.1119/perc.2017.pr.076](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.076)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview observation of three students who selected almost all options, one remarking "these are all good ideas". Because PLIC options are all in some way correct, the authors judged this non-critical; the PLIC was modified to allow no more than three answers per question and to notify students when they select too many options.

> "When urged by the interviewer to select no more than three options after this behavior was identified during the interview, students were forced to be critical of their choices and prioritizing their answers."
