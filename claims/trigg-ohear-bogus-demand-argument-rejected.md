# Trigg and O'Hear's argument that the justification demand is bogus is mistaken

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q1` argument or single case

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Trigg and O'Hear have not shown that reasons justifying rationality must be viciously circular or question-begging; their rejection of the justification demand fails. [→ Siegel 1989](#siegel-1989)

## Evidence

### Siegel 1989

Siegel, Harvey. (1989). Why Be Rational? On Thinking Critically about Critical Thinking. Resource Publication, Series 2 No. 1. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED352333

`q1 · i?`

Philosophical counter-argument. Siegel argues the claimed impossibility of non-circular justification can only be established by considering candidate answers, and that showing a justification impossible shows only that a position lacks rational justification, not that the demand is illegitimate.

> "Fortunately, they are not. They havenot shown that reasons which justifyrationality must be viciously circularor question-begging."
