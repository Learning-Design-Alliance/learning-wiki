# A declarative task placed early in training is associated with a small, transient reduction in post-rest gain (Experiment 3), with only weak statistical support

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Experiment 3, the cued-recall group showed a significantly smaller post-rest gain than the vowel group (d = -0.28), but the Bayes factor indicated only weak evidence and the effect resolved by the end of post-test. ## Evidence

### Mohan W. Gupta and Timothy C. Rickard 2025

Mohan W. Gupta and Timothy C. Rickard. (2025). The Effects of Declarative Learning on Early and Late Motor Skill Learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00386-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 3: 105 undergraduates; intervening task moved to after the first three training trials (15 sequences) with five sequences per trial and 30 s breaks. The early task produced "a significant group difference in the post -rest gain" (d = -0.28), but BF10 = 1.33 indicated only weak evidence for the effect.

> "We found a significant group difference in the post -rest gain, based on the difference between the three training trials and the first three test trials, t(103) = -2.06, p = .042, d = -0.28. The Bayes Factor test, however, indicated weak evidence for the alternative hypothesis, BF 10 = 1.33"
