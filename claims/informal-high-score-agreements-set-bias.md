# Informal agreements in which teachers solicit high SET scores, sometimes exchanging grades for evaluations, introduce positive bias and moral hazard

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Medical students report that teachers hint at or explicitly negotiate high evaluations, in some elective courses exchanging high grades for high scores. [→ Li Yuanheng 2025](#li-yuanheng-2025)
`q1 i?` The article interprets such agreements as moral hazard: teachers may lower difficulty or relax grading to obtain high evaluations that benefit promotions. [→ Li Yuanheng 2025 (2)](#li-yuanheng-2025-2)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview finding from the teacher-student interaction theme: participant DE021 describes informal score agreements, including grade-for-score exchanges reported by elective course teachers.

> "Some teachers even say, 'At the end of the term, I'll give you high grades, and you give me high scores.' Some elective course teachers say this."

### Li Yuanheng 2025 (2)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion, framed via moral hazard theory rather than a tested result: teachers may act in self-interest because evaluations feed promotions and career development.

> "teachers, for the sake of their own promotions and career development, may lower teaching difficulty, relax grading standards, or even reach agreements with students to exchange high grades for high evaluations"
