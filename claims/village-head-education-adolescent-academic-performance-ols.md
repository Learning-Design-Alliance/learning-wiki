## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After controlling for adolescent, parental, and family characteristics, each unit increase in village head educational level is associated with a 0.096 increase in adolescents' standardized academic performance scores, significant at the 1% level. [→ Li Jing 2025](#li-jing-2025)

## Evidence

### Li Jing 2025

Li Jing, Deng Huan, Li Jun. (2025). The impact of village heads' educational levels on adolescent academic performance: Evidence from rural China. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0315822

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Cross-sectional OLS analysis of 2014 CFPS data on 1,720 rural adolescents aged 10-15, with academic performance measured as z-standardized word and math test scores. The fully controlled model reports "the coefficient decreases to 0.096", significant at the 1% level; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "In Model 3, after further controlling for family characteristics, the coefficient decreases to 0.096. Nevertheless, the educational level of village heads continues to exhibit a significant positive correlation with adolescent academic performance at the 1% level."
