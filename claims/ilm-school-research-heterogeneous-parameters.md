## Evidence

### Carina Schreiber & Michael Becker 2026

Carina Schreiber & Michael Becker. (2026). Intensive Longitudinal Methods in School Research: A Systematic Literature Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10203-x

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Descriptive statistics across the 257 included articles show wide variation in sample size; the review reports that "the median of 157 participants offers a better representation of the usual sample size" given outliers with very large samples (maximum printed as 102,007).

> "The average sample size varied widely; considering the impact of outliers with very large samples, the median of 157 participants offers a better representation of the usual sample size of intensive longitudinal studies in school research."

### Carina Schreiber & Michael Becker 2026 (2)

Carina Schreiber & Michael Becker. (2026). Intensive Longitudinal Methods in School Research: A Systematic Literature Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10203-x

`q2 · i?` · `review · r?`

Descriptive analysis of study duration across included articles: "participants were assessed for an average of approximately 13 days (SD = 9.53)", with a printed range from one day to nine weeks; the review notes many studies assessed participants only on non-consecutive days such as school days.

> "Study duration also differed considerably; while participants were assessed for an average of approximately 13 days (SD = 9.53), study durations ranged from just one day to as long as nine weeks."

### Carina Schreiber & Michael Becker 2026 (3)

Carina Schreiber & Michael Becker. (2026). Intensive Longitudinal Methods in School Research: A Systematic Literature Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10203-x

`q2 · i?` · `review · r?`

Descriptive analysis of assessment frequency, reported separately for daily-perspective and lesson-focused articles. Daily-perspective articles averaged "approximately three times per day on average (SD = 2.63)"; lesson-focused articles averaged 1.68 assessments per lesson (SD = 1.23), with 64.9% assessing only once per lesson.

> "From the daily perspective, participants were assessed approximately three times per day on average (SD = 2.63), but in more than a third of all eligible articles, the daily diary method was used, and participants were assessed only once per day."
