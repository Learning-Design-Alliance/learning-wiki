## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Medical students randomized to a 10-minute task-focusing stress-coping instruction reported significantly lower overall stress/overload during simulated resuscitation than controls. [→ Hunziker S 2013](#hunziker-s-2013)

## Evidence

### Hunziker S 2013

Hunziker S, Pagani S, Fasler K, Tschan F, Semmer NK, Marsch S. (2013). Impact of a stress coping strategy on perceived stress levels and performance during a simulated cardiopulmonary resuscitation: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-227X-13-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Intention-to-treat analysis of a prospective randomized controlled trial in which 124 medical students performed a simulated witnessed cardiac arrest; the intervention group reported "significantly smaller amounts of perceived stress/overload" with a mean difference of -0.6 (95% CI −1.3, -0.1), p=0.04 on a 1–20 scale.

> "participants in the intervention group reported significantly smaller amounts of perceived stress/overload compared to the control group (difference of mean perceived stress: -0.6 (95% CI −1.3, -0.1), p=0.04)"
