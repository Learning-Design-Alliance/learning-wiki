## Subclaims

`q3 i?` Traditional averages were significantly higher in each concept category: Kinematics, Newton's 1st Law, Newton's 2nd Law, Newton's 3rd Law, and Force Identification. [→ Caballero 2011](#caballero-2011)

`q2 i?` On the FCI, traditional students at Georgia Tech showed better average item gains than M&I students on every force and motion concept measured. [→ Marcos Daniel Caballero 2011](#marcos-daniel-caballero-2011)

## Evidence

### Caballero 2011

Caballero, M. D., Greco, E. F., Murray, E. R., Schatz, M. F., Bujak, K. R., Marr, M. J., Catrambone, R., & Kohlmyer, M. A. (2011). Comparing large lecture mechanics curricula using the Force Concept Inventory: A five thousand student study. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1106.1055](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1106.1055)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Item-level analysis of FCI questions grouped into five concept categories using Hestenes' conceptual dimensions. The difference in normalized item gains was positive for almost all questions, with the highest concept-level differences on Kinematics and Force Identification.

> "The plot of ∆ gi illustrates better performance by traditional students across a ll concepts on the FCI (Fig. 4). We observed that ∆ gi is positive for almost all questions, and 45% of the questions had values of ∆ gi greater than ∆g = 0 .238."

### Marcos Daniel Caballero 2011

Marcos Daniel Caballero. (2011). Evaluating and Extending a Novel Course Reform of Introductory Mechanics. Georgia Institute of Technology. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1112.5593](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1112.5593)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Item analysis of FCI results from twenty-two traditional and six M&I sections at Georgia Tech, comparing average item gains per concept (Table 3). The table reports that "Each ∆gc is positive, indicating better average item gains for traditional students" across concepts, with the highest differences on Kinematics and Force Identification and lowest on Newton's 1st Law.

> "Each ∆gc is positive, indicating better average item gains for traditional students across all FCI force and motion concepts."
