# In a MANOVA on GPA and English proficiency, learning styles are significant while personality types reach only marginal significance after accounting for school and gender

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On the multivariate level, school, gender, and learning styles were significant at the 0.01 level, while personality types were significant only at the 0.10 level (Pillai's Trace p = .094). [→ Kaoropthai Chayaporn 2025](#kaoropthai-chayaporn-2025)
`q2 i?` The authors attribute the reduced importance of personality types to overlap: personality types were related to learning styles, so including both in the model lessened the importance of personality types. [→ Kaoropthai Chayaporn 2025](#kaoropthai-chayaporn-2025)

## Evidence

### Kaoropthai Chayaporn 2025

Kaoropthai Chayaporn, Dryver Arthur. (2025). A multivariate analysis of the relationships among the Big Five personality traits, activity-oriented learning styles, and academic performance of Grade 12 students in Thailand. BMC Psychology, 13, 1074. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-025-03387-4

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

MANOVA with GPA and QPT as dependent variables and school, gender, learning styles, and personality types as factors, on data from three Thai schools (Table 8: Learning Styles Pillai's Trace p = .001; Personality Types p = .094). No effect size is printed.

> "On the multivariate level, that is when considering GPA and English proficiency (QPT scores) at the same time, it was found that school, gender, and learning styles were all significant at the 0.01 level and the personality types were significant only at the 0.10 level."
