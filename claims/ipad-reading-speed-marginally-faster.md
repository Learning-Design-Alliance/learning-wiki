## Evidence

### Sackstein S 2015

Sackstein S, Spark L & Jenkins A. (2015). Are e-books effective tools for learning? Reading speed and comprehension: iPad vs. paper. South African Journal of Education, 35(4), Art. #1202. https://doi.org/10.15700/saje.v35n4a1202

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental within-subjects study of 68 students comparing reading speed on iPad versus paper using paired sample t-tests. The full sample read marginally faster on iPad, though the result was not statistically significant (t = 1.470, p = .146).

> "Although statistically non -significant ( t = 1.470, p = .146), the students read from the iPad read only marginally faster on average than on paper."
