Evidence¶

Moeller 2017¶

Moeller, J., Brackett, M. A., Ivcevic, Z., & White, A. E. (2017). High school students' feelings: Discoveries from a large national survey and an experience sampling study. Preprint / self-archived. https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=High+school+students%E2%80%99+feelings+Moeller+Brackett+Ivcevic+White

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Person-level multilevel regressions in the ESM study (472 students, 18,610 situational responses) with each feeling as an outcome. The article reports that "female gender identity was associated negatively with most positive feelings (except for happiness)"; coefficients were small and no standardized effect size beyond regression coefficients is printed.

"As Table 6 shows, female gender identity was associated negatively with most positive feelings (except for happiness) and associated positively with most negative feelings (except for bored and afraid)."

Moeller 2017 (2)¶

Moeller, J., Brackett, M. A., Ivcevic, Z., & White, A. E. (2017). High school students' feelings: Discoveries from a large national survey and an experience sampling study. Preprint / self-archived. https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=High+school+students%E2%80%99+feelings+Moeller+Brackett+Ivcevic+White

q2 · i? · associational · r2

ESM regression of situational anxiety on gender. The article reports "the gender difference for situational anxiety was significant" with "β = .158**" and R2 = .026; the authors question its practical relevance, and no standardized effect size beyond the regression coefficient is printed.

"The small effect size (β = .158**) and the insignificant determination coefficient (R2 = .026) could imply that even though the gender difference for situational anxiety was significant, the effect was very small and possibly not of any practical relevance."
