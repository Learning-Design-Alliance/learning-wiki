## Evidence

### Mohamad Nawal 2025

Mohamad Nawal, Rousseau Kim-Louise, Dowlut Fatimah, Gering Milton, Thomas Kevin G. F. (2025). Symptoms of ADHD and Other Common Mental Disorders Influence Academic Success in South African Undergraduates. Journal of Attention Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1177/10870547241310659

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Linear regression (Model 2, Table 5) on the three-wave longitudinal sample, predicting the AAS change-over-time slope from Time 1 ASRS total with age, Grade 12, and NBT controls; ASRS t = −3.06 with printed effect size partial eta squared ηp2 = .05. The article reports "higher symptom counts predicted a smaller magnitude of academic adjustment".

> "Although the analyses indicated that ADHD symptoms were not significant predictors of academic performance, p = .06, or of academic adjustment at the end of the semester, p = .13, they did detect a significant association between ASRS scores and magnitude of academic adjustment, p < .001."

### Mohamad Nawal 2025 (2)

Mohamad Nawal, Rousseau Kim-Louise, Dowlut Fatimah, Gering Milton, Thomas Kevin G. F. (2025). Symptoms of ADHD and Other Common Mental Disorders Influence Academic Success in South African Undergraduates. Journal of Attention Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1177/10870547241310659

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression models on the Time 1 sample (N = 506) found no significant ADHD or ADHD-plus-comorbidity prediction of weighted first-semester GPA; the discussion attributes this partly to the single-semester outcome and continuous symptom data. No effect size was printed for these nulls.

> "Specifically, analyses suggested that (a) self-reported severity of ADHD symptoms was not significantly associated with academic performance (as measured by the weighted first-semester GPA), and (b) neither symptoms of ADHD alone nor the combination of ADHD and comorbid psychiatric symptoms (depression, anxiety, risky alcohol use) was a statistically significant predictor of academic performance."
