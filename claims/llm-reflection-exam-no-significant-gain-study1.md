# LLM-guided self-reflection after an assignment did not significantly improve exam scores two weeks later compared to no reflection (Study 1)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students assigned to LLM-guided reflection scored slightly higher on a proctored exam two weeks later than the no-reflection control, but the difference was not statistically significant. [→ Harsh Kumar 2024](#harsh-kumar-2024)

## Evidence

### Harsh Kumar 2024

Harsh Kumar, Ruiwei Xiao, Benjamin Lawson, Ilya Musabirov, Jiakai Shi, Xinyuan Wang, Huayin Luo, Joseph Jay Williams, Anna Rafferty, John Stamper, Michael Liut. (2024). Supporting Self-Reflection at Scale with Large Language Models: Insights from Randomized Field Experiments in Classrooms. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1145/3657604.3662042](https://doi.org/10.1145/3657604.3662042)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized field experiment in an Introduction to Databases course (N=145); all students solved an MCQ assignment with a GPT-3 tutor, then half were randomly assigned to LLM-guided reflection. Exam means were 57.27 vs 53.99; the article reports the difference was "not statistically significant (𝑝 = 0.1008)".

> "When comparing the exam scores, those assigned to the reflection activity with the LLM achieved a slightly higher score ( 𝑀𝑒𝑎𝑛 = 57.27) compared to those who did not participate in the reflection activity (𝑀𝑒𝑎𝑛 = 53.99)"
