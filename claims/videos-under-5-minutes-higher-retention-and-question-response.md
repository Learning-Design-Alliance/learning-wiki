# Videos under 5 minutes have higher audience retention and higher response rates to embedded questions in a flipped undergraduate medical classroom

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Videos under 5 minutes showed the highest audience retention compared with videos 5 minutes or longer (p = 0.039). [→ Gutiérrez-González R 2025](#gutierrez-gonzalez-r-2025)
`q2 i?` Videos under 5 minutes showed a significantly higher response rate to embedded questions (median 51% vs 39%, p = 0.045). [→ Gutiérrez-González R 2025 (2)](#gutierrez-gonzalez-r-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Gutiérrez-González R 2025

Gutiérrez-González R, Royuela A, Zamarron A. (2025). Student engagement in a flipped undergraduate medical classroom to measure optimal video-based lecture length. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2025.2479752

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Univariable analysis of six engagement outcomes across 173 videos viewed by 152 students over three academic years, comparing duration cut-offs from 4 to 10 minutes; the 5-minute cut-off gave the largest significant audience-retention difference, "the highest AR was found in videos under 5 minutes".

> "Nevertheless, the highest AR was found in videos under 5 minutes and the lowest in the group over 5 minutes (p = 0.039)."

### Gutiérrez-González R 2025 (2)

Gutiérrez-González R, Royuela A, Zamarron A. (2025). Student engagement in a flipped undergraduate medical classroom to measure optimal video-based lecture length. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2025.2479752

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same univariable cut-off analysis of the response rate to embedded questions outcome; medians were 50% and 51% for videos under 4 and 5 minutes, "with a significant difference in videos under 5 minutes (p = 0.045)".

> "Finally, higher response rates to embedded questions were observed in the videos under 4 and 5 minutes (median 50 and 51%, respectively), with a significant difference in videos under 5 minutes (p = 0.045)."
