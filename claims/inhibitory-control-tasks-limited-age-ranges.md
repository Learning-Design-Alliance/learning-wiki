## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across a meta-analysis of 198 studies, behavioral inhibitory control tasks had limited useful age ranges (2.49 years on average) owing to ceiling and floor effects, while the CBQ questionnaire subscale was useful for 6 years. [→ Petersen 2016](#petersen-2016)

## Evidence

### Petersen 2016

Petersen, I. T., Hoyniak, C. P., McQuillan, M. E., Bates, J. E., & Staples, A. D. (2016). Measuring the development of inhibitory control: The challenge of heterotypic continuity. Developmental Review, 40, 25-71. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dr.2016.02.001

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analysis of 198 studies (327 study-by-measure combinations) computing percentage-of-maximum (POM) scores at each age, with tasks deemed useful when POM fell between 20% and 80%. The review reports that "behavioral tasks were useful for 2.49 years, on average" versus 6 years for the questionnaire subscale. No effect size is printed.

> "Whereas the Inhibitory Control questionnaire subscale was useful for 6 years, behavioral tasks were useful for 2.49 years, on average."
