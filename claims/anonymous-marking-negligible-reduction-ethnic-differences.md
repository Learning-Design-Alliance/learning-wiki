## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Post-implementation of anonymous marking, the mean mark difference between Black students and the White majority was reduced by only about two thirds of a percentage point, and Asian-White written examination differences by around 1.5%, while Asian-White coursework differences slightly widened. ## Evidence

### Hinton Daniel P. 2017

Hinton Daniel P., Higson Helen. (2017). A large-scale examination of the effectiveness of anonymous marking in reducing group performance differences in higher education assessment. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0182711

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear regression of assessment marks onto ethnicity dummy variables, pre- and post-implementation of anonymous marking, in an archival dataset of 30674 students (Table 3). The article reports the reduction amounts to "approximately two thirds of a percentage point", and that Asian-White coursework differences "appear to have slightly widened".

> "For Black students, the mean difference between their marks and those of the White majority on these assessments only appears to have been reduced by approximately two thirds of a percentage point."
