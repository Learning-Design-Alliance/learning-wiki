## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Soderstrom 2015

Nicholas C. Soderstrom and Robert A. Bjork. (2015). Learning Versus Performance: An Integrative Review. Perspectives on Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691615569000

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Narrative review of Kerr and Booth (1978), in which children tossed beanbags at a target from 2 and 4 feet (varied practice) or only 3 feet (fixed practice) and were later tested from 3 feet. The review reports the varied-practice group outperformed the fixed-practice group.

> "The results, however, showed the opposite pattern: Varying the practice distances led to more accurate tosses from 3 feet away on the final test, showcasing the benefits of variable practice in producing transfer of a motor skill, a result that has been replicated and extended (e.g., Pigott & Shapiro, 1984; Roller, Cohen, Kimball, & Bloomberg, 2001; Wulf, 1991)."

### Soderstrom 2015 (2)

Nicholas C. Soderstrom and Robert A. Bjork. (2015). Learning Versus Performance: An Integrative Review. Perspectives on Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691615569000

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Narrative review of Smith, Glenberg, and Bjork (1978), in which participants studied a 40-word list in one or two campus locations and were tested in a neutral third location. The review reports the approximately 21% recall advantage for varied study contexts.

> "Strikingly, participants who studied in different rooms recalled approximately 21% more of the words than participants who studied in the same room, demonstrating the mnemonic benefit of variable practice (Smith, Glenberg, & Bjork, 1978)."
