## Evidence

### Kimiko Ryokai 2013

Kimiko Ryokai, Faraz Farzin, Eric Kaltman, Greg Niemeyer. (2013). Assessing multiple object tracking in young children using a game. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-012-9278-x

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Slope-fitting analysis of tracking threshold as a function of play session for 30 children; seventeen showed negative slopes, dominated by children who only played 2 sessions, and two showed no change.

> "Thirty children played more than one session. Of these thirty, eleven (37 %) had performance slopes that were positive, with 2 children (both played 3 sessions) showing perfectly linear improvement over time with repeated gameplay (slope = 1). However, we did not ﬁnd a consistent learning effect over time for all 30 children."

### Kimiko Ryokai 2013 (2)

Kimiko Ryokai, Faraz Farzin, Eric Kaltman, Greg Niemeyer. (2013). Assessing multiple object tracking in young children using a game. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-012-9278-x

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

First-versus-last-session regression analysis (Fig. 7); despite the nonsignificant learning effect, a select number of children improved between the first and last gameplay session, seen in points above the no-change line.

> "Regression analysis on the threshold game level reached during the ﬁrst versus the last session revealed an essentially ﬂat, nonlinear relationship (r = 0.029; p = 0.88) across the thirty children."
