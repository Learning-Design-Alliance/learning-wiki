# Fifth and 7th graders using the Electron Sink model developed mental models relating electron speed to accumulation time, including number-speed compensation

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A 5th grader (Tara) explained that filling time T decreases when electron speed increases, interpreting electron motion both as movement in the wire and accumulation in the sink. [→ Pratim Sengupta and Uri Wilensky 2018](#pratim-sengupta-and-uri-wilensky-2018)
`q2 i?` A 5th grader (Amber) used a protoquantitative compensation schema and a 7th grader (David) used a time-event model to explain how different number-speed pairs conserve filling time. [→ Pratim Sengupta and Uri Wilensky 2018 (2)](#pratim-sengupta-and-uri-wilensky-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Pratim Sengupta and Uri Wilensky 2018

Pratim Sengupta and Uri Wilensky. (2018). Understanding Electric Current Using Agent-Based Models: Connecting the Micro-level with Flow Rate. In: Proceedings of the Eighth International Conference on Computer Supported Education (CSEDU 2016). Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1802.04165](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1802.04165)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Analysis of semi-clinical interviews conducted after Activity B: the 5th grader compared faster entry to "dropping many more balls into the sink at once" and explained that higher speed inside the wire leads to lower accumulation time.

> "Our analysis therefore suggests that Tara interpreted the motion of electrons as depicted in the model both as movement within the wire, as well as accumulation (i.e., a building up process) inside the electron-sink, and that she was also able to explain a causal relationship between these two forms of movement"

### Pratim Sengupta and Uri Wilensky 2018 (2)

Pratim Sengupta and Uri Wilensky. (2018). Understanding Electric Current Using Agent-Based Models: Connecting the Micro-level with Flow Rate. In: Proceedings of the Eighth International Conference on Computer Supported Education (CSEDU 2016). Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1802.04165](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1802.04165)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Semi-clinical interview of a 5th grader during the balancing-time activity, showing use of a compensation schema (S = A + B = (A + x) + (B - x)); a 7th grader's written response likewise stated filling time is the same when "more electrons moving with less speed, and less electrons but moving faster".

> "So.. umm.. for the electrons .. you had to decrease their number, and umm.. the speed.. .. umm.. you had to increase, to make up for the time…"
