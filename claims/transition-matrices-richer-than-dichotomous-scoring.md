# Transition matrix analysis of FCI Question 18 shows post-instruction movement to better incorrect answer choices that dichotomous scoring does not reveal

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On FCI Question 18 with Rice University students, 22.7% of students moved to a better incorrect answer choice after instruction, in addition to the 50% who arrived at the correct answer. [→ Gary A. Morris 2017](#gary-a-morris-2017)
`q2 i?` Of Rice University students initially incorrect on FCI Question 18, 72.7% got it correct or moved to a better incorrect answer after instruction, while 7.7% moved to a worse answer. [→ Gary A. Morris 2017 (2)](#gary-a-morris-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Gary A. Morris 2017

Gary A. Morris, Paul J. Walter, Spencer Skees, and Samantha Schwartz. (2017). Transition Matrices: A Tool to Assess Student Learning and Improve Instruction. [doi:10.1119/1.4976661](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4976661)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Analysis of pre-/post-instruction FCI Question 18 data from Rice University students (2000-2003) summarized in a transition-matrix derivative table. Only "50% of students arrived at the correct answer post instruction", while "another 22.7% of students moved to a better incorrect answer choice after instruction".

> "The summary table for the transition matrix, however, shows that another 22.7% of students moved to a better incorrect answer choice after instruction."

### Gary A. Morris 2017 (2)

Gary A. Morris, Paul J. Walter, Spencer Skees, and Samantha Schwartz. (2017). Transition Matrices: A Tool to Assess Student Learning and Improve Instruction. [doi:10.1119/1.4976661](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4976661)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same transition-matrix summary analysis of Rice University pre-/post-instruction data for Question 18. The authors state "only 7.7% of students who got the item incorrect initially moved to a worse answer choice after instruction" and 72.7% improved or became correct.

> "only 7.7% of students who got the item incorrect initially moved to a worse answer choice after instruction, while 72.7% of those who got it incorrect initially got it correct or moved to a better incorrect answer after instruction."
