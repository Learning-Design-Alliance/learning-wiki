# Blended teaching produced higher total OSCE scores than traditional offline teaching in a clinical skills training course

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students taught with blended teaching achieved significantly higher OSCE total scores than students taught with traditional offline teaching. [→ He Zhicheng 2024](#he-zhicheng-2024)

## Evidence

### He Zhicheng 2024

He Zhicheng, Li Hua, Lu Lan, Wang Qiang, Wu Qingming, Lu Lili. (2024). The application of blended teaching in medical practical course of clinical skills training. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05730-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Prospective randomized controlled trial at WUST: 48 fifth-semester students randomly allocated to blended teaching (Group A, n=24) or traditional offline teaching (Group B, n=24); after the 4-month course both sat a 6-station OSCE. Total score 82.13 ± 3.36 vs. 70.00 ± 5.77, "P < 0.050".

> "Significant differences were observed in various skills between Group A and Group B. These skills include cardiopulmonary resuscitation (92.50(4.00) vs. 86.00(3.50), P < 0.050)"
