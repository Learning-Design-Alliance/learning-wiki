Evidence¶

Guilherme Lichand 2026¶

Guilherme Lichand, Lêticia Lopes, Sachin Allums. (2026). Reading ability conflates SES creativity gaps. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00404-y

q3 · i0 · causal · r2

Randomized controlled trial (Study 3) with 515 students in 43 high schools in 23 Brazilian states, using two divergent thinking items from the PISA 2022 Creative Thinking Assessment, hand-coded to PISA full/partial/no credit criteria; verbal responses typed by enumerators.

"Among participants who had to read the question prompts, we estimate virtually no SES creativity gap (a 0.004 s.d. difference in favor of high-SES students, p-value = 0.903). In turn, among those for whom enumerators read the prompts on their behalf, a sizeable SES gap emerges in favor of low-SES students (0.085 s.d., p-value = 0.011)."

Guilherme Lichand 2026 (2)¶

Guilherme Lichand, Lêticia Lopes, Sachin Allums. (2026). Reading ability conflates SES creativity gaps. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00404-y

q3 · i0 · causal · r2

Secondary analysis within Study 3 of full-credit shares on the "Generate Diverse Ideas" item, scoring the difference-in-difference across experimental conditions (p = 0.043).

"Consistent with the main text, SES differences in the share of students who receive full credit in the first item (based on the PISA's scoring guidelines) are reversed in the absence of reading requirements (p-value of difference-in-difference = 0.043)."
