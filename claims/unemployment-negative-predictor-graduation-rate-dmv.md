# Unemployment rate is a statistically significant negative linear predictor of 4-year graduation rate across DMV-area counties, with a 1 percentage point unemployment increase associated with a 2 percentage point graduation-rate drop

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across 148 counties over 4 years, an increase of 1 percentage point in unemployment is associated with a 2 percentage point drop in graduation rate, statistically significant (R2 = 0.126, p<0.001). [→ Adam Raabe 2024](#adam-raabe-2024)

## Evidence

### Adam Raabe 2024

Adam Raabe, Jessica Reynolds, Akshitha Kukudala, and Huthaifa Ashqar. (2024). The Effect of Funding on Student Achievement: Evidence from District of Columbia, Virginia, and Maryland. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2411.03147](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2411.03147)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Linear regression of graduation rates on unemployment using data from 148 counties across 4 years (2015–2018), after dropping Worcester County, Maryland as an outlier; the article reports the model met regression assumptions and "was statistically significant".

> "The estimated coefficient showed that an increase of 1 percentage point in unemployment results in a 2 percentage point drop in graduation rate and was statistically significant (R2 = 0.126, F(1, 590)=84.95, p<0.001)."
