# Students' evaluation of group processing as a positive contribution to learning increased significantly from the beginning to the end of the course and again to the time of survey

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Retrospective ratings of group processing's importance to learning rose significantly from before the seminar (5.4) to after the seminar (8.6, p<0.001) and from after the seminar to the present (8.9, p=0.03). [→ Lachowsky 2021](#lachowsky-2021)

## Evidence

### Lachowsky 2021

Lachowsky, N. J., & Murray, J. (2021). Group Processing: Students Reflections on the Experience and Impact of Group Processing. Journal of Problem Based Learning in Higher Education, 9(2). https://doi.org/10.5278/ojs.jpblhe.v9i2.2883

`q2 · i?`

Paired t-tests on retrospective 10-point ratings from the survey of 46 former seminar students showed a significant increase in perceived contribution to learning from the beginning to the end of the course (5.4 to 8.6, p<0.001) and from the end of the course to the present (8.6 to 8.9, p=0.03).

> "our research found that there was a significant increase in how students evaluated group processing as a positive contribution to their learning from the beginning to the end of the course (5.4 to 8.6, p<0.001), and from the end of the course to the present (8.6 to 8.9, p=0.03)."
