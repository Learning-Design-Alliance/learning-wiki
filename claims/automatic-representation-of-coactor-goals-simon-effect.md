# People automatically represent the goals of a co-actor, impairing individual task performance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When a go/no-go task is performed alongside a partner performing the complementary part, a congruency effect returns that is absent when the task is performed alone. [→ Frith Chris D 2012](#frith-chris-d-2012)

## Evidence

### Frith Chris D 2012

Frith Chris D. (2012). The role of metacognition in human social interactions. Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B: Biological Sciences. https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2012.0123

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

A reaction-time study (Sebanz et al., as reviewed here) using a spatial-compatibility go/no-go task. When a second participant took over the other response, the congruency effect returned: "spatially incongruent responses were slowed", indicating automatic representation of the co-actor's stimulus-response requirements.

> "In this context, even though the original participant was still performing the identical go/no-go task, the congruency effect returned and spatially incongruent responses were slowed."
