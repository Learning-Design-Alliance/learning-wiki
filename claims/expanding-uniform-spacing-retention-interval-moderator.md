# Expanding and uniform spaced practice better for long retention intervals; contracting better for short intervals

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Küpper-Tetzel et al. (2014) found expanding and uniform spaced practices were better for long retention intervals while contracting spaced practice was better for shorter retention intervals. [→ Ouhao Chen & Fred Paas & John Sweller 2021](#ouhao-chen-fred-paas-john-sweller-2021)

## Evidence

### Ouhao Chen & Fred Paas & John Sweller 2021

Ouhao Chen & Fred Paas & John Sweller. (2021). Spacing and Interleaving Effects Require Distinct Theoretical Bases: a Systematic Review Testing the Cognitive Load and Discriminative-Contrast Hypotheses. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09613-w

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Systematic review of 119 spacing and interleaving studies, attributing this finding to Küpper-Tetzel et al. (2014), who compared uniform (3-day intervals), expanding (1 then 5 days), and contracting (5 then 1 day) spaced practice with word pairs. The review reports "expanding and uniform spaced practices were better for long retention intervals compared to contracting spaced practice."

> "Results indicated that expanding and uniform spaced practices were better for long retention intervals compared to contracting spaced practice, which was better for shorter retention intervals."
