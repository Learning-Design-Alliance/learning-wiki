## Evidence

### Pontifex 2016

Pontifex, M. B., Gwizdala, K. L., Parks, A. C., Pfeiffer, K. A. & Fenn, K. M. (2016). The Association between Physical Activity During the Day and Long-Term Memory Stability. Scientific Reports 6, 38148. https://doi.org/10.1038/srep38148

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Sliding-window regressions time-locked to the second session identified two critical windows prior to retrieval; less sedentary time (β = −0.28, p = 0.004) and more moderate-to-vigorous activity (β = 0.24, p = 0.012) each predicted "greater long-term memory".

> "Individuals who spent less time sedentary 0:50:40 up to the start of the second session, (β = − 0.28, t (87) = 2.9, p = 0.004), and more time in moderate-to-vigorous physical activity 0:58:40 to 0:20:00 prior to the second session, (β = 0.24, t (87) = 2.6, p = 0.012), exhibited greater long-term memory"
