Evidence¶

Stuart Luppescu 1995¶

Stuart Luppescu, Anthony S. Bryk, Paul Deabster, John Q. Easton, and Yeow Meng Thum. (1995). School Reform, Retention Policy, and Student Achievement Gains. University of Chicago draft report. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/school-reform-retention-policy-and-student-achievement-gains

q2 · i? · associational · r3

Authors' discussion of the grade-specific retention regressions and an accompanying achievement-versus-birth-month examination. They find it surprising that even in seventh grade a difference of a few months of age makes a difference in propensity for retention. No effect size is printed.

"One surprising finding is that birth-month is a significant predictor at all grades. In examining the effects of birth month on achievement, we found a substantial connection, but only for the lower primary grades. After that, the plot of achievement versus birth month is flat."
