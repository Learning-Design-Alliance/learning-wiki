## Evidence

### Saunders 2024

Saunders, R., Fitz, J., DiNapoli, M. A., Jr., & Kini, T. (2024). Teacher residencies: State and federal policy to support comprehensive teacher preparation. Learning Policy Institute & EdPrepLab. https://doi.org/10.54300/358.825

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

A 2015 regression analysis of 377 TQP-funded residency graduates vs. a representative sample of 376 teachers from other preparation programs, controlling for key district and school characteristics, found "82% of residency graduates were still teaching in the same district in Year 3 or 4, vs. 72% of non-residents." The report notes the difference was significant at p < .01.

> "82% of residency graduates were still teaching in the same district in Year 3 or 4, vs. 72% of non-residents."

### Saunders 2024 (2)

Saunders, R., Fitz, J., DiNapoli, M. A., Jr., & Kini, T. (2024). Teacher residencies: State and federal policy to support comprehensive teacher preparation. Learning Policy Institute & EdPrepLab. https://doi.org/10.54300/358.825

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Descriptive analysis of 312 Boston Teacher Residency graduates vs. 1,333 non-resident teachers hired between 2004–05 and 2010–11 found "80% of graduates were still teaching in Boston Public Schools in Year 3, vs. 63% of non-resident teachers," with the difference significant at p < .001 per the report.

> "80% of graduates were still teaching in Boston Public Schools in Year 3, vs. 63% of non-resident teachers."

### Tim Silva 2015

Tim Silva, Allison McKie, Philip Gleason. (2015). New Findings on the Retention of Novice Teachers from Teaching Residency Programs. Washington, DC: U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparative retention analysis of two cohorts of novice TRP teachers (first- and second-year teachers in 2011-2012) against a representative sample of similarly placed non-TRP teachers, tracked to fall 2013. The brief reports that "TRP teachers were more likely to remain teaching in the same district than non-TRP teachers with similar teaching placements." No effect size is printed.

> "TRP teachers were more likely to remain teaching in the same district than non-TRP teachers with similar teaching placements."

### Wojcikiewicz 2025

Wojcikiewicz, S. K., Ralph, K., Snyder, J., Bland, J. A., & Wechsler, M. E. (2025). Teacher residencies in Texas: Supporting successful implementation. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/630.675

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The report synthesizes prior research on teacher residency outcomes, citing Learning Policy Institute reviews. It reports residency completer retention "typically range from 80% to 95% after 3 years," exceeding same-district comparison teachers; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Retention rates for residency completers vary by program, but typically range from 80% to 95% after 3 years, exceeding the retention rates of comparison teachers in the same districts by at least 10 percentage points."
