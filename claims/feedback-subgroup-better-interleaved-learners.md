# In an exploratory Experiment 1 subgroup analysis, the preference for blocked studying after feedback disappeared among students who actually performed better with interleaved studying

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Among feedback recipients who performed better with interleaved studying (n = 42 of 75), blocked studying was no longer considered significantly more effective (d = -0.30) or more desirable (d = -0.23), though interleaved was not significantly favored either. [→ Janssen 2023](#janssen-2023)

## Evidence

### Janssen 2023

Janssen, E. M., van Gog, T., van de Groep, L., de Lange, A. J., Knopper, R. L., Onan, E., Wiradhany, W., & de Bruin, A. B. H. (2023). The Role of Mental Effort in Students' Perceptions of the Effectiveness of Interleaved and Blocked Study Strategies and Their Willingness to Use Them. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09797-3

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory analysis of the 94 students who performed better with interleaved studying; among the 42 feedback recipients, the blocked-study preference disappeared (t(41) = -1.94, p = 0.060, d = -0.30; willingness d = -0.23, p = 0.148).

> "Students who received feedback that they performed better with interleaved studying than with blocked studying (n = 42 out of 75) did not consider blocked studying (M = 5.48, SD = 1.76) to be significantly more effective than interleaved studying (M = 6.33, SD = 1.88), t(41) = -1.94, p = 0.060, Cohen's d = -0.30"
