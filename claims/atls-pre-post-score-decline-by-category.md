## Evidence

### Aldarsouni Fayez G. 2024

Aldarsouni Fayez G., Alkhulaiwi Hani, Alruwaite Hosam, Alsubaie Shara, Abdulkareem Faye, Alsubaie Norah. (2024). Advanced trauma life support training outcomes in Saudi Arabia: a four-year multicenter analysis of influential characteristics and factors (2019-2023). Annals of Saudi Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5144/0256-4947.2024.394

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparative pre-test/post-test analysis across all 603 participants (Table 4). Failed participants had a median post-test of 60 (IQR 10) versus 80 (IQR 10) for those who passed (P<.001).

> "The overall median reduction in scores from pre-test to post-test across all participants was −10 points (P<.01)."

### Aldarsouni Fayez G. 2024 (2)

Aldarsouni Fayez G., Alkhulaiwi Hani, Alruwaite Hosam, Alsubaie Shara, Abdulkareem Faye, Alsubaie Norah. (2024). Advanced trauma life support training outcomes in Saudi Arabia: a four-year multicenter analysis of influential characteristics and factors (2019-2023). Annals of Saudi Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5144/0256-4947.2024.394

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Category-level pre/post comparison (Table 4, Figure 4). OMFS participants had a median change of −27.5 points (P<.001); family medicine showed −40 points but was statistically insignificant due to small n.

> "Residents in non-trauma-related specialties exhibited a substantial decrease in scores, with a median change of −25 points (P=.001), whereas residents in trauma-related specialties experienced a much smaller median change of −5 points (P=.149)"
