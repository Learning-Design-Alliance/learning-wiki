# A child-centered combination therapy of executive-function and sensory-integration activities reduces attention deficit in primary-school children with ADHD (η² = .82)

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` After 24 sessions of combined executive-function and sensory-integration training, the experimental group showed a significant post-test reduction in attention deficit compared with the control group, with a group effect of η² = .82. [→ Fatemeh Salami 2017](#fatemeh-salami-2017)

## Evidence

### Fatemeh Salami 2017

Fatemeh Salami, Hassan Ashayeri, Mahnaz Estaki, Valiollah Farzad & Roya Koochak Entezar. (2017). Studying the Effectiveness of Combination Therapy (Based on Executive Function and Sensory Integration) Child-Centered on the Symptoms of Attention Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD). International Education Studies, 10(4). https://doi.org/10.5539/ies.v10n4p70

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

An analysis of covariance on 40 matched children (20 experimental, 20 control) assessed with the CSI-4 parent form, comparing post-test attention deficit scores between groups. The group effect was significant (F = 174.63, P ≤ 0.001, df = 1) with "The effect of intervention of the independent variable ... is 0.82", i.e. η² = .82.

> "According to Table 5, the results of post-test of attention deficit of children according to the type of group (P ≤ 0.001, df=1, F=174.63) were significant. Thus, the experimental group compared to the control group had reduction in attention deficit. The effect of intervention of the independent variable (combined teaching of executive functions and integrity) is 0.82."
