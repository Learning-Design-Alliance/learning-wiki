## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Because people form interests in activities where they view themselves as self-efficacious and may rely on perceived rather than tested abilities, showing girls they can succeed in science generates a new influence toward those fields rather than increasing freedom of choice. [→ Mathieu Bouville 2006](#mathieu-bouville-2006)

## Evidence

### Mathieu Bouville 2006

Mathieu Bouville. (2006). Should there be more women in science and engineering?. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0611089](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0611089)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Argument in Section IV.B, drawing on Lent et al.'s social-cognitive account of career interest (cited as background by the article). The author concludes that "outreach is bound to tamper with the girls’ interests and manipulate girls towards scientiﬁc ﬁelds".

> "By showing girls that they can be successful in science or e ngineering, one does not expunge past inﬂuences to increase their freedom of choice. Rather one generates a new inﬂuence, this time in favor of the scientiﬁc disciplines."
