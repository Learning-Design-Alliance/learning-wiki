# Focused rubric refinement that made required physics and scoring conditions explicit reduced AI–official disagreements on targeted questions (e.g., Grover item MAD 1.1 to 0.4 marks, r 0.70 to 0.82)

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Praveen Pathak 2026

Praveen Pathak, Siddharth Tiwary, Charudatt Kadolkar, Vijay Singh, David Rakestraw, Shirish Pathare, and Anwesh Mazumdar. (2026). Large-scale AI grading of handwritten physics assessments: Score agreement and Olympiad team selection outcomes. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20521

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Focused refinement run on QM question 3, part b (3 points, 40 responses), regrading with explicit rubric items. The article reports the refinement "increased Pearson'srfrom 0.70 to 0.82"; the printed r = 0.82 is the effect size.

> "For this question, the refinement reduced MAD from 1.1 to 0.4 marks, increased Pearson'srfrom 0.70 to 0.82, raised exact agreement from 15% to 45%, and raised agreement within 0.5 mark, including exact matches, from 35% to 80%."

### Praveen Pathak 2026 (2)

Praveen Pathak, Siddharth Tiwary, Charudatt Kadolkar, Vijay Singh, David Rakestraw, Shirish Pathare, and Anwesh Mazumdar. (2026). Large-scale AI grading of handwritten physics assessments: Score agreement and Olympiad team selection outcomes. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20521

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Focused regrading of only the OE1 Q2 submissions with large Round I AI–official differences (n=72), an 8-point conceptual thermodynamics question. The article reports "Pearson'srincreased from 0.58 to 0.83"; the printed r = 0.83 is the effect size.

> "Within this subset, MAD fell from 38% to 17% of the maximum possible score for the part, and Pearson'srincreased from 0.58 to 0.83."
