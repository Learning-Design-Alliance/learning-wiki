# Maternal cognitive support was only modestly associated with emotional support and largely independent of intrusiveness and negativity

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Across all time points, cognitive support correlated modestly with emotional responsiveness (r = .20–.39) but not with intrusiveness or negativity, indicating the two support dimensions are largely distinct in structured problem-solving contexts. [→ Zeytinoglu 2019](#zeytinoglu-2019)

## Evidence

### Zeytinoglu 2019

Zeytinoglu, S., Calkins, S. D., & Leerkes, E. M. (2019). Maternal emotional support but not cognitive support during problem-solving predicts increases in cognitive flexibility in early childhood. International Journal of Behavioral Development, 43(1), 12-23. https://doi.org/10.1177/0165025418757706

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis among observed maternal behavior ratings across the three waves (Table 3 prints cognitive support–responsiveness correlations of r = .20–.39, p < .01). The article concludes this support dimension is "largely independent from overall emotional supportiveness"; the mid-range correlations correspond to a medium effect.

> "cognitive support was associated modestly with responsiveness, but not with intrusiveness or negativity, suggesting that this aspect of caregiving is largely independent from overall emotional supportiveness and particularly negative aspects of caregiving"
