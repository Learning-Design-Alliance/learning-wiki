# Course focus (skills, concepts, or both) remained a significant predictor of postinstruction E-CLASS scores after adjusting for preinstruction score, major, and gender

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In 3-way ANCOVAs run separately for men and women, course focus was a significant predictor of adjusted postinstruction E-CLASS scores (F-test, p ≪ 0.01), with adjusted means ordered skills > both > concepts. [→ Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2016](#bethany-r-wilcox-and-h-j-lewandowski-2016)
`q2 i?` Student major was a significant predictor for both men and women (F-test, p < 0.01), with physics majors scoring higher. [→ Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2016](#bethany-r-wilcox-and-h-j-lewandowski-2016)

## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2016

Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski (2016). Developing skills vs reinforcing concepts in physics labs: Insight from E-CLASS. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.010108](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.010108)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

4-way ANCOVA on FY students with matched scores, major and gender (N = 3958), split by gender after a significant gender × focus interaction; both-focused adjusted means fell between skills- and concepts-focused and differed from both (Tukey's post-hoc). No effect size printed.

> "Course focus was also a signiﬁcant predictor for both men and women (F-test, p ≪ 0.01), with the highest adjusted mean for students in skills-focused courses and the lowest adjusted mean in concepts-focused courses in both cases."
