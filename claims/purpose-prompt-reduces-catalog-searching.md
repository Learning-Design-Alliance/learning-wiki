# Purpose-reflection prompts reduce students' likelihood of searching a course catalog, especially among new users

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` New users were significantly less likely to conduct a search under both purpose interventions than in the control condition. [→ Chen 2024](#chen-2024)

## Evidence

### Chen 2024

Chen, Y., Iyer, P., & Kizilcec, R. F. (2024). Reflection on Purpose Changes Students' Academic Interests: A Scalable Intervention in an Online Course Catalog. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2412.19035](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2412.19035)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Randomized field experiment on a university course catalog (study year Fall '21–Spring '22): logistic regression of whether students searched shows new-user search rates of 43.0% and 42.8% versus 52.1% in control; only unstandardized log-odds ratios ("logOR = -0.38") are printed, so no standardized effect size is claimed.

> "new users were significantly less likely to search with the self-interest purpose intervention (43.0%; logOR = -0.38, z = -3.73, p < 0.01) and self-transcendent purpose intervention (42.8%; logOR = -0.37, z = -3.64, p < 0.01) compared to the control condition (52.1%)"

### Chen 2024 (2)

Chen, Y., Iyer, P., & Kizilcec, R. F. (2024). Reflection on Purpose Changes Students' Academic Interests: A Scalable Intervention in an Online Course Catalog. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2412.19035](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2412.19035)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same experiment's existing-user analysis: the self-transcendent condition reduced searching (54.8% vs 62.5%, "logOR = -0.32"), while the self-interest condition did not differ from control (64.1%, p = 0.56). Only unstandardized log-odds ratios are printed, so no effect size magnitude is claimed.

> "They were less likely to search with the self-transcendent purpose intervention (54.8%; logOR = -0.32, z = -2.57, p = 0.01) compared to the control condition (62.5%), but remained similar with the self-interest purpose intervention (64.1%, logOR = 0.07, z = 0.58, p = 0.56)"
