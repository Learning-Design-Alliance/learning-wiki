# High intake of fruit and berries is associated with increased odds of high academic achievement in girls and boys, and high vegetable intake in girls

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` High fruit and berry intake (once a day or more) was associated with higher odds of high achievement in girls (AOR 2.09) and boys (AOR 1.47); high vegetable intake was significant in girls (AOR 1.82) but not boys. [→ Stea TH 2014](#stea-th-2014)

## Evidence

### Stea TH 2014

Stea TH, Torstveit MK. (2014). Association of lifestyle habits and academic achievement in Norwegian adolescents: a cross-sectional study. BMC Public Health 2014, 14:829. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2458-14-829

`q3 · i?`

Cross-sectional analysis of food intake versus dichotomized grades, adjusted for BMI and parental education. Girls with high fruit and berry intake had "AOR: 2.09 (1.51-2.88)"; the vegetable association in boys (AOR 1.33, CI 0.94-1.88) was not significant. No standardized effect size was printed.

> "Adjusted analyses showed increased odds of high academic achievement in girls and boys who had a high intake of fruit and berries (AOR: 2.09 (1.51-2.88) and AOR: 1.47 (1.04-2.07), respectively) and girls who had a high intake of vegetables (AOR: 1.82 (1.30-2.53))."
