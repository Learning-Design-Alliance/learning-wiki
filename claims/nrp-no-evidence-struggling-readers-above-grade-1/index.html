Evidence¶

Jeffrey S. Bowers 2021¶

Jeffrey S. Bowers. (2021). Yes Children Need to Learn Their GPCs but There Really Is Little or No Evidence that Systematic or Explicit Phonics Is Effective: a response to Fletcher, Savage, and Sharon (2020). Educational Psychology Review (2021) 33:1965–1979. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09602-z

q2 · i? · review · r1

Re-analysis of the National Reading Panel (2000) meta-analytic results discussed in this commentary. The nonsignificant effect for 'low achieving' readers was d = 0.15; the author argues most struggling readers in grades 2–6 fall in this category due to comorbidity of learning difficulties.

"based on the failure to observe significant short- or long-term benefits of phonics for “low achieving” readers (d = 0.15), I concluded that the NRP provided no evidence that phonics helped the majority of struggling readers above grade 1."

Related Claims¶

  • Systematic phonics outperforms unsystematic or no-phonics instruction on children's reading overall, but its advantage over whole-word teaching on novel words is untested in children and contested — related
  • Phonics instruction has a small but statistically confirmed effect for children with reading disabilities, while Orton-Gillingham structured-literacy programs show positive but non-significant effects — related
  • Instruction that includes systematic phonics leads to better spelling and reading outcomes than instruction that does not — related
  • The Torgerson et al. (2006) meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials found almost no strong evidence for systematic phonics — related
  • Corrective word-reading feedback effects depend on timing and reader skill: delayed feedback enhanced word-reading accuracy, and phonics feedback helped poor readers while better readers performed best with no feedback — related
