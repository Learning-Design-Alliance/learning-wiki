## Evidence

### Wolf 2016

Wolf, B., Latham, G., Laurenzano, M., Ross, S. M., Tam, W., & Cheung, A. C. K. (2016). Evaluation of Around the Corner: Funded by the Investing in Innovation Fund (i3) Grant #112209. Center for Research and Reform in Education, Johns Hopkins University. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED574582

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Qualitative analysis (Miles and Huberman coding, Denzin triangulation) of teacher questionnaires, parent surveys, snapshots, and visit reports; parents reported the DVDs reinforced school learning and increased engagement.

> "The qualitative data show that ATC improved home-to-school interactions, as well as student engagement. The introduction of Home Links (HL) created content interaction and reinforcement between school and home while affording parents and teachers overlapping understanding of what children were learning."

### Wolf 2016 (2)

Wolf, B., Latham, G., Laurenzano, M., Ross, S. M., Tam, W., & Cheung, A. C. K. (2016). Evaluation of Around the Corner: Funded by the Investing in Innovation Fund (i3) Grant #112209. Center for Research and Reform in Education, Johns Hopkins University. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED574582

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Teacher questionnaires in spring and fall 2015 (100% and 55% response rates); 93% perceived benefits for language and literacy, with mixed views on magnitude.

> "The vast majority (93%) of teachers also believed that ATC benefitted students' language and literacy skills. Forty-eight percent believed that ATC benefitted students' development "a great deal," and the other 52% believed that ATC benefitted students' development only "somewhat" or "not much.""
