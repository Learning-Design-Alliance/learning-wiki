# Modality effect sizes differ across fields of study, with social science effects negative and science effects largest

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Effect sizes differed reliably between fields: science (d = 1.20) exceeded social science (d = -0.13), and maths/logic, engineering and English comprehension also differed from social science. [→ Ginns 2005](#ginns-2005)

## Evidence

### Ginns 2005

Ginns, P. (2005). Meta-analysis of the modality effect. Learning and Instruction, 15(4), 313-331. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2005.07.001

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Holm-adjusted Bonferroni pairwise comparisons between broad fields of study in the between-subjects moderator analysis. Table 2 reports science d = 1.20, engineering d = 0.87, maths/logic d = 0.58, social science d = -0.13, English comprehension d = 0.69.

> "Significant differences were detected between science and social science, z contrast = 10.69, p < 0.001; between engineering and social science, z contrast = 6.14, p < 0.001; between maths and social science, zcontrast = 5.22, p < 0.001; and between maths/logic and science, z contrast = 4.58, p < 0.001."
