# Longer NREM periods in a short nap negatively correlate with subsequent encoding performance, possibly due to sleep inertia

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Within the Nap group, the percentage of NREM sleep correlated negatively with test performance (r = −0.46), as did spindle power density (r = −0.41). [→ Vidal 2025](#vidal-2025)

## Evidence

### Vidal 2025

Vidal, V., Pretel, M. R., Capurro, L., Tassone, L. M., Moyano, M. D., Malacari, R. G., Brusco, L. I., Ballarini, F. M. & Forcato, C. (2025). Short naps improve subsequent learning in a high school setting. npj Science of Learning 10:15. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00307-4

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Pearson correlation analysis of EEG-recorded nappers (n = 26) relating sleep-stage percentages and band power densities to test scores. The article found "a signiﬁcant negative correlation between the percentage of NREM sleep and performance" (r = −0.46) and a negative spindle correlation (r = −0.41).

> "Surprisingly, we found a signiﬁcant negative correlation between the percentage of NREM sleep and performance (Fig. 2a, r = −0.46, p = 0.015) and a negative correlation between spindles mean power density and performance (Fig.2b, r = −0.41, p = 0.034)"
