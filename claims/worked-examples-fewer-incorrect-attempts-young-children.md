# Year 3 children studying worked examples made significantly fewer incorrect attempts than children solving equivalent conventional problems

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Third graders presented with worked examples showed superior test performance to those required to solve conventional problems, measured by incorrect attempts. [→ Mwangi 1998](#mwangi-1998)

## Evidence

### Mwangi 1998

Wangari Mwangi and John Sweller. (1998). Learning to Solve Compare Word Problems: The Effect of Example Format and Generating Self-Explanations. Cognition and Instruction, 16(2). https://www.jstor.org/stable/3233720

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1, a controlled comparison with 18 Year 3 children randomly assigned to worked-example or conventional problem-solving conditions, found no difference in correct solutions but the conventional group made significantly more incorrect attempts on test problems, "t(16) = 1.95".

> "However, the conventional group made significantly more incorrect attempts (Table 1) than the worked example group, t(16) = 1.95."
