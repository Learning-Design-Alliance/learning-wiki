The paper reports, citing Flanigan et al. (2024), a meta-analysis of twenty-four studies across twenty-one articles finding handwritten note-taking leads to 'higher achievement among college students than typing notes' (Hedges' g = 0.248, p < 0.001, 95% CI [0.181, 0.315]). The paper uses this to define a boundary where transcription tool use bypasses the assessed skill.

"Flanigan et al. (2024), in a meta-analysis of twenty-four studies across twenty-one articles, report that taking and reviewing handwritten notes leads to higher achievement among college students than typing notes (Hedges' g = 0.248, p< 0.001, 95% CI [0.181, 0.315]), even though typing produces greater note-taking volume (g = 0.919)."
