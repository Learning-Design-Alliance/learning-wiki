Evidence¶

Leung-Gagné 2025¶

Leung-Gagné, M., Maria Castillo, M., Patrick, S. K., Carver-Thomas, D., Kini, T., Bingener, C., & Giani, M. V. (2025). Strengthening the educator pipeline through service scholarships: California's Golden State Teacher Grant Program. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/518.478

q2 · i? · associational · r1

GSTG teacher candidate recipient survey (CSAC, April 2025; 3,137 answered the employment question): among the 84% currently teaching, "nearly 9 in 10 (88%) reported teaching at a priority school" and most intend to remain.

"Among these respondents, nearly 9 in 10 (88%) reported teaching at a priority school, and a vast majority of recipients (91%) currently serving at a priority school indicated that they wanted to stay teaching at their schools after fulfilling their service obligations."

Leung-Gagné 2025 (2)¶

Leung-Gagné, M., Maria Castillo, M., Patrick, S. K., Carver-Thomas, D., Kini, T., Bingener, C., & Giani, M. V. (2025). Strengthening the educator pipeline through service scholarships: California's Golden State Teacher Grant Program. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/518.478

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Survey of currently teaching recipients (n = 2,546, Table 5) shows concentration in shortage fields: 37.7% special education, and "a substantial proportion of survey respondents reported teaching in mathematics (33%) and science (30%)", well above statewide first- and second-year teacher rates.

"Additionally, a substantial proportion of survey respondents reported teaching in mathematics (33%) and science (30%), much higher than the proportion reported in statewide data on teaching assignments of first- and second-year teachers (8% for math and 6% for science in 2022–23)."
