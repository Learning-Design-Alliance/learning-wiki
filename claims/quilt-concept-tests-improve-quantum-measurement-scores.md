## Subclaims

`q2 i?` The comparison group with traditional lectures averaged 26% on the measurement test, while the experimental group averaged 68% after concept tests and 91% after working through the QuILT. [→ Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#guangtian-zhu-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)
`q2 i?` On the individual questions in Table 1, performance improved from comparison to concept-test to QuILT groups, e.g., Q3 rose from 5% to 64% to 100% correct. [→ Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh 2016 (3)](#guangtian-zhu-and-chandralekha-singh-2016-3)

`q2 i?` Prior cohort comparisons in the same upper-level QM course found quiz performance of 26% after traditional lecture only, 68% after lecture plus multiple-choice questions, and 91% after a QuILT. [→ Paul Justice 2019](#paul-justice-2019)

## Evidence

### Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Improving Students' Understanding of Quantum Measurement. Originally published 2008. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06926](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06926)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Preliminary evaluation with two equivalent test versions in different but equivalent classes at two similar universities; the experimental group included 6 students on Test A and 7 on Test B. The comparison class average "was 26% including both versions (Tests A and B)."

> "The class average in the comparison group with traditional lectures was 26% including both versions (Tests A and B)."

### Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Improving Students' Understanding of Quantum Measurement. Originally published 2008. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06926](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06926)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Preliminary evaluation of the experimental group after instruction with both concept tests and the measurement QuILT; the article reports their "average score including both versions after the QuILT was 91%", up from 68% after concept tests alone.

> "Then, the experimental group students worked on the QuILT in class and their average score including both versions after the QuILT was 91%."

### Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh 2016 (3)

Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Improving Students' Understanding of Quantum Measurement. Originally published 2008. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06926](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06926)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Table 1 in the preliminary evaluation lists percentage correct for Q0 and Q1-Q3 by group: comparison 37%, 35%, 10%, 5%; concept test 71%, 86%, 36%, 64%; QuILT 100%, 100%, 90%, 100%. Numbers of students answering each question are printed in parentheses in the table.

> "Comparison Concept test QuILT Q0 37% (15) 71% (6) 100% (7) Q1 35% (10) 86% (7) 100% (5) Q2 10% (10) 36% (7) 90% (5) Q3 5% (10) 64% (7) 100% (5)"

### Paul Justice 2019

Paul Justice, Emily Marshman, and Chandralekha Singh. (2019). Improving student understanding of quantum mechanics underlying the Stern-Gerlach experiment using a research-validated multiple-choice question sequence. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ab2135](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ab2135)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The authors' own prior across-year comparison in the same course, reported in the introduction: 26% after lecture only, 68% after lecture plus multiple-choice questions, and 91% after a QuILT on quantum measurement. Percentages only; no effect size printed.

> "when students in upper-level undergraduate QM course only had traditional lecture -based instruction, their performance on a quantum measurement quiz covering those concepts was 26% [ 68]. The next year, the student pe rformance in the same course on an equivalent quiz on quantum measurement concepts was 68% after lecture and multiple-choice questions"
