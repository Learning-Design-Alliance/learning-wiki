## Evidence

### Paddock Michael T. DO 2015

Paddock Michael T. DO, MS; Bailitz John MD; Horowitz Russ MD; Khishfe Basem MD; Cosby Karen MD; Sergel Michelle J. MD. (2015). Disaster Response Team FAST Skills Training with a Portable Ultrasound Simulator Compared to Traditional Training: Pilot Study. Western Journal of Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2015.1.23720

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Prospective randomized blinded trial of 36 disaster response team members randomized to traditional, simulator, or combined training; skills measured with a FAST SDOT on healthy models and image interpretation review. The article reports no statistically significant between-group difference; equivalence was not formally tested.

> "The ANOVA procedure did not demonstrate any statistically significant differences between the training groups’ FAST image acquisition or image interpretation skills scores."

### Paddock Michael T. DO 2015 (2)

Paddock Michael T. DO, MS; Bailitz John MD; Horowitz Russ MD; Khishfe Basem MD; Cosby Karen MD; Sergel Michelle J. MD. (2015). Disaster Response Team FAST Skills Training with a Portable Ultrasound Simulator Compared to Traditional Training: Pilot Study. Western Journal of Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2015.1.23720

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Secondary ordinal analysis of the same randomized trial's SDOT image acquisition scores, reported in Results, confirming similarity across the three training groups.

> "An additional ordinal and dependent analysis of image acquisition scores using a Krukal-Wallis test confirmed similarity between all groups."
