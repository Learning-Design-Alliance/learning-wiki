## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Jeff Bale 2026

Jeff Bale, James Day, J. Ives, and D. A. Bonn. (2026). Constraints From Simulation Improve Experiential Outcomes in Laboratory Environment. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2605.30624](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.30624)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Anonymous post-lab survey with 66 physical-apparatus and 75 simulation respondents asked students to report their measured periods and uncertainties; reported t′-scores closely matched the collected data (Table III). Percentages as printed; no effect size reported.

> "53 % of the students using physical apparatus reported a t ′-score of 3 or greater, but 75% got to this level using the simulation."

### Jeff Bale 2026 (2)

Jeff Bale, James Day, J. Ives, and D. A. Bonn. (2026). Constraints From Simulation Improve Experiential Outcomes in Laboratory Environment. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2605.30624](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.30624)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Survey analysis of students whose t′-scores exceeded 3 found nearly identical rates of acknowledging disagreement across conditions, suggesting interpretation of clear data did not depend on apparatus type; the authors note roughly a quarter of students were reluctant to conclude disagreement even with supporting data.

> "74% using physical apparatus and 73% using the simulation acknowledged disagreement"
