# EWIMS did not reduce the percentage of students with low GPA on the binary indicator, though continuous GPA was higher in EWIMS schools

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` The percentage of students with a GPA of 2.0 or lower did not differ significantly between EWIMS schools (17 percent) and control schools (19 percent). [→ Faria 2017](#faria-2017)
`q4 i?` Sensitivity analyses using continuous GPA showed significantly higher average GPAs in EWIMS schools (2.98) than control schools (2.87). [→ Faria 2017 (2)](#faria-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Faria 2017

Faria, A.-M., Sorensen, N., Heppen, J., Bowdon, J., Taylor, S., Eisner, R., & Foster, S. (2017). Getting students on track for graduation: Impacts of the Early Warning Intervention and Monitoring System after one year (REL 2017–272). Washington, DC: U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Regional Educational Laboratory Midwest. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Randomized controlled trial main analysis on the binary low-GPA risk indicator from extant student records. The difference "was 17 percent in EWIMS schools and 19 percent in control schools" and was not statistically significant.

> "The percentage of students who had a GPA of 2.0 or lower was 17 percent in EWIMS schools and 19 percent in control schools (see figure 3). This difference was not statistically significant"

### Faria 2017 (2)

Faria, A.-M., Sorensen, N., Heppen, J., Bowdon, J., Taylor, S., Eisner, R., & Foster, S. (2017). Getting students on track for graduation: Impacts of the Early Warning Intervention and Monitoring System after one year (REL 2017–272). Washington, DC: U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Regional Educational Laboratory Midwest. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Sensitivity analysis replacing the binary low-GPA indicator with continuous GPA in the same randomized controlled trial. Average GPAs were "higher in EWIMS schools (2.98) than in control schools (2.87)".

> "sensitivity analyses that used continuous GPA data instead of the binary risk indicator showed that, on average, GPAs were higher in EWIMS schools (2.98) than in control schools (2.87); this difference was statistically significant"
