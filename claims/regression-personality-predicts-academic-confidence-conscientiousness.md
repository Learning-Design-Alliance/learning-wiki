## Evidence

### Sander Paul 2020

Sander Paul, de la Fuente Jesús. (2020). Undergraduate Student Gender, Personality and Academic Confidence. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 17(15). https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17155567

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple regression models (Table 7) entered sex plus the five Big Five traits as predictors of each ABC subscale. All four models were significant; the article reports the adjusted R² values suggest studying is most affected, with R² = 0.368 for studying printed in the table.

> "The results of these linear regression analyses are shown in Table 7, where it can be seen that each of the four models, one for each of the academic confidence subscales, is significant."

### Sander Paul 2020 (2)

Sander Paul, de la Fuente Jesús. (2020). Undergraduate Student Gender, Personality and Academic Confidence. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 17(15). https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17155567

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same regression analyses (Table 7) printed standardised beta coefficients; the article highlights conscientiousness's "standardised beta coefficients" as notable, with a beta of 0.587 for studying shown in the table, and neuroticism's consistent negative loading.

> "conscientiousness and openness have the greatest significant impacts across all measures of academic confidence, with the magnitude of the standardised beta coefficients for the personality trait conscientiousness being notable as is the consistent negative loading of the trait neuroticism"

### Sander Paul 2020 (3)

Sander Paul, de la Fuente Jesús. (2020). Undergraduate Student Gender, Personality and Academic Confidence. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 17(15). https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17155567

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the regression models (Table 7), sex's standardised betas were small and non-significant across subscales. The Conclusions state "gender did not appear as a significant contributor to academic confidence in the regression analyses" even though clear sex differences appeared in the raw comparisons.

> "Whilst gender did not appear as a significant contributor to academic confidence in the regression analyses, clear gender differences were found in academic confidence with the female students showing higher confidence that the male students for Grades, Studying, and Attendance."
