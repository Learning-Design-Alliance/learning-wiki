Evidence¶

Woods-Groves et al. 2017¶

Suzanne Woods-Groves, Youjia Hua, Jeremy W. Ford, Katelyn M. Neil. (2017). Efficacy of an Electronic Editing Strategy with College Students with Intellectual and Developmental Disabilities. Education and Training in Autism and Developmental Disabilities, 52(4), 422-436. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1160277

q3 · i2 · causal · r1

ANCOVAs of the five error types at posttest in the same randomized study. Spelling was significant (p = .022, d = .71); punctuation p = .030, d = .96 and substance p = .007, d = 1.66 were significant with large effects; "No significant difference was observed" for capitalization or overall appearance.

"For spelling, a signiﬁcant difference with a medium effect size was observed in favor of the intervention group p /H11005.022, d /H11005.71. No signiﬁcant difference was observed between intervention and control groups regarding capitalization p /H11005.428, d /H11005.44 and overall appearance p /H11005.495, d /H11005.20."
