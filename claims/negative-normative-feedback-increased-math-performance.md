# Negative normative feedback directly increased subsequent math performance in sixth-graders

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Negative normative feedback had a positive direct effect on subsequent math performance relative to no feedback (β = .32). [→ Van der Beek 2025](#van-der-beek-2025)

## Evidence

### Van der Beek 2025

Van der Beek, J.P.J.; Van der Ven, S.H.G.; Van de Weijer-Bergsma, E.; Kroesbergen, E.H. (2025). How feedback affects emotions, performance and self-concept in mathematics. Learning and Instruction 99, 102169. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102169

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Path analysis (Model 1) of the randomized experiment with 219 sixth-graders matched on prior achievement and assigned to positive, negative or no feedback; the article reports "a small positive direct effect of negative feedback on math performance" (Appendix C: β = .32, p = .009), while positive feedback showed no direct effect.

> "There was also a small positive direct effect of negative feedback on math performance. Positive feedback did not directly affect math performance."
