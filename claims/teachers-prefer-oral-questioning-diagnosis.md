# Teachers prefer oral questioning, interviews, and direct questioning over tests for diagnosing misconceptions

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Some teachers chose oral questions/interviews/direct questioning as the most effective diagnostic method because it needs little technical preparation, gives quick feedback, and avoids answers produced through peer collaboration. [→ Mutmainna 2025](#mutmainna-2025)

## Evidence

### Mutmainna 2025

Mutmainna, Istiyono, E., Haryanto, Retnawati, & Setiawan, C. (2025). Physics Teachers' Perceptions about Diagnostic Assessment of Students' Physics Misconceptions: A Phenomenological Study. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2501.10422](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2501.10422)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Interview finding (RQ2, theme 3): reasons given were minimal technical preparation, quick feedback, and worry that test answers reflect collaboration; Nana said with interviews "we can immediately identify students' misconceptions", and Dodi walks around class asking students to explain their written work.

> "Assessment practices in the form of oral questions/interviews/direct questioning were chosen by some participants as the most effective method for several reasons."

### Mutmainna 2025 (2)

Mutmainna, Istiyono, E., Haryanto, Retnawati, & Setiawan, C. (2025). Physics Teachers' Perceptions about Diagnostic Assessment of Students' Physics Misconceptions: A Phenomenological Study. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2501.10422](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2501.10422)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Same interview study: participant Dani reported using oral tests within teaching, stating "Oral tests are more effective in my opinion because as we teach, we can see if the students understand or no"; diagnosis was embedded in formative practice rather than standalone.

> "diagnosing misconceptions is not conducted as a separate single activity but rather integrated into formative tests."
