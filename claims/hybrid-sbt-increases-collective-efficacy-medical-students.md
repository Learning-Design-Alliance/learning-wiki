# Hybrid simulation-based training combining a virtual patient simulator with a basic manikin significantly increases medical students' collective efficacy

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` After hybrid SBT, fifth-year medical students' collective efficacy scores rose significantly from pretest to posttest, with a large effect size (d = 0.811). [→ Tam 2025](#tam-2025)

## Evidence

### Tam 2025

Tam, C. Y. W., Dizon, J. I. W. T., Sin, S. W. C., Tang, G. K. Y., Lai, V. Y. W., Wong, Y. Y., Ganotice, F. A., & Yeung, N. P. (2025). Implementation of hybrid simulation training to promote collective and self-efficacy in medical students: a two-pronged approach. Annals of Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1080/07853890.2025.2527362

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Pre-post paired analysis of the primary outcome in a cross-sectional cohort of 94 fifth-year medical students; paired t-tests were used because CE scores were normally distributed. The article reports "Large effect size (d = 0.811)" with p < 0.001.

> "after progressing through hybrid SBT their CE scores significantly increased, which is further evidenced by means and standard deviations of their pretest vs posttest CE scores (12.16 ± 3.859 vs 15.42 ± 2.87, p < 0.001) Large effect size (d = 0.811) suggests that the improvements are of practical significance after undergoing hybrid SBT."
