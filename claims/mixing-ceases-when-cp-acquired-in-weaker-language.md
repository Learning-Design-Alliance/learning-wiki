# Code-mixing decreases and ceases as the weaker language syntactic structure develops to CP level

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` When the child has acquired CP in the weaker language, intra-sentential mixing of the Ivy Hypothesis type decreases radically and eventually stops. [→ Bernardini 2004](#bernardini-2004)

## Evidence

### Bernardini 2004

Bernardini, P., & Schlyter, S. (2004). Growing syntactic structure and code-mixing in the weaker language: The Ivy Hypothesis. Bilingualism: Language and Cognition, 7(1), 49-69. https://doi.org/10.1017/S1366728904001270

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Longitudinal analysis of Lukas (Swedish-Italian) showing that at age 3;1, when CP evidence appeared in Italian, "his mixed utterances decrease radically from this age onward." The authors interpret this as the child no longer needing to supply the CP with Swedish material.

> "From this moment, we ﬁnd instances of CP in Lukas' s pure Italian utterances (cf. example (12) above) and his mixed utterances decrease radically from this age onward (cf. Table 4)."
