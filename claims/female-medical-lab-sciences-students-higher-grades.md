## Evidence

### A. R. Hakami 2021

A. R. Hakami. (2021). Effect of absenteeism on the performance of medical sciences students: gender differences. Medical Education Online, 26(1). https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2021.1875531

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Observational analysis of final grades in a mandatory third-year Clinical Virology course across two 2019 semesters at one Saudi university. Table 3 reports mean grade 77.13 (SD 7.495) for female students (n = 72) versus 71.06 (SD 10.194) for male students (n = 43); the article states "the average grades of male and female students" differed significantly. No effect size is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "A statistically significant difference was found between the average grades of male and female students; however, the absence rate was not significantly different between them."

### A. R. Hakami 2021 (2)

A. R. Hakami. (2021). Effect of absenteeism on the performance of medical sciences students: gender differences. Medical Education Online, 26(1). https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2021.1875531

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Replication of the grade comparison in a separate third-year Bacteriology course over the same two semesters; the article reports "the female section performed better than male students", significant at p = 0.0184. No effect size is printed.

> "The same trend was found with another Bacteriology course in which the female section performed better than male students (Table 2). Here, data from two semesters supported this difference and was significant (p = 0.0184)."
