Alexandra Sobczak and Nico Bunzeck 2023¶

Alexandra Sobczak and Nico Bunzeck. (2023). Effects of positive and negative social feedback on motivation, evaluative learning, and socio-emotional processing. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00178-7

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Partial Spearman correlation analysis (controlling for age) in the SID-Video study linking cue valence ratings to feedback video valence ratings; the printed effect size is r = 0.4389 (large, |r| ≥ .37). Implicit cue valence did not correlate with feedback video valence in any condition (positive: rs = −0.1302, p = 0.2012; negative: rs = 0.1144, p = 0.2619).

"Explicit cue valence correlated with feedback video valence in the positive condition (r s = 0.4389, p < 0.0001; Fig. 8b) but not in the neutral (r s = 0.0596, p = 0.5597; Fig. 8a) or negative (r s = 0.0856, p = 0.4018; Fig. 8c) condition."

Related Claims¶

  • Cues predicting positive social feedback acquire positive implicit valence (evaluative learning) in both young and older adults, while negative cues do not differ from neutral — related
  • Explicit cue valence ratings show an age × condition interaction, with smaller positive-vs-neutral and positive-vs-negative rating differences in older adults; implicit measures show no such age effect — related
  • Older adults rate negative social feedback videos as less negative than young adults do, consistent with an age-related positivity effect — related
