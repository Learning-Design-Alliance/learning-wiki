# Layered AI feedback was perceived as more encouraging and independence-supporting but imposed higher mental effort

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Layered feedback elicited significantly higher perceived encouragement (p < .001), perceived independence (p = .002), and mental effort (p = .018) than non-layered feedback, with no difference in perceived specificity. [→ Jie Cao 2026](#jie-cao-2026)
`q3 i?` Within the layered group, first-layer feedback was rated higher on encouragement and independence than later-layer feedback (both p < .001). [→ Jie Cao 2026 (2)](#jie-cao-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Jie Cao 2026

Jie Cao, Chloe Qianhui Zhao, Christian Schunn, Elizabeth A. McLaughlin, Jionghao Lin, Kenneth R. Koedinger. (2026). Assessing the Impact and Underlying Pathways of Sequenced AI Feedback on Student Learning. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07469 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2604.07469](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2604.07469)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Between-group t-tests on survey scales (layered N=100, non-layered N=99); Table 2 reports d = 0.55 for encouragement, d = 0.45 for independence, and d = 0.34 for mental effort, with no significant difference in perceived specificity (p = 0.1).

> "Learnersinthelayeredfeedbackgroupreported significantly higher perceived encouragement (p< .001) and higher perceived independence (p= .002), but also higher mental effort (p= .018)."

### Jie Cao 2026 (2)

Jie Cao, Chloe Qianhui Zhao, Christian Schunn, Elizabeth A. McLaughlin, Jionghao Lin, Kenneth R. Koedinger. (2026). Assessing the Impact and Underlying Pathways of Sequenced AI Feedback on Student Learning. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07469

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Paired t-tests within the layered group (N=100) comparing perceptions of first-layer learner-centered feedback versus later-layer elaborated feedback; Table 3 reports d = 0.35 for encouragement and d = 0.67 for independence.

> "there was no difference in perceived specificity, but learners reported significantly higher perceived encouragement (p< .001) and perceived independence (p< .001) in the first layer compared to the latter layer."
