# Intensity of learner control use relates to retention gains but not to transfer performance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Knowledge increase correlated significantly with number of slides viewed (r = .47) and menu clicks (r = .46), but interactive behavior measures showed no significant correlation with transfer. [→ Huib K. Tabbers 2010](#huib-k-tabbers-2010)

## Evidence

### Huib K. Tabbers 2010

Huib K. Tabbers, Bastiaan de Koeijer. (2010). Learner control in animated multimedia instructions. Instructional Science, 38, 441–453. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-009-9119-4

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Correlational analysis in the learner control group (n = 26), controlling prior knowledge by subtraction. Knowledge increase correlated r = .47 with slides viewed and r = .46 with menu clicks; correlations with transfer and cognitive involvement were not significant.

> "This way we only looked at the actual knowledge increase, which still had a significant correlation of .47 ( p = .02) with number of slides viewed, a significant correlation of .46 (p = .02) with number of menu clicks, and non-significant correlations of .27 with total time-on-task and .29 with number of narration segments."
