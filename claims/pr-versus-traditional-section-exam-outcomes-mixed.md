# Course outcomes between the daytime and evening sections showed a significant midterm difference only in the peer-reflection year, with no significant final-exam difference in either year

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In Fall 2007, when peer reflection was implemented, the daytime section scored significantly higher on the midterm than the evening PR section (p=0.004); final-exam differences were not significant in either year (p=0.875 in 2007, p=0.112 in 2006). [→ Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#andrew-mason-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)

## Evidence

### Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Using Reflection with Peers to Help Students Learn Effective Problem Solving Strategies. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1603.02944](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1603.02944)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

t-tests comparing daytime and evening section averages across two years (Fall 2006: N=124 vs 100; Fall 2007: N=107 vs 93). Table 1 reports 2007 midterm means of 78.8 (daytime) vs 74.3 (PR), p=0.004, and final exam means of 58.1 vs 57.7, p=0.875.

> "Statistically signiﬁcant difference (at the level of p = 0.05) between groups only exists between the average midterm scores for the year in which peer reﬂection was implemented. The evening section scored lower on average than the daytime section on the ﬁnal exam but the difference is not statistically signiﬁcant (p=0.112 for 2006 and p=0.875 for 2007)"
