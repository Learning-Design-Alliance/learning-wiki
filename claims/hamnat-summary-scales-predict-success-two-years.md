## Subclaims
`q3 i1` All summary scales derived from the 52 HAM-Nat items significantly predicted academic success during the first two years of the medical curriculum, with total-sample odds ratios around 1.5 to 2.1 per z-standard deviation of test score. [→ Hissbach Johanna C 2011](#hissbach-johanna-c-2011)

## Evidence

### Hissbach Johanna C 2011

Hissbach Johanna C, Klusmann Dietrich, Hampe Wolfgang. (2011). Dimensionality and predictive validity of the HAM-Nat, a test of natural sciences for medical school admission. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-11-83

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression in the 334-student 2006 cohort (development sample A and validation sample B) with dichotomous criterion SUCCESS, i.e. "qualification for the first medical exam after two years". Table 3 prints total-sample odds ratios from 1.48 (chemistry) to 2.12 (corrmax); no scale was clearly superior.

> "All summary scales derived from the HAM-Nat item pool predicted academic performance during the first two years of the medical curriculum. No scale definition was clearly superior to the other."
