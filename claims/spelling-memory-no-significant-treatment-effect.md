# Vocabulary training did not significantly improve memory for target-word spellings, with moderate nonsignificant effect sizes (ds .47–.73) possibly suppressed by ceiling effects

## Evidence

### Craigo L. 2017

Craigo L., Ehri, L. C., & Hart, M. (2017). Teaching community college students strategies for learning unknown words as they read expository text. Higher Learning Research Communications, 7(1). https://doi.org/10.18870/hlrc.v7i1.350

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA on spelling posttests for treatment and transfer passages in the randomized experiment; the printed effect sizes were d = .47 to d = .73 ("ds ranging from .47 to .73"), nonsignificant, with the Control group averaging 80% correct close to treatment means of 88% to 91%, and no transfer-task differences observed.

> "However, effect sizes suggested a moderate advantage of vocabulary training, with ds ranging from .47 to .73. Differences may have been suppressed by ceiling effects, as all the groups spelled 80% to 91% of the target words correctly on average."
