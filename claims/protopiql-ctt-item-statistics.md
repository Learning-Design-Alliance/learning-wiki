## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith 2019

Trevor I. Smith, Suzanne W. Brahmia, Alexis Olsho, Andrew Boudreaux, Philip Eaton, Paul J. Kelly, Kyle J. Louis, Mitchell A. Nussenbaum, and Louis J. Remy. (2019). Developing a reasoning inventory for measuring physics quantitative literacy. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1901.03351](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1901.03351)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Classical test theory analysis of each protoPIQL question's difficulty and discrimination for the 1,076-student sample. Average difficulty was 0.62; average item discrimination between upper and lower 27% was 0.41, with 15 questions above the 0.3 threshold and Q5 at 0.296.

> "The average CTT-difficulty is 0.62, and 13 of the 18 questions fall within the generally accepted range of 0.2 ≤ D ≤ 0.8, with the remaining 5 being too easy"
