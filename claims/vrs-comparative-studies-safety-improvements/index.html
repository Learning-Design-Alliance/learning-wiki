Review-level synthesis of the 18 included studies: 17 (94%) proposed patient safety assessments, mainly intraoperative complication reports (67%), and "Significant safety improvements were found in 80% of comparative virtual reality simulation studies." No effect sizes pooled.

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • An intensive 5-day cataract surgery simulation program lowered posterior capsule rupture rates versus standard training, with competency gains normalizing by 15 months — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Supervised surgery studies on actual patients were all observational, reported high surgical volumes, and showed higher complication rates than VRS studies — related
  • Virtual reality simulation training is associated with safer cataract surgery outcomes than supervised surgical training on actual patients alone — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Evidence on transfer to clinical behavior and patient outcomes is scarce: only 2 studies reached Kirkpatrick level 3 and 1 reached level 4 — reports the opposite
  • Concentrated high-duration virtual reality training (>30 h over several weeks) was associated with reduced complication rates, while spaced-out simulator sessions were not — related
  • No longitudinal studies or RCTs demonstrate that AI-acquired skills transfer to the operating room — related
