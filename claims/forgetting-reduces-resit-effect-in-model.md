## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Model simulations in which knowledge depreciates between the first attempt and the resit show the resit effect shrinking, with 50% forgetting reducing the resit effect by roughly 40% at w = 0.1. [→ Nijenkamp 2016](#nijenkamp-2016)

## Evidence

### Nijenkamp 2016

Nijenkamp, R., Nieuwenstein, M. R., de Jong, R., & Lorist, M. M. (2016). Do Resit Exams Promote Lower Investments of Study Time? Theory and Data from a Laboratory Study. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0161708

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Numerical simulation of the modified model with forgetting activated between R1 and R2, showing that "50% forgetting of the knowledge gained earlier resulted in a marked reduction (by some 40%) of the resit effect" at w = 0.1. This is a model-based result, not human data.

> "50% forgetting of the knowledge gained earlier resulted in a marked reduction (by some 40%) of the resit effect, with w set at 0.1"
