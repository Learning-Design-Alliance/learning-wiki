# Pointing outperformed tracing on the terminology test, while neither touch-based group differed significantly from the no-touch group on terminology

## Evidence

### Babette Park · Andreas Korbach · Paul Ginns · Roland Brünken 2023

Babette Park · Andreas Korbach · Paul Ginns · Roland Brünken. (2023). How Learners Use Their Hands for Learning: an Eye-Tracking Study. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09833-2

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Contrast testing in the three-group experiment (analyzed N = 83) found significantly higher terminology performance for the pointing group than the tracing group, "ΔM = − 2.593, p = 0.010"; the corresponding univariate group effect on terminology was F(2, 82) = 3.53, p = 0.034, η2 = 0.081.

> "a significant difference between the pointing and the tracing groups for terminology performance, ΔM = − 2.593, p = 0.010, LLCI = − 4.356, and ULCI = − 0.650"

### Babette Park · Andreas Korbach · Paul Ginns · Roland Brünken 2023 (2)

Babette Park · Andreas Korbach · Paul Ginns · Roland Brünken. (2023). How Learners Use Their Hands for Learning: an Eye-Tracking Study. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09833-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same experiment, the tracing–no-touch terminology contrast was not significant, "ΔM = − 1.186, p = 0.591"; the pointing–no-touch terminology contrast was likewise not significant (ΔM = − 1.406, p = 0.278). Equivalence was not tested.

> "a comparison by contrast testing was not showing any significant differences between the tracing and the no-touch group in terminology, ΔM = − 1.186, p = 0.591, LLCI = − 3.746, and ULCI = 1.374"
