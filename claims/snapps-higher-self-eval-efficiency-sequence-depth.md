## Evidence

### Seki Masayasu 2016

Seki Masayasu, Otaki Junji, Breugelmans Raoul, Komoda Takayuki, Nagata-Kobayashi Shizuko, Akaishi Yu, Hiramoto Jun, Ohno Iwao, Harada Yoshimi, Hirayama Yoji, Izumi Miki (2016). How do case presentation teaching methods affect learning outcomes?-SNAPPS and the One-Minute preceptor. BMC Medical Education 16. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0531-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Self-evaluation ratings from the same randomized comparative study (n = 71): SNAPPS residents evaluated presenting "the case efficiently", "the case in the sequence given", and giving "an in-depth case presentation" significantly more highly than OMP residents.

> "members of the SNAPPS group evaluated the statements "It was easy to present the case efficiently," "It was easy to present the case in the sequence given," and "I was able to give an in-depth case presentation" significantly more highly than did participants in the OMP group."
