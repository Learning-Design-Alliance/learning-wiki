# In algebra-based E&M, the only significant gender difference was on the first exam, with male students averaging higher and a weak effect size

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` Male students averaged significantly higher than female students only on the first exam of algebra-based E&M, with a weak effect size (d = 0.139). [→ Matthew Dew 2021](#matthew-dew-2021)

## Evidence

### Matthew Dew 2021

Matthew Dew, Jonathan Perry, Lewis Ford, William Bassichis, and Tatiana Erukhimova. (2021). Gendered Performance Diﬀerences in Introductory Physics: A Study from a Large Land-Grant University. arXiv:2009.09536. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010106](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010106)

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r1`

T-tests on transformed pooled exam data from 2 male instructors and 8 lecture sections of algebra-based E&M; the article reports the first exam as the only significant difference, with "a weak ( d = 0.139)" effect size, producing no difference in final course grades.

> "A signiﬁcant diﬀerence in per- formance was observed only for the ﬁrst exam with male students averaging higher than female students. The ef- fect size for this diﬀerence was weak ( d = 0.139)."
