Evidence¶

Takako Nomi 2010¶

Takako Nomi, Elaine M. Allensworth. (2010). The Effects of Tracking with Supports on Instructional Climate and Student Outcomes in High School Algebra. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/effects-tracking-supports-instructional-climate-and-student-outcomes-high-school

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Sequential regression models predicting PLAN algebra scores for the two cohorts. Adding the support-classmate variable made the above-norm post-policy improvement no longer significant, indicating spillover benefits from classmates' extra instruction.

"Model 5 shows that above-norm students did have higher test score gains of by about 0.6 points when their algebra classes had students who enrolled in support courses than above-norm students who did not have any such classmates (p<.01)."

Takako Nomi 2010 (2)¶

Takako Nomi, Elaine M. Allensworth. (2010). The Effects of Tracking with Supports on Instructional Climate and Student Outcomes in High School Algebra. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/effects-tracking-supports-instructional-climate-and-student-outcomes-high-school

q3 · i? · causal · r2

In the same sequential models, classroom instructional climate variables explained about 5 to 10 percent of test score improvements, and peer ability explained an additional 23 percent for above-norm students.

"adding peer ability to the model decreases the post-policy improvement by an additional 23 percent, to 0.38."
