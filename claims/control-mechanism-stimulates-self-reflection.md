# Having control over recommended exercises stimulates adolescents to reflect on their mastery level and the underlying recommendation algorithm

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Qualitative responses suggest that adolescents with control referred more often to whether exercises matched their mastery level and questioned the algorithm's competence relative to teachers. [→ Jeroen Ooge 2023](#jeroen-ooge-2023)

## Evidence

### Jeroen Ooge 2023

Jeroen Ooge, Leen Dereu, and Katrien Verbert. 2023. Steering Recommendations and Visualising Its Impact: Effects on Adolescents' Trust in E-Learning Platforms. In 28th International Conference on Intelligent User Interfaces (IUI '23). https://doi.org/10.1145/3581641.3584046

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Thematic analysis of open questionnaire responses from the randomised experiment. "participants who could control recommendations spontaneously referred twice as much to whether exercises were tailored to their personal mastery level"; some also questioned whether the algorithm was as competent as teachers.

> "while participants in none most often described the platform's utility and design while discussing trust, participants who could control recommendations spontaneously referred twice as much to whether exercises were tailored to their personal mastery level."
