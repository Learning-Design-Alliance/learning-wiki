# Experimental groups differed significantly on prior VR experience and gender composition, which were used as covariates

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The four condition groups differed significantly in previous experience with VR and in the proportion of males and females, so subsequent analyses used VR experience and gender as covariates. [→ Klingenberg 2024](#klingenberg-2024)
`q3 i?` The groups did not differ significantly on age, English proficiency, self-reported prior knowledge, or previous experience with PCs. [→ Klingenberg 2024 (2)](#klingenberg-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Klingenberg 2024

Klingenberg, S., Bosse, R., Mayer, R. E., & Makransky, G. (2024). Does Embodiment in Virtual Reality Boost Learning Transfer? Testing an Immersion-Interactivity Framework. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09956-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Preliminary one-way ANOVAs checked whether the four randomly assigned groups differed on basic characteristics. The VR-experience ANOVA was significant, F(3,173) = 2.99, p = 0.03, with the printed group means; the article states the "following analyses were conducted using previous experience with VR and gender as covariates."

> "However, there was a significant difference among the groups on previous experience with VR, F (3,173) = 2.99, p = 0.03, with mean ratings of 2.43 (SD = 1.34) for the VR congruent group, 2.95 (SD = 1.25) for the VR incidental group, 2.53 (SD = 1.18) for the PC congruent group, and 2.24 (SD = 1.13) for the PC incidental group."

### Klingenberg 2024 (2)

Klingenberg, S., Bosse, R., Mayer, R. E., & Makransky, G. (2024). Does Embodiment in Virtual Reality Boost Learning Transfer? Testing an Immersion-Interactivity Framework. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09956-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

The same preliminary ANOVA checks found no significant group differences on age, English proficiency, self-reported prior knowledge of pipetting or serial dilution, or previous PC experience, with the printed F and p values; no effect sizes are reported.

> "One-way analyses of variance (ANOVAs) indicated that the groups did not differ significantly on age, F (3,173) = 0.505, p = 0.68; English proficiency, F(3,173) = 0.051, p = 0.99; self-reported prior knowledge of using a micropipette or conducting a serial dilution, F (3,173) = 2.50, p = 0.06; or previous experience with PCs, F(3,173) = 0.798, p = 0.50."
