## Evidence

### Srdjan Verbić 2010

Srdjan Verbić. (2010). Does it matter if you answer slowly?. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1005.0714](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1005.0714)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Analysis of all student-item encounters in the PD09 test logs. The article reports boys' median total time of 1525 seconds and a significant log-RT difference, "t(28753)=-6.6, p<4e-11"; boys' log RT was significantly higher at 11 of 32 items, with no items where girls' log RT was significantly higher.

> "boys, on average, respond significantly faster than girls do. Median time needed by boys to finish the entire test was 1525 seconds while the girls, on average, finished 81 second later. If we compare all student-item encounters, we see that difference between log RT for boys and girls is statistically significant: t(28753)=-6.6, p<4e-11."

### Srdjan Verbić 2010 (2)

Srdjan Verbić. (2010). Does it matter if you answer slowly?. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1005.0714](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1005.0714)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The article's own interpretation of the PD09 results: the response-time difference "did not affect their achievement", so neither group performed better, though boys on average responded faster.

> "There is no evidence that difference in response time causes differential item functioning on the test PD09. Difference in response time for boys and girls did not affect their achievement."
