Blanka KLIMOVA 2017¶

Blanka KLIMOVA, Jaroslav KACETL. (2017). Efficacy of Computer Games on Language Learning. TOJET: The Turkish Online Journal of Educational Technology, 16(4). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1160637

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports this second-hand from Aghlara and Tamjid (2011), an Iranian study of 40 children aged 6–7 taught English vocabulary with the digital game SHAIEx versus traditional teaching over 45 days. The review's Table 1 also reports the experimental group's mean vocabulary test score was significantly higher.

"Aghlara and Tamjid (2011) found out in their study that children learning vocabulary by playing digital games are more motivated than children who are taught vocabulary through traditional methods."

Related Claims¶

  • Gamification has small positive effects on cognitive, motivational and behavioral learning outcomes — a broader claim this one bears on
  • The review reports that students learning with serious games often perform better on content knowledge assessments than those in traditional settings — related
  • Newly arrived migrant children taught Italian through games showed larger vocabulary gains than traditionally taught peers on a vocabulary checklist inventory, not a direct test. — related
  • Quizizz-based gamification improved word memorization over traditional methods for intermediate learners — related
  • Comparative evaluations against traditional teaching are rare, and the one digital-versus-paper comparison found no statistical difference — related
  • Motivation increased significantly from before activity to in activity in the game-based group but showed no significant stage-to-stage change in the traditional guide group — related
