# The SAM spacing model captures the basic characteristics of Glenberg's 1976 data, though a competing model fit better on an assumption the article calls artificial

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · theoretical `r3` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2003 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The SAM model fits Glenberg's (1976) 24-condition dataset (χ² = 41.89, df = 18), capturing the basic characteristics of the data except at very short spacing lags. [→ Raaijmakers 2003](#raaijmakers-2003)
`q2 i?` Reed's strength–resistance implementation fit the Glenberg data substantially better (χ² between 24 and 30), but the article argues this advantage rests on an artificial assumption treating lag-0 items as a single presentation held in buffer for exactly 6 s. [→ Raaijmakers 2003 (2)](#raaijmakers-2003-2)

## Evidence

### Raaijmakers 2003

Raaijmakers, J.G.W. (2003). Spacing and repetition effects in human memory: application of the SAM model. Cognitive Science 27 (2003) 431–452. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0364-0213(03)00007-7

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Numerical fit to Glenberg's (1976) Experiment I (two presentations without testing followed by a test; each data point based on 540 observations). The article reports "the χ 2 -value of 41.89 (df = 18)" and notes the model misses the very short spacing lags.

> "As can be seen from Fig. 3, the fit of the model is not as good as for the previous experiments, as indicated by the χ 2 -value of 41.89 (df = 18). However, the model does capture the basic characteristics of the data (except for the very short spacing lags, but see below)."

### Raaijmakers 2003 (2)

Raaijmakers, J.G.W. (2003). Spacing and repetition effects in human memory: application of the SAM model. Cognitive Science 27 (2003) 431–452. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0364-0213(03)00007-7

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Model-comparison reported in the article's fits section: Reed's implementation of Wickelgren's strength–resistance theory achieved "χ 2 values between 24 and 30", better than SAM's 41.89 on the same Glenberg (1976) data.

> "However, a model based on Wickelgren’s strength–resistance theory (Reed, 1976; Wickelgren, 1972, 1974a, 1974b) did perform substantially better, leading to χ 2 values between 24 and 30 depending on the exact assumptions of the model."
