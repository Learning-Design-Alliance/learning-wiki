Evidence¶

Martin A 2017¶

Martin A, Booth JN, McGeown S, Niven A, Sproule J, Saunders DH, Reilly JJ. (2017). Longitudinal Associations Between Childhood Obesity and Academic Achievement: Systematic Review with Focus Group Data. Current Obesity Reports, 6(3), 297–313. https://doi.org/10.1007/s13679-017-0272-9

q3 · i? · review · r3

Systematic review synthesis of three high-quality longitudinal cohort studies (n = 8641) found "significantly lower maths scores at age 13–14 years in girls with obesity compared to healthy-weight peers". The finding was not evident in preschool-aged girls with obesity.

"Based on three high-quality studies using data from three distinct cohorts (n = 8641), there was consistent evidence for significantly lower maths scores at age 13–14 years in girls with obesity compared to healthy-weight peers."

Learner Variables¶

  • Gender — moderator: an instructional effect differs by group

Related Claims¶

  • Little consistent evidence links overweight or obesity to reading, science or average attainment across subjects
  • Psychosocial factors (bullying, interpersonal skills) and executive cognitive functions may mediate the obesity–achievement association
  • Adolescent girls with obesity report weight-related teasing and exclusion particularly in Physical Education
  • Adolescents and parents perceive obesity affects achievement indirectly through attitude to school rather than directly
