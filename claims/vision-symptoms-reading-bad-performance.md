## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Compared with good performers, children with bad academic performance more often reported tiring while reading, headaches or itching eyes, double vision, following text with a finger, and confusing or missing words when reading. ## Evidence

### Alvarez-Peregrina Cristina 2020

Alvarez-Peregrina Cristina, Sánchez-Tena Miguel Ángel, Andreu-Vázquez Cristina, Villa-Collar Cesar. (2020). Visual Health and Academic Performance in School-Aged Children. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17072346

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Children's survey in the screening study; Table 2 prints odds ratios for answering 'yes' with bad versus good performance, e.g. OR = 2.71 (2.35–3.12) for getting tired while reading and OR = 3.05 (2.65–3.51) for getting confused or missing words, all p < 0.05.

> "the percentage of children that get tired while reading, suffer from headaches, feel that their eyes cry or itch when they read, need to follow the text with their finger, or even get confused or miss words when reading, is lower in the group of children with good academic performance."
