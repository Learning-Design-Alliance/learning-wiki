Thematic analysis of students' open-text reflections in the pre- and post-semester surveys found ethical awareness; the article reports students "recognised both the benefits and limitations of GenAI".

Related Claims¶

  • Guided reflection embedded in self-assessment supports commencing students to engage responsibly and confidently with GenAI while maintaining academic integrity — a broader claim this one bears on
  • The study used pre- and post-semester surveys with Likert-scale literacy-confidence items and open-text ethical reflection, thematically analysed — related
  • Students exercise ethical agency in GenAI use through human-AI collaboration strategies, personal ethical frameworks, strategic calibration of use, and resistance to over-reliance — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Students raised ethical concerns about GenAI-assisted data comic creation, most frequently misinformation, bias, ownership, and reduced trustworthiness — related
  • Students reported increased personal responsibility and described the reflective self-assessment process as confidence-building during their transition to university — related
  • Student perceptions of AI use in academic work are neutral and range from viewing AI as valid learning assistance to viewing it as cheating, depending on task type, transparency, and assessment design — related
  • Critical-ethical awareness plays a dual role: it negatively predicts general GenAI reliance but positively predicts shortcut reliance — related
