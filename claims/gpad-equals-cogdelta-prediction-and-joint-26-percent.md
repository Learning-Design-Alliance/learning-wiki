## Evidence

### Kaito Kawakami 2024

Kaito Kawakami, Francesca Procopio, Kaili Rimfeld, Margherita Malanchini, Sophie von Stumm, Kathryn Asbury, Robert Plomin. (2024). Exploring the genetic prediction of academic underachievement and overachievement. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00251-9

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Hierarchical regression in the TEDS sample controlling for SES, sex, and age: GPAΔ semi-partial R2 was 0.185 and cogΔ 0.145, with overlapping confidence intervals, "indicating that they do not differ signi ﬁcantly."

> "Contrary to our hypothesis, our genomic index, GPAΔ at age 7, predicted as much of the variance in achievement slopes as cogΔ at age 7."

### Kaito Kawakami 2024 (2)

Kaito Kawakami, Francesca Procopio, Kaili Rimfeld, Margherita Malanchini, Sophie von Stumm, Kathryn Asbury, Robert Plomin. (2024). Exploring the genetic prediction of academic underachievement and overachievement. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00251-9

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multiple regression with both discrepancy indices in the same TEDS sample. Jointly they predicted R2 = 0.261; standardized betas were −0.351 for GPAΔ and −0.262 for cogΔ, each significant independent of the other.

> "We tested their complementarity in a multiple regression of the twoΔ measures as they predict achievement slopes and foundthat together the measures predicted substantially more variance in achievement slopes: R2 ¼ 0:261, as compared to 0.185 for GPAΔ and 0.145 for cogΔ."
