# Cross-grade score patterns show self-selection: lower-grade students taking a course-specific test tend to outperform students at the grade the course targets, and upper-grade students tend to underperform them.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Average test scores generally decreased as grades increased for each course-specific test, with Grade 7 students highest in Algebra 1 and Geometry and Grade 8 students highest in Algebra 2. [→ Thum 2019](#thum-2019)

## Evidence

### Thum 2019

Thum, Y. M., & He, W. (2019). Achievement and growth norms for course-specific MAP® Growth™ Algebra 1, Geometry, and Algebra 2 tests. NWEA Research Report. Portland, OR: NWEA. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/achievement-and-growth-norms-for-course-specific-map-growth-algebra-1-geometry-and-algebra-2-tests/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive summary statistics (Table 3.1) of RIT means and SDs by grade and term for Grades 6–12. The report states "average test scores decreased as grades increased" and that Grade 7 students achieved the highest average score in Algebra 1 and Geometry, Grade 8 in Algebra 2; means are descriptive with no significance tests reported.

> "With few exceptions, average test scores decreased as grades increased for each course-specific test. Lower-grade students (i.e., Grades 6–8 students) tend to perform better than the students of the grade at which a course is usually targeted"
