# Physics TAs graded elaborated student solutions higher than brief solutions in both intro physics and QM contexts, with the difference statistically significant only for the QM solution pair

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` TAs assigned higher average quiz scores to elaborated than to brief solutions in both the intro and QM contexts, and the difference was more pronounced for the QM solutions. [→ Charles Henderson 2016](#charles-henderson-2016)
`q2 i?` The score difference between the brief and elaborated solutions was statistically significant for the QM pair (p = 0.008) but not for the intro physics pair (p = 0.274). [→ Charles Henderson 2016 (2)](#charles-henderson-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Charles Henderson 2016

Charles Henderson, Emily Marshman, Ryan Sayer, Chandralekha Singh, Edit Yerushalmi. (2016). Graduate teaching assistants use different criteria when grading introductory physics vs. quantum mechanics problems. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.030](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.030)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Analysis of quiz-context scores assigned by the TAs in the professional development course: "TAs tended to grade the elaborated solutions higher than the brief solutions in both the intro and QM contexts", with the difference more pronounced for QM.

> "TAs tended to grade the elaborated solutions higher than the brief solutions in both the intro and QM contexts, but the difference was more pronounced for the QM solutions than for the intro physics solutions."

### Charles Henderson 2016 (2)

Charles Henderson, Emily Marshman, Ryan Sayer, Chandralekha Singh, Edit Yerushalmi. (2016). Graduate teaching assistants use different criteria when grading introductory physics vs. quantum mechanics problems. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.030](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.030)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

T-tests compared the average scores for the two solution pairs in the quiz context: "statistically significant between the QM solutions SS1 and SS2 (𝑝 = 0.008) but not statistically different for the two intro solutions (𝑝 = 0.274)".

> "We performed t-tests for comparison, and found that the differences in averages were statistically significant between the QM solutions SS1 and SS2 (𝑝 = 0.008) but not statistically different for the two intro solutions (𝑝 = 0.274)."
