# In an in-class study, introductory students used surface-feature categories (ramps, pulleys, springs) significantly less often than the eight volunteers in Chi et al.'s earlier study

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 associational `r1`–`r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2016 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Fewer than 15% of introductory students in the in-class study selected ramps, pulleys, or springs as categories, compared with much higher rates in the earlier volunteer study. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016)

`q2 i?` The percentage of students selecting surface-feature categories like inclined planes or pulleys was significantly lower in the large-class study than in the Chi study, even for the shared Chi problems. [→ Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#andrew-mason-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)

## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Categorization of problems to assess and improve proficiency as teachers and learners. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07677](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07677)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

In-class categorization by 180 introductory students a few weeks after instruction, compared with the eight student volunteers in Chi et al.'s out-of-classroom study, who more often created categories such as "ramps, pulleys or even springs" based on surface features. The article reports surface-feature category use was "signiﬁcantly less (less than 15% for each of these catego ries)".

> "is signiﬁcantly less (less than 15% for each of these catego ries) than in the study of Ref. 14. This diﬀerence could be due to the fact that ours was an in-class study with a large number of students"

### Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Categorization of Mechanics Problems by Students in Large Introductory Physics Courses: A Comparison with the Chi, Feltovich, and Glaser Study. Originally published 2012. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.08775](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.08775)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of category frequencies between the in-class study (109, 114, and 180 students) and the Chi study's 8 novices, reported in the results section. The article states the percentage selecting " inclined planes" or "pulleys" "is significantly less than in the Chi study"; no effect size is printed.

> "Table 1 shows that the percentage of introductory students in our study who selected " inclined planes" or "pulleys" as categories (based mainly upon the surface features) is significantly less than in the Chi study."
