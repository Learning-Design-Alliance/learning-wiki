# The post-ChatGPT response-time divergence on AI-susceptible items vanishes entirely under proctored assessment

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In non-proctored ALEKS PPL assessments, response times on AI-susceptible topics declined 1.11% per quarter relative to graph-based topics (11.6% cumulative). [→ Rismanchian 2026](#rismanchian-2026)

## Evidence

### Rismanchian 2026

Rismanchian, S., Uzun, H., Matayoshi, J., Cosyn, E., & Kurd-Misto, E. (2026). Faster Completion, Less Learning: Generative AI Reduced Study Time on Math Problems and the Knowledge They Build. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21629

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Ramp models on the ALEKS PPL placement-assessment panel of 12,190,662 response-time observations across 70 items, split by supervision condition. Non-proctored estimate "β=− 0.0112, p< 0.001", an 11.6% cumulative reduction.

> "In non-proctored assessments, response times for AI-susceptible topics decline by 1.11% per quarter relative to graph-based topics after ChatGPT's release (β=− 0.0112, p< 0.001)"

### Rismanchian 2026 (2)

Rismanchian, S., Uzun, H., Matayoshi, J., Cosyn, E., & Kurd-Misto, E. (2026). Faster Completion, Less Learning: Generative AI Reduced Study Time on Math Problems and the Knowledge They Build. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21629

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Identical fixed-effects ramp models estimated on the proctored subset of the ALEKS PPL response-time panel (about 4.5 million proctored responses) show no post-ChatGPT divergence. The authors treat this as a falsification test ruling out general efficiency gains.

> "In proctored assessments, the estimated post-ChatGPT slope and cumulative effect are effectively zero and statistically indistinguishable from it"
