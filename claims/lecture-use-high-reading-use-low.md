## Evidence

### Sohn Sangho 2019

Sohn Sangho, Lee Young-Mee, Jung Jaehun, Cha Eun Shil, Chun Byung Chul. (2019). The flipped classroom model for an undergraduate epidemiology course. Korean Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.3946/kjme.2019.122

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Survey of utilization and perception of pre-class materials across five sessions (Table 2). Viewing rates also "increased from 22.5% to 101% for the first three lectures". Percentages are descriptive; no effect size printed.

> "While more than half of the students (63 [66%]–75 [77%]) answered that they had watched the online lecture for each session at least once, more than half of the students (53 [55%]–65 [67%]) were found to be unprepared for the assigned readings."

### Sohn Sangho 2019 (2)

Sohn Sangho, Lee Young-Mee, Jung Jaehun, Cha Eun Shil, Chun Byung Chul. (2019). The flipped classroom model for an undergraduate epidemiology course. Korean Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.3946/kjme.2019.122

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Supplement 1 reasons for incomplete preparedness; additionally, 85 (36%) unprepared students said they did not know the assigned readings were to be studied beforehand. Descriptive percentages only.

> "Those who were less prepared for either the online lectures or assigned readings overwhelmingly stated that they had not had enough time to study the materials in advance (40 [70%] and 114 [49%], respectively)."
