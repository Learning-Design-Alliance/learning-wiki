## Evidence

### Nixon 2026

Nixon, N., Samadi, M. A., Choi, J., Park, S., De Bastos, P. M., & JaQuay, S. (2026). The Social Cost of an AI Teammate: How an Artificial Teammate Reshapes Human-Human Communication in Small-Team Decision-Making. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2607.27179](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.27179)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Survey mixed-effects models (outcome ~ condition + (1|team)) on 75 students using an 11-item belonging composite and a 12-item status composite on 1-7 Likert scales; only fixed-effect estimates and p-values are printed, no standardized effect sizes.

> "Relative to control students, treatment students reported lower belonging (b=−0.695,SE= 0.266, 95% CI[−1.217,−0.174], q=.012), status (b=−0.332,SE= 0.106, 95% CI[−0.540,−0.124],q=.005), and felt connection (b=−1.121,SE= 0.374, 95% CI[−1.855,−0.388], q=.005)."

### Nixon 2026 (2)

Nixon, N., Samadi, M. A., Choi, J., Park, S., De Bastos, P. M., & JaQuay, S. (2026). The Social Cost of an AI Teammate: How an Artificial Teammate Reshapes Human-Human Communication in Small-Team Decision-Making. arXiv preprint. `q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The felt-value single-item outcome showed a negative but imprecise estimate (q=.122); the null is reported without claiming equivalence.

> "Felt value was also lower, but the estimate was imprecise and did not survive FDR adjustment (b=−0.444,SE= 0.287, 95% CI[−1.007,0.118], q=.122)."
