# Diagnostic accuracy is higher for standardized patients than for virtual patients, and the two methods were not equivalent in grading

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Diagnostic accuracy was higher for SPs than for VPs (2-tailed t85=2.49; P=.01; Cohen d=0.27), and equivalence within ±0.125 points was not supported. [→ Fink Maximilian C 2021](#fink-maximilian-c-2021)

## Evidence

### Fink Maximilian C 2021

Fink Maximilian C, Reitmeier Victoria, Stadler Matthias, Siebeck Matthias, Fischer Frank, Fischer Martin R. (2021). Assessment of Diagnostic Competences With Standardized Patients Versus Virtual Patients: Experimental Study in the Context of History Taking. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/21196

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Paired t test in the 86-student repeated-measures experiment; descriptive means were 0.51 (SPs) versus 0.41 (VPs). The TOST procedure could not establish equivalence within the ±0.125-point boundary (t85=−0.60; P=.28).

> "The finding that diagnostic accuracy was higher for SPs than for VPs also corresponds to the result of a paired sample t test (2-tailed t85=2.49; P=.01; Cohen d=0.27)."

### Fink Maximilian C 2021 (2)

Fink Maximilian C, Reitmeier Victoria, Stadler Matthias, Siebeck Matthias, Fischer Frank, Fischer Martin R. (2021). Assessment of Diagnostic Competences With Standardized Patients Versus Virtual Patients: Experimental Study in the Context of History Taking. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/21196

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Follow-up three-way mixed design ANOVA on diagnostic accuracy; the main effect of assessment method had "F3,82=6.30; P=.01; η2=0.07", while CG order (P=.12) and assessment method order (P=.88) were nonsignificant.

> "The assessment method itself, however, had a significant main effect (F3,82=6.30; P=.01; η2=0.07), indicating that diagnostic accuracy was higher for SPs than for VPs."
