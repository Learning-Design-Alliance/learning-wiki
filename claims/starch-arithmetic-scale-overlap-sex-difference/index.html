Evidence¶

Kelley 1915¶

Kelley, T. L. (1915). Report of the Twenty-fourth Annual Meeting of the American Psychological Association, Chicago, December 28-30, 1915. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://www.jstor.org/journal/jeducpsych

q2 · i? · review · r1

Meeting-report description of Starch's arithmetic scale of twelve problems scaled by pass percentages across grades four to eight. The report prints the overlap figure ("Approximately 33% of any grade reach or exceed the median of the grade above") and the sex difference.

"This consists of twelve problems, scaled for difficulty upon the basis of percentage of pupils who passed them. The distributions for grades four to eight are very much like the distributions in other traits which have been measured. Decided overlapping from grade to grade was shown. Approximately 33% of any grade reach or exceed the median of the grade above. An unusually strong sex difference was revealed—approximately 60% of the boys reaching or exceeding the median of the girls."

Learner Variables¶

  • Gender — predictor: outcomes differ by group: a gap reflecting circumstances and treatment, not a cause

Related Claims¶

  • Corrected measures of overlapping show 33 per cent correct classification rather than the 17 per cent previously reported, and 19.5 per cent rather than 33 per cent exceeding or falling short of the adjacent grade's median — related
  • Boys are more likely than girls to rank at both the top and the bottom of the grade scale, supporting greater variability of excellence among boys — related
  • Sex differences in arithmetic work are slight: girls do about 4% more work and make about 4% fewer errors than boys, but boys lead in the eighth grade — reports the opposite
  • School grades show very wide variation among teachers, shrinking little within a single institution (Starch) — related
