# Elementary attendance improved more in schools that were strong or growing on 5Essentials Survey measures, in both pooled and within-school models

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · associational `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Schools strong on measures improved attendance up to 0.48 percentage points more than average schools. [→ Hart 2020](#hart-2020)
`q2 i?` Schools growing more than average on measures improved attendance up to 0.51 percentage points more. [→ Hart 2020 (2)](#hart-2020-2)
`q2 i?` In years when schools grew in their measures, attendance improved up to 0.61 percentage points more than in years of average growth. [→ Hart 2020 (3)](#hart-2020-3)

## Evidence

### Hart 2020

Hart, H., Young, C., Chen, A., Zou, A., & Allensworth, E.M. (2020). Supporting school improvement: Early findings from reexamination of the 5Essentials survey. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/supporting-school-improvement

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Pooled regression model of elementary attendance, where strong means one standard deviation above average on a measure. For nearly all measures, above-average strength was positively and significantly related to improvement; the report notes "Small amounts of variation in attendance at the elementary level left little remaining variation that could be explained by 5Essentials Survey measures."

> "Schools that were strong on 5Essentials Survey measures improved their attendance rate as much as 0.48 percentage points more than schools that were average on measures (see Figure 3.A)."

### Hart 2020 (2)

Hart, H., Young, C., Chen, A., Zou, A., & Allensworth, E.M. (2020). Supporting school improvement: Early findings from reexamination of the 5Essentials survey. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/supporting-school-improvement

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Pooled regression growth results for elementary attendance: for nearly all measures, above-average measure growth was positively and significantly related to attendance rate improvement, shown in Figure 3.B.

> "Schools that grew more than average on 5Essentials Survey measures improved their attendance rate as much as 0.51 percentage points more than schools that showed an average amount of change in measures (see Figure 3.B)."

### Hart 2020 (3)

Hart, H., Young, C., Chen, A., Zou, A., & Allensworth, E.M. (2020). Supporting school improvement: Early findings from reexamination of the 5Essentials survey. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/supporting-school-improvement

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

School fixed effects model comparing schools to themselves across years; for all measures, above-average growth in a given year was positively and significantly related to attendance improvement, shown in Figure 3.D.

> "In years when schools grew in their 5Essentials Survey measures, they improved their attendance rate as much as 0.61 percentage points more than in years when measures showed an average amount of change ( see Figure 3.D)."
