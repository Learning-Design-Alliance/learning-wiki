# Students in high-IAS Astro 101 classes (IAS ≥ 25%) reach significantly higher average post-instruction IRT-estimated ability than students in low-IAS classes (Cohen's d = 0.80)

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Post-instruction mean ability differs significantly between high-IAS and low-IAS classes, with a large effect size (Cohen's d = 0.80, p < 0.0001). [→ Colin S. Wallace 2017](#colin-s-wallace-2017)

## Evidence

### Colin S. Wallace 2017

Colin S. Wallace, Timothy G. Chambers, Edward E. Prather. (2017). An item response theory evaluation of the Light and Spectroscopy Concept Inventory national data set. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1709.05255](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1709.05255)

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of post-instruction IRT-estimated abilities between students in high-IAS classes (2178 students, post mean 0.23 logits) and low-IAS classes (363 students, post mean -0.45 logits) from the LSCI national data set, using a one-tailed t-test for two independent samples. The article reports the difference was "statistically signiﬁcant ( p < 0.0001)" with "Cohen's d = 0.80".

> "A one-tailed t-test for two independent samples revealed that the diﬀerence in the post-instruction means was statistically signiﬁcant ( p < 0.0001) and of large eﬀect size (Cohen's d = 0.80)"
