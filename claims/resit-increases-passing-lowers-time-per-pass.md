## Evidence

### Nijenkamp 2016

Nijenkamp, R., Nieuwenstein, M. R., de Jong, R., & Lorist, M. M. (2016). Do Resit Exams Promote Lower Investments of Study Time? Theory and Data from a Laboratory Study. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0161708

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1 results (Table 1): overall passing probability was 89.2% (SD = 6.2) for R1&R2 combined versus 77.6% (SD = 9.5) for NR, while study time per passed exam was 5.8 (SD = 0.5) versus 6.3 (SD = 0.5), with "the overall probability for passing an exam was higher in the resit condition".

> "the overall probability for passing an exam was higher in the resit condition, but the average amount of study time invested per passed exam was lower for this condition"
