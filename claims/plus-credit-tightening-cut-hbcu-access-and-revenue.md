# The 2011 tightening of Parent PLUS credit standards sharply reduced PLUS access at HBCUs and cost them about $168 million

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After the Department of Education tightened Parent PLUS credit standards in October 2011, the share of PLUS recipients at HBCUs fell 45.7% in 2012-13 versus 28.7% at non-HBCUs serving low-income students, and HBCUs lost about $168 million in PLUS revenue. ## Evidence

### Victoria Jackson 2023

Victoria Jackson, Brittani Williams, and Jalil B. Mustaffa. (2023). Parent PLUS Loans Are a Double-Edged Sword for Black Borrowers. The Education Trust. https://edtrust.org/resource/parent-plus-loans-are-a-double-edged-sword-for-black-borrowers

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

The brief reports figures from a Mathematica study on changes in financial aid and enrollment after the tightening of PLUS credit standards, stating the "share of PLUS loan recipients at HBCUs fell by 45.7%" versus "only 28.7%" at comparable non-HBCUs, and that HBCUs lost about $168 million in PLUS loan revenue.

> "During the 2012-13 academic year, the share of PLUS loan recipients at HBCUs fell by 45.7% from the previous academic year. At non-HBCUs that serve students from low-income families, the decline was only 28.7%."
