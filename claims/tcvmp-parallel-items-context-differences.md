## Evidence

### J Guisasola 2024

J Guisasola, J Ametller, D Baccino, M Carli, S Kapon, A C Marti, M Monteiro, O Pantano, T Peer, P Sarriugarte, M Schvartzer, A Suárez and K Zuza. (2024). Connecting research in physics education, curriculum decisions and teaching practices. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2407.10674](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2407.10674)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of the first administration of the TCV-MP multiple-choice test to 1252 first-year Science and Engineering students in 2018, comparing parallel mathematics/physics items on derivatives. The authors suggest the common incorrect conceptualization of velocity as "space over time" played a crucial role in selecting distractor D in physics.

> "The answer profile was quite different between items 3M and 3P, with many more students choosing the most common incorrect answer (option D, corresponding to calculating y/x or s/t) in physics than in mathematics."

### J Guisasola 2024 (2)

J Guisasola, J Ametller, D Baccino, M Carli, S Kapon, A C Marti, M Monteiro, O Pantano, T Peer, P Sarriugarte, M Schvartzer, A Suárez and K Zuza. (2024). Connecting research in physics education, curriculum decisions and teaching practices. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2407.10674](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2407.10674)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same 2018 administration (N=1252), comparing items 3P and 2P on the same topic with different representations; item 2P's percent correct was also comparable with item 2M (not shown, 64%), indicating a role of representational format.

> "The percent of correct answers in item 2P, which did not involve calculation, was higher than in answer 3P and more aligned to that of item 3M."
