## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In one observational comparison across CINAHL, Embase, and MEDLINE, PICO searches retrieved higher sensitivity and lower precision than PICOS and SPIDER searches, but the models used different numbers of search blocks (PICO: 3, PICOS: 4, SPIDER: 6), so the results are expected on that basis. [→ Mette Brandt Eriksen 2018](#mette-brandt-eriksen-2018)

## Evidence

### Mette Brandt Eriksen 2018

Mette Brandt Eriksen, PhD; Tove Faber Frandsen, PhD. (2018). The impact of patient, intervention, comparison, outcome (PICO) as a search strategy tool on literature search quality: a systematic review. Journal of the Medical Library Association 106 (4). https://doi.org/10.5195/jmla.2018.345

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Observational comparison (Methley et al., 2014) of identical search terms combined via PICO, PICOS, or SPIDER across PubMed, Embase, and CINAHL, yielding nine searches with relevance judged against inclusion criteria. Table 3 prints, e.g., PICO sensitivity of 77.78% in CINAHL versus 66.67% for PICOS and SPIDER. The review notes "as different numbers of search blocks were used for each model... these results are expected."

> "Although hardly conclusive due to extremely limited data, the use of PICO as a search strategy tool resulted in higher sensitivity and lower precision than the use of PICOS and SPIDER."
