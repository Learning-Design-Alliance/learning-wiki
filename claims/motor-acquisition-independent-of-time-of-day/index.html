Evidence¶

Charlène Truong 2023¶

Charlène Truong, Célia Ruffino, Jérémie Gaveau, Olivier White, Pauline M. Hilt and Charalambos Papaxanthis. (2023). Time of day and sleep effects on motor acquisition and consolidation. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00176-9

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Main experiment: 36 healthy adults randomly assigned to G10am, G3pm, or G8pm trained 48 trials on a finger-tapping task; the "T1_T2 gain was not signi ﬁcantly different between groups", with Bayesian equivalence tests supporting the null. The authors conclude the "acquisition process is independent of the time-of-day".

"The T1_T2 gain was not signi ﬁcantly different between groups (one-way ANOVA: F 2,33 = 0.41, p = 0.67, η2 = 0.02; Bayesian equivalence tests: BF OH 01 >2 . 1 2 a n d BFNOH 01 > 2.38 in all cases)."

Related Claims¶

  • Twenty-four-hour consolidation of motor skill differs according to the time of day of training, with deterioration in the morning, stabilization in the afternoon, and improvement in the evening — related
  • Skill consolidation after evening training is comparable when retested 2 h versus 12 h after waking, independent of time awake the day after training — related
  • The evening group's 24 h skill improvement reflects offline learning rather than a simple continuation of practice — related
  • Evening-trained adolescents show greater gains in finger-tapping accuracy (reduced error rate) than afternoon-trained adolescents at both retention intervals — related
  • Adolescents trained on a finger-tapping task in the evening show greater off-line gains in tapping speed than afternoon-trained adolescents at both 24 hours and 7 days — related
  • Learning timing produces Group X Time interactions in both memory systems: declarative consolidation time courses differ between afternoon and evening groups, and finger-tapping speed and accuracy show highly significant Group X Time interactions — related
  • Adolescents trained on word pairs in the afternoon show higher word-pair retention after 24 hours than evening-trained adolescents, but the difference is not sustained at 7 days — related
  • Polysomnographic sleep parameters did not differ between afternoon- and evening-trained groups and did not correlate with memory consolidation measures — related
  • Participants improved tapping speed from the first block to the block where 95% of learning was achieved (d = −1.72) — related
  • Mere passage of time awake after evening training does not produce offline skill improvement; the night of sleep is the key factor — related
