## Evidence

### Jennifer K. Olsen & Nikol Rummel & Vincent Aleven 2021

Jennifer K. Olsen & Nikol Rummel & Vincent Aleven. (2021). Designing for the co-Orchestration of Social Transitions between Individual, Small-Group and Whole-Class Learning in the Classroom. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education 31:24–56. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-020-00228-w

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Finding from the Study 1 co-design sessions (thematic analysis of teacher dialogue). Teachers valued one-on-one help highly; the authors note that when orchestration load is too high, teachers cannot spend this one-on-one time with students. Qualitative; no effect size printed.

> "Across all three sessions, the teachers expressed that they “get so much more done, so much faster one-to-one while everyone else is going ” (T7) and felt that working one-on-one with students who seem to be stuck is one of the best uses of their time for supporting student learning."

### Jennifer K. Olsen & Nikol Rummel & Vincent Aleven 2021 (2)

Jennifer K. Olsen & Nikol Rummel & Vincent Aleven. (2021). Designing for the co-Orchestration of Social Transitions between Individual, Small-Group and Whole-Class Learning in the Classroom. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education 31:24–56. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-020-00228-w

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Study 1 finding on teachers' willingness to invest time: teachers described frontloading collaboration skills ('it is a ton of frontloading to get them to collaborate') and teaching routines so students can take over parts of the orchestration load. Qualitative; no effect size printed.

> "Besides teaching collaboration skills, all of the teachers felt that classroom routines were necessary for the class to run smoothly. Once students internalize these class rhythms, they are able to take some of the orchestration load off of the teacher."
