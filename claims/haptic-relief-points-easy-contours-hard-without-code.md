# In preliminary haptic testing, relief dots were easily associated with celestial objects, while contours and diverse dot densities were hardly recognizable without an explicit code

## Evidence

### Varano 2023

Varano, S., and Zanella, A. (2023). Design and evaluation of a multi-sensory representation of scientific data. Frontiers. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.3389/feduc.2023.1082249](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2023.1082249)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Qualitative questionnaire and debrief testing of two tactile maps rendered with the Minolta relief-printing technique, with 27 adult users (13 sighted, 14 BVI). Relief dots in the general map "was easily associated with the presence of celestial objects".

> "The presence of relief dots in the general map (Fig. 1, panel c) was easily associated with the presence of celestial objects."

### Varano 2023 (2)

Varano, S., and Zanella, A. (2023). Design and evaluation of a multi-sensory representation of scientific data. Frontiers. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.3389/feduc.2023.1082249](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2023.1082249)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Summary of the same preliminary haptic phase (27 users): "Contours and diverse densities of points were hardly recognizable without an explicit code", so the general map with one relief point per object was chosen for autonomous fruition.

> "Relief points were easily associated with signal. Contours and diverse densities of points were hardly recognizable without an explicit code."
