## Evidence

### Burgess A 2020

Burgess A, van Diggele C, Roberts C, Mellis C. (2020). Feedback in the clinical setting. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02280-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r1`

The review identifies lack of direct observation as a barrier, noting that "In the busy clinical setting, direct observation is often lacking" even though feedback based on direct observation of a specific task has the greatest impact on students' behaviour. No effect size is printed.

> "Feedback has the greatest impact on students' behaviour when it is provided based on direct observation of a specific task [2]. In the busy clinical setting, direct observation is often lacking."

### Burgess A 2020 (2)

Burgess A, van Diggele C, Roberts C, Mellis C. (2020). Feedback in the clinical setting. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02280-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports that reluctance to give honest critical feedback when performance is below par can produce "vanishing feedback", where meaningful feedback is avoided. This is a narrative-review assertion attributed to cited literature, with no effect size printed.

> "Although there may be a desire to avoid upsetting a learner, this can result in "vanishing feedback" [8], where meaningful feedback is avoided."
