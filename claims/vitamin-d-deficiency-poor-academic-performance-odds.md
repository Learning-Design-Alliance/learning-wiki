## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In multivariable logistic regression, vitamin D deficiency (25(OH)D < 20 ng/mL) was associated with increased odds of poor academic performance (AOR = 2.18, 95% CI 1.20–3.96). [→ Hassan Ahmed A. 2023](#hassan-ahmed-a-2023)

## Evidence

### Hassan Ahmed A. 2023

Hassan Ahmed A., Elbashir Mustafa I., Al-Nafeesah Abdullah, AlEed Ashwaq, Adam Ishag. (2023). Association between Serum 25-Hydroxyvitamin D Concentrations and Academic Performance among Adolescent Schoolchildren: A Cross-Sectional Study. Nutrients 15(21):4552. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu15214552

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariable logistic regression in the same cross-sectional study (n = 241) comparing poor versus good academic performance; deficiency was defined as serum 25(OH)D < 20 ng/mL. The article reports "AOR = 2.18, 95% CI 1.20–3.96"; no standardized effect size printed.

> "vitamin D deficiency (AOR = 2.18, 95% CI 1.20–3.96) was associated with increased odds of poor academic performance"

### Hassan Ahmed A. 2023 (2)

Hassan Ahmed A., Elbashir Mustafa I., Al-Nafeesah Abdullah, AlEed Ashwaq, Adam Ishag. (2023). Association between Serum 25-Hydroxyvitamin D Concentrations and Academic Performance among Adolescent Schoolchildren: A Cross-Sectional Study. Nutrients 15(21):4552. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu15214552

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bivariate comparison (Mann–Whitney U test) of median 25(OH)D between the poor and good performance groups in the cross-sectional study; medians and p-value printed, no effect size.

> "The median (IQR) of serum 25(OH)D was significantly lower among adolescents with poor academic performance compared to adolescents with good academic performance (17.65 [8.0–27.78] vs. 25.0 [14.72–36.44], p = 0.001)"
