# Adventure therapy shows little change during the lead-in period (.09) and follow-up (.03), indicating retention of short-term gains

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The lead-in (Base-Pre) effect is small and non-significant (.09) and the longer-term (Post-Follow-Up) effect is very small, positive and non-significant (.03), indicating the short-term gains are retained. [→ Daniel J. Bowen and James T. Neill 2013](#daniel-j-bowen-and-james-t-neill-2013)

## Evidence

### Daniel J. Bowen and James T. Neill 2013

Daniel J. Bowen and James T. Neill. (2013). A Meta-Analysis of Adventure Therapy Outcomes and Moderators. The Open Psychology Journal, 2013, 6, 28-53. http://www.daniel-bowen.com.au/meta-analysis

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analytic Post-Follow-Up comparison across 435 effect sizes found g = .03, non-significant, which the authors read as "retention of the short-term gains". The only significant longer-term change was for the Behaviour outcome category (.21).

> "The longer-term effect for the Adventure Therapy group was very small, positive and non-significant (.03), indicating retention of the short-term gains."
