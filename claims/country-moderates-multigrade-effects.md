# Country of publication moderates multigrade cognitive effects, with a small negative effect only for European studies

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` Country of publication significantly predicted variance in multigrade cognitive effects: small positive effects for United States (d = .05) and Canadian (d = .08) studies and a small negative effect for European studies (d = -.05). [→ Simon Veenman 1996](#simon-veenman-1996)

## Evidence

### Simon Veenman 1996

Simon Veenman. (1996). Effects of multigrade and multi-age classes reconsidered. Review of Educational Research, 66(3), 323-340. https://doi.org/10.2307/1170526

`q3 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Between-study analysis of the multigrade cognitive subset (Table 3): United States d = .05 (k = 22), Canada d = .08 (k = 3), Europe d = -.05 (k = 14); Q(2, N = 39) = 17.2, p < .001. Studies from developing countries were excluded as outliers.

> "The results of the between-study analysis showed the country of publication to predict a significant amount of variance in effects, Q(2,N =  39) = 17.2, p < .001."
