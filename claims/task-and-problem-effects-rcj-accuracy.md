# Retrospective metacognitive accuracy for strategy selection is higher in the selection-only task than the selection-execution task and on homogeneous than heterogeneous unit-digit problems

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` RCJ accuracy was higher for selection-only (.67) than selection-execution (.60), and higher on homogeneous (.71) than heterogeneous (.57) problems. [→ Marie Geurten & Patrick Lemaire 2018](#marie-geurten-patrick-lemaire-2018)

## Evidence

### Marie Geurten & Patrick Lemaire 2018

Geurten, M., & Lemaire, P. (2018). Metacognition for strategy selection during arithmetic problem-solving in young and older adults. *Aging, Neuropsychology, and Cognition, 26*(3), 424–446. [doi:10.1080/13825585.2018.1464114](https://doi.org/10.1080/13825585.2018.1464114)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA main effects on RCJ A'ROC in the same 66-participant experiment: "higher metacognitive accuracy for the selection-only task (.67) than for the selection-execution task (.60), F(1,61) = 13.39, MSe = 1.65, η²p = .17, B > 10", and homogeneous (.71) exceeded heterogeneous (.57), η²p = .42, B > 10.

> "Participants also had higher metacognitive accuracy for the selection-only task (.67) than for the selection-execution task (.60), F(1,61) = 13.39, MSe = 1.65, η²p = .17, B > 10. They also had higher metacognitive accuracy on problems with homogeneous unit digits (.71) than on problems with heterogeneous unit digits (.57), F(1,61) = 45.87, MSe = 1.87, η²p = .42, B > 10"
