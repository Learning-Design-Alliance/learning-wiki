## Evidence

### Zhou Jie 2026

Zhou Jie, Zhang Guowen, Song Peige, Liaw Sok Ying, Luk Tzu Tsun. (2026). Effects of High‐Fidelity Simulation Training on Learning Outcomes and Satisfaction for Practising Registered Nurses: A Systematic Review and Meta‐Analysis. Journal of Clinical Nursing. https://doi.org/10.1111/jocn.70206

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random-effects meta-analysis of two RCTs and two quasi-experiments (140 nurses) on self-confidence. The review reports "no statistically significant effect on self‐confidence"; the fixed-effect model yielded SMD = 0.48, 95% CI [0.14, 0.83], p < 0.01. Equivalence was not tested.

> "The random‐effects meta‐analysis showed no statistically significant effect on self‐confidence (SMD = 0.59, 95% CI, [−0.04, 1.22], p = 0.07)"

### Zhou Jie 2026 (2)

Zhou Jie, Zhang Guowen, Song Peige, Liaw Sok Ying, Luk Tzu Tsun. (2026). Effects of High‐Fidelity Simulation Training on Learning Outcomes and Satisfaction for Practising Registered Nurses: A Systematic Review and Meta‐Analysis. Journal of Clinical Nursing. https://doi.org/10.1111/jocn.70206

`q2 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Subgroup analysis by study design of the self-confidence meta-analysis. The review reports RCTs showed no effect (SMD = 0.08) while quasi-experiments showed a large significant effect (SMD = 1.19), indicating "the study design and context of the training might influence outcomes".

> "Subgroups analysis revealed the study design contributed to heterogeneity. Specifically, RCTs showed no effect (SMD = 0.08, 95% CI, [−0.35, 0.52]), while quasi‐experiments showed a large significant effect (SMD = 1.19, 95% CI, [0.61, 1.76])"
