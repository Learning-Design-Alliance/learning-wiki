# Students with GPT hints enabled clicked to view regular platform feedback on failed tests significantly less often than controls on those tasks

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2023 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` On tasks with GPT feedback enabled, the experimental group's median percentage of incorrect submissions with a feedback click was significantly lower than the control group's (W=282, p<0.001). [→ Maciej Pankiewicz & Ryan S. Baker 2023](#maciej-pankiewicz-ryan-s-baker-2023)
`q3 i?` On tasks where GPT feedback was unavailable, feedback-click rates did not differ significantly between groups (W=341, p=0.269). [→ Maciej Pankiewicz & Ryan S. Baker 2023 (2)](#maciej-pankiewicz-ryan-s-baker-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Maciej Pankiewicz & Ryan S. Baker 2023

Maciej Pankiewicz & Ryan S. Baker. (2023). Large Language Models (GPT) for automating feedback on programming assignments. Proceedings of the 31st International Conference on Computers in Education. [doi:10.58459/icce.2023.950](https://doi.org/10.58459/icce.2023.950)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mann-Whitney U comparison of the percentage of incorrect submissions with at least one click to view failed-test details, in the randomized two-group experiment; "The experimental group used the regular feedback significantly less than the control group for these tasks."

> "A statistically significant difference was found between the experimental (Mdn=0.3 21, N=45) and control (Mdn=0.710, N=45) groups (W=282, p<0.001, Mann-Whitney U test) for the set of tasks where experimental group had GPT feedback enabled (38 out of 46)."

### Maciej Pankiewicz & Ryan S. Baker 2023 (2)

Maciej Pankiewicz & Ryan S. Baker. (2023). Large Language Models (GPT) for automating feedback on programming assignments. Proceedings of the 31st International Conference on Computers in Education. [doi:10.58459/icce.2023.950](https://doi.org/10.58459/icce.2023.950)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same analysis on the 8 tasks without GPT hints: no significant difference in click rates; when GPT feedback was unavailable, the experimental group "utilized the platform's regular feedback in a similar amount" to controls.

> "However, no significant difference was found between the experimental (Mdn=0.76 9, N=23) and control (Mdn=0.667, N=25) groups (W=341, p=0.269, Mann-Whitney U test) for the set of tasks, where GPT feedback was not available for experimental group (8 out of 46)."
