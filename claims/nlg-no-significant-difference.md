## Evidence

### Sherry Ruan 2024

Sherry Ruan, Allen Nie, William Steenbergen, Jiayu He, JQ Zhang, Meng Guo, Yao Liu, Kyle Dang Nguyen, Catherine Y Wang, Rui Ying, James A Landay, Emma Brunskill. (2024). Reinforcement Learning Tutor Better Supported Lower Performers in a Math Task. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1007/s10994-023-06423-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-023-06423-9)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary analysis using normalized learning gains, computed to address possible ceiling effects and excluding students with maximum pretest scores. The article reports no significant condition difference in either study; equivalence was not tested.

> "There was no signiﬁcant diﬀerence between the RL narrative condition and control condition for NLG in either case (study 1, W = 4394.5, p-value = 0.6978; study 2, W = 104.5, p-value = 0.3819)."

### Sherry Ruan 2024 (2)

Sherry Ruan, Allen Nie, William Steenbergen, Jiayu He, JQ Zhang, Meng Guo, Yao Liu, Kyle Dang Nguyen, Catherine Y Wang, Rui Ying, James A Landay, Emma Brunskill. (2024). Reinforcement Learning Tutor Better Supported Lower Performers in a Math Task. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1007/s10994-023-06423-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-023-06423-9)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subgroup NLG pattern shown in Figure 4 bottom row, described in the results text. The article cautions the highest-performing group's NLG pattern should be taken lightly because the NLG analysis excludes maximum pretest scorers.

> "Like for posttest - pretest, we observe larger normalized learning gains for the RL narrative condition than the control condition for initially lower performing stu- dents, in both studies (Figure 4, bottom row)."
