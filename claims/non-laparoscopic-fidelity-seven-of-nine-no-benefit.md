## Evidence

### Lefor Alan Kawarai 2020

Lefor Alan Kawarai, Harada Kanako, Kawahira Hiroshi, Mitsuishi Mamoru. (2020). The effect of simulator fidelity on procedure skill training: a literature review. International Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.5ea6.ae73

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Synthesis of the nine non-laparoscopic studies among the 17 reviewed, covering skills such as trans-bronchial needle aspiration, intubation, and cricothyroidotomy. The review reports "7/9 showed" HF training was not better than LF.

> "Of nine studies of non-laparoscopic procedure training, 7/9 showed that training with the HF simulator was not better than training with a LF simulator."

### Lefor Alan Kawarai 2020 (2)

Lefor Alan Kawarai, Harada Kanako, Kawahira Hiroshi, Mitsuishi Mamoru. (2020). The effect of simulator fidelity on procedure skill training: a literature review. International Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.5ea6.ae73

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review identifies the two exceptional studies favoring HF training: intravenous catheter insertion and vascular anastomosis skills. In the vascular anastomosis study, time and global rating scores were similar but the HF group scored higher on overall performance in the animal surgery component.

> "The two studies that concluded that participants trained with HF simulators have better performance than those trained on LF simulators included a study of intravenous catheter insertion3 and a study of vascular anastomosis skills."
