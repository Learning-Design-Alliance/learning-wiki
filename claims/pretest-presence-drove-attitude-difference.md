# The only significant pairwise post-test difference in attitudes was between two e-learning-taught groups differing only in pre-test exposure, favouring the pre-tested group

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Scheffe pairwise comparisons showed a significant difference between E and C2 (mean difference 12.224, p = 0.006) in favour of E, while E vs C1 (p = 0.199) and C1 vs C2 (p = 0.168) were not significant. [→ Daniel M. Getuno 2015](#daniel-m-getuno-2015)
`q2 i?` The authors interpret that the presence or absence of the pre-test, not the teaching method, was solely responsible for the significant post-test difference. [→ Daniel M. Getuno 2015](#daniel-m-getuno-2015)

## Evidence

### Daniel M. Getuno 2015

Daniel M. Getuno, Joel K. Kiboss, Johnson M. Changeiywo, & Leo B. Ogola. (2015). Effects of an E-Learning Module on Students' Attitudes in an Electronics Class. Journal of Education and Practice, Vol.6, No.36. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1086475

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pairwise Scheffe comparisons on estimated marginal means of post-test attitude scores: E vs C2 mean difference 12.224 (Sig. 0.006), E vs C1 5.850 (Sig. 0.199), C1 vs C2 6.374 (Sig. 0.168). Only the E-C2 contrast was significant, "in favour of group E"; no effect size is printed.

> "The results of the pairwise comparison test in Tabl e 7 show that statistically significant differences  exist in students' attitudes between E and C 2 (12.224*) in favour of group E. No significant dif ferences however, exist between the groups E and C 1 and between C 1 and C 2."
