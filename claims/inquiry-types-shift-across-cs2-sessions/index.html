Evidence¶

Matin Amoozadeh and Amin Alipour 2026¶

Matin Amoozadeh and Amin Alipour. (2026). Analysis of Types of Inquiries in Student-AI Interaction: A case study of two CS2 tasks. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17919 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2608.17919

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Chi-square comparison of question-category distributions between the two lab sessions. The article reports χ²(17) = 27.03, p = .0576, a near-significant trend only; no effect size is printed.

"A chi-square test of independence revealed a near-significant difference between the two sessions, 𝜒 2(17)= 27.03, 𝑝=. 0576, indicating a trend toward a shift in questioning behavior from confusion-reporting in Session 1 toward more procedural and task-oriented inquiry in Session 2, though this difference did not reach conventional statistical significance."

Matin Amoozadeh and Amin Alipour 2026 (2)¶

Matin Amoozadeh and Amin Alipour. (2026). Analysis of Types of Inquiries in Student-AI Interaction: A case study of two CS2 tasks. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17919

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Descriptive category counts across sessions from the 830-prompt dataset. Instrumental/procedural rose from 79 to 104 prompts; the article also reports verification decreased (S1 = 88, S2 = 66) and Request/Directive increased (S1 = 41, S2 = 52).

"Instrumen- tal/procedural prompts became the dominant category in Session 2 (S2 = 104; S1 = 79), indicating that students increasingly asked how to accomplish tasks or follow specific steps as they engaged with more complex programming challenges."
