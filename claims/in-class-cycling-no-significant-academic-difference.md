# Low-intensity in-class cycling during a 12-week college lecture course shows no statistically significant difference from sitting on test scores or course grades

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2017 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After 12 weeks of low-intensity cycling during lectures, cycling students (CYC, n=9) did not differ significantly from sitting students (SIT, n=12) on average test scores (p = 0.431) or final course grades (p = 0.185), although CYC had descriptively higher means and a higher overall letter grade (B+ vs B). [→ Joubert Lanae; Kilgas Matthew; Riley Alexandrea; Gautam Yuba; Donath Lars; Drum Scott 2017](#joubert-lanae-kilgas-matthew-riley-alexandrea-gautam-yuba-donath-lars-drum-scott-2017)

## Evidence

### Joubert Lanae; Kilgas Matthew; Riley Alexandrea; Gautam Yuba; Donath Lars; Drum Scott 2017

Joubert Lanae; Kilgas Matthew; Riley Alexandrea; Gautam Yuba; Donath Lars; Drum Scott. (2017). In-Class Cycling to Augment College Student Academic Performance and Reduce Physical Inactivity: Results from an RCT. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph14111343

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Semester-long randomized trial in an undergraduate exercise physiology lecture course: 21 students randomized by a PA-score × GPA stratification factor; four written tests graded blind to condition. No statistical significance between CYC vs. SIT for average of test scores "(p = 0.431)" or final grades "(p = 0.185)"; no effect size printed.

> "Although no statistical significance between CYC vs. SIT for average of test scores (p = 0.431) or final course grades (p = 0.185), CYC had higher mean test scores on all tests and a higher overall course letter grade (i.e., B+ vs. B, respectively)."
