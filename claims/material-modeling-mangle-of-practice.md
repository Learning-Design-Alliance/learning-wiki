## Evidence

### Sengupta 2016

Sengupta, P., & Clark, D. (2016). Playing Modeling Games in the Science Classroom: The Case for Disciplinary Integration. Educational Technology, 56(3), 16–22. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1607.05094](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1607.05094)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

In a study (Krinks, Sengupta, & Clark, 2015) with four class periods of SURGE NextG game play followed by a physical modeling activity using "a marble, track, stopwatch and ruler", students struggled to "create[] mathematical measures (i.e., units) of speed and acceleration"; the mangle then extended into the virtual nebula level.

> "Students encountered the “mangle of practice” (Pickering, 1995) as they struggled with the material aspects of inquiry in order to develop conditions for their investigations. That is, students struggled with creating mathematical measures (i.e., units) of speed and acceleration for the rolling ball in terms of its displacement in equal intervals of time."
