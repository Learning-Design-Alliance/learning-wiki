# The augmented-cues VR group completed the first real trial significantly faster than the no-training control group, while the conventional VR group's advantage was not statistically significant

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` At the first real trial the ATV group completed the task significantly faster than the RO group (t = 2.845, p = 0.009); the NC group's faster completion did not reach significance (p = 0.198), and the two VR groups did not differ significantly (p = 0.259). ## Evidence

### Cooper Natalia 2021

Cooper Natalia, Millela Ferdinando, Cant Iain, White Mark D., Meyer Georg. (2021). Transfer of training—Virtual reality training with augmented multisensory cues improves user experience during training and task performance in the real world. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0248225

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Independent-samples t-tests on first-trial completion times from the three-group experiment (N = 42). The article reports a significant RO vs ATV difference with t = 2.845, p = 0.009, favouring the ATV group; NC vs RO (p = 0.198) and NC vs ATV (p = 0.259) were not significant. No effect size is printed.

> "Independent sample t-tests revealed that at the first trial the completion time between RO and ATC group was significantly different (t = 2.845, p = 0.009) with the ATV group completing the task faster."

### Cooper Natalia 2021 (2)

Cooper Natalia, Millela Ferdinando, Cant Iain, White Mark D., Meyer Georg. (2021). Transfer of training—Virtual reality training with augmented multisensory cues improves user experience during training and task performance in the real world. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0248225

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Final-trial comparisons in the same experiment: the ATV group was descriptively fastest, but the article reports no statistically significant differences (ATV vs RO p = 0.058; ATV vs NC p = 0.847; RO vs NC p = 0.068). Equivalence was not tested; no effect sizes are printed.

> "On the last trial, the ATV group performed the task fastest when compared to other groups although the overall times were not statistically significantly different from each other (RO group: p = 0.058; NC group: p = 0.847)."
