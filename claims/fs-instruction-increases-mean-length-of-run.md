# FS treatment group significantly increased mean length of run while the control group decreased (between-group d = 1.4)

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` The treatment group improved mean length of run by more than one syllable (within-group d = 1.1) while the control group decreased, with a significant between-group gain difference (d = 1.4). [→ Michael McGuire & Jenifer Larson-Hall 2017](#michael-mcguire-jenifer-larson-hall-2017)

## Evidence

### Michael McGuire & Jenifer Larson-Hall 2017

Michael McGuire & Jenifer Larson-Hall. (2017). Teaching Formulaic Sequences in the Classroom: Effects on Spoken Fluency. TESL Canada Journal/Revue TESL du Canada, 34(3), 1–25. http://dx.doi.org/10.18806/tesl.v34i3.1271

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Same 5-week quasi-experiment (control n = 8, treatment n = 11). The between-group t-test on MLR gains was statistical, "d = 1.4"; the treatment group's within-group gain was significant (d = 1.1) and the control's was not (d = 0.17).

> "An independent-samples t-test on mean length of run (with equal variances not assumed) comparing the gains between groups from the pretest to the posttest found that the difference was statistical (t 15.6 = -3.1, p = .007, 95%CI: -2.4, -0.4, d = 1.4)."
