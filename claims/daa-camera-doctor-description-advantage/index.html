Evidence¶

Dalrymple 2013¶

Dalrymple, O., Sears, D. A., & Evangelou, D. (2013). The Relative Pedagogical Value of Disassemble/Analyze/Assemble (DAA) Activities. Advances in Engineering Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1076105

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Independent t-test on the aggregate camera doctor (defect diagnosis) scores: DAA First M ± SD = 6.07 ± 1.78 versus Lecture First 5.97 ± 1.73. No significant difference; equivalence was not tested. Measure alpha was .551.

"the independent t-test revealed that there was no difference in performance between the students in the DAA First sequence and the students in the Lecture First sequence, t (323) = .538, p = .591"

Dalrymple 2013 (2)¶

Dalrymple, O., Sears, D. A., & Evangelou, D. (2013). The Relative Pedagogical Value of Disassemble/Analyze/Assemble (DAA) Activities. Advances in Engineering Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1076105

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Post-hoc analysis of only the description-of-malfunction component (maximum aggregate 3): DAA First M ± SD = 1.26 ± 0.90 versus Lecture First 1.06 ± 0.87. No effect size printed.

"The independent t-test revealed that students in the DAA First sequence performed significantly better on these questions than students in the Lecture First sequence, t (323) = 2.026, p = .044"
