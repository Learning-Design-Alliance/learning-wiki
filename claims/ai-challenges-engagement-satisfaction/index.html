Evidence¶

Ana Katalinic 2026¶

Ana Katalinic, Vanja Slavuj, Danijela Jaksic. (2026). Artificial Intelligence in Online Education: A Systematic Review of Its Impact on Learner Engagement and Satisfaction. Educ. Sci. 2026, 16, 389. https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci16030389

q2 · i? · review · r2

In its RQ2 synthesis of the 30 included studies, the review identifies seven challenge factors (ethics/privacy, over-reliance, bias, accuracy, motivation impact, usability, cost) and warns that unaddressed risks may lower satisfaction and engagement.

"If these risks are not properly addressed, learners may hesitate to use AI tools, potentially lowering their satisfaction and engagement levels."

Ana Katalinic 2026 (2)¶

Ana Katalinic, Vanja Slavuj, Danijela Jaksic. (2026). Artificial Intelligence in Online Education: A Systematic Review of Its Impact on Learner Engagement and Satisfaction. Educ. Sci. 2026, 16, 389. https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci16030389

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

The review reports, citing Bagunaid et al. (2022), that AI prediction and recommendation systems can misclassify performance and give suboptimal guidance. It also cites Sasaki et al. (2023) on engagement/sentiment misclassification in small samples.

"AI-based performance prediction and recommendation systems can fail to accurately evaluate individual student progress, with slow processing and misclassification of performance levels leading to suboptimal guidance and negatively affecting learner satisfaction and engagement"
