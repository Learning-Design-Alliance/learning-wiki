# Prior math self-concept moderated positive feedback on self-concept: positive feedback strengthened pre-existing self-concept

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Prior math self-concept moderated the positive-feedback-versus-control effect on post math self-concept (β = .16, p = .042): students with low prior self-concept reported lower self-concept after positive feedback than controls, while high prior self-concept students reported higher. [→ Van der Beek 2025](#van-der-beek-2025)

## Evidence

### Van der Beek 2025

Van der Beek, J.P.J.; Van der Ven, S.H.G.; Van de Weijer-Bergsma, E.; Kroesbergen, E.H. (2025). How feedback affects emotions, performance and self-concept in mathematics. Learning and Instruction 99, 102169. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102169

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Interaction in Model 2 of the randomized path analysis (n = 219); the plot (Fig. 6 Panel B) shows positive feedback "seemed to strengthen students' pre-existing perceptions of their math competence", with low prior self-concept students scoring below controls.

> "Model 2 also showed a significant interaction effect between prior math self-concept and positive feedback (i.e., the positive feedback group versus the control group) on post math self-concept, β = .16, SE = .08, p = .042."
