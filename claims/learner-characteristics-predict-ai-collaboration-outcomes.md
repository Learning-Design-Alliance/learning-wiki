# SRL skills and prior knowledge predict revision performance, while SRL predicts higher and prior knowledge lower cognitive load

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In robust main-effects models, SRL skills and pretest CSEM score positively predicted revision performance; SRL positively and pretest CSEM negatively predicted cognitive load; GenAI experience was only marginally significant for revision performance. [→ Huang 2026](#huang-2026)

## Evidence

### Huang 2026

Huang, B., Xie, L., Liu, Y., Liu, X., Tang, H., Feng, X., Qiao, C., Guo, Q., Wu, C., & Bao, L. (2026). From answer machine to collaborative partner: impact of structured generative AI training on scientific problem solving in STEM education. International Journal of STEM Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40594-026-00652-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Robust main-effects model for revision performance (pseudo R² = 0.580) with group as covariate. For cognitive load (pseudo R² = 0.187), SRL skills positively (β = 2.067, p < 0.01) and pretest CSEM negatively (β = -2.163, p < 0.001) predicted load; GenAI experience did not (p = 0.752).

> "SRL skills had a significant positive effect ( β = 2.556, 95% CI [0.791, 4.516], p < 0.01). The pretest CSEM score had a significant positive effect (β = 7.513, 95% CI [5.718, 9.514], p < 0.001). GenAI experience had a marginally significant positive effect (β = 1.349, 95% CI [-0.234, 2.935], p = 0.100)"
