## Subclaims
`q3 i?` EFL learners who constructed concept maps after reading each text scored significantly higher on the Watson-Glaser Critical Thinking Appraisal at posttest than control learners. [→ Khodadady 2011](#khodadady-2011)

## Evidence

### Khodadady 2011

Khodadady, E., & Ghanizadeh, A. (2011). The Impact of Concept Mapping on EFL Learners' Critical Thinking Ability. English Language Teaching, 4(4). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v4n4p49

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pretest–posttest control-group experiment: 36 upper-intermediate/advanced EFL learners randomly assigned to experimental (n=18) and control (n=18) groups; experimental group built concept maps after each passage for 20 sessions. Posttest CTA means were 54.88 (experimental) vs 45.11 (control), "t= 3.527, p<.05"; no effect size printed.

> "Table 6 presents the results of T-Test run on CTA held as a posttest. As can be seen, there is a statistically significant effect of concept mapping on the learners' critical thinking ability (t= 3.527, p<.05)."
