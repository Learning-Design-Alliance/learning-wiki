# Poor and minority students are systematically taught by underqualified teachers, and the inequity is driven more by race than class

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Ingersoll's national tabulations show minority and poor youngsters are systematically taught by teachers with the least content knowledge, with higher percentages of classes taught by teachers lacking a major in field in high-poverty and high-minority schools. [→ Haycock 1998](#haycock-1998)

## Evidence

### Haycock 1998

Haycock, Kati. (1998). Good Teaching Matters: How Well-Qualified Teachers Can Close the Gap. Thinking K-16, v3 n2. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED457260

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

National tabulations on out-of-field teaching prepared for the Education Trust by Richard Ingersoll from the 1993-94 Schools and Staffing Survey, comparing percentages of secondary classes taught by teachers lacking a major in field across low- versus high-poverty and low- versus high-minority schools. No effect size is printed.

> "minority and poor youngstersthe very youngsters who are most dependent on their teachers for content knowledgeare systematically taught by teachers with the least content knowledge."

### Haycock 1998 (2)

Haycock, Kati. (1998). Good Teaching Matters: How Well-Qualified Teachers Can Close the Gap. Thinking K-16, v3 n2. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED457260

`q2 · i?` · `review · r1`

Kain and Singleton's analysis of Texas data comparing teacher quality for poor children by race, finding poor white children more likely to have well qualified teachers than poor black children; the article presents this as evidence the inequity is not driven wholly by finances. No effect size is printed.

> "Poor white children, it turns out, appear to have a higher likelihood of having well qualified teachers than poor black children."
