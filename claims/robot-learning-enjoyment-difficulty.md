# Students using real programmable robots enjoyed learning more and found the material less difficult than control students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The experimental group enjoyed learning significantly more than the control group (t = 3.87; p < 0.01). [→ Pásztor et al. 2010](#pasztor-et-al-2010)
`q2 i?` The control group felt the material more difficult than the experimental group (t = 1.96; p = 0.03). [→ Pásztor et al. 2010 (2)](#pasztor-et-al-2010-2)

## Evidence

### Pásztor et al. 2010

Attila PÁSZTOR, Róbert PAP-SZIGETI, Erika LAKATOS TÖRÖK. (2010). Effects of Using Model Robots in the Education of Programming. Informatics in Education, 9(1), 133–140. https://doi.org/10.15388/infedu.2010.09

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-test Likert-style questionnaire on enjoyment and difficulty administered to both sub-samples (experimental nexp = 73, control nctrl = 76); the enjoyment difference is reported significant with no printed effect size.

> "Members of the experimental group enjoyed learning more than members of the control group (xexp =3 .47; xctrl =2 .96; t =3 .87; p< 0.01)."

### Pásztor et al. 2010 (2)

Attila PÁSZTOR, Róbert PAP-SZIGETI, Erika LAKATOS TÖRÖK. (2010). Effects of Using Model Robots in the Education of Programming. Informatics in Education, 9(1), 133–140. https://doi.org/10.15388/infedu.2010.09

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same post-test questionnaire measure of perceived difficulty in the two sub-samples; the article reports the control group rated the material as more difficult, significant at p = 0.03, with no effect size printed.

> "the control group's students felt the material more difﬁcult than the experimental group's students (xexp =3 .07; xctrl =3 .35; t =1 .96; p =0 .03)."
