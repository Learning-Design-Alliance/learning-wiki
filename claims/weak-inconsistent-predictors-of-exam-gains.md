## Evidence

### Hoskins Tyler D. 2017

Hoskins Tyler D., Gantz J. D., Chaffee Blake R., Arlinghaus Kel, Wiebler James, Hughes Michael, Fernandes Joyce J. (2017). Effectiveness of a Low-Cost, Graduate Student–Led Intervention on Study Habits and Performance in Introductory Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-01-0004

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Multiple regression with backward selection on Fall 2014 data (F(5, 38) = 5.08, p = 0.0012, R2 = 0.4008). The article notes that, counterintuitively, decreases in map content scores and number of arrows were associated with exam improvement.

> "After backward selection, the best model explained 40.08% of the variance in change in exam scores and included the intercept, lecture section C, map organization, map content, number of nodes in concept maps, and number of arrows in concept maps (Table 4A)."

### Hoskins Tyler D. 2017 (2)

Hoskins Tyler D., Gantz J. D., Chaffee Blake R., Arlinghaus Kel, Wiebler James, Hughes Michael, Fernandes Joyce J. (2017). Effectiveness of a Low-Cost, Graduate Student–Led Intervention on Study Habits and Performance in Introductory Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-01-0004

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Spring 2015 regression (F(3, 16) = 8.10, p = 0.0027, R2 = 0.6514) was based on survey responses from only 17 of 44 focal students; the outline/map-only model failed to predict exam-score change.

> "The best model after forward, stepwise selection explained 65.14% of the variance in change in exam scores and included effects of the intercept, number of arrows on concept maps, number of linking words on concept maps, and regularity of reviewing"

### Hoskins Tyler D. 2017 (3)

Hoskins Tyler D., Gantz J. D., Chaffee Blake R., Arlinghaus Kel, Wiebler James, Hughes Michael, Fernandes Joyce J. (2017). Effectiveness of a Low-Cost, Graduate Student–Led Intervention on Study Habits and Performance in Introductory Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-01-0004

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The authors' own interpretation of the four regression models: variables remaining in best models were largely inconsistent across semesters and no coefficient stood out with a particularly large slope; they acknowledge possible low statistical power.

> "Thus, our primary conclusion from this set of analyses is that there is no single habit or technique that consistently underlies the impact of our course on exam scores, nor is there a consistent subset of predictors."
