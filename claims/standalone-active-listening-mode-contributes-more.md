# Training active listening as a stand-alone component before other interpreting skills contributes more to selective listening development than training it as a built-in part

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` When active listening was trained intensively as a stand-alone component for six weeks before other interpreting skills training, students made more progress in selective listening skill than when it was built into interpreting skills training. ## Evidence

### Tongtong Zhang & Zhiwei Wu 2017

Tongtong Zhang & Zhiwei Wu. (2017). The Impact of Consecutive Interpreting Training on the L2 Listening Competence Enhancement. English Language Teaching, 10(1). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v10n1p72

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Conclusion of the 16-week two-group experiment (CG built-in mode, EG stand-alone mode; same content, only the arrangement of active listening training differed). The authors state the stand-alone mode produced more selective-listening progress; the supporting statistics are the post-test summary difference (t=-3.29, p=0.002).

> "When active listening was trained intensively as a stand-alone component  for six weeks before other interpre ting skills training started, students achieved more progress in selective listening skill development, as opposed to trained as a built-in part incorporated into interpreting skills training."
