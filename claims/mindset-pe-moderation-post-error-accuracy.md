# Growth mindset predicts post-error accuracy for children with small Pe amplitudes but not large Pe amplitudes (exploratory moderation)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In an exploratory regression, growth mindset was significantly related to post-error accuracy for children with small Pe difference amplitudes (b = 0.04, p = 0.001) but not large amplitudes (b = −0.001, p = 0.92). [→ Schroder Hans S. 2017](#schroder-hans-s-2017)

## Evidence

### Schroder Hans S. 2017

Schroder Hans S., Fisher Megan E., Lin Yanli, Lo Sharon L., Danovitch Judith H., Moser Jason S. (2017). Neural evidence for enhanced attention to mistakes among school-aged children with a growth mindset. Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2017.01.004

`q2 · i?`

Exploratory linear regression (predictors mean-centered) predicting post-error accuracy from mindset, Pe difference, and their interaction; the overall model predicted 11% of the variance and the Mindset x Pe difference interaction was significant (β = −0.21, p = 0.02).

> "growth mindset was significantly related to post-error accuracy for children with small Pe amplitudes (b = 0.04, p = 0.001, 95% CI: 0.02–.06) but not for children with large Pe amplitudes (b = −0.001, p = 0.92, 95% CI: −0.02 to 0.02)."
