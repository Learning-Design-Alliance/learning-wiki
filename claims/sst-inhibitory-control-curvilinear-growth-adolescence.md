# Task-based inhibitory control (Stop Signal Task) improves curvilinearly from ages 11 to 15, with growth slowing around middle adolescence

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Latent growth curve models showed SSRT declined linearly and quadratically across ages 11–15, indicating curvilinear improvement in task-based inhibitory control with growth slowing around middle adolescence. [→ Fosco 2019](#fosco-2019)
`q3 i?` There were significant individual differences in both initial levels and rate of change of task-based inhibitory control, and worse inhibitory control at age 11 was associated with a steeper rate of improvement. [→ Fosco 2019](#fosco-2019)

## Evidence

### Fosco 2019

Fosco, W. D., Hawk, L. W. Jr., Colder, C. R., Meisel, S. N., & Lengua, L. J. (2019). The development of inhibitory control in adolescence and prospective relations with delinquency. Journal of Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.adolescence.2019.08.008

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Latent growth curve modeling of three annual Stop Signal Task assessments in a community sample of 387 adolescents (96% retention Waves 1–3), with age centered at 11. The model showed "the average SSRT at age 11 was 206 ms" with significant linear and quadratic decline; significant intercept (σ2 = 3841, p<.001) and slope (σ2 = 236, p=.023) variance indicated individual differences in level and change.

> "the average SSRT at age 11 was 206 ms and showed both a linear, b=−39 ms, p<.001, and quadratic decline across age, b=5 ms, p<.001"
