## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Word reading had a statistically significant direct effect on reading comprehension for both ELs (β = .70) and non-ELs (β = .93) in a multigroup SEM of struggling fourth-grade readers. [→ Cho 2019](#cho-2019)

## Evidence

### Cho 2019

Cho, E., Capin, P., Roberts, G., Roberts, G. J., & Vaughn, S. (2019). Examining Sources and Mechanisms of Reading Comprehension Difficulties: Comparing English Learners and Non-English Learners within the Simple View of Reading. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000332

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multigroup structural equation model of 446 struggling fourth-grade readers (ELs and non-ELs) fit to the elaborated SVR; for the EL group, word reading showed a significant direct effect on reading comprehension, with the quoted standardized coefficient.

> "Listening comprehension (β = .61, SE = .10, p < .05) and word reading (β = .70, SE = .07, p < .05) had direct effects on reading comprehension, but vocabulary (β = .01, SE = .10, p = .88) did not."
