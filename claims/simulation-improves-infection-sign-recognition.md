Borges Francisca Edinária de Sousa; Pereira Francisco Gilberto Fernandes; de Aguiar Maria Isis Freire; Fernandes Bruna Karen Cavalcante; Machado Ana Larissa Gomes; Beserra Eveline Pinheiro; Caetano Joselany Áfio. `q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

In the before-and-after study of 50 students, Table 2 reports vital-sign checking 16(32.0) to 43(86.0) and use of pain scales 9(18.0) to 37(74.0), D = 0.87; infection-sign items such as odor reached 50(100.0) with D = 0.89, a "significant frequency of correct answers" result.

> "The verification of vital signs, as well as the use of scales to assess pain, periwound skin assessment and the presence of signs of infection, especially pain, heat, redness and odor, were items that stood out for presenting a significant frequency of correct answers after clinical simulation."
