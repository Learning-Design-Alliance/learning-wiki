# The negative response time–likelihood trend holds for boys (R=-0.22) but not for girls (R=+0.04)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Fast boys' responses are, on average, more likely than slow ones (Rboys=-0.22±0.08), while girls' responses seem equally likely regardless of response time (Rgirls=+0.04±0.08). [→ Srdjan Verbić & Boris Tomić 2008](#srdjan-verbic-boris-tomic-2008)

## Evidence

### Srdjan Verbić & Boris Tomić 2008

Srdjan Verbić & Boris Tomić. (2008). Test item response time and the response likelihood. Institute for Education Quality and Evaluation. http://sepp.ceo.edu.rs/moodle/mod/resource/view.php?id=25

`q2 · i1`

Correlational analysis of characteristic response time versus mean response likelihood for all students in the physics trial test. Overall the trend was weak (Rall=-0.08±0.05), but it differed significantly by gender, with the decreasing trend evident only among boys.

> "This trend is significantly different for boys and girls alone: Rboys=-0.22±0.08 and Rgirls=+0.04±0.08. Fast boys’ responses are, in average, more likely than for slow ones."
