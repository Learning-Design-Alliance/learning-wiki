Vu 2021¶

Vu, T., Magis-Weinberg, L., Jansen, B. R. J., van Atteveldt, N., Jansen, T. W. P. J., Lee, N. C., van der Maas, H. L. J., Raijmakers, M. E. J., Sachisthal, M. S. M., & Meeter, M. (2021). Motivation-Achievement Cycles in Learning: a Literature Review and Research Agenda. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09616-7

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing Ehm et al. (2019), that model choice changed conclusions: "a cross-lagged panel model (CLPM) supported reciprocal motivation-achievement relations, other models did not". The review attributes the RI-CLPM advantage to Hamaker et al. (2015); the underlying studies' designs are not described here.

"Ehm et al. ( 2019) specifically found that although a cross-lagged panel model (CLPM) supported reciprocal motivation-achievement relations, other models did not — such as the random-intercept CLPM, which Hamaker et al. ( 2015) showed to be more effective than CLPM in explicitly modeling within- and between-individual changes across time."

Related Claims¶

  • EF longitudinally predicts later SRL in children, supporting the notion that EF predicts SRL but not vice versa — related
  • With young primary-school readers, REM support appeared in CLPMs but not RI-CLPMs, motivating tests with older students — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Across basic and extended RI-CLPMs and CLPMs of five-wave secondary-school data, math self-concept and achievement show consistent reciprocal (bidirectional) directional ordering — related
  • Simulation evidence shows RI-CLPM estimates are biased when the true model is a CLPM with lag-2 effects, and fit statistics cannot decide between the two — related
  • RI-CLPM on simulated PALMA-like data shows positive reciprocal cross-lagged effects between within-individual math achievement and self-concept — related
  • Adding lag-2 paths to CLPMs substantially improves fit and provides stronger controls for preexisting differences, positioning extended CLPMs as viable alternatives to RI-CLPMs — related
  • The RI-CCLPM-C indicates a positive self-concept effect on achievement even when data are generated without any direct effects between self-concept and achievement — related
  • The extended REM was robust to several alternative modeling specifications proposed by Sorjonen et al. — related
  • Reanalyses of simulated and PALMA data provide converging support for the extended reciprocal effects model, including an effect of academic self-concept on achievement — related
  • RI-CLPM random-intercept correlations between grades and emotions (median |r| = 0.46) were stronger than the undecomposed CLPM correlations (median |0.34|) — related
  • Cross-lagged effects between behavioral problems and achievement are sporadic and domain-dependent, not consistently reciprocal, across elementary school — related
  • Student-teacher closeness, conflict and dependency, students' motivation, and academic achievement inform each other across the elementary grades — related
