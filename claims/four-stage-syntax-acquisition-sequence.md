# Children follow a similar four-stage sequence in acquiring specific syntactic forms, from no usage through error-filled production to correct usage

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In acquiring specific aspects of syntax, children tend to proceed from no usage, to occasional error-free production, to increased production with errors and overgeneralizations, to correct usage. [→ Butler 1973](#butler-1973)

## Evidence

### Butler 1973

Butler, Lester G. (1973). Language Acquisition of Young Children: Major Theories and Sequences. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED094403

`q2 · i?`

The paper's Sequences and Milestones section states that although children vary greatly in pace, considerable evidence suggests all children follow a similar pattern of development (Lenneberg, 1967; Cazden, 1972). It gives past tense of come, go and take as an example: used correctly at first, later overgeneralized, with mastery of exceptions coming later.

> "children tend to proceed from (1.)no usage of a particular syntactic form to (2) occasional production with no errors or overgeneralizations to (3) increased production with errors and overgeneralizations, to finally (4) correct usage"
