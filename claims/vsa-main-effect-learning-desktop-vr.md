# Learners with higher visuospatial ability outperform lower-visuospatial-ability learners on recognition and factual knowledge when learning fish movements in Desktop-VR

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In Study 1, higher visuospatial ability was associated with higher performance on easy, medium, and difficult recognition and on factual knowledge. [→ Birgit Brucker 2024](#birgit-brucker-2024)

## Evidence

### Birgit Brucker 2024

Birgit Brucker, Georg Pardi, Fabienne Uehlin, Laura Moosmann, Martin Lachmair, Marc Halfmann, Peter Gerjets. (2024). How Learners' Visuospatial Ability and Different Ways of Changing the Perspective Influence Learning About Movements in Desktop and Immersive Virtual Reality Environments. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09895-w

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVAs in Study 1 (160 Prolific participants, between-subject interaction format, visuospatial ability as continuous factor, familiarity covariate) showed significant main effects of visuospatial ability, e.g. difficult recognition "F(1, 153) = 12.111" with "η2p = .073". Factual knowledge was also significant (F(1, 153) = 9.182, p = .003, η2p = .057).

> "there was a significant main effect on all three difficulty levels of recognition performance (easy: F(1, 153) = 4.122, MSE = 740.494, p = .044, η2p = .026; medium: F(1, 153) = 4.299, MSE = 370.965, p = .040, η2p = .027; difficult: F(1, 153) = 12.111, MSE = 243.692, p < .001, η2p = .073)"
