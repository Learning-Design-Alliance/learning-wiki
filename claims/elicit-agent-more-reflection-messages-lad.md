# In a real programming course, students with an eliciting LLM agent on a learning dashboard wrote more reflection messages than students with a telling agent

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students in the elicit condition produced significantly more reflection-category messages than students in the tell condition (z = 2.47, p = 0.013). [→ Graf 2026](#graf-2026)

## Evidence

### Graf 2026

Graf, L., Bassner, P., Anzinger, M., Dietrich, F., Krusche, S., & Poquet, O. (2026). Interactive learning dashboards: rethinking learning visualisations as engagement tools. Education and Information Technologies. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10639-026-14082-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory case study: open bottom-up coding of a random sample of learner–LLM messages (over 300 messages, Cohen's Kappa = 0.8) compared reflection-category frequency between the elicit and tell conditions. The z-test found "a z-statistic of 2.47 and a p-value of 0.013".

> "A two-proportion z-test was conducted to clarify if the difference in the frequency of a reflection between the tell and elicit condition was statistically significant (p < 0.05), revealing a z-statistic of 2.47 and a p-value of 0.013."
