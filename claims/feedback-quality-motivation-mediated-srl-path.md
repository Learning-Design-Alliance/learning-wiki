# Feedback quality influences SRL only indirectly through learning motivation, not through self-efficacy or satisfaction

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The indirect path QAF → learning motivation → SRL is significant (coefficient 0.264, p < 0.01). [→ Liang 2026](#liang-2026)
`q3 i?` The indirect paths QAF → self-efficacy → LM, QAF → self-efficacy → SRL, and QAF → satisfaction → SRL are not significant. [→ Liang 2026](#liang-2026)

## Evidence

### Liang 2026

Liang, Li, Hsu and Xu. (2026). AIGC affordance and student self-regulation in private undergraduate education: a serial mediation model of self-efficacy and learning motivation. Frontiers in Psychology. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1800950

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bootstrapped indirect-effect tests (Table 9) in the 689-student survey: QAF→LM→SRL was significant (0.264, t = 2.770, p < 0.01), while QAF→SEF→LM (0.074), QAF→SEF→SRL (0.033), QAF→SAT→SRL (0.023), and the serial QAF→SEF→LM→SRL (0.039) were not significant.

> "While the quality of AIGC assessment feedback is a strong predictor of student satisfaction, it does not directly improve SRL; its influence is channeled primarily through motivation."
