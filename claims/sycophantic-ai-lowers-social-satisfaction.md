# Sycophantic AI reduces satisfaction with real-world social interactions without reducing social time, via narrowing the perceived understanding gap between AI and humans

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r3` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Sycophantic (vs. neutral) AI lowered satisfaction with real-world social interactions (d=0.20) with no effect on time spent with others, and the effect was mediated by the narrowing AI-human understanding gap. [→ Lujain Ibrahim 2026](#lujain-ibrahim-2026)

## Evidence

### Lujain Ibrahim 2026

Lujain Ibrahim, Franziska Sofia Hafner, Myra Cheng, Cinoo Lee, Rebecca Anselmetti, Robb Willer, Luc Rocher, Diyi Yang. (2026). Sycophantic AI makes human interaction feel more effortful and less satisfying over time. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07912 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.07912](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2605.07912)

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r3`

Longitudinal study outcome (N=1364): the satisfaction shift was perceptual rather than behavioral. Sycophancy narrowed the felt-understanding gap "from 0.56 points in the neutral condition to just 0.13 points in the sycophantic condition (d= 0.25,p adj <0.001)", and the gap mediated the satisfaction effect (41% mediated).

> "Participants in the sycophantic condition reported lower satisfaction with their real-world social interactions (a preregistered exploratory measure; 5.51 vs. 5.70 on a 7-point scale;d= 0.20,p adj = 0.022) compared to those in the neutral condition. This effect was not accompanied by social withdrawal or reduced time spent with others (d=−0.03,p adj = 0.719)"
