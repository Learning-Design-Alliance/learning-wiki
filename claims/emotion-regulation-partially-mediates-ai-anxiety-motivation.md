## Evidence

### Zhang Y 2026

Zhang Y, Shi Y and Lu J (2026). The relationship between AI anxiety and academic motivation among university students: the mediating role of emotion regulation and the moderating role of gender. Front. Psychol. 17:1918525. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1918525

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

PROCESS Model 4 mediation test with 5,000 bias-corrected bootstrap resamples in the survey of 1,484 students. The indirect association through emotion regulation was significant, with the 95% confidence interval excluding zero (indirect effect = −0.076).

> "Bootstrap analysis based on 5,000 resamples indicated a significant negative indirect association between AI anxiety and academic motivation through emotion regulation [indirect effect = −0.076, Boot SE = 0.016, 95% CI ( −0.108, −0.047)]."

### Zhang Y 2026 (2)

Zhang Y, Shi Y and Lu J (2026). The relationship between AI anxiety and academic motivation among university students: the mediating role of emotion regulation and the moderating role of gender. Front. Psychol. 17:1918525. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1918525

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Authors' conclusion from the mediation models (Table 2, Figure 1): the direct association between AI anxiety and academic motivation remained significant alongside the significant indirect association, consistent with partial mediation.

> "Because both the direct and indirect associations were significant, emotion regulation partially mediated the association between AI anxiety and academic motivation (Figure 1)."
