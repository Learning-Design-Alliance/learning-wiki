## Evidence

### Bayley Julie E. and Brown Katherine E 2015

Bayley Julie E. and Brown Katherine E. (2015). Translating group programmes into online formats: establishing the acceptability of a parents’ sex and relationships communication serious game. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-015-2545-0

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

A 2x2 (Time*condition) MANOVA in the randomised evaluation assessed TPB constructs. There was a small main effect of time only; the time*condition interaction for attitude approached but did not reach significance (p = .06).

> "Results showed a small main effect of time only (F (4, 88) = 2.515, p = .047; partial η2 = .103). Within subjects univariate tests showed a change in attitude by time (F(1, 91), = 6.798, p = .011, partial η2 = .07) and time*condition interaction effects approached significance for attitude (F (1,91) = 3.616, p = .06, partial η2 = .038)."

### Bayley Julie E. and Brown Katherine E 2015 (2)

Bayley Julie E. and Brown Katherine E. (2015). Translating group programmes into online formats: establishing the acceptability of a parents’ sex and relationships communication serious game. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-015-2545-0

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Telephone interviews with 13 parents and 4 stakeholder gatekeepers three months post intervention gathered qualitative feedback on transfer of learning into real life. Responses suggested the game supports reflection on and improvement of communication skills.

> "Responses (summarised in Table 9) suggest the game offers parents the means to reflect on and improve their existing communication skills. Responses indicate instances of improved skills and actual behavioural changes underpinned by changes in attitude."
