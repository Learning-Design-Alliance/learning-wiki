## Evidence

### Stanisław D. Głażek 2008

Stanisław D. Głażek. (2008). Heuristic model of teaching. https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.4796 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0804.4796](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0804.4796)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument from the MOT (Section III.D): the article reasons that experiencing effective teaching as students motivates trainees to incorporate it, and cites the University of Washington Physics Education Group program and Stanislavski's actor training as examples sharing the same elements.

> "The training must itself be an example of eﬀective teaching so that the trainees can experience the process ﬁrst from the standpoint of students."

### Stanisław D. Głażek 2008 (2)

Stanisław D. Głażek. (2008). Heuristic model of teaching. https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.4796 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0804.4796](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0804.4796)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Estimate reported in Section III.D, drawing on cited studies [53, 54] of in-service science teacher training; the article adds that at least a year of further study should follow in which trainees implement effective teaching at school. The estimate is attributed to prior research, not measured here.

> "The minimal period of training required for acquisition of physical concepts of mass, force, energy, electric current, and magnetic ﬁeld, can be estimated as not shorter than about two months of study every day of the week [53, 54]."
