# Metacognitive measures show large variability, and online measures relate to each other more than to offline measures

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The 128 participants showed large variability across all metacognitive measures, and the authors interpret the pattern as online measures being associated with each other but not much with offline measures. [→ Terneusen 2024](#terneusen-2024)

## Evidence

### Terneusen 2024

Terneusen, A., Quaedflieg, C., van Heugten, C., Ponds, R., & Winkens, I. (2024). The many facets of metacognition: comparing multiple measures of metacognition in healthy individuals. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-023-09350-1

`q2 · i?`

Descriptive finding from the single online correlational study of 128 healthy volunteers; the article reports "a large variability in scores on all the different metacognitive measures". The online/offline dissociation is the authors' interpretation of the regression pattern, not a separately tested correlation.

> "The 128 included participants show a large variability in scores on all the different metacognitive measures."
