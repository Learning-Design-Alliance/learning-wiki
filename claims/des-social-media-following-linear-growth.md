# DES Facebook and Twitter followings grew roughly linearly (about 5 new followers per day), and daily original content alone did not build the following exponentially

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` From October 2014 to April 2017 DES Facebook likes grew from about 5,100 to about 8,000 and Twitter followers from about 30 to about 1,400, increasing roughly linearly at about 5 new followers per day. [→ R. C. Wolf 2018](#r-c-wolf-2018)
`q2 i?` The authors found that providing daily original content was not sufficient to exponentially build a social media following. [→ R. C. Wolf 2018 (2)](#r-c-wolf-2018-2)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Observational analysis of DES account growth metrics from Facebook Insights and Twitter Analytics. The followings "have increased roughly linearly, with ∼5 new followers added per day" from October 2014 to April 2017.

> "From October 2014 to April 2017, the number of Facebook page “likes” increased from ∼5, 100 to ∼8, 000 and the number of Twitter followers increased from ∼30 to ∼1, 400. Since the formation of the EPOC, both the DES Facebook and Twitter followings have increased roughly linearly, with ∼5 new followers added per day."

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Authors' experience-based finding from running DEST4TD: "providing daily original content is not suﬃcient to “exponentially” build a social media following," with the following at the time of writing increasing by only one or two users per week.

> "providing daily original content is not suﬃcient to “exponentially” build a social media following (at the time of writing, the DES social media following was increasing by one or two users per week)"
