## Evidence

### Justyna P. Zwolak 2018

Justyna P. Zwolak, Remy Dou and Eric Brewe. (2018). Student perceptions of the value of out-of-class interactions: Attitudes vs. Practice. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/perc.2017.pr.115](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.115)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparison of self-reported out-of-class collaboration between one-semester (N = 19) and two-semester (N = 63) MI students. The two-semester group reached out when "really stuck" at 51% versus 26%; only descriptive percentages are printed, with no effect size.

> "students who took MI-M prior to taking MI-EM are much more likely to reach out for help when the problem is more challenging and they are “really stuck” (P3; 26% and 51% for group (1) and (2), respectively)."

### Justyna P. Zwolak 2018 (2)

Justyna P. Zwolak, Remy Dou and Eric Brewe. (2018). Student perceptions of the value of out-of-class interactions: Attitudes vs. Practice. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/perc.2017.pr.115](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.115)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same survey comparison: for individual-exam preparation (P7), 59% of two-semester students versus 32% of one-semester students reported studying with peers — "nearly twice as likely"; no effect size is printed.

> "Students who took MI-M were nearly twice as likely to study with peers when preparing for individual exams than students with no prior MI experience (P7; 59% and 32%, respectively)."

### Justyna P. Zwolak 2018 (3)

Justyna P. Zwolak, Remy Dou and Eric Brewe. (2018). Student perceptions of the value of out-of-class interactions: Attitudes vs. Practice. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/perc.2017.pr.115](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.115)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Across the 11 practice options (Fig. 5), the one-semester group reported "less favor- able approaches toward group work" on 8 of 11 choices, with two options statistically significant; no effect size is printed.

> "We ﬁnd that for 8 out of the 11 choices responses from stu- dents with no prior experience with MI present less favor- able approaches toward group work than from students who took MI-M, with two options exhibiting statistically signiﬁ- cant differences (see Fig. 5)."
