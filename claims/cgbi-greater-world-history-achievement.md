# Computer game-based instruction produces significantly greater world history learning achievement than non-game-based instruction in Chinese senior high school students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students taught World History via CGBI scored significantly higher on the posttest than students taught the same content via NCGBI. [→ Yu Zhonggen 2014](#yu-zhonggen-2014)

## Evidence

### Yu Zhonggen 2014

Yu Zhonggen, Yu Wei Hua, Fan Xiaohui, Wang Xiao. (2014). An Exploration of Computer Game-Based Instruction in the "World History" Class in Secondary Education: A Comparative Study in China. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0096865

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experiment comparing two randomly selected classes (103 students aged 17-18) taught by the same instructor for 16 weeks; paired samples comparison of posttests showed CGBI (M = 18.22) significantly higher than NCGBI (M = 16.92), described as "significantly (p  =  .05) higher".

> "The mean score of posttest in CGBI (M  =  18.22), however, was significantly (p  =  .05) higher than that in NCGBI (M  =  16.92) at the significance level .05. This demonstrated that CGBI produced significantly greater learning achievement than NCGBI."
