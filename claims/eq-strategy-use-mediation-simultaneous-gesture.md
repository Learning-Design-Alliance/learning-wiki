# Simultaneous speech-and-gesture instruction increases children's spoken use of the conceptual Equalizer strategy, which post-hoc mediates lasting learning

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q2`–`q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Children in the simultaneous condition produced the Equalizer strategy in spoken explanations significantly more than children in either sequential condition. [→ Congdon Eliza L. 2017](#congdon-eliza-l-2017)
`q2 i?` A post-hoc bootstrapped mediation analysis found a significant indirect effect of condition on composite follow-up performance through Equalizer strategy use. [→ Congdon Eliza L. 2017 (2)](#congdon-eliza-l-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Congdon Eliza L. 2017

Congdon Eliza L., Novack Miriam A., Brooks Neon, Hemani-Lopez Naureen, O'Keefe Lucy, Goldin-Meadow Susan. (2017). Better together: Simultaneous presentation of speech and gesture in math instruction supports generalization and retention. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2017.03.005

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of children's spoken explanations of basic problems at immediate posttest (coded by three independent coders, Kappas .81–.89): 70% of the simultaneous group's explanations used EQ, versus 68% AS in the speech-only group and 41% EQ in the speech-then-gesture group.

> "Indeed, in a regression model predicting EQ strategy use by condition, we found that children in the S G condition produced the EQ strategy significantly more than children in either the S S condition (β = 2.54, t = 4.19, p < .001) or the S G condition (β = 1.54, t = 2.54, p = .01)."

### Congdon Eliza L. 2017 (2)

Congdon Eliza L., Novack Miriam A., Brooks Neon, Hemani-Lopez Naureen, O'Keefe Lucy, Goldin-Meadow Susan. (2017). Better together: Simultaneous presentation of speech and gesture in math instruction supports generalization and retention. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2017.03.005

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc mediation analysis (Figure 5) on the same sample: condition predicted EQ use, EQ use predicted composite follow-up performance, and the direct effect of condition became non-significant (β = 1.02, p = .41) when EQ use was included.

> "A bootstrapping estimation approach with 1000 samples (Preacher & Hayes, 2004) confirmed that there was a significant indirect effect of condition on the composite performance score through use of the EQ strategy produced prior to all of the problems included in creating the composite score (point estimate = 2.35, 95% confidence interval =.740 to 4.784)."
