# Transcription directly constrains text generation quality in Grades 4-6 but not in Grades 7-9

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The effect of transcription on text generation was significant in Grades 4-6 (β = .60, p = .01) but not in Grades 7-9 (β = .26, p = .23). [→ Limpo 2013](#limpo-2013)

## Evidence

### Limpo 2013

Limpo, T., & Alves, R. A. (2013). Modeling writing development: Contribution of transcription and self-regulation to Portuguese students' text generation quality. Journal of Educational Psychology, 105, 401-413. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031391

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Structural equation modeling on data from 171 students in Grades 4-6 and 205 in Grades 7-9 found the transcription-to-text-generation path "significant in Grades 4-6 (βT→TG = .60, p = .01)" but non-significant in Grades 7-9; equivalence was not tested. No standardized effect size beyond the path coefficient is printed.

> "The effect of transcription on text generation (T→TG) was significant in Grades 4-6 (βT→TG = .60, p = .01), but it was not in Grades 7-9 (βT→TG = .26, p = .23)."
