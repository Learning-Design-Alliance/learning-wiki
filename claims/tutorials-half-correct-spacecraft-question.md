## Evidence

### Rachel E. Scherr 2002

Rachel E. Scherr, Peter S. Shaffer, and Stamatis Vokos. (2002). The challenge of changing deeply-held student beliefs about the relativity of simultaneity. https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1507792

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Post-tutorial results on the Spacecraft question (Table II: introductory N=173, advanced N=70). The article reports the tendency to reason from absolute simultaneity or signal reception time decreased for both populations, who did substantially better than graduate students without tutorial instruction.

> "Both introductory and advanced students seem to benefit from working through the tutorials.  About half of each group answered correctly on the Spacecraft  question whe n it was given after tutorial instruction."

### Rachel E. Scherr 2002 (2)

Rachel E. Scherr, Peter S. Shaffer, and Stamatis Vokos. (2002). The challenge of changing deeply-held student beliefs about the relativity of simultaneity. https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1507792

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Results on the Spacecraft question before tutorial instruction (Table II: introductory N=67 and N=73; advanced N=20 and N=93; graduates N=23; interviews N=11). Many students claimed Mt. Rainier erupts first because the observer is closer, or that corrected events would be simultaneous in the spacecraft frame.

> "Performance at all levels is poor, both before and after traditional instruction.  Fewer than 30% of the students in each population have given a correct response (with or without correct reasoning)."
