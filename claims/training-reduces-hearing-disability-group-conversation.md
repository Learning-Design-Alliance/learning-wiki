## Evidence

### Ferguson M. A. 2014

Ferguson M. A., Henshaw H., Clark D. P. A., Moore D. R. (2014). Benefits of Phoneme Discrimination Training in a Randomized Controlled Trial of 50- to 74-Year-Olds With Mild Hearing Loss. Ear and Hearing. https://doi.org/10.1097/AUD.0000000000000020

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Combined-group pre- to post-training analysis of GHABP scales in the RCT. Disability showed a "highly significant effect of training" with d = 0.51; Handicap's d = 0.23 did not reach significance.

> "Post hoc testing showed a highly significant effect of training for Disability (t(42) = 3.45, p = 0.001; d = 0.51) but not for Handicap (t(43) = 1.53, p = 0.13; d = 0.23)"

### Ferguson M. A. 2014 (2)

Ferguson M. A., Henshaw H., Clark D. P. A., Moore D. R. (2014). Benefits of Phoneme Discrimination Training in a Randomized Controlled Trial of 50- to 74-Year-Olds With Mild Hearing Loss. Ear and Hearing. https://doi.org/10.1097/AUD.0000000000000020

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of the four individual GHABP Disability situations found significance only for "having a group conversation"; the combined group improved with d = 0.68, and the IT group improved significantly more than the DT group.

> "The between-group ANOVA across the first two test sessions for the “group conversation” situation was significant (F(1, 42) = 4.94, p = 0.03), indicating a significant improvement for the IT group compared with the DT group"
