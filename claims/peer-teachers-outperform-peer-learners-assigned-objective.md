## Evidence

### Bradley LJ 2025

Bradley LJ, Meyer KE, Kerr MS, Maddux SD, Heck AJ, Reeves RE, Handler EK. (2025). Do active learning techniques promote higher academic performance in an online graduate anatomy course? Anatomical Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1002/ase.70066

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Comparison of objective subgroups' examination performance in the online anatomy course. Students were divided into five groups of 31–36 for the semester, with no randomisation stated, and analysed as individual students, so the group structure is not accounted for. For each unit's objective question, for each unit's objective question, subgroup means were compared (e.g., unit 4 respiration muscles H = 14.7, p = 0.0006). The pattern held consistently across 5 units and 15 objectives; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Kruskal–Wallis tests indicated statistically significant differences between the objective subgroups for each unit (Table 2). Consistently, across 5 units and 15 objectives, students who taught the content of an objective performed significantly higher on the examination question related to that objective than students who taught the other objectives."
